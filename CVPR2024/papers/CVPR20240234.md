## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal grounding methods either depend on human-annotated bounding boxes and captions (limiting generalizability) or leverage self-supervised video-text pairs but lack spatial localization ability. No prior approach jointly learns both the temporal extent and spatial region of actions in long, untrimmed, multi-action videos using only noisy ASR narration as supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240234/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes 1.2M untrimmed instructional videos from HowTo100M paired with their automatically generated ASR transcripts, which are noisy and only loosely temporally aligned with visible events. At inference, given an untrimmed video (3–5 minutes, ~41 action instances on average) and a free-text action query (e.g., 'crack egg'), the model outputs temporal start/end boundaries for the queried action and a per-frame spatial attention heatmap, which together form a 3D spatio-temporal tube. The spatial output is evaluated via pointing-game accuracy (heatmap peak within GT box) and IoU-based mAP over predicted tubes.

## Method

The core architecture learns two complementary representations: a global one (full-frame [CLS] or mean-pooled features) that encodes holistic scene context for temporal localization, and a local one (spatio-temporal grid tokens) that captures fine-grained word-to-region correspondences for spatial grounding. A key component is a frame selection strategy that formulates the problem of choosing T relevant frames from U candidates as a Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport assignment between projected word features and frame features, enforcing diverse label distribution to avoid saturating on a single frame. Local features are processed through stacked cross-attention and self-attention layers to produce contextual token representations, while both global and local branches are trained with Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE) loss using imposter samples. At inference, the global branch first performs temporal action detection via per-frame similarity matching with a threshold, and the predicted action class is then fed into the local branch to compute the spatial attention heatmap on the selected frames.

## Results

On the proposed GroundingYoutube benchmark for spatio-temporal grounding in untrimmed videos, the best model (CLIP* backbone) achieves 28.26 mAP (IoU 0.1:0.5) and 11.53 IoU+Pointing accuracy, outperforming the strongest combined baseline MIL-NCE+RegionCLIP (22.33 mAP) by 6 points. On spatial grounding (YouCook-Inter pointing game), the method reaches 58.35% accuracy, surpassing RegionCLIP (52.84%) and GLIP (53.62%). On temporal grounding (MiningYoutube), the method achieves 20.33 IoU and 29.67 IoD, improving over MIL-NCE (18.69 IoU) and the original Mining:MLP (9.80 IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method substantially outperforms all self-supervised and weakly-supervised baselines on the primary spatio-temporal grounding task: it achieves 28.26 mAP vs. 15.11 (MIL-NCE), 16.78 (RegionCLIP), 12.74 (CLIP), and 1.56 (GLIP), with the largest gap over CoMMA (1.26 mAP). The strongest combined baseline, MIL-NCE+RegionCLIP (22.33 mAP), is surpassed by 6 mAP points, demonstrating that jointly learning global and local representations is more effective than stitching together separate temporal and spatial models. On spatial grounding (YouCook-Inter), the method leads all self/weakly-supervised approaches (58.35% vs. 53.62% for GLIP and 52.84% for RegionCLIP), though fully-supervised TubeDETR (53.24%) and STCAT (55.90%) remain competitive in the instruction-video domain. However, on general-domain spatial grounding (V-HICO, Daly), the fully-supervised methods clearly win: STCAT reaches 85.42% and 63.94% mAP versus 71.35% and 52.78% for the proposed method, reflecting the domain gap. On temporal grounding (MiningYoutube), the method improves over MIL-NCE by ~1.6 IoU points (20.33 vs. 18.69) and nearly doubles the original Mining:MLP baseline (9.80 IoU), while spatial-focused CoMMA performs poorly (2.05 IoU) as it was not trained for temporal detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: supervised spatio-temporal grounding (TubeDETR, STCAT), multimodal self-supervised video-text learning (MIL-NCE, MCN), and label-free spatial grounding (GLIP, RegionCLIP, CoMMA). It departs from prior spatial grounding work by explicitly modeling temporal extent in untrimmed multi-action videos rather than assuming trimmed clips, and from prior temporal grounding work by adding fine-grained spatial localization via local cross-attention representations. The Sinkhorn optimal transport frame selection mechanism is a novel contribution that addresses the noisy, loosely-aligned ASR supervision problem specific to self-supervised video-text training, building on the NCE loss formulation from MIL-NCE and the instructional video setting of HowTo100M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GroundingYoutube benchmark is the first to provide dense spatio-temporal annotations for untrimmed multi-action videos, filling a gap where prior benchmarks only covered short clips or single-modality grounding. The superadditive effect of combining global and local losses (19.45 mAP vs. 7.29 + 9.28 individually) provides concrete evidence that spatial and temporal grounding require complementary representational capacities, a finding that could inform future multimodal grounding architectures. Limitations include reliance on ASR transcripts that may not reference visible events, evaluation restricted primarily to instructional/cooking video domains, and the pointing-game metric that avoids pixel-exact bounding box evaluation. Open questions include whether the frame selection strategy generalizes to non-instructional domains and whether the approach scales to longer videos with more complex action sequences.
