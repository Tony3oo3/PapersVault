## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods for face anti-spoofing rely on auxiliary modules (adversarial training, meta-learning, style ensembles) to extract domain-invariant features, but they do not guarantee convergence to flat minima in the loss landscape. When Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) is naively applied to multi-source FAS training, applying it to the full dataset yields ascending vectors dominated by a single domain, while domain-wise application produces conflicting gradients across domains. The paper addresses this by proposing a training objective that simultaneously seeks a flat minimum and enforces coherent alignment of per-domain generalization gradients with the global ERM gradient.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB face images detected and cropped via MTCNN, resized to 256×256 pixels, and produces a binary live/spoofed classification score. A ResNet-18 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet serves as the feature extractor, with supervised contrastive learning applied at an intermediate layer to promote inter-domain discriminability. Training uses SGD (lr=0.005) with weight decay, and the GAC-FAS objective introduces two hyperparameters: alignment strength γ=0.0002 and perturbation radius ρ=0.1.

## Method

GAC-FAS modifies the training loss to: L(θ;S) + E_{S_i} L(θ + ε̂_i − γ∇L(θ;S); S) + R(θ;S), where ε̂_i is the domain-specific ascending vector computed from the i-th domain's gradient. The key mechanism is the subtraction of γ∇L(θ;S) (the global ERM gradient) from the perturbation point, which shifts each domain's ascending point so that the resulting generalization gradient is coherently aligned with the ERM direction. A first-order Taylor expansion reveals this is equivalent to maximizing the inner product between per-domain perturbation gradients and the global ERM gradient, which indirectly matches all pairwise domain gradients. The approach requires no additional network modules—only a modified loss—and comes with a convergence guarantee of O(log T / √T) under standard smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions.

## Results

On the standard leave-one-out cross-domain FAS protocol across four benchmark datasets (CASIA-MFSD, Idiap Replay, MSU-MFSD, Oulu-NPU), GAC-FAS achieves the best HTER in all four settings: 8.60% (ICM→O), 4.29% (OCM→I), 5.00% (OCI→M), and 8.20% (OMI→C). The most notable gain is in OCM→I, where HTER drops from 5.86% (UDG-FAS) to 4.29%, a relative improvement of over 26%. On AUC, GAC-FAS leads in ICM→O (97.16) and OCM→I (98.87) but trails UDG-FAS in OCI→M (97.56 vs. 98.47) and OMI→C (95.16 vs. 96.76). Upon convergence (last 10 evaluations), GAC-FAS further narrows the gap, achieving HTER of 9.89/12.51/12.29/15.37 across the four settings, outperforming SA-FAS on three of four.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAC-FAS achieves the lowest HTER across all four leave-one-out settings, outperforming the strongest prior baselines: IADG [75] (8.86→8.60 on ICM→O), UDG-FAS [43] (5.86→4.29 on OCM→I, a 26% relative reduction), SSAN [65] (6.67→5.00 on OCI→M), and IADG [75] (8.70→8.20 on OMI→C). On AUC, GAC-FAS leads in ICM→O (97.16 vs. IADG's 97.14) and OCM→I (98.87 vs. UDG-FAS's 98.62), but trails UDG-FAS [43] on OCI→M (97.56 vs. 98.47) and OMI→C (95.16 vs. 96.76), indicating that the gradient-alignment objective prioritizes HTER reduction over AUC in some cross-domain directions. In the limited-source-domain setting (MI→C, MI→O), GAC-FAS reduces HTER by ~7% over IADG (24.07→16.91 on MI→C) and ~0.6% on MI→O (18.47→17.88). For unseen attacks, GAC-FAS achieves 93.39 AUC on unseen 2D attacks (vs. UDG-FAS's 92.43) and 89.27 AUC on unseen 3D attacks (vs. UDG-FAS's 87.26). In the convergence evaluation (Table 4), GAC-FAS outperforms SA-FAS [56] on HTER in three of four settings (e.g., 15.37 vs. 19.40 on OMI→C) and achieves the best TPR95 in all four, confirming that the flat-minimum objective yields more stable convergence rather than merely a better snapshot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) for its perturbation mechanism and on SAGM's gradient-matching structure for its alignment objective, but departs from both by identifying specific failure modes when these methods are applied to multi-domain FAS: dominated ascending vectors under whole-data application and gradient conflicts under domain-wise application. It also deliberately shifts the DG-for-FAS paradigm away from the dominant line of work that designs auxiliary modules (adversarial training, meta-learning, style ensembles) to explicitly extract domain-invariant features, instead arguing that the optimization landscape itself—specifically convergence to a flat minimum with cross-domain gradient coherence—is the more principled lever for generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a convergence-rate guarantee (O(log T / √T)) for its training objective and demonstrates through loss-landscape visualizations that GAC-FAS converges to visibly flatter minima than strong baselines, offering a mechanistic explanation for its generalization gains. However, the approach is validated only with ResNet-18 on four FAS datasets, the hyperparameters γ and ρ show sensitivity at extreme values, and it remains unclear whether the gradient-alignment principle transfers to other domain-generalization tasks beyond face anti-spoofing or to transformer-based backbones that are increasingly common in the field. An open question is whether the flat-minima objective can be combined with the feature-level domain-invariance strategies it critiques, potentially yielding complementary gains.
