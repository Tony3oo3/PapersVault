## Problem

Existing scanpath-based panoramic quality assessment methods either depend on costly human-collected scanpaths or decouple the scanpath generator from the quality predictor, making them suboptimal and difficult to automate. Moreover, prior scanpath-based approaches are designed for panoramic images, leaving their applicability to panoramic videos with spatiotemporally localized authentic distortions unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240817/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts panoramic videos or images in equirectangular projection (ERP) format and produces a single scalar quality score. Internally, it samples N=20 autoregressive scanpaths from a starting viewpoint, extracts 224×224-pixel viewports (90°×90° FoV) along each path, and feeds viewport sequences of fixed length 7 into the quality assessor regardless of the original video's duration or frame rate. Panoramic images are handled without modification by treating them as single-frame videos.

## Method

The method comprises a probabilistic scanpath generator and a pluggable planar quality assessor, jointly optimized end-to-end. The scanpath generator parameterizes future viewpoints as a 3-component Gaussian Mixture Model conditioned on a truncated historical path (H=5) and causal path, using relative uv coordinates for user-centricity; it is pre-trained by minimizing expected code length (equivalent to negative log-likelihood) and made differentiable via Gumbel-Softmax for component selection and a simplified reparameterization trick (diagonal covariance) for viewpoint sampling. A PID controller enforces scanpath smoothness, and differentiable viewport extraction uses bilinear interpolation with sub-gradients to handle the interpolation kernel's discontinuities. Training follows a three-stage protocol: (1) pre-train the scanpath generator on human scanpaths, (2) warm up the quality assessor with fixed scanpaths, and (3) end-to-end finetune both modules against human perceptual scores. The quality assessor can be any planar VQA model (ResNet-18, Video Swin-T, X-Clip, or Swin-B with temporal module), and the final score is the average over N=20 independently sampled scanpaths.

## Results

On the in-dataset setting, the proposed method with the Assessor360 quality assessor achieves the best SRCC/PLCC on all three datasets: 0.822/0.823 on VRVQW (authentic video distortions), 0.972/0.983 on CVIQD (compressed images), and 0.960/0.971 on OIQA (synthetic image distortions). The learned scanpath generator consistently boosts every quality assessor it is paired with, e.g., elevating the lightweight ScanpathVQA assessor (~11M params) to SRCC 0.801 on VRVQW, comparable to the much heavier GSR-S (~112M params) at 0.804. Cross-dataset results confirm that the end-to-end optimized scanpath generator restores Assessor360's generalizability (SRCC 0.872 on OIQA→CVIQD vs. 0.338 for the original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset. On VRVQW (the most challenging, with authentic spatiotemporally localized distortions), Ours(Assessor360) achieves SRCC 0.822, surpassing the next-best GSR-X (0.815) by 0.007 and the original Assessor360 (0.406) by a dramatic 0.416, demonstrating that the learned scanpath generator is the critical differentiator. On CVIQD, Ours(Assessor360) reaches SRCC 0.972 vs. Assessor360's 0.955 and GSR-X's 0.944. On OIQA, the gap is smaller but consistent: 0.960 vs. Assessor360's 0.946 and GSR-X's 0.945. Methods that ignore human viewing patterns (NIQE, MC360IQA) perform poorly on VRVQW (SRCC ≤ 0.669), confirming that scanpath-aware sampling is essential for authentic video distortions. In cross-dataset evaluation, the end-to-end optimized scanpath generator notably restores Assessor360's generalizability (SRCC 0.872 on OIQA→CVIQD vs. 0.338 for the original), suggesting that joint optimization mitigates overfitting to a single dataset's distortion distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scanpath-based PVQA line of work (Sui et al. [27, 29], Wu et al. [36]) by making the scanpath generator differentiable and jointly optimized with the quality assessor, rather than treating them as separate modules. The probabilistic scanpath formulation is directly inherited from Li et al. [16], who cast scanpath prediction as density estimation via expected code length minimization with a GMM; this paper adds Gumbel-Softmax relaxation and a simplified reparameterization trick to enable gradient flow through the sampling process. The key conceptual departure is that prior scanpath-based methods either depended on human-annotated scanpaths or trained the generator independently, whereas here the generator is co-optimized with the quality predictor in an end-to-end fashion, and the framework naturally extends from panoramic images to panoramic videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The differentiable scanpath generator is a reusable component that can be plugged into any planar VQA model, offering a practical path toward fully automated, human-behavior-aware quality assessment without requiring expensive human scanpath annotations. The finding that visual context (historical video frames) contributes negligibly to quality prediction—despite adding 26M parameters—suggests that the geometric/kinematic structure of viewing behavior is the dominant signal, which could simplify future system designs. Open questions include whether the GMM-based autoregressive formulation scales to much longer viewing sessions, whether the three-stage training protocol is strictly necessary or an artifact of the specific datasets, and how the method performs on emerging formats like free-viewpoint or volumetric video.
