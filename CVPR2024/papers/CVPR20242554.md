## Problem

Existing 3D mesh shape metrics (Chamfer Distance, UHD, IoU) measure overall geometric accuracy but are insensitive to local high-frequency details, causing them to disagree with human perception that values both global shape and fine detail. Learning-based alternatives like SSFID require large training datasets and behave as black boxes with limited generalizability. The paper seeks an analytic, interpretable metric that equitably captures all frequency bands of shape information while aligning with human judgment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242554/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangle meshes (a test mesh and its ground truth) as input and outputs a single scalar shape difference score. Both meshes are first transformed into the spectrum domain via a discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator and Fourier transform, then the top 0.1% highest-frequency components are pruned as noise and the spectrum AUC is normalized to 1 to eliminate scale dependence. The extended version additionally applies a 20-dimensional learnable weight vector (interpolated across all frequencies) element-wise to the amplitude difference before integration.

## Method

SAUCD decomposes each mesh into a spectrum using a revised positive semidefinite Discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator (the standard Cotan formula modified with symmetric normalization A_i^{-1/2}A_j^{-1/2} and absolute-value edge weights to guarantee non-negative eigenvalues). The shape difference is then the integral of the absolute amplitude difference between the two normalized spectrum curves, i.e., the area of the non-overlapping region. The extended Human-adjusted SAUCD multiplies the amplitude difference by a learnable weight function w(λ) that encodes human sensitivity per frequency band, trained with a combined Pearson correlation, Spearman rank-order, and L2 regularization loss via 12-fold cross-validation over the Shape Grading dataset. This design ensures high-frequency details are explicitly separated from and equitably weighted against low-frequency global shape, unlike spatial-domain metrics where details are overwhelmed by overall geometry.

## Results

On the Shape Grading benchmark (868 subjects, 24,304 scores), SAUCD achieves an overall PLCC of 0.567, SROCC of 0.598, and KROCC of 0.445 with human evaluation, outperforming all baselines. The human-adjusted version (Adjusted SAUCD) further improves to PLCC 0.598, SROCC 0.611, and KROCC 0.453. The closest competitor, the learning-based SSFID, reaches only PLCC 0.363 / SROCC 0.407 / KROCC 0.322, while spatial-domain metrics (Chamfer Distance, IoU, F-score, UHD) all fall below PLCC 0.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAUCD outperforms all five baselines across all three correlation metrics. Against the strongest baseline SSFID [46] (a learning-based metric using 3D shape features), SAUCD gains +0.204 PLCC (0.567 vs. 0.363) and +0.191 SROCC (0.598 vs. 0.407), demonstrating that an analytic spectral approach can surpass a deep-learning-based metric without requiring large training data. Against spatial-domain metrics, the margins are even larger: Chamfer Distance [6] achieves only 0.097 PLCC (vs. 0.567), IoU [16] reaches 0.225, F-score [43] reaches 0.208, and UHD [47] reaches 0.231. The human-adjusted version (Adjusted SAUCD) provides a modest but consistent improvement over the base SAUCD across all objects, with the largest gain on Object 12 (PLCC 0.08 → 0.36), suggesting that learned frequency-band weights help in cases where the unweighted spectrum is less discriminative. Notably, SAUCD maintains strong per-object correlations (≥0.21 PLCC on 11 of 12 objects), indicating good generalizability across diverse categories (humans, animals, buildings, plants).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant spatial-domain paradigm for 3D mesh evaluation (Chamfer Distance, IoU, F-score, UHD) by moving the comparison into the spectral domain via a modified Discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator and mesh Fourier transform. It also contrasts with learning-based metrics like SSFID, which rely on deep networks trained on large datasets, by proposing a fully analytic metric that requires no learned feature extractor. The core technical building block is the Cotan formula DLBO from Meyer et al. [25], which the authors revise to guarantee positive semidefiniteness, enabling a well-defined non-negative frequency spectrum. The AUC-difference formulation and the lightweight learned frequency-band weighting represent the paper's specific contributions on top of this spectral geometry foundation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Shape Grading benchmark (12 objects, 7 distortion types, 4 levels, 868 subjects) fills a notable gap: most prior 3D mesh metrics were never validated against large-scale human perception studies. The finding that a simple analytic spectral metric outperforms the SOTA learning-based SSFID without any neural network is practically useful for settings where training data or compute is limited. However, the evaluation is confined to 12 objects and pairwise mesh comparison; open questions include whether the metric generalizes to shape generation (distribution-level evaluation), non-manifold or open surfaces, and whether the 20-dimensional learned weight vector transfers across object categories without retraining.
