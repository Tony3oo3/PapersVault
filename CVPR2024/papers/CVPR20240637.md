## Problem

Cross-attention control has proven highly effective for detailed, locally-controllable image editing with pre-trained diffusion models, but no large-scale video generation models are publicly available to extend this pipeline to video. Naively applying image editing frame-by-frame (Image-P2P) fails to maintain semantic consistency across frames, and existing video editing methods (e.g., Tune-A-Video, Dreamix) cannot avoid altering unrelated regions when editing a local object.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240637/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-world video (8 or 24 frames at 512×512 resolution), a source text prompt describing the original content, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit. It produces an edited video of the same length where the target object is replaced or modified while preserving the original pose, motion, and background. The pipeline runs on a single V100 GPU with approximately 5 minutes for model initialization, 6 minutes for inversion, and 1 minute for inference on 8-frame clips.

## Method

Video-P2P adapts a pre-trained image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1-5) for video editing by converting it into a Text-to-Set (T2S) model via 1×3×3 convolution kernels and frame-attentions that use the first frame as a reference, then fine-tuning on the input video to recover generation quality. For video inversion, it optimizes a single shared unconditional embedding across all frames (rather than per-frame embeddings) to align DDIM denoising trajectories, which is both memory-efficient and crucial for maintaining semantic consistency during editing. The key innovation is a decoupled-guidance strategy: the source prompt branch uses the optimized unconditional embedding (ensuring reconstruction fidelity), while the target prompt branch uses the initialized unconditional embedding (preserving editability), and cross-attention maps from both branches are incorporated to produce the final edited video.

## Results

On a 45-sample test set derived from DAVIS, Video-P2P achieves the best CLIP score (0.3367), lowest Object Semantic Variance (OSV, 47.38), and highest temporal coherence (Temp, 0.9725) among all video editing methods. It also outperforms TAV+DDIM on Masked PSNR (20.65 vs. 17.37) and LPIPS (0.3213 vs. 0.4651), indicating substantially better preservation of unchanged regions. In the user study, Video-P2P ranks first on all three criteria (Structure Preserving, Text Alignment, Realism & Quality) with a 69.02% preference rate, compared to 10.87% for TAV+DDIM and 13.59% for Image-P2P.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TAV+DDIM, Video-P2P wins on all five metrics: higher CLIP (0.3367 vs. 0.3343), higher Masked PSNR (20.65 vs. 17.37), lower LPIPS (0.3213 vs. 0.4651), lower OSV (47.38 vs. 55.67), and higher Temp (0.9725 vs. 0.9697), with the largest gap in LPIPS (≈31% reduction) reflecting TAV+DDIM's inability to preserve unrelated regions during local edits. Against Image-P2P (frame-by-frame editing), Video-P2P dramatically improves OSV (47.38 vs. 76.92) and Temp (0.9725 vs. 0.9021), confirming that per-frame editing destroys cross-frame semantic consistency; however, Image-P2P retains a per-frame quality edge in M.PSNR (22.92 vs. 20.65) and LPIPS (0.3082 vs. 0.3213), which the authors note is expected since it does not enforce temporal constraints. Against Dreamix (a closed-source video diffusion editor), the comparison is qualitative: Video-P2P preserves fine background details (e.g., drawer textures) and exact motion timing, whereas Dreamix slightly slows the generated cat's motion relative to the original dog. In the user study, Video-P2P achieves a 69.02% preference rate on Structure Preserving and Realism & Quality, far exceeding TAV+DDIM (10.87% and 19.02%) and Image-P2P (13.59% and 4.35%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-P2P extends the Prompt-to-Prompt (P2P) cross-attention control framework from image editing to video, and adapts Null-Text Inversion's unconditional embedding optimization to a shared-across-frames setting for video inversion. For the video model backbone, it borrows Tune-A-Video's inflation strategy (converting a T2I model into a Text-to-Set model via frame-attention), but departs from TAV by adding a decoupled-guidance mechanism that separates reconstruction and editability into two branches with different unconditional embeddings. Unlike Dreamix or Imagen Video, which rely on large-scale pre-trained video diffusion models, Video-P2P demonstrates that a standard image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) suffices for detailed local video editing when combined with these two technical designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes that cross-attention-based local video editing is feasible without any video-specific pre-training, opening a practical path for video editing using widely available image diffusion models. The decoupled-guidance insight—that the embedding optimized for reconstruction actively degrades editability and vice versa—reveals a fundamental tension in diffusion-based editing that may inform future work on both image and video. Key limitations remain: the method requires per-video fine-tuning (~5 min), operates on short clips (8–24 frames at 512×512), and cannot edit motion. Scaling to longer videos and eliminating the per-video initialization cost are the most pressing open questions.
