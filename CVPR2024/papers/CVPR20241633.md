## Problem

Existing diffusion model-based blind face restoration methods suffer from two critical issues: they operate on full-resolution (512×512) images requiring thousands of denoising steps, making training and inference prohibitively slow, and they rely on unconditional generation that fails to preserve the original identity and fine-grained facial details such as wrinkles. No prior approach simultaneously addresses both the computational cost and the authenticity gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241633/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB face image at 512×512 resolution (degraded by combinations of Gaussian blur, down-sampling, noise, and JPEG compression) and produces a restored high-quality face image at the same resolution. Internally, the input is decomposed via two-level Haar DWT so the diffusion model only processes a 128×128 low-frequency sub-band (1/16 the pixel count), while a separate U-Net handles the high-frequency sub-bands at both decomposition levels. Final reconstruction via inverse wavelet transform completes in under 1 ms.

## Method

WaveFace transfers BFR from the pixel domain to the frequency domain using two-level Haar DWT, splitting the problem into a low-frequency and a high-frequency sub-problem. The Low-frequency Conditional Denoising (LCD) module applies a DDPM-style diffusion model to the 128×128 low-frequency sub-band, injecting the LQ low-frequency component as a channel-concatenated condition at every denoising step to anchor identity; this reduces the diffusion input size by 16× and cuts inference to ~2 s. The High-Frequency Recovery (HFR) module is a U-shaped CNN that takes channel-concatenated high-frequency sub-bands from both DWT levels as input and predicts the residual for each level in a single forward pass, trained with a combined L1 recovery loss (in both frequency and pixel domains) and an SSIM-based content loss. The two modules are trained independently (200K and 70K iterations respectively) and their outputs are recombined via inverse wavelet transform, yielding a 10× speedup over prior diffusion-based BFR methods while achieving the best identity-preservation score (28.3° ArcFace angle) on CelebA-Test.

## Results

On CelebA-Test, WaveFace achieves the best PSNR (26.97 dB), SSIM (0.711), LPIPS (0.343), and identity preservation (Deg. 28.26°), outperforming the second-best methods by 1.9 dB in PSNR and 6.5° in identity angle. On FID, WaveFace (13.06) ranks second behind RestoreFormer (12.00). On real-world datasets, WaveFace achieves the best FID on LFW (43.18) and WIDER (36.91), while ranking fourth on WebPhoto (81.53) behind VQFR (75.35) and RestoreFormer (77.33). Inference takes only 1.97 s at DWT level 2, roughly 10× faster than DifFace (25.04 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaveFace clearly outperforms all seven baselines on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and identity preservation (Deg.) on CelebA-Test, with the largest margins over the diffusion-based methods DR2 (PSNR +3.38 dB, Deg. −22.8°) and DifFace (PSNR +2.81 dB, Deg. −21.7°), confirming that unconditional diffusion generation severely harms identity fidelity. On FID, WaveFace (13.06) is competitive but trails RestoreFormer (12.00) by about 1 point, suggesting the latter's key-value retrieval strategy captures slightly better distributional realism. On real-world data, WaveFace achieves the best FID on LFW (43.18 vs. 45.23 for DifFace) and WIDER (36.91 vs. 37.11 for DifFace), but underperforms VQFR (75.35) and RestoreFormer (77.33) on WebPhoto (81.53), where the authors note the small sample size (407 images) makes FID less reliable. Among generative-prior methods (GPEN, GFP-GAN), WaveFace leads by 2.2 dB in PSNR and 8.7° in identity angle, while reference-prior methods (VQFR, RestoreFormer, CodeFormer) remain competitive on FID but lag significantly on identity preservation. The efficiency advantage is substantial: at DWT level 2, WaveFace uses only 25.4 M parameters and 1.97 s inference versus 175.4 M / 25.04 s for DifFace and 93.6 M / 8.76 s for DR2.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaveFace builds on the diffusion-model-based blind face restoration line initiated by DifFace and DR2, but departs fundamentally by relocating the generative process from the pixel domain to the frequency domain via Discrete Wavelet Transform. Where prior DM-based BFR methods apply unconditional denoising on full-resolution (512×512) images, WaveFace conditions a diffusion model on the low-frequency sub-band (128×128, 1/16 the size) and handles high-frequency details with a single-pass U-Net, drawing on the wavelet-based generation ideas from Phung et al. (2023) and the conditional injection strategies (concatenation, AdaGN, cross-attention) established in the broader diffusion literature. The method also inherits the degradation synthesis pipeline and evaluation protocol from the BFR benchmark tradition (GPEN, GFPGAN, VQFR, RestoreFormer, CodeFormer).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup (1.97 s vs. 25 s for DifFace) combined with improved identity preservation (6.5° lower Deg. angle) makes diffusion-based BFR practically deployable, addressing the two main criticisms of DM-based restoration. The frequency-domain decomposition offers a clean, generalizable principle—separating structure from detail—that could transfer to other restoration or generation tasks. However, the authors acknowledge a significant gap between synthetic degradations (Eq. 15) and real-world corruption, and the FID advantage on WebPhoto-Test is modest, leaving open how well the approach generalizes to truly unknown degradation types without a pre-denoising network.
