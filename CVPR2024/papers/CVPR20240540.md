## Problem

Reconstructing the 3D shape of articulated objects (e.g., animals) from a single in-the-wild image remains challenging because existing methods rely on category-specific 3D templates, manually defined 3D skeletons, or multi-view/video training data. No prior approach could learn articulation in a category-agnostic manner using only single-view image collections with self-supervision, leaving a gap for the 100+ diverse animal categories where 3D supervision is unavailable.

## Input / Output

At inference, SAOR takes a single RGB image (resized to 128×128) and produces an explicit 3D mesh, a UV texture map, a camera pose (Euler angles plus translation), and a per-vertex part assignment matrix in a single forward pass (~15 ms). During training, it additionally consumes estimated object silhouettes (from SAM) and relative monocular depth maps (from MiDaS) as weak supervision signals, but these are not required at test time. The model is trained jointly on over 100 animal categories, so the same network handles birds, quadrupeds, and bipeds without category-specific parameters.

## Method

SAOR initializes a sphere mesh and predicts shape via an MLP-based deformation field (with bilateral symmetry) followed by skeleton-free linear blend skinning, where a learned part-assignment matrix and per-part scale/rotation/translation parameters articulate the deformed shape without any predefined skeleton or 3D template. A ResNet encoder extracts a global image feature that is decoded by four separate networks for deformation, articulation, texture, and camera pose, all trained end-to-end via differentiable rendering (PyTorch3D) in an analysis-by-synthesis framework. The key training innovation is a cross-instance swap consistency loss that swaps deformed shapes between randomly paired images of the same category while preserving each image's own articulation, texture, and viewpoint, thereby disentangling shape deformation from articulation and preventing degenerate flat-plane solutions. A silhouette-based balanced sampling mechanism clusters training images by predicted mask shape and samples uniformly across clusters to ensure viewpoint diversity within each batch, making the swap loss effective without the expensive memory-bank pair selection used by Unicorn.

## Results

On quadruped keypoint transfer (PASCAL VOC, PCK@0.1), SAOR-101 achieves 44.9 on Horse, 33.6 on Cow, and 29.1 on Sheep, outperforming all methods without 3D templates or skeletons. On CUB birds, SAOR-Bird scores 51.9 (all) and 57.8 (non-aquatic), the best among methods without 3D priors or extra data. On Animal3D 3D evaluation (normalized L1 Chamfer distance), SAOR-101 achieves 0.046 (Horse), 0.043 (Cow), and 0.045 (Sheep), matching MagicPony on Horse and substantially outperforming Unicorn (0.091–0.134).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAOR-101 outperforms MagicPony [61] on Horse (44.9 vs 42.9) and Sheep (29.1 vs 26.2) but trails on Cow (33.6 vs 42.5), where MagicPony benefits from a category-specific skeleton prior. Against template-based methods A-CSM [27] and CSM [28], SAOR-101 leads on all three quadruped categories by 6–14 PCK points, demonstrating that skeleton-free part-based articulation can match or exceed approaches requiring hand-defined 3D templates. SAOR-101 substantially outperforms Unicorn [41] (14.9/12.1/11.0) by 20–34 points, as Unicorn produces degenerate flat-plane shapes for articulated animals. On CUB birds, SAOR-Bird (51.9) is competitive with UMR (51.2) and Unicorn (49.0) but falls short of MagicPony (55.5) and CMR (54.6), which use additional supervision. On Animal3D 3D Chamfer distance, SAOR-101 ties MagicPony on Horse (0.046) and is within 0.003 on Cow (0.043 vs 0.040), while halving Unicorn's error (0.091 vs 0.046 on Horse).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAOR extends the analysis-by-synthesis framework for single-view 3D reconstruction by removing the need for category-specific 3D templates (as in CSM, A-CSM, CMR) or manually defined 3D skeletons (as in DOVE, MagicPony, LASSIE). It builds directly on Unicorn's [41] cross-instance swap loss but generalizes it to articulated objects by swapping only the deformed shape while preserving each instance's articulation, and replaces Unicorn's expensive memory-bank pair selection with a simple silhouette-cluster-based balanced sampling strategy. The skeleton-free linear blend skinning formulation is inspired by Liao et al. [34]'s pose transfer work, repurposed here for self-supervised part discovery from images alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAOR demonstrates that a single model can handle 100+ diverse articulated categories (quadrupeds, bipeds, birds) without any per-category 3D modeling effort, which is a practical barrier that has limited prior methods to a handful of categories. The ablation reveals that the swap loss is not merely helpful but critical for articulated objects—removing it causes near-catastrophic degradation on quadrupeds (44.9→30.8 PCK for horses), highlighting that naive cross-instance consistency breaks down when shape variation is dominated by articulation. Open questions include whether texture quality can be improved without test-time optimization, whether the approach generalizes to categories with more extreme shape variation (e.g., insects, cephalopods), and whether the fixed number of parts K can be made adaptive per instance.
