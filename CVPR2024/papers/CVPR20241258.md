## Problem

Existing Federated Multi-Task Learning (FMTL) methods assume model congruity, requiring all clients to either handle a single task or aggregate only with peers sharing identical task sets. In practice, clients operate in diverse environments with different numbers and types of tasks, creating a model incongruity problem that invalidates standard parameter-averaging aggregation. This paper introduces the Hetero-Client FMTL (HC-FMTL) setting and addresses the compounded challenges of model incongruity, data heterogeneity, and task heterogeneity simultaneously.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (PASCAL-Context, 4998 train / 5105 test) or RGB-D images (NYUD-v2, 795 train / 654 test) and produces a subset of dense prediction maps—semantic segmentation, human parts segmentation, saliency, surface normals, edge detection, and/or depth—depending on its assigned task setup. Critically, different clients produce different numbers of output heads with different parameter dimensions, so the server must handle structurally heterogeneous models during aggregation. The Swin-T backbone operates at a fixed resolution with batch size 8, and prediction heads are excluded from aggregation entirely, remaining localized to each client.

## Method

FEDHCA² decomposes each client's encoder-decoder model and performs decoupled server-side aggregation. For encoders, a Hyper Conflict-Averse Aggregation scheme (inspired by CAGrad) solves a min-max optimization to find a unified update direction that minimizes pairwise gradient conflicts, grounded in Theorem 1 which shows FL parameter averaging equals MTL gradient descent plus an extra term maximizing gradient inner products. For decoders, a Hyper Cross Attention Aggregation scheme applies layer-wise cross attention across all K decoders from all clients, computing attention weights between local parameter updates at each decoder layer to capture fine-grained inter-task interactions while tolerating structural differences. Learnable Hyper Aggregation Weights (α for encoders, β per decoder layer) are dynamically updated via inner products between aggregated updates and subsequent local parameter changes, enabling each client to adaptively scale how much knowledge it absorbs from peers.

## Results

On the primary HC-FMTL benchmark (5 single-task PASCAL-Context clients + 1 multi-task NYUD-v2 client), FEDHCA² achieves the best average per-task performance improvement (Δm%) of +2.18% over the local baseline, the only method to show a positive overall gain. On the reversed setting (4 single-task NYUD-v2 + 1 multi-task PASCAL-Context), FEDHCA² again leads with Δm% = +0.75%. Individual task highlights include 57.55 mIoU on PASCAL-Context SemSeg, 52.30 mIoU on Parts, and 0.6281 RMSE on NYUD-v2 Depth, all surpassing the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDHCA² outperforms all eight baselines on the overall Δm% metric in both HC-FMTL settings, achieving +2.18% (Table 1) and +0.75% (Table 2), while the next-best method FedAMP reaches only +1.47% and +0.27% respectively. Traditional FL baselines FedAvg (−11.76%) and FedProx (−10.68%) suffer severe degradation due to naive averaging across heterogeneous models. Personalized FL methods FedPer (−1.11%) and FedBABU (−1.19%) mitigate some damage but still underperform the local baseline overall; Ditto (−5.57%) and FedSTA (−5.80%) show substantial drops, particularly on NYUD-v2 SemSeg (35.62 vs. 41.86 local). Among FMTL-specific methods, MaT-FL (−0.46%) is the closest competitor but remains below the local baseline, whereas FEDHCA² is the only method that consistently improves upon local training. On individual tasks, FEDHCA² leads on PASCAL-Context SemSeg (57.55 vs. MaT-FL 57.45), Parts (52.30 vs. FedAMP 52.05), and NYUD-v2 Depth (0.6281 vs. FedAMP 0.6428), though it trails FedPer on NYUD-v2 SemSeg (41.47 vs. 44.02) and FedBABU on NYUD-v2 Edge (76.50 vs. 76.70).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of personalized federated learning and multi-task learning, extending the FMTL line of work (MOCHA, FedSTA, MaT-FL, MAS) by relaxing the model congruity assumption that prior methods require clients to share identical task sets or architectures. Conceptually, it repurposes CAGrad's min-max conflict-averse optimization (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2021) from single-node MTL gradient balancing to server-side encoder aggregation in FL, and adapts cross-stitch task interaction (Misra et al., CVPR 2016) into a layer-wise cross-attention mechanism for decoder aggregation. The theoretical bridge (Theorem 1) connecting MTL's shared-encoder gradient accumulation to FL's parameter averaging is the key conceptual departure, motivating the need for conflict mitigation that standard FedAvg-style averaging does not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HC-FMTL formulation is a natural and practical generalization: real-world federated deployments rarely have all clients solving identical task sets, and the decoupled encoder/decoder aggregation strategy offers a reusable recipe for handling structural heterogeneity in FL. However, the 'model incongruity' addressed here is limited to differing numbers of decoders on a shared encoder backbone (Swin-T), not truly heterogeneous architectures, which the authors acknowledge as future work. The empirical gains over strong baselines (MaT-FL, FedAMP) are modest (≈1–2% in Δm%), and evaluation is confined to two dense-prediction datasets with a single backbone, leaving open whether the conflict-averse and cross-attention mechanisms transfer to classification or other modalities. The two-stage encoder weight dynamics observed for multi-task clients (rapid mutual learning followed by domain-specific specialization) is an interesting empirical phenomenon that could inform adaptive scheduling strategies in future FL systems.
