## Problem

Existing 3D shape completion methods based on voxels or point clouds produce rough, inaccurate results on facial data because the high inter-identity similarity of faces demands learning of subtle shape variations that these representations handle poorly. Voxel methods are limited by memory and resolution, while point clouds lack explicit surface adjacency needed for smooth, high-fidelity completion. This paper addresses the gap by operating directly on mesh topology to achieve high-fidelity completion of irregular, free-form incomplete facial scans.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240842/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts incomplete facial data in any form—meshes with arbitrary topology and vertex counts, sparse point clouds, or even scattered key points—without requiring pre-aligned correspondences or a fixed vertex number. The output is a complete 3D facial mesh with unified topology (approximately 200k faces after Loop subdivision for evaluation), suitable for direct use in clinical prosthetic fabrication or non-rigid registration. The pipeline is fully automated and processes each incomplete input independently via optimization rather than a single forward pass.

## Method

FaceCom combines three components: (1) a multi-scale graph convolutional autoencoder built on FeaStNet convolutions with separate global (downsampling) and local (linear) paths, trained on 2405 identities with a hypersphere regularization term L_reg = (||z||₂ − 1)² instead of VAE to enable stable optimization-based inference; (2) an optimization-based fitting stage that minimizes a composite loss—differentiable point-to-surface distance (via PyTorch3D) between the incomplete input and the generated mesh, plus an image inpainting guidance term computed by rendering the fitted mesh and comparing it against a fine-tuned RePaint diffusion inpainting output; and (3) a post-processing step that projects non-defective regions back onto the input surface along vertex normals and applies KNN-weighted deformation at boundaries to eliminate misalignments. The key insight is that optimization over a learned generative latent space, guided by 2D inpainting priors, outperforms end-to-end completion networks for the highly similar and detail-sensitive domain of facial shapes.

## Results

On the fitting task (evaluated on 118 held-out identities from Headspace and FaceScape), FaceCom's shape generator achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.480 mm and a mean point-to-surface distance of 0.275 mm without post-processing, outperforming all four baselines. With post-processing, the mean point-to-surface distance drops to 0.160 mm, representing a 58% improvement over the closest competitor (NPHM at 0.381 mm). In the clinical nasal defect experiment on 20 patient cases, the average margin fitness of completed prosthetics is 0.33 ± 0.21 mm, confirming practical viability for medical fabrication.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceCom outperforms all four baselines on both Chamfer Distance and mean point-to-surface distance. The closest competitor is NPHM (CD=0.578 mm, MD=0.381 mm), which FaceCom (w/o post-processing) beats by 17% on CD (0.480 vs. 0.578) and 28% on MD (0.275 vs. 0.381). FLAME (CD=0.622, MD=0.309) and ImFace (CD=0.632, MD=0.505) are further behind, while FaceScape (CD=0.685, MD=0.416) performs worst among the baselines. With post-processing, FaceCom's MD of 0.160 mm is more than 2× better than any baseline, though the corresponding CD is not reported. Notably, the paper does not conduct a direct shape-completion comparison against other methods, citing the absence of dedicated facial shape completion baselines in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

FaceCom departs from the dominant end-to-end shape completion paradigm (voxel-based or point-cloud-based networks that directly predict missing geometry) by instead training a mesh-based GNN autoencoder as a generative prior and then performing per-sample optimization to fit incomplete inputs. The mesh convolution backbone is built on FeaStNet (Verma et al., 2018) with differentiable downsampling adapted from Ranjan et al. (2018), while the image inpainting guidance module is a fine-tuned RePaint diffusion model (Lugmayr et al., 2022). Conceptually, the paper bridges parametric face modeling (FLAME, FaceScape, ImFace, NPHM) and generative shape completion by replacing fixed-basis or implicit-surface fitting with a learned hypersphere-constrained latent space that is optimized against both geometric and rendered-image losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical validation on 20 clinical nasal-defect cases (0.33 mm average margin fitness) demonstrates a concrete pathway toward personalized maxillofacial prosthetics, an area where mirrored reconstruction and database-matched prosthetics remain the clinical standard. However, the method is restricted to neutral expressions and lacks texture modeling, limiting its applicability to dynamic or full-fidelity reconstruction. The optimization-based fitting, while flexible for arbitrary input forms (mesh, point cloud, key points), introduces per-sample inference cost and potential boundary inconsistencies that the post-processing step only partially addresses. Open questions include whether the hypersphere-regularized latent space scales to expression-aware or multi-identity completion, and whether the image-inpainting guidance can be replaced by a purely 3D prior to avoid the 2D rendering bottleneck.
