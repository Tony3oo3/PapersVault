## Problem

Single-image novel view synthesis methods either fail to model view-dependent effects (VDEs) or require ground-truth depth and camera pose annotations for training. VDEs are particularly challenging from a single image because they depend on material reflectance and light incidence angles, making direct estimation highly ill-posed. This paper asks whether VDEs can be learned from image sequences alone, without any depth or pose supervision, by exploiting the geometric relationship between camera motion and specular appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242551/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single 720×1280 RGB image and a user-defined target camera pose (rotation and translation relative to the input view). The backbone (ResNet34) runs once per input image (~12 ms), after which each novel view is rendered in ~30 ms via lightweight re-calibration and sampler MLPs. Outputs include the synthesized novel view with VDEs, a depth probability volume (from which a depth map is extracted via dot-product with sample distances), and a VDE activation map highlighting the most reflective regions. Training uses 240×426 patches from randomly resized (30–85%) video frames, and evaluation renders at 1/2 resolution before bilinear upscaling to full resolution.

## Method

NVSVDE-Net's key insight is that VDEs 'follow' camera motion: specular highlights shift in the opposite direction of the reflective surface's projection, and the maximum VDE disparity is inversely proportional to scene depth. The method models VDEs by aggregating pixel colors along the negative-depth region of epipolar lines, weighted by learned VDE probabilities, to construct a VDE-infused input image. For rendering, it proposes 'relaxed volumetric rendering' that directly models the combined transmittance-density term T(t)σ(t) as a softmax-normalized depth probability volume, reducing NVS to a single-pass classification problem over ray samples rather than per-sample MLP evaluations. A sampler block then refines coarse exponential ray samples into fine-grained adaptive per-pixel distances for artifact-free rendering. The entire pipeline is trained self-supervised from image sequences using an improved PoseNet (which rotation-aligns views before estimating residual extrinsics) and a combined L1 + VGG perceptual + disparity smoothness loss, requiring no depth or pose annotations.

## Results

On RealEstate10k, NVSVDE-Net achieves 24.31 dB PSNR, 30.25 dB PSNR_lf, 0.8397 SSIM, and 0.2325 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baselines (SceneRF and Single-view MPI) by 0.7 dB in PSNR and 1.3 dB in PSNR_lf. On MannequinChallenge, it reaches 22.43 dB PSNR and 27.03 dB PSNR_lf, surpassing SceneRF by 0.8 dB in PSNR and 1.2 dB in PSNR_lf. The method is also the fastest, rendering a 640×360 view in 42 ms versus 99–160 ms for competing methods, and is the only baseline that explicitly models view-dependent effects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NVSVDE-Net outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. On RealEstate10k, it leads SceneRF (the closest competitor) by 0.7 dB PSNR and 1.3 dB PSNR_lf, and beats PixelNeRF by 1.5 dB PSNR and 2.2 dB PSNR_lf. On MannequinChallenge, the margin over SceneRF is 0.8 dB PSNR and 1.2 dB PSNR_lf, while the gap over PixelNeRF widens to 1.1 dB PSNR and 1.7 dB PSNR_lf. Notably, NVSVDE-Net is the only method that models view-dependent effects; the ablation (w/o VDE) shows a 0.15 dB PSNR drop and visible geometry holes in reflective regions, confirming that VDE synthesis contributes to both photometric quality and geometric accuracy. In terms of speed, NVSVDE-Net renders in 42 ms per view (30 ms after the one-time backbone pass), compared to 99 ms for BehindScenes, 112 ms for PixelNeRF, and 160 ms for SceneRF, owing to its single-pass relaxed volumetric rendering versus per-sample MLP evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

NVSVDE-Net extends the single-image NVS line initiated by PixelNeRF, BehindScenes, and SceneRF, which all rely on per-sample MLP forward passes and lack explicit view-dependent effect modeling. It departs from MPI-based methods (Tucker & Snavely, MINE) by replacing multi-plane intersections with a relaxed volumetric rendering that treats depth estimation as a classification problem over a probability volume, building on the MED depth logit formulation. The VDE synthesis mechanism—aggregating pixel colors along the negative disparity region of epipolar lines—is a novel geometric prior not present in any prior single-view NVS work, and the fully self-supervised training (no depth or pose GT) extends the self-supervised monocular depth estimation paradigm (Godard et al., Gonzalez Bello & Kim) into the rendering domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that photometrically plausible view-dependent effects (glossy highlights, specular reflections) can be synthesized from a single image without multi-view supervision, opening a path toward realistic single-image rendering that prior methods could not achieve. The relaxed volumetric rendering (single backbone pass + lightweight sampler) offers a practical efficiency advantage over per-sample MLP approaches, and the self-supervised training removes the need for depth or pose annotations. However, the method is limited to simple glossy/diffuse specular reflections, static forward-facing scenes, and small camera motions; whether the negative-disparity VDE prior generalizes to complex BRDFs, dynamic scenes, or large-baseline synthesis remains an open question.
