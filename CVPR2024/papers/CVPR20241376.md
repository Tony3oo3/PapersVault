## Problem

Fine-tuning a pre-trained vision transformer with a segmentation decoder causes severe overfitting to base classes, causing the model to lose the semantic grouping knowledge learned during pre-training that is critical for generalizing to novel classes. The paper identifies that the inherent patch-prototype matching capability of pre-trained ViTs is underexploited in few-shot segmentation, and that the relationship descriptor (RD) concept from ZegCLIP—originally designed for CLIP's text-image matching—has not been adapted to leverage this patch-level semantic grouping for few-shot segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241376/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image (512×512 RGB) and a set of 1 or 5 support image-mask pairs as input. Support images are processed through the same frozen ViT-B/16 backbone to compute per-class prototype embeddings via mask-weighted averaging of patch features. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask that can handle both binary foreground/background (FSS) and multi-class base+novel+background (GFSS) settings, with the model scaling naturally to arbitrary numbers of support examples and object categories.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a relationship descriptor (RD) for each class by computing a softmax-weighted sum of element-wise products between all patch embeddings and the class prototype (Eq. 2), where the softmax weights emphasize patches that already match the prototype well. This RD, concatenated with the prototype, forms the query for a 3-layer cross-attention transformer decoder whose keys and values come from the query image's patch embeddings, producing the segmentation mask. The RD construction is extended to multiple transformer layers (last three layers found optimal), enriching the class query with multi-scale semantic grouping signals. Training uses momentum-updated class prototypes (η from 0.996 to 1.0) with focal+dice loss in a standard supervised loop—no episodic training—while the ViT backbone remains frozen and only deep prompt tokens plus decoder parameters are learned.

## Results

On PASCAL-5i under the GFSS setting without test-time tuning, the multi-layer RD method achieves 51.0 mIoU(N) and 61.1 hIoU in 1-shot, and 55.3 mIoU(N) and 64.2 hIoU in 5-shot, substantially outperforming the previous best CAPL-ViT (23.2 / 28.5 mIoU(N)). With test-time tuning, the method reaches 52.6 mIoU(N) (1-shot) and 56.7 mIoU(N) (5-shot), surpassing POP (35.5 / 55.9) and DIaM (35.1 / 55.3). On the harder COCO-20i benchmark, the multi-layer RD achieves 27.0 mIoU(N) (5-shot, no tuning) and 33.1 mIoU(N) (5-shot, with tuning), outperforming all prior methods. In the binary FSS setting, the method reaches 78.9 / 80.3 mIoU on PASCAL-5i and 60.1 / 61.2 mIoU on COCO-20i (1-shot / 5-shot), matching or exceeding the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Without test-time tuning, the proposed multi-layer RD method dominates all prior GFSS baselines by a wide margin: 51.0 mIoU(N) (1-shot) vs. CAPL-ViT's 23.2 and CAPL+PSPNet's 17.5 on PASCAL-5i, a gap of over 20 points. With test-time tuning, the method still leads: 52.6 mIoU(N) (1-shot) vs. POP (35.5) and DIaM (35.1), and 56.7 mIoU(N) (5-shot) vs. POP (55.9) and DIaM (55.3), confirming consistent superiority. On COCO-20i, the multi-layer RD achieves 23.2 mIoU(N) (1-shot, tuned) and 33.1 mIoU(N) (5-shot, tuned), beating POP (15.3 / 30.0) and DIaM (17.2 / 28.7). In the binary FSS setting, the method reaches 78.9 / 80.3 mIoU on PASCAL-5i, slightly exceeding PGMA-Net with CLIP-RN101 (77.6 / 78.6), while on COCO-20i it achieves 60.1 / 61.2, marginally trailing PGMA-Net's 59.4 / 61.8 in 5-shot. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that removing RDs drops mIoU(N) from 51.0 to 15.9 (1-shot, PASCAL-5i), and using the ZegCLIP-style [CLS]-based RD instead of the proposed patch-based RD costs 2.4–6.4 mIoU(N) points, validating the tailored RD design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the relationship descriptor (RD) concept from ZegCLIP [61], originally designed to preserve CLIP's cross-modal text-image matching for zero-shot segmentation, and adapts it to exploit the intra-modal semantic grouping capability of pre-trained ViTs (matching between patch embeddings and class prototypes). It builds on the prototype-based representation from CAPL [45] for generalized few-shot segmentation and the plain ViT decoder architecture from SegViT [55], but departs from prior GFSS methods (FineTune, CCA, DIaM, POP) that rely on test-time fine-tuning or auxiliary losses to balance base/novel performance. The key conceptual departure is extending RD construction from a single [CLS]-token-based design to a weighted aggregation over all patch tokens and across multiple transformer layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the overfitting-to-base-classes problem in few-shot segmentation can be substantially mitigated by preserving the pre-trained ViT's inherent semantic grouping through RDs, without requiring test-time adaptation or task-specific losses. This offers a simpler, more scalable alternative to the complex fine-tuning strategies in prior GFSS work, and the method naturally handles both generalized and binary settings. Limitations include ViT's resolution constraints for small objects and the current restriction to ViT-B/16; open questions include whether the multi-layer RD strategy transfers to other dense prediction tasks (detection, depth) and whether it generalizes to larger or differently pre-trained backbones (DINOv2, MAE).
