## Problem

Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) suffers from inherent imbalanced accuracy and inconsistent confidence between old and new classes, particularly in low-labeling regimes, because novel classes are fully unlabeled and old-class knowledge is not fully transferable. The paper argues that some annotations of new classes are indispensable but costly, and asks whether a model can actively select a small number of valuable unlabeled samples for labeling to substantially improve category discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242538/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of labeled images covering only old classes (e.g., 20% of training samples) and a larger unlabeled pool containing both old and new classes, then iteratively queries an oracle for labels on a limited budget of samples (e.g., ~2.5 per novel class on fine-grained datasets over 5 rounds). Output is a class prediction for every image in the unlabeled pool, evaluated inductively on a disjoint test set using Hungarian optimal assignment across all old and new classes. The backbone is a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 with only the last transformer block fine-tuned, and the [CLS] token serves as the feature representation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Adaptive-Novel sampling strategy, which jointly considers novelty (prioritizing samples predicted as new classes), informativeness (margin-based uncertainty), and diversity (uniform per-class allocation of ⌊b/K_new⌋ samples). An adaptive mechanism switches between selecting confident novel samples (minimum uncertainty) at early rounds to stabilize biased clusters and informative novel samples (maximum uncertainty) at later rounds to refine decision boundaries, with the transition triggered when the label mapping function becomes stable between start and end epochs of a round. To handle the clustering-induced mismatch between ground-truth label indices and the model's internal label ordering, a stable label mapping algorithm computes a one-to-one permutation via the Hungarian algorithm using an EMA model's predictions on the accumulated labeled set, ensuring consistent supervision across active selection rounds. The entire pipeline is built on top of SimGCD's parametric prototypical classifier with supervised and self-supervised contrastive losses.

## Results

On generic datasets, Adaptive-Novel achieves 93.15% / 71.25% / 83.34% overall accuracy on CIFAR-10 / CIFAR-100 / ImageNet-100, with new-class accuracy of 92.80% / 66.78% / 76.48%. On fine-grained datasets, it reaches 66.62% / 48.36% / 53.74% overall accuracy on CUB / Stanford Cars / FGVC-Aircraft, improving new-class accuracy by 25.52% and 25.22% over the no-AGCD baseline on CUB and SCars respectively with only ~2.5 labeled samples per class. A key advantage is balanced old/new accuracy: on CUB the gap is just 0.16% (66.54% vs 66.70%), whereas other methods show gaps of 4–14%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adaptive-Novel consistently outperforms all six baselines across all three generic and three fine-grained datasets. Among uncertainty-based methods, Margin is the strongest (e.g., 69.08% All on CIFAR-100), but Adaptive-Novel surpasses it by 2.17% All and 4.20% New on CIFAR-100, and by 2.88% All and 7.96% New on ImageNet-100. Among diversity-based methods, BADGE is the closest competitor (81.48% All on ImageNet-100), yet Adaptive-Novel still leads by 1.86% All and 6.20% New. The gap is most pronounced on fine-grained datasets: on CUB, Adaptive-Novel achieves 66.62% All vs. 65.84% for BADGE and 65.08% for Margin, while on Stanford Cars it reaches 48.36% All vs. 46.03% for Margin. Notably, Adaptive-Novel is the only method that achieves near-perfect old/new balance (e.g., 66.54% vs 66.70% on CUB), whereas all baselines show a 4–14% gap between old and new class accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SimGCD (Wen et al., ICCV 2023) as the base GCD training method, extending the Generalized Category Discovery task (Vaze et al., CVPR 2022) by introducing an active learning loop. It departs from conventional active learning, which assumes a closed-world setting where labeled and unlabeled data share the same class set, by formulating an open-world variant where unlabeled data contains novel categories. The sampling strategy draws on uncertainty-based (margin, entropy) and diversity-based (CoreSet, BADGE) AL literature but adapts them to handle the unique challenges of novel-class clustering, including the label-index ordering problem that requires a stable mapping via Hungarian assignment and EMA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the inherent imbalanced accuracy and confidence gap between old and new classes in GCD can be substantially mitigated with very few additional labels (~2.5 per class), making the approach practical for real-world annotation budgets. The finding that standard AL strategies (entropy, least confidence) underperform even random sampling in this open-world setting highlights a genuine gap in the AL literature. Open questions include whether the adaptive confident-to-informative transition generalizes to more than 5 rounds, whether the approach extends to other base GCD methods beyond SimGCD, and how it behaves when the number of novel classes is truly unknown rather than estimated.
