## Problem

VLN agents suffer from dataset bias, overfitting to familiar visual environments, repeated entity components, and biased structural trajectories, which severely degrades performance in unseen environments. While prior work has focused on building more diverse datasets or adding richer inputs, these approaches cannot fully eliminate inherent biases. This paper proposes a model-level solution: applying causal inference (back-door and front-door adjustments) to explicitly identify and mitigate both observable and unobservable confounders across vision, language, and history modalities.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a natural language instruction (processed by RoBERTa), a 360° panorama split into 36 sub-images (encoded by CLIP-B/16 with directional sin/cos encoding for heading and elevation), accumulated navigation history, and current heading/elevation angles. Room references for visual confounder identification are extracted on-the-fly via BLIP with a fixed VQA prompt. The output is a discrete navigation action (select among nearby candidate nodes or emit a stop signal); for goal-oriented benchmarks (REVERIE, SOON), the agent additionally grounds a target object at the final destination.

## Method

GOAT is built on the dual-scale graph transformer (DUET) and introduces two causal learning modules. BACL handles observable confounders (direction/landmark keywords in text, room types in vision) by applying the do-operator to sever back-door paths, computing the confounder expectation via either a statistic-based frequency weighting or an attention-based mechanism (experiments show attention works best for text, statistics for vision). FACL addresses unobservable confounders (decoration styles, sentence patterns, trajectory trends) by inserting a mediator and performing cross-sampling: K-Means-clustered global features from the training set serve as the cross-sampling dictionary, and multi-head attention approximates the front-door adjustment. A Cross-modal Feature Pooling (CFP) module uses attentive pooling to compress variable-length sequences into fixed-size global representations, trained with a contrastive loss during pre-training to simultaneously improve cross-modal alignment and build the confounder dictionaries. An adaptive gate fusion (AGF) mechanism stably integrates the causality-enhanced features with the original context before cross-attention encoding and action prediction.

## Results

On R2R val-unseen, GOAT achieves 77.82% SR, 68.13% SPL, 2.40m NE, and 84.72% OSR, outperforming the previous best (BEVBert: 75% SR, 64% SPL, 2.81m NE, 84% OSR) by 2.82% SR and 4.13% SPL. On REVERIE test-unseen, GOAT reaches 57.72% SR and 26.70% RGSPL, surpassing DSRG (54.04% SR, 22.18% RGSPL) by 3.68% SR and 4.52% RGSPL. On RxR-English val-unseen, GOAT attains 68.2% SR and 61.7% SPL, exceeding VLN-PETL (57.9% SR, 54.2% SPL) by 10.3% SR. On SOON test-unseen, GOAT achieves 40.50% SR and 6.10% RGSPL, beating GridMM (36.27% SR, 4.15% RGSPL) by 4.23% SR and 1.95% RGSPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOAT outperforms all 17 compared baselines across all four datasets (R2R, REVERIE, RxR-English, SOON) on every reported metric. On R2R, the closest competitor is BEVBert [3], which GOAT surpasses by 2.82% SR and 4.13% SPL on val-unseen, and by 1.57% SR and 2.94% SPL on test-unseen; DSRG [63] is second-closest, trailing by 4.82% SR on val-unseen. On REVERIE, GOAT leads DSRG [63] by 5.68% SR and 4.52% RGSPL on test-unseen, and BEVBert [3] by 4.91% SR and 4.61% RGSPL. On RxR-English, GOAT's advantage is most pronounced, beating VLN-PETL [55] by 10.3% SR and 7.5% SPL on val-unseen, and MARM-MP [27] by 18.0% SR. On SOON, GOAT exceeds GridMM [71] by 4.23% SR and 1.95% RGSPL on test-unseen, and DUET [9] by 7.06% SR and 3.76% RGSPL. The consistent margin across seen and unseen splits (particularly the larger gains on unseen environments) supports the paper's claim that causal intervention specifically improves generalization rather than merely fitting training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOAT is architecturally built on the dual-scale graph transformer (DUET [9]) and inherits METER's [15] cross-attention encoders, but its core departure is the introduction of causal inference into VLN. Prior VLN work has primarily addressed dataset bias through data-side interventions (speaker-follower pseudo-instructions, environment editing, web-scale data collection), while prior causal learning in vision-language tasks (image captioning, VQA) applied back-door or front-door adjustments to single modalities. This paper is the first to simultaneously deploy both back-door and front-door adjustments across three modalities (vision, language, history) within a unified structural causal model for the multi-step, cross-modal VLN setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, reusable causal learning pipeline (task formulation → confounder identification → adjustment → prediction) that could transfer to other embodied or sequential decision-making tasks suffering from dataset bias. A key open question is whether the hand-crafted confounder identifications (room types, direction keywords, trajectory trends) are optimal or whether learned confounder representations could further improve debiasing. Limitations include reliance on frozen pre-trained extractors (CLIP, BLIP) for vision, the added computational overhead of maintaining confounder dictionaries and K-Means sampling, and the fact that the causal assumptions, while reasonable, remain unverified beyond the VLN domain.
