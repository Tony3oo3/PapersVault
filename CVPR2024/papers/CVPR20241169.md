## Problem

Existing LMMs for referring segmentation, such as LISA, suffer catastrophic forgetting during instruction fine-tuning: they lose the ability to detect whether a queried object is actually present in an image and to provide natural language feedback, instead hallucinating segmentation masks for non-existent objects. No prior method addresses the full 'see, say, and segment' pipeline in open-domain reasoning segmentation, where models must reject false-premise queries, suggest contextually appropriate corrections, and only produce masks when the object truly exists.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a free-form text referring expression (e.g., 'the keys with the purple heart on them') and produces a structured multi-part response: a binary presence determination, optional natural language feedback (correcting semantic errors, suggesting alternative referring expressions, or confirming the object), and a pixel-level segmentation mask only when the object is confirmed present. The language feedback is evaluated via a modified CLAIR metric that scores the suggested correction against ground-truth positive referring expressions for the same referent instance, while segmentation quality is measured by cumulative IoU (cIoU).

## Method

SESAME instantiates LLaVA-v1.5-7B as the LMM backbone with Segment Anything (SAM) as the segmentation decoder, fine-tuned via LoRA for 10 epochs. The key insight is a joint-training strategy on a unified dataset combining three sources in a 7:2:1 ratio: FP-RefCOCO(+/g) (context-aware false-premise queries with paired corrections generated by GPT-3.5-turbo by modifying a single element in positive expressions), LLaVA VQA data (to preserve general visual question-answering abilities), and R-RefCOCO(+/g) (randomly sampled non-existent objects that train the model to simply reject rather than speculate on corrections). This multi-source training prevents the model from learning superficial word-substitution heuristics (e.g., flipping 'left' to 'right') and instead grounds its responses in actual image content. The paper also proposes a simpler cascading baseline that chains an off-the-shelf LLaVA for detection and dialogue with LISA for segmentation, but SESAME's unified architecture achieves superior performance across all three capabilities without the coordination overhead of prompt chaining.

## Results

On the FP-RefCOCO(+/g) false-premise correction benchmark, SESAME achieves the best See accuracy (79.84%, 80.00%, 81.78%), Say CLAIR score (0.63, 0.61, 0.67), and Segment cIoU (57.93, 50.81, 53.79) across all three datasets, outperforming LISA by up to 55% in false-premise detection and 31% in cIoU. On standard RefCOCO(+/g) benchmarks with only positive queries, SESAME remains competitive with LISA-7B (74.7 vs 74.9 cIoU on RefCOCO; 66.1 vs 67.9 on RefCOCOg), confirming that false-premise training does not sacrifice conventional segmentation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SESAME substantially outperforms LISA [33] on all three FP-RefCOCO(+/g) metrics: See accuracy improves from 51.36% to 79.84% (+55% relative), Say CLAIR from 0.00 to 0.63, and Segment cIoU from 44.00 to 57.93 (+31.6% relative) on FP-RefCOCO. Against GRES [37], SESAME gains 14.7 points in See accuracy and 10.5 points in cIoU on FP-RefCOCO, while GRES cannot produce any language feedback (Say = –). SEEM [84] and X-Decoder [83] are weakest on false-premise detection (~51% See, equivalent to random) and offer no Say capability; SESAME surpasses them by 28+ points in See and 17–20 points in cIoU. On standard RefCOCO(+/g) (Table 4), SESAME is within 0.2–1.8 cIoU of LISA-7B (74.7 vs 74.9, 64.9 vs 65.1, 66.1 vs 67.9) and clearly beats older baselines MCN [41] (62.4), VLT [13] (67.5), CRIS [64] (70.5), and LAVT [70] (72.7) on RefCOCO, confirming that the joint false-premise training preserves conventional segmentation performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LISA's reasoning segmentation framework by addressing its critical blind spot: the inability to handle false-premise queries. Prior false-premise work in referring segmentation (GRES, R-RefCOCO) relied on auxiliary classifiers or closed-domain matching, and false-premise detection in VQA used cascaded detection-answering pipelines. SESAME departs from both by integrating 'see,' 'say,' and 'segment' into a single LMM via joint training, and by introducing the 'say' component—natural-language correction of erroneous queries—which was entirely absent from prior segmentation-focused false-premise methods. The FP-RefCOCO dataset also departs from R-RefCOCO's random-sampling negatives by using GPT-3.5-turbo to generate contextually-aware false premises paired with ground-truth corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that segmentation fine-tuning catastrophically destroys an LMM's false-premise detection (0% recall) and language feedback abilities is a practical warning for anyone adapting LMMs to new output modalities. The FP-RefCOCO(+/g) benchmark provides the community with a standardized way to evaluate this robustness dimension, which prior RefCOCO-based evaluations entirely ignored. However, absolute performance remains moderate (67.89% false-premise recall, 0.61–0.67 CLAIR scores), and the paper does not explore multi-round dialogue, multi-object false premises, or scaling to more complex reasoning chains—areas where the 'say' component would be most valuable in real deployment.
