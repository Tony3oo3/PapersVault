## Problem

Standard self-attention in vision transformers treats individual query-key correlation values independently, ignoring the geometric structure of the correlation map, which prevents the model from capturing spatial layouts in images or motion patterns in videos. Existing convolutional-projection variants (ConvSA) partially address this but can only learn a single correlation pattern, encoding diverse structural shapes into a scalar and producing identical aggregation kernels across all locations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240657/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a video clip (T×H×W×3) or a single RGB image (1×H×W×3). Video inputs are tokenized into overlapping 3D tablets of size 3×4×4×3 with stride 2×4×4, while image inputs use non-overlapping 2D patches of size 4×4×3. The network produces a class label via a standard classification head, and is trained from scratch on ImageNet-1K (images) or Kinetics-400 (video) before fine-tuning on downstream benchmarks.

## Method

Structural Self-Attention (StructSA) replaces vanilla self-attention with two coupled steps. First, Structural Query-Key Attention (SQKA) applies D convolutional kernels over the query-key correlation map q_i K_j^T to detect diverse local correlation structures, yielding a D-dimensional pattern score vector per location j. Second, Contextual Value Aggregation linearly combines these D scores with D learned context aggregators (U^V ∈ R^{M×D}) to produce a dynamic spatial kernel κ_{i,j}^{struct} that performs weighted pooling over a local chunk of value features V_j, rather than a single scalar weight. This is integrated into UniFormer to form StructViT, a four-stage hierarchical network where the last two stages replace vanilla self-attention with StructSA. The key distinction from ConvSA is that D>1 pattern detectors and D context aggregators enable diverse dynamic kernels per location, whereas ConvSA's single pattern detector and single aggregator force all locations to share the same aggregation shape.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, StructViT-L-4-1*↑384 achieves 86.7% top-1 accuracy, outperforming UniFormer-L*↑384 (86.0%) and MViTv2-L↑384 (86.0%) with fewer FLOPs (85.2G vs. 39.2G and 140.2G respectively). On Kinetics-400, StructViT-B-4-1 (32×3×4) reaches 83.4% top-1, surpassing UniFormer-B (82.9%) and MViTv2-B (82.9%). On motion-centric benchmarks, StructViT-B-4-1 sets new state-of-the-art on Something-Something V2 (71.5% top-1), Diving-48 (88.3% top-1, beating ORViT† which uses additional bbox annotations), and FineGym (54.2% Gym288 / 89.5% Gym99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructViT consistently outperforms its direct architectural baseline UniFormer [38] across all sizes and all benchmarks: +0.3 to +0.5%p on ImageNet-1K, +0.3 to +0.5%p on Kinetics-400, +0.4 to +1.3%p on Something-Something, +0.9%p on Diving-48, and +0.7%p on FineGym. On ImageNet-1K at 384×384 resolution, StructViT-B-4-1*↑384 (86.5%) surpasses MViTv2-B↑384 (85.2%) by 1.3%p and ConvNext-B↑384 (85.1%) by 1.4%p, while StructViT-L-4-1*↑384 (86.7%) beats UniFormer-L*↑384 (86.0%) and MViTv2-L↑384 (86.0%) by 0.7%p. On Kinetics-400, StructViT-B-4-1 (83.4%) outperforms Swin-B pretrained on IN-21K (82.7%) and MTV-B (81.8%) despite using only IN-1K pretraining and 55% of MTV-B's compute. On Diving-48, StructViT-B-4-1 (88.3%) surpasses ORViT† (88.0%) which relies on additional object bounding box annotations, and beats UniFormer-B* (87.4%) by 0.9%p. On Something-Something V2, StructViT-S-8-1 (69.0%) exceeds UniFormer-S (67.7%) by 1.3%p, and StructViT-B-4-1 (71.5%) sets a new state-of-the-art over MViTv2-B (70.5%) and Swin-B (69.6%). The FLOPs-matched variants (stride-2, e.g. StructViT-B-4-2) still show gains of 0.2–0.3%p over UniFormer at equal compute, confirming the benefit is not merely from extra FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructSA generalizes the convolutional self-attention (ConvSA) mechanism used in MViT, CvT, and UniFormer by showing that their channel-wise separable convolutional projections correspond to a single-pattern (D=1) instance of the proposed multi-dimensional structural attention. The paper also extends prior correlation-structure modeling work (self-similarity [57], relational self-attention [32], SELFYNet [36]) from local spatio-temporal neighborhoods to global cross-position interactions, enabling the network to capture both motion and long-range inter-object relations. Architecturally, StructViT is built by replacing all vanilla self-attention layers in UniFormer with StructSA, inheriting its hierarchical multi-scale design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is the unification showing that the field's recent push toward convolutional projections in transformers was capturing only a limited, single-pattern version of what structural attention can express; the multi-dimensional pattern detectors (D>1) enable diverse dynamic kernels that adapt aggregation to local correlation geometry. The visualization of learned kernels resembling Sobel and Laplacian filters provides an interpretable link between learned attention and classical motion cues. However, the performance gains over UniFormer are modest (0.5–1.3%p), and the additional FLOPs from the structural dimension remain a practical concern; the paper leaves open whether these structural patterns transfer to non-classification tasks or to NLP settings.
