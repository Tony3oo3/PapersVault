## Problem

Existing inlier estimation methods for partially overlapping point cloud registration score inlier confidence through coordinate-based schemes that simply capture coordinate differences, leading to massive inlier misinterpretation when significant coordinate disparities exist. This bias in confidence scoring degrades registration accuracy, and no prior method provides a principled mechanism to calibrate inlier confidence beyond raw coordinate proximity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240669/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source point cloud P (N points) and a reference point cloud Q (M points), each with 3D (x,y,z) coordinates, and expands the input to four point clouds via a 1-NN strategy before feature extraction with DGCNN. It produces a rigid transformation (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ ℝ³) computed via weighted SVD, along with per-correspondence inlier confidence scores that weight the SVD objective. The final transformation is derived from the top-Nc highest-confidence correspondences selected from the full set of estimated pairs.

## Method

The core contribution is Inlier Confidence Calibration (ICC), which constructs transformation-invariant geometric constraints (edge vectors and included angles) between the K-nearest-neighborhood patches of each source point and its matched reference copy, then scores confidence via an MLP-fused subtraction of these geometric representations weighted by learned attention coefficients. A novel soft assignment matrix optimization theorem replaces iterative Sinkhorn normalization by applying the three-sigma rule to row-wise Gaussian-normalized scores, achieving O(N) complexity versus O(G×N×M) for Sinkhorn while improving precision. The model is trained unsupervised with four loss functions: global consistency (Huber-based Chamfer), dual neighborhood consistency, geometric structure consistency (providing self-supervised signal from the ICC constraints), and spatial consistency (cross-entropy sharpening of the matching map). The pipeline follows a 'pseudo correspondences → inlier estimation' structure where the high-quality reference copy generated by the optimized soft assignment enables reliable geometric consistency measurement.

## Results

On ModelNet40 (unseen objects, 70% overlap), the proposed method achieves MAE(R)=0.0012° and MIE(R)=0.0192° for rotation, and MAE(t)=0.0000 and MIE(t)=0.0000 for translation, outperforming all 9 baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, RIE (unsupervised), records MAE(R)=0.0033° and MIE(R)=0.0210°, meaning the proposed method improves rotation error by ~2.75×. On the real-world 7Scenes dataset, the method achieves MAE(R)=0.0033° and MIE(R)=0.0181°, again the best among all compared methods. Under Gaussian noise (σ=0.5), the method maintains MAE(R)=0.0088° while supervised baselines like RPMNet degrade to 1.5890° and CEMNet to 10.7026°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 9 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Among unsupervised methods, RIE [32] is the closest competitor (MAE(R)=0.0033° vs. 0.0012° on ModelNet40), while CEMNet [17] is ~115× worse in rotation error (0.1385° vs. 0.0012°). Among supervised methods, RPMNet [38] is the strongest (MAE(R)=0.0051°), yet the proposed unsupervised method still achieves a 4.25× improvement. Traditional methods (ICP, FGR, FPFH+RANSAC) are outperformed by orders of magnitude, with ICP showing MAE(R)=3.4339° versus 0.0012°. Under Gaussian noise, the gap widens dramatically: the proposed method maintains MAE(R)=0.0088° while RPMNet degrades to 1.5890° and CEMNet to 10.7026°, highlighting the robustness advantage of geometric-structure-based inlier confidence calibration over coordinate-difference-based scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'pseudo correspondences-to-inlier estimation' pipeline established by RPM-Net [38] and RIE [32], but departs from the standard coordinate-based inlier scoring by introducing transformation-invariant geometric constraints (edge lengths and included angles) to calibrate inlier confidence. The soft assignment matrix optimization theorem is explicitly framed as a promotion of CorrNet3D [44], replacing Sinkhorn's iterative row-and-column normalization with a row-only scheme grounded in the three-sigma rule. The 1-NN input expansion strategy is carried over from the authors' own prior work [42], and DGCNN is used for local feature extraction as in the broader point cloud registration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical failure mode in partially overlapping registration where large coordinate differences cause inlier misclassification, and the geometric-structure-consistency signal provides a self-supervised training objective that eliminates the need for ground-truth transformations. The ablation showing that removing geometric constraints degrades MAE(R) from 0.0012 to 1.0123 underscores how critical the calibration step is. However, the method is evaluated only on relatively small point clouds (≤2048 points) and mild noise (N(0, 0.5)), leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale LiDAR sequences and robustness under severe occlusion or repetitive structures. The authors note future extension to cross-modality (2D-3D) registration as a natural next step.
