## Problem

In learned reweighting (LRW) classifiers, the validation set serves as the implicit generalization target, yet its composition has never been explicitly optimized. The paper asks whether selecting hard-to-classify training instances as the validation set can improve classifier generalization, and formalizes this as a meta-optimization problem over the train/validation partition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard labeled RGB image dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-100) and jointly produces three outputs: an optimized train/validation split (constrained to a fraction δ of total data), an instance-wise reweighting function parameterized by a neural network, and the final classifier. The splitter network takes both image features and ground-truth labels as input to predict soft assignment probabilities, while the reweighter takes only image features to predict per-instance weights.

## Method

MOLERE formulates a tri-level optimization jointly over data partitioning (splitter Θ), instance reweighting (φ), and classifier parameters (θ), which is simplified to a bi-level min-max game: the splitter maximizes validation-set error while the reweighter minimizes it via LRW. A soft-assignment partitioning network (inspired by Bao & Barzilay) predicts per-instance validation membership, updated by minimizing cross-entropy between its predictions and classifier accuracy, with regularizers preserving label distribution and train/test ratio. The paper proves that asymptotically the objective reduces to a robust optimization equivalent to dual DRO, identifying the hardest δ-fraction of samples. A simpler train-twice heuristic uses probabilistic margins from an ERM classifier to rank instances and assign the lowest-margin subset as validation, yielding the LRW-Hard variant. The end-to-end LRWOpt algorithm updates the splitter and reweighter every Q classifier steps, with periodic ratio and label-distribution regularization.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with a ViT-B/16 backbone, LRW-Hard achieves 85.09% top-1 accuracy, outperforming ERM (84.11%) by ~1% and LRW-Easy (83.17%) by 1.92%, confirming the strict ordering Easy < Random < Hard. On CIFAR-100 with extreme label skew (ratio 200), LRWOpt reaches 42.33% accuracy versus 40.36% for GDW and 40.11% for MWN. Under instance-dependent label noise, LRWOpt achieves 71.87% on Inst. C-10 and 73.97% on Clothing1M, surpassing the best baselines (MAPLE at 70.34% and GDW at 73.12%) by 1.53% and 0.85% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRWOpt consistently outperforms all nine baselines across in-distribution, OOD, and noisy-label settings. MWN [32] and FSR [40] are the most frequently cited competitors; LRWOpt beats MWN by 0.92% on ImageNet-1K (84.94 vs 84.02) and by 5.98% on Inst. C-10 (71.87 vs 65.89), while FSR trails by 4.29% on CIFAR-100 at skew 200 (42.33 vs 38.04). GDW [6] and L2R [31] are competitive on moderate-noise settings but still lose to LRWOpt by 1.5–2.8% on noisy benchmarks. MAPLE [41] and StableNet [39], designed for OOD robustness, are the closest competitors on domain-shift datasets (CAMELYON, iWildCam) but remain below LRWOpt; the paper notes StableNet as the nearest rival on OOD tasks. MBR [22] and RHO-Loss [24] perform at or near ERM level, and MBR is among the worst performers, indicating that simple margin-based upweighting without validation-set optimization is insufficient. BiLAW [14] is outperformed in the reweighting comparisons shown in Figures 1–2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the learned reweighting (LRW) literature—Ren et al. [31], Meta-Weight-Net [32], FSR [40], MAPLE [41]—which uses bilevel optimization to align a weighted training loss with an unweighted validation loss. The key departure is treating the validation set itself as an optimization variable rather than a fixed input: prior LRW work assumes a representative or pseudo-clean validation set, while MOLERE jointly optimizes the train/validation split and the reweighting classifier. The probabilistic-margin criterion for identifying hard instances borrows from adversarial reweighting [22], and the soft-splitting mechanism draws on bias-detection partitioning networks [3]. The authors also note a formal connection to dual DRO [5], a direction they flag as unexplored in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strict empirical ordering (LRW-Easy < LRW-Random < LRW-Hard) across a wide range of datasets, architectures, and shift settings provides strong evidence that validation-set composition is a first-order design choice in LRW, not a minor hyperparameter. The finding that using naturally hard OOD examples (ImageNet-A/R) as validation improves both in-domain and OOD accuracy simultaneously suggests a practical, zero-extra-cost recipe for practitioners. However, the authors acknowledge that the probabilistic-margin heuristic degrades under very high label noise, and the theoretical connection to dual DRO remains a proof-of-concept rather than a full characterization. The broader open question—how to formally optimize the meta-learning workflow beyond margin-based heuristics—is explicitly left for future work.
