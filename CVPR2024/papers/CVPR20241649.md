## Problem

Current multi-modality 3D pre-training methods (e.g., SLidR, Seal) rely on frame-level superpixel-superpoint contrastive consistency, which treats superpixels sharing the same semantics from different scenes as negative pairs, causing cross-scene semantic self-conflict. Additionally, no existing method establishes a globally unified semantic bond that enforces consistency of same-semantic segments across all scenes, limiting the generalization of the learned 3D representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a point cloud frame (K points, each with 3D coordinates and intensity) and L calibrated surround RGB images (H×W×3) per keyframe from the nuScenes dataset. A frozen DINOv2 VFM generates semantic-aware superpixels, while a category-sensitive variant (Mask2Former-based DINOv2 fine-tuned on ADE20K with 150 classes) provides discrete semantic cues. The output is a pre-trained 3D backbone (MinkUNet, VoxelNet, or Cylinder3D) whose point-wise features encode scene-level semantic consistency, ready for fine-tuning on downstream tasks with as little as 1% annotations.

## Method

CSC introduces scene-level semantic consistency through two components. First, VFM-Assisted Semantic Prototype Generation groups all superpixel and superpoint embeddings across diverse scenes by their VFM-assigned semantic sign (T=150 classes from ADE20K), producing separate 2D and 3D prototype sets via averaging. Second, a Multi-Modality Prototype Blending (MMPB) module aligns the heterogeneous 2D and 3D prototypes into a shared feature space through modality-specific linear projections, then fuses them into mixed prototypes via a second linear layer. A prototype-based contrastive loss (L_pro) pulls each 3D superpoint embedding toward its corresponding mixed prototype while pushing away all others, and is combined with the standard frame-level superpixel-superpoint contrastive loss (L_sp); L_pro is only activated after epoch λ=5 to allow initial feature stabilization. This design replaces the frame-level negative-pair constraint with a cross-scene positive-prototype constraint, eliminating the self-conflict between same-semantic segments from different scenes.

## Results

On nuScenes with limited 3D annotations, CSC achieves 47.0 mIoU at 1% and 57.0 mIoU at 5% for semantic segmentation (MinkUNet), outperforming the prior SOTA Seal by +1.2 and +1.4 mIoU respectively. For object detection (VoxelNet+CenterPoint), CSC reaches 45.3 mAP at 5% and 51.9 mAP at 20%, surpassing TriCC by +0.7 and +1.0 mAP. For panoptic segmentation (Cylinder3D+Panoptic-PolarNet), CSC attains 19.3 PQ at 1% and 23.1 PQ at 5%, improving over SLidR by +3.0 and +1.5 PQ. On the out-of-distribution SemanticKITTI benchmark, CSC also leads with 47.2 mIoU at 1% annotation (+0.6 over Seal).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSC consistently outperforms all compared baselines across all three downstream tasks. Against the prior SOTA Seal [32] (NeurIPS 2023), CSC gains +1.2 mIoU at 1% and +1.4 mIoU at 5% on nuScenes semantic segmentation, and +0.6 mIoU on SemanticKITTI, demonstrating that scene-level semantic consistency via category-sensitive VFM cues surpasses Seal's frame-level approach with category-insensitive VFM. Against TriCC [39] (CVPR 2023), CSC improves object detection by +0.7 mAP at 5% and +1.0 mAP at 20% without requiring explicit temporal consistency loops. Against SLidR [42] (CVPR 2022), the original superpixel-superpoint distillation framework, CSC achieves the largest gains in panoptic segmentation (+3.0 PQ at 1%), where no prior multi-modality pre-training method had been evaluated. Earlier uni-modality baselines (Point Con. [50], Depth Con. [52], PPKT [33], GCC-3D [30]) are all surpassed by a wider margin, confirming the advantage of multi-modality VFM-assisted pre-training over purely point-cloud or depth-based self-supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSC extends the SLidR → Seal lineage of multi-modality 3D pre-training, which performs superpixel-superpoint contrastive distillation between calibrated images and point clouds. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing category-insensitive VFMs (SAM, SEEM in Seal) with a category-sensitive VFM (DINOv2 + Mask2Former fine-tuned on ADE20K) to obtain coherent semantic cues across scenes, and (2) shifting from frame-level contrastive consistency to scene-level semantic consistency via multi-modality prototypes that aggregate superpixel/superpoint features across all scenes sharing the same semantic label. The multi-modality prototype blending module (MMPB) is a new architectural component that fuses 2D and 3D prototypes from heterogeneous feature spaces, drawing inspiration from prototype-based contrastive learning in 2D re-identification and unsupervised segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSC establishes the first multi-modality 3D pre-training framework to achieve cross-scene semantic consistency, and is the first to transfer such pre-trained backbones to panoptic segmentation, a task previously unexplored in this setting. The consistent gains across three granularities (segmentation, detection, panoptic) with limited annotations suggest the scene-level consistency principle is broadly applicable. However, the approach depends on a VFM fine-tuned on ADE20K (150 classes) for semantic cues, raising open questions about coverage of driving-specific semantics and scalability to open-vocabulary or novel-class scenarios. The modest absolute improvements (+1–3%) over the strong Seal baseline also leave room for further gains, particularly in instance-level discrimination (RQ) where gains are minimal.
