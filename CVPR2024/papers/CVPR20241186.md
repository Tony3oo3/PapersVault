## Problem

Single-image inverse rendering is fundamentally ill-posed because the object's reflectance acts as a low-pass angular filter on the incident illumination, irreversibly attenuating high-frequency components. Prior deterministic methods can only recover a blurry, frequency-bounded illumination estimate, limiting their utility for relighting or object insertion. The paper argues that recovering the true illumination requires a stochastic generative formulation rather than a single-point estimate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of an object with known surface normals (e.g., from an RGB-D sensor) and produces an illumination environment map (represented as the reflectance map of a perfect mirror sphere) plus parametric reflectance (Disney BSDF: diffuse color, specular strength, roughness, metallicness). The input image is first mapped onto a Gaussian hemisphere reflectance map via median aggregation over pixels sharing the same normal, then completed by a separate diffusion model (ObsNet) to fill sparse regions. Illumination is evaluated at 128×256 resolution; reflectance is evaluated in a non-parametric MERL BRDF representation for model-agnostic comparison.

## Method

DRMNet formulates the radiometric forward process on the geometry-invariant reflectance map as a deterministic reflectance-filtering operation (reducing surface roughness from the observed value toward zero, i.e., perfect mirror) compounded with additive Gaussian observation noise, making the overall forward process stochastic. The reverse process is learned by two cooperating subnetworks: IllNet, a U-Net that iteratively denoises the current reflectance map one step closer to the perfect-mirror reflectance map, and RefNet, an MLP that jointly estimates the object's reflectance parameters from the observed and current maps. The parameter-space trajectory uses geometric decay (η factor), so smaller roughness steps at higher frequencies receive denser sampling, aiding robust high-frequency recovery. Training minimizes an ELBO-derived reflectance-map loss plus a reflectance-parameter loss, with illumination, reflectance, and timestep uniformly sampled from a large synthetic dataset rendered in Mitsuba3. A separate ObsNet diffusion model handles the many-to-one pixel-to-normal mapping and inpaints the sparse reflectance map before DRMNet inference.

## Results

On the nLMVS-Real dataset, DRMNet achieves logRMSE 1.18, PSNR 14.6, SSIM 0.33, and LPIPS 0.60 for illumination estimation in 14.3 seconds, approaching the accuracy of ALP (logRMSE 0.95) which requires pre-acquired reflectance from multiple views, while being ~20× faster. On the iBRDF synthetic dataset, DRMNet attains the best illumination logRMSE of 2.62 and reflectance logRMSE of 0.66, outperforming all baselines. For downstream relighting and object replacement on nLMVS-Real, DRMNet achieves the best logRMSE of 1.68 (relighting) and 0.35 (object replacement), surpassing even ALP on object replacement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRMNet outperforms LIME [53] and DPI [49] on every metric across all three datasets, with particularly large margins on illumination logRMSE (1.18 vs. 7.05 and 3.57 on nLMVS-Real). Against iBRDF [14], DRMNet improves illumination logRMSE from 2.13 to 1.18 and reflectance logRMSE from 1.01 to 0.66, while reducing computation time from 5048 s to 14.3 s. Compared to the concurrent diffusion-based DPI [49], DRMNet achieves lower logRMSE (1.18 vs. 3.57) and better LPIPS (0.60 vs. 0.65), attributed to its integrated stochastic forward model rather than using diffusion as an external prior. Against ALP [78], which requires pre-acquired reflectance from multiple views, DRMNet is slightly worse on illumination logRMSE (1.18 vs. 0.95) but wins on LPIPS (0.60 vs. 0.66) and object replacement logRMSE (0.35 vs. 0.39), all while being ~19× faster (14.3 s vs. 267 s) and operating from a single image. On the DeLight-Net dataset, DRMNet beats the DeLight-Net baseline [27] on both illumination (0.694 vs. 0.864 logRMSE) and relighting (0.414 vs. 1.027 logRMSE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reflectance map formulation (Horn 1986; Rematas et al. 2016) and the diffusion-model framework for inverse problems (DDPM; Rissanen et al.'s heat-dissipation generative model), but departs fundamentally from prior single-image inverse rendering methods (Lombardi & Nishino 2012; iBRDF 2022; LIME 2018) that treat illumination and reflectance recovery as a deterministic estimation or optimization problem. The key conceptual departure is formulating the forward radiometric process as a stochastic diffusion on the reflectance map—where the reflectance itself acts as the unknown forward operator—rather than using diffusion as an external prior (as in the concurrent DPI [Lyu et al. 2023]) or relying on differentiable rendering to bridge image and 3D surface domains. The Disney BSDF parameterization provides a concrete trajectory space for the iterative reverse process, and the ObsNet inpainting module extends standard diffusion inpainting to handle the many-to-one pixel-to-normal mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central argument—that frequency attenuation by reflectance makes single-image inverse rendering inherently a generative problem with no unique 'true' solution—challenges the deterministic estimation paradigm that has dominated the field and provides a principled justification for stochastic methods. Practically, this enables relighting and object insertion with arbitrary (including high-frequency) reflectance, which prior methods could not support due to their low-frequency illumination estimates. Key limitations include the assumption of homogeneous reflectance, known geometry, and orthographic projection; extending to spatially varying BRDFs, multiple materials, and perspective projection remains open. The framework could generalize to other blind inverse problems where the forward operator is unknown, but the current scope (single object, single material) limits immediate broad applicability.
