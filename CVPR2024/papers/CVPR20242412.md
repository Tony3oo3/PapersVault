## Problem

Existing temporal action detection (TAD) methods treat pretrained short-term Vision Transformers as off-the-shelf snippet-level feature extractors, ignoring fine-grained temporal relationships across snippets in a broader context. It remains unclear how to adapt these pretrained ViTs into a unified long-form video transformer that captures inter-snippet structure while keeping computation and memory low enough for end-to-end training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242412/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed RGB video, divides it into non-overlapping snippets of 16 frames each (16 snippets for THUMOS14 at 8 FPS/160×160, 24 for FineAction, 48 for ActivityNet-1.3), and outputs K action instances each with a start timestamp, end timestamp, category label, and confidence score. Spatial resolution is adapted from the pretrained ViT's 224×224 via bicubic interpolation of positional embeddings to 112×112 or 160×160, and a learnable clip-level positional encoding is added to each temporal position to encode the global temporal order across snippets.

## Method

ViT-TAD adapts a pretrained plain ViT (VideoMAE) into a unified long-form video transformer by inserting cross-snippet propagation modules at two levels. Inside the backbone, the ViT is split into subsets and a Global Propagation Block (temporal self-attention across all snippets at each spatial location, reducing complexity from O(H²W²Ls²NsC) to O(HWT²C)) or a Local Propagation Block (3D convolution bottleneck) is inserted after each subset to gradually exchange information. After the backbone, a post-backbone module of 3 temporal transformer encoder layers (inspired by DETR) performs clip-level modeling on spatially-pooled snippet features to enlarge the temporal receptive field. The framework is head-agnostic, pairing with BasicTAD (one-stage anchor-free) or AFSD (two-stage anchor-free), and is trained end-to-end with a checkpoint strategy to fit within limited GPU memory.

## Results

On THUMOS14, ViT-TAD with ViT-B achieves 69.5 average mAP (tIoU 0.3–0.7), surpassing all end-to-end TAD methods and the non-end-to-end TriDet (68.2) by 1.3 mAP, while only slightly trailing VideoMAE V2 with ViT-G (69.6). On ActivityNet-1.3, ViT-TAD (ViT-B) reaches 37.40 average mAP, the best among end-to-end methods, outperforming Re2TAL (37.01) and TALLFormer (35.60). On FineAction, ViT-TAD (ViT-B) achieves 17.20 average mAP, approaching VideoMAE V2 (18.24) while using a much smaller backbone (ViT-B vs. ViT-G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViT-TAD (ViT-B) outperforms all end-to-end TAD baselines on THUMOS14: it beats TALLFormer (63.1) by 6.4 mAP, Re2TAL (61.5) by 8.0 mAP, and the BasicTAD baseline (59.4) by 10.1 mAP. Against the strongest non-end-to-end method TriDet (69.3 with I3D, 68.2 with ViT-B at 30 FPS/224×224), ViT-TAD achieves 69.5 mAP with only 8 FPS/160×160 input, demonstrating that end-to-end training with cross-snippet propagation compensates for lower input resolution. On ActivityNet-1.3, ViT-TAD (ViT-B) leads all end-to-end methods at 37.40 avg mAP, edging out Re2TAL (37.01) and TALLFormer (35.60). On FineAction, ViT-TAD (ViT-B) reaches 17.20 avg mAP, trailing only VideoMAE V2 (18.24, ViT-G) by 1.04 mAP while using a substantially smaller backbone. The paper also shows that its propagation modules transfer to TALLFormer, improving it from 63.1 to 66.1 mAP, confirming the modules' generality beyond the ViT-TAD architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the ViT-Det philosophy of windowed attention for detection to the temporal domain, but departs from prior TAD work that treated transformer backbones (VideoSwin, MViT) as off-the-shelf snippet-level feature extractors with no cross-snippet interaction. It builds directly on the plain ViT backbone and the DETR encoder design, introducing cross-snippet propagation modules (local convolutional and global temporal self-attention) to unify multiple snippets within a single transformer, while the post-backbone temporal transformer layers extend the receptive field in a DETR-inspired manner. Unlike hierarchical transformers that cannot leverage VideoMAE masked pre-training, this approach preserves the plain ViT structure, enabling direct transfer of self-supervised video pre-training weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViT-TAD establishes the first end-to-end TAD pipeline built on a plain ViT backbone, demonstrating that the powerful VideoMAE pre-training can be directly transferred to action detection without architectural compromises. The proposed propagation modules are shown to be transferable (improving TALLFormer by 3 mAP), suggesting they are a general recipe for adapting short-term ViTs to long-form video tasks. Limitations include reliance on frame downscaling for memory efficiency, the one-stage anchor-free head's susceptibility to background errors, and the finding that excessive global interaction (12 blocks) degrades performance, leaving open how to optimally balance local and global temporal modeling at scale.
