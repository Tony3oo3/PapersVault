## Problem

Existing SNN-based object detection for event cameras lacks multi-scale feature fusion, which is critical in SNNs because it integrates not only spatial but also temporal information across layers. Additionally, no prior work has systematically studied how spiking decoding strategies and loss functions affect SNN performance on event data, leaving a gap in understanding what decoding and training choices are optimal for this modality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242177/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes asynchronous event streams from event cameras, encoded as voxel cubes with T=5 time bins and n=2 micro time bins per bin (yielding C=4 channels: 2 polarities × 2 micro bins). On GEN1, it outputs bounding boxes with class labels (car, pedestrian) via an SSD head; on NCAR, it outputs binary car/background classification. Spiking Rate Decoding is applied to the feature maps before they enter the non-spiking SSD detection head, normalizing outputs to [0,1] to match the prediction range.

## Method

SFOD combines a Spiking DenseNet backbone with a novel Spiking Fusion Module that fuses the first three multi-scale feature maps (30×38, 15×19, 7×9) via 1×1 convolution, transposed-convolution upsampling, channel concatenation, and a Spiking Pyramid Extraction Submodule (SPES) built from SEW Res Blocks to regenerate refined multi-scale features. All activation functions are replaced with PLIF neurons, and the network is trained directly via surrogate gradients (not ANN-to-SNN conversion). A key empirical finding is that Spiking Rate Decoding paired with MSE loss outperforms all other decoding/loss combinations for SNNs on event data, because MSE gradients directly reflect decoded-value errors without the softmax distortion that CE introduces on discrete spike outputs. The SSD detection head operates without spiking, receiving decoded (non-spike) feature maps, and the overall architecture achieves 32.1% mAP on GEN1 with 11.9M parameters and 24.04% firing rate.

## Results

On the GEN1 object detection dataset, SFOD achieves a state-of-the-art mAP@0.5:0.95 of 32.1% (mAP@0.5 of 59.3%) among SNN-based methods, with 11.9M parameters, 24.04% firing rate, 6.7 ms inference time, and 7.26 mJ energy consumption. On the NCAR classification dataset, the pretraining backbone (DenseNet121-16 with Spiking Rate Decoding and MSE loss) reaches 93.7% accuracy at 14.70% firing rate, setting a new SNN-based record. Compared to the prior best SNN detector (EMS-YOLO at 31.0% mAP), SFOD improves mAP by 1.1 points while using fewer parameters (11.9M vs. 14.4M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SNN-based detectors on GEN1, SFOD (32.1% mAP) outperforms EMS-YOLO (31.0%), DenseNet121-24+SSD (18.9%), VGG-11+SSD (17.4%), and MobileNet-64+SSD (14.7%), with the largest gap being +13.2 points over the prior Cordone et al. baselines. Against non-SNN methods, SFOD surpasses Inception+SSD (30.1%) and MatrixLSTM (31.0%) but trails RED (40.0%) and RVT (47.2%), which leverage RNN/Transformer architectures with significantly more parameters (24.1M and 18.5M respectively). On the NCAR classification task, SFOD's backbone (93.7%) beats all SNN baselines (best prior: VGG-11 at 92.4%) and most non-SNN methods, trailing only Asynet (94.4%) by 0.7 points. In terms of efficiency, SFOD uses fewer parameters than RED and RVT while consuming less energy (7.26 mJ vs. >24.08 mJ for RED), though its 6.7 ms inference time is slower than RVT's 10.2 ms and the Cordone et al. SNN baselines (1.7–4.4 ms). Overall, SFOD establishes the best SNN-based detection performance on GEN1 while remaining competitive with non-SNN approaches in accuracy and clearly superior in energy efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN-based event camera detection line initiated by Cordone et al. [9], reusing their Spiking DenseNet backbone, voxel cube encoding, and SSD head, but introduces the first multi-scale feature fusion mechanism (Spiking Fusion Module with SPES) for SNNs applied to event data. It also draws on the broader SNN training literature—PLIF neurons, surrogate gradient optimization, and the SEW Res Block from Fang et al. [11]—to design an SNN-compatible fusion pipeline that avoids the element-wise summation and bilinear interpolation used in non-SNN FPN-style architectures. The empirical study on spiking decoding strategies and loss functions is positioned as a first-of-its-kind analysis for SNNs on event cameras, filling a gap left by prior work that treated these choices as fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-doubling of mAP over the prior SNN baseline (18.9% → 32.1%) demonstrates that multi-scale fusion, long standard in non-SNN detectors, was a critical missing ingredient in SNN event detection, and the empirical findings on Spiking Rate Decoding + MSE provide actionable guidance for future SNN design on event data. However, SFOD still trails the best non-SNN methods (RVT at 47.2% mAP) by a wide margin, and the evaluation is limited to two datasets with relatively simple scenes (cars and pedestrians). Open questions include whether the fusion strategy generalizes to more complex or cluttered scenes, whether the decoding/loss findings hold at larger scale, and how the model performs on neuromorphic hardware where the low firing rate advantage would be most impactful.
