## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: single-view implicit methods (e.g., DRDF) can predict hidden surfaces but produce inconsistent results when applied independently to multiple images, while multi-view methods (MVS, plane-based reconstruction) achieve coherence but only recover visible geometry. No prior work reconstructs complete scene geometry—including occluded surfaces—coherently from a variable number of sparse, wide-baseline posed images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241652/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary number (1–5) of posed RGB images with known relative camera transforms, where views have wide baselines (median 2.8 m translation, 63.9° rotation). At inference, it generates 128×128 query rays per camera with 256 uniformly spaced points along each ray over a 0–8 m depth range, producing N×128×128×256 query pairs. The output is a 3D point cloud covering both visible and hidden surfaces within the union of all camera frustums, derived from positive-to-negative zero-crossings of the predicted DRDF field along each ray.

## Method

3DFIRES fuses multi-view information in feature space rather than point space, enabling the network to reason about which camera best informs each 3D point. The architecture has three modules: a pretrained BEiT/DPT backbone extracts pixel-aligned appearance features per image; a Query Encoder encodes the relative viewing direction (Δr) and normalized device coordinates (NDC) of the query point in each camera frame; and a DRDF Predictor applies self-attention over the concatenated per-camera (appearance + query) features to produce a fused feature, from which an MLP predicts the DRDF value along the query ray. Training uses Gaussian-distributed point sampling centered at surface intersections (rather than uniform sampling) with L1 loss on log-space truncated DRDF, and the model is trained on randomly sampled 1–3 views. A key empirical finding is that combining ray attention with Gaussian sampling creates a train-time shortcut that fails at inference, making Gaussian sampling alone the superior strategy.

## Results

On the Gibson/Omnidata scene dataset (ρ=0.2), 3DFIRES achieves an All F-score of 65.71 with 1 view (matching SV-DRDF at 65.21), 66.56 with 3 views, and 71.41 with 5 views. The most striking gain is in multi-view consistency: 3DFIRES reaches 85.48% (3 views) and 85.92% (5 views), surpassing the next-best baseline SV-DRDF (76.44% and 78.13%) by over 9 percentage points. For hidden surfaces specifically, 3DFIRES scores 49.99 (3 views) and 49.52 (5 views), outperforming SV-DRDF (48.02, 47.51) and MCC (42.91, 38.51).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the visible-surface upper bound (MiDaS depth with ground-truth scale/shift), 3DFIRES trades ~11–12 points of visible F-score for the ability to reconstruct hidden surfaces, yielding a higher overall All F-score (e.g., 66.56 vs. 63.90 at 3 views). Against MCC [43], 3DFIRES wins decisively on every metric: at 3 views it gains +11.8 in All F-score (66.56 vs. 54.78) and +15.3 in consistency (85.48 vs. 70.20). Against SV-DRDF [20] (single-view DRDF applied independently per image then merged in point space), 3DFIRES is essentially tied at 1 view (65.71 vs. 65.21 All F-score) but pulls ahead with multiple views: +0.95 All F-score and +9.04 consistency at 3 views, and +0.87 All F-score and +7.79 consistency at 5 views. The consistency gap is the paper's headline claim—feature-space fusion produces a coherent reconstruction across frustums where point-space aggregation creates doubled or missing surfaces. Notably, 3DFIRES was trained on at most 3 views yet generalizes to 5 views without retraining, maintaining its lead over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Directed Ray Distance Function (DRDF) formulation from Kulkarni et al. [20] from single-view to multi-view scene reconstruction, introducing a self-attention mechanism that fuses pixel-aligned features across cameras in feature space rather than aggregating point clouds in 3D space. It departs from sparse-view reconstruction methods (PlaneFormers, NoPESAC) that are restricted to visible planar surfaces, and from NeRF-based approaches that optimize photometric quality at the expense of geometric fidelity. The key architectural departure is treating multi-view DRDF prediction as a query-attention problem where the network learns which camera provides the most informative evidence for each 3D point, with single-view DRDF as the N=1 special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to reconstruct hidden surfaces from sparse wide-baseline views at scene scale, filling a gap between single-view completion (which handles occlusion but ignores multi-view evidence) and multi-view stereo (which handles consistency but only visible geometry). The practical limitation is the dependence on accurate camera poses with known translation scale; the authors acknowledge that jointly solving for poses and geometry is an open challenge. The finding that ray attention combined with Gaussian sampling causes the network to exploit a train-time shortcut (query point density) and catastrophically fail at inference is a useful cautionary result for practitioners combining sampling strategies with attention mechanisms.
