## Problem

Standard absolute pose estimation relies on P3P (three 2D-3D point correspondences), but most popular feature detectors (SIFT, ORB) already output scale and orientation estimates that remain unused for pose. While affine correspondences can reduce the minimal sample to one (P1AC), affine features are expensive to compute; the paper asks whether the cheaper scale-and-orientation information available by default can similarly reduce the minimal sample size for absolute pose.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D-3D point correspondences augmented with per-feature scale ratios and orientation angles, a surface normal at each 3D point, and (for UP1SIFT) a gravity direction from an IMU or vertical vanishing point. Reference image poses are assumed known. A key design choice is that P2ORI expresses the query pose in world coordinates rather than relative to a single reference, so the two correspondences may come from different reference images, expanding the RANSAC sample pool. Output is a 6DOF pose (R_query, t_query) for P2ORI or a 4DOF pose (single yaw angle around gravity plus translation) for UP1SIFT.

## Method

The core mechanism combines the affine-to-pose constraint derived by Ventura et al. (P1AC) with the algebraic relationships between affine transformations and scale/orientation features established by Barath and Kukelova, yielding direct constraints on the absolute pose that involve only orientations and scales. For P2ORI, two oriented features each supply three constraints (one orientation-only constraint plus two projection equations); after eliminating translation via Gauss-Jordan elimination, the remaining system is parameterized with the Cayley rotation form, producing three quadratic equations in (x,y,z) solved by an efficient 3-quadric intersection algorithm yielding up to eight candidate poses. For UP1SIFT, the known gravity direction decomposes rotation into a known R_XZ and a single unknown yaw R_Y; using the tangent half-angle substitution r = tan(θ/2) reduces the problem to a single quadratic in r, giving two solutions. A supplementary decomposition solver (Sec. 3.6) recovers compatible scale/orientation values from a given affine matrix for synthetic experiments.

## Results

On Cambridge Landmarks with SuperPoint+LightGlue+SelfScaleOri features, P2ORI achieves a weighted-average position error of 14 cm and rotation error of 0.41°, outperforming P3P (120 cm / 1.56°) and P1AC (25 cm / 0.74°). On the challenging Street scene, P2ORI reduces position error from 197 cm (P3P) to 21 cm, and UP1SIFT further to 6 cm, with recall at 0.05 m/1° jumping from 7.8% (P3P) to 30.0% (P2ORI) and 46.3% (UP1SIFT). On Aachen Day-Night with SP+LG+SSO, P2ORI achieves the best Day recall at 0.25 m/2° (68.2%) and the best Night recall at 0.25 m/2° (71.7%), while UP1SIFT leads on Night at 0.5 m/5° (88.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2ORI consistently outperforms P3P [15] across all Cambridge Landmarks scenes, with the gap most dramatic on Street (21 cm vs. 197 cm position error; 30.0% vs. 7.8% recall at 0.05 m/1°). Against P1AC [57], P2ORI wins on position error (5 cm vs. 8 cm average) and recall at both thresholds (80.1% vs. 76.5% at 0.05 m/1°), while P1AC is slightly better on rotation error (0.23° vs. 0.14° is actually P2ORI winning). UP1SIFT, which uses ground-truth gravity, achieves the best position error (3 cm avg, 6 cm Street) and highest recall at 0.1 m/1° (88.8%), but P2ORI is competitive without requiring gravity. On Aachen Day-Night, P2ORI achieves the best Day recall at the strictest threshold (68.2% at 0.25 m/2° with SP+LG+SSO) and the best Night recall at 0.25 m/2° (71.7%), while UP1SIFT leads on Night at 0.5 m/5° (88.0%). UP2P [25], the prior gravity-aware two-point solver, is outperformed by both proposed methods on recall across all feature types tested (e.g., 76.2% vs. 80.1% for P2ORI at 0.05 m/1° on Cambridge Landmarks with SP+LG+SSO).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the affine-correspondence-based absolute pose solver P1AC (Ventura et al., ICCV 2023) and the algebraic relationships between affine transformations and scaled/oriented features established by Barath and Kukelova (ECCV 2022). By substituting the affine matrix in P1AC's pose constraint with expressions derived from feature scales and orientations, the authors obtain pose constraints that require only the scale and orientation outputs already produced by standard detectors like SIFT and ORB, rather than the more expensive affine covariant features. The gravity-aware solver UP1SIFT further builds on the tangent half-angle parameterization from Kukelova et al. (ACCV 2010) for the two-point gravity-aware case, reducing the rotational unknowns to a single angle. A key departure from P1AC is that the solvers are formulated in the world coordinate system rather than relative to a single reference camera, enabling correspondences from multiple reference images within a single RANSAC sample.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing the minimal sample size from three (P3P) to two (P2ORI) or one (UP1SIFT) directly lowers the exponential RANSAC sampling cost, which is most impactful in high-outlier-ratio scenarios like the Street scene where P2ORI achieves 21 cm median error versus 197 cm for P3P. The practical significance is high because scale and orientation are already computed by default in nearly all modern feature pipelines (SIFT, ORB, SuperPoint+SelfScaleOri), so no additional detection cost is incurred. Limitations include the requirement for surface normals (from SfM or learned depth) and increased sensitivity to orientation and scale noise compared to point-only solvers. Open questions include extending to uncalibrated cameras, non-minimal configurations, and integration into SfM/SLAM pipelines, as noted by the authors.
