## Problem

Existing zero-reference low-light enhancement methods lack a genuine physical concept of illumination, making them sensitive to training data distribution and requiring manual hyperparameter tuning. They cannot consistently produce well-exposed results across diverse unseen scenarios because they rely on artificially set parameters rather than learning illumination invariance from first principles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240163/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-light RGB image at inference time and outputs an enhanced RGB image at the same resolution. During training, only normal-light images from COCO-2017 are used—no low-light data or paired samples are required. The lightweight distilled version processes a 1024×1024 image in 0.03 seconds on a Tesla M40, while the full diffusion-based version requires 10 denoising steps via DPM-Solver++.

## Method

The framework extracts a physical quadruple prior (H, C, W, O) from the input image, derived from the Kubelka-Munk light transfer theory, which captures illumination-invariant spectral slope, chroma, spatial derivative, and RGB channel order. This prior conditions a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net via a ControlNet-style encoder, trained with a combined noise-prediction and latent-reconstruction loss on normal-light COCO images only. A bypass decoder fuses encoder features from the input image with the SD latent to recover fine details lost in the autoencoder compression. For practical deployment, the multi-step diffusion process is distilled into a single forward pass of a lightweight CNN-Transformer (Restormer) network with 327k parameters, achieving comparable quality at 500× faster inference.

## Results

On LOL, the proposed method (trained solely on COCO) achieves PSNR 20.31, SSIM 0.808, LPIPS 0.202, and LOE 0.281, outperforming all unsupervised baselines. On MIT-Adobe FiveK, it reaches PSNR 18.51, SSIM 0.785, LPIPS 0.163, and LOE 0.188, again leading all unsupervised methods. On the unpaired set (no ground truth), it attains the best BRISQUE (14.64) and noise level (0.423). The lightweight distilled version maintains comparable performance (PSNR 20.45/18.15 on LOL/MIT) while running 500× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 15 baselines across both LOL and MIT-Adobe FiveK among unsupervised approaches, achieving the best PSNR (20.31 vs. 19.70 for PairLIE, 19.67 for NeRCo), SSIM (0.808 vs. 0.774 for PairLIE), and LPIPS (0.202 vs. 0.235 for PairLIE) on LOL, and the best PSNR (18.51 vs. 17.33 for NeRCo, 17.00 for CLIP-LIT) and LOE (0.188 vs. 0.194 for CLIP-LIT) on MIT. Among zero-reference methods, the advantage is most pronounced: SCI trained on MIT achieves only PSNR 11.67 on LOL and 16.29 on MIT, while RUAS drops to PSNR 5.00–5.15 on MIT depending on training set, whereas Ours maintains 20.31 and 18.51 respectively. Notably, Ours generalizes better than supervised methods: Retinexformer trained on LOL scores PSNR 13.02 on MIT (vs. Ours 18.51), and DiffLL trained on LOL+ scores 15.81 on MIT (vs. Ours 18.51), demonstrating that the physical quadruple prior enables domain-agnostic illumination understanding. On the unpaired set, Ours achieves the lowest BRISQUE (14.64) and noise level (0.423), surpassing even supervised methods like Retinexformer (BRISQUE 14.77, NL 1.064). The lightweight distilled version (327k params) matches or slightly exceeds the full model's PSNR (20.45/18.15) while running 500× faster, outperforming NeRCo in visual quality with comparable metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Retinex-based paradigm in low-light enhancement, which decomposes an image into illumination and reflectance components, by instead extracting only illumination-invariant features (the physical quadruple prior H, C, W, O derived from Kubelka-Munk light transfer theory) and delegating the reconstruction of illumination-related information to a pretrained generative diffusion model (Stable Diffusion via ControlNet). It builds on the Gaussian color model framework of Gevers et al. and the learnable linear mapping of CICnv to obtain spectral quantities from RGB, but replaces their manually defined mapping with one learned from natural image distributions. Among zero-reference methods (ZeroDCE, SCI, RUAS), this work is the first to ground its prior in an explicit physical light-transfer model rather than in empirically designed curve functions or architecture search, and it is the first to leverage a large-scale pretrained diffusion model as the prior-to-image bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a physically motivated illumination-invariant prior, combined with a generative model, can match or exceed supervised methods on unseen datasets without any low-light training data, addressing a key practical limitation of current zero-reference approaches that are sensitive to training data distribution. The bypass decoder is a general-purpose technique for mitigating autoencoder detail distortion in diffusion-based restoration pipelines, with demonstrated utility beyond low-light enhancement (e.g., colorization). The 500× speedup in the distilled lightweight version (327k parameters, 0.03s per 1024×1024 image) makes the approach deployable in real-time settings. Open questions include whether the physical prior can be extended to handle more complex illumination scenarios (e.g., mixed lighting, HDR) and whether the generative filling step can be made more controllable to avoid hallucinated details.
