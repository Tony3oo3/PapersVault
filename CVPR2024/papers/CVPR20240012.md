## Problem

Temporal action detection requires simultaneously predicting action classes and precise start/end boundaries, yet existing approaches treat these as separate regression or classification sub-tasks. This paper observes that all three outputs can be uniformly formulated as images (discrete probability distributions per frame), enabling action detection to be recast as a three-image generation problem solvable via diffusion models. However, standard diffusion processes add Gaussian noise and are ill-suited for AD images, which encode discrete probability distributions rather than natural pixel values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes untrimmed video clips (padded to 2,304 frames) and extracts frozen spatio-temporal features f_ST ∈ R^{N×C_ST} via a pre-trained I3D or R(2+1)D backbone. It produces three grayscale AD images: an action-class image of shape N×C, and starting/ending point images of shape N×2, where each row is a discrete probability distribution. These are post-processed via thresholding, connected-component grouping, candidate pairing, and Soft-NMS to yield final action instances with temporal boundaries and class labels.

## Method

The ADI-Diff framework generates three AD images via a reverse diffusion process conditioned on video features and sinusoidal step embeddings. The key innovation is a Discrete Action-Detection Diffusion Process that replaces Gaussian noise with Multinomial-distributed noise, progressively diffusing each row from a one-hot ground truth toward a Uniform distribution over T=50 steps, with closed-form expressions for multi-step transitions and the forward posterior. The denoising network uses a Row-Column Transformer (L=3 blocks) that applies MHSA across columns to capture inter-class relationships and a 1×3 temporal convolution plus MHSA across rows to encode local and long-range temporal structure, respecting the anisotropic nature of AD images. The three AD images are stitched horizontally into a combined N×(C+4) image for joint processing, enabling parallel generation and cross-task knowledge sharing. Training uses MSE loss between predicted and target AD images at randomly sampled diffusion steps, with M=10 samples per step for better distribution coverage.

## Results

On THUMOS14, ADI-Diff achieves 70.8 average mAP (84.9 at tIoU 0.3, 81.5 at 0.4, 76.5 at 0.6, 63.0 at 0.7), setting a new state of the art. On ActivityNet-1.3, it reaches 38.3 average mAP (54.7 at tIoU 0.5, 38.9 at 0.75, 9.1 at 0.95), also the best reported result. Inference speed is 0.113 seconds per clip, comparable to TriDet (0.110 s) while delivering higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADI-Diff outperforms all 25 compared baselines on both THUMOS14 and ActivityNet-1.3 in average mAP. On THUMOS14, it leads the closest competitor TriDet [51] (69.3) by 1.5 mAP and DiffTAD [42] (68.0) by 2.8 mAP, with the largest gap at tIoU 0.6 (76.5 vs. 72.9 for TriDet). On ActivityNet-1.3, it surpasses TriDet (36.8) by 1.5 mAP and ActionFormer [69] (36.6) by 1.7 mAP, with the most notable margin at tIoU 0.95 (9.1 vs. 8.4 for TriDet). The advantage is consistent across all tIoU thresholds on both datasets, indicating that the discrete diffusion formulation and Row-Column Transformer contribute to more precise boundary localization and class prediction. Inference speed (0.113 s/clip) is nearly identical to TriDet (0.110 s/clip), confirming that the diffusion-based approach does not introduce a meaningful computational penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) and DDIM (Song et al., 2021), but departs fundamentally by replacing Gaussian noise with Multinomial-distribution-based noise to match the discrete probability-distribution nature of action detection outputs. It also relates to DiffTAD (Nag et al., 2023), which applies diffusion to denoise action proposals, but ADI-Diff instead generates the full set of frame-wise predictions (class, start, end) as 'AD images' from scratch, positioning itself in the proposal-free category alongside TAGS and AFSD. The Row-Column Transformer architecture is a novel departure from standard 2D spatial processing in diffusion U-Nets, motivated by the anisotropic structure of AD images (sequential temporal ordering along rows vs. categorical relationships along columns).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discrete diffusion formulation offers a principled way to apply diffusion models to tasks whose outputs are discrete probability distributions rather than continuous pixel values, a pattern that could generalize to other structured prediction problems. The SOTA results (70.8 avg mAP on THUMOS14, 38.3 on ActivityNet-1.3) with competitive inference speed (0.113s/clip) demonstrate that the image-generation reframing is not just conceptually interesting but practically effective. Limitations include reliance on frozen pre-trained video features (I3D, R(2+1)D) and a fixed post-processing pipeline (Soft-NMS), and it remains open whether the discrete diffusion formulation scales gracefully to very large class spaces or benefits from end-to-end feature learning.
