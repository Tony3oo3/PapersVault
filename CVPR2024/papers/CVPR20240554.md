## Problem

Current text-to-video diffusion models jointly denoise spatial content and temporal dynamics in a single pass, creating an excessively complex optimization landscape. This entanglement forces a trade-off: methods like ModelScopeT2V produce dynamic motion at the cost of spatial fidelity, while Gen-2 yields photorealistic frames but nearly static videos. The paper asks whether explicitly separating these two factors can resolve the quality-motion dilemma.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240554/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt plus two scalar controls — a motion factor γ^m (recommended range 300–600, controlling inter-frame pixel displacement) and an appearance factor γ^a (range 0–1.0, controlling semantic drift between first and last frames) — and produces a 32-frame video at 448×256 resolution. During inference, the spatial prior is generated purely from text via a standard T2I denoising loop in latent space (never decoded to pixels), then injected into the temporal denoising stage. The motion and appearance factors are expanded into per-frame guidance vectors via sinusoidal encoding and linear interpolation before being added to the diffusion time-step embedding.

## Method

HiGen decomposes T2V into a structure-level two-step pipeline within a single 3D-UNet: (1) spatial reasoning activates only the spatial layers to denoise a text-conditioned spatial prior z₀ˢ, and (2) temporal reasoning repeats z₀ˢ F times through a zero-initialized ConvStem_t and adds it to noisy video embeddings, letting the temporal convolutions and self-attention layers focus exclusively on inter-frame motion. At the content level, a motion factor (latent-space frame differencing) and an appearance factor (DINO cosine similarity between first and last frames) are encoded via zero-initialized MLPs and injected into every residual block as additional conditioning, enabling independent control of dynamism and semantic variation. Training uses image-video joint fine-tuning on Stable Diffusion's spatial weights: one-quarter of 8×A100 GPUs optimize only spatial parameters on 60M image-text pairs, while the remaining GPUs optimize all parameters on 17M video-text pairs with the middle frame serving as the spatial prior. This hierarchical decoupling reduces the effective complexity of the denoising task and allows the spatial and temporal components to be improved independently.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, HiGen achieves the best FID (8.60) and FVD (406) among all compared methods, outperforming the previous best FID (PyCo, 9.73) by 11.7% and the previous best FVD (ModelScopeT2V, 550) by 26.2%. CLIPSIM reaches 0.2947, slightly below Make-A-Video's 0.3049. In human evaluation, HiGen leads on all three axes: Visual Quality (84.4%), Temporal Quality (74.0%), and Text Alignment (81.2%), with the largest margin over VideoCrafter in temporal quality (+18.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiGen achieves the lowest FID (8.60) and FVD (406) across all 11 baselines on MSR-VTT. Against the closest FID competitor PyCo [12] (9.73), HiGen improves by 11.7%; against the closest FVD competitor ModelScopeT2V [53] (550), the improvement is 26.2%. On CLIPSIM, HiGen (0.2947) trails Make-A-Video [46] (0.3049) by 0.010, though it edges out VideoLDM [5] (0.2929) and ModelScopeT2V [53] (0.2930). In human evaluation (Table 2), HiGen dominates: it beats VideoCrafter [8] by 3.2% in visual quality, 18.8% in temporal quality, and 4.4% in text alignment, and surpasses Text2Video-Zero [25] by 20.8% in temporal quality. Qualitatively (Fig. 12), HiGen produces more dynamic motion than Gen-2 [10] while maintaining comparable spatial fidelity, and avoids the spatial degradation seen in ModelScopeT2V [53]. The ablation (Table 1) confirms that structure-level decoupling alone improves CLIPSIM from 0.292 to 0.313 but hurts temporal consistency (0.931→0.889), while adding content-level decoupling recovers temporal consistency to 0.944 while retaining the CLIPSIM gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiGen builds directly on the Stable Diffusion T2I pipeline and the 3D-UNet video extension paradigm established by ModelScopeT2V and VideoCrafter, but departs from their joint spatio-temporal denoising by introducing a two-stage structure-level decomposition (spatial reasoning followed by temporal reasoning) within a single unified denoiser. At the content level, it diverges from prior work that relies on FPS or noise-correlation priors (e.g., PyoCo, VideoFusion) by extracting explicit motion and appearance factors via frame differencing and DINO/CLIP semantic embeddings, respectively. The approach is positioned within the fine-tuning-based T2V paradigm, explicitly contrasting with training-free inference-time methods that sacrifice temporal continuity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical decoupling paradigm offers a practical and modular recipe for T2V systems: leveraging strong T2I priors for spatial quality while dedicating temporal layers solely to motion synthesis, with content-level factors providing interpretable control over video dynamics. The paper's ablation revealing that structure-level decoupling alone *degrades* temporal consistency (0.931→0.889) underscores that spatial and temporal factors are not simply separable without explicit content-level guidance. Limitations remain in fine-grained object detail and physically plausible human/animal motion under large displacements, and the 448×256 resolution and 32-frame length constrain real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the decoupling strategy scales to longer, higher-resolution videos and whether the DINO-based appearance factor generalizes to domain-specific content.
