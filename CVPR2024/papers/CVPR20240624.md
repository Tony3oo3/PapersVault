## Problem

Diffusion transformers (DiT) achieve state-of-the-art image generation but require large parameter counts (674M for DiT-XL/2) and perform a full forward pass at every sampling timestep, making them costly to deploy on resource-constrained devices. The fixed architecture of explicit networks prevents flexible allocation of computation across the denoising process, forcing a rigid tradeoff between timestep count and per-step accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240624/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in latent space (encoded by a pretrained VAE) and takes Gaussian noise—optionally conditioned on a class label for ImageNet—as input, producing 256×256 RGB images. A key distinction from standard diffusion models is that the number of transformer block forward passes per timestep is variable rather than fixed: the user can choose how many fixed-point iterations to run at each step, and solutions from one timestep can be carried forward to initialize the next, making total compute a flexible budget rather than a fixed product of timesteps × layers.

## Method

FPDM replaces the 28 explicit transformer blocks of DiT-XL/2 with a single implicit fixed-point layer (a DEQ-style equilibrium layer) sandwiched between one pre-processing and one post-processing transformer block, reducing parameters from 674M to 85M. Training uses Stochastic Jacobian-Free Backpropagation (S-JFB), which at each step randomly samples n gradient-free iterations (up to N) followed by m gradient-enabled iterations (up to M), backpropagating only through the last m unrolled steps—avoiding expensive implicit differentiation while outperforming the standard 1-step JFB gradient. At sampling time, two techniques exploit the implicit structure: (1) timestep smoothing, which spreads a fixed compute budget across more timesteps with fewer iterations each, reducing discretization error; and (2) solution reuse, which initializes each timestep's fixed-point iteration with the previous timestep's converged solution, accelerating convergence especially at low-noise timesteps.

## Results

On ImageNet (256×256), FPDM (85M params, 10.2 GB training memory) substantially outperforms DiT-XL/2 (674M params, 25.2 GB) under limited sampling compute: at 280 transformer block forward passes, FPDM achieves FID(DDIM) of 22.4 vs. DiT's 35.2, and at 140 passes, FID(DDIM) of 33.9 vs. DiT's 110.0. Across four datasets at 280 blocks, FPDM consistently improves FID by 2–5× (e.g., CelebA-HQ: 11.1 vs. 65.2; FFHQ: 18.2 vs. 58.1). At 560 blocks, FPDM still beats DiT on DDPM (26.1 vs. 37.9) but is slightly behind on DDIM (19.6 vs. 16.5), confirming the advantage is most pronounced under compute constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DiT-XL/2 [Peebles & Xie, 2022], the state-of-the-art diffusion transformer with 674M parameters. FPDM (85M params, 87% fewer) decisively wins at 140 and 280 transformer block forward passes on both DDPM and DDIM samplers across all four datasets (ImageNet, CelebA-HQ, FFHQ, LSUN-Church), with FID improvements of 2–5×. At 560 blocks, FPDM still outperforms DiT under DDPM (26.1 vs. 37.9) but falls slightly behind under DDIM (19.6 vs. 16.5), and the paper acknowledges that with unlimited sampling compute, the larger DiT model ultimately prevails. The advantage is further validated by a same-parameter-count comparison (Table 3): FPDM-XL/2 (86M) achieves FID 31.5 versus DiT-XL/2 with only 3 layers (86M) at FID 350.6 and DiT-B/2 (130M) at FID 55.9, confirming the gain comes from the implicit architecture rather than sheer parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPDM sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: diffusion transformers (DiT) for image generation and deep equilibrium models (DEQs) for implicit neural networks. It departs from prior attempts to merge diffusion and DEQs [39, 19], which collapsed the entire diffusion trajectory into a single fixed-point equation, by instead embedding a single implicit fixed-point layer within the standard sequential denoising process. The training procedure extends Jacobian-Free Backpropagation (JFB) [15] with a stochastic multi-step variant (S-JFB) that the authors found necessary for stable large-scale optimization, and the architecture inherits recent diffusion improvements (v-prediction, zero terminal SNR) to isolate its own contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87% parameter reduction and 60% memory savings make high-quality diffusion-based generation feasible on resource-constrained hardware (mobile, edge), addressing a key deployment bottleneck. The timestep smoothing and solution reuse techniques are architecturally enabled by the implicit layer and have no analogue in explicit networks, opening a new axis of inference-time compute allocation. The main limitation is that FPDM underperforms DiT when sampling compute is unconstrained, since the weight-shared implicit layer approximates a transformer with tied weights; scaling to larger datasets (e.g., LAION-5B) and discovering new heuristics for adaptive per-timestep iteration allocation remain open.
