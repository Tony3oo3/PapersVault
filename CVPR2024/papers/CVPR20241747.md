## Problem

Offline action segmentation models rely on future frames, but practical AR/VR task assistants require online (streaming) inference where only past and current frames are available. Directly applying an offline-trained model online causes a severe performance drop (e.g., accuracy falls from 69.5% to 13.2% on EgoProceL) due to the train-test discrepancy and the resulting oversegmentation. This paper addresses fully-supervised online action segmentation specifically for egocentric procedural task videos, where the inherent step structure provides exploitable constraints.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a stream of pre-extracted 2048-dim I3D features (computed over a 32-frame history window) one frame at a time, with no access to future frames at inference. At each timestep t, it outputs a K-dimensional probability vector over action classes (refined by progress and task-graph penalties) along with a K-dimensional progress vector in [0,1] indicating how far each action has progressed. The progress estimates are also usable as user-facing feedback in an AR/VR interface to show remaining work in the current step.

## Method

ProTAS combines three components: (1) a Causal Action Segmentation (CAS) backbone that replaces dilated convolutions with causal dilated convolutions (MSTCN) or applies causal attention masking (ASFormer), ensuring training and inference both use only past/current frames; (2) an Action Progress Prediction (APP) module—a GRU regression branch running in parallel to CAS—that estimates per-action progress as a linear fraction of segment duration, then concatenates these progress values with raw CAS probabilities and passes them through an MLP to produce refined segmentation probabilities, mitigating oversegmentation; (3) a task graph (learned from training transcripts or as learnable parameters) encoding predecessor/successor constraints, enforced during training via a graph-consistency loss that penalizes predicting an action before its predecessors complete or after its successors begin, and applied at inference by subtracting graph penalty scores from logits before softmax.

## Results

Using MSTCN as the backbone, the full ProTAS framework (CAS+APP+TG) achieves 69.2% edit similarity and 59.7% F1@0.5 on GTEA, 48.8% edit similarity and 35.7% F1@0.5 on EgoProceL, and 60.7% edit similarity and 46.3% F1@0.5 on EgoPER. The offline-trained base model applied online suffers a catastrophic drop (e.g., EgoProceL accuracy falls from 69.2% offline to 18.3% online), while the proposed causal architecture recovers most of the gap and the APP+TG modules close the remaining distance to offline performance. With ASFormer as backbone, the full method reaches 74.1% edit similarity on GTEA and 62.4% on EgoPER, approaching the offline upper bounds of 84.6% and 88.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the offline-trained model applied directly in an online (streaming) setting, which shows a dramatic performance collapse: on EgoProceL, accuracy drops from 69.2% (offline) to 18.3% (online) for MSTCN and from 69.5% to 13.2% for ASFormer. The causal modification (CAS) alone recovers most of this gap, improving EgoProceL F1@0.5 from 8.8% to 33.0% (MSTCN) and from 5.4% to 35.4% (ASFormer). The OODL loss from [27], designed for weakly-supervised online segmentation, provides no benefit in the fully-supervised setting (CAS+OODL scores are essentially identical to CAS alone), confirming that ground-truth frame labels already capture the information OODL tries to transfer. The APP module consistently improves all metrics across all three datasets (e.g., +4% F1@0.5 on both EgoProceL and EgoPER with MSTCN), while the task graph (TG) further boosts edit similarity and F1 scores by reducing oversegmentation, though it slightly reduces frame-wise accuracy on GTEA. Overall, the full CAS+APP+TG framework narrows the online-to-offline gap substantially, reaching within 10–15 points of offline edit similarity on all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends offline action segmentation architectures (MSTCN with causal dilated convolutions, ASFormer with causal masking) to the streaming/online setting, where only past and current frames are available. It departs from prior task-graph approaches that rely on external knowledge bases (e.g., WikiHow) by learning task graphs directly from training video transcripts or as learnable parameters. The Action Progress Prediction module draws on phase-progression literature but extends it to track multiple concurrent actions within a single video rather than individual short clips. Unlike the weakly-supervised online segmentation of OODL [27], this work operates under full frame-wise supervision, which the authors show makes the OODL-style offline-to-online distillation unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic offline-to-online performance collapse (e.g., 69.5% → 13.2% accuracy on EgoProceL) quantifies a critical gap for deploying action understanding in AR/VR task assistants, and the proposed framework narrows this gap substantially. A useful negative result is that OODL-style distillation from an offline teacher provides no benefit when ground-truth labels are already available, redirecting effort toward causal training and structural priors. Open questions include whether the learnable task graph can handle the high procedural diversity seen in EgoProceL, whether progress estimation generalizes to actions lacking clear visual progress cues, and how the framework performs under real-time latency constraints on wearable hardware.
