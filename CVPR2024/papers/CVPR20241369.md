## Problem

CLIP-based HOI detection methods capture high-level semantics but fail to exploit fine-grained spatial details from pre-trained detectors that are critical for discriminating subtle interactions (e.g., small-scale interactions like 'wear backpack' vs. salient ones like 'ride bicycle'). Additionally, occlusion between humans and objects in crowded scenes severely degrades interaction recognition, yet existing methods lack explicit mechanisms to recover occluded spatial details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a set of human-verb-object triplets, each with bounding boxes and a confidence score computed as the product of detector confidences (raised to λ=2.8 at inference) and the verb classification score. Internally, the pipeline operates on 7×7 ROI feature maps cropped via multi-scale ROI-Align from the frozen DETR backbone, and the final interaction score is a learnable weighted combination (α) of semantic-branch and spatial-branch logits passed through a sigmoid.

## Method

BCOM is a two-stage framework that performs bilateral adaptation by inserting lightweight adapter modules (two linear layers with ReLU) into both the frozen CLIP visual encoder (semantic branch, sequential per block) and the frozen DETR backbone (spatial branch, parallel per residual block), keeping all pre-trained weights fixed. An Occluded Part Extrapolation (OPE) module, a 2-layer transformer encoder, is trained to reconstruct a randomly occluded ROI feature (occluded by broadcasting another instance's mean over a binary mask with ratio ρ=0.25) using L2 loss, and at inference applies a residual connection to augment the original feature. A Conditional Contextual Mining (CCM) module, a 2-layer transformer decoder, uses the mean-pooled object feature as query and the flattened human spatial feature as key/value (and vice versa in a mirror operation) to attend to the most informative body parts for each interaction. The two branches are fused via late fusion with a learnable scalar α, and the full training objective combines binary cross-entropy plus focal loss for verb classification with the OPE reconstruction loss (β=0.1).

## Results

On HICO-DET (Default Setting), BCOM achieves 39.34 mAP (Full), 39.90 mAP (Rare), and 39.17 mAP (Non-rare), surpassing the previous best method ADA-CM by +0.94 mAP on Full. Under the Known-Object Setting, BCOM reaches 42.24 mAP (Full), outperforming the prior best ViPLO (40.61) by +1.63 mAP. On V-COCO, BCOM scores 65.8 (Scenario 1) and 69.9 (Scenario 2), placing second only to MUREN (68.8 / 71.0), which uses a heavier architecture and longer training schedule. Under zero-shot settings, BCOM also leads with 33.74 mAP (Full, Rare-First) and 32.03 mAP (Full, Non-rare-First).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCOM achieves the best HICO-DET performance across all splits, beating the closest CLIP-based competitor ADA-CM [29] by +0.94 mAP (Full) and +2.38 mAP (Rare), confirming the value of the spatial adaptation branch. Against the strongest non-CLIP methods, BCOM leads ViPLO [42] by +2.12 mAP (Full, Default) and AGER [50] by +2.59 mAP. On V-COCO, BCOM (65.8 / 69.9) trails only MUREN [28] (68.8 / 71.0), which employs a heavier relational-context architecture and longer training; BCOM surpasses all other methods including AGER (65.7 / 69.7) and RmLR (63.8 / 69.8). Under zero-shot evaluation, BCOM outperforms ADA-CM by +0.89 mAP (Unseen, RF) and +0.71 mAP (Unseen, NF), demonstrating that the bilateral adaptation generalizes to unseen verb-object compositions. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that removing the spatial branch drops Full mAP by 3.01, while removing the semantic branch drops it by 6.32, indicating both branches are necessary and complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based HOI detection line (ADA-CM, HOI-CLIP, GEN-VLKT) by arguing that CLIP's low-resolution pre-training makes it blind to fine-grained spatial cues critical for interaction discrimination. It departs from prior work by introducing spatial adapters on the frozen DETR backbone to complement CLIP's semantic branch, forming a bilateral adaptation structure. The Occluded Part Extrapolation module repurposes the masked-image-modeling paradigm (MAE, BEiT) at the feature-map level rather than the pixel level, and the Conditional Contextual Mining module applies a tailored cross-attention mechanism to mine object-conditioned human body-part cues. The two-stage framework and inference scoring follow the UPT protocol directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bilateral adaptation strategy offers a practical, parameter-efficient way to combine complementary pre-trained knowledge (CLIP semantics + detector spatial features) without expensive fine-tuning, which could generalize to other tasks where high-level semantics and fine-grained spatial details are both needed. The OPE module addresses a practically important but underexplored failure mode—occlusion in crowded scenes—with a clean training-time masking strategy. Open questions include whether the pixel-level occlusion strategy generalizes to more severe or structured occlusions, whether the late-fusion weighting (learnable α) is sensitive to dataset composition, and whether the approach extends to video HOI or 3D interaction settings. The paper acknowledges exploring masked-image-modeling pre-trained models as future work.
