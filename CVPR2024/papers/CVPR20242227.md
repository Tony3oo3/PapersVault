## Problem

Siamese-paradigm trackers rely solely on the initial template for similarity matching, leading to degraded performance under target deformation, occlusion, and scale variation. Existing approaches to incorporate tracking history either require full-parameter retraining, introduce substantial computational overhead through additional backbones, or fail to fully exploit historical visual features. The paper demonstrates that simply providing a frozen Siamese tracker with precise, updated historical information yields significant gains without any parameter retraining, and builds a lightweight module to operationalize this insight.

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a 192×192 template crop from the first frame and 384×384 search region crops from subsequent frames (crop factors 2 and 5). Internally, the historical prompt encoder also consumes a 4-channel input formed by concatenating the search region image with a refined foreground mask (the bitwise AND of the predicted bounding-box mask and the Candidate Elimination mask). The output is a bounding box predicted via focal loss (foreground/background classification) combined with GIoU and L1 regression losses; the foreground mask is an internal representation used to encode positional history rather than a final output.

## Method

HIPTrack freezes a ViT-B backbone (initialized from DropTrack) with an early Candidate Elimination module and adds a Historical Prompt Network consisting of an encoder and a decoder. The encoder fuses a refined foreground mask with the search region image through a lightweight ResNet-18 (first three stages, 4-channel input) to produce a position-aware feature, then merges it with the ViT search-region feature via residual convolutional blocks and a CBAM module to yield the prompt value. The decoder maintains a FIFO memory bank (capacity T=150 frames, updated every τ=20 frames) of key-value pairs where keys are 1×1-conv-compressed search-region features and values are the encoded prompt values; at each frame it computes L2-distance-based attention between the current query and all stored keys to adaptively aggregate historical prompt values into a frame-specific historical prompt. This prompt is channel-concatenated with the compressed search-region feature and fed to a prediction head (same structure as OSTrack with an added residual conv to reduce channels). Only the historical prompt network and prediction head are trained (34.1M of 120.4M total parameters), reducing trainable parameters by over 80% compared to autoregressive trackers like ARTrack.

## Results

HIPTrack achieves state-of-the-art AUC of 72.7% on LaSOT (vs. 72.6% for ARTrack and 71.8% for DropTrack), 77.4% AO on GOT-10k (vs. 75.9% for DropTrack and 75.5% for ARTrack), and 84.5% AUC on TrackingNet. On LaSOT_ext, it reaches 53.0% AUC, surpassing ARTrack (51.9%) and SeqTrack (50.5%). On NfS and OTB2015, HIPTrack scores 68.1% and 71.0% AUC respectively, outperforming all compared methods. The method requires only 34.1M trainable parameters (vs. 88.1M for SeqTrack and 181.0M for ARTrack) while running at 45.3 FPS on a single V100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIPTrack outperforms all 20 compared trackers on LaSOT (72.7% AUC vs. 72.6% for ARTrack, 71.8% for DropTrack, 71.5% for SeqTrack) and GOT-10k (77.4% AO vs. 75.9% for DropTrack, 75.5% for ARTrack), with margins of 0.1–2.5 points. On TrackingNet, HIPTrack (84.5% AUC) is narrowly outperformed by ARTrack (85.1% AUC) but beats DropTrack (84.1%), SeqTrack (83.9%), and GRM (84.0%). On LaSOT_ext, HIPTrack leads by 1.1 points over ARTrack (53.0% vs. 51.9% AUC) and by 2.5 points over SeqTrack and OSTrack (50.5%). On NfS, HIPTrack achieves 68.1% AUC, surpassing AiATrack (67.9%) and ARTrack (66.8%); on OTB2015 it scores 71.0% vs. 69.6% for DropTrack and AiATrack. On UAV123, HIPTrack (70.5%) ties ARTrack (70.5%) and is marginally behind AiATrack (70.6%), which the authors attribute to smaller target scales reducing mask-based position accuracy. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 5) confirm that adding the historical prompt network improves DropTrack by +1.5 AO, OSTrack by +1.7 AO, and SiamFC++ by +1.5 AO on GOT-10k without retraining the backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIPTrack builds directly on the one-stream Siamese paradigm (OSTrack, DropTrack) as its frozen feature extraction backbone, but departs from prior historical-information trackers (STMTrack, STARK, MixFormer, TATrack, ARTrack, SeqTrack) by reframing the injection of temporal context as a prompt-learning problem. Rather than retraining the full model, adding auxiliary templates, or processing multiple frames through the backbone, it introduces a lightweight historical prompt network that encodes refined foreground masks and visual features into a FIFO memory bank and adaptively decodes prompts for the current search region. This positions the work at the intersection of Siamese tracking and prompt learning, treating the frozen tracker as a pre-trained similarity-matching model that can be extended along the temporal dimension via external prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the plug-and-play nature of the historical prompt network: it can be attached to existing Siamese trackers (DropTrack, OSTrack, SiamFC++) to yield consistent gains without retraining the backbone, reducing trainable parameters by over 80% compared to autoregressive alternatives like ARTrack. This offers a low-cost path to improve deployed trackers. Limitations include weaker gains on small-target scenarios (UAV123), dependence on predicted bounding boxes for mask construction (which can propagate errors), and a fixed-size memory bank that may not scale to very long sequences. Open questions include whether the memory bank can be made adaptive in size, whether the approach extends to multi-object or online settings, and whether the prompt mechanism generalizes beyond the Siamese paradigm to other tracker architectures.
