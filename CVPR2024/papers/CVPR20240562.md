## Problem

Existing multi-scale deblurring networks rely on MIMO architectures that require manually downsampled input pairs (generated by unreliable interpolation) and complex fusion modules to combine RGB-domain images with deep features. Additionally, the coarse-to-fine progressive restoration inherently loses spatial detail because lower-resolution features are semantically precise but spatially ambiguous, leaving high-frequency edges and textures under-restored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a single full-resolution blurred RGB image as input (256×256 patches during training) and produces a deblurred RGB image at the original resolution. Unlike MIMO approaches, no pre-computed low-resolution inputs are needed; instead, the encoder internally extracts multi-scale features from the single input, and the decoder generates outputs at multiple scales during training for loss computation, while only the highest-scale output is used at inference.

## Method

MLWNet employs a SIMO (single-input, multiple-output) encoder-decoder architecture where an encoder with Simple Encoder Blocks (SEB) extracts features at progressively coarser scales, a Fusion stage with Wavelet Fusion Blocks (WFB) aggregates multi-scale semantics, and a Decoder with Wavelet Head Blocks (WHB) progressively restores images from coarse to fine. The key module is the Learnable Wavelet Node (LWN), which implements 2D-DWT as a group convolution with learnable analysis/synthesis filter pairs (a₀, a₁, s₀, s₁) constructed via outer products, decomposing features into low-frequency, horizontal, vertical, and diagonal high-frequency sub-bands. The wavelet filters are constrained by a self-supervised perfect-reconstruction loss (aliasing cancellation and gain conditions) to prevent degeneration into generic group convolution. Training uses a weighted multi-scale PSNR loss (with 1/k scale weights) combined with the wavelet reconstruction loss, and the architecture achieves state-of-the-art results on real-world datasets (RealBlur, RSBlur) with only 108.2 GMACs and 0.05s runtime.

## Results

On the RealBlur-J dataset, MLWNet-B achieves 33.84 dB PSNR and 0.941 SSIM, outperforming the previous best GRL-B (32.82 dB) by 1.02 dB while running 25× faster (0.05 s vs. 1.28 s per 256×256 patch). On the RSBlur dataset, MLWNet-B reaches 34.94 dB PSNR and 0.880 SSIM, surpassing the next-best SFNet by 0.59 dB. On the synthetic GoPro dataset, MLWNet-B attains 33.83 dB PSNR and 0.968 SSIM, trailing FFTformer (34.21 dB) by 0.38 dB but reducing inference time by 62% (0.05 s vs. 0.13 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RealBlur-J, MLWNet-B (33.84 dB) decisively outperforms all 14 compared methods, with the largest margins over GRL-B (+1.02 dB), FFTformer (+1.22 dB), and Stripformer (+1.36 dB); on RealBlur-R it leads GRL-B by 0.49 dB and FFTformer by 0.58 dB. On RSBlur, MLWNet-B (34.94 dB) beats SFNet (34.35 dB), Uformer-B (33.98 dB), and Restormer (33.69 dB) by 0.59, 0.96, and 1.25 dB respectively. On GoPro, MLWNet-B (33.83 dB) is the only method within 0.4 dB of FFTformer (34.21 dB) while being 2.6× faster, and it surpasses NAFNet (33.69 dB), Restormer (33.57 dB), MPRNet (32.66 dB), and MIMO-UNet+ (32.45 dB). In the cross-dataset generalization test (trained on RealBlur-J, tested on RSBlur), MLWNet-B achieves 30.91 dB PSNR, exceeding FFTformer (29.70 dB) and BANet+ (30.24 dB) by 1.21 and 0.67 dB respectively, while using only 108.2 GMACs versus 588.7 for BANet+. The paper notes that on synthetic GoPro blur, MLWNet-B does not reach the top PSNR, attributing this to the unnatural discontinuous trajectories and high-low frequency confusion inherent in synthetic blur generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MIMO (multi-input, multiple-output) multi-scale deblurring paradigm (MIMO-UNet, MPRNet) by proposing a SIMO (single-input, multiple-output) architecture that retains the original full-resolution image as input and generates progressively sharper outputs at multiple scales, eliminating the need for manually constructed downsampled image pairs and complex RGB-to-deep-feature fusion modules. Structurally, it builds on NAFNet's simple encoder block design and incorporates the perfect reconstruction principle from prior learnable wavelet CNN work (Liu et al., Wolter et al.) to constrain its novel learnable 2D-DWT module. Conceptually, it shifts the frequency-domain prior from DFT (used in FFTformer, DeepRFT) to DWT, motivated by the directional continuity of real-world blur trajectories that DWT captures more naturally than the global frequency representation of DFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SIMO simplification offers a practical architectural insight for multi-scale restoration: the network can learn effective multi-scale features from a single input without the overhead of generating and fusing multiple resolution inputs, reducing both model complexity and inference time (0.05s vs 0.13s for FFTformer). The learnable DWT with self-supervised perfect-reconstruction loss provides a principled way to incorporate directional frequency information that is particularly well-suited to real-world motion blur. However, the method's notably weaker performance on synthetic blur (GoPro) compared to real blur (RealBlur, RSBlur) remains an open question, with the authors attributing it to noise-level differences and discontinuous synthetic blur trajectories rather than a fundamental architectural limitation. Future work could explore whether the DWT advantage generalizes to other restoration tasks or whether the SIMO principle extends beyond deblurring.
