## Problem

Existing poisoning-based defenses against unauthorized subject-driven text-to-image synthesis (e.g., Anti-DreamBooth) suffer from two key limitations: they use hand-crafted heuristics to approximate the intractable bilevel optimization, yielding sub-optimal perturbations, and they are fragile under simple data transformations such as Gaussian filtering. MetaCloak asks whether a more effective and transformation-robust protection scheme can be designed for fine-tuning-based personalized generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of 8 user reference images per identity (512×512 RGB faces from VGGFace2 or CelebA-HQ) along with a transformation distribution T (Gaussian filtering with kernel size 7, horizontal flipping, center cropping, and resizing). It outputs a set of protected images with ℓ∞-norm-bounded perturbations (default radius 11/255, step size 1/255, 6 PGD steps) that are visually imperceptible but, when used for DreamBooth fine-tuning, cause severe semantic distortion and quality degradation in the generated personalized images. The crafting process requires approximately 3 GPU hours per instance using SD v2-1-base as the surrogate.

## Method

MetaCloak solves the bilevel poisoning problem via a meta-learning framework over a pool of M=5 steps-staggered surrogate diffusion models, each pre-trained to a different number of DreamBooth steps, enabling model-agnostic and transferable perturbation learning. Within each outer crafting loop, a batch of surrogates is sampled and K-step unrolled (K=1) to approximate the lower-level training trajectory, after which the upper-level objective is optimized via gradient ascent on the protected images. The guiding loss is a simple denoising-error maximization (Eq. 9) rather than a targeting loss, which empirically produces chaotic content and scattered textures by tricking the model into establishing false correlations. To ensure transformation robustness, an Expectation over Transformations (EOT) step is embedded in the PGD update, sampling J transformations from T and averaging the denoising loss before computing the perturbation gradient. The full procedure alternates between surrogate training on the current poison and perturbation updates for C=4000 crafting steps, using first-order MAML-style gradient approximation for efficiency.

## Results

Under the transformation training setting (Gaussian filtering, flipping, cropping, resizing), MetaCloak achieves the lowest (most degraded) scores across all five metrics on both datasets. On VGGFace2, MetaCloak reduces the Subject Detection Score (SDS) to 0.432 (vs. 0.903 clean), a 36.9% improvement over the best prior method (EASPL at 0.801). On CelebA-HQ, SDS drops to 0.305 (vs. 0.810 clean), a 45.0% improvement over the best baseline (ASPL at 0.755). All improvements are statistically significant (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p ≤ 0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaCloak dominates all six baselines across every metric on both datasets under the transformation training setting. Against the closest fine-tuning-based competitor ASPL (Anti-DreamBooth), MetaCloak reduces SDS from 0.879 to 0.432 on VGGFace2 and from 0.755 to 0.305 on CelebA-HQ, while also achieving substantially lower identity-matching scores (IMS-VGG: −0.151 vs. 0.193; IMS-CLIP: 0.644 vs. 0.712 on VGGFace2). The ensemble variant EASPL, which was the best prior method on SDS, is outperformed by MetaCloak by 0.369 (VGGFace2) and 0.536 (CelebA-HQ) in SDS. Methods targeting fixed diffusion models—AdvDM, Glaze, and PhotoGuard—show minimal degradation under transformation (SDS remains near clean levels, e.g., PhotoGuard at 0.928 on VGGFace2), confirming their vulnerability to simple data preprocessing. FSMG, which uses a DreamBooth model trained on clean images to craft perturbations, also fails to maintain protection under transformation (SDS 0.911 on VGGFace2). The ablation (Table 3) further shows that both the meta-learning component and the EOT transformation sampling are necessary: removing either degrades SDS from 0.432 to 0.787 or 0.515 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaCloak extends the availability-attack / unlearnable-examples lineage (Huang et al., Feng et al.) into the personalized diffusion-model setting, where the poisoning mechanism differs fundamentally from attacking a fixed model: the goal is to trick a fine-tuning process into establishing false correlations rather than exploiting a static adversarial vulnerability. The closest prior work, Anti-DreamBooth (Le et al., ICCV 2023), also targets DreamBooth fine-tuning but relies on hand-crafted heuristics for the bilevel optimization and lacks transformation robustness. MetaCloak departs by replacing those heuristics with a MAML-style meta-learning loop over a pool of steps-staggered surrogate models, and by incorporating EOT-style transformation sampling into the PGD crafting step. The denoising-error maximization loss is simpler than the targeting losses used by AdvDM/Mist/PhotoGuard (which attack fixed diffusion models) yet proves more effective against the fine-tuning-based setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first poisoning-based protection that remains effective under common data-preprocessing transformations (Gaussian filtering, flipping, cropping, resizing) that a malicious trainer would apply, and the black-box Replicate experiment (SDS dropping from 98.9% to 21.8%) demonstrates real-world threat to online training-as-a-service platforms. However, Table 4 reveals that super-resolution purification substantially recovers generation quality (SDS 0.432 → 0.824 under Trans. Training), indicating the protection is still fragile against more aggressive purification. Open questions include mechanistic understanding of why denoising-error maximization outperforms targeting losses for fine-tuning-based models, whether LoRA's inherent parameter-efficiency can be leveraged as a principled defense, and whether more efficient (fewer GPU-hours) perturbation crafting is possible.
