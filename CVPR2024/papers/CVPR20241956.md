## Problem

Existing neural dynamic scene reconstruction methods (e.g., NDR) achieve accurate surface reconstruction in observed regions but produce spurious or unrealistic geometry in unobserved regions, because the inherent smoothness of neural representations cannot incorporate domain-specific appearance priors. No prior method achieves both metrically accurate dynamic reconstruction and photo-realistic 360° completion from a single casually captured RGB-D video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241956/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a casually captured monocular RGB-D video (consumer-grade camera) with known intrinsics and poses, plus object masks from off-the-shelf segmentation. It produces a hyper-dimensional canonical SDF field (zero-level set as surface mesh), a per-frame deformation field, and novel view renderings along a 360° trajectory. The output is structured so that observed regions are metrically accurate (supervised by depth) while unobserved regions are completed via diffusion-prior distillation, yielding a single coherent mesh per frame.

## Method

MorpheuS represents the dynamic scene as a hyper-dimensional canonical field (SDF + color decoded via hash encoding) and a deformation field that warps observation-space points into canonical space, following the HyperNeRF paradigm. The key contribution is a temporal view-dependent Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) strategy that uses Zero-1-to-3 as a view-conditioned diffusion prior: for each rendered virtual view, the nearest keyframe serves as the conditioning reference, and a view-angle-dependent weighting term reduces gradient magnitude for large viewpoint differences to mitigate view-inconsistency. A canonical-space regularization (applied by skipping the deformation network and perturbing points directly in canonical space) prevents degenerate trivial solutions during SDS optimization. Training uses a coarse-to-fine schedule with a warm-up phase that freezes the deformation field and restricts diffusion timesteps to [0.02, 0.5] to establish a good canonical shape before learning accurate motion, combined with real-view rendering losses, pseudo-SDF supervision, and normal smoothness terms.

## Results

On 9 real-world scenes (KillingFusion, DeepDeform, iPhone), MorpheuS achieves an average accuracy of 0.88 cm and completion of 0.78 cm, substantially outperforming NDR (1.92 cm and 1.09 cm respectively). CLIP similarity for completion realism reaches 86.77, a 7-point improvement over NDR's 79.75. On 4 synthetic scenes (AMA, BANMo), MorpheuS achieves 2.57 cm accuracy and 91.31 CLIP similarity versus NDR's 6.73 cm and 85.65, with the largest accuracy gap on the Eagle2 scene (4.43 vs. 15.42 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is NDR [Cai et al., NeurIPS 2022], a neural RGB-D dynamic surface reconstruction method that uses MLP-based representations without diffusion priors. MorpheuS wins on all three metrics across every scene: accuracy improves from 1.92 to 0.88 cm on real-world data (a 54% reduction) and from 6.73 to 2.57 cm on synthetic data (a 62% reduction). Completion error drops from 1.09 to 0.78 cm (real) and 2.59 to 2.36 cm (synthetic). The most dramatic per-scene gain is on the Teddy sequence, where NDR's accuracy degrades to 6.12 cm due to spurious surface artifacts from its continuous MLP representation, while MorpheuS achieves 0.99 cm. CLIP similarity for photorealistic completion improves by 7.0 points on real-world data (79.75 → 86.77) and 5.7 points on synthetic data (85.65 → 91.31), confirming that the diffusion prior effectively eliminates the unrealistic geometry that NDR produces in unobserved regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MorpheuS sits at the intersection of two active research threads: neural dynamic surface reconstruction (NDR, HyperNeRF, NeRFies) and diffusion-prior-based 3D generation (DreamFusion, Zero-1-to-3). It adopts the deformation-field-plus-canonical-field architecture from NDR/HyperNeRF for metrically accurate geometry and motion, then introduces a temporal view-dependent SDS loss conditioned on Zero-1-to-3 to complete unobserved regions — a combination not previously attempted for dynamic objects. Key departures from prior work include a canonical-space regularization strategy (bypassing the deformation network to avoid degenerate solutions), a view-angle-dependent weighting to mitigate SDS view-inconsistency, and a SAL-based geometric initialization that the authors found necessary for convergence where fixed-blob initialization fails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that diffusion priors can serve a dual role in dynamic reconstruction: providing photorealistic completion of unobserved geometry while simultaneously acting as a regularizer that improves accuracy in observed regions (0.88 vs. 0.99 cm). The practical significance is that a single casual RGB-D video suffices for full 360° surface reconstruction, removing the need for multi-camera rigs or turntables. However, the method remains limited by its reliance on pure-RGB diffusion conditioning (failing under motion blur, incomplete views, or complex articulation) and cannot recover motion in self-occluded regions, pointing toward future work with RGB-D- or time-conditioned diffusion models and explicit motion priors.
