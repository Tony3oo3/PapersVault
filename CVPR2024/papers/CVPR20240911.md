## Problem

Existing hand texture reconstruction methods require multi-view images or monocular video sequences to capture full hand appearance, and no prior work reconstructs both interacting hands with detailed textures from a single image. The non-symmetric nature of hands (palm vs. back) makes the invisible side particularly challenging, as prior single-image methods produce only blurred or uniform textures on occluded regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240911/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of two interacting hands along with their 3D meshes (ground truth or estimated via IntagHand) and produces per-hand UV texture maps (206,330 pixels × RGB), an albedo image, and scene light parameters (ambient color, diffuse, specular, direction). Training is per-scene (one image per identity), completing in under 7 minutes, and the output supports rendering at arbitrary novel poses, camera viewpoints, and relighting conditions via a differentiable Phong renderer.

## Method

BiTT is a three-stage pipeline: (1) scene estimation predicts light parameters and an albedo image from the input; (2) a coarse stage uses the HTML hand texture parametric model (PCA with 101 principal components over 618,990-dim texture vectors) to generate full-texture UV maps from estimated parameter vectors; (3) a fine stage employs a Bi-directional Texture Reconstructor (BTR) that synthesizes visible albedo texture with coarse-stage estimates, then decodes full textures through bi-directional blocks where each hand's features are concatenated with the other hand's at every level, exploiting left-right texture symmetry. The key insight is that visible texture from one hand provides usable symmetric information for the other hand's invisible regions (up to ~60% coverage vs. ~35% without symmetry). Training uses a composite loss combining reconstruction (on fine, coarse, and albedo renders), non-visible pixel consistency between coarse and fine stages, albedo consistency across multiple lighting conditions, and a symmetric L1 loss between left and right textures.

## Results

On InterHand2.6M with GT meshes, BiTT achieves the best L1 (0.0101), PSNR (30.41 dB), and MS-SSIM (0.9349) in appearance reconstruction, outperforming HARP (PSNR 28.11) and S2Hand (PSNR 26.39). In the more challenging different-views setting, BiTT leads with PSNR 27.79 and MS-SSIM 0.8843, while HARP drops to 25.97 and 0.8346. On RGB2Hands (no GT mesh, using IntagHand), BiTT still achieves the best L1 (0.0148) and PSNR (26.02) in appearance reconstruction. The only metric where BiTT trails is LPIPS in appearance reconstruction (0.1019 vs. HARP's 0.0696), though BiTT recovers the lead on LPIPS for novel poses and different views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiTT outperforms S2Hand and HTML on every metric (L1, LPIPS, PSNR, MS-SSIM) across all three evaluation settings, with the largest gaps in PSNR (e.g., 30.41 vs. 26.39 for S2Hand and 24.72 for HTML in appearance reconstruction). Against HARP, the strongest single-hand UV-map baseline, BiTT wins on L1, PSNR, and MS-SSIM in all settings but loses on LPIPS in the appearance-reconstruction task (0.1019 vs. 0.0696), suggesting HARP captures visible-side perceptual detail slightly better; however, BiTT recovers the LPIPS lead in novel-pose and different-view evaluations, indicating its advantage grows under pose/view changes where invisible-side texture matters. On RGB2Hands (no GT mesh, IntagHand initialization), BiTT maintains its lead in L1 and PSNR over all baselines, though the margin narrows relative to InterHand2.6M, which the authors attribute to a lower proportion of usable symmetric texture (10.21% vs. 24.29%). HandNeRF is compared only qualitatively (Figure 8); BiTT, trained from a single image, produces comparable two-hand texture fidelity to HandNeRF, which requires hundreds of multi-view images per scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiTT builds directly on the HTML hand texture parametric model (PCA-based) for its coarse stage, and on UV texture map rendering with Phong illumination as used by HARP. It departs from the dominant paradigm in hand appearance reconstruction—multi-view or video-based NeRF methods (HandNeRF, HandAvatar, LiveHand) and multi-frame UV-map methods (HARP, S2Hand)—by requiring only a single RGB image, leveraging the left-right hand texture symmetry as a structural prior to fill in occluded regions. The bi-directional texture reconstructor (BTR) is a new architectural component that cross-attends between left and right hand feature maps, a mechanism absent from prior single-hand or independent two-hand approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end trainable method to produce relightable, pose-controllable textures for two interacting hands from a single image, which is practically important for AR/VR avatar creation where capturing multi-view data is infeasible. The symmetry-based approach is elegant but relies on the assumption that left and right hand textures are sufficiently similar, which may break down for asymmetric features (scars, tattoos, accessories on one hand only). Open questions include whether the method can be extended to detect and synthesize such asymmetric details on invisible sides, and whether the per-scene training paradigm (7 minutes per scene) can be amortized into a feed-forward model for real-time use.
