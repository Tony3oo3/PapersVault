## Problem

Existing regression-based keypoint localization methods apply global pooling to the feature map, discarding the spatial location prior and producing a single global feature for regression. This makes optimization difficult, limits accuracy, and prevents the method from differentiating multiple keypoints of the same type (e.g., in multi-person pose estimation). The paper asks whether embedding spatial location cues directly into the regression process can recover the accuracy of heatmap-based methods while retaining the efficiency and quantization-error robustness of regression.

## Input / Output

SAR takes a standard RGB image and processes it through a backbone (ResNet, HRNet, or ViT) to obtain a feature map F ∈ R^{c×h×w}. For every spatial grid (i,j) in F, the method outputs a regressed keypoint coordinate (2D or 3D) and a scalar confidence score, yielding h×w candidate predictions per keypoint. At inference, the top-m predictions whose confidence exceeds a fixed threshold γ are retained, where m is task-dependent (m=1 for top-down, m=30 for bottom-up). The continuous coordinate output avoids the quantization error inherent to discrete heatmap grids.

## Method

SAR replaces the conventional pool-then-regress pipeline with a spatial-aware regressor that predicts an offset from every grid: K_t = R(f_t) + p_t, where p_t is the grid's spatial location prior, so each grid regresses from a different starting point. A spatial-aware selector assigns a confidence score s_t = S(f_t) to each grid's prediction, and both components are trained under a single unified objective L = −log Σ w_t·r_t, where r_t = exp(−λ|K_t − K*|) is a Laplacian-kernel regression quality score and w_t is obtained by a two-step normalization (sigmoid followed by element-sum) of the confidence scores. This two-step scheme, rather than softmax, yields target-count-independent confidence values that enable fixed-threshold selection at inference. The unified loss jointly pushes accurate grids toward high regression quality and suppresses inaccurate ones, eliminating the need for heuristic selection rules or separate classification branches. SAR operates on low-resolution feature maps (e.g., 16×12), keeping computational cost comparable to standard regression while generalizing to single/multi-person, 2D/3D, and whole-body keypoint localization.

## Results

On COCO Keypoint test-dev, SAR with HRNet-W48 backbone achieves 76.3 AP, outperforming the previous best regression method RLE (75.7 AP) and heatmap-based SimCC (76.0 AP). On COCO Whole-Body, SAR reaches 66.6 AP for whole-body keypoints, surpassing ZoomNAS (65.4) and ZoomNet (63.0). For multi-person pose estimation, integrating SAR into DEKR improves COCO AP from 61.8 to 64.5. On Human3.6M 3D pose estimation, SAR achieves 47.1 MPJPE, improving over RLE (48.6) and Integral Pose (49.6), though trailing the best method Sun et al. (59.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAR consistently outperforms all regression-based baselines: it beats RLE by 0.6 AP on COCO Keypoint (76.3 vs 75.7) and by 1.5 MPJPE on Human3.6M (47.1 vs 48.6), and surpasses Integral Pose by 8.5 AP on COCO Keypoint (76.3 vs 67.8). Against heatmap-based methods, SAR with HRNet-W48 exceeds SimCC by 0.3 AP (76.3 vs 76.0) and HRNet by 0.8 AP (76.3 vs 75.5) on COCO Keypoint, and outperforms ZoomNAS by 1.2 AP on COCO Whole-Body (66.6 vs 65.4). In multi-person pose estimation, SAR as a plug-in module improves both CenterNet (57.6→60.3 AP) and DEKR (61.8→64.5 AP) on COCO, confirming its generality. However, on 3D pose estimation, SAR (47.1 MPJPE) still trails the strongest baselines Sun et al. (59.1) and PoseNet (53.3) by a significant margin, indicating that 3D localization remains a harder challenge for the spatial-aware regression paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAR departs from conventional regression-based keypoint localization (DeepPose, Integral Pose, RLE), which applies global pooling to discard spatial location information before regressing coordinates, and from heatmap-based methods (SimplePose, HRNet) that encode location in a probability map but suffer from quantization error. The paper repurposes the spatial location prior that heatmap methods exploit implicitly by making it an explicit input to a per-grid regressor, combined with a learned confidence selector optimized under a unified Laplacian-kernel objective. This positions SAR as a generalized regression that degenerates to standard regression at 1×1 feature maps but extends naturally to multi-keypoint and 3D settings where conventional regression cannot operate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAR offers a practical efficiency advantage (4.0 GFLOPs vs. 9.7 for heatmap baselines) while matching or exceeding heatmap accuracy, and its unified objective eliminates the need for heuristic multi-task pipelines (e.g., separate classification + regression branches) that are sensitive to hyperparameters. The method's generality across single/multi-person, 2D/3D, and whole-body pose estimation makes it a potentially drop-in replacement for the regression head in existing pose estimation pipelines. However, 3D results on Human3.6M remain below Integral Pose, and the modest 1.8% AP gain over heatmap on COCO suggests the spatial prior benefit may saturate at higher resolutions, leaving open whether SAR's advantage grows in very dense or heavily occluded scenarios.
