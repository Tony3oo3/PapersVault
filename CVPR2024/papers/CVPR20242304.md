## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), the dominant mechanism for text-to-3D generation, produces over-smoothed 3D models because its pseudo-ground-truths are both inconsistent (random noise and timestep sampling cause feature-averaging across views) and low-quality (single-step DDPM reconstruction is inherently inaccurate, especially at large timesteps). The paper identifies these two failure modes as the root cause of the over-smoothing effect observed across SDS-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242304/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pretrained 2D diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2.1) as the generative prior, with an optional 3D point cloud from Point-E providing geometric initialization for the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation. The output is a 3DGS scene optimized via differentiable rendering, which can be further extended to produce SMPL-conditioned avatars, LoRA-personalized subjects, or edited 2D/3D content. Unlike SDS-based methods that require large classifier-free guidance scales to compensate for weak supervision, ISM operates effectively at standard CFG (7.5).

## Method

Interval Score Matching (ISM) replaces SDS's single-step pseudo-GT matching with a two-part mechanism: (1) DDIM inversion produces a deterministic, invertible noisy latent trajectory {x_0, …, x_s, x_t} from the rendered view x_0, eliminating the randomness that causes feature-averaging; (2) instead of matching x_0 against a one-step denoised estimate, ISM minimizes the squared difference between the conditional score ε_φ(x_t, t, y) and the unconditional score ε_φ(x_s, s, ∅) at two adjacent timesteps in the trajectory. A bias term η_t arising from the multi-step denoising expansion is dropped as it contains opposing interval-score residuals that cancel out, yielding a computationally efficient objective that requires only two UNet evaluations per step. The framework uses 3D Gaussian Splatting as the 3D representation for high-resolution, large-batch training, and Point-E for prompt-conditioned point cloud initialization to accelerate convergence and improve geometry.

## Results

In a user study over 28 text prompts, LucidDreamer achieves the best average preference ranking of 1.25 (lower is better), outperforming ProlificDreamer (2.37), DreamFusion (3.28), Magic3D (3.44), Fantasia3D (4.53), and Text2Mesh (4.76). In GPT-4v automated evaluation, LucidDreamer wins against DreamFusion on 84–88% of comparisons across alignment, plausibility, color-geometry, texture, and geometry dimensions, and against ProlificDreamer on 58–63%. All experiments run on a single A100 GPU with a view batch size of 4 and δ_S=200, requiring no multi-stage training pipeline unlike several competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LucidDreamer outperforms all five baselines in both user study and GPT-4v evaluation. Against the closest competitor ProlificDreamer (which uses Variational Score Distillation), LucidDreamer wins 58–63% of GPT-4v comparisons and achieves a 1.12-point lower user ranking (1.25 vs. 2.37). Against DreamFusion (the original SDS method), the margin is largest: 84–88% GPT-4v winning rate and a 2.03-point ranking gap. Against Magic3D and Fantasia3D, LucidDreamer wins 61–83% of GPT-4v comparisons, with the strongest advantage in geometry (77% vs. Magic3D, 83% vs. Fantasia3D), reflecting the benefit of 3DGS representation and point-cloud initialization. Notably, LucidDreamer achieves these results with a single-stage training pipeline, whereas Magic3D, Fantasia3D, and ProlificDreamer all require multi-stage training, reducing overall training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework introduced by DreamFusion, which has become the dominant paradigm for text-to-3D generation. The key departure is a diagnostic analysis showing that SDS's over-smoothing stems from two specific flaws: stochastic pseudo-GT inconsistency (due to random noise and camera sampling) and low-quality one-step reconstruction at large timesteps. The proposed Interval Score Matching (ISM) replaces SDS's single-step pseudo-GT matching with a deterministic DDIM-inversion trajectory and interval-based score matching between two timesteps, while adopting 3D Gaussian Splatting as a more efficient 3D representation than NeRF or meshes used in prior work like Magic3D and ProlificDreamer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean decomposition of SDS's over-smoothing into two identifiable causes (inconsistency + one-step error) provides a principled explanation that unifies observations scattered across prior text-to-3D literature. ISM's practical advantage—achieving higher fidelity without multi-stage training pipelines or large guidance scales—makes it an attractive drop-in replacement for SDS in existing frameworks. Open questions include whether the discarded bias term η_t remains negligible at all interval lengths and prompt complexities, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the Stable Diffusion 2.1 backbone used in experiments. The method's reliance on a pretrained 2D diffusion prior also means it inherits that model's biases and limitations.
