## Problem

Data-driven trajectory prediction models produce unreliable outputs under cross-dataset distribution shifts, and existing online learning methods can only safely update the last decoder layer using regression loss from delayed ground truth, leading to underfitting or overfitting. This paper addresses two gaps: (1) how to stably optimize deeper network layers during test-time adaptation without destroying source-learned representations, and (2) how to exploit the sequential arrival of driving data to learn actor-specific motion characteristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241115/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical trajectories of N actors (sequences of 2D spatial locations), L segmented lane centerlines, and actor class labels, with data arriving sequentially one sample per time interval. At test time, delayed ground-truth future trajectories from a previous time window (offset τ = t_f) provide supervision. The model outputs K=6 multi-modal future trajectory candidates per actor, evaluated under two temporal configurations: short-term (0.9 s past → 3 s future at 0.1 s intervals) and long-term (2 s past → 6 s future at 0.5 s intervals). An actor-specific token memory persists across time steps within a scene and is reset (via class-averaging) at scene boundaries.

## Method

T4P builds on the ForecastMAE backbone (embedding layers, shared transformer encoder, reconstruction head, motion decoder) and introduces two key mechanisms for test-time training. First, a masked autoencoder reconstruction loss is added alongside the standard WTA regression loss during test-time optimization; the reconstruction objective guides the encoder to learn interaction-aware representations (actor-actor and actor-lane) that safely permit updating deeper layers, whereas regression loss alone degrades performance when applied beyond the decoder. Second, an actor-specific token memory assigns each actor instance a dedicated learnable token (cloned from a class-level token at appearance) that is updated through both losses as the actor is observed over time, capturing instance-level driving habits; at scene transitions, all tokens are averaged per class to initialize the next scene. The overall pipeline follows three phases—offline training on source data, iterative test-time training on target data using delayed GT, and online evaluation—enabling real-time adaptation (≥10 FPS) with adjustable update frequency.

## Results

T4P achieves state-of-the-art mADE6/mFDE6 across all six cross-distribution-shift scenarios. In short-term prediction (1/3/0.1), T4P reaches a mean mADE6 of 0.421 (vs. 0.691 for the best baseline MEK with τ=t_f), and in long-term prediction (2/6/0.5), it achieves a mean mADE6 of 0.774 (vs. 0.913 for MEK). The largest absolute gains appear in INTER→Lyft short-term (0.391 vs. 0.746 mADE6) and INTER→nuS short-term (0.537 vs. 0.892 mADE6). T4P also maintains real-time capability at 10+ FPS while outperforming MEK (3.3 FPS) by roughly 7× in speed at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T4P outperforms all four baselines across every distribution-shift scenario and both temporal configurations. MEK (Modified Extended Kalman Filter) is the strongest baseline, achieving 0.691/1.556 mean mADE6/mFDE6 in short-term and 0.913/1.953 in long-term, but T4P improves these to 0.421/0.923 and 0.774/1.592 respectively — a 39% and 15% relative reduction in mADE6. DUA and TENT (w/sup) perform near or below the source-only model because they only update batch-normalization statistics, which the authors argue is counterproductive for multi-agent trajectory data where each agent has distinct motion patterns. AML's K_0 variant (backbone without adaptive training) degrades severely (2.137/4.504 short-term mean), and even the full AML version (0.985/2.319) remains well behind T4P, attributed to the inferiority of Bayesian probability sampling over the non-probability sampling used in T4P's backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time training (TTT) paradigm from image classification/segmentation (Sun et al., 2020; Gandelsman et al., 2022) to trajectory prediction, adopting the ForecastMAE backbone as its architectural foundation. It departs from prior online learning methods for trajectory prediction (MEK, AML) that restrict updates to the final decoder layer or batch normalization statistics, arguing that this limitation stems from the risk of corrupting learned representations with regression loss alone. The actor-specific token memory is a novel component that exploits the sequential, instance-tracked nature of driving data, going beyond the class-level tokens used in offline training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a critical practical gap: autonomous driving systems deployed in new cities or with different driving populations face distribution shifts that offline training cannot anticipate, and the auto-labeling property of trajectory data makes online adaptation feasible without manual annotation. The actor-specific token is particularly relevant for continuous real-world deployment where the same vehicles are observed over extended periods. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to settings without reliable object tracking (where actor IDs are noisy), how it behaves under adversarial or safety-critical shifts rather than benign cross-dataset shifts, and whether the scene-boundary token averaging strategy can be improved for very short or very long scenes.
