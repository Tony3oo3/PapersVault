## Problem

Existing keypoint localization methods encode keypoint type priors directly into the network architecture (e.g., along the channel dimension of the output layer), which restricts generalization to unseen keypoint types or cross-dataset human poses. This paper asks whether a large language model, given flexible textual descriptions of keypoint type, location, and spatial relationships, can perform generalizable keypoint localization without hard-coded architectural priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242133/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image and a structured text instruction (keypoint name, a natural-language location description, and a localization question) as input, and generates keypoint coordinates as a sequence of decimal-string tokens (e.g., "0.abc, 0.def") normalized to [0,1] in spatial dimensions, or [0,1]×[0,1]×[0,1] for 3D with depth. The decimal-string format avoids adding new location tokens to the LLM vocabulary and extends to 3D by simply appending a depth coordinate. A multi-round conversation format allows querying multiple keypoints from a single image in one forward pass.

## Method

LocLLM formulates keypoint localization as a visual question-answering task: a DINOv2-pretrained ViT-L/14 visual encoder extracts image features, a single linear projector converts them into image tokens, and a Vicuna-7B LLM receives the combined image and text tokens to autoregressively generate coordinate tokens. The key design choice is constructing localization-based instruction conversations that pair each keypoint with a ChatGPT-generated (manually verified) natural-language description of its body location, enabling the LLM to reason about where a keypoint should be rather than relying on a fixed output channel. Training uses two-stage parameter-efficient fine-tuning with LoRA (r=8) inserted into the q/v projections of both the visual encoder and LLM, plus the projector, totaling only 8.7M trainable parameters: stage 1 aligns image-text representations on CC3M by training only the projector, and stage 2 fine-tunes LoRA modules and the projector on COCO Keypoint instruction conversations. This architecture allows the model to detect novel keypoint types (e.g., pelvis, neck) at test time simply by providing their textual description, without any architectural modification.

## Results

On the standard COCO Keypoint val set, LocLLM achieves 77.4 AP (94.4 AP50, 85.2 AP75), matching the SoTA ViTPose (77.4 AP) while using only 8.7M trainable parameters via LoRA. On cross-dataset generalization, LocLLM substantially outperforms all baselines: 64.8 AP on Human-Art (vs. 53.8 for ViTPose, a 11 AP gap) and 33.4 PCKh@0.1 on MPII (vs. 25.8 for ViTPose). For novel keypoint detection (Pelvis, Neck on MPII), LocLLM reaches 43.6 and 36.9 accuracy respectively, compared to 1.7 and 5.6 for the CLIP baseline. On 3D Human3.6M, LocLLM achieves 46.6 average accuracy, trailing the strongest baselines (Sun et al. 59.1, PoseNet 53.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On COCO Keypoint, LocLLM ties ViTPose at 77.4 AP but edges ahead on AP50 (94.4 vs 93.6) and AP75 (85.2 vs 84.8), while outperforming HRNet (76.8), SimCC (76.5), SimplePose (74.4), RLE (74.0), and the CLIP baseline (73.1) by 0.6–3.3 AP. The gap widens dramatically under cross-dataset evaluation: on Human-Art, LocLLM scores 64.8 AP versus 53.8 for ViTPose and 53.4 for HRNet (≈11 AP advantage), and on MPII it reaches 33.4 PCKh@0.1 versus 25.8 for ViTPose. For novel keypoint detection (Pelvis, Neck), LocLLM achieves 43.6/36.9 accuracy while the CLIP baseline collapses to 1.7/5.6, confirming that LLM reasoning over text descriptions is critical for generalizing to unseen keypoint types. On 3D Human3.6M, LocLLM (46.6 avg) underperforms all regression-based baselines (Sun et al. 59.1, PoseNet 53.3, Soft-argmax 49.6, RLE 48.6), indicating that the LLM-based approach has not yet closed the gap on depth estimation. Overall, LocLLM's primary advantage is generalization—matching SoTA on the in-distribution benchmark while substantially exceeding it on out-of-distribution and novel-keypoint settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

LocLLM repurposes the multimodal LLM pipeline (visual encoder + projector + LLM) established by LLaVA and MiniGPT4 for a fundamentally different task: sub-pixel keypoint localization rather than open-ended VQA or captioning. It departs from the dominant heatmap-based (HRNet, ViTPose) and regression-based (RLE, DeepPose) keypoint localization paradigms, which encode keypoint priors into the output layer architecture, by instead passing keypoint type, location, and relationship information as natural-language instructions to a frozen LLM. The parameter-efficient training strategy (LoRA on both visual encoder and LLM, plus a linear projector) follows the instruction-tuning recipe of LLaVA, and the CLIP-based baseline isolates the specific contribution of LLM reasoning over mere vision-language feature alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a generative LLM can perform fine-grained (sub-pixel) spatial localization, a capability previously assumed to require specialized architectures with keypoint-specific output heads. The cross-dataset and novel-keypoint results (e.g., 43.6 PCKh on unseen Pelvis vs. 1.7 for CLIP baseline) suggest a path toward truly open-vocabulary pose estimation, but the approach's reliance on accurate textual descriptions and its GPU inefficiency (7B LLM per image) limit near-term practical adoption. Open questions include whether the method extends to dense facial landmarks that resist natural-language description, whether the 3D performance gap (46.6 vs. 59.1 avg on Human3.6M) can be closed, and whether smaller LLMs or distilled variants can retain the generalization advantage at lower cost.
