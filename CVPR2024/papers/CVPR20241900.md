## Problem

Transformer-based event detectors suffer from quadratic self-attention cost that undermines the low-power advantage of event cameras, especially at high resolutions where event sparsity can fluctuate by hundreds of times. Existing sparse Transformers either use fixed pruning ratios that discard object-containing windows, or rely on global attention that remains far more expensive than window-based alternatives. Crucially, none of them adapt their sparsity level to the complexity of individual scenes, causing either information loss in dense scenes or wasted computation in sparse ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams converted into event voxel representations (50 ms accumulation window), processed at 640×360 for 1Mpx (originally 1280×720) and 304×240 for Gen1. It outputs bounding boxes with class labels (car, pedestrian, two-wheeler on 1Mpx; two classes on Gen1) via a standard FPN + detection head. Unusually, the per-sample computational cost is fully dynamic: the number of tokens participating in attention varies with scene complexity, so A-FLOPs range from very low in sparse scenes to higher in dense ones rather than remaining constant.

## Method

SAST is a hierarchical four-block Transformer that performs window-token co-sparsification at every layer. A scoring module computes per-token importance as a learned response (linear + ReLU) divided by a control factor derived from event sparsity (concatenated non-zero ratios across voxel bins), with Spatial-Temporal-Polar weighting to make both branches learnable. A selection module then applies p-norm normalization followed by Softmax to amplify score contrast, and retains windows and tokens whose scores exceed thresholds set as a fraction of the mean score—so sparser scenes automatically select fewer tokens. The Masked Sparse Window Self-Attention (MS-WSA) pads selected tokens to a uniform length, runs multi-head attention in parallel, and applies a column mask that zeros out padded-token contributions to eliminate context leakage across tokens and batches. An optional Context Broadcasting step averages information across all selected tokens to stabilize selection in subsequent layers, and an LSTM at each block propagates temporal state.

## Results

On the 1Mpx dataset, SAST achieves 48.3% mAP with only 1.8G A-FLOPs (28% of RVT's 6.5G), while SAST-CB reaches 48.7% mAP with 2.6G A-FLOPs. On Gen1, SAST and SAST-CB achieve 47.9% and 48.2% mAP respectively, surpassing all baselines including RVT (47.2%) and GET (47.9%). SAST reduces A-FLOPs by 72% compared to the RVT baseline while simultaneously improving mAP by 0.9 percentage points on 1Mpx and 0.7 on Gen1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAST outperforms the RVT baseline (MaxViT) by +0.9% mAP on 1Mpx and +0.7% on Gen1 while reducing A-FLOPs by 72% and 64% respectively. Against GET, SAST achieves comparable mAP (48.3% vs 48.4% on 1Mpx) with only 28% of the A-FLOPs, and SAST-CB surpasses GET by 0.3% mAP. SAST ties ASTMNet on 1Mpx mAP (48.3%) but uses only 7.4% of its FLOPs (5.6G vs 75.7G). SAST slightly trails AEC (CSP-Darknet-53) by 0.1% mAP on 1Mpx (48.3% vs 48.4%) but requires only 10% of its FLOPs; SAST-CB reverses this with +0.3% mAP. Among sparse Transformer baselines, SAST beats AViT by +3.8% mAP (48.3% vs 44.5%) with 7.7% of its A-FLOPs, SViT by +3.0% (48.3% vs 45.3%) with 54.5% of its A-FLOPs, and SparseTT by +0.7% (48.3% vs 47.6%) with 27.7% of its A-FLOPs. On Gen1, both SAST and SAST-CB achieve the highest mAP among all compared methods, surpassing the next-best AEC by 0.9% and 1.2% respectively with only 10–11% of its FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAST builds directly on RVT (Gehrig & Scaramuzza, CVPR 2023), adopting its MaxViT backbone, FPN, detection head, and LSTM temporal propagation, while replacing the dense window self-attention with a sparsified variant. It also incorporates the Context Broadcasting operation from Nam et al. (ICCV 2023) as an optional component. Conceptually, SAST departs from prior sparse Transformers (AViT, SparseViT, SparseTT) that perform either token-level sparsification on global attention or fixed-ratio window pruning, by introducing joint window-token co-sparsification with scene-adaptive thresholds, and by designing MS-WSA to handle the resulting unequal window sizes without context leakage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Event cameras are valued for their sub-10 mW power consumption, yet dense Transformer backbones negate this advantage; SAST's 72% reduction in attention FLOPs while simultaneously improving mAP directly addresses this tension. The scene-adaptive sparsity mechanism—where the model retains more tokens in dense scenes and prunes aggressively in sparse ones—offers a principled way to balance recall and efficiency without manual tuning. A notable practical limitation is that wall-clock runtime (19.7 ms) is actually higher than the dense RVT baseline (16.0 ms) despite the FLOPs reduction, suggesting that the scoring/selection overhead and irregular memory access patterns partially offset the arithmetic savings. Open questions include whether the event-sparsity-driven scoring generalizes to other asynchronous or sparse-input modalities, and whether the approach scales to even higher resolutions or multi-frame temporal contexts.
