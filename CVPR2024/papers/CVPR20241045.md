## Problem

Existing 3D diffusion models based on signed distance fields or occupancy functions are restricted to closed, watertight surfaces, making them unable to generate real-world shapes with open surfaces such as clothing. Additionally, prior wavelet-based compression schemes (e.g., WaveGen) rely on manually selected wavelet filters that cause significant information loss, degrading generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241045/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder) or an image (encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder) as conditioning signal, combined with a random 3D noise volume. Internally, UDF volumes are sampled at 256³ resolution with distances truncated to [0, 0.1], then decomposed into coarse and fine wavelet coefficient volumes at scale J=3. The final output is a textured 3D mesh: the recovered UDF is meshed via DCUDF (double covering) to handle non-differential zero-level sets, and textures are synthesized using Text2Tex guided by the input text.

## Method

UDiFF introduces a data-driven optimal wavelet transform for UDFs: a learnable biorthogonal filter (decomposition φ_θ^D and inversion φ_δ^I) is optimized by minimizing weighted MSE between original and reconstructed UDF volumes, with weights concentrated near the zero-level set to preserve geometric detail. The diffusion generator is a 3D U-Net (analogous to Stable Diffusion) that denoises random volumes into coarse wavelet coefficient volumes, with text conditions injected via cross-attention layers using CLIP embeddings. A separate fine-predictor U-Net maps coarse coefficients to fine coefficients, and the full UDF is recovered by wavelet inversion. At inference, the pipeline proceeds: random noise → coarse coefficients (iterative denoising) → fine coefficients (predictor) → UDF (inversion) → mesh (DCUDF) → textured mesh (Text2Tex).

## Results

On DeepFashion3D (open-surface garments), UDiFF achieves the best COV-CD (69.62) and COV-EMD (67.72), along with the best MMD-EMD (14.01) and 1-NNA-CD (81.83, closest to the ideal 50%), outperforming all four baselines. On ShapeNet (closed shapes), UDiFF matches or exceeds SALAD on MMD-CD for both Chair (11.67 vs 11.69) and Airplane (3.151 vs 3.636), while achieving the best COV-EMD on Airplane (63.78) and competitive 1-NNA scores. The ablation confirms the learned wavelet filter contributes a 1.64-point COV-CD gain and 0.64-point MMD-CD improvement over using WaveGen's manually selected Biorthogonal 6-8 filter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDiFF outperforms all four DeepFashion3D baselines on 5 of 6 metrics, with the sole exception being MMD-CD where PointDiff edges ahead (11.01 vs 11.60). The largest margins are over WaveGen (MMD-CD 15.56→11.60, a 25% reduction) and Diffusion-SDF (MMD-EMD 16.63→14.01), both of which require manifold preprocessing that distorts open-surface garments. On ShapeNet closed shapes, UDiFF achieves the best MMD-CD on Airplane (3.151 vs SALAD's 3.636) and best MMD-EMD on Chair (14.04 vs SALAD's 14.29), while SALAD retains a slight COV-CD advantage on Chair (56.42 vs 52.58). Compared to WaveGen, which also uses wavelet compression, UDiFF improves MMD-CD by 0.45 on Chair and 0.377 on Airplane, attributed to the data-driven optimal wavelet filter reducing information loss. The paper also visually compares against Shap-E (text-conditioned) and AutoSDF (text-conditioned on ShapeNet), reporting that UDiFF produces more accurate geometries and more realistic textures via Text2Tex.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDiFF extends the wavelet-domain diffusion framework of WaveGen [21] and the DDPM training procedure [17] to the unsigned distance field (UDF) domain, departing from the SDF/occupancy representations that restrict prior implicit diffusion models (Diffusion-SDF, 3D-LDM, WaveGen) to closed, watertight surfaces. The key methodological departure is replacing WaveGen's manually selected biorthogonal wavelet with a data-driven learned biorthogonal filter optimized via self-reconstruction loss, specifically targeting the non-differential zero-level set where UDFs are most complex. For conditioning, the paper follows the CLIP cross-attention pattern established in Stable Diffusion and Shap-E, and borrows the Text2Tex pipeline for texture synthesis, positioning itself as a unified geometry-plus-appearance generator rather than a pure geometry model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling diffusion-based generation of open-surface shapes (clothing, organic forms) with textures, UDiFF addresses a practical gap that SDF/Occ-based methods fundamentally cannot fill without lossy manifold preprocessing. The data-driven wavelet optimization is a reusable technique that could benefit other implicit-representation compression tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to two relatively small datasets (DeepFashion3D with ~1,800 models, ShapeNet chair/airplane), the texture pipeline is a separate post-hoc step rather than jointly learned, and it remains open whether the learned wavelet filter generalizes across shape domains or scales to much larger, more diverse 3D corpora. The reliance on DCUDF for meshing also introduces a dependency on a specific extraction method that may not transfer to all UDF generation settings.
