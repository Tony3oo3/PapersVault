## Problem

Existing CLIP-based weakly supervised semantic segmentation methods use the frozen CLIP model only to generate high-quality pseudo labels in a multi-stage pipeline, while single-stage approaches rely on trainable ImageNet-pretrained backbones. No prior work explores whether the frozen CLIP backbone can be directly used for single-stage segmentation prediction, and the static nature of pseudo labels from a frozen backbone creates an uncorrectable optimization problem that limits performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240852/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (cropped to 320×320 during training) and image-level class labels as input. Class labels are converted into text prompts fed to the frozen CLIP text encoder, while the image is processed by the frozen CLIP image encoder (ViT-B/16). The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask at the original image resolution, produced by a lightweight trainable decoder with fewer than 6M learnable parameters, requiring only 6.2 GB GPU memory.

## Method

WeCLIP uses the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder as a fixed backbone, extracting features from all 12 transformer blocks. A lightweight decoder processes these features via per-layer MLPs, concatenation, a convolution, and three cascaded transformer encoder layers to produce the segmentation prediction. To overcome the static pseudo label problem inherent to a frozen backbone, a CAM Refinement Module (RFM) dynamically updates the initial GradCAM by computing an affinity map from the decoder's fused features, using it to filter reliable multi-head attention maps from the frozen CLIP encoder, and constructing a refining map that modulates the initial CAM. The decoder and RFM form a mutual improvement loop: refined pseudo labels provide better supervision for the decoder, and the trained decoder produces more reliable affinity maps for RFM. Training uses a combined loss of cross-entropy on the segmentation prediction and a weighted cross-entropy on the affinity map against one-hot pixel-pair affinity labels derived from the refined pseudo labels.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, WeCLIP achieves 76.4% mIoU (val) and 77.2% mIoU (test) with DenseCRF post-processing, setting a new state-of-the-art for weakly supervised semantic segmentation. On MS COCO-2014 val, it reaches 47.1% mIoU, outperforming all prior single-stage and multi-stage approaches. The method requires only 6.2 GB GPU memory and fewer than 6M learnable parameters (frozen ViT-B backbone), compared to 12+ GB and 44–58M parameters for competing methods, while training in 4.5 hours on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeCLIP outperforms every baseline on both PASCAL VOC 2012 and MS COCO-2014. Against the previous best single-stage method ToCo (71.1/72.2 mIoU), WeCLIP gains +5.3/+5.0 mIoU on val/test, and against the strongest CLIP-based multi-stage method CLIP-ES (73.8/73.9), it gains +3.6/+3.3 mIoU. On MS COCO, WeCLIP (47.1%) surpasses the best multi-stage method CDLI (45.5%) by 1.6% and the best single-stage ToCo (42.3%) by 4.8%. In terms of training cost, WeCLIP uses only 6.2 GB GPU memory versus 12 GB for CLIP-ES and MCTformer, and >24 GB for ToCo, while completing training in 4.5 h (30k iterations) compared to 7 h for CLIP-ES and 25 h for MCTformer. The ablation confirms that the RFM contributes +6.2 mIoU over the decoder alone, and the dynamic refining map (combining A_f, G_e, A_s) adds +3.1 mIoU over using only static attention maps from CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) where either a trainable ImageNet-pretrained backbone is fine-tuned end-to-end (single-stage) or a separate segmentation model is trained on pseudo labels generated by a CLIP-based classifier (multi-stage, e.g., CLIP-ES [29]). WeCLIP is the first to use a completely frozen CLIP ViT-B encoder directly as the segmentation backbone, paired with a lightweight trainable transformer decoder that interprets the frozen features. The RFM module specifically addresses the static-CAM limitation inherited from CLIP-ES [29], whose GradCAM-based pseudo labels cannot improve during training, by introducing a dynamic refinement mechanism guided by the decoder's learnable affinity map. The post-processing pipeline also borrows the pixel adaptive refinement module from AFA [39].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding that a frozen CLIP backbone requires no fine-tuning yet produces SOTA segmentation (77.2% mIoU on VOC test, 47.1% on COCO val) with only ~6M learnable parameters and 6.2 GB GPU memory challenges the assumption that dense-prediction backbones must be trained or fine-tuned. This has practical implications for resource-constrained settings and suggests that large-scale contrastive pre-training may already encode sufficient spatial-semantic structure for pixel-level tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex segmentation settings (e.g., panoptic, video), whether the frozen-backbone constraint is truly optimal or whether light fine-tuning could push performance further, and whether the RFM's reliance on a specific CLIP architecture (ViT-B/16) limits transferability to other vision-language models.
