## Problem

Text-to-4D dynamic scene generation with diffusion guidance remains largely unexplored. The pioneering MAV3D approach suffers from the Janus problem due to the absence of 3D-aware priors, and its entangled hexplane representation causes the high-quality static asset to degrade during motion learning. No unified method existed for text-to-4D, image-to-4D, or personalized 4D generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240676/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and optionally one or multiple reference images (a single image for image-to-4D, or 4–6 casually captured images for personalized 4D) and produces a deformable NeRF representing a 4D dynamic scene renderable from arbitrary viewpoints over time. During motion optimization, 24-frame videos are rendered at 144×72 and upsampled to 576×320 to match the Zeroscope video diffusion model's native resolution. The canonical NeRF uses a multi-resolution hash grid up to resolution 4096, while the deformation field uses a coarser 4D grid capped at resolution 232.

## Method

Dream-in-4D employs a two-stage pipeline with a motion-disentangled deformable NeRF (D-NeRF) representation. In the static stage, a canonical 3D NeRF with multi-resolution hash encoding is optimized via Score Distillation Sampling using a combined 2D (StableDiffusion-v2.1) and 3D-aware (MVDream) diffusion guidance, ensuring view-consistent, realistic static assets free of the Janus problem. In the dynamic stage, the static NeRF is frozen and only a 4D deformation field (mapping deformed space to canonical space) is optimized using video diffusion guidance (Zeroscope) with a variant SDS loss combining latent and decoded RGB terms. The deformation field is encoded with a 4D multi-resolution hash grid to capture local motion details, and a novel total variation loss on rendered 3D displacement maps (λ_TV = 1000) suppresses spatial and temporal jitter. This disentanglement preserves static quality during motion learning and enables trivial adaptation to image-guided generation by swapping the static-stage diffusion models without modifying the motion stage.

## Results

Evaluation is conducted via user preference studies rather than standard quantitative metrics. In the text-to-4D comparison with MAV3D (13 users), Dream-in-4D is preferred 82.4% of the time for visual & 3D quality, 65.4% for text alignment, and 61.8% for motion quality. For image-to-4D generation, the method is preferred over MAV3D 98.3% of the time for image texture preservation and 100% for text alignment. Ablation studies (18 users) confirm that removing 3D guidance drops visual & 3D preference to 6.12%, and using a hexplane representation instead of D-NeRF drops it to 0.00%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is MAV3D [33], the first prior work on text-to-4D generation with diffusion guidance. Dream-in-4D wins on all three evaluated dimensions: visual & 3D quality (82.4% vs. 17.6%), text alignment (65.4% vs. 34.6%), and motion quality (61.8% vs. 38.2%). The margin is largest for visual & 3D quality, attributed to the combined 2D+3D diffusion guidance in the static stage and the motion-disentangled D-NeRF representation that preserves the high-quality static asset during motion learning. For image-to-4D generation, the advantage over MAV3D is even more pronounced, with 98.3% preference for texture preservation and 100% for text alignment, since MAV3D lacks a mechanism to condition on input images. No other external baselines are compared; the remaining comparisons in Table 2 are internal ablations (removing 2D/3D guidance, replacing multi-resolution grids with positional encoding, removing the TV loss, or substituting hexplane for D-NeRF), all of which confirm that each proposed component contributes meaningfully to the final quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the D-NeRF deformable radiance field architecture and the Score Distillation Sampling optimization framework from DreamFusion, but departs sharply from the prior text-to-4D work MAV3D in two key ways: (1) it replaces MAV3D's hexplane representation with a fully disentangled canonical NeRF + deformation field, and (2) it introduces a two-stage pipeline that combines 2D (StableDiffusion) and 3D-aware (MVDream) diffusion guidance in the static stage before applying video diffusion (Zeroscope) in the motion stage. The static stage itself extends the text-to-3D line (DreamFusion, Magic123) by showing that neither 2D nor 3D diffusion guidance alone suffices—2D guidance alone causes the Janus problem while 3D guidance alone yields synthetic textures and missing scene elements. The image-to-4D and personalized 4D extensions repurpose the Magic123/zero123 and DreamBooth pipelines without modifying the motion learning stage, a direct consequence of the disentanglement design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dream-in-4D provides the first unified framework covering text-to-4D, image-to-4D, and personalized 4D generation, which is practically important for content creation pipelines in gaming, AR/VR, and advertising where both appearance control and motion specification are needed. The core insight—that freezing a high-quality static asset and only optimizing a deformation field with video diffusion preserves quality better than joint optimization—offers a general design principle for future 4D generation methods. However, the method still fails on complex multi-object prompts (e.g., a robot playing a violin) where the static stage produces incorrect geometry that the dynamic stage cannot recover, and the reliance on short 24-frame video clips at low resolution (144×72) limits the temporal extent and fidelity of learned motion. Open questions include whether the two-stage approach can be extended to longer sequences, whether the TV loss generalizes to non-rigid deformations, and whether the disentanglement strategy transfers to other 4D representations beyond NeRF.
