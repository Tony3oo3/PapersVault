## Problem

In source-free domain adaptation, cross-domain distribution shift causes the source model's attention to drift away from discriminative object regions on target samples, leading to incorrect predictions. Existing SFDA methods (SHOT, AaD, NRC) adjust features or classifiers but fail to explicitly interpret which regions the source model relies on or to recalibrate those regions for the target domain, leaving a gap in how discriminative and transferable semantics are identified and leveraged.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241464/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained source model (ResNet-50/101 backbone with a two-layer FC classifier) and unlabeled target RGB images, with no access to source data. It produces three intermediate artifacts: a calibrated attention map (same spatial resolution as the Grad-CAM heatmap), an enhanced feature tensor obtained by semantic filling (ReLU of the difference between attention-weighted and raw-image features, added back to the raw features), and an augmented target image from attention-reversed mixup. The final output is a class prediction over the shared category space.

## Method

DPC comprises two modules. The Discriminative Semantics Enhancement (DSE) module first extracts a Grad-CAM attention map per target image, then trains a small convolutional autoencoder to calibrate that map: the loss penalizes deviation from the original map while encouraging higher variance (flatter Gaussian-like distribution) proportional to (1 minus prediction confidence), so low-confidence samples spread attention to recover missed object regions. The calibrated map is element-wise multiplied with the image to produce a weighted input; features from this weighted image and the raw image are combined via a ReLU-gated residual (semantic filling) to inject discriminative content into the representation. The Attention Induced Mixture (AIM) module augments samples by blending a primary image X_i with an auxiliary image X_j whose object region is suppressed via (1 minus calibrated_A_j) element-wise times X_j, using per-channel Beta-distributed mixing coefficients to avoid multi-object conflicts. DPC is plug-and-play: it operates only at the input layer and high-level feature stage, so it can be embedded into existing SFDA pipelines (SHOT, AaD) without modifying their core objectives.

## Results

On Office-Home (12 tasks), SHOT+DPC achieves 73.2% average accuracy (↑1.4% over SHOT) and AaD+DPC achieves 73.1% (↑1.3% over AaD), both surpassing the best prior SFDA method A²Net (72.8%). On Office-31, SHOT+DPC reaches 89.8% and AaD+DPC reaches 90.5%, approaching UDA-level performance (SRDC 90.8%). On VisDA-C, AaD+DPC attains 88.8% average accuracy, exceeding the best UDA baselines CAN and FixBi (both 87.2%) by 1.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPC is plugged into two SFDA baselines (SHOT and AaD) and consistently improves them across all three benchmarks: +1.4%/+1.3% on Office-Home, +1.2%/+0.8% on Office-31, and +1.4%/+1.1% on VisDA-C. On Office-Home, both SHOT+DPC (73.2%) and AaD+DPC (73.1%) surpass the strongest prior SFDA method A²Net (72.8%) and NRC (72.0%), and approach the best UDA method FixBi (72.7%). On VisDA-C, AaD+DPC (88.8%) exceeds all UDA baselines including CAN (87.2%) and FixBi (87.2%) by 1.6%, demonstrating that source-free adaptation with DPC can match or beat methods that have access to source data. On Office-31, AaD+DPC (90.5%) is within 0.3% of the best UDA method SRDC (90.8%) and surpasses A²Net (90.1%) and SHOT (88.6%). The gains are most pronounced on harder transfer tasks (e.g., Ar→Cl on Office-Home: +2.1% for SHOT) and on VisDA-C's synthetic-to-real shift, where attention calibration helps the model focus on domain-invariant object regions rather than background artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) lineage, building directly on SHOT (Liang et al., ICML 2020) and AaD (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022) as the two SFDA frameworks into which its DPC mechanism is plugged. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant SFDA strategies—feature alignment, clustering constraints, and prediction consistency—by introducing an interpretable-learning perspective: it leverages Grad-CAM attention maps to identify and calibrate discriminative regions in target images, then uses an autoencoder to adjust attention distributions guided by prediction confidence. The attention-reverse mixup (AIM) module further distinguishes the work from standard mixup-based augmentation in UDA, which typically blends source and target images; here, the absence of source data is addressed by inverting the attention mask of an auxiliary target image to suppress its object region before blending.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (operating only on input images and high-level features) makes DPC a practical add-on for existing SFDA pipelines without architectural changes, and the interpretable framing offers a diagnostic lens for understanding *why* source models fail on target domains (attention shift). However, the gains are modest (1.1–1.4% average accuracy across benchmarks), the method is restricted to CNN backbones where Grad-CAM is well-defined, and the Gaussian-distribution assumption for attention calibration is heuristic rather than theoretically grounded. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to ViT-based SFDA, whether the autoencoder-based attention adjustment generalizes beyond the tested ResNet backbones, and whether the attention-reverse mixup provides meaningful robustness gains over simpler target-target blending at scale.
