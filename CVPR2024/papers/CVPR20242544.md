## Problem

Existing bias detection methods for text-to-image generative models are restricted to closed sets of pre-defined biases (e.g., gender, age, race), leaving potentially harmful biases on objects, animals, and other attributes entirely unexplored. This paper asks whether arbitrary biases can be identified and quantified in a T2I model given only prompts, without any pre-specified bias taxonomy or annotated data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a dataset of real textual captions (Flickr30k or a ~123K single-person subset of COCO) and a target T2I model treated as a black box, generating 1000 images per bias (100 captions × 10 random seeds). It produces a structured knowledge base of bias triplets (bias name, associated class set, caption-specific question), per-caption and aggregated bias severity scores bounded in [0,1] via normalized entropy, and a global ranking of biases by severity. The VQA model's answers are constrained to the discrete class set proposed by the LLM for each bias, rather than free-form text.

## Method

OpenBias is a three-stage zero-shot pipeline: (1) Llama2-7B, prompted via in-context learning, proposes for each caption a set of bias triplets (bias name, class set, identifying question), which are aggregated into a dataset-level knowledge base; (2) the target diffusion model (SD 1.5/2/XL) generates images from the captions; (3) LLaVA 1.5-13B answers the caption-specific questions on generated images, selecting from the proposed class set. A two-stage filtering step removes captions where the bias answer is explicitly stated (LLM check) or where class synonyms appear (ConceptNet lookup). Bias severity is quantified as 1 minus the normalized Shannon entropy of the VQA class distribution, yielding a score in [0,1] where 0 is unbiased and 1 is maximally biased, computed both per-caption (context-aware) and averaged across all captions for a bias (context-free).

## Results

On COCO-generated images, Llava1.5-13B (the chosen default VQA for OpenBias) achieves 92.83% accuracy / 83.21% F1 on gender, 72.27% / 70.00% on age, and 55.91% / 44.33% on race, outperforming all other VQA baselines. Agreement with the FairFace classifier is confirmed via low KL divergence (0.002–0.184) across SD-1.5, SD-2, and SD-XL on both Flickr 30k and COCO captions. In the user study (55 participants, 2200 responses), OpenBias achieves an Absolute Mean Error of 0.15 on bias intensity and matches the human-identified majority class direction in 67% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five VQA models as candidate backbones for the bias-assessment stage: CLIP-L, OFA-Large, mPLUG-Large, BLIP-Large, and Llava1.5-7B. Llava1.5-13B (the selected default) wins on age (72.27% acc / 70.00% F1) and race (55.91% acc / 44.33% F1) by a wide margin over all baselines, with the largest gap over OFA-Large (53.79% / 41.72% age) and mPLUG-Large (21.46% / 23.26% race). On gender, OFA-Large and mPLUG-Large marginally edge out Llava1.5-13B in accuracy (93.03% vs. 92.83%), but Llava1.5-13B still leads in F1 (83.21% vs. 83.07% and 82.81%). CLIP-L lags on age (58.96% acc) and race (36.02% acc), while BLIP-Large is weakest on age (48.61% acc / 31.29% F1). Overall, Llava1.5-13B is the only model that simultaneously achieves strong performance across all three sensitive attributes, justifying its selection as the default VQA component in OpenBias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant closed-set bias detection paradigm in T2I models (FairDiffusion, ITIGEN, attribute-classifier-based methods) by formulating bias discovery as an open-set problem that requires no predefined bias taxonomy. Methodologically, it repurposes the LLM-plus-VQA pipeline pattern established in faithfulness evaluation (TIFA) and visual programming (VIPER-GPT, Visual Programming) for the novel task of bias proposal and quantification, replacing task-specific classifiers with a general-purpose VQA model. The use of ConceptNet for synonym-based filtering and in-context learning for bias proposal are straightforward adaptations of existing tools rather than new algorithmic contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenBias provides a practical, modular tool for auditing T2I models beyond the well-studied gender/race/age axes, surfacing previously unexamined biases (laptop brand, bed type, child race linked to socioeconomic context) that closed-set methods structurally cannot detect. The context-aware vs. context-free distinction reveals that bias direction can be entirely context-dependent (e.g., motorcycle type), complicating any single-number fairness metric. Key open questions include systematically quantifying the role of caption context, addressing the inherited biases of the LLM and VQA components themselves, and extending the pipeline to unconditional generative models and real-world multimodal datasets.
