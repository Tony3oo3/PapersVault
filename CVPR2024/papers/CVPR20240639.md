## Problem

Existing SCI reconstruction methods recover only 2D images corresponding to the applied encoding masks, ignoring the underlying 3D scene structure. This prevents multi-view consistency and makes novel-view synthesis impossible, limiting SCI to a single fixed viewpoint per mask. The paper asks whether a full 3D scene representation can be learned directly from a single snapshot compressed image, enabling arbitrary view rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240639/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single snapshot compressed image (e.g., 1024×768 for real data, 512×512 or 600×400 for synthetic) and N binary encoding masks (N = compression ratio, typically 8) as input. It produces a NeRF-based 3D scene representation, jointly estimated camera start/end poses, and can render novel-view images and high frame-rate multi-view consistent images at arbitrary viewpoints. Notably, camera poses cannot be pre-estimated via COLMAP from a single compressed image, so they must be recovered as part of the optimization.

## Method

SCINeRF formulates the SCI image formation process (element-wise multiplication of virtual frames with binary masks, summed across exposure time) as a differentiable operation within NeRF training. Camera poses are parameterized as linear interpolation between two SE(3) endpoints (start and end of trajectory), following the BARF/BAD-NeRF paradigm of jointly optimizing scene and pose from inaccurate initializations. The training objective minimizes the MSE between the NeRF-rendered compressed image (volume-rendered per-frame intensities modulated by masks and summed) and the actual SCI measurement, requiring no pre-estimated poses or external supervision. This test-time optimization avoids the generalization degradation of pre-trained deep learning decoders and naturally enforces multi-view consistency through the shared 3D representation.

## Results

On novel-view synthesis (Table 2), SCINeRF achieves 30.61 PSNR / 0.0764 LPIPS on Airplants, 36.33 PSNR / 0.0426 LPIPS on Factory, and 34.75 PSNR / 0.0257 LPIPS on Vender, outperforming the best baseline (NeRF+EfficientSCI) by 1.99, 3.61, and 1.98 dB in PSNR respectively. On image reconstruction (Table 1), SCINeRF reaches 36.60 PSNR / 0.0221 LPIPS on Factory and 36.40 PSNR / 0.0298 LPIPS on Vender, surpassing EfficientSCI by 3.73 and 3.23 dB. SCINeRF also maintains high quality under high compression ratios (CR=32: 33.95 PSNR vs. 28.43 for EfficientSCI), with only marginal degradation compared to a sharp drop in baseline methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCINeRF is compared against four SOTA SCI reconstruction methods: GAP-TV (optimization-based), PnP-FFDNet, PnP-FastDVDNet (plug-and-play denoising), and EfficientSCI (Transformer-based). GAP-TV is the weakest baseline, trailing SCINeRF by 8–11 dB in PSNR across all scenes. PnP-FFDNet and PnP-FastDVDNet are competitive on simpler scenes (e.g., Hotdog: 29.14/29.31 vs. 30.59 PSNR) but fall 3–5 dB behind on texture-rich scenes like Factory and Vender. EfficientSCI is the strongest baseline, yet SCINeRF still wins on PSNR and LPIPS in every scene for both image reconstruction and novel-view synthesis; the gap is largest on Factory (36.33 vs. 32.72 PSNR in novel-view synthesis). Notably, SCINeRF loses on SSIM in a few reconstruction scenes (e.g., Airplants: 0.9335 vs. 0.9425 for EfficientSCI), which the authors attribute to NeRF's volumetric rendering introducing slight information loss. The novel-view synthesis comparison is explicitly noted as unfair to SCINeRF, since baselines receive ground-truth camera poses while SCINeRF must jointly optimize poses from the single compressed measurement.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCINeRF sits at the intersection of two research threads: snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) decoding and NeRF-based scene reconstruction without known camera poses. On the SCI side, it departs from all prior methods (GAP-TV, PnP-FastDVDNet, EfficientSCI) that treat the problem as 2D image/video reconstruction and ignore underlying 3D structure. On the NeRF side, it follows the joint pose-and-scene optimization paradigm established by BARF and BAD-NeRF, but replaces their motion-blur or unposed-image formation model with the discrete binary-mask modulation specific to SCI hardware. The key conceptual contribution is formulating the SCI encoding equation as a differentiable rendering pipeline within NeRF training, enabling end-to-end optimization of both the radiance field and the camera trajectory from a single compressed measurement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By recovering a 3D scene representation rather than just 2D frames, SCINeRF unlocks capabilities that 2D SCI decoders fundamentally cannot provide: multi-view consistent novel-view synthesis and high frame-rate video rendering from a single exposure. The paper also highlights a practical systems implication—SCI encoding can serve as a privacy-preserving and bandwidth-efficient transmission channel between edge devices and cloud NeRF infrastructure. Limitations include the assumption of a linear camera trajectory within the exposure window, the test-time optimization cost (100–200K iterations per scene), and the lack of quantitative evaluation on real data (only qualitative comparisons). Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear or 6-DoF camera motions, larger scenes, and whether faster NeRF variants (e.g., Instant-NGP) can reduce the per-scene optimization cost to practical levels.
