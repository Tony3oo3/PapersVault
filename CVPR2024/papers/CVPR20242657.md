## Problem

In Federated Incremental Learning (FIL), edge clients face catastrophic forgetting when new tasks arrive, but existing replay-based methods either ignore Non-IID data heterogeneity across clients or rely on privacy-leaking techniques like data reconstruction and adversarial training. This paper is the first to study catastrophic forgetting under data heterogeneity in FIL, where clients have limited storage and must select which previous samples to cache for replay in a way that accounts for both local and global data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242657/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a stream of classification tasks (RGB images with labels) partitioned via Dirichlet distribution to induce Non-IID heterogeneity, and at each new task arrival has access to the server's global model, its own local model, and a fixed-size cache of M previous samples (e.g., 2000 for CIFAR-10, 300 for Office31). The method outputs an aggregated global model and, per client, a curated subset of cached samples selected by importance score. Two task types are supported: class-incremental (growing label space, fixed domain) and domain-incremental (fixed label space, shifting domain).

## Method

Re-Fed introduces a Personalized Informative Model (PIM) per client that is updated on previous local samples using a proximal-style regularization term q(λ)(v_k − w^{t−1}) that blends local and global model information, analogous to a momentum component where the global model guides the update direction. During s iterations of PIM training, per-sample gradient norms are recorded and aggregated with a 1/p decay (Eq. 5) to produce importance scores that emphasize early-training signal, where samples most influential to model fitting are identified. The client then caches the top-scoring samples within its storage budget M and trains its local model jointly on cached and new-task data. The hyperparameter λ controls the local-global balance: smaller λ (more global information) is preferred under high data heterogeneity (small Dirichlet α), while larger λ (more local information) suits near-IID settings. A convergence theorem guarantees the PIM tracks the optimal model at rate C·g(t), and the framework is modular—pluggable into any FL aggregation scheme without additional communication overhead.

## Results

Re-Fed achieves the best final test accuracy across all six datasets in both class-incremental and domain-incremental scenarios, outperforming the strongest baseline by 1.87%–19.73%. On class-incremental tasks, Re-Fed reaches 29.22% on CIFAR-10 (α=1.0), 25.61% on CIFAR-100 (α=5.0), and 32.07% on Tiny-ImageNet (α=10). On domain-incremental tasks, it achieves 85.96% on Digit10 (α=0.1), 50.80% on Office31 (α=1), and 56.66% on DomainNet (α=10). Re-Fed also requires the fewest communication rounds to reach its best accuracy (e.g., 562 rounds on CIFAR-10 vs. 738 for FedCIL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Re-Fed outperforms all five baselines on every dataset in both incremental scenarios. Against FedAvg [31], the gains range from +1.63% (CIFAR-10) to +4.93% (DomainNet); against FedProx [22], from +3.39% (CIFAR-10) to +7.54% (DomainNet). Among the FIL-specific methods, FCIL [6] is the strongest class-incremental baseline (29.58% on Tiny-ImageNet), but Re-Fed still leads by +2.49%; FedCIL [38] is the strongest domain-incremental baseline (83.85% on Digit10), yet Re-Fed exceeds it by +2.11%. The DANN+FL baseline [9] (adversarial domain adaptation) is consistently outperformed, with the largest gap on DomainNet (+6.65%). Notably, FedCIL's generative replay degrades on larger-scale datasets (CIFAR-100, DomainNet) where class/domain complexity is higher, while Re-Fed's gradient-norm-based caching remains robust across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated learning (FedAvg, FedProx) and replay-based incremental learning (iCaRL, generative replay), introducing the Personalized Informative Model (PIM) as a mechanism to balance local and global information when selecting samples for caching. It departs from prior FIL methods (FCIL, FedCIL) that rely on generative or distillation-based replay by using a simpler gradient-norm importance scoring approach, motivated by privacy concerns with data reconstruction. The gradient-norm-based sample importance draws directly on the theoretical results of Toneva et al. [42] and Paul et al. [36], while the PIM update rule is analogous to a momentum step that interpolates between the local model and the global model via a tunable hyperparameter λ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world federated systems face both incremental data arrival and Non-IID client distributions simultaneously, yet prior FIL work largely assumed IID data. The modularity of Re-Fed as an off-the-shelf add-on to any FL aggregation scheme makes it practically deployable without additional communication or storage overhead beyond standard FL. However, the paper is limited to classification tasks and does not address dynamic client requirements (varying storage, computation budgets, or asynchronous task arrival), which the authors acknowledge as important for real deployment. Open questions include whether the PIM-based sample selection generalizes to non-classification tasks (e.g., segmentation, generation) and whether the λ scheduling can be made adaptive rather than fixed per heterogeneity level.
