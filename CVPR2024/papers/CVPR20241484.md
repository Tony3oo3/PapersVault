## Problem

Existing 2D-to-3D lifting methods (C3DPO, PAUL, Deep NRSfM) require semantic correspondences across all 3D training instances, meaning each landmark index must carry the same meaning (e.g., 'left chair leg') across every sample. This constraint prevents scaling to dozens of object categories with varying joint counts and configurations, and blocks the construction of a unified, object-agnostic lifting model that generalizes to unseen categories and rigs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241484/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of 2D keypoints (N×2 matrix) with a binary mask indicating which joints are present, where N is a fixed maximum across all categories. No object category label, semantic joint names, or correspondence information is provided at inference. The output is a 3D point cloud (N×3) in a canonical reference frame, which is then rotated and scaled via Procrustean alignment to recover the final 3D pose. The model handles variable joint counts per category through masking and zero-centering/scale normalization of the input.

## Method

3D-LFM replaces learnable joint embeddings with Token Positional Encoding (TPE) computed via analytical Random Fourier Features, which encodes relative 2D positions without requiring semantic correspondence and preserves permutation equivariance. The backbone is a graph-based transformer that combines local graph attention (guided by a skeleton adjacency matrix encoding joint connectivity) with global multi-head self-attention in each layer, concatenated and refined through residual MLP blocks with GeLU nonlinearity. A Procrustean alignment step (OnP/PnP via SVD) is applied between the predicted canonical shape and ground truth during training, forcing the network to learn only non-rigid deformations rather than rigid rotations, which accelerates convergence and reduces error. The model is trained with MSE loss on the aligned 3D points, jointly across 30+ categories with imbalanced data, and generalizes to out-of-distribution categories and unseen rig configurations at inference.

## Results

On the H3WB whole-body benchmark, 3D-LFM achieves 64.13 MPJPE (mm), substantially outperforming Jointformer (88.3) and SimpleBaseline (125.4). With Procrustes Alignment (Ours-PA), the model further reduces whole-body MPJPE to 33.13 mm and face-aligned MPJPE to 6.02 mm. On PASCAL3D+ multi-object lifting, 3D-LFM matches C3DPO when given object-specific information but maintains lower MPJPE when object-specific data is withheld, demonstrating its object-agnostic advantage. Combined multi-category training reduces average MPJPE on the animal supercategory from 21.22 mm (isolated training) to 12.5 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-LFM outperforms all four baselines across every category on the H3WB benchmark. Against Jointformer (the strongest baseline), 3D-LFM reduces whole-body MPJPE from 88.3 to 64.13 mm (−27.4%), face-aligned from 17.8 to 10.44 mm (−41.3%), and hand-aligned from 43.7 to 28.22 mm (−35.4%). Against SimpleBaseline and CanonPose, the margins are even larger (e.g., 125.4 → 64.13 on whole-body). On PASCAL3D+, 3D-LFM matches C3DPO when both are given object-specific landmark semantics, but C3DPO degrades significantly when that information is removed while 3D-LFM maintains its performance, confirming its object-agnostic design advantage. The Procrustes Alignment variant (Ours-PA) further narrows the gap to category-specific methods, achieving 33.13 mm whole-body MPJPE, though it slightly underperforms the non-PA variant on the body-only split (39.36 vs. 60.83), suggesting a trade-off between global alignment and local body fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of 2D-3D lifting methods (C3DPO, PAUL, Deep NRSfM, NRSfMFormer) that all require semantic correspondence between 2D and 3D landmarks, by exploiting the permutation equivariance of transformers to eliminate explicit correspondence entirely. It builds on the Procrustean alignment strategy from PAUL to focus learning on non-rigid deformations, borrows the masking mechanism from Deep NRSfM++ for handling occlusions, and adapts analytical Random Fourier Features from point cloud processing into a Token Positional Encoding that replaces learned joint embeddings. The graph-based transformer with hybrid local-global attention is a novel architectural combination, but each component draws from established ideas in GNNs and attention mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D-LFM is the first unified model to perform 2D-3D lifting across 30+ object categories simultaneously without object-specific information, and its demonstrated OOD generalization (unseen animal species, inanimate objects) and rig transfer (different joint counts/configurations) open a path toward truly general-purpose 3D reconstruction from 2D keypoints. The finding that combined multi-category training substantially improves underrepresented categories (e.g., PASCAL3D+ from 4.31mm to 1.1mm MPJPE) while leaving well-represented ones unchanged suggests a practical pre-training recipe for long-tail 3D lifting. Open questions include how the approach scales to hundreds of categories, whether it handles strong perspective distortion (beyond weak-perspective/orthographic assumptions), and how cross-category knowledge transfer interacts with category-specific fine-tuning.
