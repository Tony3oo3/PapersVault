## Problem

Existing chain-of-thought approaches for multimodal reasoning focus primarily on text processing while overlooking the critical integration and targeted analysis of visual data. Images contain abundant, high-dimensional, and sometimes redundant visual cues that make it difficult for models to isolate question-relevant details from irrelevant content, leading to missed key information or distraction by noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240124/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question (with optional context and multiple-choice options) and a single RGB image as input. It produces a sequence of intermediate subquestion-subanswer pairs and a final answer (option letter for ScienceQA, free-form text for MM-Vet). For text-only questions, the image decomposition and matching stages are skipped, and only question decomposition with sequential subanswer generation is performed.

## Method

DIEM is a training-free, plug-and-play decomposition-integration pipeline applied on top of LLaVA-1.5-7b. In the decomposition stage, GPT-3.5 Turbo splits the question into an ordered list of subquestions (with dependency tags for cross-referencing), while Segment Anything Model segments the image into an adaptive number of sub-images. In the matching stage, CLIP computes text-image similarity scores to select the top-k sub-images most relevant to each subquestion, always retaining the original image to preserve global context. In the answer generation stage, each subquestion is answered sequentially by LLaVA-1.5-7b conditioned on its matched sub-images, with reference tags resolved by substituting prior subanswers; all subquestion-subanswer tuples are then concatenated before the original question to guide the final answer. The entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning and can be attached to any existing MLLM.

## Results

On ScienceQA, DIEM (SQ+SI, k=5) improves the average accuracy of LLaVA-1.5 from 66.85% to 68.88% (+2.03%), with the most notable gain in the image-context modality (IMG) from 65.70% to 69.10% (+3.40%). On MM-Vet, the total score increases from 31.1 to 32.4 (+1.3 points), with the largest improvements in Recognition (+2.2) and Spatial Awareness (+4.9). The method is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no fine-tuning of the underlying LLaVA-1.5-7b model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Standard LLaVA-1.5-7b model used without any question or image decomposition. DIEM (SQ+SI, k=5) outperforms the baseline on 7 out of 9 ScienceQA sub-categories, with the largest gains in Natural Science (+4.58%), Text context (+4.85%), and Image context (+3.40%). However, DIEM underperforms on Language Science (63.64% vs. 67.18%) and No-context questions (64.12% vs. 65.93%), suggesting the decomposition strategy is less beneficial when visual information is absent or when the task is purely linguistic. On MM-Vet, DIEM (SQ+SI, k=4) wins on 4 of 6 capability dimensions (Recognition, Knowledge, Generation, Spatial Awareness) but slightly trails on OCR (22.5 vs. 23.5) and Math (11.2 vs. 11.5), while achieving a higher total score (32.4 vs. 31.1). Overall, the decomposition-integration strategy provides consistent improvements in visually-grounded reasoning tasks while showing minor regressions in text-heavy or OCR-specific sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIEM extends the multimodal Chain-of-Thought line of work (e.g., multimodal-CoT [52]) by addressing its key limitation: CoT focuses on sequential text reasoning while neglecting targeted visual analysis. Rather than training a new model, DIEM composes existing off-the-shelf tools—GPT-3.5-Turbo for question decomposition, Segment Anything for image segmentation, and CLIP for text-visual matching—into a training-free, plug-and-play prompting pipeline on top of LLaVA-1.5. The decomposition-integration strategy is explicitly motivated by human cognitive processes (pre-scan, focused attention, integration), positioning it as a cognitive-inspired prompting strategy rather than an architectural change to the underlying MLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes DIEM immediately applicable to any existing MLLM without retraining, which is practically appealing. However, the gains are modest (+2.03% average, +3.40% image accuracy on ScienceQA; 31.1→32.4 on MM-Vet), and the method's effectiveness is sensitive to image complexity—simple or single-object images see little benefit, while flowcharts and diagrams can suffer from lost textual information during segmentation. Open questions include whether jointly decomposing questions with images (rather than text-only decomposition via GPT-3.5) would improve subquestion quality, how to handle images where segmentation severs critical inter-element relationships, and whether the approach scales meaningfully to more complex multi-object scenes.
