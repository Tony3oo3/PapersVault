## Problem

Existing unsupervised point cloud shape correspondence methods establish matches by directly computing point-wise feature similarity, which fails for unconventional shapes where limb adhesion, bending, and extreme postures distort or destroy the global structure. Without explicit structural guidance, direct similarity calculations between highly deformed point cloud pairs produce ambiguous and noisy correspondences, particularly for non-rigid objects like humans and animals in unusual poses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of raw point clouds (source X and target Y, each with 1024 points in 3D) as input, requiring no mesh connectivity, annotations, or post-processing. It outputs a dense one-to-one correspondence mapping each source point to its nearest target point in the learned feature space. During training, the network also consumes the ground-truth coordinates of the point cloud pairs to supervise the space aligner, but at inference only the two point clouds are needed.

## Method

TANet introduces a Template Generation Module and a Template Assistance Module. The generation module maintains a bank of K=4 learnable templates (each with learnable 3D positions and 512-dim embeddings) and a space aligner that predicts point positions from embeddings, supervised by SmoothL1 loss on true coordinates; a template construction loss (chamfer distance between the template and its cross-constructed version from point cloud pairs) enforces structural consistency. The assistance module comprises: (1) a Template Selector that scores each template via chamfer distance (geometric) and cosine similarity after mix pooling (semantic), then applies Gumbel-Softmax to pick the best template; (2) a Correlation Fusion step that injects template correlation vectors into point features through an attention mechanism to suppress noise; and (3) a Transitive Consistency loss (cross-entropy) that constrains the direct source-target similarity matrix to match the template-mediated similarity (S_XT × S_TY), guiding feature learning without increasing inference cost. The total loss combines all four terms with fixed weights, and the entire network is trained end-to-end with AdamW.

## Results

On the TOSCA animal benchmark, TANet achieves 65.1% correspondence accuracy at 0.01 error tolerance and 0.7 cm average error, surpassing the previous best point-based method HSTR (52.3% acc, 1.2 err) by 12.8 percentage points in accuracy. On the SHREC'19 human benchmark, TANet reaches 21.5% accuracy and 4.5 cm error, outperforming HSTR (19.3%, 4.9) and SE-ORNet (17.5%, 5.1). In cross-dataset generalization, TANet leads on SMAL (37.1% acc, 3.7 err) but is slightly behind SE-ORNet on SURREAL (20.6% vs 21.5% acc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANet decisively outperforms all point-based baselines on both primary benchmarks. On TOSCA, it improves accuracy by 12.8 pp over HSTR (52.3%→65.1%), 26.8 pp over SE-ORNet, and 30.4 pp over DPC, while reducing average error from 1.2 cm to 0.7 cm. On SHREC'19, the gains are more modest but consistent: +2.2 pp over HSTR, +4.0 pp over SE-ORNet, and +6.2 pp over DPC. In cross-dataset evaluation, TANet achieves the best result on SMAL (37.1% acc, 3.7 err), narrowly beating SE-ORNet (36.4%, 3.9) and substantially outperforming HSTR (33.9%, 5.6) and DPC (33.2%, 5.8). On SURREAL, TANet (20.6% acc, 4.8 err) is slightly behind SE-ORNet (21.5%, 4.6), which incorporates a dedicated orientation estimation module for symmetry handling, but still surpasses HSTR (19.4%) and DPC (17.7%). Mesh-based methods (SURFMNet, GeoFMNet) are not directly comparable due to their reliance on vertex connectivity, though SURFMNet reports lower absolute error on SHREC'19 (0.2 cm) at the cost of much lower accuracy (5.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TANet extends the unsupervised point cloud shape correspondence line initiated by DPC and HSTR, adopting HSTR's transformer encoder and DPC's construction loss as its backbone. Its key conceptual departure is introducing learnable shape templates as structural intermediaries, repurposing the shape-prior idea from 6D pose estimation and scene synthesis into a correspondence context. Unlike prior methods that rely solely on direct point-wise feature similarity, TANet adds a template assistance module (adaptive selection via Gumbel-Softmax, correlation fusion via attention, and a transitive consistency loss) that routes similarity through an explicit geometric scaffold, addressing the failure mode of direct matching on highly deformed or unconventional shapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The template-assisted formulation offers a principled way to inject structural priors into correspondence without requiring mesh connectivity or manual landmark selection, which is practically valuable for real-world point clouds where such information is absent. However, the learned templates remain sub-optimal without direct shape supervision, and the method still lags SE-ORNet on symmetry-heavy human data (SURREAL), indicating that orientation-aware mechanisms and template-based structural guidance are complementary rather than competing. Open questions include whether templates can be made fully explicit and category-agnostic, whether the transitive consistency signal generalizes to partial or heavily occluded shapes beyond the visual-only SHREC'16/Owlii tests, and whether the approach scales to larger point counts or multi-category scenes.
