## Problem

Existing LiDAR re-simulation methods either rely on manually designed 3D assets, lack physical fidelity (e.g., multi-returns, reflected intensity, ray drops), or are restricted to static scenes. No prior approach can faithfully re-simulate LiDAR in dynamic driving scenes with full physical accuracy while simultaneously supporting flexible scene editing such as adding, removing, or repositioning objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241494/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes ego-motion-compensated multi-frame LiDAR scans (each ray encoded as origin, direction, range, intensity, and a binary drop flag) together with tracked 3D bounding boxes of dynamic vehicles. It produces a re-simulated LiDAR scan from an arbitrary novel viewpoint, outputting per-ray range, intensity, and ray-drop probability. Beyond novel view synthesis, the compositional representation enables editing operations: altering sensor pose/beam count, changing object trajectories, and inserting or removing neural assets reconstructed from entirely different scenes.

## Method

DyNFL decomposes the scene into a static background and N rigidly-moving dynamic vehicles, each encoded as a separate neural SDF field using multi-resolution hash encoding and shallow MLPs that predict signed distance, intensity, and ray-drop probability. The key architectural contribution is a two-stage compositional rendering pipeline: first, a ray-box intersection test identifies which dynamic fields a query ray intersects; second, each intersecting field is rendered independently via SDF-based volume rendering adapted for active sensors (incorporating two-way transmittance and beam-width effects), and the per-field results are merged using a ray-drop test that selects the nearest valid return. Dynamic objects are reconstructed in a canonical coordinate frame and rendered at arbitrary timestamps by applying the inverse rigid transformation to queried rays. Training minimizes a composite loss combining range L1, surface-point SDF regularization, eikonal constraint, intensity L2, and ray-drop BCE+Lovász terms.

## Results

On the Waymo Dynamic dataset, DyNFL achieves a median absolute range error (MedAE) of 3.0 cm and a dynamic-vehicle MedAE of 8.5 cm, substantially outperforming UniSim (6.1 cm and 14.3 cm) and LiDARsim (11.5 cm and 16.0 cm). On static scenes, DyNFL attains the best MAE across all four benchmarks: 26.7 cm on TownClean, 33.9 cm on TownReal, 28.3 cm on Waymo Interp, and 28.6 cm on Waymo NVS, with the largest gains over NFL (32.0 cm on TownClean, 39.2 cm on TownReal). Intensity RMSE is 0.05, matching UniSim and halving LiDARsim's 0.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LiDARsim, DyNFL reduces MAE from 170.1 cm to 30.8 cm on Waymo Dynamic and from 159.6 cm to 26.7 cm on TownClean, a roughly 5× improvement, while also halving the intensity RMSE (0.10 → 0.05). Against UniSim, the closest dynamic-scene competitor, DyNFL halves the MedAE (3.0 vs 6.1 cm) and the dynamic-vehicle MedAE (8.5 vs 14.3 cm), with the gap attributable to the per-field rendering plus ray-drop composition strategy. On static scenes, DyNFL outperforms NFL on all four benchmarks (e.g., MAE 26.7 vs 32.0 on TownClean; 28.6 vs 32.6 on Waymo NVS), with the SDF-based surface regularization yielding the largest gains on planar regions. DyNFL also beats the general-purpose NeRF baselines (i-NGP, DS-NeRF, URF) on every static-scene metric, confirming that the active-sensor volume rendering formulation is essential for LiDAR fidelity. In auxiliary downstream tasks, DyNFL's re-simulated scans yield object-detection agreement (Agg. 0.87 at IoU>0.5) and segmentation IoU (81.1% vehicle) that match or exceed LiDARsim and NFL, indicating a low sim-to-real gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyNFL sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: Neural LiDAR Fields (NFL), which introduced physically-grounded LiDAR volume rendering with two-way transmittance but was restricted to static scenes, and UniSim/Neural Scene Graph, which decomposed dynamic driving scenes into a static background plus movable object fields but ignored physical sensor properties like beam width and secondary returns. The paper departs from both by (i) replacing the unconstrained density representation with an SDF-based surface model (following NeuS/Neuralangelo) to reduce geometric noise, and (ii) introducing a compositional rendering scheme with a ray drop test that independently renders each field and then merges measurements, rather than the single-pass blended sampling used by UniSim. The multi-resolution hash encoding backbone and SLERP-based temporal interpolation are standard components borrowed from i-NGP and prior dynamic NeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying physical LiDAR fidelity with flexible scene editing (object insertion/removal across scenes, trajectory manipulation, sensor reconfiguration), DyNFL provides a practical tool for generating counterfactual training and evaluation data for autonomous driving perception systems, addressing a real gap between hand-crafted simulators (CARLA) and reconstruction-based methods. The main limitations are the reliance on accurate tracked bounding boxes and the inability to synthesize unseen view angles of dynamic vehicles, which constrains the method's generality. A key open question the authors highlight is whether 4D neural representations can be developed that retain the per-asset editing flexibility that the current decomposed approach provides.
