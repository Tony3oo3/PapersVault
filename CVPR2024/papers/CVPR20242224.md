## Problem

In ObjectNav, when the target is invisible the agent must infer its likely location from contextual object relationships, but end-to-end RL methods encode these priors implicitly without geometric memory, while modular methods maintain explicit maps yet lack learned contextual reasoning. Existing supervised approaches for predicting target position are unreliable because absolute target positions vary across room layouts, whereas joint associative relations among multiple objects (e.g., couches with cushions and coffee tables) are more robust.

## Input / Output

During training, the SGM receives a 224×224 multi-channel semantic map (occupancy + object classes) cropped from a global room map, divided into 16×16 patches with 75% randomly masked; visible patches are encoded visually while observed object categories are converted into a text prompt for an LLM whose response is embedded via RoBERTa. During navigation, the agent's accumulated local semantic map is cropped to a scaled-up region (ε=140%), and a subset of ~30% patches selected by a multinomial sampling strategy (balancing informativeness and adjacency to unobserved regions) is fed to the SGM, which outputs continuous confidence values for all unobserved pixels; the agent then extracts the highest-confidence coordinate in the target channel as its long-term goal.

## Method

SGM is a ViT-Base-based masked autoencoder trained self-supervised to reconstruct randomly masked patches of cropped global maps, requiring no target annotations or simulator supervision. The visual encoder processes only visible patches (following MAE), while a parallel text branch embeds LLM-predicted contextual objects (via a fixed sentence template and RoBERTa) to provide episode-agnostic semantic priors that complement the geometric details from episodic observations. A LXMERT cross-modality encoder fuses the two modalities via cross-attention, and a lightweight Transformer decoder predicts pixel values for masked patches using a combined BCE + IoU loss. The key insight is that random masking (vs. connected masking) forces the model to learn long-range contextual associations rather than local completion, and that LLM general knowledge enables prediction of entirely unobserved object categories that pure visual reconstruction cannot achieve. During navigation, a Laplacian-based sampling strategy selects informative frontier-adjacent patches, and the generated map drives a Fast Marching Method local policy for path planning.

## Results

On Gibson (val), SGM achieves 78.0% SR, 44.0% SPL, and 1.11m DTS, outperforming the previous best modular method 3D-aware [57] by 3.5% SR and 1.9% SPL. On MP3D (val), SGM reaches 37.7% SR and 14.7% SPL, surpassing 3D-aware (34.0% SR, 14.6% SPL) and Red-Rabbit (34.6% SR, 7.9% SPL). On HM3D (val), SGM attains 60.2% SR and 30.8% SPL, edging out PEANUT (59.1% SR, 30.3% SPL) and RIM (57.8% SR, 27.2% SPL). Notably, SGM is trained entirely in a self-supervised manner without any target-oriented annotations, yet matches or exceeds supervised baselines across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGM outperforms all 15 compared baselines on both Gibson and MP3D in SR. On Gibson, SGM (78.0% SR) leads the closest competitor 3D-aware [57] (74.5%) by 3.5 points, and PONI [37] (73.6%) by 4.4 points; it also improves DTS from 1.16m to 1.11m over 3D-aware. On MP3D, SGM (37.7% SR) surpasses 3D-aware (34.0%) by 3.7 points and Red-Rabbit [53] (34.6%) by 3.1 points, while also achieving the best SPL (14.7%) among all methods. On HM3D, SGM (60.2% SR, 30.8% SPL) narrowly beats PEANUT [56] (59.1%, 30.3%) and RIM [9] (57.8%, 27.2%). A critical distinction is that SGM is the only self-supervised method in the comparison: all other baselines rely on supervised learning, imitation learning, or reinforcement learning with target-oriented annotations, yet SGM matches or exceeds them without any such supervision. The end-to-end RL baselines (DD-PPO, THDA, Red-Rabbit) and imitation learning (Habitat-Web) trail significantly behind the modular methods, confirming the advantage of explicit geometric memory for ObjectNav.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the modular ObjectNav architecture (SemExp, PONI) by replacing supervised target-prediction functions with a self-supervised generative model trained in the MAE paradigm. It departs from prior map-completion work (L2M, SSCNav) in two key ways: (1) it learns joint contextual relations among multiple objects via random masking of sub-maps rather than completing a single egocentric top-down view, and (2) it augments episodic visual observations with general knowledge from LLMs (GPT-4, ChatGLM) fused via LXMERT-style cross-attention. The navigation policy itself (Fast Marching Method on occupancy channels) follows established modular approaches, so the novelty is concentrated in the map generation and contextual reasoning component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised formulation eliminates the need for target-oriented annotations or simulator supervision, making the approach more data-efficient and scalable as LLMs improve. However, the LLM component, while substantially improving map recall (from ~79% to ~92%), yields only modest navigation gains (~4% SR), suggesting that map quality improvements do not linearly translate to navigation performance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond indoor environments, whether the absolute-position deviations in generated maps limit its utility in larger-scale tasks, and whether the reliance on external segmentation models (Mask-RCNN, RedNet) as a prerequisite constrains end-to-end applicability.
