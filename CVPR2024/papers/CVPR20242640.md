## Problem

Vanilla latent diffusion models like Zero123-XL can generate multi-view images conditioned on CLIP embeddings and camera angles, but the resulting views lack explicit 3D geometric consistency — they are semantically coherent yet do not necessarily adhere to a shared 3D structure. Existing approaches to enforce multi-view consistency either require explicit scene geometry, fixed viewpoints, or operate outside the denoising network, limiting flexibility and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB reference image of a 3D object and produces 16 multi-view consistent images from uniformly distributed surrounding viewpoints at a chosen elevation angle (0°, 15°, or 30°). The reference image is encoded via CLIP and also converted to a reference latent that is concatenated with each noisy latent at every denoising step, while relative camera rotations condition each parallel diffusion process. Inference completes in 11 seconds on a single A100 GPU using 50 DDIM steps.

## Method

ConsistNet is a lightweight plug-in block inserted after every decoder layer of a frozen Zero123-XL U-Net, connecting N parallel single-view diffusion processes through multi-view geometry. It comprises two submodules: a view aggregation module that unprojects each view's feature map into a shared 3D world volume via inverse camera projection (with sinusoidal camera-parameter encoding) and applies multi-head self-attention plus 3D convolutions across the N voxel-aligned volumes, and a ray aggregation module that warps the consistent 3D volume back to each camera frustum, samples features along depth rays with tri-linear interpolation, and projects them to 2D via cross-attention. Only the two aggregation networks and a zero-initialized per-pixel MLP residual (following ControlNet) are trainable, while the entire Zero123-XL backbone remains frozen. Training uses the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss over all N views jointly, allowing the model to learn a shared multi-view distribution rather than independent per-view denoising.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects dataset, ConsistNet achieves the best LPIPS_Alex (0.15/0.12/0.11), SSIM (0.89/0.90/0.90), and PSNR (22.75/23.93/23.67) at 0°, 15°, and 30° elevation angles respectively, while generating 16 multi-view images in 11 seconds on a single A100 GPU. At 0° and 15° elevations, ConsistNet outperforms all baselines on every metric; at 30° it matches SyncDreamer within 0.01–0.14 on most metrics while running 10× faster (11 s vs. 2 min). Compared to the Zero123-XL backbone alone, plugging in ConsistNet improves PSNR by 4.5–7.8 dB and MS-SSIM by 0.11–0.25 across all elevation angles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zero123-XL (the frozen backbone), ConsistNet wins decisively on all metrics at every elevation angle, improving PSNR by 4.5 dB (0°), 7.3 dB (15°), and 7.6 dB (30°) while adding only 6 s of inference time. Against SyncDreamer, the closest concurrent method, ConsistNet achieves substantially better LPIPS, SSIM, and PSNR at 0° and 15° elevations (e.g., PSNR 22.75 vs. 17.41 at 0°), and at 30° it is within 0.01 on LPIPS_VGG and 0.02 on PSNR while running 10× faster (11 s vs. 2 min). Against DreamFusion + Zero123-XL, ConsistNet matches or exceeds quality at 0° and 15° (e.g., PSNR 22.75 vs. 18.53 at 0°) and is roughly 100× faster (11 s vs. 18 min); at 30° DreamFusion edges ahead on LPIPS_Alex (0.14 vs. 0.11) and PSNR (21.53 vs. 23.67), but ConsistNet still surpasses it on SSIM and MS-SSIM while being two orders of magnitude faster. Overall, ConsistNet is the only method that combines top-tier multi-view consistency with sub-15-second inference for 16 views.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsistNet extends the Zero123-XL line of CLIP-conditioned single-to-multi-view diffusion by inserting a lightweight 3D-aware plug-in block into the frozen U-Net decoder, following the zero-initialized residual pattern popularized by ControlNet. It departs from concurrent work SyncDreamer, which aggregates multi-view latents into a 3D volume *outside* the denoising network and trains each view independently, by performing view aggregation *within* every decoder layer and learning a joint multi-view distribution. It also differs from MVDream (batch-dimension attention without explicit geometry) and MVDiffusion (requires scene geometry) by grounding consistency in multi-view projection/unprojection without needing depth prediction or pixel correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design makes ConsistNet a drop-in module for any pretrained LDM backbone, and the 10× speedup over SyncDreamer (11 s vs. 2 min for 16 views on a single A100) makes multi-view-consistent generation practical for downstream 3D asset pipelines (SDS-based reconstruction, VR/AR). However, the task remains severely ill-posed from a single reference image, and the authors themselves note that comparing generated views to a single ground-truth rendering is an inadequate evaluation; designing principled metrics for multi-view consistency is an open problem. Future directions include coupling the plug-in with a 3D reconstruction module and further reducing inference cost.
