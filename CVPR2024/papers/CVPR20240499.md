## Problem

Existing personalized image generation methods require test-time fine-tuning per subject, sacrificing generalizability, while head-control methods either fail to preserve identity or lack text conditioning. No prior framework simultaneously achieves generalizable identity preservation from a single reference photo, text-driven scene generation, and fine-grained 3D-consistent head control in a unified model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240499/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 reference facial photograph (cropped and aligned to the face region), a free-form text prompt, and a 3D head condition consisting of Surface Normal, Albedo, and Lambertian rendering images derived from a DECA/FLAME reconstruction whose shape, pose, expression, and lighting parameters can be freely adjusted. The output is a 512×512 photo-realistic portrait of the specific individual in the prompted context with the specified head geometry and illumination.

## Method

CapHuman builds on frozen Stable Diffusion V1.5 and introduces a trainable CapFace side network (ControlNet-style) that injects two complementary signals. For identity, it encodes global features via FaceNet and local patch features via CLIP ViT-L/14 (face region only), projects them into a latent identity vector, and injects it through cross-attention into the denoising UNet. For head control, it feeds the 3DMM-derived condition images (Normal, Albedo, Lambertian) through the CapFace encoder, predicts a facial mask, and element-wise aligns the masked feature maps with the SD decoder at each layer. A time-dependent ID dropout strategy discards identity features at early diffusion timesteps to prevent the model from over-relying on the reference pose, and an optional post-hoc fusion with a separate head-control model at inference further sharpens pose accuracy at negligible identity cost.

## Results

On the HumanIPHC benchmark (100 identities from CelebA test split), CapHuman achieves the best identity similarity of 0.8363, surpassing DreamBooth (0.6860) by 15% and FastComposer (0.6191) by 21%. For head control precision, CapHuman achieves the lowest RMSE across all four sub-metrics: Shape (0.1020), Pose (0.0436), Expression (0.1241), and Lighting (0.0965), outperforming the second-best by 5–7%. CapHuman also maintains strong text-to-image alignment with a CLIP score of 0.2256 and 74.17% prompt accuracy, while fine-tuning baselines (Textual Inversion, LoRA, DreamBooth) drop to 13.70–39.21% prompt accuracy due to overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CapHuman outperforms all five baselines on identity preservation and head control precision. Against DreamBooth (the strongest fine-tuning baseline at 0.6860 ID sim.), CapHuman gains 15% in identity similarity while also achieving 5–7% lower RMSE on Shape, Expression, and Lighting head control metrics. Compared to FastComposer (the only other tuning-free method), CapHuman improves ID similarity by 21% (0.8363 vs. 0.6191) and reduces head control RMSE by 45–53% across all sub-metrics. ControlNet, which provides no identity preservation (ID sim. 0.0534), retains the highest prompt accuracy (90.32%) and CLIP score (0.2479) since it is not constrained by identity conditioning; however, it is not a fair identity-preservation comparison. The fine-tuning baselines (Textual Inversion, LoRA, DreamBooth) all suffer severe overfitting in the one-shot setting, dropping prompt accuracy to 13.70–39.21% and CLIP scores to 0.1561–0.1897, confirming that test-time fine-tuning sacrifices generative diversity for identity memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CapHuman builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture as its generative backbone, adopts ControlNet's side-network design for injecting external control signals, and leverages the FLAME 3D morphable model for parametric facial geometry. It departs from the dominant personalization paradigm (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, LoRA) that requires per-subject test-time fine-tuning, instead proposing an 'encode then learn to align' approach where identity features are extracted by frozen encoders (FaceNet, CLIP) and injected via cross-attention. It also distinguishes itself from head-control methods (ControlNet, DiffusionRig) by simultaneously preserving identity and supporting text conditioning, a combination no prior framework achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CapHuman is the first framework to unify generalizable identity preservation, text-to-image alignment, and 3D-consistent head control in a single tuning-free pipeline, and it introduces the HumanIPHC benchmark to evaluate all three dimensions jointly. The time-dependent ID dropout ablation reveals a fundamental tension between identity fidelity and pose controllability that is likely to inform future controllable generation work. Limitations include training exclusively on CelebA (celebrity faces), the one-shot reference constraint, and the reliance on DECA for 3D reconstruction, which may degrade on extreme poses or occlusions. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to multi-person scenes, video, or non-celebrity demographics.
