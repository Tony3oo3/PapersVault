## Problem

In federated learning with non-IID data, local training causes catastrophic forgetting of global knowledge, and proximal restrictions that mitigate this conflict with local learning objectives. Direct gradient projection to enforce orthogonality between local and proximal gradients actually degrades performance below FedAvg, revealing that the two objectives are too tightly coupled for explicit negation. The paper seeks a mechanism that implicitly achieves orthogonal learning without requiring stored gradients or past data.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model weights broadcast by the server and its own private local dataset (non-IID partitions of MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN, CINIC-10, PathMNIST, or TissueMNIST). The method produces updated local model weights after several local epochs, which are uploaded and averaged into the global model. Notably, the proximal loss (KL-divergence between local and global model predictions) is consumed only to compute a perturbation direction, never added as an auxiliary loss term, and the perturbation is applied exclusively to the last classifier head layer to reduce computation to ~1.33× FedAvg FLOPs.

## Method

FedSOL performs an adversarial proximal perturbation at each local update: it first computes a weight perturbation ε_p* in the direction of the proximal gradient (scaled by an adaptive radius ρ·Λ that amplifies perturbation where local and global parameters diverge most), then computes the local loss gradient at the perturbed weights w_k + ε_p* and uses that gradient for the parameter update. This two-loss decoupling—proximal loss for perturbation, local loss for the actual gradient—implicitly steers the update direction toward orthogonality with the proximal gradient without explicit projection. Theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–2) shows the update reduces proximal loss conflicts as ρ grows while remaining equivalent to a FedAvg step at a slightly shifted point, preserving local learning speed. Partial perturbation restricted to the final classifier layer achieves near-full-model performance at substantially lower cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with LDA (α=0.1), FedSOL achieves 64.13% test accuracy, outperforming FedAvg (56.13%) by 8.0 points and the strongest baseline FedNTD (63.16%) by 1.0 points. At extreme heterogeneity (LDA α=0.05), FedSOL reaches 60.01% versus FedAvg's 42.27% and FedNTD's 58.08%. On SVHN (LDA α=0.1), FedSOL attains 83.92%, surpassing FedNTD (79.25%) by 4.7 points and FedAvg (55.36%) by 28.6 points. Across all six datasets and both partition strategies, FedSOL consistently achieves the highest or near-highest accuracy with the lowest standard deviations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSOL consistently outperforms FedAvg across all six datasets and both partition strategies, with the largest gains at high heterogeneity (e.g., +28.6 points on SVHN LDA α=0.1, +12.9 points on CINIC-10 LDA α=0.1). Against the strongest baseline FedNTD, FedSOL wins on 5 of 6 datasets under LDA (α=0.1), with margins of 1.0–4.7 points (e.g., 83.92% vs 79.25% on SVHN, 78.88% vs 74.26% on CINIC-10); the sole exception is CIFAR-10 Sharding s=2 where FedNTD edges ahead by 0.5 points (67.25% vs 66.72%). FedSOL substantially outperforms FedProx, FedSAM, and FedASAM on all datasets, and dramatically surpasses FedNova and Scaffold, which fail entirely (10.00% accuracy) on CIFAR-10 LDA at α=0.05 and α=0.1. When combined with other proximal objectives (FedProx, FedNova, Scaffold, FedDyn, MOON), FedSOL's perturbation strategy further improves their base performance by 3–51 points (Table 4), confirming its generality as a plug-in module. FedSOL also achieves the lowest standard deviations in most settings (e.g., 0.30 on CIFAR-10 LDA α=0.05 vs 0.48 for FedNTD), indicating more stable training.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSOL repurposes the adversarial perturbation mechanism from Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), but inverts its role: instead of perturbing with the same loss used for updates, it perturbs with the proximal gradient while updating with the local gradient, thereby decoupling the two objectives. Conceptually, it adapts orthogonal learning from Continual Learning—where new-task gradients are projected orthogonal to old-task loss spaces—to the FL setting, where the 'old task' is the global distribution and the 'new task' is the local distribution. Crucially, the paper departs from the natural CL-inspired solution of direct gradient projection, demonstrating that negating the proximal component of the local gradient actually degrades performance below FedAvg, and instead achieves orthogonality implicitly through adversarial weight perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result showing that direct proximal gradient projection underperforms FedAvg is practically important: it cautions against naively transplanting CL orthogonal-learning techniques into FL and redirects the design space toward implicit orthogonality. The finding that perturbing only the last classifier head achieves near-full-model performance at roughly 1.33× the FLOPs of FedAvg makes the method attractive for deployment in resource-constrained federated settings. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees (Propositions 1–2, which rely on convexity of the local Hessian) hold in practice for deep non-convex networks, and whether the approach extends to other FL challenges such as communication efficiency or privacy-preserving aggregation.
