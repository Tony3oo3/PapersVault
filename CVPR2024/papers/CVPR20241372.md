## Problem

Two-stage HOI detectors suffer from two interrelated limitations: (1) the pretrained object detector introduces task bias, producing features optimized for localization rather than the fine-grained spatial relationships needed for interaction reasoning, and (2) the combinatorial pairing of all detected humans and objects yields a large candidate set where only a small fraction receives triplet supervision, severely limiting generalization—especially for rare HOI classes with fewer than 10 training samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241372/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and operates in a two-stage pipeline: an off-the-shelf DETR-based object detector (ResNet-50/101 or Swin-Large backbone) first produces human and object bounding boxes, and a ViTPose-base pose estimator extracts 17 body keypoints per detected person. The output is a set of HOI triplets (human, object, verb) with per-verb confidence scores, where during inference the final interaction score is the product of object detection confidences and the interaction head's verb probabilities.

## Method

The core contribution combines two modules. First, Pose-Aware Feature Refinement generates adaptive body-part bounding boxes from keypoint pairs (sized by an aspect-ratio-dependent formula in Eq. 1–2), then computes a spatial encoding vector per H-O pair incorporating Intersection-over-Body-part-Area (IoB), center-of-mass offsets, body angles, relative sizes, and interaction directions; this encoding modulates a modified UPT-style attention mechanism to refine object queries into interaction-aware features. Second, Hybrid Learning exploits the two-stage architecture's flexibility by first training a full detector, then freezing its interaction head to produce probabilistic soft labels (sigmoid verb scores) for all unlabeled H-O pairs; these soft labels are masked where ground-truth triplets exist and combined with the original focal-loss triplet supervision via a weighted hybrid loss (λ=0.5 default, or a linearly decaying λ, or a layer-wise scheme applying hybrid supervision only to lower decoder layers), effectively injecting implicit verb-verb correlation structure without any additional annotated data.

## Results

On HICO-DET (Default setting), the method achieves 46.01 mAP (Full) with Swin-Large backbone, surpassing the previous SOTA PViC (44.32) by 1.69 mAP and DiffHOI_l (40.63) by 5.38 mAP. On the Rare subset (138 HOI classes with <10 training samples each), it reaches 46.74 mAP, a +2.13 mAP improvement over PViC. With ResNet-50, the method attains 35.86 mAP (Full) and 32.48 mAP (Rare); with ResNet-101, 36.82 mAP (Full) and 33.99 mAP (Rare). On V-COCO, the Swin-Large model achieves 63.0 AP_role^S1 and 68.7 AP_role^S2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every backbone scale and evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor PViC [46] with Swin-Large, it gains +1.69 mAP on HICO-DET Full (46.01 vs 44.32) and +2.13 mAP on the Rare subset (46.74 vs 44.61), while also leading on Known Object Full by +1.69 mAP (49.50 vs 47.81). The margin over DiffHOI_l [40] (which relies on 140K+ synthetic images) is substantially larger at +5.38 mAP on Full, underscoring the efficiency of the hybrid learning approach without additional data. Among one-stage methods, FGAHOI [27] (37.18) and QAHOI [4] (35.78) with Swin-Large trail by 8.83 and 10.23 mAP respectively. On V-COCO, the Swin-Large model achieves the best AP_role^S2 (68.7) but trails STIP [47] (70.7) on AP_role^S1, though STIP uses ResNet-50. The ResNet-50 variant (Ours_s) improves over PViC [46] by +1.17 mAP on Full and over DiffHOI_s [40] by +1.45 mAP, confirming consistent gains across model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage HOI detection paradigm established by UPT and PViC, which introduced modified attention mechanisms and cross-attention for interaction reasoning. It departs from prior work in two directions: (1) instead of treating human and object queries uniformly, it introduces pose-aware spatial encoding (body-part regions, IoB, keypoint geometry) to guide attention toward interaction-relevant regions, and (2) rather than relying on additional synthetic data (as DiffHOI does with diffusion models) or discarding unlabeled pairs, it generates probabilistic soft labels from a fully-trained interaction head to provide supplementary supervision. The method builds directly on UPT's attention formulation and PViC's visual context reintroduction, layering pose information and hybrid loss on top.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid learning strategy offers a practical, data-free solution to the severe long-tail problem in HOI detection (138 classes with fewer than 10 training samples in HICO-DET), and the finding that rare-class mAP can exceed full-class mAP suggests the soft-label supervision effectively transfers verb knowledge across objects. The observation that a stronger object detector (H-DETR) actually degrades HOI performance highlights a fundamental task-bias issue that the pose-aware refinement partially addresses. Limitations include reduced prediction confidence due to soft-label supervision and overconfidence in frequent verbs like 'wear' and 'hold'; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to datasets with different interaction taxonomies and whether the dependency on an external pose estimator (ViTPose) introduces a practical bottleneck.
