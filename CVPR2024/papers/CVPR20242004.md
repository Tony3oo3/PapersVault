## Problem

Radar point clouds are inherently sparse and noisy, with only ~10% of pillars being non-empty compared to LiDAR, making it difficult to learn effective representations for 3D object detection. While deep learning has dramatically improved camera- and LiDAR-based detection, radar-specific architectures have not seen comparable gains, and existing cross-modality knowledge distillation methods do not account for the extreme density mismatch between radar and LiDAR features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes only radar point clouds from five sensors (360° coverage, 13 Hz, up to 250 m range) organized into 0.075 m × 0.075 m pillars over a [-54 m, 54 m] × [-5 m, 3 m] detection volume. During training, LiDAR point clouds are additionally processed by a parallel teacher branch but are discarded at test time. The network outputs per-class BEV detection heatmaps from which 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation, velocity, and IoU score) are decoded via a CenterHead.

## Method

RadarDistill distills knowledge from a LiDAR-encoding PillarNet-18 teacher into a radar-encoding PillarNet-18 student through three mechanisms. Cross-Modality Alignment (CMA) densifies sparse radar BEV features via a U-shaped module of deformable-convolution down-sampling, ConvNeXt-V2 blocks, transposed-convolution up-sampling, and skip-connection aggregation, producing two densified feature maps at 1/8 resolution. Activation-based Feature Distillation (AFD) then partitions both radar and LiDAR low-level features into active and inactive regions using per-pixel activation thresholds, and applies an adaptively weighted L2 loss that up-weights the sparsely distributed active regions to concentrate learning on semantically meaningful features. Proposal-based Feature Distillation (PFD) operates on high-level features by classifying pixels into true-positive, false-positive, and false-negative regions from the radar prediction heatmap versus the ground-truth heatmap, then applies a channel-normalized L1 loss with region-specific weights to align object features in TP/FN regions while suppressing false activations in FP regions.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, RadarDistill achieves 20.5% mAP and 43.7% NDS for radar-only 3D object detection, surpassing the previous SOTA (KPConvPillars) by +15.6% mAP and +29.8% NDS. Over its own PillarNet-18 baseline (8.6% mAP, 34.7% NDS), RadarDistill gains +11.9% mAP and +9.0% NDS. In the radar-camera fusion setting, integrating RadarDistill into BEVFusion yields 39.6% mAP and 46.4% NDS, improving over the BEVFusion baseline by +1.3% mAP and +1.1% NDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RadarDistill decisively outperforms all radar-only baselines: it surpasses KPConvPillars [29] (previous SOTA) by +15.6% mAP and +29.8% NDS, and Radar-PointGNN [27] by a margin of +20.0% mAP. Against its own PillarNet-18 baseline [23], the knowledge distillation framework adds +11.9% mAP and +9.0% NDS. In per-class comparisons (Table 2), RadarDistill beats all four camera-only methods (CenterNet, FCOS3D, MonoDIS, CenterFusion) on the Car class (54.0 AP vs. 53.6, 52.4, 47.8, 50.9 respectively), though it trails them on most other classes due to radar's inherently lower resolution. For the radar-camera fusion setting, RadarDistill-CR improves over the BEVFusion [20] baseline by +1.8% Car AP, +1.3% mAP, and +1.1% NDS, confirming that the distilled radar features transfer effectively to multi-sensor pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modality knowledge distillation line of work (BEVDistill, DistillBEV, UniDistill, S2M2-SSD) from LiDAR-to-camera and camera-to-LiDAR settings to the previously unexplored LiDAR-to-radar direction. It builds on PillarNet as the shared backbone architecture for both teacher and student, but departs from prior radar detection methods (Radar-PointGNN, KPConvPillars) that relied on GNNs or hybrid point-grid architectures without any external knowledge source. The key conceptual departure is addressing the extreme density mismatch between radar (~10% non-empty pillars vs. LiDAR) through a dedicated densification module (CMA), which prior cross-modality KD methods did not need to handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that LiDAR data used only during training can substantially upgrade radar-only 3D detection without requiring LiDAR at inference, opening a practical path for deploying high-quality radar perception in cost-constrained or LiDAR-free settings. The ablation showing CMA is critical (4% NDS drop without it) highlights a fundamental challenge in cross-modality transfer when data densities differ by an order of magnitude, a problem likely to recur in other sparse-sensor scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other backbones or sensor pairs, whether the 1/7-training-set ablation results hold at full scale, and whether more sophisticated fusion strategies (beyond the simple BEVFusion integration) could further close the gap to LiDAR-camera performance.
