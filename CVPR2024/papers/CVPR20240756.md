## Problem

Existing synthetic human datasets (SURREAL, AGORA, HSPACE, BEDLAM) only provide independently animated single persons, making them unsuitable for tasks requiring collective human motions such as multi-person tracking and group activity recognition. No prior synthetic generator supports multi-group, multi-person scenarios with fine-grained person-level and group-level annotations, leaving a critical data gap for human-centric CV research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240756/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator consumes simulation scenario parameters (scene selection, HDRI lighting, camera placement, group activity modules, human model and animation clip assignments) and renders photorealistic RGB frames at 1920×1080, 20 FPS, alongside a full annotation suite: 2D/3D joints and SMPL-fitted meshes, 2D/3D bounding boxes, individual and group tracking IDs, atomic action and group activity labels, segmentation, depth, and normal maps. The 3D-only dataset (M3Act3D) outputs 30 FPS multi-person motions with up to 27 persons per group over 87.6 hours. For the GAG task, the model takes Gaussian noise plus an activity class label and an arbitrary group size, producing a tensor of shape (#persons × #frames × 26 × 6) representing 6D joint rotations and root positions for the entire group.

## Method

M3Act is built on Unity Engine with the Perception library, using modular group activity scripts that parameterize the number of persons, permitted atomic actions, and spatial arrangement (line, cluster, curve) for each group. Extensive domain randomization is applied across scenes, lighting, cameras, character appearances, positions, and animation selections to ensure diversity. For the novel controllable 3D group activity generation task, the authors extend the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) with an Interaction Transformer (IFormer) that operates along the person dimension, enabling the model to learn inter-person spatial and temporal dependencies and generate coordinated group motions in a single forward pass rather than independently per person. This interaction transformer reduces collision frequency by over 3× compared to vanilla MDM and produces well-aligned group formations, effectively encoding the procedural heuristics of the generator into a learned generative model.

## Results

On DanceTrack multi-person tracking, mixing M3Act synthetic data (Dance+WalkRun groups) with real data improves MOTRv2's HOTA from 68.8 to 71.9, jumping from 10th to 2nd place on the leaderboard. For group activity recognition on CAD2, pre-training with 100% of M3ActRGB boosts Composer's group-level accuracy by +4.87% (84.87→89.74%) and person-level by +7.43% (81.31→88.74%), while ActorTransformer gains +5.59% and +5.43% respectively. In the novel 3D group activity generation task, MDM+IFormer achieves a group-level FID of 3.242 (vs. 3.909 for vanilla MDM) and reduces collision frequency from 3.643 to 1.157, approaching the ground-truth value of 0.037.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3Act synthetic data substantially outperforms other synthetic sources for multi-person tracking: mixing M3Act (WalkRun+Dance) with DanceTrack yields HOTA 71.9, versus 55.9 for BEDLAM and 54.1 for GTA-Humans, a gap of ~16–18 HOTA points. Across four end-to-end tracking methods (MOTR, MeMOTR, CO-MOT, MOTRv2), M3Act consistently improves HOTA by 5.8–3.1 points respectively, confirming broad applicability. The M3Act-augmented model with extra association (HOTA 72.2) approaches the author-provided MOTRv2* checkpoint (73.4), which was trained on 62.5% more real data, suggesting synthetic data can substitute a large fraction of real annotations. For group activity recognition, M3Act pre-training improves both Composer and ActorTransformer on CAD2 and Volleyball Dataset across all input modalities (2D keypoints, RGB, optical flow), with the largest gains at the person-action level. In the GAG task, MDM+IFormer beats vanilla MDM on group-level FID (3.242 vs. 3.909) and reduces collision frequency by 3.1× (1.157 vs. 3.643), though both remain far from ground truth (FID 0.001, collision 0.037), indicating the generative approach still lags behind the procedural M3Act pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of synthetic human datasets (SURREAL, AGORA, HSPACE, BEDLAM, SynBody) that were designed primarily for single-person pose and shape estimation, by introducing multi-group, multi-person scenarios with group-level annotations and photorealistic multi-view rendering via Unity Engine. For the generative component, it builds on the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) for single-person action-conditioned motion synthesis, extending it with an interaction transformer to handle variable-size groups. The work also departs from prior group activity datasets (CAD, Volleyball, JRDB) by providing full 3D annotations and per-frame individual action labels at scale, and from dyadic motion generation works by formulating the problem for arbitrary group sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M3Act addresses a critical data bottleneck for group activity understanding and multi-person tracking, demonstrating that synthetic data can substitute for 62.5% of real-world training data without performance loss, which has direct cost implications for the field. The introduction of controllable 3D group activity generation (GAG) opens a genuinely new research direction beyond dyadic or fixed-size motion synthesis, with the social-force-based evaluation metrics providing a physically grounded assessment framework. Key limitations include the domain gap between synthetic and real data, the constraint that group complexity is bounded by the heuristics used in activity authoring, and the fact that the generative baseline still underperforms the procedural method. Open questions include whether the GAG formulation scales to more complex social interactions and whether the sim2real gap can be closed with additional asset diversity.
