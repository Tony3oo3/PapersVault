## Problem

Creating realistic, animatable human avatars from a single monocular video is hindered by two intertwined issues: implicit NeRF-based methods are inefficient at representing human surfaces and introduce ambiguous correspondences through inverse skinning, while inaccurate monocular motion estimation produces severe artifacts in dynamic cloth deformations. Existing explicit alternatives (meshes, point clouds) either lack topological flexibility or require millions of points, and prior joint motion-appearance optimization with implicit volumes remains indirect and imprecise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video as input, fits SMPL or SMPL-X parameters to each frame, and samples approximately 200,000 points on the posed mesh surface to construct a UV positional map. The output is a set of animatable 3D Gaussians in canonical space (per-point offsets, colors, and isotropic scales) together with a dynamic appearance network that, given any new pose, reposes the Gaussians via forward skinning and predicts pose-dependent properties for real-time novel-view rendering. Training completes in 0.5–6 hours on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

GaussianAvatar represents the human avatar as 3D Gaussians placed on the SMPL/SMPL-X template surface in canonical space, reposed to arbitrary poses via forward linear-blend skinning—bypassing the inverse skinning and one-to-many ambiguity of NeRF-based canonicalization. A dynamic appearance network (U-Net pose encoder + 8-layer MLP decoder) maps a UV positional map to per-point offsets, colors, and scalar scales, while an optimizable feature tensor captures coarse global appearance and pose-conditioned features decode fine details such as wrinkles. To correct inaccurate monocular pose estimates, the method jointly optimizes pose deltas and appearance in a differentiable forward-skinning pipeline. Training proceeds in two stages: first, appearances are fused into the feature tensor and motions are refined without pose-dependent information; second, pose features are introduced with a regularization loss to prevent overfitting to the limited training pose distribution. The authors use isotropic Gaussians (uniform scale, fixed identity rotation) and fixed opacity to avoid view-biased shape learning and boundary opacity collapse.

## Results

On the People-Snapshot dataset, GaussianAvatar (Baseline+Opt.+Dyn.) achieves PSNR up to 30.98, SSIM up to 0.9791, and LPIPS as low as 0.0140 across four sequences, outperforming HumanNeRF (PSNR 24.46–27.07) and InstantAvatar (PSNR 27.66–29.53). On the more challenging NeuMan dataset, the method reaches PSNR 29.94 / SSIM 0.9795 / LPIPS 0.0124, surpassing HumanNeRF (27.06) and InstantAvatar (28.47). On the self-collected DynVideo dataset with richer cloth dynamics, GaussianAvatar achieves PSNR 28.58 / SSIM 0.9616 / LPIPS 0.0169, substantially exceeding HumanNeRF (24.39) and InstantAvatar (20.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianAvatar outperforms both HumanNeRF and InstantAvatar on all three datasets and all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The margin is most pronounced on the DynVideo dataset, where the full method achieves PSNR 28.58 versus 24.39 for HumanNeRF (+4.19 dB) and 20.31 for InstantAvatar (+8.27 dB), reflecting the advantage of explicit 3D Gaussians in capturing dynamic cloth wrinkles. On People-Snapshot, the improvement over InstantAvatar is more modest (e.g., 30.98 vs. 29.53 PSNR on male-3-casual), while the gap over HumanNeRF remains substantial (30.98 vs. 26.90). On NeuMan, GaussianAvatar leads InstantAvatar by 1.47 dB in PSNR and HumanNeRF by 2.88 dB. The ablation rows in Table 1 show that the motion optimization component contributes the largest single gain (e.g., +3.27 dB PSNR on male-3-casual over the baseline), while dynamic appearance modeling adds a smaller but consistent improvement, particularly on datasets with greater pose variation (NeuMan, DynVideo).

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianAvatar builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its explicit surface representation, departing from the dominant NeRF-based line of work (HumanNeRF, InstantAvatar, NeuMan) that relies on implicit volumes and inverse skinning. The key conceptual shift is replacing inverse skinning with forward skinning via SMPL/SMPL-X, which eliminates the one-to-many correspondence ambiguity during canonicalization. The dynamic appearance network repurposes the feature-tensor architecture from point-based human modeling [28, 46], but redirects it from shape decoupling to coarse global appearance capture, with pose-dependent features layered on top for fine-grained details like wrinkles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 3D Gaussians with forward skinning can match or exceed implicit NeRF methods in both quality and speed for full-body avatars, this paper accelerates the field's transition toward explicit representations for human modeling. The joint motion-and-appearance optimization in forward skinning space offers a practical remedy for the persistent monocular pose-estimation error problem, and the newly introduced DynVideo dataset fills a gap in evaluating dynamic cloth deformations. Limitations remain for loose garments and segmentation errors, and the method still requires per-subject optimization (0.5–6 hours), leaving real-time generalizable avatar creation as an open challenge.
