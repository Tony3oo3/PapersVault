## Problem

Most video captioning models are designed for short clips of 5–15 seconds and cannot handle the hierarchical structure of real-world videos that span minutes to hours. No existing method can efficiently generate captions at multiple temporal granularities (atomic actions, intermediate steps, long-term goals) from a single long video input, and manually annotated hierarchical captioning data for hour-long videos is scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241184/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a long untrimmed video as input, divided into clips of 4 RGB frames at 224×224, with input durations ranging from 1 second to 2 hours (three orders of magnitude). For clip-level captions, dense spatiotemporal features are used; for higher hierarchies, only sparsely sampled CLS features are extracted to keep computation tractable. The output is a three-tier hierarchy of free-form text: short clip captions (atomic actions/objects), medium-length segment descriptions (intermediate activity steps spanning a few minutes), and a single long-range video summary capturing overall goals and intent.

## Method

Video ReCap employs a recursive video-language architecture with three components: a TimeSformer video encoder, a Video-Language Alignment module (frozen DistilBERT with trainable cross-attention layers that compresses thousands of video/text features into a fixed set of 256 joint embeddings), and a GPT2 text decoder with inserted cross-attention blocks. The key mechanism is recursion: captions generated at hierarchy level ℓ−1 are concatenated with sparsely sampled video features as input to generate captions at level ℓ, so the model progressively abstracts from atomic actions to video-level summaries. Training follows a hierarchical curriculum (clip captions → segment descriptions → video summaries) inspired by human action perception, and LLM-based pseudo-annotations (generated by a fine-tuned FLAN-T5-Large teacher) augment the scarce manually annotated segment and summary data, yielding a 5.14% CIDEr gain on segment descriptions.

## Results

On Ego4D-HCap, Video ReCap achieves 98.35 CIDEr on clip captions (vs. 88.56 for LaViLa), 46.88 CIDEr on segment descriptions (vs. 39.13 for LaViLa+FLAN-T5), and 29.34 CIDEr on video summaries (vs. 20.12 for LaViLa+FLAN-T5), all with 339M trainable parameters. On EgoSchema long-range VideoQA, Video ReCap with hierarchical captions achieves 50.23% accuracy, outperforming the previous best method InternVideo (32.1%) by 18.13% and Ego4D-pretrained EgoVLP (34.86%) by 15.37%. The unified variant Video ReCap-U (113M params) remains competitive, achieving 31.06 CIDEr on video summaries while using ~3x fewer parameters than the standard variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video ReCap outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. On Ego4D-HCap clip captioning, it beats LaViLa (the strongest video-based baseline) by 9.79 CIDEr (98.35 vs. 88.56) using the same encoder and decoder. For segment descriptions, Video ReCap surpasses the best text-based baseline LaViLa+FLAN-T5 by 7.75 CIDEr (46.88 vs. 39.13) despite using 247M fewer parameters, and outperforms end-to-end LaViLa by 22.25 CIDEr (46.88 vs. 24.63). On video summaries, the gap widens further: Video ReCap leads LaViLa+FLAN-T5 by 9.22 CIDEr (29.34 vs. 20.12) and LaViLa by 22.80 CIDEr (29.34 vs. 6.54), demonstrating that the recursive hierarchical design is critical for long-range understanding. Zero-shot baselines (BLIP2, BLIP2+GPT3.5, LaViLa+GPT3.5) are far behind all finetuned methods, with CIDEr scores below 12 on segment/summary tasks. On EgoSchema, Video ReCap with hierarchical captions (50.23%) beats InternVideo (32.1%) by 18.13%, EgoVLP (34.86%) by 15.37%, and LaViLa+GPT3.5 (44.27%) by 5.96%, confirming that multi-level captions provide substantially richer context for long-form video reasoning than short-clip captions alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video captioning line of work (LaViLa, BLIP2, Vid2Seq) from short-clip, single-granularity descriptions to a recursive, multi-hierarchy framework that processes videos spanning three orders of magnitude in duration (1 second to 2 hours). The recursive design—feeding lower-level captions as text input to generate higher-level descriptions—draws on the video-language alignment paradigm of Flamingo and LaViLa but introduces a sequential, hierarchy-conditional generation pipeline not present in prior captioning models. The curriculum learning strategy is explicitly motivated by psychological theories of hierarchical action perception (Barker & Wright, Cooper & Shallice), and the LLM-based pseudo-annotation for data augmentation follows the broader trend of using large language models as synthetic data generators, but applies it specifically to the multi-granularity video captioning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ego4D-HCap dataset (5.27M clip captions, 17.5K segment descriptions, 8.3K video summaries) is a significant new benchmark that fills a gap: no prior dataset offered three-level hierarchical captions for hour-long untrimmed videos. The recursive architecture provides a practical recipe for handling extremely long video inputs without prohibitive compute, and the finding that a 113M-parameter unified variant outperforms the 258M-parameter LaViLa by 20%+ on segment/summary generation suggests that hierarchical inductive bias matters more than raw model capacity for long-range video understanding. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond egocentric video, whether real-time or interactive caption generation is feasible, and whether the EgoSchema gains hold under strictly matched pretraining conditions.
