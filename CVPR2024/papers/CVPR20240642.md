## Problem

Most large multimodal models are constrained to low input resolutions (typically 448×448), limiting their ability to read dense text and resolve small or closely grouped objects. Additionally, the short, simple captions in widely used pretraining datasets (LAION, COYO, CC3M) provide insufficient detail to help models effectively leverage higher resolutions, creating a mismatch between visual fidelity and textual supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240642/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (up to 1344×896 pixels) and a text instruction or question. The image is partitioned into 448×448 patches via a sliding window (up to 6 patches, constrained by the LLM's input length), each processed independently alongside a globally resized version. Output is a free-form text caption or answer, with the model supporting both brief and detailed captioning modes via different instruction prompts.

## Method

Monkey divides high-resolution input images into 448×448 patches using a sliding window, processing each patch through a shared frozen ViT-BigG encoder augmented with LoRA adapters (rank 16 for attention, 32 for MLP) to capture region-specific details without retraining the encoder from scratch. A cross-attention visual resampler with 256 learnable queries per patch compresses local and global features into a fixed-length representation fed to a 7.7B-parameter LLM (from Qwen-VL). To address the caption-quality gap, the paper introduces a multi-level description generation pipeline that fuses outputs from BLIP2 (global captions), GRIT (region-level descriptions), PPOCR (text extraction), SAM (object segmentation), and ChatGPT (final contextual synthesis), producing rich training annotations that explicitly link scene context to individual objects and text. The full 9.8B-parameter model is instruction-tuned on 1.44M examples spanning 18 public datasets covering captioning, general VQA, scene-text VQA, and document VQA.

## Results

On Image Captioning, Monkey achieves 86.1 on Flickr30K and 93.2 on TextCaps, surpassing Qwen-VL (85.8 / 65.1) and mPLUG-Owl2 (85.1 / –). On General VQA, Monkey scores 80.3 (VQAv2), 61.3 (OKVQA), 60.7 (GQA), 69.4 (ScienceQA), and 61.2 (VizWiz), outperforming the nearest competitor by an average of 1.62%. On Scene Text-centric VQA, Monkey leads with 67.6 (TextVQA), 62.6 (AI2D), 67.7 (STVQA), and 82.6 (ESTVQA), beating Qwen-VL by an average of 4.37%. On Document-oriented VQA, Monkey achieves 40.6 on DeepForm and 32.8 on KLC versus Qwen-VL's 4.1 and 15.9, a 9.77% average improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Monkey outperforms Qwen-VL (its base model) on nearly every benchmark: TextCaps (93.2 vs 65.1), VizWiz (61.2 vs 35.2), OKVQA (61.3 vs 58.6), and DeepForm (40.6 vs 4.1), with the largest gaps on text-heavy and document tasks. Against mPLUG-Owl2, Monkey leads on VQAv2 (80.3 vs 79.4), GQA (60.7 vs 56.1), and VizWiz (61.2 vs 54.5). Compared to LLaVA1.5, Monkey wins on VQAv2 (80.3 vs 78.5), ScienceQA (69.4 vs 66.8), and VizWiz (61.2 vs 50.0), but trails slightly on GQA (60.7 vs 62.0). Against the much larger Flamingo-80B and PaLM-E-12B, Monkey achieves comparable or superior scores on all overlapping benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2 80.3 vs 56.3 and 77.7; OKVQA 61.3 vs 50.6 and 60.1). On Scene Text-centric VQA, Monkey leads all baselines by an average of 4.37%, with the most notable margin over Qwen-VL on STVQA (67.7 vs 59.1) and ESTVQA (82.6 vs 77.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

Monkey builds directly on Qwen-VL's ViT-BigG encoder and LLM, but departs from the curriculum-learning paradigm (Qwen-VL, PaLI-3, PaLI-X) that requires expensive multi-stage pretraining to increase resolution. Instead of positional interpolation or progressive resolution scaling, it introduces a sliding-window patching strategy with per-patch LoRA adapters and a shared visual resampler, preserving the encoder's original 448×448 training distribution. The multi-level description generation pipeline is a novel orchestration of existing tools (BLIP2, PPOCR, GRIT, SAM, ChatGPT) to address the well-known gap between high-resolution inputs and the short, shallow captions in datasets like LAION and CC3M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, resource-efficient recipe for pushing LMM input resolution to 1344×896 without full pretraining, which is directly applicable to any framework using a frozen ViT encoder. The multi-level description generation pipeline is a reusable data-engineering technique that could benefit other LMM training efforts. Key limitations include the hard cap of six patches (constrained by LLM context length) and the description generator's reliance on BLIP2's world knowledge, which limits geographic or domain-specific identification. Open questions include whether the patching strategy can be extended beyond six patches with longer-context LLMs and whether the description pipeline can be made more autonomous.
