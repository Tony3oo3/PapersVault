## Problem

Neural character models based on NeRFs reconstruct geometry and texture from video but lack explicit shadows and shading, causing severe artifacts in novel poses and preventing relighting in new environments. Cast shadows are especially challenging because they are global effects requiring secondary ray tracing, which introduces quadratic computational complexity in the number of volumetric samples per pixel. This paper addresses the gap by introducing an efficient, differentiable shadow model that enables hard shadow casting and relighting for dynamic neural characters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241901/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse RGB images (W×H×3) and corresponding skeleton poses (J joints, each a 4×4 local-to-world matrix) as input, requiring no reference mesh such as SMPL. It produces a disentangled 3D neural character with separated albedo, surface normals, and depth, plus a closed-form shadow map per pixel. At inference, the model can render novel poses with accurate cast shadows or relight the character using HDRi environment maps by importance-sampling up to 64 secondary rays per pixel, all without user-provided light direction.

## Method

The core contribution is a sum-of-anisotropic-Gaussians density model rigidly attached to the skeleton, whose parameters are jointly optimized with the DANBO NeRF backbone via L2 density matching (with NeRF gradients detached). The key insight is that the density of an anisotropic Gaussian along any ray reduces to a 1D Gaussian whose cumulative integral has a closed-form expression involving the error function, eliminating all sampling steps and avoiding the affine projection approximation and back-to-front sorting required by Gaussian Splatting. A deferred shading pipeline first renders albedo, depth, and normals in one pass, then casts exactly one secondary ray per pixel toward the light source, making shadow cost independent of the NeRF's sample count and reducing it from quadratic to linear. Training proceeds in three stages: gray-silhouette shape learning, Gaussian density fitting, and full RGB optimization that simultaneously recovers light direction and albedo. The result is a Lambertian shading model with analytic shadow attenuation that supports HDRi relighting at minimal overhead (~2% of render time for a single light source).

## Results

On novel-pose synthesis across three datasets (synthetic, Real S1, Real S2), GSC achieves an average PSNR of 19.30, outperforming DANBO (17.26) and NPC (17.12) by roughly 2 dB. On the harder subset of frames with strong self-casting shadows, the gap widens: GSC reaches 18.81 PSNR vs. 16.48 (DANBO) and 16.40 (NPC). GSC also leads on SSIM (0.666 vs. 0.648/0.641) but trails slightly on LPIPS (0.291 vs. 0.283), which the authors attribute to perceptual metrics normalizing contrast and thus undervaluing correct shadow rendering. The Gaussian shadow casting adds only 0.3 s per frame (~2% overhead) compared to 25% for NeRF-based secondary ray casting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DANBO and NPC (both template-free NeRF-based body models without explicit lighting), GSC consistently wins on PSNR by ~2 dB and on SSIM by ~0.02 across all three datasets, with the largest gains on the synthetic sequence (22.04 vs. 17.52/17.57 PSNR) where hard shadows are well-defined. GSC loses marginally on LPIPS (0.291 vs. 0.283), which the authors explain as an artifact of perceptual metrics normalizing brightness/contrast and thus penalizing the correct dark shadow regions. Against Relighting4D, the comparison is qualitative: R4D's NeuralBody front-end produces dark floaters to approximate hard shadows, which corrupts its downstream relighting and albedo estimation, whereas GSC cleanly separates albedo from shadow. Against RANA, GSC achieves fewer lighting artifacts in the estimated albedo (e.g., on the back of the left leg) and recovers the light direction with only 9.9° error even from monocular input. In terms of efficiency, GSC's analytic Gaussian integration adds only 0.3 s per frame (~2% overhead), while NeRF-based secondary ray casting (NeRFSC) increases render time by 25% per light source, making HDRi relighting with 64 rays impractical for NeRFSC but feasible for GSC (41.2 s vs. infeasible).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DANBO as its volumetric neural field backbone and extends the Gaussian density models of Rhodin et al. from isotropic to anisotropic Gaussians, deriving a closed-form analytic integral for ray occlusion that eliminates the sampling step required by NeRF-based secondary ray tracing. It departs from Gaussian Splatting by avoiding the affine projection approximation and back-to-front sorting, and from prior relighting methods (RANA, Relighting4D, Xu et al.) by explicitly modeling hard cast shadows in novel poses rather than relying on spherical harmonics, soft approximate shadows, or fixed-illumination assumptions. The deferred shading formulation is borrowed from graphics pipelines and adapted to the NeRF setting to decouple shadow computation from the number of volumetric samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables realistic relighting of neural characters in novel environments with minimal computational overhead (2% of render time for shadow casting), making it practical for content creation pipelines where characters must be composited into new scenes. Key limitations include the smooth Gaussian approximation lacking high-frequency geometric detail (fingers, nose) and a slight degradation in reconstruction fidelity due to the albedo/shading separation constraint. Open questions include integrating mid-scale effects via screen-space ambient occlusion, extending beyond Lambertian shading to more complex BRDFs, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-light-source or area-light scenarios beyond the single directional light tested here.
