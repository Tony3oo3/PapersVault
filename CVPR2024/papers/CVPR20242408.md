## Problem

Existing medical image segmentation solutions are fragmented: automatic and interactive setups require separate models, architectures, and training sessions, duplicating both parameters and compute. Moreover, leading 3D volume-wise methods suffer from slow inference and high memory consumption, while current interactive approaches are restricted to single-foreground-class binary segmentation. No prior framework unifies both tasks in one model trained in a single session while remaining efficient enough for clinical deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D CT or MRI volumes (e.g., 512×512 per slice) by splitting them into 2D slices along the z-axis, processed sequentially by a 2D backbone (Swin-B) whose features are computed once and reused across all refinement rounds. In interactive mode, user input takes the form of (position, class label, slice index) click tuples, with up to 20 clicks per class; inference then propagates bidirectionally from the clicked slice through the entire volume. Output is a full 3D segmentation mask (one binary score map per class across all slices) plus per-slice class confidence scores, with the final interactive prediction selected by taking the maximum across all refinement rounds.

## Method

S2VNet reformulates volumetric segmentation as iterative k-means clustering over 2D slices: a set of learnable cluster centers (initialized randomly or from user clicks) are refined per slice via k-means cross-attention in a Mask2Former-style decoder, then propagated to the next slice as initialization, bridging remote slices without any 3D convolution. Only centers matched to foreground classes (via bipartite matching) are forwarded, preventing background contamination. For interactive segmentation, cluster centers are initialized by sampling backbone features at click positions and projecting them through an FFN, requiring no architectural change from the automatic pipeline; multiple classes can be handled simultaneously, each initializing its own center. An adaptive weighted-sum strategy (decay β=0.8) fuses features from successive refinement rounds, and a recurrent centroid aggregation module (self-attention over concatenated historic and current centroids) prevents drift from outliers while retaining long-range structural cues at negligible extra cost.

## Results

On the WORD benchmark (16 abdominal organs), S2VNet achieves 87.36% DSC and 4.64 HD95 under the automatic setup, surpassing the previous best 3D method SwinMM (86.18% DSC, 9.35 HD95) by 1.18% DSC and nearly halving the HD95. Under the interactive setup, S2VNet reaches 91.41% DSC and 3.28 HD95, a 4.05% DSC gain over its own automatic mode. On BTCV, S2VNet attains 83.81% DSC (automatic) and 86.11% DSC (interactive), outperforming the prior SOTA nnFormer by 2.19% DSC. On AMOS (CT+MRI), S2VNet scores 86.22% DSC / 77.23% NSD (automatic) and 88.75% DSC / 80.94% NSD (interactive), exceeding 3D-UX-Net by 0.52% DSC and 6.41% NSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2VNet outperforms all 13 baselines across all three datasets and both automatic/interactive setups. Against the strongest 3D automatic baselines, S2VNet beats SwinMM [56] by 1.18% DSC and 4.71 HD95 on WORD, nnFormer [58] by 2.19% DSC on BTCV, and 3D-UX-Net [119] by 0.52% DSC / 6.41% NSD on AMOS. The HD95 advantage is particularly pronounced (4.64 vs. 9.35 for SwinMM on WORD), reflecting the slice-to-volume propagation's ability to enforce cross-slice coherence that 3D sliding-window methods struggle with. For interactive segmentation, S2VNet surpasses iSegFormer [81] and Mem3D [4] by large margins (e.g., 83.91% vs. 69.52% DSC on the 'Duo' organ in WORD), and also outperforms the adapted automatic baseline SwinMM† [56] (e.g., 96.22% vs. 95.11% on Kid R). Notably, S2VNet handles all 16 WORD organs in a single unified model, whereas prior interactive methods require a separate binary model per class, and the unified training uses only half the parameters and training time of task-specific models.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2VNet builds directly on the k-means cross-attention mechanism from the k-means Mask Transformer (ECCV 2022) and the Mask2Former architecture (CVPR 2022), extending their pixel-level clustering to a volumetric setting by propagating cluster centers sequentially across slices. Conceptually, it departs from both slice-wise methods (which ignore 3D structure) and volume-wise 3D methods (which are slow and memory-intensive) by treating the z-axis as a temporal dimension over which clustering state evolves, drawing an analogy to object association in video segmentation. The interactive segmentation component reinterprets user clicks not as additional input channels (as in prior IMIS work) but as initialization signals for cluster centroids, enabling multi-class interaction within the same architecture used for automatic segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of automatic and interactive segmentation into a single model with one training session addresses a practical inefficiency in clinical deployment, halving both parameters and training time while enabling a natural coarse-to-fine diagnostic workflow. The 15× inference speedup and 48% memory reduction over 3D baselines make the approach viable on hospital hardware with limited compute, and the multi-class interactive capability fills a gap where prior IMIS methods were restricted to single-foreground-class binary segmentation. Open questions include whether the slice-wise consistency assumption holds for highly anisotropic or pathologically irregular structures, and whether the propagation mechanism degrades over very long volumes where cumulative centroid drift may accumulate despite the recurrent aggregation module.
