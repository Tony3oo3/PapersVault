## Problem

VQA datasets are inherently small (e.g., KoNViD-1k has only 1,200 videos) because MOS annotation is expensive, limiting the effectiveness of data-driven methods. Existing approaches either fine-tune a single content-based pretrained model or require additional labeled data, failing to exploit the diverse quality-relevant knowledge embedded in the hundreds of pretrained models available across different pre-text tasks (action recognition, image-text alignment, masked autoencoding, etc.).

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip as input, uniformly samples 16–32 frames along the temporal axis, and center-crops each frame to 224×224. At inference time, a 4×5 multi-view strategy is used: 4 temporal clips are sampled, each with 5 spatial crops (four corners + center), and the final MOS score is the average of all 20 predictions. The output is a single scalar quality score on the 1.0–5.0 MOS scale.

## Method

PTM-VQA uses N frozen pretrained models (e.g., ConvNeXt, CLIP, ir-CSN-152, TimeSformer) as feature extractors, applying a lightweight learnable transformation (two FC layers with normalization and GELU) to align each model's output to a shared 128-dim space. Features are integrated via a weighted average where weights are inversely proportional to each model's Davies-Bouldin Index (DBI) score, so models whose frozen features cluster better by MOS contribute more. Training uses an ICID loss: an intra-consistency term (cosine similarity) pulls features from different models of the same video into a unified quality-aware space, while an inter-divisibility term (triplet loss on cluster centroids defined by MOS intervals) separates features of videos with different quality levels. This avoids expensive fine-tuning of large pretrained models—only ~0.3–0.9M learnable parameters are trained, completing in ~2 hours on a single GPU.

## Results

PTM-VQA achieves the best weighted-average Mean score of 0.8523 across KoNViD-1k, LIVE-VQC, and YouTube-UGC, outperforming the previous best methods VQT (0.8475) and BVQA* (0.8351). On KoNViD-1k, it reaches PLCC 0.8718 / SRCC 0.8568 (Mean 0.8643), and on YouTube-UGC it reaches PLCC 0.8570 / SRCC 0.8578 (Mean 0.8574). On the largest LSVQ dataset, PTM-VQA-1k attains PLCC 0.8536 / SRCC 0.8530, surpassing PVQ (0.828/0.827). The entire training takes only ~2 hours on a single GPU with fewer than 1M learnable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTM-VQA achieves the best weighted-average Mean (0.8523) across all three main datasets, surpassing VQT (0.8475) and BVQA* (0.8351). On KoNViD-1k, it edges out VQT (0.8633→0.8643 Mean) and substantially outperforms BVQA* (0.8348) and STDAM* (0.8431), both of which rely on extra IQA training data. On YouTube-UGC, PTM-VQA clearly wins over BVQA* (0.8253→0.8574 Mean, +3.2%) and VQT (0.8436→0.8574). However, on LIVE-VQC, PTM-VQA (0.8154 Mean) trails BVQA* (0.8413 Mean) by ~2.6%, the one dataset where the extra-data advantage of BVQA* is most pronounced. On the large-scale LSVQ benchmark, PTM-VQA-1k (PLCC 0.8536) outperforms PVQ with patch (0.828) by 2.6% PLCC, and cross-database transfer from LSVQ to KoNViD-1k yields PLCC 0.8303, still beating all baselines without fine-tuning. Notably, PTM-VQA uses no extra labeled data and only ~0.3–0.9M learnable parameters, yet matches or exceeds methods that fine-tune full networks on additional IQA datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VQA work that leverages pretrained models (VSFA, RIRNet, BVQA) by taking a fundamentally different approach: instead of fine-tuning a single pretrained model on quality labels, it keeps multiple diverse pretrained models frozen and uses them purely as feature extractors. The ICID loss draws on metric learning (triplet loss variants from RankIQA, UNIQUE, FPR) but repurposes it to align features across heterogeneous models and enforce quality-aware cluster separation. The DBI-based model selection scheme is a practical departure from prior work like Ada-DQA, which also used diverse pretrained models but at much higher training cost, and from the common practice of trial-and-error model selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frozen-weight, multi-model approach offers a practical recipe for VQA practitioners facing the chronic data scarcity problem (KoNViD-1k has only 1,200 videos), reducing training to ~2 hours on a single GPU while achieving SOTA results. The DBI-based selection provides a scalable criterion as the pretrained model zoo grows (700+ in Timm alone), addressing a real practical bottleneck. Open questions include whether the pseudo-cluster granularity (K=6) generalizes across MOS distributions, how the method performs when pretrained models are updated or new architectures emerge, and whether the approach transfers to other quality assessment domains (audio, 360° video) without re-deriving the ICID constraints.
