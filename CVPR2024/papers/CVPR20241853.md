## Problem

Existing MOT datasets exhibit a pronounced long-tail distribution in trajectory lengths across pedestrian identities, where a few head-class pedestrians occupy many frames while most tail-class pedestrians appear only briefly. This imbalance causes the Re-ID branch to overfit head-class appearance features, and the standard softmax loss further amplifies the suppression of tail classes, degrading tracking accuracy for short-lived tracks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frame sequences with per-frame bounding box annotations from MOTChallenge datasets, and uses the camera motion type (stationary vs. dynamic) to route each sequence to the appropriate augmentation pipeline. SAM segmentation and Kalman filter prediction operate on the annotated bounding boxes to generate synthetic trajectory extensions, while Stable Diffusion processes inpainted backgrounds for dynamic-camera sequences. The final output is standard MOT output—per-frame bounding boxes, persistent track identities, and Re-ID appearance feature vectors—produced by the modified FairMOT or CSTrack backbone.

## Method

The approach combines two data augmentation strategies with a modified loss module. SVA targets stationary-camera sequences by extending tail-class pedestrian trajectories: backtracking continuation reverses the original track and overlays it on subsequent frames, while prediction continuation uses a Kalman filter to extrapolate future positions and composites the pedestrian onto preceding frames. DVA targets dynamic-camera sequences by segmenting pedestrians with SAM, inpainting the exposed background via Navier-Stokes flow, applying Stable Diffusion to restyle the background, and merging the original pedestrian back, with a per-epoch probability threshold balancing original and augmented samples. The Group Softmax module partitions all M pedestrian classes into K ordered groups by training-sample count (group boundaries set at j/K × max(N(i))), then computes softmax and cross-entropy independently within each group so that tail classes compete only against similarly-sized classes rather than being dominated by head-class logits.

## Results

On the MOT20 test set (crowded scenes), FairMOT+Ours achieves 65.9 MOTA, 70.3 IDF1, and 54.4 HOTA, improving over the FairMOT baseline by +9.1 MOTA, +5.3 IDF1, and +2.1 HOTA. On MOT15, gains are also substantial: FairMOT+Ours reaches 56.7 MOTA (+3.6) and 61.5 IDF1 (+2.1), while CSTrack+Ours reaches 55.0 MOTA (+5.2) and 60.2 IDF1 (+3.5). On the MOT20 test set against other SOTA Joint Detection and Tracking methods, FairMOT+Ours achieves the best MOTA (67.8), IDF1 (70.7), and HOTA (55.4). Notably, FairMOT+Ours trained on only MOT20 data (9K images, 2.2K identities) outperforms the baseline trained on mixed data (77K images, 10.4K identities) by +4.1 MOTA and +3.0 IDF1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FairMOT (IJCV 2021), the proposed method consistently improves tracking performance, with the largest gains on MOT20 (+9.1 MOTA, +5.3 IDF1) and MOT15 (+3.6 MOTA, +2.1 IDF1), while gains on MOT16/MOT17 are modest (+0.1 to +0.5 HOTA). Against CSTrack (TIP 2022), improvements are also consistent, with MOT15 showing the strongest lift (+5.2 MOTA, +3.5 IDF1, +25.8% MT). On the MOT20 test set, FairMOT+Ours (67.8 MOTA, 70.7 IDF1, 55.4 HOTA) outperforms RelationTrack (TMM 2022: 67.2 MOTA, 70.5 IDF1, 55.1 HOTA) and MTrack (CVPR 2022: 63.5 MOTA, 69.2 IDF1), establishing the best overall performance among Joint Detection and Tracking methods. In the long-tail solution comparison (Table 1), the proposed method (69.3 MOTA, 73.4 IDF1 on all classes) surpasses Class-Balanced Loss (CVPR 2019: 67.9 MOTA, 72.0 IDF1), Focal Loss, Triplet Loss, and Logit Adjustment, while uniquely improving both head and tail class performance simultaneously. A key data-efficiency finding is that FairMOT+Ours trained solely on MOT20 data (65.9 MOTA, 70.3 IDF1) exceeds the baseline trained on seven mixed datasets (61.8 MOTA, 67.3 IDF1) by +4.1 MOTA and +3.0 IDF1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the long-tail distribution toolkit from image classification and detection (class re-balancing, data augmentation, classifier redesign) into the multi-object tracking domain for the first time, identifying trajectory-length imbalance across pedestrian identities as the underlying cause. The Group Softmax module is directly inspired by balanced group softmax for long-tail object detection [29], while the data augmentation pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf tools (SAM for segmentation, Navier-Stokes inpainting, Stable Diffusion for background style transfer, Kalman filtering for trajectory prediction) in a MOT-specific combination. The method is applied within the Joint Detection and Tracking framework, building on FairMOT and CSTrack as baselines, and is positioned as complementary to existing tracking architecture improvements rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core contribution is diagnostic: it shows that trajectory-length imbalance in MOT datasets is a previously unacknowledged source of performance degradation, particularly for tail-class pedestrians, and that targeted augmentation plus a grouped softmax can recover meaningful accuracy without architectural changes. The data-efficiency finding—that a model trained solely on MOT20 with their augmentation outperforms one trained on seven mixed datasets—is practically relevant for practitioners with limited data. However, the method's complexity (per-frame SAM segmentation, inpainting, and diffusion) raises scalability concerns, and the modest gains on MOT16/MOT17 (≤0.5% HOTA) suggest the long-tail effect is most pronounced in crowded or short-trajectory scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-pedestrian MOT (vehicles, drones) and whether lighter-weight augmentation could capture most of the benefit.
