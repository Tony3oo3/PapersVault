## Problem

Single-view hand-held object reconstruction methods typically require 3D ground-truth meshes for supervision, which are expensive and impractical to collect at scale. Readily available hand-object videos offer a natural training source, but the object observations are heavily occluded by the holding hand, making them unreliable as multi-view geometric supervision. The paper addresses how to exploit these occluded 2D video observations to reconstruct complete, textured object meshes without any 3D ground truth.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB hand-object image along with its camera pose, an offline-estimated object segmentation mask, and MANO hand pose parameters (obtained from a separate FrankMocap system) as input. It outputs a complete textured 3D mesh of the object (including hand-occluded and self-occluded regions) via geometric volume rendering, as well as photorealistic novel view renderings. Notably, no 3D ground-truth mesh is required at any stage; all supervision is 2D (rendered or real-world images and masks).

## Method

MOHO employs a two-stage synthetic-to-real training framework. In the synthetic pre-training stage, it is trained on the newly rendered SOMVideo dataset (141,550 scenes, 2,772 objects from ShapeNet) where occlusion-free multi-view supervisions are available, and an auxiliary 2D amodal mask recovery head learns to predict hand-covered object regions. In the real-world finetuning stage, the frozen 2D head's predictions are used to construct an amodal-mask-weighted geometric supervision (Eq. 2) that down-weights unreliable occluded regions in the loss. To combat object self-occlusion throughout both stages, MOHO conditions a NeuS-style geometric volume rendering network on domain-consistent occlusion-aware features: DINO patch features reduced to 3 PCA components for generic semantic cues, and explicit geodesic-distance embeddings to the 6 nearest MANO hand joints for hand-articulated geometric priors. These features, concatenated with ResNet-34 color features, condition separate SDF and RGB-density MLPs that are rendered via the unbiased occlusion-aware volume rendering formulation from NeuS.

## Results

On HO3D, MOHO achieves F-5=0.31, F-10=0.50, and CD=0.91 mm, outperforming the best 3D-supervised method IHOI (F-5=0.28, CD=1.53) and the best 2D-supervised method SSDNeRF (F-5=0.25, CD=2.60). On the larger DexYCB dataset, MOHO reaches F-5=0.60, F-10=0.81, and CD=0.15 mm, surpassing the top 3D-supervised gSDF (F-5=0.44, CD=0.34) by 36.4% on F-5 and the top 2D-supervised SSDNeRF (F-5=0.27, CD=0.58) by 122.2% on F-5. For novel view synthesis, MOHO achieves PSNR=35.80 and LPIPS=0.013 on DexYCB, leading PixelNeRF (PSNR=32.77) and SSDNeRF (PSNR=32.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOHO outperforms all 3D-supervised baselines (HO, GF, IHOI, AlignSDF, gSDF) on both HO3D and DexYCB despite using only 2D video supervision. On HO3D, MOHO reduces Chamfer Distance by 40.5% compared to IHOI (0.91 vs 1.53 mm) and by 78% compared to HO (0.91 vs 4.19 mm). On DexYCB, MOHO leads gSDF by 36.4% on F-5 (0.60 vs 0.44) and by 56% on CD (0.15 vs 0.34 mm). Against 2D-supervised NeRF-based baselines, MOHO exceeds PixelNeRF by 82.3% on F-5 on HO3D (0.31 vs 0.17) and by 122.2% on DexYCB (0.60 vs 0.25). The gap over SSDNeRF is 24% on HO3D F-5 and 122.2% on DexYCB F-5, demonstrating that the synthetic-to-real framework and occlusion-aware features are critical for handling heavy hand-induced occlusion that causes prior 2D-supervised methods to produce incomplete, patchy reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOHO builds on the NeuS volume rendering framework [57] for SDF-based geometric reconstruction and extends it with a color field for texture recovery. It departs from the dominant line of single-view hand-held object reconstruction (IHOI, gSDF, AlignSDF, HO, GF) that requires 3D ground-truth meshes for supervision, and from 2D-supervised NeRF-based methods (PixelNeRF, SSDNeRF) that assume occlusion-free conditions. The key conceptual departure is a synthetic-to-real training paradigm that pairs occlusion-free synthetic supervision (SOMVideo) with amodal-mask-weighted real-world supervision, combined with domain-consistent occlusion-aware features (DINO semantic cues and local hand-articulated geometric embeddings) to handle both hand-induced and self-occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 3D ground-truth meshes and instead leveraging readily available hand-object videos, MOHO makes single-view hand-held object reconstruction far more practical for real-world deployment. The synthetic-to-real framework with occlusion-aware features is a transferable recipe that also benefits other 2D-supervised baselines (e.g., PixelNeRF), suggesting broader applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond hand-held objects to other occluded interaction scenarios, how sensitive it is to hand pose estimation errors, and whether the reliance on offline segmentation and pose estimation limits real-time applicability. The authors note future work toward robotic grasping in human-robot interaction scenes.
