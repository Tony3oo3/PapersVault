## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models are highly sensitive to prompt wording, and while users can manually assign per-word weights and injection time steps to improve generation quality, this process is labor-intensive and requires expert trial-and-error. Existing learning-based prompt optimizers (e.g., Promptist) can rephrase or append modifiers but offer no mechanism to control *when* during the denoising process a modifier takes effect or *how strongly* it influences the output, leaving fine-grained prompt engineering to manual heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short plain-text prompt (the primary description before the first comma, e.g., 'a red horse on the yellow grass') and produces a Dynamic Fine-control Prompt (DF-Prompt) in which each appended modifier token is paired with a normalized effect range [b→e] (specifying the fraction of denoising timesteps during which the token is active, with 1≥b≥e≥0) and a scalar weight (e.g., 0.5–1.5) that scales its embedding. The DF-Prompt is then consumed by a modified Stable Diffusion v1.4 text encoder that supports per-token weighting and time-gated injection, ultimately yielding a 512×512 RGB image.

## Method

PAE (Prompt Auto-Editing) is a two-stage pipeline built on GPT-2. Stage 1 fine-tunes the language model via teacher forcing on ~450K prompt-image pairs filtered by a confidence score that requires both aesthetic improvement and CLIP-score preservation after modifier addition, yielding a refined-prompt model E_ReP. Stage 2 initializes a policy model E_DFP from E_ReP with two additional linear heads (predicting effect range and weight per token) and trains it with PPO against the diffusion model as the environment; the discrete action space comprises word tokens, three time-range options {1→0, 1→0.5, 0.5→0}, and five weight values {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5}. The reward combines a CLIP-score penalty (guarding semantic drift), a PickScore penalty (human preference), and a weighted aesthetic-score gain, with a KL regularization term anchoring the policy to E_ReP. The key architectural change to the diffusion model is a modified text encoder that applies per-token weight scaling and time-gated prompt injection during the iterative denoising loop, enabling the learned triples to exert precise, step-dependent control over generation.

## Results

On the in-domain Lexica.art dataset, PAE achieves a PickScore of 73.9%, CLIP Score of 0.26, and Aesthetic Score of 6.12, surpassing human-written prompts (72.5% PickScore, 6.07 Aes) and the Promptist baseline (68.4% PickScore, 6.11 Aes). On the out-of-domain COCO dataset, PAE maintains competitive performance with 53.8% PickScore and 6.09 Aes Score, slightly outperforming Promptist (53.4%, 6.15). PAE also achieves a lower CMMD score (1.125) than Promptist (1.147), indicating more realistic generated images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Promptist (Hao et al., 2022), PAE wins on PickScore across all three datasets (73.9% vs. 68.4% on Lexica.art, 64.4% vs. 62.3% on DiffusionDB, 53.8% vs. 53.4% on COCO) and on Aesthetic Score for the two in-domain datasets (6.12 vs. 6.11, 6.07 vs. 6.06), though it trails slightly on COCO Aes Score (6.09 vs. 6.15). PAE also achieves a lower CMMD (1.125 vs. 1.147), confirming better image realism. Against the pre-trained GPT-2 baseline, PAE dominates on every metric and dataset, with PickScore gaps of 25–26 percentage points. Compared to human-written prompts, PAE edges out humans on Lexica.art (73.9% vs. 72.5% PickScore, 6.12 vs. 6.07 Aes) but falls short on DiffusionDB PickScore (64.4% vs. 70.5%), suggesting that human prompt engineering still holds an advantage in some in-domain settings. CLIP scores remain relatively flat across all methods (0.25–0.28), indicating that prompt editing primarily affects aesthetics and preference rather than semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based prompt optimization framework of Hao et al. (Promptist) by introducing the Dynamic Fine-control Prompt (DF-Prompt) format, which augments each modifier token with an effect time range and a scalar weight, enabling granular control over when and how strongly a word influences the diffusion denoising process. It departs from earlier heuristic prompt-engineering approaches (genetic algorithms, manual modifier curation) by automating the entire pipeline through a two-stage process: supervised fine-tuning of GPT-2 on confidence-filtered data, followed by online PPO exploration over a tripartite action space (word, time range, weight). The DF-Prompt formulation is the key conceptual departure from prior work, which treated prompts as flat sequences of tokens without temporal or intensity parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAE offers a practical, automated alternative to the labor-intensive trial-and-error prompt engineering that dominates current text-to-image workflows, and the DF-Prompt format provides a reusable interface for fine-grained generation control. The empirical finding that texture and style modifiers are most effective when injected in the latter half of the diffusion schedule, and that lower weights (0.75) for complex terms yield more balanced images than higher weights (1.25), gives practitioners actionable heuristics. However, the method is validated only on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with a small discrete action space (3 time ranges, 5 weights), and the performance gains over strong baselines (human-written prompts, Promptist) are modest, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, Flux) or continuous parameter spaces.
