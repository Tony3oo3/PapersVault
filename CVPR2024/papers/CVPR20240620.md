## Problem

CUT-like unpaired dehazing methods maximize mutual information between the dehazed output and the hazy input to preserve content, but this creates an inherent contradiction: pulling features toward the hazy input conflicts with the goal of removing haze. The paper addresses this by decoupling image features into haze-related (encoding haze level) and haze-unrelated (encoding texture, semantics) components, so each can be aligned with the appropriate domain without conflict.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240620/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image and produces a corresponding dehazed RGB image of the same resolution. Training uses only unpaired data (RESIDE ITS indoor set), yet the model generalizes to outdoor (SOTS-outdoor) and non-homogeneous haze (NH-HAZE 2) test sets without domain-specific training. The generator has 11.38M parameters and runs at ~10 ms per 256×256 image on an RTX 4090, matching the computational footprint of the CUT baseline it builds upon.

## Method

ODCR introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, Orthogonal MLPs with parameters constrained to the Stiefel manifold (via Riemannian gradient projection and retraction) project encoder features into a strictly orthogonal space, minimizing inter-channel redundancy far more effectively than a soft orthogonal loss. Second, a self-supervised Depth-wise Feature Classifier (DWFC) uses depth-wise convolutions to prevent cross-channel information leakage, then derives per-channel heat-vectors (via softmax of |θ⊙x|) that weight each orthogonal feature by its haze relevance. Third, a Weighted PatchNCE (WPNCE) loss repartitions samples into a dual-subscript scheme—hazy-domain same-location patches are positive for haze-unrelated features while clear-domain patches are positive for haze-related features—and applies the heat-vectors to modulate the InfoNCE numerator and denominator, pulling haze-related features toward the clear domain and haze-unrelated features toward the hazy input simultaneously.

## Results

On SOTS-indoor, ODCR achieves 26.32 dB PSNR and 0.945 SSIM, outperforming all unpaired baselines (best prior: D⁴ at 25.42 dB) and ranking second only to the supervised GDN (32.16 dB). On SOTS-outdoor and NH-HAZE 2, ODCR attains the best results among all methods (paired and unpaired), reaching 26.16 dB / 0.960 SSIM and 17.56 dB / 0.766 SSIM respectively, surpassing the next-best unpaired method CUT by 2.49 dB and 1.64 dB on those two sets. The model has 11.38M parameters and a latency of 10.14 ms on 256×256 images, comparable to CUT and CycleGAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODCR outperforms all unpaired baselines across all three quantitative test sets. On SOTS-indoor, it leads the unpaired group by 0.90 dB over D⁴ (26.32 vs. 25.42) and by 2.02 dB over CUT (26.32 vs. 24.30), while the supervised GDN remains ahead at 32.16 dB. On SOTS-outdoor, ODCR (26.16 dB) surpasses D⁴ (25.83 dB) and CUT (23.67 dB), and also exceeds the supervised GDN (17.69 dB), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution. On NH-HAZE 2, ODCR (17.56 dB) leads CUT (15.92 dB) by 1.64 dB and USID-Net (15.62 dB) by 1.94 dB, again outperforming all supervised methods. Among CycleGAN-based methods (CycleGAN, CycleDehaze, D⁴), ODCR gains 4.82–10.32 dB in PSNR, highlighting the advantage of the CUT-style single-GAN architecture with orthogonal decoupling over cycle-consistency. In terms of efficiency, ODCR matches CUT and CycleGAN in parameter count (11.38M) and latency (~10 ms), while RefineDNet is 24× slower (248.5 ms) and YOLY uses 2.8× more parameters (32M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CUT (Park et al., ECCV 2020), adopting its single-GAN architecture and identity loss while replacing the standard PatchNCE with a Weighted PatchNCE (WPNCE) that resolves a fundamental contradiction: CUT's mutual-information objective pulls the dehazed output toward the hazy input, conflicting with the goal of haze removal. The resolution comes from orthogonal feature decoupling via MLPs optimized on the Stiefel manifold, combined with a self-supervised depth-wise classifier to assign channels as haze-related or haze-unrelated. This departs from CycleGAN-based UID methods (CycleDehaze, D4) that rely on the overly strict bijection assumption, and from other CUT-like methods (UCL-Dehaze, Farewell to CycleGAN) that do not explicitly address the mutual-information-versus-dehazing tension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled resolution to a recognized tension in unpaired image restoration: preserving content (via mutual information with the input) while simultaneously altering the image (removing haze). The orthogonal decoupling framework is modular and could in principle extend to other unpaired restoration tasks (deraining, denoising) where a similar 'preserve structure, remove degradation' contradiction exists. However, the method adds non-trivial complexity (Stiefel manifold optimization, a separate DWFC network, channel-wise weighting), and the paper does not explore whether simpler decoupling mechanisms (e.g., learned soft masks) could achieve comparable results. The DWFC's self-supervised reliability is assumed rather than deeply validated, and generalization beyond dehazing remains untested.
