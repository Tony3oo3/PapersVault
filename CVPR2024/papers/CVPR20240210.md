## Problem

Estimating relative 3D rotation between image pairs with limited or no overlap remains challenging because feature-correspondence-based methods fail when sufficient matching points cannot be established. Prior deep-learning approaches, notably Cai et al.'s DenseCorrVol, rely on 4D correlation volumes computed via simple inner products between embedding maps, which the authors argue underutilize the rich interaction structure between image regions. This paper asks whether attention-based mechanisms can better encode these inter-image relationships for extreme rotation estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240210/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 128×128 RGB perspective crops (randomly sampled from panoramic images) and regresses the relative 3D rotation as a unit quaternion [q_w, q_x, q_y, q_z]. Only yaw and pitch are estimated, with roll assumed to be zero; pitch is constrained to [-45°, 45°] for outdoor and [-30°, 30°] for indoor scenes to avoid textureless views. The backbone produces 128-channel feature maps at 32×32 spatial resolution, which are then flattened to 2048-token sequences for the Transformer stages.

## Method

The architecture uses a weight-sharing Siamese Residual U-Net to encode both images, followed by three key Transformer stages. First, a cross-decoding step (Transformer Decoder-0) distills inter-image information by using each image's embedding as the other's query. Second, a Transformer-Encoder with an attention mask that zeros self-attention terms computes a cross-attention tensor that serves as an enhanced equivalent of the 4D correlation volume, leveraging multi-head attention (h=4, l=2) to capture richer pairwise interactions than simple inner products. Third, a cascaded pair of Transformer Decoders (TD1, TD2) alternately refines the cross-attention encoding and a learnable quaternion rotation query, jointly optimizing both before a two-layer MLP regresses the final quaternion. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with an L2 regression loss on normalized quaternions, outperforming the discrete cross-entropy formulation used by prior work.

## Results

On the StreetLearn dataset, the proposed method achieves a mean geodesic error of 0.58° (large overlap), 1.21° (small overlap), and 5.33° (non-overlapping), with 99.31%, 99.12%, and 96.22% of pairs estimated within 10° respectively. On InteriorNet, the non-overlapping case yields a mean error of 35.13° and 65.20% within 10°, outperforming the prior SOTA DenseCorrVol (37.69° / 61.97%). Correspondence-based baselines (SIFT, SuperPoint, 8PointViT) fail entirely on non-overlapping pairs, reporting errors exceeding 80° or no valid estimates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms DenseCorrVol (Cai et al. [7]), the closest prior SOTA, across all overlap categories and datasets: e.g., on StreetLearn non-overlapping pairs it reduces mean error from 5.77° to 5.33° and on InteriorNet from 37.69° to 35.13°. In the non-overlapping regime, correspondence-based methods (SIFT, SuperPoint) and 8PointViT fail entirely, reporting errors above 80° or no valid estimates, while Reg6D achieves only 24–28° mean error on StreetLearn. On large-overlap pairs, 8PointViT is competitive (0.48° on InteriorNet) but the proposed method edges it out (0.43°) and also handles SUN360 where 8PointViT reports no results. On small-overlap pairs, the proposed method beats 8PointViT by 0.29° on InteriorNet (1.55° vs. 1.84°) and by 0.25° on StreetLearn (1.21° vs. 1.46°). In cross-dataset generalization (Manhattan→London), the proposed method improves over Cai et al. by 1.7° (large), 4.5° (small), and 2.3° (none) in mean geodesic error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Cai et al.'s (DenseCorrVol, CVPR 2021) framework for extreme rotation estimation, which computed a 4D correlation volume via inner products between CNN embeddings. The key architectural departure is replacing the 4DCV with a Transformer-Encoder-based cross-attention mechanism (using an attention mask to isolate cross-image interactions), and adding two novel components: inter-image embedding distillation via cross-decoding (Transformer Decoder-0) and a cascaded dual-decoder scheme that alternately refines the cross-attention encoding and a learnable quaternion query. The work also positions itself within the broader Transformer-for-vision trend (DETR, ViT) but applies it to a multi-stage pipeline for pairwise geometric estimation rather than single-image classification or detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end trainable architecture that handles the genuinely difficult non-overlapping image pair regime where correspondence-based methods (SIFT, SuperPoint, 8PointViT) completely fail, achieving SOTA geodesic errors across indoor and outdoor benchmarks. However, the margins over DenseCorrVol are modest in the most challenging non-overlapping category (e.g., 35.13° vs 37.69° average on InteriorNet), and the method assumes null roll and operates on 128×128 crops, limiting real-world applicability. The authors note the architecture is general-purpose and could extend to optical flow or registration, but these applications are deferred. Open questions include whether the cascaded decoder design scales to larger spatial resolutions or more complex rotation distributions, and whether the cross-dataset generalization gap (Manhattan→London) can be further closed.
