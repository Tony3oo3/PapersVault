## Problem

Existing implicit 3D reconstruction methods rely on a single latent topology—grid, point, or vector—each with inherent trade-offs: grid latents are stable but lose fine detail through quantization, while point latents preserve detail but suffer from noise sensitivity and query ambiguity. ALTO was the first attempt to combine both, but it primarily decodes from grid latents and performs limited point feature extraction, leaving the potential of point latents underexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241666/cropped_page0_idx11.webp)

## Input / Output

DITTO takes a noisy, sparse point cloud (tested at 300 to 10K points with Gaussian noise levels 0.005–0.025) and produces an occupancy probability at arbitrary 3D query coordinates, from which a mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes. Grid latents are represented as triplanes at R=64 (object-level) or R=128 (scene-level), or as voxels at R=64 for scene-level tasks. The decoder queries the occupancy field at M arbitrary locations, making it resolution-independent at inference time.

## Method

DITTO employs a dual latent encoder–decoder architecture. The encoder extracts point latents via FKAConv, projects them into triplane/voxel grid latents, and refines both in a UNet of Dual Latent Layers (DLL). Each DLL refines point features with a Dynamic Sparse Point Transformer (DSPT)—a sorting-based windowed self-attention applied sequentially along x, y, z axes with point-adapted RoPE—and refines grid features with 3D-aware convolutions, while cross-projecting between the two to maintain their distinct shapes. The Integrated Implicit Decoder (IID) constructs 'neighbor integrated features' by concatenating KNN point-neighbor features with interpolated grid features at those neighbor locations, then applies self-attention between the grid-interpolated query feature and these integrated features to estimate occupancy. Training uses binary cross-entropy on occupancy labels, with the adaptive neighbor region (defined by query-to-neighbor distances) enabling clear surface boundaries for thin structures.

## Results

On the Synthetic Rooms scene-level benchmark (10K points, 0.005 noise), DITTO achieves IoU 0.928, Chamfer-L1 0.34, NC 0.930, and F-score 0.984, surpassing the previous SOTA ALTO (IoU 0.914, F-score 0.981) by 1.4% in IoU. On ShapeNet object-level (3K points), DITTO reaches IoU 0.949 and F-score 0.988, a four-times larger IoU gap over ALTO (0.930) than ALTO had over POCO. On the real-world ScanNet-v2 dataset, DITTO attains Chamfer-L1 0.70 and F-score 0.808, outperforming ALTO (0.79 / 0.779) and POCO (0.87 / 0.757).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DITTO outperforms all six baselines across every metric on both object-level (ShapeNet) and scene-level (Synthetic Rooms) benchmarks. Against the closest competitor ALTO [44], DITTO gains +1.4% IoU on Synthetic Rooms (0.928 vs. 0.914) and +1.9% IoU on ShapeNet at 3K points (0.949 vs. 0.930), with the gap widening to +1.9% IoU at the sparsest setting (300 points: 0.882 vs. 0.863). Versus the point-latent method POCO [1], DITTO improves IoU by +4.4% on Synthetic Rooms (0.928 vs. 0.884) and +6.5% on ShapeNet at 3K points (0.949 vs. 0.926), while also being more stable under noise (Table 3: IoU 0.811 vs. 0.701 at 0.025 noise). Against the grid-latent ConvONet [29], DITTO leads by +7.9% IoU on Synthetic Rooms and +10.0% on ShapeNet. On the real-world ScanNet-v2 dataset, DITTO achieves the best Chamfer-L1 (0.70) and F-score (0.808), beating ALTO by 0.09 in Chamfer-L1 and 0.029 in F-score. Notably, DITTO with triplane grid latents (IoU 0.931) even surpasses ALTO with voxel latents (IoU 0.914), demonstrating that the dual-latent integration compensates for the lower resolution of triplanes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DITTO directly extends ALTO (Wang et al., CVPR 2023), the first method to combine grid and point latents for implicit 3D reconstruction, by departing from ALTO's alternating projection strategy in favor of maintaining both latents in parallel through a U-shaped encoder (Dual Latent Layer) and integrating them at decode time (Integrated Implicit Decoder). The point-latent branch draws on POCO's KNN-based decoding and FKAConv for initial feature extraction, while the grid-latent branch follows ConvONet's triplane/voxel conventions. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a Dynamic Sparse Point Transformer (DSPT) for point feature refinement and a decoder that fuses point and grid features per-query rather than relying exclusively on grid interpolation, addressing ALTO's underutilization of point latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DITTO establishes a new SOTA on both object-level (ShapeNet) and scene-level (Synthetic Rooms) implicit reconstruction, with particularly notable gains on thin and intricate structures where grid-based methods hit resolution limits and point-based methods suffer from ambiguity. The dual-latent design principle—preserving each representation's shape while enabling cross-latent interaction—is a reusable architectural pattern that could inform future hybrid-representation methods. Limitations include added model complexity (UNet with DLL, DSPT, and IID), and the triplane comparison on ScanNet-v2 reveals that out-of-distribution generalization still degrades, suggesting the geometric inductive bias of voxels remains important. Open questions include whether the dual-latent framework extends to other implicit tasks (e.g., SDF-based reconstruction, generative modeling) and whether DSPT's sorting-based windowing can be made more efficient at very large point counts.
