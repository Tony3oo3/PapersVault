## Problem

Vision-based hand trackers produce physically implausible artifacts (hand-object penetration, unrealistic manipulation) during hand-object interaction due to heavy occlusions. Existing denoising approaches either rely on optimization that gets stuck at local optima under severe noise, or use purely data-driven networks with no guarantee of physical awareness. The paper addresses how to combine data priors with explicit, differentiable physics modeling to produce physically plausible hand poses in an end-to-end trainable framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy MANO hand mesh sequence (K×3 vertices per frame over T frames) together with an assumed-accurate rigid object trajectory (L×3 vertices per frame) and the object's linear acceleration computed via finite differences. It operates on a dual HOI intermediate representation — 21 hand keypoints plus a TOCH implicit field encoding object-centric hand-object correspondence — rather than raw mesh vertices. The output is a de-noised hand pose sequence fitted back from the refined dual representation, with the object trajectory held fixed throughout.

## Method

The architecture is a two-stage denoising autoencoder: a PointNet encodes the TOCH field into a global object feature, concatenated with hand keypoints to form a per-frame HOI feature, which an RNN processes temporally before decoding the refined representation. Stage I trains the autoencoder with standard data supervision to learn a noisy-to-clean projection. Stage II freezes the autoencoder and inserts a residual MLP mapping layer φ (conditioned on object acceleration) trained with two learned neural physical losses: a grasp credibility loss (trained from a novel penetration depth metric with soft+hard BCE targets) and a manipulation feasibility loss (trained from force error and manipulation expense metrics under friction-cone constraints). The soft targets ensure smooth loss landscapes that provide corrective gradient directions rather than binary signals, enabling the network to learn physically-aware corrections without over-smoothing.

## Results

On HO-3D (real tracker errors from Hasson et al.), the proposed method achieves the best scores across all four metrics: MPJPE 10.7 mm, MPVPE 11.2 mm, Intersection Volume 5.95, and plausible rate 0.85. On GRAB with synthetic noise (T-0.01, θ-0.3), the method reduces MPJPE from 18.86 to 7.39 mm, contact IoU from 3.62 to 23.94, and plausible rate from 0.42 to 0.91, while also generalizing to unseen noise patterns (e.g., T-0.02 θ-0.5: MPJPE 31.49→11.97, plausible rate 0.31→0.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric in the HO-3D comparison. Against TOCH [31], it improves MPJPE by 0.2 mm (10.7 vs 10.9), reduces Intersection Volume by 18% (5.95 vs 7.24), and raises the plausible rate from 0.73 to 0.85 — a 12-point gain indicating substantially fewer physically implausible frames. Against Physical Interaction [14], it achieves lower MPJPE (10.7 vs 11.4), lower MPVPE (11.2 vs 11.6), and a markedly lower IV (5.95 vs 8.33), while also edging out on plausible rate (0.85 vs 0.83). Compared to the raw Hasson et al. tracker output, the method cuts IV by 32% (8.75→5.95) and lifts the plausible rate by 14 points (0.71→0.85). The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that both the grasp credibility loss and the manipulation feasibility loss are necessary: removing either one degrades the plausible rate to 0.81–0.82 and worsens IV or PD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: data-driven hand pose denoising (TOCH, GraspTTA) and physics-based hand-object interaction refinement (Physical Interaction, ManipNet). It departs from both by unifying them into a single end-to-end trainable autoencoder framework, where differentiable neural physical losses (grasp credibility via a learned penetration-depth predictor, manipulation feasibility via learned force-error/manipulation-expense predictors) replace the non-differentiable physics checks used in optimization-based methods. The dual HOI representation borrows TOCH's object-centric implicit field and augments it with hand keypoints, and the two-stage training (data prior first, then physics-aware mapping layer) is a novel architectural choice not present in either prior line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in vision-based hand tracking: physically implausible artifacts (penetration, hovering) that degrade downstream applications in VR/AR, robotics, and retargeting. The key open limitation is the assumption of a clean object trajectory and single rigid object, which restricts applicability to multi-object or articulated scenarios. Whether the neural physical losses generalize beyond the GRAB-trained regime to truly novel object geometries or contact-rich multi-hand settings remains untested. The two-stage training with a frozen encoder plus a small mapping layer is a clean recipe that could be adapted to other denoising tasks where physics constraints are available but non-differentiable.
