## Problem

Existing fMRI-to-image reconstruction methods train separate subject-specific models for each individual, consuming substantial computational resources and ignoring commonalities across subjects. Naively amalgamating fMRI data from all subjects to train a unified model causes significant performance degradation in state-of-the-art methods, revealing that individual brain variability is not easily absorbed by a single shared architecture. The paper asks how to build one omnifit model that simultaneously captures inter-subject commonality and individual specificity.

## Input / Output

The input is a preprocessed 1D fMRI voxel vector of dimension d (number of voxels) recorded while a subject views a natural scene image. The model predicts CLIP image embeddings (v×c) and text embeddings (t×c) as intermediate representations, which then condition a pretrained Versatile Diffusion model to generate the final reconstructed RGB image. Evaluation is on the NSD dataset (4 subjects, 982 test images each), with images and captions sourced from MS-COCO.

## Method

Psychometry uses a ViT backbone with an Omni MoE layer inserted into the last 4 of 12 transformer blocks. Unlike sparse MoE that routes to top-K experts, Omni MoE engages all E experts collaboratively to learn inter-subject commonalities, while each expert carries subject-specific parameter vectors (c-dim per subject) trained only on that subject's data to handle individual differences. A split-then-lump mechanism (softmax-weighted token compression into E experts, then softmax-weighted expert aggregation back to m tokens) keeps computation efficient since E ≪ m. At inference, an Ecphory strategy retrieves the most similar CLIP embedding from prestored subject-specific training memories and blends it with the predicted fMRI embedding via mix-up, providing a more reliable conditional signal to the diffusion model. Training uses a bidirectional contrastive loss to align predicted fMRI embeddings with CLIP image and text spaces.

## Results

On the NSD test set, Psychometry (trained as a single unified model on amalgamated fMRI data from 4 subjects) achieves 96.4% AlexNet(2), 98.6% AlexNet(5), 95.8% Inception, and 96.8% CLIP similarity, along with 0.297 PixCorr, 0.340 SSIM, 0.628 EffNet-B distance, and 0.345 SwAV distance. These results surpass the best subject-specific model (MindEye SSM) on all four high-level metrics (Inception, CLIP, EffNet-B, SwAV) while matching or exceeding it on low-level metrics. Compared to MindEye trained as a unified model, Psychometry gains 12.2%/9.4%/11.7%/11.8% on AlexNet(2)/AlexNet(5)/Inception/CLIP, demonstrating that the Omni MoE + Ecphory design effectively mitigates the performance collapse that other methods suffer when moving from subject-specific to unified training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Psychometry is compared against five baselines: Mind-Reader (NeurIPS 2022), Mind-Vis (CVPR 2023), Takagi et al. (CVPR 2023), Gu et al. (MIDL 2023), and MindEye (NeurIPS 2023), each evaluated in both subject-specific (SSM) and unified-model (UM) settings. Against the strongest SSM baseline (MindEye), Psychometry wins on all four high-level metrics (Inception +2.0%, CLIP +2.7%, EffNet-B +2.6%, SwAV +6.0%) and on SSIM (+0.017), while trailing slightly on PixCorr (0.297 vs 0.309). Against Mind-Vis SSM, the gains are substantially larger: +17.0%/+20.6%/+26.5%/+29.7% on the four high-level metrics. The most striking comparison is against MindEye UM, where MindEye drops from 94.1% to 85.0% CLIP (a 9.1-point collapse), while Psychometry maintains 96.8% CLIP — a 11.8-point advantage — confirming that the Omni MoE with subject-specific parameters and Ecphory retrieval effectively preserve individual specificity within a single shared model. All other baselines (Mind-Reader, Takagi, Gu) show even more severe degradation in the UM setting, with Inception scores dropping 10–12 points, whereas Psychometry's unified model outperforms their SSM counterparts on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in fMRI-to-image reconstruction (MindEye, Mind-Reader, Mind-Vis, Takagi et al., Gu et al.), where each subject requires a separately trained model, by proposing a single omnifit model trained on amalgamated multi-subject data. Architecturally, it repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts concept but inverts the standard sparse-routing design: instead of selecting top-K experts per input, all experts participate jointly to capture inter-subject commonalities, while lightweight subject-specific parameter vectors handle individual differences. The split-then-lump mechanism borrows the efficiency motivation of sparse MoE without its discrete routing, and the Ecphory inference strategy draws on the neuroscience concept of memory retrieval to augment predicted embeddings via nearest-neighbor lookup in prestored CLIP memories before feeding a pretrained Versatile Diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the fMRI-to-image community: training one model instead of S subject-specific models reduces computational cost and opens the door to leveraging cross-subject statistical regularities that were previously inaccessible. The paper also highlights an open problem it does not fully solve—fMRI data privacy when amalgamating recordings from multiple individuals—which is likely to constrain real-world deployment. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (NSD) with only four subjects, a single GPU, and the Ecphory strategy's dependence on having access to training-time CLIP embeddings at inference, which may not generalize to truly unseen stimulus distributions.
