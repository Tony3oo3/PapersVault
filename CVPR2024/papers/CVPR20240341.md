## Problem

Existing point-based dragging methods (DragGAN, DragDiffusion) suffer from two failure modes: 'miss tracking,' where handle points drift outside the search region after drastic content changes, and 'ambiguous tracking,' where tracked points land in visually similar but semantically wrong regions. Both issues cause cumulative error in the iterative motion supervision loop, leading to lost handle points, distorted outputs, and misdirected editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240341/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image, the user specifies pairs of handle points and target points (and optionally a binary mask restricting the editing region). The method operates on the feature map of a pre-trained StyleGAN2 (extracted after the 6th block, optimized in W+ space) or a LoRA-fine-tuned Latent Diffusion Model (features from the U-Net), and outputs a single edited image where the semantic content at each handle point has been displaced to its corresponding target. No per-image training is required for the GAN variant; the diffusion variant requires a one-time LoRA fine-tune (rank 16) per image for inversion.

## Method

FreeDrag replaces the fragile point-tracking step with a feature-dragging pipeline built on two mechanisms. First, an adaptive template feature (an exponential moving average of the handle-point feature patch) is updated with a coefficient λ that shrinks when the end-of-drag discrepancy L_en is large (chaotic) and grows when L_en is small (stable), preventing catastrophic feature drift during miss tracking. Second, line search with backtracking constrains the next handle position to the 1-D line segment from the original handle to the target, eliminating ambiguity from similar-looking regions; a three-branch backtracking rule then either advances the point, freezes it (insufficient progress), or falls back with a doubled search range (abnormal motion). The drag loss aligns the sum of features in a radius-3 patch at the current position with the template feature, and up to 5 optimization steps are taken per drag before the next position is computed, with termination when the remaining distance to the target drops below 2 pixels.

## Results

On the proposed FreeDragBench (GAN-based), FreeDrag consistently outperforms DragGAN across all five categories: e.g., on Face, FID drops from 38.07 to 29.50 and CCSD from 0.83 to 0.35, while inference time is reduced from 8.26 s to 2.74 s. On the diffusion-based DragBench, FreeDrag achieves a lower mean distance (33.49 vs. 38.76) and lower LPIPS (1.02 vs. 1.38) than DragDiffusion, with a 11% speedup (63.62 s vs. 71.77 s). The CCSD metric (lower is better) shows the largest relative improvement on Horse (1.20 → 0.68, a 43% reduction), indicating FreeDrag's particular strength in handling ambiguous tracking scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DragGAN (the primary GAN-based baseline), FreeDrag wins on every metric in every category of FreeDragBench: FID improves by 8–12 points (e.g., 38.07→29.50 on Face) and CCSD is roughly halved or better (e.g., 0.83→0.35 on Face, 1.20→0.68 on Horse), while also running 3× faster (2.74 s vs. 8.26 s). Against DragDiffusion on the diffusion-based DragBench, FreeDrag reduces mean distance by 5.27 points (38.76→33.49) and LPIPS by 0.36 (1.38→1.02), with a modest 8 s speedup, confirming that the feature-dragging pipeline transfers effectively to diffusion backbones. Qualitatively, FreeDrag also outperforms EditGAN in following fine-grained editing instructions (Fig. 8), though no quantitative metric is reported for that comparison. Overall, FreeDrag dominates all three baselines on every reported metric, with the largest gains appearing in scenarios involving similar structures (Horse, Elephant) where ambiguous tracking is most problematic.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeDrag is a direct methodological successor to DragGAN (Pan et al., 2023), which established the iterative point-dragging paradigm of alternating motion supervision and point tracking in latent space. The key conceptual departure is replacing exact point tracking with feature-level supervision: instead of searching for the relocated handle point in a 2D neighborhood, FreeDrag maintains an adaptively-updated template feature per handle point and constrains the search to the line segment between the original handle and target positions. This line-search-with-backtracking design, combined with the exponential-moving-average template update, is the paper's core architectural contribution. The approach is model-agnostic, demonstrated on both StyleGAN2 (in W+ space) and Latent Diffusion Models (with per-image LoRA fine-tuning, following DragDiffusion), and is accompanied by a new evaluation benchmark (FreeDragBench) and metric (CCSD) tailored to GAN-based dragging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete, well-illustrated failure modes (miss tracking and ambiguous tracking) that limit the practical usability of interactive point-based editing, and the proposed solutions are simple yet effective, yielding a ~3× speedup on GAN models alongside improved accuracy. The CCSD metric and FreeDragBench dataset fill a gap in evaluation infrastructure for GAN-based dragging, which previously lacked a standardized benchmark. However, the line constraint on movement direction may limit the method's expressiveness for complex, non-linear deformations, and the per-image LoRA fine-tuning requirement for diffusion models remains a practical bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D editing, and whether the template feature mechanism can be made more robust to very large displacements that exceed the single-step search range.
