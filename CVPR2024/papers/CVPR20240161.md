## Problem

Diffusion models have achieved remarkable generative quality, yet their representational capacity remains largely unexplored. Prior attempts either repurpose pre-trained text-to-image models (conflating diffusion's contribution with scale and captioned data) or study diffusion-based encoding only theoretically on small datasets like MNIST. No diffusion model had previously succeeded at ImageNet linear-probe classification while simultaneously handling reconstruction, editing, and synthesis tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder (ResNet-50) consumes a clean source view x' (optionally with camera pose r for 3D tasks, or multiple source views aggregated via a shallow transformer) and outputs a 2048-dimensional latent vector z. This z modulates a UNet denoising decoder that produces the target view x from noisy input x_t. The 2048-dim bottleneck is orders of magnitude smaller than discrete codebooks used by VQGAN (65K–524K) or DALL-E, yet supports high-fidelity reconstruction and semantic classification via a linear probe on z.

## Method

SODA couples a ResNet-50 image encoder with a UNet denoising decoder through a tight information bottleneck, where the encoder's latent z guides denoising via adaptive group normalization (scale and bias) applied to each decoder layer. Three key mechanisms enhance representation quality: (1) layer modulation partitions z into m+1 sub-vectors, each modulating a specific pair of decoder layers to promote granularity-based specialization; (2) layer masking zeros random subsets of sub-vectors during training (a layer-wise generalization of classifier-free guidance), decoupling sub-vectors and enabling selective conditioning at inference; (3) an inverted noise schedule prioritizes medium noise levels, avoiding the extremes that either trivialize denoising or force pixel-level reconstruction. The self-supervised objective is novel view synthesis—source and target views are distinct augmentations or perspectives—rather than auto-encoding, which the authors show yields a 17% improvement in downstream classification over the auto-encoding variant.

## Results

On ImageNet linear-probe classification, SODA achieves 72.2% top-1 accuracy (90.5% top-5), outperforming all generative baselines including MAE (68.0%), iGPT-L (65.2%), and BigBiGAN (61.3%), while remaining competitive with discriminative methods like DINO+multi-crop (78.2%) and BYOL (77.4%). On CelebA attribute classification, SODA reaches 72.7% F1, surpassing even language-supervised CLIP embeddings (71.1% F1). For novel view synthesis, SODA achieves FID of 1.51 (GSO), 0.74 (ShapeNet), and 0.81 (NMR), outperforming geometry-aware methods like PixelNeRF (FID 48.72/25.34/34.54) and geometry-free methods like SRT (FID 38.64/17.96/7.90). On disentanglement (DCI), SODA improves Disentanglement scores by 27.2–58.3% over the best variational baselines across five datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SODA (72.2% top-1) decisively outperforms all prior diffusion-based approaches on ImageNet linear-probe: Unconditional Diffusion [19] scores only 24.5% and Palette [30] just 11.4%, demonstrating that the encoder-bottleneck architecture is essential. Among generative methods, SODA surpasses MAE [53] (68.0%), iGPT-L [55] (65.2%), BigBiGAN [73] (61.3%), and BEiT [54] (56.7%), establishing it as the strongest generative representation learner. Against discriminative/contrastive methods, SODA narrows the gap substantially: it trails DINO+multi-crop [56] (78.2%) by 6.0 points and BYOL [72] (77.4%) by 5.2 points, but exceeds SimCLR [58] (74.2%) is close to DINO [56] (74.9%), and notably shows far less sensitivity to augmentation choice (only 3.1% drop with crop+flip vs. 27.5% for SimCLR). On novel view synthesis (Table 3), SODA achieves the best FID and LPIPS across GSO, ShapeNet, and NMR, beating geometry-aware PixelNeRF [60] and NeRF-VAE [59] by large margins (e.g., FID 1.51 vs. 48.72 on GSO) and geometry-free SRT [80] and DALL-E2 [8]. On disentanglement (Table 4), SODA's DCI Disentanglement scores (e.g., 74.67 on CelebA, 73.41 on MPI3D) exceed all variational baselines (β-VAE [67], FactorVAE [63], DIP-VAE [107], AnnealedVAE [106]) by 27–58%, while matching or exceeding their Informativeness. On CelebA, SODA (72.7% F1) also outperforms CLIP [128] (71.1% F1), showing that purely image-based self-supervised diffusion pretraining can rival language-supervised representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SODA extends the diffusion-based representation learning line initiated by DRL (Abstreiter et al., 2021) and DiffAE (Preechakul et al., 2022), which condition a denoiser on an encoded version of its target, but departs critically by generating novel views rather than auto-encoding the same image. It also draws on classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), generalized here to a layer-wise masking scheme, and on AdaIN-style feature modulation from StyleGAN (Karras et al., 2019) to couple a ResNet encoder to a UNet decoder. Unlike hybrid approaches such as unCLIP and Latent Diffusion Models, which rely on frozen pre-trained encoders (CLIP, VQGAN) for their latent space, SODA trains the encoder jointly with the diffusion decoder under a tight information bottleneck, making the latent representations genuinely diffusion-derived.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SODA demonstrates that diffusion models, long associated primarily with high-fidelity generation, can serve as competitive self-supervised representation learners, closing much of the gap with discriminative methods like DINO and BYOL on ImageNet while exhibiting notably greater robustness to data augmentation choices. The finding that novel view synthesis (rather than auto-encoding) as a training objective yields a 17-point improvement, and that an inverted noise schedule favoring medium noise levels further boosts performance, provides actionable design principles for future diffusion-based pre-training. Limitations include the focus on single-object images and the computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling; the authors identify compositional scenes and dynamic environments as promising directions for extending the framework.
