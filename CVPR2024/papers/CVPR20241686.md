## Problem

Existing fast reconstruction methods based on feature grids (HashGrid, VMGrid) are constrained to small-scale, object-centric scenes due to their reliance on bounded centralized space and scene contraction. Extending to large-scale scenes via local bounding boxes introduces optimization instability (floaters) and requires additional regularization or appearance-matching heuristics. The paper addresses the need for a unified representation that handles scenes of arbitrary scale and spatial granularity while maintaining compactness, fast reconstruction, and high rendering quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view RGB images and their camera poses as input, with no requirement for a sparse point cloud prior or precomputed near-far bounds. Probes are initialized via Farthest Point Sampling on camera positions, and the number of probes (L) and core factors (C) scales with scene size (e.g., L=64 for small scenes up to L=256 for large scenes). The output is a novel view rendering produced by volumetric light field integration, where a decoder MLP maps aggregated probe features and viewing direction to per-sample opacity and radiance, with a two-layer proposal network ([256, 96, 48] samples per stage) guiding ray sampling.

## Method

NeLF-Pro represents a scene as spatially distributed light field probes, each a multi-channel spherical feature map (H×W×D tensor) encoding local radiance and geometry. The key architectural contribution is a Vector-Matrix-Matrix (VMM) factorization that decomposes each probe tensor into shared core factors (a vector V̄ and matrix M̄, with C≪L) and per-probe basis matrices (M), capturing multilinear interactions while reducing parameter count through spatial sharing. For querying, the method selects K nearest probes based on camera-to-probe distance (not point-to-probe), applies a permutation-invariant self-attention blending over the selected factors, and combines them via Hadamard products before passing to a decoder MLP. The spherical projection with periodic sawtooth scaling naturally produces a mipmap-like multi-scale representation where nearby content is encoded at higher resolution, eliminating the need for cone tracing or multi-sample anti-aliasing. Training uses supervised L2 photometric loss plus an interlevel loss, optimized via gradient descent on a single GPU without custom CUDA kernels.

## Results

On the Free dataset, NeLF-Pro achieves PSNR 27.02, SSIM 0.802, and LPIPS 0.256 in 16 minutes with 20M parameters, matching mip-NeRF360 (PSNR 27.01) while outperforming F2-NeRF (26.32) and Instant-NGP (24.43). On mip-NeRF360, it reaches PSNR 27.27, SSIM 0.753, LPIPS 0.360, surpassing F2-NeRF (26.39) and Instant-NGP (26.24) but trailing the well-trained MLP-based mip-NeRF360 (28.94). On large-scale scenes (KITTI-360-big, ~1500m trajectory), NeLF-Pro achieves PSNR 22.68 vs. F2-NeRF-Big (21.36), 3DGS (15.17), and mip-NeRF360 (16.63), demonstrating strong scalability. On the KITTI-360 medium-scale NVS task, NeLF-Pro scores PSNR 22.55 / SSIM 0.829 / LPIPS 0.201 in 45 minutes, outperforming all prior leaderboard submissions (NeRF: 21.28, PNF: 22.07) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Free dataset, NeLF-Pro (PSNR 27.02) clearly outperforms all feature-grid baselines: F²-NeRF (26.32), Instant-NGP (24.43), DVGO (23.90), and Plenoxels (19.13), while essentially tying the MLP-based mip-NeRF360 (27.01) at a fraction of the training time (16 min vs. hours). On mip-NeRF360, NeLF-Pro (27.27) beats F²-NeRF (26.39) and Instant-NGP (26.24) but remains ~1.7 dB below the well-trained mip-NeRF360 (28.94), which uses 250K iterations vs. NeLF-Pro's 20K. On large-scale scenes, NeLF-Pro dominates: on KITTI-360-big it achieves PSNR 22.68 vs. F²-NeRF-Big (21.36), 3DGS (15.17), and mip-NeRF360 (16.63); on Scuol it scores 27.84 vs. F²-NeRF-Big (26.43) and 3DGS (22.74). The one notable loss is on 56-Leonard where 3DGS (29.23) slightly edges NeLF-Pro (28.86), though the paper notes 3DGS degrades in zoomed-out views. On the KITTI-360 medium-scale NVS leaderboard, NeLF-Pro (PSNR 22.55, SSIM 0.829, LPIPS 0.201) outperforms all prior RGB-only submissions (NeRF 21.28, mip-NeRF 21.54, PNF 22.07) while training 20×+ faster than the MLP-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeLF-Pro extends the volumetric rendering framework of NeRF and the fast-convergence feature grid paradigm (Instant-NGP, F2-NeRF) by replacing the single global representation with spatially distributed light field probes, each encoded via a novel Vector-Matrix-Matrix (VMM) factorization. It draws on Factor Fields for the spherical coordinate transformation with periodic scaling, and on mip-NeRF360 for the two-layer proposal network and interlevel loss. Conceptually, it departs from both scene-contraction-based methods (which assume a bounded centralized space) and large-scale partitioning approaches (BlockNeRF, Mega-NeRF, GridNeRF) that rely on bounding boxes with hard or soft boundaries, instead using camera-trajectory-driven probe placement with a shared core factor to capture inter-probe relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical path to unified multi-scale novel view synthesis without scene contraction or manual bounding-box partitioning, which are the two main bottlenecks preventing feature-grid methods from scaling to city-level scenes. The VMM factorization with shared core factors is a compact encoding that could be useful beyond this specific rendering pipeline. However, the method still requires posed multi-view input, manual tuning of probe counts (L, C) per scene scale, and a fixed number of neighboring probes (L̂=16), limiting its fully automated applicability. Open questions include whether the probe placement can be learned end-to-end, how the approach handles dynamic or partially observed scenes, and whether the self-attention-based factor aggregation scales gracefully to very large probe counts.
