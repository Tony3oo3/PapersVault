## Problem

Existing joint moment retrieval and highlight detection methods follow a data-driven bottom-up paradigm that relies on a shared backbone and task-specific heads, implicitly assuming that common features suffice for both tasks. This overlooks the distinct objectives of MR (localizing precise temporal intervals) and HD (ranking clip-level saliency), as well as the potential interplay between the two tasks, leading to suboptimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query and an untrimmed video segmented into fixed-length clips (e.g., 75 clips of 2 s each on QVHighlights), with video and text features extracted by frozen backbones (SlowFast/I3D/VGG for video; CLIP/GloVe for text) and projected to a common dimension D. It outputs N_q = 10 predicted temporal intervals parameterized as (center, width) pairs for moment retrieval, plus a clip-wise saliency score vector of length N for highlight detection. The MR decoder uses dynamic anchor queries in a DAB-DETR-style Transformer, while the HD decoder is a lightweight CNN of alternating Linear and Conv1d layers.

## Method

TaskWeave introduces a task-decoupled unit where a shared expert and two task-specific experts (MR-specific CNN, HD-specific identity mapping) process query-related video representations through task-specific mappers, combining outputs via element-wise product to capture both common and task-specific features. An inter-task feedback mechanism converts MR predictions into moment-aware binary masks (M2M) that modulate the HD decoder input, and binarizes HD saliency scores into highlightness-aware masks (H2M) that modulate the MR decoder input, enabling each task to guide the other. A principled task-dependent joint loss replaces fixed manual weights with learnable uncertainty parameters (δ_mr, δ_hd) derived from Gaussian and Boltzmann likelihood formulations, allowing the relative contribution of each task's loss to adapt dynamically during training. Multi-modal fusion is performed via a 2-layer cross-attention Transformer, followed by 1D MaxPool to mitigate rank loss in attention.

## Results

On QVHighlights (the joint MR+HD benchmark), TaskWeave achieves 64.26% R1@0.5, 50.06% R1@0.7, and 45.38% Avg. mAP for moment retrieval, and 39.28% mAP / 63.68% HIT@1 for highlight detection, surpassing the previous best (QD-DETR) by +1.58% R1@0.5, +3.40% R1@0.7, and +4.16% Avg. mAP. On TVSum, TaskWeave reaches 87.3% average mAP, outperforming QD-DETR (85.0%) by 2.3 points across 10 highlight categories. On Charades-STA, TaskWeave with VGG backbone achieves 56.51% R1@0.5 and 33.66% R1@0.7, improving over QD-DETR (52.77% / 31.13%) by +3.74% and +2.53% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaskWeave outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all three datasets. On QVHighlights, it beats the strongest competitor QD-DETR [36] by +4.16% Avg. mAP and +1.58% R1@0.5 for MR, while also achieving the best HD mAP (39.28 vs. 39.13); notably, UMT [32] uses audio features yet still trails TaskWeave on MR metrics. On TVSum, TaskWeave (87.3% Avg. mAP) surpasses QD-DETR (85.0%) and UMT (83.1%), winning in 9 of 10 individual highlight categories. On Charades-STA, TaskWeave improves over QD-DETR by +3.74% R1@0.5 (VGG backbone) and +2.69% R1@0.7 (I3D backbone), and over UMT by +8.20% R1@0.5 (VGG). The paper notes that UniVTG [29] follows a pretrain-finetune paradigm, so its non-pretrained results are used for fair comparison, and even under that adjustment TaskWeave leads by a wide margin. The only near-tie is on HD HIT@1 where UMT (64.19%) slightly edges TaskWeave (63.68%), but UMT relies on audio modality unavailable to TaskWeave.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant bottom-up, data-driven paradigm in joint moment retrieval (MR) and highlight detection (HD) — where a shared backbone extracts common features fed to separate task heads (as in Moment-DETR, UMT, QD-DETR) — by proposing a top-down, task-driven framework. The task-decoupled unit draws on the PLE/MoE architecture (shared expert + task-specific experts) but applies it to video temporal grounding, while the MR decoder follows DAB-DETR's dynamic anchor formulation. The inter-task feedback mechanism, which converts one task's predictions into spatial masks guiding the other, is a novel contribution not present in prior joint MR+HD work. The task-dependent joint loss extends Kendall et al.'s uncertainty-weighted multi-task loss to the video grounding setting with task-specific likelihood formulations (Gaussian for MR, Boltzmann/softmax for HD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in joint MR+HD modeling: existing methods assume task commonality dominates, but the distinct objectives of MR (localizing intervals) and HD (ranking clip saliency) benefit from task-specific representations. The inter-task feedback mechanism offers a principled way to exploit the correlation between tasks without forcing a single shared representation, and the dynamic loss weighting removes manual hyperparameter tuning. However, the HD results remain second-best (UMT with audio features outperforms), and the inter-task feedback contributes only modest gains (~1–2%) in ablation, raising questions about whether the feedback signal is sufficiently informative or whether the mechanism generalizes to broader multi-task video understanding settings. The approach is also evaluated on relatively small-scale datasets (75 clips per video), leaving open whether the task-decoupled design and feedback loop scale to longer, more complex videos.
