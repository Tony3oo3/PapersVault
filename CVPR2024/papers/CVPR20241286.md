## Problem

Generative object compositing requires simultaneously preserving the identity of a reference object and adapting its color and geometry to a new background, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: approaches like TF-ICON preserve identity well yet produce copy-paste-like results with minimal pose variation, while methods like Paint-by-Example and ObjectStitch achieve natural blending but lose fine object details. No prior method achieves both high-fidelity identity retention and flexible geometric/color harmonization.

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs at 512×512 resolution: a masked object image, a background image, and a coarse mask that specifies the target location, scale, and shape of the composited object. The mask supports four levels of precision (from a simple bounding box to a fine pixel-level mask), enabling user-directed control over the object's pose and view. The output is a single composite image where the object appears naturally integrated into the background with harmonized lighting, color, and geometry, including synthesized shadows and interactions with nearby objects.

## Method

IMPRINT decouples compositing into two specialized stages. In the first (context-agnostic ID-preserving) stage, a DINOv2-based image encoder is trained on multi-view object pairs (MVImgNet, Objaverse) via a view-synthesis task: given one view of an object, a diffusion decoder reconstructs another view, forcing the encoder to learn view-invariant representations that retain identity-critical details. In the second (compositing) stage, the DINOv2 backbone is frozen while the content adapter and Stable Diffusion UNet are fine-tuned on paired compositing data (Pixabay, video segmentation datasets) with a Sequential Collaborative Training schedule that alternates learning-rate emphasis between the adapter and UNet across phases. Object tokens from the frozen encoder are injected into cross-attention layers of the UNet, and the coarse mask conditions generation to guide pose and shape, allowing large color and geometric variations without degrading identity fidelity.

## Results

On the Pixabay test set, IMPRINT achieves a FID of 16.45, substantially outperforming ObjectStitch (22.49), Paint-by-Example (23.27), and TF-ICON (63.97). For identity preservation, IMPRINT attains a DINO-score of 94.71 and DreamSim of 0.083, compared to 90.39/0.142 (ObjectStitch), 85.23/0.191 (Paint-by-Example), and 73.22/0.322 (TF-ICON). On the more challenging DreamBooth test set, IMPRINT leads with a DINO-score of 43.45 and DreamSim of 0.290, again surpassing all baselines. A user study on DreamBooth confirms these gains, with IMPRINT preferred for fidelity over ObjectStitch (80.4% vs 19.6%), Paint-by-Example (86.5% vs 13.5%), and TF-ICON (73.7% vs 26.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMPRINT outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across both test sets. Against Paint-by-Example, IMPRINT improves FID from 23.27 to 16.45 and DINO-score from 85.23 to 94.71 on Pixabay, indicating both better realism and stronger identity retention. Against ObjectStitch (the closest competitor), IMPRINT reduces FID by 6.04 points (22.49→16.45) and DreamSim by 0.059 (0.142→0.083), showing meaningful gains in both compositing quality and detail preservation. Against TF-ICON, the margin is largest: FID drops from 63.97 to 16.45 and DINO-score rises from 73.22 to 94.71, reflecting TF-ICON's limited pose adaptability and copy-paste tendency. The user study further confirms that IMPRINT is preferred for fidelity over ObjectStitch by a wide 80.4% margin, while realism is roughly on par with ObjectStitch (50.7% vs 49.3%) but clearly ahead of Paint-by-Example (62.8%) and TF-ICON (53.4%). AnyDoor is compared in the appendix, where IMPRINT also achieves better ID-preservation while maintaining greater flexibility in color and geometry adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

IMPRINT builds directly on diffusion-based object compositing methods (ObjectStitch, Paint-by-Example, TF-ICON) that condition a Stable Diffusion UNet on reference-object features via CLIP or DINOv2 adapters, but departs from them by decoupling identity preservation from background harmonization into two sequential training stages. The first stage repurposes a multi-view object reconstruction task (inspired by view-synthesis literature) to pretrain a DINOv2 encoder for view-invariant, detail-preserving representations, while the second stage freezes that backbone and trains the generator for compositing with shape-guided mask control. This contrasts with concurrent works (AnyDoor, ControlCom) that improve fidelity through high-frequency filters or local enhancement modules but remain limited in geometric adaptability, and with TF-ICON's noise-modeling approach that sacrifices pose variation for subject consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage decoupling provides a practical recipe for resolving the fundamental trade-off between identity preservation and background harmonization that has constrained prior compositing methods, and the ablation showing that object-centric data (MVImgNet) degrades compositing quality when backgrounds are absent offers useful guidance for dataset curation. However, the paper acknowledges that large view changes still cause notable identity loss and small text/logo consistency degrades, pointing toward 3D/NeRF integration and higher-resolution encoders as open directions. The shape-guided mask control adds practical editing flexibility that prior compositing methods lacked, making the framework more applicable to real-world creative workflows.
