## Problem

Diffusion models accumulate score estimation and discretization errors throughout the reverse sampling trajectory, causing the model distribution to deviate from the true data distribution and producing residual artifacts in generated images. Prior work addresses this by improving numerical solvers or adjusting scores, but no method directly learns and corrects the accumulated residual as a plug-and-play post-processing step on frozen pre-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240628/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a batch of ground-truth images from the target dataset and a frozen pre-trained diffusion model; it forward-diffuses each image via a VE SDE to a random time step t, then reverses via PF ODE to an intermediate step i to form the residual pair (x_0, x_i^ODE). During inference, the input is a random prior sample x_T; the pre-trained DM's reverse S/ODE runs until an adaptive cutoff i* = nearest step ≥ max(0.01, ε), after which the correction function maps x_i* directly to the final image, bypassing the remaining low-noise steps where score matching is unreliable. The correction function is a standalone network (e.g., EDM backbone without time embedding) that takes an image and a time index and outputs a corrected image of the same spatial resolution.

## Method

The framework defines the residual Ω = x_0 − x_0^ODE as the difference between a true sample and its PF-ODE reconstruction, then trains a parameterized correction function f_φ(x_i^ODE, i) → x_0 to absorb this residual. Training uses a two-stage simulation: forward SDE noise injection followed by PF-ODE reversal to construct residual pairs, optimized with a weighted L2/L1/LPIPS loss plus data-augmentation regularization (Gaussian noise, rotation, cropping, pixel masking) to encourage transferability. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) shows that under Lipschitz continuity and bounded local error, the correction function's output varies only as O((Δi)^p) across different DMs on the same dataset, justifying that a single correction function trained on one DM can rectify sampling trajectories of arbitrary other DMs sharing the same data distribution. For efficient deployment, the heavy correction function is distilled into a 4–64× smaller lightweight variant via knowledge distillation on the residual pairs, retaining most of the FID improvement with minimal overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the residual learning framework improves FID across all five pre-trained DMs, with the largest gain on EDM (2.04→1.93 FID, 9.84→9.89 IS) and NSCNv2 (10.87→9.89 FID). On ImageNet 512×512, the most dramatic improvement is on ADM, reducing FID from 23.24 to 19.97 and IS from 58.06 to 61.54. On ImageNet 256×256, DiT improves from 9.62 to 9.49 FID and ADM-G from 4.59 to 4.47 FID. The framework also improves training-free fast samplers (DPM-Solver, DEIS) at 10–50 NFE, e.g., DPM-Solver-2(VP) at 10 NFE drops from 5.28 to 5.12 FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The framework improves every baseline it is tested against, with no losses. The largest absolute FID gains appear on weaker or higher-resolution models: NSCNv2 on CIFAR-10 (10.87→9.89, −0.98) and ADM on ImageNet 512 (23.24→19.97, −3.27). On already-strong models the gains are smaller but consistent: EDM on CIFAR-10 (2.04→1.93), ADM-G on ImageNet 256 (4.59→4.47), and INDM on CelebA (1.75→1.71). On ImageNet 256, DiT improves from 9.62→9.49 FID and LDM-8 from 15.51→14.47 FID, while ADM-G shows the smallest relative gain (4.59→4.47). The transferable correction (Table 5) confirms that a single correction function trained on EDM can improve NSCNv2 (10.87→10.01), DDPM (3.17→3.06), and SDE-VE (2.55→2.46) without retraining. On fast samplers (Table 7), DPM-Solver-2(VP) at 10 NFE improves from 5.28→5.12 FID and DEIS(VE) at 10 NFE from 20.89→19.94 FID, demonstrating that the correction generalizes to non-standard sampling trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SDE/PF ODE formulation of diffusion models (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and the score matching training paradigm (Song & Ermon, NeurIPS 2019), identifying the accumulated score estimation and discretization errors as the source of a sampling gap. It departs from prior gap-reduction strategies—high-order numerical solvers, latent-space score matching, MCMC initialization, discriminator guidance, and distillation—by instead training a separate lightweight correction function that maps intermediate ODE samples back toward the true data distribution, without modifying the base DM. The transferable correction property (one correction function improving multiple architectures on the same dataset) is positioned as distinct from prior transfer learning in generative models, which typically transfers weights or features across domains rather than correcting a shared sampling error pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the correction function offers a practical path to improving pre-trained diffusion models without retraining, and its demonstrated compatibility with fast samplers (DPM-Solver, DEIS) addresses the quality-speed tradeoff that plagues accelerated generation. However, the theoretical justification (Theorem 1) is a thin Lipschitz argument that does not rigorously establish why the residual pattern is architecture-invariant, and the FID improvements, while consistent, are modest (typically 0.02–0.11 on CIFAR-10). Open questions include whether the approach extends to conditional and text-to-image generation, how it interacts with classifier-free guidance, and whether the correction function degrades when the base DM is significantly undertrained or overfit.
