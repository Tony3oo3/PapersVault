## Problem

Video emotion analysis (VEA) suffers from limited labeled training data, as emotional annotation is expensive and subjective. Existing self-supervised MAE-style methods recover low-level pixel or contour information but fail to capture the high-level affective semantics and temporal emotion distribution that characterize how emotion degree varies across video segments. This paper bridges the affective gap between low-level visual information and high-level emotional cognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242143/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames, audio spectrograms, and text transcripts (extracted without human correction) from the video. Affective cues are obtained by looking up emotional words in SenticNet-5, yielding sentiment category, emotion category, and emotion degree per word. The output is a learned affective representation (encoder features) plus a recovered temporal emotion distribution per segment, which is then used to predict discrete emotion class labels and binary sentiment labels via a task-specific head.

## Method

MART consists of two modules: (1) Emotional Lexicon Extraction, which parses transcripts for emotional words via SenticNet-5 and verifies them through a three-stage hierarchical strategy (sentiment match → emotion class match → emotion degree match) using class activation maps from the VEA backbone; (2) Masked Emotion Recovery, which first applies Temporal Affective Complementary Learning (TACL) — a contrastive loss that pulls cross-modal features sharing the same sentiment toward the linguistic embedding while pushing modality-specific intrinsic features apart — then generates attention-guided masks from the cross-modal attention maps, masking high-attention tokens with a progressive ratio (10%→50%) while preserving the top-S tokens as recovery cues. The decoder recovers both patch-level features (Euclidean loss) and temporal emotion distribution (KL divergence), making the approach a plug-in self-supervised module for existing VEA methods.

## Results

On Ekman6, MART achieves 79.10% sentiment accuracy and 52.66% emotion accuracy, surpassing the best SSL baselines (MaskFeat: 76.14%/52.41%, MGMAE: 76.20%/51.98%). On VideoEmotion8, MART reaches 50.83% emotion accuracy (vs. 49.44% for VMAE), and on PERR it attains 57.89% emotion accuracy. For multimodal tasks, MART scores 66.29% emotion accuracy on IEMOCAP and 68.82% sentiment accuracy on AffWild2, outperforming all compared SSL methods. Overall, MART improves the supervised baseline by 6.04% on average across the twelve metrics in Table 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MART outperforms all ten SSL baselines on Ekman6 across all four metrics, with the largest gap over MaskFeat (79.10% vs. 76.14% Acc^S, +2.96%) and MGMAE (79.10% vs. 76.20% Acc^S, +2.90%). On VideoEmotion8, MART leads on emotion metrics (50.83% Acc^E vs. 49.44% for VMAE) but trails VMAE on sentiment accuracy (73.61% vs. 75.00% Acc^S). On PERR, MART achieves the best emotion accuracy (57.89%) but MGMAE edges ahead on sentiment F1 (61.22% vs. 59.25%) and emotion F1 (56.19% vs. 54.65%). For multimodal tasks, MART wins on all four IEMOCAP metrics (66.29% Acc^E vs. 66.08% for MoCov3) and all four AffWild2 metrics (68.82% Acc^S vs. 66.71% for VMAE), with the AffWild2 sentiment gap of +2.11% being the largest single-metric improvement in Table 2. Overall, MART is the top method on 10 of 12 metrics in Table 1 and all 8 metrics in Table 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

MART builds directly on the MAE encoder-decoder paradigm (He et al., CVPR 2022) but departs from its random masking strategy by introducing affective-attention-guided masking informed by Plutchik's emotion theory and SenticNet-5 lexicon lookups. The temporal affective complementary learning (TACL) loss is a novel contrastive objective that explicitly separates complementary (same-sentiment) and intrinsic (cross-modal) components, extending the triplet-loss perspective to multimodal affective alignment. Unlike prior video MAE methods (VideoMAE, MAEst, MGMAE) that recover low-level pixel or HOG targets, MART recovers temporal emotion distributions via KL divergence, positioning affective semantics as the reconstruction target. The method is designed as a plug-in module that can be integrated into existing VEA backbones such as MBT without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MART addresses the persistent data-scarcity bottleneck in video emotion analysis by extracting affective cues from unlabeled video without requiring large-scale external pretraining datasets (e.g., ImageNet or Kinetics), which is practically significant given the high cost of emotional annotation. However, the method's reliance on extracted text transcripts (subtitles) limits its applicability to videos lacking spoken or written language, and the handcrafted SenticNet-5 lexicon may not generalize to novel or culturally specific emotion expressions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-form video, whether learned affective embeddings could replace the fixed lexicon, and whether the progressive masking schedule generalizes beyond the tested 10–50% range.
