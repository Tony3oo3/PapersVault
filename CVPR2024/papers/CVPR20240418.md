## Problem

Existing feed-forward NeRF methods for sparse view synthesis are specialized to either small baselines (relying on feature matching and depth-guided sampling) or large baselines (data-driven, correspondence-unaware architectures), and no prior method performs well across both regimes. This limits practical applicability since real-world capture scenarios span a wide range of camera separations and view counts.

## Input / Output

The method takes K (K≥2) sparse RGB images with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics and a target view camera pose, and produces a rendered novel view image via volumetric rendering. To handle high-resolution inputs efficiently, the 3D volume is constructed at 1/8 spatial resolution (or 1/4 for 256×256 RealEstate10K) and upsampled to full resolution with a lightweight sub-pixel convolutional network. The radiance field is a 4-channel (RGB + density) volume over H×W×D sampled points, with hierarchical coarse-to-fine sampling (64 + 16 points per ray).

## Method

The key architectural insight is constructing the cost volume aligned with the target view frustum rather than a pre-defined reference input view, which prevents information loss from regions outside the reference view's epipolar geometry—critical for large baselines. A multi-view feature encoder (weight-sharing 2D CNN + K-view cross-attention Transformer built on GMFlow) extracts multi-scale features, and for each 3D point in the target volume, the method samples a 9×9 color window, multi-scale features, and pairwise cosine similarities (aggregated with learned visibility weights) to form the volume elements. The radiance field is decoded by a (2+1)D CNN—factorizing 3×3×3 convolutions into 2D spatial and 1D depth convolutions—that models 3D context between neighboring points, followed by an 8× upsampler. A hierarchical coarse-to-fine sampling stage refines the prediction using a compact 3D U-Net at full resolution, and training uses L1 + SSIM + LPIPS losses on random image crops.

## Results

On the DTU 3-view small-baseline setting, MuRF achieves 28.76 dB PSNR / 0.961 SSIM / 0.077 LPIPS, surpassing the previous best (ENeRF) by 1.15 dB PSNR. On the RealEstate10K 2-view large-baseline setting, MuRF reaches 24.20 dB PSNR / 0.865 SSIM / 0.170 LPIPS, outperforming the previous best (AttnRend) by 2.82 dB PSNR. On LLFF, MuRF trained with only 4 input views (25.95 dB PSNR) already exceeds all prior 10-view methods. In zero-shot generalization (trained on RealEstate10K, tested unseen), MuRF achieves 23.98 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 versus 14.00 dB for AttnRend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuRF outperforms the previous small-baseline SOTA ENeRF [16] by 1.15 dB PSNR on DTU (28.76 vs 27.61) and also beats a retrained ENeRF on RealEstate10K (24.20 vs 19.52), demonstrating that ENeRF's depth-guided sampling degrades severely under large baselines. Against the large-baseline SOTA AttnRend [8], MuRF gains 2.82 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K (24.20 vs 21.38) and a dramatic 10.19 dB on DTU (28.76 vs 18.57), confirming that AttnRend's correspondence-free Transformer design fails under small baselines. Among other feed-forward methods, MuRF surpasses MVSNeRF [3] (26.63), IBRNet [32] (26.04), GeoNeRF [15] (26.76), MatchNeRF [5] (26.91), GPNR [26] (18.55 on RE10K), and GNT [27] (25.53 on LLFF) across all settings. In the multi-view DTU setting (3/6/9 views, Table 4), MuRF beats even an enhanced ENeRF baseline (with doubled sampling points) by 1–2 dB PSNR, and matches or exceeds per-scene optimization methods like SPARF [29] (21.01 dB) in the 3-view case without any per-scene fitting. For zero-shot generalization, MuRF trained on RealEstate10K transfers to Mip-NeRF 360 at 23.98 dB PSNR versus AttnRend's 14.00 dB, a nearly 10 dB gap attributed to MuRF's explicit geometric inductive biases from the target view frustum volume and CNN decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuRF bridges two previously disjoint lines of sparse view synthesis: small-baseline methods (ENeRF, MVSNeRF, GeoNeRF) that rely on reference-view cost volumes and feature matching, and large-baseline methods (AttnRend, SRT) that discard geometric cues in favor of data-driven generic architectures. The key conceptual departure is constructing the volume in the target view frustum rather than a pre-defined reference view, which the authors argue is the critical mismatch that causes reference-volume methods to fail at large baselines. For feature extraction, the method builds on GMFlow's multi-view Transformer and MatchNeRF's cosine similarity matching, while the (2+1)D CNN decoder departs from the point-wise MLP or ray-wise Transformer decoders used by prior feed-forward NeRF methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuRF is the first feed-forward method to achieve competitive performance simultaneously across small and large baselines, different view counts, and diverse scene types (objects, indoor, unbounded outdoor), removing the need to choose a specialized model per setting. The target view frustum volume provides a clean, geometry-aware inductive bias that explains why prior reference-volume approaches fail at large baselines, offering a principled design principle for future volume-based methods. Open questions include extending to pose-free and dynamic scenes, and whether the approach scales to even larger scene-level datasets, which the authors note remain scarce.
