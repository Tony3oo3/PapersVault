## Problem

Data mixing methods (MixUp, CutMix, SaliencyMix) have been widely adopted in semi-supervised learning but remain unexplored in long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL), because in-batch mixing cannot adequately sample infrequent tail classes. Moreover, existing LTSSL re-balancing methods focus exclusively on data quantity and ignore class-wise uncertainty (entropy), which independently affects training difficulty and class performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images under long-tailed class distributions (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, STL-10 at 96×96, ImageNet-127 at 32×32 or 64×64), split into labeled and unlabeled sets with potentially different imbalance ratios (γ_l ≠ γ_u). It outputs standard class labels and operates as a plug-and-play module on top of existing SSL frameworks (FixMatch, ACR, DASO, etc.), requiring only weak and strong augmented views of each sample plus access to a class-wise mix bank.

## Method

BEM comprises two sub-modules. The Class Balanced Mix Bank (CBMB) stores samples per class and samples them using the effective number (β=0.999) to re-balance data quantity, while CamMix uses Class Activation Maps to extract tight bounding boxes for region-wise mixing, avoiding the redundant context of SaliencyMix. The Entropy-based Learning (EL) module re-balances class-wise uncertainty through three mechanisms: an entropy-based sampling strategy (ESS) that blends quantity-based and entropy-based sampling probabilities, an entropy-based selection module (ESM) that adaptively chooses between labeled and unlabeled data for mixing based on sample-wise entropy thresholds, and an entropy-based class balanced loss (ECB) that weights the classification loss by both effective number and class-wise entropy. The entire pipeline is end-to-end trainable and complements existing logit/pseudo-label re-balancing methods rather than replacing them.

## Results

On CIFAR10-LT (γ_l=100, γ_u=1), BEM combined with FixMatch achieves 86.8% top-1 accuracy, slightly surpassing DASO (86.6%) and substantially outperforming FixMatch alone (73.0%). When combined with ACR, BEM reaches 94.3% on the same setting, a 2.2-point gain over ACR alone (92.1%). On ImageNet-127 (32×32), ACR+BEM achieves 58.0% accuracy versus 57.2% for ACR alone and 29.7% for FixMatch, demonstrating a 28.3-point absolute gain over the base SSL framework. Across all benchmarks, BEM yields average gains of 11.8% over FixMatch, 4.4% over FixMatch+LA, 1.4% over FixMatch+ABC, and 2.5% over FixMatch+ACR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEM consistently outperforms all re-balancing baselines when combined with ACR, the strongest existing LTSSL method: on CIFAR10-LT (γ_u=1), ACR+BEM reaches 94.3% vs. ACR's 92.1% (+2.2 pts) and DASO's 86.6% (+7.7 pts); on STL10-LT (γ_l=20), ACR+BEM achieves 84.2% vs. ACR's 83.0% and DASO's 78.4%. Against DARP and CReST/CReST+, BEM shows larger margins (e.g., 86.8% vs. 82.5% for DARP and 83.2% for CReST on CIFAR10-LT γ_u=1). However, BEM underperforms DASO in the reversed setting (γ_u=1/100) on CIFAR10-LT (70.0% vs. 71.0%) and STL10-LT γ_l=10 (68.3% vs. 70.0%), which the authors attribute to data mixing's limited re-balancing capacity in extreme imbalance. On ImageNet-127, BEM alone (53.3% at 32×32) surpasses all prior methods including TRAS (46.2%) and CoSSL (43.7%), and ACR+BEM (58.0%) sets the new state of the art. BEM also generalizes across SSL learners, improving MeanTeacher by 41.1% on CIFAR10-LT and SoftMatch by 5.5% on the same dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of data mixing (MixUp, CutMix, SaliencyMix) and long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL), where data mixing had been entirely unexplored due to the incompatibility of in-batch mixing with class imbalance. It builds directly on SaliencyMix's localization idea but replaces the saliency map with Class Activation Maps for tighter region selection, and repurposes the effective number and class-balanced loss from Cui et al. [10] for both sampling and loss weighting. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant LTSSL paradigm (re-sampling, logit adjustment, pseudo-label alignment) by arguing that re-balancing data quantity alone is insufficient and that class-wise uncertainty (entropy) must also be addressed, introducing a dual re-balancing framework that is complementary to rather than competitive with existing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of BEM makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of LTSSL frameworks (FixMatch, ACR, DASO, FlexMatch, SoftMatch, MeanTeacher), and the consistent gains across datasets and imbalance ratios demonstrate practical utility. The conceptual insight that high-entropy classes with sufficient samples still suffer from training difficulty challenges the field's assumption that quantity-based re-balancing is the primary (or only) lever for class balance. However, the method shows diminished effectiveness under reversed distribution settings (γ_l ≠ γ_u), suggesting that data mixing alone cannot fully compensate for severe distributional mismatches, and the reliance on EMA-based entropy estimation introduces a dependency on training stability that may not transfer to all regimes.
