## Problem

Vision-only end-to-end autonomous driving systems (e.g., UniAD, VAD) achieve strong in-distribution planning performance but struggle with out-of-domain generalization to new cities and long-tail scenarios due to the absence of common-sense reasoning. The paper asks how the reasoning and world-knowledge capabilities of pretrained language models can be integrated into the perception-to-planning pipeline of an ADS to close this generalization gap.

## Input / Output

At inference, VLP consumes only 6 surround-camera images (same as the underlying UniAD/VAD backbone) and outputs planned 2D waypoints over 6 timesteps (3 s) plus all standard P3 downstream predictions (tracking, mapping, forecasting, occupancy, detection, segmentation). During training, ground-truth driving information (agent labels, 3D bounding boxes, future trajectories, high-level commands) is formatted into structured text prompts and fed to a frozen CLIP text encoder; these prompts are the sole additional input and are entirely discarded at test time, so no extra parameters or FLOPs are introduced.

## Method

VLP adds two contrastive-alignment modules to an existing vision-based ADS (UniAD or VAD). ALP (Agent-centric Learning Paradigm) crops the BEV feature map at ground-truth locations for ego-vehicle, foreground objects, and lane segments, pools each crop into a single vector, and applies an agent-wise InfoNCE loss to align these local BEV features with LM-encoded text descriptions of the expected agent state, thereby refining the BEV source memory. SLP (Self-driving-car-centric Learning Paradigm) encodes a text prompt describing the ego-vehicle's driving command and planned trajectory through the same frozen CLIP LM, then applies a sample-wise contrastive loss to align the ego-vehicle planning query with this linguistic planning feature, grounding the global decision-making in world-knowledge. Both modules use only a small trainable MLP adaptation layer (the LM weights are frozen), and the total training loss is a weighted sum of the standard ADS task losses plus the ALP and SLP contrastive terms. Because the LM head is removed at inference, VLP introduces zero additional parameters or computation relative to the base ADS.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, VLP-VAD (full SLP+ALP) achieves 0.55 m average L2 error and 0.15% average collision rate for open-loop planning, reducing L2 error by 29.5% and collision rate by 60.5% over the VAD baseline (0.78 m, 0.38%). VLP-UniAD achieves 0.74 m L2 and 0.16% collision rate, a 28.1% and 48.4% reduction over UniAD (1.03 m, 0.31%). Across secondary tasks, VLP consistently improves multi-object tracking (AMOTA 36.8 vs. 35.9 for UniAD), motion forecasting (EPA 0.621 vs. 0.598 for VAD), 3D detection (NDS 0.406 vs. 0.389 for VAD), and vectorized scene segmentation (mIoU 47.2 vs. 39.8 for VAD). In new-city generalization (train Boston → test Singapore), VLP-VAD reduces L2 error by 19.2% and collision rate by 48.7% over VAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLP outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every evaluated task. In open-loop planning (Table 1), VLP-VAD (0.55 m L2, 0.15% collision) dominates the strongest vision-only baselines VAD (0.78 m, 0.38%) and UniAD (1.03 m, 0.31%), and substantially surpasses earlier planners like ST-P3 (2.11 m, 0.71%), FF (1.43 m, 0.43%), and NMP (2.31 m, 1.92%). In multi-object tracking (Table 2), VLP-UniAD achieves AMOTA 36.8 and IDS 678, beating UniAD (35.9, 906), MUTR3D (29.4, 3822), QD3DT (24.2), and ViP3D (21.7). For motion forecasting (Table 4), VLP-VAD reaches EPA 0.621 vs. VAD's 0.598 and UniAD's 0.456, while also outperforming PnPNet (0.222) and ViP3D (0.226). In 3D detection (Table 6), VLP-VAD improves NDS from 0.389 to 0.406 over VAD. For occupancy prediction (Table 5), VLP-UniAD edges out UniAD (IoU-n 64.2 vs. 63.4) and clearly surpasses FIERY (59.4) and StretchBEV (55.5). In vectorized scene segmentation (Table 7), VLP-VAD with SLP alone jumps mIoU from 39.8 to 47.2 over VAD, a 18.6% relative gain. No baseline is matched or beaten by VLP in any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLP extends the end-to-end autonomous driving frameworks UniAD and VAD by injecting a frozen CLIP text encoder as a training-time contrastive supervision signal at two levels: agent-wise (ALP) to refine local BEV features, and sample-wise (SLP) to shape the ego-vehicle planning query. This departs from prior vision-only ADS that rely exclusively on numeric ground-truth supervision, and from earlier LM-for-driving works (DiLu, GPT-Driver, DriveGPT4) that use LLMs at inference time for closed-loop simulation or commentary rather than as a feature-space regularizer. The contrastive alignment mechanism is directly inspired by CLIP's vision-text pretraining objective, repurposed here to align BEV features with linguistic descriptions of driving ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLP demonstrates that a frozen, off-the-shelf language model can meaningfully improve end-to-end driving performance at zero inference cost, offering a practical path to incorporate common-sense reasoning into production ADS without architectural changes. The first new-city generalization study on nuScenes (Boston↔Singapore) is a valuable evaluation protocol the field lacked. However, the evidence is confined to a single dataset, camera-only modality, and two base architectures; whether the linguistic supervision transfers to LiDAR/radar inputs, larger-scale datasets, or truly out-of-distribution environments remains open. The reliance on ground-truth prompts during training also raises questions about how to obtain such supervision in unsupervised or self-supervised settings.
