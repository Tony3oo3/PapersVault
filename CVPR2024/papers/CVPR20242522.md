## Problem

Existing poisoned model detectors suffer from poor generalizability to unseen attack types, high computational overhead from training numerous shadow models, and requirements for large clean datasets or white-box model access. This paper addresses the gap of needing a trigger-agnostic detector that generalizes across diverse poisoning attacks while requiring only black-box query access to the target model and a small clean set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242522/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defender queries the target model as a black box (no knowledge of architecture, parameters, or attack characteristics) and provides a small clean set of only 2% of the original training samples. The output is a continuous SAP sensitivity score in [0,1] that is thresholded (typically τ ∈ [0.6, 0.7]) to yield a binary benign/trojan decision. The reference model is trained from scratch on the clean set, so no pre-existing model knowledge is needed.

## Method

A2D exploits the observation that poisoned models are more sensitive to adversarial perturbations than benign models, because the backdoor pathway inserted during poisoning creates an exploitable route through the network. The framework trains an unrelated reference model (e.g., ResNet-18) on the small clean set, then generates strong PGD adversarial samples against it at the minimal perturbation bound ε_min where the reference model's SAP (relative accuracy drop) reaches 1−ω. These adversarial samples are transferred to probe the target model, and the target's SAP score is computed from its clean and robust accuracy on the transferred attacks. If the target's SAP exceeds threshold τ, it is flagged as trojan. The SAP metric normalizes the accuracy reduction by clean accuracy, enabling fair comparison across models with different base accuracies, and the entire pipeline requires no knowledge of the target architecture or attack specifics.

## Results

On the three main image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, GTSRB), A2D achieves 93–100% detection accuracy (ACC) across all seven poisoning attack types, with FDR typically 0–6.8% and AUC ranging from 86.2% to 100%. The most striking results are on stealthy attacks (LC, SIG, ISS), where A2D reaches 95–100% ACC while most baselines collapse to 50% (random guessing). On the real-world chest X-ray dataset, A2D achieves 80–100% ACC versus 50–75% for the ET baseline. The method requires only black-box query access to the target model and a small clean set (2% of training data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2D outperforms all five main baselines (MNTD, ULP, NC, FRE, CAS) on the majority of attack–dataset combinations, with the largest margins on stealthy attacks. On CIFAR-10 and GTSRB, CAS [56]—the closest competitor sharing the same adversarial-sensitivity hypothesis—drops to 50% (random) on LC, SIG, and ISS attacks, while A2D maintains 95–100% ACC. NC [49] and FRE [50] also fail on clean-label and invisible attacks (e.g., NC scores 50% on LC and ISS for CIFAR-10; FRE scores 50% on LC and SIG for GTSRB), whereas A2D achieves 95–100%. MNTD [53] and ULP [24] are consistently the weakest, often scoring near or below 50% on sophisticated attacks. A2D's only marginal losses are on simple visible triggers: CAS achieves 99% vs. A2D's 96.8% on GTSRB Modify, and FRE reaches 90% vs. A2D's 86.6% on CIFAR-10 Blend. On the chest X-ray binary classification task, A2D (80–100% ACC) clearly beats ET [52] (50–75% ACC), particularly on WaNet and IAD where ET is at chance level.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the adversarial attack literature (PGD, FGSM) for the task of poisoned model detection, a direction only loosely explored in prior work. It is closest in spirit to Cassandra (CAS) [56], which also hypothesizes that poisoned models are more adversarially sensitive, but departs from CAS by eliminating the need for a meta-classifier trained on labeled benign/poisoned model pairs and by using standard per-sample adversarial attacks transferred from a reference model rather than universal perturbations. The SAP metric is a new normalized measure that enables cross-model comparison of adversarial sensitivity, and the IOU-based pathway analysis provides a mechanistic explanation absent from prior detection methods. Unlike meta-learning detectors (MNTD, ULP) or reverse-engineering purifiers (NC, FRE), A2D requires no knowledge of the attack type, no white-box access, and no shadow model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the combination of black-box access, zero attack knowledge, and a small clean set—conditions that match real-world model procurement scenarios where a defender receives a pre-trained model from a third party. The consistent generalization across seven diverse attack types (visible, invisible, clean-label, sample-specific) addresses a key weakness of prior detectors that fail on stealthy attacks. Limitations include the requirement for a clean sample set, evaluation restricted to CNN architectures (no ViT or non-visual modalities), and the adaptive-attack experiment showing that a sufficiently informed adversary could in principle reduce adversarial sensitivity at the cost of ASR. Open questions include whether the SAP signal persists for transformer-based models and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities such as NLP or audio.
