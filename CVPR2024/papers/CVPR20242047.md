## Problem

Unsupervised optical flow methods rely on brightness constancy and spatial smoothness assumptions that break down at occlusions and motion boundaries, where object-level information is absent. Traditional boundary-aware smoothness losses suffer from poor gradient landscapes that make them difficult to optimize. The paper addresses this by integrating object-level information from the Segment Anything Model (SAM) into unsupervised flow estimation, which had not been effectively combined before.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242047/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes two consecutive RGB frames resized to 256×832 (KITTI) or 448×1024 (Sintel) and produces a dense optical flow field at original resolution. SAM masks are generated by prompting the ViT-H model with ~1k grid points plus NMS post-processing, yielding ~64 masks per KITTI frame and ~83 per Sintel frame. Two settings are supported: SAM masks used only during training for loss computation (no inference overhead), or also fed as additional network inputs for mask feature extraction (slight inference overhead).

## Method

Built on the ARFlow iterative decoder backbone, UnSAMFlow introduces three SAM-guided adaptations. First, a semantic augmentation module performs self-supervised training by copy-pasting key objects (identified as SAM masks with maximal overlap with other masks) across samples with simple motion. Second, a homography smoothness loss replaces traditional boundary-aware smoothness: for each object region, a RANSAC homography is fitted to reliable flow correspondences, and the L1 distance between the current estimate and the homography-refined flow serves as a non-local, region-level regularization that avoids the flat gradient landscape of boundary-based losses. Third, a mask feature module converts SAM masks into a full segmentation representation (smallest-area mask wins for overlapping pixels, background mask for unassigned pixels), then applies max-pooling of image features within each mask region and concatenates the pooled features with the original features before correlation. The total loss combines photometric, semantic augmentation, and homography smoothness terms with equal weights of 0.1.

## Results

On KITTI-2015, UnSAMFlow (full model with +aug+hg+mf) achieves 7.83% Fl-all, outperforming the best prior unsupervised method UPFlow (9.38%) by 1.55 percentage points and SemARFlow (8.38%) by 0.55 points. On Sintel, the full model reaches 3.93 EPE (clean, all) and 5.20 EPE (final, all), beating UPFlow (4.68 / 5.32) and ARFlow (4.78 / 5.89). The model uses only 2.6M parameters, comparable to ARFlow (2.2M) and UPFlow (3.5M), and runs at 0.0334 s per frame on a Tesla P100 GPU (excluding SAM mask computation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnSAMFlow outperforms all unsupervised baselines on both KITTI and Sintel. On KITTI-2015 Fl-all, it beats UPFlow (9.38%) by 1.55 points, ARFlow (11.80%) by 3.97 points, and UFlow (11.13%) by 3.30 points. On Sintel clean-pass EPE (all), it improves over UPFlow (4.68) by 0.75 px and ARFlow (4.78) by 0.85 px. Against the closest semantic-guided unsupervised competitor SemARFlow [69] (8.38% Fl-all on KITTI), UnSAMFlow gains 0.55 points, attributed to SAM's instance-level masks versus SemARFlow's class-level semantic segmentation. Compared to supervised methods, the gap remains substantial (e.g., 7.83% vs. 4.49% for SAMFlow on KITTI), but UnSAMFlow requires no ground-truth flow labels. The paper also shows that even without SAM at inference (+aug+hg only, 8.18%), the method still surpasses UPFlow, confirming that SAM-guided training alone provides meaningful gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ARFlow (CVPR 2020) as its baseline network and adapts the semantic augmentation module from SemARFlow (ICCV 2023), but replaces class-labeled semantic segmentation with SAM's instance-level masks. The key conceptual departure is the homography-based regional smoothness loss, which the authors argue addresses a fundamental gradient-landscape problem in traditional boundary-aware smoothness losses that prior work overlooked. Unlike the concurrent SAMFlow (supervised), UnSAMFlow imports SAM outputs rather than SAM features, making the trained network reusable with any segmentation model that produces SAM-style masks, and requiring no ground-truth flow labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first effective integration of SAM into unsupervised optical flow, demonstrating that a zero-shot, class-agnostic foundation model can outperform class-labeled semantic segmentation for flow refinement. The homography smoothness loss offers a reusable technique for any task where regional parametric consistency is desirable. However, performance is bounded by SAM mask quality (degraded under motion blur, extreme lighting), and the absence of semantic class information in SAM output limits the type of object reasoning possible. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-frame flow, 3D scene flow, or video segmentation tasks where SAM's instance-level granularity would be even more valuable.
