## Problem

Existing zero-shot video editing methods extend self-attention across multiple frames to encourage consistency, but they still produce visible jittering and detail misalignment between frames, and the multi-frame self-attention incurs quadratic memory growth (e.g., 16× for four frames). The paper addresses both the lack of strict temporal coherence and the prohibitive memory cost of joint multi-frame attention in diffusion-based video editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241065/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (32 frames at 512×512) and a text edit prompt, and produces a temporally consistent edited video preserving the source motion and structure. Source frames are first inverted to noisy latents via DDIM inversion, then denoised jointly in chunks of B=4 frames using an off-the-shelf text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 or SD2-Depth) combined with a controlling method (PnP, ControlNet, or depth conditioning). The entire pipeline is zero-shot with no additional training.

## Method

VidToMe merges self-attention tokens across video frames using a bipartite soft matching algorithm (from ToMe) to enforce temporal consistency while reducing memory. Within each chunk of B frames, local token merging matches and merges tokens from B−1 frames into a randomly selected target frame (merging ratio p=0.9), so the self-attention module processes a unified token set and outputs consistent features. For long-term consistency, a set of global tokens is maintained across chunks and merged with each chunk's locally merged tokens before self-attention. After self-attention, tokens are unmerged back to their original per-frame sizes by assigning the merged token value to all restored positions. The merging is applied only in the first two downsample and last two upsample blocks of the UNet, reducing self-attention complexity from O(4N²) to approximately O(2N²) for four frames, and the entire operation is parameter-free and plug-and-play with any existing image editing controller.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark (20 videos, 32 frames each, 512×512), VidToMe+PnP achieves the best temporal consistency with Interpolation Error of 0.111, Inp. PSNR of 25.0, Warp Error of 0.013, and Frame CLIP Score of 0.975. VidToMe+SD2-Depth (same base model as Pix2Video) further improves Warp Error to 0.012 and Inp. PSNR to 25.6 while matching Pix2Video on Directional CLIP Score (0.168 vs. 0.166). In the user study, VidToMe variants achieve a 54.4% preference rate, more than triple the best baseline (Pix2Video at 17.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidToMe decisively outperforms all four baselines on temporal consistency metrics. Against the strongest baseline Pix2Video (which uses the superior SD2-Depth base model), VidToMe+SD2-Depth reduces Interpolation Error from 0.139 to 0.105, Warp Error from 0.020 to 0.012, and raises Inp. PSNR from 22.5 to 25.6. The three weaker baselines (Text2Video-Zero, Tune-A-Video, vid2vid-zero) are clearly inferior on all temporal consistency measures, with Interpolation Errors 1.5–2.2× higher than VidToMe. On text alignment, Pix2Video retains a slight edge in Directional CLIP Score (0.166) and Text CLIP Score (0.285) when compared against VidToMe+PnP (0.140, 0.271), but VidToMe+SD2-Depth matches or exceeds Pix2Video on both (0.168, 0.282). In the user study, VidToMe achieves a 54.4% preference rate versus 17.0% for Pix2Video and ≤16.7% for all other baselines, confirming a substantial perceived quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidToMe repurposes the Token Merging (ToMe) algorithm originally designed for ViT inference acceleration [5] and its Stable Diffusion adaptation [4] into a cross-frame consistency mechanism for zero-shot video editing. It departs from the dominant paradigm in prior video editing methods (Pix2Video, Tune-A-Video, vid2vid-zero, Rerender-A-Video, Fate/Zero) that extend self-attention into the spatio-temporal domain, which the authors argue increases memory quadratically without enforcing strict detail-level consistency. The closest conceptual relative is TokenFlow [13], which also unifies self-attention tokens for temporal consistency but relies on precomputed frame correspondences and joint keyframe editing, whereas VidToMe performs sequential local and global merging along the video without explicit correspondence extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, plug-and-play nature of VidToMe makes it immediately composable with any existing image editing controller (PnP, ControlNet, SD2-Depth), lowering the barrier to temporally consistent video editing without retraining. The memory reduction (from O(4N²) to ~O(2N²) for four frames) and sub-7 GB footprint make zero-shot video editing feasible on consumer hardware, broadening accessibility. However, the method inherits the quality ceiling of the underlying image editing technique, and similarity-based token matching can incorrectly merge visually similar but semantically distinct objects. Open questions include whether more precise matching (e.g., learned or flow-guided) can resolve these ambiguities and whether the approach scales gracefully to much longer videos or higher resolutions.
