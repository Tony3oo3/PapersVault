## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models produce images with uncontrollable and often physically inconsistent lighting, forcing artists to rely on uncertain prompt engineering. While recent control methods handle geometry (depth, normals) or object placement, none provide explicit, consistent illumination control, which is essential for setting mood and achieving cinematic or photo-realistic appearance.

## Input / Output

For image generation, the method takes a 512×512 direct shading map (encoding single-bounce illumination with cast shadows) concatenated with a normal map, plus a text prompt, and produces a lighting-controlled image. For relighting, it takes an input RGB image and a target shading map as conditioning. The shading maps are automatically estimated from a single image given a light direction and solid angle, and the dataset consists of 51,250 samples cropped from HDR panoramas in the Outdoor Laval Dataset with BLIP-2-generated captions.

## Method

The core pipeline has two stages. First, a shading estimation module (FeatureNet → multi-plane NDC unprojection → DensityNet 3D CNN → volumetric raymarching for cast shadows → ShadingNet → RefinementNet) predicts single-bounce direct shading maps from a single image, enabling paired image-shading dataset generation. Second, a ControlNet-inspired lighting control module is trained on frozen Stable Diffusion, but replaces the standard encoder with a custom Residual Control Encoder (RCE) and adds a Residual Control Decoder (RCD) with an auxiliary L2 reconstruction loss to prevent the encoder from dropping parts of the control signal—a failure mode observed with vanilla ControlNet. The key insight is that estimated single-bounce shading (including cast shadows) provides sufficient lighting information for coherent generation without requiring full light-transport decomposition, and the diffusion prior enables strong generalization to real-world relighting beyond synthetic training data.

## Results

For image generation, a user study (42 participants) shows LightIt is strongly preferred over Stable Diffusion on lighting consistency (95.57% vs 4.43%), overall image quality (60.86% vs 39.14%), and text alignment (55.86% vs 44.14%). For relighting, LightIt outperforms OutCast on PSNR (20.44 vs 19.79 dB), FID (64.18 vs 71.08), and image quality (63.53% vs 36.47% I-PQ), though OutCast retains a slight edge on lighting consistency (54.27% vs 45.74% L-PQ) due to stronger shadow rendering. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that direct shading conditioning yields substantially better lighting PSNR (13.04 vs 6.43) and angular error (27.30° vs 37.23°) compared to simple N-dot-L shading.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Stable Diffusion (SD) for image generation and OutCast for relighting. Against SD, LightIt wins decisively on all three perceptual metrics in the user study—lighting consistency by a margin of over 91 percentage points (95.57% vs 4.43%), image quality by ~22 points, and text alignment by ~12 points—confirming that explicit shading conditioning produces both more physically consistent lighting and higher overall image quality. Against OutCast on relighting, LightIt achieves better PSNR (+0.65 dB), lower FID (64.18 vs 71.08), and substantially higher image quality (63.53% vs 36.47% I-PQ), but concedes the lighting consistency metric (45.74% vs 54.27% L-PQ) because OutCast produces stronger, more perceptually distinct shadows. The authors attribute OutCast's L-PQ advantage to its synthetic-data training yielding more pronounced shadow contrast, while LightIt's diffusion prior yields more realistic overall appearance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet paradigm for conditional diffusion models by introducing lighting (shading + normal maps) as a new control modality, building directly on the zero-convolution architecture of ControlNet [50] and the frozen Stable Diffusion backbone [34]. For the shading estimation component, it adapts the density-field and volumetric ray-marching approach from OutCast [8], but repurposes it to generate paired training data from real-world panoramas rather than performing direct relighting. The key architectural departure is the Residual Control Encoder/Decoder (RCE/RCD), which addresses the 'sudden control pickup' problem observed in vanilla ControlNet by adding a reconstruction loss that ensures the full control signal is preserved in the encoded features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide explicit, physically-motivated lighting control in diffusion-based image generation, filling a gap that prompt engineering has been the only (unreliable) workaround for. The single-bounce shading representation is a practical simplification: it shows that diffusion models do not require full light transport, only direct shading with cast shadows, which can be estimated automatically from a single image. Limitations include restriction to directional (outdoor) lighting, dependence on a known light direction for optimal results, and the relatively small 51K-sample dataset. Open questions include extending to point/area lights and indoor scenes, combining with robust automatic light-direction estimation to scale the dataset, and whether the RCE/RCD architecture generalizes to other ControlNet-style conditioning signals.
