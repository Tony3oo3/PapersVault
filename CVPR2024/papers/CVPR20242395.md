## Problem

DreamBooth personalizes text-to-image diffusion models with strong subject fidelity and style diversity, but requires ~5 minutes of GPU training per subject and produces models exceeding 1GB in size. This limits practical deployment, as storing and training a full personalized model for each new subject is computationally and storage-prohibitive. The paper asks whether a single image can be used to generate a compact, high-fidelity personalization in seconds rather than minutes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image and a text prompt (e.g., "a [V] face in impressionist style") and produces personalized text-to-image generations of that face in diverse styles and contexts. The intermediate output is a set of Lightweight DreamBooth (LiDB) weights—only 30K parameters (~120KB)—that are composed into the frozen Stable Diffusion UNet and CLIP text encoder. The full pipeline (HyperNetwork prediction + 40-iteration rank-relaxed finetuning) completes in roughly 20 seconds, and the resulting personalized model is 10,000× smaller than a standard DreamBooth checkpoint.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-part pipeline. First, Lightweight DreamBooth (LiDB) further decomposes rank-1 LoRA residuals by factoring the A and B matrices through frozen random orthogonal incomplete bases (A = A_aux·A_train, B = B_train·B_aux), reducing the personalization space to 30K learnable variables. Second, a HyperNetwork with a ViT-H image encoder and a 2-layer transformer decoder (with positional embeddings to model inter-layer weight dependencies) predicts these LiDB weights from a single face image, trained on 15K CelebAHQ identities with a combined diffusion denoising loss and L2 weight-space loss, using iterative refinement (k prediction rounds). Third, rank-relaxed fast finetuning relaxes the LoRA rank from r=1 to r>1 after the HyperNetwork initialization and runs only 40 diffusion-loss iterations to capture high-frequency subject details, achieving 25× speedup over DreamBooth with comparable or superior fidelity metrics.

## Results

On 100 CelebAHQ identities with 30 prompts (30,000 samples total), HyperDreamBooth achieves 0.655 Face Rec., 0.473 DINO, 0.577 CLIP-I, and 0.286 CLIP-T, outperforming DreamBooth (0.618/0.441/0.546/0.282) and Textual Inversion (0.623/0.289/0.472/0.277) on all four metrics. The method personalizes a face in roughly 20 seconds—25× faster than DreamBooth and 125× faster than Textual Inversion—while producing a model 10,000× smaller (120 KB vs. >1 GB). In a user study, 64.8% of raters preferred HyperDreamBooth's identity preservation over DreamBooth, and 70.6% over Textual Inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamBooth, HyperDreamBooth wins on all four metrics (Face Rec. +0.037, DINO +0.032, CLIP-I +0.031, CLIP-T +0.004) while being 25× faster (40 iterations vs. 1,200) and 10,000× smaller in model size. Against Textual Inversion, the advantage is more pronounced on subject-fidelity metrics: DINO improves by +0.184 and CLIP-I by +0.105, reflecting that weight-space personalization captures far more subject detail than a single learned text token. Against E4T (the strongest concurrent fast-personalization method), HyperDreamBooth wins a user-study preference test 60% vs. 37.5% on combined identity and style fidelity, despite being trained on only 15K identities versus E4T's 100K; the authors note E4T tends to overfit to the reference pose and realistic appearance under stylized prompts. Aggressive DreamBooth variants (400 or 40 iterations) degrade substantially (DINO drops to 0.323–0.360), confirming that the HyperNetwork initialization is essential for fast convergence rather than simply reducing DreamBooth's iteration count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamBooth's subject-driven personalization paradigm and LoRA's low-rank weight-residual formulation, but departs from both by replacing iterative optimization with a learned HyperNetwork that predicts personalized weights in a single forward pass. The Lightweight DreamBooth (LiDB) concept further decomposes rank-1 LoRA residuals via a random orthogonal incomplete basis, reducing the personalization footprint from ~1.6 MB (LoRA) to ~120 KB. The use of a transformer decoder as the HyperNetwork's weight-prediction backbone is positioned as novel relative to prior HyperNetwork work (Ha et al., HyperStyle), which did not explicitly model inter-layer weight dependencies. The rank-relaxed finetuning step—expanding LoRA rank after HyperNetwork initialization—is a new optimization strategy not present in prior DreamBooth or LoRA personalization pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25× speedup over DreamBooth and 10,000× size reduction make per-subject personalization practically deployable at scale (e.g., millions of users each with a 120 KB model), which was previously infeasible. The rank-relaxed finetuning idea and the random-orthogonal-basis decomposition of LoRA space are transferable techniques that could apply beyond face personalization to other subject types and diffusion architectures. However, the method is currently validated only on faces with Stable Diffusion 1.5, and the HyperNetwork requires 15K training identities—open questions include generalization to objects, multi-subject scenes, and newer diffusion backbones. The reliance on a single reference image also limits the method's ability to capture full-body or multi-view identity.
