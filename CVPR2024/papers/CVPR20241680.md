## Problem

Existing video-based 3D human pose and shape (HPS) methods are paradoxically less accurate than the best single-frame methods, and prior approaches that estimate humans in global coordinates either assume a flat ground plane (causing foot sliding) or rely on computationally expensive optimization pipelines. No prior method efficiently recovers accurate, temporally smooth 3D human motion in a global coordinate system from a moving monocular camera while handling non-planar terrain such as stairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241680/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WHAM takes a monocular video sequence and the camera's angular velocity (from a SLAM system like DPVO/DROID-SLAM or a device gyroscope) as input, with 2D keypoints extracted by a frozen ViTPose detector. It outputs per-frame SMPL pose and shape parameters, a global root orientation and translation trajectory, and a foot-ground contact probability, all computed online at 200 fps (excluding bounding box, keypoint, and person-ID preprocessing). The unidirectional RNN design means the network processes frames sequentially with no future context, making it suitable for streaming applications.

## Method

WHAM uses a two-stage training scheme: first pretraining a unidirectional RNN motion encoder/decoder on AMASS synthetic 2D keypoint sequences (generated with dynamic cameras) to learn 2D-to-3D lifting, then fine-tuning on real video datasets while training a feature integrator that fuses the motion features with dense image features from a pretrained ViT/ResNet/HRNet encoder via a residual connection. For global trajectory estimation, a separate trajectory decoder recursively predicts root orientation and velocity by conditioning on the camera angular velocity appended to the motion features, effectively factoring camera motion from human motion. A contact-aware trajectory refiner then adjusts the egocentric root velocity using predicted foot-ground contact probability and a learned refinement network to eliminate foot sliding and handle elevation changes like stair climbing. The final global translation is obtained by integrating the refined per-frame velocities over time.

## Results

WHAM (ViT) achieves the best per-frame accuracy across all three benchmarks: 35.9 PA-MPJPE / 57.8 MPJPE / 68.7 PVE on 3DPW, 44.3 / 80.0 / 91.2 on RICH, and 50.4 / 79.7 / 94.4 on EMDB (all in mm), outperforming every prior per-frame and video-based method. For global trajectory estimation on EMDB 2, WHAM with DROID-SLAM achieves 133.3 WA-MPJPE₁₀₀, 343.9 W-MPJPE₁₀₀, 4.6% RTE, 21.5 jitter (10 m/s³), and 4.4 mm foot sliding, substantially outperforming SLAHMR (326.9 / 776.1 / 10.2 / 31.3 / 14.5) and TRACE (529.0 / 1702.3 / 17.7 / 2987.6 / 370.7). WHAM also maintains low acceleration error (6.6 m/s² on 3DPW), comparable to the smoothest temporal methods while retaining superior per-frame accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WHAM (ViT) outperforms all six per-frame baselines (SPIN, PARE, CLIFF, HybrIK, HMR2.0, ReFit) on every per-frame metric across all three datasets; the largest gap is on 3DPW where WHAM (ViT) achieves 57.8 MPJPE vs. ReFit's 65.3 and SPIN's 96.9. Against temporal methods, WHAM (ViT) also wins on per-frame accuracy: it beats TCMR (86.5 MPJPE on 3DPW), GLoT (80.7), MPS-Net (84.3), and VIBE (82.9) by 10–29 mm, while maintaining comparable acceleration error (6.6 vs. 5.0–6.5 m/s²). On global trajectory estimation (EMDB 2), WHAM with DROID-SLAM dramatically outperforms TRACE (jitter 21.5 vs. 2987.6; foot sliding 4.4 vs. 370.7 mm) and SLAHMR (WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ 133.3 vs. 326.9; RTE 4.6% vs. 10.2%), and also surpasses GLAMR (WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ 133.3 vs. 280.8). PACE is only reported on RICH (49.3 PA-MPJPE, 8.8 Accel); WHAM (ViT) improves PA-MPJPE to 44.3 while matching its smoothness (5.3 Accel). The only metric where WHAM does not lead is acceleration error among temporal methods, where TCMR and GLoT achieve slightly lower values (5.0–6.0 vs. 6.6 m/s²), though the paper attributes this to over-smoothing that degrades per-frame accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

WHAM extends the 2D-to-3D lifting pretraining paradigm from MotionBERT and ProxyCap (which use AMASS to synthesize keypoint sequences) by adding a feature integrator that fuses motion context with dense visual features from a pretrained image encoder, addressing the ambiguity of lifting sparse keypoints to full meshes. For global trajectory estimation, it departs from optimization-based methods (SLAHMR, PACE) that jointly fit camera and human motion against a learned prior, instead using a lightweight recurrent decoder conditioned on camera angular velocity from SLAM or a gyroscope. The contact-aware trajectory refiner is a novel addition not present in prior global tracking work, which typically assumes flat ground. The Neural Initialization scheme is borrowed from PIP for RNN hidden-state bootstrapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WHAM is the first video-based method to simultaneously outperform all single-frame methods on per-frame accuracy while maintaining temporal smoothness, closing a long-standing gap the authors identify as counter-intuitive. Its 200 fps online operation makes accurate world-grounded 3D human motion capture feasible for real-time applications (gaming, AR/VR, robotics) where prior optimization-based methods were impractical. Key limitations include reliance on AMASS (limiting generalization to out-of-distribution motions like complex interactions) and an assumption that the full body is within the field of view. Open directions include leveraging full SLAM scene geometry for scene-human consistency, optimization-based post-processing, and phone-based deployment.
