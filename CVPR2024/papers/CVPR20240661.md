## Problem

Existing personalized vision-language benchmarks (PerVL, DreamBooth, CustomConcepts101) fail to evaluate two critical properties of personalized concept learning: the ability to learn genuinely new meanings (rather than new names for known objects) and the ability to compose those new concepts with diverse known contexts. These datasets use common objects as concepts, offer very few contexts per concept, and often pair each context with only one concept, making it impossible to distinguish true concept-context compositionality from context or concept bias.

## Input / Output

For personalized TIR, the system receives 1–5 training images of a concept (photographed standing against a background) and at test time must rank a pool of 4,008 images against a text query that composes up to 3 concept identifiers with one of 101 context descriptions. For personalized TIG, the same 5 training images are used to personalize a diffusion model, which then generates 4 images per prompt. The dataset's concept-context matrix structure (20 concepts × 101 contexts, ~6 ground-truth images per query) ensures each concept appears in ~47 contexts on average and each context combines with ~9 concepts, preventing shortcut solutions based on context or concept alone.

## Method

ConCon-Chi introduces chimeric concepts—physical puppets crafted by combining two unrelated objects (e.g., a bird made from a spray bottle)—to model genuinely novel visual meanings, alongside 14 common concepts that serve as hard negatives from the same semantic categories. The 101 contexts are organized into 9 kinds (accessorizations, property modifications, renditions, actions, interactions, re-contextualizations) and include both general contexts applicable to all concepts and specific contexts targeting particular categories, with fine-grained variants that require distinguishing different interactions with the same entities. The evaluation protocol benchmarks personalized TIR methods (PALAVRA, Pic2Word, SEARLE) and TIG methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) against CLIP-based description baselines, revealing that while personalization methods learn to recognize chimeric appearances, they fail to compose these new concepts with known contexts or with each other in multi-concept queries. For TIG, the availability of real ground-truth images enables Density and Coverage metrics beyond the standard FID_cpt/FID_ctx trade-off, exposing that generated images have lower diversity and realism on chimeric concepts than on common ones.

## Results

On the ConCon-Chi personalized TIR benchmark (k=5 training images), SEARLE achieves the best mAP of 30.74%, mRR of 43.83%, and R@1 of 33.49%, outperforming both PALAVRA (23.59% mAP) and Pic2Word (26.39% mAP). Notably, SEARLE also surpasses the Discriminative CLIP oracle baseline (30.16% mAP), while PALAVRA and Pic2Word fall below it. For personalized TIG, DreamBooth (DB) with Stable Diffusion achieves the best Density (6.55% common, 4.81% chimeric at k=3) and improves both FID_cpt and FID_ctx over the pre-trained SDM, whereas Textual Inversion (TI) with SDM improves FID_cpt but degrades FID_ctx.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEARLE [2] is the strongest method on ConCon-Chi TIR, achieving 30.74% mAP at k=5, which exceeds the Discriminative CLIP oracle (30.16%) and outperforms PALAVRA [5] (23.59%) by 7.15 points and Pic2Word [30] (26.39%) by 4.35 points on mAP. PALAVRA and Pic2Word both underperform the Discriminative baseline, indicating that their learned tokens do not compose well with known contexts. For TIG, DreamBooth [27] with Stable Diffusion improves both FID_cpt (0.80 vs 0.74) and FID_ctx (0.24 vs 0.23) over the pre-trained SDM baseline, while Textual Inversion [6] with SDM improves FID_cpt (0.71 vs 0.74) but degrades FID_ctx (0.25 vs 0.23), suggesting over-fitting to training contexts. DreamBooth also achieves the highest Density (6.55% common, 4.81% chimeric) among all TIG methods, though Coverage remains low across all approaches, indicating limited diversity in generated samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of personalized vision-language modeling and the NLP concept of learning novel word meanings. It repurposes the chimeric-object idea from the Chimera dataset [17] (which distinguished learning new meanings from learning new names for known concepts) and applies it to the personalized TIR/TIG setting introduced by PerVL [5] and Textual Inversion [6]. The key departure from existing benchmarks (PerVL DF2, CustomConcepts101, ZS-CIR datasets) is the structured concept-context matrix design that simultaneously ensures high context diversity per concept and high concept diversity per context, addressing the context-bias and concept-bias problems identified in prior work. The paper does not propose a new method but instead positions itself as an evaluation framework, benchmarking existing methods (PALAVRA, Pic2Word, SEARLE, TI, DreamBooth) under a more rigorous compositional protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that current personalization methods, while capable of recognizing novel chimeric concepts in isolation, fail to compose them with known contexts or with each other—a gap that existing benchmarks with their limited context diversity could not expose. The finding that a hand-crafted Discriminative text description (an oracle with access to all concept distinctions) outperforms most learned methods, yet is itself beaten by SEARLE, suggests that the compositionality of learned tokens remains the central bottleneck. Open questions include whether the compositionality deficit is inherent to the token-learning paradigm or addressable through architectural changes, and whether the gap between generated and real image populations (measured via Density/Coverage) can be closed for chimeric concepts. The dataset's availability of real ground-truth images for every prompt also enables more rigorous generative evaluation than prior TIG benchmarks, which relied solely on CLIP-based similarity scores.
