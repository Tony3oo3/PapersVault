## Problem

Multi-modal LLMs rely on autoregressive generation conditioned entirely on input context, making output control dependent on prompt engineering that requires careful, task-specific design and is inaccessible to non-experts. No existing mechanism allows users to selectively emphasize specific spans of the input (text or visual) during generation without retraining or redesigning the full prompt, leaving fine-grained, token-level controllability unexplored for LLMs and VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241008/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and an optional RGB image, plus a user-defined binary highlight mask m that marks which tokens (text or visual) to emphasize. For direct-mapping VLMs (e.g., LLaVA), the mask aligns with image patches; for Q-Former-based models (e.g., InstructBLIP), it is applied inside the Q-Former's cross-attention layers over learnable queries. The output is a single autoregressively generated text sequence, produced with greedy decoding and KV-cache reuse, requiring no architectural modification to the base model.

## Method

Prompt Highlighter is a training-free, plug-and-play inference method that extends classifier-free guidance (CFG) to token-level partial highlighting. The user's binary mask m constructs two context branches: a normal context s and an unconditional context s̄ where highlighted token embeddings are scaled by a small factor α (0.01), effectively removing their semantic contribution. The CFG formula is then applied to next-token logits with guidance strength γ, amplifying the difference between the two branches. A second mechanism, attention activation, adds log(β)·m_i to the raw attention scores before softmax in every self-attention layer, multiplicatively boosting attention probability on highlighted tokens by a factor of β. For Q-Former-based VLMs, the same activation is applied to cross-attention scores between learnable queries and image patch features, steering visual focus without modifying the LLM backbone.

## Results

On the LLaVA-v1.5 13B backbone, Prompt Highlighter (with the full image highlighted) achieves 1552.5 on MME-perception, 69.7 on MMBench-dev, 69.5 on MMBench-test, and 87.8 on POPE, improving over the vanilla LLaVA-v1.5 baseline by +21.2, +2.0, +2.5, and +1.9 respectively. On MSCOCO captioning, the method reaches a CLIP score (S-CLIP) of 0.829 and image-to-text retrieval R@1 of 62.2, both state-of-the-art among caption-focused methods. The method requires no additional training and operates as a plug-and-play inference-time modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the directly comparable LLaVA-v1.5 baseline, Prompt Highlighter improves every reported metric: MME +21.2 (1531.3→1552.5), MMBench-dev +2.0, MMBench-test +2.5, and POPE +1.9, all without any fine-tuning. Compared to other strong VLMs, Prompt Highlighter on LLaVA-v1.5 surpasses QWen-VL-Chat by +65 on MME and +7.7 on MMBench-test, and outperforms mPLUG-Owl-2 by +12.3 on MME and +3.5 on MMBench-test. On the MSCOCO captioning task (Table 2), Prompt Highlighter achieves S-CLIP 0.829, beating FuseCap (0.785) by +0.044 and CoCa-CFG (0.808) by +0.021; it also leads on image-to-text retrieval R@1 (62.2 vs. 50.7 for LLaVA-v1.5) and R@5 (85.0 vs. 76.4). The method's advantage is most pronounced on retrieval and captioning metrics, where the attention-activation mechanism most directly anchors generation to visual content.

## Relation to Prior Work

Prompt Highlighter extends the classifier-free guidance (CFG) framework from diffusion models [Ho et al., 2022] and its autoregressive language-model adaptation (LLM-CFG) [Sanchez et al., 2023] in two key ways: (1) it replaces the requirement for a complete positive/negative prompt pair with a token-level binary mask that selectively highlights arbitrary spans within a single context, and (2) it introduces an attention-activation mechanism that reweights cross-token attention scores on highlighted tokens, drawing on the observation that attention maps in well-trained LLMs exhibit stable band-like patterns correlated with semantic significance. The method is positioned as a complement to prompt engineering rather than a replacement, and it generalizes across both direct patch-to-token mapping (LLaVA) and query-based mapping (Q-Former/BLIP-2) architectures in VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of Prompt Highlighter makes it immediately deployable on any pretrained transformer-based LLM or VLM, lowering the barrier for non-expert users to steer generation without crafting elaborate prompts. The demonstrated reduction in hallucination (via full-context highlighting) and the SOTA CLIP scores on MSCOCO captioning suggest practical value for reliable image description pipelines. However, the method incurs roughly 11% additional GPU memory and ~11% slower inference due to the dual-branch decoding, and its effectiveness remains dependent on the base model's quality. Open questions include whether more intuitive highlighting interfaces (e.g., natural-language descriptions of regions) can replace manual token selection, and whether the approach extends to multi-turn dialogue or video understanding.
