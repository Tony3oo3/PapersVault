## Problem

Existing 3D few-shot segmentation methods rely on a two-stage training pipeline (pre-training a learnable encoder on 'seen' classes, then episodic fine-tuning) that is both time-consuming and introduces a domain gap when generalizing to 'unseen' test classes. This paper asks whether a training-free, non-parametric encoder can match or exceed parametric baselines while eliminating both the domain gap and the training overhead entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241249/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds with per-point RGB color (M=2048 points per block) in an episodic N-way-K-shot format: K labeled support point clouds per target class and a set of unlabeled query point clouds. It outputs a point-level segmentation mask assigning each query point to one of N+1 semantic classes (N target + background) via cosine similarity to class prototypes. The non-parametric Seg-NN requires zero training; the parametric Seg-PN trains only a 0.24M-parameter QUEST module via episodic cross-entropy loss, with no pre-training stage.

## Method

Seg-NN encodes each point by projecting its 3D position and RGB color through trigonometric positional encodings with log-spaced frequencies into a shared 6d-dimensional space, then stacks embedding manipulation layers that apply hand-crafted linear projections sampled from low-frequency bands (Gaussian, uniform, or Laplace) to extract hierarchical local shape features while suppressing noisy high-frequency components. A U-Net-style upsampling integrates multi-scale features, and segmentation is performed by cosine similarity matching between query point embeddings and class prototypes obtained via masked average pooling. The parametric variant Seg-PN appends a QUEST module that computes cross-correlation (inner product of support/query feature statistics) and self-correlation (Gram matrix difference) to shift prototypes from the support-set domain toward the query-set domain, mitigating prototype bias from small support sets. This design eliminates pre-training entirely, reducing total training time by over 90% compared to prior methods while achieving SOTA mIoU on S3DIS and ScanNet.

## Results

On S3DIS, Seg-PN achieves 66.41% mIoU (2-way-1-shot) and 63.56% mIoU (3-way-5-shot), surpassing the previous SOTA (PAP3D) by +4.19% average mIoU across all four settings. On ScanNet, Seg-PN improves by +7.71% average mIoU over PAP3D, reaching 63.74% (2-way-1-shot) and 65.60% (3-way-5-shot). The non-parametric Seg-NN, requiring zero training, achieves 49.53% mIoU on S3DIS 2-way-1-shot—outperforming the trained DGCNN encoder (37.57%) and nearly matching ProtoNet (49.19%)—while Seg-PN reduces total training time from 4.7 h (PAP3D) to 0.5 h, a >90% reduction, using only 0.24M parameters versus PAP3D's 2.45M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seg-PN outperforms all 11 baselines across every setting on both S3DIS and ScanNet. Against the strongest prior method PAP3D [6], Seg-PN gains +3.65% (2-way-1-shot) to +2.52% (3-way-5-shot) mIoU on S3DIS and +7.23% to +9.51% on ScanNet, while using 10× fewer parameters (0.24M vs 2.45M) and 90% less training time. The non-parametric Seg-NN, despite having zero learnable parameters, surpasses the trained DGCNN encoder [24] by +11.96% mIoU (2-way-1-shot, S3DIS) and nearly matches ProtoNet [5] (+0.34%), demonstrating that hand-crafted frequency filters can extract competitive 3D features without any optimization. Compared to the closest non-parametric baseline Point-NN [35], Seg-NN improves by +7.16% (2-way-1-shot) and +9.81% (2-way-5-shot) on S3DIS, attributed to low-frequency filtering and position-color fusion. Among parametric few-shot methods, Seg-PN also beats 2CBR [38] by +7.47% (2-way-1-shot) and AttMPTI [37] by +11.55% on S3DIS, confirming that the QUEST module's query-support correlation effectively rectifies prototype bias. On the auxiliary tasks, Seg-NN sets a new training-free SOTA on ModelNet40 classification (84.2% vs Point-NN's 81.8%) and ShapeNetPart segmentation (73.2% vs 70.4%), while also outperforming all parametric few-shot classifiers in Table 8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Point-NN [35], a non-parametric 3D encoder based on trigonometric positional encodings, from general point cloud analysis to the few-shot 3D scene segmentation setting. It departs fundamentally from the standard few-shot 3D segmentation pipeline (pre-train a parametric encoder like DGCNN on seen classes, then episodically fine-tune a prototype head) by eliminating both training stages entirely. The parametric variant Seg-PN introduces the QUEST module, which uses cross-correlation and Gram-matrix self-correlation to shift prototypes from the support-set to the query-set domain, building on but differing from prior bias-rectification approaches like 2CBR [38] and cross-attention in PAP3D [6].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating pre-training reduces total training time by over 90% while achieving new SOTA on both S3DIS and ScanNet, making 3D few-shot segmentation far more accessible. The finding that a training-free encoder generalizes comparably across seen and unseen classes (Fig. 2a) challenges the assumption that learned 3D encoders are necessary for competitive few-shot performance. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted frequency sampling strategy scales to outdoor or large-scale 3D scenes, whether the non-parametric encoder can replace learned backbones in other 3D tasks beyond the classification and part-segmentation experiments shown, and whether QUEST's query-support interaction generalizes to zero-shot or long-tail settings.
