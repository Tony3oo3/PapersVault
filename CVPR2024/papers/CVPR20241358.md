## Problem

Existing VFI methods suffer from persistent blur and ghosting artifacts due to two root causes: unavoidable motion estimation errors that misalign warped features, and temporal supervision misalignment where a single ground-truth frame cannot capture the full distribution of valid intermediate frames. Symmetric blending of misaligned features from both sides exacerbates these artifacts, and conventional pixel-wise losses are inadequate for the inherent temporal uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241358/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive RGB frames (H×W×3) and a target temporal position t∈(0,1), along with bidirectional optical flows from a frozen RAFT or GMFlow estimator. It encodes both frames into L-level pyramid features and produces a single interpolated frame I_t by decoding a latent code z~N(0, τ) through an invertible normalizing flow conditioned on the blended pyramid features. Training uses 256×256 patches at batch size 16, while evaluation spans resolutions from 480P to 4K (2048×1080).

## Method

PerVFI introduces an Asymmetric Synergistic Blending (ASB) module that treats the two reference frames asymmetrically: one frame provides primary content while the other compensates for occluded regions, controlled by a self-learned sparse quasi-binary mask generated by an Adaptive Dilation Module (ADM) that dilates a binary occlusion mask with sample-dynamic attention. A Pyramid Alignment Module (PAM) uses multiscale deformable convolution guided by optical flow priors to improve alignment accuracy. For generation, a conditional normalizing flow (built on SRFlow's invertible layers) models the output distribution p(I_t|f_t), trained with negative log-likelihood loss plus a VGG-based perceptual auxiliary loss, avoiding the training instability of GANs and multi-step inference of diffusion models.

## Results

On DAVIS-2017 (480P), PerVFI achieves the best perceptual quality across all three video metrics: LPIPS 0.077, FloLPIPS 0.058, and VFIPS 87.51, outperforming the next-best method (AMT) by 7.94 VFIPS points. On DAVIS-2017 (1080P), PerVFI leads with LPIPS 0.114, FloLPIPS 0.087, and VFIPS 72.52, surpassing EMA-VFI by 11.65 VFIPS points. On Xiph-2K, PerVFI achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.038) and FloLPIPS (0.032) and highest VFIPS (68.67). However, PerVFI trades some PSNR/SSIM (e.g., 26.83 vs. 28.83 for EMA-VFI on DAVIS 480P) for substantially better perceptual quality, which the authors argue is the more meaningful criterion for VFI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerVFI consistently achieves the best perceptual quality (LPIPS, FloLPIPS, VFIPS) across all evaluated datasets, while trailing on PSNR/SSIM. On DAVIS 480P, PerVFI's VFIPS of 87.51 exceeds AMT (80.57), EMA-VFI (78.84), and STMFNet (77.38) by 7–10 points, and its LPIPS of 0.077 is 0.024–0.050 lower than all competitors. On DAVIS 1080P, the gap widens: PerVFI's VFIPS of 72.52 beats EMA-VFI (60.87) by over 11 points, and its LPIPS of 0.114 is 0.064 lower than EMA-VFI's 0.203. On Xiph-2K and Xiph-4K, PerVFI leads all perceptual metrics (e.g., VFIPS 68.67 vs. AMT's 65.60 on 2K; 57.47 vs. RIFE's 54.31 on 4K). The main trade-off is PSNR: STMFNet (28.55) and EMA-VFI (28.83) outperform PerVFI (26.83) on DAVIS 480P, and VFIFormer (36.38) and EMA-VFI (36.34) beat PerVFI (33.89) on Vimeo-90K. The authors argue this PSNR gap reflects the known poor correlation between PSNR and human-perceived quality in VFI, and their user study (33 participants, 98 videos) confirms PerVFI is preferred over all baselines in A/B perceptual tests.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerVFI builds on the conditional normalizing flow architecture from SRFlow [35] for its generator and adopts the softmax splatting and forward warping formulation from Niklaus and Liu [37] for feature alignment. However, it departs fundamentally from the standard VFI pipeline's symmetric blending assumption, arguing that equal-weight fusion of two warped features is the root cause of ghosting and blur when motion estimates are imperfect. The paper positions itself as the first to apply normalizing flows to VFI, contrasting its stable single-pass inference against GAN-based methods (e.g., STMFNet) that suffer training instability and diffusion-based methods (e.g., LDMVFI) that require many iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric blending paradigm with a quasi-binary mask offers a principled way to handle occlusion and motion errors without requiring perfect optical flow, which is a practical concern for real-world deployment. The demonstrated PSNR-perceptual quality trade-off (lower PSNR but substantially higher VFIPS) reinforces the growing recognition that pixel-level fidelity metrics are insufficient for VFI evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-frame interpolation beyond the two-reference setting, how it performs on very long sequences with cumulative drift, and whether the normalizing flow generator can be made faster for real-time applications at 4K resolution.
