## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D object detection suffers from point cloud sparsity and occlusion, which degrade detection accuracy. Existing remedies (shape priors, completion modules, occupancy estimation) require architectural modifications, limiting their transferability to new detector designs. No universal, plug-in technique existed that could improve arbitrary 3D detectors without altering their architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241526/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Student model consumes a single original LiDAR point cloud frame (sparse, potentially occluded), while the frozen Teacher consumes an Object-Complete frame constructed by aggregating points of the same object across multiple temporal frames via tracking and point cloud registration. Both produce 3D bounding boxes with class labels, but the Teacher's input is a superset of the Student's (F ⊂ F̃), giving it a near-complete geometric view of each object. In the semi-supervised setting, Object-Complete frames are generated without ground truth tracking labels using a pretrained detector, greedy tracking, and deep point cloud registration.

## Method

The framework inverts standard knowledge distillation: instead of simplifying the Student, it enriches the Teacher's input data (Object-Complete frames) and makes the Student architecturally more complex (extra convolutional layers on the BEV encoder) to handle the harder task of inferring complete shapes from sparse, occluded single-frame data. Object-Complete frames are built by tracking objects across a LiDAR sequence and merging their point clouds from different viewpoints using Point Cloud Registration (GeDi, Greedy Grid, or box-geometry alignment). The distillation loss combines backbone feature matching (MSE with a 1×1 conv adapter), head-level matching (KL divergence for classification, MSE for regression), and a detection loss on the Teacher's postprocessed predictions. In the semi-supervised variant, an Objects Temporal Fusion block (pretrained detection → greedy tracking → PCR) generates Object-Complete frames for unlabeled data, and the fine-tuned X-Ray Teacher produces higher-quality pseudo labels that replace the EMA-updated teacher in standard semi-supervised pipelines.

## Results

On Waymo Open Dataset (Level 1), X-Ray distillation improves SECOND-Scaled from 67.2 to 68.3 mAP (+1.1), CenterPoint-Scaled from 74.4 to 75.2 mAP (+0.8), and DSVT Pillar-Scaled from 79.5 to 80.1 mAP (+0.6). On NuScenes, gains are 0.7–0.9 mAP across CBGS, CenterPoint-Voxel, and Transfusion-L. In the semi-supervised setting on ONCE (Large split, 1M unlabeled), X-Ray Proficient Teacher achieves 69.10 mAP (SECOND) and 70.55 mAP (CenterPoint), outperforming the prior SOTA by +1.21 and +0.87 mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SECOND on Waymo L1, X-Ray SECOND-Scaled achieves 68.3 mAP vs. 67.2 mAP for the baseline (+1.1), while the unscaled X-Ray SECOND (67.0) is slightly below the baseline (67.2), indicating the distillation benefit is most pronounced with the scaled student. CenterPoint-Scaled improves from 74.4 to 75.2 mAP (+0.8) with X-Ray distillation, and DSVT Pillar-Scaled gains +0.6 mAP (79.5→80.1). On NuScenes, X-Ray consistently adds 0.7–0.9 mAP over CBGS (50.0→50.8), CenterPoint-Voxel (53.4→54.3), and Transfusion-L (56.1→56.8). In the semi-supervised ONCE benchmark, X-Ray Proficient Teacher outperforms the prior SOTA (Proficient Teacher) by +1.21 mAP on SECOND (67.89→69.10) and +0.87 mAP on CenterPoint (69.68→70.55) in the Large split, and by +0.89 to +1.42 mAP across all splits for both Mean Teacher and Proficient Teacher variants. Notably, the X-Ray Teacher itself (trained on Object-Complete frames) reaches 85.1–89.3 mAP on Waymo L1, far exceeding any student, confirming the information gap that distillation exploits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the standard Knowledge Distillation paradigm (Hinton et al., 2015) by training a 'weaker' Teacher on enriched Object-Complete frames and distilling to a 'stronger' Student on original sparse data — the first weak-to-strong generalization in 3D computer vision. It departs from prior occlusion-handling approaches (DDiT, SINE, Shape Occupancy Probability) that require new architectural modules or shape priors, instead offering a plug-in training technique compatible with any existing LiDAR detector. For semi-supervised settings, it integrates with established pseudo-labeling frameworks (Mean Teacher, Proficient Teacher) by replacing their teacher's input with temporally-fused object-complete point clouds, rather than proposing a standalone semi-supervised method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in nature of X-Ray Distillation means it can be applied to any future 3D detector without architectural modification, addressing a practical limitation of prior occlusion-handling methods. The 20 mAP diagnostic gap between original and object-complete frames quantifies how severely occlusion and sparsity bottleneck current detectors, suggesting substantial headroom if complete-shape information could be accessed at inference. Open questions include whether the Objects Temporal Fusion block generalizes to highly dynamic scenes with frequent occlusion, whether the approach extends to camera-based or radar-based detection, and whether the modest 1–2 mAP gains scale with more complex student architectures or larger datasets.
