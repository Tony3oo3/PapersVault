## Problem

Recent single-view 3D inference methods built on 2D diffusion (e.g., Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer) generate novel views that are not mutually 3D-consistent, necessitating a costly score-distillation optimization to recover a coherent 3D shape. This distillation step reduces both inference efficiency and the diversity of generated outputs. The paper asks whether multi-view consistency can be enforced directly during generation, eliminating the need for a separate 3D fitting stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240712/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a set of desired camera poses (full perspective camera matrices, not restricted to 3-DoF orbit cameras) and produces a set of mutually consistent novel-view RGB-D images. RGB is decoded at high resolution via the frozen Stable Diffusion VAE, while depth is predicted at the latent resolution. The multi-view depth maps can be trivially unprojected to yield a coarse 3D point cloud without any additional optimization.

## Method

MVD-Fusion adapts the pretrained Zero-1-to-3 single-view diffusion backbone to model the joint distribution over multiple views by inserting depth-guided 3D cross-attention layers into the U-Net. At each denoising timestep, the current noisy depth estimate is used to sample D=3 depth points from a Gaussian centered on the unbiased depth (scaled by the diffusion variance schedule); these 3D points are then used to project features from all target views and the input view, with Plücker embeddings of query and reference rays providing geometric context. A transformer with learned attention weights aggregates these multi-view features into view-aligned conditioning vectors that are cross-attended at multiple U-Net levels. The U-Net is further modified to predict an additional normalized depth channel alongside RGB, and the camera parameterization is extended from 3-DoF to full perspective matrices to handle real-world datasets like CO3D. Training uses a standard denoising diffusion objective on ~400K filtered Objaverse instances with 5 randomly sampled views per instance, and inference employs classifier-free guidance at 2.0.

## Results

On the Objaverse test set (100 instances), MVD-Fusion achieves 21.19 PSNR, 0.835 SSIM, and 0.146 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming SyncDreamer (19.22/0.817/0.176) and Zero-1-to-3 (17.37/0.783/0.211). On the out-of-distribution Google Scanned Objects dataset, MVD-Fusion reaches 19.53 PSNR and 0.175 LPIPS, maintaining consistent gains over both baselines. For 3D reconstruction on GSO, MVD-Fusion achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.031, the best among all direct (optimization-free) methods and competitive with optimization-based approaches like SyncDreamer (0.026) and Zero-1-to-3 (0.034).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVD-Fusion consistently outperforms Zero-1-to-3 [19] across all three view synthesis metrics on both Objaverse (+3.82 PSNR) and GSO (+2.11 PSNR), and also beats it on 3D reconstruction Chamfer Distance (0.031 vs. 0.034). Against SyncDreamer [20], MVD-Fusion wins on PSNR and LPIPS for view synthesis (e.g., 21.19 vs. 19.22 PSNR on Objaverse) but is slightly worse on SSIM for GSO (0.790 vs. 0.796) and on Chamfer Distance for 3D reconstruction (0.031 vs. 0.026), where SyncDreamer's optimization-based NeuS fitting gives it a small edge. On CO3D with perspective cameras, MVD-Fusion substantially outperforms PixelNeRF [49] on perceptual quality (0.701 vs. 0.484 SSIM; 0.220 vs. 0.378 LPIPS) despite a slight PSNR deficit (17.16 vs. 17.64), which the authors attribute to PixelNeRF's blurry mean predictions. For 3D reconstruction, MVD-Fusion (0.031) beats all other direct methods—One-2-3-45 (0.063), Point-E (0.043), and Shap-E (0.044)—as well as optimization-based RealFusion (0.082) and Magic123 (0.052), establishing it as the strongest optimization-free 3D inference approach tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVD-Fusion builds directly on Zero-1-to-3's pre-trained single-view novel-view diffusion backbone and adapts it to model the joint distribution over multiple views, following the multi-view generation paradigm established by MVDream, Viewset Diffusion, and MVDiffusion. Its key conceptual departure is from SyncDreamer's 3D volume bottleneck (which unprojects features onto a full 3D grid and interpolates frustums); instead, MVD-Fusion uses intermediate noisy depth estimates to guide where along each ray to sample cross-view features, making the consistency mechanism both more efficient and directly yielding a 2.5D geometry estimate. This positions the work as a geometrically-grounded alternative to score-distillation pipelines (DreamFusion, RealFusion, Magic123) that require costly per-object optimization to extract 3D from generated views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the score-distillation step, MVD-Fusion enables diverse multi-sample generation from a single image and directly produces a coarse point cloud from the predicted depth maps without additional optimization, which is practically significant for applications requiring fast, multi-modal 3D inference. However, the paper acknowledges that multi-view consistency is only encouraged via inductive bias (not guaranteed), the depth maps capture only coarse geometry missing fine details visible in the RGB, and the method assumes clean unoccluded objects, leaving cluttered or partially visible scenes as an open challenge. The gap between the coarse depth-derived geometry and the high-fidelity RGB suggests that a hybrid approach combining direct depth extraction with a lightweight optimization pass may be the natural next step.
