## Problem

Existing head avatar methods face a fundamental trade-off: radiance-field approaches (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) achieve photorealistic novel-view rendering but lack explicit animation handles, while parametric-model-based methods offer controllability but struggle with fine geometric details like hair, wrinkles, and mouth interior. The paper tackles the gap of producing a single representation that is simultaneously photorealistic and fully controllable in expression, pose, and viewpoint, generalizing to unseen motions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video (16 synchronized RGB cameras at 802×550 resolution) of a single subject performing prescribed and free expressions, along with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics. A photometric head tracker (Face2Face-based) pre-extracts per-frame FLAME parameters (shape β, translation t, pose θ, expression ψ, vertex offset Δv). The output is a set of 3D Gaussian splats each bound to a specific FLAME triangle, enabling real-time rendering from arbitrary viewpoints and animation via FLAME parameter manipulation (self-reenactment, cross-identity reenactment, or manual control).

## Method

Each FLAME triangle is paired with a 3D Gaussian whose position, rotation, and anisotropic scaling are defined in the triangle's local coordinate frame (origin at triangle centroid, axes from edge direction, normal, and cross product, scaled by edge length). During rendering, these local parameters are transformed to global space, so the Gaussian translates, rotates, and scales rigidly with its parent triangle as the mesh animates. A binding inheritance strategy ensures that when adaptive density control splits or clones splats, the new splats inherit the parent triangle index, preserving the rigging throughout optimization. Two thresholded regularization losses—position (penalizing local offset beyond 1× triangle scale) and scaling (penalizing local size beyond 0.6× triangle scale)—prevent splats from drifting off the mesh or growing large enough to cause jittering artifacts under novel expressions. FLAME parameters are jointly fine-tuned with the Gaussian parameters under an L1 + D-SSIM photometric loss over 600K iterations, allowing the mesh to correct tracking inaccuracies while the Gaussians compensate for geometric details the parametric model cannot capture.

## Results

On the NeRSemble dataset (9 subjects, 16 views, 802×550 resolution), GaussianAvatars achieves the best novel-view synthesis across all metrics: PSNR 31.6 dB, SSIM 0.938, and LPIPS 0.065, outperforming the strongest baseline AvatarMAV (PSNR 29.5, SSIM 0.913, LPIPS 0.152) by a wide margin. For self-reenactment, the method achieves the best SSIM (0.910) and LPIPS (0.076), while INSTA edges it slightly on PSNR (26.3 vs. 26.0), which the authors attribute to imperfect FLAME tracking affecting pixel-wise alignment. The LPIPS advantage in self-reenactment is particularly large (0.076 vs. 0.168 for AvatarMAV), indicating substantially better perceptual fidelity in expression transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianAvatars decisively outperforms all three baselines on novel-view synthesis, gaining +2.1 dB PSNR over AvatarMAV (the closest competitor) and +4.9 dB over PointAvatar, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.065 vs. 0.097–0.152). In self-reenactment, the method wins on SSIM and LPIPS against all baselines, with a particularly large perceptual gap over AvatarMAV (LPIPS 0.076 vs. 0.168), but INSTA narrowly leads on PSNR (26.3 vs. 26.0) due to pixel-level misalignment from imperfect FLAME tracking. Qualitatively, the paper notes that PointAvatar exhibits dotted artifacts from its fixed point size, INSTA produces noisy regions around the neck/shoulders where tracked meshes misalign, and AvatarMAV struggles with novel expressions because it conditions only on 3DMM expression vectors without explicit deformation priors. Cross-identity reenactment (Fig. 4) further confirms the advantage: GaussianAvatars transfers complex dynamics like wrinkles and eye blinks faithfully, while INSTA suffers aliasing beyond its occupancy grid, PointAvatar's deformation is inconsistent with FLAME, and AvatarMAV shows large degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established pillars—3D Gaussian Splatting for high-fidelity radiance field rendering and the FLAME parametric face model for expression/pose controllability—into a unified avatar representation. It departs from prior NeRF-based head avatars (INSTA, HeadNeRF, IMavatar) that rely on implicit representations and nearest-triangle warping by instead explicitly attaching anisotropic Gaussians to fixed triangles via a local coordinate frame, and from point-based methods (PointAvatar) by leveraging the optimizable scale and rotation of Gaussians. The key methodological departure is the binding inheritance strategy, which allows the adaptive density control (splitting/cloning/pruning) of 3D Gaussian Splatting to operate without breaking the parametric rigging, a problem not addressed in concurrent articulated-Gaussian works (SplatArmor, HUGS, GART).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit Gaussian-to-triangle rigging with local-space parameterization and targeted regularization (position and scaling losses with thresholds) yields substantially better animation fidelity than implicit or nearest-neighbor warping approaches, closing a notable gap in photorealistic head avatar reenactment. However, the method inherits the limitation of 3D Gaussian Splatting in that it captures the radiance field directly without decoupling material and lighting, precluding relighting. Open questions include extending controllability beyond the FLAME mesh to hair and accessories, and whether the binding inheritance strategy generalizes to full-body or multi-person scenarios.
