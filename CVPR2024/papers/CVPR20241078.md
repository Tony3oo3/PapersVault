## Problem

Existing 3D motion estimation methods target general large motions and fail to reliably detect or quantify subtle vehicle motions (e.g., a parked car beginning to move or a car reversing), which are safety-critical indicators of changing driving intent. The core difficulty is the 'swimming effect': sparse LiDAR points lack cross-frame correspondences even for static objects, so apparent point displacement from sensor motion obfuscates true small motions, making them indistinguishable from noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes five consecutive LiDAR frames (0.5 s window) for a single object, with ego-motion already factored out via ICP/GPS, and voxelizes them into a binary spatial-temporal grid of shape [T, Wx, Wy, Wz] (100×100×100 per frame). It outputs a static/moving classification, a per-point 3D motion flow vector (from frame 1 to frame 5, extracted by looking up the flow in the voxel containing each raw point), and an intermediate densified local occupancy grid. The network is object-centric, processing one object at a time, and runs at 27 fps for a 30-object scene.

## Method

S'More uses a two-stage encoder-decoder pipeline: the first network performs local occupancy completion, densifying the sparse visible surface by warping points from the other four frames into each target frame (using ground-truth object motion) to build supervision, thereby providing stronger shape cues for correspondence reasoning. The second encoder-decoder consumes the completed occupancy grid and predicts both a static/moving label and a per-voxel flow vector. A key design choice is restricting completion to locally observed regions rather than full shape reconstruction, avoiding noise from hallucinated geometry. Training is end-to-end supervised with five losses (BCE for occupancy, BCE for motion class, L1 flow, a scale-aware relative loss inverse-scaled by flow magnitude to emphasize small displacements, and an angular loss for direction). The model is trained exclusively on small-motion samples (f_min < 0.2 m), which empirically outperforms mixed large+small training, and uses 2D convolutions by folding height and temporal axes into the channel dimension for efficiency.

## Results

On the Waymo subtle-motion benchmark (f_min < 0.2 m, f_thre = 0.05 m), S'More achieves an end-point error (EPE) of 0.0437 m, an angular error of 0.3189 rad, and an F1 score of 0.8323 for static/moving classification, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. The method runs at 27 fps for a scene of 30 objects. On large motions (f_min > 0.2 m), S'More still achieves near-perfect detection (F1 = 0.9847) but trails CenterPoint on flow precision (EPE 0.2790 vs. 0.1155), confirming the paper's focus on the small-motion regime where the swimming effect is most damaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S'More outperforms all five baselines on every metric in the subtle-motion regime. Against the classical ICP baseline [5], S'More reduces EPE by 21% (0.0437 vs. 0.0554) and angular error by 29% (0.3189 vs. 0.4499), while improving F1 by 8.7 points (0.8323 vs. 0.7456). The leading scene-flow method FastNSF [24] is the weakest baseline, with S'More cutting its EPE by 63% and angular error by 43%, and lifting F1 by 21.4 points. Among the ICP variants, Point-to-Plane ICP [43] achieves the best F1 (0.7856) but has the worst EPE (0.2263); S'More dominates it on all three metrics. The detection-and-tracking method CenterPoint [54] is competitive on F1 (0.7270) but suffers from high angular error (0.5622) due to imperfect 3D box localization, which the authors identify as the key limitation for small motions. On large motions (Table 3), CenterPoint reverses the EPE advantage (0.1155 vs. 0.2790) and angular error (0.0247 vs. 0.0486), though both methods reach near-perfect F1, underscoring that S'More's occupancy-completion strategy is specifically designed for the small-motion regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

S'More carves out a new sub-problem within 3D motion perception: instantaneous detection and estimation of subtle (small-magnitude) vehicle motions from LiDAR, as opposed to the general large-motion focus of prior scene flow methods (FastNSF, FlowNet3D) or sequence-level moving object detection (PointMotionNet, SemanticKITTI). It repurposes occupancy completion—previously used for self-supervised feature learning (ALSO) or 4D occupancy forecasting (Khurana et al.)—as a denoising mechanism to counteract the LiDAR swimming effect, which the authors identify as the core obstacle to accurate small-motion estimation. The framework is object-centric and trained exclusively on the small-motion regime, departing from both detection-tracking pipelines (CenterPoint) and classical registration (ICP variants) that the authors show degrade precisely in the subtle-motion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap in autonomous driving: detecting the instant a parked car begins to move or a vehicle starts reversing, where safety-critical decisions must be made. The curated benchmark (~140k training, 9k test samples from Waymo) fills a genuine void, as no prior dataset or evaluation protocol targeted subtle motions specifically. Limitations include handling only vehicles (not pedestrians/cyclists), sensitivity to extreme sparsity or heavy occlusion, and reliance on reliable ego-motion estimation via ICP/GPS. Open questions include whether the occupancy-completion strategy generalizes to other sensor modalities or object categories, and whether the small-motion-only training regime can be extended to a unified model that handles both subtle and large motions without the observed performance trade-off.
