## Problem

Few-Shot Class Incremental Learning (FSCIL) has been dominated by shallow backbones like ResNet-18 because large pre-trained models suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned and fail to acquire domain knowledge when frozen. Existing prompt-based continual learning methods (L2P, DualPrompt) also underperform in FSCIL due to insufficient knowledge transfer from limited prompt capacity. The paper asks whether large pre-trained vision and language transformers can be made into effective few-shot incremental learners and, if so, how.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242392/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone) and class name strings (e.g., 'a photo of [class]') fed into a frozen BERT encoder to produce language embeddings. Classification is performed via a frozen prototype classifier (mean feature vectors per class) rather than a learned linear head, and the output is a single class label. During the base session the model sees many classes with full training sets; during each incremental session it receives only k=5 samples per novel class, with no access to prior-session data.

## Method

PriViLege introduces Pre-trained Knowledge Tuning (PKT), which fine-tunes only the first L=2 layers of a pre-trained ViT while freezing the rest, and injects two prompts: a B-Prompt (prefix-tuned with input-dependent modulation prompts extracted via 1×1 convolutions from the MSA and MLP outputs to scale key/value vectors) and a VL-Prompt (prompt-tuned, containing a vision token and a language token). An entropy-based divergence loss (L_ED) forces the vision token to learn discriminative knowledge distinct from the [CLS] token by minimizing both tokens' cross-entropy against a frozen prototype classifier while maximizing the KL divergence between their softmax outputs. A semantic knowledge distillation loss (L_SKD) distills BERT class-name embeddings into the language token of VL-Prompt, regularized by a cross-entropy term to prevent the language token from drifting into an undesirable region of the visual feature space. After the base session the ViT is frozen and only the prompts are updated in incremental sessions, where L_ED is dropped due to insufficient samples.

## Results

PriViLege achieves state-of-the-art FSCIL performance across all three benchmarks using a ViT-B/16 backbone. On CUB200 (10-way 5-shot), it reaches 75.08% ALast and 77.50% AAvg, surpassing the previous best (CEC) by +9.38% in ALast. On CIFAR-100 (5-way 5-shot), it attains 86.06% ALast and 88.08% AAvg, outperforming WaRP by +20.58% in ALast. On miniImageNet (5-way 5-shot), it achieves 94.10% ALast and 95.27% AAvg, exceeding CEC by +13.36% in ALast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriViLege outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest FSCIL-specific baselines, it gains +9.38% ALast over CEC on CUB200 (75.08 vs. 65.70), +20.58% ALast over WaRP on CIFAR-100 (86.06 vs. 65.48), and +13.36% ALast over CEC on miniImageNet (94.10 vs. 80.74). Against prompt-based CIL methods adapted to FSCIL, the margins are even larger: L2P achieves only 15.41% ALast on CUB200 and 49.87% on CIFAR-100, while DualPrompt reaches 23.25% and 50.93% respectively, both far below PriViLege. NC-FSCIL, the strongest prototype-based FSCIL method, trails by 36.28% ALast on CUB200 (38.80 vs. 75.08) and 32.36% on CIFAR-100 (53.70 vs. 86.06). In the CLIP experiment (Figure 3), PriViLege also surpasses zero-shot CLIP performance, whereas CEC falls below it, confirming that the proposed losses are essential for leveraging large pre-trained vision-language models in FSCIL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on prompt tuning (Lester et al.) and prefix tuning (Li & Liang) as the core parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism, and on Hinton et al.'s knowledge distillation for the semantic loss. It departs from the dominant FSCIL paradigm of using shallow backbones (ResNet-18) by arguing that large pre-trained ViT/CLIP models can serve as effective few-shot incremental learners if the pre-trained knowledge is selectively preserved. The key conceptual departure from prior prompt-based CIL methods (L2P, DualPrompt) is the introduction of modulation prompts that scale the base prompt via input-dependent features from frozen ViT layers, addressing the limited plasticity of randomly initialized prompts in few-shot settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that shallow models are necessary for FSCIL by demonstrating that large pre-trained transformers, when properly tuned, can outperform shallow-model SOTA by 9–20% across standard benchmarks. This opens a path toward leveraging the rich domain knowledge in large pre-trained models for incremental learning, potentially reducing the data requirements for new classes. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets with modest class counts, and the method still requires a well-populated base session; the authors themselves flag the no-base-session scenario as future work. Whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-1000) or to more incremental sessions remains an open question.
