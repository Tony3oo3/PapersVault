## Problem

Existing nighttime dehazing methods fail to address two critical issues: (1) nighttime distortions (haze, glow, noise) from multiple active light sources are localized, coupled, and have inconsistent frequency characteristics that standard spatial-domain architectures cannot handle simultaneously, and (2) the domain gap between synthetic training data and real-world images causes dehazed outputs to exhibit unrealistic brightness levels. No prior work jointly tackles both the frequency-inconsistent distortion removal and the brightness realism problem in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single nighttime hazy RGB image (256×256×3) and produces a dehazed nighttime image at the same resolution. During training, the network operates at three scales (full, half, and quarter resolution) simultaneously, and the semi-supervised pipeline additionally consumes real-world hazy images without ground-truth labels, using them as brightness-realistic supervision signals. The output is a single dehazed image per input, evaluated with both full-reference (PSNR, SSIM) and no-reference (BRISQUE, MUSIQ) metrics.

## Method

SFSNiD is a multi-scale (3-scale) network whose core building block is the Spatial-Frequency Information Interaction (SFII) module. SFII first projects features into the frequency domain via FFT, applies a dynamic spectrum filter (FSDA) that learns channel-wise amplitude and phase weights through squeeze-and-excitation-style gating, then performs local 8×8-window self-attention (BLP) on the frequency-projected Q/K/V, and finally fuses frequency-domain and spatial-domain nonlinear mappings (BNM) via channel concatenation. For training, the paper uses a two-stage semi-supervised strategy: first supervised training on synthetic paired data, then retraining with pseudo-labels generated by the initial model on real-world hazy images, combined with a local window-based brightness loss (LBM) that constrains each non-overlapping window's dehazed brightness to a power-scaled version of the corresponding hazy window, ensuring globally realistic brightness while suppressing haze and glow.

## Results

On the seven imaging-model synthetic benchmarks (NHR, NHM, NHCL, NHCM, NHCD, NightHaze, YellowHaze), SFSNiD achieves the best SSIM and PSNR on 6 out of 7 datasets, with headline numbers of SSIM 0.978 / PSNR 33.18 on NHR and SSIM 0.979 / PSNR 38.15 on NHCL. On the game-engine datasets, it reaches SSIM 0.935 / PSNR 33.71 on GTA5 and SSIM 0.862 / PSNR 25.91 on UNREAL-NH. On the real-world RWNH dataset (no-reference metrics), SFSNiD attains the lowest BRISQUE score (30.98) among all methods, though AECRNet achieves a higher MUSIQ score (37.03 vs. 32.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFSNiD outperforms all 11 baselines on 6 of 7 imaging-model datasets in both SSIM and PSNR. The closest competitors are DF (TIP'23) and FSDGN (ECCV'22): on NHCL, DF achieves PSNR 37.38 vs. SFSNiD's 38.15 (+0.77 dB), and on NHCM, DF reaches PSNR 35.04 vs. 36.15 (+1.11 dB). On YellowHaze, SFSNiD wins SSIM (0.965 vs. FSDGN's 0.955) but trails FSDGN on PSNR by 0.08 dB (32.98 vs. 33.06), the only metric where a baseline edges ahead. On the real-world RWNH dataset, SFSNiD achieves the best BRISQUE score (30.98, lower is better), beating the next-best AECRNet (27.08) by a wide margin, but AECRNet scores higher on MUSIQ (37.03 vs. 32.12), suggesting SFSNiD's dehazed images may have slightly less perceptual quality in some regions despite better overall structural fidelity. Non-deep-learning methods (MRP, OSFD) are substantially outperformed across all metrics, with gaps exceeding 10 dB in PSNR on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nighttime single image dehazing (NiSID) line initiated by MRP, OSFD, NightHazeFormer, and GAC, which have primarily relied on synthetic data from imaging models or game engines. It departs from prior deep-learning NiSID methods by explicitly modeling the frequency-inconsistent nature of nighttime distortions (haze, glow, noise) through a Fourier-domain dynamic filtering module combined with Swin-style local window attention, and by introducing a semi-supervised retraining strategy with a local brightness loss to bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap. The spatial-frequency interaction design draws on frequency-domain image restoration work (Selective Frequency Network, Fourmer) and channel-attention mechanisms (SE-Net), but applies them in a coupled bidomain architecture specifically tailored to the multi-source, localized character of nighttime haze.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing NiSID methods trained on synthetic data produce dehazed images with unrealistic brightness when applied to real-world nighttime scenes, limiting their deployment value. The semi-supervised pseudo-label retraining combined with the local window brightness loss offers a lightweight, training-time-only solution that does not require real-world ground truth. However, the approach still depends on game-engine synthetic data for pseudo-label generation, and the brightness constraint is a heuristic power-function supervision rather than a learned model of scene illumination. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain filtering provides genuine gains over well-tuned spatial-only architectures at scale, and whether the semi-supervised strategy generalizes to other nighttime enhancement tasks (low-light, glare removal) beyond dehazing.
