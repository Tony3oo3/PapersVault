## Problem

High-resolution diffusion models rely on self-attention for long-range modeling, but attention's quadratic complexity in sequence length becomes prohibitive at high resolutions. Existing work addresses this through patchification or multi-scale downsampling, which compresses spatial representations and degrades high-frequency detail. No prior diffusion architecture handles full-resolution image sequences without either attention or global representation compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240449/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a noised latent vector from a pre-trained VAE encoder (32×32 for 256×256 images, 64×64 for 512×512 images, yielding sequence lengths of 1024 and 4096 respectively) along with a class label and diffusion timestep, and produces a noise prediction and diagonal covariance prediction at the same spatial resolution. Unlike DiT, no patchification is applied, so the full spatial grid is preserved as the sequence length throughout the network. For unconditional generation on LSUN, the class label embedding is simply removed.

## Method

DIFFUSSM replaces the attention mechanism in diffusion transformers with a gated bidirectional state space model (S4D) backbone, achieving O(L log L) sequence processing via FFT-based long convolution instead of O(L²) attention. The architecture flattens the image latent into a sequence and stacks 29 layers of a custom block that alternates a full-length bidirectional SSM core with hourglass feed-forward networks that expand and contract the sequence by a factor M=2, reducing MLP computation while preserving global context in the SSM. The SSM is made bidirectional by concatenating forward and backward passes, and multiplicative gating is applied to the output. Training follows the DiT recipe exactly (same VAE, variance schedule, EMA, and classifier-free guidance), with the noise-prediction network trained via MSE and the covariance network via the full variational objective, yielding a 673M-parameter model that uses ~20% fewer total Gflops than DiT-XL/2 while matching or exceeding its FID and sFID.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 without classifier-free guidance, DIFFUSSM-XL achieves FID 9.07 and sFID 5.52, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 9.62, sFID 6.85) while using ~20% fewer total Gflops (1.85×10¹¹ vs 2.13×10¹¹) and ~3× fewer training steps. With classifier-free guidance, DIFFUSSM-XL-G reaches FID 2.28 and sFID 4.49, nearly matching DiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.27, sFID 4.60) on FID and beating it on sFID. On ImageNet 512×512 with CFG, DIFFUSSM-XL-G achieves FID 3.41 and sFID 5.84, trailing DiT-XL/2-G (FID 3.04, sFID 5.02) but using 40% fewer training images and 25% fewer Gflops. On LSUN 256×256, DIFFUSSM achieves FID 3.94 (Church) and 3.02 (Bedroom), comparable to LDM (4.02 and 2.95 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against DiT-XL/2 [40], the strongest attention-based diffusion transformer. Without CFG, DIFFUSSM-XL clearly wins on both FID (9.07 vs 9.62) and sFID (5.52 vs 6.85) while using 20% fewer total Gflops and roughly 3× fewer training steps. With CFG, the FID gap narrows to 0.01 (2.28 vs 2.27) and DIFFUSSM wins on sFID (4.49 vs 4.60), but DiT retains a slight edge on IS (278.24 vs 259.13). At 512×512, DIFFUSSM trails DiT on FID (3.41 vs 3.04) and sFID (5.84 vs 5.02), though it was trained on only 302M images versus DiT's 768M (40% fewer) with 25% fewer Gflops. Against LDM [47], DIFFUSSM substantially outperforms LDM-4-G on FID (2.28 vs 3.95) and LDM-4 without CFG (9.07 vs 10.56). Against U-ViT [1], DIFFUSSM-XL-G slightly beats U-ViT-H/2-G on FID at 256 (2.28 vs 2.29) and clearly wins on sFID at 512 (5.84 vs 8.44). GAN-based methods (StyleGAN-XL FID 2.30, ProjGAN FID 1.59 on LSUN) still outperform all diffusion models on raw FID, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation of the diffusion paradigm rather than their specific architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFFUSSM sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based image generation (DDPMs, DiT, LDM) and state-space sequence models (S4, S4D, gated SSMs). It directly builds on DiT's formulation of image generation as sequence modeling over flattened latent representations, but replaces the self-attention mechanism with a bidirectional gated SSM backbone (S4D), drawing on the gated SSM encoder design from Mehta et al. and Wang et al. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion architectures is the elimination of both patchification and multi-scale downsampling for long-range modeling, arguing that the sub-quadratic SSM core can process full-resolution sequences without the representational compression that U-Nets and Transformers require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that attention is not a strict prerequisite for high-fidelity diffusion-based image generation, opening a path toward higher-resolution synthesis without the quadratic compute bottleneck. However, the evidence is strongest at 256×256 (matching or beating DiT with ~20% fewer FLOPs), while at 512×512 DIFFUSSM still trails DiT on FID (3.41 vs 3.04), suggesting the advantage may narrow or reverse at scale. The authors note limitations including the absence of text-to-image conditioning and masked training objectives, and they speculate that removing the attention bottleneck could benefit long-range diffusion in audio, video, and 3D domains—claims that remain untested.
