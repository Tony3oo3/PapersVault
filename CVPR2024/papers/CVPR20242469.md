## Problem

RGB-based 6D object pose estimation is degraded by noise and indeterminacy introduced by occlusions, cluttered backgrounds, and varying environments, leading to unreliable keypoint localization. While diffusion models excel at recovering determinate samples from noisy distributions, standard formulations start denoising from random Gaussian noise, discarding the useful pose priors already encoded in intermediate representations such as keypoint heatmaps. The paper addresses how to adapt the diffusion framework so that the denoising process begins from a meaningful, non-Gaussian distribution that preserves these priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and N=128 pre-selected 3D keypoints (via farthest point sampling on the object CAD model) as input. The image is cropped to a 256×256 ROI using Faster R-CNN/FCOS before processing. The output is a 6D pose (rotation and translation) obtained by solving a PnP problem (Progressive-X) from the predicted 2D keypoint coordinates. During inference, M=5 samples are drawn from the initial distribution and denoised over 10 DDIM steps, with the final prediction being the mean of the M denoised coordinate sets.

## Method

The core contribution is a Mixture-of-Cauchy (MoC)-based diffusion process that replaces the standard Gaussian terminal distribution. A ResNet-34-based initializer predicts keypoint heatmaps, which are normalized into probability distributions and characterized by a 9-component MoC model fitted via an EM algorithm. The forward process (Eq. 5) interpolates between ground-truth coordinates and the MoC distribution rather than standard Gaussian noise, generating supervision signals for training. The reverse process is implemented as a transformer encoder-decoder: the encoder extracts context from appearance features (f_app) via 1×1 convolution and positional encodings, while the decoder iteratively denoises keypoint coordinates using cross-attention with the encoder output and sinusoidal step embeddings. Training is two-stage (heatmap initializer, then diffusion model with L2 loss over all K steps and M samples), and inference is accelerated from 100 to 10 steps via DDIM.

## Results

On the LM-O dataset, 6D-Diff achieves a mean ADD(-S) of 79.6%, outperforming the previous best method CheckerPose (77.5%) by 2.1 points and ZebraPose (76.9%) by 2.7 points. On the YCB-V dataset, the method achieves 83.8% ADD(-S) and 87.0% AUC of ADD(-S), both the best among all compared methods, while being nearly tied with GDR-Net (91.6% vs. 91.5%) on AUC of ADD-S. The largest per-object gains on LM-O are on the 'ape' object (60.6% vs. 58.3% for CheckerPose) and 'glue' (92.0% vs. 88.7% for ZebraPose).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LM-O, 6D-Diff achieves the best mean ADD(-S) of 79.6%, beating the closest competitor CheckerPose (77.5%) by 2.1 points and ZebraPose (76.9%) by 2.7 points; the gap widens substantially against earlier methods such as CRT-6D (66.3%), GDR-Net (62.2%), and PVNet (40.8%). The only per-object loss is on 'duck' (67.2% vs. CheckerPose's 69.9%), while the method leads on all other seven objects. On YCB-V, 6D-Diff attains the best ADD(-S) (83.8%) and AUC of ADD(-S) (87.0%), surpassing CheckerPose (81.4%, 86.4%) and ZebraPose (80.5%, 85.3%); it is marginally behind GDR-Net on AUC of ADD-S (91.5% vs. 91.6%) but leads on the other two YCB-V metrics. Overall, the method consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary ADD(-S) metric across both datasets, with the largest margins over direct-regression methods (RePose, GDR-Net, SO-Pose) and correspondence-based methods (PVNet, HybridPose).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the denoising diffusion framework (DDPM, DDIM) for 6D object pose estimation, a task previously addressed by correspondence-based methods (PVNet, ZebraPose, CheckerPose) that directly regress or vote for 2D-3D correspondences. The key conceptual departure is replacing the standard Gaussian-noise starting point with a structured Mixture-of-Cauchy distribution derived from keypoint heatmaps, so the reverse process begins from a representation that already encodes pose-relevant priors rather than pure randomness. The MoC characterization and its EM-based fitting draw on finite Cauchy mixture estimation (Kalantan & Einbeck, 2019), while the overall pipeline (FPS keypoint selection, PnP solver, ResNet-34 initializer) follows established 6D pose estimation conventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating structured prior knowledge into the diffusion starting distribution (via MoC) yields meaningful gains over both standard Gaussian diffusion and direct-regression baselines, suggesting a broader principle: diffusion models for structured prediction tasks benefit from informed initializations rather than random noise. Limitations include dependence on 3D CAD models, an off-the-shelf detector, and the added overhead of EM-based MoC fitting per sample. Open questions include whether the MoC model is optimal for characterizing complex keypoint distributions, whether the approach extends to pose estimation without CAD models, and whether the 10-step DDIM acceleration can be pushed further without quality loss.
