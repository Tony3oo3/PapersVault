## Problem

GAN-based one-shot stylization methods struggle to preserve the structural integrity of input images, particularly for images containing rare or complex structural elements (e.g., hands, microphones) that were infrequently seen during training. Existing diffusion-based stylization approaches (DiffuseIT, InST) address the domain gap but still fail to robustly maintain the original structure and quality of the input image. The paper identifies that structure preservation does not inherently come from using a diffusion model, but requires an explicit mechanism to disentangle and control structural versus semantic information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a photorealistic input image and a single reference style image (which can be out-of-domain, e.g., a painting or cartoon) and produces a stylized version of the input that preserves its structure while adopting the style. Optionally, a text prompt can be provided to manipulate attributes of the input before stylization. The method requires per-style-image finetuning of the diffusion model, and is evaluated on face images (FFHQ, AAHQ, MetFaces) as well as non-face domains (AFHQ-dog, LSUN-church, DeepFashion).

## Method

OSASIS disentangles an image into a structural latent code (x_{t0}, obtained by forward DiffAE encoding at a tunable timestep t0) and a semantic latent code (z_sem, from the DiffAE semantic encoder). A Structure-Preserving Network (SPN), implemented as a 1×1 convolution, injects spatial information from the input image back into the denoising process to counteract structural degradation from the noisy encoding. The pretrained DDIM is finetuned (as ε_θ^B) using a combination of cross-domain and in-domain directional CLIP losses plus a reconstruction loss, following the MTG paradigm, to bridge the domain gap between photorealistic and stylized images without requiring a paired dataset. At inference, style and content are mixed by conditioning z_sem^style on low-level UNet feature maps and z_sem^in on high-level feature maps (with a tunable change point f_ch), while the structural latent x_{t0}^in anchors the overall layout. Text-driven manipulation is achieved by directly optimizing z_sem^in with CLIP directional loss before passing it through the same stylization pipeline.

## Results

On low-density FFHQ images (those with rarely encountered structural elements), OSASIS achieves the best ArtFID scores of 34.89 (AAHQ) and 33.20 (Prev), indicating strong stylization quality, while also attaining the highest ID Similarity of 0.7323 (MetFaces) and 0.7029 (Prev) for content preservation. For structure preservation, OSASIS records the lowest Structure Distance of 0.0295 on MetFaces, competitive with DiffuseIT (0.0300) on the same dataset. Overall, OSASIS is the most balanced method, leading or near-leading on all three metrics (ArtFID↓, ID Similarity↑, Structure Distance↓) across the three style-image sources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSASIS clearly outperforms both GAN-based baselines (MTG+HFGI and JoJoGAN+HFGI) on ID Similarity by a large margin (e.g., 0.7029 vs. 0.4063 and 0.4743 on Prev), confirming that diffusion-model-based encoding preserves identity far better than GAN inversion. Against MTG+HFGI, OSASIS also wins on ArtFID for AAHQ (34.89 vs. 36.39) and Prev (33.20 vs. 37.27), though MTG retains an edge on MetFaces ArtFID (38.02 vs. 43.20). Compared to DiffuseIT, OSASIS achieves substantially better stylization quality (ArtFID 33.20 vs. 48.18 on Prev) and matches or exceeds it on ID Similarity (0.7029 vs. 0.6994 on Prev), while DiffuseIT holds a slight advantage on Structure Distance for AAHQ (0.0309 vs. 0.0361) and Prev (0.0310 vs. 0.0391). Against InST, OSASIS dominates across all three metrics and all datasets, with the largest gap in ID Similarity (0.7029 vs. 0.2238 on Prev), reflecting InST's tendency to alter facial identity during stylization. In summary, OSASIS is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier stylization quality, strong content preservation, and competitive structural fidelity, whereas each baseline excels on at most one axis.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSASIS builds on two key prior works: DiffAE (Preechakul et al., CVPR 2022) for its semantic encoder and forward/reverse DiffAE encoding pipeline, and MTG (Zhu et al., ICLR 2021) for the directional CLIP loss strategy to bridge domain gaps in one-shot adaptation. It departs from the GAN-based one-shot stylization line (MTG, JoJoGAN, StyleGAN-NADA) by operating in a diffusion model framework, and differentiates itself from other diffusion-based stylization methods (DiffuseIT, InST) by explicitly prioritizing structure preservation through a dedicated structural latent code and a structure-preserving network (SPN). The content/style mixing mechanism—conditioning the semantic latent to low-level versus high-level UNet feature maps—is a new architectural choice not present in prior diffusion-based stylization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to stylize with out-of-domain reference images without structural artifacts is a practical capability that GAN-based methods fundamentally lack due to entangled style/structure codes, making OSASIS relevant for real-world applications where reference images may not match the training domain. However, the requirement to train a separate model per style image and the longer training time limit rapid deployment, and the paper acknowledges these as key limitations. Open questions include whether per-style training can be eliminated (e.g., via a universal adapter), whether the SPN approach generalizes to non-face domains without retraining, and whether the content/style mixing via feature-map-level conditioning can be made more interpretable or controllable.
