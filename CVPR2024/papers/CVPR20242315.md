## Problem

Recognizing multiple atomic activities (defined by road user type and motion pattern grounded in road topology) in traffic videos remains challenging: video-level representations from 3D CNNs or transformers cannot decompose individual activities within complex scenes, while object-aware models fail to link road structures with object motions. Additionally, the existing OATS benchmark suffers from severe class imbalance, using only 35 of 64 possible activity classes and omitting entire categories like group two-wheelers, which impedes evaluation of rare activities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242315/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of T frames (32 for OATS at 224×224, 16 for TACO/nuScenes at 256×768) and produces a binary multi-label vector over N_cl activity classes (64 total, 35 evaluated on OATS) via per-slot binary classifiers. Unlike standard slot attention where slots are permutation-invariant, each slot is rigidly allocated to a specific activity class, and an additional background slot (excluded from classification) is trained with a spatial mask to absorb non-activity regions. Attention maps per slot are also available for visualization, with thresholds of 0.5 (OATS) or 0.2 (TACO) for display.

## Method

Action-slot modifies slot attention in four ways: (1) a fixed number of slots K = N_cl is allocated one-to-one to activity classes, each feeding an independent binary classifier with BCE loss; (2) a background slot with mask supervision (L_bg) competes via softmax to push action slots toward activity-relevant regions; (3) a regularization term (L_neg) forces slots assigned to absent (negative) classes to attend nowhere, sharpening competition for positive-class slots; (4) slots are updated in parallel over the full spatial-temporal volume (N = T×H×W tokens) in a single pass rather than recurrently frame-by-frame, preserving holistic temporal context. Features are extracted by a 3D CNN backbone (X3D by default) with learnable 3D positional embeddings, and the total loss is L_act + 0.5·L_bg + 1.0·L_neg. The paper also introduces TACO, a 5178-clip synthetic dataset from CARLA with balanced coverage of all 64 activity classes, which serves both as a new benchmark and a pretraining source that yields 8–11 mAP gains when transferred to real-world OATS and nuScenes.

## Results

On the OATS dataset with Kinetics-400 pretraining, Action-slot achieves 48.2 mAP, outperforming the strongest video-level baseline CSN (44.8 mAP) by 3.4 points and the prior state-of-the-art OATS method (26.7 mAP) by 21.5 points. On the newly introduced TACO dataset (balanced, 64 classes), Action-slot reaches 54.4 mAP, surpassing CSN (44.0), X3D (38.3), and the best slot-based baseline Slot-VPS (36.0) by 10.4, 16.1, and 18.4 points respectively. With TACO pretraining followed by fine-tuning, Action-slot further improves to 59.5 mAP on OATS and 32.3 mAP on nuScenes, demonstrating strong cross-dataset transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Action-slot outperforms all 15 baselines across both OATS and TACO. Against video-level models, it beats CSN (44.8→48.2 mAP on OATS; 44.0→54.4 on TACO) and X3D (31.4→48.2; 38.3→54.4), with the largest gap over X3D at +16.8 mAP on OATS. Against object-aware models (ORN, ARG, OATS), Action-slot gains 26.4, 21.5, and 21.5 mAP respectively on OATS, and 32.2, 31.2, and 31.2 on TACO, indicating that graph-based object relation modeling is insufficient for holistic traffic activity understanding. Among slot-based baselines re-implemented with allocated slots, Action-slot surpasses SAVi (20.9→48.2), MO (24.8→48.2), and Slot-VPS (24.9→48.2) on OATS, with the parallel updating strategy and background/negative-class attention guidance being the key differentiators. On TACO, Action-slot excels particularly on grouped road-user activities (C+: 70.1, K+: 62.6, P+: 52.8 mAP), where object-aware models drop sharply (e.g., ORN P+: 13.9, ARG K+: 13.7), confirming that action-centric representations better capture group motion patterns without explicit object proposals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the slot attention mechanism (Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020) from unsupervised object discovery to supervised action-centric representation learning in traffic scenes. It departs from prior video slot-attention methods (SAVi, MO, Slot-VPS) in three key ways: allocating each slot to a fixed activity class rather than discovering arbitrary objects, replacing recurrent temporal slot updates with a parallel scheme that processes all frames simultaneously, and introducing a background slot with explicit attention guidance plus negative-class regularization. The work also positions itself against object-aware traffic models (ORN, ARG, OATS) that depend on external detectors and trackers, arguing that holistic video-level representations and object-level features each fail to capture the joint motion-topology structure needed for atomic activity recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TACO dataset (5,178 clips, 64 balanced classes, 4× larger than OATS) addresses a critical gap in traffic activity benchmarks where rare classes (e.g., group two-wheelers) are entirely absent, enabling fair evaluation of long-tail activities. The finding that explicit object-level supervision (ground-truth instance segmentation masks) actually degrades performance relative to learned attention guidance challenges the common assumption that more perceptual supervision always helps, suggesting that not all objects participate in activities and that forcing slot-object alignment can be counterproductive. The demonstrated transferability of TACO pretraining to real-world datasets (OATS, nuScenes) with large gains (+8 to +11 mAP) positions synthetic data as a practical tool for traffic understanding. An acknowledged limitation is that occluded activities still confuse slot attention, leaving open the question of how to handle overlapping spatial-temporal activity regions.
