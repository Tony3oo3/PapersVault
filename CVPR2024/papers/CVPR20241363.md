## Problem

Existing deep unfolding networks for compressive sensing suffer from two intertwined limitations: (1) insufficient feature extraction because CNN-based or Transformer-based feature representations are constrained to local inductive biases, and (2) inherent feature attrition between iterative stages, since each stage must produce a full reconstructed image output, causing information loss that degrades quality especially at low sampling rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241363/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives compressed measurements y = A·F_vec(F_B(x)) obtained by applying a learnable sampling matrix A to a block-partitioned, vectorized input image, and produces a fully reconstructed image after n=10 iterative stages. For CS-MRI, the sampling matrix is redefined as A = M·T (Cartesian undersampling mask times DFT) while the reconstruction module remains unchanged. Sampling rates range from δ=0.01 to 0.25 for natural images and δ=0.05 to 0.15 for FastMRI knee scans, with default feature width of 32 channels.

## Method

UFC-Net unfurls the Fixed-Point Continuation (FPC) optimization algorithm into n concatenated blocks, each comprising a Gradient Descent Update (GDU) module for data-fidelity minimization and a Convolution-guided Attention Module (CAM) for proximal mapping. Within CAM, the Multi-head Attention Residual Block (MARB) synergizes multi-head self-attention with AConv (3×3 + 1×1 convolution) structures to capture both local textures and long-range spatial dependencies in a single residual block. The Auxiliary Iterative Reconstruction Block (AIRB) introduces auxiliary variables z^k that are concatenated with the current feature map, processed through MARB, and carried forward to the next stage, thereby amplifying inter-stage information exchange and reducing feature loss. All FPC parameters (μ^k, τ^k, β^k, λ^k) are made learnable and updated via backpropagation, eliminating manual tuning. The network is trained end-to-end with MSE loss on the final reconstruction, and the soft-thresholding operator ν^k = τ^k/μ^k with its continuation schedule is embedded as a differentiable operation within each stage.

## Results

On the four standard image CS benchmarks (Set11, Set14, Urban100, General100), UFC-Net achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all sampling rates δ∈{0.01, 0.04, 0.10, 0.25}, with gains of 0.08–0.61 dB in PSNR over the strongest baseline (LTwIST or TCS-Net). On large-scale CIFAR-10 at δ=0.01, UFC-Net reaches 25.39 dB PSNR / 0.8308 SSIM, surpassing the previous best (OCTUF, 22.48 dB / 0.6930) by 2.91 dB. On the FastMRI CS-MRI task at δ=0.15, UFC-Net attains 29.46 dB PSNR / 0.7098 SSIM, outperforming the runner-up HiTDUN (29.10 dB / 0.6977) by 0.36 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFC-Net consistently outperforms all 25 compared baselines across every dataset and sampling rate tested. The closest competitor is LTwIST [20], which UFC-Net surpasses by 0.08–0.61 dB PSNR (e.g., 30.15 vs. 29.84 dB on Set11 at δ=0.10; 32.82 vs. 31.79 dB on Urban100 at δ=0.25). On large-scale CIFAR-10/100 at the extreme low rate δ=0.01, UFC-Net leads OCTUF [42] by 2.91 dB (CIFAR-10) and 3.96 dB (CIFAR-100), a substantially larger margin than at higher rates. On the CS-MRI task (FastMRI), UFC-Net beats HiTDUN [59] by 0.36 dB PSNR at δ=0.15 and by 0.39 dB at δ=0.05, while also improving SSIM by ~0.012. Among pure model-based methods (CSGAN, CSNet+, AutoBCS, CSformer), UFC-Net leads by 1.5–3.5 dB PSNR on CIFAR-10 at δ=0.01, confirming the advantage of the unfolding paradigm. No baseline is reported to outperform UFC-Net on any metric in any setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFC-Net extends the deep unfolding network (DUN) paradigm for compressive sensing by replacing the more common ISTA/TwIST-based iterative templates with the Fixed-Point Continuation (FPC) algorithm, which introduces an adaptive soft-thresholding schedule rather than fixed thresholds. Within the DUN lineage (ISTA-Net, AMP-Net, OCTUF, DPC-DUN, LTwIST), the paper's key architectural departures are the Convolution-guided Attention Module (CAM), which fuses multi-head attention with ConvNext V2 convolutions to capture both local and long-range dependencies, and the Auxiliary Iterative Reconstruction Block (AIRB), which passes auxiliary variables between stages to mitigate the information loss inherent in requiring a full image output at each iteration. The method also draws on the broader trend of hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures in CS (e.g., CSformer, TransCS) but embeds them within a principled optimization-unfolding framework rather than a purely end-to-end design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete, well-identified weaknesses of existing DUNs—insufficient feature extraction within each stage and feature attrition across stages—through targeted architectural components (MARB and AIRB) that are modular and potentially transferable to other unfolding-based inverse problems. The FPC-based unfolding offers a theoretically grounded alternative to ISTA/TwIST templates, and the learnable FPC parameters (μ, τ, β, λ) reduce manual tuning. However, the improvements over strong baselines like OCTUF and LTwIST are consistent but modest (typically 0.1–0.6 dB PSNR), and the fixed 10-stage depth, single-channel evaluation on most benchmarks, and lack of analysis on very low sampling rates (below 0.01) leave open questions about scalability and robustness. The auxiliary-variable mechanism is an interesting design choice whose interaction with deeper or variable-depth networks remains unexplored.
