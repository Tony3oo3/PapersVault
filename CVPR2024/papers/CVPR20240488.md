## Problem

State-of-the-art learning-based hair reconstruction methods rely on pre-training with manually created synthetic CG data, which is labor-intensive, costly, and introduces domain gaps with real-world captures. Existing optimization-based methods struggle with the 180° orientation ambiguity and cannot reconstruct the internal hair flow connected to the scalp, which is the standard representation used in film and gaming pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240488/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images, calibrated camera parameters, and a raw hair mesh (typically from OpenMVS) as input. It produces explicit 3D hair strand geometry as line segments organized in a guide-child hierarchy (653 guide strands growing from scalp vertices and 50,000 child strands interpolated via Sobol sampling), directly compatible with real-time rendering engines and standard CG tools like XGen or Blender. Unlike volumetric or neural representations, the output is editable strand geometry with correct absolute direction from scalp roots to tips.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) 3D orientation estimation via global optimization, where Gabor-filter 2D orientations are lifted to 3D via modified LPMVS, then a minimum spanning tree is built over neighboring points and orientations are propagated along it (100 randomized trials, best inner-product score selected) to resolve the 180° ambiguity; (2) gradient-domain strand initialization, which solves Laplace's equation in the volume between scalp and hair surface with Dirichlet boundary conditions from surface orientations and a heuristic scalp direction, then extracts guide strands by traversing the resulting orientation field from scalp to surface; (3) hierarchical strand optimization using a novel rasterization-based differentiable renderer for line segments that applies anti-aliasing across all pixels with differing adjacent triangle IDs for smooth gradients, combined with a Laplacian reparameterization (incorporating both geometric connectivity and kNN spatial proximity) to propagate dense gradients through the geometry. Optimization proceeds coarse-to-fine: guide strands are optimized first with depth, mask, and 3D tangent losses, then child strands are refined in two stages adding a 2D orientation loss, all using the Adam optimizer.

## Results

On synthetic hair data (Straight Hair, 2mm/20° threshold), Dr.Hair achieves F1=86.2 (P=88.2, R=84.3), substantially outperforming NeuralHaircut (F1=42.2), Strand Integration (F1=48.1), and LPMVS (F1=45.1). On the more challenging Curly Hair dataset at the same threshold, Dr.Hair reaches F1=68.9 (P=79.1, R=61.0) versus NeuralHaircut's F1=23.2, Strand Integration's 21.9, and LPMVS's 20.9. The most striking gap is in recall: Dr.Hair recovers 84.3% of internal strands on Straight Hair and 61.0% on Curly Hair, while NeuralHaircut recovers only 29.2% and 14.5% respectively. In terms of speed, Dr.Hair completes strand reconstruction in 25× the baseline surface-reconstruction time, compared to 2160× for NeuralHaircut, 81× for Strand Integration, and 57× for LPMVS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr.Hair outperforms all three baselines on every metric (P, R, F1) across both Straight and Curly Hair at all three threshold settings. Against LPMVS and Strand Integration, the gains are large in both precision and recall (e.g., F1 of 86.2 vs. 45.1/48.1 on Straight Hair at 2mm/20°), reflecting that these methods produce short, directionally inconsistent strands that fail to connect to the scalp. Against NeuralHaircut, the advantage is most dramatic in recall (84.3% vs. 29.2% on Straight Hair; 61.0% vs. 14.5% on Curly Hair), indicating that NeuralHaircut's soft rasterization and pre-trained prior cause it to miss the majority of internal hair flow, while its precision is also lower (76.1% vs. 88.2%). On the real H3DS dataset and a monocular smartphone video, Dr.Hair produces coherent scalp-connected strands whereas NeuralHaircut exhibits reversed orientation and LPMVS/Strand Integration show flying noise. In speed, Dr.Hair is roughly 86× faster than NeuralHaircut (25× vs. 2160× relative time) and about 3× faster than Strand Integration, making it practical for production pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: optimization-based hair capture (LPMVS, Strand Integration) that extracts 3D orientations from Gabor-filtered multi-view images, and learning-based strand reconstruction (NeuralHaircut, NeuralStrands) that relies on pre-trained priors and differentiable rendering. It departs from the learning-based camp by eliminating pre-training entirely, and from the optimization-based camp by introducing a novel differentiable rendering algorithm specifically for line segments (rather than meshes or point clouds) combined with a guide-child hierarchy borrowed from production CG tools. The Laplace-equation-based strand initialization and the reparameterization via a hybrid connectivity/proximity Laplacian are new contributions that extend gradient-domain processing and mesh reparameterization ideas to the line-segment domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for labor-intensive synthetic CG pre-training and achieving a ~100× speedup over NeuralHaircut (under one hour vs. days), this method makes high-quality scalp-connected strand reconstruction accessible in practical pipelines without domain-gap concerns. The substantial recall improvement over all baselines demonstrates that internal hair flow—previously inaccessible to surface-only methods—can be recovered through volumetric Laplace interpolation. Limitations include sensitivity to raw mesh quality, inability to handle discontinuous hairstyles (braids, spiky hair), and no material/lighting recovery, leaving open questions about dynamic capture, single-view settings, and integration with appearance estimation.
