## Problem

Existing text-to-video generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: template-based approaches (e.g., Tune-A-Video) achieve low training cost but severely limit generation freedom by locking motion to a single template video, while open-domain methods (e.g., AnimateDiff, Make-A-Video) offer high freedom but require millions of text-video pairs and massive compute. No prior work explores a few-shot setting that learns a common motion pattern from a small video set (8–16 clips) on a single GPU while preserving the semantic generalization of the underlying diffusion model.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a free-form text prompt and produces a 16-frame video at 320×512 resolution. The first frame is generated by SD-XL from the prompt, then the tuned SD-v1.4-based video model denoises the remaining 15 frames conditioned on that first frame's latent. Training requires only 8–16 self-collected videos (16-frame clips at 320×512) and a single motion prompt, fitting in ~15 GB vRAM on one A100 GPU with ~6 GB for inference.

## Method

LAMP decouples the T2V task into first-frame T2I generation (SD-XL) and subsequent-frame prediction (tuned SD-v1.4), so the video model focuses on motion rather than content, avoiding overfitting on the small training set. To capture temporal dynamics, 2D convolutions are inflated into temporal-spatial motion learning layers: a video-prediction-based 1D convolution (using frames i−1 and i to predict i+1) combined with a 2D convolution gated by a Sigmoid for spatial features, all zero-initialized. Self-attention is modified so that keys and values come exclusively from the first frame, anchoring subsequent frames to the initial content, and temporal self-attention layers are added across frames. During training, noise is added and loss is computed only on frames 2 through l while the first frame's latent is preserved; at inference, a shared-noise sampling strategy (ε^i = 0.2·ε^s + 0.8·ε^i) reduces inter-frame noise variance for improved temporal stability at negligible cost.

## Results

On the 48-video evaluation set covering 8 motion patterns, LAMP achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CLIP alignment of 31.35, frame consistency of 98.31, and generation diversity of 71.65 (lower is better). In a user study with 70 responses, LAMP is preferred by 46.84% of participants, compared to 22.15% for Tune-A-Video, 19.11% for AnimateDiff, and 11.90% for T2V-Zero. All experiments require only 8–16 training videos and ~15 GB VRAM on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAMP outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against AnimateDiff (the strongest baseline), LAMP improves alignment by +2.47 (31.35 vs. 28.88), consistency by +0.50 (98.31 vs. 97.81), and diversity by −1.82 (71.65 vs. 73.47, lower is better). Against Tune-A-Video, the gains are larger: +4.13 alignment, +3.44 consistency, and −13.07 diversity, reflecting Tune-A-Video's tendency to overfit the template video's content. Against T2V-Zero, LAMP leads by +4.41 alignment, +6.84 consistency, and −1.36 diversity, confirming that zero-shot T2I-to-video transfer cannot capture meaningful motion patterns. Notably, even when AnimateDiff is fine-tuned on the same data (AnimateDiff*†), its diversity degrades severely (81.91) due to content overfitting, whereas LAMP's motion-content decoupled pipeline avoids this failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAMP positions itself as a middle ground between open-domain T2V methods (AnimateDiff, Imagen Video) that require millions of text-video pairs and template-based methods (Tune-A-Video, FateZero) that lock generation to a single reference video. It builds directly on the LDM/SD v1.4 backbone, adopting ControlNet's zero-initialization for new layers, Tune-A-Video's temporal attention, and Text2Video-Zero's first-frame K/V attention trick, while introducing a motion-content decoupled pipeline that offloads content generation to SD-XL and a video-prediction-inspired 1D convolution for temporal modeling. The key conceptual departure is reframing T2V as first-frame T2I plus subsequent-frame prediction, so the tuned model only needs to learn motion rather than content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 8–16 videos on a single A100 (≈15 GB VRAM) can produce coherent motion-pattern videos lowers the barrier to video diffusion research substantially and suggests a practical path for researchers without large-scale data or compute. The motion-content decoupling insight—that a strong T2I model can supply content while a lightly tuned video model handles only temporal dynamics—is a reusable architectural principle likely to inform future efficient video generation. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 16 frames and 320×512 resolution, whether multiple motion patterns can be learned simultaneously, and whether the quality gap versus large-scale models narrows with more training data or longer sequences.
