## Problem

Existing lane graph detection methods from aerial imagery predict discrete 2D node positions, producing noisy, non-physical lane center lines that require extensive downstream smoothing and human oversight to become drivable. No prior method enforces direction continuity at lane boundaries or represents lane geometry with smooth parametric curves within a graph structure, leaving a gap between detected lane topology and the physically realistic, drivable lanes that autonomous vehicles require.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB aerial image crops (512×512 for full lane graph prediction, 256×256 for successor lane graph prediction) and outputs a Bezier Graph: a directed graph where each node carries a 2D position and a 2D direction unit vector, and each edge carries two positive length parameters that, together with the shared node positions and directions, fully define a cubic Bezier curve. This representation is approximately 90% more compact than the source lane graph in node count, and for large-area prediction the model tiles the image into overlapping 512×512 crops (14-pixel overlap) and aggregates local graphs into a global one.

## Method

The key insight is a shared-parameterisation of cubic Bezier curves over a graph: positions and direction vectors are attached to nodes (shared across all incident edges, enforcing C1 direction continuity at merges and splits), while per-edge length parameters allow different control-point distances for edges leaving the same node. BGFormer is a Deformable-DETR-style transformer encoder-decoder (based on Relationformer) that uses N learnable queries—N−1 node tokens and a single edge token—where a node head regresses position and direction per token, and an edge head predicts edge existence and length parameters from context triples (node context vectors concatenated with the edge token). Training uses Hungarian matching between predicted and ground-truth nodes with a combined loss of L1 position/direction regression, Focal classification loss, BCE edge probability loss, and MSE edge length loss. For large scenes, a tiling-and-aggregation pipeline applies the model to overlapping crops, prunes singleton nodes and 'corner-cutting' triangle edges, then merges boundary nodes across tiles using Hungarian matching with a direction-alignment penalty, averaging matched node positions and directions to maintain global consistency.

## Results

On the Full-LGP task (5000×5000 aerial images), BGFormer achieves an APLS of 0.229 and GEO F1 of 0.538 (with rotation and low threshold), substantially outperforming LaneGNN's APLS of 0.065 and GEO F1 of 0.491, while reducing inference time from 9,784 s to 416 s (>95% speedup). On the Succ-LGP task (256×256 crops), BGFormer achieves SDA20 of 0.492 and APLS of 0.772, compared to LaneGNN's 0.227 and 0.202, though at the cost of lower TOPO recall (0.501 vs. 0.699). The Bezier Graph representation also yields ~90% reduction in node count relative to the source lane graph while maintaining sub-pixel Hausdorff fit quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LaneGNN [3] on Full-LGP, BGFormer wins decisively on APLS (0.229 vs. 0.065, a 3.5× improvement), Graph IoU (0.137 vs. 0.112), and inference time (416 s vs. 9,784 s, a >95% reduction), while achieving comparable GEO F1 (0.538 vs. 0.491) and higher TOPO recall (0.464 vs. 0.303) at the expense of lower TOPO precision (0.431 vs. 0.554). On Succ-LGP, BGFormer substantially outperforms LaneGNN on SDA20 (0.492 vs. 0.227), SDA50 (0.549 vs. 0.377), and APLS (0.772 vs. 0.202), but trails on TOPO recall (0.501 vs. 0.699) and GEO recall (0.576 vs. 0.695), which the authors attribute to its additive prediction style omitting entire lanes when a single node is missed. Against LaneGraphNet [28] on Succ-LGP, BGFormer dominates across all metrics, as LaneGraphNet scores 0.0 on SDA and TOPO/GEO, indicating it fails to produce valid lane graphs on this task. The paper also reports a KL-divergence-based curvature metric showing BGFormer's predicted curvature distribution is consistently closer to ground truth (0.04±0.02 vs. 0.12±0.03 for LaneGNN), supporting the claim of more physically drivable lane geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Relationformer's deformable-DETR-style transformer for image-to-graph generation, adapting its learnable-query decoder to predict a novel Bezier Graph representation of lane topology. It departs from prior aerial-imagery lane detection methods (LaneExtraction, LaneGNN) that rely on multi-stage pipelines—segmentation, node sampling, GNN filtering—by predicting the full lane graph end-to-end in a single forward pass. The key conceptual departure from prior Bezier-curve lane work (LaneGAP, [8, 20]) is the shared-parameterisation: direction vectors live at nodes and are shared across all incident edges, enforcing direction continuity at merges and splits, while edge-specific length parameters allow branching. This is the first method to retain the Bezier parameterisation directly in the graph structure rather than discretising curves into nodes before aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bezier Graph formulation addresses a practical pain point in HD map generation: prior methods produce noisy, non-physical lane centre lines requiring extensive downstream smoothing and human curation, whereas BGFormer's output is inherently smooth and drivable, reducing post-processing to simple pruning. The >95% inference time reduction over LaneGNN (108s vs 9784s on 5000×5000 images) makes large-scale aerial lane mapping substantially more tractable. Open questions include whether the approach generalises beyond the single UrbanLaneGraph dataset, how it performs on denser or more complex road networks, and whether the Bezier Graph prior can be combined with real-time onboard sensing as the authors suggest. The lower recall on Succ-LGP (omitting entire lanes when a single node is missed) remains a limitation that the current architecture does not fully resolve.
