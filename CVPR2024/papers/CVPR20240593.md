## Problem

Existing reference-based 3D stylization methods (e.g., Ref-NPR) assume static scenes, leaving dynamic 3D scenes unaddressed. The core difficulty is the scarcity of stylized reference images along the temporal axis: a user provides one or a few stylized views at a single timestamp, yet the style must be coherently propagated to all other times and novel viewpoints without explicit per-timestamp supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240593/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained photorealistic dynamic radiance field (HexPlane) and a single stylized reference image rendered from a fixed camera pose at one timestamp (produced via NNST, ControlNet, or manual editing). It outputs a stylized dynamic radiance field capable of synthesizing novel views at fixed times, novel times at fixed views, or joint space-time view synthesis. The density component of the radiance field is frozen during stylization; only the appearance component is optimized, and view directions are zeroed to avoid view-dependent artifacts.

## Method

The key insight is generating temporal pseudo-references from the single stylized reference using a U-Net-based video style transfer model trained on small patches, thereby creating explicit style supervision at every timestamp. Coarse style transfer establishes a content-style mapping via VGG16 features: for each pixel in a rendered content view, the nearest matching content feature among pseudo-references is found, and the corresponding style features are concatenated into a guidance map that the stylized field must match (cosine distance loss plus a color-level L2 loss). Fine style transfer introduces Temporal Reference Ray Registration: pseudo-references are lifted into 3D via pseudo-depth into a voxel dictionary D(x,y,z,t), and rays from training views are matched to reference rays by spatial proximity and angular constraint, receiving stylized colors that the model must reproduce (MSE loss). Training uses a hierarchical strategy (keyframes first, then full sequence) with deferred back-propagation and a total-variation loss for spatio-temporal continuity.

## Results

On the real-world Plenoptic Video dataset, S-DyRF achieves the best Ref-LPIPS in both evaluation settings: 0.298 (fixing camera viewpoint) and 0.561 (fixing time), outperforming the next-best baselines by a clear margin. On the synthetic D-NeRF dataset, S-DyRF attains the best short-range and long-range temporal consistency in both settings (e.g., 0.025/0.051 fixing camera; 0.030/0.211 fixing time), though Texler et al. edges it out on Ref-LPIPS (0.593 vs. 0.605 fixing camera). A user study with 56 participants confirms strong human preference for S-DyRF over all three baselines (88%, 77.4%, and 70% win rates respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ARF* (adapted for dynamic scenes), S-DyRF substantially improves Ref-LPIPS on Plenoptic Video (0.298 vs. 0.513 fixing camera; 0.561 vs. 0.657 fixing time), while ARF* retains a slight edge in short-range consistency (0.006 vs. 0.012). Against Ref-NPR* (also adapted), S-DyRF wins on Ref-LPIPS in both settings on both datasets, with comparable consistency scores. Against Texler et al. (video style transfer), S-DyRF clearly wins on Plenoptic Video Ref-LPIPS (0.298 vs. 0.353; 0.561 vs. 0.585) and on all consistency metrics on D-NeRF, but Texler et al. achieves a marginally lower Ref-LPIPS on D-NeRF (0.593 vs. 0.605 fixing camera; 0.621 vs. 0.639 fixing time), suggesting that 2D video stylization retains some style fidelity advantage on synthetic data where geometric complexity is lower. Overall, S-DyRF is the only method that simultaneously leads in style fidelity on real-world data and temporal/spatial consistency on synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends reference-based 3D scene stylization (Ref-NPR) from static to dynamic scenes by introducing temporal pseudo-references generated via a video style transfer network, and adapts Ref-NPR's ray registration mechanism to the 4D (spatio-temporal) domain. It builds on HexPlane as the underlying dynamic radiance field representation and borrows ARF's deferred back-propagation for memory-efficient optimization. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit propagation of style information across time using generated pseudo-references, rather than relying solely on the single provided stylized reference, which is insufficient for dynamic scenes where most timestamps lack direct supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to enable controllable, reference-based stylization of dynamic 3D scenes, opening a path toward artist-driven editing of 4D content in gaming, film, and mixed reality. The temporal pseudo-reference strategy is a practical and generalizable solution to the supervision gap along the time axis, though it introduces an additional training step for the video style transfer network. Limitations include dependence on a pre-trained photorealistic dynamic radiance field, sensitivity to the quality of the pseudo-reference generation, and evaluation on relatively short sequences. Open questions include scalability to longer or more complex dynamic scenes, real-time stylization, and whether the pseudo-reference approach can be replaced by learned temporal style propagation.
