## Problem

3D hand-object interaction data is scarce because multi-camera capture pipelines are expensive and non-scalable, yielding datasets with only dozens of objects. Existing text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2) frequently produce physically implausible hand-object interactions—extra fingers, incorrect contact geometry—and offer no mechanism to condition generation on explicit 3D structure. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can be made to generate realistic hand-object images that are simultaneously controllable in geometry (shape, pose, skeleton) and appearance (object style, background) in a fully disentangled manner.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D object mesh (for grasp synthesis via GrabNet), a 2D hand skeleton projection, a hand-object segmentation mask, an estimated surface normal map (derived from MiDaS depth), and a free-form text prompt describing object and background appearance. It outputs a photorealistic RGB hand-object interaction image paired with full 3D ground-truth annotations (segmentation, depth, mask) at every frame along the grasping trajectory, and optionally a temporally consistent video clip. The key design property is that geometry inputs and text inputs are fully disentangled: fixing the 3D structure while varying the prompt changes only appearance, and vice versa.

## Method

HOIDiffusion is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 uses the pre-trained GrabNet VAE to predict MANO hand parameters from a randomly transformed object mesh, then constructs a smooth grasping trajectory via spherical linear interpolation between a zeroed starting pose and the predicted ending pose, from which segmentation, normal, and skeleton conditions are rendered. Stage 2 fine-tunes Stable Diffusion by injecting three conditional encoders (skeleton projection, hand-object segmentation, estimated normal map) into the U-Net alongside the standard CLIP text encoder, so the denoising step becomes ε̂_t = f_θ(x_t, t, [s, m, n]). To prevent the fine-tuned model from collapsing to the uniform laboratory background of DexYCB, the authors introduce an appearance regularization term: batches of scenery images generated by the pre-trained diffusion model with ChatGPT prompts form a 'background buffer' that is intermittently mixed into training with a matched loss weight, while blank structural conditions on those images act as classifier-free guidance. LLaVA captions each training image with detailed foreground/background descriptions to enrich the text conditioning. Training runs for ~50k steps at lr 1e-5 on 8 A100 GPUs (~12 hours).

## Results

On the DexYCB dataset, HOIDiffusion achieves the best FID of 55.22 (vs. 63.71 for LDM, 134.40 for DreamBooth, and 87.99 for ControlNet) and the best sFID of 91.28, indicating superior image fidelity and spatial quality. For hand-specific metrics, HOIDiffusion attains a mean hand contact recall of 95.49% and PCK of 0.85, substantially outperforming ControlNet (89.85% recall, 0.67 PCK) and the unconditional baselines (≈73% recall, 0.10–0.15 PCK). On CLIPScore (text–image alignment), HOIDiffusion scores 0.78, marginally ahead of ControlNet (0.77) and DreamBooth (0.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOIDiffusion outperforms all four baselines on the primary image-quality metrics: it reduces FID by 8.5 points over the next-best LDM (55.22 vs. 63.71) and by 32.8 points over ControlNet (55.22 vs. 87.99), while also achieving the lowest sFID (91.28 vs. 248.56 for ControlNet). On hand contact recall, HOIDiffusion leads by 5.6 points over ControlNet (95.49% vs. 89.85%) and by over 21 points over the unconditional LDM and DreamBooth baselines (~73%), and its PCK of 0.85 is more than double that of ControlNet (0.67). The only metric where HOIDiffusion does not rank first is Inception Score, where DreamBooth edges it out slightly (7.99 vs. 7.73), though DreamBooth's FID is more than double that of HOIDiffusion, indicating its diversity comes at a large fidelity cost. Against Affordance Diffusion (compared only on contact recall), HOIDiffusion gains 11.2 points in mean recall (95.49% vs. 84.33%). Overall, the disentangled geometry-plus-text conditioning gives HOIDiffusion a clear advantage in both physical plausibility and prompt adherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional diffusion model paradigm (Stable Diffusion/LDM, ControlNet) to the specific domain of hand-object interaction synthesis by injecting three 3D-derived geometric conditions—hand skeleton projection, hand-object segmentation, and estimated normal maps—into the U-Net alongside text prompts. For the structural prior, it relies on GrabNet's VAE-based grasp prediction to produce MANO hand poses, which are then interpolated into trajectories via spherical linear interpolation. The key departure from prior conditional generation work (ControlNet, CoAdapter, Affordance Diffusion) is the explicit disentanglement of geometry from appearance, enforced through a background-buffer regularization strategy that prevents the fine-tuned model from collapsing to the DexYCB training style while preserving text-driven style editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: 3D-annotated hand-object interaction data is extremely expensive to collect, and existing datasets cover only dozens of objects. By demonstrating that generated HOI images with full 3D ground truths can improve downstream 6D pose estimation (e.g., DualPoseNet IoU@25 from 84.1 to 90.9), the work validates synthetic data as a viable augmentation source for perception systems. Limitations include reliance on a single training dataset (DexYCB) with limited object diversity, zero-shot video generation that remains basic, and the fact that the 3D conditions are extracted from rendered geometry rather than learned end-to-end. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary object categories, whether the geometric conditions can be replaced by learned latent representations, and whether the disentangled control generalizes to multi-hand or multi-object scenes.
