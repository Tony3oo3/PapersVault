## Problem

Diffusion models achieve unprecedented image quality but lack fine-grained spatial control because their fixed forward noising process prevents learning spatially disentangled mid-level representations. GAN-era methods (e.g., BlobGAN, GIRAFFE) provided layout disentanglement but at the cost of image quality, leaving a gap for high-quality, rearrangeable scene generation with modern diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242476/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes per-layer local text prompts, binary object masks (bounding boxes or segmentation masks), and spatial offset ranges defining where each object can be placed; for editing, a reference image is additionally provided. The output is a K-layer scene representation where each layer holds a feature map, mask, offset, and prompt, which can be re-rendered to a new image at any layout via a short τ-step diffusion schedule (typically 25–50% of total steps, under one second on a single GPU). The layered representation itself is the persistent artifact—once optimized, arbitrary layout changes require only re-rendering, not re-optimization.

## Method

SceneDiffusion's key insight is that spatial-content disentanglement emerges from jointly denoising multiple scene layouts at each diffusion step. At step t, the method samples N random layouts, renders the layered scene (via α-blending with depth-ordered occlusion) into N views, denoises each view in parallel using locally conditioned diffusion with per-layer text prompts, and then analytically updates the feature maps via a closed-form least-squares solution that averages the denoised views weighted by mask visibility. This entire process is training-free and agnostic to the underlying denoiser architecture. For in-the-wild image editing, the reference image's forward-noising trajectory serves as a high-weight anchor (w=10⁴) in the optimization objective, preserving content fidelity across layout changes. A final τ-step vanilla diffusion stage acts as a neural renderer, refining the rough mask-based geometry into a coherent image.

## Results

On the controllable scene generation task, SceneDiffusion achieves a Mask IoU of 0.522 and Consistency of 0.721, substantially outperforming MultiDiffusion (0.466 and 0.436 respectively). Visual Consistency drops from 0.236 (MultiDiffusion) to 0.112 (Ours), and LPIPS improves from 0.519 to 0.215, indicating much better cross-layout appearance preservation. On the object moving editing task, SceneDiffusion achieves FID 5.289 and Mask IoU 0.817, beating both RePaint (FID 10.267, IoU 0.620) and a specialized inpainting model (FID 6.383, IoU 0.747) on all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines across two tasks. For controllable scene generation, SceneDiffusion outperforms MultiDiffusion [1] on every metric: Mask IoU improves from 0.466 to 0.522, Consistency from 0.436 to 0.721, Visual Consistency from 0.236 to 0.112 (lower is better), LPIPS from 0.519 to 0.215, and SSIM from 0.471 to 0.762. The gap is especially large on consistency metrics, reflecting that MultiDiffusion fails to preserve object appearance across different spatial layouts. For object moving on in-the-wild images, SceneDiffusion beats RePaint [20] by a wide margin (FID 5.289 vs. 10.267; Mask IoU 0.817 vs. 0.620) and also surpasses a specialized inpainting model (FID 5.289 vs. 6.383; Mask IoU 0.817 vs. 0.747). The inpainting baseline is the closest competitor, but SceneDiffusion still leads on all five metrics, with the largest advantage on Visual Consistency (0.075 vs. 0.112) and Mask IoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneDiffusion directly extends MultiDiffusion's [1] strategy of jointly denoising multiple masked regions into a spatially disentangled layered scene representation, adapting the closed-form feature-map update to a depth-ordered alpha-blending compositing scheme inherited from layered scene work (Isola & Liu [15], Kasten et al. [16], Lu et al. [19]). It departs from GAN-era controllable scene generation (BlobGAN, GIRAFFE) by operating entirely within the fixed forward process of a pretrained T2I diffusion model, and from concurrent diffusion editing methods (self-guidance, feature tracking) by using an explicit per-layer representation that enables precise, gradient-free spatial manipulation. The locally conditioned diffusion formulation of Po & Wetzstein [28] provides the per-region noise prediction mechanism that makes multi-layout denoising tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature of SceneDiffusion makes it immediately applicable to any pretrained T2I diffusion model without paired-data finetuning or mask-specific training, and the sub-second rendering of new layouts enables interactive scene manipulation. Key limitations include high memory consumption from simultaneously denoising N layouts (restricting use on resource-limited hardware) and imperfect mask adherence in the final render. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scenes with many interacting objects, whether the memory cost can be reduced via sequential or approximate multi-layout denoising, and how well the method handles non-rigid or deformable objects beyond the current rigid-offset formulation.
