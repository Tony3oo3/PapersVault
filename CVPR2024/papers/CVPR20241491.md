## Problem

Vision Transformers suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity that scales with token count, and deep ViTs additionally exhibit attention saturation where attention matrices become nearly identical across successive layers. The paper questions whether full self-attention computation is necessary in every layer of each stage, arguing that the dependency structure captured early can be efficiently reused.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241491/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 800×1280 for COCO detection, 512×512 for ADE20K segmentation) and tokenizes them into non-overlapping patches via strided convolution. Outputs are task-specific: class labels for classification, bounding boxes with confidence scores for detection (via RetinaNet head), and per-pixel class maps for segmentation (via Semantic FPN or UperNet heads). The hierarchical 4-stage design progressively reduces spatial resolution while increasing channel depth.

## Method

LaViT replaces standard MHSA in later layers of each stage with Less-Attention (LA) layers that generate attention scores by applying two linear transformations (Ψ and Θ, with a transposition between them) to the attention matrix stored from the preceding layer, reducing per-layer complexity from O(N²D) to O(N²). Only the first few layers per stage (Vanilla Attention layers) compute full QK^T attention; subsequent LA layers reuse and transform the stored scores. An Attention Residual module (DWConv with stride 2 + Conv1×1) downsamples the last attention map from the previous stage and adds it to the first VA layer of the current stage via a Layer-Scale residual, preserving cross-stage global context. A Diagonality Preserving Loss enforces symmetry (A_ij = A_ji) and diagonal dominance (A_ii > A_ij) on all transformed attention matrices, which is critical—removing it causes accuracy to drop by over 20 points.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, LaViT-T achieves 79.2% top-1 accuracy with only 1.6G FLOPs and 10.9M parameters, surpassing PVTv2-b1 (78.7%) and RegNetY-1.6G (78.0%) at comparable or lower compute. LaViT-S reaches 82.6% top-1 at 3.3G FLOPs, outperforming EfficientNet-B4 (82.4%), DeepViT-S (82.3%), and Swin-T (81.2%) while using fewer FLOPs. LaViT-B attains 83.1% top-1 at just 6.1G FLOPs and 39.6M params, matching Swin-S (83.1%) with roughly half the FLOPs. On COCO detection, LaViT-S achieves 46.7 AP^b (1×) and 48.9 AP^b (3×+MS), exceeding Swin-S by 2.2 and 2.6 AP^b respectively; on ADE20K segmentation, LaViT-S with UperNet reaches 47.2 mIoU (+2.7 over Swin-S).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the tiny model group (~2G FLOPs), LaViT-T (79.2%) outperforms all listed baselines, beating PVTv2-b1 by 0.5% and RegNetY-1.6G by 1.2% while using fewer FLOPs (1.6G vs. 2.1G and 1.6G respectively). In the small group (~4G FLOPs), LaViT-S (82.6%) leads all competitors: it surpasses EfficientNet-B4 (82.4%) by 0.2%, DeepViT-S (82.3%) by 0.3%, PVTv2-b2 (82.0%) by 0.6%, and Swin-T (81.2%) by 1.4%, all at 3.3G FLOPs—lower than every ViT baseline except PPT-S (3.1G, but 79.8% accuracy). In the base group, LaViT-B (83.1%) matches Swin-S (83.1%) at roughly half the FLOPs (6.1G vs. 8.7G) and 39.6M params vs. 49.6M, and exceeds PVT-L (81.7%) by 1.4%. For COCO detection, LaViT-T gains 4.7 AP^b over Swin-T (46.2 vs. 41.5 at 1×) and LaViT-S gains 2.2 AP^b over Swin-S (46.7 vs. 44.5). For ADE20K segmentation, LaViT-S with UperNet achieves 47.2 mIoU, exceeding Swin-S (44.5) by 2.7 and Focal-T (45.8) by 1.4, while using fewer FLOPs (920G vs. 945G and 998G). The paper also demonstrates plug-and-play extensibility: adding the Less-Attention module to existing backbones (ViT, DeiT, DeepViT, PVT, Swin) consistently improves accuracy by 0.3–1.2% while reducing FLOPs by 8.8–25.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaViT builds directly on the hierarchical ViT architecture of PVT, adopting its four-stage downsampling structure, but fundamentally rethinks the self-attention computation within each stage. Rather than following the token-reduction or sparse-attention line of work (DynamicViT, EViT, PPT), the paper departs from the assumption that every layer must independently compute QK^T, instead re-parameterizing attention scores from earlier layers via lightweight linear transformations. The attention saturation phenomenon, previously documented in DeepViT and CaiT, motivates the core design choice, while the Diagonality Preserving Loss and the cross-stage Attention Residual module are novel contributions not present in prior efficient-ViT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the Less-Attention module—requiring only matrix multiplications and a few lines of code—makes it an attractive drop-in for existing ViT backbones, as demonstrated by consistent gains across seven different architectures. The catastrophic performance drop (20+ points) when removing the Diagonality Preserving Loss reveals that naive linear re-parameterization of attention is insufficient without explicit structural constraints, an insight that may inform future attention-efficient designs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very deep or very high-resolution settings, whether the linear transformation is the optimal re-parameterization (vs. learned nonlinear maps), and whether the technique transfers to non-visual modalities where attention saturation has also been reported.
