## Problem

Scene text recognition models struggle with domain variation, font diversity, and shape deformation in the wild, and the standard remedy—fine-tuning per scenario—is computationally expensive and requires maintaining multiple model copies. While large language models demonstrate in-context learning (ICL), applying multi-modal LLMs as text recognizers is prohibitively resource-consuming, and pilot experiments show that naively concatenating scene text samples during training fails to impart ICL capabilities to regular-sized STR models due to the absence of meaningful contextual connections between samples.

## Input / Output

At inference, E²STR takes a single scene text image plus N in-context prompts (default N=2) auto-selected via kNN cosine similarity over pre-computed vision-encoder token embeddings from a small pool (e.g., 1000 images, <0.025% of training data). The pool stores only vision encoder output tokens rather than raw images, so selected prompts bypass the vision encoder at test time and are fed directly as visual tokens to the language decoder. The output is a recognized text string generated auto-regressively by an OPT-125M decoder, with inference time of 0.094s per sample—comparable to MAERec's 0.092s.

## Method

E²STR employs a two-stage training paradigm: first, standard auto-regressive STR training on Union14M-L with a MAE-pretrained ViT encoder and OPT-125M decoder bridged by cross-attention layers; second, in-context training on context-rich scene text sequences generated by the ST strategy. The Split Strategy uses character-level bounding boxes from 600k synthesized images to produce overlapping sub-samples that share visual and linguistic connections with the parent sample, while the Transform Strategy applies color/direction augmentations to create same-label variants; both are interleaved with a special </i> placeholder token so the decoder learns to distinguish sample boundaries. At inference, the model computes the test image's embedding by averaging its visual tokens, retrieves the top-N most similar samples from the in-context pool, and the language decoder attends jointly to context and test image features via cross-attention, enabling it to rectify ambiguous characters (e.g., 'a' vs 'q') by leveraging visual-linguistic patterns from the prompts.

## Results

On 12 common STR benchmarks, E²STR-ICL achieves 91.33% average word accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA MAERec (90.50%) by 0.83% in a training-free manner. On cross-domain scenarios (MPSC, EIST, IAM), E²STR-ICL reaches 78.17% average word accuracy, outperforming MAERec by 4.03% without any fine-tuning, and even exceeding fine-tuned MAERec by 1.16%. In hard-case rectification, annotating just 20% of misrecognized samples and adding them to the in-context pool reduces the error rate by up to 32%, compared to only 2.23% from fine-tuning MAERec on the same annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²STR-ICL outperforms all seven baselines on the 12-benchmark average (91.33% vs. MAERec's 90.50%, SATRN's 85.10%, ABINet's 84.53%, SAR's 81.91%, NRTR's 80.31%, and ASTER's 77.26%). On the six near-saturated regular/irregular benchmarks (IIIT, SVT, IC13, IC15, SVTP, CT80), E²STR-ICL pushes the average from MAERec's 97.02% to 97.74%, a 24% relative error reduction. In cross-domain evaluation (Table 2), E²STR-ICL (78.17% avg) beats MAERec (74.14%) by 4.03% in a training-free setting, and surpasses fine-tuned MAERec by 1.16% (Figure 5) without any parameter update. On the hardest benchmarks (WordArt, HOST, WOST), E²STR-ICL gains the largest margins over MAERec (+3.58%, +0.78%, +0.87% respectively), indicating ICL is most beneficial for visually challenging text. PARSeq is not directly comparable as it was trained on a different dataset and does not report results on the harder benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MAERec architecture (ViT-MAE vision encoder + auto-regressive language decoder) and the Flamingo-style cross-attention mechanism for bridging vision and language tokens. Its key departure from prior STR work is the introduction of in-context learning as a training-free adaptation mechanism, replacing the standard fine-tuning pipeline. The critical technical novelty is the ST (Split-Transform) strategy for generating context-rich training sequences, which the authors show is essential—naive random concatenation of samples completely fails to impart ICL capability. The paper also draws on the multi-modal ICL literature (Flamingo, Otter) but demonstrates that the sequential training paradigm must be adapted with structured sub-sample connectivity to work in the STR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a regular-sized STR model (~125M language decoder) can achieve effective ICL challenges the assumption that in-context learning requires billion-parameter models, opening the door to lightweight adaptive recognizers for deployment. The hard-case rectification result (32% error reduction with 10–20% annotation vs. 2.23% for fine-tuning) suggests a practical workflow for iterative quality improvement without retraining. Limitations include inability to recognize out-of-lexicon characters and a small risk of erroneous rectification from misleading prompts. Open questions include whether the ST strategy generalizes to other vision-language tasks and how the in-context pool should be maintained or updated in streaming/online settings.
