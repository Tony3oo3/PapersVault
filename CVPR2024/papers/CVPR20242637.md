## Problem

Lightweight Vision Transformers have demonstrated superior performance and lower latency than lightweight CNNs on mobile devices, yet they face practical deployment challenges including inadequate hardware/library support and sensitivity to high-resolution inputs. Despite known structural connections between the two families, the notable architectural disparities in block structure, macro design, and micro design have not been adequately examined. The paper asks whether integrating the efficient architectural principles of lightweight ViTs into a standard lightweight CNN can close or reverse this performance gap.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 for classification, 512×512 crops for detection/segmentation backbones, and 1024×1024 for the SAM variant, and produces class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, or pixel-wise segmentation maps depending on the downstream task. All latency measurements are taken on an iPhone 12 via Core ML Tools at batch size 1, making on-device inference time the primary evaluation metric rather than FLOPs or parameter count. The architecture is a pure CNN with no attention modules, ensuring compatibility with highly optimized convolution kernels on edge hardware.

## Method

RepViT is obtained by incrementally modernizing MobileNetV3-L with ViT-inspired designs at three granularities. At the block level, the token mixer (depthwise convolution) and channel mixer (1×1 convolution) are decoupled following the MetaFormer principle, the expansion ratio is reduced to 2, and structural re-parameterization is applied to the DW layer to eliminate skip-connection overhead at inference. At the macro level, the complex stem is replaced with two stride-2 3×3 early convolutions, downsampling layers are deepened with a sandwich layout, the classifier is simplified to global average pooling plus a linear layer, and the stage ratio is set to 2:2:14:2. At the micro level, all kernels are unified to 3×3 for compiler optimization, and SE layers are placed in a cross-block pattern (every other block) to balance accuracy gain against latency cost. The final architecture is a family of pure reparameterization-convolution CNNs (RepViT-M0.9 through M2.3) trained with the DeiT recipe (AdamW, cosine schedule, 300/450 epochs, optional RegNetY-16GF distillation) on ImageNet-1K.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, RepViT-M1.0 achieves 80.0% top-1 accuracy (80.3% with 450-epoch distillation) at 1.0 ms latency on iPhone 12, the first lightweight model to surpass 80% at this speed. The largest variant, RepViT-M2.3, reaches 83.7% top-1 at 2.3 ms latency, outperforming EfficientFormerV2-L (83.3% at 2.7 ms) and SwiftFormer-L3 (83.0% at 2.9 ms) while being faster. On COCO-2017 with Mask R-CNN, RepViT-M2.3 achieves 44.6 AP^box and 40.8 AP^mask at 9.9 ms backbone latency, matching EfficientFormerV2-L (44.7/40.4) at roughly 50% lower latency. RepViT-SAM runs at 48.9 ms on iPhone 12 where MobileSAM and ViT-B-SAM both OOM, and is nearly 10× faster than MobileSAM on Macbook M1 Pro while achieving better zero-shot transfer scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 0.9 ms latency tier, RepViT-M0.9 (78.7%) outperforms EfficientFormerV2-S0 (75.7%) by 3.0% and FastViT-T8 (76.7%) by 2.0% top-1 accuracy. At 1.1 ms, RepViT-M1.1 (80.7%) beats EfficientFormerV2-S1 (79.0%) by 1.7%. At the 1.5–1.6 ms tier, RepViT-M1.5 (82.3%) edges out EfficientFormerV2-S2 (81.6%) by 0.7% while being 0.1 ms faster. At the largest scale, RepViT-M2.3 (83.3% at 2.3 ms) matches EfficientFormerV2-L (83.3% at 2.7 ms) in accuracy while being 0.4 ms faster, and surpasses SwiftFormer-L3 (83.0% at 2.9 ms) and EfficientFormer-L3 (82.4% at 2.7 ms). On COCO detection, RepViT-M1.1 gains 1.9 AP^box over EfficientFormer-L1 at lower latency, and RepViT-M2.3 matches EfficientFormerV2-L in AP^box while reducing backbone latency by ~45%. For SAM integration, RepViT-SAM is the only model that runs on iPhone 12 at 1024×1024 (48.9 ms), whereas MobileSAM and ViT-B-SAM both OOM; on Macbook it is ~10× faster than MobileSAM (44.8 ms vs 482.2 ms) and achieves higher zero-shot edge detection (ODS 0.764 vs 0.756) and instance segmentation (AP 44.4 vs 42.7) scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper takes MobileNetV3 as its starting point and systematically transplants architectural principles from lightweight ViTs—MetaFormer block structure (separate token mixer and channel mixer), early convolutions for the stem, deeper downsampling layers, and dimension-consistent width design—into a pure CNN. Unlike hybrid CNN-ViT models (MobileViT, FastViT, EfficientFormer) that retain self-attention or other transformer components, RepViT is composed entirely of reparameterized convolutions, drawing on structural re-parameterization from RepVGG to enable multi-branch training with single-branch inference. The paper's positioning is explicitly a 'revisit' of lightweight CNN design from the ViT perspective, arguing that the architectural insights behind ViT success (MetaFormer structure, early convolutions, stage ratio choices) are transferable to pure convolutional networks without requiring any attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RepViT demonstrates that pure lightweight CNNs can outperform all existing lightweight ViTs on mobile devices across classification, detection, and segmentation, challenging the prevailing narrative that ViTs are inherently superior for efficient vision. The practical impact is significant: CNNs benefit from highly optimized hardware support and linear complexity with input resolution, making them more robust for edge deployment, and RepViT-SAM's ~10× speedup over MobileSAM with better zero-shot transfer shows immediate utility for segment-anything on phones. Open questions include whether this CNN-centric approach generalizes to higher-resolution or video tasks where ViTs' global attention may still hold an advantage, and whether the gap will persist as ViT efficiency techniques continue to mature.
