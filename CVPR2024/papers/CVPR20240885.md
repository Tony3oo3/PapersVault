## Problem

Existing generalizable NeRF methods aggregate cross-view and along-epipolar information independently or sequentially, which the authors show leads to distinct failure modes: cross-view-only aggregation produces rendering artifacts from missing appearance continuity between adjacent depths, while epipolar-only aggregation causes depth discontinuities from absent cross-view geometry consistency. The paper addresses how to entangle both information dimensions simultaneously to recover the complementary priors that single-dimensional aggregation loses.

## Input / Output

The method takes M source RGB images (M=10 for generalizable setting, M=3 for few-shot) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and synthesizes a novel view by sampling 2,048 rays per batch with 88 points each. A lightweight shared-weight CNN (deliberately avoiding deep U-Net architectures) extracts pixel-aligned features at re-projected sampling points guided by epipolar geometry. The output is a rendered novel-view image via NeRF volume rendering, with the model also producing interpretable depth maps and per-ray volume density distributions. Training requires only photometric loss on RGB targets—no ground-truth depth or 3D supervision.

## Method

EVE-NeRF alternates two dual-branch transformer modules: the View-Epipolar Interaction (VEI) module and the Epipolar-View Interaction (EVI) module. In VEI, a View Transformer performs multi-head self-attention across the M source views (with relative pose encoding), while a parallel Along-Epipolar Perception branch uses 1D convolutional adjacent-depth attention along the ray to predict per-point importance weights that inject appearance continuity prior; the two branches are fused by element-wise multiplication. In EVI, an Epipolar Transformer performs self-attention across the N sampling points along each epipolar line, while a Multi-View Calibration branch uses cross-view attention over the epipolar transformer's value matrix to predict per-view importance weights that inject geometry consistency prior; again the branches are fused by element-wise multiplication. This entangled design means each dimension's aggregation is calibrated by the complementary prior from the other dimension, rather than treating them as independent or sequential stages. The final generalizable 3D representation feeds a conditioned NeRF decoder that incorporates cross-point interactions via a ray Transformer (from IBRNet) before volume rendering.

## Results

On the generalizable NVS setting (Setting 1), EVE-NeRF achieves 27.16 PSNR / 0.912 SSIM / 0.134 LPIPS on LLFF, 27.03 / 0.952 / 0.072 on Blender, and 28.01 / 0.935 / 0.083 on Shiny, outperforming the strongest baseline GNT by ~4.4% PSNR, ~4.8% SSIM, and ~14.3% LPIPS averaged across the three datasets. In the few-shot NVS setting (Setting 2, 3 source views), EVE-NeRF reaches 27.80 PSNR / 0.937 SSIM / 0.149 LPIPS on DTU and 23.45 / 0.903 / 0.132 on Blender, surpassing MatchNeRF by ~2.2% PSNR and ~13% LPIPS. The method also requires less storage (53.8 MB vs. 110–112 MB) and renders faster (194 s vs. 208 s for GNT) while delivering higher quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVE-NeRF outperforms all six baselines on the majority of metrics across both evaluation settings. Against GNT [49] (the strongest generalizable baseline using sequential cross-view then depth interaction), EVE-NeRF gains +1.63 dB PSNR on LLFF, +1.02 dB on Blender, and +0.82 dB on Shiny, while reducing LPIPS by up to 25% on LLFF (0.134 vs. 0.178). GPNR [43] achieves the highest Blender PSNR (28.29) among baselines but is surpassed by EVE-NeRF on LLFF (+1.81 dB) and Shiny (+2.29 dB); EVE-NeRF also leads GPNR on LPIPS across all three datasets. IBRNet [52] and NeuRay [25] trail EVE-NeRF by 2+ dB PSNR on LLFF and Shiny, reflecting the advantage of entangled view-epipolar aggregation over single-dimension ray transformers. In the few-shot setting (3 views), EVE-NeRF beats MatchNeRF [7] by +0.89 dB PSNR on DTU and +0.25 dB on Blender, while MVSNeRF [4] and PixelNeRF [63] lag further behind (26.63 and 19.31 PSNR on DTU respectively). Notably, EVE-NeRF also uses roughly half the model storage (53.8 MB) compared to GNT (112 MB) and GPNR (110 MB) while rendering faster than GNT (194 s vs. 208 s per image on an RTX 4090).

## Relation to Prior Work

EVE-NeRF extends the generalizable NeRF line of work (PixelNeRF, IBRNet, GNT, GPNR) that aggregates multi-view features via transformer-based attention along a single dimension—either cross-view or along-epipolar, or in a fixed sequential order. The key departure is the entangled dual-branch design: the View-Epipolar Interaction module injects an appearance-continuity prior (via adjacent-depth attention) into the cross-view aggregation, while the Epipolar-View Interaction module injects a geometry-consistency prior (via multi-view calibration) into the along-epipolar aggregation. The NeRF decoder borrows the ray Transformer from IBRNet for cross-point interactions, and the lightweight CNN feature extractor deliberately avoids the deep U-Net used by MVSNeRF and GNT, following GPNR's observation that large receptive fields hinder scene generalizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of single-dimensional aggregation—rendering artifacts from missing appearance continuity or depth discontinuities from missing geometry consistency—and shows that entangling the two dimensions in a dual-branch, alternating architecture resolves both simultaneously without requiring ground-truth depth supervision. The practical efficiency gain (53.8 MB vs. 112 MB storage, faster per-image rendering than GNT and GPNR while achieving higher PSNR) makes the approach attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the specific prior-injection mechanism (sigmoid-weighted element-wise multiplication) is optimal, whether the entangled design generalizes to dynamic or unbounded scenes, and whether the approach scales to very large view counts beyond the 8–12 views used in training.
