## Problem

Motion blur decomposition from a single blurry image is highly ill-posed because the averaging effect destroys temporal ordering of latent frames, and multiple physically distinct motion sequences can produce identical blur observations. Existing approaches rely on neighboring blurry frames or ordering-invariant losses, which degrade under long exposures or fast motion. This paper identifies that rolling shutter (RS) images inherently encode temporal ordering through their scanline-wise exposure delay, making them a natural complement to global shutter (GS) blur images for disambiguating motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a strictly aligned pair of images — a global shutter blurred image B and a rolling shutter image R of the same scene — and reconstructs a sharp video sequence of N frames (tested at N=3, 5, 9). The RS view provides row-by-row temporal cues and local details, while the GS blur supplies full global context. The authors built a triaxial optical system with two beam splitters to simultaneously capture the Blur-RS pair and a high-speed ground-truth sequence (500 fps, 2 ms exposure per frame), yielding the RealBR dataset of real street scenes with vehicles, pedestrians, and camera motion.

## Method

The architecture comprises two stages: a Motion Interpretation (MI) stage with three iterative Motion Interpretation Blocks, and a Blur Decomposition stage implemented as a GenNet encoder-decoder. Each MIB processes blur and RS inputs through separate parallel branches — the blur branch for contextual characterization and the RS branch for temporal abstraction — coupled by a Shutter Alignment and Aggregation (SAA) module that predicts bidirectional displacement maps between the two views, warps features via spatial transformer networks, and concatenates aligned and aggregated features back into each stream. A temporal positional encoding, derived from the RS scanline geometry, is concatenated to the RS input to explicitly encode the relative timing of each latent frame. The MI stage outputs bidirectional optical flow fields (from latent frames to both B and R) plus a mask, which the GenNet uses to warp and refine the final sharp video sequence. This dual-stream mutual-incentive design, rather than simple concatenation of cross-shutter inputs, is the key architectural distinction, validated by ablation showing ~3 dB PSNR gain over a single-branch variant.

## Results

On the RealBR dataset, the proposed method achieves PSNR of 30.87, 31.05, and 31.15 dB for reconstructing 3, 5, and 9 latent sharp frames from a single blurred image, respectively, with corresponding SSIM of 0.9073–0.9120 and LPIPS of 0.0678–0.0696. The method outperforms the strongest dual-view baselines RIFE_BR (30.67 dB) and IFED_BR (30.84 dB) at ×9 by 0.48 and 0.31 dB in PSNR, while the best single-blur-frame method DeMFI (4·B) reaches only 26.20 dB. The model runs in 1.30 s per 9-frame sequence with 105M parameters and 183 GFLOPs on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines across all sequence lengths (×3, ×5, ×9) on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The closest competitors are RIFE_BR (30.67 dB) and IFED_BR (30.84 dB) at ×9, which also use the dual Blur-RS input but lack the dedicated motion interpretation module; the paper gains 0.48 and 0.31 dB over them respectively. The best single-blur-frame method, DeMFI using 4 consecutive blur frames (26.20 dB), trails the paper by 4.95 dB, underscoring the value of the RS view for temporal disambiguation. Methods relying on 2–3 blur frames (RIFE_B, IFED_B, BiT, AfB) cluster between 21.8–24.8 dB, confirming that neighboring blur frames alone cannot resolve motion ambiguity. Notably, the dual Blur-RS setting yields at least 5.6 dB improvement over the same architectures using only blur frames (e.g., RIFE_BR vs. RIFE_B: 30.67 vs. 24.83 dB), validating the cross-shutter complementarity as the primary source of gain. The paper's model uses 105M parameters (≈10× IFED_BR) but runs in comparable time (1.30 s vs. 1.33 s), trading parameter count for the iterative dual-stream motion interpretation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion blur decomposition task (LEVS, AfB, BiT, DeMFI) by introducing a dual Blur-RS input setting, drawing on the complementary exposure characteristics of global and rolling shutter cameras. Conceptually, it repurposes the dual-camera paradigm established in RS correction (RS-RS, RS-Event) and deblurring (GS-Event) to a new pairing: a single GS blur frame plus a single RS frame, rather than relying on neighboring blur frames for motion disambiguation. The architecture borrows spatial transformer networks and MIMO-UNet-style encoders for cross-view alignment, and introduces a temporal positional encoding derived from the row-by-row exposure geometry of RS sensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RealBR dataset, collected with a purpose-built triaxial imaging system, is a practical contribution that enables reproducible research on cross-shutter decomposition with real-world motion. The demonstrated robustness to spatial misalignment (up to 8 px) and low-light noise in the RS view makes the setting more deployable than idealized assumptions suggest. However, the requirement for a beamsplitter-based optical system limits immediate adoption on compact devices, and the authors acknowledge that exploring synchronized parallel sensors is future work. An open question is whether the dual-stream mutual-incentive design generalizes to other cross-modal pairs (e.g., blur-event) or to longer exposure sequences where the RS temporal ordering becomes more critical.
