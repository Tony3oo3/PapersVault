## Problem

Continual semantic segmentation is particularly challenging when framed as per-pixel classification, as the dense prediction paradigm exacerbates catastrophic forgetting. Prior query-based continual segmentation methods (e.g., CoMFormer) fail to exploit the inherent objectness of query-based segmenters, which exhibits strong transferability to unseen classes and resistance to forgetting of old-class mask proposals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 512×512 RGB images (augmented with random rescaling, flipping, and color jittering) and produces per-pixel segmentation masks with class labels via a two-stage pipeline: stage 1 outputs N class-agnostic binary mask proposals with objectness scores, and stage 2 assigns multi-label class probabilities to each proposal. During inference, an objectness threshold (α=0.8) filters low-confidence proposals before class recognition, and panoptic-style inference is used to evaluate background IoU.

## Method

CoMasTRe decouples continual segmentation into a forgetting-resistant objectness learning stage and a class recognition stage. Stage 1 uses a 9-block Transformer mask decoder with 20–100 learnable positional queries to produce class-agnostic masks and objectness scores via bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm). Stage 2 employs task queries and task-specific classifiers (one per learning step) to perform multi-label class recognition on matched embeddings, reducing inter-task interference. For continual learning, three objectness distillation losses (score KL, reweighted mask, and reweighted positional-embedding cosine) preserve stage-1 knowledge, while a multi-label class distillation strategy distills old-class probabilities from both high-objectness unmatched embeddings and matched embeddings through a frozen teacher model. The overall loss combines segmentation (focal + mask), distillation, and an auxiliary merged-class classification loss, trained jointly with AdamW on a ResNet101 backbone.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, CoMasTRe achieves 69.51% mIoU on incremented classes in the 19-1 setting, a 32.16 p.p. improvement over the previous best query-based method CoMFormer (24.06%). In the more challenging 15-1 setting (6 learning steps), CoMasTRe reaches 63.54% all-class mIoU, surpassing RCIL by 4.14 p.p. On ADE20K, CoMasTRe outperforms CoMFormer by up to 2.11 p.p. on all classes (100-10 setting) and boosts base-class mIoU by 4.76 p.p. over REMINDER in the 100-5 setting. The method also approaches the joint-training upper bound, e.g., 74.86% vs. 78.08% all-class mIoU on VOC 19-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMasTRe dramatically outperforms the previous best query-based method CoMFormer [5] on all settings: 32.16 p.p. on new classes in VOC 19-1 (69.51% vs. 24.06%), 20.34 p.p. in 15-1 (43.62% vs. 23.28%), and up to 2.82 p.p. on ADE20K new classes. Against per-pixel baselines, CoMasTRe surpasses RCIL [57] by 4.14 p.p. on all-class mIoU in VOC 15-1 (63.54% vs. 59.40%) and exceeds the per-pixel joint upper bound by 0.85 p.p. on base classes in 15-5 (79.73% vs. 78.88%). On ADE20K, CoMasTRe beats REMINDER [36] by 3.36 p.p. on base classes in 100-10 and 4.76 p.p. in 100-5, while matching or exceeding its new-class performance. Compared to PLOP [12] and MiB [3], CoMasTRe achieves substantially higher new-class mIoU (e.g., 69.51% vs. 37.35% and 23.59% in VOC 19-1). In joint training (Table 3), CoMasTRe with R101 reaches 78.08% on VOC and 44.36% on ADE20K, outperforming Mask2Former [10] (80.04%/43.51%) on ADE20K and CoMFormer (76.82%/43.10%) on both datasets, confirming the architecture's strong single-shot capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMasTRe builds on the query-based segmentation paradigm (Mask2Former, MaskFormer) and the continual segmentation literature (MiB, PLOP, REMINDER, RCIL, CoMFormer), but departs from the dominant per-pixel classification framing by explicitly decoupling segmentation into a forgetting-resistant objectness stage and a continual multi-label classification stage. The architectural lineage traces to Mask2Former's mask decoder, while the continual learning components (task-specific classifiers, task queries, multi-label distillation) draw from continual classification work such as DyTox and DER. The key conceptual departure from CoMFormer—the first query-based continual segmenter—is the deliberate exploitation of objectness transferability and the design of separate distillation losses for objectness versus class knowledge, rather than applying a uniform distillation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling insight—that objectness in query-based segmenters is inherently transferable across classes and resistant to catastrophic forgetting—provides a clean conceptual lens for understanding why per-pixel continual segmentation is so difficult and offers a principled path forward. The method achieves strong SOTA results on both VOC and ADE20K without replay, but remains limited to semantic segmentation; the authors note extension to panoptic and instance segmentation as future work. An open question is whether the objectness-transfer advantage persists under more aggressive continual learning protocols (e.g., very long task sequences or domain shifts), and whether the two-stage decoupling generalizes to other dense prediction tasks like detection.
