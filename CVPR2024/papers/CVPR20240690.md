## Problem

Existing video question-answering methods target short third-person clips, leaving long egocentric videos—where a person or robot must recall specific past visual experiences—largely unaddressed. Prior work either ignores temporal grounding (yielding answers barely above blind guessing) or performs grounding without QA ability, and chaining the two suffers from error propagation. Additionally, the limited size of annotated egocentric QA data and the ambiguity of evaluating free-form answers further hinder progress.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a long egocentric video (averaging 8.2 minutes, uniformly sampled to 1,200 feature vectors of 2,304 dimensions from frozen EgoVLP and InternVideo backbones) together with a natural language question. It jointly outputs a temporal window (start, end) identifying the relevant video segment and an answer, which is either a free-form sentence (OpenQA) or a selection from K candidate choices (CloseQA). In CloseQA mode, the question is augmented with shuffled candidate answers as part of the input prompt.

## Method

GroundVQA is a unified multi-task Transformer architecture that fuses projected video features and question embeddings in a visual-language encoder, then branches into a temporal localizer (classification + regression heads adapted from ActionFormer) and a causal Flan-T5 language decoder for answer generation, trained end-to-end with a weighted combination of Focal+DIoU loss (VLG) and cross-entropy loss (QA). To overcome the 11K-sample bottleneck of QAEGO4D, the authors build an automated pipeline using Llama2-13B to convert Ego4D's timestamped narrations into 303K grounded QA pairs (EGOTIMEQA), a 30× data increase that substantially mitigates overfitting. They further introduce CloseQA by prompting the LLM to generate three plausible distractors per question and filtering out items answerable by a text-only model, ensuring the task genuinely requires visual evidence. Multi-task training (OpenQA + CloseQA + VLG) is shown to outperform two-stage grounding-then-answering pipelines, with the VLG supervision improving QA by teaching the encoder to attend to question-relevant segments.

## Results

On the QAEGO4D test set, GroundVQA_B achieves a Sentence Similarity of 58.2, ROUGE-L of 30.4, METEOR of 21.5, and CloseQA Accuracy of 50.2±0.5, outperforming the best prior method (SimpleVQA+*) by 2.5 points in Sim. and 8.8 points in CloseQA Accuracy. On the NLQv2 test set, GroundVQA_B† (pre-trained on NLQv2+NaQ) achieves a Mean Recall@1 of 22.15, surpassing the previous SOTA GroundNLQ (20.91) by 1.24 points. The model uses 252M parameters, comparable to baselines (247–368M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroundVQA_B decisively outperforms all QAEGO4D baselines from Bärmann & Waibel [3]: it beats SimpleVQA+* (the strongest prior method) by 2.5 Sim. points (58.2 vs 55.7), 1.6 ROUGE points, 2.0 METEOR points, and 8.8 CloseQA Accuracy points (50.2 vs 41.4). Against Rehearsal Memory [44], GroundVQA_B leads by 4.1 Sim. points and 10.3 CloseQA Accuracy points while using fewer parameters (252M vs 368M). On the NLQv2 grounding benchmark, GroundVQA_B† achieves a Mean Recall@1 of 22.15, surpassing GroundNLQ [13] (20.91), NaQ++ [29] (17.67), ReLER [23] (10.51), EgoVLP [19] (8.35), and VSLNet [42] (4.08). The only near-tie is with GroundNLQ on Recall@5 at IoU=0.5, where GroundNLQ edges ahead slightly (29.17 vs 27.70), though GroundVQA_B† leads on the primary Mean R@1 metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads on long egocentric videos: video-language grounding (NLQ, GroundNLQ, NaQ) and episodic-memory question answering (QAEGO4D). The temporal localizer architecture is adapted from ActionFormer and GroundNLQ, while the temporal window estimation strategy follows EgoVLP. The key departure is the joint training of grounding and answering in a single model, motivated by the error propagation problem in two-stage pipelines, and the use of Llama2 to synthesize 303K QA samples from Ego4D narrations—a 30× expansion over the existing QAEGO4D training set. The CloseQA evaluation task is a novel addition that addresses the well-known inadequacy of BLEU/ROUGE/METEOR for short, ambiguous open-ended answers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for building episodic-memory systems for robots or AR devices that can answer questions about their own past visual experiences, with the unified model offering interpretable failure analysis through its temporal window predictions. The LLM-based data synthesis pipeline is a broadly applicable technique for scaling video QA training data, and the CloseQA benchmark offers a more reliable evaluation protocol than open-ended metrics. However, the oracle gap (57.7 vs. 58.4 Sim.) reveals that temporal grounding remains the bottleneck, and the reliance on Ego4D narrations for data generation limits generalization to domains without such annotations. Open questions include whether the unified formulation scales to even longer videos (hours) and whether the CloseQA filtering strategy generalizes beyond the Ego4D domain.
