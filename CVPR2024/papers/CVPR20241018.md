## Problem

Existing audio-driven 3D facial animation methods rely on 3D morphable models with linear blendshapes, which cannot represent fine-scale geometric details such as hair, skin creasing, eye blinks, and complex irregular expressions. No generative approach existed for synthesizing temporally coherent, high-fidelity volumetric head motion sequences from speech, leaving a gap between the expressivity of neural parametric head models and the practical need for audio-conditioned animation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw audio waveform (16 kHz) and a fixed NPHM identity code (1344-dim) as input, producing a sequence of 200-dim NPHM expression codes (48 frames / 2 s during training) that are fed to the frozen NPHM to yield per-frame SDFs and variable-topology meshes via Marching Cubes. Because the expression codes are identity-agnostic, the same generated sequence can be transferred to any identity in the NPHM space. The audio is resampled from 16 kHz to 24 Hz to align with the video framerate before being encoded by Wave2Vec 2.0.

## Method

FaceTalk is a transformer-based latent diffusion model that denoises NPHM expression sequences (N×200) conditioned on Wave2Vec 2.0 audio features via cross-attention with a Kronecker-delta expression-audio alignment mask, ensuring frame-accurate lip synchronization. Diffusion timestamps are injected through sinusoidal embeddings processed by an MLP and fused via FiLM layers interleaved between self-attention, cross-attention, and feedforward blocks; a look-ahead binary mask in self-attention prevents the model from attending to future frames. Training uses classifier-free guidance (25% null-conditioning dropout) and a novel expression augmentation that randomly scales expression codes to encourage diversity. The training dataset is created by optimizing NPHM expression codes against COLMAP point clouds from NeRSemble multi-view recordings using a sliding-window (10-frame) temporal regularization and L2 expression prior, yielding 1000 temporally consistent sequences. At inference, a Gaussian-kernel smoothing centered on the mouth region suppresses head/neck wobble before mesh extraction.

## Results

FaceTalk achieves the best score on all five evaluation metrics in Table 1. The most striking improvement is in perceptual quality: FID drops to 40.69 (vs. 201.31 for the best baseline EmoTalk) and KID to 0.009 (vs. 0.201), indicating dramatically more realistic and diverse expressions. Lip synchronization (LSE-D) improves to 11.27, edging out the best baseline Imitator (11.61). In a 40-participant user study, FaceTalk is preferred over the best baseline by 75% on overall animation quality, lip synchronization, and facial realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceTalk outperforms all seven baselines on every metric. Against the strongest lip-sync baseline Imitator (LSE-D 11.61), FaceTalk improves to 11.27, a modest but consistent gain. The gap widens dramatically on perceptual quality: FID of 40.69 is roughly 5× lower than EmoTalk (201.31) and 6× lower than VOCA (239.04), while KID of 0.009 is over 20× lower than the best baseline (EmoTalk, 0.201). On face image quality (FIQA), FaceTalk scores 45.75 versus EMOTE's 39.44 and Imitator's 38.44, and on video quality (VQA) it reaches 0.6145 versus Imitator's 0.5384. The older template-based methods (VOCA, MeshTalk, FaceFormer) trail by a wider margin on all metrics, with VOCA showing the worst LSE-D (13.62) and FID (239.04). The emotion-focused baselines EmoTalk and EMOTE achieve competitive lip sync but sacrifice perceptual realism, particularly EmoTalk's low FIQA of 31.80.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends audio-driven facial animation from the 3DMM/linear-blendshape representation (used by VOCA, MeshTalk, FaceFormer, CodeTalker) to the volumetric latent space of Neural Parametric Head Models (NPHMs), which can capture fine-scale details like wrinkles, eye blinks, and hair. It also departs from prior diffusion-based talking-head methods (DiffTalk, DiffPoseTalk, FaceDiffuser) that operate in pixel space or 3DMM parameter space, instead applying latent diffusion directly in the NPHM expression code space with a transformer decoder. The dataset creation pipeline, which optimizes temporally consistent NPHM expressions from NeRSemble multi-view recordings, is a new contribution that addresses the absence of paired audio-NPHM training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FaceTalk establishes that generative diffusion in the NPHM latent space can produce volumetric head animations with fine-grained detail (creases, blinks) that 3DMM-based methods fundamentally cannot represent, opening a path toward high-fidelity digital avatars for content creation. Key limitations include the multi-step denoising process precluding real-time use, the method synthesizing only expression codes (not identities), and the evaluation being somewhat confounded by the representation-space advantage over 3DMM baselines. Open questions include whether efficient sampling can make this practical, whether the approach extends to identity synthesis, and whether the large metric gaps over 3DMM baselines reflect true perceptual superiority or partly the inherent expressivity gap of the underlying representation.
