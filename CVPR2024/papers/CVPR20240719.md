## Problem

Absolute pose regression (APR) methods for LiDAR localization perform a single forward pass to estimate 6-DoF poses, lacking both robust scene-geometry feature learning and the iterative denoising process (e.g., RANSAC) that structure-based methods rely on. This paper identifies these two deficiencies as the primary causes of APR's accuracy gap relative to state-of-the-art correspondence-matching approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240719/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N=3 LiDAR point cloud frames (spaced 2 frames apart) as input, projecting each into a 2D range image of size 32×512 that stores (range, x, y, z, frame-index) per pixel. It outputs a 6-DoF pose (position in meters, orientation in degrees) per frame, and optionally a pose uncertainty estimate computed as the variance across multiple stochastic denoising runs. Inference uses DDIM with 10 steps on Oxford and 15 steps on NCLT, achieving 33–44 ms runtime.

## Method

DiffLoc formulates LiDAR localization as conditional pose generation via a diffusion model with three components: (1) a range-projection preprocessor converting point clouds to 2D images; (2) a feature learner built on DINOv2 (ViT-S/16) augmented with a Static-Object-Aware Pool (SOAP) module that applies a sigmoid-gated channel-squeezed attention mask—trained against SPVCNN-segmented moving-object labels—to reweight features toward static geometry; and (3) an 8-layer transformer denoiser that iteratively refines a noisy pose toward the ground truth, conditioned on the SOAP feature embedding and a sinusoidal step embedding. Training uses L1 loss on predicted noise plus binary cross-entropy for the segmentation mask. Two diffusion-specific extensions are added: pose uncertainty via aggregating multiple stochastic inferences, and Geometric Constraint-Guided Denoising (GCGD), which perturbs the denoiser's noise prediction with gradients of relative-pose consistency from odometry during the final 5 denoising steps.

## Results

On the NCLT dataset, DiffLoc achieves 1.19 m / 2.31° mean position/orientation error, ranking first in both metrics across all four test trajectories. On Oxford Radar RobotCar, DiffLoc achieves 3.53 m / 0.72° average error, ranking second in position and first in orientation. The headline improvement is 35% in position and 34.7% in orientation over the structure-based SOTA (SGLoc) on NCLT, and 38.5%/31.4% over the APR SOTA (HypLiLoc) on Oxford. Runtime is 33 ms (Oxford, 10 denoising steps) and 44 ms (NCLT, 15 steps), both faster than SGLoc (38 ms and 75 ms respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffLoc outperforms all 11 baselines on the NCLT dataset in both position and orientation, achieving 1.19 m / 2.31° versus SGLoc's 1.83 m / 3.54° (35%/34.7% improvement) and HypLiLoc's 1.95 m / 3.16° (39%/26.9% improvement). On Oxford, DiffLoc (3.53 m / 0.72°) trails SGLoc (3.14 m / 1.88°) by 12.4% in position but surpasses it by 61.7% in orientation, and beats the APR SOTA HypLiLoc (5.74 m / 1.05°) by 38.5%/31.4%. Against structure-based retrieval/registration methods (PNVLD, DCP), DiffLoc improves position error by roughly 8× on NCLT (e.g., 0.99 m vs. 7.75 m for PNVLD on trajectory 2012-02-12). Among APR baselines, the closest competitor is NIDALoc (4.47 m / 3.53° on NCLT), which DiffLoc outperforms by 73%/35%. The largest single-trajectory gain is on NCLT 2012-05-26, where DiffLoc (1.88 m / 2.43°) beats SGLoc (3.81 m / 4.74°) by 50.7%/48.7%, indicating robustness in distribution-shift scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffLoc sits at the intersection of two research threads: foundation-model-based feature learning (DINOv2) and diffusion models for iterative denoising. It explicitly targets the performance gap between absolute pose regression (APR) methods and structure-based methods like SGLoc, which rely on RANSAC for robust pose estimation. The key conceptual departure is reframing RANSAC's outlier rejection as a denoising process and implementing it via a diffusion model conditioned on foundation-model features, thereby making the entire pipeline end-to-end trainable without a separate RANSAC stage. The static-object-aware pool (SOAP) module further differentiates the feature learner from prior APR work by explicitly suppressing moving-object interference, building on prior observations that dynamic objects degrade geometric feature quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing much of the accuracy gap between APR and structure-based methods while retaining APR's practical advantages (no preconstructed maps, lower memory, real-time inference at 33–44 ms), DiffLoc makes high-accuracy LiDAR localization more accessible for deployment. The diffusion formulation unlocks two capabilities unavailable to prior APR methods: principled pose uncertainty estimation via multi-sample inference and seamless integration of odometry-based geometric constraints during denoising. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even larger-scale or more diverse environments, whether the DINOv2 dependency limits adaptability to novel sensor configurations, and whether the modest remaining position-accuracy gap on Oxford (3.53 m vs. SGLoc's 3.14 m) can be closed with more denoising steps or richer conditioning.
