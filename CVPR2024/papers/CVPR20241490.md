## Problem

Existing motion prediction methods either generate multiple trajectories without identifying which intention each corresponds to, or rely on a unified latent space that cannot cleanly separate spatially disjoint modes. Moreover, most approaches either ignore prediction uncertainty entirely or require expensive recursive sampling to approximate it. This paper addresses both gaps by explicitly modeling the multi-modal trajectory distribution as a Bayesian mixture and making uncertainty directly readable from the model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241490/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a target-centric vectorized representation: the target agent's H-step history (position, heading, velocity per step) and surrounding HD map polylines (head/tail coordinates), encoded via VectorNet subgraphs. It outputs the full conditional distribution p(s_f | x) as a K-component mixture of multivariate Gaussians over inter-step displacements, where each component's mean and covariance directly encode the predicted trajectory and its uncertainty. A separate assignment network outputs mixture weights from context alone, and an NMS-based sampler converts the distribution into a small set of representative trajectories for standard minADE/minFDE/MR evaluation.

## Method

SeNeVA decomposes the trajectory distribution into K mixture components, each with its own neural network (MLP for displacement prediction, LSTM for temporal latent variable v) sharing a common upstream encoder built from VectorNet subgraphs and LaneGCN-style cascaded multi-head attention. The generative process is trained via variational inference with a mean-field approximation; the z-posterior is reparameterized analytically (Eq. 8) to avoid an extra network while still allowing gradient flow. A separate assignment network (MLP on context x) is trained with focal loss to predict mixture weights, enabling efficient component selection at inference without Monte-Carlo sampling over z. Trajectory sampling uses NMS on the final destination y_{H+T} (whose covariance accumulates over all timesteps), guided by the assignment network's weights, then back-fills intermediate positions assuming uniform uncertainty for smoothness.

## Results

On the INTERACTION test set, SeNeVA achieves a best-in-class minFDE₆ of 0.446 m (outperforming HDGT's 0.478 m by 6.8%), a competitive minADE₆ of 0.203 m (second only to HDGT's 0.168 m), and a 5.35% Miss Rate (second only to MultiModalTransformer's 5.1%). On the Argoverse 2 validation set, SeNeVA leads all baselines with minFDE₆ = 1.319 m, minADE₆ = 0.713 m, and MR = 17.5%, improving over Forecast-MAE by 6.8% in minFDE₆ and 26.0% in minADE₆. The model achieves these results with only 1.3M parameters, substantially fewer than HDGT (15.3M) or MultiModalTransformer (6.3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the INTERACTION test set, SeNeVA outperforms HDGT (the strongest competitor) on minFDE₆ (0.446 vs. 0.478) and MR (0.053 vs. 0.056), but trails on minADE₆ (0.203 vs. 0.168), indicating HDGT produces slightly more accurate intermediate paths while SeNeVA better captures final destinations. Against MultiModalTransformer, SeNeVA wins on both minFDE₆ (0.446 vs. 0.551) and minADE₆ (0.203 vs. 0.213) but has a marginally higher MR (0.053 vs. 0.051). On the Argoverse 2 validation set, SeNeVA dominates both DenseTNT and Forecast-MAE across all three metrics, with a 26.0% improvement in minADE₆ over Forecast-MAE. On the INTERACTION validation set, SeNeVA achieves a 35.7% improvement in minFDE₆ over the second-best method (TNT). For uncertainty quantification, SeNeVA better distinguishes OOD from in-distribution data than Deep Ensembles (Table 2) while running 31× faster (51 ms vs. 1,590 ms inference time).

## Relation to Prior Work

SeNeVA builds on the VectorNet [9] and LaneGCN [27] feature-encoding pipeline for vectorized HD maps and agent trajectories, but departs from prior generative motion predictors (VAEs like Trajectron++ [37], GMM-based models [3,17], and goal-based samplers like TNT/DenseTNT [12,46]) by explicitly decomposing the trajectory distribution into K disjoint mixture components, each parameterized by its own neural network, rather than relying on a unified latent space or endpoint sampling. The variational inference formulation with an LSTM-based latent variable for temporal correlation and a separate assignment network trained with focal loss is the key architectural departure, enabling direct uncertainty quantification from distribution parameters without recursive sampling. The paper positions itself against Deep Ensembles [25] as a more efficient alternative for OOD detection in the driving context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Uncertainty quantification in motion prediction remains underexplored, and SeNeVA provides a principled Bayesian framework that yields per-component covariance and entropy directly from model parameters, which is valuable for downstream risk and safety analysis in autonomous driving. The model's 1.3M-parameter footprint achieving competitive SOTA (0.446 minFDE on INTERACTION test) against 6–15M-parameter baselines suggests the explicit mixture decomposition is a parameter-efficient route to multi-modal prediction. However, the single-agent, target-centric formulation limits scalability to dense traffic scenes, and the mixed OOD results (intersection entropy actually lower for OOD) indicate the uncertainty signal is not uniformly reliable across scenario types. Extending to joint multi-agent distributions and validating OOD detection on larger, more diverse benchmarks are natural next steps.
