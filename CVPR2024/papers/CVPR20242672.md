## Problem

Vision GNNs rely on KNN-based graph construction that requires comparing every patch to every other patch, making them computationally expensive. The existing efficient alternative, static axial graph construction (SVGA in MobileViG), eliminates KNN cost but produces an identical graph for all inputs, negating the core advantage of a dynamic graph-based model. This paper addresses the gap between dynamic representational power and computational efficiency in vision GNN graph construction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 1333×800 for COCO detection/segmentation, 512×512 for ADE20K semantic segmentation) which are split into patches forming graph nodes. Unlike static graph methods, the constructed graph topology changes per input image based on patch similarity. Outputs are task-specific: class labels for classification, bounding boxes and instance masks via Mask R-CNN for detection, and pixel-level semantic masks via Semantic FPN for segmentation.

## Method

The core contribution is Dynamic Axial Graph Construction (DAGC), which estimates the mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) of inter-patch Euclidean distances using a quadrant-flipping trick (comparing diagonal quadrants of the image to reduce comparisons), then applies axial rolling at fixed strides K along rows and columns. A binary mask is generated where two patches are connected only if their Euclidean distance is less than μ − σ, producing a variable number of connections per image. The masked differences feed into a max-relative graph convolution (DynConv) combined with conditional positional encoding (CPE) in a Dynamic Grapher module, followed by an FFN to form the DAGC block. The full GreedyViG architecture interleaves MBConv blocks (local processing) with DAGC blocks (global processing) across four stages with K=8,4,2,1, using a convolutional stem, downsampling between stages, and knowledge distillation from RegNetY-16GF during training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, GreedyViG-S achieves 81.1% top-1 accuracy with 12.0M parameters and 1.6 GMACs, while GreedyViG-B reaches 83.9% top-1 accuracy with only 30.9M parameters and 5.2 GMACs. On COCO object detection, GreedyViG-B achieves 46.3 AP^box and 42.1 AP^mask, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation it reaches 47.4 mIoU. The largest model matches PViHGNN-B's 83.9% top-1 accuracy while using 67.3% fewer parameters and 71.3% fewer GMACs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest GNN baselines, GreedyViG-S (81.1%) outperforms PViG-Ti (78.2%) by 2.9% and PViHGNN-Ti (78.9%) by 2.2% top-1 accuracy with fewer GMACs and similar parameters. GreedyViG-B (83.9%) matches PViHGNN-B (83.9%) exactly while reducing parameters by 67.3% (94.4M → 30.9M) and GMACs by 71.3% (18.1 → 5.2), and beats PViG-B (83.7%) by 0.2% with 66.6% fewer parameters. Compared to the prior CNN-GNN architecture MobileViG-B (82.6%), GreedyViG-B gains 1.3% top-1 accuracy with only 4.2M more parameters and 2.4 more GMACs. Among efficient CNN/ViT hybrids, GreedyViG-M (82.9%) beats ConvNext-T (82.7%) with 6.7M fewer parameters and 4.2 fewer GMACs, and GreedyViG-B (83.9%) surpasses EfficientFormerV2-L (83.3%) by 0.6% with comparable parameters. On downstream tasks, GreedyViG-B achieves 46.3 AP^box on COCO detection, outperforming MobileViG-B (42.0) by 4.3 AP and FastViT-SA36 (43.8) by 2.5 AP, and reaches 47.4 mIoU on ADE20K, beating EfficientFormerV2-L (45.2) by 2.2 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

GreedyViG sits at the intersection of two lines of work: KNN-based dynamic graph construction (Vision GNN, ViHGNN) and static axial graph construction (MobileViG's SVGA). DAGC departs from both by retaining the axial structure of SVGA for efficiency while introducing a data-dependent μ−σ threshold on Euclidean distance to make the graph dynamic across inputs, and by adapting the max-relative graph convolution from DeepGCNs into a rolling-and-masking operation (DynConv) that avoids the 4D↔3D reshaping required by KNN-based methods. The overall architecture also departs from MobileViG by inserting DAGC blocks at every resolution stage rather than only the lowest, combining local MBConv processing with global graph processing throughout the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple statistical threshold on axial distances can replace expensive all-pairs KNN while preserving input-dependent graph structure, yielding 66–71% reductions in parameters and GMACs at matched accuracy versus prior ViG/ViHGNN models. This makes dynamic graph-based vision backbones practically competitive with efficient CNN and ViT architectures across classification, detection, and segmentation. Open questions include whether the fixed axial constraint (only row/column connections) limits expressiveness at higher resolutions, whether the μ−σ threshold generalizes well to non-natural or very high-resolution inputs, and whether the approach extends to 3D or video settings where the axial assumption may break down.
