## Problem

While text-guided image inpainting is well-established, extending it to video introduces three underexplored challenges: maintaining temporal consistency of edited content across frames, supporting diverse inpainting types (object swap, re-texturing, uncropping) that demand different levels of structural fidelity to the source, and robustly handling videos of arbitrary duration without retraining. Prior video editing methods either lack explicit mask conditioning or constrain the target region to be uniform across all frames, limiting flexibility and quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240960/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of arbitrary length, a binary mask on the initial frame specifying the region to edit, and a free-form text prompt describing the desired modification. The mask is propagated to subsequent frames via XMem tracking (for object swap and re-texturing) or replicated identically across frames (for uncropping). The output is a full-length edited video at 512×512 resolution where the masked region is regenerated per the prompt while all out-of-mask pixels remain pixel-identical to the input. Training uses 16-frame clips at 6 FPS; the zero-shot inference pipeline extends this to any number of frames.

## Method

AVID is built in two training stages on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion inpainting LDM. First, following AnimateDiff, the 2D UNet is inflated with pseudo-3D temporal self-attention motion modules, and only these modules are trained on video data with synthetic random masks to preserve the base model's generative quality. Second, a ControlNet-style structure guidance module is trained (with the UNet frozen) using HED edge maps as structural conditions, and at inference a scaling factor ω_s (set to 0 for object swap/uncropping, 1 for re-texturing) modulates how strongly the output adheres to the source geometry. For videos longer than the 16-frame training length, a Temporal MultiDiffusion pipeline applies a sliding window of N frames with stride o and averages overlapping denoised results, while a middle-frame attention guidance mechanism injects K and V vectors from the video's central frame into every self-attention layer to anchor identity and prevent gradual drift across clips.

## Results

On 16-frame videos across three inpainting subtasks (uncropping, object swap, re-texturing), AVID achieves the best temporal consistency (TC) scores of 97.2, 96.5, and 96.3, outperforming all baselines by 0.7–4.7 points. It also attains the best background preservation (BP, lower is better) at 42.3, 41.1, and 40.7 (×10⁻³), substantially ahead of VideoComposer (55.7, 71.0, 64.5). Text-video alignment (TA) is competitive, with AVID matching or exceeding baselines on object swap (31.5) and re-texturing (32.0). In a user study, AVID is preferred 69.1% (uncropping), 74.5% (object swap), and 73.2% (re-texturing) of the time over competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVID is compared against three baselines: per-frame inpainting with LDM (PF), Text2Video-Zero (T2V0), and VideoComposer (VC). AVID clearly wins on temporal consistency across all three subtasks, with the largest margin over PF (up to +4.7 on uncropping) and a consistent +0.7 to +1.3 advantage over T2V0 and VC. On background preservation, AVID is the best or near-best method, while VideoComposer suffers severe degradation (BP of 71.0 on object swap vs. AVID's 41.1), likely due to its uniform-mask constraint altering out-of-mask regions. On text-video alignment, AVID ties VC on object swap (31.5) and is within 0.1 of VC on re-texturing (32.0 vs. 32.1), while T2V0 lags by 1–2 points on all tasks. The user study confirms these automatic metrics: AVID is preferred over all baselines in 69–75% of pairwise comparisons, with the largest gap over PF and T2V0.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVID composes three established techniques into a unified video inpainting pipeline: AnimateDiff-style motion modules for temporal coherence, a ControlNet-style structure guidance module for adjustable structural fidelity, and a temporal extension of MultiDiffusion for zero-shot long-video inference. It departs from prior video editing approaches (Text2Video-Zero, VideoComposer) by explicitly conditioning on a per-frame mask (propagated via XMem) rather than relying solely on text prompts or a uniform mask, and by supporting variable video lengths without retraining. The base model is a pre-trained text-guided image inpainting LDM, which is inflated to video via pseudo-3D layers, preserving the generative quality of the image model while adding temporal dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVID provides the first unified framework that handles multiple video inpainting sub-tasks (object swap, re-texturing, uncropping) at different structural fidelity levels and arbitrary video durations in a single model, making it practically useful for real-world video editing. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the learned motion module (struggles with complex actions), a manually set structure guidance scale that must be tuned per task type, and the fact that automatic metrics (CLIP score, L1 background preservation) do not always align with human perception. Open questions include making the structure guidance scale learnable and prompt-conditioned, and whether stronger text-to-video foundation models would obviate the need for the Temporal MultiDiffusion pipeline.
