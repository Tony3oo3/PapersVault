## Problem

3D scene grouping is inherently ambiguous because a scene can be decomposed at multiple levels of granularity (e.g., an excavator as a whole versus its individual wheels and cabin), and 2D segmentation masks from different viewpoints often conflict. Existing NeRF-based segmentation methods produce only flat, single-level instance masks and do not capture this multi-scale hierarchy or guarantee view consistency across the scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240822/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed RGB images and camera poses as input; SAM's automatic mask generator is run on each image to produce overlapping 2D mask candidates, and a partially trained NeRF renders depth maps to assign each mask a physical 3D scale (2×std of its back-projected points). The output is a scale-conditioned 3D affinity field (queryable at any continuous Euclidean scale up to 2× camera extent) plus a standard NeRF radiance field; from the affinity field, a hierarchical tree of 3D groups is extracted via recursive HDBSCAN clustering at descending scales, yielding view-consistent pointclouds or image-space masks at arbitrary granularity.

## Method

GARField optimizes a scale-conditioned 3D feature field (hashgrid with 24 layers + 4-layer MLP taking scale as an extra input, built on Nerfacto) using a margin-based contrastive loss that pulls features of points in the same mask together and pushes features of points in different masks apart, applied only within each training image. The key insight is that scale-conditioning resolves conflicting 2D masks: the same 3D point can have high affinity at a large scale (belonging to the whole object) and low affinity at a small scale (belonging to a subpart), so contradictory masks coexist rather than fighting during training. Two auxiliary mechanisms enforce hierarchy: continuous scale supervision (uniformly augmenting the query scale between adjacent mask scales to eliminate unsupervised gaps) and a containment loss (additionally pulling together points grouped at scale s at a randomly sampled larger scale s′). After training, the scene is decomposed by recursively running HDBSCAN on volumetrically rendered features at decreasing scales (ε=0.05) until no further splits emerge, producing a coarse-to-fine tree of 3D groups.

## Results

On the 3D Completeness metric (mIOU against multi-view human annotations), GARField outperforms SAM across all five scenes and all three granularity levels (Fine, Medium, Coarse). The largest gains appear at fine granularity, where GARField improves from 17.4 to 76.0 mIOU on the bouquet scene and from 65.3 to 88.8 on the keyboard scene. On Hierarchical Grouping Recall (Table 2), GARField achieves 76.4–86.6 mIOU versus SAM's 23.2–74.9 mIOU, with the most dramatic improvement on the keyboard scene (23.2 → 77.9). Ablations confirm that both scale-conditioning and dense hierarchy supervision are necessary, with removing either dropping performance by 5–30 mIOU points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is Segment Anything (SAM) [15], compared on both 3D Completeness and Hierarchical Grouping Recall across five in-the-wild scenes. GARField wins on every scene and every granularity level in both metrics. The advantage is most pronounced at fine granularity, where SAM struggles to recall small groups from a single viewpoint (e.g., 17.4 mIOU on bouquet, 23.2 on keyboard), while GARField's multi-view 3D fusion recovers these groups (76.0 and 77.9 respectively). At coarse granularity the gap narrows (e.g., 92.6 vs 95.5 on ramen) because SAM's larger masks are already more view-consistent. Structurally, GARField guarantees view-consistent groups by construction since it operates in 3D, whereas SAM's per-view masks have no such guarantee, which is the fundamental reason for the consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

GARField builds directly on LERF's scale-conditioned feature field formulation, repurposing multi-scale features not to reconstruct 2D embeddings (as in LERF) but to resolve grouping ambiguity in 3D. It extends the contrastive-loss paradigm from Contrastive Lift and NeRF-SOS by making the loss scale-conditioned, which eliminates the need for their slow-fast training formulation. The 2D mask candidates come from SAM's automatic mask generator, and the hierarchical decomposition via recursive HDBSCAN clustering draws on the 2D hierarchical segmentation literature (spectral clustering, multiscale combinatorial grouping), but lifts it into a view-consistent 3D volumetric setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GARField provides a practical pipeline for extracting hierarchical 3D scene structure from posed images without user interaction, enabling downstream tasks like 3D asset extraction, interactive segmentation, and robot scene understanding. Key limitations include dependence on SAM's mask quality (missing groups won't appear in 3D), the assumption that physical scale is the correct ambiguity-resolution cue (same-scale conflicts, e.g., container vs. contained object, remain unresolved), and scale clamping that can fail for large background groups. Open questions include whether affordance-based or semantic cues could complement scale for disambiguation, and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes.
