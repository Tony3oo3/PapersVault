## Problem

Existing adversarially robust knowledge distillation methods align teacher and student predictions only on natural samples and their final adversarial counterparts, ignoring the intermediate adversarial samples generated along the multi-step PGD path that encode rich decision-boundary structure. Moreover, prior work relies exclusively on untargeted adversaries (perturbation toward the nearest boundary), overlooking targeted adversarial paths that capture complementary inter-class boundary geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240750/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and outputs C-class probability vectors. During training, each natural sample is augmented with n−1 intermediate adversarial samples generated via multi-step PGD (both untargeted and targeted variants), and the teacher's prediction on the *natural* sample serves as the alignment target for the student's predictions on all intermediate adversaries. At inference, only the student model with its primary BatchNorm layer is used for classification.

## Method

DARWIN combines three loss terms: (1) ARKD, which aligns teacher and student softmax outputs on natural and final adversarial samples via KL divergence; (2) IAKD, which extends alignment to intermediate adversarial samples using an adaptive per-sample weight that interpolates between an index-based prior (closer to the final adversary → higher weight) and a batch-normalized prediction-discrepancy signal, effectively emphasizing samples where the student's confidence in the true class drops fastest; and (3) DBKD, a dual-branch triplet loss that simultaneously generates untargeted adversaries (gradient ascent away from the true class) and targeted adversaries (gradient descent toward a different class), attracting each branch's student predictions toward its own teacher natural prediction while repelling them from the other branch's teacher prediction. Separate BatchNorm layers disentangle the two adversarial distributions, and the theoretical analysis shows the weighting scheme captures localized κ-Lipschitz smoothness, so minimizing the combined loss reduces an upper bound on the adversarially robust risk.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a WRN-34 teacher and ResNet-18 student (ε=8/255), DARWIN achieves 84.48% natural accuracy and 55.07% PGD-20 robust accuracy, outperforming the closest baseline RSLAD (83.12% / 53.91%) by +1.36 and +1.16 points respectively. On CIFAR-100 under the same setting, DARWIN reaches 59.12% natural and 32.30% PGD-20 robust accuracy, again leading all baselines. The label-free variant DARWIN-LF matches these gains (84.35% / 55.02% on CIFAR-10) without requiring ground-truth labels during adversary generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARWIN outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The closest competitor is RSLAD [64], which DARWIN surpasses by +1.36% natural and +1.16% PGD-20 robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), and by +1.89% natural and +1.22% PGD-20 on CIFAR-100. Against ARD [15], the gains are larger: +2.81% PGD-20 on CIFAR-10, reflecting ARD's reliance on only the final adversarial sample. CRDND [54] achieves the second-best natural accuracy (83.92%) but trails DARWIN by +2.37% PGD-20 and +3.19% AA on CIFAR-10, indicating weaker adversarial robustness transfer. GACD [1] and IAD [63] are consistently outperformed by 1.5–3.2% in robust accuracy. Notably, the label-free DARWIN-LF variant matches or slightly exceeds the label-based DARWIN on AutoAttack (52.33% vs 52.24% on CIFAR-10), confirming that teacher-predicted labels suffice for effective adversary generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarially robust knowledge distillation (ARKD) line of work initiated by ARD [15], which aligns student and teacher predictions on natural and final adversarial samples only. Prior methods (RSLAD [64], IAD [63], CRDND [54], GACD [1]) have refined the alignment objective—using robust soft labels, contrastive features, or adaptive denoising—but all treat the adversarial sample as a single endpoint. DARWIN departs by exploiting the full multi-step PGD trajectory (intermediate adversarial samples) and introduces a dual-branch mechanism that jointly uses untargeted and targeted adversarial paths, a combination not explored in prior robust distillation work. The adaptive weighting scheme and the theoretical connection to an upper bound on robust risk (Theorem 3) further distinguish the contribution from purely empirical distillation variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lightweight models remain the primary deployment target for safety-critical applications, yet adversarial training is computationally prohibitive for them; robust distillation is the practical bridge, and DARWIN's consistent 1–3% robust-accuracy gains across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100, and ViT-teacher settings make it a strong default choice for practitioners. The label-free variant (DARWIN-LF) matching the labeled version nearly exactly broadens applicability to black-box model extraction and unlabeled-data scenarios. A key open question highlighted by the authors is the condition τ_bdy ≥ R_nat(D) from Theorem 3: when it fails (as observed for ResNet-18 self-distillation), a small natural-accuracy drop occurs, and it remains unclear whether the dual-branch mechanism or the weighting scheme can be adapted to guarantee the bound holds universally. Scaling to full ImageNet-1k and to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation) is left for future work.
