## Problem

Existing single-image-to-3D generation methods that leverage 2D diffusion priors suffer from distorted outline shapes and poor texture quality, particularly under large viewpoint changes. Directly combining view-conditioned diffusion (for shape fidelity) with stable diffusion (for texture quality) leads to entanglement between the two types of prior knowledge, making it difficult to achieve both faithful geometry and visually pleasing appearance simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240804/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (assumed to be a front view at polar angle 90° and azimuth angle 0°) and produces a textured 3D mesh via DMTet. The pipeline operates at two resolutions: a coarse NeRF stage at 128×128 followed by a high-resolution mesh refinement at 1024×1024. A text prompt is automatically derived from the input image via textual inversion, and 2D outline shape masks are computed from the intermediate NeRF via opacity calculation to bridge the two optimization stages.

## Method

MPOD123 is a two-stage progressive optimization framework that disentangles outline shape optimization from detail appearance optimization. In Stage 1, Zero-1-to-3 (a view-conditioned diffusion model) guides NeRF optimization via SDS loss to produce a coarse 3D shape with faithful outlines; 2D masks are then extracted by computing per-pixel opacity (rather than volume-rendered color) to transfer shape priors. In Stage 2, the NeRF is converted to a DMTet mesh and refined using Detail Appearance Inpainting (DAI), which modifies Stable Diffusion with a Mask Rectified Cross-Attention (MRCA) module that sets the attention map entries for the object token ⟨e⟩ to −inf outside the mask, forcing semantic injection only within the outline region. A supplementary novel-view mask loss (L_nvm) further constrains rendered opacity to match the mask, preventing shape drift during texture refinement.

## Results

On RealFusion15, MPOD123 achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CX distance of 1.54 (lower is better), CLIP score of 0.86, and multi-view consistency (MVC) of 0.90. On the newly introduced TBPR-Shoes e-commerce dataset, MPOD123 similarly leads with CX 1.29, CLIP 0.78, and MVC 0.86. The largest margin over the strongest baseline (Magic123) appears on TBPR-Shoes, where MPOD123 improves CLIP by 0.07 and MVC by 0.12, indicating substantially better semantic fidelity and cross-view consistency on real-world product images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPOD123 outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against Magic123 (the strongest competitor), MPOD123 gains 0.03 in CLIP and 0.03 in MVC on RealFusion15, and a larger 0.07 in CLIP and 0.12 in MVC on TBPR-Shoes, showing that the advantage grows on out-of-distribution real product images. Compared to Zero-1-to-3, MPOD123 improves CLIP by 0.12 (0.86 vs 0.74) on RealFusion15 and 0.22 (0.78 vs 0.56) on TBPR-Shoes, confirming that the detail appearance inpainting stage substantially recovers texture quality that the view-conditioned prior alone cannot provide. Make-it-3D achieves competitive texture (CLIP 0.77 on RealFusion15) but lags in shape consistency (MVC 0.84) and pixel-level fidelity (CX 1.79), while RealFusion is weakest overall across all metrics. Qualitatively, the paper notes that Magic123 exhibits multi-face Janus artifacts and shape distortions, whereas MPOD123 maintains faithful outlines with high-quality local geometry and textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPOD123 extends the line of single-image-to-3D generation methods that leverage 2D diffusion priors via Score Distillation Sampling (RealFusion, Make-it-3D, Magic123), but departs from the common practice of applying a single diffusion prior throughout optimization. It specifically builds on Zero-1-to-3 for view-conditioned shape guidance and on DMTet for high-resolution mesh extraction, while introducing a novel disentanglement strategy: opacity-based 2D masks transfer outline shape priors from a NeRF stage to a mesh refinement stage. The Mask Rectified Cross-Attention (MRCA) module is a lightweight alternative to heavier mask-injection approaches like ControlNet, repurposing the cross-attention mechanism already present in Stable Diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive shape-then-detail framework addresses a persistent tension in single-image-to-3D: view-conditioned models preserve geometry but produce poor textures, while general diffusion models produce rich textures but distort shape. The MRCA module offers a simple, plug-and-play mechanism for mask-guided generation that could be adopted beyond this specific pipeline. However, the evaluation is limited to small test sets (15 and 17 examples), the method is restricted to single objects, and the reliance on Zero-1-to-3 for shape priors inherits its training distribution biases. Open questions include whether the two-stage approach generalizes to multi-object scenes and whether the mask-based shape transfer degrades at extreme viewpoint changes.
