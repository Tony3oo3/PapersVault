## Problem

The boundary discontinuity problem in oriented object detection—where angular predictions mutate near the π boundary—has been long attributed to sharp loss increases, and IoU-like joint-optimization methods were widely believed to solve it. This paper experimentally demonstrates that even state-of-the-art IoU-like methods (KFIoU, KLD) still suffer severe angular mutation, and identifies the true root cause: the model is forced to fit a piecewise function (box angle = object angle mod π) with a breakpoint, which is inherently difficult for continuous neural networks regardless of loss smoothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241204/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes RGB aerial/satellite images (cropped to 1024×1024 patches for DOTA, 640-pixel long side for HRSC2016/UCAS-AOD) and outputs rotated bounding boxes (x, y, w, h, θ) with class labels. Unusually, the angle head outputs a 2-dimensional complex-exponential encoding f_p = (cos 2θ, sin 2θ) rather than the angle directly; the actual angle is recovered via θ_p = -½·atan2(sin 2θ, cos 2θ), making the network's fitting target a smooth periodic function instead of a discontinuous one.

## Method

The paper proposes a dual-optimization paradigm that decouples angle correction from joint parameter blending into two distinct encoding functions. The Angle Correct Module (ACM) uses complex-exponential encoding f(θ) = e^(jωθ) with ω=2, which is continuous, differentiable, reversible, and satisfies f(θ_box) = f(θ_obj) (eliminating the breakpoint). Training combines two losses: L_acm = smooth-L1(f_p, f_t) supervises the angular encoding directly, while L_box applies a standard IoU-like loss (KLD, KFIoU, GWD, or SkewIoU) on the decoded angle θ_p = f_p^(-1) jointly with position and scale. This design ensures the network fits a smooth target (correcting the boundary) while still benefiting from joint optimization (blending angle with other parameters), achieving rotation equivariance without sacrificing the advantages of IoU-like losses.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, CenterNet-ACM achieves 78.53% AP50 and RoI-Transformer-ACM achieves 79.45% AP50, outperforming the strongest baselines KLD (78.32%) and PSC (78.07%). On HRSC2016, ACM plugged into KFIoU boosts AP75 from 62.95% to 87.77% (+24.82), and on UCAS-AOD(Plane) the improvement is even more dramatic: KFIoU's AP75 jumps from 16.81% to 74.48% (+57.67). The paper demonstrates that all four IoU-like losses (GWD, KLD, KFIoU, SkewIoU) converge to similar performance levels (~73.7–74.5% AP50, ~40.5–43.0% AP75 on DOTA) once ACM corrects the angular boundary, confirming that the primary distinction among them was their inability to handle angle regression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DOTA-v1.0 (AP50), RoI-Transformer-ACM (79.45%) surpasses all 21 baselines, including KLD (78.32%), PSC (78.07%), and RIDet (77.62%), while CenterNet-ACM (78.53%) edges out KLD by 0.21 points. The more striking gains appear in AP75 on single-category datasets: ACM plugged into KFIoU improves AP75 by +24.82 on HRSC2016 (62.95→87.77) and by +57.67 on UCAS-AOD(Plane) (16.81→74.48), where 2D-Gaussian-based losses (GWD, KLD, KFIoU) fundamentally fail on square-like objects. Similarly, ACM-KLD gains +46.46 AP75 on UCAS-AOD(Plane) and ACM-GWD gains +37.78. On DOTA (complex multi-category scenes), all four IoU-like losses converge to a narrow band (AP50: 73.71–74.51%, AP75: 40.49–42.97%) after ACM correction, indicating their prior differences were dominated by the unresolved boundary discontinuity rather than by the loss formulation itself. The paper does not report a head-to-head loss to any baseline; every comparison is a win or near-tie.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the entire lineage of boundary discontinuity solutions in oriented object detection (CSL, GWD, KLD, KFIoU, SkewIoU, PSC) by arguing that the critical distinction is not joint vs. independent optimization but rather the *encoding mode* of the smoothing function: whether the model directly outputs a smooth encoding (implicit) or outputs the raw angle and applies smoothing only in the loss (explicit). It departs from the mainstream IoU-like paradigm by proposing a dual-optimization framework where the model outputs a complex-exponential angular encoding (ACM) subject to explicit supervision, and a separate joint-optim loss is applied on the decoded angle. The complex-exponential function is inspired by PSC's phase-shift coding from communications but is fundamentally different in form and constraint structure, requiring only 2 dimensions versus PSC's N≥3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art IoU-like methods (KLD, KFIoU) do not actually eliminate boundary discontinuity corrects a long-held misconception in the oriented detection community and reframes the design space for angular regression. The ACM module is a lightweight, plug-and-play component that yields substantial AP75 gains (up to ~57% on square-like objects) across multiple detectors and datasets, making it practically attractive. Open questions include whether the dual-optimization paradigm generalizes to 3D oriented detection or other periodic parameter spaces, and whether the fixed ω=2 choice is universally optimal or should be adapted per object aspect ratio.
