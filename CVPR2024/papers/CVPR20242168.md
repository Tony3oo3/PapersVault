## Problem

Existing 3D affordance segmentation methods rely solely on visual cues (geometry or 2D image demonstrations), tying predictions to predefined affordance categories and preventing integration with LLM-based task planners that decompose commands into affordance-aware subtasks. This visual-only paradigm limits generalization to unseen object-affordance pairings and leaves a critical gap in translating natural language instructions into actionable 3D part-level knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object point cloud (N×3 coordinates) and a free-form natural language question (varying in length and sentence structure) as input, and produces a per-point binary segmentation mask (N,) delineating the functional region relevant to the question. Notably, no explicit affordance category labels are used during training or testing; the model must infer the target region purely from question semantics. The dataset comprises 19,751 point-question pairs over 8,434 shapes and 870 expert-crafted questions, with questions diversified via GPT-4 under principles of contextual enrichment, concise phrasing, and structural diversity.

## Method

PointRefer combines two key modules: (1) an Adaptive Fusion Module (AFM) injected at multiple decoder stages of a PointNet++ backbone, which groups point features by text tokens via lightweight cross-attention, mixes them with an MLP-Mixer, and ungroups back to point space, enabling multi-scale text-point fusion that adapts to target regions of varying size; (2) a Referred Point Decoder (RPD) that forms question-conditioned affordance queries by adding RoBERTa-encoded question embeddings to learnable position embeddings, processes them through a transformer decoder, and generates L dynamic 1×1 convolution kernels via an MLP. These kernels are convolved with the AFM-enhanced point features to yield L point masks, which are mean-pooled and sigmoid-activated into the final segmentation mask. Training uses only Dice and BCE losses on the mask, deliberately avoiding auxiliary affordance-type predictions to enforce a direct language-to-geometry mapping.

## Results

On the LASO dataset, PointRefer achieves 20.8 mIoU and 87.3% AUC in the Seen setting, and 14.6 mIoU and 80.2% AUC in the Unseen setting, outperforming all baselines on every metric (mIoU, AUC, SIM, MAE). The closest competitor, IAGNet, reaches 17.8 mIoU (Seen) and 12.9 mIoU (Unseen), yielding a 3.0-point and 1.7-point mIoU gap respectively. PointRefer also attains the best SIM (0.629 Seen / 0.507 Unseen) and lowest MAE (0.093 Seen / 0.119 Unseen) across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointRefer outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both Seen and Unseen settings. The strongest baseline is IAGNet [44], a 3D affordance method adapted by replacing its image backbone with a language model; PointRefer surpasses it by 3.0 mIoU (20.8 vs. 17.8) and 5.0 AUC points (87.3 vs. 82.3) in Seen, and by 1.7 mIoU (14.6 vs. 12.9) and 2.4 AUC points (80.2 vs. 77.8) in Unseen. Among the image-based referring segmentation baselines, ReLA [22] (15.2 mIoU Seen) and ReferTrans [15] (13.7 mIoU Seen) trail PointRefer by 5.6 and 7.1 mIoU respectively, with the gap widening in Unseen (3.9 and 4.4 mIoU). 3D-SPS [26], a language-guided 3D grounding model, performs worst overall (11.4 mIoU Seen, 7.9 mIoU Unseen), likely due to its non-differentiable progressive point selection mechanism being ill-suited for dense segmentation. The consistent performance drop from Seen to Unseen across all methods (e.g., IAGNet drops 4.9 mIoU, PointRefer drops 6.2 mIoU) highlights the difficulty of generalizing affordance knowledge to unseen object-affordance pairings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of 3D affordance segmentation and language-conditioned perception. Prior 3D affordance work (3D-AffordanceNet, IAGNet) conditions on visual cues—either explicit affordance categories or 2D image demonstrations—while text-point cloud cross-modal work (ScanRefer, 3D-SPS, BUTD-DETR) handles scene-level grounding or QA but not dense part-level prediction. LASO repurposes the DETR-style learnable query mechanism and cross-attention fusion from referring segmentation and 3D grounding, but redirects them toward a novel objective: using a natural language question to isolate a functional part of a single 3D object, thereby creating a direct interface for LLM-generated subtask instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LASO addresses a concrete gap in the LLM-to-robot pipeline: while LLMs can decompose commands into affordance-aware sub-questions (e.g., 'how to open the microwave'), no prior model could translate such a question into a 3D segmentation of the relevant part. The significant seen-to-unseen performance drop (mIoU 20.8 → 14.6) highlights that current models struggle to generalize affordance knowledge across object classes, suggesting that integrating LLM commonsense reasoning could be a key future direction. Limitations include the relatively small dataset (19,751 pairs, 870 questions) and the very low absolute accuracy, indicating the task remains far from solved.
