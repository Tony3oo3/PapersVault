## Problem

Existing VideoQA methods operate as black-boxes that cannot transparently identify which video clips support their answers or consistently reason over decomposed sub-questions, leading to poor compositional consistency—especially on questions involving temporal relations and multiple visual clues. Additionally, the compositional consistency metrics proposed by AGQA-Decomp (CA, RWR, Delta) are asymmetric and unstable, failing to distinguish models with equivalent reasoning quality.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a video represented as three hierarchical feature tensors (object-level F_o ∈ R^{n_c×n_f×n_o×h_v}, appearance-level F_a ∈ R^{n_c×n_f×h_v}, motion-level F_m ∈ R^{n_c×h_v}), a main question with its decomposed sub-questions, and a Question Decomposition Graph (QDG) whose edges carry operator-type attributes. It outputs predicted answers for both the main question and all sub-questions, plus a binary indicator of relevant video clips per question. The framework is model-agnostic: any existing VidQA backbone F:(v,q)→f_{v,q} can be wrapped, and for datasets lacking QDGs (e.g., MSVD, NExT-QA), an LLM-based pipeline auto-generates the decomposition.

## Method

VA³ adds two modules around a frozen VidQA backbone. The hierarchical video aligner performs bottom-up question-conditioned aggregation (object→appearance→motion) via transformer layers where video features serve as queries and question features as keys/values, then applies Gumbel-Softmax to produce a differentiable binary clip-selection indicator trained with a contrastive loss that pulls relevant-clip features toward positive samples and away from irrelevant-clip features. The answer aggregator uses a multi-layer GAT to propagate joint video-question features along the QDG edges, enabling sub-question information to correct main-question predictions; a triplet contrastive loss on edge-type representations enforces that same-type decomposition operators yield similar edge embeddings while different types are pushed apart. Both modules are trained jointly with cross-entropy answer losses, and the entire framework is plug-and-play across memory-based, graph-based, and hierarchy-based VidQA architectures.

## Results

On AGQA-Decomp, the VA³ framework improves overall main-question accuracy by 1.23%–2.71% and sub-question accuracy by 3.16%–3.29% across three baselines (HME, HGA, HQGA). Compositional consistency, measured by c-F₁, improves by 2.97%–3.54%, while Nc-F₁ rises by 0.11%–0.64%. When extended to MSVD and NExT-QA via the LLM-based question decomposition pipeline, overall accuracy gains reach 3.23%–4.76% (MSVD) and 3.58%–4.51% (NExT-QA), confirming broad applicability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three VidQA baselines representing different architectural families: HME (memory-based, CVPR 2019), HGA (graph-based, AAAI 2020), and HQGA (hierarchy-based, AAAI 2022). VA³ consistently outperforms all three on every reported metric. Against HME, the largest gains appear: main accuracy +2.71%, sub-question accuracy +3.16%, and c-F₁ +3.54%. Against HGA (the strongest baseline at 46.61% main accuracy), VA³(HGA) reaches 47.88% main accuracy and 63.75% c-F₁, a +2.97% consistency gain. Against HQGA, improvements are +1.23% main accuracy and +3.17% c-F₁. In the ablation (Table 4), the proposed hierarchical video aligner also outperforms the EIGV [34] grounding module, confirming that the multi-level (object→appearance→motion) interaction yields better clip selection than the prior single-level approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two main threads: (1) video grounding for VidQA, where EIGV [34] introduced contrastive learning to identify causal vs. environment clips, and (2) compositional consistency evaluation from AGQA-Decomp [12], which defined the Question Decomposition Graph (QDG) and the CA/RWR/Delta metrics. The key departure is making the framework model-agnostic: rather than proposing a new end-to-end VidQA architecture (as NMN-based methods like DSTN do with predefined modules), VA³ wraps around any existing backbone (memory-, graph-, or hierarchy-based) by adding a hierarchical video aligner (object→appearance→motion, extending EIGV's flat grounding) and a GAT-based answer aggregator that propagates information along the QDG. The LLM-based automatic question decomposition pipeline further generalizes the framework to datasets lacking QDGs, a practical extension not addressed by AGQA-Decomp.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a real interpretability gap in VidQA: black-box models can answer correctly for the wrong reasons, and the new c-F1/Nc-F1 metrics expose asymmetries in the widely-used CA/RWR/Delta system that could mislead model comparison. The model-agnostic design means practitioners can adopt VA³ without retraining their backbone from scratch, and the LLM decomposition pipeline lowers the barrier to applying compositional evaluation to arbitrary VidQA benchmarks. However, the accuracy gains are modest (1–5%), the framework adds non-trivial training complexity (two contrastive losses, Gumbel-Softmax, GAT), and the LLM decomposition quality is an uncontrolled variable that could limit real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos with deeper QDGs and whether the contrastive edge-type prior generalizes beyond the small set of edge types in AGQA-Decomp.
