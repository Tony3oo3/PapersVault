## Problem

Conventional visual localization systems rely on learned or hand-crafted image descriptors, which are costly to store, vulnerable to privacy leakage in client-server settings, and brittle under illumination or scene changes. While line-based geometric methods exist, they typically still extract photometric features along lines or require neural network inference, and prior descriptor-free approaches could not simultaneously achieve high accuracy and scalable runtime on large multi-room maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241608/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360° panorama image and a pre-built 3D line map (line segments with endpoints in R³ from SfM or laser scans) as input, and outputs a full 6DoF camera pose (rotation R ∈ SO(3), translation t ∈ R³). 2D lines are extracted from the panorama and represented as start-end point pairs on the unit sphere S²; 3D lines are similarly represented in R³. No pixel-level descriptors, color information, or trained features are consumed at any stage—only the geometric positions and directions of line segments and their pairwise intersections.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, lines in both 2D and 3D are clustered into three groups by their principal (vanishing-point) directions, and intersection points are computed between all pairs of lines belonging to different clusters, yielding three labeled intersection sets per view. Second, pose search scores a pool of N_t × N_r candidate poses by comparing combined line distance functions (LDF) and point distance functions (PDF) on the sphere; a key efficiency trick decouples rotation from translation by pre-computing and caching 3D distance functions in a canonical frame, then only rotating the 2D distance functions at query time, with nearest-neighbor interpolation further reducing cost (Theorem 1 bounds the approximation error by the query-point spacing δ). Third, top-K poses are refined via an ICP-like loop: translation is optimized by minimizing L1 reprojection error over cluster-guided and close-projection intersection matches, then rotation is refined by aligning the incident line directions of the matched intersections, all without any visual descriptor.

## Results

On the combined OmniScenes and Stanford 2D-3D-S evaluation, the proposed fully geometric method achieves 0.67 accuracy at (0.1 m, 5°) and 0.76 at (0.3 m, 15°) for pose refinement, outperforming the best prior geometric method GoMatch (0.61 / 0.74) by 6 and 2 percentage points respectively. In large-scale localization (Table 1), the method reaches 0.76 accuracy on the 7-room OmniScenes map while completing pose search in 0.02 s (vs. 0.23–0.24 s for descriptor-based baselines) and requiring only 2.56 GB of map storage (vs. 129–134 GB). Under illumination variations, the method maintains accuracy within a 0.05 range (std 0.02), roughly half the variability of descriptor-based baselines like GlueStick (range 0.12, std 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest geometric baseline GoMatch [61], the proposed method improves accuracy from 0.61 to 0.67 at (0.1 m, 5°) and from 0.74 to 0.76 at (0.3 m, 15°), a consistent 5–6 point gain attributable to the line-intersection-based refinement. Compared to descriptor-based point matchers (LightGlue 0.79, SuperGlue 0.78 at 0.1 m/5°), the geometric method trails by roughly 12 percentage points, but this gap narrows to 5 points at the looser (0.3 m, 15°) threshold. In large-scale localization (Table 1), the method matches or exceeds SFRS+LightGlue (0.60–0.68) and CosPlace+LightGlue (0.58–0.69) across four multi-room maps while running pose search 10× faster (0.02 s vs. 0.23 s) and using 50× less map storage (2.56 GB vs. ~130 GB). Under illumination changes (Table 5), the method (0.74–0.77) stays within 0.05 of its original accuracy, whereas GlueStick drops to 0.80 under intensity changes and GoMatch falls to 0.63 under gamma shifts. For floorplan localization on Structured3D (Table 6), the method achieves 0.95 at (0.1 m, 5°) with full 6DoF estimation, outperforming Laser2D (0.79) and LaLaLoc++ (0.72) which only estimate 2D translation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LDL (Kim et al., ICCV 2023), which introduced line distance functions and principal-direction-based pose search for panoramic localization. The key departures are: (1) replacing LDL's descriptor-based refinement (PnP-RANSAC with local features) with a fully geometric refinement using line intersections and cluster-guided matching, and (2) introducing point distance functions from pairwise line intersections as a finer-grained representation alongside the original line distance functions. The paper also positions itself against the broader descriptor-free localization literature (GoMatch, BPnPNet), arguing that those methods still rely on visual descriptors in parts of the pipeline, whereas this work eliminates them entirely. The efficient decoupling of rotation and translation for 3D distance functions, supported by a theoretical bound (Theorem 1), is a novel computational contribution not present in LDL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 50× reduction in map size (2.56 GB vs. 129+ GB) and an order-of-magnitude speedup in pose search make fully geometric localization viable for large-scale, privacy-sensitive deployments where storing or transmitting visual descriptors is infeasible. The robustness to illumination changes (much smaller variance than descriptor-based methods) addresses a persistent failure mode in real-world localization. Open questions include the method's dependence on reliable line extraction (performance may degrade in textureless or highly cluttered scenes), the need for 2D layout annotations in the floorplan application, and whether the geometric-only approach can match learned descriptors in outdoor or highly repetitive urban environments beyond the tested indoor settings.
