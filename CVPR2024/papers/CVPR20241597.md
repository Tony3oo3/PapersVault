## Problem

Medical image analysis suffers from a long-tailed distribution where rare tumor categories are underrepresented, making it difficult to build a unified model that segments unseen tumor types without prior annotated exposure. Existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods assume generated mask proposals cover all objects of interest, but tumor visual cues are far more subtle and ambiguous than natural-image objects, causing this assumption to break down and leaving unseen tumors poorly covered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241597/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D CT volumes (processed as 96×96×96 patches) and produces per-voxel segmentation masks for both 25 seen organ classes and tumor categories, along with class-level predictions via query embeddings. During inference, no fine-tuning or retraining is required for unseen tumor types; the model also consumes GPT-4-generated textual descriptions of each class (covering location, shape, size, and anatomical structure) encoded by ClinicalBERT to provide cross-modal semantic grounding at the feature level.

## Method

ZePT partitions object queries into two subsets and decouples learning into two stages. In Stage-I, fundamental queries are trained with an object-aware feature grouping strategy that uses Gumbel-Softmax assignment and a straight-through trick to force each query to represent a distinct organ region without overlap. In Stage-II, advanced queries are guided by self-generated visual prompts: anomaly score maps computed as the negative maximum affinity between fundamental query embeddings and multi-scale visual features highlight regions deviating from known organ anatomy, and a prompt-based masked attention mechanism (setting attention to −∞ outside anomalous regions) directs advanced queries to focus exclusively on pathological changes. A Query Refinement Decoder further concatenates fundamental and advanced queries for self-attention to enforce semantic separation between organs and tumors. Finally, query-knowledge alignment performs feature-level cross-modal matching between query embeddings and ClinicalBERT-encoded GPT-4 medical descriptions, replacing the CLIP-based proposal classification used in conventional OVSS methods.

## Results

On the MSD dataset, ZePT achieves an average DSC of 37.07% across four unseen tumor categories (pancreas 37.67%, lung 27.22%, hepatic vessel 52.94%, colon 30.45%), surpassing the best OVSS baseline OVSeg by 15.85% in DSC, 17.43% in AUROC, and 23.27% in FPR95. On the real-world colon tumor dataset, ZePT reaches 36.23% DSC and 84.35% AUROC, outperforming all baselines by at least 20.18% in DSC. For seen categories, ZePT improves over nnUNet by 4.83% DSC on BTCV, 4.51% on LiTS, and 2.21% on KiTS, while also matching or exceeding the Universal model trained on 3,410 CT scans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among OVSS baselines, OVSeg is the strongest competitor, achieving 19.36% DSC on pancreas tumors and 35.66% on hepatic vessel tumors; ZePT surpasses it by 18.31% and 17.28% DSC respectively, with even larger gaps on lung (27.22% vs 14.11%) and colon (30.45% vs 15.76%) tumors. Against OOD detection baselines, ZePT outperforms the best method MaxQuery by 21.81% AUROC, 25.47% FPR95, and 17.78% DSC on average across four MSD tasks, demonstrating that cross-modal alignment with medical domain knowledge provides substantial gains over purely visual anomaly detection. On seen-category segmentation (Table 3), ZePT beats nnUNet by 4.83% DSC on BTCV and 4.51% on LiTS, and slightly exceeds the Universal model (87.09 vs 86.38 on BTCV, 81.66 vs 80.58 on LiTS, 87.73 vs 87.05 on KiTS) despite being trained on fewer datasets (10 vs 14). On the real-world colon tumor dataset, ZePT's 36.23% DSC exceeds the next-best OVSeg (16.05%) by 20.18% and MaxQuery (15.24%) by 20.99%, with a notably lower FPR95 of 38.29% versus 64.84% and 67.65%, which is critical for clinical safety. ZePT also uses fewer parameters (745.94M vs 950–1078M) and ~23% of the FLOPs compared to OVSS methods that require a frozen CLIP vision encoder for proposal classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZePT builds on the MaskFormer/Mask2Former query-based segmentation framework and the open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) paradigm (ZegFormer, OVSeg, FreeSeg, zsseg), but departs from them in two fundamental ways. First, it rejects the OVSS assumption that class-agnostic proposals cover all objects of interest, arguing that tumor visual cues are too subtle for a single query set to generate adequate proposals; instead it disentangles queries into fundamental (organ) and advanced (tumor) subsets trained in separate stages. Second, it replaces the CLIP-based proposal classification step with a self-prompting mechanism where anomaly score maps derived from fundamental queries guide advanced queries, and with feature-level query-knowledge alignment using GPT-4-generated medical descriptions encoded by ClinicalBERT rather than simple text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot tumor segmentation addresses a critical clinical bottleneck: obtaining expert annotations for every rare or novel tumor category is prohibitively expensive, and the long-tailed distribution of diseases means most categories will never have sufficient training data. The finding that self-generated visual prompts can match or exceed manual bounding-box prompts (Table 5) is practically significant, as it removes the need for any user interaction in a fully automated pipeline. However, the absolute DSC on unseen tumors (30–37%) remains well below fully supervised performance, and the approach is validated on a limited set of four unseen tumor types from MSD plus one private colon dataset, leaving open whether the self-prompting strategy generalizes to more diverse or morphologically ambiguous pathologies.
