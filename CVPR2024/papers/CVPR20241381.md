## Problem

Synthesizing non-stationary textures (with large-scale irregular structures, varying color, orientation, and scale) from a single reference exemplar remains challenging. Existing approaches either require tedious auxiliary guidance (label maps, vector fields, age maps) or overfit a GAN per texture, sacrificing both user controllability and editability. No prior method allows a user to quickly sketch a rough layout and automatically obtain a coherent, seamless texture that faithfully reproduces the reference's local characteristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 reference texture and a rough user-edited target image (created by freely placing and rotating patches from the reference in a standard editor, leaving the background filled with random source pixels). It produces a 512×512 coherent, seamless texture that respects the target's global layout while exhibiting the reference's fine texture details. The self-rectification is executed twice in a coarse-to-fine pipeline: the coarse stage output serves as the target input for the fine stage, with the original user-edited target retained as the inversion reference throughout.

## Method

The core mechanism is KV-injection of self-attention features from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net, applied in both DDIM inversion and sampling without any training. Structure-preserving inversion DDIM-inverts the target twice: the second inversion injects KV features from the first inversion in reverse temporal order (step T−t), which suppresses the noise that would otherwise scatter the user's structural edits. Fine texture sampling then DDIM-samples from the preserved latent code, injecting KV features from the reference's own inversion during the last T−S denoising steps to transfer local texture patterns via cross-image attention. The process runs in two passes (coarse: P=20, S=20; fine: P=5, S=5) to first establish global structure and then refine local details. For textures with dominant directional structure, the reference is augmented with rotated/flipped copies whose KV features are concatenated, increasing pattern diversity and allowing the output to conform to arbitrary user-edit orientations.

## Results

The paper presents entirely qualitative evaluations with no quantitative metrics or numerical benchmarks. The main results (Figure 6) show a visual gallery of synthesized textures across multiple non-stationary references, demonstrating that the method faithfully reproduces fine texture details while respecting the sparse user-edited target layout. Ablation studies (Figures 8–9) confirm that both KV-injection in sampling (S₁=20, S₂=5) and in structure-preserving inversion (P₁=20, P₂=5) are necessary for quality, and that data augmentation is essential for directional textures. Inference time is approximately three minutes per 512×512 image on a single Quadro P6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines, both evaluated qualitatively on the same set of non-stationary texture references. Against TexExp (adversarial expansion, Zhou et al. SIGGRAPH 2018), the proposed method clearly wins: TexExp fails to reproduce the fine textures of the reference because the user-edited target images constitute unseen data for its overfitted GAN, whereas self-rectification transfers local texture patterns via KV-injection from the reference. Against GCD Loss (guided correspondence, Zhou et al. CVPR 2023), the proposed method also wins on structural fidelity: GCD Loss produces plausible local textures but does not always conform to the user-edited global layout, while self-rectification preserves the target's structure through structure-preserving inversion. Both baselines are from the same research group (Zhou et al.), making the comparison a direct progression of their prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the KV-injection mechanism introduced for cross-image appearance transfer (Tumanyan et al. 2023; Cao et al. 2023 MasaCtrl; Alaluf et al. 2023), repurposing it not for style transfer between arbitrary images but for self-rectification of a user-edited target toward its own source reference. It departs from prior non-stationary texture synthesis methods—particularly Zhou et al.'s adversarial expansion (TexExp, SIGGRAPH 2018), which requires per-texture GAN overfitting and offers no editability—by replacing the trained generative model with a pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone and a two-pass coarse-to-fine DDIM inversion/sampling pipeline. The structure-preserving inversion (injecting KV features from late noising steps into early ones) is a novel application of the attention-injection idea to the inversion direction, which prior work had not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'lazy editing' workflow—where a user performs a few rough cut-and-paste operations and the system automatically produces a coherent, seamless texture—offers a practical controllability that prior non-stationary texture methods lacked, making the technique accessible to artists without requiring per-texture training or complex control maps. However, the method is constrained to 512×512 resolution with ~3-minute inference, and the self-rectification paradigm inherently limits output to textures derived from the provided reference, precluding truly novel texture generation. Open questions include scaling to large-scale textures, incorporating semantic understanding for more intent-aligned synthesis, and whether the structure-preserving inversion trick generalizes beyond texture synthesis to other structure-conditioned generation tasks.
