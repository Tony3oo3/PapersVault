## Problem

Group Activity Recognition (GAR) relies on supervised learning with manually annotated group activity labels, which are labor-intensive and difficult to define at fine-grained levels due to visually subtle but contextually important differences among similar activities. This paper addresses the annotation bottleneck by learning a compact Group Activity Feature (GAF) vector without any group activity annotations, using person-level attribute prediction as a self-supervised pretext task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T video frames with per-person bounding boxes as input; person appearance features are extracted via RoIAlign on VGG-19 (320×640 for VBD) or Inception-v3 (240×360 for CAD) feature maps, and location features are derived from bounding-box centers via spatial positional encoding. The output is a single compact GAF vector G ∈ R^{2C} (concatenation of two branch outputs) that encodes the multi-person activity context. During training, the GAF is used to predict each person's action class (cross-entropy) or appearance feature (MSE), but at inference the GAF itself serves as the scene representation for retrieval, clustering, or fine-tuning on downstream GAR.

## Method

The network has three stages: (1) a person feature extractor producing per-person appearance and location features, (2) Masked Person Modeling (MPM) that zeros out N_mask randomly sampled people's features during training to force the model to infer masked attributes from group context, and (3) a dual-branch Transformer (TS and ST branches, each applying temporal then spatial or spatial then temporal self-attention) that pools the masked features into the GAF via temporal and spatial max pooling. A key mechanism is location-guided attribute prediction: each person's location feature is concatenated with the GAF and fed through a small MLP to predict that specific person's attribute, which disentangles the entangled group representation and forces the GAF to encode spatial interaction structure. Two variants are provided—GAFL-PAC (predicting action classes with cross-entropy) and GAFL-PAF (predicting frozen VGG appearance features with MSE, requiring zero manual annotations)—and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end so the GAF is shaped by the attribute prediction loss.

## Results

On the Volleyball Dataset (VBD) with person action labels (GAFL-PAC), the proposed Ours-grp achieves 83.0% Hit@1 and 64.2% mAP for action set retrieval (IoU), 80.1% Hit@1 and 59.9% mAP for action set retrieval (AF-IDF), and 84.8% Hit@1 for group activity retrieval, outperforming all baselines. In the annotation-free setting (GAFL-PAF), Ours-grp reaches 61.1% Hit@1 for group activity retrieval, substantially exceeding the best baseline (B2-VGG19 at 35.4%). On the Collective Activity Dataset (CAD) in GAFL-PAC, Ours-grp achieves 94.9% Hit@1 for group activity retrieval and 96.1% Hit@1 for action set retrieval (AF-IDF), again leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the GAFL-PAC setting on VBD, the proposed method outperforms all three supervised GAR baselines across every retrieval metric. The largest margin is in group activity retrieval, where Ours-grp (84.8% Hit@1) surpasses Dual-AI (64.4%), DIN (57.0%), and HiGCIN (50.0%) by 20.4, 27.8, and 34.8 points respectively, demonstrating that the GAF captures multi-person interaction context far better than person-feature-based retrieval. In the annotation-free GAFL-PAF setting, Ours-grp beats Ibrahim et al.'s HRN [14] by 29.9 points in group activity Hit@1 (61.1% vs. 31.2%) and by 11.5 points in AF-IDF Hit@1 (52.3% vs. 40.8%), though HRN edges out Ours-grp by 0.5 points in Action IoU mAP (46.9 vs. 46.4). On CAD in GAFL-PAC, Ours-grp leads Dual-AI by 12.8 points in group activity Hit@1 (94.9% vs. 82.1%) and by 10.6 points in AF-IDF mAP (93.9% vs. 82.7%). The paper further shows that fine-tuning the GAF-pretrained network with group activity labels yields 96.6% GAR accuracy on CAD, exceeding Dual-AI's 94.1% trained from scratch, confirming the GAF as an effective pre-training signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant supervised GAR paradigm (Dual-AI, HiGCIN, DIN) by eliminating group activity labels entirely, instead learning a compact Group Activity Feature (GAF) through person attribute prediction as a self-supervised pretext task. It builds on the Transformer-based spatial-temporal interaction modeling established in Dual-AI and GroupFormer, and borrows the masked-modeling idea from self-supervised representation learning, but applies it specifically to person features within a group. The closest prior work is Ibrahim et al.'s HRN, which also learns group features without group labels, but the authors argue it fails to capture the intimate person-to-group relation (e.g., a specific person's role within a spike play), which their location-guided attribute prediction explicitly enforces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: group activity annotations are expensive and difficult for fine-grained classes, and this method removes that bottleneck while producing a GAF that outperforms supervised GAR methods in retrieval and serves as an effective pre-training stage (2.5% gain on CAD). The t-SNE visualizations showing the GAF spontaneously separating sub-activities not present in the manual annotation taxonomy suggest the learned features may be more informative than the human-defined class structure. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or very large group scenes, whether other pretext tasks (e.g., joint attention prediction) can further enhance the GAF, and whether the current inferiority to fully supervised GAR can be closed with richer attribute signals.
