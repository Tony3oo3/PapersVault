## Problem

Existing low-light enhancement methods rely on factorization criteria (e.g., Retinex's multiplicative reflectance-illumination split) that assume Lambertian diffuse reflection, ignoring prevalent non-Lambertian effects like specularity. This paper introduces a novel additive specularity-based factorization that decomposes an image into a variable number of soft-segmented specular components, each capturing a distinct illumination characteristic from bright glares to dark shadows, enabling more interpretable and flexible enhancement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242607/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw low-light RGB image (400×600 in experiments) with no preprocessing and produces an enhanced well-exposed image along with K=5 additive specular factors that sum exactly to the input. Each factor is a soft mask highlighting regions with similar illumination, and the successive differences between factors (F^k = E^k − E^{k−1}) serve as interpretable region indicators. These factors can be directly manipulated as image layers for user-controlled relighting or concatenated as structural priors into other enhancement networks (dehazing, deraining, deblurring) with negligible overhead.

## Method

RSFNet unrolls a recursive ADMM optimization for L0-norm-approximated specular component estimation into differentiable network layers, where each of K factors is extracted by progressively relaxing the sparsity threshold (α^k = (1−ν^k)·X̂^k). Following ALISTA's analytic weight simplification, only 3KT scalar parameters (soft-thresholding values α, β and ADMM step sizes μ per iteration) are learned, yielding an extremely lightweight factorization module (~200 parameters). A novel factorization loss constrains the signal-energy ratio of each factor to its expected sparsity level ν^k=k/K. A small 7-layer UNet fusion module then takes the concatenated input and successive factor differences, producing per-factor gamma correction maps applied via a curve adjustment equation, followed by a differentiable bilateral filter for denoising. Training is fully zero-reference, using only color-balance, exposure, and pixel-smoothing losses with no paired or unpaired ground truth.

## Results

RSFNet achieves the best or near-best scores across all six metrics on both LOLv1 (PSNRy 22.17, SSIMy 0.860, NIQE 3.129, LPIPS 0.265) and LOLv2-real (PSNRy 21.46, SSIMy 0.836, NIQE 3.769, LPIPS 0.280). On the mean across four benchmarks (LOLv1, LOLv2-real, LOLv2-syn, VE-LOL), RSFNet leads in PSNRy (21.16), SSIMy (0.854), PSNRc (18.45), SSIMc (0.758), and NIQE (3.763), while LPIPS (0.276) is marginally behind SDD (0.266) and ZDCE (0.273). The method uses only ~2.11K trainable parameters, roughly 10× fewer than the next-lightest zero-reference model (SCI at 0.26K) and orders of magnitude fewer than GDP (552K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSFNet outperforms all 11 baselines on PSNRy, SSIMy, PSNRc, SSIMc, and NIQE across the four-dataset mean, with the largest margins over traditional model-based methods (LIME, DUAL, SDD) and the diffusion-based GDP. Among zero-reference competitors, the closest challenger is PNet [63] on PSNRy (19.65 vs. 21.16, +1.51 dB) and PSNRc (17.35 vs. 18.45, +1.10 dB), while SDD [31] edges out RSFNet on mean LPIPS (0.266 vs. 0.276) and ZDCE [27] is second (0.273). On the individual LOLv1 and LOLv2-real splits, RSFNet achieves the best LPIPS (0.265 and 0.280 respectively), surpassing even SDD. The model-driven baselines RUAS [72] and SCI [57] trail by 3–4 dB in PSNRy and 0.1+ in SSIMy, confirming that the recursive specularity factorization provides a stronger structural prior than Retinex-based unrolling. For the extension tasks (Table 5), RSFNet factors plugged into AirNet [45] improve PSNR by 1.78 dB (dehazing), 1.29 dB (deraining), and 0.87 dB (deblurring) over the single-task AirNet baseline, demonstrating cross-task utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithm-unrolling paradigm for low-light enhancement (as in RUAS [72] and SCI [57]) but replaces the Retinex-based multiplicative decomposition with a novel additive specularity factorization. It directly builds on ALISTA [51], which showed that analytic weight simplification reduces unrolled networks to only thresholds and step sizes, and adopts the ZeroDCE [27] curve-adjustment equation for the fusion step. The key departure is using recursive L0-norm sparsity estimation (from the highlight-removal literature) as the factorization criterion, yielding a variable number of soft-segmented, illumination-ordered factors rather than the fixed two-factor Retinex split. This positions RSFNet as the first method to exploit specularity estimation as a structural prior for LLE and related enhancement tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With only ~200 trainable parameters, RSFNet achieves state-of-the-art zero-reference LLE performance across four benchmarks and five no-reference generalization datasets, demonstrating that a physically-motivated factorization can outperform heavier data-driven approaches. The interpretable specular factors serve as a plug-and-play structural prior that improves dehazing, deraining, and deblurring when concatenated into an existing base model (AirNet), suggesting broad utility beyond LLE. Open questions include whether the fixed dataset-mean initialization can be replaced by per-image adaptive thresholds, and whether the factorization generalizes to video, multi-spectral, or non-photographic signals. The sensitivity to initialization and the reliance on a small UNet for fusion remain practical limitations for deployment in diverse real-world conditions.
