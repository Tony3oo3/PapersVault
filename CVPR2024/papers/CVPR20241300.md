## Problem

Recent state-of-the-art zero-shot 3D shape reconstruction methods rely on computationally expensive generative diffusion models, raising the question of whether regression-based approaches can still be competitive. Additionally, the field lacks a standardized, large-scale evaluation benchmark, with prior works reporting results on small, heterogeneous test sets that make cross-paper comparison difficult.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single object-centric RGB image (foreground-segmented, center-cropped) and predicts a continuous 3D occupancy field in a view-centric coordinate system, where the camera frame serves as the world frame. The occupancy is evaluated at 4096 randomly sampled 3D query points per iteration during training, and at inference the isosurface is extracted via Marching Cubes to yield a mesh. Unlike prior regression methods that assume known depth and intrinsics, ZeroShape jointly estimates both from the image as intermediate representations.

## Method

ZeroShape decomposes reconstruction into three modules: a DPT-based estimator that jointly predicts depth and camera intrinsics, a differentiable geometric unprojection unit that converts these into a normalized 3D projection map encoding the visible surface, and a ResNet encoder with a cross-attention decoder (following MCC) that queries the projection map to predict occupancy at arbitrary 3D points. The key insight is that jointly learning intrinsics alongside depth—rather than using fixed intrinsics—produces geometrically accurate visible surfaces, which in turn enables better 3D geometric reasoning (symmetry, curvature) for completing the occluded shape. Training is two-stage: first pretraining the depth/camera estimator with SSIMAE depth loss and a projection-map MSE loss, then jointly fine-tuning the full model with binary cross-entropy occupancy loss. The model is trained on ~1.1M synthetic images rendered from ~90K meshes (ShapeNet + Objaverse-LVIS) using varied focal lengths (30–70mm) and diverse camera-object geometries, requiring only ~5 days on 4× RTX 2080 Ti.

## Results

On the primary OmniObject3D benchmark, ZeroShape achieves FS@1 of 0.2297, FS@5 of 0.8169, and Chamfer Distance of 0.310, outperforming the best generative baseline (OpenLRM) by 36% on FS@1 and 24% on CD. On Ocrtoc3D, ZeroShape leads all baselines with FS@1 of 0.2410 and CD of 0.286. On Pix3D (heavily biased toward furniture), ZeroShape is competitive with Shap-E, trailing slightly on FS@1 (0.1928 vs. 0.2016) but matching on FS@2 (0.4290 vs. 0.4287).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroShape outperforms all six baselines on OmniObject3D and Ocrtoc3D across every metric. On OmniObject3D, the largest gap is against the regression-based MCC (FS@1: 0.2297 vs. 0.1362, a 69% relative improvement) and the generative OpenLRM (FS@1: 0.2297 vs. 0.1683, +36%). On Ocrtoc3D, ZeroShape leads Shap-E (the strongest generative baseline) by 40% on FS@1 (0.2410 vs. 0.1725) and by 28% on CD (0.286 vs. 0.395). On Pix3D, the results are more nuanced: Shap-E slightly edges ZeroShape on FS@1 (0.2016 vs. 0.1928) and FS@5 (0.7833 vs. 0.7759), while ZeroShape matches on FS@2 and is marginally worse on CD (0.345 vs. 0.340); the authors attribute this to Pix3D's heavy bias toward furniture categories well-represented in Shap-E's training data. Notably, ZeroShape achieves these results using only ~90K training meshes versus millions used by generative methods, and requires a single forward pass rather than iterative diffusion sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroShape builds directly on the regression-based 3D reconstruction lineage (MCC, MarrNet, DISN) that uses intermediate geometric representations and local spatial features, but departs from the field's recent pivot toward generative diffusion models (Point-E, Shap-E, One-2-3-45) by demonstrating that a carefully designed deterministic regressor remains competitive. The key architectural departure from MCC is the joint estimation of depth and camera intrinsics via a DPT backbone, replacing MCC's assumption of known intrinsics during inference. The paper also introduces a unified large-scale evaluation benchmark pooling OmniObject3D, Ocrtoc3D, and Pix3D, addressing the fragmented and small-scale evaluation practices that have characterized prior work in this area.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single-forward-pass regression model trained on ~90K synthetic meshes outperforms diffusion-based methods trained on millions of 3D assets challenges the field's implicit assumption that generative modeling is necessary for high-quality zero-shot 3D shape reconstruction, and offers a substantially more computationally efficient alternative for practical deployment. The unified benchmark is a practical contribution that standardizes evaluation across the community. Open questions include whether the regression paradigm can match generative methods on extremely novel or complex objects where the visible surface provides insufficient geometric cues, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-object or scene-level reconstruction.
