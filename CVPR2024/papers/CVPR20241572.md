## Problem

Input-adaptive efficient vision transformers reduce computation based on input complexity, but their vulnerability to adversarial attacks targeting compute and energy consumption (rather than accuracy) has been largely unexplored. Prior energy attacks rely on image-specific perturbations that are impractical in real-world deployment, leaving a gap for universal, patch-based attacks that generalize across images and can be physically applied to a victim's input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241572/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained input-adaptive efficient ViT (A-ViT, ATS, or AdaViT) and optimizes a single universal adversarial patch of size 64×64 pixels (occupying only 8% of a 224×224 input image), pasted at a fixed top-left corner location. The patch is trained on the training set and applied unchanged at inference to any test image, producing increased model FLOPs without requiring per-sample optimization. The patch is universal across images but not across different efficient model architectures.

## Method

SlowFormer optimizes a single universal patch to maximize the inference computation of input-adaptive efficient ViTs by exploiting each method's token-pruning mechanism. For A-ViT, the patch is trained to minimize the negated ponder and distribution losses, preventing tokens from halting early; for AdaViT, it maximizes the computation usage loss by setting target budgets to zero, forcing the decision network to keep all patches, heads, and blocks; for ATS, it drives the classification token's attention distribution toward uniform (high entropy) via an MSE loss, maximizing the number of retained tokens through inverse transform sampling. The patch is initialized randomly, optimized with AdamW on the training set only, and projected to [0,255] after each iteration. A defense is proposed that maintains a set of adversarial patches and trains the model robustly against random samples from this set, reducing but not eliminating the attack's effectiveness.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, SlowFormer achieves 100% Attack Success on A-ViT (ViT-Tiny: 0.87→1.3 GFLOPs; ViT-Small: 3.7→4.6 GFLOPs), completely undoing the efficiency gains. On ATS, the attack recovers 35–60% of the pruned computation (e.g., ViT-Small: 3.1→4.0 GFLOPs), and on AdaViT it recovers 40% (ViT-Small: 2.25→3.2 GFLOPs). On CIFAR-10, the attack increases A-ViT FLOPs from 0.11 to 0.58 (41% Attack Success) and ATS from 0.85 to 0.99 (34%). A 64×64 patch occupying only 8% of the image area is sufficient to achieve these results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against A-ViT, SlowFormer completely reverses the token-pruning efficiency, achieving 100% Attack Success on ViT-Tiny and 99% on ViT-Small, restoring FLOPs to the full-compute baseline. Against ATS, the attack is less effective (35% on ViT-Tiny, 60% on ViT-Small, 52% on ViT-Base) because ATS uses inverse-transform sampling where a uniform attention distribution only partially increases retained tokens. Against AdaViT, the attack achieves 40% Attack Success on ViT-Small, limited by the fact that even a full-image patch caps computation at 4.18 GFLOPs (vs. 4.6 maximum). In contrast, the standard adversarial patch baselines (TAP and NTAP) actually *decrease* computation (negative Attack Success of −5% to −50%), because they increase the importance of patch tokens and cause more other tokens to be pruned. Random patches have negligible effect (−1% to −2%), confirming that the attack requires targeted optimization of the patch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the universal adversarial patch framework (originally designed for accuracy attacks, Brown et al. 2017) to target the computational efficiency of input-adaptive vision transformers. Prior energy/compute attacks (ILFO, DeepSloth, GradAuto, SlothBomb) relied on image-specific perturbations, which the authors argue are impractical for real-world deployment; SlowFormer instead optimizes a single patch that generalizes across all test images. The targets—A-ViT, ATS, and AdaViT—represent the emerging class of token-pruning and dynamic-computation ViTs that adaptively reduce FLOPs based on input complexity, a direction the authors argue will grow as non-adaptive efficiency methods hit their limits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a patch occupying only 8% of the image area can fully reverse the efficiency gains of A-ViT (100% Attack Success) highlights a practical vulnerability for battery-powered, safety-critical systems (drones, delivery robots, AR/VR headsets) that rely on adaptive inference. However, the defense (adversarial training with a patch set) only partially mitigates the attack, and the paper does not validate power consumption on real hardware, relying instead on FLOPs as a proxy. Open questions include whether stronger defenses exist, whether the attack transfers across architectures, and how it interacts with other efficiency mechanisms (quantization, distillation) in deployed systems.
