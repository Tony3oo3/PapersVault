## Problem

Existing LLMs and MLLMs for document understanding fail to effectively capture document layout information: plain text discards spatial structure, layout text (text+coordinates) does not reliably improve LLM performance, and MLLMs rely on general visual encoders with pre-training tasks (captioning, plain-text generation) that provide no explicit layout signal. This limits zero-shot document understanding accuracy and interpretability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a document image, OCR text tokens with their bounding-box coordinates, and a natural-language question instruction. The document image, text, and boxes are jointly encoded by a LayoutLMv3 encoder, whose visual and text-layout features are projected into the LLM's embedding space via separate MLP projectors. The output is a final answer preceded by three structured LayoutCoT intermediate steps: a question-type analysis, a bounding-box location of the relevant document region, and a layout-conditioned reasoning trace to the answer.

## Method

LayoutLLM integrates a document pre-trained model (LayoutLMv3-large) as the multimodal encoder rather than a general vision model, followed by two MLP projectors and a 7B LLM backbone (Vicuna-1.5-7B or Llama2-7B-chat). Training uses a two-stage layout instruction tuning strategy: (1) layout-aware pre-training with 5.7M instruction-format tasks at three hierarchical levels—document-level (dense description, text-and-layout reconstruction), region-level (layout analysis, table understanding), and segment-level (masked vision-language modeling, position masking, geometric pre-training)—and (2) layout-aware SFT with 300K LayoutCoT examples where the model is supervised to produce a three-step chain-of-thought (Question Analysis → Relevant Area Concentration → Answer Formation) that explicitly reasons over layout at each step. During pre-training the LLM is frozen while the encoder and projectors are updated; during SFT the LLM and projectors are fine-tuned while the encoder is frozen. LayoutCoT training data is generated without manual labeling by prompting GPT-3.5 Turbo on layout-aware document representations and then converting text CoT into layout CoT via rule-based bounding-box extraction.

## Results

LayoutLLM-7B (Vicuna-1.5-7B backbone) achieves 74.27 ANLS on DocVQA, 55.76 Rouge-L on VisualMRC, 79.98 ANLS on FUNSD, 63.10 ANLS on CORD, and 72.12 ANLS on SROIE, all under zero-shot evaluation. These results surpass the strongest LLM baseline (Vicuna-1.5-7B with plain text: 66.99 DocVQA) by ~7 points and the strongest MLLM baseline (Qwen-VL-7B: 65.1 DocVQA) by ~9 points on DocVQA. On VIE tasks, LayoutLLM outperforms Qwen-VL by 32.9 points on FUNSD (79.98 vs. 47.09) and 33.1 points on CORD (63.10 vs. 30.00). The fine-tuned LayoutLMv3 still leads on DocVQA (83.37) and FUNSD (92.08), but LayoutLLM achieves competitive zero-shot performance without any downstream training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayoutLLM achieves consistent and significant improvements over all seven baselines across all five benchmarks. Against the strongest LLM baseline (Vicuna-1.5-7B with plain text), LayoutLLM gains +7.28 on DocVQA (74.27 vs. 66.99), +3.63 on VisualMRC (55.76 vs. 52.13), +31.92 on FUNSD (79.98 vs. 48.06), +11.70 on CORD (63.10 vs. 51.40), and +3.92 on SROIE (72.12 vs. 68.20). Against the strongest MLLM baseline Qwen-VL-7B, LayoutLLM leads by +9.17 on DocVQA, +13.24 on VisualMRC, +32.89 on FUNSD, +33.10 on CORD, and +13.53 on SROIE. The general-purpose MLLMs LLaVAR and LLaVA-1.5 perform very poorly (DocVQA < 14%, FUNSD < 2%), confirming that standard visual pre-training is insufficient for document layout understanding. Notably, LayoutLLM's zero-shot DocVQA score (74.27) exceeds mPLUG-DocOWL (62.2) and Qwen-VL (65.1), both of which were trained on the DocVQA training set, demonstrating the robustness of the layout instruction tuning strategy. The fine-tuned LayoutLMv3 still outperforms LayoutLLM on DocVQA (83.37 vs. 74.27) and FUNSD (92.08 vs. 79.98), but requires task-specific training data, whereas LayoutLLM operates entirely zero-shot.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayoutLLM departs from existing document-based MLLMs (LLaVAR, mPLUG-DocOwl, Qwen-VL) that pair general visual encoders (ViT/CLIP) with LLMs, instead integrating a document-specific pre-trained model (LayoutLMv3) as the encoder to preserve layout-aware representations. The layout instruction tuning strategy unifies document-level, region-level, and segment-level pre-training tasks in an instruction format, extending the self-supervised layout pre-training objectives from the LayoutLM family into the LLM instruction-tuning paradigm. The LayoutCoT module adapts chain-of-thought reasoning from LLMs to explicitly incorporate layout-aware intermediate steps (question analysis, relevant area concentration, answer formation), which prior document MLLMs lacked in their SFT stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that zero-shot document understanding can surpass methods trained on the target dataset (outperforming mPLUG-DocOwl and Qwen-VL by ~10% on DocVQA), suggesting that proper layout modeling in the encoder and instruction tuning is more effective than task-specific fine-tuning. The LayoutCoT mechanism provides interpretability and enables human-in-the-loop correction, which is valuable for high-stakes document processing (e.g., financial forms). However, the authors acknowledge limitations: the model still struggles with precise region-level spatial relationships and lacks the ability to refuse when an answer is not present in the document, leaving open questions about robustness in real-world deployment.
