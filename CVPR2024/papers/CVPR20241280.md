## Problem

Existing action quality assessment (AQA) methods learn video-level representations without fine-grained parsing of actions in both time and space, resulting in low credibility and poor visual interpretability. This is particularly problematic for stringent applications like Olympic diving, where subtle differences in take-off angle, body position, and entry verticality determine scores. The paper argues that credible AQA requires the model to perceive and parse the internal structure of actions at fine-grained spatial and temporal granularity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241280/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of query and exemplar videos (same action type) as input, where each video is 96 frames split into 9 snippets of 16 frames with stride 10; the exemplar's ground truth score is provided as a reference. At inference, a multi-exemplar voting strategy selects E exemplars from the training set and averages their predicted scores. Beyond the final scalar score, the model also produces per-frame target action masks (spatial parsing) and step transition timestamps (temporal parsing), enabling visual interpretability of which regions and time segments drive the assessment.

## Method

FineParser comprises four components: a Spatial Action Parser (SAP) that uses an I3D backbone with transposed-convolution upsampling blocks at multiple scales to predict human-centric foreground action masks and gate video features via element-wise multiplication with the fused mask embedding; a Temporal Action Parser (TAP) that employs PSNet to identify step transition timestamps and segment actions into consecutive sub-steps; a Static Visual Encoder (SVE) using ResNet34 to capture per-step contextual details; and a Fine-grained Contrastive Regression (FineReg) module that applies transformer cross-attention between query and exemplar step representations to compute relative quality differences, summed with the exemplar score via learned step weights. Training jointly optimizes focal loss for mask prediction, binary cross-entropy for step transitions, and MSE for score regression. The key differentiator is the simultaneous fine-grained alignment of target actions in both space (mask-guided feature gating) and time (step-wise cross-attention), minimizing the influence of irrelevant background and enabling interpretable, region-specific quality assessment.

## Results

On FineDiving-HM, FineParser achieves a Spearman's rank correlation of 0.9435 and Relative ℓ₂ distance of 0.2602, outperforming the previous best (TSA at 0.9324 / 0.3022) by 1.11% and 0.0420 respectively. On MTL-AQA, FineParser reaches ρ=0.9585 and R-ℓ₂=0.2411, surpassing CoRe (0.9512 / 0.2600) and TSA-Net (0.9422). For the auxiliary tasks, FineParser attains AIoU@0.5=0.9946 and AIoU@0.75=0.9467 for temporal action parsing, and MAE=0.0408, F_β=0.1273, S_m=0.8357 for spatial action mask prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineParser outperforms all seven compared baselines on both datasets. On FineDiving-HM, the gains over the closest competitors are: +1.27% ρ and −0.0546 R-ℓ₂ over CoRe [37], and +1.11% ρ and −0.0420 R-ℓ₂ over TSA [36]. Against earlier methods the margins are larger: +1.94% ρ over MUSDL [31], +6.59% over I3D+MLP [31], and +24.66% over C3D-LSTM [26]. On MTL-AQA, FineParser (ρ=0.9585) beats CoRe (0.9512) by 0.73% and TSA-Net (0.9422) by 1.63%, while also achieving the lowest R-ℓ₂ (0.2411 vs. CoRe's 0.2600). The TAP sub-task shows the most dramatic improvement: FineParser's AIoU@0.75 jumps from 0.5007 (TSA) to 0.9467, indicating far more accurate step-transition localization. No baseline is competitive on the SAP mask-prediction metrics, which are unique to FineParser's fine-grained spatial parsing.

## Relation to Prior Work

FineParser extends the procedure-aware AQA framework of Xu et al. (CVPR 2022), which introduced PSNet for temporal step parsing and fine-grained contrastive regression, by adding a spatial action parser that learns human-centric foreground masks via multi-scale transposed convolutions on an I3D backbone. It also adopts the multi-exemplar voting inference strategy from CoRe (Yu et al., ICCV 2021). The key conceptual departure from prior AQA methods (C3D-LSTM, USDL, MUSDL, TSA) is the explicit joint parsing of actions in both space and time, whereas previous approaches operated at the video level without spatially localizing the target action, limiting interpretability and credibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in AQA: the lack of spatial interpretability that is critical for high-stakes applications like Olympic judging. The FineDiving-HM dataset (312,256 annotated mask frames) provides the first quantitative metrics (MAE, Fβ, Sm) for evaluating whether an AQA model actually focuses on the relevant foreground action, which is a useful benchmark for the community. However, the authors acknowledge that the mask annotations are sport-specific (diving) and require manual labeling, making direct transfer to other competitive sports non-trivial. An open question is whether the fine-grained spatial-temporal parsing paradigm generalizes to sports with less structured procedural knowledge or to multi-person scenarios.
