## Problem

Transformer-based point cloud registration methods rely on vanilla cross-attention for inter-frame feature aggregation, which disperses attention across irrelevant regions and introduces interference that disrupts the capture of spatial consistency relations. This leads to ambiguous matching, particularly in scenes with numerous similar structures and low overlap ratios, where inlier ratios can drop below 1%. Existing consistency-based methods only operate on sparse pre-computed correspondences rather than guiding the feature learning process itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes two partially overlapping point clouds P ∈ R^{n×3} and Q ∈ R^{m×3} and outputs a rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R^3) along with dense point correspondences. A KPConv-FPN backbone first downsamples to coarse-level superpoints and upsamples to fine-level points; superpoint matches are then propagated to individual points via a point-to-node grouping strategy. The method is evaluated at varying correspondence counts (250–5000) and supports both RANSAC and LGR pose estimators.

## Method

DCATr introduces a cues-assisted attention mechanism that restricts inter-frame attention to non-local cue regions—top-k geometrically correlated points identified via the geometric self-attention matrix—thereby suppressing interference from irrelevant regions. A dynamic updating fashion iterates L times: at each step, the global prior is fused with consistency features, potential correspondences are re-sought via dual-softmax confidence, and cues are re-extracted, so the cues-assisted attention continuously receives updated inputs and re-learns consistency relations. The cues-assisted attention computes pairwise scores between cue feature matrices of potential corresponding superpoints (Eq. 9), assigns higher weight to pairs with high consistency confidence during feature aggregation, and passes the result through a feed-forward layer. The final hybrid features drive coarse superpoint matching (Gaussian correlation + dual normalization + Top-k selection) and fine point matching (Sinkhorn-normalized group similarity), followed by RANSAC or LGR for pose estimation.

## Results

On 3DMatch (LGR estimator), DCATr achieves 92.1% Registration Recall, 75.0% Inlier Ratio, and 1.536° RRE, outperforming the previous SOTA GeoTrans (91.5% RR, 70.3% IR, 1.625° RRE). On the more challenging 3DLoMatch (10–30% overlap), DCATr reaches 75.7% RR and 48.2% IR, improving over GeoTrans by 1.7% and 4.9% respectively. On KITTI odometry, DCATr attains 6.6 cm RTE and 0.22° RRE, marginally better than GeoTrans (6.8 cm, 0.24°) while maintaining comparable 99.7% RR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCATr consistently outperforms GeoTrans (the prior SOTA transformer-based method) across nearly all metrics: on 3DMatch it gains +4.7% IR and +0.6° RRE improvement, and on 3DLoMatch it gains +4.9% IR and +1.7% RR. Against keypoint-free methods like CoFiNet and Predator, DCATr leads by a wide margin (e.g., 75.0% vs 51.9% IR on 3DMatch; 48.2% vs 26.7% IR on 3DLoMatch). The only metric where DCATr trails GeoTrans is Feature Matching Recall on 3DLoMatch (87.3% vs 88.1%), a negligible 0.8% gap. On KITTI odometry, DCATr achieves the best RTE (6.6 cm) and RRE (0.22°) but is marginally behind GeoTrans in RR (99.7% vs 99.8%), indicating comparable outdoor robustness. Overall, DCATr dominates in matching accuracy (IR) and registration success (RR) on both indoor benchmarks, with the largest gains appearing in the low-overlap 3DLoMatch setting where consistency modeling is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCATr builds directly on the coarse-to-fine matching paradigm established by CoFiNet and GeoTrans, reusing their KPConv-FPN backbone, geometric self-attention, and Sinkhorn-based fine-level matching. Its key departure is replacing vanilla global cross-attention with a cues-assisted attention that restricts information flow to geometrically correlated regions (non-local cues extracted via the self-attention matrix), thereby suppressing interference from irrelevant regions. The dynamic updating fashion—iteratively re-extracting correspondences and cues over L rounds—has no direct analogue in prior registration methods, which typically perform a single forward pass. The paper also positions itself against post-hoc consistency-based outlier removal (SC2-PCR, PointDSC), arguing that modeling consistency during feature aggregation is more effective than filtering after the fact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly constraining attention to structurally consistent regions can meaningfully reduce matching ambiguity in low-overlap and repetitive-structure scenarios, a persistent weakness of transformer-based registration. The ablation showing that dynamic updating with residual connections substantially outperforms both static variants and the serial connection provides useful architectural guidance. However, the gains over GeoTrans, while consistent, are modest (1–5% in most metrics), and the method inherits GeoTrans's reliance on a separate pose estimator (RANSAC/LGR) rather than end-to-end pose prediction. Open questions include whether the fixed number of cues (k=6) and iterations (L=3) can be made adaptive, and whether the approach scales efficiently to very large outdoor scenes where the quadratic cost of cues-assisted attention over all point pairs may become prohibitive.
