## Problem

Existing gradient manipulation methods for multi-task learning (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, Aligned-MTL) align conflicting gradients toward an intermediate direction without identifying which shared parameters are most critical for each task. The paper proves this leads to sub-optimal Pareto solutions because the relative importance of shared parameters across tasks—termed task priority—is ignored, causing negative transfer to persist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242046/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB (or RGB-D) image as input and simultaneously produces multiple task outputs—depth maps, semantic segmentation masks, surface normal maps, edge maps, saliency maps, and human part maps—depending on the dataset. It is architecture-agnostic, operating on any CNN backbone (HRNet-18, ResNet-18, SegNet) with task-specific heads, and requires only the addition of task-specific batch normalization layers (≈0.05% extra parameters) to compute connection strength per output channel.

## Method

The proposed connection strength-based optimization divides training into two phases. Phase 1 sequentially updates each task's gradient on shared parameters (one task per sub-step) so the network learns which parameters serve which task, effectively partitioning shared parameters by task priority. Phase 2 computes a normalized connection strength per output channel using the scale factor γ of task-specific batch normalization multiplied by the squared kernel weights (Eq. 6–7), assigns each channel to its top-priority task, and projects any conflicting task gradient onto the perpendicular plane of the priority task's gradient before summing. The two phases are blended via a random draw from U(0,1) compared against the training progress e/E, so Phase 2's probability increases toward the end of training to preserve learned priorities. This mechanism differs from prior gradient surgery by using a priority-based reference vector rather than symmetric alignment, enabling the discovery of new Pareto-optimal solutions.

## Results

On NYUD-v2 with HRNet-18, the proposed method achieves a multi-task performance (MTP) improvement of +15.00% over single-task baselines, substantially outperforming the best gradient-manipulation baseline Aligned-MTL (+10.17%) and standard GD (+9.53%). Specifically, it reaches 41.10 mIoU on semantic segmentation (vs. 39.02 for Aligned-MTL), 0.565 RMSE on depth (vs. 0.592), and 19.54° mean angle distance on surface normals (vs. 20.40°). On PASCAL-Context, the method achieves +3.70% MTP versus +1.86% for PCGrad, and on Cityscapes it attains 74.75 mIoU with only 95.48M parameters, outperforming Recon-based methods that require the same parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all gradient-manipulation baselines (MGDA, PCGrad, CAGrad, Aligned-MTL) across all three datasets. On NYUD-v2, it improves MTP by +4.83 percentage points over the strongest baseline Aligned-MTL (+15.00% vs. +10.17%), with the largest gains on semantic segmentation (+2.08 mIoU) and surface normals (−0.86° mean angle distance). On PASCAL-Context, the advantage is +1.84 MTP points over PCGrad (+3.70% vs. +1.86%), with notable improvements in part segmentation (+1.59 mIoU) and surface normals (−0.43° mean). On Cityscapes, the method achieves the best MTP (−10.08%) among all methods tested, outperforming MGDA w/ Recon (−16.22%) and CAGrad w/ Recon (−53.81%) while using fewer parameters (95.48M vs. 190.59M for Cross-Stitch). The method also surpasses architecture-based approaches: compared to Recon, it adds only ~0.05% parameters (task-specific batch normalization) yet achieves substantially better multi-task performance. The paper further demonstrates robustness across four loss-scaling strategies (equal, manually tuned, uncertainty-based, DWA), where the proposed method maintains its advantage regardless of weighting scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the connection-strength concept from network pruning (Saxena et al., 2016) to quantify which shared parameters matter most to which task in multi-task learning, and adapts PCGrad's gradient-projection mechanism (Yu et al., 2020) by replacing the symmetric conflict-resolution with an asymmetric, priority-based reference vector. It departs from the broader gradient-manipulation lineage (MGDA, CAGrad, Aligned-MTL) by arguing that aligning conflicting gradients toward a shared intermediate direction is provably suboptimal because it treats all shared parameters as equally important to every task. The two-phase design—first learning task-specific parameter roles via sequential gradient updates, then conserving those roles via priority-aware projection—is a novel structural contribution not present in prior MTL optimizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The task-priority framing offers a more principled explanation for why negative transfer persists even after gradient alignment, and the connection-strength mechanism is architecture-agnostic in principle (extensible to transformers and linear layers), making it a potentially reusable component for future MTL systems. Limitations include the dependence on task-specific batch normalization for the strength signal, the ad-hoc random mixing schedule between phases, and evaluation restricted to mid-scale CNNs on vision tasks; whether the approach transfers cleanly to very deep or non-convolutional backbones remains open. The consistent but moderate gains across three datasets, two architectures, and four loss-scaling strategies suggest the idea is sound but not yet a drop-in replacement that eliminates the need for careful loss weighting.
