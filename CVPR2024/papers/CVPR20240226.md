## Problem

Person-to-person occlusion in crowded scenes creates ambiguity about which body part belongs to which individual, causing existing 3D human mesh reconstruction methods to produce confused or incorrect meshes. Prior approaches either provide only a single center point per person (insufficient spatial detail) or use 2D pose heatmaps that can inadvertently fuse features from non-target persons into the reconstruction. The paper identifies that the model fundamentally lacks a mechanism to learn the possession relationship between each human instance and its visible body joints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240226/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×512 with random cropping and rotation during training) and produces SMPL pose and shape parameters for every person in the scene via a bottom-up pipeline. Internally it predicts a body center heatmap, 24 joint heatmaps (one per SMPL joint), and an SMPL parameter map; final parameters are sampled at predicted center locations. The backbone is ResNet-50 (default) or HRNet-32, and the feature maps for the instance-aware branches operate at 64×64 spatial resolution with 64-dimensional features.

## Method

The core contribution is an instance-aware contrastive learning scheme that embeds identity information into latent features. The backbone is encoded into two parallel feature maps—a center-aligned instance map and a joint-aligned instance map—where center features are sampled at body center positions and joint features at visible joint positions. After projection into a latent space, a push loss (based on cosine similarity) maximizes the distance between center features of different persons, while a pull loss minimizes the distance between a person's center feature and their own joint features, effectively teaching each joint feature to carry the identity of its owner. Positional encoding is applied to the center-aligned instance map to disambiguate visually similar individuals. During inference, both instance maps (concatenated with the center and joint heatmaps) are fed into the SMPL parameter map branch, allowing the regressor to attend to the correct spatial region for each person. The scheme is applied only during training; at test time the network simply uses the learned feature representations without any explicit contrastive computation.

## Results

On the occlusion-focused benchmarks, the proposed method (with CrowdPose training) achieves MPJPE 102.0 and MPVPE 131.0 on 3DPW-PC, AP 34.9 on OCHuman, and AP 31.6 on CrowdPose. On OCHuman, it surpasses the previous best (OCHMR, AP 24.8) by over 10 points in AP, and on CrowdPose it outperforms ROMP (AP 28.5) by 3.1 points. On the full 3DPW test set (Table 3), the method with HRNet-32 backbone achieves MPJPE 73.2 and MPVPE 80.3, competitive with the best model-free approaches like HMDiff (MPJPE 72.7). On CMU-Panoptic, it attains the lowest mean MPJPE of 126.1 across all sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 16 baselines on the occlusion-specific benchmarks. On OCHuman, it achieves AP 34.9, surpassing the previous best OCHMR (24.8) by 10.1 points and CLIFF (23.2) by 11.7 points, with AP⁵⁰ reaching 80.0 vs. 60.7 for OCHMR. On CrowdPose, it leads with AP 31.6 over ROMP (28.5) and CLIFF (27.4), while on 3DPW-PC it attains the best MPJPE (99.9 without CrowdPose training) and MPVPE (126.4), beating CoordFormer (101.5 MPJPE) and HMDiff (114.2 MPJPE). On CMU-Panoptic, it achieves the lowest mean MPJPE (126.1) compared to 3DCrowdNet (127.6), OCHMR (133.1), and SPIN (133.1). On the full 3DPW test set, the method with HRNet-32 (MPJPE 73.2) is competitive with HMDiff (72.7) and CLIFF (72.0), confirming generalizability beyond occlusion cases. Notably, even without CrowdPose training (Ours*), the method already surpasses all prior methods on 3DPW-PC, indicating the contrastive learning scheme itself provides substantial occlusion robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the bottom-up architecture of ROMP [38], which samples SMPL parameters from a parameter map at predicted body center positions. It departs from prior occlusion-handling strategies that rely on explicit spatial cues—OCHMR [16] fuses global/local center heatmaps, 3DCrowdNet [7] uses 2D pose heatmaps for region focus, and CRMH [11]/BMP [47] enforce depth ordering via auxiliary losses—by instead embedding instance identity into latent features through a contrastive learning objective. The key conceptual shift is treating body-part ownership as a feature-space discrimination problem (pulling same-person center-joint pairs, pushing different-person center pairs) rather than a spatial-reasoning problem solved by additional input channels or loss terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Person-to-person occlusion remains a persistent failure mode for multi-person mesh reconstruction, and this paper offers a clean, training-only mechanism (contrastive identity embedding) that requires no additional inference-time overhead or explicit depth/segmentation supervision. The approach is modular and could potentially be combined with other occlusion-handling techniques (e.g., depth ordering or synthetic occlusion augmentation). Open questions include whether the identity embedding generalizes to very large crowds (beyond the tested 2–4 person scenes), whether the reliance on ground-truth joint positions for feature sampling during training limits applicability to datasets without precise annotations, and whether the contrastive objective transfers to model-free (vertex-level) reconstruction pipelines.
