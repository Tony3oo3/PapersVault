## Problem

Existing visual storytelling models either generate frames independently without conditioning on preceding context, rely solely on text prompts that introduce ambiguity for character appearance, or are restricted to closed character vocabularies requiring per-character optimization (e.g., LoRA fine-tuning). This paper tackles the open-ended setting: generating a coherent image sequence for arbitrary, unseen characters and novel storylines without any test-time adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242209/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, StoryGen takes a sequence of natural-language text prompts (one per frame) and, for frames beyond the first, the previously generated image-caption pairs as visual-language context; for story continuation, a reference character image is additionally supplied. The model produces an auto-regressive sequence of animation-style RGB images in latent space (Stable Diffusion v1.5, 512×512), where each frame is conditioned on the current prompt plus all preceding frames, enabling both open-ended story generation from scratch and continuation from a given character.

## Method

StoryGen augments a pre-trained Stable Diffusion UNet with a vision-language context module that inserts image cross-attention layers in parallel to the existing text cross-attention layers, summing their outputs (inspired by ControlNet). Preceding frames are encoded by adding a small amount of noise (t′ = t/10, scaled by temporal distance to serve as positional encoding) and running one denoising step through the same SDM under their caption guidance, extracting multi-level self-attention features as visual context. Training follows a two-stage strategy: first fine-tuning self-attention layers for single-frame generation, then training only the new image cross-attention layers on multi-frame context while freezing all other parameters. Inference uses an extended classifier-free guidance formulation with separate scales w_v = 7.0 and w_t = 3.5 for visual and text conditions respectively, and DDIM sampling with 40 steps. The model is trained on the newly constructed StorySalon dataset (~160K animation frames, 446 character categories, average 14 frames per story) sourced from YouTube videos and CC-licensed E-books.

## Results

On the StorySalon test set (~7K pairs), StoryGen achieves a FID of 33.90, CLIP-I of 0.7467, and CLIP-T of 0.2875, outperforming all baselines on FID and CLIP-I while maintaining competitive CLIP-T. In human evaluation, StoryGen scores 4.79/5 on style consistency, 4.64/5 on character consistency, and 4.26/5 on content consistency, with a 67.14% preference rate in story generation and 96.87% in story continuation. The model is built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and trained with a two-stage strategy (single-frame pre-training followed by multi-frame fine-tuning of the vision-language context module).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StoryGen outperforms all three baselines on both FID and CLIP-I. Against the strongest baseline AR-LDM (FID 39.55, CLIP-I 0.6864), StoryGen improves FID by ~5.7 points and CLIP-I by 0.06, while also surpassing it on CLIP-T (0.2875 vs. 0.2614). StoryDALL-E trails further behind with FID 38.34 and CLIP-I 0.6823, and is notably weaker on CLIP-T (0.2366). Compared to the unmodified Stable Diffusion Model (SDM), StoryGen reduces FID from 73.50 to 33.90 and raises CLIP-I from 0.6155 to 0.7467, though it sacrifices a small amount of CLIP-T (0.2875 vs. 0.3218), which the authors attribute to CLIP's bias toward natural images. In human evaluation, StoryGen dominates: it achieves 67.14% preference in story generation (vs. 14.05% for SDM, 2.50% for AR-LDM, 0.63% for StoryDALL-E) and 96.87% preference in story continuation, with character consistency scores of 4.64/5 versus 1.87 (AR-LDM) and 1.14 (StoryDALL-E).

## Relation to Prior Work

StoryGen builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent-space UNet architecture, extending it with a vision-language context module that injects image cross-attention layers in parallel to the existing text cross-attention (a design borrowed from ControlNet's summation strategy). It departs from prior story visualization and continuation work (StoryGAN, StoryDALL-E, AR-LDM, TaleCrafter) in two key ways: (1) it is fully learning-based, requiring no per-character LoRA optimization, and (2) it conditions on diffusion denoising features extracted from the same pre-trained SDM at a reduced noise level, rather than on separate encoder representations (CLIP, BLIP, VAE). The classifier-free guidance formulation is extended with independent guidance scales for visual and text conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open-ended framing—generating coherent stories for arbitrary unseen characters without any test-time optimization—addresses a practical bottleneck in prior story visualization systems that required costly per-character fine-tuning. The StorySalon dataset (160K frames, 446 character categories) is a meaningful resource for the field, though its animation-style focus limits immediate applicability to photorealistic or diverse artistic domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences (beyond ~14 frames), whether the reduced-noise context encoding generalizes to video generation, and whether the CLIP bias toward natural images (noted by the authors) can be mitigated for broader style coverage.
