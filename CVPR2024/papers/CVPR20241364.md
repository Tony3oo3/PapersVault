## Problem

Diffusion-based monocular depth estimation models (e.g., VPD, TADP) condition their denoising UNet on pseudo-captions or CLIP text embeddings to supply semantic context, but textual descriptions inherently focus on large salient objects and miss finer scene details. The paper asks whether a richer, image-native representation—specifically the class-wise probability vector from a pretrained ViT—can provide more effective conditioning for depth prediction than text-based embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241364/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (640×480 for NYU Depth v2, 1242×375 for KITTI) and produces a per-pixel metric depth map in meters. The input image is first encoded into a latent representation by a VAE encoder, and the final depth is regressed from hierarchical UNet decoder features aggregated at 1/4 latent resolution, yielding a continuous depth value at every pixel without requiring per-image affine normalization.

## Method

The core contribution is the CIDE (Comprehensive Image Detail Embedding) module, which extracts a 768-dimensional semantic context vector from a frozen pretrained ViT: the 1000-dim logit vector is projected through a two-layer MLP to 100 dimensions, used as weights for a linear combination of 100 learnable embeddings, and then linearly transformed to 768 dimensions. This vector conditions a latent diffusion UNet (from Stable Diffusion) that denoises the VAE-encoded image latent, replacing the text-prompt conditioning used by VPD and TADP. Hierarchical feature maps from the UNet decoder are upsampled to 1/4 latent resolution, concatenated, and passed through a two-layer convolutional depth regressor to produce the final depth map. The key insight is that ViT's class-wise probability distribution preserves information about smaller objects and scene ambiguities that pseudo-captions discard, leading to a 14% Abs Rel improvement on NYU Depth v2 and substantially better zero-shot transfer with training on a single dataset.

## Results

On NYU Depth v2, ECoDepth achieves a new SOTA Abs Rel of 0.059 and RMSE of 0.218, improving 14% over the previous SOTA VPD (0.069 / 0.254). On KITTI, it achieves Sq Rel of 0.139 and Abs Rel of 0.048, matching or exceeding all prior methods including GEDepth (Sq Rel 0.142) and MIM (Sq Rel 0.139). For zero-shot transfer trained only on NYU Depth v2, ECoDepth reports mean relative improvements of 20.5%, 23.1%, 81.3%, and 25.2% over NeWCRF on Sun-RGBD, iBims1, DIODE, and HyperSim respectively, substantially outperforming ZoEDepth (16%, 18.5%, 45%, 8.6%) which was trained on 12 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECoDepth outperforms all 22 compared baselines on NYU Depth v2 across every reported metric, with the most significant gap over the previous SOTA VPD (Abs Rel 0.059 vs 0.069, RMSE 0.218 vs 0.254) and TADP (RMSE 0.218 vs 0.225). On KITTI, ECoDepth ties MIM on Sq Rel (0.139) and Abs Rel (0.048) while achieving the best δ₁ (0.979) and log RMSE (0.074), and marginally beats GEDepth on Sq Rel (0.139 vs 0.142). For zero-shot transfer, ECoDepth trained solely on NYU Depth v2 surpasses ZoEDepth (trained on 12 datasets) by large margins: 20.5% vs 16% mRI on Sun-RGBD, 23.1% vs 18.5% on iBims1, 81.3% vs 45% on DIODE, and 25.2% vs 8.6% on HyperSim. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that ViT-based scene embeddings (RMSE 0.218) outperform both pseudo-caption CLIP embeddings (0.225) and one-hot scene label vectors (0.244), validating the core design choice over VPD and TADP's text-conditioning approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), repurposing its UNet backbone and VAE encoder for dense depth prediction rather than image generation. It departs from the recent line of work by VPD (Zhao et al., ICCV 2023) and TADP (Kondapaneni et al., 2023), which condition diffusion-based perception models on text embeddings derived from pseudo-captions or CLIP. The key conceptual departure is replacing this text-based conditioning with class-probability embeddings from a frozen pretrained ViT, packaged in the novel CIDE module, arguing that the richer, object-agnostic representation captures more scene detail relevant to depth than salient-object-focused textual descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple ViT classification embedding outperforms the more elaborate caption-generation-plus-CLIP pipeline challenges the prevailing assumption that text is the natural interface for injecting semantic context into diffusion-based perception models. The zero-shot transfer results—matching or exceeding ZoEDepth (trained on 12 datasets) while training on only NYU Depth v2—suggest that richer visual conditioning can compensate for limited training diversity, which has practical implications for deployment in new environments. Open questions include whether the ViT conditioning generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, surface normals), whether the computational cost of the diffusion backbone limits real-time use, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales or on more challenging outdoor benchmarks.
