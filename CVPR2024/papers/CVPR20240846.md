## Problem

Existing insect datasets are far too small and taxonomically shallow to support foundation-model-scale training: the largest prior benchmark (IP102) covers only 102 species across 75K images, while the natural world contains over 5.5 million insect species. Moreover, generic foundation models pre-trained on ImageNet fail to capture the micro-features (tiny textures, limb details) that distinguish closely related insect species, because these features are small and diverse compared to the objects in generic datasets.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB insect images split into 16×16 non-overlapping patches (196 total), of which a 50% subset is retained for encoding while the remainder is placed into a shared patch pool drawn from multiple images in the batch. It also takes hierarchical insect description text (spanning Subphylum through Species levels) tokenized and embedded via a BERT-based text encoder. Downstream, the model outputs species class labels (IP102, 102 classes), bounding boxes for detection (via a Faster R-CNN + FPN head), zero-shot species predictions by matching image features to description embeddings, and autoregressively generated insect descriptions.

## Method

The core architecture is a ViT-B/16 image encoder paired with a BERT text encoder and a multi-modal text decoder, trained with three jointly optimized losses. The key self-supervised mechanism is Patch-wise Relevant Attention (PRS): after encoding a masked subset of patches, the model computes a global contextual token via attention pooling, then scores every candidate patch in the pool against this token using a similarity function, with a binary cross-entropy loss teaching the model to identify which patches originally belonged to the image. This forces the encoder to learn discriminative micro-features rather than generic context. A second loss performs image-text contrastive learning between the global image token and the description's contextual token, while a third autoregressive Description Consistency loss trains the decoder to generate the full species description conditioned on the image features, aligning fine-grained visual details with textual semantics. Pre-training runs for 200 epochs on 16 A100 GPUs with AdamW and cosine scheduling, and the resulting weights transfer to classification, detection, and zero-shot tasks via linear probing or fine-tuning.

## Results

On the IP102 Classification benchmark, Insect-Foundation with insect descriptions achieves 75.8% top-1 accuracy and 92.1% top-5 accuracy, outperforming the best prior self-supervised method (MAE at 72.0%) by 3.8 points and the best ImageNet-pretrained model (ViT at 71.6%) by 4.2 points. On IP102 Detection, the model paired with FPN achieves 36.6% AP (59.1% AP@50, 40.3% AP@75), surpassing MAE+FPN (34.7% AP) by 1.9 points. In zero-shot classification, Insect-Foundation reaches 49.9% accuracy, exceeding CoCa (45.3%) by 4.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Insect-Foundation (with descriptions) outperforms all 16 baselines across classification, detection, and zero-shot tasks. Among ImageNet-pretrained classifiers, it beats ViT (71.6%) by 4.2 points, GAEnsemble (67.1%) by 8.7 points, and ResNet (49.4%) by 26.4 points on IP102 top-1 accuracy. Among self-supervised methods pre-trained on the same 1M-Insect dataset, it surpasses MAE (72.0%) by 3.8 points, DINO (71.5%) by 4.3 points, MoCo (70.6%) by 5.2 points, and CoCa (72.8%) by 3.0 points. For detection, FPN+Insect-Foundation (36.6% AP) exceeds FPN+MAE (34.7%), FPN+DINO (34.0%), FPN+MoCo (33.6%), and FPN+ViT-ImageNet (32.8%), with the largest gap over YOLOv3 (25.7%) and FRCNN (21.1%). In zero-shot classification, Insect-Foundation (49.9%) leads CoCa (45.3%), LiT (43.6%), and CLIP (41.1%), confirming superior image-text alignment for fine-grained insect descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from generic self-supervised pre-training (MAE, DINO, MoCo) and vision-language pre-training (CLIP, CoCa) by introducing a domain-specific pretext task—Patch-wise Relevant Attention—that explicitly targets micro-feature discrimination among visually similar insect species, a capability these general-purpose methods lack. The dataset contribution extends the insect recognition line (IP102, iNat-2021) by an order of magnitude in both scale and taxonomic richness, adding hierarchical labels across six levels and per-species textual descriptions that no prior insect dataset provided. Conceptually, the work repurposes the attention-pooling mechanism from CoCa and the autoregressive decoding from CoCa/BLIP into a unified insect-specific foundation model, but the core novelty lies in the patch-relevance scoring as a self-supervised signal for fine-grained visual discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Insect-1M dataset (1M images, 34K species, hierarchical taxonomy, descriptions) is a substantial resource that could become a standard benchmark for fine-grained biological vision and agricultural AI, filling a gap where prior datasets covered at most a few hundred species. The demonstrated advantage of micro-feature-aware pre-training over generic self-supervised methods (MAE, DINO) on the same data suggests that domain-specific pretext tasks matter for fine-grained domains, a finding that could generalize to other taxonomic or medical imaging settings. However, the acknowledged limitation that background and foreground patches are treated equally in the attention mechanism, combined with the moderate absolute improvements (~2–3% over MAE), leaves open whether the approach scales to truly diverse backgrounds or whether more sophisticated foreground-aware mechanisms are needed.
