## Problem

Cycle-consistency-based rotation synchronization methods (e.g., CEMP) are limited to 3-cycles because computing cycle inconsistencies for longer cycles scales exponentially with cycle length, making them intractable. This limitation excludes critical applications such as distributed SfM (where inter-cluster edges form a bipartite graph with no odd-length cycles), jigsaw puzzles on 2D lattices, and sparse viewing graphs. Additionally, theoretical guarantees for long-cycle inference are fundamentally harder due to correlated cycle inconsistency measures sharing common edges.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pairwise rotation matrix R ∈ ℝ^{dn×dn} (with zero blocks for non-edges), a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n}, a fixed cycle length c (typically 3–6), and a sequence of positive parameters {β_t}. It outputs per-edge corruption levels S^{(T)} and edge weights W^{(T+1)}, which are then used to construct a maximum spanning tree for initialization and to weight an IRLS refinement step, ultimately yielding absolute camera rotations up to a global rotation. The framework generalizes to any compact group G, not just SO(3).

## Method

LongSync modifies CEMP in two key ways: it adopts the chordal distance on SO(d) and replaces the weighted arithmetic average of cycle inconsistencies with a weighted quadratic average. Because d_L² = 1 − ⟨R_L, R_ij⟩/d is linear in both R_L and R_ij, the weighted sum over all c-cycles containing edge ij can be expressed as a matrix power (W⊗1_d ⊙ R)^{c−1}, enabling full vectorization. To restrict to simple cycles (no repeated nodes), the authors derive explicit inclusion-exclusion formulas for the cycle-sum functions g_c and f_c, reducing per-iteration complexity from O(n^c) to O(n³) for c ≤ 6 (or O(n^{(c+3)/2}) for c > 6). The algorithm alternates between updating corruption levels via a blockwise inner product and updating edge weights via elementwise exponential decay. A complementary graph preprocessing pipeline uses Jaccard-index spectral clustering for partitioning and CEMP-based filtering of low-quality nodes and intra-cluster edges.

## Results

On the PhotoTourism dataset (15 scenes, 247–5433 cameras), LongSync outperforms all other distributed methods on 13 of 15 datasets, with the largest improvement being a 28.6% reduction in mean error and 46.4% reduction in median error (Notre Dame) over the best competing distributed method. Compared to the non-distributed state-of-the-art MPLS, LongSync achieves a 38.3% average mean error gap and 18.4% average median error gap while running in less than 6% of MPLS's total runtime. On synthetic bipartite corruption (UBCM) where no 3-cycles exist, LongSync with 4-cycles nearly exactly recovers ground-truth rotations, whereas IRLS and CEMP+IRLS fail completely. The theoretical sample complexity for LongSync with c→∞ is O(p^{-1-ε} q_g^{-7/3}), approaching the information-theoretic bound of O(p^{-1} q_g^{-2}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CEMP+IRLS (3-cycle message passing), LongSync wins decisively on the bipartite UBCM synthetic benchmark where 3-cycles do not exist—LongSync with 4-cycles nearly exactly recovers rotations while CEMP+IRLS produces estimates far from ground truth. On the UCM synthetic benchmark with corruption q > 0.86 (where expected clean 3-cycles per edge drop below 4), LongSync with c=4,5 uniformly reduces mean error over CEMP+IRLS. Against MultiSync (multi-graph least-squares synchronization), LongSync achieves lower median error on 13 of 15 PhotoTourism datasets and is uniformly faster in runtime. Against the non-distributed MPLS baseline, LongSync incurs a 38.3% average mean error gap and 18.4% median error gap but runs in under 6% of MPLS's time, making it the clear choice for large-scale distributed settings. The paper also shows that its graph preprocessing step (Jaccard-index spectral clustering + CEMP-based node/edge filtering) reduces mean error by 59.2% and median error by 28.5% on 14/15 datasets when applied to LongSync without the extra processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Cycle Edge Message Passing (CEMP) framework of Lerman and Shi [25], which estimated edge corruption levels using only 3-cycles, by developing a vectorized variant (LongSync) that efficiently handles cycles of length 4–6. The key conceptual departure is replacing the explicit enumeration of cycle inconsistencies (which scales as O(n^c)) with matrix-power operations that reduce complexity to O(n^3), enabling the first practical use of long-cycle consistency in rotation synchronization. The paper also positions itself against robust synchronization methods (IRLS, Weiszfeld, SDP) and distributed SfM approaches (MultiSync, incremental pipelines), showing that cycle-based corruption estimation with long cycles outperforms both robust objective minimization and naive edge averaging in the distributed setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for distributed SfM pipelines, where inter-cluster edges form bipartite graphs with no 3-cycles, making prior cycle-consistency methods inapplicable. The theoretical contribution—achieving the best known sample complexity among all practical rotation synchronization methods and approaching the information-theoretic bound—provides a strong foundation for future work. Open questions include closing the remaining gap to the information-theoretic bound, automating the heuristic graph preprocessing parameters, and extending the framework to partial permutation synchronization for multi-image matching and angular synchronization for Cryo-EM.
