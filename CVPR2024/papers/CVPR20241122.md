## Problem

Existing portrait relighting architectures rely on the empirical Phong specular model, which cannot accurately capture spatially varying roughness and reflectivity of diverse human surfaces (skin, hair, clothing). Additionally, high-quality light stage data required for supervised training is scarce and expensive to acquire, limiting the scale and diversity of training pairs. SwitchLight addresses both gaps by co-designing a Cook-Torrance-based rendering architecture with a self-supervised pre-training framework that expands effective training data beyond light stage captures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB portrait image and a target HDRI environment map (32×64 resolution) as input. A matting network first separates the foreground, after which the pipeline predicts a normal map, a convolved HDRI (64×128, computed via Phong lobes at exponents {1, 16, 32, 64}), a diffuse render from which albedo is derived by division, and Cook-Torrance parameters (roughness α and Fresnel reflectivity f₀). The final output is a relit portrait image produced by combining a physically-based Cook-Torrance render with a neural network enhancement that recovers fine specular and brightness details.

## Method

The architecture is a UNet decomposed into five sub-networks: Normal Net (surface normals), Illum Net (cross-attention where predefined Phong lobes query bottleneck image features to synthesize convolved HDRIs), Diffuse Net (predicts the diffuse render rather than albedo directly, then derives albedo as diffuse_render / diffuse_shading to decouple illumination from surface color), Specular Net (roughness and Fresnel reflectivity), and Render Net (computes both a PBR render via the Cook-Torrance model and a neural render that refines it). The key architectural advance over Total Relight is replacing the Phong specular term with the full Cook-Torrance microfacet model (D·G·F formulation), enabling spatially varying roughness and viewing-angle-dependent reflectance. For pre-training, the authors introduce MMAE (Multi-Masked Autoencoder), which generalizes standard MAE by replacing fixed-size masks with dynamic masking (overlapping patches of varying sizes, outpainting, and free-form masks) and augmenting the reconstruction loss with perceptual and adversarial objectives to enforce global coherence. The full UNet is pre-trained with MMAE on unlabeled data, then fine-tuned end-to-end on OLAT light stage pairs using a weighted combination of L1, VGG perceptual, PatchGAN adversarial, and specular-weighted reconstruction losses.

## Results

On the OLAT test set (35 subjects, 11 lighting environments), SwitchLight with MMAE pre-training achieves MAE 0.0933, MSE 0.0235, SSIM 0.9051, and LPIPS 2.059, substantially outperforming Total Relight (MAE 0.1643, MSE 0.0658, SSIM 0.8465, LPIPS 3.425) and SIPR (MAE 0.1715, MSE 0.0748, SSIM 0.8432, LPIPS 3.648). In a user study on FFHQ (256 images, 47 workers), SwitchLight is preferred over Lumos and TR on all three criteria: consistent lighting (0.7671 vs. 0.1852 and 0.0478), facial details (0.5376 vs. 0.2602 and 0.2022), and identity preservation (0.5819 vs. 0.2440 and 0.1741).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwitchLight outperforms all three baselines across every reported metric. Against SIPR [44], the improvement is most dramatic on MSE (0.0235 vs. 0.0748, a 69% reduction) and LPIPS (2.059 vs. 3.648, a 44% reduction), reflecting the benefit of explicit physical decomposition over a single black-box network. Against Total Relight [33], the key architectural upgrade—replacing the empirical Phong specular model with the Cook-Torrance microfacet model—yields a 43% MAE reduction (0.0933 vs. 0.1643) and a 40% LPIPS reduction (2.059 vs. 3.425), while also improving SSIM from 0.8465 to 0.9051. In the user study, SwitchLight dominates Lumos [51] on all three subjective criteria, with the largest gap on consistent lighting (0.7671 vs. 0.0478), suggesting Lumos's synthetic-to-real adaptation struggles with real-world lighting coherence. The MMAE pre-training contributes an additional ~9% MAE improvement over the architecture alone (0.0933 vs. 0.1023), confirming the value of the self-supervised stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwitchLight directly extends the Total Relight (TR) architecture by replacing its empirical Phong specular model with the physics-based Cook-Torrance microfacet model, enabling spatially varying roughness and Fresnel-dependent reflectivity. It also adapts the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) framework into a Multi-Masked Autoencoder (MMAE) tailored for convolutional UNet architectures, introducing dynamic masking strategies and generative objectives. The paper's central positioning is a co-design philosophy: the physics-driven architecture and the self-supervised pre-training are developed jointly rather than independently, which the authors frame as the first application of self-supervised pre-training specifically to the relighting task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial performance gap over TR (MAE 0.1023 vs. 0.1643; LPIPS 2.137 vs. 3.425) and the very large user-study preference margins suggest that the Cook-Torrance upgrade and diffuse-render-based albedo estimation are practically significant for portrait relighting quality. The MMAE pre-training addresses the real-world bottleneck of scarce, expensive light-stage data, though its ablation gain over vanilla MAE is modest (MAE 0.0933 vs. 0.0952), leaving open whether the self-supervised component scales further with more unlabeled data. Limitations in handling strong shadows, reflective surfaces (e.g., sunglasses), and face paint indicate that intrinsic decomposition for human portraits remains an open problem, and the authors note future plans to extend the framework to video and 3D data.
