## Problem

Adapting Stitchable Neural Networks (SN-Net) to new target domains requires a costly three-stage pipeline (individually fine-tune anchors, stitch, then evaluate all stitches), enormous training memory (e.g., >80 GB VRAM for LLaMA), and linear storage growth per task. The paper asks how to efficiently produce a palette of fine-tuned models with diverse accuracy-efficiency trade-offs while drastically reducing trainable parameters, memory, and deployment time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241754/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ESTA takes a pre-trained model family (e.g., ViT-Ti/S/B on ImageNet-21k, or LLaMA-7B/13B) and a small downstream dataset (800–10K samples for vision; 52K instruction pairs for language) as input. It outputs a set of stitched models spanning a wide FLOPs range (1.3–17.6 G for ViT; 6.9–13.0 B parameters for LLaMA), each ready for deployment at a chosen resource budget. The accumulated importance scores from training directly identify which stitches to deploy, eliminating a separate evaluation stage.

## Method

ESTA introduces two key designs. First, Parameter-efficient Stitch Fine-tuning (PST) freezes all anchor and stitching-layer weights, approximating their updates with shared low-rank LoRA matrices (stitch-agnostic) plus per-stitch bias terms (stitch-specific), reducing trainable parameters from 124.2 M to 4.6 M while mitigating gradient conflicts among stitches. Second, a one-stage deployment pipeline simultaneously adapts and stitches anchors (removing the separate anchor-adaptation stage) and employs task-specific stitch sampling: each stitch's importance is estimated via the SNIP saliency metric, accumulated with a moving average, and used to define a categorical sampling distribution within each resource-constraint interval, so that Pareto-frontier-likely stitches receive higher sampling probability. After training, the same importance scores directly select deployment stitches without costly evaluation.

## Results

ESTA produces stitches with smooth FLOPs-accuracy trade-offs across 25 visual recognition tasks (FGVC, CIFAR-100, VTAB-1k), outperforming direct SN-Net adaptation by significant margins while using only 4.6M trainable parameters (vs. 124.2M for SN-Net) and 5.0 GPU hours (vs. 19.3). On the instruction-following task, Stitched LLaMA models (6.9B–13.0B parameters) achieve relative response qualities of 113–123% on Vicuna Bench, interpolating smoothly between Alpaca-LoRA 7B (100%) and 13B (123%). Fine-tuning memory is reduced to 9,685M (vs. 13,235M for SN-Net) when stitching ViT-Ti/S/B anchors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SN-Net [51], ESTA achieves substantially better FLOPs-accuracy curves on all 25 visual recognition tasks while reducing trainable parameters from 124.2M to 4.6M (96% reduction) and deployment time from 19.3 to 5.0 GPU hours (74% reduction). Compared to individually fine-tuned LoRA anchors [28], ESTA's stitches outperform anchors with comparable or lower FLOPs on NABirds, CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, and Stanford Dogs, suggesting weight sharing acts as regularization. On the LLM instruction-following task, Stitched LLaMA models (6.9B, 7.8B, 11.5B, 12.5B) achieve Vicuna Bench qualities of 113%, 114%, 115%, and 120% respectively, interpolating between Alpaca-LoRA 7B (100%) and 13B (123%) [62]. Against supernet-based methods NAT [47] and TOFA [35], ESTA covers a much wider FLOPs range (1.3–17.6G vs. 0.2–0.6G and 0.2–2.5G) with orders-of-magnitude fewer deployment GPU hours (5.0 vs. >1,000) and is the only method scalable to LLMs with billions of parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SN-Net (Stitchable Neural Networks, CVPR 2023), which stitches pre-trained model families to produce diverse-capacity networks, from its original scope (pre-training task on the same source domain) to the task adaptation setting where anchors must be fine-tuned on a new target domain. It repurposes LoRA (ICLR 2022) as the backbone of a parameter-efficient stitch fine-tuning (PST) method, augmented with stitch-specific bias terms to mitigate gradient conflicts among shared weights, and adapts the SNIP saliency metric (ICLR 2019) from one-shot pruning to a training-time stitch importance estimator that drives non-uniform sampling. The paper positions itself as the first systematic treatment of efficient SN-Net adaptation, departing from the expensive three-stage pipeline (adapt anchors → stitch → evaluate all stitches) with a unified one-stage pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains are substantial: 4× fewer deployment GPU hours (5.0 vs 19.3), 27× fewer trainable parameters (4.6M vs 124.2M), and lower fine-tuning memory, making diverse-capacity model deployment feasible on a single GPU. The extension to LLaMA-family LLMs for instruction-following demonstrates scalability to billion-parameter models, opening a path toward producing chatbots of assorted sizes from a single fine-tuning run. However, the framework is limited to model families with compatible architectures (same family, different sizes), and experiments are restricted to visual recognition and instruction-tuning; dense prediction and multi-modal adaptation remain open. The finding that weight sharing among stitches acts as a regularizer (stitches outperform individually fine-tuned anchors at comparable FLOPs) suggests a broader principle about multi-model training that could inform other ensemble or supernet settings.
