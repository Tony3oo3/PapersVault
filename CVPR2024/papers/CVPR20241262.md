## Problem

Existing feature-matching-based visual localization methods discard the majority of detected 2D keypoints during 2D-3D matching because they can only match against 3D points already present in a pre-reconstructed model. This underutilization of dense 2D-2D matches degrades pose accuracy in challenging conditions such as nighttime, adverse weather, and when the 3D point cloud is noisy or sparse (e.g., from SIFT-based SfM). The paper asks whether semi-dense 2D-2D matches can be directly converted into 2D-3D correspondences without requiring a high-fidelity 3D reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query image, top-k retrieved reference images, a sparse SIFT-based 3D point cloud from COLMAP SfM, and known camera intrinsics/extrinsics for the reference views. Rather than producing a dense depth map, the Point Inference Network predicts a discrete set of 3D scene coordinates and per-point confidence scores for every detected 2D keypoint in each reference image. These semi-dense 2D-3D matches (with confidence) are then fed into a standard RANSAC-PnP solver to yield the final 6-DoF camera pose; the confidence values also serve as a learned outlier-rejection signal during multi-view aggregation.

## Method

DeViLoc introduces two modules that convert semi-dense 2D-2D matches (produced by a detector-free matcher like TopicFM) into 2D-3D correspondences. The Point Inference Network (PIN) regresses 3D coordinates for all reference-image keypoints by first encoding observed 3D points into latent codes via MLPs and a self-attention layer, then propagating depth information to unobserved positions through a cross-attention layer (geometric guidance), and finally refining with visual features sampled from the matching network's feature map via additional self/cross-attention layers (visual guidance); two MLP heads output per-keypoint depth and confidence. The Confidence-based Point Aggregation (CPA) module then merges 2D-3D matches across multiple query-reference pairs by quantizing nearby 2D keypoints into groups and computing confidence-weighted averages of their 3D positions, effectively suppressing outliers. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on MegaDepth with an L1 point-matching loss plus a binary cross-entropy confidence loss, and requires no per-scene fine-tuning at test time.

## Results

On the Aachen Day-Night benchmark, DeViLoc achieves 87.8/93.9/100% recall at (25cm,2°)/(50cm,5°)/(5m,10°) for night queries, outperforming NeuMap (48.0/67.3/87.8) by nearly 40 points at the strictest threshold. On Extended CMU Seasons, DeViLoc reaches 92.1/95.1/96.3% on the Park scene, surpassing HLoc[SP+SG] (85.7/89.0/91.6) by over 6 points. On 7scenes, DeViLoc secures first-place median translation errors in three scenes (Fire: 0.74°, Heads: 0.65°, Pumpkin: 1.02°) while being trained only on MegaDepth, unlike per-scene SCR baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeViLoc's most dramatic advantage is over NeuMap [57], the closest semi-dense competitor: on Aachen Night, DeViLoc achieves 87.8% recall at (25cm,2°) versus NeuMap's 48.0%, a gap of nearly 40 points. Against the strong HLoc[SP+SG] pipeline, DeViLoc matches or slightly exceeds performance on Aachen Day (87.4 vs 89.6 at 25cm) but pulls ahead on Aachen Night (87.8 vs 86.7) and RobotCar Night (68.9 vs 65.9 at 50cm/5°). On Extended CMU Seasons, DeViLoc dominates all three scenes, improving over HLoc[SP+SG] by 6.4 points on Park (92.1 vs 85.7 at 25cm) and 6.2 points on Suburban (97.1 vs 90.9). On 7scenes, DeViLoc outperforms all SCR-based dense methods (HSCNet, DSAC++, DSAC*, SANet, DSM) in median rotation error despite being trained only on MegaDepth, while those baselines require per-scene training. Notably, DeViLoc uses noisy SIFT-based point clouds directly, whereas HLoc[SP+SG] requires retriangulating with SuperPoint/SuperGlue features, yet DeViLoc still achieves comparable or better results.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeViLoc builds directly on the HLoc coarse-to-fine localization paradigm (retrieval → 2D-3D matching → PnP), but departs from the standard FM pipeline by introducing a Point Inference Network that regresses 3D coordinates for *all* detected 2D keypoints rather than only those with pre-existing 3D observations. Conceptually, it occupies a 'semi-dense' middle ground between sparse feature-matching methods (which discard unobserved keypoints) and dense scene-coordinate-regression methods (which predict every pixel but struggle at scale), drawing architectural inspiration from depth completion while operating on discrete keypoints with attention and MLP layers. The Confidence-based Point Aggregation module further distinguishes it by fusing matches across multiple reference views with learned uncertainty, a step absent from prior FM or SCR pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling localization quality from 3D-model fidelity: in SLAM, robot navigation, and online settings where point clouds are built on-the-fly and are inherently noisy or sparse, DeViLoc maintains competitive accuracy without requiring expensive re-triangulation with learned features. A key open question is computational efficiency—runtime scales linearly with the number of query-reference pairs, limiting real-time deployment. Additionally, the method was trained exclusively on outdoor MegaDepth data yet generalizes to indoor 7scenes, raising questions about how much of the PIN's geometric reasoning is truly scene-agnostic versus dataset-specific. Future work could explore whether the semi-dense formulation extends to dynamic scenes or multi-modal inputs (RGB-D, LiDAR).
