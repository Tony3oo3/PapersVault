## Problem

In federated learning with heterogeneous client data, local gradient updates deviate from the global direction, slowing convergence. While supervised contrastive learning (SCL) theoretically mitigates these deviations, naively applying SCL in FL causes intra-class representation collapse—features within the same class become excessively similar—reducing feature diversity and transferability across clients, which paradoxically hinders the collaborative aggregation that FL relies on.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local subset of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) with class labels, distributed non-i.i.d. via a Dirichlet process. The model (ResNet-18 with group normalization) produces a class label prediction, but the contrastive loss is computed at five intermediate convolutional layers (conv1 through conv5_x) rather than only at the final feature output, making the input to the loss a multi-level set of feature vectors per sample.

## Method

FedRCL introduces a relaxed contrastive loss that augments the standard SCL objective with an adaptive divergence penalty: for any intra-class pair whose cosine similarity exceeds a threshold λ, an additional repulsion term (weighted by β) is added to push their representations apart, preventing the within-class collapse that plagues vanilla SCL under limited, skewed local data. Theoretically, the paper reformulates the sample-wise gradient deviation bound from Zhang et al. to show it depends on feature similarity distributions, and derives that minimizing the RCL loss upper-bounds a surrogate objective that increases the deviation bound, thereby ensuring more consistent local updates. The contrastive loss is applied at all intermediate layers (not just the final one) to propagate regularization throughout the network. The overall framework uses standard FedAvg aggregation with no additional communication overhead, no global prototypes, and no reliance on the global model for contrastive alignment, making it robust even at very low participation rates (2%) and large client counts (500).

## Results

On the most challenging non-i.i.d. setting (α=0.05, 1000 rounds), FedRCL achieves 76.74% on CIFAR-10, 54.63% on CIFAR-100, and 34.60% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming the strongest baseline (MOON) by 15.0, 13.99, and 8.26 percentage points respectively. Under i.i.d. conditions, FedRCL reaches 91.90% on CIFAR-10, 64.81% on CIFAR-100, and 47.25% on Tiny-ImageNet. The method also shows consistent gains at 500 rounds, indicating faster convergence (e.g., 64.44% vs. 51.47% for FedAvg on CIFAR-10 at α=0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRCL outperforms all nine client-side baselines on every dataset and heterogeneity level. The largest gaps appear under severe non-i.i.d. conditions (α=0.05): on CIFAR-100, FedRCL (54.63%) surpasses the next-best FedMLB (43.89%) by 10.7 points and MOON (40.64%) by 14.0 points; on Tiny-ImageNet, it leads FedNTD (28.96%) by 5.6 points. FedProc is the weakest baseline overall, dropping to 18.41% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.05) due to its reliance on global prototypes that degrade under low participation. MOON is the strongest competitor in moderate-to-mild heterogeneity (α≥0.3) and i.i.d. settings but still trails FedRCL by 5–11 points. FedDecorr, which targets dimensional collapse directly, shows only marginal gains over FedAvg and even degrades in some settings, confirming that blind decorrelation is insufficient. When combined with server-side optimizers (Table 5), FedRCL adds 3–11 points over FedAvgM, FedADAM, and FedACG individually, demonstrating orthogonality and consistent additive benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the FedAvg framework and the supervised contrastive learning (SCL) objective, reformulating the gradient deviation bound from Zhang et al. (FedLC, ICML 2022) to show that SCL theoretically mitigates inconsistent local updates. It departs from prior contrastive-learning-in-FL methods (MOON, FedProc, FedBR) that explicitly align local models to global models or prototypes, instead addressing local deviations self-containedly without any additional communication overhead. The key conceptual departure is identifying that naive SCL in the FL setting causes representation collapse due to limited and skewed local data, and proposing a relaxed contrastive loss with an adaptive intra-class divergence penalty to prevent this collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The representation collapse diagnosis is practically important: it explains why a theoretically-motivated SCL objective underperforms in FL and provides a principled fix that is simple to implement (a single penalty term) and requires no extra communication. However, the experimental scope is limited to ResNet-18 on three relatively small image datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet), leaving open whether the approach transfers to transformer backbones, larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet, or non-vision modalities. The multi-level contrastive extension is straightforward but its interaction with deeper architectures or different normalization schemes remains unexplored.
