## Problem

Diffusion models in specialized domains like digital histopathology and satellite imagery require fine-grained per-image annotations (captions, class labels) that are impractical to procure at scale, as they demand expert knowledge across hundreds of millions of patches. The paper asks whether modern self-supervised learning (SSL) representations, which already encode rich semantic and visual information, are expressive enough to serve as conditioning signals that replace these human annotations for high-quality image synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single d-dimensional SSL embedding vector (from HIPT, iBOT, CTransPath, or DINO) per 256×256 patch as conditioning, or a P×P spatial grid of such vectors for large-image synthesis. For text-to-image, a text prompt is first mapped to a grid of SSL embeddings by an auxiliary diffusion model before image generation. Outputs are 256×256 patches or 1024×1024 large images at 20×/5× magnification (histopathology) or 1 m resolution (satellite), with the large-image output assembled from spatially consistent overlapping patches rather than generated in a single forward pass.

## Method

The core contribution is training Latent Diffusion Models (VAE + U-Net denoiser) where the conditioning mechanism is a cross-attention layer that injects a pre-trained SSL embedding vector, replacing the need for class labels or text captions. For large-image synthesis, the paper adopts a MultiDiffusion-style framework: a P×P grid of SSL embeddings is spatially interpolated to produce a conditioning vector at every pixel location, and the large-image denoising step is solved in closed form by averaging the patch-level diffusion updates from all overlapping crops. This allows generating images of virtually any size with a single patch-level model while preserving both local semantics (from per-patch conditioning) and global structure (from the spatial arrangement of the embedding grid). For text-to-large-image synthesis, an auxiliary diffusion model p(z|c) is trained to sample spatial grids of SSL embeddings from text or image embeddings (via Quilt, CLIP, or BLIP), decoupling the text-to-embedding mapping from the embedding-to-image generation.

## Results

On patch-level generation, the SSL-guided LDM achieves a Vanilla FID of 6.98 on TCGA-BRCA (20×), comparable to the prior state-of-the-art PathLDM (7.64 at 10×), and 6.78 on TCGA-CRC. For large-image synthesis (1024×1024), the model attains Crop FID of 15.51 (BRCA) and 8.8 (CRC), with CLIP FID scores of 7.43 and 7.34 respectively, and high embedding similarity (0.924 and 0.938) confirming preservation of global semantics. For data augmentation, adding synthetic images to the BACH training set improves classification accuracy from 78% to 83%, and on CRC-VAL-HE from 93.8% to 96.27%, matching the prior SoTA ensemble.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior SoTA methods on classification tasks augmented with synthetic data. On the BACH breast cancer dataset, the proposed method (Real+synthetic) achieves 83% test accuracy, falling 4 percentage points short of the ensemble-based SoTA of Chennamsetty et al. [7] (87%), though it substantially outperforms the real-only baseline (78%). On the CRC-VAL-HE-7K colorectal tissue classification task, the proposed method reaches 96.27% validation accuracy, essentially matching the SoTA of Kumar et al. [20] (96.26%) while using a single ResNet-50 rather than an ensemble of deep models. Notably, in both augmentation experiments the diffusion model was trained on a different dataset (TCGA-BRCA or TCGA-CRC) than the target classification dataset, demonstrating out-of-distribution generalization. For patch-level generation quality, the BRCA model's FID of 6.98 is competitive with PathLDM [48] (7.64 at 10× magnification), though a direct head-to-head at identical magnification is not provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as the core generative framework and MultiDiffusion (Bar-Tal et al., ICML 2023) for assembling large images from patch-level diffusion, but departs from both by replacing their conditioning signals (class labels, text, or segmentation masks) with self-supervised learning embeddings from domain-specific models like HIPT, iBOT, and DINO. It explicitly contrasts with self-guided diffusion [17], which quantizes SSL embeddings and discards information, and with DiffInfinite [1], which requires human-annotated masks. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that SSL representations are expressive enough to serve as fine-grained conditioning proxies, eliminating the need for the expensive patch-level annotation that has limited diffusion model adoption in histopathology and remote sensing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck: gigapixel-scale domains like digital pathology and satellite imagery lack the fine-grained annotations that diffusion models typically require, and the paper demonstrates that pre-trained SSL features can substitute for them effectively. The out-of-distribution augmentation results (a TCGA-trained model generating realistic BACH or NCT-CRC images) suggest the approach could serve as a general-purpose data augmentation tool across related but distinct datasets. Open questions include whether the SSL conditioning can be made more interpretable or controllable at the semantic level, whether adaptive (rather than uniform) augmentation strategies would further improve downstream performance, and how the framework scales to even larger images or more complex spatial dependencies beyond the current grid-based interpolation.
