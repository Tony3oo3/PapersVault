## Problem

Existing methods for identifying interpretable latent directions in diffusion models cannot discover vectors for arbitrary user-specified concepts, particularly ethical ones related to fairness and safety. Unsupervised approaches yield vectors whose semantic meaning is unknown, while supervised approaches require external classifiers trained on human annotations and cannot easily generalize to new concepts. This leaves the internal mechanism by which diffusion models produce biased or harmful content poorly understood and difficult to manipulate.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt and a pre-learned concept vector (a single D-dimensional vector in the U-Net bottleneck h-space) that is linearly added to the bottleneck activations at every denoising timestep. The concept vectors themselves are learned offline by generating images with a full prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a female face') and then optimizing the vector to reconstruct those images under a modified prompt that omits the target concept (e.g., 'a photo of a face'). The output is a generated image whose attributes are steered by the injected vector, with the same Stable Diffusion v1.4 architecture and guidance scale (7.5) as the base model.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-supervised optimization that discovers a latent direction for any given concept without external classifiers or labeled data. Images are first synthesized by the frozen diffusion model using a prompt containing the target concept; a randomly initialized vector c in h-space is then optimized to minimize the denoising reconstruction loss when the model is conditioned on a modified prompt that removes the concept, forcing c to encode the missing semantic information. For safety, the method learns 'anti-concept' vectors (e.g., anti-sexual) by generating training images with a negative prompt and optimizing c on those safe images. At generation time, the learned vector is simply added to the bottleneck activations (h ← h + c), enabling fair generation via uniform sampling over societal-group vectors, safe generation via fixed safety vectors, and responsible text-enhancing by extracting safety phrases from the prompt and activating the corresponding learned directions. The approach uses a single vector per concept across all timesteps and restricts the operation to linearity, yet the vectors generalize across professions, objects, and prompt contexts.

## Results

On the Winobias benchmark for fair generation, the proposed method achieves a deviation ratio of 0.17 (gender) and 0.23 (race), substantially outperforming Stable Diffusion (0.68 / 0.56) and UCE (0.22 / 0.21). On the harder Gender+/Race+ variants with bias-inducing words, the gap widens: Ours reaches 0.23 (gender) vs. UCE's 0.52, while SD scores 0.70. For safe generation on the I2P benchmark, combining the learned safety vectors with SLD reduces the overall inappropriate-content rate from 0.20 to 0.16, and with ESD from 0.32 to 0.27, with the largest relative gain (40%) in the sexual category when paired with SLD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Diffusion v1.4 (SD), the proposed method consistently wins on all three tasks: fair generation (0.17 vs. 0.68 deviation ratio on Winobias gender), safe generation (0.27 vs. 0.41 inappropriate rate on I2P), and responsible text-enhancing generation (0.1433 vs. 0.2199 gender deviation). Against UCE (Unified Concept Editing), the method achieves a clear advantage on the harder Gender+ and Race+ prompts (0.23 vs. 0.52 and 0.20 vs. 0.35), because UCE must debias each profession individually while the learned concept vectors generalize from a single 'person' prompt. Against SLD (Safe Latent Diffusion), the method acts as a complementary add-on: Ours-SLD reduces the overall I2P inappropriate rate from 0.20 to 0.16, with a 40% relative improvement in the sexual category (0.15 → 0.09). Against ESD (Erasing Concepts from Diffusion), Ours-ESD improves the overall rate from 0.32 to 0.27, particularly in sexual (0.18 → 0.13) and violence (0.41 → 0.30) categories, though the gain is smaller than with SLD. Image quality (FID/CLIP on COCO-30K) remains comparable to SD (FID 15.98 vs. 14.09; CLIP 31.03 vs. 31.33), confirming that the latent-space manipulation does not significantly degrade generation fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Kwon et al.'s identification of the U-Net bottleneck layer (h-space) as a semantic representation space and on prior work using PCA or Riemannian geometry to find interpretable directions in that space. It departs from those approaches by introducing a self-supervised optimization loop: the model generates images with a concept-specific prompt, then a vector is trained to reconstruct those images when the concept is removed from the text, eliminating the need for CLIP classifiers, human annotations, or external attribute models. The method is positioned as orthogonal to existing safety mechanisms (SLD, ESD, classifier-free guidance), which operate in the noise or text-embedding space, whereas this work manipulates the semantic latent space directly and can be composed with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-discovery mechanism offers a practical, training-free, and label-free way to identify and manipulate specific semantic concepts in frozen diffusion models, with the notable property that vectors learned on a generic prompt (e.g., 'person') generalize to diverse contexts (e.g., 'doctor', 'manager'). This opens a path toward plug-and-play responsible generation that does not require retraining model weights or maintaining exhaustive keyword lists. Limitations include evaluation only on Stable Diffusion v1.4, restriction to linear operations, and quality degradation for abstract concepts like 'hate'; open questions include whether the approach transfers to transformer-based diffusion architectures (SDXL, DiT) and whether non-linear or multi-vector representations are needed for more complex ethical concepts.
