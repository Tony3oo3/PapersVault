## Problem

Existing generalizable synthetic image detectors following the fixed pre-trained paradigm (e.g., UniFD) freeze a pre-trained CLIP-ViT and train only an attached linear classifier, which the authors show yields insufficient learning of forgery representations. The core gap is the lack of forgery adaptation: without adapting the pre-trained model to the detection task, the detector cannot fully exploit forgery-specific cues, leading to large real/fake overlap in logit distributions on unseen GANs and diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240198/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256, then center-cropped to 224×224) and produces a real/fake probability via softmax over cosine similarities between the image CLS token and two fixed text prompt embeddings ('this photo is real' / 'this photo is fake'). During training, text prompts are dynamically enhanced by conditioning on image patch tokens, but at test time only the image is required since the text prompts are pre-computed.

## Method

FatFormer adapts a pre-trained CLIP (ViT-L/14 image encoder + transformer text encoder) through two core mechanisms. First, a forgery-aware adapter (FAA) is inserted between ViT stages, combining a lightweight convolutional image forgery extractor for low-level artifacts and a frequency forgery extractor that applies inter-band and intra-band multi-head attention over four DWT frequency bands (LL, LH, HL, HH) to dynamically aggregate spatially-aware frequency clues. Second, a language-guided alignment (LGA) module supervises the adaptation via augmented contrastive objectives: a patch-based enhancer conditions learnable context embeddings on image patch tokens to build forgery-relevant text prompts, and a text-guided interactor aligns local image patch tokens with global text prompt embeddings, directing the encoder toward forgery-related representations. Training uses cross-entropy on the merged similarity (CLS-token + patch-level) with real/fake labels on ProGAN data, and the approach generalizes to unseen GANs (98.4% ACC) and diffusion models (95.0% ACC) without task-specific retraining.

## Results

Trained on 4-class ProGAN data, FatFormer achieves a mean of 98.4% ACC and 99.7% AP across 8 unseen GAN types (ProGAN, StyleGAN, StyleGAN2, BigGAN, CycleGAN, StargAN, GauGAN, DeepFake). On 10 unseen diffusion model variants (PNDM, Guided, DALL-E, VQ-Diffusion, LDM, Glide), it reaches 95.0% ACC and 98.8% AP. The method outperforms the prior state-of-the-art UniFD by 9.3% ACC on GANs and 9.6% ACC on diffusion models, while using the same frozen CLIP backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FatFormer is compared against 10 baselines spanning image-based, frequency-based, and pre-trained-based paradigms. Against the closest pre-trained-based competitors, it surpasses UniFD [43] by 9.3% ACC (98.4 vs. 89.1) and 1.4% AP on GANs, and by 9.6% ACC (95.0 vs. 85.4) and 4.2% AP on diffusion models; it also outperforms LGrad [55] by 12.3% ACC on GANs and 5.6% ACC on diffusion models. Among frequency-based methods, F3Net [45] (80.4% ACC) and FrePGAN [22] (79.4% ACC) are the strongest, yet both fall 18+ points behind FatFormer on mean GAN accuracy, with particularly large gaps on BigGAN and GauGAN. Image-based baselines Wang [59] (67.1% ACC) and PatchFor [3] (84.5% ACC on diffusion) are also substantially outperformed. The only setting where FatFormer does not achieve near-perfect scores is the Guided diffusion model (76.1% ACC), where LGrad [55] (86.6%) and UniFD [43] (75.7%) are closer, though FatFormer still leads UniFD by 0.4% ACC and 6.9% AP on that specific model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the 'fixed pre-trained paradigm' popularized by UniFD (Ojha et al., CVPR 2023) and LGrad (Tan et al., CVPR 2023), which freeze a pre-trained CLIP-ViT and train only an attached linear classifier. FatFormer instead introduces a forgery-aware adapter (combining image-domain convolutions and DWT-based frequency-domain grouped attention) inserted between ViT stages, plus a language-guided alignment module that leverages contrastive image-text objectives for supervision. The soft prompt design in the patch-based enhancer draws on conditional prompt learning for vision-language models (Zhou et al., CVPR 2022 / IJCV 2022), repurposing it to condition text prompts on local image patch tokens rather than global features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear conceptual argument—supported by logit-distribution visualizations and ablations—that freezing pre-trained models limits forgery representation learning, and that lightweight adaptation with contrastive vision-language supervision substantially improves cross-generative-model generalization (98.4% ACC on 8 GANs, 95.0% ACC on 10 diffusion models from 4-class ProGAN training). This challenges the prevailing assumption in the field that frozen pre-trained features are inherently more generalizable. Open questions remain: performance on certain diffusion models (e.g., Guided) is still weaker, the paper acknowledges the need to better understand distinctions between diffusion- and GAN-generated artifacts, and designing task-specific pretext tasks for pre-training is left for future work.
