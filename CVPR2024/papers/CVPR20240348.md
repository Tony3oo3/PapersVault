## Problem

Existing referring expression segmentation (RES) tasks and benchmarks are constrained to object-level targets, with no dataset or evaluation protocol supporting part-level visual grounding. This limits multimodal agents from performing fine-grained perception of real-world scenes, where natural language descriptions frequently reference specific parts of objects rather than whole objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240348/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form natural language referring expression (average length ~4.6–5.1 words) that may describe either a whole object or a specific part of an object, and outputs a pixel-level binary segmentation mask. The RefCOCOm benchmark spans 80 object and 391 part categories with 34k masks and 92k references, while the MRES-32M training set covers 365 object and 2,299 part categories across 1M images. Bounding box coordinates for the dense captioner are normalized to integer values in [0, 999].

## Method

UniRES builds on a CLIP backbone (ViT image encoder + Transformer text encoder) and introduces a query-based grouping design: 64 learnable tokens are inserted into the first half and 8 into the middle of the ViT, exploiting self-attention to implicitly form visual groups that capture low-level local and high-level global features respectively. A Language-Guided Region Filter (LRF) then uses cross-attention to select expression-relevant group tokens from both levels. A two-stage Transformer decoder first fuses backbone visual and textual features, then integrates the grouped region features at both semantic levels before a linear projection produces the final mask. The MRES-32M dataset is generated by an automated pipeline that fine-tunes an LVLM as a multi-granularity dense captioner, uses SAM for object masks, GPT-4 to decompose object categories into part vocabularies, an open-vocabulary segmenter for part masks, and CLIP similarity filtering (threshold 0.5) to ensure visual-linguistic consistency.

## Results

On the proposed RefCOCOm benchmark for multi-granularity RES, UniRES achieves 19.6 mIoU (Part-only) and 34.3 mIoU (Obj&Part) on the validation set, outperforming the best specialist (LAVT: 15.3/29.9) and generalist (X-Decoder: 16.2/29.5) baselines. On classic object-level RES, UniRES achieves 79.2/81.6/76.6 mIoU on RefCOCO (val/testA/testB), 73.0/78.1/65.8 on RefCOCO+, and 71.7/73.2 on RefCOCOg after fine-tuning, setting new SOTA across all three benchmarks. In the zero-shot setting, UniRES reaches 71.2 mIoU on RefCOCO val without any task-specific fine-tuning, surpassing the previous best zero-shot method (TAS: 39.8) by over 31 mIoU points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniRES outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Among fine-tuned specialist RES models, UniRES leads LAVT (the strongest prior specialist) by +4.7 mIoU on RefCOCO val, +7.2 on RefCOCO+ val, and +8.4 on RefCOCOg val; the gap widens to +13.5 mIoU over VLT on RefCOCO val. Among generalist models (X-Decoder, SEEM), UniRES surpasses them by +6.1 mIoU on RefCOCOg val in the fine-tuned setting. In the zero-shot regime, UniRES (71.2 mIoU on RefCOCO val) exceeds the best prior zero-shot method TAS (39.8) by 31.4 mIoU, and even outperforms fine-tuned specialists like CRIS (70.5) and ReSTR (67.2). On the new RefCOCOm MRES benchmark, UniRES (19.6 Part / 34.3 Obj&Part on val) beats the best specialist LAVT (15.3/29.9) by +4.3/+4.4 mIoU and the best generalist X-Decoder (16.2/29.5) by +3.4/+4.8 mIoU, confirming that part-level grounding remains a significant challenge where prior methods fall short.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of classic object-level referring expression segmentation (RES) and part-level visual perception. Prior RES methods (SeqTR, CRIS, LAVT, etc.) assume a single object-level target per expression, while part-level datasets (Pascal-Part, PartImageNet, PACO) provide fine-grained masks but lack any language grounding. The paper bridges this gap by repurposing CLIP as a backbone, SAM for segmentation, and GPT-4 for part vocabulary decomposition into a unified data engine, then introduces learnable grouping tokens and a two-stage decoder to handle both granularities within a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32M-sample MRES-32M dataset and RefCOCOm benchmark establish the first large-scale, part-level vision-language grounding resource, opening a research direction that was previously infeasible due to annotation cost. The finding that part-level pretraining data improves object-level RES accuracy (75.2% → 78.0% mIoU) suggests a cross-granularity synergy that could benefit broader visual grounding. However, part-level accuracy remains very low (19.6% mIoU), indicating the task is far from solved, and the dataset's reliance on Object365 bounding boxes and GPT-4-generated part vocabularies introduces potential noise that limits the ceiling of what can be learned.
