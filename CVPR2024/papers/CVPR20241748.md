## Problem

Existing V2X cooperative perception datasets are either simulated, lack track IDs, cover only a single communication type (V2V or V2I), or omit challenging real-world scenarios such as traffic violations and near-miss events. There is no publicly available, real-world V2I dataset that combines multi-sensor infrastructure (5 sensors) and vehicle (4 sensors) data with dense 3D annotations, track IDs, and OpenLABEL-standard formatting to benchmark cooperative 3D detection and tracking.

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized camera images (1920×1200 from 4 roadside and 1 onboard Basler cameras) and LiDAR point clouds (64-layer Ouster OS1-64 roadside, 32-layer Robosense RS-LiDAR-32 onboard) with a restricted point cloud range of [-75, 75] m in x/y and [-8, 0] m in z. It outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels across 8 object categories and persistent track IDs, evaluated via BEV mAP (center-distance based, nuScenes protocol) and 3D mAP (IoU-based). The dataset itself provides 2,000 labeled point clouds and 5,000 labeled images containing 29,38k 3D boxes, distributed in OpenLABEL format with a devkit for conversion to KITTI or custom nuScenes formats.

## Method

CoopDet3D is a three-stage cooperative fusion architecture built on BEVFusion and PillarGrid. Stage 1 fuses roadside camera and LiDAR features into a BEV representation; Stage 2 fuses vehicle camera and LiDAR features via a convolutional fuser; Stage 3 combines the two BEV feature maps with an element-wise max-pooling operation (the PillarGrid-inspired cooperative fusion step). A TransFusion 3D detection head then predicts bounding boxes from the fused features. The best backbone configuration, found through ablation over 11 combinations, is PointPillars 512_2x (512×512 train / 1024×1024 test grid) with YOLOv8s for the camera branch, achieving 94.22 BEV mAP at 11.2 FPS on a single RTX 3090. Training proceeds in two phases: 20 epochs of LiDAR-only pretraining with camera nodes disabled, followed by 8 epochs of full multi-modal fine-tuning using pre-trained cooperative weights, on 3× RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Results

On the TUMTraf-V2X test set (south2 FOV), CoopDet3D achieves 90.76 mAP_3D (avg) and 94.22 mAP_BEV with cooperative camera-LiDAR fusion, compared to 76.40 mAP_3D for vehicle-only camera-LiDAR fusion — a headline improvement of +14.36 3D mAP. Infrastructure-only camera-LiDAR fusion reaches 87.01 mAP_3D, confirming the general ordering: cooperative > infrastructure > vehicle. LiDAR-only cooperative detection achieves 85.86 mAP_3D, while camera-only cooperative detection reaches 45.74 mAP_3D, highlighting the complementary strengths of the two modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CoopDet3D (deep fusion) against InfraDet3D (late fusion, [47]) on the TUMTraf Intersection test set. CoopDet3D outperforms InfraDet3D in average mAP_3D across all four configurations: south1 LiDAR (69.47 vs. 55.21, +14.26), south2 LiDAR (69.94 vs. 43.13, +26.81), south1 Cam+LiDAR (66.75 vs. 44.55, +22.20), and south2 Cam+LiDAR (66.89 vs. 37.06, +29.83). The only exception is south1 LiDAR hard difficulty, where InfraDet3D scores 42.16 vs. CoopDet3D's 35.19. The paper also notes that deep feature fusion (transmitting features over V2I) is preferred over late fusion (transmitting bounding boxes) for real-world deployment, as it yields consistently higher efficacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoopDet3D extends BEVFusion's unified BEV camera-LiDAR fusion backbone to the cooperative setting by adding a PillarGrid-inspired element-wise max-pooling operation to merge vehicle and infrastructure feature maps, with a TransFusion detection head on top. The TUMTraf-V2X dataset positions itself among existing V2X benchmarks (DAIR-V2X, V2X-Seq, V2V4Real, OPV2V) but differentiates through track IDs, traffic-violation scenarios, OpenLABEL format, and a 360° four-camera infrastructure setup. Unlike prior cooperative datasets that focus on scale (DAIR-V2X's 39k point clouds) or simulation (OPV2V, V2X-Sim), this work emphasizes challenging real-world maneuvers and high-quality labeling at a single German intersection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a gap for V2X research with rare-event scenarios (traffic violations, near-misses, U-turns) and provides a devkit with OpenLABEL support, lowering the barrier for cooperative perception research. However, the dataset's small scale (2,000 point clouds, 5,000 images from a single intersection) limits generalizability, and the paper explicitly notes that real deployment must account for transmission bandwidth and latency, which are ignored in the offline evaluation. Open questions include whether the deep-feature-fusion advantage over late fusion (bounding-box sharing) persists under realistic V2I bandwidth constraints, and whether the +14.36 mAP cooperative gain holds across diverse intersections and weather conditions beyond the eight recorded scenes.
