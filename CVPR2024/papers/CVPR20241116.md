## Problem

Existing text-to-image person re-identification (TIReID) methods implicitly assume all training image-text pairs are correctly aligned, yet real-world datasets inevitably contain noisy correspondences (NC) where negative pairs are wrongly treated as positive. These false positive pairs misguide cross-modal learning, causing overfitting and even model collapse under high noise rates. This paper is the first to formally identify and study the NC problem specifically in the TIReID setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes person images (384×128 pixels) and natural language descriptions (up to 77 BPE word tokens) as input, encoded by a pretrained CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. It produces a joint cross-modal similarity score computed as the average of two granularities: a Basic Global Embedding (BGE) cosine similarity between [CLS] and [EOS] tokens, and a Token Selection Embedding (TSE) similarity from top-K informative local tokens. At inference, this scalar score ranks gallery images against a text query for retrieval.

## Method

RDE combines two key mechanisms for robust TIReID under noisy correspondence. First, the Confident Consensus Division (CCD) module fits per-sample losses from both BGE and TSE with a two-component GMM, then takes the intersection of the clean sets identified by each module to produce a confident clean subset, recalibrating correspondence labels (clean→1, noisy→0, uncertain→random). Second, the Triplet Alignment Loss (TAL) replaces the standard hardest-negative triplet loss with a log-exponential upper bound over all in-batch negatives, controlled by a temperature τ that interpolates between focusing on hard negatives (τ→0, recovering TRL) and treating all negatives equally (large τ, recovering TRL-S). The TSE module selects informative local tokens via self-attention correlation weights from the last Transformer block, applies an MLP+FC+MaxPool residual transformation, and computes a fine-grained similarity complementary to the global BGE score. Training uses the recalibrated labels to weight the joint TAL loss from both embedding modules, preventing the model from memorizing noisy supervision.

## Results

On clean data (0% noise), RDE achieves 75.94% Rank-1 on CUHK-PEDES, 67.68% on ICFG-PEDES, and 65.35% on RSTPReID, outperforming the best prior method IRRA by +2.56%, +4.22%, and +5.15% respectively. Under 50% synthetic noisy correspondence, RDE retains 71.25% Rank-1 on CUHK-PEDES (vs. 65.22% for the best robust baseline DECL and 64.02% for IRRA), demonstrating strong robustness. The method also reports mAP of 63.59% and mINP of 47.50% on CUHK-PEDES at 50% noise, and shows minimal performance gap between best and last checkpoints (71.25% vs. 71.25%), indicating effective overfitting prevention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDE outperforms all ordinary baselines (SSAN, IVT, CFine, IRRA) across all three datasets and all noise levels. On clean data, RDE surpasses IRRA (the strongest ordinary baseline) by +2.56% R-1 on CUHK-PEDES, +4.22% on ICFG-PEDES, and +5.15% on RSTPReID. Under 50% noise, RDE leads the best robust baseline DECL by +6.03% R-1 on CUHK-PEDES (71.25% vs. 65.22%) and by +6.26% on ICFG-PEDES (63.76% vs. 57.50%), while matching DECL on RSTPReID (62.85% vs. 62.85%). Compared to the CLIP-C baseline (fine-tuned on clean pairs only), RDE gains +6.03% on CUHK-PEDES and +6.26% on ICFG-PEDES at 50% noise. Notably, RDE's last-checkpoint performance at 50% noise (71.25%) nearly matches its best checkpoint, whereas DECL drops from 65.22% (Best) to 65.09% (Last) and IRRA drops from 64.02% to 42.79%, confirming RDE's superior resistance to overfitting under noisy correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IRRA (CVPR 2023) for its CLIP-based cross-modal embedding architecture and tokenization strategy, and on the Triplet Ranking Loss from VSE++ (2017) as the baseline loss it relaxes. The Confident Consensus Division module adapts the GMM-based clean/noisy data separation from Huang et al. (NeurIPS 2021) and DivideMix (2020), but extends it to a dual-grained consensus mechanism using two embedding modules (BGE and TSE) rather than a single model. The key departure from prior TIReID work is the explicit treatment of noisy correspondence (false positive pairs) as a first-class problem, whereas all existing methods implicitly assume perfectly aligned training pairs. The Triplet Alignment Loss is a novel log-sum-exp relaxation of TRL that interpolates between hardest-negative mining and all-negative consideration, positioned between TRL and its summation variant TRL-S.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the standard Triplet Ranking Loss catastrophically collapses under 50% noisy correspondence (3.64% Rank-1), a failure mode that practitioners deploying TIReID on real-world surveillance data would encounter but that prior literature has not addressed. The gradient analysis explaining why hardest-negative mining destabilizes training under noise provides a useful conceptual tool for designing robust losses in other cross-modal retrieval settings. Open questions include whether the GMM-based division generalizes to non-bimodal loss distributions, whether the approach scales to higher noise rates beyond 50%, and whether the dual-embedding consensus mechanism is necessary or whether a single well-designed loss suffices. The method's reliance on CLIP as a frozen backbone also limits its applicability to settings where modality-specific fine-tuning is required.
