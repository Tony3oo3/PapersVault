## Problem

Domain adaptation for point cloud semantic segmentation is hampered by a context gap: LiDAR scans from different sensors and environments exhibit varying geometric structures (density, beam angles, dropout noise), causing distribution shifts in local geometric context that previous input-transformation-based methods (noise rendering, scan mixing) fail to address at the spatial level. Without explicit context modeling, these methods improve adaptation only in regions with mild geometric shifts and degrade in distant or sparser regions where the context gap is most severe.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (x, y, z, intensity) that are first projected via spherical projection into 2D range-view images of resolution H×W with 5 channels (range, intensity, x, y, z). The proposed CCL module replaces a standard 3×3 convolution in the first encoder block of a projection-based backbone (SqueezeSegV3-21, SalsaNext, or MinkowskiNet), operating on the projected grid where k-NN neighbor acquisition is done efficiently via im2col. The output is a per-point semantic class assignment over 17 classes (SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI) or 13 classes (SynLiDAR→nuScenes), evaluated by mIoU.

## Method

Cooperative Context Learning (CCL) combines two complementary mechanisms in a cooperative loop. First, Context Embedding ('Construct') models local geometric context by feeding relative distances between a point and its k=8 nearest neighbors into a two-layer MLP to produce learnable aggregation weights, then zeroing out weights for dropout-noise pixels via a binary mask before concatenating weighted neighbor features. Second, Context Attention ('Associate') introduces n_con=8 learnable prototypes shared across both domains that attend to the context embeddings via dot-product attention (prototypes serve as Key and Value, embeddings as Query), establishing long-range cross-region and cross-domain dependencies. The key insight is the cooperative synergy: the global attention forces prototypes to retain only domain-invariant context patterns, and this signal back-propagates to attune the local MLP-encoded weights toward domain-invariant geometric relationships. The entire module adds negligible FLOPS (−0.5G on SqueezeSegV3) and parameters (−0.1M) relative to the replaced convolution, and is trained with standard cross-entropy plus Lovász-Softmax losses alongside the host method's domain alignment objective.

## Results

On SynLiDAR → SemKITTI (SqueezeSegV3-21 backbone), the best configuration SqzV1+CCL achieves 28.2% mIoU, outperforming the strongest 3D baseline SqzV1 (25.0%) by +3.2% and the best 2D method AdaptSeg (20.6%) by +7.6%. On SynLiDAR → nuScenes (SalsaNext backbone), SqzV2+CCL reaches 18.3% mIoU, a +5.1% gain over SqzV2 alone (13.2%). On the MinkowskiNet backbone (SynLiDAR → SemanticKITTI), CoSMix+CCL attains 34.5% mIoU versus 32.2% for CoSMix and 30.2% for SALUDA. The method adds negligible overhead, reducing FLOPS by 0.5G and parameters by 0.1M relative to the backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL is integrated as a plug-in module into 11 existing domain adaptation methods across both 2D and 3D paradigms, and consistently improves every baseline. Against 2D methods (CBST, AdaptSeg, CCM, PLCA, MMD), CCL yields gains of +2.5 to +5.1 mIoU, with the largest improvement over AdaptSeg (+5.1 on nuScenes) and PLCA (+4.2 on SemKITTI). Against 3D methods, SqzV1+CCL achieves the overall best result (28.2% mIoU on SemKITTI), beating SqzV1 by +3.2%, CoSMix by +5.8%, and ASM by +5.4%. On the harder SynLiDAR → nuScenes transfer (larger context gap due to different beam counts and FOV), SqzV2+CCL and AdaptSeg+CCL both gain +5.1 mIoU, while MMD+CCL gains only +1.0%, suggesting CCL is most beneficial when the geometric context shift is severe. On the MinkowskiNet backbone, CoSMix+CCL (34.5%) surpasses both CoSMix (32.2%) and the recent SALUDA (30.2%) by +2.3% and +4.3% respectively. The paper also shows that 2D-only methods (without CCL) provide only marginal gains over source-only, confirming that appearance-based alignment does not transfer well to the 3D geometric context setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two lines of prior work: (1) 2D domain adaptive segmentation methods (AdaptSeg, PLCA, CCM) that model context on appearance features and are shown to transfer poorly to point clouds, and (2) 3D domain adaptation methods (SqueezeSegV1/V2, CoSMix, ASM, ePointDA) that apply input-level transformations (noise rendering, semantic mixing, adversarial noise injection) treating each scene as a whole without explicit spatial context modeling. The paper repurposes the attention/prototype mechanism from Transformer architectures and the relative-distance encoding from KPConv/Point Transformer, but combines them in a cooperative local-global scheme specifically targeting geometric context variation (density, beam angle, sparsity) across LiDAR domains — a gap neither line of work addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies geometric context discrepancy (not just noise or appearance) as a distinct and under-addressed source of domain gap in LiDAR segmentation, supported by the range-based analysis showing prior methods fail at distant regions. The plug-and-play nature of the module (replacing a single 3×3 conv with negligible overhead) makes it broadly applicable across backbones and adaptation paradigms. However, all experiments are synthetic-to-real (SynLiDAR→SemKITTI/nuScenes), leaving open whether the context-gap framing and the prototype mechanism generalize to real-to-real adaptation or to more severe sensor differences. The modest per-baseline gains (1–5% mIoU) suggest the context gap, while real, is one of several contributing factors rather than the dominant one.
