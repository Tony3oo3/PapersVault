## Problem

No publicly available real-world benchmark exists for instance segmentation of wide-ranging, thin filamentous, and tightly interweaving objects, a setting that is central to light microscopy neuron segmentation but absent from both natural-image and EM connectomics benchmarks. Methods designed to capture long-range data dependencies (structured state space models, continuous CNNs) have only been validated on synthetic data, leaving their applicability to real-world thin-structure segmentation unassessed.

## Input / Output

Input consists of 3D multicolor confocal microscopy volumes (MCFO) of Drosophila melanogaster nervous systems at 0.44 μm isotropic resolution, averaging ~400×700×700 pixels across three color channels. Output is pixel-wise instance segmentation masks assigning a unique label per neuron, with the unusual property that overlapping instances can share pixels (a single pixel may carry multiple labels). The dataset contains 101 images (30 fully labeled with 139 neurons, 71 partly labeled with 451 neurons), totaling ~600 expert-annotated neuron masks, with train/validation/test splits of 61/17/23 images.

## Method

This is a dataset and benchmark paper whose core technical contribution is a purpose-built evaluation framework rather than a new segmentation architecture. The framework replaces pixel-level IoU with clDice (centerline Dice via medial-axis skeletonization) for instance localization, computes an instance-level F1 score (avF1) over thresholds 0.1–0.9, and introduces an average ground truth coverage metric (C) using one-to-many matching to penalize false merges and false negatives; the two are combined into an aggregate score S = 0.5·avF1 + 0.5·C. A greedy many-to-many matching algorithm (Algorithm 1) handles overlapping instances to compute false split and false merge error counts. Three baselines are evaluated—PatchPerPix, Flood Filling Networks (trained on both fully and partly labeled data), and Duan et al.'s non-learnt color clustering—all achieving low avF1 scores (max 0.34), confirming that extensive further method development is needed for this task.

## Results

On the full FISBe test set, PatchPerPix achieves the highest aggregate benchmark score S = 0.35 ± 0.00 and avF1 = 0.34 ± 0.01, followed by Duan et al.'s color clustering (S = 0.30, avF1 = 0.27) and FFN+partly (S = 0.27 ± 0.01, avF1 = 0.24 ± 0.02). All methods score well below 0.5 on S, confirming the extreme difficulty of instance segmentation for thin, interweaving filamentous structures. Training FFN on the full dataset (including partly labeled data) improves its test-set S from 0.25 to 0.27, but the gap to PatchPerPix remains substantial.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchPerPix [39] achieves the best aggregate score S and avF1 on both validation (S=0.38, avF1=0.41) and test (S=0.35, avF1=0.34) sets, outperforming FFN [26] by 0.10–0.13 in S and Duan et al. [13] by 0.05–0.14. However, PatchPerPix suffers the most false merges (FM=52 on test), indicating it frequently joins touching neurons of different colors. Duan et al.'s color clustering [13] produces the fewest false merges (FM=29 on test) but the most false splits (FS=45 on test), meaning it over-segments individual neurons. FFN [26] trained on the full dataset (FFN+partly) improves over FFN trained only on completely labeled data (test S: 0.27 vs. 0.25), confirming the benefit of partly labeled data for one-versus-all training. All three baselines achieve clDice_TP around 0.75–0.80, showing that when a neuron is correctly identified, its centerline is reasonably well reconstructed, but the primary failure mode is incorrect instance separation (merges and splits) rather than poor per-neuron tracing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying that no prior benchmark exists for instance segmentation of the specific combination of properties found in 3D light microscopy neuron data: very thin (single-pixel-width) filamentous structures, wide spatial extent, and tight interweaving of multiple instances. It departs from EM-based instance segmentation benchmarks (CREMI, AxonEM) which feature dense foreground but not thin filamentous shapes, and from semantic segmentation benchmarks for thin structures (DIADEM, BigNeuron, REtA) which do not address instance-level separation. The closest prior resource (Winnubst et al., 2019) lacks tightly interweaved objects and pixel-wise ground truth. The baselines (PatchPerPix, Flood Filling Networks, color clustering) are repurposed from other domains rather than designed for this specific challenge, and the paper explicitly notes that most proposal-free instance segmentation methods are architecturally unsuitable for thin, long-range objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FISBe fills a clear gap by providing the first public, pixel-wise annotated benchmark for instance segmentation of long-range thin filamentous intertwined objects in real 3D data, enabling the field to move beyond synthetic benchmarks for long-range dependency modeling. The carefully designed metrics (clDice-based F1 combined with ground truth coverage) address the well-known inadequacy of standard IoU/mAP for thin structures. The baselines confirm that current methods are far from solving this task (best aggregate score ~0.38), motivating future work on structured state space models, continuous CNNs, and self-supervised pre-training on the large non-annotated FlyLight resource. A key limitation is the dataset's bias toward sparser expression densities, which limits generalization to denser samples.
