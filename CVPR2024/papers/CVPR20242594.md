## Problem

Existing person re-identification methods are designed for homogeneous camera networks (ground-only or aerial-only) and fail under the dramatic view discrepancy of aerial-ground matching, where identity features suffer from low intra-identity and high inter-identity similarity. This view-heterogeneous scenario is far more practical in real-world surveillance but has received minimal research attention, with only one prior dataset (AG-ReID) and one prior method relying on attribute priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242594/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB person images from either aerial or ground cameras with extreme resolution variation (22×7 to 1009×539 pixels), which are split into non-overlapping patches and tokenized via 1×1 convolution. Two additional learnable tokens (meta and view) are prepended/appended to the patch sequence before positional encoding. At inference, only the final meta token serves as the identity embedding for retrieval; the view token is discarded, keeping the retrieval interface identical to standard ViT-based ReID.

## Method

VDT extends ViT-Base by appending a meta token (global representation) and a view token (view-related features) to the patch token sequence, then stacking N VDT blocks where each block performs standard self-attention followed by a subtractive operation that removes the updated view token from the updated meta token (Eq. 4), hierarchically stripping view-related information layer by layer. An orthogonal loss (Eq. 9) further constrains the final meta and view tokens to be mutually independent, completing the decoupling. Training combines cross-entropy and triplet losses on the meta token for identity, cross-entropy on the view token for view classification, and the orthogonal loss, all balanced by a single hyperparameter λ. The added operations contribute only constant complexity relative to the baseline ViT, preserving the O(4Td² + 2T²d) scaling.

## Results

On the newly introduced CARGO dataset (5,000 IDs, 108,563 images, 13 cameras), VDT achieves 64.10% Rank1 / 55.20% mAP / 41.13% mINP under the ALL protocol, surpassing the ViT-Base backbone by 2.56%/1.66%/1.51% and the strongest CNN baseline AGW by 3.84%/1.76%/0.91%. On the most challenging aerial-ground (A↔G) protocol, VDT reaches 48.12% Rank1 / 42.76% mAP, a 4.99% mAP gain over ViT. On the real-world AG-ReID dataset, VDT attains 82.91% Rank1 / 74.44% mAP (A→G) and 86.59% Rank1 / 78.57% mAP (G→A), outperforming the previous AGPReID method Explain by up to 3.74% mAP and 5.18% Rank1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDT outperforms all nine baselines across both datasets and all protocols. Against the same-backbone ViT-Base, VDT gains 2.56% Rank1 and 1.66% mAP on CARGO-ALL, with the largest margin on the heterogeneous A↔G protocol (+4.99% mAP, +4.99% Rank1), confirming that view decoupling specifically targets the aerial-ground gap. Against the strongest CNN baseline AGW, VDT improves by 3.84% Rank1 and 1.76% mAP on CARGO-ALL. On AG-ReID, VDT surpasses the previous dedicated AGPReID method Explain [22] by 1.44% Rank1 / 1.83% mAP (A→G) and 3.74% mAP / 5.18% Rank1 (G→A), while requiring no attribute priors that Explain depends on. Among CNN baselines, MGN and BoT show the weakest A↔G performance (31.87% and 36.25% Rank1 respectively), highlighting that view-homogeneous methods degrade severely under heterogeneous matching. VV (a vehicle ReID baseline) performs worst overall on CARGO-ALL (45.83% Rank1), indicating that domain transfer from vehicle ReID is ineffective for person ReID under view discrepancy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established person ReID task from homogeneous camera networks (ground-only or aerial-only) to the heterogeneous aerial-ground setting, a scenario the authors argue is more representative of real-world surveillance deployments. Conceptually, it builds on the ViT-Base architecture by repurposing the token mechanism: a dedicated 'view token' is introduced alongside the standard meta token, and a layer-wise subtraction operation (Eq. 4) hierarchically strips view-related information from the global representation. The only direct prior work is Nguyen et al. [22], which introduced the AG-ReID dataset and an attribute-guided explainable model; VDT departs from this by requiring no attribute priors and instead learning view decoupling end-to-end through an orthogonal loss. The CARGO dataset extends the synthetic ReID dataset lineage (PersonX, UnrealPerson, WePerson) to the aerial-ground domain for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CARGO dataset (5,000 IDs, 108,563 images, 13 cameras) is a substantial contribution that brings AGPReID to a scale comparable to established homogeneous benchmarks, enabling more rigorous evaluation of view-heterogeneous methods. The view-decoupling mechanism is simple, adds negligible computational overhead, and the ablation confirms both the subtractive separation and orthogonal loss are necessary. However, the dataset is entirely synthetic (Unity3D + MakeHuman), raising questions about domain transfer to real aerial-ground footage, and the authors acknowledge that view discrepancy is not the only source of disruption—illumination, occlusion, and resolution variations remain underexplored. A key open question is whether the learned view-unrelated features generalize across different camera geometries beyond the two-class aerial/ground split.
