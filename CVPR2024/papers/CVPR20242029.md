## Problem

Creating high-quality SVBRDF materials remains a time-consuming, expertise-heavy task in computer graphics. Prior generative approaches (GANs like MaterialGAN, TileGen) suffer from training instability, mode collapse, and limited controllability, while recent diffusion-based methods lack a unified framework for combining multiple diverse conditioning sources. No existing method supports fine-grained, map-level editing of individual reflectance properties within a single material.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242029/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MatFuse accepts up to four conditioning modalities simultaneously: text prompts and image prompts (embedded via a pre-trained CLIP model), a color palette (top-5 colors extracted via CIE76 clustering, projected through an MLP), and a pattern sketch (hand-drawn or Canny-edge-derived, projected to latent resolution by a small convolutional network). The output is a set of four 256×256 or 512×512 SVBRDF maps (diffuse, normal, roughness, specular) whose latent representations are stored in a shared concatenated channel space, enabling per-map manipulation. For editing, the user specifies a volumetric binary mask over both spatial and channel dimensions to selectively regenerate individual maps or spatial regions.

## Method

MatFuse replaces the single-encoder VQ-GAN of standard LDMs with a multi-encoder architecture: four separate encoders (one per SVBRDF map) produce disentangled latent codes that are quantized with per-map codebooks and concatenated along the channel dimension before a shared decoder reconstructs all maps. A rendering loss (MSE between ground-truth and predicted renders) is added to the VQ-GAN training objective to enforce inter-map physical consistency. The diffusion model is a U-Net with self-attention operating on the concatenated latent, trained with a standard denoising objective. Global conditions (text, image, palette) are injected via multi-head cross-attention at each denoising step, while local conditions (sketches) are concatenated to the noisy latent; classifier-free guidance is extended to support arbitrary combinations by randomly dropping each condition with 50% probability and all conditions with 10% probability. The multi-encoder latent structure enables 'volumetric inpainting,' where masking specific channels and spatial regions forces the model to regenerate only selected maps or portions thereof, providing unprecedented map-level editing control.

## Results

On the SVBRDF dataset, MatFuse achieves a CLIP-IQA score of 0.431±0.151 and FID of 158.53 on 2,000 unconditionally generated 512×512 material renders, approaching the ground-truth CLIP-IQA upper bound of 0.471±0.191. MatFuse substantially outperforms the vanilla LDM baseline (CLIP-IQA 0.269, FID 231.64) and achieves a lower FID than TileGen (158.53 vs. 184.81), indicating greater sample diversity. In a user study with 100 participants, MatFuse received 1,078 preference votes versus 949 for TileGen (χ²=16.41, p<0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatFuse is compared against two baselines: TileGen [54], a class-specific GAN-based material generator, and a vanilla LDM (Stable Diffusion) trained unconditionally on materials. Against the LDM baseline, MatFuse wins decisively on both metrics, improving CLIP-IQA from 0.269 to 0.431 and reducing FID from 231.64 to 158.53, demonstrating the benefit of the multi-encoder architecture and multimodal conditioning. Against TileGen, MatFuse nearly matches on CLIP-IQA (0.431 vs. 0.433) but achieves a notably lower FID (158.53 vs. 184.81), which the authors attribute to TileGen's limited sample diversity from class-specific training. In the user study, MatFuse also receives a statistically significant preference over TileGen (1,078 vs. 949 votes, p<0.05), despite not being specifically trained per semantic class. Overall, MatFuse matches or exceeds TileGen on all reported metrics while offering substantially greater controllability through multimodal conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatFuse extends the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) framework of Rombach et al. to the domain of SVBRDF material synthesis, replacing the single VQ-GAN encoder with a multi-encoder architecture that learns disentangled latent spaces per reflectance map (diffuse, normal, roughness, specular). It adopts the compositionality paradigm from Composer to combine multiple conditioning modalities (text, image, color palette, sketch) via an extended classifier-free guidance strategy, and introduces a rendering loss into the VQ-GAN training to enforce inter-map coherence. This departs from prior GAN-based material generators (MaterialGAN, TileGen) that offered limited controllability and suffered from training instability, and from Text2Mat which supported only text conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MatFuse provides a unified, multi-conditional pipeline for material generation and editing that is practically relevant to game development, architectural visualization, and industrial prototyping, where artists need fine-grained control over reflectance properties. The volumetric inpainting mechanism—enabled by the multi-encoder's disentangled latent structure—opens a new avenue for map-level material editing that was not accessible with single-encoder approaches. Key limitations remain: generation is capped at 512×512 (~18 GB GPU memory), outputs lack tileability, and the method has not been validated on real-world (non-synthetic) materials. Open questions include scaling to higher resolutions via patch-based approaches, enforcing tileability, and extending local conditioning (e.g., via ControlNet) for single-image SVBRDF estimation.
