## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as LoRA and adapters are designed for single-task adaptation, requiring separate model executions per task and thus scaling linearly in training and inference cost with the number of tasks. Parameter-efficient training for multi-task learning (MTL) architectures—where a single shared backbone serves multiple downstream tasks simultaneously—remains largely unexplored, and the conflicting-gradient problem in MTL becomes more pronounced under the constrained parameter budgets of low-rank adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and, in one forward pass through the shared Swin Transformer backbone, simultaneously produces four dense prediction outputs: a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask, a human part segmentation mask, a surface normal map, and a saliency map. Unlike single-task PEFT baselines that require a separate inference pass per task, MTLoRA executes all tasks in a single inference path because only the lightweight task-specific decoders (linear + bilinear upsampling, ~6% of total parameters) branch per task. Evaluation is on PASCAL-Context (4,998 train / 5,105 val images) using mIoU for segmentation tasks and RMSE of predicted angles for normals.

## Method

MTLoRA inserts two complementary types of low-rank decomposition modules into a hierarchical Swin Transformer backbone. Task-Agnostic LoRA (TA-LoRA) modules—shared across all tasks—are placed in the QKV, projection, and MLP layers of every transformer block except the last in each stage, learning generalized features that promote positive cross-task knowledge sharing. Task-Specific LoRA (TS-LoRA) modules—separate rank-r matrices per task—are placed at the final block of each stage; because each TS-LoRA matrix is connected only to its corresponding task's decoder in the computation graph, backpropagation updates it solely with that task's loss, effectively disentangling the parameter space and mitigating gradient conflicts. A lighter variant, MTLoRA+, replaces full unfreezing of the patch-merging layers with additional low-rank modules, further reducing trainable parameters. The multi-scale task-specific features produced at each stage are combined by learnable residual fusion blocks before feeding into the task-specific decoders, enabling per-task specialization at every receptive field scale.

## Results

On the PASCAL-Context dataset with a Swin-Tiny backbone, MTLoRA (r=64) achieves an overall MTL accuracy improvement of +2.55% (Δm) over single-task baselines, surpassing full fine-tuning of the MTL model (+2.23%) while training only 8.34M parameters versus 30.06M (a 3.6× reduction). MTLoRA (r=32) achieves +2.16% Δm with just 6.08M trainable parameters, and MTLoRA+ (r=16) reaches +1.19% Δm with only 4.29M parameters. Per-task, MTLoRA (r=64) attains 67.9 mIoU on semantic segmentation, 59.84 mIoU on human parts, 65.40 mIoU on saliency, and 16.60 rmse on surface normals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTLoRA (r=64) outperforms MTL-Full Fine Tuning on overall Δm (+2.55% vs. +2.23%) while training 3.6× fewer parameters (8.34M vs. 30.06M), and also beats it on surface normals (16.60 vs. 16.64 rmse) and saliency (65.40 vs. 65.21 mIoU). Against the closest multi-task PEFT baseline, Polyhistor [21], MTLoRA (r=64) achieves a higher Δm (+2.55% vs. +2.34%) with fewer trainable parameters (8.34M vs. 8.96M), and MTLoRA (r=32) achieves a comparable Δm (+2.16%) with 32% fewer parameters (6.08M vs. 8.96M). HyperFormer [23] achieves a slightly higher Δm (+2.64%) but requires 8.7× more trainable parameters (72.77M). All single-task PEFT baselines (Adapter, Bitfit, VPT, Compacter, LoRA, VL-Adapter) show negative Δm values (ranging from −1.83% to −11.18%), indicating they degrade multi-task performance relative to single-task models, whereas MTLoRA consistently achieves positive Δm. A critical practical advantage is that MTLoRA supports single-inference execution for all tasks simultaneously, unlike all compared baselines which require separate forward passes per task.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTLoRA extends the LoRA low-rank decomposition mechanism (Hu et al., 2021) from single-task adaptation to the multi-task learning setting, introducing a dual-module design that splits the parameter space into Task-Agnostic (TA-LoRA) and Task-Specific (TS-LoRA) components. It departs from prior multi-task PEFT approaches like HyperFormer and Polyhistor, which still require separate execution paths per task, by enabling a single shared backbone to serve all tasks in one forward pass. The placement of low-rank modules at patch-merging layers (MTLoRA+) and the use of TS-LoRA at stage boundaries for multi-scale task-specific feature sharing represent novel architectural choices not explored in prior PEFT or MTL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap by being the first to address parameter-efficient training within true MTL architectures (shared backbone, single inference path), which is critical for latency-sensitive multi-task deployment. The finding that a carefully structured low-rank adaptation can outperform full fine-tuning while training 3.6× fewer parameters suggests that the conflicting-gradient problem in MTL can be mitigated through parameter-space disentanglement rather than gradient surgery. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (PASCAL-Context), one backbone family (Swin Transformer), and four dense prediction tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more tasks, different architectures, or non-dense tasks like detection or classification.
