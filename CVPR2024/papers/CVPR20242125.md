## Problem

Existing neural active reconstruction systems are constrained by the slow convergence of NeRF-based representations and restrict agent motion to 2D planes, hemispheres, or discrete teleportation points, preventing full 3D exploration. Additionally, prior uncertainty quantification methods either rely on implicit MLPs that produce unstable uncertainty estimates in unobserved regions or ignore depth information entirely, limiting their utility for guiding active exploration in large-scale indoor environments.

## Input / Output

The system consumes posed RGB-D images at 680×1200 resolution (60° vertical, 90° horizontal FOV) streamed from a Habitat simulator, with the agent initialized at a random traversable position. It produces a continuous SDF-based surface reconstruction, a 3D uncertainty volume (10 cm voxel resolution) encoding per-vertex geometric uncertainty, and a sequence of planned camera actions (translations ≤10 cm, rotations ≤10°) that guide the agent through the scene over 2000–5000 steps depending on scene scale.

## Method

NARUTO builds on Co-SLAM's hybrid representation (multi-resolution hash grid + one-blob coordinate encoding + SDF/color decoders) for real-time mapping, but introduces three key innovations. First, an explicit learnable uncertainty volume V_σ is optimized jointly with geometry via a heteroscedastic depth loss (Eq. 7), ensuring only observed regions update their uncertainty while unobserved regions retain high initial values—avoiding the instability of implicit uncertainty MLPs. Second, an uncertainty aggregation strategy for goal searching accumulates the top-300 uncertain points visible from each candidate goal location (within a 0.5–2 m optimal range, verified via SDF raycasting) to select the observation pose that maximally reduces map uncertainty. Third, an active ray sampling module replaces a subset of random rays in bundle adjustment with the highest-uncertainty rays, improving reconstruction consistency as a plug-and-play enhancement. Path planning uses an efficient RRT variant that simultaneously grows toward random samples and the goal, with reachability filtering to exclude unreachable vertices from the uncertainty aggregation.

## Results

On Matterport3D (MP3D), NARUTO achieves a completion ratio of 90.18%, substantially surpassing the previous best (ANM at 73.15%) and other baselines (FBE 71.18%, OccAnt 71.72%, UPEN 69.06%). The method also reduces mean absolute distance (MAD) to 1.44 cm (vs. 4.29 cm for ANM) and completion distance to 3.00 cm (vs. 9.11 cm for ANM). On Replica, the full system reaches 97.20% completion ratio with 1.61 cm accuracy, outperforming Co-SLAM (92.74%) and NICE-SLAM (91.13%) in the active mapping setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NARUTO decisively outperforms all compared baselines across every reported metric. On MP3D, it improves completion ratio by 17 percentage points over ANM (90.18% vs. 73.15%), the closest neural implicit mapping baseline, and by over 20 points over UPEN (69.06%) and OccAnt (71.72%), which rely on reinforcement-learning planners restricted to 2D motion. On Replica, in the active neural mapping setting, NARUTO (97.20% completion ratio, 1.61 cm accuracy) surpasses Co-SLAM (92.74%, 2.30 cm) and NICE-SLAM (91.13%, 2.37 cm), which are passive neural SLAM systems without active planning. The MAD metric (1.44 cm on MP3D) is roughly 3× lower than ANM's 4.29 cm, indicating substantially more precise SDF estimation. The gains are attributed to the combination of 6DoF unrestricted exploration, uncertainty-aware goal searching, and active ray sampling, none of which are present in the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

NARUTO builds directly on Co-SLAM's hybrid neural representation (multi-resolution hash grid with oneblob encoding) as its mapping backbone, extending it with an uncertainty learning module and an active planning layer. It departs from prior neural active reconstruction work (ActiveNeRF, NeuRAR, ActiveRMAP, ANM) by removing the degree-of-freedom and spatial constraints that limited those systems to hemispheres, 2D planes, or object-centric settings, enabling full 6DoF exploration in large-scale indoor scenes. A key conceptual departure is the use of an explicit learnable uncertainty volume rather than an implicit uncertainty MLP, motivated by the observation that implicit representations produce unstable, uncorrelated uncertainty in unobserved regions. The active ray sampling module is positioned as a plug-and-play enhancement applicable to existing neural SLAM systems beyond NARUTO itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NARUTO establishes a practical template for uncertainty-driven active reconstruction at scale, with the active ray sampling strategy offering immediate utility to the broader neural SLAM community as a drop-in stability improvement. The 17-point completeness gain over the closest baseline (ANM) on MP3D is meaningful, though the comparison set includes several older or less directly comparable methods. Key limitations remain: the system assumes perfect localization and action execution, operates only in simulation, and uses a single-resolution uncertainty grid focused on completeness rather than fidelity. Open questions include whether the uncertainty-aware planning generalizes to real-world sensor noise, whether multi-resolution uncertainty can capture both completeness and fine geometric detail, and how the approach scales to outdoor or very large-scale environments.
