## Problem

Existing video understanding tasks address action evaluation from only one perspective: action quality assessment outputs a single score, while video captioning produces superficial descriptions lacking professional rigor. No prior work generates detailed natural-language commentary that simultaneously provides accurate scores, fine-grained action labels, and qualitative assessments. The core challenge is that evaluation information (scores, actions) demands high precision but occupies only a small fraction of generated text, directly conflicting with the diversity that language models naturally pursue.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single video clip as input and autoregressively generates a narrative evaluation paragraph that weaves together action descriptions, a numerical score, and qualitative judgments (e.g., 'flawless entry,' 'impressive control'). The generated text is constrained to embed specific professional tokens—predicted score values and fine-grained action category labels—within otherwise free-form commentary. Auxiliary heads also output a continuous score regression and per-part action classification, but the primary evaluation metric (NAE mAP) is computed on information extracted from the generated text itself.

## Method

The Prompt-Guided Multimodal Interaction framework replaces parallel multi-task branches with two sequential cross-attention interactions that couple score prediction and text generation. First, Context-Aware Prompt Learning refines K-class learnable prompt embeddings (each encoding a score interval in textual form) using video features as key/value, fusing language and video modalities. Second, Score-Guided Tokens Learning symmetrically lets video embeddings attend to the refined prompts, reformulating score regression as a video-text matching classification over score intervals supervised by cross-entropy loss. A Multimodal-Aware Text Generator then decodes narrative text from concatenated word tokens, a learnable template (containing predicted score and action tokens interleaved with learnable tokens), and score-aware video embeddings, guided by a Tri-Token Attention Mask that forces the decoder to attend to professional evaluation tokens and video tokens while a sparse mask regularizes video-to-video attention.

## Results

On MTL-NAE, the proposed method achieves a NAE mAP of 0.383, Spearman's ρ of 0.943, R-ℓ₂ of 0.340, CIDEr of 20.5, and Action Acc of 0.97, outperforming the strongest baseline SwinBert (mAP 0.261, ρ 0.881, R-ℓ₂ 0.706, CIDEr 16.2, Acc 0.92) by 46.7% in mAP. On FineGym-NAE, the method reaches mAP 0.162, ρ 0.749, R-ℓ₂ 1.55, CIDEr 20.7, and Acc 0.93, a 37.3% mAP improvement over SwinBert (mAP 0.118). The method also surpasses all single-video AQA baselines (USDL, MUSDL, C3D-LSTM) in score prediction accuracy, achieving ρ 0.943 and R-ℓ₂ 0.340 on MTL-NAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on both datasets. The closest competitor is SwinBert [14], a video captioning model with sparse attention, which the proposed method surpasses by 46.7% in NAE mAP on MTL-NAE (0.383 vs. 0.261) and 37.3% on FineGym-NAE (0.162 vs. 0.118), while also improving AQA ρ by 0.062 and reducing R-ℓ₂ by 51.8% on MTL-NAE. The multi-task learning baselines C3D-AVG and MSCADC [23], which train parallel task branches without cross-modal interaction, show the weakest NAE performance (mAP 0.157 and 0.074 on MTL-NAE), confirming that naive parallel multi-task learning fails to transfer evaluation accuracy into generated text. Among video captioning baselines, VLTinT [39] and VLCap [38] achieve moderate mAP (0.214 and 0.197) but lag significantly in AQA metrics (ρ 0.868 and 0.851 vs. 0.943), reflecting their inability to embed accurate score information. For AQA-specific comparison (Table 7), the method's single-video score prediction (ρ 0.943, R-ℓ₂ 0.340 on MTL-NAE) exceeds USDL [33] (ρ 0.923, R-ℓ₂ 0.468) and C3D-LSTM [24] (ρ 0.849), and approaches multi-video methods CoRe [40] (ρ 0.951) and TPT [2] (ρ 0.961) that require additional exemplar videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lines of work: the SwinBERT architecture (Video Swin Transformer + Transformer decoder with sparse attention) for video captioning, and the multi-task AQA paradigm of Parmar & Morris (CVPR 2019) that jointly trains score regression, captioning, and action classification with independent branches. The key departure is replacing the parallel-branch multi-task design with a prompt-guided multimodal interaction framework that uses cross-attention to couple the score prediction and text generation tasks, arguing that independent branches fail to share information across modalities. Additionally, the paper re-annotates MTL-AQA and FineGym using ChatGPT to produce higher-quality narrative evaluations, addressing the noisy, colloquial text labels in the original datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NAE task formulation bridges a genuine gap between single-score AQA and superficial video captioning, offering a more realistic target for AI commentators and fitness coaches. The re-annotated MTL-NAE and FineGym-NAE datasets provide a useful benchmark for the community, though their reliance on ChatGPT for annotation introduces potential quality concerns that are only partially mitigated by human verification. Open questions include whether the prompt-guided interaction framework generalizes beyond sports actions to other domains (e.g., healthcare, dance), whether the score-interval discretization strategy scales to continuous scoring in other contexts, and whether the narrow evaluation metric (mAP across threshold combinations) captures the full quality of generated narratives.
