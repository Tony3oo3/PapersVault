## Problem

Contrastively trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) are highly vulnerable to backdooring and poisoning attacks, where even a tiny fraction of malicious training samples (as few as 3 out of 3 million) can cause the model to associate spurious visual signals with attacker-chosen concepts. While prior defenses address either backdooring (CleanCLIP) or supervised settings (ABL), no unified defense exists that is effective against both backdooring and non-backdoored poisoning attacks on contrastive VL models.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by a ViT-B/16 encoder) and text captions (processed by a BERT-style encoder, max sequence length 100) as primary inputs, and additionally consumes a set of up to 25 Knowledge Elements (KEs) per caption generated offline by prompting the Vicuna LLM. At inference time, the model produces standard image-text similarity scores for retrieval (Hit@k, Recall@k) with no architectural changes or additional inputs required. The KEs serve purely as a train-time supervision signal and are discarded at test time.

## Method

Semantic Shield introduces three train-time losses that leverage externally extracted Knowledge Elements (KEs) to prevent the model from learning spurious attack-to-label correlations. First, a multi-instance patch-KE alignment loss (L_KE) uses max/min similarity across patches and KEs to learn which image regions correspond to which semantic concepts, without requiring explicit patch-KE correspondence. Second, a KE-guided attention loss (L_Attention) modulates the ViT's attention weights so that attention to each patch is proportional to its alignment with the relevant KEs, thereby suppressing attention to attack patches that lack semantic alignment. Third, a dynamic per-sample weighting of the contrastive loss (L_WeightedCL) downweights samples whose overall patch-KE alignment is low, reducing the influence of poisoned samples on the embedding space. The final objective is a linear combination of the weighted contrastive loss and the attention loss, requiring no changes at inference time.

## Results

On the backdoor defense task (Table 1), Semantic Shield (Weighted CL+Attention) reduces attack success to near-zero across all settings: Hit@1 drops from 90.66% (undefended) to 0.9% on COCO Patch, and to 0.0% on all BPP and Wanet attacks for both COCO and Flickr30k. On the poisoning defense task (Table 2), Semantic Shield achieves Hit@1 of 0.04% for single-target dog→boat on COCO (vs. 18.0% undefended) and 0.0% for multi-target train→zebra (vs. 55.32% undefended). Model utility (Recall@10) is largely preserved, with Semantic Shield scoring 74.22% on COCO image retrieval (vs. 75.13% clean) and 61.07% on Flickr30k (vs. 59.68% clean).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semantic Shield (Weighted CL+Attention) decisively outperforms both baselines across all attack types and datasets. Against Anti-Backdoor Learning (ABL) [27], Semantic Shield achieves 0.0% Hit@1 on all BPP and Wanet attacks for both COCO and Flickr30k, whereas ABL still suffers 100% attack success on COCO Wanet and 98–100% on Flickr30k Wanet, indicating ABL is essentially ineffective against imperceptible backdoor triggers. Against CleanCLIP [3], Semantic Shield reduces COCO Patch Hit@1 from 5.35% to 0.9%, COCO BPP Hit@1 from 36.12% to 0.0%, and COCO Wanet Hit@1 from 8.23% to 0.0%; on Flickr30k the gap is even more dramatic (e.g., Patch Hit@1: 2.20% → 0.0%, BPP Hit@1: 12.43% → 0.0%). On the poisoning task (Table 2), Semantic Shield also dominates: for multi-target dog→boat on COCO it achieves Hit@1 of 2.23% versus CleanCLIP's 57.69%, and for train→zebra it reaches 0.0% versus CleanCLIP's 69.49%. The ablation variants (CL+KE, CL+Attention) show that the attention-guided constraint is the dominant contributor, while the weighted contrastive loss provides the final push to near-zero attack success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first unified defense for contrastively trained vision-language models against both backdooring and poisoning attacks, departing from CleanCLIP (which addresses only backdooring) and Anti-Backdoor Learning (which targets supervised object recognition rather than VL models). The core methodological departure is the use of externally sourced semantic knowledge—Knowledge Elements extracted via LLM prompting—to impose fine-grained patch-level alignment constraints and modulate ViT attention, rather than the modality-level realignment or gradient-based sample isolation used by prior defenses. The multi-instance learning formulation for patch-KE alignment and the dynamic per-sample contrastive weighting are novel loss constructions not present in the prior VL defense literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Given the widespread deployment of CLIP-like models in security-critical applications (navigation, healthcare, disinformation detection), a train-time-only defense that requires no inference-time changes and achieves near-complete attack neutralization is practically valuable. However, the approach is limited to natural images with extractable visual KEs and does not address attacks on the LLM itself or on the caption channel. Open questions include whether dynamic, model-state-aware KE generation (rather than static pre-computed KEs) would improve robustness, whether multimodal LLMs could extract more relevant KEs, and whether the defense generalizes to larger architectures, more diverse datasets, or more sophisticated adaptive attacks.
