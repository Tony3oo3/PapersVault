## Problem

Existing self-training-based test-time adaptation methods suffer from three key limitations: complex test-time augmentations degrade performance on class-dense or fine-grained tasks, pseudo-label correction underutilizes the model's intermediate latent representations, and iterative adaptation leads to error accumulation and catastrophic forgetting. This paper addresses all three by proposing a unified improved self-training framework that works under the fully test-time setting where no source data, features, or statistics are available.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled test images (or a full test set in offline mode) along with a pre-trained source model (CLIP for classification, YOLOv3 for detection, DeepLab-V2 for segmentation), requiring no source data or labels. It maintains a fixed-size memory queue (default 10K) of feature vectors and predictive probabilities from previously seen target samples to support graph-based label correction. Outputs are standard task-specific predictions—class labels, bounding boxes with confidence scores, or per-pixel segmentation masks—produced after the model has been adapted on-the-fly using only the unlabeled target data.

## Method

IST combines three components: (1) SimpAug generates 8 augmented views per sample using only crops, scales, and horizontal flips (preserving position information), then selects the most confident prediction via a Non-Maximum Suppression-style rule rather than averaging; (2) PLCA constructs a KNN graph (K=50) over latent feature vectors in a memory queue and iteratively propagates label information through the normalized adjacency matrix S with a decay factor α, yielding a closed-form corrected label matrix Y* = (1−α)(I−αS)⁻¹Y(0) that refines noisy pseudo-labels by exploiting local cluster structure; (3) PMA maintains a moving average of model parameters θ̂_t = π·θ̂_{t−1} + (1−π)·θ_t to smooth updates and preserve source knowledge against catastrophic forgetting. The method freezes all normalization layers, uses vanilla SGD with a small learning rate, and is applied to CLIP (RN50, RN101, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16) as the foundation model, making it scalable across classification, detection, and segmentation without task-specific modifications.

## Results

On CIFAR-10C (level 5), IST (Online) achieves a mean top-1 error of 10.2%, outperforming the source model (18.3%) and all compared TTA baselines including MEMO (10.9%), TENT (11.5%), and TTT++ Online (13.6%). On ImageNet-C (level 5), IST reduces mean error to 63.4% versus 86.2% for the source and 70.7% for MEMO. For object detection on KITTI under severe weather, IST (Online) reaches 43.2/81.6/52.3 mAP@50 for fog/rain/snow, surpassing the source model (22.8/65.5/25.6) and ActMAD (47.7/81.4/64.5). On CarlaTTA semantic segmentation, IST (Online) achieves 62.4/56.8/73.9/51.5/34.7 mIoU across five scenarios, beating CoTTA (61.4/56.8/70.7/46.4/33.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IST (Online) outperforms all online TTA baselines on CIFAR-10C: it beats MEMO by 0.7% (10.2 vs. 10.9), TENT by 1.3% (10.2 vs. 11.5), SHOT Online by 1.3% (10.2 vs. 11.5), and TTT++ Online by 3.4% (10.2 vs. 13.6). On ImageNet-C, IST achieves 63.4% mean error, surpassing MEMO (70.7%), TENT (67.2%), SHOT Online (69.3%), and TTT (66.3%). For continuous online adaptation (Table 4), IST reduces CIFAR-10C error to 10.4% and ImageNet-C to 65.4%, outperforming CoTTA (11.5% and 67.8%) and MEMO (10.9% and 79.8%). On KITTI detection, IST (Online) beats ActMAD in fog (43.2 vs. 47.7 mAP) and snow (52.3 vs. 64.5 mAP), while ActMAD retains a slight edge in rain (81.4 vs. 81.6 mAP, essentially tied). On CarlaTTA segmentation, IST (Online) exceeds CoTTA by up to 5.1 mIoU (dynamic scenario: 51.5 vs. 46.4) and 0.9 mIoU (clear2rain: 73.9 vs. 70.7), while matching or slightly trailing on clear2fog (56.8 vs. 56.8, tied). The offline variant of IST further improves over all online baselines, reaching 9.6% on CIFAR-10C (matching TTT++ Offline) and 62.5% on ImageNet-C (matching SHOT Offline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-training paradigm for test-time adaptation (SHOT, MEMO, AdaContrast, CoTTA) but departs from prior work in three key ways: it replaces complex augmentations (e.g., AugMix) with a simple crop-scale-flip strategy, substitutes local KNN or global K-means pseudo-label correction with a graph-based label propagation algorithm inspired by Zhou et al., and introduces parameter moving average to stabilize iterative updates. A notable conceptual departure is the use of CLIP as the source model, shifting TTA from task-specific pre-trained networks to a foundation model with zero-shot capability, thereby removing assumptions about label distribution and source data. The method also extends TTA beyond image classification to object detection and semantic segmentation, a multi-task scope not systematically explored in prior TTA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that foundation models like CLIP can be effectively adapted at test time without any source data, which has practical value for deploying pre-trained models in shifting real-world environments. The finding that simple geometric augmentations outperform complex photometric ones for TTA challenges the prevailing assumption that richer augmentation yields better invariance, and the graph-based correction offers a principled middle ground between local and global label propagation. Open questions include the computational cost of maintaining and updating the graph structure at scale, the gap between offline and online performance (suggesting limited learning from single-pass pseudo-labels), and whether the approach generalizes to even larger foundation models or video tasks.
