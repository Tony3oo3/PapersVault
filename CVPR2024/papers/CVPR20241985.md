## Problem

Prior sketch-to-3D generation methods (e.g., LAS-D) faithfully reproduce the geometric inaccuracies of poorly drawn sketches, yielding deformed 3D shapes — 'what you sketch is literally what you get.' This paper addresses the gap of enabling highly abstract, freehand doodles to produce precise 3D shapes without requiring any paired human sketch–3D shape training data, while also supporting fine-grained localised editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241985/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts single-view or multi-view freehand sketches (including highly abstract Quick-Draw! doodles with <20 strokes) and produces a 3D mesh via Marching Cubes over sampled occupancy values. Internally, sketches are encoded as m×64 part-disentangled feature vectors (m=16 parts) by a ResNet-18 encoder, and the generated shape is represented as m×512 part-latents decoded through a pre-trained Spaghetti implicit decoder. Multi-view sketches are combined by simple averaging of their part-level encodings before conditioning the diffusion process.

## Method

The core contribution is a part-level modelling and alignment framework that operates in a low-dimensional latent space rather than voxel space. Shapes are decomposed into m part-latents via a pre-trained Spaghetti decoder, and parts are aligned across shapes by minimising Wasserstein distance between their 3D Gaussian volume parameters, ensuring consistent part indexing. A latent diffusion model (DDPM, 1000 steps) is then trained on these aligned part-latents, with sketch conditioning injected through a cross-attention module that uses part-disentangled sketch features as queries and intermediate denoiser outputs as key-value pairs. Crucially, sketch part-annotations are obtained without any human sketch data by rendering 3D part segmentations onto synthetic edgemaps and passing them through CLIPasso to simulate human-like abstraction, establishing a one-to-one correspondence between sketch regions and shape parts. This part-aligned cross-modal correspondence enables in-position editing: local sketch edits are mapped to specific part indices, and only the corresponding part-latents are regenerated while the rest of the shape is preserved.

## Results

On the AmateurSketch-3D dataset, the method achieves CD 0.109 and EMD 0.091 (×10⁻¹), outperforming the previous SOTA LAS-D (CD 0.159, EMD 0.128) by 0.0076/0.0049 on average CD/EMD, and SENS (CD 0.121, EMD 0.096) by 0.0012/0.0005, all without training on any human sketches. On ProSketch-3D, the method achieves CD 0.093 and EMD 0.087, beating LAS-D (CD 0.195, EMD 0.147) by a wide margin. The model is also significantly more efficient: 46M parameters vs. 767M for LAS-D, with 2s inference time vs. 6s for LAS-D and 90s for Sketch2Mesh.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all sketch-based baselines on both evaluation datasets. Against the closest competitor LAS-D [90] (voxel-space SDF diffusion), it reduces average CD by 0.0076 and EMD by 0.0049 while using only 46M parameters (vs. 767M) and running 3× faster (2s vs. 6s). Against SENS [9] (deterministic part-aware sketch-to-implicit mapping), it improves CD by 0.0012 and EMD by 0.0005 on AmateurSketch-3D, and notably generalises to ProSketch-3D where SENS was trained. Against deterministic reconstruction methods (Sketch2Mesh [26], Sketch2Model [87], SketchSampler [22]), the gap is substantial — e.g., CD 0.109 vs. 0.257 for Sketch2Mesh — reflecting the advantage of generative modelling over one-to-one mapping. Against image-based SDFusion [13], the method achieves lower CD (0.109 vs. 0.632) with 24× fewer parameters. For unconditional generation (Table 2), the method is competitive with LAS-D and Diffusion-SDF on FID (22.32 vs. 20.45 and 21.07) while matching or exceeding them on Coverage (63.39% vs. 53.76% and 65.35%) and MMD (0.103 vs. 0.138 and 0.106).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Spaghetti decoder [31] for part-disentangled GMM-based shape decomposition and on latent diffusion models [63] for generative modelling, but departs from prior sketch-to-3D methods (LAS-D, SENS, Sketch2Model) in two key ways: it performs diffusion in a low-dimensional part-aligned latent space rather than voxelized SDF/occupancy space, and it establishes cross-modal part correspondence between sketches and shapes without any paired human sketch–3D training data. The CLIPasso-based synthetic sketch generation and Wasserstein-distance-based part alignment are the paper's distinctive technical contributions, repurposing a pre-trained shape decoder to also serve as a sketch part-segmentation backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate precise 3D shapes from highly abstract doodles without requiring paired sketch–shape data lowers the barrier to 3D content creation and removes a significant data-collection bottleneck. The 0.06× parameter count and 3× speedup over voxel-space diffusion (LAS-D) make the approach practical for interactive use, and the in-position editing capability emerges naturally from the part-aligned formulation. However, evaluation is restricted to a single ShapeNet category (chairs), leaving open whether the part-alignment strategy generalises to categories with more complex or variable part structures, and whether the CLIPasso abstraction level adequately captures the full range of human drawing variability.
