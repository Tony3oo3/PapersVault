## Problem

Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) for diffusion models improves generation quality by conditioning on retrieved private images, but this direct access causes the model to copy identifiable information from retrieved samples into outputs, leaking private data. Existing provable copyright-protection methods based on Near Access Freeness (NAF) rely on computationally expensive rejection sampling, making them impractical for slow diffusion models. The paper asks: can we achieve NAF-guaranteed copyright protection during a single backward diffusion pass, without rejection sampling?

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded via CLIP) and a set of m images retrieved from a private data store using L2-CLIP similarity. The retrieved images are consumed only through their averaged CLIP embedding, which serves as a retrieval prompt fed into the base diffusion model—no fine-tuning or adapter training is performed. The output is a single generated image that provably contains at most k_c bits of unique information about any retrieved sample, with k_c tunable at inference via the mixture weight ŵ₁ or the timestep selection set J. The framework is model-agnostic and applies to any pre-trained CLIP-conditioned diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 2.1, unCLIP).

## Method

CPR samples from a weighted mixture of a safe (public) distribution and a private (retrieval-conditioned) distribution by merging their diffusion scores at inference, eliminating the need for rejection sampling. The retrieval score is approximated by prompting the base model with the mean CLIP embedding of retrieved images (Eq. 7), and the final retrieval-mixture score (Eq. 8) linearly combines the safe and retrieval scores with a user-controlled weight. Two algorithms are provided: CPR-KL averages both scores at every backward-diffusion step via Langevin dynamics (satisfying k_c-NAF w.r.t. KL-divergence, Lemma 1), while CPR-Choose selects either the safe or private score at each timestep according to a user-defined interval set J (satisfying k_c-NAF w.r.t. max-divergence, Lemma 2). CPR-Choose variants—CPR-Min (picks the model with larger MSE at each step) and CPR-Alt (alternates every t̃ steps)—match the computational cost of a single base-model forward pass. The key theoretical contribution is proving that these score-merging constructions, applied within a single backward diffusion trajectory, yield the same NAF guarantees as the product/minimum distribution of Theorem 3.1 in [62] without any rejection loop.

## Results

On the TIFA benchmark (text-to-image alignment, higher is better), CPR-Alt improves the average score from 81.4 (Safe Model, no retrieval) to 83.17 with m=1 retrieved image, while simultaneously providing Near Access Freeness (NAF) copyright protection. CPR-KL achieves a comparable 83.16 TIFA average under the same setting. The improvement is consistent across COCO (85.5 → 86.73) and Non-COCO (77.43 → 79.72) splits, and scales with retrieval set size (m=5: CPR-KL reaches 82.78 average). Critically, CPR achieves these gains with a single backward diffusion pass, whereas the prior CP-K baseline requires 5–10× more computation due to rejection sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is CP-K from Vyas et al. [62], which implements NAF via rejection sampling over a fine-tuned diffusion model. At the same privacy level (k=1500), CP-K produces a nearly uniform distribution of log p(x|c)/safe(x|c), forcing 5–10× more rejection-sampling iterations and making inference non-deterministic in wall-clock time. CPR, by contrast, achieves the same NAF guarantee in a single backward diffusion pass with deterministic, constant-time cost (Fig. 4c). Internally, the paper shows that naive retrieval-score (Eq. 7) actually degrades TIFA from 81.4 to 66.45, while the retrieval-mixture-score (Eq. 8) recovers to 80.49; CPR-KL and CPR-Alt then push further to ~83.2, demonstrating that the NAF constraint (averaging or alternating between safe and private scores) acts as a regularizer that improves alignment beyond the unprotected mixture. CPR also offers a user-tunable privacy knob (ŵ₁) that directly controls the NAF constant k_c, a flexibility that CP-Δ [62] does not provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Near Access Freeness (NAF) framework of Vyas et al. [62] from general generative models to diffusion models, replacing the computationally expensive rejection-sampling-based CP-K algorithm with a single-pass score-merging procedure (CPR-KL) and a time-interval selection strategy (CPR-Choose) grounded in the MMSE denoiser likelihood estimation of Kong et al. [32,33]. The safe/private data split follows the mixed-privacy paradigm of Golatkar et al. [21,22], and the retrieval-augmented score construction builds on compositional diffusion and score-merging techniques [12,23,38]. A notable departure from prior RAG-for-diffusion work is the use of a pre-trained unCLIP model as the retrieval-score estimator rather than training a dedicated retrieval architecture, and the observation that classifier-free guidance with appropriate model substitution is a natural candidate for NAF-protected generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPR provides a practical, single-pass mechanism for copyright-protected text-to-image generation with diffusion models, eliminating the 5–10× overhead of rejection sampling in prior NAF methods while enabling constant-time unlearning by simply removing samples from the retrieval store. The empirical finding that privacy-protected generation (CPR) actually improves TIFA alignment over the unprotected base model (81.4 → 83.17) is encouraging but the margin is modest and evaluated on a single dataset (MSCOCO subset). Key open questions include how the NAF bound k_c scales with retrieval function quality, whether the score-merging approach generalizes to flow-based or autoregressive models where exact likelihoods are available, and whether the fixed hyperparameter approximation of the mixture weights (Proposition 1) degrades guarantees at scale.
