## Problem

Monocular HMR methods recover human meshes in per-frame camera coordinates without a coherent global space, while visual SLAM methods estimate camera trajectories and scene structure only up to scale and are disrupted by dynamic humans. These two problems have been addressed largely independently, with existing world-frame HMR pipelines relying on complex multi-stage optimization that is slow and brittle. The paper asks how to jointly recover metric-scale cameras, humans, and dense scenes in one global coordinate by exploiting the natural synergy between HMR and SLAM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video (no depth sensor, no IMU, no pre-scanned scene) and produces three outputs in a common world frame: metric-scale SE(3) camera poses, per-person SMPL meshes (6890 vertices each), and per-frame dense scene point clouds with color and human semantic masks. Internally, the pipeline consumes estimated monocular depth (ZoeDepth) as a pseudo-depth channel for SLAM and per-frame human instance segmentation masks (Mask2Former) for dynamic foreground exclusion, both of which are auxiliary inputs not present in the raw video.

## Method

SynCHMR is a two-phase pipeline. Phase 1 (Human-aware Metric SLAM) calibrates per-frame estimated depths to metric scale by jointly optimizing a global scale and offset that align unprojected human point clouds with camera-frame SMPL mesh vertices in both absolute depth and body size (L-BFGS, 30 iterations); the calibrated depths are then fed as pseudo-RGB-D to DROID-SLAM whose confidence map is masked to exclude dynamic human pixels, resolving depth, scale, and dynamic ambiguities simultaneously. Phase 2 (Scene-aware SMPL Denoiser) transforms camera-frame SMPL parameters into the world frame using the metric camera poses, then refines them with a 6-layer Transformer decoder that conditions on scene point clouds encoded by SPVCNN (7 channels: XYZ + RGB + human mask), learning implicit scene-contact constraints without explicit annotation of which body parts interact with which scene regions. The denoiser is trained end-to-end on 3DPW, EgoBody, and EMDB with direct supervision on world-frame SMPL parameters.

## Results

On EgoBody-Val, SynCHMR achieves the lowest PA-MPJPE (57.7 mm), FA-MPJPE (115.1 mm), and WA-MPJPE (81.1 mm) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best PACE (66.5/147.9/101.0 mm) and SLAHMR with PHALP+ (79.1/141.1/101.2 mm). On the held-out EgoBody-Test set, SynCHMR maintains its lead with PA=61.3, FA=122.1, WA=84.6 mm, beating SLAHMR w/ PHALP+ (63.1/163.9/99.4 mm) and GLAMR (112.8/351.4/216.3 mm). On 3DPW-Test, the full pipeline achieves 52.4 mm PA-MPJPE, improving over SLAHMR w/ 4DHumans (57.4 mm). Runtime is 5 min per 100 images, substantially faster than SLAHMR's 40 min while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynCHMR outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric for global human motion estimation. Against SLAHMR (Ye et al., CVPR 2023), the most direct competitor, SynCHMR reduces WA-MPJPE from 101.2 mm to 81.1 mm (Val) and from 99.4 mm to 84.6 mm (Test), while also cutting runtime from 40 min to 5 min per 100 images; notably, even initializing SLAHMR's optimization with the more accurate 4DHumans instead of PHALP+ does not close the gap. Against PACE (Kocabas et al., 3DV 2024), which tightly integrates SLAM and body fitting, SynCHMR achieves lower PA (57.7 vs 66.5 mm), FA (115.1 vs 147.9 mm), and WA (81.1 vs 101.0 mm) on Val, though PACE reports a much lower acceleration error (6.7 vs 64.8 mm/frame²) due to its explicit zero-velocity smoothing term. Against GLAMR (Yuan et al., CVPR 2022), which relies on a data-driven motion prior without explicit SLAM, SynCHMR is dramatically better on global metrics (WA 81.1 vs 239.0 mm on Val) and handles multi-person and selfie scenarios where GLAMR fails entirely. The paper also reports that PACE, despite its integrated objective, still uses vanilla DROID-SLAM for camera initialization (unaware of body information), which the authors argue leads to uncorrectable errors in the subsequent global optimization stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynCHMR sits at the intersection of two largely independent research threads: camera-frame HMR (e.g., 4DHumans) and monocular visual SLAM (e.g., DROID-SLAM). Prior world-frame HMR methods (SLAHMR, PACE, BodySLAM) bridge these via multi-stage optimization that refines SLAM poses and body parameters jointly, while motion-prior approaches (GLAMR, HuMoR) ignore scene geometry entirely. This paper departs from both by replacing the optimization bridge with a learned, feed-forward Scene-aware SMPL Denoiser and by feeding HMR outputs (depth, masks) back into the SLAM process to resolve scale and dynamic ambiguities, creating a bidirectional synergy rather than a one-way pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynCHMR is the first method to jointly recover metric-scale camera trajectories, human meshes, and dense scene point clouds in a single world frame from casual monocular video without pre-scanned environments, which opens the door to applications in VFX, AR, and robotics where all three are needed simultaneously. The practical 8× speedup over optimization-based baselines (5 min vs. 40 min per 100 frames) makes the pipeline more deployable. Open questions remain around atypical body shapes (children, obese individuals) where the SMPL-based depth calibration degrades, the untested behavior on synthetic or generated videos, and how to incorporate truly dynamic scene elements (moving objects) as constraints beyond the current static-background assumption.
