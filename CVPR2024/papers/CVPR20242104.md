## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs are limited to coarse-grained grounding (bounding boxes) and rely exclusively on textual referring instructions, leaving a gap in pixel-level visual perception and multi-modality interaction. No prior model simultaneously supports pixel-wise segmentation and region-aware expression generation from diverse reference types (text, bounding boxes, images, audio) within a single unified framework.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (224×224 for the LLM backbone, 1024×1024 for the SAM decoder) alongside a referring prompt that can be a free-text description, a bounding box (encoded as 4×4 RoIAlign features), a cropped reference image (pooled to 4×4 CLIP features), or a 5-second audio clip (projected to 3 fixed embeddings via ImageBind). It produces two outputs: a natural-language description of the referred object and a binary pixel-level segmentation mask decoded by a SAM-H network conditioned on the <obj> token embedding.

## Method

AnyRef builds on LLaVA-7B (CLIP ViT-L/14 + LLaMA-7B) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, a Unified Referring Representation maps all modality-specific references into fixed-size token embeddings wrapped in special delimiters (<img_ref>, <aud_ref>), so the LLM processes them identically to text without modality-specific heads. Second, a Refocusing Mechanism augments the single <obj> token embedding with a weighted sum of preceding token embeddings, where weights are normalized attention scores from the LLM's self-attention layers (λ_f=0.1); this expands the mask embedding's representational range beyond a single token's fixed feature space and creates an implicit pixel-level supervision pathway that also improves the quality of generated referring expressions. Training is end-to-end with combined text (next-token) loss, binary cross-entropy, and dice loss, using LoRA (rank 8) on the LLM and ~7% trainable parameters total.

## Results

On referring expression segmentation (cIoU), AnyRef achieves 74.1/75.5/70.8 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 64.1/68.7/57.5 on RefCOCO+, and 68.1/69.9 on RefCOCOg val/test without task-specific fine-tuning. With fine-tuning on the target datasets, AnyRef improves to 76.9/79.9/74.2 (RefCOCO), 70.3/73.5/61.8 (RefCOCO+), and 70.0/70.7 (RefCOCOg), surpassing all generalist MLLMs and most specialist segmentation models. On audio-visual segmentation, AnyRef achieves 82.8 mIOU on single-source AVSBench, outperforming AVSegFormer (82.1) and BG (81.7). On few-shot image referring segmentation, AnyRef reaches 43.5/51.3 cIoU on COCO-20 and 74.8/78.6 on PASCAL-5, competitive with specialist models like VAT and HSNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyRef outperforms LISA-7B [16] on RefCOCO+ (64.1 vs 62.4 val, 68.7 vs 67.4 testA) and RefCOCOg (68.1 vs 66.4 val, 69.9 vs 68.4 test) without fine-tuning, though it trails slightly on RefCOCO testA/testB (75.5/70.8 vs 76.5/71.1). With fine-tuning, AnyRef(ft) surpasses LISA-7B(ft) across all splits, gaining +2.0 cIoU on RefCOCO val and +5.2 on RefCOCO+ val. Among generalist MLLMs, AnyRef clearly beats X-Decoder [62] (68.1 vs 64.6 on RefCOCOg val) and SEEM [63] (68.1 vs 65.7). Against specialist segmentation models, AnyRef(ft) exceeds PolyFormer [25] on RefCOCO+ (70.3 vs 69.3 val) and GRES [22] on all splits, but remains behind UNINEXT-H [48] (70.0 vs 74.7 on RefCOCOg val), which is trained on a substantially larger dataset including video. On audio-visual segmentation, AnyRef beats AVSegFormer [8] and BG [10] on single-source mIOU (82.8 vs 82.1/81.7) but falls short of AUSS [21] on multi-source (55.6 vs 63.5 mIOU). On referring expression generation, AnyRef is competitive with KOSMOS-2 [31] and Shikra [4] in human evaluation (87%/80% on RefCOCO testA/testB) while trailing specialist models like IREG [50] on automated CIDEr scores, which the authors attribute to LLMs generating richer descriptions than concise ground-truth phrases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA-7B as its MLLM backbone and adopts LISA's <obj> token mechanism for triggering SAM-based segmentation, but departs from both by introducing a unified referring representation that maps bounding boxes, reference images, and audio clips into the LLM's token space via modality-specific projection layers. Unlike SEEM, which converts audio to text via Whisper before processing, AnyRef feeds raw audio features from ImageBind directly into the LLM. The key architectural departure is the refocusing mechanism, which augments the single <obj> embedding with attention-weighted sums of preceding token embeddings, addressing the representational bottleneck of a fixed-vocabulary token for pixel-level grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyRef is the first general MLLM to handle pixel-level grounding and region-aware expression generation from text, bounding box, image, and audio references in a single model without modality-specific heads, offering a practical template for multi-modal interactive segmentation. The refocusing mechanism's use of existing attention scores (no extra forward pass) makes it an attractive, low-cost augmentation for any LLM-based segmentation pipeline. However, the model still depends on SAM as a frozen decoder, struggles with multi-source audio segmentation, and the performance gains over LISA are modest (e.g., +1.7 cIoU on RefCOCOg val), leaving open whether the unified multi-modality framing scales to video or benefits from end-to-end mask decoding.
