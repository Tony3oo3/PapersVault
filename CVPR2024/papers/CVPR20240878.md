## Problem

Supervised distant point cloud registration (5–50 m vehicle separation) requires expensive ground-truth pose labels and fails to generalize to new data distributions, while existing unsupervised methods (BYOC, Unsupervised R&R, UDPReg) rely on RGB-D images or GMM matching that break down for sparse outdoor LiDAR. No prior solution addresses fully unsupervised outdoor distant point cloud registration without pose labels or auxiliary modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of sparse outdoor LiDAR point clouds (n×3 and m×3) from two vehicles, selected by a random frame interval I ∈ [1, B] from a consecutive LiDAR sequence, where B grows linearly from 1 to 30 over 200 training epochs. It outputs a relative rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) aligning the source to the target, with point-wise feature maps (n×k) as intermediate representations. No pose labels, RGB images, or other modalities are required at any stage.

## Method

EYOC employs a siamese student–labeler architecture synchronized via exponential moving average (EMA, λ=0.2), where the labeler generates pseudo-correspondences for the student in a progressive self-labeling loop. The key insight is the near-far diversity phenomenon: features in low-density regions far from the LiDAR are more stable under viewpoint displacement, so a spatial filtering step (hard threshold d_thresh=40 m or adaptive similarity threshold s_thresh=0.6) discards unreliable close-range correspondences, cutting >70% of false matches while retaining 91% of correct ones. Filtered correspondences feed a speculative registration (SC²-PCR) to estimate a coarse pose, which is then used to re-align the point clouds and rediscover dense correspondences via GPU-accelerated nearest-neighbor search with a loosened inlier threshold. The student is trained with a Hardest-Contrastive Loss on these rediscovered correspondences, and the entire pipeline repeats with increasing frame intervals, gradually extending the effective registration distance from identity (B=1) to 50 m (B=30).

## Results

On KITTI, EYOC trained from scratch achieves 83.2% mRR, within 4.7% of the best supervised method (Predator at 87.9%) and only 1.4% behind FCGF+C (84.6%), while requiring no pose labels. On nuScenes, EYOC (61.7% mRR from scratch) surpasses FCGF+C (59.3%) and Predator (51.0%) by 2.4% and 10.7% respectively. In the out-of-domain generalization setting, EYOC outperforms supervised baselines by a mean of 17.4% mRR across datasets, e.g., 83.2% vs. 71.8% (FCGF) and 72.3% (Predator) on KITTI, and 61.7% vs. 67.1% (FCGF) and 34.5% (Predator) on nuScenes. EYOC also achieves the lowest total training time (865 ms/pass × 1) compared to FCGF (1176 ms/pass × 2) and FCGF* (503 ms/pass × 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FCGF [9] (the primary fully-convolutional baseline), EYOC wins decisively in the generalization setting: 83.2% vs. 71.8% mRR on KITTI (+11.4%), 78.4% vs. 69.9% on WOD (+8.5%), and 61.7% vs. 67.1% on nuScenes (−5.3%, the only loss). In the finetuning setting on the same dataset, EYOC is within 4.7% of FCGF+C on KITTI (83.2% vs. 84.6%) but surpasses it on nuScenes (61.7% vs. 59.3%). Against Predator [20], EYOC outperforms generalization by 10.9% on KITTI and 27.2% on nuScenes, but trails the oracle finetuned Predator by 4.7% on KITTI (83.2% vs. 87.9%) and 8.0% on WOD (78.4% vs. 86.4%). Against keypoint-based methods (SpinNet, D3Feat, GeoTransformer), EYOC dominates on KITTI finetuning (83.2% vs. 39.1%, 66.4%, 42.2% mRR), as these methods diverge on outdoor low-overlap pairs. CoFiNet [54] is the closest supervised competitor on KITTI (82.1% mRR), with EYOC edging ahead by 1.1%. The paper's headline claim—17.4% mean mRR improvement over supervised generalization in the out-of-domain unlabelled setting—is confirmed across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

EYOC extends the progressive/staged training strategy of APR [28] from supervised to unsupervised settings, replacing ground-truth poses with self-generated correspondence labels via a Siamese labeler-student architecture (analogous to EMA-based self-supervision in image classification). It departs from prior unsupervised registration work (BYOC, Unsupervised R&R, UDPReg) that relied on RGB-D images or GMM matching for correspondence acquisition in indoor scenes, by showing these approaches fail in outdoor distant settings due to density-variation and low-overlap. The spatial filtering module is a direct response to the empirical finding that Lowe's ratio—a standard correspondence quality filter in BYOC—actually degrades 3D outdoor correspondences, motivating a distance-based alternative grounded in the near-far diversity of LiDAR point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EYOC addresses a practical bottleneck in collaborative sensing for autonomous driving: the inability to deploy registration networks on new geographic or sensor distributions without expensive pose annotation. The near-far diversity finding (far-from-LiDAR regions produce more stable features under viewpoint change) provides a reusable design principle for outdoor feature learning. Limitations include the requirement for sequential LiDAR data (the progressive dataset strategy depends on frame locality), the need for a pretrained model from a related dataset, and the fact that the method has only been validated on three driving datasets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-sequential or single-frame registration, and whether the progressive extension can be accelerated or made more data-efficient.
