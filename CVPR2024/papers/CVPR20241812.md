## Problem

Coarse-to-fine 3D instance segmentation underperforms recent grouping, kernel, and transformer-based methods due to two structural limitations: axis-aligned bounding boxes overestimate instance sizes (since points lie only on surfaces, AABBs include redundant empty space), and false negatives from inaccurate coarse detection propagate irrecoverably into the refinement stage, capping the upper bound of accuracy. The paper argues that the rigid disentanglement between coarse detection and fine refinement is the root cause.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a voxelized 3D point cloud (0.02 m voxels for indoor scenes, 0.3 m for aerial) with per-point RGB color, cropped to at most 250,000 points during training. It outputs K local binary masks (one per vote proposal), each assigning a 0/1 label to every point, along with per-proposal class predictions and confidence scores; these are merged via 3D NMS (confidence threshold 0.2) and optionally superpoint-aggregated to yield final per-point instance masks.

## Method

Spherical Mask replaces AABBs with a 3D polygon defined in spherical coordinates: a Radial Instance Detection (RID) head predicts an instance center and N_θ×N_φ radial distances (one per angular sector), giving tighter boundaries than AABBs. A Radial Point Migration (RPM) module, implemented with dynamic convolution, predicts a single 1-D radial offset per point so that every point—inside or outside the coarse polygon—is virtually shifted along its ray, enabling correction of both false positives and false negatives without modifying the point cloud. Two margin-based losses train the migration: L_mc pushes misclassified points across the sector boundary with a soft-margin penalty, and L_sc pulls true-positive points toward the sector centroid to enforce cohesion. Training uses Hungarian-algorithm matching between votes and ground-truth instances (duplicated 4×) to assign coarse, fine, and classification losses, with the final mask assembled by thresholding migrated radial distances against the predicted ray lengths.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 validation, Spherical Mask achieves mAP 62.3, AP50 79.9, and AP25 88.2, surpassing the previous best (MAFT, Transformer-based) by 3.9 mAP and 4.3 AP50. On the ScanNetV2 hidden test set, it reaches mAP 61.6 and AP50 81.2, improving over the prior SOTA by 4.1 and 2.4 points respectively. On S3DIS Area 5, the method attains mAP 60.5, AP50 72.3, and mRec50 76.3, outperforming QueryFormer (57.7 mAP) by 2.8 points. On STPLS3D, it achieves mAP 52.2 and AP50 68.3, beating ISBNet (49.2 mAP) by 3.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spherical Mask outperforms all 18 baselines across all three datasets and all reported metrics. On ScanNetV2 validation, it leads the strongest Transformer-based competitor MAFT [15] by 3.9 mAP (62.3 vs 58.4) and 4.3 AP50 (79.9 vs 75.6), and the kernel-based ISBNet [29] by 7.8 mAP. On S3DIS Area 5, it surpasses QueryFormer [21] (57.7 mAP) by 2.8 points and ISBNet [29] (56.3 mAP) by 4.2 points, while also achieving the highest mRec50 (76.3 vs 72.2 for QueryFormer). On STPLS3D, it beats ISBNet [29] by 3.0 mAP (52.2 vs 49.2) and PointGroup [13] by 28.9 mAP. Notably, as a coarse-to-fine method, Spherical Mask outperforms all grouping-based (e.g., PBNet 54.3, SoftGroup 46.0), kernel-based (e.g., DKNet 50.8, ISBNet 54.5), and Transformer-based (e.g., MAFT 58.4, QueryFormer 56.5) approaches on ScanNetV2, closing the historical gap where coarse-to-fine methods lagged behind the other three families. The per-class results on ScanNetV2 test show particular gains on closely-spaced instances (pictures: 69.8 vs 62.1 for ISBNet; desks: 44.7 vs 40.8 for MAFT), attributed to the misclassification correction loss in RPM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives the coarse-to-fine (proposal-based) paradigm for 3D instance segmentation, which had been overshadowed by grouping, kernel, and Transformer-based methods. It departs from prior coarse-to-fine work (3D-SIS, 3D-BoNet, GSPN, TD3D) by replacing the axis-aligned bounding box with a spherical polygon representation (inspired by PolarMask's 2D polar formulation) and by treating the coarse detection as a soft reference rather than a hard boundary during refinement. The backbone (sparse U-Net + set-abstraction voting) and the dynamic convolution for per-point prediction are borrowed from ISBNet and DyCo3D, but the core contribution—the Radial Instance Detection and Radial Point Migration modules operating in spherical coordinates—is a new architectural and loss design that breaks the error-propagation chain inherent in prior coarse-to-fine pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a carefully designed coarse-to-fine method can surpass Transformer-based SOTA by 4+ mAP points across three datasets, the paper challenges the field's implicit assumption that the proposal-based paradigm is fundamentally limited. The spherical representation offers a geometrically tighter and more interpretable instance boundary than AABB, and the radial migration mechanism provides a principled way to correct both false positives and false negatives without requiring 3D offset learning. Open questions include whether the fixed sector resolution (5×5) generalizes to highly irregular or elongated objects, whether the superpoint post-processing step can be eliminated, and whether the approach transfers to outdoor or large-scale scenes without architectural modifications.
