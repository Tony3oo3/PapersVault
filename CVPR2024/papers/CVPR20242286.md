## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video editing methods are limited to short clips with small motions because frame-wise editing inherently conflicts with long-range temporal consistency. Prior attempts to bridge this gap using video-2D representations (layered neural atlases, UV maps, canonical images) fail on large-scale motion and viewpoint changes, particularly in human-centric scenarios where the subject undergoes complex non-rigid deformations. No prior method can consistently edit long, large-motion human-centric videos while preserving both temporal and 3D geometric consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a human-centric video (30–400 frames at 1280×720), a reference subject image (generated via ControlNet conditioned on a source human pose to ensure pose alignment), a reference style image for background transfer, and text prompts. The reference image is further processed with a monocular depth estimator and SAM to obtain pseudo-depth and mask for reconstruction supervision. The output is an edited video rendered along the source camera trajectory, plus 360° free-viewpoint novel view synthesis of the edited dynamic scene. Due to GPU memory constraints, the video-NeRF is trained at 128×128 resolution, with text-guided local parts super-resolution applied to recover fine geometric and textural details.

## Method

DynVideo-E introduces dynamic NeRF (a simplified HOSNeRF) as the video representation, decomposing the scene into a 3D canonical human space with a pose-guided deformation field and a Mip-NeRF 360 unbounded background, so that editing in 3D propagates consistently across all frames via the deformation field. The editing pipeline combines four key mechanisms: (1) a reconstruction loss (RGB, mask, depth) at the reference pose and front view to inject subject identity; (2) multi-view multi-pose Score Distillation Sampling from both a 3D diffusion prior (Zero1-to-3) and a 2D personalized diffusion prior (Dreambooth-LoRA finetuned on the reference image) to enforce 3D consistency and animatability under arbitrary poses; (3) text-guided local parts super-resolution over 7 semantic body regions with view-conditional prompts to overcome the low training resolution; and (4) a nearest-neighbor feature matching (NNFM) style loss to transfer the reference style to the 3D background. Training proceeds in two stages—first reconstructing the video-NeRF, then editing under a multi-view multi-pose schedule that alternates between reference-view supervision, random-view SDS at the source pose, and random-view 2D SDS at per-frame poses.

## Results

On the combined HOSNeRF (300–400 frames) and NeuMan (30–90 frames) datasets with 24 editing prompts across 11 dynamic human-centric videos, DynVideo-E achieves a CLIPScore of 31.31, outperforming the best baseline Text2Video-Zero (26.70) by 4.61 points. In human preference studies (1,140 pairwise comparisons from 32 raters), DynVideo-E is preferred over all baselines by margins of 50%–95% across textual faithfulness, temporal consistency, and overall quality. The largest gaps appear against CoDeF (98.75% preference for DynVideo-E on temporal consistency and overall quality) and Text2LIVE (96.19% on textual faithfulness).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynVideo-E outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor Text2Video-Zero (CLIPScore 26.70), DynVideo-E leads by 4.61 CLIPScore points and is preferred 90.83% of the time on textual faithfulness. Rerender-A-Video (26.11) and Text2LIVE (22.77) are intermediate, with DynVideo-E winning 93.33% and 96.19% of textual-faithfulness comparisons respectively. The 2D-representation-based methods (Text2LIVE, StableVideo, CoDeF) struggle most with large-scale motion and viewpoint changes, largely overfitting to input frames and producing meaningless canonical images or neural atlases; CoDeF in particular is preferred by raters only 1.25% of the time on both temporal consistency and overall quality. The paper notes that for challenging videos with large motions, CoDeF, Text2LIVE, and StableVideo fail to generate meaningful editing results at all, while DynVideo-E maintains high temporal consistency and accurate foreground/background editing throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 2D video-representation paradigm for editing (layered neural atlases, canonical images, UV maps) by introducing dynamic NeRF as the video representation, building directly on HOSNeRF's state-conditional human-object-scene model and Mip-NeRF 360's unbounded scene formulation. For the editing pipeline itself, it combines Score Distillation Sampling from both a 3D diffusion prior (Zero-1-to-3) and a 2D personalized diffusion prior (DreamBooth-LoRA fine-tuned on the reference image), extending the DreamFusion-style SDS framework to a multi-view, multi-pose, animatable 3D human space. The background style transfer adapts ARF's nearest-neighbor feature matching loss to the unbounded NeRF setting, and the reconstruction loss follows Magic123's multi-signal supervision at a reference viewpoint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By moving video editing into a 3D dynamic space, DynVideo-E resolves the fundamental tension between frame-wise editing and long-range temporal consistency that has limited prior diffusion-based video editors to short clips with small motions. The ability to render free-viewpoint novel views of the edited scene opens a new dimension of controllability not available in 2D-representation methods. However, the NeRF-based representation is computationally expensive (the authors acknowledge this as a key limitation), the method is restricted to single-person human-centric scenes, and scaling to multi-person interactions or longer videos remains open. Replacing the NeRF backbone with voxel or hash-grid representations is identified as the most promising direction for practical adoption.
