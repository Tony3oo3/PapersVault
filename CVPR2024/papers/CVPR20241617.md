## Problem

Existing Vision Transformer Compression methods follow a two-stage scheme that first evaluates or presets importance scores, then separately searches for sparsity under a target constraint. This separation creates a distributional gap between importance and sparsity scores, leading to sub-optimal pruning decisions and high search costs (e.g., 24–32 GPU days for TAS methods). The paper asks whether importance and sparsity can be jointly learned in a single differentiable stage to close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT (DeiT-S, DeiT-B, or Swin-Ti) and a target FLOPs/parameter budget as input, operating on 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K. It outputs a structurally pruned ViT with reduced Q-K-V channel ratios, MLP expansion ratios, attention head counts, and patch-embedding dimensions across all layers. The search also produces a compressed model that can be fine-tuned for downstream tasks (CIFAR-10/100 classification, Cityscapes segmentation) without architectural modification.

## Method

OFB introduces a bi-mask weight-sharing strategy where each unit's prunability is a time-varying convex combination m_ij(t) = λ(t)·S_ij + [1−λ(t)]·V_ij(α), entangling a learnable soft importance mask (sigmoid-normalized, ℓ₁-regularized) with a DARTS-style differentiable sparsity score (softmax over architecture parameters with weight sharing). The coefficient λ linearly decays from 1 to 0, shifting optimization focus from importance ranking to sparsity discretization as search progresses. An adaptive one-hot loss drives the sparsity distribution toward a binary pruning decision by aligning its entropy and variance with those of a one-hot vector, while a sparsity-constraint term enforces the target budget. Progressive Masked Image Modeling (PMIM) is added during search, gradually increasing the masking ratio to regularize the dimension-reduced feature space and maintain reliable prunability evaluation under aggressive compression.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, OFB compresses DeiT-S to 0.9B FLOPs (80% reduction) with 75.0% Top-1 accuracy using only 1 GPU day of search, compared to 32 GPU days for ViTAS-B (72.4%). In the lightweight regime, OFB-compressed DeiT-S achieves 76.1% Top-1 at 1.1B FLOPs, surpassing DeiT-Ti (72.2%) and TNT-Ti (73.9%) by 3.9% and 2.2% respectively. For DeiT-B, OFB achieves 81.7% Top-1 at 8.7B FLOPs (50% reduction) with only 0.1% accuracy drop, outperforming WDPruning (80.8% at 9.9B) and matching ViT-Slim (82.4% at 10.6B) with 1.9B fewer FLOPs. On Swin-Ti, OFB achieves 76.5% Top-1 at 1.0B FLOPs (78% reduction) in 1.1 GPU days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TAS baselines, OFB achieves 2.6% higher Top-1 than ViTAS-B (75.0% vs 72.4%) at the same 0.9B FLOPs budget for DeiT-S, while reducing search cost from 32 to 1 GPU day. Compared to AutoFormer-Ti (74.7% at 1.3B FLOPs, 24 GPU days), OFB delivers 1.3% higher accuracy at 0.4B fewer FLOPs with 24× less search time. Against the efficient TF-TAS (75.3% at 1.4B, 0.5 GPU days), OFB is within 0.3% Top-1 at 0.5B fewer FLOPs with 2× more search time. Among TP methods, OFB outperforms WDPruning by 0.9% Top-1 at 50% FLOPs reduction on DeiT-B (81.7% vs 80.8%), and surpasses ViT-Slim by 0.1% at 1.9B fewer FLOPs on DeiT-S (78.0% vs 77.9%). In the lightweight regime, OFB-compressed DeiT-S (76.1% at 1.1B) beats all dedicated lightweight models including DeiT-Ti (72.2%), TNT-Ti (73.9%), HVT (78.0% at 2.4B), and PVT-Ti (75.1% at 1.9B), demonstrating that compression of a larger model can outperform purpose-built smaller architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

OFB unifies two previously separate VTC paradigms—SPOS-based Transformer Architecture Search (which implicitly encodes importance via ordinal weight-sharing in a supernet) and threshold-based Transformer Pruning (which explicitly scores importance then applies a global sparsity threshold)—into a single differentiable search stage. It draws the differentiable relaxation and weight-sharing mechanism from DARTS, the soft-mask importance scoring and L1 regularization from ViT-Slim, and the masked image modeling objective from SimMIM, but departs from all three by entangling importance and sparsity scores in a bi-mask that is jointly optimized with an adaptive one-hot loss, rather than evaluating them sequentially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-stage formulation eliminates the expensive supernet pre-training and separate sparsity search that dominate the cost of existing TAS methods, reducing search to roughly 1 GPU day for DeiT-S versus 24–32 GPU days for AutoFormer/ViTAS, while matching or exceeding their accuracy. This makes high-quality ViT compression accessible to groups without large compute budgets. Open questions include whether the bi-mask strategy generalizes beyond DeiT and Swin-Ti to hybrid or non-Transformer backbones, whether the fixed four-submodule search space captures all meaningful compression degrees of freedom, and whether the progressive masking schedule in PMIM is optimal or could be learned end-to-end.
