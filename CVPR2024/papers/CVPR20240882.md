## Problem

Existing deep learning approaches for vegetation forecasting either rely on coarse-resolution data (AVHRR, MODIS) that overlooks pixel-level heterogeneity, or use high-resolution imagery (Sentinel-2, Landsat) but optimize for perceptual image quality rather than actual vegetation dynamics. No prior work has demonstrated continental-scale vegetation modeling at fine (20 m) resolution that captures anomalies beyond the seasonal cycle, which is essential for applications like drought early warning, agricultural planning, and carbon monitoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240882/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10 historical 5-day Sentinel-2 images (blue, green, red, NIR bands at 20 m; 128×128 px ≈ 2.56×2.56 km), 150 daily E-OBS meteorological observations (wind, humidity, radiation, rainfall, pressure, temperature), and a static elevation map, and produces 20 future 5-day NDVI maps at the same spatial resolution. Rather than predicting full multispectral imagery, the output is a single-band vegetation greenness proxy, and the model uses a learned cloud mask to exclude faulty pixels during both training and evaluation. The prediction is structured as a delta from the last cloud-free NDVI observation, so the network learns deviations from persistence rather than absolute values.

## Method

Contextformer is a multi-modal transformer with an encode–process–decode architecture. The vision encoder is an ImageNet-pretrained Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT v2 B0) that extracts multi-scale spatial features from 4×4 px patches of each satellite image and elevation map; the meteo encoder is a lightweight MLP that embeds weather time series. A temporal transformer then processes tokens in parallel across all 30 time steps but spatially only within each 4×4 px local context patch, reducing memory by 16× and allowing larger batch sizes while preserving the small spatial context needed to handle Sentinel-2 sub-pixel registration shifts. During training, masked token modeling (alternating between dropping future patches and randomly masking 70% of patches) provides self-supervised regularization, and weather is injected via FiLM conditioning. The delta-prediction scheme uses the last cloud-free NDVI as an initial state and the decoder predicts per-step deviations, which the ablations show improves outperformance over climatology from 62.3% to 66.8%. The full model has only 6.1 M parameters, making it substantially lighter than competing transformer baselines like Earthformer (60.6 M).

## Results

On the GreenEarthNet OOD-t test set (100-day lead time, 20 m resolution over Europe), Contextformer achieves R²=0.62, RMSE=0.14, NSE=0.09, and |bias|=0.09, outperforming all baselines on RMSE and NSE. It improves RMSE to 0.08 over the first 25 days and attains a 66.8% outperformance score over the NDVI climatology baseline—the highest among all tested models. The model uses only 6.1M parameters, comparable to SimVP (6.6M) and far smaller than Earthformer (60.6M), while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Contextformer outperforms all baselines on RMSE (0.14 vs. 0.15 for PredRNN and SimVP, 0.16 for retrained ConvLSTM and Earthformer) and NSE (0.09 vs. 0.03 for PredRNN/SimVP, −0.13 for ConvLSTM/Earthformer). It ties with PredRNN on R² (both 0.62) and with SimVP on |bias| (both 0.09), while achieving the highest climatology outperformance score (66.8% vs. 64.7% for PredRNN, 64.1% for SimVP, 60.6% for Prophet). The EarthNet2021 pre-trained models (ConvLSTM, SGED-ConvLSTM, Earthformer) perform poorly, with R² ranging from 0.49 to 0.53 and none consistently beating the climatology baseline, likely due to their focus on perceptual quality and a faulty cloud mask. Among local time-series models, Prophet is the strongest (60.6% outperformance) but still trails Contextformer by 6.2 percentage points, indicating that spatial context provides meaningful predictive signal beyond per-pixel temporal dynamics. The retrained Earthformer (60.6M params) underperforms Contextformer (6.1M params) on every metric, highlighting the efficiency of the proposed architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

The Contextformer architecture draws on two recent lines of work: the PVT v2 B0 vision encoder design from MMST-ViT (Lin et al., ICCV 2023) for spatial context extraction, and the Presto temporal transformer encoder (Tseng et al., 2023) for modeling time-series dynamics in remote sensing. Conceptually, the paper departs from the EarthNet2021 paradigm, which framed satellite image forecasting as a guided video prediction task optimized for perceptual quality, by reframing the objective around ecological vegetation dynamics (NDVI) with a proper cloud mask and landcover-aware evaluation. It also positions itself against video prediction models (PredRNN, SimVP) and time series forecasters (Prophet, LightGBM), arguing that vegetation forecasting requires multi-modal fusion (satellite + weather + elevation) and across-scale relationships that neither family handles natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that fine-resolution (20 m) continental-scale vegetation forecasts can consistently outperform the NDVI climatology (66.8% outperformance score), which is a prerequisite for practical applications in humanitarian anticipatory action, agricultural planning, and carbon monitoring. The GreenEarthNet dataset and its evaluation protocol (learned cloud mask, landcover-stratified metrics, OOD spatial/temporal splits) address a gap in the field where prior benchmarks conflated data corruption with ecological signal. Key open questions include how weather forecast uncertainty at inference time degrades skill, whether the 16 M-parameter ceiling reflects a data bottleneck that larger datasets could break, and whether the modest spatial-interaction gains (R² drop of ~0.07 under shuffling) justify the architectural complexity over simpler pixel-wise models.
