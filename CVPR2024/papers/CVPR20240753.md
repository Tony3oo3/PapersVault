## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) suffers from robust overfitting, where models achieve near-perfect training robustness but significantly lower test robustness. This paper identifies that the root cause is excessive memorization of noisy labels—arising from the distribution mismatch between clean and adversarial samples—evidenced by a non-monotonical growth in gradient norm during training. While prior work regularized inputs or weights, the label assignment itself remained largely unexplored as a lever for mitigating this gap.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 under an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of 8/255. During training, instead of consuming one-hot labels directly, the method generates a refined soft label distribution by interpolating between the hard label and an EMA-smoothed prediction from the model itself (combining clean and adversarial forward passes). The final output is a standard class prediction, but the training signal is shaped by this self-generated soft distribution, which encodes inter-class similarity information absent from one-hot encoding.

## Method

Self-Guided Label Refinement (SGLR) replaces one-hot labels in adversarial training with a dynamically updated soft label: y = r·p̃_t + (1−r)·y_hard, where p̃_t is an exponential moving average of the model's own interpolated prediction f̃(x,x′;w) = λ·f(x;w) + (1−λ)·f(x′;w) over clean and adversarial inputs. The theoretical grounding comes from an information-theoretic decomposition showing that soft labels reduce the 'Information In Weights' (IIW), i.e., the mutual information I(w; y′|x′), which quantifies how much label noise the model memorizes into its parameters. By interpolating toward the model's own predictive distribution rather than a uniform one (as in vanilla label smoothing), SGLR retains semantic inter-class structure while suppressing over-confident memorization. The approach requires no external teacher model, no architectural changes, and adds only one extra forward pass per iteration, making it a lightweight plug-in to existing AT pipelines like PGD-AT and TRADES.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=8/255, ResNet-18), PGD-AT+SGLR achieves 56.4% PGD-20 robust accuracy and 51.2% AutoAttack robust accuracy, closing the generalization gap to just 0.5% (vs. 9.3% for vanilla PGD-AT). On CIFAR-100, AT+SGLR reaches 34.5% PGD-20 robust accuracy (vs. 27.3% for AT) with a gap of only 0.2% (vs. 7.5% for AT). The method also improves natural accuracy by 2.2% on CIFAR-10 and 3.0% on CIFAR-100 over the corresponding AT baselines, demonstrating simultaneous gains in standard and robust performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PGD-AT (Madry et al., ICLR 2018), SGLR improves PGD-20 robust accuracy from 50.7% to 56.4% (+5.7%) and AutoAttack from 47.7% to 51.2% (+3.5%) on CIFAR-10, while reducing the robust generalization gap from 9.3% to 0.5%. Against TRADES (Zhang et al., ICML 2019), SGLR gains +2.5% PGD-20 and +1.7% AutoAttack on CIFAR-10, and +4.0% PGD-20 and +2.5% AutoAttack on CIFAR-100. Against AWP (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2020), SGLR achieves higher PGD-20 (56.4% vs. 55.4%) and AutoAttack (51.2% vs. 50.6%) on CIFAR-10. Against KD-AT (Dong et al., NeurIPS 2022), SGLR leads by +1.8% PGD-20 and +2.1% AutoAttack without requiring a separately trained teacher. Against KD-SWA (Chen et al., ICLR 2021), SGLR matches natural accuracy (82.9% vs. 84.7% best) but surpasses it on PGD-20 (56.4% vs. 54.9%) and AutoAttack (51.2% vs. 49.3%), while requiring only 2 forward passes per iteration versus 4 for KD-SWA and no external teacher model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of adversarial training (PGD-AT, TRADES) and label refinement, departing from the dominant line of work that combats robust overfitting through input or weight regularization (AWP, weight smoothing, data augmentation). It draws on the label smoothing literature (Pereyra et al.) but replaces the uniform distribution with the model's own predictive distribution, and on knowledge distillation by eliminating the need for an external teacher via exponential moving average of the model's own history. The theoretical grounding via PAC-Bayesian decomposition and mutual information (IIW) connects to prior analyses of memorization in ERM, extending that lens to the adversarial setting where perturbation-induced distribution mismatch creates an additional source of label noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, zero-overhead plug-in (SGLR) that closes the robust generalization gap from ~9% to ~0.5% on CIFAR-10, which is meaningful for practitioners who want to avoid early-stopping heuristics. However, the evaluation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18 and a single perturbation budget (ε=8/255), leaving open whether the approach transfers to ImageNet-scale data, deeper architectures, or larger perturbation radii. The authors themselves note the model remains poorly calibrated in a sample-wise sense, and the EMA-based soft label is a heuristic whose theoretical justification (Theorem 1) relies on a uniform-distribution interpolation that may not capture the true adversarial label distribution well.
