## Problem

In-context learning has enabled generalist models for images and point clouds, but remains unexplored for 3D skeleton sequences. Directly applying Masked Image Modeling-style in-context frameworks to skeleton data fails because the high similarity between adjacent frames causes the model to overfit to trivial frame interpolation rather than learning meaningful task representations, producing progressively shrinking outputs at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241543/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a task-guided prompt (an input-target skeleton pair from a specific task) and a query skeleton sequence, all unified to shape (F=16, J, 3). For pose estimation, 2D inputs are zero-padded in the third dimension; for joint completion, missing joint coordinates are set to zero. The output is a 3D skeleton sequence of shape (F, J, 3), taken as the second half of the transformer's output, with no task-specific heads or fine-tuning required at inference.

## Method

SiC introduces two complementary prompts: a Task-Guided Prompt (TGP), which is an input-target pair from a target task that the model uses as an analogy template, and a Task-Unified Prompt (TUP), a task-agnostic module (static: embedding of the mean target across all tasks; dynamic: learnable parameters) that injects shared prior knowledge and prevents the model from collapsing to simple interpolation. The architecture is a two-stream transformer with alternating spatial and temporal self-attention blocks: the TGP and the query (concatenated with TUP) are processed in parallel for n₁ iterations, aggregated via weighted summation, then jointly refined for n₂ iterations. Training is a single end-to-end supervised pass with 3D joint-level L2 reconstruction loss over a skeleton bank of 176K samples from H3.6M, AMASS, and 3DPW, requiring no task-specific heads, multi-stage pretraining, or fine-tuning.

## Results

On the proposed in-context skeleton benchmark, Dynamic-SiC achieves the best average MPJPE of 24.4 mm on motion prediction (AMASS), outperforming all multi-task baselines and even matching task-specific models (siMLPe: 26.7, MotionBERT: 26.7). On pose estimation (H3.6M), Dynamic-SiC reports 51.8 mm MPJPE, competitive with the best task-specific model GLA-GCN (49.6 mm). For joint completion (3DPW), Dynamic-SiC achieves 44.0 mm (40% missing) and 66.5 mm (60% missing), surpassing all multi-task-capable baselines. On the new future pose estimation task, Dynamic-SiC reports 62.9 mm (200 ms) and 66.5 mm (300 ms) average MPJPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dynamic-SiC outperforms all multi-task-capable baselines across every task: on motion prediction it achieves 24.4 mm vs. the best multi-task model MotionBERT‡ (28.4 mm), and on joint completion 40% it reaches 44.0 mm vs. MotionBERT‡ (46.7 mm). Against in-context baselines from other modalities, the advantage is dramatic: PIC [16] scores 51.7 mm on motion prediction and 181.6 mm on pose estimation, while Dynamic-SiC achieves 24.4 mm and 51.8 mm respectively, indicating that directly transferring point-cloud in-context methods to skeleton sequences fails. On pose estimation, Dynamic-SiC (51.8 mm) is within 2.2 mm of the best task-specific model GLA-GCN (49.6 mm) and 2.4 mm of MotionBERT (49.4 mm), showing that the in-context framework nearly closes the gap to dedicated architectures. On joint completion 60%, Dynamic-SiC (66.5 mm) beats the best task-specific model STCFormer (60.9 mm) is actually slightly worse, but it substantially outperforms all multi-task restructured models (best: siMLPe† at 64.4 mm). The Copy baseline [5,16] confirms that the model is not simply memorizing prompt outputs, as it scores over 130 mm on motion prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the in-context learning paradigm—established in NLP and extended to images (Visual Prompt, Painter) and 3D point clouds (Point-In-Context, PointMAE)—into the domain of dynamic skeleton sequences, a modality where no prior in-context framework existed. It departs from the standard MIM-style training used in those vision in-context models, which the authors show catastrophically fails for skeleton data due to inter-frame pose similarity, and instead introduces Task-Guided Prompts (input-target skeleton pairs) and Task-Unified Prompts (static mean-pose or learnable pseudo-pose) as the core mechanism for task perception. On the unified-model side, it contrasts with MotionBERT (two-stage pretrain/fine-tune), UniHCP (task-specific heads on a shared backbone), and UPS (language-sequence unification with per-task fine-tuning), all of which require task-specific architectural components or multi-stage training, whereas SiC uses a single task-agnostic transformer trained once end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single transformer trained once can handle motion prediction, pose estimation, joint completion, and the newly proposed future pose estimation—and further generalize to an unseen task (motion in-between) and unseen datasets without fine-tuning—this work opens a path toward truly generalist skeleton-sequence models. Key limitations include the small model scale (5 layers, 256-dim), the limited task set (four tasks across three datasets), and the absence of comparison against other in-context skeleton approaches (none exist yet). Open questions include whether the TGP+TUP prompt design scales to dozens of tasks or longer sequences, whether the random-prompting finding holds at larger scale, and whether the framework can incorporate richer modalities (e.g., text instructions) for task specification.
