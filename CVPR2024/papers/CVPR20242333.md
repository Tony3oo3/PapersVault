## Problem

Concept-based interpretability has been studied extensively for image models (CNNs and ViTs), but no methodology exists for discovering and ranking human-interpretable concepts in video transformer representations. The added temporal dimension introduces dynamic, spatiotemporal concepts that are far more numerous and complex than their image-level counterparts, and existing proposal and importance-estimation techniques do not scale to or align with video transformer architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242333/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

VTCD takes a dataset of RGB videos (e.g., 30 randomly sampled clips) and a pretrained video transformer as input, extracting intermediate features at every layer and self-attention head. It produces, per layer, a set of spatiotemporal concept centroids with binary support masks over the space-time volume, along with importance scores in [0,1] quantifying each concept's contribution to the model's prediction. Additionally, by applying the pipeline to multiple models and comparing concept supports via mIoU, it outputs Rosetta concepts—tuples of concepts shared across models that encode the same information.

## Method

VTCD operates in three stages. First, it generates tubelet proposals by running SLIC superpixel clustering directly in the model's feature space (rather than pixel space), producing connected spatiotemporal segments that naturally align with the information encoded at each layer. Second, it clusters these tubelets across all videos into high-level concepts using Convex Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (CNMF), which extends NMF to handle the negative activations produced by GeLU non-linearities in transformers. Third, it estimates concept importance via Concept Randomized Importance Sampling (CRIS): because transformers are robust to single-concept perturbations in self-attention, CRIS instead masks random subsets of concepts simultaneously across all layers and heads, then averages the performance drop over thousands of samples to obtain stable, noise-robust importance rankings for every concept and attention head.

## Results

On the tubelet proposal comparison (Table 1), VTCD achieves the best fidelity scores across both TCOW and VideoMAE: positive perturbation scores of 0.102 (TCOW) and 0.094 (VideoMAE), and negative perturbation scores of 0.288 and 0.625, respectively, outperforming the random-crop baseline with occlusion importance (0.174/0.274 and 0.240/0.300). For concept importance ranking (Figure 4), CRIS produces steeper attribution curves than both occlusion-based and gradient-based (Integrated Gradients) baselines, with the gap most pronounced on VideoMAE. As a practical downstream application, pruning the 33% least-important heads in VideoMAE via CRIS improves top-1 accuracy from 37.1% to 41.4% (+4.3%) while reducing FLOPs from 180.5 to 121.5 (−33%). On DAVIS'16 VOS, VTCD-discovered concepts combined with SAM refinement achieve 68.1 mIoU (SSL VideoMAE) and 68.0 mIoU (InternVideo), substantially outperforming raw concept masks (45.0 and 45.8 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTCD consistently outperforms the random-crop tubelet baselines derived from Fel et al. [24] (Lens), Fel et al. [25] (CRAFT), and Yeh et al. [72] (ConceptSHAP) on both TCOW and VideoMAE, achieving lower positive perturbation scores (0.102 vs. 0.174 on TCOW; 0.094 vs. 0.240 on VideoMAE) and higher negative perturbation scores, confirming that feature-space SLIC proposals yield more faithful concepts than pixel-space random crops. Even when the random-crop baseline is equipped with VTCD's own CRIS importance scorer (Baseline + CRIS), VTCD still wins on all four metrics, isolating the benefit to the proposal generation step. For importance scoring, CRIS dramatically outperforms the occlusion-based method [24] on both models and also surpasses the Integrated Gradients approach [37] on VideoMAE, though the two are comparable on TCOW. The practical impact is demonstrated in Table 2, where CRIS-guided head pruning of VideoMAE improves accuracy by 4.3% at 33% FLOP reduction, showing that the importance rankings are not merely diagnostic but actionable for model compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-based interpretability pipeline established for image models (Fel et al., NeurIPS 2023; CRAFT, CVPR 2023) to video transformers, addressing the added spatiotemporal dimension that makes prior 2D proposal and importance methods inapplicable. It adapts Convex NMF (Ding et al., TPAMI 2008) to handle GeLU activations in transformers, and generalizes the Rosetta neuron mining framework (Dravid et al., ICCV 2023) from single neurons in image models to distributed spatiotemporal concepts across video models. The key methodological departure is the introduction of CRIS, a randomized multi-concept masking strategy that overcomes the robustness of self-attention heads to single-concept perturbations—a failure mode absent in CNN-based interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By revealing that spatiotemporal positional bases in early layers and object-centric representations in later layers emerge universally across supervised, self-supervised, and video-language video transformers, the paper provides a unifying lens for understanding video model internals that prior proxy-task analyses (static vs. dynamic information, scene bias) could not capture. The practical demonstration that pruning unimportant heads improves accuracy and that discovered concepts enable competitive VOS without task-specific training suggests these interpretability tools can directly inform model design and compression. Open questions include whether the discovered object-container and physics-related concepts in self-supervised models reflect genuine causal understanding or spurious correlations, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or larger model families.
