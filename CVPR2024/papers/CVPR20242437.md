## Problem

Existing OoD detection methods in semantic segmentation produce pixel-wise anomaly scores, but converting these scores into usable object-level masks requires carefully tuned thresholds that are often unavailable in deployment and still yield fragmented, discontinuous masks. This paper asks whether anomaly scores can be directly converted into clean, complete segmentation masks for OoD objects without any threshold selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a per-pixel anomaly score map (e.g., from RPL or PEBAL) as joint inputs. It first produces bounding box prompts via a Faster R-CNN prompt generator, then feeds those boxes plus the original image into SAM to output a binary segmentation mask covering the entire OoD object. When multiple box prompts are generated, the final mask is the union of all SAM outputs, and no threshold is applied to the anomaly scores for mask generation.

## Method

S2M (Score To Mask) is a two-stage pipeline that decouples anomaly detection from mask generation. A Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50 backbone is trained as a prompt generator that maps anomaly score maps to bounding box prompts, using synthetic training data created via outlier exposure (Cityscapes inliers composited with COCO outliers). The frozen OoD detector (RPL with DeepLabv3+/WiderResNet38) provides the anomaly scores, while only the prompt generator is trained with a box regression loss and 1% random noise augmentation on the score maps. At inference, the generated box prompts are fed into SAM (ViT-B) alongside the original image to produce precise object-level masks, with the union of all masks used when multiple prompts are emitted. The key insight is that box prompts are substantially more robust to noise in anomaly scores than point prompts, and SAM's promptable segmentation capability naturally produces complete, non-fragmented object boundaries.

## Results

S2M achieves IoU of 69.99, 30.46, 77.54, 67.64, and 58.49 on FS Static, FS Lost&Found, SMIYC Anomaly, SMIYC Obstacle, and RoadAnomaly respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline (RPL+CoroCL) by approximately 20% in IoU and 40% in mean F1 on average across all five benchmarks. The method also introduces AuIoU (Area under IoU Curve) as a new metric measuring threshold sensitivity, where S2M scores 64.78 on FS Static versus 8.46 for RPL+CoroCL, demonstrating its threshold-free advantage. On pixel-level metrics, S2M achieves FPR95 of 1.04 and 0.02 on SMIYC Anomaly and Obstacle, substantially lower than RPL+CoroCL (7.18 and 0.09) and Meta-OoD (17.43 and 0.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2M dominates all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitor RPL+CoroCL [32], S2M improves IoU by 33.53 points on FS Static (69.99 vs 36.46), 38.98 points on SMIYC Obstacle (67.64 vs 28.66), and 8.74 points on SMIYC Anomaly (77.54 vs 68.80), while mean F1 gains range from 26 to 59 points. PEBAL [46] and DenseHybrid [17] are substantially weaker, with PEBAL achieving only 6.74 IoU on SMIYC Obstacle versus S2M's 67.64. Synboost [11] is the second-strongest baseline on FS Static (32.81 IoU) but still trails S2M by 37 points. On pixel-level metrics (Table 3), S2M achieves the lowest FPR95 (1.04 on SMIYC Anomaly, 0.02 on SMIYC Obstacle), outperforming Meta-OoD [6] (17.43, 0.41) and RPL+CoroCL (7.18, 0.09); however, Meta-OoD retains a slight AuPRC edge on SMIYC Obstacle (94.14 vs 91.73). The simpler baselines—Maximum softmax [22], Mahalanobis [27], and SML [25]—are far behind on both AuPRC and FPR95, confirming that S2M's prompt-based approach provides a clear advantage over score-thresholding methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2M bridges two previously separate research threads: anomaly score-based OoD detection in semantic segmentation (PEBAL, RPL, Synboost, DenseHybrid), which produces pixel-wise scores requiring fragile thresholding to yield masks, and promptable segmentation models (SAM, CLIPSeg), which generate precise masks from spatial prompts. Rather than improving the anomaly score computation itself, S2M repurposes existing scores as input to a lightweight Faster R-CNN prompt generator that emits box prompts, which are then fed to SAM for mask generation. The training data is synthesized via outlier exposure (OE), a technique already established in the OoD detection literature (Hendrycks et al., RPL), but here applied to train the prompt generator rather than the OoD detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the threshold-selection step, S2M addresses a practical deployment bottleneck that has limited the real-world utility of anomaly score-based OoD detectors; the ~20% IoU and ~40% mean F1 gains across five benchmarks demonstrate that mask quality was the binding constraint, not score quality. The framework's generality—plugging in any anomaly score method and any promptable segmenter—makes it a drop-in upgrade for existing pipelines. Open questions include robustness to extremely small or translucent OoD objects (where the anomaly score map lacks signal), dependence on SAM's generalization to unseen anomaly categories, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation settings.
