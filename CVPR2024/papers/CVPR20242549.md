## Problem

Conventional 3D semantic occupancy prediction methods densely encode all voxels regardless of occupancy status, but over 95% of voxels in outdoor driving scenes represent empty space concentrated in the upper region. This spatial redundancy causes overfitting and incurs cubic computational complexity O(XYZ), while existing efficient representations (BEV, TPV) sacrifice height information or lack a principled mechanism to recover full voxel geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242549/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes monocular RGB images (SemanticKITTI) or multi-camera surround views at 1600×900 (nuScenes) as input and produces a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid with per-voxel class labels (256×256×32 at 0.2m resolution for SemanticKITTI; 200×200×16 at 0.5m resolution for nuScenes). The 2D features are lifted to 3D via the LSS paradigm with discrete depth distributions, and the final prediction is produced by a Mask2Former-style head with bipartite matching over sampled positions, supervised jointly by mask classification loss and depth distribution BCE loss.

## Method

LowRankOcc factorizes 3D voxel tensors into vertical vectors (along the z-axis) and horizontal matrices (XY plane) via a Vertical-Horizontal (VH) decomposition, exploiting the observation that the z-axis has the highest information density with fewer occlusions in driving scenes. A Recursive Residual Decomposition strategy ensures each successive VH component captures progressively higher-frequency information by decomposing the residual after subtracting previously recovered tensors. The 'decomposition-encoding-recovery' pipeline uses only 1D convolutions (for vertical vectors) and 2D convolutions with windowed self-attention and dilated convolutions (for horizontal matrices), completely avoiding 3D convolutions; decomposed components are regrouped into temporary mini-batches for parallel multi-scale feature encoding within 1D and 2D UNet structures. The final low-rank tensor is recovered as a weighted sum of outer products of the updated VH components, achieving O(R(XY+Z)) complexity with R≪Z, and is fed to a Mask2Former occupancy head for semantic prediction.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI test set, LowRankOcc achieves 38.47 SC IoU and 13.56 SSC mIoU, surpassing the previous best (SurroundOcc) by +3.75 SC IoU and +1.70 SSC mIoU. On the nuScenes validation set, it reaches 32.78 SC IoU and 21.51 SSC mIoU, outperforming SurroundOcc (31.49 / 20.30) and OccFormer (31.39 / 19.03) by at least 1.3 points on both metrics. The method uses only 1D/2D convolutions and pooling (no 3D convolutions) while achieving these gains, with 89.5M parameters and 8.4G memory versus 138.2M / 11.9G for a 3D UNet baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LowRankOcc outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both benchmarks. On SemanticKITTI (monocular), it leads SurroundOcc by +3.75 SC IoU (38.47 vs. 34.72) and OccFormer by +3.94 SC IoU (38.47 vs. 34.53), with particularly large per-class gains on vegetation (+7.0 over OccFormer), building (+6.4), fence (+2.5), and pole (+3.2). On nuScenes (multi-camera), the margin is more modest but consistent: +1.29 SC IoU over SurroundOcc and +1.39 over OccFormer, with the largest per-class improvements on car (+2.77 over SurroundOcc), driveable surface (+2.23), and vegetation (+0.80). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that the VH decomposition-based encoding (37.85 IoU, 89.5M params) outperforms a 3D UNet (37.52 IoU, 138.2M params) and a TPV+point-query baseline (35.11 IoU, 88.9M params) while using fewer parameters, confirming that the low-rank factorization is the source of the gain rather than additional model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of classical tensor decomposition theory (CP and VM decompositions from Carroll & Chang and TensorF) and vision-based 3D occupancy prediction. It departs from the dominant dense-voxel paradigm (MonoScene, SurroundOcc) and the tri-plane projection approach (TPVFormer) by arguing that 3D outdoor occupancy data is inherently low-rank, and that explicitly exploiting this structure via a z-axis-specific Vertical-Horizontal decomposition avoids the spatial redundancy that causes overfitting. The LSS lifting mechanism and the Mask2Former-based occupancy head are borrowed directly from prior work, while the recursive residual decomposition strategy and the parallel 1D/2D UNet encoding are novel architectural contributions that replace computationally expensive 3D convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a low-rank representation (R=4) outperforms a full 3D UNet decoder challenges the implicit assumption in the field that denser voxel representations are always beneficial, suggesting that the 95%+ empty-space redundancy actively harms generalization. This opens the question of whether other structured priors (e.g., sparsity patterns, physical constraints) could further reduce the effective rank needed. Limitations include the reliance on a fixed VH rank R and the assumption that z-axis decomposition dominates, which may not hold in urban canyons or multi-level parking scenarios. The approach's efficiency gains (reduced memory and latency) make it practically attractive for real-time autonomous driving deployment.
