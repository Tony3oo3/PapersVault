## Problem

Existing medical zero-shot classification methods rely on hand-crafted cosine similarity to align image and text features, which is suboptimal for the complex, multi-finding relationships between radiology images and reports. Additionally, the design of effective prompts for zero-shot inference in the medical domain remains a manual, labor-intensive challenge that limits generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241936/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a chest X-ray image (resized to 224×224) and a text prompt of the form 'There is [disease].' as input. During training, the text encoder (fine-tuned BioBERT) randomly selects one sentence (max length 97) from each report per iteration, while the image encoder (ViT-B/16 pretrained via M3AE) extracts both local patch-level and global features. The output is a multi-label similarity score per disease class, and the cross-attention maps can be linearly interpolated to produce spatial attention maps for zero-shot lesion grounding.

## Method

CARZero replaces CLIP's cosine similarity with a cross-attention alignment module that directly generates a high-dimensional Similarity Representation (SimR) capturing the complex image-report relationship. For text-to-image alignment, sentence-level text features serve as queries while local image patch features serve as keys and values; for image-to-text, the roles reverse with global image features as queries and word-level text features as keys/values. The SimR is then projected via a learnable linear layer into a low-dimensional similarity matrix, optimized bidirectionally with InfoNCE loss. A second contribution is an LLM-based prompt alignment strategy that uses a large language model to insert the fixed inference prompt template ('There is [disease].') into training reports, ensuring consistent prompt format across training and zero-shot inference without manual prompt search.

## Results

CARZero achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot AUC across all six evaluated CXR datasets, with headline results of 0.810 on PadChest (192 diseases, long-tail), 0.837 on PadChest20 (20 rare-disease classes), 0.811 on ChestXray14, and 0.923 on CheXpert. Notably, the zero-shot AUC of 0.811 on ChestXray14 surpasses the best 1%-fine-tuned baseline (MRM, 0.794) by 1.7 points. On the zero-shot grounding task (Pointing Game) on ChestXDet10, CARZero achieves a mean score of 0.543, outperforming the next-best method MedKLIP (0.481) by 6.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARZero outperforms all seven zero-shot baselines (MedCLIP, BiomedCLIP, GLoRIA, BioViL, CheXzero, MedKLIP, KAD) on every one of the six datasets. The closest competitor is KAD, which CARZero surpasses by 3.1 AUC points on Open-I (0.838 vs 0.807), 6.0 on PadChest (0.810 vs 0.750), 10.2 on PadChest20 (0.837 vs 0.735), 2.2 on ChestXray14 (0.811 vs 0.789), 1.8 on CheXpert (0.923 vs 0.905), and 6.1 on ChestXDet10 (0.796 vs 0.735). The largest margins appear on the long-tail PadChest20 rare-disease subset and on ChestXDet10 grounding, suggesting the cross-attention SimR is particularly effective for underrepresented and spatially localized findings. In the 1%-fine-tuning comparison (Table 2), CARZero's zero-shot score of 0.811 on ChestXray14 exceeds MRM (0.794), KAD (0.787), MGCA (0.782), MedKLIP (0.772), and GLoRIA (0.707), all of which were fine-tuned on 1% of labeled data. On the Pointing Game grounding metric, CARZero leads MedKLIP by 6.2 points in mean score and achieves the best per-disease scores on 7 of 10 categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARZero builds directly on the CLIP contrastive learning framework (dual-encoder, InfoNCE loss) and the medical VLP lineage of ConVIRT, GLoRIA, CheXzero, MedKLIP, and KAD, all of which align image and text features via hand-crafted cosine similarity. The key departure is replacing this fixed similarity metric with a learned cross-attention module that produces a high-dimensional Similarity Representation (SimR), which is then projected to a scalar similarity score — a 'third type' of cross-attention use distinct from modality fusion (ALBEF, BLIP) and modality projection (MGCA, TEFAL). Additionally, the LLM-based prompt alignment strategy repurposes large language models to inject consistent prompt templates into training reports, a practical twist on the broader prompt-learning literature (CoOP, MaPLe) applied here to the medical domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for radiology: CARZero's zero-shot AUC of 0.811 on ChestXray14 exceeds the 0.794 achieved by fine-tuning on 1% of labeled data, and its 0.837 AUC on PadChest20 (20 rare-disease classes) suggests a viable path toward diagnosing conditions with scarce annotations. The cross-attention alignment mechanism is simple and modular, making it a drop-in replacement for cosine similarity in existing medical VLP pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond chest X-rays to other modalities, whether the MLP projection (which marginally outperforms linear) hints at even richer structure in SimR, and whether the LLM prompt alignment step can be replaced by learned prompts to reduce dependency on a specific LLM.
