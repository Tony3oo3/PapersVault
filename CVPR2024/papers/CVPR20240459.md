## Problem

Event-based keypoint detection suffers from three intertwined limitations: ground-truth event keypoints are unavailable (existing methods rely on SuperPoint trained on conventional images, producing poor supervisory signals), CNN-based descriptors have limited receptive fields and lose discriminability under event noise, and fixed detectors trained on one dataset cannot adapt to the diverse keypoint patterns encountered across different event streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240459/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (pixel coordinates, timestamps, polarity) and encodes them into 240×180 artificial frames via a modified Tencode representation that couples polarity and time into RGB channels. It produces a single-channel keypoint heatmap per frame, from which discrete keypoints are extracted by local-maxima filtering and a threshold constraint. The full pipeline runs at 9 ms per frame on a single GPU.

## Method

SD2Event comprises two modules trained end-to-end in a self-supervised manner. The Contextual Feature Descriptor Learning (CFDL) module introduces M=32 learnable descriptor agents that attend over flattened event features (extracted by a two-layer MLP) via multi-head attention; each event feature is then concatenated with its most similar updated agent, and a final MLP fuses the results into contextual descriptors—capturing long-range context while suppressing irrelevant noise. The Dynamic Keypoint Detector Learning (DKDL) module initializes N=16 detector prototypes that attend over the contextual descriptors to form detector agents, which are further refined by attending over the updated descriptor agents (serving as cluster centers); a final MLP fuses these into input-adaptive detectors. Training uses SE(3) camera-pose warps to create event-frame pairs, with a cosine-similarity loss on the corresponding heatmap pairs, a peaky loss encouraging salient responses, and a diversity loss keeping agents distinct.

## Results

On the Event-Camera dataset, SD2Event achieves a reprojection error of 1.64 px, outperforming the best prior method SILC (2.16 px) by 0.52 px. On N-Caltech101, it reaches 0.89 IoU in feature matching, surpassing EventPoint (0.83) by 6%. On HVGA ATIS Corner, SD2Event obtains reprojection errors of 0.67/0.76/0.93 px at 25/50/100 ms intervals, improving over EventPoint (1.27/1.41/1.72 px) by 0.60–0.79 px across all intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD2Event outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest data-driven competitor EventPoint [15], it reduces reprojection error by 0.60–0.79 px on HVGA ATIS Corner and improves IoU by 6% on N-Caltech101. Compared to the best hand-crafted methods (evFast [20], evHarris [29], Arc [1]), the gains are even larger: on Event-Camera, SD2Event's 1.64 px beats evHarris (2.46 px) and evFast (2.50 px) by roughly 0.8–0.9 px. Against SILC [18], the improvement is 0.52 px on Event-Camera and 1.78–2.75 px on HVGA ATIS Corner. DART [25] (0.67–0.72 IoU) and Chiberre et al. [6] (2.56 px at 25 ms) are also clearly surpassed. No baseline matches or exceeds SD2Event on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

SD2Event extends the data-driven line of event-based keypoint detection initiated by SILC and EventPoint, but departs from both in three fundamental ways. Where EventPoint relies on SuperPoint (trained on conventional images) to generate supervisory signals and uses a CNN backbone with a fixed detector, SD2Event replaces these with self-supervised camera-pose transformations, an agent-based attention mechanism for long-range context, and input-adaptive dynamic detectors. The event encoding builds directly on EventPoint's Tencode representation [15] and the Time Surface paradigm [3], but couples polarity and timestamp more tightly. Conceptually, the agent-based attention draws on the learnable-query attention from super-feature learning [31] and the broader transformer/attention literature, repurposing it as a noise-filtering mechanism specific to the sparse, asynchronous nature of event streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Event-based keypoint detection underpins downstream tasks like event SLAM, 3D reconstruction, and optical flow, so robust detectors with discriminative descriptors have direct practical value. The dynamic-detector idea—where the detector itself is generated per input rather than fixed after training—is a conceptually useful departure that could transfer to other modalities with diverse pattern distributions. However, the evaluation is limited to three relatively small benchmarks with a modest set of baselines, and the self-supervised signal (random SE(3) warping) is a standard technique borrowed from image-based self-supervised detection; its specific advantage over EventPoint's SuperPoint-based signal is demonstrated but not deeply analyzed. Open questions include scalability to higher-resolution event streams, real-time deployment on embedded hardware, and whether the dynamic-detector paradigm generalizes beyond the tested datasets.
