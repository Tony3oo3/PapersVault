## Problem

Existing federated prompt learning methods lack specialized mechanisms to handle severe data heterogeneity involving both label shift and feature shift simultaneously. Methods like pFedPrompt rely on a single shared text prompt plus a personalized attention module, which is inadequate when clients exhibit substantial domain discrepancies or imbalanced class distributions. The paper asks how to systematically balance global consensus knowledge with client-specific category traits in a federated prompt learning setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242255/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client feeds RGB images through a frozen CLIP image encoder (ViT-B/16 or ResNet-50) to obtain a visual feature map of V patch embeddings plus a class token, while text prompts (global and local) are encoded by a frozen text encoder into two d_f-dimensional vectors per class. The output is a class probability distribution computed via unbalanced OT-based Wasserstein distances between the visual feature map and the concatenated global-local textual features, replacing the standard cosine-similarity matching of vanilla CLIP. Only the s learnable prompt vectors in each prompt are optimized; all backbone parameters remain frozen.

## Method

FedOTP equips each client with two prompts—a global prompt P_g aggregated across clients via weighted averaging at the server, and a local prompt P_{l,i} retained on the client—to simultaneously capture consensus and client-specific category traits. The key mechanism is unbalanced Optimal Transport: the visual feature map G_m ∈ R^{V×d_f} and the concatenated textual features H_k = [H_{k,g}, H_{k,l}] ∈ R^{d_f×2} are treated as discrete distributions, and a transport plan T is computed by minimizing a cost matrix C = 1 − G_m^T H_k under the relaxed constraint T·1_2 ≤ α (instead of equality), allowing prompts to attend only to the most relevant image patches. A fast Dykstra's algorithm with entropic regularization solves the unbalanced OT in a few iterations, and the resulting Wasserstein distance d_{C,k} replaces cosine similarity in the softmax prediction. The transport plan is fixed during gradient descent, and both global and local prompt vectors are jointly optimized via cross-entropy, so the final prediction is a synthesis of information from both prompts rather than a simple addition of their individual scores.

## Results

On the Pathological Non-IID setting (10 clients, non-overlapping classes), FedOTP achieves 92.73% on Food101, 87.67% on DTD, 97.02% on Caltech101, 96.23% on Flowers102, and 98.82% on OxfordPets, outperforming the strongest baseline pFedPrompt by up to 9.8 points (Flowers102) and 7.0 points (OxfordPets). On CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 with 100 clients under Dirichlet(α=0.3) partitioning, FedOTP reaches 96.05% and 78.03% respectively, surpassing CoOp by ~3 points. On DomainNet with combined feature and label shifts, FedOTP achieves 48.08% average accuracy versus 44.32% for CoOp and 10.29% for PromptFL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedOTP outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset in the main comparison. Against the strongest federated baseline pFedPrompt [26], FedOTP gains +0.47 on Food101, +10.53 on DTD, +0.48 on Caltech101, +9.77 on Flowers102, and +6.98 on OxfordPets, with the largest margins on datasets with more severe label imbalance. Against local-training baselines, FedOTP surpasses CoOp [78] by 10.19 points on DTD and 4.30 on OxfordPets, and Zero-Shot CLIP [60] by up to 58.6 points (DTD). Among the adapted PFL variants, PromptFL+FedPer [1] and PromptFL+FedAMP [32] suffer the largest drops (e.g., 71.29% and 74.48% on Food101 respectively), while PromptFL+FedProx [42] and PromptFL+FT [24] remain closer to the base PromptFL [27] but still trail FedOTP by 15–20 points on most datasets. On the 100-client CIFAR-10/100 setting, FedOTP leads CoOp by 2.94 and 3.20 points respectively, and PromptFL by 3.75 and 4.36 points, confirming consistent gains at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated prompt learning line initiated by PromptFL and pFedPrompt, which either learn a single shared prompt or pair a shared text prompt with a personalized visual attention module. It departs from both by introducing a dual global+local prompt architecture where each client simultaneously learns a shared prompt (aggregated at the server) and a private prompt (retained locally), and from PLOT's use of classical OT by replacing it with unbalanced OT that relaxes one marginal constraint, allowing prompts to attend only to the most relevant image patches. The Dykstra's algorithm implementation for fast unbalanced OT solving is borrowed from the noisy-label literature (Chang et al., 2023) and adapted here for the vision-language alignment setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedOTP provides a principled mechanism to handle both label shift and feature shift simultaneously in federated prompt learning, a combination that prior methods (e.g., pFedPrompt) address only partially. The generalization bound (Theorem 1) gives theoretical grounding for the global-local split, and the clean ablation hierarchy (unbalanced OT > classical OT > similarity averaging) validates each design choice. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to novel clients unseen during training (acknowledged by the authors), whether it scales to hundreds of clients with very few local samples, and whether the unbalanced OT relaxation transfers to other VLM architectures beyond CLIP.
