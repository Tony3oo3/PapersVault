## Problem

Existing super-resolution and image generation methods operate only at fixed integer scale factors, suffer from over-smoothing and artifacts, and fail to maintain consistency across different scales. The closest prior work, IDM, embeds implicit neural representations inside the denoising U-Net at every diffusion step, causing exponential memory growth and slow inference as output resolution increases, while still producing poor scale consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240100/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For super-resolution, the method takes a low-resolution RGB image and an arbitrary (non-integer) target scale factor; the LR image is linearly interpolated to the latent resolution and its encoder features are concatenated with intermediate denoising feature maps as conditioning. For generation, the input is pure Gaussian noise in the latent space. The output is a continuous-resolution RGB image at the requested scale, with the MLP decoder queried at arbitrary 2D pixel coordinates, enabling scales from 1× up to 200× without retraining.

## Method

The architecture decouples the diffusion process from the implicit decoding: a pre-trained auto-encoder (encoder + symmetric convolutional decoder without upsampling layers) defines the latent space, a U-Net-based latent diffusion model denoises in that space, and a series combination of the auto-decoder and a 4-layer MLP (LIIF-style) maps the denoised latent to pixel values at arbitrary coordinates. A key design choice is that the decoder is frozen during diffusion training, so the implicit network is invoked only once at inference rather than at every denoising step, yielding ~12.7× faster inference than IDM. A two-stage alignment process fine-tunes the diffusion model by backpropagating an image-space reconstruction loss (weighted by α̅_t/(1−α̅_t)) through the fixed decoder to the predicted z_0, combined with the standard denoising objective, reducing misalignment errors between the latent and pixel spaces. Sampling uses DDIM for accelerated inference.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ arbitrary-scale SR, the method achieves PSNR of 24.66 / 24.13 / 23.64 / 23.52 dB and LPIPS of 0.0455 / 0.0690 / 0.1110 / 0.1427 at 5.3×, 7×, 10×, and 12×, outperforming IDM at every scale. On LSUN Bedroom image generation, it attains FID of 7.20–7.73 across 128/160/192 resolutions with SelfSSIM ≥ 0.95, surpassing ScaleParty (FID 9.85–9.91) and MSPIE (FID 10.15–16.45). Inference is approximately 12.7× faster than IDM at 8× (0.257 vs 0.020 FPS) and can generate 200× upsampling (0.098 FPS) where IDM fails due to memory overflow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IDM (the closest prior work), the proposed method wins on both PSNR and LPIPS at all five CelebA-HQ scales (e.g., 24.66 vs 23.34 PSNR and 0.0455 vs 0.0526 LPIPS at 5.3×), and on LSUN Tower at 16× it improves LPIPS from 0.2549 to 0.1897 while also gaining PSNR (21.24 vs 19.44); on LSUN Bedroom at 16× it trades a marginal 0.25 dB PSNR loss for a small LPIPS gain. Versus LIIF, the method matches or exceeds PSNR at in-distribution scales but substantially reduces LPIPS (e.g., 0.0455 vs 0.1207 at 5.3×), and at out-of-distribution 17× on DIV2K it leads on LPIPS (0.6225 vs 0.6560). Against SR3, the method dominates on both metrics at every comparable scale (e.g., 23.64 vs 20.25 PSNR at 10×). For image generation, the method outperforms ScaleParty on FID (7.20–7.73 vs 9.85–9.91) and SelfSSIM (≥0.95 vs 0.89–0.95), and beats MSPIE by a wide margin on both FID and consistency. Inference speed is the most dramatic advantage: the method runs at 0.257 FPS at 8× versus IDM's 0.020 FPS (~12.7× faster) and remains functional at 200× where IDM cannot allocate memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., 2022) with Local Implicit Image Functions (Chen et al., 2021) into a unified pipeline for arbitrary-scale image generation and super-resolution. The key architectural departure from the most relevant prior work, IDM (Gao et al., 2023), is that the implicit neural decoder is applied only once after the full latent-space denoising process, rather than at every diffusion step as in IDM's pixel-space approach. This separation, combined with a two-stage alignment loss that backpropagates image-space reconstruction errors through the frozen decoder into the diffusion model, is the paper's central methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.7× inference speedup over IDM at 8× scale and the ability to generate at 200× magnification (where IDM fails due to memory overflow) make arbitrary-scale diffusion-based SR practically deployable on a single GPU. The unified framework handling both SR and novel image generation at arbitrary scales with strong SelfSSIM consistency is a useful consolidation for practitioners. However, the method remains slower than GAN- or regression-based SR, and the two-stage training pipeline (pre-train AE, train LDM, then alignment fine-tune) adds practical complexity. Open questions include whether the alignment strategy generalizes to other latent spaces (e.g., video, 3D) and whether the approach can be extended to conditional generation tasks like text-to-image at arbitrary resolutions.
