## Problem

Current WSI representation learning relies on weakly supervised MIL, producing slide embeddings tightly coupled to specific clinical tasks and limiting generalization under data scarcity. Attention-based MIL architectures excel at 'needle-in-a-haystack' localization but lack expressivity for 'panoramic' tasks (e.g., survival prediction) that require integrating spatial heterogeneity, proportions, and mixtures of distinct morphological concepts across the tissue microenvironment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input is a WSI at 20× magnification (0.5 μm/pixel) tessellated into non-overlapping 256×256 patches, each encoded by UNI (ViT-L/16 DINOv2 pretrained on ~10⁸ patches from ~10⁶ WSIs) into a d-dimensional embedding. Output is a fixed-length slide embedding z_WSI ∈ R^(C·(1+2d)) formed by concatenating per-prototype GMM parameters (mixture weight π_c, mean μ_c, diagonal covariance Σ_c) for C=8–32 shared prototypes, plus a per-patch prototype assignment map for spatial interpretability. The embedding is computed per-slide via EM, so the same shared prototypes yield different parameter estimates for each WSI.

## Method

PANTHER models the set of patch embeddings in a WSI as samples from a C-component Gaussian Mixture Model, where each component represents a morphological prototype. The EM algorithm estimates per-slide GMM parameters (mixture probabilities, means, diagonal covariances) initialized with K-means centroids from the cohort-aggregated patch set; a single EM step suffices for convergence. The slide embedding is the concatenation of all estimated GMM parameters across prototypes, explicitly encoding both cardinality (via π_c) and morphological description (via μ_c, Σ_c) for each visual concept. This generalizes optimal transport (which assumes uniform mixture weights) and differs from counting or averaging baselines by preserving per-prototype deep features alongside their proportions. For downstream prediction, a structured MLP applies per-prototype nonlinear mappings g_c^indiv before a final prediction head, exploiting the decomposable structure of the concatenated embedding.

## Results

On survival prediction across six TCGA cancer types (disease-specific survival, c-index), PANTHER_All+MLP achieves 0.758 on BRCA, 0.757 on UCEC, 0.716 on KIRC, and 0.685 on LUAD, outperforming or matching the best supervised baseline (TransMIL) on 5 of 6 cancer types. On subtyping, PANTHER_All+MLP reaches 0.980 balanced accuracy on TCGA-NSCLC (matching DSMIL) and 0.931 Cohen's κ on PANDA Radboud (best overall), while remaining competitive on EBRAINS fine subtyping (0.693 balanced accuracy vs. TransMIL's 0.701). External validation on CPTAC-KIRC (0.691) and NLST-LUAD (0.634) further confirms generalization, with PANTHER leading all baselines on both external sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANTHER_All+MLP outperforms the strongest supervised baseline TransMIL on 5 of 6 TCGA survival tasks (e.g., BRCA 0.758 vs. 0.612, UCEC 0.757 vs. 0.695, KIRC 0.716 vs. 0.671), losing only on CRC (0.665 vs. 0.684). Against the best unsupervised baseline OT, PANTHER wins on 7 of 9 evaluation columns, with the closest margin on LUAD-TCGA (0.685 vs. 0.687) and a clear gap on external CPTAC-KIRC (0.691 vs. 0.650) and NLST-LUAD (0.634 vs. 0.495). Among prototype-based unsupervised methods, PANTHER substantially exceeds H2T (e.g., KIRC 0.716 vs. 0.703, LUAD-NLST 0.634 vs. 0.603) and ProtoCounts (e.g., UCEC 0.757 vs. 0.441), confirming that encoding both prototype identity and cardinality via GMM parameters is critical. On subtyping, PANTHER_All+MLP matches or exceeds TransMIL on NSCLC (0.980 vs. 0.959) and PANDA (0.931 vs. 0.922 Cohen's κ), while trailing slightly on EBRAINS fine (0.693 vs. 0.701). The ablation PANTHER_WA+lin. (weighted averaging instead of concatenation) drops 3–10 c-index points across tasks, validating that per-prototype concatenation rather than pooling is essential for preserving discriminative morphological information.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANTHER extends the differentiable EM framework for set representation learning (Kim, ICLR 2022) and the trainable optimal transport embedding (Mialon et al., ICLR 2021) to the computational pathology domain, where the 'set' is a collection of WSI patch embeddings and the 'prototypes' are morphological exemplars. It departs from prior unsupervised slide representation methods (H2T, ProtoCounts/HPL) by encoding full GMM parameters—mixture probability, mean, and covariance—rather than only cluster counts or averaged centroids, and by using soft EM assignments instead of hard K-means. The paper also explicitly positions PANTHER as a generalization of the OT baseline, showing that OT with uniform mixture weights is a special case of the GMM formulation, and contrasts with supervised MIL (ABMIL, TransMIL, AttnMISL) by being entirely task-agnostic in its representation construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PANTHER demonstrates that a simple, unsupervised GMM-based aggregation can match or exceed supervised MIL across 13 subtyping and survival datasets, offering a practical, task-agnostic slide embedding that also yields per-prototype interpretability maps validated by a board-certified pathologist. The ablation showing that concatenation of prototype parameters substantially outperforms weighted averaging challenges the common pooling intuition and provides a clear design principle for future set-aggregation methods. Key limitations include a fixed number of prototypes (C=16) across all cancer types, reliance on a single EM step, and the absence of a data-driven mechanism for selecting C; future work on more expressive mixture models and evaluation on rare-cancer cohorts with very small sample sizes remains open.
