## Problem

Existing zero-shot sketch-based image retrieval (ZS-SBIR) methods rely on discriminatively pre-trained backbones (ImageNet CNNs, ViTs, CLIP) that are inherently biased toward texture over shape, creating a bottleneck for sketch-photo matching where sketches lack texture cues. Text-to-image diffusion models, pre-trained with generative objectives on large-scale image-text corpora, have never been explored as feature extractors for cross-modal retrieval despite their known shape bias and cross-modal alignment properties.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256×3 sketch (or photo) as input, encodes it via a frozen VAE to a 32×32×4 latent, adds Gaussian noise at time-step t=273, and feeds the noisy latent through the frozen UNet alongside a learned visual prompt (a border-only pixel-space perturbation with width d=16) and a learned 77×1024 continuous textual prompt. The output is a fixed-dimensional feature vector—1280-dim (mean of upsampling layers 1–2) for category-level retrieval or 960-dim (concatenation of layers 3–4) for fine-grained retrieval—obtained via global max-pooling and averaged over 6 stochastic forward passes. In the sketch+text extension, actual class names or captions replace the learned textual prompt.

## Method

The core contribution is using a frozen Stable Diffusion v2.1 UNet as a backbone feature extractor for ZS-SBIR, exploiting the finding that its intermediate upsampling block features encode robust cross-modal shape representations that naturally align sketches with photos. Three design choices make this work: (i) layer selection—earlier upsampling blocks (n=1,2) capture coarse shape/structure for category-level retrieval while later blocks (n=3,4) capture fine-grained details for instance-level matching; (ii) visual prompting—a learnable spatial perturbation confined to the image border (2×3×d×(h+w−2d) parameters) is added to the input to adapt the frozen model to the retrieval task without fine-tuning; (iii) textual prompt learning—a continuous 77×d embedding matrix replaces actual text, influencing feature extraction via cross-attention to leverage SD's visio-linguistic prior. Only these prompts are trained via a standard triplet loss (margin μ) with the entire SD model (UNet + VAE encoder) kept frozen, and feature ensembling over 6 noise samples mitigates the stochasticity of the forward diffusion process. This avoids the catastrophic forgetting that occurs when fine-tuning the generative UNet with discriminative losses, as confirmed by ablations showing B-Fine-Tuning and B-Linear-Probing baselines fail drastically.

## Results

On category-level ZS-SBIR, the method achieves mAP@200 of 0.746 on Sketchy, mAP@all of 0.680 on TU-Berlin, and mAP@all of 0.231 on Quick, Draw!, surpassing the strongest SoTA (ZS-LVM) by 2.3, 2.9, and 2.9 points respectively. On cross-category ZS-FG-SBIR (Sketchy), it reaches Acc.@1 of 31.94% and Acc.@5 of 65.81%, outperforming the best prior method (CC-LVM) by 3.26 and 3.47 points. The paper reports an average mAP@200 gain of 43.96% over SoTA ZS-SBIR methods on Sketchy and a 32.49% average Acc.@1 margin over SoTA ZS-FG-SBIR methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. The strongest SoTA competitor, ZS-LVM [77] (CLIP-based prompt learning), is beaten on all three category-level benchmarks (e.g., 0.746 vs 0.723 mAP@200 on Sketchy) and on ZS-FG-SBIR (31.94% vs 28.68% Acc.@1). Earlier SoTA methods show progressively larger gaps: ZS-Sketch3T [76] (0.579), ZS-SAKE [51] (0.497), ZS-GRL [15] (0.369), and ZS-CVAE [102] (0.225) on Sketchy mAP@200. Among the self-designed baselines, B-Fine-Tuning (0.115) and B-Linear-Probing (0.441) confirm that directly fine-tuning or probing the frozen SD UNet destroys its generative prior, while B-Triplet+VP with discriminative backbones (VGG 0.651, ResNet 0.326, ViT 0.681) shows that the generative pre-training of SD is essential even when the same visual prompt mechanism is applied. For ZS-FG-SBIR, the method also surpasses CC-Gen [61] (22.60%) and CC-Grad [84] (13.40%) by wide margins, and for sketch+text retrieval (Table 5) it leads all baselines including SceneTrilogy [10] and TaskFormer [81] on category-level and fine-grained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the SBIR backbone lineage, which has relied on discriminatively pre-trained models (ImageNet CNNs, ViT, CLIP) for cross-modal feature extraction. Instead, it repurposes a text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) trained purely on a generative objective as a frozen feature extractor, drawing on the visual prompting mechanism from Bahng et al. [1] to adapt the model without fine-tuning. The conceptual shift is from 'use a model trained to discriminate' to 'use a model trained to generate,' with the hypothesis that generative pre-training on large-scale image-text corpora (LAION) implicitly encodes shape priors more robustly than discriminative pre-training encodes texture priors. The prompt learning framework (learnable visual border prompts + continuous textual prompt embeddings) is positioned as a lightweight alternative to the fine-tuning, knowledge distillation, or test-time adaptation strategies used in prior zero-shot SBIR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that generative pre-training confers a shape bias that is particularly well-suited to sketch-photo matching, where texture cues are absent — a finding supported by the cue-conflict experiment (72.82% vs. 49.14% for VGG-16) and the 43.96% mAP@200 gain over the strongest CLIP-based baseline. The practical implication is that frozen diffusion models, already widely deployed, can serve as retrieval backbones with only a few thousand learnable prompt parameters, avoiding the risk of catastrophic forgetting from fine-tuning. Open questions include the computational overhead of 6× stochastic feature extraction for ensembling, whether the approach generalizes to other cross-modal retrieval tasks beyond sketches, and how it scales to very large galleries or open-vocabulary settings beyond the 125-class Sketchy benchmark.
