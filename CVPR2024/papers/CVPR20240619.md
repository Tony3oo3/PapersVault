## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion frequently fail to respect spatial layout instructions in prompts, especially superlative relations (e.g., 'a flower on the left') that are underrepresented in existing benchmarks. Prior solutions require training auxiliary modules or fine-tuning on layout-annotated data, making them resource-intensive and incompatible with rapid base-model iterations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240619/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a single RGB image as output, operating entirely within the inference pipeline of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. Unlike competing methods that require user-provided bounding boxes, SimM autonomously derives target object positions by parsing the prompt with dependency parsing and heuristic rules, so no additional layout annotation is needed at inference time.

## Method

SimM follows a training-free 'check-locate-rectify' pipeline that intervenes on cross-attention maps during denoising. In the check stage, it parses the prompt to assign target bounding boxes and compares them against a layer-averaged attention map at the first denoising step to detect misplacement. In the locate stage, it temporally merges attention maps over early denoising steps and uses a sliding window to find where each object's activations actually reside. In the rectify stage, it directly transfers activation values from the source region to the target box, then applies intra-map enhancement/suppression (scaling by α inside the target box and dividing by α outside) and inter-map suppression (multiplying other objects' maps by a softmax-derived mask) to prevent positional conflicts. The entire process modifies pre-softmax attention values without any gradient updates or loss-based optimization, keeping computational overhead negligible.

## Results

On DrawBench, SimM achieves 53.00% generation accuracy and 0.3423 CLIP-Score, outperforming the strongest baseline (Attention-Refocusing at 43.50%) by 9.5 percentage points. On the newly introduced SimMBench (203 prompts with superlative spatial relations), SimM reaches 65.16% accuracy and 0.3001 CLIP-Score, surpassing the best baseline (Attention-Refocusing at 50.71%) by 14.45 percentage points. SimM also attains the highest CLIP-Score on DrawBench (0.3423), though its CLIP-Score on SimMBench (0.3001) is marginally below BoxDiff (0.3032).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimM outperforms all four baselines on generation accuracy across both datasets. On DrawBench (relative spatial relations), SimM leads Attention-Refocusing (43.50%) by 9.5 points and Layout-Guidance (36.50%) by 16.5 points. On SimMBench (superlative spatial relations), the gap widens: SimM (65.16%) beats Attention-Refocusing (50.71%) by 14.45 points and BoxDiff (24.08%) by over 41 points, indicating that the check-locate-rectify pipeline is particularly effective for superlative layout descriptions that prior methods struggle with. In terms of CLIP-Score, SimM achieves the best result on DrawBench (0.3423) but is slightly behind BoxDiff (0.3032) on SimMBench (0.3001), though the paper notes CLIP-Score is a limited metric for spatial understanding. Qualitatively, the baselines exhibit blockiness, tearing artifacts, and object deformations, whereas SimM preserves image quality by directly transferring activations rather than imposing loss-based constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimM builds directly on Stable Diffusion's cross-attention architecture, intervening at inference time to modify attention activations rather than retraining or fine-tuning any parameters. It departs from the dominant line of training-free layout control (Layout-Guidance, Attention-Refocusing, BoxDiff) that relies on gradient-based loss updates during denoising, instead performing direct activation transfer and intra-/inter-map enhancement/suppression with negligible computational overhead. It also diverges from prior layout-to-image methods that require user-provided bounding boxes or LLM-generated layouts, opting for a lightweight dependency-parsing-plus-heuristic-rules pipeline to auto-generate target positions. The introduction of superlative spatial relations (e.g., 'on the left' meaning leftmost) as a first-class layout requirement extends the scope beyond the relative relations ('to the left of') that prior benchmarks and methods have addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, inference-time intervention design makes SimM immediately applicable to any Stable Diffusion checkpoint without retraining, addressing a practical pain point as base models iterate rapidly. The SimMBench benchmark (203 prompts with superlative relations) fills a genuine evaluation gap, since DrawBench only covers relative spatial relations. However, the heuristic layout generation is limited in expressiveness compared to LLM-based approaches, the method is validated only on SD (not other diffusion architectures), and the reliance on a fixed threshold for the 'check' stage may not generalize to all prompt complexities. Open questions include whether the activation-transfer paradigm extends to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) and whether the heuristic rules can be replaced by learned components without sacrificing the training-free property.
