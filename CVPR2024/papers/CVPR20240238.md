## Problem

Creating high-quality, relightable textures for 3D assets traditionally requires professional artists or densely sampled multi-view captures with precisely aligned geometry, while existing learning-based methods are restricted to category-specific shapes. Text-guided diffusion approaches cannot faithfully capture the complex, detailed patterns present in real-world images. The paper addresses how to transfer highly detailed, semantically meaningful textures from just 3–5 casually captured images to arbitrary target meshes across different object categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–5 casually captured RGB images (background masked to white, resized to 512px shorter edge with random 512×512 crops) and a target 3D mesh with UV mapping. It produces a neural implicit BRDF field (multi-scale hash encoding + small MLP) parameterizing diffuse albedo, roughness, and metallic, which can be queried at any surface point and exported as standard 2D texture maps compatible with graphics pipelines. Normal maps rendered from the target mesh serve as an additional conditioning signal during optimization.

## Method

TextureDreamer introduces Personalized Geometry-aware Score Distillation (PGSD), a three-component optimization. First, Dreambooth fine-tunes a latent diffusion model on the sparse input images with a unique text token [V] to distill texture appearance into model weights. Second, instead of standard SDS, the method uses Variational Score Distillation (VSD) with a LoRA network to denoise rendered images, avoiding the over-smoothing and oversaturation caused by high classifier-free guidance weights. Third, a pre-trained ControlNet conditioned on normal maps rendered from the target mesh is injected into both the personalized and generic diffusion models to enforce geometry-texture alignment. Key design choices include setting CFG weight to 1.0 (no guidance), removing LoRA from the generic denoiser ε_φ while retaining only the camera embedding, and initializing ε_φ with original pre-trained weights to preserve texture fidelity. The texture is optimized as a neural BRDF field rendered via Nvdiffrecast under a fixed HDR environment map with a white background matching the Dreambooth training condition.

## Results

On CLIP similarity (measuring texture fidelity to input images), TextureDreamer achieves 0.8296, outperforming Latent-Paint (0.7969) and TEXTure (0.7988). In a user study with 20 raters across 24 questions per task, TextureDreamer is preferred over Latent-Paint on image fidelity (71.82%), texture photorealism (77.03%), and shape-texture consistency (78.49%), and over TEXTure on the same metrics (69.43%, 85.52%, and 85.16% respectively). The method operates on 3–5 casually captured images and transfers textures to arbitrary meshes from 3D-FUTURE and online repositories across four object categories (sofa, bed, mug/bowl, plush toy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextureDreamer is compared against two recent text-guided texturing methods that also support image input: Latent-Paint [38] and TEXTure [55]. Latent-Paint uses Texture Inversion to encode image information into text embeddings and then applies standard SDS for texture distillation; it tends to produce textures with colors and patterns diverging from the input images. TEXTure combines Texture Inversion with Dreambooth fine-tuning and an iterative mesh painting algorithm; it preserves color better than Latent-Paint but exhibits visible seams from the iterative painting process. TextureDreamer outperforms both baselines on all three user-study metrics and on CLIP similarity (0.8296 vs. 0.7969/0.7988). The largest margin over TEXTure is in texture photorealism (85.52% preference) and shape-texture consistency (85.16%), attributed to the geometry-aware ControlNet conditioning and the VSD-based optimization that avoids the over-smoothing and multi-face artifacts of SDS. Qualitatively, TextureDreamer produces seamless, geometry-aware textures that respect semantic structure (e.g., placing eyes correctly on a face), whereas baselines show misalignment or seam artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextureDreamer unifies three recent diffusion-model components—Dreambooth for personalized texture extraction from sparse images, ControlNet for geometry-aware spatial conditioning, and Variational Score Distillation (VSD) from ProlificDreamer for photorealistic optimization—into a single framework (PGSD) that prior work had not combined. It departs from text-guided texturing methods (TEXTure, Latent-Paint) by conditioning on actual input images rather than text captions, and from Dreambooth3D by targeting texture transfer to arbitrary, potentially cross-category geometry rather than whole-object generation. The key methodological departures from vanilla VSD are the removal of LoRA weights in the generic denoiser and the elimination of classifier-free guidance, both motivated by the overfitting behavior of Dreambooth-finetuned models on small image sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically significant bottleneck in 3D content pipelines: texture creation currently relies on professional artists and expensive manual workflows, and prior automated methods either require dense multi-view captures with aligned geometry or are restricted to category-specific shapes. By enabling cross-category, relightable texture transfer from just 3–5 casually captured images, the work opens a path toward democratizing texture creation for AR/VR, gaming, and robotics. Remaining limitations—baking lighting into albedo under strong specular highlights, Janus artifacts when input views are incomplete, and inability to transfer unique non-repeating patterns—suggest that robust, fully relightable texture synthesis from truly sparse and uncontrolled captures remains an open problem.
