## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation requires recognizing arbitrary objects in point clouds without predefined categories, but (point cloud, text) pairs are extremely scarce compared to (image, text) pairs. Existing methods that bridge 3D and text via images either align point features with image-level features (coarse, indirect alignment) or generate scene-level captions (lacking the fine-grained, per-point guidance needed for dense segmentation). This paper tackles the gap by establishing a direct, fine-grained correspondence between individual 3D points and entity-level text descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a 3D point cloud paired with multi-view 2D images (e.g., from ScanNet's RGB-D captures) to generate (point, EntityText) supervision pairs; during inference, it operates solely on the 3D point cloud with no additional images, taking an arbitrary set of text class names as queries. The 3D backbone is SparseConvNet operating on sparse voxels, and the text encoder is the frozen CLIP model. Output is a per-point semantic label assigned by maximum cosine similarity between the point feature and the CLIP text embedding of each candidate class, supporting both annotation-free and base-annotated evaluation protocols.

## Method

OV3D builds a fine-grained point-to-text pipeline in three stages: (1) a Large Vision-Language Model (LLaVA-1.5) is prompted in a chain-of-thought conversation to generate entity-level text descriptions (EntityText) — both names and diverse contextual phrases — for each object in multi-view images; (2) a vision foundation model (SEEM) produces class-agnostic segments, and a vision-language model assigns each segment the most similar EntityText via cosine similarity in a joint embedding space, yielding pixel-to-EntityText correspondence; (3) 3D points are projected onto image planes and linked to EntityText through segment membership, producing (point, EntityText) pairs. Training aligns SparseConvNet point features with CLIP text embeddings of the associated EntityText using a mean cosine-similarity loss, embedding point features directly into the CLIP text space. At inference, the model is fully zero-shot: points are classified by cosine similarity against CLIP text features of any user-specified class set, requiring no 2D images or category annotations at test time.

## Results

In the annotation-free setting on ScanNet, OV3D achieves 57.3% mIoU and 64.0% foreground mIoU, outperforming the previous best method OpenScene (54.2% mIoU) by +3.1%. On Matterport3D, OV3D reaches 45.8% mIoU and 50.4% f-mIoU, a +3.2% mIoU gain over OpenScene. When combined with OpenScene-3D's complementary alignment, OV3D further improves to 59.6% mIoU on ScanNet and 48.2% on Matterport3D, narrowing the gap to fully-supervised performance (72.0% and 55.7% mIoU) to just −12.4% and −7.5% respectively. On the challenging ScanNet200 benchmark (200 classes), OV3D achieves 8.7% mIoU, surpassing OpenScene-3D (7.3%) and RegionPLC (6.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OV3D outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest prior method OpenScene [40], OV3D gains +3.1% mIoU on ScanNet (57.3 vs 54.2) and +3.2% on Matterport3D (45.8 vs 42.6), while requiring far less storage (~6 GB vs >300 GB for image features). Compared to RegionPLC w/ OpenScene-3D [64], the closest competitor on foreground metrics, OV3D improves ScanNet f-mIoU from 60.0 to 64.0 (+4.0%). On the 200-class ScanNet200 benchmark, OV3D (8.7% mIoU) beats OpenScene-3D (7.3%) and RegionPLC (6.5%), attributed to its direct alignment with CLIP text features rather than distillation from 2D segmentation models. In the base-annotated setting (Table 4), OV3D surpasses RegionPLC by +2.5% hIoU in B15/N4 and +6.0% in B10/N9, with the gap widening as more novel classes are introduced. On outdoor driving (nuScenes), OV3D achieves 44.6% mIoU vs OpenScene-3D's 41.3%, confirming generality beyond indoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OV3D extends the open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation line initiated by OpenScene (point-to-pixel feature alignment) and PLA/RegionPLC (scene/region-level caption-to-point alignment). It departs from both by introducing entity-level text descriptions generated via LVLM conversation (LLaVA-1.5) and by using vision foundation models (SAM/SEEM) to establish fine-grained pixel-to-EntityText correspondence before projecting to 3D points. Rather than relying on knowledge distillation from 2D segmentation models or coarse image captions, OV3D repurposes a pipeline of existing foundation models (LVLM → VFM → VLM → CLIP text encoder) to create dense (point, EntityText) pairs that directly align 3D features with open text embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OV3D narrows the gap between zero-shot and fully-supervised 3D segmentation to just −7.5% mIoU on Matterport3D, and does so with dramatically lower storage overhead (~6 GB vs. >300 GB for OpenScene), making open-vocabulary 3D understanding more practical for real-world deployment. The entity-level granularity and context-enriched text embeddings (combining names with diverse descriptions) offer a reusable recipe for fine-grained cross-modal alignment that could transfer to other 3D tasks. Open questions include sensitivity to LVLM quality, dependence on multi-view image availability at training time, and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or outdoor scenes beyond nuScenes without additional adaptation.
