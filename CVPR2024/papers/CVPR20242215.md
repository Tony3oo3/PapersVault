## Problem

Existing decolorization methods focus on preserving structural information and contrast during color-to-grayscale conversion, but leave the aesthetic quality of the resulting black-and-white image unaddressed. Professional photographers apply distinct, subject-dependent retouching schemes (global exposure/contrast adjustments followed by spatially-varying local edits), and no prior method can imitate these expert-specific, scene-aware retouching styles to produce visually pleasing BW photography.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a user-specified style index (one of four: RGB2Gray operator or three expert styles) combined with a subject class (human, non-human, nature, building) to produce a single-channel black-and-white image. The style index selects a proxy from a 16-entry Proxy Bank (4 subjects × 4 styles), and the framework is extensible to arbitrary new styles by fine-tuning only the proxy generation network on unpaired internet-downloaded BW photos, without retraining the decolorization network.

## Method

DeColorful-Net is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (proxy generation) uses dual encoders—ResNet for subject classification and VGG for style classification—whose features are embedded via a lightweight MLP and trained with a hierarchical proxy-based loss (HPL) that combines Proxy-Anchor loss at two levels: 16 subject-style-specific proxies at the lower level and 4 style-only proxies at the upper level, capturing both expert-discriminative and class-sharing characteristics. Stage 2 (decolorization) applies Dual-AdaIN to transfer the selected proxy's style into the encoder's feature maps, then uses a multi-level bilateral grid decoder that outputs per-cell weights {w^r, w^g, w^b, w^d} (channel weights plus a bias term for local brightness/contrast) at multiple spatial scales. The multi-level design mimics the experts' coarse-to-fine retouching workflow and eliminates the grid artifacts and halo effects inherent to single-level bilateral grids, while the unconstrained weight sum (unlike prior methods that normalize to 1) enables local contrast manipulation.

## Results

On the B&W Adobe 5K dataset, DeColorful-Net achieves PSNR 27.50 dB, SSIM 0.947, and LPIPS 0.057, outperforming all six state-of-the-art image enhancement baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, CSNorm, reaches PSNR 26.62 and SSIM 0.931, while the weakest baseline, HDRNet, scores only PSNR 23.93. In user studies (80 participants across four age groups), DeColorful-Net with expert styles A and C scores 4.94 and 4.80 (out of 6) respectively, far exceeding commercial filters (3.54–4.33) and prior decolorization methods (2.01–3.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeColorful-Net outperforms all six image-enhancement baselines (HDRNet, StarEnhancer, NeurOp, MAXIM, RSFNet, CSNorm) on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS simultaneously, with the largest margin over HDRNet (+3.57 dB PSNR) and the smallest over CSNorm (+0.88 dB PSNR). The paper attributes the baselines' underperformance to two factors: (1) image-to-image translation models trained per-style ignore intra-style subject variation, and (2) color-specific operations (e.g., RSFNet's temperature/hue adjustments, StarEnhancer's RGB curve tuning) are ill-suited for monochrome retouching. In user Study 1, DeColorful-Net with expert styles A and C (scores 4.94 and 4.80) dominates prior decolorization methods PDecolor (2.32), LeDecolor (2.56), and SPDecolor (2.89) by a wide margin. In Study 2, DeColorful-Net (4.58) also surpasses commercial smartphone filters (iPhone 14 Pro: 4.12, Galaxy S21 Ultra: 4.11, Instagram: 3.54, TikTok: 3.81) and even the experts' own camera BW-mode photos (4.01), which the authors attribute to the narrower spectral range of color DSLR sensors versus dedicated monochrome cameras.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes deep metric learning (specifically Proxy-Anchor loss and hierarchical proxy-based loss) from its traditional role in classification/retrieval into a low-level vision task: image decolorization. It combines this with the bilateral grid framework from HDRNet [15] and Dual-AdaIN from StarEnhancer [53], but departs from prior decolorization work (which focused on contrast/texture preservation) by framing the task as expert-style imitation in an aesthetic domain. The multi-level bilateral grid is a novel extension of the single-level grid, motivated by the need to capture the coarse-to-fine retouching workflow that professional photographers follow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a useful large-scale B&W retouching dataset (B&W Adobe 5K) and demonstrates that DML can encode expert-specific, subject-aware retouching styles in a compact proxy representation, enabling personalization without per-user training. The scalability to internet photos (100 images per style, unpaired) suggests a practical path toward user-defined styles. Open questions include whether the 4-class subject taxonomy generalizes, whether the approach extends to color retouching or multi-style blending, and whether the aesthetic gains hold for non-photography domains. The user study's finding that the method outperforms actual experts' monochrome-camera photos is intriguing but confounded by the spectral-range limitation of color DSLRs.
