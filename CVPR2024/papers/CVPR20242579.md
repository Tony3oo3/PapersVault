## Problem

Dynamic channel pruning achieves higher accuracy by selecting input-dependent sub-networks but must store all weights, incurring extra storage costs that prevent deployment on resource-limited devices. Static pruning permanently removes redundant channels to save storage but is limited by a fixed sub-network. Prior attempts to combine both (e.g., SEP) treat the two sub-networks independently and fuse them with hand-designed functions, leading to sub-optimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242579/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN (ResNet, MobileNet-V2, CifarNet) and a pruning dataset, with user-specified budgets for remaining FLOPs (p_r) and parameters (p_p). It produces a pruned CNN in which channels with zero static gate values are permanently removed, while the surviving channels retain input-dependent dynamic routing via SE-based gating modules. The pruned model is then fine-tuned end-to-end on the target dataset, and at inference time it dynamically selects a subset of the remaining channels per input image.

## Method

The paper formulates unified static and dynamic channel pruning as a bilevel optimization problem: the inner problem optimizes the static gate parameters θ_s, and the outer problem optimizes the dynamic routing parameters θ_d conditioned on the (approximately) optimal static solution. Channel selection uses Gumbel-sigmoid with the straight-through estimator to produce differentiable binary masks, and the dynamic routing function is a global-average-pooling + squeeze-and-excitation block. A key technical contribution is an efficient gradient derivation showing that the costly Hessian-vector product in the bilevel update decomposes into a product of two first-order Jacobians, avoiding finite-difference approximations. After solving the bilevel problem, channels with zero static gates are permanently deleted, and the reduced model is fine-tuned with dynamic flexibility under FLOPs and anti-collapse regularization, yielding a single end-to-end learned compression without handcrafted fusion functions.

## Results

On ImageNet, UDSP achieves 69.48% Top-1 on ResNet-18 (Δ -0.28%), 72.91% on ResNet-34 (Δ -0.39%), 76.51% on ResNet-50 (Δ +0.38%), and 71.72% on MobileNet-V2 (Δ -0.16%), while pruning 50–58% FLOPs and 15–25% parameters. On CIFAR-10, UDSP reaches 91.89% on CifarNet (Δ -0.47%) and 93.78% on ResNet-56 (Δ +0.66%) at ~50% FLOPs reduction. Notably, on ResNet-50 UDSP is the only method to achieve a positive Δ Top-1 accuracy while pruning 58.4% FLOPs, outperforming all static and dynamic baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDSP consistently outperforms the closest competitor SEP [5] (which also combines static and dynamic pruning) across all architectures: +0.75% Δ Top-1 on ResNet-18 (69.48% vs 68.73%), +0.34% on ResNet-56/CIFAR-10 (93.78% vs 93.44%), with comparable FLOPs and parameter reductions. Against dynamic-only methods, UDSP beats FBS [23] by 2.26% Δ Top-1 on ResNet-18 and 1.02% on CifarNet while saving 20% parameters (vs. FBS's 12% parameter increase). Among static pruning baselines, UDSP surpasses DSA [53] by 0.83% Δ Top-1 on ResNet-18 with 10.2% more FLOPs pruned, HRank [43] by 0.87% on ResNet-56, and ResRe [11] by 0.54% Top-1 on ResNet-50. On MobileNet-V2, UDSP outperforms MetaPruning [48] by 0.52% Top-1 while pruning 6% more FLOPs, and GSS [70] by 0.64% Δ Top-1 at similar FLOPs. The only setting where UDSP does not lead is ResNet-34, where IE [51] achieves a slightly better Δ Top-1 (-0.48% vs -0.39%) but prunes only 22.3% FLOPs versus UDSP's 50.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SEP (Storage Efficient Pruning, Chen et al. 2020), which first attempted to combine static and dynamic channel pruning but did so heuristically via reinforcement learning and a hand-designed fusion function, treating the two sub-networks independently. The dynamic routing mechanism (global average pooling + squeeze-and-excitation) is inherited from FBS (Gao et al. 2019), and the bilevel optimization framework with one-step approximation follows the template established by DARTS (Liu et al. 2019) for differentiable architecture search. The key departure is formulating the joint static-dynamic pruning problem as a bilevel optimization where the static sub-network is never directly evaluated but is implicitly trained through the dynamic sub-networks, replacing SEP's separated training and hand-crafted fusion with an end-to-end differentiable pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying dynamic pruning on resource-limited devices: the need to store all weights. By unifying static and dynamic pruning through bilevel optimization, the method achieves a principled trade-off between storage savings (permanently removing channels) and dynamic flexibility (input-dependent routing), with directly settable FLOPs and parameter budgets rather than implicit hyperparameters. The efficient gradient computation—showing the costly matrix-vector product decomposes into a product of two Jacobians—makes bilevel optimization tractable for this setting. Limitations include reliance on a three-stage train-prune-finetune pipeline, the one-step approximation for the inner problem (which may be suboptimal for very deep networks), and evaluation limited to standard image classification architectures; whether the bilevel formulation generalizes to other structural pruning types (layer, kernel) or non-vision modalities remains open.
