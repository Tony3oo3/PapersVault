## Problem

Adversarial fine-tuning of pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP improves robustness on the training dataset but causes the model to overfit, deviating from the pre-trained model and degrading zero-shot generalization on unseen datasets. The key gap is how to enhance zero-shot adversarial robustness while preserving the generalization features already captured during pre-training, rather than merely constraining parameter-space distance.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 3×224×224 and a set of text prompts (one per candidate class, formatted as 'This is a photo of a {class}') as input. The text encoder is frozen throughout training; only the image encoder (ViT-B/32) parameters are updated. Classification is performed by computing cosine similarity between the image embedding and each text embedding, selecting the class with maximum similarity. Adversarial examples are generated via PGD with l∞ perturbation bounds (1/255, 2/255, or 4/255) and 10 steps.

## Method

PMG-AFT splits adversarial fine-tuning into two branches: a robustness branch that minimizes cross-entropy loss on PGD-generated adversarial examples (using TeCoA-style text-supervised generation), and a generalization branch that minimizes KL divergence between the target model's softmax output on adversarial examples and the frozen pre-trained CLIP's output on the same adversarial examples. A third regularization term (KL divergence between the target model's adversarial and clean outputs) further anchors predictions. The total loss is L = L_robust + α·L_general + β·L_clean (α=β=1). The algorithm alternates between adversarial example generation and parameter updates, with the pre-trained model serving as a fixed teacher in feature space rather than parameter space, enabling the target model to retain generalizable representations while learning perturbation-invariant features.

## Results

Fine-tuned on TinyImageNet and evaluated zero-shot on 16 datasets under PGD-10 (ε=1/255), PMG-AFT achieves an average zero-shot robust accuracy of 31.95%, improving over the original CLIP (17.38%) by 14.57% and over the SOTA FT-TeCoA (26.96%) by 4.99%. On clean accuracy, PMG-AFT reaches 55.71% average, surpassing FT-TeCoA (46.99%) by 8.72% while remaining close to the original CLIP (59.98%). Under the stronger AutoAttack (ε=1/255), PMG-AFT achieves 17.91% average robust accuracy versus 10.33% for FT-TeCoA and 1.74% for CLIP. The visual-prompt variant (VPT-PMG-AFT) also outperforms VP-TeCoA by 4.47% in robust accuracy (22.74% vs. 18.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original CLIP model, PMG-AFT improves average zero-shot robust accuracy by 14.57% (31.95% vs. 17.38%) while retaining 93% of CLIP's clean accuracy (55.71% vs. 59.98%), demonstrating that adversarial fine-tuning need not sacrifice generalization. Compared to the primary SOTA baseline FT-TeCoA [32], PMG-AFT wins on 14 out of 16 datasets in robust accuracy (losing only on TinyImageNet, the training set, where FT-TeCoA scores 19.33% vs. 14.84%), with the largest per-dataset gains on CIFAR-10 (+26.17%), CIFAR-100 (+13.87%), and EuroSAT (+11.58%). On clean accuracy, PMG-AFT outperforms FT-TeCoA by 8.72% on average (55.71% vs. 46.99%), indicating that the generalization branch effectively prevents the clean-accuracy collapse typical of adversarial fine-tuning. Under the stronger AutoAttack evaluation, PMG-AFT achieves 17.91% average robust accuracy, nearly 7.6 percentage points above FT-TeCoA (10.33%) and 16.2 points above CLIP (1.74%). The visual-prompt variant VPT-PMG-AFT also consistently beats VP-TeCoA by 4.47% in robust accuracy and 7.53% in clean accuracy, confirming that the method's gains are not specific to full fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TeCoA (Mao et al., ICLR 2023), which first formulated zero-shot adversarial robustness for CLIP and introduced text-supervised adversarial fine-tuning. PMG-AFT retains TeCoA's text-supervised PGD attack generation as its robustness branch but departs from prior overfitting-mitigation strategies (parameter interpolation, MixOut-style regularization) by constraining the target model in feature space rather than parameter space. The key conceptual departure is treating the frozen pre-trained CLIP image encoder as a teacher: the generalization information branch minimizes KL divergence between the target model's and the original model's softmax outputs on adversarial examples, effectively distilling the pre-trained model's generalization into the fine-tuned one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck: adversarial fine-tuning of large VLP models typically destroys the zero-shot generalization that makes them valuable, and prior parameter-space regularizers do not explicitly target robustness generalization. The simultaneous improvement in both robust and clean accuracy (recovering most of the clean-accuracy loss that FT-TeCoA incurs) is practically significant for deployment. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/32, a single fine-tuning dataset (TinyImageNet) for main results, and the fact that in-distribution robust accuracy on TinyImageNet actually drops relative to FT-TeCoA, suggesting a deliberate in-distribution/out-of-distribution trade-off. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger CLIP variants, other VLP architectures, and tasks beyond image classification.
