## Problem

Existing generalizable neural implicit surface reconstruction methods are trained and evaluated exclusively on favorable view combinations selected by predefined view-selection scores, producing degenerate geometry when tested on arbitrary or unfavorable sets with limited overlap. This paper introduces the concept of view-combination generalizability and demonstrates that prior methods overfit to specific learned view configurations, severely limiting practical deployment where camera arrangements cannot be controlled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240873/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N=4 posed RGB source images at 640×512 resolution and produces a 3D signed distance function (SDF) surface via volume rendering, along with rendered color images. A hierarchical sampling strategy uses 64 points per ray for both coarse and fine stages, and the cascaded correlation frustum employs 48, 32, and 8 depth hypotheses across three levels. Unlike prior work that assumes a fixed favorable view set, UFORecon is designed to accept any arbitrary combination of source views at inference time without retraining or fine-tuning.

## Method

UFORecon integrates three key mechanisms for robust view-combination generalizability: (1) a cross-view matching transformer that extracts pairwise correlation features between all source images at multiple FPN levels, (2) cascaded correlation frustums built by warping cross-view features into each reference view and processing them with 3D CNNs to produce global geometry volumes and intermediate depths, and (3) explicit geometry-aware similarity encoding that computes group-wise cosine similarities of cross-view features for all source pairs as a view-consistent prior. A view-aggregation transformer fuses the volume features, 2D cross-view features, and similarity scores via linear self-attention, while a geometry-aware ray transformer uses positional encoding of depth differences from the intermediate frustum depths to predict SDF values. The training introduces a random set strategy that randomly selects source views during training, which stabilizes generalization to arbitrary combinations and yields a 17% improvement on unfavorable sets without additional data or losses.

## Results

On the DTU dataset with 3 input views, UFORecon achieves a mean Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.99 on favorable view combinations, outperforming the previous best C2F2NeuS (1.11) and ReTR (1.17). On unfavorable view combinations—the paper's primary evaluation—the method achieves a mean CD of 1.56, substantially outperforming ReTR (2.94), VolRecon (3.18), and SparseNeuS (4.16). With the proposed random set training strategy, the unfavorable-set CD further improves to 1.28, a 17% reduction. The method also generalizes to the BlendedMVS dataset without fine-tuning, maintaining competitive reconstruction quality under unfavorable 4-view settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On favorable sets (Table 1), UFORecon (CD 0.99) outperforms all generalizable surface reconstruction baselines: C2F2NeuS (1.11), ReTR (1.17), VolRecon (1.38), SparseNeuS (1.64), and TransMVSNet (1.35), as well as per-scene methods (NeuS 4.90, VolSDF 3.41) and generalizable NVS methods (MVSNeRF 2.09, IBRNet 2.32, PixelNeRF 6.28). On the paper's key unfavorable-set evaluation (Table 2), UFORecon (1.56) achieves a 47% CD reduction over the next-best ReTR (2.94), a 51% reduction over VolRecon (3.18), and a 63% reduction over SparseNeuS (4.16); TransMVSNet degrades most severely to 8.35. The hybrid VolRecon+MatchNeRF baseline (Table 4) improves VolRecon from 3.18 to 2.47 on unfavorable sets but still trails UFORecon (1.56) by 37%, while using 4.97M parameters versus UFORecon's 1.5M. Random set training further narrows the gap to favorable-set performance (1.28 vs. 0.99), whereas applying it to VolRecon destabilizes training and yields suboptimal results.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFORecon sits at the intersection of two research threads: generalizable neural implicit surface reconstruction (VolRecon, SparseNeuS, ReTR, C2F2NeuS) and transformer-based multi-view stereo (TransMVSNet). It departs from prior generalizable SDF methods by rejecting their implicit assumption that source views are pre-selected for high overlap and small baseline, instead borrowing cross-view matching transformers from MVS to explicitly model inter-view correlations. The cascaded correlation frustum design follows C2F2NeuS but replaces its independently extracted features with transformer-matched cross-view features and adds a single-channel correlation volume for lower memory cost. The explicit similarity encoding draws on MatchNeRF's correspondence-matching prior, repurposing it from novel-view synthesis to surface reconstruction as a view-consistent regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world capture (robotics, AR/VR, autonomous driving) rarely guarantees the favorable, high-overlap view combinations that current generalizable methods are trained and evaluated on, and the paper demonstrates that existing approaches can produce degenerate geometry when this assumption is violated. The VC score metric provides a useful diagnostic for quantifying view-combination quality, and the finding that random set training destabilizes VolRecon but stabilizes with cross-view correlation features suggests a broader principle about what inductive biases are needed for view-robust learning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unknown camera poses (the paper assumes ground-truth poses), whether it scales to higher-complexity scenes beyond DTU, and whether the cross-view matching transformer can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives without losing the robustness gain.
