## Problem

Existing dance synthesis methods produce videos with a single fixed camera view, failing to capture the dynamic, human-centric cinematography that professional dance videos require. No paired dataset of 3D dance motion, camera movement, and music existed, and the interplay between music-driven movement style and dance-driven framing makes camera synthesis for dance fundamentally harder than either task alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241670/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes aligned music audio (encoded via Jukebox into 4800-dimensional per-frame features) and a 3D dance pose sequence of 60 joint global positions at 30 FPS. It outputs a camera movement sequence in MMD format (reference-point position, rotation, distance, and FoV — 8 scalars per frame) at 30 FPS. Generation proceeds in 5-second subsequences with a 2.5-second stride, overlapping slices are blended with linearly decaying weights, and a total-variation denoiser plus Savitzky-Golay filter post-processes the trajectory for keyframe detection and smoothing.

## Method

DanceCamera3D is a transformer-based DDPM (T=1000) that denoises a noisy camera trajectory conditioned on music and dance embeddings. Two key innovations distinguish it: (1) a strong-weak condition separation strategy for classifier-free guidance, where dance motion (strongly correlated to camera framing) and music (weakly correlated, influencing movement style) receive independent guidance weights ω₁ and ω₂, enabling finer control over the quality–diversity–fidelity trade-off; and (2) a body attention loss L_ba computed from per-joint visibility masks that penalizes the model for placing significant body parts outside the camera view, directly addressing the failure mode of standard reconstruction/velocity/acceleration losses. The full training objective is a weighted sum of reconstruction, velocity, acceleration, and body-attention losses, trained with 128 batch size for 3000 epochs using the Adan optimizer (52.7M parameters, 6× RTX 3090, 13 hours).

## Results

On the DCM test set, DanceCamera3D achieves the best kinetic quality (FID_k = 3.749) and shot quality (FID_s = 0.280) among all compared methods, substantially outperforming DanceRevolution (FID_k = 10.267, FID_s = 2.368) and FACT (FID_k = 5.205, FID_s = 0.960). In terms of dancer fidelity, DanceCamera3D attains the lowest Dancer Missing Rate (DMR = 0.0025) and Limbs Capture Difference (LCD = 0.147), compared to DMR = 0.0062 / LCD = 0.154 for DanceRevolution and DMR = 0.0070 / LCD = 0.151 for FACT. In the user study, DanceCamera3D wins 71.90% of pairwise comparisons against DanceRevolution and 65.71% against FACT, though ground-truth camera movements still win 37.62% of comparisons against DanceCamera3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DanceCamera3D outperforms both autoregressive baselines across nearly all metrics. Against DanceRevolution (LSTM-based), DanceCamera3D reduces FID_k from 10.267 to 3.749 (a 63% improvement) and FID_s from 2.368 to 0.280 (an 88% improvement), while also halving the DMR (0.0062 → 0.0025) and winning 71.90% of user-study comparisons. Against FACT (transformer-based), DanceCamera3D lowers FID_k from 5.205 to 3.749 and FID_s from 0.960 to 0.280, with a DMR of 0.0025 versus 0.0070, and wins 65.71% of user-study pairs. The only metric where DanceCamera3D does not lead is shot diversity (Dist_s = 1.326 vs. ground truth's 1.731), reflecting that manually edited camera work still exhibits greater shot-type variety. In the user study, ground-truth camera movements retain a 37.62% win rate over DanceCamera3D, indicating a remaining gap in fine-grained artistic control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: music-conditioned dance synthesis (FACT, DanceRevolution, Bailando) and automatic camera planning (Jiang et al., Bonatti et al., Evin et al.). It adopts the DDPM diffusion framework and classifier-free guidance as its generative backbone, but departs from standard CFG by introducing a strong-weak condition separation strategy that treats dance motion (strong condition) and music audio (weak condition) with independent guidance weights. The body attention loss is a novel addition not present in prior movement synthesis losses, specifically designed to prevent the dancer from leaving the camera frame. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the denoising diffusion paradigm from image/dance generation to the underexplored domain of camera trajectory synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DCM dataset is the first to pair 3D camera movement with dance and music, opening a research direction that was previously infeasible due to data scarcity. However, the dataset is limited to anime-community (MMD) content with only 108 sequences (3.2 hours) and 4 music genres, raising questions about generalization to real-world dance cinematography. The strong-weak condition separation strategy offers a practical recipe for multi-condition diffusion where conditions have asymmetric influence, which could transfer to other multi-modal generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, more diverse dance styles, and whether the anime-derived camera behaviors transfer to live-action dance video production.
