## Problem

Video Transformers incur quadratic computational cost from the large number of spatio-temporal tokens, making training and deployment expensive. Existing training-free token reduction methods designed for images are suboptimal for video because attention-based informativeness scores are unreliable in early layers and suffer from temporal bias that confuses foreground objects with backgrounds, preventing effective early-layer token merging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241323/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free plug-in on a pre-trained video Transformer (e.g., UMT-B/L), applied after self-attention in the first four layers. A video clip is tokenized into N = (T/t)×(H/P)×(W/P) spatio-temporal tube tokens, which are progressively merged in early layers to reduce the token count flowing through subsequent layers. The output format is identical to the base model—retrieval rankings (R@1/5/10), QA accuracy, or action class labels—but computed with 30–59% fewer FLOPs.

## Method

vid-TLDR consists of two components. First, saliency detection via attention sharpness: the negative entropy of each token's attention row in the first self-attention layer is normalized to produce a saliency score, exploiting the observation that foreground tokens attend more sharply (lower entropy) than background tokens, and this signal is reliable even in layer 1 where standard attentiveness and attention rollout fail. Second, saliency-aware token merging: building on ToMe's bipartite soft matching, a background drop mask zeros out tokens below the mean saliency, and the remaining scores are rescaled to form masked saliency scores that serve as the merging mass—foreground tokens receive higher mass while background tokens are effectively pruned, so the weighted feature aggregation (Eq. 11) preserves object information while suppressing irrelevant background contributions.

## Results

On video-text retrieval with UMT-B, vid-TLDR achieves R@1 of 50.8 on MSRVTT (+0.8% over base), 62.7 on MSVD (+0.6%), and 62.4 on DiDeMo (+0.3%), while reducing FLOPs by 41.3%, 40.2%, and 29.8% respectively. On video question answering, UMT-B+vid-TLDR maintains 44.8% on MSR-QA and 49.4% on MSVD-QA (within 0.1% of base) at 188.5 GFLOPs (37.9% reduction). When applied to VideoMAE for action recognition, vid-TLDR reduces FLOPs by ~70% (180.5G→56.1G) with only 1.2% accuracy drop on UCF101, outperforming ToMe by 14.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base UMT-B model, vid-TLDR achieves equal or better R@1 on MSRVTT (+0.8%), MSVD (+0.6%), and DiDeMo (+0.3%) while cutting FLOPs by 30–41%; on ActivityNet and LSMDC it incurs a negligible 0.3–0.6% drop. Compared to ToMe (the prior training-free merging method), vid-TLDR consistently wins by +4.0% average R@1 on UMT-B and +2.1% on UMT-L across all retrieval datasets, while also using fewer FLOPs. In video QA (Table 4), UMT-B+vid-TLDR matches base UMT-B within 0.1% on both MSR-QA and MSVD-QA at 37.9% lower FLOPs, and remains competitive with larger models like InternVideo (646M params, 666.2 GFLOPs) and FrozenBiLM (400M, 340.7 GFLOPs). On action recognition with VideoMAE (Table 5), vid-TLDR outperforms ToMe by 14.4% on UCF101 (90.1% vs 75.7%) and 11.1% on SSV2 (69.6% vs 58.5%) at similar FLOPs. On base-to-novel generalization with ViFi-CLIP (Table 6), vid-TLDR surpasses ToMe by 19.4% in harmonic mean (74.7% vs 55.3%) at the same 279 GFLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ToMe (Bolya et al., ICLR 2022), which introduced training-free token merging for image ViTs via bipartite soft matching, by adapting it to video transformers where the absence of class tokens and the presence of temporal bias render standard attentiveness-based scoring unreliable. It departs from prior token reduction methods (DynamicViT, ToMe) that either require retraining or cannot operate in early layers, by proposing an entropy-based attention sharpness metric that identifies salient foreground tokens even in the first self-attention layer. The saliency-aware mass assignment (Eqs. 8–11) further differentiates it from ToMe's uniform constituent-count mass, effectively combining token merging with selective background pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free plug-in, vid-TLDR offers a practical path to reducing video transformer inference cost by 30–40% without retraining, which is valuable given the prohibitive cost of retraining large video models. The finding that early-layer token reduction is most effective (Table 1) and that removing background tokens can slightly improve generalization (up to +0.8% R@1) suggests that irrelevant tokens actively harm video transformer performance rather than merely wasting compute. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or higher-resolution inputs where token counts grow further, and whether the entropy-based sharpness signal remains discriminative in deeper or more heterogeneous architectures beyond UMT, VideoMAE, and ViFi-CLIP.
