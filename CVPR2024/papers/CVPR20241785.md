## Problem

Prior unsupervised video instance segmentation methods rely heavily on optical flow estimates, which fail for static objects, non-rigid deformations, overlapping instances, and illumination changes. No existing approach achieves competitive multi-instance video segmentation without optical flow or without training on natural videos, leaving a large performance gap (29.1% AP50) between unsupervised and supervised methods on YouTubeVIS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241785/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes RGB video frames and produces per-instance segmentation masks with consistent tracking IDs across all frames, including objects that are absent in some frames or highly overlapping. During training, the input is not real video but synthetic 2-frame sequences generated at 480×480 resolution from pairs of unlabeled ImageNet images, where objects from one image are cropped, randomly resized (scale 0.8–1.0), repositioned, and pasted onto a duplicated copy of another image. The output during training is 3D volume mask trajectories (one mask per frame per instance) used as pseudo ground-truth labels.

## Method

VideoCutLER is a three-stage cut-synthesize-and-learn pipeline. First, MaskCut (from CutLER) generates up to 3 pseudo-masks per image by computing a patch-wise cosine similarity matrix from DINO ViT-Base/8 key features and iteratively applying Normalized Cuts spectral clustering, with CRF post-processing for boundary refinement. Second, ImageCut2Video synthesizes 2-frame training videos by duplicating one image and pasting randomly transformed objects (brightness, contrast, rotation, crop, resize, shift) from a second image in the minibatch, creating both static and mobile object trajectories. Third, a VideoMask2Former model with ResNet50 backbone is trained in two stages—first pretraining Mask2Former on ImageNet with MaskCut pseudo-masks, then fine-tuning on the synthetic videos—using shared queries across frames so the model learns to segment and track instances purely by appearance (feature) similarity rather than motion cues. The key insight is that this extremely simple synthetic video construction, requiring no optical flow and no natural video data, is surprisingly sufficient to produce a model that achieves 50.7% AP50 on YouTubeVIS-2019, surpassing the previous SOTA by a factor of 10.

## Results

On YouTubeVIS-2019, VideoCutLER achieves 50.7% AP50^video and 42.4% AR10^video in a zero-shot unsupervised setting, surpassing the previous SOTA OCLR (4.8% AP50) by a factor of ~10×. On YouTubeVIS-2021, it reaches 38.9% AP50^video, again outperforming OCLR (4.4%) by over 8×. As a pretrained model for supervised learning, VideoCutLER exceeds DINO by 15.9% AP on YouTubeVIS-2019 and 8.8% on YouTubeVIS-2021 when fine-tuned with full labels. On DAVIS-2017, it achieves 43.6% J&F, outperforming the flow-based OCLR (39.6%) by 4.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoCutLER decisively outperforms the two flow-based unsupervised baselines: OCLR [37] (4.8% AP50) and MotionGroup [38] (1.3% AP50) on YouTubeVIS-2019, achieving 50.7% AP50 without any optical flow input. Against the image-only CutLER [35] baseline (37.5% AP50), VideoCutLER gains +12.8% AP50, demonstrating that the synthetic video training signal adds substantial value over single-image segmentation. In the supervised pretraining comparison (Table 5), VideoCutLER-initialized Mask2Former reaches 38.9% AP on YouTubeVIS-2019, exceeding the DINO [4]-initialized baseline (23.0% AP) by 15.9 points, because VideoCutLER pretrains all model weights rather than only the backbone. On DAVIS-2017 (Table 3), VideoCutLER (43.6% J&F) beats OCLR (39.6%) and MaskR-CNN w/ flow (not reported on DAVIS2017 val) despite using no flow and no natural video data. The method also narrows the supervised–unsupervised gap on YouTubeVIS-2019 from 29.1% to 11.0% in AP50 when compared to MaskTrackR-CNN trained with full labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoCutLER extends the CutLER image-based pipeline (DINO features + Normalized Cuts spectral clustering for pseudo-mask generation) into the video domain by introducing ImageCut2Video, a synthetic video construction step that pairs images from a minibatch to create short sequences with object trajectories. It deliberately departs from the dominant prior approach to unsupervised video segmentation (OCLR, MotionGroup), which relies on off-the-shelf optical flow estimators (RAFT) pretrained on human-annotated data, arguing that motion-based signals are unreliable under occlusion, non-rigid deformation, and illumination change. The video segmentation architecture is VideoMask2Former with a ResNet-50 backbone, initialized from a Mask2Former model pretrained on MaskCut pseudo-masks, making the entire pipeline trainable from unlabeled ImageNet images alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that unsupervised multi-instance video segmentation can be competitive without optical flow or natural video data, a claim that challenges a core assumption in the field and opens a simpler training recipe (images only, no flow network). The practical impact is twofold: it narrows the supervised–unsupervised gap on YouTubeVIS from 29.1% to 11.0% in AP50, and it provides a strong full-model pretraining initialization that outperforms DINO by 15.9% AP when fine-tuned on labeled video. The authors acknowledge that integrating natural videos or joint optical-flow training could yield further gains, and the method's reliance on a fixed 2-frame synthetic video and a single ResNet-50 backbone leaves open questions about scaling to longer sequences, higher resolutions, or transformer backbones.
