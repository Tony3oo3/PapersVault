## Problem

Prompting, a proven mechanism for few-shot inference in language and vision-language models, remains under-explored for dense prediction tasks such as semantic segmentation. In the generalized few-shot segmentation (GFSS) setting, the challenge is to learn class-specific visual prompts from only a handful of novel-class examples while simultaneously preserving strong base-class performance, without relying on meta-learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241347/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB query image and k-shot support images (each paired with a binary ground-truth mask) for the novel classes. Support images are consumed only at initialization time via masked average pooling to seed the novel visual prompts; at inference, only the query image is processed. The output is a per-pixel, per-class probability map covering both base and novel classes, produced by a custom segmentation head that computes dot-product similarity between refined class prompts and pixel features (rather than the mask-classification formulation of Mask2Former).

## Method

The architecture is a multi-scale transformer decoder (built on a PSPNet/ResNet-50 backbone and MSDeformAttn pixel decoder) in which each class is represented by a learnable visual prompt that cross-attends with image features at every decoder layer. Novel prompts are initialized by masked average pooling of the k-shot support images and are then refined through a unidirectional novel-to-base causal attention module—shared across all decoder layers—that lets base prompts (learned with abundant data) contextualize and repulse novel prompts without allowing the reverse, thereby reducing base-novel confusion. The novel segmentation head learns only a residual weight matrix on top of the frozen base MLP to avoid overfitting on few examples. Optionally, a transductive prompt-tuning stage fine-tunes all prompts at test time using mutual-information maximization and a knowledge-distillation loss to further improve predictions on unlabelled query images.

## Results

On COCO-20i, the proposed method achieves a mean mIoU of 34.78 (1-shot) and 40.83 (5-shot) in the inductive setting, and 36.05 (1-shot) and 42.48 (5-shot) in the transductive setting. On PASCAL-5i, the corresponding mean mIoU values are 54.79 (1-shot) and 62.60 (5-shot) inductively, and 58.11 (1-shot) and 66.27 (5-shot) transductively. Notably, the inductive variant already outperforms the transductive DIaM baseline on COCO-20i for both 1-shot and 5-shot, and on PASCAL-5i for 1-shot, without requiring per-image test-time optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all ten baselines across both datasets and both shot settings. Against the strongest inductive competitor BAM [19], the inductive variant gains +2.78% mean mIoU (1-shot) and +7.59% (5-shot) on COCO-20i, and +5.24% (1-shot) and +12.32% (5-shot) on PASCAL-5i. Against the strongest transductive competitor DIaM [13], the transductive variant improves by +3.30% (1-shot) and +3.93% (5-shot) on COCO-20i, and +5.11% (1-shot) and +3.19% (5-shot) on PASCAL-5i. Notably, the inductive variant already surpasses transductive DIaM on COCO-20i (34.78 vs 32.75 for 1-shot; 40.83 vs 38.55 for 5-shot) without any test-time optimization. Against POP [21] in the transductive setting, the method leads on COCO-20i 1-shot (36.05 vs 35.01) and PASCAL-5i 1-shot (58.11 vs 54.72), while nearly matching on COCO-20i 5-shot (42.48 vs 42.44). Older few-shot segmentation baselines (CANeT, PFENET, PANet, SCL, MiB) on PASCAL-5i are surpassed by large margins, with the largest gap being over 50% mean mIoU against CANeT and PFENET.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mask2Former architecture [10], repurposing its MSDeformAttn-based pixel decoder but replacing mask classification with per-pixel classification driven by class-aligned visual prompts. It departs from the dominant meta-learning paradigm in few-shot segmentation by adopting a non-meta-learning, two-stage (base pre-training + novel fine-tuning) framework, and extends the visual prompting idea from detection (FS-DETR [6]) and classification (visual prompt tuning [16]) to dense prediction. The key architectural departure is the unidirectional novel-to-base causal attention, which has no direct analogue in prior GFSS methods like DIaM [13] or POP [21], and the transductive prompt tuning that optimizes prompts on unlabeled test images rather than the full model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple prompting architecture with a single causal attention module can match or exceed more complex meta-learning and transductive baselines on GFSS, suggesting that the core challenge in few-shot segmentation may be better addressed through representation separation (novel vs. base prompts) than through elaborate task sampling. The finding that the inductive setting outperforms transductive DIaM is practically significant, as it eliminates per-image test-time optimization and its associated inference cost. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or truly open-world settings, whether the causal attention design generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., instance segmentation), and whether the modest gains over DIaM in the transductive setting persist with stronger backbones or larger support sets.
