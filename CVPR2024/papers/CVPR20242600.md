## Problem

Existing emotion models—categorical, compound, Action Units (AUs), and arousal-valence (AV)—are mutually inconsistent and none provides a single interpretable representation that unifies all four. Furthermore, large text-to-image diffusion models cannot natively interpret the continuous numerical vectors needed to specify fine-grained compound expressions, limiting their utility for controlled facial expression generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242600/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D emotion vector Y = [A, V, Z] (arousal, valence, and a learned third dimension) together with a natural-language text prompt containing a placeholder token (e.g., 'a high-quality realistic color photo of a sks person'). The 3D vector is projected through an MLP number encoder into a 768-dimensional embedding that is concatenated with CLIP text embeddings before conditioning the latent diffusion decoder, which outputs a single RGB facial expression image at the resolution supported by the underlying Stable Diffusion model.

## Method

The paper introduces the C2A2 emotion model, a 3D continuous space that preserves the 2D AV plane and lifts 'fear' to positive Z and 'sad' to negative Z at 60° angles, enabling representation of 15 out of 17 compound emotions (vs. 6/17 for 2D AV). The Z-axis labels are learned implicitly via a GANmut-style conditional-space framework augmented with two new losses: an AV regression loss on the discriminator and a symmetric KL-divergence AU loss using OpenGraphAU pseudo-labels, avoiding the need for direct Z annotations. For generation, a DreamBooth-like fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion is performed with a frozen CLIP text encoder and a trainable MLP number encoder; the emotion embedding is fused with text embeddings, and training alternates between a prior-preservation loss (without emotion conditioning) and an emotion-reconstruction loss (with it) to retain base-model knowledge while learning fine-grained expression control.

## Results

The proposed 3D C2A2 model achieves the highest average psychologist rating of 4.19/5 on seven compound emotions (vs. 3.01 for 2D-AV, 3.31 for DALL-E 3, 2.04 for Stable Diffusion, and 1.47 for DreamBooth), with an overall average of 4.03/5. On emotion fidelity metrics, the 3D model attains a Fréchet Emotion Distance (FED) of 16.06, Emotion Reconstruction Error (ERE) of 0.0536, and Smoothness Score (SS) of 0.806, outperforming the 2D-AV model (FED 20.35, ERE 0.0774, SS 0.905) and DreamBooth (FED 61.15). The 3D model can represent 15/17 target compound emotions, compared to only 6/17 for the 2D AV representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 3D C2A2 model decisively outperforms DreamBooth on all metrics: FED drops from 61.15 to 16.06, and the average compound-emotion psychologist rating jumps from 1.47 to 4.19, indicating DreamBooth largely fails to produce the targeted compound expressions. Against the 2D-AV model (the closest internal baseline), the 3D model wins on FED (16.06 vs. 20.35), ERE (0.0536 vs. 0.0774), and compound-emotion ratings (4.19 vs. 3.01), though it trails slightly on Smoothness Score (0.806 vs. 0.905), suggesting the 2D model produces marginally smoother intensity transitions. Compared to DALL-E 3, the 3D model achieves higher compound-emotion ratings (4.19 vs. 3.31) and overall ratings (4.03 vs. 3.72), confirming that explicit 3D numerical conditioning surpasses purely text-driven generation for fine-grained expression control. Stable Diffusion and the AUs-based model score well below the proposed method on both fidelity and perceptual quality, underscoring the advantage of the unified 3D representation combined with the number+text diffusion pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GANmut's conditional-space learning framework to implicitly learn a third emotion dimension (Z) using Action Unit pseudo-labels, and on DreamBooth's fine-tuning paradigm to condition a latent diffusion model on continuous emotion vectors. Conceptually, it departs from prior emotion-controlled generation (StarGAN, GANmut, ICface) by unifying four previously separate emotion models—Canonical, Compound, AUs, and Arousal-Valence—into a single 3D interpretable space (C2A2), which the authors claim is the first such unification. The number encoder that maps a 3D emotion vector into CLIP's text embedding space is a new architectural component not present in prior text-to-image or emotion-generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The C2A2 representation provides a practical new label space that covers 15/17 compound emotions versus only 6/17 for 2D AV, enabling fine-grained, continuous expression generation that was previously inexpressible. However, the emotion conditioning is not fully disentangled from other attributes (e.g., age), identity preservation is absent, and the method has only been validated on AffectNet with a single DreamBooth-style training setup. Open questions include whether the 3D geometry generalizes beyond the specific choice of lifting fear/sadness, whether the approach extends to video (temporal consistency), and whether the number encoder can handle more complex multi-attribute conditioning beyond facial expressions.
