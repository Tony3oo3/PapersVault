## Problem

Traditional scene text recognition methods rely on sequence-to-sequence pipelines that align character embeddings with sequences of visual tokens, making them vulnerable to attention drift and CTC blank/repetition artifacts. This paper asks whether a single global token can fully characterize a cropped text image and support accurate sequential character prediction, thereby eliminating the alignment bottleneck entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped scene text image resized to 32×128 pixels and produces a recognized text sequence of up to 25 characters from a 36-symbol alphabet (a–z, 0–9). The intermediate representation is a single global token of dimension d (384 for ViT-S/SVTR, 768 for ViT-B). For the retrieval task, the same frozen global token is projected into a CLIP-style visual-text joint space and matched against split-character text queries to return the most similar word.

## Method

OTE comprises a ViT-based image-to-vector encoder and a vector-to-sequence (V2S) decoder. The encoder uses a ViT backbone (ViT-S, ViT-B, or SVTR) followed by a Multi-grained Aggregation Module (CLS token or global average pooling) to compress all spatial features into one global token z ∈ R^{1×d}. The V2S decoder first applies Channel-wise Parallel Attention (CPA), inspired by SENet, which projects the global token through a linear layer, adds positional embeddings, and generates k attention maps via tanh + softmax to produce position-specific features f_o ∈ R^{k×d}. A lingual decoder layer then applies masked multi-head self-attention (causal mask for autoregressive, no mask for non-autoregressive) plus an FFN to capture inter-character dependencies, with a context fusion step in AR mode that merges CPA outputs with previously predicted character embeddings. Training uses standard cross-entropy for recognition and a CLIP-style contrastive loss for retrieval, with no external language model or complex post-processing required.

## Results

On six standard STR benchmarks (IIIT, SVT, IC13, IC15, SVTP, CUTE80) with synthetic training (MJ+ST), OTE_A/ViT-B achieves 93.5% average word accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art among all methods including language-aware models. The lighter OTE_A/ViT-S variant (24M params) reaches 92.8%, surpassing ABINet (92.6%) and ParseqA (93.3%) with fewer parameters. On the real-world Union14M-L benchmark, OTE_A/SVTR attains 96.1% average on common benchmarks and 77.9% on the challenging Union14M-Benchmark. For scene text retrieval on IIITSTR, OTE achieves 80.90 mAP, outperforming the previous best (Wang et al., 77.09 mAP) by 3.81 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTE_A/ViT-B (93.5% avg) surpasses the strongest language-aware baseline ParseqA (93.3%) by 0.2% and ABINet (92.6%) by 0.9%, while OTE_A/SVTR (93.4%) also edges out ParseqA by 0.1% with only 25.2M parameters versus ParseqA's 23.8M. Among language-free methods, OTE_N/ViT-B (92.5%) outperforms SVTR (92.3%) and ParseqN (92.0%), and the compact OTE_A/ViT-S (92.8%, 24M params) beats ABINet-Vision (89.8%) by 3.0%. On challenging datasets (ArT, COCO-Text, Uber-Text), OTE_A/SVTR leads ParseqA on COCO (64.5 vs. 64.0) and Uber (47.8 vs. 42.0), though it trails slightly on ArT (69.1 vs. 70.7). On the real-world Union14M-Benchmark, OTE_A/ViT-B (78.5%) is competitive with MAERec-S with pre-training (78.6%) but without any pre-training stage. For scene text retrieval, OTE achieves 80.90 mAP on IIITSTR, beating Wang et al. (77.09) by 3.81 points and Mask TextSpotter v3 (74.48) by 6.42 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

OTE departs fundamentally from the dominant sequence-to-sequence (S2S) paradigm in scene text recognition, where image-to-sequence encoders produce visual feature sequences that are aligned with character embeddings via CTC or cross-attention decoders. Instead, it compresses the entire text image into a single global token using a ViT-based image-to-vector encoder (drawing on the CLS-token philosophy of ViT and CLIP's single-vector image representation), then decodes the character sequence through a novel vector-to-sequence (V2S) decoder inspired by SENet's channel attention. For the retrieval component, it adapts CLIP's contrastive learning framework with a character-level text encoder, building on prior scene text retrieval work by Wang et al. The key conceptual departure is eliminating sequential visual tokens entirely, thereby sidestepping the attention-drift and alignment problems that plague both CTC and attention-based S2S methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single token suffices for accurate STR challenges the field's implicit assumption that sequential visual features are necessary for sequential character prediction, offering a simpler, alignment-free pipeline that also unifies recognition and retrieval under one representation. The V2S decoding paradigm (reusing one global vector across the channel dimension rather than querying spatial positions) could inspire similar simplifications in other sequence-prediction-from-image tasks. However, the method still struggles with long multi-word text, where the encoder's compression bottleneck becomes apparent, and the practical SOTA gains over strong baselines like ParseqA and ABINet are modest (≤0.5% on average), limiting immediate adoption impact. Open questions include whether the single-token bottleneck can be allevated for longer sequences and whether the V2S idea generalizes beyond English alphanumeric text.
