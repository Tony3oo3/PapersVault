## Problem

Existing concept erasing methods for diffusion models (ESD, Concept Ablation, Selective Amnesia) rely on full-parameter or specification-based fine-tuning, which causes unpredictable generation alterations and progressive concept erosion across non-target concepts—especially when erasing multiple concepts simultaneously. Additionally, these methods are model-specific, requiring repetitive retraining for each new model or concept combination, leading to linear cost growth as deployment scenarios expand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input, which is encoded by a CLIP text encoder and fed into a latent diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) for image generation. The SPM operates on intermediate U-Net layer activations during the iterative denoising process, with the Facilitated Transport mechanism reading the raw prompt at inference to compute per-SPM activation gates. The output is a standard generated image (e.g., 512×512 for SD v1.4), where targeted concepts are suppressed while all other content remains visually consistent with the unmodified model.

## Method

The core contribution is the SPM (Semi-Permeable Membrane), a 1-dimensional adapter consisting of two vectors per layer (v_sig ∈ R^m for the erasing signal and v_reg ∈ R^n for amplitude control) that modifies the U-Net forward pass as y = Wx + (v_reg^T x)·v_sig, adding only 0.0005× parameter overhead. Training combines an erasing loss that aligns the target concept's noise prediction with a surrogate concept (scaled by intensity η) with a novel Latent Anchoring loss that samples non-target concepts from the CLIP latent space using a distance-weighted probability distribution, preserving their original generation without requiring any synthetic image data. At inference, the Facilitated Transport mechanism computes a per-SPM permeability gate γ^c(p) = max(cosine_similarity, token_overlap) between the prompt and each target concept, dynamically amplifying erasure only for threatening prompts while leaving safe generations untouched. Because SPMs are learned in a model-agnostic manner, a corpus of pre-trained SPMs can be freely combined and plugged into any compatible diffusion model without retraining, enabling over 160× speed improvement over SOTA for multi-concept, multi-model deployment.

## Results

On single-concept erasure (Snoopy from SD v1.4), SPM achieves a CLIP Score of 55.48 (vs. 74.43 original) and CER of 20.12%, while maintaining the best preservation on all non-target concepts: FID of 28.39 (Mickey), 30.75 (Spongebob), 18.61 (Pikachu), 10.11 (Dog), and 7.40 (Legislator), with general FIDg remaining at 13.24 (identical to the original model). In the three-concept erasure setting, SPM's FIDg increases by only 0.02 (to 13.26), whereas SA degrades to 18.32 and ConAbl to 15.50. On the I2P explicit-content benchmark, SPM achieves an 85.3% reduction in nudity generation using a single 'nudity' SPM. Efficiency-wise, SPM requires only 24.5 GPU-hours for c=20 concepts on n=5 models, a 167.4× speedup over SA (4100.25 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPM outperforms all three fine-tuning baselines on every non-target preservation metric (FID for Mickey, Spongebob, Pikachu, Dog, Legislator) and matches the original model's FIDg exactly (13.24), while ESD, ConAbl, and SA all degrade general generation (13.68, 15.42, 16.84 respectively). ESD achieves the most aggressive erasure (CS 44.50, CER 77.62%) but at the cost of severe concept erosion—Mickey FID jumps to 129.07 and Dog FID to 45.94—making it the worst preserver. ConAbl and SA, which use generate-and-relearn strategies, show intermediate erasure but still suffer substantial FID inflation on non-targets (e.g., ConAbl Dog FID 96.36, SA Legislator FID 47.42). In the multi-concept setting (3 concepts), the gap widens dramatically: SA's FIDg balloons to 18.32 and its non-target FIDs exceed 167, while SPM stays at FIDg 13.26 with non-target FIDs under 34. SLD [43] is compared only in the efficiency table (1.1 h total) and in qualitative artistic-style/explicit-content experiments, where it under-erases styles and relies on iterative hyperparameter tuning; SPM surpasses it in both erasure completeness and deployment efficiency (24.5 h for 20 concepts × 5 models vs. SLD's per-concept, per-model cost scaling).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ESD's (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023) distribution-alignment formulation for concept erasure, reparameterizing it onto a 1-dimensional adapter (SPM) inspired by PEFT methods like LoRA and adapters. It departs from the full-parameter fine-tuning paradigm of ESD, ConAbl, and SA by introducing a non-invasive, addition-based membrane that keeps the base model frozen, and replaces their pixel-space generative replay with a Latent Anchoring strategy that samples in the continuous CLIP latent space. The Facilitated Transport mechanism at inference time is novel, combining CLIP cosine similarity with token-level unigram matching to dynamically gate each SPM's permeability per prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 0.0005× parameter overhead, 160× speed improvement over SOTA erasure pipelines, and training-free transferability across SD-family models make multi-concept erasure economically viable for deployment at scale. The key open question is whether the 1-dim constraint and addition-based intervention generalize beyond the U-Net/CLIP architecture family (e.g., DiT-based models like SDXL or FLUX), since all transfer experiments remain within the SD lineage. Additionally, the reliance on CLIP similarity for both Latent Anchoring sampling and Facilitated Transport gating may miss semantically distant but functionally related concepts, and the erasure strength is bounded by what a rank-1 perturbation can express.
