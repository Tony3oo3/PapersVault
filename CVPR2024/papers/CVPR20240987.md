## Problem

When CLIP is adapted to pixel-level semantic segmentation, two systematic biases emerge: a space-preference bias (CLIP segments central objects far better than boundary ones) and a class-preference bias (CLIP confuses semantically similar classes, e.g., 'cow' as 'dog'). Previous CLIP-based unsupervised segmentation methods did not explicitly model or rectify these biases, which substantially limits their segmentation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240987/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images and a fixed set of C class names, with no pixel-level or image-level annotations required during training. Images are encoded by CLIP's frozen ViT-B/16 into patch-level features (n×D), while class names are encoded via two text pathways: manually designed Query prompts and a shared learnable Reference prompt. The output is a per-pixel class assignment (C-channel logit map) over the image, ultimately distilled into a full-resolution segmentation mask by a DeepLab V2 student network.

## Method

The framework has two stages. In the rectification stage, a learnable Reference prompt (73 word embeddings shared across all classes, inspired by CoOp) encodes class-preference bias, while a 3-layer 3×3 convolutional network projects CLIP's positional embeddings to encode space-preference bias; both bias logit maps are subtracted element-wise from the Query logit map to produce rectified logits. Gumbel-Softmax converts rectified logits into differentiable masks, and a contrastive loss aligns masked visual features with the corresponding text embeddings, driving the bias parameters toward meaningful representations. In the distillation stage, the rectified CLIP acts as a frozen teacher for a DeepLab V2 student, supervised by three losses: cross-entropy on pseudo-label masks (mask-guided), L1 feature alignment (feature-guided), and a second contrastive loss on student features (text-guided), with the student inheriting the Query and Reference segmentation heads from the rectification stage.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, the full method (rectification + distillation) achieves 75.4 mIoU, outperforming the strongest CLIP-based baseline CLIP-S4 (72.0) by 3.4% and MaskCLIP+ (70.0) by 5.4%. On PASCAL Context, it reaches 33.8 mIoU (vs. CLIP-S4's 33.6 and MaskCLIP+'s 31.1), and on ADE20K it achieves 14.3 mIoU (vs. MaskCLIP+'s 12.2 and CLIPpy's 13.5). After the rectification stage alone (before distillation), the method already surpasses MaskCLIP by 9.0% on VOC, 4.1% on Context, and 1.6% on ADE20K, demonstrating that bias rectification is the primary driver of improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset where comparisons are available. Against non-language-guided USS methods (Table 1), the distillation result of 75.4 mIoU on VOC exceeds MaskContrast (49.6), ACSeg (47.1), HSG (41.9), Deep Spectral Methods (37.2), SegSort (11.7), and IIC (9.8) by margins of 25.8–65.6 points. Among CLIP-based methods (Table 2), the method beats CLIP-S4 by 3.4% on VOC and 0.2% on Context, MaskCLIP+ by 5.4%/2.7%/2.1% on VOC/Context/ADE20K, CLIPpy by 20.8% on VOC and 0.8% on ADE20K, and ReCo (even with its context-elimination trick) by 20.2% on VOC and 7.6% on Context. Notably, the rectification stage alone (58.5 mIoU on VOC) already surpasses ReCo (55.2) and CLIPpy (54.6), indicating that bias rectification provides the bulk of the gain before any architectural distillation is applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based unsupervised semantic segmentation line initiated by MaskCLIP, which first adapted CLIP's ViT-B/16 image encoder for dense prediction by converting the final attention layers into 1×1 convolutions and using text embeddings as segmentation head weights. The key departure is the explicit identification and modeling of two systematic biases—class-preference (CLIP over-predicts certain semantically related classes) and space-preference (CLIP performs worse near image boundaries)—that prior CLIP-based USS methods (MaskCLIP, CLIP-S4, ReCo, CLIPpy) implicitly absorbed without addressing. The class-preference bias is captured via a learnable Reference prompt (inspired by CoOp), while the space-preference bias is captured by projecting ViT positional embeddings through a small convolutional network; both are then subtracted from the Query logits in a simple element-wise operation. A distillation stage transfers the rectified CLIP's knowledge to a DeepLab V2 student using mask-, feature-, and text-guided losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete diagnostic for why CLIP-based segmentation underperforms its image-level classification potential: the image-text alignment learned at the global level does not transfer cleanly to the pixel level, and the resulting biases are systematic rather than random. The rectification mechanism is lightweight (a learnable prompt plus a 3-layer conv on positional embeddings) and the logit-subtraction formulation is easy to adopt in other CLIP-based dense prediction pipelines. Limitations include the fact that CLIP remains frozen throughout, the bias parameters are dataset-specific (retraining is needed for a new category set), and the distillation student (DeepLab V2) is a relatively old architecture. Open questions include whether the identified biases generalize to other vision-language models (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2+text) and whether a single learned bias representation could transfer across datasets.
