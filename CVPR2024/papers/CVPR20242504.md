## Problem

Existing learning-based Event Stream Super-Resolution (ESR) methods process positive and negative events in a mixed paradigm, which forces a single network to simultaneously model the distinct data distributions of each polarity and capture their complementary correlations. This limits the ability to effectively mine unique characteristics of each event type and to mutually refine them, requiring elaborate designs and high model capacities to achieve competitive performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution event stream, decouples it into separate positive and negative event count images (each H×W), and processes them through parallel streams. The output is a high-resolution event count image produced via pixel shuffle, which is then resampled into a high-resolution event stream. Training operates on segments of T=9 consecutive frames with a sliding-window MSE loss, and the method supports 2×, 4×, and 8× super-resolution factors on both synthetic (NFS-syn, RGB-syn) and real (EventNFS) data.

## Method

BMCNET employs a two-stream architecture with L-layer parallel streams for positive and negative events, each containing spatial and temporal sub-streams. The core innovation is the Bilateral Information Exchange (BIE) module, which models correlations between the two event types along the channel dimension (treating each channel as a structural representation) rather than the spatial dimension, thereby mitigating misleading effects from noise and empty pixels inherent in sparse event data. BIE uses scaled dot-product attention to aggregate global structural representations across channels, combined with a gated mixing module that fuses exchanged global information with local structural details from a residual branch. A Cross-Level Interaction Representation (CIR) is maintained across layers and time steps to propagate hierarchical spatial-temporal contexts, and BIE is applied both inter-stream (between P and N streams) and inner-stream (between spatial and temporal sub-streams) at every layer for multi-level information exchange.

## Results

On the 4× super-resolution task, BMCNET achieves RMSE of 0.552 on NFS-syn, 0.274 on RGB-syn, and 0.625 on EventNFS-real, outperforming the previous SOTA RecEvSR (0.607, 0.296, 0.670) by an average of 11%. The lighter variant BMCNET-plain (inter-stream BIE only) reaches 0.577 / 0.285 / 0.619 with only 1.0M parameters (vs. 1.8M for RecEvSR), yielding a 7.5% average improvement with 40% fewer parameters. On downstream object recognition (NCars, 4×), BMCNET attains 68.95% ACC and 69.23% AUC, surpassing RecEvSR (62.37% / 63.07%) and RSTT (63.02% / 64.29%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMCNET outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and scale. Against the closest ESR-specific competitor RecEvSR [41], BMCNET reduces 4× RMSE from 0.607 to 0.552 on NFS-syn (9.1% relative improvement) and from 0.670 to 0.625 on EventNFS-real (6.7%), while BMCNET-plain already beats RecEvSR with 40% fewer parameters. Against the Transformer-based video SR method RSTT [13], BMCNET-plain achieves a 3.5% average RMSE reduction with 73% fewer parameters (1.0M vs. 4.1M). The image SR baseline SRFBN [21] and video SR baseline RLSP [8] both trail BMCNET by roughly 7–12% RMSE at 4×, confirming that generic SR architectures do not exploit event-specific structure. EventZoom [7], the first learning-based ESR method, performs worst among learned methods (4× NFS-syn RMSE 1.024 vs. 0.552 for BMCNET), largely due to error accumulation from cascaded 2× runs and its 3D U-Net's difficulty with high-factor upsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RecEvSR (Weng et al., ECCV 2022), which introduced recurrent architectures for event stream super-resolution, and departs from the mixed-processing paradigm shared by both EventZoom and RecEvSR, where positive and negative events are handled jointly in a single network. The key conceptual shift is a decoupled two-stream architecture with a bilateral information exchange (BIE) module that performs attention along the channel dimension (treating each channel as a structural representation) rather than the spatial dimension, explicitly motivated by the sparsity and noise inherent in event data. The design also draws on scaled dot-product attention and gated mixing mechanisms from the broader transformer and feature-fusion literature, repurposing them for cross-polarity event interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11% RMSE improvement over the previous SOTA (RecEvSR) with a two-stream decoupled design demonstrates that the mixed-processing assumption in prior ESR work was a genuine bottleneck, and the BIE module offers a reusable mechanism for selective cross-stream information exchange that could transfer to other event-based tasks. However, the evaluation is constrained by the scarcity of real LR-HR event pairs (EventNFS only provides 2× and 4×), and the synthetic datasets (NFS-syn, RGB-syn) may not fully capture sensor-specific noise characteristics. Open questions include whether the decoupled paradigm and channel-dimension attention generalize to higher-resolution event sensors, longer temporal sequences, or other event-based downstream tasks beyond recognition and reconstruction.
