## Problem

Finetuning large pre-trained transformer models (e.g., 70B-parameter LLMs) requires tens to hundreds of GB of GPU memory, making it infeasible under limited resources. Existing pruning techniques that preserve accuracy require finetuning the full model on GPU before or after pruning, creating a circular dependency: you need the full model in GPU memory to prune it effectively. This paper addresses how to prune and finetune large transformers without ever loading the complete model onto the GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241083/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained transformer model (ViT, BERT, Mask2Former/Swin variants), a downstream finetuning dataset, and a target structured pruning ratio (e.g., 33.3% or 50%). It produces a pruned finetuned model where attention heads and feedforward hidden dimensions are removed, along with a measured reduction in peak GPU memory (up to 65%). The full model is always resident on CPU; only a pruned subset plus a finetuning mask is transferred to GPU at each iteration, and the pruned model's weight matrices are physically smaller (fewer heads, narrower FF layers) rather than merely masked.

## Method

RECAP cycles through three stages—pruning, finetuning, and updating—over K iterations, with the CPU orchestrating which model chunks to process and which weights to update. In the pruning stage, importance is estimated via first-order Taylor expansion over a small sampled subset, and structured groups (attention heads or FF hidden dimensions) are removed iteratively until the target sparsity is reached. In the finetuning stage, a Fisher-information-based mask selects only the highest-gradient weight groups for update, so optimizer states and gradients are stored exclusively for that subset, further shrinking GPU memory. After finetuning, updated weights and momentum values are written back to the full CPU-resident model, and the loop repeats on a different chunk. This iterative exploration prevents the early saturation that plagues pre-finetune pruning while avoiding the full-model GPU load required by post-finetune pruning.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ViT-base (86M params), RECAP achieves 88.34% top-1 accuracy at 431 MB GPU memory, compared to 88.93% for Post-FT pruning at 1200 MB and 88.40% for Movement pruning at 1216 MB. For ViT-large (307M), RECAP reaches 91.93% at 1251 MB versus 92.41% for Post-FT at 3854 MB, a 68% memory reduction. On semantic segmentation (Mask2Former w/ Swin-large, Cityscapes), RECAP achieves 79.50 mIoU at 3647 MB, matching Post-FT (79.48 mIoU) while using 40% less memory. On GLUE NLU tasks with BERT-base at 50% pruning, RECAP matches Post-FT on SST-2 (92.20) and is within 0.11 on CoLA (57.67 vs 57.78) at 45% of the memory footprint (702 vs 1553 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA [20], RECAP consistently outperforms in accuracy across all model sizes (e.g., 88.34% vs 86.43% on ViT-base CIFAR-100; 91.93% vs 90.81% on ViT-large) while using comparable or lower memory (431 vs 480 MB for ViT-base), because LoRA still requires loading the full model weights on GPU. Against Post-FT pruning [28], RECAP achieves within 0.5–1.2% accuracy (88.34 vs 88.93 on ViT-base CIFAR-100; 91.93 vs 92.41 on ViT-large) but reduces GPU memory by 64–69% (431 vs 1200 MB; 1251 vs 3854 MB), since Post-FT requires finetuning the full model before pruning. Against Movement pruning [34], RECAP matches or slightly trails in accuracy (88.34 vs 88.40 on ViT-base; 91.93 vs 92.08 on ViT-large) while using roughly 64% less memory (431 vs 1216 MB), as Movement pruning also operates on the full model during joint optimization. On semantic segmentation with Mask2Former, RECAP nearly ties Post-FT and Movement on Cityscapes (79.50 vs 79.48 vs 79.66 mIoU for Swin-large) at 40% lower memory. On GLUE NLU tasks, RECAP matches Post-FT exactly on SST-2 (92.20) and is within 0.11 MCC on CoLA (57.67 vs 57.78) at 45% of the memory cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECAP builds directly on the Taylor-approximation-based weight importance estimation introduced by Molchanov et al. [27] for CNN pruning, extending it to structured (head-level and hidden-dimension-level) pruning of transformer architectures. It departs from both pre-finetune pruning (which ignores task-specific information during the pruning decision) and post-finetune pruning (which requires loading and finetuning the full model on GPU before pruning), by interleaving pruning and finetuning in an iterative CPU-GPU loop. The Fisher-information-based finetuning mask, which selectively updates only a subset of pruned weights, is a novel mechanism not present in prior pruning or adapter-based methods like LoRA [20] or Movement Pruning [34].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RECAP makes finetuning of large transformer models feasible on hardware that cannot accommodate the full model, reducing peak GPU memory by up to 65% while staying within 1–2% accuracy of post-finetune pruning across vision and NLU benchmarks. This is practically significant for researchers and practitioners working with proprietary or privacy-sensitive data who must finetune locally on limited GPUs. Open questions include whether the iterative approach scales to truly massive models (70B+ parameters) where even the pruned subnetwork may exceed single-GPU capacity, how the method interacts with quantization in practice, and whether the structured-pruning constraint limits achievable compression ratios (performance degrades sharply beyond ~66% pruning).
