## Problem

Category-level object pose refinement remains challenging because of large shape variations between the target object and the category-level shape prior. The existing method CATRE relies on a PointNet encoder designed for classification/segmentation, which cannot capture fine-grained geometric relationships needed to align differently-shaped point clouds, and it processes the observed point cloud and shape prior features independently until a late concatenation stage, missing opportunities for early geometric alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240306/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method receives an initial pose and size estimate (R₀, t₀, s₀), an observed point cloud of 512 points, and a category shape prior of 512 points. Both point clouds are first focalized (translated/scaled/rotated) using the initial estimate before entering the network. The output is a rotation error matrix ΔR (parameterized by two 3D vectors with the third column computed via cross product), a translation error vector Δt, and a size error vector Δs, which are applied iteratively to progressively refine the pose estimate.

## Method

GeoReF replaces CATRE's PointNet encoder with an HS-layer (hybrid-scope geometric feature extraction via 3D graph convolution) augmented with three learnable affine transformation (LAT) matrices: one applied to the input point cloud in Euclidean space, one to the translation/size features, and one to the rotation features. These LATs allow the network to dynamically warp geometric correspondences between the observed object and the shape prior, addressing the shape variation problem that causes standard graph convolutions to fail in refinement. A cross-cloud transformation (CCT) mechanism then uses the shape prior's feature transformation matrices to transform the observed point cloud's features, enabling early and efficient information mixing rather than late concatenation. Finally, unlike CATRE, the translation and size prediction branch also consumes shape prior features (via CCT and concatenation), not just observed point cloud features. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with supervised loss and applied iteratively for progressive refinement.

## Results

On REAL275, GeoReF (applied to SPD initial estimates) achieves 51.8% IoU75, 54.4% 5°2cm, 60.3% 5°5cm, 71.9% 10°2cm, and 79.4% 10°5cm, outperforming the prior SOTA refinement method CATRE by 8.2% on IoU75, 8.6% on 5°2cm, and 10.5% on 10°2cm. On CAMERA25, GeoReF trained on only 2% of the training images (5K) already surpasses fully-trained CATRE (275K images) on IoU75 (77.5 vs. 76.1) and 5°5cm (81.1 vs. 80.3). The method also consistently improves initial estimates from diverse pose estimation backbones (HS-Pose, GPV-Pose, Self-DPDN) by 4–15% across metrics, whereas CATRE degrades on some high-accuracy initializations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoReF outperforms CATRE (the direct refinement baseline) on all five REAL275 metrics, with the largest gains on 10°2cm (+10.5%) and 5°2cm (+8.6%), confirming that the learnable affine transformations and cross-cloud transformation address shape-variation failures in CATRE's PointNet encoder. Against the strongest pose estimation method HS-Pose, GeoReF-on-SPD leads on four of five metrics (IoU75: 51.8 vs. 39.1; 5°2cm: 54.4 vs. 46.5; 5°5cm: 60.3 vs. 55.2; 10°2cm: 71.9 vs. 68.6) but trails on 10°5cm (79.4 vs. 82.7). GeoReF also surpasses GPV-Pose, SGPA, RBP-Pose, DualPoseNet, CR-Net, and NOCS by wide margins on every metric. In the generalizability test on CAMERA25, GeoReF trained on only 5K images (2% of training data) matches or exceeds fully-trained CATRE (275K images) on all reported metrics, demonstrating substantially better data efficiency. When applied as a refinement on top of different initial estimators, GeoReF consistently improves Self-DPDN (+9.6% on 5°2cm), GPV-Pose (+15.4% on 5°2cm), and HS-Pose (+5.2% on 5°2cm), while CATRE actually degrades Self-DPDN and HS-Pose on some metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CATRE (ECCV 2022), the first category-level pose refinement framework, by replacing its PointNet encoder with an HS-layer augmented by learnable affine transformations (LATs) to handle geometric correspondences across shape variations. It also draws on HS-Pose (CVPR 2023) for the hybrid-scope feature extraction backbone. The key conceptual departure from CATRE is the introduction of cross-cloud transformation for early feature fusion between observed and prior point clouds, and the incorporation of shape prior information into translation and size prediction—both of which CATRE handled only at a late concatenation stage or not at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art geometric encoders (3DGCN, HS-Encoder) actually degrade performance in the refinement setting compared to PointNet—due to unaddressed shape variation—challenges the assumption that stronger geometric feature extractors universally improve pose tasks. The method's strong generalization (outperforming fully-trained CATRE with only 2% of training data) suggests the LAT mechanism captures transferable geometric alignment principles. Open questions include whether the approach extends to articulated objects, categories with extreme shape diversity, and whether the iterative refinement loop can be made more efficient for real-time deployment.
