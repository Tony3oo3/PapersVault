## Problem

Existing pseudo-labeling methods for semi-supervised monocular 3D object detection rely solely on 2D classification confidence to filter pseudo-labels, ignoring the significant quality misalignment between 2D and 3D attribute predictions (e.g., high-confidence detections often have poor depth estimates). Additionally, noisy depth supervision from pseudo-labels frequently causes gradient conflicts with reliable supervision sources, undermining overall optimization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a single RGB image and outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized by category, depth, 3D dimensions, orientation, and 3D center location. In the semi-supervised setting, a teacher network generates pseudo-labels on unlabeled images while a student network is trained jointly on labeled images (with ground truth) and unlabeled images (with pseudo-labels). The pseudo-labels are decoupled into 2D attributes (classification, 2D size, projected 3D center) and 3D attributes (depth, 3D size, orientation), each supervised independently.

## Method

The Decoupled Pseudo-labeling (DPL) approach introduces two complementary modules within a teacher-student framework. The Decoupled Pseudo-label Generation (DPG) module separates 2D and 3D pseudo-label filtering: 2D attributes use a confidence threshold, while 3D attributes are validated via a Homography-based Pseudo-label Mining (HPM) algorithm that iteratively estimates a 2D-to-BEV homography matrix from initial reliable pseudo-labels (selected by depth uncertainty) and then identifies additional reliable 3D pseudo-labels by measuring the deviation between homography-projected and directly-computed BEV coordinates. The Depth Gradient Projection (DGP) module addresses residual noise by projecting the pseudo-label depth gradient onto the principal reliable gradient direction (combined ground-truth depth and other-attribute gradients) whenever a negative cosine similarity indicates conflict, thereby eliminating the harmful gradient component while preserving the beneficial one.

## Results

On the KITTI test set (Car category), DPL_FLEX achieves 16.67 AP₃D (Mod.) and 22.12 AP_BEV (Mod.), surpassing the supervised MonoFlex baseline by +4.10 and +4.18 respectively. It also outperforms the best prior semi-supervised methods Mix-Teaching (14.34 AP₃D) and MVC-MonoDet (14.56 AP₃D) by +2.33 and +2.11 AP₃D, and +2.61 and +2.01 AP_BEV. The method further improves over the strongest fully supervised baseline HomoLoss (14.94 AP₃D) by +1.73 AP₃D, establishing a new state-of-the-art across all evaluated settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPL_FLEX outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the KITTI test set. Against the supervised MonoFlex baseline, it gains +4.10 AP₃D (Mod.) and +4.18 AP_BEV (Mod.), demonstrating the substantial value of unlabeled data when properly exploited. Among prior semi-supervised methods, it beats Mix-Teaching by +2.33 AP₃D and +2.61 AP_BEV, and MVC-MonoDet by +2.11 AP₃D and +2.01 AP_BEV, confirming that decoupled pseudo-label generation and gradient projection are more effective than database-oriented pseudo-labeling or multi-view consistency. It also surpasses the strongest fully supervised method HomoLoss (14.94 AP₃D) by +1.73 AP₃D, showing that semi-supervised learning with DPL can exceed fully supervised performance. Cross-detector experiments (Table 4) confirm consistent gains of +1.17 to +2.12 AP₃D (Mod.) across MonoDLE, PGD, GUPNet, and MonoDETR, underscoring the method's generalizability beyond MonoFlex.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the teacher-student semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen et al.) and the MonoFlex detector, while specifically targeting the pseudo-labeling strategy in SSM3OD that prior methods like Mix-Teaching and MVC-MonoDet have addressed. The key departure is identifying a quality misalignment between 2D and 3D attribute predictions that existing pseudo-labeling approaches overlook: prior work filters pseudo-labels using only 2D confidence scores, which the authors show is a poor proxy for 3D attribute (especially depth) quality. The homography-based pseudo-label mining repurposes the geometric relationship already exploited in supervised M3OD methods (e.g., HomoLoss) for a new purpose—iteratively selecting reliable 3D pseudo-labels in BEV space—while the depth gradient projection is a novel gradient-level intervention not seen in prior SSM3OD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D/3D quality misalignment observation (PCC ≈ −0.20 between depth error and classification confidence) is a useful diagnostic for the broader pseudo-labeling literature in multi-task detection, suggesting that confidence-based filtering may be systematically biased for geometric attributes. The method's consistent gains across five different base detectors (MonoDLE, PGD, GUPNet, MonoDETR, MonoFlex) indicate the decoupling principle is architecture-agnostic. However, evaluation is limited to KITTI with a flat-ground assumption inherent to the homography approach, and it remains open whether the gradient projection strategy generalizes to other semi-supervised settings or to datasets without a well-defined ground plane. The practical impact is significant for reducing annotation cost in autonomous driving pipelines, but the single-dataset validation limits broader claims.
