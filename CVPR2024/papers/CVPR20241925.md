## Problem

Simultaneous relighting and novel-view synthesis from a single portrait image remains challenging because it requires accurate 3D geometry estimation from monocular input, and prior methods either rely on multi-view capture rigs or impose simplified physical reflectance/lighting models (Lambertian, Phong, spherical harmonics) that limit expressiveness and produce unrealistic shading. Additionally, head-pose-dependent lighting effects (e.g., moving specular highlights and cast shadows as the head rotates) are largely unexplored in existing free-view relighting approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 face crop, a target environment map (reshaped into 1D tensors and tiled to 2D maps), a 3×3 head-pose rotation matrix, and a camera pose as inputs. It outputs a relit tri-plane (three 2D feature maps on orthogonal planes) from which high-resolution RGB images are rendered via volume rendering at 64×64 resolution followed by a super-resolution network. The tri-plane representation is the key intermediate 3D artifact that enables rendering to any arbitrary viewpoint and head pose combination.

## Method

Holo-Relighting operates in three stages: (1) a U-Net-based delighting stage estimates albedo and normals from the input; (2) GAN inversion on pretrained EG3D lifts the albedo into 3D-aware features via a tri-plane generator; (3) a pyramidal relighting network consumes the GAN features, environment map, head pose, and camera pose to predict a shading tri-plane that is added to the albedo tri-plane, producing a relit tri-plane for volume rendering. The relighting net uses zero-initialized convolutions for training stability and a coarse-to-fine feature fusion strategy to synthesize both global diffuse lighting and high-frequency specular details without any explicit physical lighting model. Two data-rendering techniques improve training quality: multi-view GAN inversion (extending single-view inversion with multi-view regularization and camera pose optimization to resolve depth ambiguity) and a quotient-image-inspired shading transfer that creates pseudo ground-truth images consistent with the inverted appearance, avoiding blurriness from per-pixel supervision on misaligned details.

## Results

On the free-view relighting benchmark (235 OLAT test images), Holo-Relighting achieves LPIPS 0.0917, NIQE 5.273, identity preservation (Deg) 0.8978, PSNR 27.35, and SSIM 0.8511, substantially outperforming both NeRFFaceLighting and FaceLit across all five metrics. On the 2D portrait relighting benchmark (940 OLAT test images), the method achieves LPIPS 0.0981, PSNR 26.17, and SSIM 0.8544, again surpassing Total Relighting on every metric. The largest gains are in perceptual quality (LPIPS reduced by ~58% vs. NeRFFaceLighting) and fidelity (PSNR improved by ~7.9 dB over FaceLit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Holo-Relighting dominates all three baselines on every reported metric. Against NeRFFaceLighting [28] (a NeRF-based method using spherical harmonics lighting), the improvement is the most dramatic: LPIPS drops from 0.2179 to 0.0917, PSNR rises from 19.40 to 27.35 dB, and identity preservation (Deg) jumps from 0.6953 to 0.8978, indicating that the implicit tri-plane relighting captures far more geometric detail than the SH-based approach. Against FaceLit [48] (a 3D GAN with explicit Phong reflectance), Holo-Relighting still leads by a wide margin (PSNR 27.35 vs. 20.86, SSIM 0.8511 vs. 0.7281), confirming that the learned non-physical relighting module outperforms simplified reflectance models. In the 2D relighting setting, Holo-Relighting (rendered at the input pose) outperforms Total Relighting [45] by a large gap (PSNR 26.17 vs. 19.58, SSIM 0.8544 vs. 0.6515), showing that the volumetric formulation also yields superior 2D results. Qualitatively, the paper reports that NeRFFaceLighting and FaceLit produce visible artifacts and fail to reproduce complex lighting effects (specular highlights, cast shadows) present in the reference renders, while Holo-Relighting matches the reference most closely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on EG3D's tri-plane representation and its GAN inversion pipeline to extract 3D-aware features from a single portrait, extending Xie et al.'s pseudo-multi-view inversion with a multi-view regularization strategy for more accurate geometry encoding. It departs from prior 3D relighting methods (FaceLit, NeRFFaceLighting, VoluX-GAN) that embed explicit physical reflectance models (Lambertian, Phong, spherical harmonics) by instead training a learned relighting module on GAN features to implicitly capture complex non-Lambertian effects. The shading transfer technique is inspired by the classical quotient image decomposition, repurposed here to create pseudo ground-truth training targets that align with imperfect GAN inversion outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Holo-Relighting demonstrates that a fully implicit, physics-free relighting module can synthesize cast shadows and specular highlights with state-of-the-art quality while simultaneously supporting viewpoint and head-pose control from a single image—capabilities that prior methods required multi-view capture or explicit physical models to approximate. The practical implication is that consumer photographers could achieve studio-level lighting and viewpoint editing without specialized hardware. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond faces to full-body relighting, whether real-time inference is feasible given the GAN inversion step, and how well the method handles extreme lighting conditions or subjects outside the training distribution (e.g., heavy accessories, unusual skin tones).
