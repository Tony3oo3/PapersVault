## Problem

Existing layout generation methods assume a fixed background image is already provided, leaving the creation of appropriate backgrounds unaddressed. Standard text-to-image diffusion models lack the spatial control to preserve non-salient regions needed for overlaying layout elements, and no prior work jointly refines both the background and layout for a harmonious composition.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a free-form text prompt describing the design theme, an optional binary layout mask specifying regions to keep non-salient, and a layout element specification (types and count, e.g., '2 × Text Box'). It outputs a 512×512 background image and discretized bounding-box positions for each layout element, which together form the design template. The layout generator consumes the background at 224×224 through an image encoder, and the iterative refinement loop can re-generate the background from its inverted latent using the synthesized layout as a new attention-reduction mask.

## Method

Desigen is a two-stage pipeline: a Stable Diffusion-based background generator augmented with a salient attention constraint loss (L_c) that forces cross-attention maps to approximate externally detected saliency during training, plus a training-free attention reduction step that multiplicatively scales cross-attention weights (β=0.01) in user-specified mask regions at inference. The layout generator is an autoregressive Transformer decoder (adapted from LayoutTransformer) conditioned on a Swin image encoder of the background, predicting a flattened token sequence of element categories and discretized positions. The key differentiator is an iterative refinement strategy: after an initial background–layout pair is produced, the layout is converted into a region mask, attention reduction is applied to re-generate the background with reduced saliency in those regions, and the layout is re-synthesized on the adjusted background, repeating over multiple rounds to lower occlusion and improve visual accessibility.

## Results

On the Web-design dataset, Desigen achieves a salient ratio of 20.65 and FID of 31.52 for background generation, reducing the salient ratio by over 50% compared to Stable Diffusion (35.92) and outperforming the fine-tuned SD baseline (23.61) on both cleanliness and aesthetics. For layout generation, the method achieves an occlusion score of 13.47 (close to real data at 11.89) and overlap of 14.41, substantially outperforming LayoutTransformer (occlusion 28.26) while matching its alignment quality (0.35 vs. 0.23). In human evaluation, Desigen scores highest among all methods on cleanliness (3.95/5) and relevancy (3.99/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For background generation, Desigen decisively beats Stable Diffusion on salient ratio (20.65 vs. 35.92) and FID (31.52 vs. 39.36), and also outperforms the fine-tuned SD variant (23.61 / 37.41) on both metrics, confirming that the salient attention constraint and attention reduction are more effective than simple fine-tuning. The CLIP score is slightly lower than vanilla SD (29.20 vs. 31.21), which the authors attribute to the inherent trade-off between preserving empty space and maintaining text-image relevance. For layout generation, Desigen dramatically outperforms LayoutTransformer on occlusion (13.47 vs. 28.26), indicating far better visual accessibility, while LayoutTransformer retains a small edge on alignment (0.23 vs. 0.35). Against CanvasVAE and DS-GAN, Desigen wins on alignment (0.35 vs. 1.09 and 1.37) and overlap (14.41 vs. 20.16 and 16.02), with occlusion scores comparable to or slightly better than both (13.47 vs. 13.95 and 13.53). Overall, Desigen is the only method that simultaneously achieves low occlusion and competitive alignment/overlap, approaching real-data values across all three layout metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Desigen sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2) and content-aware layout generation (LayoutTransformer, CanvasVAE, DS-GAN). The key technical departure is the observation that cross-attention maps in diffusion U-Nets correlate strongly with image saliency (cosine similarity > 0.70 for dominant pairs), which the authors exploit to add a saliency-aware attention constraint during training and a training-free attention reduction during inference—building on the attention-editing insights from prompt-to-prompt and DiffEdit but repurposing them for spatial preservation rather than semantic editing. The iterative refinement loop, where the layout mask feeds back into background regeneration, has no direct precedent in prior layout-generation literature, which treats the background as a fixed input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end pipeline that jointly generates both the background and layout of a design template with an explicit harmonization step, and the accompanying Web-design dataset (40k+ banners with layout metadata and text descriptions) fills a gap in publicly available design data. However, the pipeline is limited to text boxes and buttons, does not incorporate graphic design principles (alignment rules, visual hierarchy, typography), and the iterative refinement yields only modest metric improvements (occlusion drops from 11.66 to 8.69 over three rounds). Open questions include whether the attention-manipulation techniques generalize to more complex design elements (decorations, fonts, multi-layer compositions), whether the iterative loop can be made differentiable or learned end-to-end, and how the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-page design systems beyond the demonstrated slide-deck application.
