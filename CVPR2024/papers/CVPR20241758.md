## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting scenes consist of millions of Gaussians requiring several gigabytes of storage and a specialized compute-shader renderer that cannot be integrated into VR/AR engines or games. No compression scheme existed for point-based radiance fields to enable interactive novel view synthesis on low-power devices with limited video memory and bandwidth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241758/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an already-optimized 3D Gaussian scene (position, quaternion rotation, scaling vector, opacity, and up to 48 SH coefficients per Gaussian, typically millions of Gaussians) and produces a compressed scene file using 8-bit Min-Max quantized parameters, 32-bit codebook indices, and DEFLATE entropy encoding in Morton order. For rendering, the compressed file plus camera extrinsics/intrinsics are consumed by a WebGPU hardware-rasterization pipeline that outputs novel-view RGB images at 1080p in real time, even on integrated GPUs like Intel UHD 11 or AMD Radeon R9 380.

## Method

The compression pipeline has three stages. First, sensitivity-aware vector clustering computes a per-parameter sensitivity score (gradient of total image energy w.r.t. each parameter) and uses weighted k-means to cluster SH coefficients and scale-normalized covariance matrices into 4096-entry codebooks, excluding high-sensitivity Gaussians (above thresholds βc and βg) which are stored directly. Second, quantization-aware fine-tuning optimizes the compressed scene on training images with simulated 8-bit Min-Max quantization (16-bit for positions) to recover information lost during clustering. Third, Gaussians are linearized along a Z-order Morton curve and compressed with DEFLATE (LZ77 + Huffman) to exploit spatial coherence. For rendering, the compute-shader pipeline is replaced with hardware rasterization: a pre-pass projects and depth-sorts Gaussians via Onesweep, then each Gaussian is rendered as a planar quad whose pixel shader computes the exponential Gaussian falloff and order-dependent alpha blending.

## Results

The compressed 3D Gaussian Splatting representation achieves an average compression ratio of 25.8× (up to 31× on individual scenes) while maintaining nearly identical reconstruction quality, with only a 0.23 dB average PSNR drop across real-world scenes (e.g., 27.21→26.98 on Mip-NeRF360, 23.36→23.32 on Tanks & Temples). Scene file sizes shrink from hundreds of MB to under 30 MB (e.g., 795 MB → 28.8 MB on Mip-NeRF360). The hardware-rasterization renderer delivers up to 4× higher framerates than the original 3DGS compute pipeline (e.g., 321 vs. 93 FPS on Bicycle at 1080p on an RTX A5000), and enables rendering on integrated GPUs (Intel UHD 11, AMD R9 380) where the original pipeline cannot run at all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), which serves as both the scene representation being compressed and the rendering speed reference. On reconstruction quality, the compressed method essentially ties the baseline: the average PSNR drops by only 0.02 dB (26.58→26.56) and SSIM by 0.009 across real-world scenes, well below the 0.5 dB threshold considered perceptually indistinguishable. On storage, the compressed method wins decisively, reducing file size by 25.8× on average (640 MB → 24 MB). On rendering speed, the compressed hardware-rasterization renderer outperforms the original compute pipeline by up to 4× (321 vs. 93 FPS on Bicycle, 502 vs. 184 FPS on Bonsai at 1080p on an RTX A5000), and uniquely supports low-end integrated GPUs (Intel UHD 11, AMD R9 380) where the original pipeline produces no usable output. The compression overhead is modest: roughly 5–6 minutes of additional processing, increasing total reconstruction time by about 10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al.) by compressing its optimized scene parameters and replacing the compute-shader renderer with a hardware rasterization pipeline. The compression strategy adapts sensitivity-aware vector quantization from volumetric NeRF compression (Li et al., Takikawa et al.) to the point-based Gaussian representation, and borrows quantization-aware training from neural network weight compression (Rastegari et al.). The key conceptual departure is that prior scene compression work targeted grid-based NeRFs, whereas this is the first to compress a point-based radiance field, exploiting the spatial coherence of Gaussian parameters along a Morton curve for entropy encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 31× compression with sub-0.25 PSNR degradation makes 3D Gaussian Splatting viable on low-power devices (mobile, VR/AR headsets) and for network streaming, a practical gap that grid-based NeRF compression addressed but point-based representations had not. The hardware rasterization renderer, implemented in WebGPU/Rust, enables seamless integration into existing polygon-rendering applications and achieves up to 4× speedup over the original compute pipeline. A notable limitation is the inability to aggressively compress 3D positions without significant quality loss, leaving the positional data as the dominant remaining storage cost. Future work on end-to-end positional compression and reduced training memory would further close the gap for on-device reconstruction.
