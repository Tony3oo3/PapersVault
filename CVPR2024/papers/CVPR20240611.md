## Problem

Transformer-based trajectory prediction models trained at a fixed observation length suffer significant performance degradation when evaluated at different lengths, a phenomenon the authors term Observation Length Shift. This gap is distinct from domain shift or occlusion issues and remains largely unexplored. The paper identifies two root causes specific to Transformer architectures: positional encoding deviation (sinusoidal encodings shift when sequence length changes) and normalization shift (layer normalization statistics become mismatched across lengths).

## Input / Output

During training, the method simultaneously consumes three truncated versions of the same trajectory (short, medium, long lengths, e.g., H={2,3,4} for nuScenes or H={10,20,30} for Argoverse 1) along with HD map context, feeding each into a corresponding sub-network. At inference, only the single sub-network whose length matches the available observation is activated, producing a parametric distribution (e.g., bivariate Gaussian or Laplace mixture) over K future trajectory samples. The framework also supports evaluation at unseen lengths by routing to the nearest-length sub-network.

## Method

FLN wraps any Transformer-based predictor with two mechanisms. FlexiLength Calibration (FLC) shares spatial encoder, temporal encoder, and decoder weights across three length-specific sub-networks and applies Temporal Distillation: the longest-length prediction serves as a teacher, and KL-divergence losses pull the shorter-length predictions toward it, while a standard negative log-likelihood loss supervises the longest prediction against ground truth. FlexiLength Adaptation (FLA) addresses the two identified failure modes by assigning each sub-network its own learnable positional encoding (Independent Positional Encoding) and its own layer normalization parameters (Specialized Layer Normalization), so that normalization statistics and positional signals are calibrated per length. The total loss is L_reg + λ·L_kl with λ=1, requiring only a single training pass. At inference, the sub-network matching the observation length is selected, and for unseen lengths the nearest sub-network is activated, enabling flexible evaluation without retraining.

## Results

On nuScenes (AgentFormer, K=5), FLN achieves ADE5/FDE5 of 1.92/3.91 at 2 timesteps, 1.88/3.89 at 3 timesteps, and 1.83/3.78 at 4 timesteps, outperforming Isolated Training (2.02/4.23, 1.93/3.97, 1.86/3.89) at every length. On Argoverse 1 (HiVT-64, K=6), FLN achieves ADE6/FDE6 of 0.81/1.25 at 10 timesteps, 0.72/1.08 at 20 timesteps, and 0.65/0.98 at 30 timesteps, again surpassing IT (0.92/1.43, 0.81/1.17, 0.69/1.04) across all lengths. FLN requires only 6.68M parameters (vs. 6.67×3M for IT/Joint) while delivering the best results, and on ETH/UCY it consistently beats IT at observation lengths of 2, 6, and 8 timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AgentFormer baseline (trained at 4 timesteps, tested at 2/3/4), FLN reduces ADE5 by 0.34 at 2 timesteps (1.92 vs. 2.26) and by 0.12 at 3 timesteps (1.88 vs. 2.00), while matching the 4-timestep result (1.83 vs. 1.86). Against Isolated Training on nuScenes, FLN wins at all three lengths with the largest gap at 2 timesteps (1.92 vs. 2.02 ADE5) and 4 timesteps (1.83 vs. 1.86 ADE5, 3.78 vs. 3.89 FDE5). On Argoverse 1 with HiVT-64, FLN outperforms IT by 0.11 ADE6 at 10 timesteps (0.81 vs. 0.92), 0.09 at 20 timesteps (0.72 vs. 0.81), and 0.04 at 30 timesteps (0.65 vs. 0.69). FLN also surpasses the Joint and Mixed baselines at every observation length on both datasets, while using only a single set of shared weights (6.68M) rather than three separate models (6.67×3M), making it substantially more parameter-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Transformer-based trajectory prediction architectures (AgentFormer and HiVT) and positions its contribution as a general wrapper framework rather than a new model. It draws on prior analyses of positional encoding deviation in Transformers [13, 42] and normalization shift in vision models [71, 72], but applies these insights to the specific and previously underexplored setting of observation length variation in trajectory prediction. The Temporal Distillation component borrows the teacher-student KL divergence mechanism from knowledge distillation literature, while the overall framing departs from domain-shift and incomplete-observation work by isolating length as the sole varying factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FLN offers a drop-in, single-training-session solution for a problem that real-world deployment will inevitably encounter (sensors with variable history availability), avoiding the 3× parameter and compute cost of Isolated Training. The limitation of increased per-iteration training time (processing three sequence lengths simultaneously) is acknowledged but left for future work. An open question is whether the framework's reliance on Transformer-specific components (positional encoding, layer normalization) limits its applicability to non-Transformer architectures like RNNs or state-space models, which the paper mentions but does not test.
