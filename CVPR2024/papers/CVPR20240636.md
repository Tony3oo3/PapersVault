## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods embed object attributes (color, size, spatial relations) but treat them as independent features, ignoring how multiple attributes interact to influence the final prediction. This leads to biased unimodal representations where the model over-focuses on the most salient attribute, and misaligned multimodal fusion where attributes suppressed in one modality are critical in the other, causing incorrect grounding especially in scenes with multiple similar objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240636/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene (K points with XYZ coordinates and RGB colors), a free-form language sentence, and a pool of n=256 pre-detected object proposals from GroupFree. It additionally extracts per-proposal attribute embeddings comprising location (pairwise Euclidean distance and horizontal/vertical angles), size (3D box center plus 24 corner coordinates), and color/material (multi-view RGB features). The output is a grounding confidence score per proposal, with the highest-scoring proposal selected as the grounded target object.

## Method

MA²TransVG introduces two key modules. The Attribute Causal Analysis Module (ACAM) computes Attribute-Vision and Attribute-Text cross-attentions via a multi-head Transformer, then quantifies the causal effect of each attention on the final grounding score by replacing it with a counterfactual (random, uniform, shuffle, or reversed) attention and measuring the prediction difference; this causal effect is supervised with a cross-entropy attribute loss that forces the model to assign discriminative attention across all attributes rather than collapsing onto the most salient one. The Exchanging-based Multimodal Fusion Module (EMFM) then performs attribute-level cross-modal alignment by replacing the θ=10% lowest-attention tokens in each modality with the mean embedding of all tokens from the other modality, iterated over η=4 exchanging layers with shared parameters, before a final FFN produces the fused multimodal embedding for grounding prediction.

## Results

On ScanRefer, MA²TransVG achieves 57.9% Acc@0.25 and 45.7% Acc@0.5 overall, surpassing the previous best (3DRefTR-SP) by +2.4% and +2.2% respectively. On Nr3D, the method reaches 65.2% overall accuracy, outperforming ViL3DRef (64.4%) by +0.8% and EDA (52.1%) by +13.1%. On Sr3D, it achieves 73.9% overall accuracy, exceeding ViL3DRef (72.8%) by +1.1% and 3DRefTR-SR (68.5%) by +5.4%. The 'Multiple' split on ScanRefer (53.8% Acc@0.25) shows the largest relative gains, confirming the benefit of multi-attribute interactions for distinguishing co-occurring objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA²TransVG outperforms all 19 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On ScanRefer, it beats the closest competitor 3DRefTR-SP [26] by +2.4% (Acc@0.25) and +2.2% (Acc@0.5), and surpasses EDA [45] by +3.3% and +3.4% respectively; the gap is largest on the 'Multiple' split (+3.7% over 3DRefTR-SP at Acc@0.25), where distinguishing co-occurring objects is hardest. On Nr3D, the method leads ViL3DRef [7] by +0.8% and EDA [45] by +13.1% overall, with the biggest margin over EDA attributable to explicit multi-attribute causal supervision rather than text decoupling alone. On Sr3D, it exceeds ViL3DRef [7] by +1.1% and 3DRefTR-SR [26] by +5.4%. The method also outperforms M3DRef-CLIP [49] by +15.8% on Nr3D overall, and surpasses the single-stage 3D-SPS [31] by +10.2% on Nr3D, indicating that the two-stage detection-then-matching paradigm combined with attribute-level fusion remains advantageous. No baseline is matched or exceeded by MA²TransVG on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViL3DRef line of work [7], which introduced language-conditioned spatial relation reasoning and sigsoftmax attention fusion for 3D grounding, by generalizing from a single spatial attribute to a multi-attribute framework (location, size, color) and explicitly modeling their interactions. It also draws on the causal inference literature in vision—particularly counterfactual attention learning [38] and Pearl's causal effect formulation [33]—to introduce the first causal-effect-based supervision signal for attention in 3D visual grounding. The token-exchanging fusion mechanism departs from the standard cross-attention paradigm used in most prior 3D VG Transformers, instead performing attribute-level token replacement between modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation revealing that multi-attribute interactions contribute +15.3% accuracy while simply adding the color attribute contributes only +1.2% suggests that how attributes are combined matters far more than which attributes are included—a finding that could reshape how future grounding systems design their feature pipelines. The causal-effect loss provides a principled, interpretable supervision signal for intermediate attention layers, which is underexplored in vision-language tasks. However, the paper acknowledges that view-dependent descriptions remain a weakness, and the causal analysis is limited to a single counterfactual strategy (random attention) that was empirically best, leaving open whether richer causal interventions could yield further gains.
