## Problem

Template-based object pose estimation methods suffer a significant accuracy drop on unseen objects (e.g., 99.1% seen vs. 93.5% unseen on LINEMOD), primarily because the image features used for template matching lack sufficient generalization. This paper identifies the root cause as inadequate discriminative features and explores whether intermediate features from text-to-image diffusion models, which are known to generalize well across diverse scenarios, can close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image cropped at the object center (using ground-truth pose) and feeds it into a frozen Stable Diffusion v1-5 model at timestep t=0 with an unconditioned text embedding, extracting intermediate features from all UNet layers. These features are aggregated into a 64×64 representation, which is then compared via cosine similarity (with template-mask filtering) against pre-rendered template images to output the object class and 3D rotation (rigid transformation from object to camera coordinates).

## Method

The core insight is that different UNet layers of a diffusion model capture features at different granularities (e.g., Layer 5 best for one object, Layer 12 for another), and aggregating them yields a more generalizable representation than any single layer. Three aggregation architectures are proposed: (a) vanilla aggregation with 3×3 convolutions and element-wise addition, (b) nonlinear aggregation replacing linear projections with bottleneck modules (three convolutions + ReLU + skip connection, with zero-initialized last conv inspired by ControlNet), and (c) context-aware weight aggregation that applies average pooling to each extracted feature, concatenates them, and uses an MLP + softmax to learn input-dependent weights for a weighted sum. Only the aggregation network is trained (SD weights frozen) using InfoNCE contrastive loss with positive/negative template pairs, following the Template-pose protocol. The context-aware variant (CWA) achieves the best results, reducing the seen-unseen gap to under 1.5% on LM and outperforming the prior best by 10.9% on unseen O-LM.

## Results

On LINEMOD (LM), the proposed context-aware weight aggregation (CWA) with SD-V1-5 achieves 99.7% Seen and 98.2% Unseen accuracy (Acc15), compared to 99.1% and 93.5% for the previous best method Template-pose. On Occlusion-LINEMOD (O-LM), CWA reaches 82.8% Seen and 85.9% Unseen, outperforming Template-pose (79.4% Seen, 76.3% Unseen) by 3.4% and 9.6% respectively. On T-LESS, the method achieves 71.03% average Recall@VSD with only 21K templates, surpassing Template-pose's 58.87% (with 92K templates) by 12.16%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five prior methods: Balntas et al. [1], Wohlhart et al. [40], MPL [36], Zhao et al. [46], and Template-pose [24]. The proposed CWA with SD-V1-5 decisively outperforms all baselines on both Seen and Unseen splits across all three datasets. The most significant gain is on Unseen O-LM, where CWA achieves 85.9% vs. Template-pose's 76.3% (+9.6%), and on Unseen LM, 98.2% vs. 93.5% (+4.7%). On T-LESS, CWA with only 21K templates (71.03% Recall@VSD) surpasses Template-pose with 92K templates (58.87%) by 12.16%, and notably achieves higher accuracy on unseen objects (71.26%) than seen objects (70.87%). The ablation also shows that replacing the SD backbone with DINOv2 or OpenCLIP degrades unseen performance (97.8% and 97.1% on Unseen LM vs. 98.2%), confirming that diffusion model intermediate features are particularly well-suited for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the template-based object pose estimation paradigm established by Template-Pose (Nguyen et al., CVPR 2022), which uses self-supervised features (MoCo) for matching query images to rendered templates. The key conceptual departure is replacing discriminative self-supervised features with intermediate features extracted from a frozen text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion), motivated by the observation that diffusion features encode richer, more generalizable representations across objects. The paper also draws on the broader literature showing diffusion models capture 3D geometry and multi-granularity information, and borrows the zero-initialization trick from ControlNet for its nonlinear aggregation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen generative model's intermediate features can outperform purpose-built discriminative models (DINOv2, OpenCLIP) for pose estimation, narrowing the seen/unseen generalization gap from ~5.6% to ~1.5% on LM. This suggests that the representational richness of diffusion models, trained on massive image-text corpora, is underexploited for downstream geometric tasks. Limitations include the assumption of 2D localization, restriction to 3D orientation (not full 6DoF), and reliance on a specific frozen model; open questions include whether the approach extends to full 6DoF estimation, more diverse object categories, or whether fine-tuning the diffusion backbone (rather than just the aggregation head) yields further gains.
