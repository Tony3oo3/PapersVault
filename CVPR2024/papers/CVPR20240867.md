## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time, high-quality novel view synthesis from dense views but suffers significant geometry degradation under sparse inputs, where a large portion of scene geometry is incorrectly learned. Existing depth regularization strategies designed for continuous NeRF representations do not transfer to discrete Gaussian primitives, as they cause shape parameters to overfit smooth monocular depth at the expense of fine-grained color appearance, and fixed-scale depth losses overlook small local errors that lead to noisy primitive distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240867/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3 sparse RGB images (8 for Blender) with known camera poses as input, and uses DPT-predicted monocular depth maps as auxiliary supervision. Unlike standard 3DGS which initializes from COLMAP point clouds, DNGaussian starts from a random Gaussian initialization due to SfM instability in sparse-view settings. The output is an optimized 3D Gaussian point cloud that renders novel views at 300 FPS with only 2 GB GPU memory, and a neural color renderer (hash encoder + 5-layer MLP) replaces spherical harmonics to prevent color overfitting under limited views.

## Method

DNGaussian introduces two key mechanisms for depth-regularized Gaussian optimization. First, a Hard and Soft Depth Regularization freezes shape parameters (scale and rotation) and separately renders a 'hard depth' (with a large fixed opacity τ=0.95 to isolate nearest Gaussians) to regularize center positions, and a 'soft depth' (with frozen centers) to regularize opacity, enabling spatial reshaping without blurring color. Second, a Global-Local Depth Normalization applies patch-wise local normalization (zero mean, unit std) to refocus the L2 depth loss on small local errors, combined with a global normalization using the whole-image standard deviation to preserve absolute scale awareness. The full loss combines color reconstruction (L1 + D-SSIM), hard depth regularization, and soft depth regularization (applied after 1000 iterations for stability), trained for 6000 iterations with γ=0.1 weighting the local term.

## Results

On LLFF (3 views), DNGaussian achieves the best LPIPS (0.294) among all methods, with competitive PSNR (19.12) and SSIM (0.591) close to SparseNeRF (19.86/0.624) and FreeNeRF (19.63/0.612). On DTU (3 views), DNGaussian attains the best LPIPS (0.176) and SSIM (0.790), with PSNR (18.91) slightly below FreeNeRF (19.92). On Blender (8 views), DNGaussian achieves the best scores across all metrics: PSNR 24.305, SSIM 0.886, and LPIPS 0.088. In terms of efficiency, DNGaussian trains in 3.5 minutes on 2 GB GPU and renders at 300 FPS, compared to 1.5–2.3 hours on 12–48 GB and 0.09 FPS for the strongest NeRF baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNGaussian outperforms all NeRF-based baselines on LPIPS across both LLFF and DTU, indicating superior perceptual quality and detail preservation. On Blender (8 views), DNGaussian achieves the best PSNR (24.305), SSIM (0.886), and LPIPS (0.088), beating the previous best FreeNeRF (24.259/0.883/0.098) on all three metrics. On LLFF, DNGaussian trails SparseNeRF by 0.74 dB in PSNR (19.12 vs. 19.86) and FreeNeRF by 0.51 dB, but leads both in LPIPS by 0.014–0.034; the authors attribute the PSNR gap to Gaussian fields exposing black background in invisible regions. On DTU, DNGaussian surpasses FreeNeRF in SSIM (0.790 vs. 0.787) and LPIPS (0.176 vs. 0.182) but falls short in PSNR (18.91 vs. 19.92), which the authors link to scale variance and textureless-board noise. In efficiency, DNGaussian is 25× faster in training (3.5 min vs. 1.5–2.3 h) and over 3000× faster in rendering (300 FPS vs. 0.09 FPS) while using only 2 GB GPU memory versus 12–48 GB for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DNGaussian builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its geometric representation and rendering pipeline, inheriting its explicit primitive-based formulation and differentiable rasterizer. It departs from prior depth-regularized sparse-view NeRFs (SparseNeRF, FreeNeRF, DäRF) by recognizing that the all-parameter depth regularization used for continuous NeRF fields creates a geometry-color conflict in discrete Gaussian primitives, and instead freezes shape parameters to regularize only position and opacity through separate Hard and Soft depth channels. The Global-Local Depth Normalization loss is a novel design that addresses the heightened sensitivity of Gaussian primitives to small depth errors, going beyond the fixed-scale or rank-based depth losses used in prior NeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 25× training time reduction and 3000× rendering speedup over NeRF-based baselines make sparse-view synthesis feasible on commodity hardware (2 GB GPU, 3.5 min training), which is valuable for real-time applications and per-scene optimization workflows. The conceptual insight that Gaussian geometry degradation under sparse views is primarily a positioning problem (solvable by moving primitives) rather than a shape problem provides a useful design principle for future 3DGS-based methods. However, the approach still depends on pre-trained monocular depth estimators (DPT) whose quality bounds the achievable geometry, and the per-scene optimization requirement limits scalability to large-scale or dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the depth regularization can be made self-supervised or whether the Global-Local normalization transfers to other primitive-based representations.
