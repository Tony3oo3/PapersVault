## Problem

Spatial-domain knowledge distillation for dense prediction tasks is hindered by consecutive downsampling in the teacher, which corrupts feature maps and obscures what the student should imitate. Shifting to the frequency domain reveals a complementary challenge: low-frequency bands carry minimal context while high-frequency bands introduce noise, and not all pixels within either band contribute equally to student performance. The paper asks how to selectively localize and distill only the informative pixels across frequency bands.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images and distills intermediate feature maps (e.g., FPN outputs for detection, predicted segmentation maps for segmentation) between a larger teacher and a smaller student network. The Frequency Prompt produces pixel-wise binary masks over each frequency band (LL, HL, LH, HH at DWT level 3), and the position-aware relational module outputs a channel-wise weight vector that gates which channels participate in the distillation loss. The student ultimately produces bounding boxes with class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks as in the base task.

## Method

FreeKD decomposes teacher and student feature maps into frequency bands via Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT at level 3) and distills in the frequency domain rather than spatially. A learnable Semantic Frequency Prompt (T×C matrix) is finetuned with the teacher using task loss plus a Jaccard-based dissimilarity loss to prevent mask collapse; during distillation, the frozen prompt multiplies with teacher frequency bands to generate pixel-wise PoI masks that suppress noise in high-frequency bands and uninformative pixels in low-frequency bands. A Position-aware Relational Loss computes cross-layer relational attention (softmax of ψ(F)F^T) and passes it through an MLP to produce channel-wise gating weights, which are combined (element-wise product) between teacher and student to modulate the masked L1 frequency loss. The final student loss is the sum of the task loss and the weighted, masked frequency distillation loss, applied at intermediate feature pyramid levels.

## Results

On COCO object detection (baseline R101→R50 setting), FreeKD achieves 42.9 AP with FCOS-R50 (+4.4 AP over student), 40.8 AP with Faster RCNN-R50 (+2.4 AP), and 39.9 AP with RetinaNet-R50 (+2.5 AP), consistently outperforming all spatial-based KD baselines. In the stronger X101 teacher setting, FreeKD gains 3.8 AP for RepPoints-R50 (42.4 AP). On Cityscapes semantic segmentation, FreeKD achieves 74.40 mIoU with PSPNet-R18 (+0.77 over MGD) and 76.45 mIoU with DeepLabV3-R18 (+0.43 over MGD). On the COCO-C robustness benchmark, FreeKD attains 52.1% rPC, surpassing DiffKD by 1.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeKD consistently outperforms all six spatial-based KD baselines across every detector architecture and segmentation model tested. Against the most recent baseline DiffKD (NeurIPS 2023), FreeKD gains +0.2 AP on RetinaNet-R50, +0.2 AP on Faster RCNN-R50, and +0.5 AP on FCOS-R50 in the baseline setting, and +0.7 AP on RepPoints-R50 in the stronger X101 setting. Against FGD (CVPR 2022), the margins are larger: +0.3 AP (RetinaNet), +0.4 AP (Faster RCNN), and +0.8 AP (FCOS). On Cityscapes segmentation, FreeKD surpasses MGD (ECCV 2022) by 0.77 mIoU (PSPNet-R18) and 0.43 mIoU (DeepLabV3-R18), and outperforms CWD (ICCV 2021) by 0.87 and 0.52 mIoU respectively. On the COCO-C robustness benchmark, FreeKD achieves 52.1% rPC versus 51.1% for DiffKD and 51.3% for FGD, indicating that frequency-domain distillation confers additional domain generalization. In the large-scale model setting, FreeKD improves DINO-R50 by 2.2 AP and SAM ViT-Tiny by 2.21 mIoU over the MSE spatial baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant spatial-domain knowledge distillation paradigm (FGD, DiffKD, FRS, CWD, MGD) by arguing that consecutive downsampling in the spatial domain constitutes a form of corruption that misleads the student. It builds on wavelet-based distillation concepts from image-to-image translation [54] but is the first to apply frequency-domain distillation to dense prediction tasks (object detection, semantic segmentation). The Frequency Prompt mechanism is conceptually distinct from prior spatial prompts (VPT, CTAP) in both insertion location (interacting with intermediate frequency bands rather than encoder tokens or input pixels) and the substance transferred (pixel-wise imitation principles rather than feature-level guidance). The position-aware relational loss further differentiates the method by incorporating cross-layer positional information as a channel-wise gating operation specific to dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeKD provides a principled alternative to spatial distillation that consistently outperforms strong baselines across diverse architectures (CNN, Transformer, DETR-based) and scales to foundation models (DINO, SAM), suggesting frequency-domain distillation may be a broadly applicable paradigm. The robustness gains on COCO-C indicate that frequency-aware supervision imparts domain generalization benefits beyond clean-set accuracy. However, the frequency prompt requires an additional finetuning stage on the teacher, adding practical overhead, and the gains over the strongest spatial baseline (DiffKD) remain modest (0.5–0.7 AP). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D tasks, whether the number of prompt tokens T and decomposition level l can be adapted per task, and whether the method's benefits persist under extreme teacher-student capacity gaps.
