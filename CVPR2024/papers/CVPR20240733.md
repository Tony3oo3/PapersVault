## Problem

Segment Anything Model (SAM) exhibits significant performance degradation under distribution shift, performing poorly on corrupted natural images, camouflaged objects, medical images, and robotic scenes. Existing remedies either require retraining on web-scale data (impractical for foundation models) or depend on source-domain access (infeasible due to privacy and storage constraints), leaving a gap for a source-free, memory-efficient adaptation strategy that also leverages the prompt-based interface of SAM.

## Input / Output

During adaptation, the method consumes unlabeled target-domain RGB images together with weak supervision in one of three forms—minimal bounding boxes, 5 positive + 5 negative sparse points, or coarse polygon masks (P/20 vertices)—all of which are fed directly into SAM's prompt encoder. At inference, the same prompt type used during adaptation is supplied, and the model outputs per-instance binary segmentation masks (sigmoid-normalized, thresholded at 0.5) at the original image resolution. The prompt type must match between training and testing to ensure fair evaluation, and the method is evaluated across 10 datasets spanning 5 task categories.

## Method

The proposed framework is a source-free self-training architecture maintaining three encoder copies: a frozen anchor (original SAM weights), a teacher, and a student (teacher and student share weights). The teacher generates pseudo-labels via weakly augmented views, and the student is supervised by focal + dice loss on binarized teacher predictions; a frozen anchor network regularizes both student and teacher outputs via dice loss to prevent confirmation bias from accumulating incorrect pseudo-labels. A contrastive loss is additionally applied in encoder feature space, where instance features cropped by anchor and teacher masks form positive pairs (same prompt) and negative pairs (different prompts), directly regularizing the ViT-B encoder representations. To keep memory cost tractable, only low-rank (rank-4) LoRA modules on the encoder are updated via backpropagation, while the original weights remain frozen and are added back at inference. Weak supervisions (boxes, points, coarse polygons) serve as SAM prompts, yielding more localized and less ambiguous pseudo-predictions than fully automated grid-point prompting, which the ablation shows is critical—self-training without weak supervision actually degrades point-prompt performance below the unadapted baseline.

## Results

On COCO 2017 with bounding-box prompts, the proposed method achieves 80.12 mIoU, outperforming the Direct SAM baseline (74.29) by 5.83 points and approaching the fully supervised upper bound (81.50). On the more challenging COCO-C corrupted images, the method reaches 71.05 average mIoU across 15 corruption types, surpassing the best competing method TRIBE (70.15) and the Direct baseline (66.73). On camouflaged object detection (CHAMELEON), the method achieves 75.94 mIoU with box prompts versus 51.32 for Direct SAM, a 24.6-point improvement. Across all 10 datasets and 5 task types, the method consistently outperforms all six competing adaptation approaches on the majority of prompt types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on most evaluation settings. The closest competitor is WDASS (weakly supervised domain adaptive segmentation), which the method beats by 2.83 mIoU on COCO box (80.12 vs 77.29) and 1.54 on COCO point (62.09 vs 60.55), though WDASS edges ahead on Pascal VOC point (76.15 vs 74.15) and poly (66.98 vs 66.72). Among generic source-free adaptation methods, TRIBE is the strongest baseline, but the proposed method surpasses it by 2.56 mIoU on COCO box and 12.53 on COCO point. TENT and SHOT show inconsistent gains across prompt types, with TENT sometimes degrading point-prompt performance below the Direct baseline (52.99 vs 55.06 on COCO). DePT performs worst among all baselines, often below the Direct SAM, suggesting that prompt-only tuning is insufficient for distribution shift. On the most challenging camouflaged and medical tasks, the gap over all baselines widens substantially (e.g., 4.03 mIoU over WDASS on CHAMELEON box), confirming the benefit of the anchor regularization and contrastive loss under severe distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of source-free domain adaptation (building on SHOT's self-training paradigm) and foundation model adaptation, specifically targeting SAM's architecture. It departs from prior source-free DA work by introducing an anchor network regularization to combat confirmation bias in self-training, and by exploiting SAM's prompt interface to seamlessly integrate weak supervisions (boxes, points, coarse masks) as adaptation signals. The use of LoRA for memory-efficient encoder updates extends parameter-efficient fine-tuning from NLP to the segmentation foundation model setting, while the contrastive loss on encoder features adds a representation-level regularizer absent from prior segmentation adaptation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SAM becomes a default starting point for segmentation tasks, a practical, source-free adaptation recipe that requires only weak labels (not pixel-level masks) and modest compute (LoRA on ViT-B) is directly useful for practitioners facing distribution shift in medical, robotic, or corrupted-image settings. The task-agnostic nature across five diverse domains strengthens the claim, though the method still requires some labeled target data and the gap to the fully supervised upper bound remains substantial on the hardest tasks (e.g., camouflaged objects with polygon prompts). Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger SAM encoders (ViT-L/H), whether the anchor regularization generalizes to other foundation models, and whether fully automated prompt generation (without any weak labels) can close the remaining gap.
