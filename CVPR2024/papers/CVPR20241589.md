## Problem

Cross-modal retrieval models trained on web-collected datasets inevitably encounter Partially Mismatched Pairs (PMPs) that are semantically irrelevant yet treated as matched, degrading retrieval performance. Existing robust-learning methods only down-weight the contribution of mismatched pairs via soft margins, leaving their potential semantic information underutilized. This paper asks whether useful knowledge can be actively excavated from mismatched pairs rather than merely suppressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on batches of image-text pairs from a training set that has been pre-partitioned into matched and mismatched subsets (via a beta mixture model on per-sample triplet loss). For mismatched pairs, it produces a refined alignment matrix (a partial transport plan) that reassigns matching probabilities across the batch, which is then used as a soft supervision signal. The framework is model-agnostic and plugs into existing retrieval backbones (SGR, SAF, SGRAF), ultimately outputting standard cross-modal retrieval rankings at test time.

## Method

L2RM formalizes rematching as a partial optimal transport problem: it seeks a minimal-cost transport plan between uniform distributions over the batch's images and captions, with a mask that zeros out the diagonal to prevent false-positive self-transport, and a mass constraint ρ that allows some samples to remain untransported. The transport cost is not a fixed feature distance but a learnable single-layer feedforward network trained self-supervisedly by minimizing an OT loss against a reconstructed matching matrix derived from known matched pairs. The entropy-regularized partial OT is solved efficiently via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm in a single optimization loop per batch. The resulting refined alignment is converted into a symmetric KL-divergence rematching loss that pulls the model's predicted matching probabilities toward the OT-derived soft labels, while matched pairs continue to be trained with standard triplet loss.

## Results

On the real-world CC152K dataset (containing ~3–20% unknown mismatched pairs), L2RM-SGRAF achieves an rSum of 374.2, outperforming the best baseline RCL-SGRAF (364.4) by 9.8%. On synthesized PMPs, L2RM-SGRAF leads all methods across every mismatching rate (0.2–0.8) on both Flickr30K and MS-COCO; the gap widens at higher noise, e.g., at 0.8 MRate on Flickr30K L2RM-SGRAF reaches rSum 404.0 versus 378.3 for RCL-SGRAF. On CC152K, L2RM-SGR surpasses all SGR variants by up to 16.9% absolute rSum improvement, attributed to the model's strength in capturing local alignments that dominate real-world rematched pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2RM consistently outperforms all six baseline families (IMRAM, NCR, BiCro, DECL, RCL, and the base SGR/SAF/SGRAF models) across every mismatching rate and dataset. The advantage is most pronounced at high noise: at 0.8 MRate on Flickr30K, L2RM-SGRAF (rSum 404.0) beats RCL-SGRAF (378.3) by 25.7 points and DECL-SGRAF (394.4) by 9.6 points. On MS-COCO at 0.8 MRate, L2RM-SGRAF (482.6) edges out RCL-SGRAF (475.9) by 6.7 points. On the real-world CC152K benchmark, L2RM-SGRAF leads RCL-SGRAF by 9.8% in rSum (374.2 vs. 364.4), while L2RM-SGR surpasses the original SGR (196.6) by 167.7 points and the best SGR variant (RCL-SGR, 346.4) by 17.9 points. The paper notes that at lower mismatching rates (0.2), the margin over the strongest baselines is smaller (e.g., 503.8 vs. 496.8 on Flickr30K), but the rematching strategy becomes increasingly valuable as noise grows, confirming that excavating useful signal from mismatched pairs is the key differentiator over prior down-weighting approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the partial optimal transport framework (Chapel et al., 2020; Figalli, 2010) and the Sinkhorn solver (Cuturi, 2013) to formalize cross-modal rematching as a minimal-cost transport problem. It departs from prior PMP-robust methods (NCR, DECL, BiCro, RCL) that merely estimate soft correspondences to down-weight mismatched pairs, instead proposing to actively excavate useful cross-modal alignments among mismatched samples. The self-supervised cost function is a novel departure from standard OT practice, where cost matrices are typically fixed feature-driven distances; here the cost is learned from explicit similarity-cost mapping to avoid the self-reinforcing error cycle caused by PMP-corrupted representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: web-collected datasets like Conceptual Captions inherently contain 3–20% mismatched pairs, and the OT-based rematching framework is model-agnostic, plugging into existing architectures (SGR, SAF, SGRAF) without modification. The ablation showing an 11.9-point rSum drop when replacing the learned cost with cosine distance underscores that naive feature-driven costs are fundamentally inadequate under PMPs, a finding that may inform other OT-based learning tasks with noisy data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modality pairs (video-text, audio-text), how it behaves at mismatch rates beyond 0.8, and whether the warm-up + beta-mixture identification pipeline can be simplified or made fully end-to-end.
