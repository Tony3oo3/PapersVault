## Problem

In continuous vision-language navigation, agents must evaluate candidate actions by anticipating future environments, but existing approaches either rely on single-view observations (limited by visual occlusions) or generate future RGB images (suffering from pixel-level distortion and high computational cost). This paper addresses how to produce robust, efficient future environment representations that capture semantic content relevant to navigation without the difficulty of accurate pixel reconstruction in unseen 3D scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each navigation step, the agent receives 12 panoramic RGB views (30° separation, 90° HFOV for R2R-CE) with corresponding depth maps plus a natural language instruction, and outputs low-level actions (15° turns or 0.25 m forward). The HNR model internally produces multi-level CLIP-aligned semantic features for 12 future views per candidate location, which are then organized into a future path tree of visited, candidate, and lookahead nodes. The model is pre-trained on 800 HM3D scenes before being fine-tuned for VLN, and inference runs at 87.3 Hz (11.5 ms) on an RTX 3090.

## Method

The HNR model stores CLIP-ViT-B/32 grid features with 3D positions, orientations, and region sizes into a growing feature cloud. For each future view, it samples points along camera rays, aggregates k-nearest features (K=4) via an MLP with relative positional embeddings to produce latent vectors and volume densities, then composites them into region features using volume rendering. A 4-layer transformer view encoder with a learnable view token integrates region features into a full-view representation, and a panorama encoder combines 12 views per candidate. The lookahead VLN model uses a 4-layer cross-modal transformer with graph-aware self-attention (GASA) to evaluate all path branches in the future path tree in parallel, selecting the optimal candidate via max-pooled goal scores. Training uses a combined loss of RGBD rendering error, region-level CLIP alignment, and view-level CLIP alignment, pre-trained on HM3D before VLN fine-tuning.

## Results

On R2R-CE (val unseen), HNR achieves 61% SR, 51% SPL, and 4.42 NE, outperforming the prior best ETPNav (57% SR, 49% SPL, 4.71 NE) by 4% on SR and 2% on SPL. On the test unseen split, HNR reaches 58% SR and 50% SPL versus ETPNav's 55% SR and 48% SPL. On RxR-CE (val unseen), HNR attains 56.39% SR and 46.73% SPL, improving over ETPNav (54.79% SR, 44.89% SPL) by roughly 2% across most metrics. The method also runs at 87.3 Hz (11.5 ms per step) on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HNR outperforms its direct baseline ETPNav [4] by 4% SR (61 vs. 57) and 2% SPL (51 vs. 49) on R2R-CE val unseen, and by 3% SR / 2% SPL on test unseen. Against DREAMWALKER [39], which also uses lookahead exploration but relies on image generation for future views, HNR gains approximately 10% SR (61 vs. 49) on val unseen, attributed to both the unified future path tree (vs. step-by-step search) and superior CLIP-embedding-based representations over pixel-level image synthesis. HNR also surpasses BEVBert [3] (59% SR, 50% SPL) and ScaleVLN [43] (55% SR, 51% SPL) on R2R-CE val unseen, and improves over ETPNav on RxR-CE by ~1.6% SR and ~1.8% SPL. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that HNR's hierarchical encoding beats NeRF-based RGB rendering (56.55% SR) and image generation (58.35% SR) as future-view representation strategies, approaching the ground-truth upper bound of 63.13% SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the volume rendering formulation from NeRF [28] and the renderable radiance map idea from RNR-Map [24], but departs from both by predicting CLIP semantic features rather than RGB pixels, and by replacing the 2D latent grid with a 3D feature cloud plus hierarchical (region → view → panorama) encoding. The navigation framework extends ETPNav's [4] topological map and GASA-based graph attention with a lookahead path tree that evaluates multiple future branches in parallel, contrasting with DREAMWALKER's [39] step-by-step image-generation-based planning. The key conceptual shift is from pixel-level future-view synthesis (PathDreamer, DREAMWALKER) to direct multi-level semantic feature prediction, motivated by the observation that RGB reconstruction in unseen environments is both difficult and unnecessary for navigation decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that predicting CLIP-aligned semantic features for future views is both more robust and more efficient than generating or rendering RGB images, offering a practical recipe for future-environment anticipation in embodied agents. The ~10% SR gain over DREAMWALKER and 4% over ETPNav on R2R-CE val-unseen are meaningful for a task where recent improvements have been incremental. Limitations include dependence on CLIP's feature space (which may miss fine-grained spatial cues), pre-training on only 800 HM3D scenes, and evaluation restricted to indoor VLN-CE benchmarks; whether the hierarchical radiance representation generalizes to outdoor, long-horizon, or multi-agent settings remains open.
