## Problem

Open Domain Generalization (ODG) requires a classifier to handle both domain shifts and category shifts simultaneously, yet existing solutions rely on CNN backbones with limited generalization and confidence-based outlier detection that fails without prior knowledge of the target. CLIP-based prompt learning methods excel at closed-set domain generalization but lack mechanisms for open-set sample recognition and do not address the degradation of visual embeddings across pronounced domain differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242064/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an unlabeled RGB image from an unseen target domain and outputs one of C+1 class labels (C known classes plus a single 'unknown' class). The target image is first augmented into a latent visual representation by concatenating it with a class-discriminative channel derived from prompt-space differences, then passed through the frozen ViT-B/32 vision encoder. During training, the model additionally consumes pseudo-open images synthesized by Stable Diffusion v1-5 (filtered by a grayscale entropy threshold of 0.2) to supervise the unknown-class prompt.

## Method

ODG-CLIP reframes ODG as a unified (C+1)-class classification problem on frozen CLIP encoders, introducing three coupled mechanisms. First, a dedicated unknown-class prompt is trained on pseudo-open images generated by Stable Diffusion using positive prompts ('a [domain] of an unknown class') and negative prompts listing all known class names, ensuring semantic distinctiveness from training categories. Second, a dual-prompt scheme is learned per image: one prompt conditioned on both a domain token (projected from visual feature mean/std) and a class token, and a second conditioned only on the domain token, with the remaining tokens as learnable generic variables. Third, the element-wise difference between these two prompt embeddings is upsampled and concatenated with the image to form an augmented latent input to the vision encoder, injecting class-discriminative knowledge into visual features. Training minimizes a contrastive loss over the augmented visual-textual pairs plus a cross-domain semantic consistency loss that enforces uniform cosine similarity of the prompt differential vectors for shared classes across all source domain pairs.

## Results

On the ODG task across six benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, Office-Home, Digits-DG, Multi-Dataset, Mini-DomainNet), ODG-CLIP achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 94.23% and H-score of 90.84%, substantially outperforming the best prior method STYLIP+SD (85.67% Acc, 78.64% H-score) by 8.56% in accuracy and 12.20% in H-score. Against zero-shot CLIP, the H-score improves by 21.44% (90.84% vs. 69.40%). In the closed-set DG setting (Table 2), ODG-CLIP reaches 97.10% average accuracy, surpassing STYLIP (86.67%) by 10.43% and the best CNN method DandelionNet (80.40%) by 16.70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODG-CLIP outperforms all 17 baselines on every dataset in both ODG and closed-set DG settings. Among CNN-based ODG methods, it surpasses MEDIC (the strongest CNN baseline) by 30.54% in average H-score (90.84% vs. 60.30%), and beats Cumix, MixStyle, and DAML by 44.03%, 42.18%, and 39.55% respectively. Among CLIP-based prompt-learning methods, ODG-CLIP leads CoOp by 49.19% in H-score, MaPLe by 42.26%, and STYLIP by 28.07%; the gap narrows when these methods are augmented with Stable Diffusion pseudo-open images (STYLIP+SD reaches 78.64% H-score), but ODG-CLIP still wins by 12.20%. CLIPN, which introduces a negative prompt for open-set recognition, achieves 52.27% H-score, and even when combined with STYLIP's prompt learning (CLIPN+STYLIP, 69.43%), it remains 21.41% below ODG-CLIP. In closed-set DG (Table 2), ODG-CLIP (97.10% avg Acc) beats SWAD by 17.83%, EoA by 17.03%, DandelionNet by 16.70%, CoOp by 14.87%, CoCoOp by 14.40%, MaPLe by 12.68%, and STYLIP by 10.43%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODG-CLIP sits at the intersection of three research threads: CLIP-based prompt learning for domain generalization (STYLIP, CoOp, MaPLe), open-set recognition via explicit unknown-class modeling (CLIPN, OpenMax), and diffusion-based image synthesis (Stable Diffusion). It departs from CNN-based ODG methods (DAML, MEDIC) by replacing confidence-driven outlier detection with a unified (C+1)-class classifier where the unknown class is learned through a dedicated prompt trained on diffusion-generated pseudo-open images. Relative to STYLIP, it simplifies the style-conditioning mechanism from multi-scale visual feature initialization to a single domain token plus learnable generic tokens, and adds a prompt-difference-based latent visual space with a cross-domain consistency loss (L_sem) that has no counterpart in prior prompt-learning-for-DG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a frozen VLM can handle ODG end-to-end without confidence thresholds or separate outlier detectors, which simplifies deployment and unifies the known/unknown decision into a single softmax. The prompt-difference → latent visual space mechanism offers a lightweight, training-free-at-inference way to inject class-discriminative information into CLIP's visual encoder, potentially transferable to other VLM-based tasks. Limitations include evaluation only on six relatively small benchmarks (the 99.53% PACS accuracy warrants scrutiny), dependence on a pre-trained diffusion model for pseudo-open generation (adding a one-time compute cost), and no exploration of dense prediction or very large-scale ODG. The authors themselves flag dense prediction in ODG as a natural next step.
