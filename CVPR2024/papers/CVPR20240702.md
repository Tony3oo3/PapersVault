## Problem

Existing video diffusion models produce only short clips of a few seconds, while vlogs require minute-level videos with complex multi-scene narratives. Prior long-video generation methods either demand extensive training on large well-captioned datasets or suffer from noticeable incoherence at shot changes, making it infeasible to generate coherent, multi-scene vlogs from open-world text descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240702/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form user story as the sole user input. Internally, the LLM Director decomposes it into per-scene text descriptions with allocated durations and generates actor reference images via Stable Diffusion XL; ShowMaker then consumes each scene's text description and corresponding actor image as joint prompts. The output is a complete vlog video at 320×512 resolution with a Bark-generated dubbed audio track, where each scene's duration is controllable per the script's allocation, yielding vlogs exceeding 5 minutes.

## Method

Vlogger decomposes vlog generation into four LLM-orchestrated stages (Script, Actor, ShowMaker, Voicer), mimicking a human production pipeline. The core video model, ShowMaker, is a latent diffusion U-Net built on SD-1.4 with two key architectural additions in each block: a spatial image cross-attention that conditions on the actor reference image for character consistency, and a temporal text cross-attention that re-injects the scene description along the temporal axis for narrative coherence. For training, a probabilistic mode-selection mechanism samples k preserved frames (k=0 for pure generation, k>0 for prediction) with a geometric distribution, unifying T2V generation and video prediction in a single model. At inference, ShowMaker alternates between generation (first clip from noise) and prediction (subsequent clips conditioned on the last k frames) to produce a snippet of any target duration, which the Vlogger assembles into the final vlog.

## Results

On UCF-101, ShowMaker achieves the best zero-shot FVD of 292.43 with hand-crafted prompts (vs. 355.19 for PyoCo) and 525.01 with class labels (vs. 550.61 for VideoLDM). On Kinetics-400, it attains an FID of 37.23, outperforming Phenaki (37.74) while using only 66.7% of the pre-training videos (10M vs. 15M) and at a higher resolution (320×512 vs. 256×256). On MSR-VTT, the method reports CLIPSIM 0.2908 and CLIP-FID 12.67, remaining competitive with Make-A-Video (CLIP-FID 13.17) and PyoCo (CLIP-FID 10.21). For long-video generation on UCF-101 (1000 frames), Vlogger significantly surpasses TATS without the progressive quality degradation observed in TATS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On UCF-101, ShowMaker achieves the lowest FVD among all compared methods in both evaluation settings: 292.43 with hand-crafted prompts (beating PyoCo by 62.76 points) and 525.01 with class labels (beating VideoLDM by 25.60 points). On Kinetics-400, Vlogger outperforms Phenaki in FID (37.23 vs. 37.74) while training on only 10M videos versus Phenaki's 15M, and at a higher output resolution. On MSR-VTT, the method is competitive but does not clearly dominate: PyoCo achieves a lower CLIP-FID (10.21 vs. 12.67), while Make-A-Video reports a slightly higher CLIP-FID (13.17) but a higher CLIPSIM (0.3049 vs. 0.2908). For long-video generation (1000 frames on UCF-101), Vlogger significantly surpasses TATS, the only open-source long-video baseline, without the progressive quality degradation that TATS exhibits. In the vlog generation ablation (Table 4), Vlogger's planning-and-shooting pipeline outperforms both MovieFactory (no autoregression) and Phenaki's fully autoregressive approach in CLIP-I (0.6294 vs. 0.5683/0.5642) while matching the longer duration (3 min 58 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent diffusion models (SD-1.4) and the temporal self-attention adaptation from Make-A-Video as its generative backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior long-video generation approaches (Phenaki's autoregressive sliding window, parallel coarse-to-fine methods) by reframing the problem as a production pipeline. Rather than scaling a single model to handle longer sequences, Vlogger introduces an LLM-as-Director that decomposes the narrative into scenes, actors, and durations, then delegates each scene to a purpose-built diffusion model (ShowMaker) with actor-image and script-text conditioning. The STEB block and mixed generation/prediction training paradigm are novel architectural contributions that sit between standard T2V diffusion and video prediction, unifying both tasks in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Vlogger demonstrates that a system-level decomposition (LLM planning + per-scene diffusion shooting) can produce coherent 5+ minute vlogs without the massive long-video training data or autoregressive drift that plague prior methods. The STEB's spatial-actor and temporal-text cross-attention mechanisms offer a practical recipe for maintaining character and narrative consistency across generated clips, which is a key bottleneck for any multi-scene video generation. However, the approach's reliance on GPT-4 for scripting, SD-XL for actor images, and Bark for audio makes it a research prototype rather than a deployable system, and the evaluation of actual vlog quality remains largely qualitative. Open questions include whether the LLM planning step can be replaced by smaller models, whether the ShowMaker architecture generalizes to higher resolutions, and how the system handles user-interactive or branching narratives.
