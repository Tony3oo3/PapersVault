## Problem

LIME, the most widely used post-hoc model-agnostic explanation method, produces inconsistent explanations across runs for the same model and image: superpixels flip between positive and negative contributions (sign entropy) and their relative importance ranks shift arbitrarily. This instability undermines trust in LIME-based explanations and hinders their practical use in safety-critical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241750/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SLICE takes an RGB image and a frozen pre-trained classifier (InceptionV3 or ResNet50 with ImageNet weights) as input. The image is segmented into superpixels, and the method outputs a ranked list of superpixels with their contribution direction (positive or negative) to the top predicted class probability. Unlike standard LIME which assigns zero to all pixels in a perturbed superpixel, SLICE perturbs superpixels via Gaussian Blur, and the final explanation only includes superpixels that passed the sign-entropy stability filter.

## Method

SLICE combines two novel components to stabilize LIME. First, Sign Entropy based Feature Elimination (SEFE) iteratively bootstraps the perturbation dataset, fits Ridge Regression surrogates, computes the sign entropy of each coefficient via Kernel Density Estimation (Scott's rule bandwidth), and eliminates superpixels whose coefficients exhibit positive sign entropy; this process repeats until a stable subset remains or a maximum iteration count is reached. Second, Ada-Blur replaces LIME's hard zero-pixel perturbation with Gaussian Blur (5×5 kernel), where the sigma hyperparameter is selected adaptively per image-model pair by maximizing the adjusted R² of the surrogate model, ensuring perturbed images stay close to the original while retaining enough variance for the linear surrogate to learn. The two components are complementary: Ada-Blur reduces output probability variance so SEFE can more reliably estimate sign entropy, and SEFE removes unstable superpixels so the remaining importance ranks are consistent across runs.

## Results

SLICE achieves significantly higher Combined Consistency Metric (CCM) scores than both LIME and BayLIME across all four dataset–model combinations (Oxford-IIIT Pets and Pascal VOC 2007 with InceptionV3 and ResNet50), with Wilcoxon signed-rank test p-values as low as 1.3×10⁻⁹. On fidelity, SLICE reduces the probability of a negative AOPC score (Pr⁻) to 0–0.055 for insertion and 0 for deletion, compared to 0.32–0.60 for LIME and 0.23–0.64 for BayLIME. SLICE also attains higher insertion AUC and lower deletion AUC than both baselines. The trade-off is runtime: SLICE takes ~89 s/image (InceptionV3) and ~45 s/image (ResNet50) versus ~19 s and ~12 s for LIME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLICE is compared against LIME (the original local surrogate explanation method) and BayLIME (a Bayesian variant with Grad-CAM prior). On consistency (CCM), SLICE substantially outperforms both baselines across all four dataset–model pairs, with Wilcoxon test p-values ranging from 1.3×10⁻⁹ to 8.2×10⁻⁷, confirming the improvement is statistically significant. On fidelity, SLICE's near-zero Pr⁻ for deletion (0.0000 in all settings) contrasts sharply with LIME (0.33–0.41) and BayLIME (0.23–0.53), meaning SLICE explanations virtually never produce counter-intuitive prediction changes. For insertion, SLICE's Pr⁻ is at most 0.055 versus 0.32–0.60 for the baselines. SLICE also achieves higher insertion AUC and lower deletion AUC than both LIME and BayLIME in every setting (p-values ≤ 1.1×10⁻³). The sole metric where SLICE underperforms is runtime: it is approximately 4.6× slower than LIME with InceptionV3 (88.9 s vs 19.5 s) and 3.8× slower with ResNet50 (44.7 s vs 11.7 s), a cost attributed to the iterative SEFE procedure and adaptive sigma search.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLICE is a direct stabilization of LIME's local surrogate model framework, addressing the well-documented inconsistency problem (sign flips and rank variance across runs) that has been identified by multiple prior studies. It departs from LIME in two specific ways: replacing the zero-pixel perturbation with adaptive Gaussian Blur (Ada-Blur) to keep perturbed samples closer to the instance being explained, and introducing a sign-entropy-based feature elimination step (SEFE) that iteratively removes superpixels whose coefficient signs are unstable across bootstrap samples. Among existing stabilization attempts—DLIME (clustering-based locality), S-LIME (hypothesis-testing sample size), ALIME (autoencoder weighting), and BayLIME (Bayesian priors)—SLICE is the first to jointly target both sign entropy and rank variance through an integrated perturbation-plus-selection pipeline, and it introduces a dedicated consistency metric (CCM) combining sign-flip entropy and Rank Biased Overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Consistent explanations are a prerequisite for deploying XAI in safety-critical or regulated settings where a user must trust that the same input will always yield the same rationale; SLICE's near-elimination of sign flips and its substantially higher fidelity (near-zero probability of negative AOPC scores versus 30–65% for LIME/BayLIME) make it a practical candidate for such use. However, the ~4.5× runtime overhead (89 s vs. 19 s per image for InceptionV3) limits real-time applicability, and the evaluation is confined to 50 images per dataset across only two architectures, leaving open whether the gains persist at scale or on more diverse datasets. The authors also acknowledge that the reliability of the fidelity metrics themselves (AOPC, insertion/deletion AUC) has not been independently validated, an important open question for the XAI evaluation community.
