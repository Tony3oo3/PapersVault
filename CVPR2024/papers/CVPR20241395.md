## Problem

Most real-world object-centric datasets capture only RGB, forcing reliance on COLMAP for 3D annotations that succeed on roughly 20% of videos and cannot provide real-world scale depths. Existing RGB-D object datasets like Wild6D are small-scale (6 categories, limited view coverage), leaving a gap in large-scale, full 360-degree RGB-D object data suitable for diverse downstream 3D learning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 360-degree RGB-D video sequences (~20K videos across 8.5K objects in 46 categories) captured with iPhone front cameras, annotated with real-world scale SLAM camera poses, per-frame object masks, and aggregated scene point clouds. The four benchmark tasks consume different subsets: NVS uses RGB or RGB-D frames with optional object masks, camera pose estimation uses sparse multi-view image sets (2–20 frames), surface reconstruction uses RGB-D sequences plus masks, and 6D pose estimation uses RGB-D images in a fully self-supervised regime with no pose labels.

## Method

The collection pipeline records RGB-D video via iPhone Record3D with controlled rotation speed, then recovers real-world scale camera poses using BAD-SLAM and Open3D SLAM directly from depth (bypassing COLMAP), achieving >90% success rate; object masks are generated through a Grounding-DINO → SAM → XMem pipeline for text-prompted, video-tracked segmentation. The paper benchmarks four tasks to validate the dataset: (i) single-scene and cross-scene NVS where adding L1 depth supervision consistently boosts PSNR and reduces depth MAE across NeRF, Mip-NeRF 360, Instant-NGP, Pixel-NeRF, MVSNeRF, and IBRNet; (ii) sparse-view camera pose estimation with RelPose and RelPose++ showing strong generalization from seen to unseen categories; (iii) SDF-based surface reconstruction where Neusfacto with RGB-D outperforms Instant-NGP, demonstrating that depth particularly benefits implicit-surface methods; and (iv) self-supervised 6D pose estimation trained on WildRGB-D and evaluated cross-distribution on Wild6D, showing that large-scale RGB-D data improves IOU metrics even without pose annotations.

## Results

On single-scene NVS, Mip-NeRF 360 with object masks achieves the best visual quality (PSNR 35.60, SSIM 0.949, LPIPS 0.024, MAE 0.020), while Instant-NGP with masks reaches PSNR 35.65. Depth supervision improves Instant-NGP's MAE from 0.366 to 0.108 and Pixel-NeRF's MAE from 0.336 to 0.147, confirming the value of the dataset's depth channel. For cross-scene NVS, IBRNet leads in visual quality across all three difficulty levels (e.g., PSNR 20.93 on Easy, 19.77 on Middle, 17.92 on Hard). In surface reconstruction, Neusfacto with RGBD achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (25.83/34.07) versus Instant-NGP RGBD (28.46/29.28), and for 6D pose estimation, joint training on Wild6D+WildRGB-D improves IOU@0.5 for Bottle from 85.2 to 85.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-scene NVS methods, Mip-NeRF 360 and Instant-NGP both outperform the original NeRF in visual quality (PSNR 23.84 and 23.67 vs. 23.03 without masks), and all three benefit dramatically from object masks, jumping ~12 dB in PSNR. In cross-scene NVS, IBRNet consistently outperforms Pixel-NeRF and MVSNeRF in visual quality across Easy/Middle/Hard difficulty levels (e.g., PSNR 20.93 vs. 20.28 and 19.95 on Easy), though geometry quality (depth MAE) does not correlate tightly with rendering quality. For depth-supervised NVS, Instant-NGP gains the most from depth supervision (MAE drops from 0.366 to 0.108), while MVSNeRF shows negligible improvement since it already uses depth for ray construction. In surface reconstruction, Neusfacto with RGBD (Chamfer 25.83) beats Instant-NGP with RGBD (28.46), indicating SDF-based methods exploit depth more effectively for boundary accuracy. For 6D pose estimation, training solely on WildRGB-D (ROW) degrades 5-degree rotation+translation metrics (e.g., Bottle drops from 71.3 to 34.1) due to distribution shift, but joint Wild6D+ROW training recovers and slightly exceeds Wild6D-only performance on IOU metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the RGB-D object dataset space by directly leveraging iPhone depth sensors for full 360-degree video capture, departing from prior real-world datasets (CO3D, OmniObject3D) that rely on COLMAP for 3D annotations or separate scanning hardware. It repurposes the SLAM pipeline (BAD-SLAM, Open3D) to obtain real-world-scale camera poses and aggregated point clouds, and chains Grounding-DINO → SAM → XMem for automatic object mask annotation, avoiding the labor-intensive manual annotation of smaller datasets like Wild6D. Conceptually, it extends the data-scaling paradigm that has driven 2D vision (ImageNet, COCO) into the 3D object domain, but with the critical addition of native depth capture that prior RGB-only datasets lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WildRGB-D is the largest object-centric RGB-D video dataset to date (8500 objects, ~20K videos, 46 categories), providing a much-needed benchmark where depth is natively available rather than estimated, which consistently improves NVS, surface reconstruction, and 6D pose estimation results. A key open limitation is the absence of 6D pose annotations, which the authors acknowledge requires further crowdsourcing; this constrains supervised evaluation of pose estimation. The finding that out-of-distribution transfer (WildRGB-D-trained → Wild6D-tested) degrades rotation+translation metrics due to camera-rotation distribution mismatch highlights that scale alone does not guarantee generalization across capture protocols. The dataset is likely to become a standard training and evaluation resource for category-level 3D object learning.
