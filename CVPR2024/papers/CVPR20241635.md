## Problem

Existing 3D avatar generation methods face two critical limitations: SDS-based approaches produce over-smoothed avatars lacking the facial detail diversity of 2D diffusion sampling, while single-image reconstruction methods struggle to recover true diffuse colors and complete PBR textures due to unwanted lighting effects (specular highlights, shadows) and perspective distortions in the input image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241635/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a free-form text prompt (rendered to a face image via SDXL) or a single RGB face image as input. It outputs a FLAME parametric mesh (5023 vertices, 9976 faces) with a complete set of 2K-resolution PBR UV textures—diffuse color, normal, specular, and roughness maps—that are lighting-free and compatible with standard rendering engines for relighting under arbitrary environment maps.

## Method

The framework has two core components. First, a Diffuse Color Extraction (DCE) model exploits the observation that self-attention query/key features in a diffusion U-Net encode lighting variations within semantic regions; by performing DDIM inversion on both the input image and a face-parsing mask, then swapping the image's query/key features with the mask's during denoising, lighting is removed while res-features preserve diffuse color details. Second, an Authenticity Guided Texture Diffusion Model (AGT-DM) fine-tunes SD-2.1's U-Net on 3DScan Store PBR UV maps to inpaint incomplete textures and generate normal/specular/roughness maps via separate decoders, with two gradient-based guidance terms—a photometric loss (L2 + LPIPS between rendered and source image) and a Canny edge-matching loss—added to the classifier-free guidance sampling to enforce identity fidelity and retain high-frequency facial details like wrinkles and pores.

## Results

UltrAvatar achieves the best FID (45.50) and KID (0.029) among all compared methods for both text-to-avatar and image-to-avatar generation, evaluated against masked FFHQ images. It also attains the highest CLIP Score (0.301±0.023), surpassing the closest text-to-avatar baseline DreamFace (0.291±0.020). Inference takes approximately 2 minutes on a single Nvidia A6000 GPU, compared to 5 minutes for DreamFace. GPT-4V-based qualitative evaluation further confirms UltrAvatar's superiority in photorealism, artifact minimization, skin texture quality, and prompt alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltrAvatar outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest text-to-avatar baseline DreamFace, UltrAvatar reduces FID by 31.2 points (45.50 vs. 76.70), KID by 0.032 (0.029 vs. 0.061), and improves CLIP Score by 0.010 (0.301 vs. 0.291). The closest image-to-avatar competitor, PanoHead (FID 48.64, KID 0.039), is surpassed by UltrAvatar on both FID and KID, though PanoHead does not report CLIP Score. The remaining text-to-avatar baselines (ClipFace, Latent3d, ClipMatrix, Text2Mesh) are substantially worse, with FID scores ranging from 80 to 220. The paper also notes that DreamFace fails on challenging prompts (e.g., extreme facial features, uncommon celebrities) while UltrAvatar handles them successfully, and that PanoHead's NeRF-based approach is limited in relighting capability compared to UltrAvatar's PBR texture pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDIM inversion and classifier-free diffusion guidance, extending the latter by injecting photometric and edge gradient terms into the sampling loop for 3D texture generation. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant SDS-based text-to-3D paradigm (DreamFusion, DreamFace) that the authors argue produces over-smoothed, low-diversity avatars, and from NeRF-based approaches (PanoHead, HeadNeRF) that are computationally heavy and incompatible with mesh-based animation. The key novel repurposing is the observation that self-attention query/key features in a diffusion U-Net encode lighting variation within semantic regions, enabling a lightweight diffuse-color-extraction step by swapping those features with ones derived from a face-parsing mask. The overall pipeline also leverages the FLAME parametric model and MICA/EMOCA for geometry, positioning itself as a diffusion-texture + parametric-geometry hybrid rather than a purely generative or purely fitting approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DCE model offers a practical, training-light mechanism for separating diffuse albedo from a single image by exploiting internal diffusion features, which could generalize to other relighting and material-estimation tasks beyond faces. The AGT-DM's gradient-guided sampling provides a template for incorporating 3D-consistency constraints (photometric, edge) into diffusion-based texture synthesis without SDS, potentially applicable to other PBR asset generation. Limitations include reliance on the FLAME topology (human faces only), the DCE mechanism being validated primarily on face images with qualitative PCA visualizations, and the AGT-DM being fine-tuned on a single commercial scan dataset (3DScan Store), raising questions about robustness to extreme poses, occlusions, and non-photorealistic inputs. Open questions include whether the attention-features-encode-lighting relationship holds across different diffusion architectures and scales, and whether the approach extends to full-body or non-human avatars.
