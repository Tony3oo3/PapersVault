## Problem

Existing deep unfolding methods for Snapshot Compressive Imaging reconstruction all follow a single-image-prior iterative framework, which limits iteration efficiency and makes it difficult to incorporate additional priors such as degradation information and mask knowledge in a simple and effective way. Moreover, prior methods acknowledged mask degradation but overlooked the effects of spatial shift and spectral compression inherent in the CASSI imaging process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242074/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D coded compressive measurement (e.g., 256×(256+d·27) for 28-band simulation, or 660×714 for real CASSI captures) together with the known sensing matrix (coded mask pattern and inter-band shift) as input, and produces a full 3D hyperspectral image (256×256×28 for simulation) as output. The sensing matrix is not learned end-to-end but is used analytically in the data-fidelity step via a closed-form Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury inversion, while the mask and shift patterns are fed explicitly into the Degraded Prior Block to guide residual estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Dual Prior Framework (DPF), which augments the standard image-prior iteration with a residual term solved by a Degraded Prior Block (DPB) that learns mask, shift, and compression degradations, then fuses the two outputs via a Fusion Block—effectively performing dual reconstruction (image prior + degraded prior) within a single iteration. The image prior is implemented as a Focused Transformer (FT) built on a Swin-style shifted-window attention with two key modifications: a Focused Attention (FA) mechanism that uses a learnable principal-component projection to scale the similarity matrix (PCA-inspired) and a threshold network to zero out irrelevant attention entries, and an asymmetric U-Net backbone that splits local and shifted-window attention into separate encoder/decoder branches, halving transformer parameters and FLOPs. The full unfolding is derived from an Augmented Lagrange formulation of a joint optimization over the image, residual, and data-fidelity terms, with 5 or 9 unrolled stages trained end-to-end using a multi-stage RMSE loss.

## Results

On the KAIST dataset (10 scenes, 256×256, 28 bands), DPU-9stg achieves an average PSNR of 40.52 dB and SSIM of 0.977, while DPU-5stg reaches 39.62 dB / 0.973 SSIM. DPU-5stg uses only 1.59M parameters and 27.41 GFLOPs, the lowest among all compared methods, yet outperforms the previous SOTA RDLUF (39.57 dB, 115.16 GFLOPs) by 0.05 dB with less than 1/4 the FLOPs. DPU-9stg improves over RDLUF by 0.95 dB in PSNR while requiring less than half the FLOPs (49.26 vs. 115.16 GFLOPs) and fewer parameters (2.85M vs. 1.81M per stage).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPU-5stg (39.62 dB) surpasses the previous SOTA unfolding method RDLUF (39.57 dB) by 0.05 dB in PSNR while using only 27.41 GFLOPs versus RDLUF's 115.16 GFLOPs (less than 1/4) and fewer parameters (1.59M vs. 1.81M). DPU-9stg (40.52 dB) extends this lead to +0.95 dB over RDLUF with less than half the FLOPs. Against the RNN-based BIRNAT (37.58 dB, 2122.66 GFLOPs), DPU-5stg gains 2.04 dB in PSNR while requiring roughly 1/2 the parameters and 1/77 the FLOPs. Compared to the transformer-based MST (35.18 dB) and CST (36.12 dB), DPU-5stg improves PSNR by 4.44 dB and 3.50 dB respectively. Among all unfolding methods, DPU-5stg achieves the best 5-stage performance (39.62 dB) that even exceeds the 9-stage results of GAP-Net (39.30 dB), DAUF (39.55 dB), and RDLF (39.60 dB) with fewer parameters and FLOPs, demonstrating superior iteration efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm for snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction, building directly on Swin Transformer's shifted-window attention and the mask-guided initialization from MST. The key conceptual departure is from prior unfolding methods (GAP, DAUF, RDLF) that all follow a single-image-prior iterative framework: DPU introduces a residual into the ALM-based iteration to construct a second 'degraded prior' for the residual, enabling joint utilization of image and degradation priors within a single iteration. The Focused Attention mechanism, inspired by PCA denoising, and the asymmetric U-Net backbone are novel architectural contributions that reduce transformer cost while preserving hierarchical structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual prior framework offers a generalizable recipe for incorporating multiple priors into unfolding methods without simply stacking iterations, which could benefit other inverse problems beyond SCI. The asymmetric backbone and Focused Attention are positioned as broadly applicable to hierarchical transformers and general vision tasks, respectively. However, the evaluation is limited to the CASSI system with 28-band HSIs on two standard datasets (CAVE/KAIST), and the real-data experiments use only 5 scenes without quantitative metrics, leaving open questions about generalization to other SCI hardware configurations, higher spectral resolutions, or different degradation regimes.
