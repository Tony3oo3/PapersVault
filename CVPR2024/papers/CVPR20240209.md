## Problem

Semi-supervised semantic segmentation methods typically rely on fixed confidence thresholds to select pseudo labels from unlabeled data, creating a difficult trade-off between label coverage and accuracy. Previous approaches also overlook the rich pairwise similarity and shape information embedded in correlation maps computed from encoder features, limiting how efficiently unlabeled data can be exploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240209/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in two augmented views (weak and strong) for unlabeled data, processed through a DeepLabV3+ with ResNet-101 backbone at crop sizes of 321×321 or 513×513 (Pascal VOC) and 801×801 (Cityscapes). It outputs a pixel-wise class prediction map with K classes, and internally computes HW×HW correlation matrices from D-dimensional encoder features that drive both propagation strategies. All correlation-map operations occur only during training, adding no inference overhead.

## Method

CorrMatch is a single-stage semi-supervised framework built on weak-to-strong consistency regularization that introduces two label propagation strategies driven by correlation maps. Pixel propagation computes a softmax-normalized correlation matrix C = Softmax(w₁ᵀ·w₂/√D) from encoder features, then multiplies it with bilinearly-interpolated logits to produce a similarity-aware prediction z, supervised by a correlation loss against high-confidence pseudo labels. Region propagation exploits the observation that each row of C encodes class-agnostic shape information: rows are binarized into binary masks, and when a mask overlaps sufficiently with the current high-confidence region, the most salient class within that intersection is propagated to fill the mask, expanding the high-confidence region. A dynamic threshold updated via exponential moving average of per-class max confidences replaces fixed thresholds, and random sampling of 128 rows from the correlation map balances computational cost with coverage.

## Results

On the classic Pascal VOC 2012 dataset (321×321 crop), CorrMatch achieves 76.4% mIoU with only 92 labeled images (1/16 split) and 81.8% mIoU on the full 1,464-image split, outperforming all prior methods across every split. On the augmented Pascal VOC 2012 (513×513), it reaches 81.3% mIoU on the 1/16 split, surpassing the previous best (AugSeg, 79.3%) by 2.0%. On Cityscapes, CorrMatch attains 77.3% mIoU on the 1/16 split and 80.4% on the 1/2 split, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorrMatch consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every split on both Pascal VOC 2012 and Cityscapes. The most direct competitor, UniMatch [63] (also a single-stage weak-to-strong consistency method), is beaten by 1.2–1.3% mIoU on the low-data splits (1/16, 1/8) of classic Pascal VOC 2012 and by 0.6–0.9% on Cityscapes. On the 1/16 split of classic Pascal VOC 2012, CorrMatch (76.4%) leads the next-best methods (GTA 70.0%, PCR 70.1%, CCVC 70.2%) by over 6 mIoU points, a particularly large margin given the extreme scarcity of labeled data. On the augmented Pascal VOC 2012 setting (513×513), CorrMatch surpasses AugSeg [73] by 2.0% on the 1/16 split and by ~2% on 1/8. Multi-network methods (PS-MT, ELN) and multi-stage self-training methods (ST++) are all outperformed, confirming that the correlation-based label propagation strategies provide gains without the architectural or training-stage overhead those approaches require.

## Relation to Prior Work

CorrMatch builds directly on the UniMatch [63] weak-to-strong consistency regularization framework, adopting its single-stage, single-network design. It departs from the broader semi-supervised segmentation literature (Mean Teacher variants, multi-stage self-training, multi-stream strong augmentation) by introducing correlation maps—computed from pairwise feature similarities—as the mechanism for label propagation, a concept previously underutilized in this setting. The dynamic EMA-based threshold strategy extends FreeMatch [51]'s adaptive thresholding idea from classification to segmentation, where per-class confidence distributions make fixed thresholds particularly problematic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight that correlation maps simultaneously encode global pairwise semantic similarity and local class-agnostic shape information provides a principled alternative to threshold-tuning for expanding pseudo-label coverage, and the resulting framework is notably simpler than competing SOTA methods (no extra networks, no multi-stage training, no multiple SDA streams). Limitations include evaluation restricted to DeepLabV3+ with ResNet-101, and the correlation map computation (O(HW)² matrix multiplication) adds non-trivial training cost that scales poorly with resolution. Open questions include whether the propagation strategies transfer to transformer-based segmentation backbones or to other dense prediction tasks like depth estimation, and whether the random sampling of shapes from the correlation map can be made more principled.
