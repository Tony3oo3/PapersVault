## Problem

Existing style transfer methods either rely on explicit style constraints (Gram matrices, contrastive losses) that fail to capture fine-grained artistic patterns, or require per-style training of embeddings in diffusion models (e.g., InST, VCT), making them slow and prone to content deviation. The paper asks whether a vanilla pre-trained diffusion model can extract and fuse style directly from an image without any retraining, and how to prevent the naive cross-attention mechanism from destroying content structure during this fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240031/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a style image (standard RGB) and produces a single stylized image that preserves the content layout while adopting the style's visual patterns. It operates entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.5 with null text prompts and 30 DDIM denoising steps, requiring no training or tuning per style. The cross-attention reweighting module is injected only at U-Net decoder layers 10–15 during denoising steps 5–30, leaving early structural reconstruction untouched.

## Method

Z-STAR performs dual-path DDIM inversion on both the content and style images to obtain temporally aligned latent features, then fuses them via a reweighted cross-attention mechanism. Instead of the naive cross-attention Attn(Q_c, K_s, V_s) or a fixed-λ linear combination with self-attention, the method concatenates the scaled attention scores [λ·Q_c K_s^T/√d, Q_c K_c^T/√d] into a single 2N-dimensional vector and applies one Softmax, so that each pixel's content-vs-style weighting is determined adaptively by the relative magnitudes of both score sets. Theoretical analysis shows this formulation reduces to pure content self-attention when style–content correlation is negligible and to the simple-addition form when correlation is strong, while avoiding the Softmax amplification of weakly-correlated style scores that plagues the fixed-λ baseline. The reweighting is applied only at high-resolution decoder layers (10–15) and mid-to-late denoising steps (5–30) to preserve early structural reconstruction while injecting fine style details.

## Results

The paper evaluates via a user study with 55 participants and 1,760 total votes across 100 stylized results (10 content × 10 style image pairs). Z* is preferred over all 9 baselines on average for content preservation (ranging from 15.9% to 45.9% of votes going to baselines) and overall quality (15.9%–36.4%). For style fidelity, only CAST (56.4%) and StyTr² (51.4%) marginally exceed Z*, which the authors note is an insignificant difference. No standard benchmark dataset is used; evaluation is entirely perceptual.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Z* outperforms all 9 baselines on content preservation and overall quality in the user study. The closest competitors on overall quality are InST (15.9%) and StyleDiff (16.4%), both diffusion-based methods that still require per-style training (~20 min/style), whereas Z* is fully training-free. On style fidelity, CAST (56.4%) and StyTr² (51.4%) narrowly edge out Z*, but the authors argue the gap is insignificant and that these CNN/transformer methods sacrifice fine-grained style details (e.g., hair, eyes) due to reliance on Gram-matrix or contrastive style losses. Among diffusion-based baselines, VCT shows better style than InST but still suffers from content identity drift (e.g., altering a person's face). CNN-based methods (ArtFlow, AdaAttN, IEST) and the transformer-based StyTr² all trail Z* on both content and overall metrics, with IEST and StyTr² being the weakest on content preservation (45.9% and 45.0% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion U-Net and DDIM inversion, repurposing the cross-attention mechanism originally designed for text conditioning to fuse image-derived style features. It departs from prior diffusion-based style transfer methods (InST, VCT) that require per-style embedding training, and from CNN/Transformer-based approaches (Gatys, AdaIN, CAST, StyTr²) that rely on Gram matrices or contrastive losses for style representation. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that the generative prior of a vanilla diffusion model already encodes artistic style transfer capability, requiring only a reweighted cross-attention to balance content and style without any retraining or text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, training-free nature eliminates the ~20-minute per-style training bottleneck of InST/VCT and the fixed style-loss constraints of CNN-based methods, making style transfer immediately applicable to arbitrary style references. The attention reweighting mechanism is a lightweight, plug-and-play modification to the U-Net that could inform other diffusion-based editing tasks. However, the evaluation relies primarily on qualitative comparisons and a user study without standard quantitative metrics (FID, LPIPS, CLIP scores), and the method is validated only on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with a fixed λ=1.2. Open questions include generalization to other diffusion architectures, the one-to-many style transfer setting (acknowledged but deferred), and whether the approach extends to video or 3D content.
