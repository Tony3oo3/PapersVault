## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for semantic segmentation assume a shared label space between source and target domains, but real-world target data often contains novel categories absent from the source. This paper introduces the Open-Set Domain Adaptation for Semantic Segmentation (OSDA-SS) task for the first time and identifies two specific failure modes of naive baselines: (1) models produce low-confidence, inaccurate predictions near the boundaries of unknown classes, and (2) models fail to capture the correct shape of unknown objects due to over-reliance on size cues.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source images (GTA5 at 1914×1052 or SYNTHIA at 1280×760) and unlabeled target images (Cityscapes at 2048×1024), trained on 512×512 random crops. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation map with C+1 channels, where the (C+1)-th channel is a single aggregated 'unknown' label covering all target-private classes (e.g., person, rider, truck, train, pole, traffic sign). Evaluation uses H-Score, the harmonic mean of the mean IoU over known classes and the IoU of the single unknown class, rather than standard mIoU, to avoid diluting the impact of the private class.

## Method

BUS builds on a head-expansion self-training baseline (DAFormer with MiT-B5 encoder, EMA teacher-student, confidence-thresholded pseudo-labels refined by MobileSAM) and adds two components. First, the DECON loss applies dilation and erosion morphological operations to the target private mask: the dilated-minus-original region forms negative samples (known-class boundary pixels) while the eroded region forms positive samples (confident private-class pixels), and a contrastive objective pulls the anchor (mean of positives) toward positives and away from negatives, sharpening boundary discrimination. Second, OpenReMix extends ClassMix by (a) resizing a random source 'thing' class and pasting it onto the target image to enforce size-invariant feature learning, and (b) cutting pseudo-labeled unknown regions from the target and pasting them into the source image so the expanded (C+1)-th head receives supervised signal during source training. Together, these mechanisms improve both boundary precision and shape fidelity for unknown classes.

## Results

On GTA5→Cityscapes, BUS achieves an H-Score of 62.81 (Common mIoU 72.47, Private IoU 55.42), outperforming the best UDA baseline DAFormer (H-Score 23.36) by +39.45%. On SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, BUS reaches an H-Score of 44.01 (Common mIoU 64.62, Private IoU 33.37), surpassing the best baseline HRDA (H-Score 20.82) by +23.19%. The H-Score is the harmonic mean of the mean IoU over known (common) classes and the IoU of the single unknown (private) class, ensuring that poor private-class detection is not masked by strong common-class performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BUS outperforms all eight baselines on both benchmarks by large margins. The classification-oriented OSDA/UniDA methods (OSBP, UAN, UniOT) perform worst (H-Score 6.5–7.9), confirming that pixel-level spatial reasoning is essential for open-set segmentation and that image-level rejection strategies do not transfer. Among UDA baselines, DAFormer leads on GTA5→Cityscapes (H-Score 23.36) and HRDA leads on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes (H-Score 20.82), but both rely on a simple confidence threshold at inference to flag unknown pixels, yielding Private IoU of only 14.63 and 12.68 respectively. BUS improves Private IoU to 55.42 (GTA5) and 33.37 (SYNTHIA) while simultaneously boosting Common mIoU by +8.65% over HRDA, demonstrating that DECON loss and OpenReMix improve both known and unknown class detection. The gap between BUS and the strongest UDA baseline is roughly 2.7× in H-Score on GTA5 and 2.1× on SYNTHIA, underscoring that existing closed-set UDA methods are fundamentally inadequate for open-set segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-training paradigm for UDA semantic segmentation (DAFormer, MIC, HRDA) by relaxing the closed-set assumption that source and target share an identical label space. The most direct methodological ancestor is ClassMix [51], which the authors augment with a resizing step (OpenReMix) to inject size-invariance, and the contrastive learning framework of SimCLR [64] is repurposed via morphological dilation/erosion masks to create a boundary-aware loss (DECON). In the broader OSDA literature, prior work (OSBP, UAN, UniOT) addresses open-set recognition only at the image level for classification; the closest segmentation-adjacent work (BUDA [48]) requires pre-defined private category definitions, which this paper explicitly avoids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing OSDA-SS and providing a strong baseline (BUS), this paper opens a practically important direction for safety-critical perception where novel objects (e.g., new vehicle types, pedestrians in unusual contexts) must be flagged as unknown rather than misclassified. The acknowledged limitation—that the entire pipeline depends on pseudo-label quality and can degrade under miscalibration—leaves open the question of how to make unknown detection robust without relying on confident teacher predictions. Broader generalization beyond the two synthetic-to-real driving benchmarks (GTA5/SYNTHIA → Cityscapes) and to more diverse unknown-class distributions remains untested.
