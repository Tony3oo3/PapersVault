## Problem

Existing mask-based DiT training (e.g., MaskDiT, MDT) suffers from two inherent limitations: a training-inference discrepancy caused by learnable mask tokens present only during training, and fuzzy relations between mask reconstruction and generative diffusion objectives that cause the reconstruction loss to overwhelm the generative objective at late training stages, resulting in sub-optimal generative capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242103/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label as conditioning and generates 256×256 RGB images via class-conditional sampling. Generation operates in the latent space of a frozen pre-trained VAE (32×32×4 latent from a 256×256×3 image), following the LDM paradigm. During training, the student view is divided into visible and invisible patches via a 20% mask ratio, while the teacher view receives the full image with only minimal noise (σ_min=0.002), creating an asymmetric discriminative pair.

## Method

SD-DiT decouples the DiT encoder and decoder to separately handle discriminative and generative objectives in a teacher-student framework. The teacher DiT encoder (EMA of student) processes a near-clean view (σ_min noise), while the student DiT encoder processes a regularly noised view (σ ∈ [σ_min, σ_max] per EDM); a DINO/iBOT-style cross-entropy loss enforces inter-image alignment of visible tokens between the two encoders in a joint embedding space. Critically, the mask reconstruction loss is discarded entirely: invisible patches are directly inserted into the student decoder's token set, and the decoder is optimized solely with the standard EDM denoising loss. This design eliminates the training-inference discrepancy because the student branch retains the same noise schedule and architecture as inference, and the teacher is completely removed at test time, incurring zero additional cost.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, SD-DiT-XL/2 achieves FID-50K of 9.01 at 1300k training steps, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 9.62 at 7000k steps) by ~5× faster convergence and MDT-XL/2 (FID 9.60 at 1300k steps). At the smaller DiT-S/2 scale with 400k steps, SD-DiT reaches FID 48.39 versus 68.40 for DiT-S/2 and 53.46 for MDT-S/2. Training speed is 9.2 steps/sec (0.11 sec/step) on 8×A100 GPUs, faster than DiT (5.03 steps/sec) and MDT (2.4 steps/sec).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-DiT consistently outperforms all three main DiT-based baselines (DiT, MDT, MaskDiT) across every backbone scale tested. At DiT-S/2 (400k steps), SD-DiT achieves FID 48.39, a 20-point improvement over DiT-S/2 (68.40) and a 5-point improvement over MDT-S/2 (53.46). At DiT-B/2 (400k steps), the gap widens: SD-DiT-B/2 (28.62) beats MDT-B/2 (34.33) by 5.7 FID points and DiT-B/2 (43.47) by 14.9 points. At the XL scale, SD-DiT-XL/2 (9.01 at 1300k steps) surpasses MDT-XL/2 (9.60 at 1300k steps) and MaskDiT-XL/2 (12.15 at 1300k steps), while matching DiT-XL/2's FID of 9.62 with only 1300k steps versus DiT's 7000k steps (~5× faster). In terms of training speed, SD-DiT runs at 9.2 steps/sec, roughly 3.8× faster than MDT (2.4 steps/sec) and 1.8× faster than DiT (5.03 steps/sec), due to processing only 80% of patches in the encoder rather than 100%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SD-DiT sits at the intersection of two research threads: mask-based DiT training (MDT, MaskDiT) and self-supervised discrimination (DINO, iBOT). It departs from the mask-strategy line by replacing intra-image mask reconstruction with inter-image discriminative alignment in a teacher-student framework, while retaining the asymmetric encoder-decoder structure from MAE for training efficiency. The teacher view design draws on Consistency Models and the InfoMin principle, using minimal noise to approximate the data distribution. The core architectural departure is the decoupling of encoder (discriminative) and decoder (generative) objectives, in contrast to MaskDiT's joint optimization of both tasks through the full encoder-decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why mask reconstruction and generative diffusion objectives conflict (intra-image reconstruction vs. inter-distribution translation), and offers a clean architectural solution that eliminates the training-inference discrepancy inherent to mask tokens. The ~5× training speedup over standard DiT (1300k vs. 7000k steps to match FID) is practically significant for large-scale DiT training. However, the evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, leaving open whether the discriminative objective transfers to text-conditioned generation, video, or other modalities where the generative objective is more complex. The approach also assumes access to a frozen VAE and operates in latent space, which constrains its applicability to pixel-space or non-VAE-based diffusion models.
