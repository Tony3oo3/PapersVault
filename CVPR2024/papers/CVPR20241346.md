## Problem

State-of-the-art deep learning methods for local feature extraction (SuperPoint, DISK, ALIKE) achieve strong matching accuracy but incur high computational costs that preclude real-time deployment on resource-constrained devices such as mobile robots and AR headsets. Existing lightweight alternatives either sacrifice accuracy significantly or require hardware-specific optimizations (quantization, binarization) that limit portability. The paper asks whether a carefully designed lightweight CNN can match or exceed the accuracy of much larger models while running in real-time on a commodity laptop CPU without any hardware-specific compilation.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single-channel grayscale image (typically resized so the maximum dimension is 600–1200 px) and produces three outputs from a shared backbone: a dense 64-dimensional descriptor map at 1/8 spatial resolution, a keypoint heatmap at 1/8 resolution where each cell encodes a 64-way classification over sub-pixel positions within an 8×8 grid, and a reliability heatmap at 1/8 resolution. For semi-dense matching (XFeat*), a lightweight MLP additionally predicts an 8×8 pixel-level offset from a pair of coarsely matched 64-dim descriptors, enabling sub-cell localization without high-resolution feature maps. The 64-dim descriptor is notably compact compared to the 128–256 dimensions used by DISK and SuperPoint, reducing matching cost.

## Method

XFeat's core architectural insight is to minimize channel depth in early convolutional layers (starting at just 4 channels) where spatial resolution is highest, then triple the channel count at each downsampling step (4→8→24→64→64→128) rather than doubling as in VGG-style networks, yielding a backbone that reaches 1/32 resolution with far fewer FLOPs. Keypoint detection is decoupled from descriptor extraction into a parallel branch that reshapes the input into 8×8 pixel cells (64-dim vectors) and applies 1×1 convolutions to classify sub-pixel keypoint positions, avoiding the capacity conflict that joint detection/description causes in small backbones. The descriptor head merges multi-scale features at 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 via bilinear upsampling and element-wise summation, followed by a fusion block. For semi-dense matching, a novel match refinement MLP takes the concatenation of two coarsely matched 64-dim descriptors and predicts an 8×8 offset distribution, recovering pixel-level correspondences without high-resolution feature maps. Training combines a dual-softmax descriptor loss, L1 reliability supervision, NLL offset loss, and knowledge distillation from ALIKE's tiny backbone for the keypoint head, all trained end-to-end on Megadepth plus synthetic COCO warps.

## Results

On Megadepth-1500 relative pose estimation, XFeat (sparse, 4k keypoints) achieves AUC@10°=56.4 and AUC@20°=67.7 at 27.1 FPS on a CPU-only laptop, while XFeat* (semi-dense, 10k descriptors) reaches AUC@20°=77.1 and Acc@10°=85.1 at 19.2 FPS. In the semi-dense setting, XFeat* surpasses DISK* (AUC@20°=75.3, Acc@10°=81.3) on AUC@20°, Acc@10°, and mean inlier ratio (0.74 vs 0.71) while running 16× faster (19.2 vs 1.2 FPS). On ScanNet-1500 indoor scenes, XFeat/XFeat* achieves AUC@10°=32.6/34.7, outperforming all baselines including SuperPoint (24.4) and DISK (19.3/22.3), demonstrating superior generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XFeat (sparse) is 5× faster than ALIKE (27.1 vs 5.3 FPS) while achieving competitive accuracy (AUC@10°: 56.4 vs 61.8), and 9× faster than SuperPoint (27.1 vs 3.0 FPS) with substantially better accuracy (AUC@10°: 56.4 vs 50.1). In the semi-dense setting, XFeat* outperforms DISK* on AUC@20° (77.1 vs 75.3), Acc@10° (85.1 vs 81.3), and mean inlier ratio (0.74 vs 0.71) while running 16× faster (19.2 vs 1.2 FPS) and using half the descriptor dimensionality (64 vs 128). Against ORB, the fastest handcrafted method (44.3 FPS), XFeat* achieves dramatically higher accuracy (AUC@20°: 77.1 vs 39.0) at roughly half the speed. On ScanNet-1500, XFeat/XFeat* leads all baselines by a wide margin (AUC@10°: 32.6/34.7 vs SuperPoint 24.4, DISK 19.3/22.3, ALIKE 16.4), indicating better generalization to indoor scenes. On HPatches homography estimation, XFeat matches or exceeds SuperPoint and DISK on viewpoint splits (MHA@5: 81.1 vs 79.6/77.5) while being far faster, and on Aachen visual localization it is on par with DISK at 0.5m/5° (89.8 vs 89.8) while being 9× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

XFeat builds on the CNN-based local feature extraction lineage established by SuperPoint (self-supervised keypoint detection via 8×8 cell classification), DISK (feature pyramid for robustness), and ALIKE (lightweight backbone with differentiable keypoint detection), but departs from them in three key architectural decisions: a featherweight backbone that triples (rather than doubles) channel counts as spatial resolution halves starting from just 4 channels, a dedicated parallel keypoint branch operating on 8×8 cell reshaped patches with 1×1 convolutions, and a novel match refinement MLP that recovers pixel-level offsets from coarse 1/8-resolution descriptors without requiring high-resolution feature maps as in LoFTR or ASpanFormer. The paper explicitly positions against both the accuracy-at-all-costs trend (DISK, LoFTR) and prior lightweight attempts (ALIKE, SiLK, ZippyPoint) that either still rely on high-resolution final feature maps or require hardware-specific compilation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XFeat demonstrates that a 64-dimensional descriptor from a very small backbone can match or exceed 128-dimensional descriptors from much larger networks (DISK) in pose estimation and localization while running 5–16× faster on a laptop CPU, directly enabling real-time visual correspondence on resource-constrained devices without GPU or custom hardware. The semi-dense matching capability at 1/8 resolution with a lightweight offset predictor is the first efficient approach to this setting, opening the door to denser correspondences in mobile AR and robotics pipelines. Limitations include the fixed 64-dim descriptor (vs. 128 for DISK), the 8×8 offset search space that caps sub-pixel refinement, and evaluation primarily on outdoor/indoor scenes with moderate viewpoint changes; whether the architecture generalizes to extreme viewpoint or textureless scenes remains open.
