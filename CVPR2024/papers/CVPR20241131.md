## Problem

Existing human motion prediction methods focus on active, planned motions and cannot handle purely reactive motions caused by unexpected physical perturbations (e.g., being pushed). This task introduces new challenges: reactive motions are less smooth and coordinated, perturbations can propagate through groups of people in complex ways, and training data is extremely scarce (only 108 total motions). No prior deep learning method addresses this setting.

## Input / Output

The model requires only a single initial frame of 22-joint skeletal poses per person plus explicit external perturbation forces, rather than the multiple history frames typical of motion prediction. It outputs T future frames of full-body joint positions for all N people simultaneously. The minimal one-frame input is a deliberate design choice driven by data scarcity, and the perturbation force is the only additional signal beyond the pose, making the problem an initial-value problem rather than a sequence-to-sequence task.

## Method

The Latent Differentiable Physics (LDP) model maps full-body poses into a 4D Inverted Pendulum Model (IPM) space (cart position + rod angles), simulates differentiable physics there, and recovers full-body poses via a two-stage CVAE (lower body then upper body). In the IPM space, single-person dynamics are governed by a PD balance-recovery controller plus an LSTM correction term, a learnable ground-friction damping coefficient, and an MLP-predicted variable rod length. For multi-person scenarios, a Differential Interaction Model adds pairwise repulsive forces (Gaussian potential in position, sign-based in angles) plus an MLP residual, all shared across people to enable generalization to arbitrary group sizes. The explicit physics provides strong inductive bias, enabling training on only 45 single-person and 63 multi-person samples with MSE loss on joint positions plus auxiliary foot-sliding and IPM-state losses.

## Results

On the FZJ Push dataset, LDP achieves the best performance across all five metrics in both single-person and multi-people scenarios. In single-person prediction, LDP reaches MPJPE 0.097 m, hipADE 0.086 m, hipFDE 0.171 m, MBLE 0.002 m, and FSE 0.131 cm, outperforming the best baseline (siMLPe) by 25.4%, 26.5%, 24.3%, 66.7%, and 22.9% respectively. In multi-people prediction, LDP achieves MPJPE 0.106 m, hipADE 0.092 m, hipFDE 0.218 m, MBLE 0.003 m, and FSE 0.069 cm, beating the best baseline (MRT) by 34.6%, 34.3%, 22.7%, 70%, and 73.0% respectively. Notably, LDP requires only the initial frame and perturbation force as input, whereas baselines typically need multiple history frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the single-person setting, LDP outperforms all seven baselines on every metric except MBLE (where MDM trivially achieves 0 due to joint-angle parameterization). The closest competitor is siMLPe (MPJPE 0.130 vs. 0.097), with LDP improving MPJPE by 25.4% and FSE by 22.9%. RMDiffuse and PhyVae, the next-best methods, are 57% and 67% worse on MPJPE respectively. In the multi-people setting, LDP dominates all four baselines by wide margins: MRT (the best baseline) is 34.6% worse on MPJPE and 73% worse on FSE, while DuMMF suffers severe foot skating (FSE 3.194 vs. 0.069) and JRFormer produces only subtle movements that deviate considerably from ground truth. The paper attributes LDP's advantage to explicit physics-based inductive biases (differentiable IPM and interaction model) that provide strong guidance under extreme data scarcity (45 single-person and 63 multi-people samples), whereas pure data-driven baselines struggle to learn interaction onset, duration, and propagation without sufficient training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the inverted pendulum model (IPM) from bipedal robotics into a latent differentiable physics space for human motion prediction, combined with CVAE-based skeleton restoration and a novel differentiable interaction model for multi-person force propagation. It departs from deep-learning motion prediction (which assumes active, unperturbed motions) and from traditional balance-recovery research (which uses optimization or hand-tuned controllers without learning from data), positioning itself at the intersection of differentiable physics and neural networks. The explicit, physically-meaningful dimensionality reduction to a 4-DOF IPM distinguishes it from prior latent physics approaches where the reduction is implicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that physics-based inductive bias can overcome extreme data scarcity (45 single-person, 63 multi-person motions) in a task where pure data-driven methods struggle, and the generalization from 5-person training to a 13-person diamond formation suggests potential for crowd-scale simulation. However, the evaluation rests on a single dataset (FZJ Push) with adapted baselines from unrelated tasks (motion generation, synthesis), making direct comparison difficult. Open questions include whether the IPM abstraction generalizes to active motions, more complex contact scenarios, and whether the explicit physics requirement limits applicability compared to black-box alternatives.
