## Problem

Existing consistency-training-based semi-supervised learning methods measure prediction discrepancy between augmented views using Euclidean distance, implicitly assuming the embedding space is flat. In practice, embedding spaces exhibit non-Euclidean curvature that varies across classes, so a single Euclidean metric cannot capture the true semantic distance, limiting the effectiveness of consistency regularization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes weakly and strongly augmented RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 96×96 for STL-10) through a Wide-ResNet-24 encoder, alongside a small labeled subset with ground-truth labels. Beyond the standard class prediction vector, the model jointly learns a global d×d metric tensor and C per-class local metric tensors (each d×d) that parameterize the intrinsic geometry of the embedding space. A three-layer linear projector maps encoder outputs to a separate feature space used exclusively for local metric tensor estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is a metric-tensor-based consistency regularization that replaces Euclidean distance with a learned Riemannian distance in the consistency loss. A global metric tensor M^G is optimized by minimizing the quadratic form u M^G u^T over difference vectors between weakly and strongly augmented embeddings, capturing class-invariant geometry. Per-class local metric tensors M_c^L are optimized on projected embeddings (via a 3-layer projector) using pseudo-labels to partition samples by predicted class, capturing class-variant geometry. Two auxiliary losses regularize the tensors: an MSE consistency loss between M^G and each M_c^L prevents divergent optimization, and a negative-trace loss on M_c^L emphasizes diagonal (per-axis curvature) elements over off-diagonal (inter-axis) couplings. The total training objective combines standard supervised cross-entropy with weighted global, local, consistency, and trace losses, and an EMA model is used at inference.

## Results

The proposed metric-tensor-based consistency regularization achieves the lowest average top-1 classification error rate across all four benchmark datasets: 4.33% on CIFAR-10, 27.70% on CIFAR-100, 2.10% on SVHN, and 11.39% on STL-10. In the most challenging low-label regime (40 labeled samples on CIFAR-10), the method reaches 4.37% error, outperforming the next-best ConMatch (4.43%) and FlexMatch (4.97%). On SVHN with 40 labels, the proposed method achieves 2.13% error, beating SoftMatch (2.33%) and DASH (3.03%). The method uses Wide-ResNet-24-w2 (or w8 for CIFAR-100) as the backbone with RandAugment for strong augmentation and EMA (momentum 0.99) for inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best average error rate on all four datasets, with the most significant margins over older baselines: it outperforms FixMatch by 1.27% on CIFAR-10 (4.33% vs. 5.60%), 5.55% on CIFAR-100 (27.70% vs. 33.25%), and 0.81% on SVHN (2.10% vs. 2.91%). Among the most recent competitors, the method edges out ConMatch on CIFAR-10 (4.33% vs. 4.34% avg.) and SimMatch on CIFAR-100 (27.70% vs. 27.82%), while matching ConMatch at 3.92% on CIFAR-10 with 4,000 labels. On SVHN, the proposed method beats SoftMatch (2.10% vs. 2.18% avg.) and ShrinkMatch in the 250-label setting (2.14% vs. 1.96% is a loss, but the method wins at 40 labels: 2.13% vs. 2.51%). On STL-10, the method achieves 11.39% average error, improving over SoftMatch (12.12%) and FlexMatch (14.38%), though ShrinkMatch reports 8.45% at 250 labels matching the proposed method's 8.45%. Overall, the gains over the strongest recent baselines (ConMatch, SimMatch, SoftMatch, ShrinkMatch) are modest (0.1–0.8% in most settings), while the improvements over FixMatch-era methods are substantial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FixMatch-style consistency training framework (weak/strong augmentation + pseudo-labeling) by replacing the implicit Euclidean distance assumption with a metric-tensor-based distance measurement on the embedding manifold. While prior SSL methods (FixMatch, FlexMatch, ConMatch, etc.) treat the local neighborhood in feature space as approximately flat, this work explicitly models the intrinsic curvature via a global metric tensor (class-invariant geometry) and a set of local metric tensors (class-variant geometry), drawing on differential geometry concepts. The pseudo-labeling mechanism for assigning samples to class-specific metric tensors follows the standard argmax approach, and the overall training pipeline (Wide-ResNet encoder, EMA evaluation, label smoothing) remains conventional, making the metric tensor the sole architectural addition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled geometric justification for why consistency training works and provides a mechanism to adapt distance measurement per class, which could inform future representation learning beyond SSL. However, the practical gains are modest (sub-1% error reduction over strong baselines like ConMatch and SimMatch on CIFAR-10), and the authors themselves note the heuristic nature of the manifold hypothesis and the simplicity of the pseudo-labeling as limitations. Open questions include whether the metric tensor approach generalizes to larger-scale datasets (ImageNet), other SSL tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the class-variant geometry assumption holds for foundation-model-scale embeddings.
