## Problem

Supervised deep learning for phase unwrapping requires ground-truth phase images that are often impractical to acquire due to physical imaging limits, destructive measurement, or safety constraints. Existing unsupervised alternatives (e.g., PUDIP) rely on per-sample test-time training, making them computationally prohibitive. This paper introduces the first external (end-to-end) unsupervised deep learning approach for phase unwrapping, eliminating both the need for ground truths and the cost of per-sample optimization.

## Input / Output

The network consumes the wrapped gradient of the noisy wrapped phase, W(∇Y), as its input rather than the raw wrapped image, operating in the gradient domain. It outputs the unwrapped phase image X (up to an additive constant) by predicting ∇X and integrating with boundary conditions. Experiments use 256×256 phase images with Gaussian noise at SNR levels from 0 to 30 dB, and the model is lightweight (0.74M parameters, 8.77 GFLOPs, ~10 ms inference per sample).

## Method

The approach unfolds a variational model min_{X,E} ‖∇X − W(∇Y) + E‖²_F + φ(X) + ψ(E) into a deep unrolling network that alternates Nesterov-accelerated gradient descent steps (for X) with a convolutional sub-network (for the sparse outlier matrix E), where E absorbs errors at points violating Itoh's continuity condition. Training is fully unsupervised via two losses: a recorruption-based self-reconstruction loss that pairs random noise {+∇U, −∇U} to nullify label noise in ∇Y (justified by Proposition 2), and a self-distillation loss using a stop-gradient copy of the network for pseudo-supervision. A condition-aware module predicts stage-dependent parameters (step sizes, thresholding) from the noise level. The architecture encodes the physics of phase wrapping directly into the unrolling structure, with an attentive U-shaped proximal sub-network at each stage, yielding interpretability and strong generalization even with limited training data.

## Results

On the MoGR synthetic dataset, U3Net (unsupervised) achieves NRMSE of 0.69%, 0.25%, 0.19%, 0.16%, and 0.10% at SNR 0, 5, 10, 20, and 30 dB respectively, outperforming all GT-free methods and most supervised baselines in 8 out of 10 settings. On the RME dataset, U3Net achieves 1.12%, 0.38%, 0.27%, 0.17%, and 0.12% NRMSE across the same SNR range. On real InSAR phase data, U3Net ranks first among all 13 compared methods at every noise level (1.00%, 0.82%, 0.46% NRMSE at SNR 5, 10, 20 dB). The model is lightweight with only 0.74M parameters, 8.77G FLOPs, and 10.28 ms inference time per 256×256 sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U3Net decisively outperforms all three GT-free baselines: it reduces NRMSE by over 10× compared to PUDIP (the only other unsupervised method) at low SNR (e.g., 0.69% vs. 17.53% on MoGR at SNR=0), and is ~10,000× faster in inference (10.28 ms vs. 99,695 ms). Against supervised CNN/RNN baselines (SQD-LSTM, EESANet, TriNet, PhaseNet2.0, Ryu. et al.), U3Net achieves lower NRMSE in nearly all settings, e.g., 0.19% vs. 0.70% (SQD-LSTM) and 0.76% (EESANet) on MoGR at SNR=10. The only methods that beat U3Net are the two supervised transformers (UFormer and Restormer), and only at the lowest SNR=0 on MoGR (0.50/0.45% vs. 0.69%); at SNR≥5, U3Net matches or surpasses them on MoGR and clearly wins on RME. On the InSAR dataset (Table 2), U3Net achieves the best NRMSE at all three noise levels (1.00%, 0.82%, 0.46%), beating the best supervised method Restormer (1.06%, 0.93%, 0.91%) and InSAR-specific methods PU-GAN and PUNet. U3Net also has the smallest model size (0.74M params) and second-lowest FLOPs (8.77G) among all DL-based methods, indicating its physics-encoded DUN architecture is more parameter-efficient than general-purpose transformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground as the first external (end-to-end) unsupervised deep learning approach for phase unwrapping, departing from both supervised DL methods that require ground-truth phases and internal unsupervised methods like PUDIP that demand costly per-sample test-time training. Conceptually, it repurposes the recorruption-based denoising framework (Noisier2Noise, Recorrupted-to-Recorrupted) into the gradient domain of PU, leveraging the Itoh continuity condition to justify wrapped gradients as noisy labels for true phase gradients. The deep unrolling network is the first application of the DUN paradigm to PU, unfolding a variational model whose data-fitting term uses wrapped gradients of wrapped phases, with a sparse error variable to handle outliers that violate Itoh's condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for ground-truth phases during training, this work addresses a fundamental practical barrier in PU where GT acquisition is often physically impossible or destructive. The physics-encoded DUN architecture provides interpretability and strong generalization with limited data, outperforming most supervised baselines while using the smallest model among all DL methods. A key limitation is the assumption of known noise statistics, and the authors identify handling unknown noise profiles as an important open direction. Broader implications include the potential transfer of the recorruption-plus-self-distillation training recipe to other inverse problems where ground truth is unavailable but structural self-consistency (e.g., via wrapping or other operators) can be exploited.
