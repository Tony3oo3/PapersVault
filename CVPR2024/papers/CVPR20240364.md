## Problem

Most prior subgroup discovery methods assume a single biased subgroup or require expensive structured attribute annotations, yet real-world image classifiers learn multiple unknown biased subgroups simultaneously. Existing multi-bias approaches either rely on clustering proxies that distort interpretation or detect only one shortcut direction, leaving the practical problem of discovering, interpreting, and mitigating multiple unknown biased subgroups largely unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240364/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates per-class: it takes CLIP-encoded image embeddings (d-dimensional) from a fixed class along with per-sample training dynamics (loss, correctness, or logit values, M-dimensional) from a biased classifier as supervision. It outputs n subgroup direction vectors in latent space, per-image pseudo-subgroup scores (hard or soft labels), natural language descriptions generated via CLIP retrieval from LAION-5B plus LLM summarization, and a retrained classifier with improved worst-subgroup accuracy. The soft-label output is particularly notable, as it allows a single image to carry fractional membership across multiple subgroups for downstream mitigation.

## Method

DIM decomposes class-level CLIP image embeddings into multiple subgroup directions using Partial Least Squares (PLS) supervised by the biased model's training dynamics (loss, correctness, or logit), iteratively finding directions that maximize correlation between image features and supervision, then deflating both sides. Unlike PCA (unsupervised) or single-direction SVM approaches, PLS yields n distinct subgroup vectors whose latent scores serve as pseudo-subgroup labels; biased subgroups are identified as those with the worst validation accuracy. Interpretation is achieved by CLIP-retrieving images and metadata from LAION-5B for each subgroup direction, then summarizing with an LLM. Mitigation employs two strategies: a data-centric approach that filters external data by subgroup scores to augment underrepresented subgroups, and a model-centric approach that integrates hard or soft pseudo-subgroup labels into supervised methods (groupDRO, DI), with a novel soft-label adaptation of DI that computes a weighted combination of per-group classifier outputs during training.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, DIM achieves a cosine similarity of 22.97 between discovered subgroup directions and ground-truth subgroup text embeddings, substantially outperforming Jain et al. (3.06) and Domino (12.20). For biased subgroup detection, DIM identifies 57.5% of under-represented subgroups versus 45.0% (Jain et al.) and 35.0% (Domino). In mitigation, the data-centric strategy using DIM-discovered subgroups lifts worst-subgroup accuracy from 24.8%/33.6% (ERM) to 35.6%/45.1% and overall accuracy from 44.4% to 54.7% on CIFAR-100. On Breeds, DIM's data-centric approach improves the worst four subgroup accuracies from 52.4%/58.4%/64.4%/69.0% (ERM) to 72.5%/82.1%/84.6%/91.3%, with overall accuracy rising from 72.7% to 89.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIM outperforms all discovery baselines across both CIFAR-100 and Breeds: it achieves 22.97 cosine similarity vs. Jain et al.'s 3.06 (a 10.77-point margin) and Domino's 12.20, and 57.5% biased-subgroup detection success vs. 45.0% (Jain) and 35.0% (Domino). For mitigation, DIM's own pseudo-labels consistently improve supervised methods (gDRO, DI) over labels from Jain et al. or Domino — e.g., Soft-DI with DIM labels reaches 48.9% overall accuracy vs. 47.5% with Jain et al. labels and 47.1% with Domino labels on CIFAR-100. Among unsupervised model-centric baselines (JTT, SubY, LfF, EIIL), the best (JTT at 48.5% overall) is still surpassed by DIM's supervised approaches (48.3–49.8%) and far exceeded by the data-centric strategy (54.7%). On Breeds, DIM's data-centric method achieves 89.7% overall accuracy, matching Jain et al.'s data-centric result (89.9%) while improving the worst subgroup by 0.9 points (72.5% vs. 71.6%). The ablation DIM-PCA (removing supervision) drops to 10.76 similarity and 52.5% detection, confirming that model training dynamics as PLS supervision is essential for the gains over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-bias discovery framework of Jain et al. [12], which distills a model's failure mode as one direction in CLIP latent space, to the multi-bias setting by replacing the single-direction search with a supervised Partial Least Squares (PLS) decomposition guided by training dynamics (loss, logit, correctness). It departs from Domino [7]'s unsupervised Gaussian-mixture clustering, which the authors show produces confounded subgroup proxies, by using model supervision to align decomposition components with actual classifier behavior. The interpretation stage repurposes CLIP retrieval and LLM summarization (similar to Jain et al.'s use of cross-modal captions), while the mitigation stage plugs discovered pseudo-labels into existing supervised debiasing methods (groupDRO, DI) and a data-filtering strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world classifiers typically exhibit multiple simultaneous biases, and mitigating one can amplify others (the 'whack-a-mole' problem), yet most prior discovery methods are limited to a single bias direction. The finding that Jain et al.'s negative direction contributes near-zero similarity to any actual biased subgroup (0.82 out of 3.06 total) is a useful diagnostic showing that single-direction methods silently fail on the very subgroups they claim to capture. Open questions include how to select optimal supervisory signals, whether the PLS decomposition scales to very large numbers of subgroups, and whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-based encoders to other foundation models.
