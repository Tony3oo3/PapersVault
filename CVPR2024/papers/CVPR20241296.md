## Problem

Conventional lidar sensors discard the polarization state of returned light, losing geometric and material information encoded in surface orientation and reflectance properties. Existing shape-from-polarization and active polarimetric ToF methods are either passive (failing at night) or restricted to indoor, object-level scenes, leaving large outdoor automotive scenes up to 100 m unaddressed by any polarization-aware reconstruction approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw voltage wavefronts sampled at 1 Gs/s from an APD across 36 finely rotated polarization states (HWP/QWP/LP angles in 0.01° increments), each wavefront spanning 1488 temporal bins of 1 ns (15 cm) over a 150×236 pixel field of view. After peak-based segmentation and ellipsometric reconstruction, the network receives 51-bin sliced wavefronts, peak-derived distance priors, a 51×16 Mueller matrix per pixel, and the viewing direction. Outputs are per-pixel surface normals, a refined distance map, and material parameters (refractive index, roughness, and depolarization matrix amplitudes) recovered via a separate optimization over the forward model.

## Method

The core contribution is a TransUnet (3 encoder layers, 8 transformer bottleneck layers, 3 decoder layers with skip connections) trained end-to-end to predict normals and distance directly from raw polarimetric wavefronts rather than post-processed ToF peaks. Preprocessing applies peak-based segmentation to reduce the temporal dimension from 1488 to 51 bins and solves a least-squares ellipsometric inversion to recover the time-dependent Mueller matrix, disentangling emitter/receiver optics from scene reflectance. The forward model follows the Stokes-Mueller formalism with a temporal-polarimetric BRDF decomposed into specular and diffuse components, integrated into CARLA to generate synthetic training data with realistic beam divergence and APD/ADC noise. Training uses cosine-similarity loss for normals and MAE for distance, with augmentation via varying laser power and APD bias. Ablations confirm that each input channel—raw wavefront temporal structure, polarization cues, and the Mueller matrix—contributes independently, with removing any one degrading mean angular error by 12–22%.

## Results

On synthetic automotive scenes, the proposed method achieves a mean angular error of 8.71° for normal estimation, outperforming the best baseline (PCA at 18.64°) by 53%. For distance estimation, the method yields a mean absolute error of 19 cm versus 32 cm for conventional argmax peak-finding, a 41% improvement. On real-world experimental data, the method achieves 15.76° mean angular error (vs. 18.75° for PCA) and 20 cm MAE for distance (vs. 24 cm for conventional ToF), confirming consistent gains in long-range outdoor settings up to 100 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: classical shape-from-polarization (SfP-DoP [2]), the polarimetric ToF method of Baek et al. [6], and point-cloud PCA normal estimation [60]. On synthetic data, the proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every metric: it reduces mean angular error from 18.64° (PCA) to 8.71° (53% improvement), from 31.03° (Baek et al.) to 8.71° (72% improvement), and from 49.82° (SfP-DoP) to 8.71° (83% improvement). SfP-DoP fails catastrophically in outdoor scenes (49.82° mean error) because its constant-refractive-index and pure-diffuse assumptions break down with varying DoP. Baek et al. [6] is designed for object-level indoor ToF imaging and cannot reconstruct normals in low-DoP regions (e.g., walls facing the sensor). PCA [60] is the strongest baseline but degrades at long range where point density is sparse, while the proposed method leverages polarization cues to resolve normals in those sparse regions. On experimental data, the gap narrows (15.76° vs. 18.75° for PCA, a 16% improvement) due to noisier ground truth and sensor imperfections, but the proposed method still wins on all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal-polarimetric reflectance model of Baek et al. [6] and the ellipsometric reconstruction framework of Baek et al. [7] from indoor, object-level polarimetric ToF imaging to long-range outdoor scene reconstruction. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) replacing the shared optical path with separate emission/reception modules using MEMS and DMD scanning for 100 m range, (2) operating on raw APD wavefronts rather than post-processed ToF peaks, and (3) introducing a TransUnet that jointly predicts normals, distance, and material properties from 36 polarization states. The CARLA-based simulator builds on the wavefront lidar model of Goudreault et al. [26], extended with a Stokes-Mueller polarimetric forward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first automotive polarization lidar dataset and the first polarimetric lidar designed for large outdoor scenes, bridging the gap between km-range environmental polarimetric lidars (low spatial resolution) and meter-range indoor polarimetric ToF (high resolution, short range). The 53% and 41% improvements in normal and distance estimation over SfP and ToF baselines demonstrate that polarization carries meaningful geometric information even at 100 m. However, the 5-minute sequential capture time severely limits real-time deployment, and the authors acknowledge that parallelized acquisition is needed for practical autonomous driving use. Open questions include whether the learned reconstruction generalizes to dynamic scenes, adverse weather, and whether the polarization benefit persists under multi-path reflections and scattering media.
