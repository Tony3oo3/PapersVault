## Problem

RL agents overfit to training environments and fail to generalize to unseen domains, and while parameter uncertainty networks (PUNs) offer a promising middle ground between noise injection and parameter regularization, the standard KL divergence regularization used in Bayes-by-Backprop is ill-suited for RL due to noisy gradient estimates, causing learned distributions to collapse or become under-regulated. No prior work has properly regulated parameter uncertainty specifically for generalization in RL.

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment observation vectors of varying dimensionality (e.g., 78-dim for Quadruped, 18-dim for Walker) and outputs a policy action (continuous or discrete). A key operational detail is that network parameters are resampled from their learned Gaussian distributions at every timestep within a rollout rather than once per episode, making the policy inherently stochastic without requiring an explicit entropy bonus. The actor is a PUN while the critic is a separate deterministic network to stabilize advantage estimates.

## Method

SNR PUN replaces the standard KL divergence regularization in Bayes-by-Backprop with a max-SNR constraint: each parameter's signal-to-noise ratio Ω_SNR_i = |μ_i|/σ_i is penalized if it exceeds a threshold Ω_MaxSNR, via a squared hinge loss. The authors prove this is equivalent to computing KL divergence against a broader family of prior distributions (those matching the posterior's σ and sharing the posterior's sign), making it less restrictive than zero-mean Gaussian KL while still preventing distribution collapse. The training scheme integrates this into PPO by continuously resampling parameters at every timestep, separating the stochastic PUN actor from a deterministic critic, initializing σ from a wide uniform range (an order of magnitude larger than prior work), and omitting the entropy bonus since resampling provides inherent stochasticity.

## Results

SNR PUN achieves the best Average Task Performance (ATP) across all four simulated environments: 670.48 on CartPoleSwingUp, 421.77 on Acrobot-v1, 750.47 on Quadruped, and 310.96 on Walker. The closest competitor, VIB, trails by 47.8 (CartPole), 118.8 (Acrobot), 34.4 (Quadruped), and 55.7 (Walker) ATP points. On the physical Cartpole, SNR PUN achieves a 43% higher successful swingup percentage compared to the best baseline, confirming that simulated generalization gains transfer to real-world deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNR PUN outperforms all six baselines across all four environments. The closest competitor is VIB (Igl et al., 2019), which trails by 47.8 ATP on CartPole (670.5 vs 622.7), 118.8 on Acrobot (421.8 vs 303.0), 34.4 on Quadruped (750.5 vs 716.1), and 55.7 on Walker (311.0 vs 255.3). Among MLP-based baselines, Entropy Maximization is the strongest on CartPole (662.0) and Walker (301.2) but collapses on Quadruped (540.3), while Weight Decay performs best on Acrobot (213.7) and Quadruped (702.0) among MLP variants. The unregularized PUN baseline (534.4, 246.6, 739.0, 250.1) shows that adding SNR regularization yields large gains on CartPole (+136.1) and Acrobot (+175.2), confirming that the SNR constraint is the critical component. On the physical Cartpole, only SNR PUN successfully transfers to the real system, achieving a 43% higher successful swingup percentage than the best simulated baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayes-by-Backprop parameter uncertainty framework (MacKay 1992; Blundell 2015) into RL by replacing the standard KL divergence regularization with a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) constraint, motivated by the observation that KL terms are typically dropped in RL due to noisy gradient estimates, leaving PUNs under-regulated. It builds directly on the PUN-for-exploration line of work (Fortunato 2017; Plappert 2017) but repurposes parameter uncertainty for generalization rather than exploration, and integrates it into PPO with a modified sampling strategy and separated actor-critic architecture. The formal equivalence analysis showing SNR regularization corresponds to KL against a broader family of priors provides a principled justification for why this simpler constraint is more suitable for RL than the standard zero-mean Gaussian prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: parameter uncertainty methods were previously applied to RL exploration but not to the generalization problem that is critical for real-world deployment. The sim-to-real result—where only SNR PUN successfully transferred to a physical cartpole while all other models (including the close VIB baseline) failed—suggests that the SNR constraint captures a form of robustness that simulation-only metrics miss. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively simple control tasks and a single physical system, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or visually-rich environments. The authors note future directions in continual learning and exploration strategies, but the core method's applicability beyond low-dimensional continuous control remains untested.
