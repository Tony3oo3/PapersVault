## Problem

Existing referring multi-object tracking (RMOT) methods tightly couple the tracking and referring subtasks within a single framework, requiring full retraining whenever the underlying tracker is swapped. This tight bundling causes optimization competition between detection, association, and referring, incurs high engineering and training costs, and limits flexibility. The paper asks whether these subtasks can be decoupled so that a referring module can be plugged onto any frozen off-the-shelf tracker without retraining it.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a K-frame video sequence and a free-form textual description (L words, e.g., 'moving car in the left'). The video is first processed by a frozen off-the-shelf tracker (e.g., DeepSORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT) to produce N candidate trajectories; iKUN then scores each trajectory against the text using CLIP-RN50 encoders (visual dim 2048, textual/final dim 1024, temporal window T=8, stride 4). The output is a filtered set of M ≤ N trajectories with bounding boxes and persistent IDs, ranked by a similarity score that has been refined by a test-time pseudo-frequency calibration to counter long-tail description distributions.

## Method

iKUN is a two-stream plug-and-play module that decouples referring from tracking. Its core component, the Knowledge Unification Module (KUM), conditions visual feature extraction on the textual embedding to handle the one-to-many correspondence between a single trajectory and multiple valid descriptions; three variants (cascade attention, cross correlation, text-first modulation) are proposed, with cascade attention selected as default. A test-time similarity calibration step estimates pseudo frequencies for unseen test descriptions by soft-matching them to training-set descriptions via a language model, then applies a linear correction to the raw similarity score. For the tracking side, a Neural Kalman Filter (NKF) replaces the fixed process and observation noise matrices with two small fully-connected networks (R-Net and Q-Net) that dynamically predict R_k and Q_k from the current observation and previous state, improving motion modelling without architectural changes to the tracker. The referring module is trained with focal loss for 100 epochs using ground-truth tracklets, while the tracker remains frozen throughout.

## Results

On Refer-KITTI, NeuralSORT+iKUN with DeformableDETR achieves 48.84% HOTA, 35.74% DetA, and 66.80% AssA, surpassing the previous SOTA TransRMOT (38.06% HOTA) by 10.78% HOTA, 3.23% MOTA, and 7.65% IDF1. In oracle experiments (localization corrected to GT), the gap widens to 61.54% vs. 54.50% HOTA, confirming the advantage lies in association and referring. On the newly introduced Refer-Dance dataset, iKUN achieves 29.06% HOTA versus TransRMOT's 9.58%. For the underlying MOT task on KITTI, NeuralSORT (with NKF) reaches 61.48% HOTA for cars and 50.47% for pedestrians, outperforming all compared trackers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against TransRMOT [37], the previous SOTA for RMOT, which integrates a textual module directly into the tracker and requires full retraining. iKUN with DeformableDETR outperforms TransRMOT by 10.78% HOTA (48.84 vs. 38.06), 3.23% MOTA, and 7.65% IDF1, and the gap widens to 7.04% HOTA in oracle settings (61.54 vs. 54.50), indicating superior association and referring capability. Among MOT baselines on Refer-KITTI, iKUN plugged into various trackers (ByteTrack, OC-SORT, DeepSORT, StrongSORT, Deep OC-SORT) consistently outperforms their standalone versions, with NeuralSORT+iKUN achieving the best HOTA of 44.56% (YOLOv8) or 48.84% (DeformableDETR). On the KITTI MOT benchmark, NeuralSORT surpasses StrongSORT (61.48 vs. 59.78 car HOTA; 50.47 vs. 48.89 ped HOTA), Deep OC-SORT, OC-SORT, ByteTrack, and the DeepSORT baseline, with the NKF component alone contributing +1.32% (car) and +3.50% (ped) HOTA over vanilla Kalman filter. On DanceTrack, NKF improves ByteTrack's pedestrian HOTA from 46.88 to 54.52. On the new Refer-Dance dataset, iKUN achieves 29.06% HOTA versus TransRMOT's 9.58%, a nearly 3× margin, demonstrating the plug-and-play framework's advantage on more challenging motion- and appearance-based queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RMOT paradigm (exemplified by TransRMOT) that tightly couples the referring module inside the tracker, requiring full retraining whenever the backbone changes. Instead, iKUN is positioned as an insertable post-processing module that operates on frozen tracker outputs, borrowing its two-stream architecture from CLIP but extending it with a knowledge unification module to handle the one-to-many correspondence between a single trajectory and multiple textual descriptions. The neural Kalman filter component builds on DeepSORT's motion modelling by replacing hand-crafted noise parameters with lightweight learned networks, a direction previously explored in single-object tracking but not systematically applied to multi-object trackers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play decoupling of tracking and referring is practically significant: it reduces training cost by ~18× (44h53m → 2h25m) and allows swapping trackers without retraining the referring head, lowering the barrier to RMOT research. The Refer-Dance dataset addresses a real gap by providing a more challenging benchmark with motion and dressing descriptions beyond the vehicle-centric Refer-KITTI. However, the multi-stage pipeline introduces engineering complexity (multiple hyperparameters across tracking, referring, and post-processing), and the limited temporal modelling constrains performance on motion-heavy queries. An open question is whether the decoupled approach can match or exceed end-to-end methods as query complexity grows (e.g., compositional or counterfactual descriptions).
