## Problem

Existing spiking Vision Transformers suffer from two critical limitations: their spiking self-attention mechanisms lack proper scaling methods, restricting them to small feature maps, and they rely on shallow convolutional front-ends for local feature extraction, creating a representational bottleneck. This prevents effective scaling to multi-stage architectures that could combine the local feature hierarchy of ResNets with global self-attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241899/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 224×224 (or 288×288 for the largest variant) RGB images or event streams converted to frames by stacking positive, negative, and total event channels over a time window. Processing occurs over 4 discrete time-steps with LIF neurons, and the multi-stage backbone progressively downsamples spatial resolution (56×56 → 28×28 → 14×14) while doubling channel dimensions at each stage, ultimately producing a 1000-class label via global average pooling and a linear classifier.

## Method

The core contribution is Dual Spike Self-Attention (DSSA), which computes attention via Dual Spike Transformation (DST): both the query-side and key-side inputs are spike matrices, so their product reduces to summation of weights (no float-point multiplication), and a spiking neuron layer replaces softmax to produce a binary attention map. Scaling factors are derived analytically from the Bernoulli statistics of spike inputs (c₁ = 1/√(f_X·d), c₂ = 1/√(f_Attn·HW/p²)), where f_X and f_Attn are average firing rates, enabling DSSA to handle arbitrary spatial scales. A p×p strided convolution within DST further reduces spatial size to control computational cost. The full SpikingResformer architecture stacks these DSSA blocks (as Multi-Head DSSA) with a Group-Wise Spiking Feed-Forward Network (GWSFFN, which inserts a 3×3 group-wise convolution between two point-wise layers for local feature extraction) inside a ResNet-style multi-stage backbone with a 7×7 stem and progressive 2× downsampling.

## Results

On ImageNet (224×224, T=4), SpikingResformer-L achieves 78.77% top-1 accuracy with 60.38M parameters and 8.76 mJ energy, and reaches 79.40% at 288×288 input, setting a new SOTA for SNNs. The smaller SpikingResformer-Ti achieves 74.34% with only 11.14M parameters and 2.46 mJ, outperforming Spike-driven Transformer-8-384 (72.28%) by 2.06% while using 5.67M fewer parameters. SpikingResformer-M (77.24%) surpasses Spike-driven Transformer-8-768 (76.32%) by 0.92% while saving 30.82M parameters. In transfer learning, SpikingResformer reaches 97.40% on CIFAR-10 and 85.98% on CIFAR-100, both SOTA among spiking methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest spiking Vision Transformer baseline, Spike-driven Transformer [41], SpikingResformer wins across all model scales: SpikingResformer-Ti beats Spike-driven Transformer-8-384 by 2.06% (74.34% vs 72.28%) with 5.67M fewer parameters, and SpikingResformer-M beats Spike-driven Transformer-8-768 by 0.92% (77.24% vs 76.32%) while saving 30.82M parameters. Against Spikformer [45] and Spikingformer [44], SpikingResformer-S (75.95%) outperforms Spikformer-8-512 (73.38%) by 2.57% and Spikingformer-8-512 (74.79%) by 1.16%, all at comparable or lower parameter counts. Against SCNN baselines, SpikingResformer-Ti (74.34%) surpasses SEW ResNet-152 (69.26%) by 5.08% with fewer parameters (11.14M vs 60.19M) and substantially lower energy (2.46 mJ vs 12.89 mJ). In transfer learning, SpikingResformer outperforms Spikformer's transfer results by 0.37% on CIFAR-10 and 2.15% on CIFAR-100, and beats all direct-training SNN baselines on CIFAR10-DVS (84.8% vs 81.3% for Spikingformer). The one notable loss is on DVSGesture, where SpikingResformer's transfer result (93.4%) trails Spike-driven Transformer's direct training (99.3%) by 5.9%, attributed to the rich temporal information in that dataset that static-image pretraining does not capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two SNN research threads: the multi-stage ResNet-based spiking architectures (SEW ResNet, SpikingResNet) that excel at local feature extraction but lack global attention, and the spiking Vision Transformer line (Spikformer, Spikingformer, Spike-driven Transformer) that introduces spiking self-attention but relies on a shallow convolutional front-end producing only single-scale feature maps. The key departure is the Dual Spike Self-Attention (DSSA) mechanism, which introduces a theoretically grounded scaling factor derived from the firing-rate statistics of spike inputs—something absent in all prior spiking attention designs—and thereby enables the attention to operate across the multi-scale feature maps of a ResNet-style backbone. Architecturally, SpikingResformer fuses the multi-stage ResNet skeleton with DSSA-based transformer blocks, a combination not attempted in prior spiking ViT work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 79.40% top-1 on ImageNet with only 4 time steps and fewer parameters/energy than competing spiking ViTs, this work sets a new SOTA for directly-trained SNNs and demonstrates that the ResNet+Transformer hybrid design is viable in the spiking regime. The scaling theory (Theorem 1) provides a principled explanation for why prior spiking attention mechanisms fail at multi-scale settings, which is a useful diagnostic for the community. However, the transfer-learning results on DVSGesture (93.4% vs. 99.3% for direct training) reveal that static-image pretraining does not transfer well to genuinely temporal neuromorphic data, leaving open the question of how to bridge the domain gap for event-based tasks. Broader impact depends on whether the DSSA scaling framework generalizes to other spiking architectures beyond image classification.
