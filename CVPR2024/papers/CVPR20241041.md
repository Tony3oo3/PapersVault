## Problem

CLIP-based Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) rely on pre-generated concept banks (from ConceptNet, LLMs, or manual curation) that are inherently incomplete, causing significant accuracy loss compared to black-box models. The paper identifies three interrelated challenges—purity (concepts should be atomic), precision (concepts must be recognizable by both humans and CLIP), and completeness (the bank must capture all task-relevant visual information)—and notes that no existing method can automatically recover missing concepts while preserving interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder (RN50 or ViT-L/14) into d-dimensional visual representations, and a user-defined base concept bank of text tokens encoded by CLIP's text encoder into unit-norm concept embeddings. The output is a class label predicted by a linear combination of concept activation scores (cosine similarities between visual representations and concept vectors), plus a set of discovered concept vectors that are mapped back to human-readable concepts from a candidate bank. The method is designed as a post-hoc module applicable to any existing CBM's concept bank.

## Method

Res-CBM introduces D randomly initialized optimizable vectors (residual concept vectors) that are projected against visual representations to produce residual concept scores, which are combined additively with the original concept scores in a two-branch linear classifier. Training alternates two forward/backward passes per batch: first optimizing only the original concept classifier, then fixing it and jointly optimizing the residual classifier and the residual vectors. The key novelty is the Incremental Concept Discovery module, which iteratively initializes a discovered concept vector (mean of base embeddings plus noise), constrains it via a concept similarity loss that pulls it toward the top-M most similar concepts in a large candidate bank, and then replaces one residual vector with the discovered concept's nearest candidate. This process repeats until all residual vectors are converted into interpretable concepts, yielding a fully interpretable, expanded concept bank without sacrificing accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Res-CBM achieves 88.03% accuracy with 237+10 concepts and a CUE of 5.09, outperforming the best interpretable baselines (Lf-CBM at 86.77%, LaBo-30c at 87.52%) and even the non-interpretable PCBM-h (87.57%), while approaching the black-box Linear Probing result (88.58%). On CIFAR-100, Res-CBM reaches 67.91% accuracy with a CUE of 2.54, surpassing Lf-CBM (67.45%) and PCBM-2r (54.50%). On LAD and CUB-200 with expert annotations, Res-CBM-after achieves a mean accuracy of 70.09%, exceeding the supervised Original CBM (66.89%) and PCBM-annotation (66.31%) on most sub-datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Res-CBM outperforms PCBM (all radii) by 5.95% on CIFAR-10 and 13.41% on CIFAR-100, and also surpasses the non-interpretable PCBM-h (87.57% vs. 88.03% on CIFAR-10), demonstrating that interpretable residual concepts can replace the uninterpretable residual linear layer. Against Lf-CBM, Res-CBM gains +1.26% accuracy on CIFAR-10 and +0.46% on CIFAR-100 while achieving a substantially higher CUE (5.09 vs. 5.06 on CIFAR-10; 2.54 vs. 0.63 on CIFAR-100), indicating far more efficient concept utilization. Compared to LaBo (10c/20c/30c), Res-CBM achieves higher accuracy with fewer concepts and a much shorter average concept length (7 vs. 26–27 characters), yielding CUE values of 5.09 and 2.54 versus LaBo's 1.12–3.14 and 0.12–0.25. On LAD and CUB-200 with expert annotations, Res-CBM-after (mean 70.09%) surpasses the supervised Original CBM (66.89%) and PCBM-annotation (66.31%) on 4 out of 6 sub-datasets, though it remains below the black-box LP (77.14%). Overall, Res-CBM approaches LP accuracy (within 0.55% on CIFAR-10, 2.04% on CIFAR-100) while maintaining full interpretability through human-readable concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based post-hoc Concept Bottleneck Model (PCBM) lineage by replacing the uninterpretable residual linear layer of PCBM-h with a set of optimizable concept vectors that are subsequently mapped back to human-readable concepts from a candidate bank. Where prior methods (PCBM, LaBo, Lf-CBM) follow a decremental strategy—pre-generating a large concept set and selecting a subset—Res-CBM adopts an incremental approach, starting from a small base bank and discovering missing concepts from data. The concept similarity loss that constrains discovered vectors to the candidate bank draws on the scene-graph atom structure, and the cosine-similarity bottleneck with activation standardization follows Label-free CBM [38].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CUE metric and the theoretical argument that a reasonable concept count should lie in [⌈log₂n⌉, d) provide the field with a principled way to evaluate concept-bank efficiency rather than simply maximizing accuracy with more concepts. The post-hoc, plug-and-play nature of the residual module makes it broadly applicable to any existing CBM, lowering the barrier for adoption. Open questions remain around the computational cost of sequential concept discovery, the difficulty of constructing adequate candidate banks for fine-grained datasets, and the ignored hierarchical structure of concepts (simple atoms composing complex descriptions).
