## Problem

Existing subject-driven image generation methods either require expensive test-time fine-tuning for each new subject or encode the entire reference image indiscriminately, failing to selectively focus on specific subjects of interest. There is no finetuning-free, model-generalizable encoder that can extract selective subject representations guided by user queries (text or mask) while preserving fine-grained visual details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242072/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (resized to 224×224 for the CLIP-based SSR-Encoder and 512×512 for the diffusion U-Net), a text query or binary mask query specifying which subject to extract, and optionally a ControlNet signal (canny, OpenPose, etc.). It produces a 512×512 generated image conditioned on the selected subject, with the intermediate representation being multi-scale subject embeddings (K=6 scales) that are concatenated along the token dimension before injection into the U-Net's cross-attention layers.

## Method

The SSR-Encoder comprises two components: a Token-to-Patch (T2P) Aligner that uses trainable linear projections to compute a token-to-patch attention map between CLIP text token features and image patch features, enabling selective region highlighting via text or mask queries; and a Detail-Preserving Subject Encoder that extracts visual features from K=6 intermediate layers of the CLIP visual backbone (rather than only the last layer) to retain fine-grained details, with each scale projected through separate linear layers and weighted by the T2P attention map to produce multi-scale subject embeddings. These embeddings are injected into the diffusion U-Net via parallel subject cross-attention layers (trainable copies of the original text cross-attention key/value projections), added additively to the text-conditioned output with a learnable weight λ. Training uses a two-stage procedure on 10M LAION-5B image-text pairs: first 1M steps without multi-scale features, then 100K steps with them, optimizing the standard diffusion denoising loss plus a novel Embedding Consistency Regularization Loss (cosine similarity between mean subject embeddings and query text embeddings, weighted by τ=0.01) that enforces token-to-patch alignment in the subject embedding space.

## Results

On the Multi-Subject Bench, SSR-Encoder achieves the best CLIP-T (0.302) and CLIP-ES (0.182) among all methods, with a DINO-M score of 0.556. On DreamBench, it attains the highest CLIP-T (0.308) and Aesthetic Score (0.821), though its DINO score (0.612) trails Dreambooth (0.668). Among finetuning-free methods specifically, SSR-Encoder leads on every reported metric across both benchmarks, surpassing the next-best finetune-free method (BLIP-Diffusion) by 0.015 in CLIP-T and 0.016 in CLIP-ES on the Multi-Subject Bench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR-Encoder outperforms all finetuning-free baselines (BLIP-Diffusion, IP-Adapter, ELITE, Reference Only) on every metric across both benchmarks, with the largest margins in CLIP-ES (0.182 vs. 0.194–0.201) and Aesthetic Score (6.563 vs. 5.812–6.432). Against finetuning-based methods, SSR-Encoder beats Textual Inversion and Break-A-Scene on CLIP-T and Aesthetic Score, and matches or exceeds them on CLIP-ES, but trails Dreambooth on DINO-M (0.556 vs. 0.681) and DINO (0.612 vs. 0.668), indicating Dreambooth's per-subject fine-tuning still captures finer identity details. Notably, SSR-Encoder achieves the best CLIP-ES (0.182) overall, surpassing even Break-A-Scene (0.187), confirming its superior subject exclusivity without any test-time training. The method also leads in Aesthetic Score (6.563) across all compared methods, including Dreambooth (6.330), suggesting better overall image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSR-Encoder builds directly on Stable Diffusion's U-Net architecture and CLIP's vision-language alignment, extending the IP-Adapter paradigm of injecting image information via parallel cross-attention layers. It departs from prior encoder-based methods (ELITE, Taming, InstantBooth) that require joint training with a specific diffusion model, and from fine-tuning-based selective methods (Break-A-Scene, DreamBooth) that optimize per-subject parameters. The key conceptual departure is the Token-to-Patch Aligner, which performs fine-grained token-to-patch attention to selectively extract subject representations, combined with multi-scale CLIP feature extraction that moves beyond the standard last-layer embedding practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first method to simultaneously offer finetuning-free operation, model generalizability, and selective subject representation, SSR-Encoder provides a practical, plug-and-play component for the Stable Diffusion ecosystem that can be attached to any customized model or ControlNet without retraining. The Embedding Consistency Regularization Loss offers a simple but effective mechanism for enforcing token-to-patch alignment during training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-based encoders, how it scales to more than two or three subjects in a single image, and whether the reliance on text queries for subject selection limits its applicability to visually ambiguous or novel subjects.
