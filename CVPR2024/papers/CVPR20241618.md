## Problem

Classical mesh deformation methods (e.g., ARAP) preserve local geometric properties like rigidity but ignore perceptual and semantic plausibility, producing unnatural bends and distortions that require tedious manual refinement. Data-driven alternatives require large category-specific shape collections, limiting generalization to novel objects. The paper asks whether Internet-scale 2D diffusion priors can be integrated into an optimization-based deformation framework to produce visually plausible edits without such data requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textured 2D or 3D triangular mesh, a set of handle vertices with user-specified displacement direction vectors, and anchor vertices that remain fixed. The mesh is internally reparameterized as a per-face Jacobian field (3×3 matrices per triangle), and the diffusion prior is first fine-tuned via LoRA on rendered views of the source mesh (1 view for 2D, 4 canonical views for 3D) before deformation begins. The output is a deformed mesh with the same topology as the input, where vertex positions are recovered by solving a constrained Poisson equation from the optimized Jacobians.

## Method

APAP represents the mesh as a learnable per-face Jacobian field and deforms it through a two-stage iterative gradient-descent loop. In the FirstStage, only a soft handle constraint (L_h) drives the Jacobians toward the user's edit targets via a differentiable Poisson solve, establishing a geometrically valid initial deformation. In the SecondStage, the mesh is rendered with a differentiable renderer and scored by a LoRA-finetuned Stable Diffusion model via Score Distillation Sampling (SDS); the SDS gradient is backpropagated through the renderer and Poisson solver to the Jacobians, jointly with L_h, to refine plausibility while respecting the user's edit. The LoRA fine-tuning on source-mesh renderings is critical for preserving object identity, preventing the diffusion prior from drifting toward generic category-level appearances. This design exploits the robustness of geometric parameterization to noisy SDS gradients while leveraging the diffusion model's learned visual priors for symmetry, articulation, and structural coherence.

## Results

On APAP-BENCH 2D, APAP achieves a k-NN GIQA score of 4.887×10⁻², outperforming ARAP (4.753×10⁻²) and DragDiffusion (4.545×10⁻²). In a user study with 102 valid responses on MTurk, APAP outputs are preferred over ARAP in 59.17% of trials versus 40.83%. For 3D shape deformation (APAP-BENCH 3D), evaluation is qualitative: APAP produces symmetry-aware and semantically plausible deformations (e.g., expanding both axe blades, recovering car symmetry) that ARAP cannot achieve with its local-rigidity prior alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APAP is compared against two baselines: ARAP [50], a classical geometric-prior deformation method enforcing local rigidity, and DragDiffusion [48], a pixel-space image editing technique that manipulates diffusion latents directly. On the primary quantitative metric (k-NN GIQA on APAP-BENCH 2D), APAP (4.887) beats ARAP (4.753) by ~2.8% and DragDiffusion (4.545) by ~7.5%. The user study confirms this gap: APAP is preferred in 59.17% of pairwise comparisons against ARAP's 40.83%. DragDiffusion underperforms because its latent-space manipulation corrupts instance identity during encoding/decoding, whereas APAP's explicit mesh parameterization preserves the source geometry. In 3D qualitative comparisons, ARAP produces visible distortions (excess bending, broken symmetry) that APAP corrects via the diffusion prior, though no quantitative 3D metric is reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: classical geometry-processing-based mesh deformation (ARAP, Laplacian, Jacobian-field methods) and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from the text-to-3D generation literature (DreamFusion). It repurposes SDS—previously used to *generate* novel 3D shapes from text—instead as a *deformation critic* that scores the plausibility of a user-directed edit, and it grounds the optimization in the differentiable per-face Jacobian representation and Poisson solver from Neural Jacobian Fields [2]. The LoRA fine-tuning step, borrowed from personalized image editing (DreamBooth, DragDiffusion), is a practical addition to prevent the diffusion prior from drifting away from the source object's identity during deformation. Unlike DragDiffusion, which operates in pixel/latent space and cannot preserve parametric structure, APAP maintains an explicit mesh representation throughout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 2D diffusion priors can be effectively injected into a classical deformation pipeline to produce perceptually plausible edits without requiring category-specific training data, which is a meaningful step toward general-purpose interactive 3D editing. However, the approach is computationally expensive (iterative Poisson solves + SDS gradient evaluations + per-instance LoRA fine-tuning), making real-time interaction impractical, and the reliance on 2D renderings as the plausibility signal may struggle with edits that require genuine 3D structural understanding (e.g., occluded geometry). The modest quantitative gains (≈3% k-NN GIQA improvement over ARAP) and the need for careful initialization (the ablation showing ARAP/Poisson initializations degrade results) suggest the method is sensitive to hyperparameters and the two-stage design is not yet fully robust. Open questions include whether 3D-native diffusion priors could replace the 2D rendering bottleneck and whether the framework scales to articulated multi-part scenes.
