## Problem

Existing attention prediction research focuses on salient instances (objects, humans) with relatively explicit visual cues, leaving the more cognitively demanding interaction-oriented attention—where observers must comprehend how instances relate through actions—largely unexplored. The inherent diversity and near-infinite granularity of interactions further necessitates a zero-shot formulation, as no model can be trained on every possible interaction category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242542/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (H×W×3) along with a human bounding box, an object bounding box, an object class label, and an interaction class label, and produces a full-resolution spatial attention heatmap (H×W×1) highlighting fine-grained interaction cues such as body parts, contact regions, and scene context rather than coarse instance-level saliency. The ZeroIA setting requires the model to generalize to interaction categories never seen during training, while the fully supervised setting evaluates on held-out categories from the same distribution.

## Method

The Interactive Attention (IA) model emulates human cognitive processes in three phases: (1) an interaction-oriented prompt block constructs human-centric, object-centric, and interaction-centric text prompts fed to CLIP's text encoder, while CLIP's image encoder extracts visual features; (2) a positional adapter (Fourier-embedded bounding boxes concatenated with text representations through MLPs) and a visual adapter (two transformer encoder layers) produce scene-adaptive knowledge prototypes for human, object, and interaction, overcoming CLIP's fixed noun-biased representations; (3) a Human-Object Cognitive Block applies gated self-attention between instance KPs and visual features, followed by an Interaction Cognitive Block that uses gated cross-attention with the interaction KP to capture subtle relational cues, and a lightweight 2-layer 1×1 convolutional decoder with bilinear upsampling generates the final attention map. Training uses binary cross-entropy against human observer fixation heatmaps from the IG dataset, and the model is further deployed to generate pseudo-labels that supervise cross-attention maps in downstream HOI detectors (MUREN, UPT, STIP), establishing a bidirectional link between attention prediction and interaction recognition.

## Results

On the IG dataset, IA with CLIP-L achieves CC=0.4488, KLdiv=2.8409, SIM=0.7152, and AUC=0.6068 under the ZeroIA setting, outperforming the best baseline MLNet (CC=0.2937) by 52.8% on CC. Under the fully supervised setting, IA with CLIP-L reaches CC=0.5106 and AUC=0.6386, surpassing MLNet (CC=0.3195) by 59.8% on CC. When applied to HOI detection, the attention-guided training strategy improves MUREN from 66.9 to 68.0 mAP on VCOCO and from 32.09 to 32.48 mAP on HICO-det, with the largest rare-class gains reaching +6.0% on MUREN (HICO-det).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IA decisively outperforms all 10 attention prediction baselines across every metric in both ZeroIA and fully supervised settings. The manual-feature methods ITTI and GBVS fail catastrophically (CC < 0.08), confirming that interaction-oriented attention demands cognitive capabilities beyond low-level saliency. Among learning-based baselines trained on IG, MLNet is the strongest competitor (CC=0.2937 ZeroIA, 0.3195 FS), yet IA-CLIP-L still exceeds it by 52.8% and 59.8% on CC respectively. Raw CLIP similarity maps (CC≈0.14–0.17) are far below IA, demonstrating that the prompt-adapter architecture effectively activates CLIP's knowledge rather than merely retrieving fixed noun-biased representations. For HOI detection, the attention-guided strategy consistently improves MUREN, UPT, and STIP over their original versions (e.g., STIP on VCOCO: 66.0→67.2 mAP with IA-L; on HICO-det rare: 28.15→28.90), and IA-generated pseudo-labels outperform limited genuine human attention (e.g., MUREN VCOCO Scenario 2: 69.7 with human vs. 70.4 with IA-L). The remaining HOI baselines (UnionDet, IP-Net, GG-Net, HOTR, QPIC, VCL, SCG, PPDM, MSTR, ATL) serve as context in Tables 4–5 but are not directly modified by the proposed strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of attention prediction research (SALICON, POET, People900, COCO-Search18), which has consistently targeted instance-level cues (objects, humans, animals, texts), by formulating interaction-oriented attention as a fundamentally different cognitive task requiring comprehension of relational visual cues. Methodologically, it builds on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture (Radford et al., ICML 2021) but repurposes it through interaction-specific prompts and lightweight adapters rather than using CLIP's similarity maps directly, addressing the known noun-bias problem in CLIP's representations. The paper also carves new conceptual ground by establishing a bidirectional link between attention prediction and HOI detection—a connection that prior work in either community had not explicitly drawn—by using attention as both a prediction target and a supervisory signal for interaction comprehension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IG dataset (530K fixations, 740 interaction categories) fills a clear gap as the first interaction-centric gaze fixation resource, and the ZeroIA task formulation opens a new research direction at the intersection of goal-directed attention and action understanding. A notable open question is whether the bidirectional attention–HOI pathway can generalize beyond detection to other action-related tasks (classification, VQA, captioning) as the authors speculate. Limitations include the use of mouse clicks rather than true eye-tracking to simulate fixation, a relatively small observer pool (32), and modest absolute mAP gains (0.4–1.3) when transferring attention supervision to HOI models, suggesting the practical ceiling of this approach may be constrained by the quality and granularity of the attention signal.
