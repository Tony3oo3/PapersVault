## Problem

Continual learning of vision-language models like CLIP faces a dual challenge: dynamic expansion methods that incrementally add task-specific components cannot distinguish unseen data and thus lose zero-shot transfer capability, while full-model fine-tuning approaches (e.g., ZSCL) impose large computational burdens and exhibit limited long-term memorization. The paper asks whether one can combine the parameter efficiency of adapter-based tuning with the zero-shot generalization of a frozen foundation model within a unified continual learning framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone) and class name text prompts as input, producing a class label via cosine similarity between image and text embeddings. At inference, the DDAS module first computes reconstruction scores from task-specific autoencoders (built on AlexNet) operating on intermediate image features; if all scores exceed a threshold, the image is routed to the frozen CLIP for zero-shot prediction, otherwise it is directed to the MoE-Adapters with the router corresponding to the lowest-scoring task. In the CIL setting, a single shared router with two experts handles all subsets since task identity is unavailable.

## Method

The framework freezes CLIP's dual encoders and inserts LoRA adapters as sparse experts (22 total) into every Transformer block of both image and text encoders, with task-specific single-MLP routers that take the [CLS] token and select top-2 experts via softmax gating. An incremental activate-freeze strategy is applied: after each task, the top-k most-activated experts are frozen, so subsequent tasks can jointly leverage frozen experts (for inter-task knowledge sharing) and unfrozen experts (for intra-task specialization), reducing the need for task identity at inference. The Distribution Discriminative Auto-Selector (DDAS) trains task-specific autoencoders with MSE reconstruction loss plus a reference autoencoder on TinyImageNet; at test time, if all reconstruction scores exceed a threshold the input is classified as out-of-distribution and sent to the frozen CLIP, otherwise it is routed to the MoE-Adapters via the router with the lowest score, unifying anti-forgetting and zero-shot transfer in one model.

## Results

On the full-shot MTIL benchmark (Table 1), the proposed method (1k iterations) achieves 68.9% Transfer, 76.7% Average, and 85.0% Last, outperforming the prior SOTA ZSCL by +0.8%, +1.3%, and +1.4% respectively. With 3k iterations (Ours†), Average and Last improve further to 77.2% and 87.4% (+1.8% and +3.8% over ZSCL), at a slight cost of −0.7% on Transfer. In the 5-shot few-shot MTIL setting (Table 2), the gains are substantially larger: +3.6% Transfer, +7.0% Average, and +4.2% Last over ZSCL. On CIL benchmarks, the method leads by 3.06% Average and 3.87% Last on CIFAR-100 (10-step) and by 3.04% Average and 7.47% Last on TinyImageNet (20-step) over ZSCL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 15 baselines across every evaluation setting. The closest competitor is ZSCL [79], which the method surpasses by +0.8%/+1.3%/+1.4% (Transfer/Average/Last) on full-shot MTIL and by a much wider margin of +3.6%/+7.0%/+4.2% in the 5-shot few-shot setting. On CIFAR-100 CIL (10-step), the method achieves 85.21% Average vs. ZSCL's 82.15% and iCaRL's 79.35%; on TinyImageNet CIL (20-step), it reaches 79.96% Average vs. ZSCL's 77.18% and LwF-VR's 69.94%. Traditional regularization-based methods (LwF, EWC, PODNet) and memory-based methods (iCaRL, UCIR, PASS) all trail by 5–20+ points on Average accuracy, while dynamic expansion methods (DyTox, DNE, DER) are competitive on CIL but do not address zero-shot transfer. The method also reduces trainable parameters by 60% (59.8M vs. 149.6M) and iteration time by ~60% compared to ZSCL, making it both more accurate and more efficient than every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: dynamic expansion for continual learning (DyTox, DNE), parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA adapters, and Mixture-of-Experts routing. Its most direct predecessor is ZSCL, which first brought zero-shot transfer into continual learning of CLIP through full fine-tuning with knowledge distillation and parameter regularization. The key departure here is replacing ZSCL's dense fine-tuning with sparse MoE-Adapters (LoRA experts + task-specific routers) on a frozen CLIP, and adding the DDAS autoencoder-based selector to eliminate the need for explicit task identity at inference — a practical gap that prior dynamic-expansion and zero-shot CL methods left unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% reduction in trainable parameters relative to ZSCL while matching or exceeding its accuracy makes continual learning of large VLMs substantially more accessible in resource-constrained settings, and the DDAS component enables a single unified model to handle both seen-class classification and zero-shot generalization without manual task labels. However, the reliance on a fixed threshold in DDAS limits scalability to many tasks, and the paper does not explore whether the learned adapter knowledge can be distilled back into the frozen CLIP to improve its zero-shot baseline over time. The approach is validated primarily on ViT-B/16 CLIP and moderate-scale benchmarks, leaving open questions about behavior at larger model scales or with very long task sequences.
