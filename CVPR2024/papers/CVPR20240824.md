## Problem

Online continual learning with single-epoch training cannot reach the terminal phase of training (TPT) required for neural collapse, and a 'bias problem' causes new-class features to be pulled toward existing-class clusters, destroying the ETF structure. This results in poor anytime inference accuracy compared to offline CL methods that benefit from multi-epoch convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240824/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images streamed one-at-a-time (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) through a ResNet-18 backbone followed by an MLP projection layer, producing an L2-normalized feature vector in a d-dimensional space. At inference, the raw feature is corrected by adding a weighted residual term retrieved from a feature-residual memory, and the final class prediction is the ETF classifier vector with maximum dot-product similarity to the corrected feature. The model also uses episodic memory samples and synthesized preparatory data (rotated memory samples) during training.

## Method

EARL induces neural collapse in online CL using a fixed ETF classifier with d+1 vectors (the maximum for the embedding dimension) and dot regression loss. Two key mechanisms address the convergence gap: (1) Preparatory data training synthesizes OOD-like samples by applying 90°/180°/270° rotations to episodic memory images and assigning them to unseen-class labels via a random mapping function, training the model to push new-class representations away from existing-class clusters and accelerating ETF convergence; (2) Residual correction stores (feature, residual) pairs in a dedicated memory during training, where the residual is the difference between the target ETF vector and the model's L2-normalized output, and at inference retrieves k nearest-neighbor residuals weighted by exponential distance to add a correction term to the model output, compensating for incomplete convergence. The training objective jointly optimizes on real episodic memory samples and preparatory data with a balancing hyperparameter λ.

## Results

EARL achieves the best A_AUC across all five datasets and both disjoint and Gaussian-scheduled setups. On CIFAR-10 (disjoint), EARL reaches 78.31% A_AUC and 65.71% A_last, outperforming the next-best X-DER (77.59% A_AUC). On the largest benchmark, ImageNet-1K (disjoint), EARL attains 34.33% A_AUC, a +4.0% absolute gain over the strongest baseline (X-DER at 31.67%). The method also leads on Gaussian-scheduled setups, e.g., 69.52% A_AUC on CIFAR-10 and 30.53% on ImageNet-1K, demonstrating strong anytime inference performance throughout the data stream.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EARL outperforms all eight baselines on A_AUC across every dataset and setup tested. The closest competitor is X-DER (task-aware, distillation-based), which trails EARL by 0.72% A_AUC on CIFAR-10 disjoint (77.59 vs 78.31) but is surpassed by a much larger margin on harder benchmarks: 4.32% on CIFAR-100 disjoint (52.80 vs 57.12) and 2.66% on ImageNet-1K disjoint (31.67 vs 34.33). Notably, EARL is task-free (no task-boundary information), yet it still beats task-aware methods like EWC, MEMO, and REMIND that exploit explicit task boundaries. On Gaussian-scheduled (boundary-free) setups where task-aware methods lose their advantage, EARL's lead widens substantially—e.g., 11.24% A_AUC over X-DER on CIFAR-100 (47.89 vs 41.94) and 5.35% on ImageNet-1K (30.53 vs 25.18). Among replay-based baselines, DER++ and ER are competitive on A_last for CIFAR-10 but fall well behind EARL on A_AUC, confirming that EARL's advantage is most pronounced in anytime inference rather than just final accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural collapse induction framework from balanced training [34] and imbalanced training with fixed ETF classifiers [55] into the online continual learning setting, where single-epoch training prevents reaching the terminal phase of training. It builds directly on NC-FSCIL [56], which induced neural collapse in offline CL, and adapts the residual-correction idea from ResMem [57] to the incremental, anytime-inference context. The key departure is identifying the 'bias problem'—where new-class features are pulled toward existing-class clusters—as the primary obstacle to fast ETF convergence in online CL, and addressing it with preparatory data synthesized via negative transformations (rotations), a technique borrowed from OOD detection and self-supervised learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Online CL with anytime inference is a more realistic deployment scenario than offline CL, and the consistent +4–6% A_AUC gains across CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet, ImageNet-200, and ImageNet-1K make EARL a strong practical baseline for streaming classification. A fundamental limitation remains: the ETF structure caps the number of classifier vectors at d+1, which constrains scalability for truly lifelong learning with unbounded class growth—the authors flag dynamic ETF expansion as an open direction. The method also assumes a fixed backbone (ResNet-18) and memory-only training, so its transfer to larger backbones or memory-constrained edge settings is untested.
