## Problem

Diffusion models have achieved impressive results in 2D image synthesis and object-level 3D generation, but room-level 3D scene generation remains unexplored due to the enormous computational cost of denoising large TSDF volumes. Indoor scenes are extremely sparse (only ~2.57% of voxels are informative), making dense diffusion infeasible, while existing scene synthesis methods produce fragmentary or distorted geometry unsuitable for practical applications like AR and gaming.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240349/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation from scratch, the model takes pure Gaussian noise plus a binary bounding-box mask specifying the target scene dimensions (up to 512×512×128 voxels at 0.04m resolution). For refinement of existing reconstructions, it accepts an occupancy volume or TSDF volume from MVS methods (e.g., NeuralRecon) as a conditioning signal. The output is a full-room TSDF volume that can be extracted into a mesh; during training, the model operates on 96×96×96 random crops, while inference reconstructs the complete scene via overlapping-crop fusion.

## Method

DiffInDScene employs a 3-stage cascaded sparse diffusion pipeline that progressively refines geometry from coarse occupancy to fine TSDF values. A multi-scale Patch-VQGAN (inspired by VQ-VAE-2) encodes TSDF volumes into two hierarchical occupancy latent codes, which serve as conditioning signals for the subsequent diffusion stages. Each stage uses a sparse UNet (implemented with TorchSparse) that denoises only on occupied voxels, reducing compute and memory by two orders of magnitude compared to dense diffusion. For inference on full rooms, the scene is split into overlapping 3D crops that are diffused concurrently; a stochastic TSDF fusion algorithm randomly selects from overlapping crop predictions at each timestep to preserve the sample distribution and ensure global geometric consistency, avoiding the variance collapse of naive averaging.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT scene generation task, DiffInDScene achieves a mean Circularity of 0.781 and Shape Regularity of 0.816, substantially outperforming Text2Room (0.674 / 0.716) and Text2Room+Poisson (0.709 / 0.730). In the user study, DiffInDScene scores 4.856/5 on completeness and 4.836/5 on perceptual quality, nearly double the scores of Text2Room (2.532 / 2.472). For MVS refinement on ScanNet, the method reduces mean normal error below 30° from 6.45° (NeuralRecon) to 5.05°, and in the user study achieves an overall score of 87.14, surpassing even the ground truth mesh (85.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffInDScene is compared against five baselines across two tasks. For scene generation (3D-FRONT), it decisively beats Text2Room [15] on all mesh quality metrics (e.g., Circularity 0.781 vs 0.674) and user study scores (Completeness 4.856 vs 2.532). Adding Poisson reconstruction [18] to Text2Room narrows the gap but still trails DiffInDScene by a wide margin (Circularity 0.709 vs 0.781; Completeness 3.228 vs 4.856). For MVS refinement (ScanNet), DiffInDScene outperforms NeuralRecon [41] on mean normal error at every threshold (e.g., <30°: 5.05° vs 6.45°) and on all mesh quality scores. It also surpasses NeuralRecon+Laplacian denoising [40] and SimpleRecon [35] on both normal error and mesh quality. Notably, in the MVS user study, DiffInDScene's overall score (87.14) exceeds the ground truth ScanNet mesh (85.62), particularly on tight planes (25.85 vs 18.17) and sharp edges (22.58 vs 21.19), though it trails GT slightly on detail (17.41 vs 25.50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM diffusion framework from object-level 3D shape generation (DiffusionSDF, Point-E, etc.) to room-level indoor scene geometry, addressing the scale barrier through sparse convolution over occupied voxels and a cascaded coarse-to-fine pipeline. The multi-scale Patch-VQGAN for occupancy encoding draws directly from VQ-VAE-2's hierarchical codebook design, while the stochastic TSDF fusion algorithm reinterprets KinectFusion's incremental volume fusion in a generative (denoising) context rather than a reconstruction one. The closest prior work, Text2Room, generates room geometry indirectly via 2D text-to-image models followed by iterative reconstruction, whereas DiffInDScene operates natively in 3D TSDF space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based framework to generate complete room-level 3D geometry directly in 3D space, and the sparse formulation (2 orders of magnitude fewer FLOPs) makes room-scale diffusion practically feasible on consumer GPUs. The dual capability—generation from scratch and refinement of MVS reconstructions—broadens its applicability to AR, gaming, and embodied AI pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-room or outdoor scenes, how semantic/text conditioning could be integrated into the 3D diffusion process, and whether the 'outperforming ground truth' mesh quality results (based on 10–11 scenes) generalize across diverse architectural styles and reconstruction artifacts.
