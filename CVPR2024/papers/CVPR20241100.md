## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image methods embed identity into the text embedding space, where a single pseudo-word struggles to disentangle identity-relevant features from identity-irrelevant attributes (expression, pose, etc.) in the reference image. This entanglement causes synthesized faces to either overfit the reference's specific attributes or lose identity fidelity, and makes text-guided attribute editing unreliable. No prior work has leveraged StyleGAN's semantically disentangled W+ latent space as the identity embedding for diffusion-based generation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method requires a single reference face image (inverted to a w+ ∈ R^{18×512} vector via the pretrained e4e encoder), a free-form text prompt, and optionally a Δw editing direction for attribute modification. The output is a full image (SD v1.5, 512×512, DDIM 50 steps) that preserves the target identity in the face region while following the text prompt for background, clothing, and scene. The λ parameter at inference (default 1.0) controls the strength of identity conditioning, and w+ interpolation between two identities produces smooth facial transitions with consistent layout.

## Method

The method aligns StyleGAN's W+ space with Stable Diffusion through a two-stage training procedure. Stage I trains a mapping network F_w (four groups of linear layers projecting w+ from R^{18×512} to 4 tokens of dim 768) and a residual cross-attention module that injects the projected w+ as an additional identity condition into all cross-attention layers of the frozen SD UNet; the residual design (f_z'' = f_z' + λ·Attention(Q†, K†, V†)) avoids degrading the original text-conditioned generation. Stage II fine-tunes only the cross-attention projections on in-the-wild images (FFHQ-wild + SHHQ) with three joint losses: a standard reconstruction loss, a disentanglement loss (L_disen) that penalizes differences in non-face regions when w+ is augmented, and a regularization loss (L_reg) that constrains the identity condition's influence to the face region. The mapping network is frozen in Stage II to ensure W+ exclusively encodes identity, and the inherited Δw directions from StyleGAN enable fine-grained attribute editing (smile, age, eye-opening) without altering the background.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ (120 identities, 20 prompts each), the W+ adapter achieves the best CLIP Score of 0.267, outperforming all baselines including FastComposer (0.265) and CelebBasis (0.253). Identity preservation (ArcFace ID, lower is better) is competitive at 0.418, slightly behind IP-Adapter-Face (0.407) but substantially ahead of CelebBasis (0.448) and all text-embedding-based methods. Face detection score is 0.950, tied with FastComposer. In the Stage I inversion/editing ablation (Table 2), the method matches e4e on inversion ID (0.435 vs. 0.431) while achieving better editing fidelity (smile ID 0.275 vs. 0.312; age ID 0.393 vs. 0.409).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The W+ adapter outperforms all text-embedding-based personalization methods (Textual Inversion, Dreambooth, Custom Diffusion) by a wide margin on every metric, with CLIP Score improvements of 0.05–0.09 and ID reductions of 0.08–0.16. Against the closest face-specific baselines, it achieves the highest CLIP Score (0.267 vs. 0.265 for FastComposer, 0.241 for IP-Adapter-Face, 0.253 for CelebBasis), indicating superior text-prompt alignment. On identity preservation, IP-Adapter-Face edges out the proposed method (ID 0.407 vs. 0.418), though the authors note IP-Adapter was fine-tuned on the much larger LAION2B dataset while their model uses only ~110K images. The key differentiator is editability: only the W+ adapter supports fine-grained facial attribute editing (smile, age, etc.) via Δw directions while keeping non-facial regions unchanged, a capability absent in all compared baselines. In the Stage I inversion/editing comparison, the method matches e4e on inversion quality but surpasses it on editing fidelity (smile ID 0.275 vs. 0.312; age ID 0.393 vs. 0.409), confirming that the W+→SD alignment preserves StyleGAN's editability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in personalized T2I generation—embedding identity into the text embedding space (Textual Inversion, Dreambooth, Custom Diffusion, IP-Adapter, CelebBasis)—by instead inverting a reference face into StyleGAN's W+ latent space and projecting it into Stable Diffusion via a learned mapping network and residual cross-attention. It builds directly on the e4e inversion encoder for obtaining W+ vectors and on the adapter architecture pattern established by ControlNet and IP-Adapter, but repurposes the disentangled GAN latent space as the identity condition rather than a raw image or text token. The disentanglement and regularization losses in Stage II extend the adapter training recipe to explicitly constrain W+ influence to the face region, addressing a limitation the authors identify across all prior personalization methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a single reference image yields both identity-preserving generation and fine-grained semantic editing (smile, age, eye-opening) along known Δw directions—something no prior text-embedding-based method supports simultaneously. Limitations are acknowledged: the W+ inversion step loses fine detail (hurting ID fidelity slightly vs. IP-Adapter-Face), the framework handles only single-face images, and editing of pose or accessories remains unreliable. Open questions include whether the W+ adapter generalizes to multi-face scenes via localized injections, whether the approach transfers to non-face object domains with their own structured latent spaces, and whether the detail loss from GAN inversion can be mitigated with higher-fidelity encoders.
