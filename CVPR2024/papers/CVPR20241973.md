## Problem

Neural SDF reconstruction of indoor scenes benefits from monocular normal priors to constrain texture-less regions, but these priors are inherently multi-view inconsistent because the estimation networks are trained on single views. Existing remedies—handcrafted consistency filtering (NeuRIS) or per-view uncertainty maps—are fragile to real-world noise, leading to noisy or missing surfaces in reconstructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241973/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images at 384×384 resolution with known camera poses, along with monocular surface normal maps predicted by the pretrained OmniData network. It outputs a continuous neural SDF (extracted as a mesh via Marching Cubes), rendered novel-view images, and a learned view-dependent normal compensation field. Training uses 1024 pixels per batch with a two-stage schedule (≈20k + 60–80k iterations) on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

NC-SDF models three neural implicit fields: a hybrid geometry model (MLP branch for low-frequency smoothness + 8-layer voxel grid branch for high-frequency detail, fused and decoded by a shallow MLP to produce SDF values and geometry features), a view-dependent radiance field, and a normal compensation model that predicts per-point rotation angles (γ, β, θ) as a function of viewing direction, SDF normal, and geometry features. Rather than directly supervising SDF normals with noisy monocular priors, the method rotates the SDF normal by the learned compensation angles and matches the resulting compensated normal to the prior, thereby absorbing view-dependent bias into the implicit representation. A two-stage training strategy first optimizes color and geometry without compensation to establish a clean initialization, then introduces the compensation model so the coupled RGB and normal losses jointly disentangle true geometry from prior bias. An informative pixel sampling strategy uses Canny edge intensity to progressively shift sampling from random toward high-information regions, improving recovery of thin structures like chair legs and doorknobs.

## Results

On the real-world ScanNet dataset, NC-SDF achieves the best F-score of 0.781, outperforming the closest competitor HelixSurf (0.756) by 2.5 percentage points, while also attaining the highest precision (0.811) and recall (0.754). On the synthetic ICL-NUIM dataset, NC-SDF achieves an F-score of 0.852 with a precision of 0.955, substantially surpassing MonoSDF (MLP) at 0.650 F-score. The method also achieves the lowest accuracy error (0.033 on ScanNet, 0.021 on ICL-NUIM) and completeness error (0.039 and 0.105 respectively) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NC-SDF outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets. On ScanNet, it beats the strongest prior method HelixSurf (F-score 0.756) by 2.5 points and MonoSDF-MLP (0.748) by 3.3 points, while also achieving the best precision (0.811 vs. 0.795) and recall (0.754 vs. 0.725). On ICL-NUIM, the margin is much larger: NC-SDF's F-score of 0.852 exceeds MonoSDF-MLP (0.650) by 20.2 points and NeuRIS (0.626) by 22.6 points, with precision jumping from 0.741 to 0.955. Methods without additional supervision (NeuS, VolSDF, COLMAP) perform substantially worse, with F-scores below 0.55, confirming the importance of geometric priors for indoor scenes. Among prior methods using priors, NeuRIS's handcrafted consistency filtering proves less robust than NC-SDF's adaptive compensation, yielding a 9.7-point F-score gap on ScanNet. MonoSDF-Grid, which uses voxel grids for expressiveness, produces noisier surfaces (lower precision at 0.727) compared to NC-SDF's hybrid approach (0.811), validating the benefit of the proposed feature-fusion geometry model.

## Relation to Prior Work

NC-SDF extends the line of prior-guided neural implicit surface reconstruction (NeuS, MonoSDF, NeuRIS) by reframing the treatment of monocular normal priors: rather than filtering out inconsistent priors (NeuRIS) or using them as direct supervision (MonoSDF), it explicitly models view-dependent biases as a learnable component of the implicit scene representation. The volume rendering and SDF-to-density reparameterization follow NeuS, while the hybrid MLP+voxel-grid geometry model builds on the trend of combining smooth inductive biases with high-frequency encodings. The informative pixel sampling strategy draws on class-imbalance literature to address the under-representation of geometric detail in texture-less indoor scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The view-dependent normal compensation model offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc consistency filtering for handling noisy priors, and the two-stage training schedule (let the network lock onto consistent regions before introducing the compensation model) is a practical insight likely transferable to other prior-guided reconstruction settings. However, the evaluation is limited to four scenes per dataset, the method depends on OmniData for normal priors (introducing a fixed upstream bias), and the Canny-based sampling is heuristic. Open questions include whether the compensation framework generalizes to other types of view-dependent priors (e.g., depth), to outdoor or unstructured scenes, and whether the approach scales to larger scene collections without per-scene optimization.
