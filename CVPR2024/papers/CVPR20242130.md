## Problem

Knowledge distillation has been well-studied for CNN-based detectors but lacks effective application to DETR-family architectures. The core challenge is that DETR's set-prediction formulation with independently learned object queries provides neither sufficient nor consistent distillation points between teacher and student, unlike CNN detectors where shared anchor grids guarantee strict spatial correspondence. This paper identifies and addresses this inconsistency as the fundamental barrier to effective DETR distillation.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces bounding boxes with class labels as output, following the standard DETR set-prediction format. Unusually, the distillation mechanism requires feeding a shared set of specialized object queries (in addition to the image) into both teacher and student decoders simultaneously; these queries are unlearnable and resampled each iteration for the general component. For heterogeneous distillation, anchor coordinates from the CNN detector are converted into the object-query formulation of DETR to bridge the architectural gap.

## Method

KD-DETR decouples detection and distillation by introducing a set of specialized, unlearnable object queries shared between teacher and student to construct consistent distillation points. A general-to-specific sampling strategy combines randomly initialized queries (resampled every iteration to probe the full feature map) with the teacher's own optimized queries (to capture foreground-focused knowledge), and a foreground rebalance weight derived from teacher classification scores reweights the distillation loss. The distillation loss combines temperature-scaled KL divergence for classification with L1 and GIoU losses for box regression, added to the standard detection loss. The paradigm extends to heterogeneous distillation between DETR and CNN detectors by converting anchor coordinates into object-query format and selecting top-k anchors by IoU with ground truth as distillation points.

## Results

On MS COCO 2017, KD-DETR achieves consistent mAP gains across three DETR architectures: DAB-DETR ResNet-18 reaches 41.4% mAP (+5.2% over baseline), Deformable DETR ResNet-50 reaches 48.3% mAP (+3.8%), and DINO ResNet-18 reaches 48.4% mAP (+4.4%). In the direct comparison on Deformable DETR ResNet-50, KD-DETR achieves 48.3% mAP, outperforming the strongest prior method DETRDistill (46.6%) by 1.7%. For heterogeneous distillation from DINO to Faster R-CNN ResNet-50, KD-DETR achieves 40.5% mAP, a 2.1% improvement over the student baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Deformable DETR ResNet-50 (Table 3), KD-DETR (48.3 AP) outperforms DETRDistill (46.6 AP) by 1.7%, FitNet (44.9 AP) by 3.4%, and FGD (44.1 AP) by 4.2%, with the largest margin on small objects (AP_S: 30.8 vs. 28.5 for DETRDistill). On heterogeneous distillation to Faster R-CNN ResNet-50 (Table 4), KD-DETR (40.5 AP) edges out FGD (40.4 AP) by 0.1%, GID (40.2 AP) by 0.3%, and FGFI (39.3 AP) by 1.2%, confirming that the consistent distillation points paradigm transfers effectively across architectures. Notably, KD-DETR uses only simple logit-based distillation (KL + L1 + GIoU) without feature-matching or relation-based losses, yet surpasses methods that employ more complex distillation objectives. The gains are consistent across object scales, with the largest improvements on small objects (AP_S), suggesting the distillation points help the student better localize small instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DETR family of end-to-end detectors (DETR, DAB-DETR, Deformable DETR, DINO) and the well-established knowledge distillation literature for CNN-based object detectors (Chen et al. 2017, Wang et al. 2019, Dai et al. 2021). It departs from DETRDistill (Chang et al. 2023), the closest prior work, by arguing that Hungarian-matched object queries are unstable and merely 'similar' rather than truly consistent distillation points. The key conceptual departure is reframing DETR distillation around the notion of shared, unlearnable distillation points that decouple the distillation task from the detection task, and extending the paradigm to heterogeneous distillation between DETR and CNN detectors, which no prior work addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'distillation points' framing provides a clear diagnostic for why naive logit-based distillation fails on DETR (inconsistent points actually degrade performance below baseline), which is a useful negative result for the community. The practical impact is solid: 2.6–5.2% mAP gains across three DETR architectures with two backbone scales, plus a 2.1% gain in the novel heterogeneous DETR→Faster R-CNN setting, making KD-DETR a plug-and-play option for compressing transformer detectors. Open questions include whether the unlearnable-query formulation generalizes to other transformer-based tasks (segmentation, tracking), whether the number of distillation points can be learned adaptively, and whether the approach scales to much larger teacher models or multi-dataset training.
