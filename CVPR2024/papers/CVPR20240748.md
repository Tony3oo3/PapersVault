## Problem

Existing action recognition datasets are incompatible with each other due to ambiguous class names, inconsistent granularities, and semantic gaps, forming 'isolated islands' that prevent cross-dataset integration and transfer learning. No principled, unified semantic space exists to align these heterogeneous benchmarks, making it impossible to leverage all available data jointly for generalizable action understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-modal physical inputs—RGB images, video clips (sampled at 3 FPS), 3D SMPL point clouds (24 keypoints), and skeleton sequences—and produces a one-to-many mapping to 898 VerbNet verb nodes, outputting per-node classification probabilities. For transfer learning to downstream benchmarks, a small learnable MLP converts the fixed node prediction vector into action class scores specific to the target dataset's label set. The Pangea test set for node classification contains 178K images split into rare (133) and non-rare (157) leaf-node subsets to evaluate few/zero-shot capability.

## Method

The P2S (Physical-to-Semantic) mapping first encodes each modality via a dedicated encoder (CNN/Transformer for 2D, PointNet++ for 3D point clouds), then disentangles the entangled physical representation into N node-specific sub-representations via learnable MLPs, enabling weakly-supervised learning under partial labels. Verb nodes are encoded by concatenating semantic descriptions (verb members, example sentences, WordNet/FrameNet definitions) with geometric prompts (ancestor/descendant paths in the VerbNet tree) and passing them through a shared Transformer text encoder. Alignment occurs in a Lorentz hyperboloid where a classification loss (BCE on negative Lorentzian distance) and an entailment loss (constraining positive samples inside the node's entailment cone) jointly train the model. The structured VerbNet hierarchy (898 nodes, 5,800+ verbs) provides unambiguous, multi-granularity semantics, and label augmentation exploits tree structure to generate pseudo-labels for missing nodes, addressing the partial-label problem inherent in mapping diverse dataset classes onto a unified space.

## Results

On the Pangea verb node classification test set (178K images), P2S-aug achieves 34.25 mAP overall (21.56 on 133 rare nodes, 45.00 on 157 non-rare nodes), substantially outperforming the CLIP baseline (28.25 / 16.90 / 37.87). In transfer learning, P2S pretrained on Pangea outperforms CLIP across all six downstream benchmarks: HICO (47.74 vs 46.35 mAP), HAA (73.80 vs 72.82% Top-1), HMDB51 (68.37 vs 67.47%), Kinetics-400 (71.40 vs 66.33%), BABEL-120 (49.69 vs 32.42%), and HAA4D (54.1 vs 38.0%). Fusing P2S with the SOTA EVL model yields further gains, reaching 90.22% Top-1 on HAA, 85.09% on HMDB51, and 80.87% on Kinetics-400.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2S consistently outperforms the vanilla CLIP baseline on every benchmark: +1.39 mAP on HICO, +0.98% on HAA, +0.90% on HMDB51, +5.07% on Kinetics-400, +17.27% on BABEL-120, and +16.1% on HAA4D. The CLIP-Pangea variant (CLIP fine-tuned on Pangea with contrastive loss) is inconsistent — it beats CLIP on HAA, HMDB51, BABEL, and HAA4D but underperforms on HICO (45.09 vs 46.35 mAP) and Kinetics-400 (70.45 vs 72.82%), suggesting that simply mixing Pangea data into CLIP's training does not capture the structured semantic-geometric knowledge. Against ad-hoc SOTA video models (TSN, EVL, I3D, TPN), P2S alone is competitive or slightly behind, but the P2S+EVL fusion surpasses all of them by 2.58–4.47% Top-1. On 3D benchmarks, P2S (using PointNet++ encoder) beats the standalone PointNet++ baseline by 7.43% on BABEL and 15.5% on HAA4D, and also outperforms the ad-hoc SGN method on HAA4D (54.1 vs 53.3%). On HICO, P2S surpasses RelViT (47.74 vs 40.12 mAP) and R*CNN (28.50 mAP), confirming that the structured semantic space provides meaningful gains over purely visual relational reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of designing action recognition benchmarks with independently hand-crafted class taxonomies, arguing that such datasets form incompatible 'isolated islands' due to semantic gaps and inconsistent granularity. It builds on CLIP's visual-language contrastive framework for encoding, on hyperbolic (Lorentz) geometry and entailment cones for capturing hierarchical structure, and on PointNet++ for 3D point-cloud encoding, but the core novelty is using VerbNet's linguistic verb taxonomy as a principled, domain-independent semantic space rather than an ad-hoc class list. The P2S mapping system unifies these components into a multi-modal, multi-label classifier over verb nodes, positioned as a plug-and-play baseline complementary to existing SOTA models rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Pangea database (19.5M images, 1.1M videos, 840K 3D humans across 28 datasets, 4K+ original classes aligned to 513 VerbNet nodes) is a substantial unified resource that could concentrate community effort and enable cross-dataset transfer learning that was previously infeasible. The finding that simply mixing datasets for CLIP pretraining (CLIP-Pangea) underperforms vanilla CLIP on several benchmarks validates the paper's central thesis that structured semantic alignment matters beyond mere data volume. Open questions include whether the VerbNet-based space generalizes to non-English or non-human actions, whether the pseudo-3D labels from ROMP/EFT introduce systematic biases, and whether larger backbones (beyond the 'concise' models used here) would extract more value from the unified space.
