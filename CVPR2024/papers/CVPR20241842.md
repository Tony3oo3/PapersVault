## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time, high-quality rendering for static scenes but lacks a principled way to incorporate object motion without sacrificing speed or quality. Existing dynamic extensions either assign per-Gaussian flow vectors (incurring high training cost and noisy trajectories) or rely on implicit deformation fields that are slow to query. The paper identifies that real-world motions are sparse, spatially continuous, and locally rigid, motivating a compact control-point-based motion representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241842/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular dynamic video (a sequence of RGB images with known or estimated camera poses) as input and produces a canonical-space 3D Gaussian scene (~100K Gaussians) together with ~512 sparse control points, each carrying a learnable RBF radius and a time-varying 6-DoF transformation (quaternion rotation + translation) predicted by a deformation MLP. At inference, arbitrary novel views at any timestep can be rendered in real time via differentiable rasterization, and the learned control-point graph supports user-driven motion editing by deforming handle points through ARAP optimization.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces per-Gaussian motion with a set of sparse control points whose 6-DoF transformations are predicted by a single MLP conditioned on canonical position and time. Each Gaussian's motion is obtained by linear blend skinning over its K=4 nearest control points, with interpolation weights computed from a Gaussian-kernel RBF whose radius is jointly learned. An ARAP loss, computed over trajectory-aligned neighborhoods of control points, enforces local rigidity of the learned motion field, while an adaptive density strategy clones or prunes control points based on gradient magnitude and impact weight to match local motion complexity. Training proceeds in two stages: a pre-training phase that fits control points and the MLP with fixed Gaussians, followed by joint optimization of all components under a combined L1 + D-SSIM rendering loss plus the ARAP regularizer.

## Results

On the D-NeRF benchmark (7 dynamic scenes, 400×400 resolution), SC-GS achieves an average PSNR of 43.31, SSIM of 0.997, and LPIPS of 0.0063, substantially outperforming the next-best method 4D-GS (PSNR 34.01, SSIM 0.987, LPIPS 0.0316) by over 9 dB in PSNR. On NeRF-DS (7 real-world videos with estimated camera poses), SC-GS attains an average PSNR of 24.1, MS-SSIM of 0.891, and LPIPS of 0.140, edging out the baseline (PSNR 23.7) and surpassing NeRF-DS (PSNR 23.9) in overall average despite lacking a dedicated specular model. The ablation confirms that removing control points drops PSNR from 43.31 to 38.51, and removing the ARAP loss drops it to 42.62.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SC-GS decisively outperforms all NeRF-based baselines on D-NeRF: D-NeRF (31.69 PSNR), Tensor4D (27.62), K-Planes (32.32), FF-NVS (33.73), and TiNeuVox-B (33.76) all trail by 5–16 dB. Among Gaussian-based methods, 4D-GS (34.01 PSNR) is the closest competitor but still lags SC-GS by 9.3 dB in PSNR and 0.032 in LPIPS. The per-Gaussian deformation MLP baseline (38.51 PSNR) demonstrates that 3D Gaussians alone provide a strong foundation, but the sparse control-point representation adds another 4.8 dB by regularizing the motion space and avoiding local minima. On NeRF-DS, the comparison is more nuanced: NeRF-DS (23.9 PSNR) slightly edges out SC-GS (24.1) on individual scenes like Sheet and Basin due to its specialized specular modeling, yet SC-GS achieves the best overall average and matches or exceeds NeRF-DS on Bell, Cup, and Sieve. The paper notes that camera pose estimation errors in NeRF-DS degrade SC-GS performance, and that incorporating Spec-Gaussian would close the remaining specular gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from static to dynamic scenes by introducing a sparse set of ~512 control points with time-varying 6-DoF transformations to drive ~100K Gaussians, drawing on classical linear blend skinning (LBS) from computer graphics. It departs from concurrent dynamic Gaussian splatting approaches (4D-GS, Deformable 3DGS) that learn per-Gaussian deformation fields or hexplane-based offsets, instead using a compact motion basis that reduces MLP query overhead and enables spatially coherent motion. The ARAP principle (Sorkine & Alexa, 2007) is repurposed both as a training regularizer for local rigidity and as the deformation solver for user-driven motion editing, bridging classical mesh editing with neural rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sparse control point representation uniquely enables appearance-preserved motion editing as a byproduct of the scene model, a capability absent in prior dynamic NeRF or Gaussian splatting methods. However, the approach remains sensitive to camera pose accuracy (failing on HyperNeRF) and struggles with specular surfaces (limited gains on NeRF-DS), leaving open questions about robustness to real-world capture conditions. Future work on incorporating specular modeling (e.g., Spec-Gaussian) and motion deblurring would be needed to handle more challenging real-world videos.
