## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality and speed but produces heavily redundant Gaussians that overfit to every training view while neglecting underlying scene geometry. This redundancy limits scalability on large scenes and makes the representation brittle to significant view changes, texture-less regions, and view-dependent lighting effects, since each Gaussian's attributes are static and lack interpolation capability across viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242478/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and a sparse SfM point cloud (from COLMAP) as initialization. At inference, only anchors falling within the current view frustum are activated to spawn neural Gaussians, and a learnable opacity-based selector filters out trivial Gaussians before rasterization. The output is a compact scene model (anchor points plus small MLP predictors) that renders novel views at ~100 FPS at 1K resolution while using 4–10× less storage than 3D-GS.

## Method

Scaffold-GS builds a dual-layered hierarchy: a sparse voxel grid of anchor points (initialized from SfM points) each carries a learnable 32-dim feature, a scaling factor, and k=10 learnable 3D offsets. For each visible anchor, k neural Gaussians are spawned at offset positions, and their attributes (opacity, color, quaternion, scale) are decoded on-the-fly by small 2-layer MLPs conditioned on a view-dependent anchor feature (a multi-resolution feature bank blended by a softmax-weighted MLP of camera-anchor distance and direction) plus the relative viewing geometry. This view-dependent decoding replaces 3D-GS's static per-Gaussian parameters, enabling the same anchor to produce different Gaussian properties for different viewpoints. Anchor growing (gradient-thresholded multi-resolution voxel quantization) and pruning (opacity accumulation) operations iteratively refine the anchor set during training, while a volume regularization loss discourages oversized, overlapping Gaussians.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, Scaffold-GS achieves 27.72 PSNR / 0.811 SSIM / 0.228 LPIPS, matching or slightly exceeding 3D-GS (27.21 PSNR) and Mip-NeRF360 (27.69 PSNR). On the more challenging Tanks&Temples and DeepBlending datasets, Scaffold-GS surpasses 3D-GS by 0.90 and 1.02 dB PSNR respectively (24.04 vs 23.14; 30.43 vs 29.41). In terms of efficiency, Scaffold-GS renders at 102–139 FPS while reducing storage by 4.2×–10.2× compared to 3D-GS (e.g., 171 MB vs 721 MB on Mip-NeRF360). On multi-scale BungeeNeRF, Scaffold-GS gains 2.12 dB PSNR over 3D-GS (27.01 vs 24.89) with 7.9× less storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scaffold-GS is compared against four baselines: 3D-GS (primary), Mip-NeRF360, iNGP, and Plenoxels. Against 3D-GS, Scaffold-GS wins on Tanks&Temples (+0.90 dB PSNR) and DeepBlending (+1.02 dB PSNR) while matching on Mip-NeRF360 (+0.51 dB PSNR); it also achieves comparable or higher FPS (102 vs 97 on Mip-NeRF360) with 4–10× less storage. Against Mip-NeRF360, Scaffold-GS leads on all three datasets by 0.03–1.82 dB PSNR, with the largest margin on Tanks&Temples. Against iNGP and Plenoxels, Scaffold-GS dominates by a wide margin (2.1–7.4 dB PSNR), confirming that the Gaussian-splatting paradigm with structured anchors substantially outperforms grid-based and hash-based neural field approaches. On multi-scale BungeeNeRF, Scaffold-GS gains 2.12 dB over 3D-GS, demonstrating its view-adaptive advantage in handling varying observation distances.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scaffold-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS, Kerbl et al. 2023), inheriting its anisotropic Gaussian primitives, tile-based rasterization, and α-blending pipeline, but departs fundamentally from 3D-GS's free-drift and split-based optimization by imposing a hierarchical structure: a sparse voxel grid of anchor points constrains where Gaussians can exist, and small MLPs predict each Gaussian's attributes (opacity, color, scale, rotation) on-the-fly from anchor features and camera-relative viewing direction/distance. This view-dependent decoding borrows the spirit of neural radiance fields (NeRF, Mip-NeRF) but applies it at the Gaussian level rather than through volumetric ray-marching, while the voxel-grid anchoring echoes grid-based representations like Plenoxel and InstantNGP. The growing/pruning strategy for anchors is analogous to the densification in 3D-GS and Point-NeRF but operates at the anchor level with multi-resolution voxel quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4–10× storage reduction while matching or exceeding 3D-GS quality is practically significant for large-scale scene applications (urban environments, VR) where model size and memory are bottlenecks. The view-adaptive Gaussian attributes address a core limitation of 3D-GS—baked-in view-dependent effects that fail under novel viewing angles or distances—and the demonstrated local continuity of decoded attributes (Fig. 8) suggests the learned features encode meaningful scene structure. However, the method still depends on SfM initialization quality and struggles with extremely texture-less regions, and the per-anchor MLP prediction, while fast, adds architectural complexity that may complicate downstream tasks like editing or compression. The semantic clustering of anchor features (Fig. 6) hints at potential for scene understanding and manipulation, but this remains exploratory.
