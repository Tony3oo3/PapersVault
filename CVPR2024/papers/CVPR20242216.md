## Problem

Existing image-to-video diffusion models (e.g., VideoComposer) independently predict the backward diffusion noise for each frame, leaving the inherent correlation between the given static image and subsequent frames under-exploited. This results in poor temporal coherence, where synthesized frames drift away from the input image in both foreground and background content. The paper addresses this gap by reformulating I2V noise prediction as temporal residual learning guided by an image noise prior that explicitly encodes the first-frame-to-subsequent-frame relationship.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static RGB image (the first frame) and a free-form text prompt as input. The image is encoded by a pre-trained 2D VAE into a latent code, which is temporally concatenated at the beginning of the noised video latent sequence (16 frames at 256×256, 4 fps) before being fed to the 3D-UNet. The text prompt is processed by CLIP ViT-L/14 to produce conditioning features. The output is a 16-frame video clip that faithfully preserves the first frame's content while exhibiting temporally coherent motion aligned with the text description.

## Method

TRIP executes a residual-like dual-path noise prediction scheme. The shortcut path computes an image noise prior ε_t^{i→1} = (z_t^i − √(ᾱ_t)·z_0^1) / √(1−ᾱ_t) via a one-step backward diffusion, which serves as a reference noise encoding the correlation between the first frame and each noised frame. The residual path fine-tunes a 3D-UNet (initialized from Stable Diffusion v2.1) to predict the residual noise Δε̃_t^i by processing the temporally concatenated image and video latent codes. A Transformer-based Temporal Noise Fusion (TNF) module then dynamically merges the reference and residual noises through adaptive layer normalization, self-attention, and cross-attention (with the residual noise as query and the concatenated [reference, residual] as key/value), eliminating the need for hand-tuned linear fusion weights. Both the 3D-UNet and TNF module are jointly trained with standard MSE loss under a linear noise schedule (T=1000 steps), and inference uses DDIM with 50 steps.

## Results

On WebVid-10M, TRIP achieves F-Consistency₄ of 95.36% and F-Consistency_all of 96.41%, substantially outperforming T2V-Zero (91.59%/92.15%) and VideoComposer (88.78%/92.52%), while reducing FVD from 279/231 to 38.9. On DTDB (zero-shot), TRIP attains FID 24.8 and FVD 433.9, beating cINN (FID 31.9, FVD 451.6) and AL (FID 65.1, FVD 934.2). On MSR-VTT (zero-shot), TRIP achieves FID 9.68 and FVD 91.3, surpassing the best T2V baseline ModelScopeT2V (FID 11.09, FVD 550) and VideoComposer I2V (FID 31.29, FVD 208).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIP outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. On WebVid-10M, TRIP beats T2V-Zero by 3.77 points in F-Consistency₄ and reduces FVD by 7.2× (38.9 vs. 279), while surpassing VideoComposer by 6.58 points in F-Consistency₄ and 5.9× in FVD. On DTDB, TRIP (zero-shot) improves FID over cINN from 31.9 to 24.8 and FVD from 451.6 to 433.9, and dominates AL by a wide margin (FID 24.8 vs. 65.1). On MSR-VTT, TRIP achieves FID 9.68, beating the strongest T2V baseline ModelScopeT2V (11.09) and CogVideo (23.59), while its FVD of 91.3 is 6× lower than VideoComposer I2V (208) and 14× lower than CogVideo (1294). Human evaluation further confirms TRIP's superiority, with evaluators preferring it over T2V-Zero 96.9% of the time on temporal coherence and over VideoComposer 84.4% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIP builds directly on the ModelScope T2V 3D-UNet backbone [59], fine-tuning it for image-to-video generation. It departs from prior I2V approaches like VideoComposer [60] and T2V-Zero [32], which independently predict the denoising noise for each frame and rely solely on temporal modules to propagate first-frame information. The key conceptual departure is reframing noise prediction as temporal residual learning: a shortcut path derives an 'image noise prior' via one-step backward diffusion between the conditioning image and noised frames, serving as a reference noise, while a residual path learns the deviation from this reference. This borrows the residual learning principle from deep networks [20, 29] and applies it to the diffusion denoising objective, with a Transformer-based Temporal Noise Fusion module replacing the handcrafted linear combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly encoding the first-frame-to-subsequent-frame relationship as a noise prior substantially improves temporal coherence (F-Consistency) and video quality (FVD) in I2V generation, with a particularly large FVD gap (38.9 vs. 231 for VideoComposer) on WebVid-10M. The zero-shot generalization to DTDB and MSR-VTT suggests the residual noise formulation is not overfit to the training distribution. However, the evaluation is limited to 16-frame clips at 256×256 and 4 fps, leaving open whether the image noise prior remains effective for longer, higher-resolution videos. A broader open question is whether this residual noise decomposition generalizes to other conditional generation settings (e.g., multi-image conditioning, video editing) beyond single-image I2V.
