## Problem

Existing Video Individual Counting (VIC) methods require per-target trajectory labels to supervise identity association across frames, making annotation prohibitively expensive and limiting dataset scale. The paper identifies that exact individual-level matching is unnecessary for counting: as long as the model can distinguish which group of people is new (inflow) versus already present, the total unique count can be correctly inferred. This motivates a weakly supervised VIC task using only identity-agnostic inflow/outflow labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames sampled at a fixed temporal interval δ (default 3 s), with per-person inflow/outflow binary labels available only during training. A crowd locator (FIDT) predicts pedestrian coordinates in each frame; patches of size 96×64 (or bounding boxes) are cropped and resized to 224×224 for the encoder. The output is a single scalar: the total number of unique individuals in the video, computed as the initial-frame count plus the cumulative per-frame inflow counts predicted by the memory-based predictor.

## Method

CGNet comprises three components: a frame-level crowd locator, a ConvNext-S encoder producing per-individual features, and a Memory-based Count Predictor (MCP) that stores recent individual templates with a time-to-live mechanism and uses the Hungarian algorithm for one-to-one matching at inference. The core training signal is a Group-level Matching Loss (GML) with two terms: (1) a soft contrastive loss L_scon formulated as a balanced Optimal Transport problem (solved via Sinkhorn) over a latent matching-probability matrix Ω, which pulls the shared-individual group (X, Y) toward a permutation-matrix similarity while pushing cross-group similarities (S₁, S₂) toward zero; and (2) a hinge L1 loss L_hinge that softly constrains the outflow–inflow similarity block S₃ below a threshold θ to avoid feature collapse. The key insight is that group-level matching—knowing which subset is 'new' without resolving exact pairwise identities—suffices for counting, and the OT-based soft contrastive loss provides an end-to-end differentiable way to enforce this group structure without trajectory annotations.

## Results

On SenseCrowd (634 videos), CGNet achieves MAE 8.86, MSE 17.69, and WRAE 12.6%, outperforming the only supervised VIC method DRNet (MAE 12.3, MSE 24.7, WRAE 12.7%) by ~28% in MAE. On CroHD, CGNet reduces MAE from 141.1 (DRNet) to 75.0, a 46% improvement. On the new UAVVIC dataset (4 object categories), CGNet achieves MAE 12.9 vs. DRNet's 18.4, a ~30% improvement, while all MOT baselines remain far behind (best MOT MAE: 37.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGNet (weakly supervised) outperforms DRNet (the only supervised VIC method requiring trajectory labels) on all three datasets: MAE 8.86 vs. 12.3 on SenseCrowd (28% lower), 75.0 vs. 141.1 on CroHD (46% lower), and 12.9 vs. 18.4 on UAVVIC (30% lower). Among MOT baselines, BoT-SORT is the strongest (MAE 24.67 on SenseCrowd, 154.8 on CroHD), yet CGNet still surpasses it by a wide margin (2.8× and 2.1× lower MAE respectively). The LOI crowd-counting method (MAE 24.7 on SenseCrowd, 305.0 on CroHD) is also substantially outperformed. On UAVVIC, where no trajectory annotations exist, CGNet beats Deep-OC-SORT (MAE 37.1) and BoT-SORT (MAE 49.9) by large margins across all four object categories (pedestrian, car, bus, van). Notably, CGNet's advantage over DRNet is largest in high-density scenes (D3, D4 on SenseCrowd: MAE 25.0 vs. 50.0 and 63.4 vs. 77.0), suggesting the group-level matching loss is particularly effective when individual-level identity association is most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reformulating Video Individual Counting (VIC) under a weaker supervision regime. The only prior VIC method, DRNet (Han et al., CVPR 2022), requires expensive per-individual trajectory labels to supervise identity association across frames. CGNet departs from this by replacing individual-level matching with group-level matching, repurposing optimal transport (Sinkhorn) and soft contrastive learning to constrain only the collective similarity structure between shared, inflow, and outflow groups. It also borrows memory-based template tracking ideas from the MOT literature (Hungarian assignment, time-to-live updates) but applies them to counting rather than trajectory prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is a ~70% reduction in annotation time, which could enable VIC datasets an order of magnitude larger than the current 4–634 video collections, potentially unlocking larger-scale deep learning for this task. The finding that a weakly supervised baseline outperforms the fully supervised DRNet by 28–46% on MAE suggests that individual-level identity associations were over-specified for the counting objective, opening the door to simpler annotation pipelines. Open questions include whether the group-level matching formulation scales to very high-density crowds (D4 performance still lags), whether it generalizes beyond pedestrians to arbitrary object categories in uncontrolled settings, and whether the UAVVIC moving-camera setting introduces biases not present in static-camera benchmarks.
