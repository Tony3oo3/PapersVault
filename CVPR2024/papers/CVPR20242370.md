## Problem

Existing vision-language benchmarks for autonomous driving are either secondary annotations on limited datasets like nuScenes or lack critical modalities such as 3D LiDAR point clouds and HD map text, making them insufficient for evaluating multi-modal LLMs on real-world traffic and map understanding. This paper introduces MAPLM, a large-scale real-world dataset with 2M frames of panoramic images, LiDAR point clouds, and HD map annotations, along with a 14K-frame QA benchmark (MAPLM-QA) to enable instruction-tuning and evaluation of domain-specific vision-language models.

## Input / Output

The model ingests six panoramic 2D camera images, a BEV-projected LiDAR point cloud rendered as a grayscale image (pixel intensity encodes reflection strength, rotated to align with vehicle heading), structured HD map text context (eight categories: front/rear lane markings, stop lines, road signs, cross zones, intersection zones, lane change zones), and a natural-language question. Outputs are free-form text answers spanning five question types: scene classification (SCN), point cloud quality (QLT), lane counting (LAN), intersection detection (INT), and lane attribute description (DES), with DES requiring both a lane count and per-lane attribute labels from left to right.

## Method

The baseline follows a LLaVA-style dual-encoder architecture: a CLIP ViT-L/14-336 visual encoder processes concatenated panoramic image tokens, while a second CLIP encoder processes the BEV point cloud image, with separate trainable projection (adaptor) layers mapping each modality's features into the LLM's embedding space. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the BEV CLIP encoder is pretrained on 510K image–HD-map-text pairs (filtered from 2M frames) to learn traffic-scene-specific visual representations; second, both CLIP encoders are frozen and only the adaptor layers plus LoRA adapters (rank 128) on the LLM (LLaMA-2-7B or Vicuna-7B) are fine-tuned on MAPLM-QA instruction data. The key design choice is using HD map annotations as dense text supervision for visual encoder pretraining, enabling the model to ground visual features in structured map semantics before instruction tuning. Evaluation uses both open-ended metrics (rule-based lane counting, per-lane description accuracy) and fine-grained classification accuracy, aggregated into frame-level (FRM) and question-level (QNS) overall scores.

## Results

On the MAPLM-QA benchmark (1,500 test frames), the best model (Baseline with IT+LoRA, Vicuna-7B backbone) achieves 85.52% question-level accuracy (QNS) and 57.99% frame-level accuracy (FRM), with per-question scores of 78.53% (LAN), 70.60% (DES), 83.20% (INT), 84.33% (QLT), and 96.00% (SCN). GPT-4V in 0-shot and 5-shot settings achieves only 57.81% and 60.94% QNS respectively, with particularly weak lane counting (56.25% and 58.32% LAN). The LLaVA-based instruction-tuned model with Vicuna-7B reaches 82.72% QNS, still trailing the proposed baseline by 2.8 points. No method exceeds 60% FRM, indicating that answering all questions correctly for a single frame remains challenging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed baseline (IT+LoRA, Vicuna-7B) outperforms GPT-4V (0-shot) by a wide margin on every metric: +22.3 pts on LAN (78.53 vs 56.25), +20.7 pts on INT (83.20 vs 62.53), +40.6 pts on QLT (84.33 vs 43.75), and +27.7 pts on QNS (85.52 vs 57.81). Against GPT-4V 5-shot, the gap narrows on INT (83.20 vs 74.33) but remains substantial on LAN (+20.2 pts) and QNS (+24.6 pts), confirming that in-domain instruction tuning is critical for lane counting and fine-grained map reasoning. Compared to LLaVA with the same Vicuna-7B backbone and IT+LoRA training, the MAPLM baseline gains +3.1 pts on LAN, +5.7 pts on DES, +5.7 pts on INT, and +2.8 pts on QNS, demonstrating the benefit of the dedicated BEV point-cloud encoder and HD map context. The LLaMA-2-7B backbone consistently underperforms Vicuna-7B across all configurations (e.g., 81.65% vs 85.52% QNS for IT+LoRA), highlighting the sensitivity of initial LLM weights to traffic-domain instruction tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA/InstructBLIP instruction-tuning paradigm (CLIP visual encoder + adaptor + LLM) to the autonomous driving and HD map domain, introducing HD map text annotations as a third input modality alongside panoramic 2D images and LiDAR BEV projections. It departs from prior driving VQA datasets (nuScenes-QA, DriveLM, Talk2Car) that are either re-annotations of the nuScenes benchmark or lack HD map context entirely, by collecting 2M real-world frames with full HD map production annotations. The two-stage training strategy (pretrain CLIP on BEV images, then finetune adaptors with LoRA) follows the InstructBLIP recipe, while the multi-view panoramic token concatenation mirrors LLaVA's approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAPLM fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale, real-world benchmark that jointly covers panoramic images, LiDAR point clouds, and HD map text for traffic scene VQA, enabling evaluation of multi-modal alignment that prior nuScenes-based datasets cannot support. The finding that GPT-4V struggles with lane counting (56–58% LAN) in this out-of-domain setting, while a 7B instruction-tuned model reaches 78%, highlights that domain-specific data remains essential even for frontier VLMs. Key open questions include whether RLHF or MoE architectures can close the remaining FRM gap (all methods stay below 60%), and whether the benchmark generalizes to weather-variant and geographically diverse scenarios beyond the current Chinese-road collection.
