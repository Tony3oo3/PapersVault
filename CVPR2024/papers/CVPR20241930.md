## Problem

Model probing methods for transfer learning underperform state-of-the-art parameter-efficient tuning methods, particularly when the target domain is dissimilar to the source domain. However, simply adding more features or non-linear transformations to the probing model improves difficult cases while degrading easy cases, creating a conflicting behavior that no existing probing framework resolves.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and extracts structured features from every internal position of a frozen ViT-B/16 (pre-trained on ImageNet-21k): tokenized inputs, pre/post self-attention, and post-FFN representations for each transformer layer, plus pre-logits. Each set of tokens is reduced via 1D average pooling along both the token and channel dimensions to produce two compact vectors per structure, which are concatenated into a single high-dimensional feature vector. The output is a standard class label prediction via a combined linear + MLP head.

## Method

SMP introduces two coupled regularizers that jointly perform feature selection and model complexity control. The structured sparsity regularizer applies group lasso using the pre-trained model's architecture as the grouping prior (each structure's pooled features form one group), so the model automatically selects which layers/positions are informative for a given task. The structured non-linearity regularizer couples a linear classifier with an MLP, constraining the MLP's input-layer norm by the L2 norm of the corresponding linear weights and the MLP's deeper-layer norm by the total linear weight norm; this means easy tasks (few selected structures, small norm) yield a nearly linear model, while difficult tasks (many selected structures, large norm) unlock increasing non-linearity. The loss decouples the two components via a stop-gradient on the linear part, preventing the non-linear branch from interfering with the sparsity signal.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 datasets, 1k training samples per task), SMP achieves a mean accuracy of 70.9%, outperforming the best tuning method VPT (69.4%) by 1.5% and the best probing method Head2Toe (67.5%) by 3.4%. The advantage is most pronounced on the Structured (difficult) group, where SMP reaches 55.4% versus 55.0% for VPT and 47.7% for Head2Toe. SMP also maintains a significant efficiency advantage, requiring only 15% of the adaptation time of fine-tuning and introducing just 0.01G extra inference FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMP outperforms all four compared baselines across the full VTAB-1k benchmark. Against the strongest tuning method VPT [23], SMP gains +1.5% overall (70.9% vs 69.4%), with the gap widening to +0.4% on the difficult Structured group (55.4% vs 55.0%) while matching or exceeding VPT on Natural (+2.2%) and Specialized (+2.4%). Against the strongest probing method Head2Toe [16], SMP improves by +3.4% overall, driven primarily by a +7.7% gain on the Structured group (55.4% vs 47.7%), confirming that the structured non-linearity regularizer is critical for difficult adaptation tasks. Against Bias [6] (TinyTL), SMP leads by +8.9% overall, and against Side-Tuning [45], the margin is +25.3%. Notably, SMP achieves these gains while requiring only 15% of fine-tuning's adaptation time and 0.01G extra inference FLOPs, compared to VPT's 97% adaptation time and 11.01G extra FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model probing line of work (linear probing, Head2Toe, Side-Tuning) by identifying a fundamental tension: complex probing models with multi-layer features and non-linear heads improve difficult-domain adaptation but degrade easy-domain performance due to overfitting. It draws on group lasso (Yuan et al., 2006) for structured sparsity and hierarchical sparse modeling for the non-linearity regularizer, but departs from prior probing methods by using the pre-trained model's architecture as the grouping prior rather than arbitrary feature partitions. The structured non-linearity regularizer, which couples the MLP's complexity to the ℓ₂ norm of selected linear weights, is the key departure from both standard probing and parameter-efficient tuning approaches like VPT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMP closes the long-standing performance gap between probing and tuning methods (70.9% vs 69.4% on VTAB-1k) while retaining the core efficiency advantage of probing (15% of fine-tuning adaptation time vs 97% for VPT), making it practically attractive for resource-constrained deployment. The structured regularization framework is architecture-agnostic and demonstrated on both ViT and ResNet, suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger backbones (ViT-H/14 or beyond), whether the structured prior generalizes to non-transformer architectures with less natural grouping, and whether the method extends to other modalities (audio, video) where the 'structure' of the pre-trained model differs substantially.
