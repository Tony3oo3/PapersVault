## Problem

Two-stage one-shot NAS methods (e.g., SPOS, FairNAS) ensure global order-preserving by uniform supernet training but suffer from poor local order-preserving: the Kendall's tau between supernet evaluation and retrain accuracy for top-10 architectures is only 0.17, meaning the supernet cannot reliably rank similar high-performing architectures. Additionally, pre-trained supernets are not transferable to new datasets, forcing expensive retraining from scratch for each target task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242446/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained single-path supernet (trained via uniform sampling on a source dataset such as ImageNet-1K) and a target dataset, and outputs Pareto-optimal architectures within a FLOPS constraint (≤330M) from a 4^20 discrete search space based on ShuffleNet-V2 (20 choice blocks × 4 operations). For transfer, the feature-extraction encoder is inherited from the source supernet while the final classifier layer is reset and trained from scratch; input images are resized to 224×224 across all datasets to ensure comparability.

## Method

The core mechanism is Supernet Shifting: during evolutionary searching, each sampled architecture triggers both a validation evaluation and a short training step whose gradient is accumulated; after each EA iteration the supernet weights are updated with the accumulated gradient. Because EA preferentially samples architectures similar to current top performers, the supernet is implicitly biased toward superior architectures, improving local order-preserving without the early bias of architecture-parameter methods (DARTS, GDAS). For transfer, the pre-trained supernet's encoder is reused and fine-tuned jointly with a new classifier during the same shifting procedure, converging to near-scratch-training accuracy within ~4 EA iterations. The approach is plug-and-play: it can be appended to any existing two-stage NAS baseline (SPOS, FairNAS, GreedyNAS, AttentiveNAS) with only ~2 additional GPU hours of search time.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed method (Supernet Shifting) achieves 74.28% Top-1 accuracy with only 318M FLOPs, outperforming SPOS (74.01%, 323M) and FairNAS (74.03%, 326M) while using fewer FLOPs. On ImageNet-100, it reaches 85.61% Top-1 at 299M FLOPs, again dominating all baselines in both accuracy and efficiency. For supernet transfer to ImageNet-100, the method achieves 85.83% Top-1 in just 6 GPU hours (vs. 50 GPU hours for training a new supernet with shifting), a ~10× speedup with no accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on both ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-100 in Top-1 accuracy while simultaneously achieving the lowest FLOPs. Against the strongest baseline SPOS, it gains +0.27% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K (74.28 vs. 74.01) and +0.11% on ImageNet-100 (85.61 vs. 85.50), while reducing FLOPs by 5M and 5M respectively. FairNAS is the closest competitor on ImageNet-1K (74.03%) but still trails by 0.25% and uses 8M more FLOPs. OneShot and random selection are clearly weaker, lagging by 0.76% and 0.99% on ImageNet-1K respectively. In the transfer setting (Table 5), the method matches or exceeds the accuracy of training a new supernet from scratch on ImageNet-100 (85.83% vs. 85.61%) while being ~10× faster (6 vs. 50 GPU hours); on CIFAR-100 it is slightly lower (75.38% vs. 75.62%) but still 7× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage one-shot NAS paradigm established by SPOS and FairNAS, which train a single-path supernet via uniform sampling and then apply evolutionary search. It departs from prior work by modifying the *searching* stage rather than the training stage: instead of introducing an architecture parameter (as in DARTS/GDAS) or non-uniform sampling during training (as in AttentiveNAS/GreedyNAS), it fine-tunes the supernet in-place during evolutionary search, leveraging the EA's naturally biased sampling toward superior architectures. The transferability contribution—reusing a pre-trained supernet's feature encoder and only retraining the classifier during search—has no direct precedent in the NAS literature, where prior transfer approaches either search on small datasets and scale up or use meta-learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The supernet shifting mechanism is a simple, plug-and-play addition that improves local order-preserving (Kendall's tau) without degrading global order-preserving, and it can be layered onto existing two-stage NAS methods (SPOS, FairNAS, GreedyNAS, AttentiveNAS) as shown in Table 4. The transferability result—achieving a 10× speedup over training a new supernet while finding a dominating architecture on ImageNet-100—suggests a practical path toward open-source pre-trained supernets that eliminate the most time-consuming step of NAS. Open questions include whether the method's reliance on EA's implicit sampling distribution generalizes to other search algorithms, whether fairness is maintained when comparing architectures on an ever-shifting supernet, and whether the approach scales to more complex search spaces where fine-tuning the full supernet becomes costly.
