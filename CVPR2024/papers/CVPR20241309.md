## Problem

Existing binaural audio generation methods use the entire visual scene as guidance, which introduces interference from silent objects and background, degrading generation fidelity. Meanwhile, visual sounding object localization and binaural audio generation have been treated as independent tasks, despite the fact that localized objects can guide generation and spatial cues in binaural audio can refine localization. This paper is the first to unify both tasks into a single cyclic learning framework where each task improves the other.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 1-second mono audio clip (16 kHz) paired with a positive image frame (448×224 crop) and a negative image frame from a different video, processed in batches of 12. Rather than directly generating two binaural channels, the model predicts a differential audio waveform, from which left and right channels are recovered via simple addition and subtraction with the mono input. At inference, a 0.1-second sliding window is used to produce continuous binaural output. The localization branch simultaneously outputs a heat map over the visual feature map indicating the sounding object region.

## Method

The CLUP framework couples an unsupervised localization model with a diffusion-based upmixing model in a cyclic loop. The localization model uses an Object-Scene Awareness (OSA) module that computes contrastive losses at both object level (via sigmoid-gated feature modulation with audio features from VGGish) and scene level (via global average pooling), trained with ResNet-18 visual features; a Semantic-Spatial Mining (SSM) module then extends this by concatenating left and right channel features to exploit interaural spatial cues. The upmixing model is a conditional diffusion model operating directly on raw waveforms (not spectrograms), where 1D convolutional waveform features are concatenated with repeated visual features and localized object features, fused through a convolutional layer to condition the denoising network. The cyclic mechanism works by feeding generated binaural audio back into the localization model (replacing mono audio), so spatial information from the generated signal further refines object localization, which in turn provides sharper guidance for the next generation pass.

## Results

On the FAIR-Play dataset, the proposed CLUP framework achieves STFT distance of 0.787, ENV distance of 0.128, WAV L2 of 5.244, and SNR of 7.546 dB, outperforming the strongest baseline SAGM (0.851, 0.134, 5.684, 7.044) across all six metrics. On YT-Music, the method similarly leads with STFT 0.856, ENV 0.124, WAV 5.774, and SNR 5.711 dB versus SAGM's 0.875, 0.126, 5.792, and 5.601. The method also achieves the best amplitude (AMP 0.234) and phase (PHA 0.568) fidelity on FAIR-Play, indicating superior spatial cue preservation critical for binaural localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed CLUP framework outperforms all five baseline families on every reported metric for both FAIR-Play and YT-Music. The closest competitor is SAGM [23], a GAN-based cross-modal generative model; CLUP improves STFT by 0.064 (0.851→0.787) and SNR by 0.50 dB (7.044→7.546) on FAIR-Play, and by 0.019 STFT and 0.11 dB SNR on YT-Music. The multi-task methods APNet and Sep-stereo [50] trail by a larger margin (STFT gap of ~0.09–0.10 on FAIR-Play), while the attention-based Main/Complete networks [47] and the autoencoder MONO2BINAURAL [6] are further behind. L2BNet [33] reports only STFT and ENV, where CLUP still leads by 0.241 and 0.020 respectively on FAIR-Play. Notably, CLUP also achieves the best AMP and PHA scores, confirming that the cyclic object-localization signal yields more accurate interaural amplitude and phase cues than scene-level visual guidance alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: visual sounding object localization (Senocak et al., Hu et al., Qian et al.) and vision-guided binaural audio generation (Gao et al. MONO2BINAURAL, Zhou et al. Sep-Stereo, Li et al. SAGM). Prior binaural generation methods use the entire visual scene as a driver, while prior localization methods rely on manual annotations or treat audio-visual alignment in isolation. The key departure is the cyclic learning formulation: the authors are the first to jointly train a localization model and a generation model so that each provides supervisory signal to the other (object-level visual cues guide generation; spatial cues in generated binaural audio refine localization), rather than treating them as independent or sequentially connected tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cyclic framework offers a principled way to avoid expensive binaural recording while still producing spatially faithful audio, and the object-level guidance addresses a real failure mode of scene-level methods (interference from silent objects). However, the ablation (Table 4) reveals that object-only guidance actually underperforms full-scene guidance, suggesting the unsupervised localization is not yet reliable enough to replace holistic visual context; the full method's gains come from fusing both. Open questions include whether supervised or semi-supervised localization would close this gap, whether the approach extends to bounding-box-level localization or multi-frame video, and whether the cyclic benefit persists at scale or with more complex multi-source scenes.
