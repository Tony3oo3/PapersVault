## Problem

Understanding what information large pre-trained vision models encode and where it resides within their layers remains an open challenge, as prior interpretability and segmentation methods require guessing which specific layer or feature set to extract. This paper asks whether a wholistic analysis of the full activation state—spanning all attention layers—can simultaneously reveal both the spatial layout and semantic content of images without any a-priori knowledge of where relevant information is stored, and whether the internal key, query, and value vectors of attention mechanisms naturally decompose into distinct functional pathways.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained vision model (e.g., Stable Diffusion, CLIP, DINO, MAE) and an image or dataset of RGB images as input, extracting query, key, and value vectors from every self-attention head at every layer (and, for diffusion models, at randomly sampled noise timesteps). It produces a dense eigenvector feature map X at the spatial resolution of the finest attention layer with C learnable channels (C=10 for per-image, C=50 for dataset-level analysis), which after orthogonalization and K-Means clustering yields discrete segmentation regions. The same procedure applied to Q-K affinity yields spatial-layout eigenvectors, while V-V affinity yields semantic-identity eigenvectors, enabling both 'where' and 'what' representations from a single optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a gradient-based approximation to multi-scale spectral clustering that couples all attention layers into a single optimization objective. Rather than constructing an intractable block-diagonal affinity matrix across layers, the method defines a set of per-layer affinity matrices (from pre-softmax Q-K or V-V dot products) and optimizes a learnable feature map X to maximize an averaged Rayleigh quotient over these affinities, with a bilinear resampling function g(·) aligning X's spatial resolution to each layer's grid. For dataset-level analysis, the affinity graph is extended with sparsified inter-image connections (top-k intra- and inter-image edges per location), and optimization proceeds in mini-batches via Adam. A final orthogonalization step (eigendecomposition of X^T X) separates distinct features into channels before K-Means decoding. The key empirical finding is that Q-K affinity eigenvectors encode spatial layout (a 'where' pathway) while V-V affinity eigenvectors encode object identity (a 'what' pathway), revealing a clean functional split in how attention layers route spatial versus semantic information.

## Results

On unsupervised semantic segmentation, the V-V graph features achieve 25.4 mIoU on COCO-Stuff-27 and 16.2 mIoU on Cityscapes, approaching STEGO's 26.8 and 18.2 while using a generative model (Stable Diffusion) and a simpler mini-batch strategy. For per-image region extraction on PASCAL VOC, the method achieves 0.82 mIoU with Stable Diffusion, 0.78 with CLIP and DINO, and 0.74 with MAE, consistently outperforming single-layer baselines. On the 'where' pathway, the Q-K graph achieves 19.5 MSE on coordinate regression and 10.1 mIoU on spatial semantic segmentation, substantially outperforming STEGO (42.4 MSE, 6.9 mIoU) and the V-V graph (43.1 MSE, 5.1 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is most directly comparable to STEGO [24], which achieves 26.8 mIoU on COCO-Stuff-27 and 18.2 on Cityscapes; the paper's V-V graph reaches 25.4 and 16.2 respectively, a gap of ~1.4 and ~2.0 mIoU, but STEGO relies on discriminative DINO features and a complex two-stage global nearest-neighbor search, whereas this work uses a generative Stable Diffusion backbone with only mini-batch connections. Against PiCIE [14] (13.8/12.3 mIoU), DSM [47] (8.9 mIoU), ACSeg [39] (16.4 mIoU), and HP [58] (24.6/18.4 mIoU), the method clearly wins on COCO-Stuff-27, though HP edges ahead on Cityscapes (18.4 vs 16.2). On per-image region extraction (Table 1), the method outperforms TokenCut/Ncut [71] and MaskCut [70] across all tested backbones, with the largest gains on CLIP (0.78 vs 0.45–0.57) and MAE (0.74 vs 0.48–0.62) where final-layer features are not discriminative. For the spatial 'where' pathway (Table 5), the Q-K graph decisively outperforms STEGO (19.5 vs 42.4 MSE on coordinate regression; 10.1 vs 6.9 mIoU on spatial semantic segmentation) and DINO [8] (3.2 MSE but only 6.0 mIoU), confirming that Q-K features encode spatial structure that semantic methods discard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral clustering framework (Ng et al., Meila & Shi) from a single affinity matrix to a set of layer-distributed affinity matrices, replacing the intractable block-diagonal eigenproblem of multi-scale spectral clustering (Cour et al.) with a scalable gradient-based Rayleigh quotient optimization. Unlike prior segmentation methods that extract features from a single (typically final) layer of a discriminative backbone (TokenCut, MaskCut, DSM on DINO), this work simultaneously leverages attention statistics across all layers and all heads of a pre-trained model, including generative models like Stable Diffusion. The conceptual departure is the discovery that key-query and value-value affinities encode fundamentally different information—spatial layout versus semantic identity—revealing a 'where'/'what' pathway split within the transformer's internal computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pathway split between Q-K (spatial) and V-V (semantic) representations offers a concrete, testable hypothesis about how transformers organize information flow, potentially guiding architecture design and feature selection for downstream tasks. The method's model-agnosticism—working across Stable Diffusion, DINO, CLIP, and MAE without task-specific tuning—makes it a broadly applicable analysis tool for probing pre-trained models. However, the pathway discovery is demonstrated primarily on Stable Diffusion, and the spatial semantic segmentation benchmark is somewhat artificial (splitting labels left/right), leaving open whether the split generalizes to other architectures or training objectives. The modest gap to STEGO (25.4 vs 26.8 mIoU on COCO-Stuff) also suggests the generative-model advantage has not yet been fully exploited.
