## Problem

Edge detection is inherently subjective—different annotators produce edge maps at varying granularities, from rough object contours to fine texture edges—yet existing deterministic methods fuse multiple annotations into a single ground truth and output only one edge map. Probabilistic methods like UAED and BetaNet can sample multiple predictions, but the resulting diversity is limited and uncontrollable, failing to reflect the full range of human perceptual variation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (321×481 for BSDS500, 512×512 random crops for Multicue) and a continuous scalar edge granularity value α ∈ [0,1] as input. At inference, α is swept from 0 to 1 in fixed intervals t_α (e.g., 0.1 for 11 maps, 0.5 for 3 maps), producing M binary edge maps that progressively increase in detail from simple object boundaries (α=0) to rich texture-level edges (α=1). The granularity value is not a discrete class label but a continuous control parameter that modulates feature responses.

## Method

MuGE builds on the UAED backbone (EfficientNet encoder + two UNet++ decoders with Gaussian-distribution prediction heads) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, an Edge Granularity Network (a VGG16 binary classifier) is trained on the simplest and most complex annotations per image to encode each annotation's granularity as a scalar α ∈ [0,1]; at inference, α is set to arbitrary values to control output detail. Second, the multi-scale feature maps are split along channels into F₁ and F₂: F₁ is multiplied by α in the spatial domain, while F₂ is decomposed via Discrete Fourier Transform into low-frequency (object contours) and high-frequency (texture details) components, with the high-frequency part scaled by α before inverse DFT. Training uses a three-part loss combining the UAED uncertainty-driven edge loss, a Focal Frequency Loss for crisp edge recovery, and a CLIP-based loss that enforces semantic consistency between predicted and ground-truth granularity levels.

## Results

On BSDS500 under the MS-VOC setting (multi-scale test with extra PASCAL VOC training data), MuGE (M=11) achieves ODS=0.861, OIS=0.867, and AP=0.909, setting a new state-of-the-art. On the Multicue dataset, MuGE achieves ODS=0.898, OIS=0.900, AP=0.950 for edge detection and ODS=0.875, OIS=0.879, AP=0.932 for boundary detection, both surpassing the previous best UAED. In the single-scale BSDS500 setting, MuGE (M=11) reaches ODS=0.850, OIS=0.854, AP=0.896, outperforming UAED by 0.9%, 0.9%, and 1.5% respectively. The LPIPS diversity metric confirms MuGE generates substantially more diverse predictions (0.1065 vs. 0.0118 for UAED at M=11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuGE outperforms the closest prior method UAED [65] across all BSDS500 settings: in the single-scale setting, MuGE (M=11) gains 0.9% ODS, 0.9% OIS, and 1.5% AP over UAED; in the MS-VOC setting, the gains are 1.7% ODS, 0.3% OIS, and 0.4% AP. On Multicue, MuGE surpasses UAED by 1.1% ODS, 0.7% OIS, and 0.5% AP on boundary detection, and by 0.3% ODS, 0.2% OIS, and 0.1% AP on edge detection. MuGE also significantly outperforms the previous Transformer-based SOTA EDTER [42] (ODS 0.848→0.861, AP 0.903→0.909 on MS-VOC). Notably, even a single fixed granularity (α=0.5 or 0.6) from MuGE matches or exceeds UAED's single-prediction performance (e.g., ODS 0.831 vs. 0.829 in SS), while the best-matching strategy over M=11 predictions yields further gains. The diversity advantage is quantified by LPIPS: MuGE scores 0.1065 vs. UAED's 0.0118 at M=11, indicating far more distinct and controllable predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MuGE builds directly on the UAED framework (EfficientNet encoder, UNet++ decoders, Gaussian-distribution prediction head) but departs from both deterministic detectors (which output a single edge map) and probabilistic methods like UAED and BetaNet (which sample from learned distributions with limited, uncontrollable diversity). The key conceptual departure is introducing an explicit 'edge granularity' scalar, encoded by a VGG16 binary classifier, and embedding it into multi-scale features in both spatial and frequency (DFT) domains to controllably modulate detail level. This draws on diverse image generation literature (StarGAN, Probabilistic UNet) but replaces generative adversarial or variational machinery with a simpler, interpretable frequency-domain modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuGE is the first edge detector to produce a controllable spectrum of edge maps from coarse object contours to fine texture edges via a single scalar, which is practically useful for downstream tasks with varying detail requirements. The authors acknowledge that encoding granularity from pixel counts in annotations is not the only (or necessarily best) control mechanism, and suggest text prompts or other schemes as future directions. An open question is whether the DFT-based high-frequency modulation generalizes beyond the specific EfficientNet/UNet++ architecture, and whether the modest SOTA gains (≈0.9% ODS over UAED) scale to larger or more diverse benchmarks.
