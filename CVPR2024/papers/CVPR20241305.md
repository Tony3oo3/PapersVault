## Problem

In online blurry class incremental learning (OBCIL), all classes are shared across tasks but their data volumes shift dramatically, creating two under-addressed challenges: (1) data imbalance within tasks that worsens during buffer replay, and (2) varying class-wise data volume where previously major classes shrink while future major classes grow from a small, biased initial exposure. Existing coreset selection methods compute scores within mini-batches or the full current task, making them skewed by class imbalance and unable to provide reliable, class-specific guidance for these shifting distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of labeled RGB images (each sample seen only once) alongside a fixed-size memory buffer (500–20,000 samples depending on dataset). At each step, the incoming sample's class is identified via the balanced memory, and the buffer is updated in real time by replacing the lowest-scoring sample in the target class. The model is trained in two stages per task: first on the current stream plus buffer, then retrained solely on the updated buffer, producing final class predictions and a class-wise balanced coreset for future replay.

## Method

DECO builds on a real-time class-wise balanced memory (CBM) that maintains equal per-class buffer occupancy by swapping samples from over-represented classes into under-represented ones. On top of CBM, a class-wise gradient-based scoring system computes three scores—similarity (CW_S), diversity (CW_V), and affinity (CW_A)—within each class's buffer subset rather than across the whole batch, eliminating cross-class score skew. A Diverse Score Guidance (DSG) strategy then assigns different score combinations per class type: past-major classes use all three scores, current-major classes combine class-wise reservoir sampling with affinity, and future-major classes rely solely on affinity from well-established classes to avoid unreliable intra-class guidance. Finally, an Adaptive Score Constraint (ASC) splits the FC-layer gradient vector into well-exposed and less-exposed class components, computing affinity only on the well-exposed part to relax constraints and preserve plasticity for under-represented classes while still consolidating knowledge in established ones.

## Results

Under the Blurry10 setting, DECO achieves the best Final Average Accuracy (FAA) and Final Forgetting (FF) across all four datasets. On CIFAR-10, DECO reaches 60.03% FAA with a negative FF of −1.96, outperforming the strongest fair competitor DVC (56.95% FAA, 11.20 FF) by 3.08% in accuracy. On ImageNet, DECO attains 42.58% FAA and 1.03 FF, surpassing ER-ACE (41.13% FAA, 12.80 FF) by 1.45% in accuracy. Notably, DECO even outperforms RM† (which reuses all stream data for buffer selection) on every dataset, e.g., 90.89 vs. 90.24 FAA on MNIST and 42.58 vs. 41.29 FAA on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECO outperforms all 10 baselines on both FAA and FF across all four datasets. Against the strongest fair (no stream-data-reuse) competitor DVC, DECO gains +3.08% FAA on CIFAR-10 (60.03 vs. 56.95) and +1.47% on ImageNet (42.58 vs. 41.11), while reducing FF from 11.20 to −1.96 on CIFAR-10. Compared to the gradient-based coreset selection method OCS, DECO improves FAA by +1.77% on CIFAR-10 and +3.59% on ImageNet, with FF dropping from 13.15 to −1.96 on CIFAR-10. Against the stream-data-reusing RM†, DECO still leads on every dataset (e.g., +0.65% FAA on MNIST, +1.89% on CIFAR-10, +1.29% on ImageNet), confirming that the class-wise collaboration strategies provide gains beyond what exhaustive buffer re-selection can achieve. On CIFAR-100, where the gap between methods is smaller, DECO still leads by +0.95% FAA over DVC and +1.04% over RM†. The negative FF on CIFAR-10 (−1.96) indicates that DECO's model actually improves on previously learned major classes over time, a property no other baseline achieves except RM† (−0.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

DECO extends the gradient-based coreset selection framework of OCS (Yoon et al., 2021) by reformulating its similarity, diversity, and affinity scores into a class-wise scoring system, addressing the bias introduced by class imbalances in the OBCIL setting. It adapts the reservoir sampling mechanism from ER (Chaudhry et al., 2019) into a class-wise variant (CW_RSV) and adopts the two-stage training paradigm from Gdumb (Prabhu et al., 2020) and the OBCIL formulation from Bang et al. (2021). The key conceptual departure from prior rehearsal-based methods is the class-wise collaboration principle: using well-established classes to provide reliable score guidance for less-exposed classes, rather than treating all classes symmetrically in buffer management.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DECO provides a practical, fully online solution to the OBCIL setting without requiring stream data reuse (unlike RM), making it applicable to truly single-pass scenarios. The negative final forgetting on CIFAR10 and the consistent outperformance of RM (which has access to all stream data) suggest that intelligent class-aware buffer management can compensate for limited data access. Open questions include whether the class-wise collaboration principle generalizes beyond rehearsal-based methods to regularization-based or architecture-based approaches, and whether the OBCIL setting's assumption of known class sharing across tasks holds in truly open-world deployments.
