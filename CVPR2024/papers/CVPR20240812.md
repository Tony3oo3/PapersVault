## Problem

Softassign-based graph matching algorithms rely on an inflation parameter β that controls the trade-off between accuracy and computational cost, yet existing methods tune it empirically without theoretical guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by deriving an automatic, error-bounded strategy for selecting β that adapts to the problem structure rather than requiring manual tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts two graphs with edge attribute matrices (A, Ã) and node attribute matrices (F, F̃), where graphs may be unweighted (PPI, social networks) or weighted with attributed nodes extracted from RGB images via SIFT keypoints. It produces a discrete n×n permutation matrix M representing the optimal node correspondence, obtained by solving a quadratic assignment problem relaxed onto the doubly stochastic polytope and discretized via the Hungarian method. For graphs with unequal node counts, a square slack matrix is introduced to handle the size mismatch.

## Method

The core contribution is an adaptive softassign that automatically selects β by monitoring the convergence criterion ‖S_X^β − S_X^{β+Δβ}‖ ≤ ε, justified by exponential decay bounds (Propositions 1–2) linking the gap to the optimal assignment score. To make this efficient, the paper introduces Hadamard-Equipped Sinkhorn formulas (Lemmas 1–3) that enable a softassign transition: computing S_X^{β+Δβ} from the already-computed S_X^β via a Hadamard power followed by Sinkhorn normalization, rather than re-exponentiating the full matrix. This transition yields a 6.7× speedup and avoids numerical overflow/underflow by decomposing large β into incremental steps. The full ASM algorithm embeds adaptive softassign within a projected fixed-point iteration, reusing β_ε^k − Δβ as the starting point for the next iteration to minimize redundant computation, achieving O(n³) per-iteration complexity with O(n²) space.

## Results

On the Yeast PPI network (1,004 nodes), ASM achieves 75.1% node accuracy at 25% noise, outperforming the next-best method GWL (57.6%) by 17.5 percentage points while running ~10x faster (22.6s vs. 246.5s). On the Facebook social network (4,039 nodes), ASM reaches 85.7% at 25% noise versus DSPFP's 62.2% and GWL's 60.8%, with a runtime of ~393s compared to GWL's ~4,454s. On real-image attributed graph matching (1,000 nodes), ASM attains the lowest matching error across all four image sets (e.g., 2.3×10⁴ on Leuven vs. 2.7×10⁴ for SCG) with comparable runtime (~3–5s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASM dominates all eight baselines on both network benchmarks. Against the strongest prior method GWL [42], ASM gains 17.5 pp at 25% noise on PPI (75.1% vs. 57.6%) and 24.9 pp on Facebook (85.7% vs. 60.8%), while being roughly 10x faster. Compared to the authors' own prior work SCG [32], ASM improves accuracy by 32 pp at 25% noise on PPI (75.1% vs. 43.0%) and 42.6 pp on Facebook (85.7% vs. 43.1%), at a modest runtime increase (~2x). DSPFP [23] is the fastest baseline on PPI (~10s) but trails ASM by 32 pp at 25% noise; on Facebook, DSPFP is closer (62.2% vs. 85.7%) yet still 23.5 pp behind. GA [8] and GRASP [11] perform poorly under high noise (below 10% at 25% noise on PPI), while AIPFP [19] and MAGNA++ [36] are both slower and less accurate than ASM across all settings. On real-image matching, ASM achieves the lowest error in all four image sets, beating SCG [32] by 0.3–0.5×10⁴ in error with comparable runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the softassign-based graph matching lineage initiated by Kosowsky and Yuille (1994) and the projected fixed-point framework of Lu et al. (2016), with direct methodological dependence on Shen et al. (2022) for adaptive step-size strategies and error-bound analysis. The key departure is the introduction of Hadamard-Equipped Sinkhorn formulas (Proposition 3 and Lemmas 1–3), which recast Sinkhorn normalization as a Hadamard-product operation and enable a recursive 'softassign transition' that computes S_X^{β+Δβ} from S_X^β without re-exponentiating the full matrix. This algebraic reformulation also bridges softassign to the regularized optimal transport framework of Cuturi (2013), where the same balancing structure appears with unequal marginals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive softassign removes the most tedious practical bottleneck in softassign-based matching—empirical β tuning—by guaranteeing a user-specified error bound, and the resulting ASM algorithm achieves roughly 20% higher node accuracy than the best prior method (GWL) at 25% noise while remaining ~10× faster. The Hadamard-Equipped Sinkhorn formulas are potentially reusable beyond graph matching, offering new algebraic tools for Sinkhorn-based optimal transport computations. However, the authors note that ASM's efficiency in plain (unattributed) graph matching is not top-tier, and the O(n³) per-iteration cost limits scalability; the paper also flags the group-like properties of the Hadamard-Equipped Sinkhorn as an open theoretical direction.
