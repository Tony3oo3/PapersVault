## Problem

In few-shot segmentation, support prototypes extracted from foreground regions are assumed to align with query foreground features, but different background contexts between support and query images cause CNN/transformer features to incorporate background information differently, leading to foreground feature misalignment. This background context bias degrades segmentation quality when support and query images share different environments, yet it has not been explicitly identified or addressed in prior FSS work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241024/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a support image with its binary ground-truth mask (1-shot or 5-shot) and a query RGB image, both processed through a shared ResNet backbone (ResNet-50 or ResNet-101) with correlation modules. Input images are randomly scaled, horizontally flipped, and center-cropped to 473×473 (PASCAL-5^i) or 641×641 (COCO-20^i). The output is a per-pixel binary segmentation mask for the query image, produced by the Query Prediction step in the final iteration of the T=3 iterative loop.

## Method

The core architecture is an iterative loop of T=3 steps: (1) Query Prediction segments the query via channel-wise concatenation of the averaged support foreground feature with query backbone features through 1×1 convolutions; (2) Support Modulation extracts background context by computing an evolution feature E that models how query foreground features change from the context-independent input layer to the context-influenced output layer, combining a pixel-wise component (E^p, from concatenated input/output features through an MLP) and a structure-wise component (E^s, from cosine-similarity affinity maps converted to fixed-size histograms), then uses cross-attention to modulate the support feature with this context; (3) Information Cleansing identifies noise introduced by the coarse QP prediction via a confidence-biased attention that adds the entropy-confidence variance between two predictions to the softmax matrix, then subtracts the captured noise from the modulated feature. The iterative structure creates a recurrent optimization where each step's output refines the next iteration's input, progressively reducing feature misalignment and noise accumulation.

## Results

On PASCAL-5^i with ResNet101, ABCB achieves 72.0% and 74.9% mean mIoU in the 1-shot and 5-shot settings, outperforming the second-best method (MSI) by 1.9% and 2.7% respectively. With ResNet50, it reaches 70.6% (1-shot) and 73.6% (5-shot), surpassing HDMNet (69.4% / 71.8%). On the more challenging COCO-20^i dataset, ABCB with ResNet101 attains 51.5% and 58.8% mean mIoU for 1-shot and 5-shot, beating the runner-up by 1.7% and 1.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABCB outperforms all 13 compared baselines on both PASCAL-5^i and COCO-20^i across both 1-shot and 5-shot settings with both ResNet50 and ResNet101 backbones. On PASCAL-5^i (ResNet101), it beats the closest competitor MSI [27] by 1.9% (1-shot: 72.0 vs 70.1) and 2.7% (5-shot: 74.9 vs 72.2), and surpasses SCCAN [40] by 3.7% and 3.4% respectively. On COCO-20^i (ResNet101), the margins are 1.7% (1-shot: 51.5 vs 49.8 MSI) and 1.8% (5-shot: 58.8 vs 57.0 SCCAN). The advantage is particularly pronounced on COCO-20^i, which has more complex backgrounds, suggesting the background context modulation is most beneficial in challenging scenarios. Methods that also leverage background information (SSP [8], BAM [16], SCCAN [40]) are consistently outperformed, confirming that explicitly addressing background context bias yields gains beyond simply incorporating background features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the standard few-shot segmentation paradigm of extracting support foreground prototypes to guide query segmentation, but identifies a previously underexplored failure mode: background context bias, where differing backgrounds between support and query images cause foreground feature misalignment due to the large receptive fields of CNN/Transformer backbones. While prior FSS methods (e.g., NTRENet, BAM, SSP) incorporate background information, they do so to suppress background regions or segment interfering objects rather than to correct the background-induced distortion of foreground features. The iterative modulation framework repurposes cross-attention (common in transformer-based FSS) in a novel three-step loop, and the confidence-biased attention for noise removal is adapted from semi-supervised learning [38] but tailored to the FSS setting. The evolution feature concept—modeling input-to-output feature changes to capture background influence—is a new mechanism not directly borrowed from a specific prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly identifying and addressing background context bias, this work provides a clearer diagnostic for why support prototypes fail in cross-background scenarios, which is a common practical setting in few-shot deployment. The iterative modulation framework achieves SOTA on both PASCAL-5i and COCO-20i with modest additional compute (~22% more MACs), making it a practical improvement for practitioners. Open questions include whether the evolution feature and confidence-biased attention components generalize to other few-shot tasks (e.g., classification, detection), whether the fixed 3-iteration schedule is optimal across all domains, and whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or zero-shot segmentation settings where support images may be even more diverse.
