## Problem

Dual-encoder models enable efficient image-text retrieval but suffer from limited accuracy due to lack of cross-modal interaction, while cross-encoders achieve higher accuracy at the cost of requiring pairwise scoring over all candidates. Existing distillation approaches (attention distillation, KL-divergence logit distillation) fail to transfer knowledge effectively because the two architectures produce fundamentally different similarity score distributions—cross-encoder scores concentrate near 0/1 while dual-encoder scores are approximately normal—making direct score-matching infeasible.

## Input / Output

The dual-encoder student takes an RGB image (256×256 for pre-training, 384×384 for fine-tuning) and a text caption, producing a single image embedding via ViT-B/16 and a single text embedding via BERT-base, whose dot product yields the similarity score. The frozen cross-encoder teacher (ALBEF or BLIP) consumes the same image-text pair jointly and outputs a matching probability in [0,1]. During training, the dual-encoder maintains momentum queues of size 57,856 for negative sampling, and the cross-encoder uses a separate queue of size 16,384 to store inputs for online scoring of hard negatives.

## Method

The paper proposes Contrastive Partial Ranking Distillation (CPRD), built on three empirical findings: (1) ranking-based distillation is robust to score-distribution mismatch unlike KL-divergence logit distillation; (2) only the relative order among top-K hard negatives (K=16) carries valid cross-modal knowledge, as re-ranking beyond top-32 yields negligible gains; (3) the distillation loss must be in contrastive form to avoid disrupting the dual-encoder's InfoNCE alignment learning. Concretely, CPRD mines top-K hard negatives via dual-encoder scores from momentum queues, scores them with the cross-encoder, and constructs a partial ranking target where only negatives with cross-encoder score above a threshold m=0.75 are 'valid.' The loss is an InfoNCE-style contrastive objective that, for each valid negative, pulls its embedding closer to the image than all lower-ranked negatives (both valid and invalid), while imposing no ordering constraint among invalid negatives. The total training loss is the sum of the standard bidirectional InfoNCE alignment loss and the CPRD loss, enabling simultaneous coarse alignment and fine-grained hard-negative ranking.

## Results

On MSCOCO (5K) with fine-tuning, DE+CPRD (5.1M pre-training pairs) achieves R@1 of 69.7 (I2T) and 52.1 (T2I), surpassing the dual-encoder baseline (66.7/48.5) by 3.0% and 3.6% respectively. On Flickr30K (1K), the method reaches R@1 of 90.8 (I2T) and 76.4 (T2I), improving over the baseline by 1.7% and 2.3%. In the zero-shot setting (Table 7), DE+CPRD achieves R@1 of 62.4 (I2T) and 45.1 (T2I) on MSCOCO, outperforming COTS (60.4/43.8) and approaching ALIGN (58.6/45.6) which uses 356× more pre-training data. On the CrissCrossed Caption ranking task, CPRD improves SITS Spearman's R from 61.8 to 64.3 (zero-shot) and to 66.9 (fine-tuned), approaching the cross-encoder's 67.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest dual-encoder baseline COTS (5.3M pairs), DE+CPRD (5.1M) wins on MSCOCO R@1 by 1.8% (I2T: 69.7 vs 66.9) and 1.6% (T2I: 52.1 vs 50.5), and also surpasses COTS trained on 15.3M pairs on MSCOCO I2T R@1 (69.7 vs 69.0). Against other dual-encoder methods (COOKIE, LightningDOT), the improvement is substantial (e.g., +8.0% MSCOCO I2T R@1 over LightningDOT). In the zero-shot setting, DE+CPRD outperforms CLIP (58.4/37.8) and COTS (60.4/43.8) on MSCOCO R@1, and achieves comparable T2I R@1 to ALIGN (45.1 vs 45.6) despite using 356× less pre-training data. Among distillation methods (Table 5), CPRD (R@S=313.2) clearly outperforms KL-divergence distillation (303.3), M3SE (290.4, which actually degrades performance), and R-M3SE (302.3), confirming that contrastive partial ranking is more effective than logit or margin-based distillation. The method achieves performance comparable to cross-encoder methods like ALBEF and VinVL-base while retaining the retrieval efficiency of a dual-encoder (no per-pair computation needed at inference time).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the dual-encoder architecture with momentum queues (following ALBEF [14]) and the broader knowledge distillation literature for image-text retrieval, but departs fundamentally from prior distillation strategies. Where attention distillation [32] requires architectural compatibility and logit distillation [24, 23] enforces score-distribution alignment via KL-divergence or MSE, this work identifies that the concentrated 0/1 similarity distribution of cross-encoders is fundamentally incompatible with the near-normal distribution of contrastively-trained dual-encoders. The key conceptual departure is reframing distillation as partial ranking consistency over valid hard negatives, implemented through InfoNCE contrastive loss rather than regression-based ranking objectives, thereby aligning the distillation signal with the dual-encoder's native training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe for transferring cross-encoder matching knowledge into dual-encoders without requiring attention-map alignment or score-distribution matching, making it broadly applicable to any dual-encoder/cross-encoder pair. The empirical finding that performance saturates when re-ranking only the top-32 candidates (Table 1) gives a clear, actionable principle for where distillation effort should focus. Limitations include the dependency on a pre-trained cross-encoder teacher and the additional (though mitigable via offline pre-computation) cost of cross-encoder scoring. Open questions include whether the partial-ranking principle generalizes to other retrieval modalities (e.g., video-text) and whether the method can be adapted for iterative self-distillation without a separate teacher.
