## Problem

Existing 3D building wireframe reconstruction methods from point clouds rely on intermediate heuristic steps—corner prediction followed by edge classification from combinatorial edge proposals—causing error accumulation and limiting performance. On sparse aerial LiDAR point clouds, these multi-stage pipelines are particularly fragile, with roughly 85% of generated edge proposals being useless. The paper asks whether edges can be regressed directly from point clouds without any intermediate corner or edge-classification stage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse aerial LiDAR roof point cloud (N points, each with 7 features: x/y/z coordinates, RGB, and reflection) plus M=128 initial edge entities (query points sampled via farthest-point sampling, each augmented with an initial direction and length) as input. It outputs a set of parameterized 3D edges, each defined by seven values: a midpoint (regressed as a residual offset from the query point), three projected component lengths along the x/y/z axes, and a three-bit symbolic (quadrant) vector encoding direction. A confidence score per edge is also produced, and a post-hoc DBSCAN step merges nearby endpoints into connected corners for final wireframe assembly.

## Method

PBWR uses a standard transformer encoder-decoder: an MLP input-embedding layer feeds a transformer encoder that produces per-point features, and a transformer decoder takes M query embeddings (from farthest-point-sampled points with initial edge entities) plus encoder features to produce per-edge features. Three dedicated MLPs then regress the parametric edge representation—midpoint residual offset, component lengths, and quadrant class—bypassing any corner-prediction or edge-classification stage. A novel edge-similarity metric combining Hausdorff distance, cosine direction similarity, and length-ratio similarity drives bipartite matching via the Hungarian algorithm, and an edge non-maximum suppression (E-NMS) module removes redundant positive edges. The loss function pairs L1 losses on midpoint and component lengths with cross-entropy losses on confidence (using soft labels derived from edge similarity to handle extreme class imbalance) and quadrant classification, plus a direct edge-similarity loss term.

## Results

On the Building3D Entry-level dataset, PBWR achieves an Edge Recall (ER) of 82% and Edge F1 (EF1) of 86%, improving ER by ~36% over the Building3D-supervised baseline (46%) and reducing ACO from 0.26 m to 0.22 m. On the Tallinn city dataset, PBWR-Tallinn reaches ER of 65% and EF1 of 76%, a ~42% improvement in ER over the Building3D-supervised baseline (23%). Corner Recall also improves substantially, from 66% to 85% (Entry-level) and from 53% to 68% (Tallinn), while Corner Precision reaches 97% and 96% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBWR outperforms all nine baselines on every metric in both datasets. Against the strongest prior wireframe method, Building3D-supervised [34], PBWR improves ER from 46% to 82% (+36 pp) and EF1 from 61% to 86% (+25 pp) on Entry-level, and from 23% to 65% (+42 pp) and 36% to 76% (+40 pp) on Tallinn. Point2Roof [18] achieves ER of only 26% (Entry-level) and 8% (Tallinn), while PC2WF [21] performs worst among dedicated wireframe methods with ER of 14% and 15% respectively, likely due to its reliance on corner prediction from sparse aerial LiDAR. Feature-extractor baselines (PointNet, PointNet++, RandLA-Net, DGCNN, PAConv, Stratified Transformer) all fall well below PBWR, with the best (PointNet++) reaching only 33% ER on Entry-level. The cross-dataset transfer (PBWR-Tallinn evaluated on Entry-level) further demonstrates robustness, achieving the highest scores across all metrics (EF1 = 0.90, ACO = 0.18 m).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant corner-prediction-then-edge-regression pipeline used by PC2WF, Point2Roof, and the Building3D baseline, which generate C(N,2) edge proposals from predicted corners and then classify them. Instead, PBWR directly regresses parameterized edges (midpoint, direction, length) from a standard transformer encoder-decoder without any 3D-specific architectural modifications or intermediate heuristic modules. The bipartite matching strategy also moves beyond simple endpoint-distance matching to a composite edge similarity incorporating Hausdorff distance, cosine similarity, and length ratio, and introduces an edge-specific NMS and loss function not present in prior wireframe reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial performance gains (36–42% in edge accuracy over the Building3D baseline) validate the hypothesis that intermediate corner prediction is a major source of error accumulation in wireframe reconstruction, offering a cleaner end-to-end formulation for the task. However, the generated edges are not topologically connected, requiring a DBSCAN post-processing step that the authors acknowledge as fragile and a target for future work. The approach is currently validated only on building rooftops from sparse aerial LiDAR, leaving open whether the direct parametric edge regression strategy generalizes to denser point clouds, non-Manhattan structures, or other wireframe reconstruction domains.
