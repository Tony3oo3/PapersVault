## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks are limited to assessing single image-text comprehension (capability level L1), failing to evaluate the broader hierarchical capabilities of modern MLLMs that handle interleaved multimodal inputs and generate images. This gap hinders the community's ability to track progress toward general multimodal AI, where models must digest and create content across arbitrary interleaved image-text sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes five input formats: single image + text question, multiple images + text question, video clip + text question, interleaved image-text + text question, and text prompts for image generation. For text-output questions, the model's prediction is determined by an answer-ranking strategy that computes the generation likelihood of each A/B/C/D choice given the question, eliminating dependence on instruction-following or option ordering. For image-output questions, CLIP similarity between the generated image and each candidate image option selects the prediction, and for combined text-image questions, both modalities must match the ground truth for a correct answer.

## Method

SEED-Bench organizes 24,371 human-annotated multiple-choice questions into 27 evaluation dimensions spanning three hierarchical capability levels: L1 (fixed-format image/video + text comprehension), L2 (interleaved image-text comprehension), and L3 (image and text generation). Questions are constructed via three complementary pipelines: (1) an automatic pipeline where foundation models extract visual information (captions, instance descriptions, textual elements) and GPT-4 generates questions with four options, followed by LLM-based filtering to remove questions answerable without visual information; (2) adaptation of existing annotated datasets into multiple-choice format with distractors from other samples or GPT generation; and (3) human-designed questions with GPT assistance for dimensions lacking suitable data. The evaluation strategy replaces GPT-based or human-based scoring with objective multiple-choice accuracy, using answer ranking for text outputs and CLIP similarity for image outputs, enabling reproducible and efficient assessment without external evaluator intervention.

## Results

On SEED-Bench, InternLM-Xcomposer-VL achieves the highest averaged accuracy at capability level L1 (59.2%) and L2 (56.9%), while Emu ranks first at L3 (42.3%) with NExt-GPT as the only other model reaching that level (31.4%). Even the top-ranked model reaches only ~60% accuracy on L1, indicating that existing MLLMs have not yet saturated basic multimodal comprehension. LLaVA-1.5 (47.3% L1, 46.0% L2) and Kosmos-2 (46.3% L1, 44.4% L2) follow as the next strongest performers, while the majority of 22 evaluated models score between 30–47% on L1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternLM-Xcomposer-VL dominates L1 and L2, outperforming the second-best LLaVA-1.5 by 11.9 and 10.9 points respectively, though it does not reach L3 because it retrieves rather than generates images. Among L3-capable models, Emu (42.3%) substantially outperforms NExt-GPT (31.4%) by over 10 points, yet NExt-GPT's L1 score (30.7%) is the lowest among all 22 models, revealing a clear trade-off between generation and comprehension. Most text-only-output models (BLIP-2, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, OpenFlamingo, etc.) cluster in the 37–42% range on L1, with no single model clearly separating from the pack. Notably, models strong on L1 (e.g., InternLM-Xcomposer-VL, LLaVA-1.5) do not necessarily transfer that advantage to L2's interleaved inputs, where IDEFICS-9B-Instruct (40.3% Part-2) and Emu (41.1% Part-2) outperform the L1 leaders on that specific sub-part. The benchmark reveals that no single open-source MLLM excels across all three capability levels simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEED-Bench departs from prior MLLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, LLaVA-Bench, Touchstone) that are confined to single image-text comprehension (what the authors term L1) and rely on GPT or human evaluators for free-form answers. It introduces a hierarchical capability taxonomy (L0–L4) that organizes evaluation by input/output modality, and extends the evaluation scope to interleaved image-text comprehension (L2) and image generation (L3). The answer-ranking evaluation strategy, borrowed from language-model probing, replaces the GPT-based answer matching used by MMBench, and the multiple-choice format with human-verified ground truth eliminates the subjectivity of open-ended scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest open-source MLLM (InternLM-Xcomposer-VL) plateaus at ~60% on basic L1 comprehension, and that interleaved input understanding (L2) is consistently harder than fixed-format comprehension, exposing a training-data mismatch. Only two open-source models (Emu, NExt-GPT) reach L3, and NExt-GPT's poor L1 score (30.7%) highlights the unresolved tension between image generation and comprehension. The L0–L4 pyramid provides a concrete roadmap toward AGI-level multimodality, and the maintained leaderboard positions SEED-Bench as a long-term reference for tracking MLLM progress.
