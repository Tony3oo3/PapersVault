limitations: The method relies on human-annotated attribute vectors and the semantic embedding is only active during training (not inference), limiting applicability to datasets with such annotations and raising questions about whether learned representations fully generalize beyond training-time semantic cues. surprise_factor: The ablation showing that the visual reconstruction loss (L_VR, semantic→visual direction) is far more critical than the semantic reconstruction loss (L_SR, visual→semantic direction) — with a 9.9% harmonic mean drop on CUB when L_VR is removed versus only 0.8% for L_SR — is a somewhat counterintuitive finding, suggesting that pushing semantic information *into* visual tokens matters much more than verifying visual information can be decoded back to semantics. ## Problem

Existing zero-shot learning methods rely on pre-trained CNN or ViT backbones to extract visual features, but these backbones lack semantic guidance during feature extraction and therefore fail to learn matched visual-semantic correspondences. The resulting visual features either capture meaningless background (CNN) or mislocalize semantic attributes (standard ViT), leading to poor visual-semantic interactions and limited knowledge transfer from seen to unseen classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242702/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (processed at ViT-Base resolution) and human-annotated semantic attribute vectors (e.g., 312-dim for CUB, 102-dim for SUN, 85-dim for AWA2) as input during training. At inference, only the RGB image is required; the semantic vectors are used solely for training-time guidance. The output is a predicted class label for unseen classes, with an explicit calibration term (τ=0.4) added to the softmax scores to handle seen-unseen bias in the GZSL setting.

## Method

ZSLViT inserts two novel modules—Semantic-Embedded Token Learning (SET) and Visual Enhancement (ViE)—into specific encoder layers (4th, 7th, 10th) of a ViT-Base backbone, between the multi-head self-attention and feed-forward network layers. SET first performs bidirectional visual-semantic reconstruction via two MLPs (Visual→Semantic and Semantic→Visual) with L1 reconstruction losses, then embeds the reconstructed visual features back into the [cls] token via a weighted combination (γ=0.9); the enhanced [cls] token then serves as a query in a cross-attention against the self-attention outputs (as keys/values) to compute per-token visual-semantic correspondence scores. ViE uses these scores to partition tokens into semantic-related (top-k) and semantic-unrelated (bottom-k) sets, preserving the former and fusing the latter into a single weighted token, progressively purifying the representation across layers. Training combines the SET reconstruction losses (with λ_VR > λ_SR to prioritize semantic injection into visual tokens) with an attribute-based cross-entropy prediction loss, and the model automatically avoids seen-unseen bias without requiring an additional self-calibration loss.

## Results

ZSLViT achieves state-of-the-art results on all three benchmark datasets under both CZSL and GZSL settings. On CUB, it reaches 78.9% CZSL accuracy and 73.6% GZSL harmonic mean; on SUN, 68.3% CZSL accuracy and 47.3% GZSL H; and on AWA2, 70.7% CZSL accuracy and 74.2% GZSL H. The most notable gain is on CUB GZSL, where ZSLViT outperforms the previous best ViT-based method (I2MVFormer) by 30.8% in harmonic mean, and surpasses large-scale vision-language models (CLIP, CoOp) by over 18% in GZSL H on CUB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZSLViT outperforms all 22 baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against the strongest CNN-based methods, it gains +1.1% CZSL acc and +3.2% GZSL H over GEM-ZSL [33] on CUB, and +2.8% CZSL acc over MSDN [8]. Among ViT-based methods, ZSLViT surpasses DUET [13] (the prior best ViT-based ZSL) by +6.6% CZSL acc and +6.1% GZSL H on CUB, and +3.9% CZSL acc on SUN. The most dramatic improvement is over I2MVFormer [34] on CUB GZSL, where ZSLViT achieves 73.6% H versus 42.8% (+30.8%), though on AWA2 GZSL the margin narrows to +0.4% (74.2 vs 73.8). ZSLViT also substantially outperforms large-scale vision-language models: CLIP [40] (55.0% H) and CoOp [57] (55.6% H) on CUB GZSL, with gaps exceeding 18%. On the coarse-grained AWA2 dataset, gains over ViT-based baselines are smaller (e.g., +0.8% CZSL acc over DUET), which the authors attribute to the presence of visually-unrelated semantic attributes (e.g., 'eat fish') that limit the benefit of visual-semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ViT architecture (Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021) but departs from prior ZSL methods that treat the pre-trained backbone as a fixed feature extractor. While CNN-based ZSL methods (AREN, APN, MSDN) apply attention post-hoc to refine already-fixed visual features, and recent ViT-based ZSL methods (DUET, I2DFormer, I2MVFormer) simply extract global features without explicit semantic-visual alignment, ZSLViT inserts semantic-guided operations (semantic-embedded token learning and visual enhancement) progressively into the encoder layers themselves. The key conceptual departure is that semantic information should actively shape feature extraction at multiple depths rather than only informing the final classification head, and that semantic-unrelated tokens should be explicitly discarded rather than implicitly downweighted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that integrating semantic guidance into the feature extraction process (rather than only at the classification stage) yields consistent SOTA improvements across fine-grained and coarse-grained ZSL benchmarks, validating the principle that matched visual-semantic correspondences are essential for effective knowledge transfer. However, the approach is limited to attribute-based ZSL with human-annotated semantic vectors, and the semantic embedding is only active during training, raising questions about whether the learned representations truly generalize or partially memorize training-time semantic cues. Open questions include whether progressive semantic guidance extends to other semantic representations (e.g., free-text descriptions, LLM-generated embeddings), whether the token-fusion strategy in ViE is optimal compared to learned pooling, and whether the approach transfers to related tasks like few-shot or open-set recognition.
