## Problem

Existing unsupervised CAD reconstruction methods are limited to a single type of modeling operation (e.g., extrusion only) or rely on simple volumetric primitives (CSG), which restricts both reconstruction accuracy on complex shapes and the interpretability/editability of the resulting models. No prior unsupervised approach supports the full set of industry-standard sketch-based feature modeling operations (extrude, sweep, loft, revolve) that underpin modern CAD workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240353/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D voxel shape (typically at 16³–64³ resolution) as input and produces a typed sketch+path representation: a set of 2D implicit sketches (SDF-defined profiles) paired with 3D cubic Bézier sweeping paths, parameterized by control points, box dimensions, and twist angles. The output is a parametric CAD model executable via standard CAD commands, enabling users to adjust geometric details (sketches) and structural layout (paths) independently. Evaluation uses Marching Cubes at 256³ resolution with Chamfer Distance, Edge Chamfer Distance, and Normal Consistency metrics.

## Method

SfmCAD employs a two-stage coarse-to-fine strategy that decouples 3D structure from local geometric detail. Stage 1 learns a Box+Path representation: a 3D convolutional encoder produces a feature vector that an MLP maps to Bézier control points and box parameters (length, width, twist angles), approximating curved SDFs via discrete rigid box proxies sampled uniformly along the path. Stage 2 refines this by learning implicit 2D sketches (SDF values predicted by an MLP conditioned on local features and test-point coordinates) that are swept or lofted along the learned paths to recover fine geometric detail. A key efficiency contribution is a realtime sampling strategy that exploits sketch consistency along sweep paths, reducing the per-point computation from N_p × N_s evaluations to a single sketch-plane evaluation per path. The training is fully unsupervised, using MSE loss on occupancy with additional regularization terms for curve smoothness and dimension constraints.

## Results

On the ABC dataset, SfmCAD achieves CD 0.395, ECD 5.038, and NC 0.919, outperforming all baselines across all three metrics. On ShapeNet, SfmCAD reaches CD 0.626 and NC 0.867, again leading all competitors. On the Tree dataset, SfmCAD dramatically reduces CD to 0.686 (vs. 2.197 for SECAD-Net and 7.242 for ExtrudeNet) while achieving the highest NC of 0.722. On PartNet (segmented shapes), SfmCAD attains CD 1.410 and ECD 0.446, improving CD by 25.3% over the closest competitor SECAD-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SfmCAD consistently outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor SECAD-Net [23], SfmCAD improves CD by 22% on ABC (0.395 vs. 0.506), 25% on ShapeNet (0.626 vs. 0.836), and 25.3% on PartNet (1.410 vs. 1.888), while also achieving the best NC on ABC (0.919 vs. 0.884) and ShapeNet (0.867 vs. 0.837). On the Tree dataset, SfmCAD's advantage is most dramatic: CD drops from 2.197 (SECAD-Net) and 7.242 (ExtrudeNet) to 0.686, reflecting the benefit of free-form sweep paths over straight extrusions for cylindrical branch geometry. CSG-based methods (UCSG-Net, CSG-Stump) lag substantially on CD and ECD, confirming that simple volumetric primitives cannot capture the fine details that sketch+path representations handle. The only metric where SfmCAD does not lead is NC on PartNet (0.814 vs. 0.818 for SECAD-Net), a marginal 0.4% difference likely attributable to the reassembly of independently reconstructed parts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SfmCAD extends the sketch+extrude reconstruction line (ExtrudeNet, SECAD-Net, Point2Cyl) by generalizing from a single extrude operation to a unified typed sketch+path framework supporting sweep, loft, and revolve. It departs from CSG-based methods (UCSG-Net, CSG-Stump) by replacing volumetric primitives with parametric 2D sketches swept along 3D Bézier paths, and from generative CAD sequence models (DeepCAD, Fusion360) by focusing on unsupervised reconstruction with geometric losses rather than autoregressive operation prediction. The two-stage Box+Path → implicit sketch learning strategy is a novel training decomposition not present in prior sketch-based reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By supporting multiple CAD operations in a single unsupervised framework, SfmCAD broadens the class of reconstructable shapes beyond what extrude-only methods can handle, as evidenced by the large gap on tree-branch shapes. The decoupling of global structure (Bézier paths) from local detail (implicit 2D sketches) offers a practical editing interface for downstream CAD workflows. Limitations include the restriction of loft to two sketches with a linear path, the need to pre-select the operation type per category, and evaluation primarily on voxelized inputs at moderate resolutions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-operation sequences and whether the two-stage strategy generalizes to fully concurrent operation learning.
