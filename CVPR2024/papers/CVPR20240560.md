## Problem

Motion coherence-based outlier rejection methods face a trade-off: explicit regularization (e.g., LMCNet) is computationally expensive due to graph construction and matrix decomposition, while CNN-based local smoothing (e.g., ConvMatch) over-smooths discontinuities in scenes with large disparities. The paper seeks a formulation that achieves implicit regularization at lower cost while naturally preserving piecewise smoothness across multiple motion flows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240560/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse putative correspondences from a two-view image pair, each represented as a 4D motion vector (source keypoint coordinates plus displacement), which are upgraded to 128-dimensional features via positional embedding. It outputs per-correspondence inlier/outlier logits, inlier probabilities (used for downstream weighted eight-point estimation or RANSAC), and corrected high-dimensional motion vectors. Training is weakly supervised: pseudo-labels are generated by thresholding Sampson distance on the essential matrix, and a regression loss on the Sampson distance of the predicted essential matrix provides geometric supervision.

## Method

DeMatch decomposes the contaminated dense motion field into a finite set of highly smooth sub-fields by treating K learnable motion-pattern tokens as a basis, analogous to retaining only low-frequency terms in a Fourier expansion. The decomposition is implemented as a graph attention network where basis tokens attend to motion vector features (soft clustering via softmax similarity), and a masked decomposition strategy multiplies features by inlier probabilities from the previous layer to suppress known outliers. Global self-attention on the sub-field representations embeds cross-cluster context, and a recovery step attends from motion vectors back to the enhanced sub-fields to produce corrected vectors. The entire decomposition-recovery cycle is stacked L=5 times progressively, with the inlier predictor comparing corrected vs. original vectors to classify matches. This yields implicit regularization (no explicit Laplacian or smoothing filter) and natural piecewise smoothness, since each sub-field corresponds to a unique motion pattern.

## Results

On YFCC100M (outdoor), DeMatch achieves AUC of 30.89°/52.67°/70.33° at 5°/10°/20° rotation thresholds using the weighted eight-point algorithm, outperforming the closest competitor ConvMatch (26.83/49.14/67.91) by 4.1/3.5/2.4 points. On SUN3D (indoor), DeMatch reaches 9.31/23.10/41.55, again leading ConvMatch (8.76/22.23/40.49). For visual localization on Aachen Day-Night, DeMatch attains 85.2/92.8/97.1 (day) and 73.5/84.7/94.9 (night) at (0.25m,2°)/(0.5m,5°)/(5m,10°), surpassing all baselines especially in the challenging night-time setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeMatch outperforms all 11 baselines across both YFCC100M and SUN3D. Against the closest motion-coherence competitor ConvMatch [43], DeMatch gains +4.06 AUC points at 5° on YFCC100M (weighted 8pt) and +0.55 at 5° on SUN3D, with consistent 1–3 point margins at 10° and 20° thresholds. LMCNet [17], the other coherence-based method, trails DeMatch by 8.54 points at 5° on YFCC100M and 2.23 on SUN3D, and is also ~10× slower (227 ms vs. 26 ms). Among directly-classifying methods, NCMNet [16] is the strongest (26.89 at 5° on YFCC100M) yet still lags DeMatch by 4 points; CLNet [44] and MS²DGNet [6] are further behind at 24.34 and 20.61 respectively. Classical methods (GMS, LPM, CRC, VFC) are all surpassed by 13+ points at 5° on YFCC100M. On visual localization (Aachen Day-Night), DeMatch leads ConvMatch by +0.7/+1.0/+3.1 points on night-time metrics, confirming the advantage of decomposition-based piecewise smoothness under large viewpoint changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeMatch sits at the intersection of two lines of work on two-view outlier rejection: classical motion-consensus methods (VFC, CRC, GMS) that enforce smoothness of the displacement field, and recent learning-based approaches that either classify inliers directly (PointCN, OANet, CLNet) or incorporate coherence priors (LMCNet's Laplacian regularization, ConvMatch's CNN smoothing). Rather than applying explicit regularization or local filtering, DeMatch repurposes the Fourier-decomposition idea—projecting a signal onto a finite low-frequency basis—into a learnable attention mechanism over sparse motion vectors, where learnable 'motion pattern' tokens serve as basis vectors. This makes it the first method to frame outlier rejection as a deep decomposition of the motion field into smooth sub-fields, implemented via graph attention networks with a masked strategy to suppress known outliers before decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition-as-implicit-regularization framing elegantly resolves two practical limitations of prior coherence-based methods: LMCNet's heavy matrix-decomposition cost (227 ms vs. DeMatch's 26 ms) and ConvMatch's tendency to over-smooth across motion discontinuities. The natural piecewise-smoothness property and demonstrated compatibility with diverse learned matchers (SuperGlue, LightGlue, LoFTR, DKM) make DeMatch a practical drop-in module. Open questions include whether the fixed number of basis vectors K generalizes across scene complexity, whether the approach extends to multi-view or video settings, and whether the modest gains over ConvMatch (~1–4 AUC points) persist under more challenging conditions such as extreme viewpoint changes or learned descriptors without SIFT.
