## Problem

Existing faithfulness metrics for Vision Transformer explanations (AUC, AOPC, LOdds, Comprehensiveness) rely on cumulative pixel perturbation and ignore the actual magnitudes of salience scores, making them unable to verify whether higher salience truly corresponds to greater predictive influence. As a result, these metrics fail to distinguish state-of-the-art explanation methods from Random Attribution, which assigns purely uniform salience scores with no model information.

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT-B, ViT-L, or DeiT-B), a chosen explanation method, and a single input image, SaCo produces a scalar faithfulness coefficient F in [-1, 1]. The image's HW pixels are ranked by salience and partitioned into K equally-sized subsets (K=5, 10, or 20 in experiments); each subset is individually perturbed by replacing its pixels with the per-sample mean, and the resulting drop in predicted-class probability is recorded. The coefficient's sign indicates whether higher salience correlates with greater or lesser influence, while its absolute value quantifies the strength of that correlation.

## Method

SaCo performs pairwise comparisons among all K pixel subsets, checking for each pair (G_i, G_j) with s(G_i) ≥ s(G_j) whether the higher-salience subset also causes a larger drop in model confidence upon individual perturbation. Each pair's contribution is weighted by the salience difference s(G_i) − s(G_j): positive when the faithfulness inequality holds, negative when violated, inspired by the Kendall τ statistic. The final coefficient is the signed sum of all pair weights normalized by the total absolute weight, yielding a scale-invariant score in [-1, 1]. This design ensures that larger salience gaps carry proportionally more evaluation weight, directly encoding the core assumption that salience magnitude should predict influence magnitude. Ablative experiments further show that incorporating gradient information and cross-layer attention aggregation into attention-based explanations significantly improves SaCo scores (from 0.18 to 0.46), identifying concrete design levers for more faithful Vision Transformer interpretability.

## Results

On ImageNet (averaged over ViT-B, ViT-L, DeiT-B), Transformer-MM achieves the highest SaCo score of 0.6041 (K=5) and 0.4558 (K=10), followed by Transformer Attribution (0.5680 / 0.3902) and Partial LRP (0.4628 / 0.3066). Random Attribution, as theoretically expected, scores near zero under SaCo, while existing metrics (AUC, AOPC, LOdds, Comprehensiveness) fail to distinguish it from state-of-the-art methods. Correlation analysis reveals that SaCo's rankings are nearly uncorrelated with existing metrics (Spearman ρ = 0.18–0.22), whereas those metrics correlate with each other at ρ = 0.4764, confirming SaCo captures a complementary faithfulness dimension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under SaCo, Transformer-MM [11] consistently outperforms all other methods across K values, benefiting from its gradient-informed, cross-layer attention aggregation. Transformer Attribution [12] and Partial LRP [49] rank second and third, while Raw Attention [24] and Rollout [1] (pure attention-based, no gradient) score substantially lower, confirming that auxiliary gradient information is critical for faithfulness. Random Attribution [50] scores near zero as expected, yet existing metrics (AUC, AOPC, LOdds, Comprehensiveness) assign it scores comparable to or even exceeding those of Partial LRP, Transformer Attribution, and ATTCAT, exposing their inability to detect the absence of meaningful signal. Integrated Gradients [46] shows an inconsistency: it ranks best on AOPC, LOdds, and AUC (most-relevant-first removal) but worst on Comprehensiveness (least-relevant-first removal), highlighting the removal-order sensitivity of cumulative-perturbation metrics. LRP [9] and Conservative LRP [4] (attribution-based) perform poorly under SaCo (≤0.05), suggesting that backward propagation without attention-specific design is insufficient for faithful ViT explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation of post-hoc explanation faithfulness by departing from the cumulative perturbation paradigm (AUC, AOPC, LOdds, Comprehensiveness) that dominates the field. It repurposes the perturbation-based evaluation idea but fundamentally restructures it: instead of progressively removing pixels in a fixed order, SaCo individually perturbs each pixel subset and performs salience-weighted pairwise comparisons inspired by the Kendall τ statistic. The faithfulness criterion itself is inherited from Jacovi & Goldberg (2020) and the broader NLP interpretability literature, but the paper is the first to operationalize it for Vision Transformers with explicit attention to the magnitude (not just ordering) of salience scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely adopted metrics (AUC, AOPC, LOdds, Comprehensiveness) score Random Attribution comparably to or even above state-of-the-art methods like Transformer Attribution and ATTCAT is a significant methodological concern for the interpretability community, as it undermines confidence in current evaluation practices. The ablation showing that gradient information and multi-layer aggregation substantially improve attention-based explanation faithfulness provides concrete design guidance for future methods. Open questions include whether SaCo's mean-replacement perturbation strategy is optimal, whether the findings extend to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), and whether the low absolute SaCo scores across all methods indicate a fundamental limitation of post-hoc explanations or simply a need for better methods.
