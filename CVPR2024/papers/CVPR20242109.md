## Problem

Existing unsupervised gaze representation learning methods rely on cropped eye images and degrade severely at large head poses, while face images contain rich confounding information (head pose, identity) that becomes interference without gaze labels to constrain learning. The paper addresses how to disentangle gaze (eyeball rotation) from general facial information in an unsupervised setting using face images, specifically under unconstrained head movements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes synchronized multi-view face images (224×224, histogram-equalized) from multiple cameras capturing the same frame; at inference it takes a single-view face image. The Face Encoder produces a 512×7×7 face representation encoding head pose and appearance, while the Gaze Encoder produces a compact 1×7×7 gaze representation encoding only eyeball rotation in the Head Coordinate System. This gaze representation can be fed to a small MLP head for HCS gaze direction prediction or swapped between identities for gaze redirection.

## Method

The MV-DE framework uses a two-stage training pipeline with a Dual-Encoder architecture (both ResNet-18 backbones). First, the Face Encoder is trained with eye-region masking plus multi-task learning (head pose regression and adversarial image reconstruction) to extract facial representations that explicitly exclude gaze. Then, with the Face Encoder frozen, the Gaze Encoder is trained to compensate for the missing gaze information via image reconstruction, while a multi-view gaze representation swapping strategy enforces that the gaze representation captures only the view-invariant eyeball rotation: gaze vectors from different camera views of the same frame are exchanged and the decoder must still reconstruct the correct eye regions. The final loss combines full-face reconstruction, eye-region reconstruction, and swapped-eye reconstruction terms, ensuring the gaze representation is disentangled from head pose and identity without any gaze label.

## Results

On the few-shot gaze estimation task (200 calibration samples), MV-DE achieves an average gaze estimation error of 7.91° across three datasets, substantially outperforming the best unsupervised baseline Cross-Encoder (11.49°) by 3.58°. The advantage is most pronounced on ETH-XGaze (8.17° vs. 15.16°, a 6.99° gain) and MPII-NV (6.73° vs. 11.53°, a 4.31° gain), where head poses span a wide range. On the frontal-face-only EVE dataset, MV-DE (8.82°) is slightly worse than Cross-Encoder (7.79°) by 1.03°, confirming that the multi-view constraint is most beneficial under large head pose variation. General contrastive learning methods (BYOL: 15.66°, SimCLR: 16.43°) perform far worse, validating that gaze-specific representation learning is necessary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-DE decisively outperforms Cross-Encoder [33] on the two datasets with large head pose variation: 8.17° vs. 15.16° on ETH-XGaze (6.99° improvement) and 6.73° vs. 11.53° on MPII-NV (4.31° improvement), with the gap widening at extreme gaze yaw angles approaching 100°. On the frontal-face-only EVE dataset, Cross-Encoder retains a slight edge (7.79° vs. 8.82°), consistent with the paper's argument that the multi-view swapping constraint is most valuable when head pose is unconstrained. Both general contrastive learning baselines—BYOL [14] (15.66° avg) and SimCLR [4] (16.43° avg)—perform substantially worse than the gaze-specialized methods, confirming that generic visual representations are poorly suited for gaze estimation. The ablation removing the multi-view constraint (DE, 13.79° avg) shows that the swapping strategy contributes a 5.88° improvement over the dual-encoder architecture alone, while the dual-encoder design itself still beats BYOL and SimCLR by roughly 2–3°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of unsupervised gaze representation learning initiated by the Cross-Encoder (Sun et al.) and its multi-view adaptation (Gideon et al.), which operate on cropped eye images and degrade at large head poses. It repurposes the multi-view consistency constraint from supervised multi-view gaze estimation (e.g., DVGaze) into an unsupervised setting, and introduces a dual-encoder architecture (Face Encoder + Gaze Encoder) with a gaze representation swapping strategy to disentangle eyeball rotation from head pose and identity. The approach also draws on adversarial image reconstruction and perceptual loss from GAN literature, but applies them in a two-stage disentangled training pipeline rather than for generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that face-image-based unsupervised gaze representations can be robust to extreme head poses (up to ~100° yaw), this work opens a path toward gaze estimation without expensive eye-tracker annotation in unconstrained settings. However, the method's reliance on synchronized multi-view training data (18 cameras for ETH-XGaze) is a significant practical constraint that limits immediate deployment; single-view unsupervised gaze representation learning remains an open challenge. The observed linearity invariance of the learned gaze space is a promising property for downstream manipulation tasks, but whether it holds across subjects and domains beyond the tested datasets is unverified.
