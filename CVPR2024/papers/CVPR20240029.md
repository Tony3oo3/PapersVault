## Problem

Under domain skew in federated learning, existing methods suffer from two fairness failures: (1) Parameter Update Conflict, where varying parameter importance and inconsistent update directions across clients cause crucial updates from underperforming domains to be overwhelmed by unimportant parameter changes from others; and (2) Model Aggregation Bias, where conventional weighting strategies (proportional to sample size or equal) neglect domain diversity, biasing the global model toward easier-to-fit domains and producing large performance disparities across clients.

## Input / Output

The server receives per-client model updates (Δw_m^t, a vector of G parameter changes) and maintains a per-client consistency table L_m tracking the historical proportion of increasing updates for each parameter. It outputs per-parameter aggregation weights q_{m,i}^t (which zero out parameters below a consistency threshold τ and renormalize the rest) and client-level weights p_m^t (updated via momentum based on the squared L2 distance of each client's important-parameter update). The final output is the updated global model W^{t+1}. No additional data, features, or validation sets are transmitted beyond standard model updates.

## Method

FedHEAL combines two server-side aggregation mechanisms. First, Federated Parameter-Harmonized Learning (FPHL) exploits a discovered property called Parameter Update Consistency (PUC): parameters that consistently move in the same direction across consecutive local training rounds are deemed important for a specific domain. Using a threshold τ, FPHL discards unimportant parameter updates (setting their aggregation weights to zero) and renormalizes the remaining weights, so that crucial updates from underperforming domains are no longer diluted by noise from less significant parameters. Second, Federated Aggregation-Equalized Learning (FAEL) addresses aggregation bias by formulating an objective that minimizes the variance of squared L2 distances between the new global model and all local models; since this is computationally expensive, it is approximated by a momentum-based weight update (hyperparameter β) that assigns higher client weights to those with larger important-parameter update magnitudes, effectively pulling the global model toward neglected domains. The two components operate sequentially on the same model updates and require no changes to client-side training, making FedHEAL a drop-in aggregation module compatible with existing FL methods.

## Results

On the Digits benchmark, FedHEAL achieves 78.09% average accuracy with 22.08 standard deviation across four domains, outperforming Ditto (76.16%, 23.62 STD) and FedFV (76.97%, 23.17 STD). On Office-Caltech, FedHEAL reaches 64.85% average accuracy with 3.80 STD, substantially surpassing Ditto (61.92%, 6.20 STD) and FedFV (62.41%, 6.72 STD). When integrated with FedAvg, FedHEAL reduces the Office-Caltech STD from 11.13 to 3.80 (a 65.9% reduction) while increasing average accuracy from 58.35% to 64.85%, demonstrating both improved fairness and overall performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedHEAL outperforms both Ditto and FedFV on every metric across both datasets. On Digits, FedHEAL improves average accuracy by 1.93% over Ditto and 1.12% over FedFV, while reducing STD by 1.54 and 1.09 points respectively. On Office-Caltech, the gains are more pronounced: FedHEAL achieves +2.93% AVG over Ditto and +2.44% over FedFV, with STD reduced from 6.20/6.72 to 3.80. When compared against fairness-integrated baselines (FedAvg+AFL, FedAvg+q-FFL), FedHEAL consistently wins on both AVG and STD; for example, on Office-Caltech it achieves 64.85% AVG vs. 58.83% (FedAvg+AFL) and 57.28% (FedAvg+q-FFL), with STD of 3.80 vs. 7.84 and 5.73. The compatibility study further shows FedHEAL improves every tested FL backbone (FedProx, SCAFFOLD, MOON, FedDyn, FedProc, FedProto), with the largest gain on FedProto+Office-Caltech (STD reduced from 12.04 to 6.44, AVG increased from 59.18 to 74.30).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedHEAL builds directly on the FedAvg aggregation framework, modifying how parameter updates are combined at the server. It departs from prior Performance Fairness methods (AFL, q-FFL, FedFV) that adjust client-level weights based on loss or accuracy metrics by instead operating at the parameter level: discarding updates with low Parameter Update Consistency (PUC) and re-weighting clients based on model-distance (domain diversity). The PUC observation—that a substantial fraction of parameters maintain consistent update directions across many rounds even near convergence—is novel and not studied in prior FL literature. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-in layer composable with existing FL algorithms (FedProx, SCAFFOLD, MOON, FedDyn, FedProto, FedProc), positioning it as an orthogonal fairness enhancement rather than a standalone training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical governance concern in FL: if clients perceive the global model as biased toward other domains, their incentive to participate diminishes, undermining the collaborative premise. The PUC observation provides a mechanistic explanation for why standard aggregation degrades underperforming domains, and the parameter-level filtering offers a lightweight, privacy-preserving remedy (no extra data or features transmitted). However, the method's sensitivity to hyperparameters (τ, β) and its requirement for identical network architectures across clients limit immediate broad applicability. Open questions include whether PUC-based filtering generalizes to heterogeneous architectures, larger-scale deployments, and whether the momentum-based weight update can be replaced with a more principled optimization of the variance-minimization objective.
