## Problem

Existing audio-visual representation learning methods are not human-centric and lack spatial grounding: they either operate on exocentric (third-person) video with monaural audio or explore multi-channel audio in non-egocentric, non-social contexts. This leaves a gap in learning spatially-grounded audio-visual features from real-world egocentric video of social conversations, where understanding sound source location relative to the camera wearer and the surrounding environment is critical for tasks like identifying who is speaking or separating target speech from distractors.

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric video clip (T frames split into P non-overlapping tubelets) and its paired binaural audio (two-channel, represented as Mel-spectrograms split into Q non-overlapping patches). During pretraining, a subset of audio tokens is masked and the model must reconstruct them from the remaining audio tokens and all visual tokens. The trained encoder produces spatial audio-visual features that are fused with downstream backbones (TalkNet, SPELL for active speaker detection; U-Net for spatial audio denoising) via cross-attention or convolutional projection at the bottleneck.

## Method

The core contribution is a masked autoencoding pretext task for binaural audio inpainting guided by egocentric video, combined with a novel dual-strategy masking protocol. The encoder comprises separate video and audio transformer encoders (8 layers each) followed by a shared audio-visual transformer (6 layers) that jointly encodes spatio-temporal correspondences; the decoder is a lightweight two-stage transformer (1+3 layers) that reconstructs masked audio tokens. The key masking innovation mixes random token masking (which provides local spatial cues and stable optimization) with full-channel masking at 20% probability (which forces the model to solve a harder binauralization task from vision alone, encouraging global spatial reasoning about camera motion and scene layout). Training minimizes MSE on masked audio tokens, and the resulting encoder features are transferred to downstream tasks by fusing them with task-specific backbones and fine-tuning.

## Results

On active speaker detection (mAP), the proposed method achieves 63.9% with TalkNet and 65.6% with SPELL on EgoCom, and 71.8% and 70.2% respectively on EasyCom, outperforming all baselines. On spatial audio denoising (EgoCom, 0 dB SNR), it reaches SI-SDRi of 2.20 and STFT distance of 6.51, again the best among all compared methods. Ablations confirm that self-supervised pretraining with binaural audio (vs. monaural or no pretraining) is essential for the gains, with monaural pretraining dropping TalkNet/EgoCom from 63.9 to 61.0 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four spatial audio-visual feature learning baselines across both downstream tasks and both datasets. On ASD, it beats the strongest competitor 2.5D-VS++ [18] by 0.5 mAP (TalkNet/EgoCom) and 3.3 mAP (SPELL/EasyCom), and SSR++ [60] by 3.2 mAP (TalkNet/EgoCom) and 2.8 mAP (SPELL/EasyCom). AV-MAE [23] is the closest on SPELL/EasyCom (65.2 vs. 70.2, a 5.0 mAP gap) but trails more on TalkNet/EasyCom (61.3 vs. 71.8). TLR [70] is consistently the weakest spatial baseline, lagging the proposed method by 16+ mAP on TalkNet/EgoCom. On denoising (EgoCom, 0 dB), the method leads 2.5D-VS++ by 0.09 SI-SDRi and 0.09 STFT, SSR++ by 0.16 SI-SDRi, and TLR by 0.79 SI-SDRi, with margins widening at higher noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoding (MAE) paradigm for audio-visual representation learning [23, 24, 29, 30] from exocentric video with monaural audio to egocentric video with binaural (two-channel) audio, reframing the pretext task as spatial binaural audio inpainting rather than generic modality inpainting. It departs from prior spatial audio-visual correspondence methods (TLR [70], 2.5D-VS [18], SSR [60]) that operate on exocentric data and provide only coarse spatial signals, by introducing a fine-grained inpainting objective combined with a novel masking strategy that mixes random token masking with full-channel masking to balance local and global spatial reasoning. The method is positioned as the first self-supervised approach specifically targeting spatial audio-visual features in social egocentric settings, where camera motion and multiple concurrent speakers create challenges absent in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial audio-visual features learned via binaural inpainting are generic enough to improve multiple downstream backbones (TalkNet, SPELL, U-Net) on two distinct tasks (active speaker detection, spatial audio denoising) across two datasets, suggesting a reusable pretraining recipe for egocentric AR applications. Limitations include degraded performance under drastic camera motion, high speech overlap, and semantically similar distractor noise, and the evaluation is confined to only two small public datasets (EgoCom, EasyCom), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger or more diverse egocentric corpora. The authors note future directions toward inferring social attention (who is listening to whom) from the learned spatial cues, which could extend the utility beyond the two demonstrated tasks.
