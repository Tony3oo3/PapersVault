## Problem

Conventional video understanding methods represent frames as real-valued vectors and focus on short clips, making it difficult to capture high-order spatiotemporal relationships among objects across long videos. This limits the ability to understand multi-granular semantic structures (objects, scenes, video-wide contexts) and, critically, to generalize compositionally to unseen combinations of previously learned semantic units.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240232/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video as a spatiotemporal symbolic graph: N snapshot graphs (one per frame), each containing n object nodes with attribute vectors (shape, color, material, size) and edges encoding pairwise spatial relationships (distance, direction). Graph construction relies on object detection and attribute classification. Outputs are structured as multi-label predictions: 14 atomic action labels (object×action type), 301 compositional labels (two atomic actions + Allen's temporal relation), and snitch cell position in the final frame.

## Method

The core architecture is the Spatiotemporal Graph Transformer (ST-GT), which builds a total adjacency matrix over N×n nodes by adding temporal auxiliary edges linking the same object across adjacent frames, then tokenizes all nodes, spatial edges, and temporal edges with graph-Laplacian eigenvector positional encodings and learnable type identifiers. A GPT-2-style object-oriented video encoder with causal masking groups tokens by object index to encode per-object temporal trajectories. The key training innovation is compositional learning via disentangled embeddings: entangled object-action features are projected into separate object and action subspaces through learnable embedding layers, and a multi-label classification head predicts each semantic element independently. The total loss combines video-level binary cross-entropy (L_vid) with compositional losses (L_comp) that supervise object, action, and temporal-relation predictions separately, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen label combinations.

## Results

On CATER Task 1 (atomic action recognition), the proposed ST-GT method achieves 99.88 mAP, surpassing the best baseline R3D+NL (98.9 mAP). On the more challenging Task 2 (compositional action recognition with temporal relations), it reaches 75.40 mAP, outperforming the strongest baseline Single-stream SCI3D+LSTM (69.76 mAP) by 5.6 points. On the real-world MOMA-LRG dataset, the method achieves 72.83 mAP with only 7.3M parameters, beating FROZEN (69.36 mAP, 115M params) and ViViT (65.09 mAP, 99M params). In the compositional generalization setting, the method scores 71.53 mAP on unseen Task 1 labels versus 25.05 for R3D, and 69.2 mAP on unseen Task 2 labels versus 3.51 for R3D+LSTM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the CNN-based baselines (R3D, R3D+NL, R3D+LSTM, R3D+NL+LSTM from Carreira et al. and Girdhar et al.), ST-GT achieves 75.40 mAP on Task 2 versus 44.2–53.4 mAP, a margin of 22–31 points, demonstrating that object-centric spatiotemporal graph representations capture compositional structure far better than frame-level convolutional features. Against the set-based SCI3D variants (Singh et al.), ST-GT leads by 5.6 mAP on Task 2 (75.40 vs. 69.76) and by 1.0 mAP on Task 1 (99.88 vs. 96.77). Against the transformer-based video models ViViT (Arnab et al.) and FROZEN (Bain et al.), ST-GT gains 9.2 and 8.8 mAP respectively on Task 2, and on MOMA-LRG it surpasses FROZEN by 3.5 mAP while using roughly 15× fewer parameters (7.3M vs. 115M). In the compositional generalization splits, the gap widens dramatically: on unseen Task 2 labels, R3D+LSTM drops to 3.51 mAP while ST-GT maintains 69.2 mAP, confirming that disentangled object/action embeddings enable zero-shot composition of novel label combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant frame-level video understanding paradigm (I3D, R3D, ViViT, FROZEN) by representing videos as object-centric spatiotemporal symbolic graphs rather than sequences of pixel features. It extends the graph transformer literature (TokenGT, spectral attention) to the temporal graph setting, which the authors explicitly identify as an underexplored gap, by introducing temporal auxiliary edges that connect corresponding object nodes across adjacent frames. The compositional learning objective draws on the CATER dataset's two-level action hierarchy and Allen's temporal algebra, but the paper's key methodological departure is the disentangled embedding strategy that projects object and action information into independent subspaces, enabling zero-shot composition of unseen label combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional generalization splits introduced here provide a much-needed evaluation protocol for testing whether video models truly decompose actions into semantic units rather than memorizing label combinations—a gap that standard CATER splits do not probe. The efficiency result (7.3M parameters outperforming 99M-parameter ViViT on MOMA-LRG) suggests that structured, object-centric representations can be more parameter-efficient than raw pixel-level transformers for compositional tasks. However, the method's reliance on accurate object detection and attribute classification limits its applicability to unstructured real-world videos, and the real-world validation is confined to a single dataset (MOMA-LRG). Open questions include scalability to truly long videos (minutes to hours), robustness to detection errors, and whether the graph-based inductive bias generalizes beyond the controlled CATER/MOMA-LRG settings.
