## Problem

Camouflaged vision perception datasets are severely limited in species diversity due to the extreme cost of pixel-wise annotation (e.g., 60 minutes per instance in COD10K vs. 3 minutes in COCO-Stuff). Existing camouflaged image generation methods all require a manually specified background, which constrains diversity to human cognitive range and makes large-scale synthetic data generation impractical. No prior method can automatically synthesize a suitable concealing background from the foreground object alone.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 512×512 RGB foreground object image paired with a binary mask of the same size (object region = 0, editable background = 1), and produces a complete 512×512 camouflaged image in which the object is concealed within an automatically generated background. The foreground region is preserved exactly via the inpainting mask, so the segmentation annotation remains pixel-accurate without boundary blurring. The image is processed in a 128×128×3 latent space via a pre-trained VQ-VAE autoencoder, and the output is decoded back to full resolution.

## Method

LAKE-RED is a background-free inpainting pipeline built on a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model, exploiting the key insight that camouflaged foregrounds and backgrounds share strong textural consistency, making background knowledge retrievable from foreground features. Three modules are added to the standard LDM inpainting condition: (1) Localized Masked Pooling (LMP) segments the foreground into SLIC superpixels and computes per-superpixel masked averages to extract rich local features rather than a single global vector; (2) the Background Knowledge Retrieval Module (BKRM) uses multi-head attention with foreground features as queries to retrieve background-aligned visual embeddings from the VQ-VAE codebook, explicitly modeling foreground-to-background texture correspondence; (3) the Reasoning-Driven Condition Enhancement (RCEM) reconstructs the ground-truth background latent from the retrieved knowledge via an MLP, then injects this reconstruction into the background region of the inpainting condition, adding a background reconstruction loss that forces the model to reason about real background features. The overall training objective combines the standard diffusion denoising loss with the background reconstruction loss, and the added modules contribute only ~0.01M parameters and 0.02G MACs over the base LDM.

## Results

On the overall test set (combining Camouflaged, Salient, and General Objects), LAKE-RED achieves FID 64.27 and KID 0.0355, outperforming the best inpainting baseline TFill (FID 80.39, KID 0.0438) by 20.1% and 18.7% respectively. On the most challenging General Objects subset, the gap widens to FID 102.67 vs. 122.44 for TFill. Image blending methods (AB, CI, AdaIN, DCI, LCGNet) are substantially worse, with overall FID ranging from 120.21 to 130.52, confirming that inpainting-based generation is far superior for camouflage synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAKE-RED outperforms all eight baselines on every subset and both metrics (FID and KID). Among inpainting baselines, TFill is the strongest competitor (Overall FID 80.39), but LAKE-RED reduces FID by 16.12 points (20.1%) and KID by 0.0083 (18.7%). LDM achieves the best Camouflaged Objects FID (58.65) among baselines but degrades sharply on General Objects (129.04), while LAKE-RED maintains a consistent advantage across all three domains. RePaint-L performs worst among inpainting methods (Overall FID 96.14), struggling with background complementation. All five image blending methods (AB, CI, AdaIN, DCI, LCGNet) are far inferior, with Overall FID between 120 and 131, because they require a manually specified background and mechanically shift foreground features toward it. Notably, LCGNet achieves the lowest KID among blending methods (0.0550) by nearly erasing foreground features, but this comes at the cost of visual naturalness, as confirmed by the user study where LAKE-RED was rated most natural and closest to real camouflage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) for image inpainting, repurposing the pre-trained VQ-VAE codebook as an external knowledge base for background feature retrieval—a use of the codebook that goes beyond its original role in reconstruction. It departs from the established camouflage generation lineage (Chu et al. 2010, DCI 2020, LCGNet 2023), which all require a manually specified background, by formulating the problem as pure foreground-to-background inpainting. The knowledge-retrieval-plus-reasoning decomposition (BKRM + RCEM) is conceptually inspired by retrieval-augmented generation in NLP but applied here to latent visual features, and the Localized Masked Pooling module extends standard masked averaging with SLIC superpixel clustering to preserve local texture diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The background-free paradigm removes the primary practical bottleneck in large-scale camouflage dataset synthesis: the need for human-curated background images, which limits both diversity and scalability. This is particularly impactful for camouflaged object detection, where annotation costs are extreme (60 min per instance vs. 3 min for COCO) and current datasets cover only a narrow set of species. Open questions include whether the VQ-VAE codebook constrains the diversity of generable backgrounds, whether the approach extends to video or 3D camouflage, and whether the retrieval mechanism can be replaced with more flexible or domain-specific knowledge sources. The method's reliance on a single pre-trained LDM checkpoint also raises questions about robustness across different diffusion backbones.
