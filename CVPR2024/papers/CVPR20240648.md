## Problem

Conventional pixel-wise cross-view localization methods rely on ground-plane homography to match features between ground and aerial views, which inherently ignores off-ground features (e.g., streetlights, traffic poles) and fails to handle visual occlusions (e.g., tree canopies obscuring road details in aerial views). This neglect of elevated cues and occlusion-awareness leads to unreliable localization, particularly under large initial pose uncertainty or degraded road markings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground-view RGB image from an on-board camera (processed at 256×1024 for KITTI or 432×816 for Ford Multi-AV), a corresponding satellite image from Google Maps (512×512 or 1280×1280), and an initial coarse 3-DoF pose with noise up to ±45° orientation and ±20 m translation. It outputs a refined 3-DoF vehicle pose (longitudinal translation, lateral translation, and heading orientation) on the satellite map, refined iteratively via Levenberg-Marquardt optimization over 20 iterations using top-N confidence-selected keypoints.

## Method

Building on the PureACL baseline (shared-weight U-Net feature extraction, confidence-guided keypoint selection, and LM-based pose refinement), the paper introduces three components. First, the Top-to-Ground Aggregation (T2GA) module applies a column-wise attention mechanism where each on-ground pixel queries its vertically stacked elevated pixels, fusing off-ground appearance cues (e.g., streetlight tops) into the ground-level feature while the attention naturally suppresses irrelevant elevated pixels in the absence of occlusion. Second, a Cycle Domain Adaptation (CycDA) loss enforces view-invariant representations through three L2 terms—ground-space, satellite-space, and latent-space—using encoder/decoder projections conditioned on camera focal lengths to bridge tone, resolution, and lighting differences. Third, an Equidistant Re-projection (ERP) loss normalizes the re-projection residual by each keypoint's distance from the vehicle on the satellite image, preventing the standard re-projection loss from over-penalizing distant keypoints and thereby encouraging a more uniform spatial distribution of selected keypoints for improved orientation estimation.

## Results

On the KITTI-CVL dataset under challenging initial pose noise (±45°, ±20m), the method achieves 0.17m mean lateral error and 0.09m mean longitudinal error in the Cross split, with 99.97% recall at 1m for both lateral and longitudinal translations. On the Ford Multi-AV dataset (4-camera setting), it reaches 0.13m mean lateral and 0.20m mean longitudinal error with 100% r@1m lateral recall. Compared to the PureACL baseline, this represents a >91% reduction in spatial mean error and >52% reduction in orientation mean error. In accumulated pose estimation, the method completes 100% of all routes, whereas BoostAcc and single-camera PureACL fail after covering less than 11% of the distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all nine baselines on spatial accuracy (lateral and longitudinal translation) across every dataset and noise setting. Against the closest pixel-wise baseline PureACL [24], it reduces mean lateral error from 4.24m to 0.17m (K-Cross) and from 1.69m to 0.13m (F-4C), while also improving orientation from 10.96° to 2.88° and 3.07° to 2.00° respectively. BoostAcc [17] achieves a lower orientation error (0.55°) under the easier ±10° noise setting but suffers severe location errors (13.01m mean) and fails accumulated pose estimation after only 4.88% of the route. Patch-wise methods (CCVPE [28], SliceMatch [9]) and image-level retrieval (DSM [15]) are far less accurate, with location errors an order of magnitude larger. Homography-based pixel-wise methods (HighlyAcc [14], HC-Net [26]) also trail significantly, confirming the benefit of the T2GA module's use of off-ground cues. The hybrid LiDAR-visual SIBCL [25] and BEV-based CVGL [4] are outperformed on all reported metrics in the Ford 4-camera setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PureACL [24], a pixel-wise cross-view localization method that uses ground-plane homography to project ground-view keypoints onto satellite imagery and refines pose via Levenberg-Marquardt optimization. It departs from the broader family of geometry-alignment methods (HighlyAcc, HC-Net, BoostAcc) that assume all informative features lie on the ground plane, by introducing a top-to-ground attention aggregation (T2GA) that explicitly incorporates off-ground structures like streetlights and handles aerial occlusions. The CycDA loss extends the domain-adaptation idea from generative cross-view synthesis (GAN/diffusion-based methods) into a feature-space cycle-consistency constraint, while the ERP loss corrects a known bias in re-projection losses that over-penalizes distant keypoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to complete an entire driving route via continual pose estimation—requiring only an initial pose at the start point—addresses a critical practical gap for GPS-denied autonomous driving, where prior methods drift beyond satellite map coverage within 5–10% of the route. The near-perfect localization (>99.9% within 1 m) and the demonstration that off-ground cues (traffic poles, streetlights) are underutilized by existing homography-based pipelines provide a clear recipe for improving robustness in real deployments. Open questions include whether the method generalizes beyond the 3-DoF vehicle setting, whether reliance on Google Maps satellite imagery limits applicability, and how it integrates into full SLAM pipelines as the authors note for future work.
