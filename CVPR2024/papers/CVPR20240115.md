## Problem

The paper identifies the 'low FLOPs pitfall': while high-FLOPs models consistently improve with large-scale pretraining data (e.g., ImageNet-22K over ImageNet-1K), low-FLOPs models (<4G FLOPs) show no benefit or even degrade. This blocks mobile and edge deployment scenarios where computational budgets are tight but the quality gains of large-scale pretraining are still desired.

## Input / Output

For the vision task, the model takes a 224×224 RGB image and outputs a class label over 1,000 (ImageNet-1K) or 21,841 (ImageNet-22K) categories; the architecture targets mobile-level budgets of ~300M–600M FLOPs. For the language extension, the model consumes a 2048-token text sequence and produces next-token predictions, evaluated via zero-shot multiple-choice benchmarks (ARC-easy, BoolQ, SST-2, HellaSwag) on a scaled-down LLaMA-1B (0.94B params, 919G FLOPs).

## Method

ParameterNet decouples parameter count from FLOPs by replacing standard convolutions with dynamic (conditional) convolutions: M expert weight tensors are combined via input-dependent coefficients produced by a tiny MLP over the global-average-pooled feature, yielding an ~M× parameter increase while the FLOPs ratio stays ≈1. The base vision architecture is GhostNet (a mobile CNN), and the same principle is extended to transformers (Swin-300M, EfficientNetV2-B0) and to language via sparse-activated top-1 MoE routing on LLaMA-1B's FFN linear projections. Training follows a two-stage supervised protocol: 90-epoch pretraining on ImageNet-22K (batch 4096, AdamW, cosine schedule) followed by 30-epoch finetuning on ImageNet-1K. The key empirical finding is that this parameter augmentation alone—without any architectural redesign—recovers the large-scale pretraining benefit for low-FLOPs models, e.g., ParameterNet-600M reaches 81.6% top-1 at 599M FLOPs versus Swin-T's 80.9% at 4.5G FLOPs.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K validation, ParameterNet-600M (pretrained on ImageNet-22K) achieves 81.6% top-1 accuracy with only 599M FLOPs and 34.5M parameters, outperforming Swin-T (80.9%) by 0.7% while using ~7× fewer FLOPs. ParameterNet-300M reaches 79.1% top-1 at 298M FLOPs, surpassing EfficientNetV2-B1 (79.0% at 1.2G FLOPs) and EfficientNet-B0 (78.1% at 390M FLOPs). In the language domain, adding sparse-activated MoE (8 experts on up-projection) to LLaMA-1B improves average zero-shot accuracy from 58.19% to 60.56% (+2.37%) while keeping FLOPs unchanged at 919G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParameterNet-600M (81.6%) outperforms Swin-T (80.9%) by 0.7% top-1 while using only 599M FLOPs versus 4.5G (~7× less), and beats ResNet50-BiT (80.0%) by 1.6% with ~20× fewer FLOPs. Against the closest high-FLOPs competitor, ResNet50 pretrained on IG-1B-Targeted (81.2% at 4.1G FLOPs), ParameterNet-600M leads by 0.4% with roughly 7× lower computational cost. Compared to the direct GhostNet baselines, ParameterNet gains +3.4% (300M) and +3.3% (600M) top-1 accuracy, confirming that the added parameters from dynamic convolution enable large-scale pretraining benefits that the original GhostNet cannot exploit. At the 300M FLOPs level, ParameterNet-300M (79.1%) edges out EfficientNetV2-B1 (79.0% at 1.2G FLOPs) and EfficientNet-B0 (78.1% at 390M FLOPs) with fewer or comparable FLOPs. In the language domain, the MoE-based ParameterNet consistently improves over the LLaMA-1B baseline across all four zero-shot benchmarks (ARC-easy, BoolQ, SST-2, HellaSwag), with the 8-expert up-projection variant achieving the best average gain of +2.37%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on dynamic convolution (CondConv, Chen et al. 2020) as its core mechanism for decoupling parameter count from FLOPs, applied atop the GhostNet mobile architecture. It also draws on the Mixture-of-Experts literature from NLP (Switch Transformer, Shazeer et al.) to extend the same parameter-augmentation principle to language models via sparse-activated routing in a scaled-down LLaMA. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard scaling-law narrative (more data + more FLOPs = better) by identifying a regime where more data actively harms low-FLOPs models, and positions its contribution as a design principle rather than a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'low FLOPs pitfall' observation is practically important: it warns mobile/edge practitioners that simply pretraining a small model on ImageNet-22K can degrade performance, and provides a concrete fix (parameter augmentation via dynamic convolution) that yields ~2% gains at negligible FLOPs cost. The generality of the principle—validated across CNNs, transformers, and language models—suggests it could inform future efficient foundation model design. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at even larger pretraining scales (beyond 22K), whether other parameter-augmentation strategies (e.g., LoRA-style adapters, hash layers) offer comparable or superior trade-offs, and whether the approach transfers to dense prediction tasks like detection and segmentation.
