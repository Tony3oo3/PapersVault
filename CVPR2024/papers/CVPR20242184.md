## Problem

Existing personalization methods for text-to-image models excel at 'identity re-contextualization' (changing a subject's context while preserving identity) but struggle with the more nuanced task of 'identity fine editing'—precisely modifying specific facial features like lip color or accessories while preserving both the subject's identity and the surrounding context. Current diffusion-based editing methods either require time-consuming per-identity optimization, train additional encoders, or rely on manual stage selection, and they fail to balance source and target prompts to avoid altering prompt-unrelated regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image (from CelebA-HQ or FFHQ-unaligned), a source text prompt describing the original image, a target text prompt specifying the desired edit, and personalized attention weights pre-computed via HyperDreambooth from 2–4 reference images of the identity. It operates within Stable Diffusion 1.5's latent space (VAE-encoded) using 50 DDIM timesteps with the PLMS sampler and classifier-free guidance scale of 5, producing a single edited face image where only the prompted features change while identity, lighting, texture, and background remain intact.

## Method

DreamSalon introduces a noise-guided, two-stage editing framework that automatically discerns an aggressive-editing stage (early timesteps, identified by high-frequency predicted noise at the 75th quantile) and a quality-boosting stage (late timesteps, identified by low gradient of predicted noise at the 25th quantile). In the editing stage, it adaptively mixes source and target CLIP text embeddings using a frequency-derived weighting (FFT of decoded latents) that emphasizes the target prompt for detailed manipulation; in the boosting stage, it shifts weight toward the source prompt and applies stochastic denoising to enhance fine details without content drift. A covariance-guidance mechanism (CovDiff) computes the element-wise maximum difference between source and target prompt embedding covariance matrices, then applies Hamilton multiplication to modulate token-level editing intensity, ensuring only semantically divergent tokens drive the edit. An optional 3-iteration optimization of the mixing weights λ_t is performed using a combined directional CLIP loss and perceptual loss for further refinement.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, DreamSalon achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CLIP-I 0.837 (identity-context preservation), CLIP-T 0.247 (text-image alignment), and DINO-I 0.958 (conceptual similarity). On the more challenging FFE-Bench (unaligned faces with occlusions and varied views), DreamSalon again leads with CLIP-I 0.815, CLIP-T 0.242, and DINO-I 0.932. In terms of efficiency, DreamSalon's fine-tuning via HyperDreambooth takes only 106 s (6× faster than Dreambooth's 640 s) with 10× less storage, and its per-edit inference time of 26 s is competitive with fine-tuning-free methods like ELITE (28 s) and EDICT (42 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamSalon outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across both datasets. On CelebA-HQ, the closest competitor is EDICT (CLIP-I 0.812, DINO-I 0.948), which DreamSalon surpasses by +0.025 on CLIP-I and +0.010 on DINO-I; PnP is the nearest on CLIP-T (0.245 vs. 0.247). On FFE-Bench, EDICT again trails most closely (CLIP-I 0.808 vs. 0.815), while PnP is closest on DINO-I (0.924 vs. 0.932). Dreambooth and HyperDreambooth, the fine-tuning-based personalization methods, show substantially lower DINO-I scores (0.150 and 0.620 on CelebA-HQ), indicating poor identity uniqueness preservation. Fine-tuning-free inversion methods (P2P, PnP, LEDITS) achieve reasonable DINO-I but sacrifice CLIP-I (0.606–0.731), reflecting identity or context drift during editing. DiffDis [50], a concurrent weighted-mixing approach, scores 0.779/0.205/0.881 on CelebA-HQ, underperforming DreamSalon on all three metrics, confirming the benefit of covariance-guided semantic mixing over uniform token weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamSalon builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (SD 1.5) and DDIM sampling framework, using HyperDreambooth for fast per-identity personalization weights. It departs from the dominant 'identity re-contextualization' paradigm (Dreambooth, P2P, PnP, LEDITS) that focuses on changing a subject's context while preserving identity, instead targeting the finer-grained task of editing specific facial features while preserving both identity and source context. The staged, noise-guided editing strategy—using FFT frequency of intermediate latents to time aggressive edits and gradient magnitude to time stochastic quality boosting—repurposes properties of the DDIM sampling trajectory that prior diffusion editing methods (which rely on manual stage selection or attention manipulation) had not exploited. The covariance-based prompt mixing extends the simple weighted-sum approach used by concurrent work (DiffDis) with token-level semantic awareness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: precise, detail-level face editing (lip color, accessories, eye state) in diffusion models without degrading identity or background context, a need central to professional photo editing and social media applications. The FFE-Bench, while small (600 images), is a useful first step toward standardized evaluation of fine-grained face editing under challenging conditions (occlusion, non-frontal views). Limitations include evaluation restricted to SD 1.5 and face-only editing; whether the frequency/gradient-based stage discernment generalizes to non-face objects or higher-resolution models remains open. The optional optimization of λ_t (only 3 iterations) is lightweight but the method still requires per-identity fine-tuning, limiting its applicability to one-shot or zero-shot editing scenarios.
