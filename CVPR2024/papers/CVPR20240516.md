## Problem

Posterior sampling in latent diffusion models for inverse problems relies on first-order Tweedie approximations that introduce systematic bias, degrading reconstruction quality. Existing second-order corrections require O(d²) Hessian or Jacobian computations, making them computationally intractable for standard reverse diffusion processes. This leaves a gap: no efficient second-order approximation exists that enables both tractable sampling and improved fidelity.

## Input / Output

For inversion, the method takes a noisy linear measurement y = Ax + n (where A encodes the corruption: blur kernel, downsampling matrix, inpainting mask, or noise model) and a known measurement operator A, producing a 512×512 reconstructed image via the Stable Diffusion decoder. For text-guided editing, it additionally accepts a text prompt and a corrupted source image, outputting an edited image that preserves content while applying the semantic change. All experiments use 512×512 RGB images in the latent space of Stable Diffusion, with DDIM sampling over ~50 steps.

## Method

STSL introduces a surrogate loss L(y, Z_t) = λ‖y − AD(Z̄_T)‖² + (η/d)E_ε[εᵀ(∇log p(Z_t+ε) − ∇log p(Z_t))] whose second term approximates the trace of the Hessian via Hutchinson's estimator at O(1) cost, providing a tractable second-order correction to the first-order Tweedie drift. The reverse process uses a modified drift b̃(Z_t,t) = Z_t + G(y,Z_t) + 2∇log p(Z_t) with G(y,Z_t) = −∇L(y,Z_t), and is initialized from p_T(Z_0|E(Aᵀy)) rather than a standard Gaussian to reduce discretization error. Stochastic averaging over K=5 proximal gradient steps per diffusion step accumulates into an expectation estimate, and a contrastive ViT loss optionally improves perceptual quality. For image editing, STSL-CAT refines latents after each Cross-Attention-Control update via a single STSL gradient step, aligning null-text embeddings with a clean image rather than the corrupted one, enabling faithful text-guided edits on corrupted inputs.

## Results

On FFHQ-1K (512×512), STSL achieves the best LPIPS across all three inverse tasks: 0.335 (SR×8), 0.321 (Motion Deblur), and 0.308 (Gaussian Deblur), outperforming P2L by 4.6–12.1% and PSLD by 10.2–25.5% in relative LPIPS reduction. On ImageNet-1K, STSL similarly leads with LPIPS of 0.392 (SR×8), 0.420 (Motion Deblur), and 0.349 (Gaussian Deblur). In terms of efficiency, STSL requires only 50 DDIM steps and 250 NFEs (45 s runtime), achieving a 4× reduction in NFEs over PSLD (1000 NFEs) and 8× over P2L (2000 NFEs), while also surpassing NTI in text-guided editing of corrupted images with 93% vs. 70% CLIP accuracy (32% relative improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STSL outperforms the two primary LDM-based SoTA solvers—P2L [10] and PSLD [43]—on LPIPS across all six task-dataset combinations in Table 1, with the largest gains on FFHQ Gaussian Deblur (0.308 vs. 0.382/0.371) and ImageNet SR×8 (0.392 vs. 0.441/0.484). Against the PSLD variants GML-DPS and LDPS [43], STSL wins on LPIPS in 5 of 6 settings, though GML-DPS edges STSL on PSNR for FFHQ SR×8 (32.34 vs. 31.77), reflecting SSIM/PSNR's sensitivity to high-frequency artifacts. Compared to PDM-based solvers DPS [8] and DiffPIR [55], STSL achieves substantially lower LPIPS (e.g., 0.335 vs. 0.538/0.791 on FFHQ SR×8), confirming the advantage of latent-space foundation models. LDIR [16] and DMPS [33] are consistently outperformed on both quality and efficiency. For image editing, STSL-CAT surpasses NTI [35] on corrupted images by 32% relative CLIP accuracy (93% vs. 70%), while on clean images NTI-CAT (NTI + our CAT module) matches NTI's CLIP score (96%) but improves LPIPS (0.425 vs. 0.434), demonstrating that CAT alone adds value even without the full STSL pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PSLD/P2L line of work on solving inverse problems with latent diffusion models by replacing the widely-used first-order Tweedie likelihood approximation with a tractable second-order surrogate loss. Prior second-order Tweedie corrections [5, 32] required full Hessian or Jacobian computations (O(d²) cost), rendering them impractical; STSL shows that only the trace of the Hessian is needed, estimable in O(1) via Hutchinson's estimator applied to the already-available score network. For image editing, the paper departs from NTI's null-text inversion by first restoring corrupted latents through the inverse-problem solver before applying Cross-Attention-Tuning, addressing a failure mode (residual corruption in edits) that NTI and commercial tools exhibit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4×/8× reduction in neural function evaluations (50 vs. 1000 diffusion steps) makes latent-diffusion inverse solvers substantially more practical for real-time or resource-constrained settings, and the first efficient second-order correction removes a known quality ceiling (Jensen's gap bias) that has limited first-order methods. The extension to text-guided editing of corrupted images opens a practical use case—editing real-world photos with noise, blur, or low resolution—that prior editing pipelines cannot handle. Open questions include whether the trace-only second-order approximation suffices for highly non-Gaussian posteriors, how the method scales to non-linear measurement operators, and whether the SSIM trade-off (favoring LPIPS) reflects a genuine perceptual improvement or a metric artifact.
