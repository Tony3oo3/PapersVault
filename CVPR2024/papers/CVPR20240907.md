## Problem

Generating diverse scenes around user-specified composited foreground elements guided by text prompts remains underexplored. Existing alternatives—Canny-edge ControlNet, inpainting models, and subject-driven fine-tuning—suffer from scene disharmony, foreground distortion, redundant element generation, or loss of fine details like logos and text, making them impractical for applications such as e-commerce product placement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240907/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a composited foreground image (one or more objects placed on a blank canvas) and a free-form text prompt describing the target scene as input, and produces a 512×512 RGB image where the foreground is seamlessly integrated into a textually consistent background. The foreground mask is derived from the composited image, and user-specified foreground-related words in the prompt are used to identify which attention channels to constrain during inference.

## Method

AnyScene builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net and introduces a Foreground Injection Module (F_c) based on ControlNet that extracts foreground features and injects them into the diffusion process. Training combines three losses: standard denoising loss, a pixel-level content loss that aligns reconstructed foreground regions with the original, and a novel edge gradient loss using the Laplacian operator to enforce realistic boundary transitions. During inference, a Foreground Layout Control strategy edits cross-attention maps to confine foreground-related token activations within the foreground mask, preventing distortion and redundant generation. Finally, a Poisson blending step reintegrates the original foreground details into the generated scene, matching local lighting and color for visual harmony. The module is trained on 240K foreground-scene pairs with random color/contrast augmentations, initialized from a ControlNet-Inpaint checkpoint.

## Results

On the proposed COCO-based benchmark (3,108 foreground-scene pairs), AnyScene achieves the best FID of 16.09 and CLIP-Score of 15.18, outperforming the Canny Edge ControlNet baseline (FID 24.07, CLIP-S 14.64) and Inpaint Anything (FID 16.52, CLIP-S 14.72). The Aesthetic score of 5.94 is close to the default Stable Diffusion baseline (6.02), indicating that the foreground conditioning does not significantly degrade overall image quality. Subject-driven methods (BLIP-Diffusion, DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion) are compared qualitatively only, with AnyScene showing superior multi-object composition and detail preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyScene outperforms both direct alternatives on all three quantitative metrics: it reduces FID by 7.98 over Canny Edge ControlNet (16.09 vs. 24.07) and by 0.43 over Inpaint Anything (16.09 vs. 16.52), while achieving the highest CLIP-Score (15.18 vs. 14.64 and 14.72). On the Aesthetic metric, AnyScene (5.94) slightly edges out Inpaint Anything (5.83) and Canny Edge (5.56), and remains close to the unconditioned Stable Diffusion baseline (6.02), confirming that foreground conditioning does not substantially harm overall image quality. Against subject-driven methods (BLIP-Diffusion, DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion), the comparison is qualitative only: AnyScene better preserves foreground details (logos, text) and handles multi-object compositing, whereas subject-driven methods struggle with combining multiple visual concepts and often distort fine details. The paper positions AnyScene as complementary to subject-driven approaches rather than a direct replacement, noting its strength lies in robust layout control and scene synthesis quality for composited foregrounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyScene builds directly on the ControlNet architecture [45] and Stable Diffusion [30] as its generative backbone, but reframes the conditioning problem: rather than treating scene completion as inpainting (filling around a mask) or edge-guided generation (Canny ControlNet), it introduces a dedicated Foreground Injection Module trained with a scene-reconstruction paradigm that explicitly learns foreground-to-scene correspondence. The layout control strategy repurposes cross-attention editing techniques from Prompt-to-Prompt [12] and Attend-and-Excite [5] to confine foreground semantics spatially, while the final blending step applies classical Poisson Blending [25] to a diffusion-generated output. The paper positions itself against both inpainting-based methods (Inpaint Anything [42]) and subject-driven fine-tuning approaches (DreamBooth [32], CustomDiffusion [17], BLIP-Diffusion [19]), arguing that none simultaneously handle multi-object composited foregrounds with detail preservation and scene harmony.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—placing user-specified product or object composites into contextually appropriate scenes for e-commerce, advertising, and design workflows—where existing methods either distort foreground details or produce visually incoherent backgrounds. The proposed benchmark (3,108 pairs) and evaluation protocol provide a starting point for this task, though the dataset is relatively small and drawn from a single source (COCO). A key limitation acknowledged implicitly is that the framework offers less foreground variability than subject-driven methods (which can generate novel poses/angles), and the layout control requires user-specified foreground-related words in the prompt. Open questions include scalability to very complex multi-object scenes, handling of foregrounds with transparent or semi-transparent regions, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the 512×512 resolution used in experiments.
