## Problem

Existing camera-based collaborative perception methods represent the environment using simplified formats such as 3D bounding boxes or BEV segmentation, which miss critical 3D semantic details needed for holistic scene understanding. Camera-based collaborative 3D semantic occupancy prediction has not been explored, and no collaborative perception dataset provides 3D semantic occupancy ground truth for evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241392/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB camera images from multiple CAVs along with their relative pose information, operating within a 40×40×3.2 m detection volume discretized into a 100×100×8 voxel grid (0.4 m³ voxels). Rather than transmitting full 3D voxel features (which would require ~39 MB), the system communicates only two orthogonal plane projections (xz and yz) with 128-dim feature vectors, reducing communication to ~0.78 MB per CAV. The output is a per-voxel semantic occupancy grid with 12 semantic classes plus an empty label, encoded as a one-hot tensor of shape X×Y×Z×C.

## Method

CoHFF decomposes semantic occupancy prediction into two independently pre-trained subtasks: an occupancy prediction network (CaDNN depth estimation → 3D voxel encoder) and a semantic segmentation network (ResNet-101 + FPN → deformable cross-attention projecting features onto three orthogonal planes inspired by K-Planes and TPVFormer). V2X Feature Fusion applies a sparsification mask to remote CAV plane features, injects relative pose via an MLP+Sigmoid, then uses deformable self-attention (Plane Self-Attention) to update the ego vehicle's three planes with the filtered remote features; 3D semantic features are reconstructed by summing the three plane contributions at each voxel. Task Feature Fusion concatenates the occupancy and semantic feature tensors and passes them through a 3D depth-wise convolution to produce the final semantic occupancy map. The key insight is that independently trained task features act as complementary priors—occupancy geometry aids semantic segmentation of small objects while semantic cues improve occupancy of large structures—and that plane-based representation enables a 50× communication reduction while preserving 3D scene information.

## Results

On the OPV2V dataset with up to 7 collaborating agents, CoHFF achieves AP@0.5 of 48.51 and AP@0.7 of 36.39 for 3D vehicle detection, and BEV IoU of 64.44 (Vehicle), 57.28 (Road), and 45.89 (Others) for semantic segmentation. The ablation study confirms that collaboration yields approximately 31% improvement in overall IoU and 37% improvement in mIoU for semantic occupancy prediction over single-vehicle baselines. The plane-based feature compression reduces communication volume by 50× (from 39.05 MB to 0.78 MB) while maintaining performance, with only a 5% IoU drop even at 97× sparsification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Where2Comm [11], CoHFF wins on both AP@0.5 (48.51 vs. 47.30) and AP@0.7 (36.39 vs. 19.30, an 88.5% relative improvement), attributed to voxel-level occupancy prediction capturing actual object shapes rather than inferring bounding boxes. Against CoCa3D [12] in the 7-agent setting, CoHFF trails (48.51 vs. 69.10 AP@0.5), but this is confounded by CoCa3D being trained on the extended OPV2V+ dataset with more agents; in the fair 2-agent comparison, CoHFF clearly outperforms CoCa3D-2 (36.63 vs. 25.90 AP@0.5 and 27.95 vs. 12.60 AP@0.7). Against CoBEVT [40] for BEV segmentation with up to 7 agents, CoHFF wins on Vehicle IoU (64.44 vs. 60.40) and uniquely reports Others IoU (45.89), but loses on Road IoU (57.28 vs. 63.00). Against the LiDAR-based baselines DiscoNet [16] and V2X-ViT [42], CoHFF achieves substantially higher AP scores (48.51/36.39 vs. 36.00/12.50 and 39.82/16.43 respectively), demonstrating that camera-only collaborative occupancy prediction can surpass LiDAR-based collaborative detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the tri-plane feature decomposition from TPVFormer [13] and the K-Planes orthogonal projection idea [6] from single-vehicle 3D semantic occupancy prediction to a multi-agent collaborative setting. It departs from prior collaborative perception work (CoCa3D, Where2Comm, V2X-ViT), which transmits simplified representations like 3D bounding boxes or BEV maps, by instead sharing compressed plane-based semantic features over V2X links. The hybrid fusion design—combining inter-CAV V2X feature fusion with intra-CAV task feature fusion between occupancy and semantic subtasks—has no direct precedent in the collaborative perception literature, which typically fuses a single task's features across agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first camera-based collaborative semantic occupancy prediction framework, filling a gap between single-vehicle occupancy methods and collaborative perception that was limited to boxes or BEV. The 50× communication reduction (39 MB → 0.8 MB) by transmitting only two orthogonal planes rather than full voxel features makes the approach practical under V2X bandwidth constraints, and the robustness to 97× sparsification with only 5% IoU loss is practically significant. However, all results are on simulation data (CARLA replay of OPV2V), and the authors explicitly note that real-world multi-agent validation remains open. The extended OPV2V dataset with 12-class 3D semantic occupancy labels provides a useful benchmark for future work in this emerging sub-area.
