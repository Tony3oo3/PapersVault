## Problem

Generalizable NeRF methods struggle in challenging regions (occlusions, reflections, object boundaries) due to three interrelated issues: variance-based cost volumes produce misleading geometry when multi-view features of the same 3D point are inconsistent, descriptor encoding ignores 3D spatial context leading to discontinuities, and the two existing color decoding strategies (blending vs. regression) have complementary strengths that have never been analyzed or unified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241864/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB source images (typically 2 or 3 views) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, at resolutions ranging from 512×640 (DTU) to 800×800 (NeRF Synthetic). It produces a rendered novel view image at an arbitrary camera pose, a corresponding depth map, and per-point 3D feature descriptors. Training is end-to-end with only RGB image supervision (MSE + perceptual + SSIM losses), requiring no depth or normal annotations.

## Method

GeFu wraps three modules in a coarse-to-fine framework. (1) Adaptive Cost Aggregation (ACA) replaces the standard equal-weight variance cost volume with per-view adaptive reweighting: a coarse-stage rendered view provides a proxy target feature, and views whose warped features are inconsistent with it (due to occlusion or reflection) are suppressed via a learned weight α(·). (2) Spatial-View Aggregator (SVA) first applies a 3D U-Net to pooled inter-view features to produce spatially smooth descriptors, then uses those smoothed features as queries in a cross-attention module over the raw per-view features to re-gather high-frequency geometric details, yielding 3D context-aware point descriptors. (3) Consistency-Aware Fusion (CAF) decodes descriptors into two intermediate views—one via blending weights over source-view colors, one via direct regression from accumulated features—then fuses them with pixel-wise weights predicted from multi-view feature variance at the rendered depth, so that the strategy better matching local geometry dominates. The coarse stage's geometry guides fine-stage sampling and its rendered features feed back into ACA for improved cost volume construction.

## Results

Under the 3-view generalization setting (no fine-tuning), GeFu achieves PSNR of 29.36 on DTU, 24.28 on Real Forward-facing, and 26.99 on NeRF Synthetic, outperforming the strongest baseline ENeRF by 1.75, 0.65, and 0.82 dB respectively. In the 2-view setting, GeFu still leads with PSNR of 26.98 (DTU), 23.39 (Real Forward-facing), and 25.30 (NeRF Synthetic). After only 15 minutes of per-scene fine-tuning, GeFu reaches 30.10 dB on DTU, surpassing NeRF optimized for 10.2 hours (27.01 dB). For depth reconstruction, GeFu achieves 2.47 mm absolute error on DTU, beating even MVSNet (3.60 mm) which is trained with explicit depth supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeFu outperforms all seven generalizable NeRF baselines (PixelNeRF, IBRNet, MVSNeRF, NeuRay, ENeRF, GNT, MatchNeRF) across all three datasets in both 3-view and 2-view generalization settings. The closest competitor is ENeRF, which GeFu surpasses by 1.75 dB on DTU, 0.65 dB on Real Forward-facing, and 0.82 dB on NeRF Synthetic (3-view); the gap widens to 1.50 dB on DTU in the 2-view setting. PixelNeRF, which uses appearance descriptors without explicit geometry, shows the weakest generalization (19.31 dB on DTU, 11.24 dB on Real Forward-facing), confirming the importance of geometry-aware reconstruction. In per-scene fine-tuning, GeFu after just 15 minutes (30.10 dB on DTU) already exceeds NeRF optimized for 10.2 hours (27.01 dB) and all other fine-tuned baselines. On depth reconstruction, GeFu (2.47 mm abs err) beats ENeRF (3.80 mm) by 35% and even surpasses MVSNet (3.60 mm), which is trained with explicit depth supervision, demonstrating that the geometry-aware design transfers to accurate depth estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeFu extends the generalizable NeRF pipeline established by MVSNeRF and ENeRF, which introduced cost-volume-based geometry estimation for scene-agnostic view synthesis. It departs from prior work in three targeted ways: (1) replacing uniform variance-based cost aggregation with adaptive view reweighting inspired by MVS methods like AA-RMSNet, (2) augmenting inter-view feature pooling with 3D spatial context via a 3D U-Net and attention mechanism, and (3) unifying the two previously competing rendering strategies (blending vs. regression) into a single consistency-guided fusion. The coarse-to-fine framework that feeds rendered features back into geometry estimation at the fine stage is a structural departure from prior single-pass or purely depth-guided pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeFu provides a practical recipe for improving generalizable NeRFs across all three pipeline stages (geometry, descriptors, rendering), and the CAF finding that blending and regression are spatially complementary rather than one being universally superior offers a useful design principle for future rendering decoders. The observation that a rendering-stage component (CAF) significantly improves depth accuracy suggests tighter coupling between geometry and appearance than previously exploited. Limitations include reliance on posed input views, evaluation primarily on object-centric and forward-facing scenes, and the added complexity of the two-stage coarse-to-fine architecture; open questions include scalability to unbounded or dynamic scenes and whether the adaptive weighting generalizes to very sparse (2-view) or uncalibrated settings.
