## Problem

Video-language models are severely data-starved: human video annotation costs 1–2 orders of magnitude more than image annotation, and the largest public video-text datasets (S-MiT ~500K, VidLN ~50K) are tiny compared to billion-scale image-text corpora. Existing automated captioning approaches—alt-text retrieval, audio transcription, and frame-wise image captioning—produce captions that are irrelevant, misaligned, or biased toward static appearance, losing the temporal and causal structure that defines video.

## Input / Output

The adapted VLM consumes 8 video frames sampled at 2 FPS (resized to 224×224) concatenated as visual tokens, plus a free-form text instruction, and autoregressively generates a text response (caption, QA answer, or multi-granularity description) via nucleus sampling. The downstream dual-encoder model takes 4 frames at 2 FPS through a ViT-st-L/14 with joint spatial-temporal attention and a single caption (≤77 BPE tokens) through a 12-layer GPT-like text encoder, producing aligned video and text embeddings for retrieval and classification.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage adaptation of PaLI-3 (ViT-G/14 + UL-2) to video: first, the visual encoder is fine-tuned on 480K S-MiT videos with short spoken captions while the language model is frozen, teaching the encoder to attend to scene dynamics; second, the language model is fine-tuned on 47K VidLN videos with LLM-generated causal and temporal instruction-following pairs while the visual encoder is frozen, instilling temporal reasoning beyond scene description. The adapted VLM then auto-labels ~17M web-scraped video clips (VideoCC and InternVid) with detailed pseudo-captions at multiple granularities. A CLIP-style dual-encoder (ViT-st-L/14 + GPT text encoder) is contrastively trained with InfoNCE loss on these pseudo-captions, exhibiting a clear scaling effect with data size that the original alt-text does not, and achieving 48.4% R@1 on MSR-VTT zero-shot retrieval—6% above prior state of the art.

## Results

The dual-encoder model trained on the combined VideoCC+ and InternVid+ pseudo-captions achieves 48.4% Recall@1 on MSR-VTT zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, 6% higher than the best prior method (ViCLIP at 42.4%), and 65.6% R@1 on VATEX. On Kinetics-600 zero-shot classification, it reaches 62.8% Top-1 accuracy. The adapted video-language model (5B params) attains 29.5 WUPS on open-ended NExT-QA, surpassing Flamingo-80B (26.7) by 2.8%, and 48.2 CIDEr on MSR-VTT captioning, nearly 3× the prior SOTA (DeCap at 18.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (WIT, 31.2% R@1) and CLIP4Clip (WIT→VideoCC, 33.7% R@1), the proposed VideoCC+ pseudo-captions yield a 11.2-point and 14.5-point gain respectively on MSR-VTT R@1, demonstrating that VLM-generated captions far outperform raw alt-text. Compared to InternVideo (WIT→Mixed 12M, 40.0% R@1), the combined VideoCC+ InternVid+ model leads by 8.4 points. The closest competitor is ViCLIP trained on InternVid (42.4% R@1); the proposed method surpasses it by 6.0 points on MSR-VTT and 3.8 points on InternVid alone, while also reporting VATEX and Kinetics-600 results that ViCLIP omits. For the VLM evaluation (Table 3), the adapted model outperforms Flamingo-80B on NExT-OE-QA (29.5 vs 26.7 WUPS) using only 1/16 the parameters, and beats FrozenBiLM on MSR-VTT QA (24.4 vs 16.8 Acc) and ActivityNet QA (29.6 vs 25.9 Acc). On captioning, it nearly triples DeCap's CIDEr on MSR-VTT (48.2 vs 18.6) and ActivityNet (31.0 vs 15.0). The PaLI-3 8-frame baseline (same architecture, no video adaptation) is outperformed across all five video-language tasks, confirming the benefit of the two-stage video adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PaLI-3 image-based VLM (ViT-G/14 + UL-2 encoder-decoder) to the video domain through a two-stage adaptation: first fine-tuning the visual encoder on video-captioning data (S-MiT), then instruction-tuning the language model on temporal/causal reasoning data (VidLN). It departs from prior video-captioning pipelines (InternVid, VideoChatGPT) that apply image-level VLMs frame-by-frame and rely on LLM summarization, arguing this loses temporal dynamics. The downstream dual-encoder model follows the CLIP/VideoCLIP contrastive-learning paradigm but is trained on the generated pseudo-captions rather than noisy alt-text or frame-level captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the core data bottleneck for video-language models by demonstrating that a single adapted VLM can generate scalable, high-quality pseudo-captions that exhibit a positive scaling effect (unlike alt-text, which saturates at ~1% of the data). The resulting largest video caption dataset to date and the 6% MSR-VTT retrieval improvement make this practically important for the video-language community. Open questions include whether the two-stage adaptation remains necessary as video-text data grows, whether the approach generalizes to longer-form or egocentric video, and how the quality gap between pseudo-captions and human annotations evolves as VLMs improve.
