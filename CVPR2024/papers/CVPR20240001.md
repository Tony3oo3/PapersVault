limitations: The generative nature of the diffusion model can produce spurious details (over-enhancement artifacts), as acknowledged in the paper's Limitations section. ## Problem

Supervised HSI denoising requires paired clean/noisy datasets that are impractical to collect across diverse scenes, cameras, and scan parameters. Existing self-supervised approaches struggle to simultaneously suppress noise and preserve fine spectral-spatial details, while directly applying diffusion models to full-band HSIs is computationally prohibitive and lacks suitable pre-trained generative priors for the high-dimensional spectral domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240001/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy HSI tensor (H×W×B, cropped to 256×256 patches) and produces a denoised HSI of identical dimensions. No clean reference is required at training or inference time; the only external input is a pre-trained unconditional DDPM (trained on RGB images) whose weights are frozen. The reconstruction is formed by a mode-3 tensor product of the diffusion-refined abundance map with the learned spectral endmembers, so the output inherits the physical interpretability of spectral unmixing.

## Method

Diff-Unmix decomposes the HSI into abundance maps and spectral endmembers via a Spectral Transformer Unmixing Network (STU) that uses a novel Global Spectral Attention mechanism to compute attention along the spectral axis, reducing quadratic spatial attention cost. A self-supervised U-Net-like conditioning function Φ, trained via J-Invariance (Noise2Self) on paired noise-variant inputs, produces a refined abundance estimate that serves as the diffusion condition. The key insight is Markov chain state matching: the noisy abundance is fitted to a Gaussian noise model (G-Fit) and matched to an intermediate diffusion timestep t, so reverse sampling starts from t rather than T, cutting inference from ~311s to ~41s. The conditional reverse diffusion then iteratively denoises the abundance in this low-dimensional space, guided by both the conditioning function and the endmembers, before reconstructing the HSI via tensor mixing.

## Results

On the KAIST dataset with Gaussian noise N(0, 0.2), Diff-Unmix achieves 33.06 dB PSNR, 0.964 SSIM, 0.940 FSIM, and 0.116 SAM, outperforming all 15 baselines. Under heavier noise N(0, 0.3), it maintains 31.41 dB PSNR and 0.902 SSIM, surpassing the next-best method HLRTF by 1.07 dB in PSNR. On the CAVE dataset, Diff-Unmix reaches 32.71 dB PSNR and 0.940 SSIM, while on CAVE-Toy it achieves the highest SSIM (0.945) and FSIM (0.993) among all methods. Inference time is 37–43 s, substantially faster than DDS2M (318–354 s) while remaining competitive with DDRM (16–23 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-Unmix achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, FSIM, and SAM simultaneously on KAIST under both noise levels, with the most dramatic gains in SSIM (0.964 vs. 0.808 for HLRTF, 0.786 for DDS2M) and SAM (0.116 vs. 0.275 for HLRTF, 0.334 for DDS2M), indicating superior spectral fidelity. On CAVE-Toy, Diff-Unmix trails DDS2M in PSNR (28.05 vs. 29.34 dB) but leads in SSIM (0.945 vs. 0.844) and FSIM (0.993 vs. 0.977), suggesting better structural and perceptual quality despite slightly lower pixel-level accuracy. On the CAVE dataset, E3DTV is the closest competitor in PSNR (32.34 vs. 32.71 dB) and runs 4× faster (11 s vs. 43 s), but Diff-Unmix still edges it out on SSIM (0.940 vs. 0.951 is actually E3DTV winning slightly) and FSIM (0.957 vs. 0.955). Among diffusion-based baselines, Diff-Unmix is 8–10× faster than DDS2M (37 s vs. 354 s) while matching or exceeding its quality. Model-based methods (NonLRMA, LRTDTV, FGSLR, etc.) are consistently 2–12 dB below Diff-Unmix in PSNR, with FGSLR being the slowest at 249–1758 s. The self-supervised tensor method HLRTF is the strongest non-diffusion baseline, within 0.05 dB PSNR on KAIST N(0,0.2) but significantly behind on SSIM and SAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the Markov chain state matching strategy from DDM2 (Xiang et al., 2023) for HSI denoising, but operates in a physically motivated spectral unmixing space rather than directly on image bands. The self-supervised conditioning function draws on Noise2Self (Batson & Royer, 2019) and J-Invariance theory, while the Transformer-based unmixing network (STU) with Global Spectral Attention extends recent spectral-spatial Transformer work (Li et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023) into a decomposition-specific architecture. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior diffusion-based HSI denoisers (DDRM, DDS2M) by reframing the problem as conditional abundance generation conditioned on a pre-trained RGB diffusion model, rather than training a dedicated HSI diffusion model from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised formulation addresses a critical practical bottleneck: creating paired clean/noisy HSI training data across diverse cameras and scenes is infeasible, yet the method achieves competitive or superior performance without any ground truth. The spectral unmixing perspective provides physical interpretability (abundance maps and endmembers have clear meaning in remote sensing) that pure end-to-end denoisers lack. However, the generative nature introduces over-enhancement artifacts (Fig. 11), and the approach inherits the iterative sampling cost of diffusion models, though state matching reduces this substantially (41s vs 311s). Open questions include whether the unmixing decomposition is always the optimal latent space for diffusion-based restoration, and whether the method generalizes beyond Gaussian noise to more complex real-world degradation models.
