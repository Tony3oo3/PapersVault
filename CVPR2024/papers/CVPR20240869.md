## Problem

Shadow degradation is spatially non-uniform and diverse in pattern, which fundamentally conflicts with the weight-sharing property of dominant models (CNNs and window-based Transformers) that must apply a single parameter set across regions of vastly different degradation severity. Existing solutions either adopt global self-attention with prohibitive quadratic complexity or accept the compromise of local models. The paper asks whether the non-uniform distribution itself can be homogenized, removing the constraint on weight-sharing architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240869/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB shadow image as input and produces a shadow-free RGB image. Evaluation is performed at 256×256 resolution on ISTD+ and SRD benchmarks, while training uses higher-resolution inputs (e.g., 840×640). Because random shuffle introduces stochasticity, the final prediction is obtained by Monte Carlo averaging over 8 independent forward passes (denoted HomoFormer+), marginalizing the random permutation factors from a Bayesian perspective.

## Method

HomoFormer integrates a random shuffle operation S(·) and its exact inverse I_S(·) around local window self-attention: the input is stochastically permuted across the entire spatial space to homogenize the shadow distribution, local self-attention (without position encoding) processes the homogenized features, and the inverse shuffle restores the original spatial order. Since self-attention is equivariant to token reordering, the stochasticity does not destabilize training. Structural information lost by discarding position encoding is recovered in a custom SMLP feed-forward layer that inserts two depth-wise convolutions with element-wise multiplication between two MLPs. The overall architecture follows a UNet encoder-decoder with HomoFormer blocks, trained with a single Charbonnier loss, achieving linear complexity in input resolution while bypassing the non-uniformity challenge.

## Results

On the ISTD+ dataset, HomoFormer achieves an All-region MAE of 2.7 (Shadow MAE 5.0, Non-Shadow MAE 2.3), matching the previous best ShadowDiffusion (2.7) and outperforming ShadowFormer (2.8) and BMNet (3.0). With Monte Carlo averaging (HomoFormer+), the All-region MAE improves to 2.6. On the SRD dataset, HomoFormer achieves the best All-region PSNR of 35.37 dB, SSIM of 0.972, and MAE of 3.33, surpassing ShadowDiffusion (34.73 dB / 0.970 / 3.63) and ShadowFormer (32.90 dB / 0.958 / 4.04) by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ISTD+, HomoFormer (All MAE 2.7) ties with the strongest prior method ShadowDiffusion (2.7) and beats ShadowFormer (2.8) by 0.1 and BMNet (3.0) by 0.3; however, ShadowDiffusion retains a slight edge on Shadow-region MAE (4.9 vs. 5.0). On SRD, HomoFormer clearly dominates: it outperforms ShadowDiffusion by 0.30 dB PSNR and 0.30 MAE (All-region), and beats ShadowFormer by 2.47 dB PSNR and 0.71 MAE. Against the earlier CNN-based baselines (DeshadowNet, DHAN, DSC), the gap is even larger, with HomoFormer improving All-region PSNR by over 4 dB. The Monte Carlo averaging variant (HomoFormer+) further narrows the gap on ISTD+ (All MAE 2.6) and improves Non-Shadow MAE to 2.2, suggesting the stochastic shuffle provides a natural ensemble benefit. Overall, HomoFormer is competitive or best across both benchmarks, with the most decisive wins on SRD where prior transformer-based methods (ShadowFormer) lag significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

HomoFormer builds on Swin Transformer's local window-based self-attention but fundamentally rethinks how to handle the non-uniform spatial distribution of shadow degradation. Rather than escalating to global self-attention (as in vanilla ViT or ShadowFormer's channel attention) to model spatial heterogeneity, the paper introduces a parameter-free random shuffle operation that homogenizes the degradation distribution before local attention processes it, with an inverse shuffle restoring spatial order afterward. The SMLP module, which inserts depth-wise convolution between MLPs to recover structural information lost by discarding position encoding, draws on CNN's inherent position-based weight allocation. The overall UNet architecture and Charbonnier loss follow standard image restoration conventions, while the Monte Carlo averaging strategy for marginalizing shuffle stochasticity connects to Bayesian deep learning practices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The homogenization perspective offers a general principle—transform the input to match the model's inductive bias rather than making the model more complex—that could transfer to other restoration tasks with non-uniform degradation (e.g., inpainting, deblurring), as the authors note. The random shuffle is extremely cheap (no extra parameters or FLOPs), making it an attractive drop-in modification for existing window-based transformers. However, the best results require Monte Carlo averaging (8 forward passes), which multiplies inference cost, and the approach has only been validated on shadow removal; whether the homogenization principle generalizes to other degradation types remains an open question. The uncertainty estimation as a free byproduct of the stochastic shuffle is a practical bonus for real-world deployment where ground truth is unavailable.
