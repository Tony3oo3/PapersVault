## Problem

Removing text-specified objects from 360° NeRF scenes while inpainting perceptually consistent content remains unsolved. Existing NeRF inpainting methods rely on 2D segmentation and inpainting that cannot extrapolate across the drastically varying viewpoints of unbounded scenes, and the resulting per-view inconsistencies accumulate as floater artifacts in the 3D representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242700/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained NeRF (with its source training images and camera poses) and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'Remove the flowerpot and flowers'). It produces an inpainted NeRF model with the specified objects removed and a visually consistent background, along with multiview binary segmentation masks. The text is consumed via GLIP for object localization and SAM for promptable segmentation, while depth is extracted from the NeRF itself via alpha-composited ray integration rather than from a separate depth sensor.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, multiview consistent segmentation: GLIP generates bounding boxes from the text, SAM produces initial masks, and a depth-warping refinement step projects sampled points from other views (using NeRF-rendered depth) into the current view to create out-of-box point prompts that guide SAM toward accurate, view-consistent masks. Second, 360° NeRF inpainting: a 2D latent diffusion inpainter fills each masked view, and a NeRFacto model is trained on the inpainted images. To eliminate floaters from inconsistent 2D inpaintings, a DDPM trained on ShapeNet voxels serves as a local geometric prior via a masked DSDS loss (Eq. 6) that penalizes density in regions the diffusion model deems empty, restricted to inpainted regions by the segmentation masks. A patch-based LPIPS perceptual loss on inpainted regions ensures texture consistency without blurring.

## Results

On the Cup dataset, InNeRF360 achieves LPIPS 0.5377 and FID 159.76, outperforming Per-Frame inpainting (LPIPS 0.6149, FID 201.70) and SPN-360 (LPIPS 0.6421, FID 252.34). On Starbucks, the method reaches LPIPS 0.4523 and FID 153.46, again the best among all baselines. In a user study on view consistency across 5 scenes, InNeRF360 is preferred over per-frame inpainting in 71–92% of cases (vs. 8–29% for per-frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InNeRF360 outperforms SPN-360 (SPIn-NeRF adapted for 360°) on both LPIPS and FID across both datasets, with the largest gap on Cup (FID 159.76 vs. 252.34), attributed to SPN-360's reliance on 2D depth-map inpainting that introduces geometric inconsistencies. Against per-frame latent diffusion inpainting [36], InNeRF360 achieves lower LPIPS (0.5377 vs. 0.6149 on Cup) and substantially lower FID (159.76 vs. 201.70), and wins the user study on temporal consistency by a wide margin (e.g., 92% vs. 8% on Bear). For segmentation, InNeRF360 produces more complete and accurate masks than Dino [5] and SPN on challenging cases involving transparent objects (vase) or partially visible objects (slippers), where both baselines yield incomplete segmentations. For object-level editing, InNeRF360 combined with a mask-conditioned editor [8] localizes edits to the target object, whereas Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (In2n) [9] applies incorrect textures to unwanted regions because it operates in latent space without explicit object segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

InNeRF360 extends the NeRF inpainting line of work (SPInNeRF, NeRF-In) from frontal-facing to 360° scenes by replacing user-drawn masks with text-guided semantic segmentation via GLIP and SAM, and by introducing depth-warping prompt refinement to enforce cross-view mask consistency. For the inpainting stage, it builds on NeRFacto as the base architecture and adapts the DSDS loss from NeRFBusters by restricting the geometric prior to inpainted regions via a visibility mask, while adding a patch-based LPIPS appearance prior. This departs from latent-space editing approaches like Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, which cannot perform object-level removal, and from per-frame 2D inpainting, which lacks 3D geometric understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to enable text-guided object removal on unbounded 360° NeRF scenes, a setting where view-consistency challenges are significantly harder than in frontal-facing scenes due to drastic shape changes and occlusions across viewpoints. The depth-warping refinement—leveraging the insight that semantic identity and 3D position are more stable across views than 2D geometry—offers a transferable strategy for any multiview segmentation task on NeRFs. Key limitations include dependence on vision-language model localization accuracy (failing when text cannot be reliably grounded in the image) and the need for a pre-trained NeRF with source images. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the ShapeNet-trained geometric prior is sufficient for arbitrary real-world geometries, and how to handle objects with highly transparent or reflective surfaces.
