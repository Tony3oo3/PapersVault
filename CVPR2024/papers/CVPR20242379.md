## Problem

Existing navigation planners face a fundamental trade-off under partial observability: autoregressive differentiable planners are myopic and degrade on long horizons, while diffusion-based behavior cloners either assume full observability or fail in scenarios beyond expert demonstrations (e.g., dead ends, unseen obstacles). No prior diffusion-based method has been explicitly designed to handle the uncertainty and limited information inherent in partially observable navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242379/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cumulative partial environment map (a BEV grid built by accumulating local observations over the agent's trajectory) as the primary conditioning signal, plus Gaussian noise of the same shape as the target action trajectory for diffusion sampling. In 3D, first-person-view RGB-D frames are converted to a point cloud, semantically segmented (via SWIN3D), and projected onto a 2D BEV grid to match the 2D-trained policy's input format. The output is a multi-step action trajectory (8-directional moves + Done, bit-encoded) spanning a prediction horizon T_h, of which the first T_a steps are executed before re-planning.

## Method

The framework couples a diffusion-based plan generator with a QMDP value-guided planner. The diffusion model (trained via MSE noise-prediction loss on expert action trajectories) generates diverse multi-step plans conditioned on the partial map, providing foresight absent in autoregressive methods. A separate differentiable planner embeds a Bayesian state-estimation module (transition prediction + belief correction) and iterates a learned reward function through K rounds of QMDP value iteration to produce a belief-weighted Q function. At inference, multiple candidate plans are sampled, scored by summing per-step Q values weighted by the evolving belief, and the highest-valued plan is selected; a backtracking buffer retains the previous best plan to prevent suboptimal overwrites during receding-horizon re-planning. For 3D transfer, the 2D-trained policy is applied zero-shot by projecting semantically segmented point clouds onto BEV grids, eliminating the need for 3D-specific training.

## Results

On the standard GridMaze2D setup (15×15 mazes, view range 2), the proposed method achieves a 90.6% success rate, outperforming CALVIN (85.5%) and Diffusion Policy (6.0%). Scalability to larger unseen mazes is a key differentiator: on 30×30 mazes, the method reaches 62.4% success while CALVIN drops to 32.6% and Diffusion Policy fails entirely (0.0%). On the AVD 3D embodied navigation benchmark, the zero-shot 2D-to-3D transfer achieves 67.9% mean success rate (comparable to CALVIN-3D at 68.2%), and retraining with additional RGB inputs pushes this to 70.6%, surpassing CALVIN-3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: CALVIN (an autoregressive differentiable planner) and Diffusion Policy (a diffusion-based behavioral cloner). On GridMaze2D, the proposed method consistently outperforms CALVIN across all maze sizes and view ranges, with the largest gap appearing on 30×30 mazes (62.4% vs. 32.6%, a ~30-point improvement). Diffusion Policy is dramatically outperformed in every setting, achieving near-zero success on larger mazes because its pure behavior-cloning approach cannot handle dead ends or unknown goal locations under partial observability. On the AVD 3D benchmark, the zero-shot 2D-to-3D transfer (67.9%) is essentially on par with CALVIN-3D (68.2%), while the retrained variant (70.6%) surpasses CALVIN-3D by ~2.4 points and CALVIN-2D by ~7 points. The ablation further confirms that value guidance is the critical differentiator: removing it drops success from 90.6% to 11.4% on GridMaze2D, showing that the diffusion backbone alone is insufficient without the QMDP-based value function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: diffusion-based trajectory generation (Diffusion Policy [4], Planning with Diffusion [12]) and differentiable value-guided planning under partial observability (CALVIN [11], QMDP-Net [14], VINs [31]). It departs from prior diffusion-based planners by explicitly modeling partial observability through a POMDP formulation with a learned state-estimation module and QMDP value iteration, a setting no prior diffusion policy has addressed. It also departs from autoregressive differentiable planners like CALVIN by generating entire action trajectories simultaneously rather than step-by-step, and introduces a best-plan backtracking mechanism to mitigate covariate shift in receding-horizon re-planning. The 2D-to-3D transfer via semantic-segmentation-based point-cloud projection to BEV maps is a novel practical bridge that repurposes 2D-trained policies for 3D embodied navigation without additional 3D training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap by showing that diffusion-based trajectory planning can be made effective under partial observability when augmented with learned value guidance, and the zero-shot 2D-to-3D transfer via BEV projection offers a practical route around the data scarcity problem in real-world 3D navigation. However, performance still degrades noticeably in larger mazes (0.906 → 0.624 from 15×15 to 30×30), and the 3D evaluation is limited to 8 indoor scenes with grid-based actions, leaving open whether the approach scales to continuous action spaces, outdoor environments, or more complex 3D geometries. The continued reliance on expert demonstrations for training the diffusion policy (even if value guidance reduces sensitivity to their coverage) remains a constraint for fully autonomous deployment.
