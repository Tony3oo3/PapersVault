## Problem

In frustum-based BEV methods, the standard voxel pooling step accumulates image features from each 3D frustum point to a single nearest BEV grid center, introducing an irrecoverable position approximation error. This error is particularly detrimental in roadside scenarios, where the large perception range forces BEV grids to be sparse (e.g., 0.4 m spacing) to maintain real-time inference, amplifying the positional mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241267/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular roadside image (processed through a ResNet backbone + SECONDFPN to yield multi-scale features at 1/16 resolution) together with the camera extrinsic matrix E ∈ R^{3×4} and intrinsic matrix K ∈ R^{3×3}. It outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized by (x, y, z, l, w, h, r) over a configurable detection range (0–100 m or 0–200 m). As a plug-in, it replaces only the voxel pooling stage within an existing frustum-based pipeline (e.g., BEVHeight or BEVDepth), leaving the depth/height estimation and detection head unchanged.

## Method

BEVSpread replaces the single-grid accumulation with a spread operation: each 3D frustum point p is projected to BEV space, and its context feature is distributed to the top-k nearest BEV grid centers Ω_{p,k} with adaptive weights. The weight for each neighbor p̂ follows a Gaussian decay ω_{p,p̂} = exp(−d²_{p,p̂}/σ²), where the variance σ² = α·D_p is proportional to the predicted depth D_p, so distant objects (which occupy fewer image pixels) spread more broadly across neighboring grids. The scalar α is a learnable parameter constrained to [0, 2] that controls the overall decay speed. Customized CUDA kernels parallelize the neighbor lookup and weighted accumulation, keeping the additional pooling latency to roughly 0.8 ms at k=1 and 4.9 ms at k=2, comparable to the original single-grid pooling.

## Results

On DAIR-V2X-I (0–100 m, ResNet-101, 0.4 m grid), BEVSpread achieves AP₃D|R40 of 66.88 (Vehicle-Hard), 44.71 (Pedestrian-Hard), and 63.75 (Cyclist-Hard), outperforming the strongest prior method BEVHeight by (1.03, 5.25, 3.21) AP in the three categories. On Rope3D, BEVSpread reaches 70.03 (Vehicle-Hard), 34.25 (Pedestrian-Hard), and 59.03 (Cyclist-Hard), improving over BEVHeight by (2.54, 3.95, 2.13) AP. As a plug-in on BEVDepth, the method gains an even larger margin of (3.73, 8.72, 8.89) AP on DAIR-V2X-I, confirming its generality across frustum-based pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEVSpread is deployed as a plug-in on BEVHeight [44] and BEVDepth [16], the two strongest frustum-based roadside detectors, and outperforms both across all three object categories on both DAIR-V2X-I and Rope3D. Against BEVHeight (the direct host), gains are (1.03, 5.25, 3.21) AP on DAIR-V2X-I and (2.54, 3.95, 2.13) AP on Rope3D, with the largest relative improvement on pedestrians. Against BEVDepth, the margin is substantially larger—(3.21, 11.44, 8.41) AP on DAIR-V2X-I—because BEVDepth's depth-based frustum projection introduces greater positional uncertainty that spread pooling corrects more effectively. Compared to the transformer-based BEVFormer [17], BEVSpread leads by (16.15, 28.76, 41.69) AP on DAIR-V2X-I, reflecting the inherent advantage of explicit frustum geometry over query-based attention for roadside monocular settings. Against the older camera method ImVoxelNet [28], the gap is even wider (29.33, 37.97, 50.58) AP, confirming that the improvement is not merely an artifact of a stronger backbone. On the LiDAR baselines (PointPillars [12], SECOND [42], MVXNet [32]), BEVSpread surpasses all of them in pedestrian and cyclist AP while remaining competitive on vehicle AP, demonstrating that a single roadside camera with spread voxel pooling can match or exceed multi-sensor LiDAR pipelines for small-object detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper identifies a position approximation error in the voxel pooling step of frustum-based BEV methods (originating from LSS) that prior work has largely overlooked, as the field has focused on improving depth or height estimation for 2D-to-3D mapping. BEVSpread deploys as a plug-in on BEVHeight (the leading roadside BEV detector), replacing the single-grid accumulation with a Gaussian-weighted spread to top-k neighboring BEV grids, where the spread variance is modulated by predicted depth. The approach is conceptually distinct from prior voxel pooling variants like SA-BEVPool, which filter background points but still use single-grid accumulation for retained points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple modification to the pooling step—spreading features to neighboring grids with depth-adaptive Gaussian weights—yields large gains (especially +5.26 AP for pedestrians) suggests that position quantization error has been a silent bottleneck in frustum-based BEV detection, particularly in roadside scenarios where sparse grids are necessary for large perception ranges. The plug-in nature and CUDA-optimized implementation (k=2 adds no latency) make it immediately deployable on existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at even sparser grid resolutions, whether the online coordinate computation can be fully replaced by lookup tables in production, and whether the depth-dependent spread interacts favorably with temporal fusion or multi-camera settings.
