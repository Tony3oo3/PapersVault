## Problem

Existing cross-attention mechanisms in infrared-visible image fusion capture only second-order spatial interactions, neglecting higher-order interactions in both spatial and channel dimensions. This limitation restricts the model's ability to exploit synergistic correlations between modalities, as cascading self-attention blocks merely repeat second-order operations while incurring prohibitive computational costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240186/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel infrared image (H×W×1) and a three-channel visible image (H×W×3), extracts modality-specific features via separate convolution layers, and produces a single-channel fused image (H×W×1) targeting the Y channel in YCbCr color space. Training is unsupervised with 128×128 patches, guided by a saliency-weighted intensity loss and a gradient preservation loss that enforces the fused gradient to match the element-wise maximum of source gradients.

## Method

SHIP alternates spatial and channel high-order interactions over L iterations. For spatial interactions, it leverages the convolution theorem to replace the costly matrix multiplication in self-attention with an FFT-based Hadamard product, then recursively applies this element-wise operation to achieve N-order spatial interactions at negligible extra cost. For channel interactions, it extends the SE block's first-order mean-pooling recalibration into a high-order recursive formulation that progressively distinguishes inter-dependencies between modalities based on global statistics. The key insight is that the frequency-domain equivalence of correlation to element-wise multiplication enables arbitrary-order spatial modeling without the quadratic complexity of cascaded attention, while the channel extension moves beyond first-order statistics to capture richer global inter-modality relationships.

## Results

On the primary M3FD benchmark (300 test pairs), SHIP achieves the best score across all six metrics: SF=0.060, MI=4.813, VIF=0.922, AG=5.177, Q_abf=0.549, and FMI=0.892. The most striking margin is in mutual information (MI), where SHIP scores 4.813 versus 3.231 for the next-best method (SDNet), a ~49% improvement. On the cross-dataset generalization tests (RoadScene and TNO, 25 pairs each), SHIP similarly leads on all metrics, e.g., MI=3.914 on RoadScene and MI=3.849 on TNO. In the pan-sharpening extension, SHIP attains the best PSNR/SSIM/SAM/ERGAS on all three satellite datasets (WorldView II, GaoFen2, WorldView III), surpassing the second-best INNformer by 0.105 dB PSNR on GaoFen2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHIP outperforms all nine infrared-visible fusion baselines on every metric across all three datasets (M3FD, RoadScene, TNO), with no losses or ties. The largest relative gains appear in MI: SHIP scores 4.813 on M3FD versus 3.231 (SDNet, the closest competitor), 3.914 on RoadScene versus 3.464 (TarDAL), and 3.849 on TNO versus 2.648 (TarDAL). Among the GAN-based methods (DDcGAN, TarDAL), SHIP leads by a wide margin on MI and VIF, while among the CNN/Transformer-based methods (DenseFuse, U2Fusion, UMFusion, ReCoNet), the advantage is most pronounced in MI and Q_abf. On the pan-sharpening task, SHIP beats all six baselines (GS, PANNet, SRPPNN, GPPNN, MutNet, INNformer) on all four metrics across WorldView II, GaoFen2, and WorldView III, with the closest competitor being INNformer (e.g., PSNR 47.458 vs 47.353 on GaoFen2). Notably, DDcGAN achieves the second-best SF on M3FD (0.059 vs 0.060) but trails significantly on MI (2.540) and VIF (0.768), indicating that GAN-based texture generation does not compensate for weaker information transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two well-established building blocks—Transformer self-attention (which captures second-order spatial interactions via Q·K matrix multiplication) and the Squeeze-and-Excitation block (which captures first-order channel interactions via mean pooling)—into a unified high-order interaction framework. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that cascading standard self-attention layers only produces repeated second-order interactions, not genuinely higher-order ones, and that the convolution theorem allows replacing costly matrix multiplication with FFT-based element-wise (Hadamard) products to make N-order spatial interactions computationally tractable. In the infrared-visible fusion literature, this positions SHIP beyond prior transformer-based fusion methods (YDTR, SwinFusion, PanFormer) that remain confined to second-order spatial modeling, and beyond GAN- or AE-based approaches that lack explicit inter-modality interaction modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural limitation in transformer-based fusion: the inability to model interactions beyond second order without prohibitive computational cost. The FFT-based reformulation is a clean and practical trick that could be borrowed by other tasks requiring high-order spatial or channel interactions. However, the approach is validated primarily on infrared-visible fusion (a relatively narrow two-modal setting), and the pan-sharpening extension, while positive, shows only modest gains over strong transformer baselines. Open questions include whether the high-order interactions capture genuinely distinct synergistic information versus simply adding expressive capacity, whether the framework scales to more than two modalities, and whether the specific choice of N=5 and L=3 generalizes across scenes and modalities.
