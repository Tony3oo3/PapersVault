## Problem

Most pre-trained foundation models for remote sensing are designed for RGB imagery and fail to exploit the rich spectral information in multi/hyperspectral data. The closest prior work, SatMAE, uses a group-based masking strategy that suffers from inadequate inter-group spectral interaction, limited adaptability to varying band counts, and extra inductive bias from fixed band groupings. The paper asks whether a vanilla MAE with 3D random masking can learn strong spectral representations with minimal inductive bias across arbitrary numbers of bands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242632/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D spectral tensors of shape H×W×C (e.g., 96×96×12 for fMoW-S2, 128×128×12 for BigEarthNet, 64×64×13 for EuroSAT), partitioned into non-overlapping 3D patches of size 8×8×3. During pretraining, 90% of these 3D patches are randomly masked and the decoder reconstructs them via MSE loss; during downstream tasks, only the encoder is retained, producing a feature vector (via average pooling) that feeds task-specific heads for single-label classification, multi-label classification, or pixel-level change detection masks.

## Method

S2MAE extends the MAE asymmetric encoder-decoder framework to spectral data by replacing 2D patch masking with 3D random masking over spatial-spectral tensor cubes (p×p×k), which simultaneously captures local spectral continuity along the band axis and spatial invariance within each cube. Patches are projected via a linear layer plus separable learnable spatial and spectral positional embeddings (rather than a single 3D embedding), keeping embedding size manageable while decoupling the two axes. The encoder is a standard ViT operating only on the 10% visible patches, while a lightweight 4-block ViT decoder reconstructs the masked 90% using mask tokens. A key design choice is the 90% masking ratio (vs. the typical 75% for RGB), justified by the high redundancy in spectral bands. Progressive pretraining is performed in two stages—first on fMoW-S2 (712K images, 96×96) then continued on BigEarthNet (354K images, 128×128)—leveraging the transformer's adaptability to varying input sizes and geographic distributions without requiring a unified dataset.

## Results

On EuroSAT single-label classification, S2MAE achieves 99.16% top-1 accuracy (99.19% with progressive pretraining as S2MAE*), outperforming SatMAE (99.09%) and ViT pretrained on ImageNet-22k (98.91%). On BigEarthNet multi-label classification using only 10% of training data, S2MAE* reaches 87.41% mAP, surpassing SatMAE (84.93%) by 2.48 points and ViT-22k (84.67%) by 2.74 points. On OSCD change detection, S2MAE* attains the best F1 score of 54.26%, exceeding SatMAE (52.76%) by 1.50 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2MAE outperforms all four baselines across all three downstream tasks. Against SatMAE (the closest spectral-specific competitor), S2MAE gains 0.07% accuracy on EuroSAT, 0.66% mAP on BigEarthNet, and 0.52% F1 on OSCD; with progressive pretraining (S2MAE*), the BigEarthNet gap widens to 2.48% mAP. Compared to ViT pretrained on ImageNet-22k, S2MAE* leads by 2.74% mAP on BigEarthNet and 2.03% F1 on OSCD, while the EuroSAT margin is smaller (0.28%). Against ResNet50 (ImageNet-1k) and SeCo, the advantages are substantial: S2MAE* exceeds ResNet50 by 7.35% mAP and SeCo by 4.59% mAP on BigEarthNet. Notably, S2MAE achieves these results using only 10% of BigEarthNet training samples for fine-tuning, whereas most baselines use the full training set, underscoring the data efficiency of the 3D masked pretraining approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2MAE extends the masked autoencoder framework (MAE) by repurposing the 3D random masking strategy originally designed for video (MAE-ST) to the spectral remote sensing domain, replacing SatMAE's group-based masking with a bias-free 3D tensor-cube approach that captures local spectral continuity without fixed band-grouping inductive bias. It also draws on progressive/continual pretraining concepts from NLP, applying them to spectral RS data for the first time by sequentially training on fMoW-S2 and BigEarthNet with differing image sizes and geographic coverage. The separable spatial-spectral positional embedding design distinguishes it from both vanilla ViT positional encodings and the unified embedding used in the authors' extended SpectralGPT model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-inductive-bias recipe for spectral RS pretraining: a single 3D MAE architecture handles arbitrary band counts and image sizes, and the 90% masking ratio both improves representation quality and reduces pretraining compute. The progressive pretraining workflow (fMoW-S2 → BigEarthNet) demonstrates that diverse spectral datasets can be combined without unification, yielding meaningful downstream gains (e.g., 85.59 → 87.41 mAP on BigEarthNet with only 10% labeled data). Open questions remain around masking strategies for longer spectral sequences (hyperspectral data with hundreds of bands), the persistent computational cost of ViT at inference for downstream tasks, and whether the 3D masking advantage generalizes beyond Sentinel-2's 12-band setting.
