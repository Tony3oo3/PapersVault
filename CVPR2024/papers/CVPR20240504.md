## Problem

Closed-world semantic segmentation systems overconfidently assign pixels of previously unseen objects to known classes, failing to flag them as anomalies. While anomaly segmentation (binary known/unknown) and novel class discovery (distinguishing among multiple unknown categories) have been studied separately, jointly solving both in a single network while also providing a similarity score linking each novel class to its closest known category remains unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at arbitrary resolution and produces a pixel-wise open-world segmentation mask where known classes retain their original labels and each distinct novel class receives a unique new label (determined online via clustering of activation vectors). Additionally, it outputs a binary anomaly mask (known vs. unknown) and, for every discovered unknown class, a similarity score identifying the most similar known category via a Gaussian fitting score over pre-softmax feature vectors.

## Method

The architecture is a ResNet34 encoder (with NonBottleneck-1D blocks and a pyramid pooling module) feeding two parallel decoders built from SwiftNet modules. The semantic decoder is trained with a weighted cross-entropy loss plus a novel feature loss that pulls each pixel's pre-softmax activation toward a running per-class mean (normalized by per-class variance), effectively fitting a diagonal Gaussian to each known class in feature space. The contrastive decoder is trained with an objectosphere loss (pushing known-class feature norms above a threshold ξ while squashing unknown-class norms to zero) combined with a contrastive loss that scatters class means on a unit hypersphere. At test time, the two decoders are fused: the semantic decoder yields an unknown score via a squared-exponential kernel against each class Gaussian, the contrastive decoder yields one via feature-norm thresholding, and their average determines the final anomaly mask; unknown pixels are then clustered by activation-vector distance to form distinct novel classes, and the highest-scoring Gaussian identifies the most similar known class.

## Results

On the SegmentMeIfYouCan public benchmark, ContMAV achieves 90.2% AUPR, 3.8% FPR95, 54.5% sIoU, 61.9% PPV, and 63.6% mean F1, ranking first in FPR95, PPV, and mean F1 among all methods without external OoD data. On BDDAnomaly, the method achieves 96.1% AUPR and 6.9% FPR95, dramatically outperforming the best baseline (MaxLogit at 5.4% AUPR, 14.0% FPR95). The open-world variant incurs only a 0.3% mIoU drop on Cityscapes (70.8% vs. 71.1%) and 1.3% on BDDAnomaly (62.8% vs. 64.1%) compared to the closed-world model, confirming that anomaly detection does not degrade known-class segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SegmentMeIfYouCan, ContMAV (without OoD data) outperforms Maskomaly [1] on FPR95 (3.8% vs. 6.9%), PPV (61.9% vs. 51.2%), and mean F1 (63.6% vs. 49.9%), but trails it on AUPR (90.2% vs. 93.4%) and sIoU (54.5% vs. 55.4%). Against RbA [44] with OoD data, ContMAV wins on FPR95 (3.8% vs. 4.6%), PPV (61.9% vs. 47.5%), and mean F1 (63.6% vs. 51.9%), while RbA leads on AUPR (94.5% vs. 90.2%) and sIoU (64.9% vs. 54.5%). ContMAV dominates RbA without OoD and DenseHybrid [19] across all five metrics. On BDDAnomaly, the improvement is overwhelming: ContMAV reaches 96.1% AUPR and 6.9% FPR95, compared to MaxLogit [23] (5.4%, 14.0%), MC Dropout [16] (4.3%, 16.6%), MaxSoftmax [22] (3.7%, 24.5%), Confidence [14] (3.9%, 24.5%), and Background [6] (1.1%, 40.1%)—a gap of over 90 AUPR points over the strongest baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the objectosphere loss framework [15] and contrastive learning [11] from the classification setting to dense pixel-wise prediction, combining them in a dedicated contrastive decoder that pushes known-class features onto a hypersphere surface while collapsing unknown-class features toward the origin. It departs from prior anomaly segmentation approaches that rely on uncertainty estimation (Bayesian methods, MC dropout), generative reconstruction, or additional out-of-distribution training data by instead operating directly on the semantic segmentation feature space: a novel feature loss constrains each known class's activations to a per-class Gaussian, and a squared-exponential kernel over these Gaussians provides the anomaly score. The dual-decoder architecture (semantic + contrastive) fusing both signals in post-processing is the paper's structural contribution, building on standard encoder-decoder CNNs (ResNet34 with NonBottleneck-1D blocks, SwiftNet, PPM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach achieves state-of-the-art anomaly segmentation on SegmentMeIfYouCan (top-1 in three of five metrics) while preserving competitive closed-world mIoU, and uniquely provides per-pixel class similarity scores that are directly useful for downstream autonomous-driving tasks such as motion planning and map maintenance. However, the class similarity accuracy remains moderate (≈59% for motorcycle, ≈50% for train), and the method has only been validated on driving-domain datasets with a relatively small backbone, leaving open whether the Gaussian feature-space approach scales to larger class taxonomies or non-driving domains. The reliance on a running mean/variance accumulated during training also raises questions about robustness to gradual domain shift at deployment time.
