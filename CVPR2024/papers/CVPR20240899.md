## Problem

Cloud ML platforms face a fundamental tension: strong privacy protections (differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, or heavy inter-environment communication) either degrade model utility or incur prohibitive computational and communication costs. Existing private training/inference frameworks that split work between trusted execution environments and public GPUs still suffer from high complexity due to frequent, costly data exchanges between the two sides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240899/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels. Unusually, the input is consumed asymmetrically: after a private backbone extracts intermediate representations, the IR is split into a low-dimensional sensitive component (r principal channels, reduced spatial size via DCT) that never leaves the private environment, and a high-dimensional residual that is Gaussian-perturbed, binarized, and offloaded to the public environment. The final prediction is the element-wise sum of logits from both sub-models, computed privately.

## Method

Delta exploits the asymmetric structure of neural network intermediate representations—most information concentrates in a low-rank, low-frequency subspace—by decomposing IRs via SVD (channel dimension) and DCT (spatial dimension) into a compact IR_main and a high-dimensional IR_res. A purpose-built low-complexity model M_main (with reduced channel counts justified by a rank-preservation theorem for convolutional layers) processes IR_main entirely within the private environment, while the residuals are protected by a Gaussian mechanism and binary quantization (32× communication reduction) before being fed to a large residual model M_res on public GPUs. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the backbone and M_main are trained privately with no data leaving the enclave; second, the backbone is frozen, cached perturbed residuals are reused for M_res, and joint training continues with a private backpropagation scheme where M_main sees combined logits but M_res only sees its own, preventing logit leakage. The DP analysis shows that because residuals carry little information, the required noise is far smaller than in naive-DP (which perturbs the full IR), yielding up to 31% accuracy improvement under the same privacy budget.

## Results

Under the same privacy budget (ε=1.4), Delta achieves 92.4% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 71.4% on CIFAR-100, and 65.9% on ImageNet, compared to 69.6%, 48.3%, and 34.4% for the naive-DP baseline that directly perturbs the full IR. In terms of running time (ResNet-18/CIFAR-100, batch=32), Delta completes training in 62 ms per iteration (22× faster than private-only execution) and inference in 20 ms (25× faster), substantially outpacing 3LegRace (237 ms train, 95 ms infer) and Slalom (84 ms infer). Against model inversion (SecretRevealer), DP with ε=1.4 reduces reconstruction SSIM from 0.18 to 0.09 and target-model accuracy on reconstructed images from 6.75% to 2.13%, approaching the no-residuals lower bound (SSIM 0.08, Acc 1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the naive-DP baseline (directly adding Gaussian noise to the full intermediate representation), Delta improves top-1 accuracy by 22.8 points on CIFAR-10 (92.4% vs. 69.6%), 23.1 points on CIFAR-100 (71.4% vs. 48.3%), and 31.5 points on ImageNet (65.9% vs. 34.4%) under the identical ε=1.4 privacy budget, demonstrating that the asymmetric decomposition allows far less noise to be added while preserving the same DP guarantee. In running-time comparisons (ResNet-18/CIFAR-100, batch 32), Delta achieves a 22× speedup over private-only execution for training (62 ms vs. 1372 ms) and a 25× speedup for inference (20 ms vs. 510 ms). Compared to 3LegRace [41], Delta is 3.8× faster in training (62 ms vs. 237 ms) and 4.8× faster in inference (20 ms vs. 95 ms), primarily because Delta avoids the frequent inter-environment communication that 3LegRace requires. Against Slalom [55] (private inference only), Delta is 4.2× faster in inference (20 ms vs. 84 ms). The paper does not report a direct accuracy comparison against 3LegRace or Slalom, as those systems use different model architectures and privacy mechanisms; the accuracy comparison is exclusively against the naive-DP scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

Delta builds directly on 3LegRace [41], which first observed that intermediate representations in CNNs exhibit low-rank structure along the channel dimension, but departs by exploiting this property to design a dedicated low-complexity model (M_main) that runs entirely in the private environment, rather than merely using it to reduce communication. The paper also positions itself against two broader PPML paradigms—DP-based perturbation of raw data (which degrades utility) and cryptographic approaches (which are computationally prohibitive)—and against prior TEE+GPU frameworks like Slalom that suffer from heavy inter-environment communication. The key conceptual advance is the two-axis asymmetric decomposition (SVD for channels, DCT for spatial frequency) that concentrates sensitive information into a compact low-dimensional representation while pushing information-insensitive residuals to the public side, combined with binary quantization to further reduce communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 31% accuracy gap over naive-DP under identical privacy budgets (ε=1.4) and the 22×/25× speedups over private-only execution make Delta a practically compelling option for cloud ML platforms that must balance privacy guarantees with GPU-level throughput. However, the evaluation is limited to ResNet-18/34 on image classification, the threat model is semi-honest (no protection against malicious adversaries), and the SVD/DCT decomposition parameters are hand-tuned per dataset, leaving open whether the asymmetric structure generalizes to transformers, video, or other modalities. The paper also notes that verifiable computing could extend Delta to malicious settings, and the framework is amenable to federated learning, but neither direction is explored experimentally.
