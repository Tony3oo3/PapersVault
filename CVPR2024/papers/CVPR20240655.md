## Problem

Point clouds from RGB-D cameras (dense, uniform) and LiDAR (sparse, large-scale) exhibit significant domain gaps that prevent a single network architecture from handling both effectively. Point-based methods capture fine geometric detail but scale poorly to LiDAR, while voxel-based methods are efficient but lose local structure at deeper stages. Existing point-voxel hybrids (PVCNN, SPVCNN, PVT) fail to meaningfully transfer point-level geometric information into voxel representations, leaving a gap in universal multi-sensor 3D representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds with sensor-specific features (RGB and surface normals for RGB-D cameras; intensity for LiDAR), voxelized at 0.05 m resolution. The primary output is per-voxel semantic segmentation labels produced by a sparse-convolutional decoder, while an auxiliary output provides per-point labels from a point decoder operating on local patches of at most 2×10⁴ points. Critically, the point branch and its associated geometry pools are entirely discarded at inference, so the deployed model processes only voxels.

## Method

GeoAuxNet introduces geometry-to-voxel auxiliary learning: a voxel-guided dynamic point network (a hypernetwork conditioned on relative intra-voxel positions, voxel features, and per-stage latent codes) extracts fine-grained point-level geometric features on local patches, which are then stored in sensor-aware hierarchical geometry pools of increasing capacity (32→256 entries) across network stages. Pools are updated via a similarity-thresholded merge strategy (cosine similarity ≥ 0.9 triggers exponential moving-average blending; otherwise new entries are appended or randomly sampled to respect the pool cap). At each stage, a cross-attention mechanism queries the pool with the current voxel feature to retrieve complementary geometric patterns, which are concatenated back into the voxel representation before subsequent sparse convolutions. Training is multi-task (per-voxel + per-point cross-entropy, weighted 1:0.1), and because the point network is only active during training, inference retains the efficiency of a pure voxel backbone while benefiting from point-level geometric priors.

## Results

GeoAuxNet, trained jointly on S3DIS, ScanNet, and SemanticKITTI with a shared ~65M-parameter backbone, achieves 69.5% mIoU / 74.5% mAcc on S3DIS Area 5, 71.3% mIoU / 79.3% mAcc on ScanNet val, and 63.8% mIoU / 69.3% mAcc on SemanticKITTI val. These results surpass the strongest joint-training baseline (PPT†) by 1.6–2.0 mIoU on each dataset and outperform SPVCNN by over 10 mIoU on every benchmark. On inference efficiency, GeoAuxNet runs at 51.8 ms per scene with 572 instances/sec throughput, comparable to pure voxel-based methods and 16.8× faster than PointTransformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against joint-training baselines, GeoAuxNet clearly wins: it beats PPT† (the strongest joint baseline) by +1.6 mIoU on S3DIS, +1.7 on ScanNet, and +2.0 on SemanticKITTI, and outperforms SPVCNN by 10.9, 14.6, and 11.8 mIoU respectively. Compared to single-dataset experts on RGB-D data, GeoAuxNet is competitive but slightly behind: it trails StratifiedFormer by 2.5 mIoU on S3DIS and 3.0 on ScanNet, and PointNeXt by 1.0 and 0.2 mIoU, while matching or exceeding PointTransformer on ScanNet (71.3 vs 70.6). On SemanticKITTI, GeoAuxNet (63.8 mIoU) falls short of the LiDAR-specific experts SphereFormer (67.8) and Cylinder3D (64.3), though it surpasses the joint-trained SPVCNN (52.0) by 11.8 mIoU. In efficiency, GeoAuxNet (51.8 ms, 572 ins/s) is 16.8× faster than PointTransformer and 2.3× faster than PPT† while maintaining higher accuracy, and is comparable to MinkowskiNet (45.4 ms) and SPVCNN (48.4 ms) in inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoAuxNet builds on the MinkowskiNet sparse convolutional backbone and the multi-dataset training paradigm of PPT, but departs fundamentally from prior point-voxel hybrid methods (PVCNN, SPVCNN, PVT) that couple point and voxel branches only through voxelization/devoxelization. Instead of running a point network at inference, it introduces hierarchical geometry pools updated by a voxel-guided dynamic point network during training, then accesses stored point-level geometric features via cross-attention at inference. This positions the work at the intersection of multi-domain learning (analogous to Universal-RCNN and CDCL in 2D) and 3D point cloud representation learning, specifically addressing the sensor-domain gap that prior multi-dataset 3D methods (e.g., PPT) have not resolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single voxel-based model can approach single-dataset expert performance across fundamentally different sensors (dense RGB-D vs. sparse LiDAR) without sacrificing inference efficiency, which is practically important for systems that must handle heterogeneous sensor inputs. The failure of PPT's language-guided alignment to converge on cross-sensor data highlights that sensor diversity in 3D is a harder domain gap than previously assumed. Open questions include whether the geometry pool mechanism generalizes to other 3D tasks (detection, instance segmentation), whether pools can be pre-trained and frozen for new sensors, and whether the approach scales to even larger outdoor scenes beyond the tested datasets.
