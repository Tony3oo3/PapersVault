## Problem

Existing multimodal generative models rely on diffusion backbones that lack in-context understanding, cannot follow modality-interleaved instructions (e.g., text mixed with images and audio), and require task-specific finetuning for zero-shot fine-grained control such as exemplar learning or subject-driven generation. Additionally, prior models are limited to one or two input modalities and struggle to maintain consistency across multi-round interactive conversations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240354/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes truly modality-interleaved sequences where text instructions are embedded with inline image, audio, and video-frame tokens (e.g., 'A cat sitting on <image>[features]</image> is making the sound of <audio>[features]</audio>'), and produces any combination of text, images, audio, or synchronized video+sound as output. For higher-fidelity editing tasks, the original input image or audio is additionally concatenated into the diffusion noise to preserve perceptual features. In-context example pairs (input→output demonstrations) are provided inline within the prompt to enable few-shot transfer without finetuning.

## Method

CoDi-2 uses Llama-2-7B as a reasoning 'brain' that processes modality-interleaved inputs via ImageBind-aligned encoders projecting vision and audio features into the LLM's token space, with special delimiters marking each modality span. For generation, the LLM autoregressively predicts conditional features (c = C_y(y)) that are fed to synchronized diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 2.1 for images, AudioLDM2 for audio) rather than generating discrete tokens directly. The training loss combines three terms: MSE between the LLM's predicted features and the target modality's encoder features, the diffusion model's denoising loss (which backpropagates perceptual supervision into the LLM), and standard text token prediction loss. A key data-construction insight is that since all modalities share an aligned encoder space, text-only datasets (e.g., Alpaca) can be repurposed for interleaved multimodal in-context learning by replacing selected phrases with their text-encoder feature embeddings, effectively simulating multimodal inputs without requiring actual image or audio data.

## Results

On DreamBench (subject-driven image generation), CoDi-2 achieves DINO 0.703, CLIP-I 0.852, and CLIP-T 0.311, surpassing all tuning-free baselines and even the fine-tuned DreamBooth on CLIP-T. On MS-COCO image editing, CoDi-2 scores CLIPSIM 0.147, between SDEdit (0.134) and InstructPix2Pix (0.151). On AudioSet audio editing, CoDi-2 achieves the best scores across all three tasks (adding, dropping, replacement) on all three metrics (LSD, KL, FD), e.g., LSD 1.21 / KL 0.88 / FD 19.72 for adding, outperforming AUDIT (1.35 / 0.92 / 21.80) and all SDEdit variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DreamBench subject-driven generation, CoDi-2 outperforms all three tuning-free baselines: it beats Kosmos-G (DINO 0.703 vs 0.694, CLIP-I 0.852 vs 0.847, CLIP-T 0.311 vs 0.287) and Re-Imagen (0.703 vs 0.600, 0.852 vs 0.740, 0.311 vs 0.270), and also surpasses the fine-tuned DreamBooth on CLIP-T (0.311 vs 0.305) while trailing on DINO (0.703 vs 0.668) and CLIP-I (0.852 vs 0.803). On MS-COCO image editing, CoDi-2 (CLIPSIM 0.147) beats SDEdit-1/2T (0.134) but falls slightly short of InstructPix2Pix (0.151), suggesting the LLM-guided approach is competitive but not yet superior to dedicated instruction-editing models on this narrow task. On AudioSet audio editing, CoDi-2 achieves the best LSD, KL, and FD scores across all three sub-tasks (adding, dropping, replacement), consistently outperforming AUDIT by 0.1–0.14 in LSD and 2.3–4.3 in FD, and beating all SDEdit variants by a wider margin (e.g., FD 19.72 vs 28.25 for SDEdit-1/4T on adding). Overall, CoDi-2 wins or ties on 5 of 6 baselines across the evaluated tasks, with the sole loss being a narrow CLIPSIM gap versus InstructPix2Pix on image editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDi-2 directly extends CoDi [32], the first any-to-any composable diffusion model, by replacing the diffusion-model-only backbone with an LLM (Llama-2) as the reasoning engine that understands modality-interleaved instructions and in-context examples, while retaining diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, AudioLDM2) as synchronized decoders. It also builds on the multimodal in-context learning line (Kosmos-G [25], MIMIC-IT [16]) and the 'images as foreign language' paradigm, but generalizes these ideas from image-only composition to a unified text-vision-audio-video setting with multi-round interaction. The key architectural departure from prior MGMs is that the LLM performs the in-context reasoning and instruction following, whereas diffusion models are demoted to pure generation decoders whose training loss backpropagates into the LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoDi-2 demonstrates that a single LLM-centric architecture can handle a wide spectrum of multimodal generation tasks (editing, exemplar learning, subject-driven generation, audio manipulation, video+sound prediction) in both zero-shot and few-shot settings without task-specific fine-tuning, pointing toward a GPT-like unified multimodal system. However, the empirical evaluation is relatively narrow (DreamBench, MS-COCO, AudioSet) with limited baselines, and the in-context learning capability—central to the paper's claim—is demonstrated qualitatively rather than through rigorous ablation or scaling analysis. Open questions include whether the LLM-as-brain approach can match specialized models on individual tasks at scale, how well it generalizes to truly novel in-context examples beyond training distribution, and the computational cost of multi-round interleaved generation.
