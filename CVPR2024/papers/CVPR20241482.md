## Problem

Existing methods for learning audio-visual correspondence rely on curated datasets with known action-sound pairings or fixed taxonomies, limiting the scope of discoverable sounding actions. In-the-wild egocentric video contains a long tail of subtle, scenario-dependent action sounds, but naively applying contrastive audio-visual learning captures general correlations (e.g., traffic noise on a street) rather than the specific sounds produced by the camera wearer's action. The paper asks: can we learn which actions sound and how they sound from narrated egocentric video without any audio labels?

## Input / Output

The model consumes 1.5-second video clips (sampled 0.5 s before to 1 s after narration timestamps), their synchronized audio, and free-form text narrations describing the camera wearer's current action. It produces unit-normalized embeddings for each modality via separate encoders (TimeSformer, AST, DistillBERT). At inference, language is not required: the primary output is a cosine similarity score between audio and video embeddings indicating whether the visible action sounds, which supports discovery, cross-modal retrieval, and linear-probe/fine-tuned audio classification.

## Method

MC3 (Multimodal Contrastive-Consensus Coding) is a two-stage self-supervised training framework. In the align stage, pairwise InfoNCE contrastive losses are applied across all three modality pairs (audio-video, audio-language, video-language) to establish initial cross-modal alignment. In the refine stage, a novel consensus loss is added: for each sample, the minimum (bottleneck) pairwise cosine similarity—computed relative to an anchor modality (audio) after modality-specific rescaling—is taken as the consensus target, and all other modality pairs are pushed to match it via an L2 loss, while the contrastive losses continue to be optimized jointly. This mechanism ensures that audio-visual embeddings are pulled together only when the language narration also agrees with both, effectively filtering out cases where audio and video co-occur but are not caused by the camera wearer's action. The two-stage schedule is critical: directly optimizing the combined loss from random initialization fails, as confirmed by ablation.

## Results

On the primary sounding action discovery task (Ego4D, 30K test clips), MC3 achieves 0.598 ROC-AUC and 0.666 PR-AUC for audio-visual discovery, and 0.658 ROC-AUC / 0.715 PR-AUC for audio-language discovery, outperforming the strongest baselines (ImageBind: 0.554/0.605 AV; CMC: 0.635/0.693 AL) by 4–7 AUC points. On cross-modal retrieval, MC3 reaches 72.8% Recall@10 for video-to-audio and 88.5% for language-to-audio, surpassing CMC (67.9% and 81.8%) by 5–7 points. On the EPIC-Sounds audio classification benchmark, MC3's fine-tuned model achieves 55.97% Top-1 and 0.885 mAUC, beating both the self-supervised SSAST (53.47% Top-1) and the supervised ASF (53.75% Top-1) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC3 outperforms all six baselines on the primary sounding action discovery task. Against the two-modality baselines, MC3 beats CM-ACC [35] by 5.8 ROC-AUC points on AV discovery (0.598 vs 0.540) and CLAP [14] by 2.1 points on AL discovery (0.658 vs 0.637). Against the multi-modality baselines, MC3 surpasses CMC [55] (joint contrastive training) by 4.8 AV ROC points and ImageBind [23] (sequential alignment) by 4.4 AV ROC points, with similar margins on AL. On EPIC-Sounds audio classification, MC3's fine-tuned model (55.97% Top-1, 0.885 mAUC) beats the self-supervised SSAST [25] (53.47% Top-1, 0.879 mAUC) and the supervised ASF [31] (53.75% Top-1, 0.873 mAUC) on Top-1 and mAUC, though ASF retains a slight edge on MAP (0.254 vs 0.242). In the linear-probe setting, MC3 (42.44% Top-1) substantially outperforms SSAST (28.74%) but trails the supervised ASF (45.53%), confirming that MC3's self-supervised pretraining yields strong but not fully supervised-level audio representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of learning audio-visual correspondences via curated datasets with predefined action taxonomies (Greatest Hits, DiffImpact, Swoosh) or general self-supervised audio-visual alignment (CM-ACC, ImageBind, CMC). Its key conceptual departure is the use of free-form narrations from egocentric video as weak semantic supervision to isolate *action-specific* audio-visual correspondence, rather than any co-occurring audio-visual correlation. The consensus coding loss is novel in that it explicitly models the case where modalities disagree (e.g., a visible but silent action, or an audible but off-screen action), whereas prior multimodal contrastive methods (CMC, ImageBind, VATT) implicitly assume all modalities share information. The two-stage align-then-refine training is motivated by the optimization difficulty of jointly optimizing contrastive and reconstruction objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that the long tail of sounding actions can be discovered organically from narrated in-the-wild egocentric video without any audio labels, and the 33K-clip professional annotation set provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating such models. The consensus mechanism offers a general principle for multimodal learning that could extend beyond audio-visual-language to other modality combinations where not all views are informative. Limitations include reliance on a single training dataset (Ego4D), the empirical choice of audio as the consensus anchor, and the necessity of two-stage training (suggesting the loss landscape is fragile). The authors note future work on asynchronous multimodal streams, and the approach has not been validated on substantially different video domains or scales.
