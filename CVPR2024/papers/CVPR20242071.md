## Problem

3D visual grounding models typically require dense object-level classification labels for every bounding box in a scene, which is expensive and difficult to annotate. In the more practical naturally supervised setting—where models see only 3D scenes and question-answer pairs—prior neuro-symbolic and end-to-end methods underperform significantly, lacking the structured inductive biases needed to learn from indirect supervision alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242071/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB-colored 3D point cloud scene (C×6) and a natural language utterance describing a target object's spatial relation to other objects. Object detections are generated by VoteNet rather than ground-truth bounding boxes, and no classification labels are provided during training. The output is the index of the target object among all detected objects, determined by argmax over the final score vector produced by executing a symbolic program.

## Method

LARC builds on the NS3D neuro-symbolic concept learner and injects language-derived constraints as regularization on its structured intermediate representations. Three constraint types—symmetry, exclusivity, and synonymity—are distilled from GPT-3.5 by prompting it to classify relational and object concepts, then enforced via dedicated losses: an L1 sparsity loss on probability matrices, a Frobenius-norm symmetry loss (‖P − Pᵀ‖²), and an L1 exclusivity loss (‖max(0,P) ⊙ max(0,P)ᵀ‖₁). Synonymity is applied through data augmentation, where parsed symbolic programs are duplicated with a randomly selected LLM-identified synonym substituted, encouraging similar execution traces. At inference, LLM-specified composition rules (e.g., center = left ∧ right) allow zero-shot execution of unseen concepts by combining learned probability matrices, while the overall training objective is simply the target prediction cross-entropy plus weighted regularization terms.

## Results

On ReferIt3D (SR3D setting, naturally supervised), LARC achieves 36.6% test accuracy, 34.5% symmetric-concept accuracy, and 38.4% exclusive-concept accuracy. This represents a 9-point improvement over the prior neuro-symbolic baseline NS3D (27.6% test accuracy) and outperforms all end-to-end methods except BUTD-DETR (38.5%), which uses classification labels from a pretrained detector as input. LARC also demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to unseen ternary relations (37.4% vs. 10.5% for the best prior method) and antonyms (32.6% vs. 22.0%), as well as transfer to ScanRefer (32.9% vs. 22.4% for NS3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LARC substantially outperforms the prior neuro-symbolic method NS3D by 9.0 points on test accuracy (36.6% vs. 27.6%), 9.6 points on symmetric concepts, and 7.2 points on exclusive concepts, all in the naturally supervised setting without object-level classification labels. Among end-to-end baselines, LARC surpasses MVT (35.7%), LAR (32.2%), TransRefer3D (30.6%), and LanguageRefer (29.8%) by 0.9, 4.4, 6.0, and 6.8 points respectively. LARC trails BUTD-DETR (38.5%) by 1.9 points, though BUTD-DETR uses classification labels from a pretrained detector as additional input, a form of supervision LARC does not employ. On zero-shot generalization to unseen ternary relations, LARC achieves 37.4% versus 10.5% (MVT, best prior), and on antonym generalization it reaches 32.6% versus 22.0% (MVT). For transfer to the unseen ScanRefer dataset, LARC scores 32.9% compared to 22.4% for NS3D and 18.1% for BUTD-DETR, a 14.8-point margin over the strongest end-to-end baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

LARC builds directly on NS3D, a neuro-symbolic concept learner that decomposes 3D referring expressions into symbolic programs executed by modular neural networks. The key departure is shifting from dense supervision (per-object classification labels) to a naturally supervised setting using only scene-QA pairs, with language-based constraints (symmetry, exclusivity, synonymity) distilled from LLMs injected as regularization on the structured intermediate representations. This contrasts with prior neuro-symbolic works that use LLMs solely for parsing language into programs, and with constraint-based learning literature that typically encodes expert-defined logical rules rather than language-semantic relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that simple, broadly applicable language priors can substitute for expensive 3D annotations, making structured visual reasoning more practical in data-scarce settings. The zero-shot composition of unseen concepts (e.g., 'center' from 'left' and 'right') and strong transfer to ScanRefer suggest a path toward more flexible 3D reasoning systems. However, the evaluation is limited to a single primary dataset (ReferIt3D), the antonym test set is very small (50 examples), and the composition rules are manually selected as a proof of concept rather than fully automated, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex or open-vocabulary scenes.
