## Problem

Learning 3D scene flow from LiDAR point clouds suffers from a critical data gap: synthetic training sets (e.g., FlyingThings3D) generalize poorly to real sparse LiDAR scenes, while real-world per-point motion labels are prohibitively expensive to annotate. Existing supervised methods trained on synthetic data show order-of-magnitude performance drops on real datasets (e.g., GMSF drops from 0.056 m EPE3D on stereoKITTI to >10 m on Argoverse), and unsupervised methods still trail supervised baselines, leaving a persistent accuracy gap for LiDAR-based autonomous driving.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes two consecutive raw LiDAR point clouds (source and target, sampled to 8192 points with ground points filtered), a set of initial 3D anchor boxes parameterized by center, size, and heading, and coarse per-point normals computed via Open3D. It outputs per-point 3D scene flow pseudo labels (a 3×N vector of displacement vectors) derived from the difference between the augmented-warped source cloud and the original source cloud, along with the optimized box motion parameters and augmented global/local motion attributes. No object tracking, semantic segmentation, or ego-motion ground truth is required at any stage.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes scene motion into global ego-motion (R_ego, t_ego) and per-object rigid motions (R_perbox, t_perbox, motion probability P_M) by optimizing differentiable 3D bounding boxes against the real target point cloud using background and foreground losses (nearest-neighbor matching plus pairwise distance with location encoding). A key novelty is a global-local data augmentation module that perturbs rotation via Lie algebra (Rodrigues' formula), translation, and motion probability with Gaussian noise applied selectively to dynamic objects, generating diverse motion patterns from a single source frame. The pseudo labels are then obtained by warping each source point with its assigned (augmented) motion parameters and subtracting the original position, yielding a perfectly paired training set. The framework is plug-and-play: it feeds these synthetic labels into standard supervised training of existing architectures (FLOT, MSBRN, GMSF), achieving 95–99% EPE3D reduction over their unsupervised counterparts on LiDAR KITTI, Argoverse, and nuScenes without any manual annotation.

## Results

On LiDAR KITTI, the proposed 3DSFlabelling (with GMSF backbone) achieves an EPE3D of 0.0078 m, representing a ~24× reduction over the supervised GMSF baseline (0.190 m) and outperforming all prior supervised, unsupervised, and optimization-based methods. On Argoverse, EPE3D drops to 0.0093 m (vs. 7.28 m for GMSF trained on synthetic data), and on nuScenes it reaches 0.0185 m (vs. 9.42 m for GMSF). The method also achieves Acc3DS above 0.95 on all three datasets, with the highest being 0.9924 on LiDAR KITTI, while requiring no manual scene flow labels or synthetic training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all 16 baselines across every dataset and metric. On LiDAR KITTI, it reduces EPE3D from 0.085 m (RSF, best optimization method) and 0.094 m (R3DSF, best weakly supervised) to 0.0078 m, and from 0.0139 m (MSBRN, best supervised) to 0.0078 m. On Argoverse, the improvement is most dramatic: GMSF trained on synthetic FT3D data fails catastrophically (EPE3D = 7.28 m), while 3DSFlabelling + GMSF achieves 0.0093 m — a 99.9% reduction. On nuScenes, the same pattern holds (9.42 m → 0.0185 m). Even the strongest self-supervised baselines (Mittal et al. at 0.977 m, Jiang et al. at 0.491 m on KITTI) are outperformed by over an order of magnitude. The method also wins on efficiency: FLOT+3DSFlabelling uses only 0.1M parameters and runs in 78 ms, surpassing heavier optimization methods like MBNSF (5000+ ms) and NSFP (2864 ms) in both accuracy and speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the optimization-based rigid scene flow framework from RSF [5] (and the rigid motion prior from RigidFlow [22]) from a test-time estimation tool into a training-time pseudo-label generator. While prior optimization methods (RSF, NSFP, FastNSF) solve scene flow at inference with high computational cost, and supervised methods rely on synthetic data (FT3D) that fails to generalize to real LiDAR, this work inverts the pipeline: it runs box-parameter optimization offline on unlabelled real LiDAR pairs to produce dense per-point flow labels, then trains a fast neural network (FLOT, MSBRN, or GMSF) on those labels. The global-local data augmentation via Lie algebra perturbation of ego-motion and per-box motion parameters is a novel contribution not present in prior scene flow augmentation, which typically applies only uniform spatial transforms to input points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method directly addresses the annotation bottleneck in 3D scene flow for autonomous driving, eliminating the need for both synthetic training data and manual per-point flow labels while achieving near-perfect accuracy (Acc3DS > 0.97) on three real LiDAR benchmarks. The plug-and-play nature—demonstrated across three architecturally distinct backbones—makes it immediately adoptable. Key limitations include the rigid-body assumption (inapplicable to deformable objects like pedestrians or cyclists), dependence on initial anchor box placement, and the fact that pseudo-label quality is bounded by the optimization's ability to recover true motion from sparse LiDAR. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-rigid or multi-modal (camera+LiDAR) settings, and how it behaves in highly dynamic urban scenes with many overlapping objects.
