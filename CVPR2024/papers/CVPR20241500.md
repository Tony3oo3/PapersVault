## Problem

Existing few-shot segmentation methods generate prior masks from frozen ImageNet-pretrained CNN features, which suffer from coarse granularity and strong category bias (e.g., over-responding to 'Person'), leading to incorrect target localization and poor generalization to novel classes. This paper asks whether the visual-text alignment capability of CLIP can replace purely visual feature similarity to produce more accurate and class-agnostic prior guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a support image with its ground-truth mask, a query image, and the target class name as text. All images are resized to 473×473 and processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B-16 encoder. The output is a pair of prior information maps (refined VTP and initial VVP) that directly replace the original prior channel in any existing few-shot segmentation decoder, ultimately producing a pixel-wise segmentation mask. The prior generation itself is entirely training-free; only the downstream FSS model is trained (30 epochs, batch size 4 for 1-shot / 2 for 5-shot).

## Method

PI-CLIP generates two complementary priors from the frozen CLIP model. The Visual-Text Prior (VTP) constructs target and non-target prompts ('a photo of {class}' / 'a photo without {class}'), encodes them with CLIP's text encoder, and applies softmax-GradCAM to produce a pixel-level classification map that precisely localizes the target region. The Visual-Visual Prior (VVP) computes per-pixel cosine similarity between the mask-weighted support features and query features, taking the maximum match per query pixel to yield a broader, more general localization. A Prior Information Refinement (PIR) module then builds a high-order matrix R = max(D, D·Dᵀ) from the Sinkhorn-normalized average of the last 8 CLIP attention blocks and applies it (via Hadamard product with a box mask) to refine only the VTP, recovering whole-object structure while suppressing non-target responses. The refined VTP and unrefined VVP are concatenated to replace the original prior in baselines such as HDMNet, BAM, and PFENet, yielding plug-and-play improvements without modifying the segmentation backbone.

## Results

On PASCAL-5i, PI-CLIP plugged into HDMNet achieves 76.8% mIoU (1-shot) and 77.2% mIoU (5-shot), setting new state-of-the-art by +7.4% and +5.4% over the HDMNet baseline respectively. On COCO-20i, PI-CLIP (HDMNet) reaches 56.8% mIoU (1-shot) and 59.1% mIoU (5-shot), improving over HDMNet by +6.8% and +3.1%. The method is training-free for prior generation and requires only 30 epochs of training for the segmentation backbone, less than any prior method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PI-CLIP is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on three baselines (HDMNet, BAM, PFENet) and consistently outperforms each. On PASCAL-5i 1-shot, PI-CLIP (HDMNet) achieves 76.8% mIoU vs. HDMNet's 69.4% (+7.4%), BAM's 67.8% (+5.9%), and PFENet's 60.8% (+10.4%). On PASCAL-5i 5-shot, the gains are +5.4% over HDMNet (77.2% vs. 71.8%), +3.8% over BAM (74.7% vs. 70.9%), and +10.9% over PFENet (72.8% vs. 61.9%). On the more challenging COCO-20i, PI-CLIP (HDMNet) reaches 56.8% (1-shot) and 59.1% (5-shot) mIoU, surpassing HDMNet by +6.8% and +3.1% respectively, and outperforming the next-best methods (MIANet at 47.7%/51.7%, MSI at 46.8%/52.0%) by a wide margin. The consistent improvements across all three baselines demonstrate that the CLIP-based prior generation is architecture-agnostic and provides complementary information to existing matching mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in few-shot segmentation where prior masks are generated from frozen ImageNet-pretrained CNN features, which the authors argue suffer from category bias and coarse granularity. Instead, it repurposes CLIP's visual-text alignment (via softmax-GradCAM, borrowed from Lin et al. 2023) and visual-visual matching to produce two complementary prior signals: an accurate but locally-focused visual-text prior (VTP) and a more general visual-visual prior (VVP). A high-order attention matrix built from CLIP's self-attention maps (with Sinkhorn normalization) refines VTP to recover global structure. The resulting module is explicitly training-free and plug-and-play, directly replacing the prior channel in existing FSS backbones (PFENet, BAM, HDMNet) without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design makes this a low-cost drop-in improvement for any prior-guided FSS pipeline, and the consistent gains across three different baselines and two datasets (PASCAL-5i, COCO-20i) demonstrate broad applicability. However, the method is bounded by CLIP's frozen representations and the quality of its attention maps; the authors themselves note future work on better extracting useful information from CLIP. An open question is whether the approach transfers to settings where text prompts are unavailable or ambiguous (e.g., fine-grained or compositional classes), and whether the 30-epoch training budget (shorter than baselines) masks potential instability at scale.
