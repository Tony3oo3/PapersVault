## Problem

Existing privacy validation methods for privacy-preserving face recognition (PPFR) systems rely on the unrealistic 2k2c assumption, granting adversaries access to protected user data, unlimited server queries, and the ability to train dedicated reconstruction networks. This paper identifies that such overpowered threat models obscure the true privacy vulnerabilities of PPFR schemes and introduces the 1k1c framework, where the adversary operates as a normal user with only black-box query access and generic public image priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242210/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes decomposed facial data transmitted by a target PPFR system and interacts with it as a black-box oracle that returns only similarity scores (no feature vectors or gradients). It also leverages a pre-trained StyleGAN2 generator and a public face dataset to construct a prior space of n=500 latent modes. The output consists of high-resolution reconstructed face images, two privacy scores (PSI for same-image identity matching and PSII for cross-image matching against a validation system), and per-attribute matching rates (race, gender, age) from a face attribute classifier. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires only ~6,900 queries to the PPFR system, compared to ~1 million for native 2k2c validation.

## Method

Map²V formulates privacy inference as a zeroth-order optimization problem that minimizes the distance between the target face's embedding and a GAN-generated face's embedding in the PPFR model's feature space, without accessing any internal gradients. Three modules collaborate: (1) Prior Space Construction uses a StyleGAN2 encoder to map public face images into the W+ latent space, creating n distinct modes that serve as structured initialization rather than random noise; (2) Rank-and-Ensemble Initialization queries the target PPFR with all n generated faces, ranks them by similarity score, and averages the top-k latent vectors to produce an initial point optimally aligned with the target identity; (3) Zeroth-order gradient estimation approximates the inaccessible gradient by sampling random unit-sphere perturbations u_j and computing finite differences of the similarity-score-based loss, enabling iterative latent refinement in a purely black-box, training-free manner. The attack complexity is O(nmt) queries, directly quantifying the minimum effort needed for privacy inference.

## Results

Under the 1k1c assumption, Map²V achieves privacy scores (PSI) exceeding 95% for all three SOTA PPFR systems (DuetFace: 97.46%, DCTDP: 96.43%, PartialFace: 98.90%) using only 6,900 queries, compared to ~1 million queries required by native 2k2c validation. On CelebA, cross-system identity inference (PSII) still reaches 70–89%, and face attribute matching rates exceed 70% on LFW and 60% on CelebA. Human evaluation confirms that reconstructed faces deceive observers, with an overall Subjective Privacy Score of 93.97% and recognition rates above 90% for 27 out of 30 identities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Map²V is compared against the native 2k2c autoencoder-based validation methods from DuetFace [22], DCTDP [11], and PartialFace [23]. For DuetFace, both methods report similar privacy scores (97.46% vs. 96.52%), but Map²V requires only 6,900 queries versus ~1 million—a ~145× reduction. The gap is far more dramatic for DCTDP, where Map²V reveals a privacy score of 96.43% compared to the native 2k2c result of 79.60% (+16.83 percentage points), and for PartialFace, where Map²V achieves 98.90% versus 65.35% (+33.55 percentage points). These results demonstrate that the 2k2c native validations substantially understate the true privacy leakage of DCTDP and PartialFace, while Map²V exposes near-complete identity recoverability under a far more realistic and query-efficient threat model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on StyleGAN2's W+ latent space and the encoder-based inversion strategy of Tov et al. (2021) to construct a prior space of facial modes, but repurposes them for a fundamentally different goal: black-box privacy validation of PPFR systems. It departs sharply from the dominant '2k2c' validation paradigm (used by DCTDP, DuetFace, PartialFace) that requires white-box access, unlimited server queries, and a separately trained autoencoder; instead, Map²V formulates the attack as a zeroth-order optimization over generic deep image priors under a '1k1c' black-box setting with only thousands of queries. The formalization draws on Wasserstein distance theory and GAN generalization results to justify why minimizing neural-network distance between empirical distributions suffices without enforcing 1-Lipschitz constraints on the target model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant gap between the privacy guarantees claimed by SOTA PPFR authors and the actual vulnerability under a more realistic threat model: PartialFace's native validation reports 65% reconstruction accuracy, while Map²V achieves 99% with 100× fewer queries, suggesting the field has been systematically underestimating privacy leakage. The 1k1c framework and training-free, query-efficient pipeline offer a practical, generalizable tool that could be adopted as a standard evaluation protocol for future PPFR submissions. Open questions include whether the findings extend beyond the DnR paradigm to encryption-based or transformation-based PPFR, whether the 200-subject, two-dataset evaluation is sufficient for robust conclusions, and whether the zeroth-order approach scales to higher-dimensional or multi-modal biometric settings.
