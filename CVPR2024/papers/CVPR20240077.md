## Problem

Existing multi-modality visual RL methods that fuse RGB frames and DVS events simply aggregate the two modalities, injecting modality-specific noise and task-irrelevant information into the latent state space and degrading policy performance. Because RL provides only indirect reward signals (TD loss) rather than pixel-level supervision, there is no mechanism to distinguish useful task-relevant features from noise when combining heterogeneous sensors. The paper asks how to explicitly separate task-relevant co-features from modality-specific noise in a multi-modality visual RL setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB frames sampled at 20 Hz and asynchronous DVS events, which are discretized into spatio-temporal voxel grids with B=5 temporal bins synchronized to the 0.05 s RGB frame interval. Three consecutive observations from each modality are stacked to form the state. The output is a continuous driving action produced by a stochastic policy; during inference, only the co-feature encoder and policy network are retained, and the two noise-branch encoders are discarded for efficiency.

## Method

DMR decomposes the multi-modality representation into three components via a three-branch architecture: an upper branch encodes RGB-specific noise, a lower branch encodes DVS-specific noise, and a middle branch takes the concatenation of both modalities to extract task-relevant co-features. Three complementary constraints govern training: (1) co-features are shaped by DeepMDP reward and state predictive heads so they remain useful for the RL task; (2) a contrastive loss (with moving-average targets) maximizes the distance between noise representations and co-features; (3) reconstruction decoders ensure that co-features plus the corresponding modality noise can recover the original observation, preventing information leakage. Policy learning is performed with SAC operating solely on the co-features, so the noise branches serve only as auxiliary regularizers during training and are removed at test time.

## Results

On the proposed CARLA benchmark with four traffic-weather scenarios, DMR achieves the best distance in all four settings (230±77 on JW-Midnight, 146±58 on JW-Hardrain, 117±68 on HB-Midnight, 150±51 on HB-Hardrain) and the best reward in three of four (194±73, 111±57, 112±51). The largest margin over the best multi-modality baseline is on JW-Midnight, where DMR's 230±77 distance exceeds RENet's 189±107 by over 20%. On HB-Midnight, FPNet slightly edges DMR on reward (85±62 vs. 71±72), though DMR still leads on distance (117±68 vs. 116±68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMR outperforms all four multi-modality baselines (TransFuser, EFNet, FPNet, RENet) on distance in every scenario and on reward in 7 of 8 scenario-metric combinations. The closest competitor is FPNet, which narrowly beats DMR on HB-Midnight reward (85±62 vs. 71±72) but is otherwise consistently outperformed. RENet, the strongest multi-modality baseline on JW-Midnight (189±107 distance), is surpassed by DMR by 41 points in distance and 36 in reward. EFNet and TransFuser are substantially weaker, with EFNet dropping to 47±34 distance on JW-Hardrain and TransFuser to 111±79 on JW-Midnight. DMR also dominates all single-modality baselines (RGB, DVS, DVS-F) by a wide margin in every scenario, confirming the benefit of explicit multi-modality decomposition over relying on either sensor alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DeepMDP for latent-space representation learning and SAC for policy optimization, but departs fundamentally from prior multi-modality fusion approaches (TransFuser, EFNet, FPNet, RENet) that implicitly mix task-relevant and task-irrelevant information through attention or multi-scale aggregation. The key conceptual departure is an explicit three-branch decomposition that treats modality-specific noise as a first-class learned component, contrasted against co-features, rather than relying on the fusion network to implicitly filter irrelevant signals. No prior RL method had combined RGB frames with DVS events, making this the first work to address the specific challenge of fusing these two highly complementary but mechanistically distinct visual modalities under TD-loss-only supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that sophisticated multi-modality fusion architectures (designed for supervised perception tasks) actually degrade RL performance compared to single-modality baselines challenges the assumption that more complex fusion always helps, and suggests that explicit noise separation is a more appropriate inductive bias for reward-driven learning. The DMR framework is modular and can be plugged into other RL algorithms and modality combinations (demonstrated with Depth and LiDAR), making it a potentially reusable component. However, the evaluation is limited to two Carla scenarios under two weather conditions in simulation, and the authors themselves flag sim2real generalization as open work; whether the decomposition principle holds in more diverse, real-world driving or robotics settings remains untested.
