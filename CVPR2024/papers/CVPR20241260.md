## Problem

Most DocVQA methods are designed for single-page documents, yet real-world documents (contracts, manuals, scientific papers) span many pages. Naively concatenating all pages into one sequence incurs quadratic attention cost, while existing long-sequence NLP approaches (Longformer, BigBird) treat the document as a flat token stream without respecting page boundaries, and require extensive pretraining on scarce multi-page data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-page document images rendered as visual feature vectors, OCR tokens with 2D bounding-box coordinates, and a natural-language question. During training, each page is limited to 800 tokens with up to 4 pages per document; at test time the maximum sequence length extends to 8,000 tokens. The output is a free-form text answer generated autoregressively by a T5-style decoder, supporting both extractive and abstractive question types. An optional C-Former stage can compress the concatenated encoder output to a fixed 256-token representation before decoding, trading a small ANLS drop for substantially lower latency on long documents.

## Method

GRAM extends a pretrained single-page DocFormerV2 encoder-decoder to multi-page documents without additional pretraining by inserting a bi-level global-local encoder: each block first applies the existing page-level self-attention (shared weights across pages) over page tokens plus 32 learnable doc tokens, then applies a lightweight document-level self-attention layer that operates only on the doc tokens gathered from all pages, enabling cross-page information exchange at O(N²·K) rather than O((N·K)²) cost. A decaying bias-adaptation mechanism (c·1/2^a per attention head, inspired by ALiBi) is injected into the attention bias matrix to force the pretrained encoder to attend to the newly introduced doc tokens, preventing them from being ignored during fine-tuning. An optional C-Former—a randomly initialized T5-tiny decoder with cross-attention—sits between the encoder and the main decoder, compressing the full multi-page encoder output into N_c=256 learnable tokens to reduce autoregressive decoding latency. The entire system is fine-tuned end-to-end on MP-DocVQA and/or DUDE with differential learning rates (3e-5 for pretrained weights, 1e-4 for new doc sub-layers) over 200k steps on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Results

GRAM (T5-large) achieves state-of-the-art ANLS of 80.32 on MPDocVQA and 51.15 on DUDE, outperforming the primary baseline DocFormerv2-concat (76.40 / 48.44) by +3.9% and +2.7% respectively. When trained on both datasets (GRAM†), it reaches 79.67 on MPDocVQA and 53.36 on DUDE. The optional C-Former compression variant (GRAM_C-Former) retains competitive performance (77.60 / 45.47) while enabling a controllable quality-latency tradeoff, and gracefully handles documents exceeding 300 pages where DocFormerv2-concat hits memory limits at ~20 pages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary same-architecture baseline DocFormerv2-concat, GRAM (large) improves MPDocVQA ANLS by +3.9% (80.32 vs. 76.40) and DUDE by +2.7% (51.15 vs. 48.44); with joint training (GRAM†), the gains are +2.9% and +2.6% respectively. GRAM also surpasses the much larger DocGptVQA (>3.5B params) by +1.1% on DUDE (51.15 vs. 50.02) and DocBlipVQA by +3.5%, while using roughly 4× fewer parameters. Compared to the hierarchical Hi-VT5 family, GRAM achieves substantially higher DUDE scores (51.15 vs. 28.89 for Hi-VT5* large), indicating that the global-local attention design is far more effective than per-page compression for cross-page reasoning. On the encoder-only NLP baselines (Longformer, BigBird, LayoutLMv3), GRAM leads by 20+ ANLS points on DUDE, where abstractive and multi-hop questions dominate. The C-Former variant trades a small ANLS drop (−2.7% on DUDE) for a 3.5-second latency reduction at 20 pages and the ability to process 300+ page documents that exceed DocFormerv2-concat's memory limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAM extends DocFormerV2, a T5-based single-page encoder-decoder for DocVQA, into the multi-page setting by introducing a bi-level global-local encoder with learnable document tokens, avoiding the need for expensive multi-page pretraining. The design draws on NLP long-sequence techniques—particularly ALiBi-style per-head bias for the doc-token attention mechanism and the local-global token separation seen in Longformer and BigBird—but restructures them around the natural page boundary rather than arbitrary token windows. Unlike Hi-VT5, which compresses each page independently and only communicates at decoding, GRAM allows cross-page information flow throughout all encoder layers, and unlike the naive concatenation approach (T5-2D), it avoids quadratic scaling in the number of pages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-page DocVQA is a practically critical but previously under-served setting, and GRAM provides a recipe to upgrade existing single-page models without retraining from scratch, lowering the barrier to entry. The global-local architecture and C-Former compression offer a principled quality-latency trade-off that makes long-document inference feasible (handling 300+ pages where concatenation-based methods hit memory limits). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond OCR+visual features to pure vision-language models, how the method behaves on documents with highly irregular page structures, and whether the fixed number of doc tokens (32) is optimal across diverse document types.
