## Problem

Reconstructing full-body 3D avatars from sparse HMD observations (head and two hands only) is difficult because no direct tracking signals exist for lower-body joints, and existing methods model the entire body in a single unified motion space, making it hard for networks to capture the full kinematic complexity from such limited input. The paper identifies that the SMPL kinematic tree connects upper and lower body through a single root joint, suggesting a natural decoupling that prior work has not exploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240370/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a sequence of 6-DoF poses (3D rotation in six-axis representation + 3D translation) for the head, left hand, and right hand, augmented with positional and angular velocities to yield 54D per timestamp. For online inference, a fixed 20-frame sliding window is used, retaining only the last predicted pose per step. The output is full-body SMPL joint rotations (22 joints × 6D = 132D per frame), produced by first generating a 14-joint upper-body latent and a 9-joint lower-body latent (sharing the root joint), then combining them through a dedicated full-body decoder.

## Method

SAGE disentangles full-body motion into upper and lower halves using two independent VQ-VAE autoencoders (transformer backbones, codebook of 512×384, temporal down-sampling rate 2) that encode each half-body into a discrete latent space. A stratified latent diffusion model then generates the upper-body latent conditioned on the sparse observations, and the lower-body latent conditioned on both the sparse observations and the predicted upper-body latent, explicitly modeling inter-body correlations in a cascaded manner. A full-body decoder trained from scratch (rather than reusing the half-body decoders) fuses the two latents, and a lightweight two-layer GRU Refiner smooths the output temporally. The diffusion models directly predict the latent z rather than the noise ε, reducing inference steps, and the full pipeline runs at 0.74 ms per frame on an RTX 3090.

## Results

Under the standard S1 setting (CMU, BMLrub, HDM05 with 90/10 split, 3-joint input), SAGE achieves the best MPJRE (2.53°), MPJPE (3.28 cm), Lower PE (6.01 cm), and Jitter (6.55) among all compared methods, while matching AvatarJLM on MPJVE (20.62 vs. 20.79). Under the authors' proposed S3 setting (90/10 split over 15 AMASS subsets), SAGE further widens its lead, achieving MPJRE 2.41°, MPJPE 2.95 cm, Lower PE 5.37 cm, and Jitter 5.27, outperforming the next-best method (AvatarJLM) by 0.73° in MPJRE and 0.76 cm in Lower PE. Inference runs at 0.74 ms per frame on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of metrics under both S1 and S3 settings. Against the strongest regression-based baseline AvatarJLM [53], SAGE improves MPJRE by 0.37° (2.53 vs. 2.90), Lower PE by 0.13 cm (6.01 vs. 6.14), and Jitter by 1.84 (6.55 vs. 8.39) on S1; on S3 the gap widens to 0.73° in MPJRE and 0.76 cm in Lower PE, though AvatarJLM retains a slight edge on Hand PE (0.69 vs. 1.15) and MPJVE (15.75 vs. 16.94). Against the diffusion-based AGRoL [11] in offline mode, SAGE wins on MPJRE (2.53 vs. 2.66), MPJPE (3.28 vs. 3.71), Lower PE (6.01 vs. 6.84), and Jitter (6.55 vs. 7.26), but AGRoL-Offline achieves a lower MPJVE (18.59 vs. 20.62) by exploiting future frames in a non-causal pass. AGRoL in online mode is dramatically worse on temporal smoothness (Jitter 84.79 vs. 6.55), confirming the practical advantage of SAGE's online-friendly design. Older baselines (AvatarPoser, VAR-HMD, LoBSTr, Final IK) are consistently outperformed by large margins across all reported metrics, with SAGE reducing Lower PE by 1.58 cm over AvatarPoser and 1.58 cm over AGRoL-Online.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE framework for discrete motion tokenization and the latent diffusion model for conditional generation, but departs from prior full-body avatar generation methods (AvatarPoser, AvatarJLM, AGRoL, FLAG) that model the entire body in a single unified motion space. The key conceptual departure is the stratified decomposition: leveraging the SMPL kinematic tree's single root-joint connection between upper and lower body to split reconstruction into two sequential stages, with the lower-body diffusion conditioned on the predicted upper-body latent. This contrasts with both regression-based approaches that predict all joints simultaneously and prior generative methods that sample from a monolithic full-body distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stratified design directly addresses the most challenging aspect of HMD-based avatar generation—lower-body reconstruction from no direct tracking signals—and the ablations confirm that conditioning lower-body generation on upper-body latents yields meaningful gains in both accuracy and temporal smoothness. The finding that further disentanglement (5-part split along kinematic paths) degrades performance suggests that the natural upper/lower boundary is the optimal granularity, a useful design principle for future hierarchical motion models. Limitations remain for external-force-induced movements and unconventional poses, and the approach is currently validated only on mocap data (AMASS), leaving open whether it generalizes to in-the-wild sparse observations with sensor noise.
