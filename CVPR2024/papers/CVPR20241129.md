## Problem

Existing high-fidelity animatable hand avatar methods require dense multi-view or sequential image inputs, which are labor-intensive and often impractical for general users. This paper addresses the challenge of creating a detailed, animatable hand avatar from a single image by leveraging data-driven hand priors to compensate for the severe data scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB hand image (or a text prompt via ControlNet for text-to-avatar generation) and an off-the-shelf pose estimator's output (MANO shape β, pose θ, camera parameters) as input. It produces a fully animatable neural implicit hand avatar comprising an occupancy field (geometry via PairOF) and a multi-resolution texture field (disentangled albedo and shadow), renderable at 256×256 from arbitrary viewpoints and poses. The full reconstruction pipeline (50 inversion steps + 100 fitting steps) takes approximately 56 minutes on an A100 GPU.

## Method

OHTA employs a two-stage framework built around a Hand Prior Network (HPNet). In stage 1, HPNet is trained on 21 multi-identity subjects from InterHand2.6M to learn transferable geometry (PairOF occupancy), identity-specific albedo (conditioned on identity code z), and identity-shared shadow (conditioned on pose θ without global rotation) priors, all encoded in a multi-resolution field sampled on a MANO-HD mesh scaffold with barycentric-coordinate interpolation. In stage 2, one-shot reconstruction proceeds via texture inversion (optimizing identity code z* and per-channel color calibration {w,b} with frozen network weights to find a similar appearance in latent space) followed by texture fitting (fine-tuning only the resolution-specific MLPs and fusing MLP of the albedo field while keeping point encodings and the shadow field frozen, with view regularization across 7 reference poses to prevent overfitting). The key architectural insight is the albedo-shadow disentanglement: shadows from self-occlusion are largely consistent across identities for the same pose, making them a transferable prior that regularizes the one-shot fit and yields realistic animation.

## Results

On InterHand2.6M, OHTA achieves PSNR 26.11, LPIPS 12.93, and SSIM 0.864 using only a single input image, substantially outperforming all prior one-shot methods (best prior: Handy at PSNR 25.56, LPIPS 14.98, SSIM 0.794). Notably, OHTA's one-shot performance approaches that of HandAvatar trained on 11,757 images (PSNR 28.23, LPIPS 10.35, SSIM 0.894), closing the gap to within ~2 dB PSNR. On the out-of-distribution HanCo dataset, OHTA further leads with PSNR 22.15, LPIPS 11.55, and SSIM 0.886, surpassing both HandAvatar (PSNR 19.76) and Handy (PSNR 21.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OHTA outperforms all four one-shot baselines on InterHand2.6M: it beats Handy (the strongest prior one-shot method) by +0.55 dB PSNR and −2.05 LPIPS, and surpasses HandAvatar-1img by +2.32 dB PSNR and −4.85 LPIPS. The margins over HARP (+6.29 dB PSNR) and LiveHand (+3.10 dB PSNR) are even larger, indicating that explicit-mesh and NeRF-based one-shot approaches struggle significantly more with texture fidelity. Against multi-image methods, OHTA's single-image result (PSNR 26.11) is within 2.1 dB of HandAvatar trained on 11,757 images (PSNR 28.23) and within 1.5 dB of HumanNeRF (PSNR 27.64), though it trails both on LPIPS by ~1.5–2.6 points. On the out-of-distribution HanCo dataset, OHTA leads both HandAvatar (+2.39 dB PSNR, +0.040 SSIM) and Handy (+1.04 dB PSNR, +0.118 SSIM), confirming that the learned hand priors generalize beyond the training distribution. Novel-view synthesis comparisons (Fig. 5) show OHTA captures more detail than LISA and produces more plausible animatable views than the general-purpose One-2-3-45, which lacks hand-specific domain knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

OHTA extends the one-shot avatar paradigm established for faces (PhoneScan, Preface) and bodies (ActorsNeRF, ELICIT) to the hand domain, adapting the prior-learning-then-inversion-and-fitting pipeline with hand-specific components. For geometry, it builds on PairOF/COAP mesh-guided occupancy fields; for texture, it introduces a multi-resolution field on a MANO-HD mesh scaffold that disentangles identity-specific albedo from identity-shared (pose-conditioned) self-occlusion shadows. This departs from prior hand avatar methods (HandAvatar, HARP, LiveHand, Handy) that all require multi-view or sequential inputs, and from general single-image-to-3D approaches (One-2-3-45, Magic123) that lack the animatable, pose-controllable structure needed for hands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OHTA makes personalized, animatable hand avatars accessible from a single photo, which is critical for digital-human and AR/VR applications where dense multi-view capture is impractical. The identity-shared shadow field is a useful conceptual contribution: recognizing that self-occlusion patterns are largely pose-determined rather than identity-determined enables transferable shading without per-identity lighting modeling. However, the 56-minute per-identity optimization on an A100 GPU limits real-time deployment, and the authors acknowledge degraded performance under uneven lighting or inaccurate pose estimation—both common in-the-wild conditions. Open questions include whether the prior can be distilled into a feed-forward model to eliminate per-identity optimization, and whether the shadow disentanglement generalizes to other articulated body parts.
