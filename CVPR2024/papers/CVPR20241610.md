## Problem

View selection for NeRF training and evaluation remains largely unexplored, despite its critical impact on rendering quality and fair benchmarking. The paper demonstrates that a simple rotation of test camera poses in the standard NeRF Synthetic dataset can invert the SOTA rankings of leading methods, and that the choice of training view sampling strategy can produce up to 1.9 dB PSNR differences. This motivates a systematic study of how to select a given number of views from a large pool to maximize NeRF rendering performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of posed RGB training images with camera parameters from SfM, a sparse 3D point cloud with 2D feature correspondences between view pairs, and a target view budget n. For the IGS variant, a trained NeRF model checkpoint is additionally required at each iteration to compute per-view error or uncertainty scores. The output is a selected subset of n training views; when applied to test set generation, it produces a uniformly distributed set of evaluation cameras on a sphere around the object, yielding a coverage density measure that quantifies scene coverage uniformity.

## Method

NeRF Director introduces two complementary view selection strategies. Farthest View Sampling (FVS) adapts Farthest Point Sampling to the view domain using a composite distance metric that combines spatial separation (great-circle or Euclidean distance between camera centers) with a photogrammetric distance derived from the count of shared SfM triangulated points between view pairs, ensuring both geometric spread and perceptual diversity without any model training. Information Gain-based Sampling (IGS) is an incremental procedure that trains a NeRF on the current subset, scores remaining views by rendering error or ensemble uncertainty, and selects new views via probabilistic samplers (a Pareto-Zipf law or a mixture of von Mises-Fisher distributions on the unit sphere) to balance exploitation of high-error regions with exploration. A key design choice is a Lloyd-Max relaxation step applied to newly proposed views, which uniformizes their placement via Voronoi tessellation over the convex hull of all available cameras, preventing the greedy clustering on complex scene regions that otherwise degrades performance below random sampling.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset with InstantNGP, FVS reaches 33 dB PSNR using only 70 training views, whereas random sampling (RS) requires 150 views to achieve comparable performance — a 4× speedup in reaching converged quality. Varying the view selection scheme can produce a PSNR difference of up to 1.9 dB across methods. On TanksAndTemples, FVS demonstrates superior PSNR and SSIM over all other methods once the training view budget exceeds 30 views, with IGS following as the second-best approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FVS and IGS against random sampling (RS), ActiveNeRF [27], and Density-aware NeRF Ensembles [31] on both NeRF Synthetic and TanksAndTemples. On NeRF Synthetic, both FVS and IGS significantly outperform RS, while ActiveNeRF and Density-aware NeRF Ensembles actually underperform RS — the authors attribute this to noisy uncertainty estimates and oversampling in complex regions. On TanksAndTemples, FVS is the clear winner at all view budgets above 30 views, followed by IGS. Density-aware NeRF Ensembles surpasses RS only in the higher view regime (>60 views), likely due to improved uncertainty quantification on realistic, non-uniformly distributed data. ActiveNeRF consistently yields the lowest performance across both datasets, which the authors link to its design for a different training regime (<30 views) and a limited 100-view pool.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) from point cloud processing into a training-free view selection method (FVS) for NeRF, and repurposes the Lloyd-Max quantization algorithm as a relaxation step to prevent over-sampling in information gain-based strategies. It departs from the dominant uncertainty-guided and error-guided view selection literature (ActiveNeRF, Density-aware NeRF Ensembles) by demonstrating that these methods can underperform simple random sampling, and by introducing a coverage-based paradigm that requires no model training. The paper also extends the view selection problem to the evaluation side—selecting test views for fair model comparison—which prior work has largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple rotation of test camera poses can invert SOTA rankings of NeRF methods is a practical warning for the community's evaluation practices, suggesting that many published comparisons may be unreliable. FVS offers a simple, training-free technique with up to 4× speedup over random sampling, making it immediately adoptable for practitioners working with limited view budgets. Key limitations include the object-centric assumption, requirement for uniform camera resolution, and reliance on SfM point clouds for the photogrammetric distance, which restricts applicability to unstructured or indoor capture scenarios. Open questions include how view quality (not just quantity and placement) interacts with selection strategy, and whether these findings generalize to unstructured lumigraph-style captures.
