## Problem

While large-scale datasets have driven breakthroughs in 2D vision (SA-1B, LAION-5B) and 3D object reconstruction (Objaverse, MVImgNet), 3D human-centric tasks remain constrained by the limited scale of available human capture data. Existing multi-view human datasets contain only a few dozen to a few hundred subjects, and high-fidelity 3D scans are prohibitively expensive at scale, preventing the data-driven progress observed in other vision domains.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized multi-view RGB video from 48 calibrated industrial cameras at up to 12MP (4096×3000) resolution and 25 fps, arranged in a 16-sided prism (~2.4 m height, ~4.5 m diameter). Each of the 4,500 identities is captured in two outfits performing 12 actions (drawn from a pool of 500) with in-place rotations, yielding 60,000 motion sequences and 645 million frames. Per-frame annotations include human masks, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, 2D/3D keypoints, SMPL/SMPLX parameters, action labels, and paired text descriptions of clothing.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable multi-view capture system purpose-built for everyday clothing, enabling efficient collection of 4,500 identities in 9,000 outfits over six months. The annotation pipeline combines manual action segmentation with automated processing: RVM for coarse segmentation refined by SAM for fine masks, OpenPose for 2D skeletons, multi-view triangulation for 3D skeletons, and constrained SMPL/SMPLX fitting. Four pilot studies validate the dataset's utility: view-consistent action recognition (CTR-GCN, InfoGCN, FR-Head), generalizable NeRF reconstruction (IBRNet, GPNeRF) with data-scaling and cross-dataset pretraining experiments, text-driven human image generation (Stable Diffusion fine-tuned with SMPL pose conditioning), and 2D/3D generative models (StyleGAN2, GET3D) trained on multi-view A-pose images with camera conditioning.

## Results

On MVHumanNet's action recognition benchmark (500 action classes, 400 test outfits), FR-Head achieves the best 8-view Top-1 accuracy of 78.19%, while CTR-GCN leads at 4 views (72.16%); single-view accuracy is only ~30%, confirming the multi-view advantage. For NeRF reconstruction, IBRNet trained on 5,000 outfits reaches PSNR 29.00 / SSIM 0.9706 / LPIPS 0.0377, substantially outperforming GPNeRF (PSNR 24.69 / SSIM 0.8878 / LPIPS 0.1961) at the same scale. Pretraining on MVHumanNet then finetuning on HuMMan improves IBRNet PSNR from 28.06 (scratch) to 29.46 and GPNeRF PSNR from 20.95 to 21.89. For generative models, scaling from 3,000 to 5,500 subjects reduces StyleGAN2 FID from 14.05 to 7.08 and GET3D FID from 41.54 to 25.12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For action recognition, FR-Head [82] achieves the highest 8-view Top-1 (78.19%) and Top-5 (92.45%) accuracy, edging out CTR-GCN [10] (76.73% / 91.34%) and InfoGCN [14] (76.55% / 91.00%); however, CTR-GCN leads at 4 views (72.16% Top-1) and InfoGCN is closest at that setting (73.59%). For NeRF reconstruction, IBRNet [70] consistently outperforms GPNeRF [8] across all data scales (e.g., PSNR 29.00 vs 24.69 at 5,000 outfits), confirming that scene-general rendering surpasses SMPL-prior-based human rendering on diverse daily clothing. The pretraining experiment (Table 4) shows that both IBRNet and GPNeRF benefit from MVHumanNet pretraining: IBRNet gains +1.40 PSNR (29.46 vs 28.06) and GPNeRF gains +0.94 PSNR (21.89 vs 20.95) over training from scratch on HuMMan alone, with GPNeRF showing a larger relative improvement in LPIPS (0.1364 vs 0.1809). For 2D generation, StyleGAN2 [37] achieves FID 7.08 at 5,500 subjects, while for 3D generation, GET3D [22] reaches FID 25.12 at the same scale—both showing clear scaling benefits but no direct head-to-head comparison between the two modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by scaling multi-view human capture datasets to an unprecedented magnitude, departing from the mid-sized collections (tens to hundreds of subjects) that have characterized the field since Human3.6M and CMU Panoptic. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes the multi-view capture paradigm by restricting to everyday casual clothing—a deliberate simplification that enables efficient, large-scale data collection within six months. The pilot experiments apply existing methods (IBRNet, GPNeRF, StyleGAN2, GET3D, Stable Diffusion) as-is to demonstrate the dataset's utility, positioning MVHumanNet as the missing data infrastructure that prior 3D human reconstruction and generation works have lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck: 3D human-centric tasks have lagged behind object-centric progress (Objaverse, MVImgNet) largely due to data scarcity, and MVHumanNet (4,500 identities, 9,000 outfits, 645M frames) is the first dataset to close this gap at scale. However, the pilot studies reveal only moderate improvements from scaling (e.g., NeRF PSNR gains of ~3 dB from 100 to 5000 outfits), and the focus on daily clothing excludes complex costumes and human-object interactions that limit real-world applicability. Open questions include whether further scaling yields diminishing returns, how to incorporate the excluded complexity factors without sacrificing collection efficiency, and whether the current annotation pipeline (OpenPose + multi-view triangulation + SMPL fitting) is sufficient for the most demanding downstream tasks.
