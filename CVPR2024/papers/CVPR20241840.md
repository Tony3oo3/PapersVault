## Problem

Existing generative ZSL methods condition their generators on Gaussian noise and a fixed, predefined semantic prototype, which constrains the generator to optimize only for specific seen classes rather than characterizing individual visual instances. This leads to synthesized features that fail to capture the true distribution of unseen classes, causing the classifier's decision boundary to overfit to seen classes and resulting in poor generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241840/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 768-dimensional visual features (extracted by a frozen ViT-Base backbone) and class-level semantic prototypes (attribute vectors of 85, 102, or 1024 dimensions depending on the dataset) as inputs. It produces synthesized visual features for unseen classes that are concatenated with VE-extracted latent features to form enhanced representations, which are then fed into a CZSL or GZSL classifier to yield class labels. During unseen-class synthesis, no real unseen samples are available; instead, Gaussian noise is fed through the DKL network to transfer domain knowledge learned from seen classes.

## Method

VADS constructs a visual-augmented dynamic semantic prototype by concatenating two learnable modules' outputs, which replaces the static Gaussian-noise-plus-attribute condition used by prior generative ZSL methods. The VDKL module encodes visual features via a Visual Encoder (trained with contrastive and KL losses) into a latent code, which a DKL network transforms into a local bias; this bias is combined with a learnable global prior vector to form a domain-specific visual prior noise Z'. The VOSU module performs a two-stage semantic update: first, a Visual-Semantic Prediction network and a Semantic Updation Mapping network jointly refine the predefined prototype using cross-entropy supervision, and second, the SUM network dynamically updates the prototype during GAN training under a semantic consistency loss. This dynamic prototype conditions an off-the-shelf generator (CLSWGAN, TFVAEGAN, or FREE) to learn instance-level semantic-to-visual mappings, and the same mechanism is applied at inference to synthesize unseen-class features by passing Gaussian noise through DKL and updating unseen prototypes via SUM.

## Results

Using CLSWGAN as the generative backbone, VADS achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks: CZSL accuracy of 82.5% (AWA2), 76.3% (SUN), and 86.8% (CUB), and GZSL harmonic mean of 79.3 (AWA2), 55.7 (SUN), and 74.3 (CUB). These represent average harmonic-mean improvements of 6.4%, 5.9%, and 4.2% over prior generative methods on SUN, CUB, and AWA2 respectively. When integrated into TFVAEGAN and FREE, VADS still yields consistent gains (e.g., +11.5 Acc and +10.0 H on AWA2 for CLSWGAN; +3.2 Acc and +1.9 H on SUN for TFVAEGAN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VADS outperforms all 21 compared baselines on every dataset in both CZSL and GZSL settings. Against the closest generative competitor ICCE [24], VADS gains +9.8 Acc and +6.5 H on AWA2, and +8.4 Acc and +7.9 H on CUB. The most directly related prior work, CLSWGAN+DSP [12] (which also updates semantic prototypes), is surpassed by a large margin: H improves from 70.7→79.3 (AWA2), 45.5→55.7 (SUN), and 56.9→74.3 (CUB). Among embedding-based methods, VADS exceeds PSVMA [26] (the strongest embedding baseline) by +3.9 H on AWA2, +3.4 H on SUN, and +0.5 H on CUB, while also reporting CZSL accuracy where PSVMA does not. On CUB, VADS dramatically outperforms TFVAEGAN+SHIP [43] (H: 74.3 vs. 35.3), which suffers severe unseen-class degradation (U=22.5). The ablation in Table 4 further confirms VADS as a plug-and-play module: adding it to CLSWGAN, TFVAEGAN, and FREE yields average H gains of +9.1, +1.2, and +3.2 on CUB respectively, with the largest absolute improvement (+11.5 Acc) observed when paired with CLSWGAN on AWA2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative ZSL line of work (CLSWGAN, TFVAEGAN, FREE) by rethinking the generator's conditioning mechanism rather than proposing a new generative architecture. It departs from the standard practice of using Gaussian noise and a fixed semantic prototype as conditions, instead introducing a visual-augmented dynamic semantic prototype that combines learned domain visual prior (VDKL) with an instance-level updated semantic embedding (VOSU). Conceptually, it draws from image captioning's instance-conditional paradigm and from prompt learning in vision-language models, repurposing the idea of visual-conditioned adaptation to the ZSL feature synthesis setting. The closest prior work is DSP [12], which also updates semantic prototypes, but VADS goes further by jointly learning visual prior noise and performing two-stage semantic updation with a consistency loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the conditioning mechanism (noise + semantic prototype) is a critical bottleneck in generative ZSL, and that instance-level conditioning substantially improves generalization to unseen classes across multiple generator architectures. This insight is broadly applicable: any generative ZSL pipeline could potentially benefit from replacing static conditions with dynamic, visual-augmented ones. However, the approach still relies on attribute-based semantic prototypes and is validated on three relatively small, attribute-annotated datasets (AWA2, SUN, CUB), leaving open whether the visual prior transfer generalizes to larger-scale or less structured semantic spaces. The negative cross-domain transfer result (SUN→CUB) also highlights that the learned global prior is dataset-specific, limiting plug-and-play applicability.
