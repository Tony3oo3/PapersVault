## Problem

Learning-based no-reference point cloud quality assessment (NR-PCQA) suffers from severe label scarcity, as existing PCQA datasets contain only hundreds of annotated samples, limiting model generalizability especially in cross-dataset settings. Prior attempts to address this—domain adaptation from natural images or distortion-type auxiliary tasks—either fail due to the 2D-to-3D perceptual gap or lack robustness to unseen distortion types. The paper asks whether contrastive pre-training on unlabeled point clouds can learn quality-aware representations that transfer effectively to downstream quality prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240127/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a distorted 3D point cloud (with geometric coordinates, color, and opacity) as input and outputs a single predicted quality score aligned to the MOS scale. During pre-training, point clouds are randomly rotated and rendered into 512×512 RGB images via PyTorch3D; during fine-tuning, each point cloud is projected into six 512×512 images from perpendicular viewpoints (±x, ±y, ±z axes) at a fixed viewing distance. The final score is regressed through fully-connected layers after multi-view feature fusion, with a differential ranking loss encouraging correct ordering of close-quality samples.

## Method

CoPA is a two-stage framework. In the contrastive pre-training stage, point clouds impaired by different distortions are projected into images, and anchors are generated by randomly mixing 16×16 local patches from two distorted views of the same reference (preserving local distortion patterns without introducing new artifacts). A Swin-T encoder is trained with a combined content-wise and distortion-wise contrastive loss—pulling the two source images toward the anchor while pushing apart images from other references (content negatives) and other distortions of the same reference (distortion negatives)—using a momentum contrast queue to scale negative samples. In the fine-tuning stage, the pre-trained Swin-T encodes six multi-view projections, and a ResNet-50 extracts a global semantic feature from a stitched composed image; this semantic feature acts as a query in a multi-headed cross-attention mechanism that selectively fuses the six view-specific quality-aware features, followed by MSE and ranking losses for score regression.

## Results

CoPA+FT achieves SROCC of 0.613, 0.897, and 0.779 on LS-PCQA, SJTU-PCQA, and WPC respectively, outperforming all NR-PCQA baselines on all three datasets. On LS-PCQA, it improves over the best NR baseline (ResSCNN) by 3.2% SROCC (0.613 vs. 0.594), 1.9% PLCC (0.636 vs. 0.624), and 6.4% RMSE (0.161 vs. 0.172). On SJTU-PCQA, it surpasses GPA-Net by 2.2% SROCC (0.897 vs. 0.878) and 21.3% RMSE (0.092 vs. 0.122). In cross-dataset evaluation, CoPA+FT also leads by a large margin (e.g., 0.644 PLCC on SJTU-PCQA when trained on LS-PCQA vs. 0.581 for MM-PCQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPA+FT outperforms all five NR-PCQA baselines across all three datasets. On LS-PCQA, it beats ResSCNN (best NR baseline) by 1.9 SROCC points and GPA-Net by 2.1 points; on SJTU-PCQA, it surpasses GPA-Net by 1.9 SROCC and MM-PCQA by 2.1 SROCC; on WPC, it leads MM-PCQA by 1.8 SROCC and GPA-Net by 2.1 SROCC. The margin is most pronounced in RMSE on SJTU-PCQA (0.092 vs. 0.122 for GPA-Net, a 24.6% reduction). In cross-dataset evaluation (Table 2), CoPA+FT consistently leads by 4–12 PLCC points over all NR baselines, e.g., 0.644 vs. 0.581 (MM-PCQA) when trained on LS-PCQA and tested on SJTU-PCQA. The paper also shows CoPA pre-training can be plugged into PQA-Net and MM-PCQA, yielding PLCC gains of 6.2 points (PQA-Net on SJTU) and 1.2 points (MM-PCQA on SJTU), confirming the framework's generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the contrastive pre-training paradigm (MoCo, SimCLR) to the no-reference point cloud quality assessment (NR-PCQA) setting, departing from prior strategies that rely on domain adaptation from natural images (IT-PCQA) or distortion-type auxiliary tasks (PQA-Net, GPA-Net). The key conceptual departure is the anchor generation via local patch mixing of projected images from different distortions, which preserves intrinsic quality information unlike standard augmentations (cropping, noise) used in image/video QA contrastive methods (QPT, CSPT, CONTRIQUE). The fine-tuning stage introduces a semantic-guided multi-view fusion via cross-attention, building on the multi-view projection idea from PQA-Net but replacing simple aggregation with a content-aware attention mechanism guided by an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that contrastive pre-training on unlabeled point clouds can meaningfully reduce the reliance on scarce MOS annotations in PCQA, with the largest gains appearing when labeled data is limited. However, the practical impact is somewhat bounded: pre-training is only validated on LS-PCQA, cross-dataset gains, while consistent, remain modest (e.g., ~0.06 PLCC on LS→SJTU), and the 2D projection step inherently discards 3D structural information that may matter for certain distortion types. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse point cloud corpora, whether the patch-mixing anchor strategy generalizes to other 3D quality tasks (e.g., mesh or Gaussian splatting), and whether the semantic-guided fusion can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives without sacrificing performance.
