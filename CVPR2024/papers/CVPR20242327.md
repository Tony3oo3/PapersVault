## Problem

Existing ego-view accident video understanding focuses on detecting when and where accidents occur, but lacks the ability to explain why an accident happens and how to prevent it. Moreover, no large-scale multimodal dataset exists that pairs ego-view accident videos with temporally aligned text descriptions of accident reasons, prevention advice, and categories, making abductive (causal) accident understanding infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-frame ego-view video clips partitioned into normal, near-accident, and accident segments, paired with text prompts (accident reason, prevention advice, or accident category) and per-frame bounding boxes for 7 road-participant classes (cars, trucks, buses, pedestrians, motorbikes, cyclists, traffic lights). The diffusion output is a 16-frame generated video clip that either makes causal objects appear in advance (given accident reason) or eliminates them (given prevention advice). The ArA task outputs a multi-choice answer selected from 5 options (1 correct + 4 distractors) for 58 accident categories.

## Method

AdVersa-SD comprises two components: an Abductive CLIP and an Object-centric Accident Video Diffusion (OAVD) model. Abductive CLIP uses XCLIP as backbone and trains with a Contrastive Interaction Loss (CILoss) over four interaction groups of positive and negative Co-occurrence Pairs (Co-CPs), including virtual pairs such as normal video with negated accident-category text and time-reversed near-accident video with prevention-advice text, to learn fine-grained text-video semantic alignment. OAVD extends the Latent Diffusion Model to video via a 3D U-net with 3D convolutions, cross-attention, spatial/temporal attention, and a Gated self-attention layer that injects bounding-box location embeddings (Fourier-encoded through an MLP) to enforce object-region learning while a masked latent reconstruction term (L_MASK) fixes background pixels during denoising. The combined training minimizes MSE plus a weighted L1 masked-reconstruction loss over 1000 diffusion steps, and inference uses DDIM sampling conditioned on the text prompt and bounding boxes to generate causally consistent accident video clips.

## Results

On the video diffusion task, OAVD driven by Abductive CLIP (A-CLIP) with bounding boxes achieves a CLIP score of 27.24 and FVD of 5238.1, substantially outperforming Tune-A-Video (CLIP_S=21.77, FVD=9545.6) and ControlVideo (CLIP_S=22.51, FVD=12275.2). On the Accident Reason Answering benchmark, SeViLA achieves the best accuracy of 89.02% (test), surpassing CoVGT (79.97%) by ~9%. For object detection in accident windows (V2-Train), DiffusionDet leads with mAP50=0.701 and AR=0.729, outperforming YOLOv5s (mAP50=0.660, AR=0.716) and demonstrating superior generalization to accident scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OAVD with Abductive CLIP (A-CLIP) decisively outperforms both video diffusion baselines: it improves CLIP_S by 5.47 points over Tune-A-Video (27.24 vs. 21.77) and 4.73 over ControlVideo (27.24 vs. 22.51), while reducing FVD by 45% vs. TAV (5238 vs. 9546) and 57% vs. CVideo (5238 vs. 12275), at a modest FPS cost (1.2 vs. 1.7 for TAV). Against the original CLIP backbone, A-CLIP gains 5.34 CLIP_S points and reduces FVD by nearly half (5238 vs. 10123), confirming the value of the contrastive interaction loss. The Sequential-CLIP (S-CLIP) variant, which retains only positive Co-CPs, already achieves 27.14 CLIP_S, showing that the negative Co-CPs in A-CLIP provide a small but consistent additional boost (+0.10 CLIP_S, −134 FVD). On the ArA benchmark, SeViLA (89.02% test accuracy) dominates all other methods by 7–9% over CoVGT (79.97%), with LLM-based commonsense knowledge proving critical for rare accident participants like cyclists. For object detection, DiffusionDet shows the best generalization to accident windows (V2-Train mAP50=0.701 vs. YOLOv5s 0.660), suggesting diffusion-based detection is more robust under the distribution shift of accident scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ego-view accident video understanding beyond the established detection, anticipation, and classification tasks by introducing an 'abductive' causal-reasoning formulation that asks why accidents occur and how to prevent them. Methodologically, it builds on XCLIP for contrastive text-video alignment, the Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) as the generative backbone, and Tune-A-Video's 3D U-net architecture, but departs from all three by adding object-centric constraints (bounding-box conditioning via Fourier embeddings, masked latent reconstruction) and a novel Contrastive Interaction Loss that jointly trains positive and negative co-occurrence pairs across normal, near-accident, and accident segments. The conceptual departure from prior VQA-based accident-reason work (SUTD-TrafficQA, Liu et al.) is the use of video diffusion as a verification mechanism for causal chains rather than a mere generation tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MM-AU is the first large-scale ego-view accident dataset with temporally aligned multi-modal text annotations (reasons, prevention advice, categories) and 2.23M object boxes, filling a clear gap identified in the related-work comparison. The abductive diffusion framework is conceptually appealing—using generative models to test whether causal text-video associations hold—but the quantitative evidence is limited to 1,500 generated clips with still-high FVD scores (~5,200) and primarily qualitative visual demonstrations of the 'appear in advance / disappear' phenomenon. Open questions include whether the abductive signal generalizes beyond the 58 annotated accident categories, whether the object-box conditioning is truly necessary versus simpler spatial priors, and whether the framework scales to real-time deployment in autonomous driving stacks.
