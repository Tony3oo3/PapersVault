## Problem

SAM, pretrained on one billion natural images, lacks domain-specific prior knowledge for underwater scenes and its single-position prompt provides insufficient guidance for complex marine animal segmentation. Existing MAS methods based on CNNs or Transformers struggle with long-range dependencies and the unique challenges of variable lighting, turbidity, and color distortion in underwater imagery.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB underwater image (resized to 512×512×3) and internally creates a second branch via gamma correction for illumination compensation. Rather than outputting a standard binary mask directly, the network predicts an 8-channel criss-cross connectivity map per pixel (encoding relationships to neighbors at Manhattan distances 1 and 2), which is then converted to a binary segmentation mask through mutual confirmation between the two decoder branches.

## Method

Dual-SAM employs a dual-branch architecture where both the original and gamma-corrected images pass through a frozen SAM-B encoder augmented with LoRA adapters on Q/V projections and bottleneck adapters in FFN layers. A Multi-level Coupled Prompt (MCP) module generates auto-prompts via cross-attention between the dual-branch encoder features and a separate prompt encoder, injecting multi-level underwater priors at four scales. A Dilated Fusion Attention Module (DFAM) builds a feature pyramid decoder using dilated convolutions and channel attention to progressively integrate multi-level features. The key prediction innovation is Criss-Cross Connectivity Prediction (C³P), which replaces pixel-wise mask prediction with 8-channel connectivity maps capturing inter-pixel relationships in a criss-cross neighborhood, yielding more structured boundaries. Finally, Pseudo-label Mutual Supervision (PMS) thresholds each branch's predictions and uses them as soft targets for the other branch with a dynamically increasing coefficient, fostering complementary feature learning across the dual decoders.

## Results

On the primary MAS3K dataset, Dual-SAM achieves mIoU 0.789, Sα 0.884, Fβw 0.838, mEφ 0.933, and MAE 0.023, outperforming the best CNN-based method (MASNet, mIoU 0.742) by 4.7% and the best Transformer-based method (H2Former, mIoU 0.748) by 4.1%. On RMAS, it reaches mIoU 0.735 and MAE 0.022. On the underwater salient object detection benchmark USOD10K, Dual-SAM attains Sα 0.9238, mEφ 0.9684, maxF 0.9311, and MAE 0.0185, surpassing the previous best (TC-USOD) on maxF and MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-SAM achieves state-of-the-art results across all five datasets, winning on every metric in every comparison. Against raw SAM, the improvement is dramatic: on MAS3K, mIoU jumps from 0.566 to 0.789 (+22.3%) and MAE drops from 0.059 to 0.023, confirming that SAM's natural-image pretraining is severely mismatched for underwater scenes. Against the closest SAM-based competitors (SAM-Ad and SAM-DA), Dual-SAM gains 4.7–7.5% mIoU on MAS3K and 4.9–8.8% on RMAS, attributed to the multi-level coupled prompts and dual-branch mutual supervision. Against the strongest CNN-based MAS method (MASNet), Dual-SAM improves mIoU by 4.7% on MAS3K and 0.4% on RMAS, while against the best Transformer-based method (H2Former), the gain is 4.1% on MAS3K. On USOD10K, Dual-SAM edges out TC-USOD (the previous best) by 0.75% on maxF (0.9311 vs 0.9236) and 0.16% on MAE (0.0185 vs 0.0201), while matching Sα and mEφ within 0.01%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM adaptation line (SAM-Adapter, SAM-DA) by introducing a dual-branch architecture that processes both the original and gamma-corrected underwater image through SAM's encoder, with LoRA and FFN adapters for parameter-efficient domain transfer. The Criss-Cross Connectivity Prediction (C3P) paradigm directly extends ConnNet's adjacent-pixel connectivity prediction to a broader criss-cross neighborhood, while the Multi-level Coupled Prompt (MCP) departs from SAM's single-position prompt by generating multi-level auto-prompts via cross-attention between the dual encoder branches. The overall framework positions itself as a systematic re-engineering of SAM's encoder-decoder pipeline specifically for the underwater domain, combining known components (gamma correction, dilated convolutions, channel attention, pseudo-label mutual supervision) in a novel dual-branch configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that SAM's zero-shot performance on underwater images is severely degraded (0.566 mIoU on MAS3K vs. 0.789 for Dual-SAM), highlighting a significant domain gap that simple prompt engineering cannot bridge. The practical takeaway is that combining illumination correction with parameter-efficient adapters and structured connectivity prediction yields a robust recipe for adapting foundation models to visually challenging domains. Open questions include whether the dual-branch overhead (processing two images through the encoder) is justified over single-branch alternatives with stronger augmentation, whether the MCP strategy generalizes to other underwater tasks beyond segmentation, and whether the C3P paradigm's gains over pixel-wise prediction persist at larger scales or with more diverse marine species.
