## Problem

Existing Active Domain Adaptation (ADA) methods assume all labeled training data originates from a single source domain, ignoring the practical reality that data is typically collected from multiple heterogeneous domains. When conventional ADA is naively extended to this multi-source setting, two critical failures emerge: multi-grained domain shifts make reliable source-target alignment difficult, and deterministic uncertainty estimates become miscalibrated on out-of-distribution target data, leading to poor sample selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M fully labeled source domains and an unlabeled target domain of RGB images (Office-Home at 256×256 with 65 classes, miniDomainNet at 96×96 with 126 classes, Digits-five with 10 classes). Over 5 active selection rounds, it produces a small set of target samples (5% of the target pool) flagged for annotation, and ultimately an adapted classifier that outputs class labels for unseen target images. The selection process operates iteratively: each round queries the model for uncertainty scores, applies diversity filtering, and the newly labeled samples are fed back into training.

## Method

Detective comprises three modules. First, the Universal Dynamic Network (UDN) uses a HyperNetwork to generate sample-conditioned classifier weights while keeping the backbone static, effectively collapsing M source domains into a single 'union' distribution and eliminating the need for explicit multi-source alignment. Second, the Integrated Uncertainty Selector (IUS) replaces point-estimate softmax predictions with a Dirichlet distribution over the probability simplex via Evidential Deep Learning, decomposing uncertainty into domain uncertainty (spread of the Dirichlet, capturing unfamiliarity to the source) and predictive uncertainty (expected entropy), then fusing them with a weighted mix-up to rank target samples. Third, the Contextual Diversity Calculator (CDC) removes the most feature-space-dense selected samples to enforce representational diversity within the labeling budget. The evidential model is trained jointly with a marginal-likelihood loss (encouraging sharp Dirichlet peaks on labeled data) and a KL-divergence loss (suppressing spurious evidence on non-target classes).

## Results

On Office-Home (ResNet-50 backbone), Detective achieves a mean accuracy of 89.99% across four adaptation scenarios (U→A, U→P, U→C, U→R), outperforming the strongest ADA baseline EADA (83.03%) by 6.96% and the strongest MSDA baseline MFSAN (74.1%) by 15.89%. The largest per-scenario gain is on U→C (Clipart), where Detective reaches 86.91% versus 71.75% for EADA. Additional results on miniDomainNet and Digits-five (reported in the appendix) confirm consistent improvements over both MSDA and ADA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Detective outperforms all eight baselines across every Office-Home adaptation scenario. Against the strongest ADA method EADA (83.03% mean), Detective gains 6.96% on average, with the largest per-scenario margin on U→C (+15.16%) where EADA's uncertainty-based selection struggles most with the clipart domain shift. Compared to DUC (82.62%), which also uses Dirichlet-based uncertainty, Detective's advantage (7.37%) stems from its integrated domain-and-predictive uncertainty rather than predictive uncertainty alone. Against TQS (79.93%) and CLUE (78.50%), the gaps widen to 10.06% and 11.49% respectively, indicating that adversarial and clustering-based selection criteria are less effective under multi-source settings. On the MSDA side, Detective surpasses MFSAN (74.1%), SImpAI (72.2%), MDDA (71.0%), and MADAN (70.4%) by 15.89%, 17.79%, 18.99%, and 19.59% respectively, confirming that active target annotation combined with dynamic multi-source modeling yields substantially better adaptation than purely unsupervised multi-source strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Active Domain Adaptation (ADA) and Multi-source Domain Adaptation (MSDA), two threads that have developed largely in parallel. It builds on HyperNetworks (Ha et al.) to generate input-conditioned classifier parameters, repurposing a model-compression tool as a mechanism for dynamic multi-domain adaptation, and on Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al.) to replace point-estimate predictive uncertainty with a Dirichlet-distribution-based measure that captures both domain unfamiliarity and prediction ambiguity. The key conceptual departure from prior ADA work is the use of a dynamic (sample-conditioned) classifier to collapse multiple source domains into an effective single-domain model, thereby avoiding the need for explicit multi-source-to-target alignment, and the key departure from prior MSDA work is the integration of active sample selection with uncertainty-aware criteria rather than purely feature-level alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing MADA, the paper addresses a practical gap: real-world adaptation data rarely comes from a single homogeneous source, yet standard ADA methods assume it does. The finding that domain uncertainty (measured via EDL) should dominate predictive uncertainty in sample selection (λ_dom ≈ 7.5 vs. λ_pre ≈ 0.5) challenges the common practice of relying on predictive entropy alone for active selection under distribution shift. Open questions include whether the dynamic-model approach scales to many more source domains or to non-ResNet backbones, whether the 5% labeling budget assumption holds in more realistic settings, and whether the HyperNetwork-based parameter generation remains efficient as the number of classes or domains grows substantially.
