## Problem

Existing perceptual metrics used to evaluate tileable texture synthesis (SSIM, LPIPS, FID, BRISQUE, etc.) do not explicitly measure a texture's intrinsic repeatability, producing uncorrelated scores that hinder objective comparison of synthesis methods. There is no differentiable, no-reference metric that quantifies how seamlessly a texture tiles, preventing both fair benchmarking and direct use as a loss term to guide synthesis toward tileable outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240997/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB texture image, tiles it 2×2 across both spatial dimensions to form I_tiled, and feeds this to the network. Training uses 384×384 crops while inference operates at 512×512. The output is a scalar tileability score in (0,1) computed via a logistic function with a temperature parameter λ=0.25 that prevents saturation at the boundaries, preserving discriminative resolution for ambiguous cases while maintaining strict monotonicity for relative ordering.

## Method

TexTile is a binary classifier built on a pretrained ConvNeXt backbone augmented with two Linear Self-Attention modules inserted as residual layers (x ← x + λ·f_att(x)) in the deeper stages, initialized with λ=1e-6 to preserve pretrained features during early training. The convolutional layers detect local border discontinuities while the attention layers capture global repeating patterns and artifacts. Training uses NAdam with Lookahead optimizer, mixed precision, and a comprehensive augmentation policy that includes tileability-preserving operations (color/gamma shifts, flips, translations, UnFold mirroring) and tileability-breaking operations (rotations, shears, thin-plate spline warping), plus two custom policies (AUG_T→F and AUG_F→F') that generate cross-class samples to reduce distribution shift between the tileable and non-tileable training sets. The model is trained for 100 epochs on a curated dataset of ~4,276 tileable and ~20,000 non-tileable textures from diverse sources, achieving 98.2% accuracy and 0.997 AUC on the validation set.

## Results

On the tileability classification task, TexTile achieves 98.2% accuracy, 0.983 F1-score, and 0.997 AUC, substantially outperforming distribution-based alternatives (FID: 0.764 AUC, GS: 0.510 AUC, MSID: 0.503 AUC). When plugged in as a differentiable loss, TexTile dramatically improves tileability scores: SinFusion [47] rises from 0.388 to 0.798, and Heitz et al. [25] from 0.431 to 0.781, while preserving or slightly improving perceptual quality metrics (e.g., LPIPS improves from 0.591 to 0.569 for SinFusion). Among existing tileable synthesis methods, Niklasson et al. [48] (0.731) and SeamlessGAN [53] (0.729) achieve the highest native tileability, while border-blending approaches like Rodriguez-Pardo et al. [54] (0.403) score lowest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against distribution-based metrics (FID, GS, MSID), TexTile dominates on the tileability classification task: FID reaches only 0.764 AUC while GS (0.510) and MSID (0.503) perform near random, confirming that generic distribution distances cannot capture tileability. Among tileable synthesis baselines, holistic methods (Niklasson et al. [48]: 0.731, SeamlessGAN [53]: 0.729, Li et al. [36]: 0.707) substantially outperform border-blending approaches (Rodriguez-Pardo et al. [54]: 0.403, Deliot et al. [11]: 0.639), revealing that seamless borders alone are insufficient for high tileability. The most impactful comparison is TexTile-as-loss: adding it to SinFusion [47] nearly doubles the tileability score (0.388→0.798) and to Heitz et al. [25] nearly doubles it as well (0.431→0.781), surpassing all native tileable methods while keeping LPIPS and SSIM competitive. Notably, the correlation matrix (Fig. 7) shows TexTile is only weakly correlated with existing metrics (|r|≤0.55), confirming it captures a distinct property not measured by SSIM, LPIPS, BRISQUE, or CLIP-IQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating texture tileability as a distinct, measurable property separate from general image quality. While no-reference IQA methods (BRISQUE, CLIPIQA, MANIQA) assess overall perceptual quality and distribution-based metrics (FID, GS, MSID) compare generative model outputs, none explicitly quantify whether a texture tiles seamlessly. Architecturally, the approach repurposes ConvNeXt with linear self-attention modules—a pattern seen in texture-specific networks like U-Attention and UMAT—but applies it to a binary tileability classification task with custom augmentation policies (AUG_T→F, AUG_F→F') that have no direct precedent in IQA literature. The key departure from prior tileable synthesis work (SeamlessGAN, Periodic Spatial GAN, Neural Cellular Automata) is that those methods rely on general quality metrics for evaluation, which the paper demonstrates are uncorrelated with actual tileability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TexTile fills a critical gap: for the first time, tileable texture synthesis methods can be quantitatively benchmarked on the property they actually aim to optimize, rather than on proxy metrics that the paper shows are largely uncorrelated with tileability. Its differentiability makes it immediately pluggable into existing synthesis pipelines (optimization-based and diffusion-based) without retraining, lowering the barrier to producing tileable outputs. Key limitations include the absence of perceptual quality measurement (tileability and quality are treated separately), potential ImageNet bias inheritance, and the lack of human perceptual validation for the tileability score itself. Open questions include whether combining TexTile with perceptual metrics yields a more holistic texture quality measure, and whether the model generalizes to texture types underrepresented in its training data.
