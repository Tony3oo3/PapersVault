## Problem

Quantifying fine-scale animal behaviors (e.g., ecstatic display in Chinstrap penguins) in remote, battery-powered field settings is infeasible with conventional cameras: motion-triggered image traps lack temporal resolution, and continuous video recording drains power too quickly for multi-week deployments. Event cameras offer low power consumption and high dynamic range but had never been applied to wildlife behavior monitoring, and no dataset or detection pipeline existed for event-based temporal action detection in outdoor conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous per-nest event streams (pixel coordinates, timestamp, polarity) from a DAVIS346 sensor (346×260 px), already cropped to user-supplied nest bounding boxes so that only temporal boundaries must be detected. Events are converted into 2D tensor-like snapshots (histograms or exponentially decaying time maps) at sparsely sampled timestamps within each proposal, resized to 224×224 and replicated to 3 channels for CNN compatibility. The output is a set of temporal action intervals (start and end times) per nest, each classified as ecstatic display or background, with a final NMS at tIoU 0.6.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a non-learned proposal generator (reTAG) computes a robustly-normalized event rate (using 1st/99th percentile clipping) as an actioness score, applies the watershed algorithm across a grid of thresholds (λ, μ) to extract high-activity intervals, merges nearby proposals to bridge short gaps, and suppresses duplicates via NMS. Second, an Augmented Temporal Segment Network (ATSN) classifies each proposal: the interval is extended by 33% on each side to provide boundary context, five timestamps are sparsely sampled (3 central, 1 per augmentation), events in a 1 s window around each timestamp are rendered as a 2D time map or histogram, and a ResNet18 backbone (ImageNet-pretrained, then fine-tuned on the penguin task) encodes each snapshot. Features from the central interval are pooled by consensus and concatenated with start/end features before a fully connected classifier, making the approach data-efficient (11 M parameters) relative to end-to-end transformers.

## Results

On the EventPenguins dataset, the proposed two-stage pipeline (reTAG + ATSN with time-map representation) achieves a mean average precision (mAP) of 58% across IoU thresholds {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}, with the histogram variant reaching 56%. Under undisturbed daylight conditions, mAP rises to 63%, while night sequences maintain 62% and snow sequences drop to 37%. The proposal stage (reTAG) achieves 66% average recall at top-50 proposals, substantially outperforming the event-TAG baseline (49%) and sliding-window baseline (8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the frame-based ActionFormer adapted to event data (R3D + ActionFormer), the proposed method wins by 3% average mAP (58% vs. 55%), with the advantage growing at higher IoU thresholds (0.43 vs. 0.32 at IoU 0.7), indicating more precise boundary localization. The top-down approach outperforms the self-designed bottom-up baseline by a substantial margin (58% vs. 38% without morphological filtering, 58% vs. 45% with), confirming that sparse multi-timestamp sampling in the ATSN captures temporal structure that per-snapshot classification misses. At the proposal stage, reTAG surpasses the event-TAG method from [47] by more than 25% average recall at every proposal count (e.g., 66% vs. 49% at top-50), attributed to robust percentile-based normalization that mitigates the impact of event-rate peaks. The sliding-window baseline [54] is far inferior (8% AR at top-50), confirming that event-rate-guided proposal generation is essential. Notably, the proposed method uses only 11M parameters versus 27M for ActionFormer and requires far less labeled data, making it more practical for conservation researchers with limited annotation resources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal action detection (TAD) pipeline from RGB video to event camera data, adapting two key components: the TAG proposal generator (Zhao et al., IJCV 2020) becomes reTAG with robust percentile-based event-rate normalization, and the TSN sparse-sampling classifier (Wang et al., ECCV 2016) becomes ATSN with temporal augmentation and event-to-tensor conversion (histograms or time maps). The work departs from prior event-camera pattern recognition, which has focused on short indoor clips (gestures, sign language, automotive detection), by tackling long-duration (10-minute) outdoor sequences with natural lighting variation. It also departs from prior wildlife monitoring, which relies on frame-based camera traps or invasive accelerometers, by demonstrating that the event camera's asynchronous, motion-sensitive output is a natural fit for continuous behavioral quantification under power constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first publicly available event-camera dataset for wildlife behavior and the first deployment of an event camera in Antarctica, establishing a benchmark that could catalyze research at the intersection of neuromorphic vision and conservation science. The demonstrated robustness to night conditions (mAP 62% vs. 63% in daylight) while grayscale frames become entirely unusable validates event cameras as a practical sensor for remote, battery-limited monitoring. Key limitations remain: the method requires user-supplied nest bounding boxes, assumes high event rate correlates with the target behavior, and degrades significantly under heavy precipitation (mAP drops to 37%). Open questions include generalization to other species and behaviors, removing the bounding-box requirement, and whether the approach scales to denser colonies or more complex multi-behavior scenes.
