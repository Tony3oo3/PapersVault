## Problem

Existing interactive segmentation methods face a fundamental trade-off: single-granularity pipelines ignore spatial ambiguity (a click may correspond to a whole object or a specific part), while multi-granularity pipelines like SAM produce redundant masks with limited user control. Moreover, the prohibitive cost of annotating multi-granularity ground-truth masks makes it difficult to train models that can flexibly adapt their output granularity to user intent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242085/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image, a set of positive and negative clicks (encoded as a 2-channel disk map), and a continuous granularity parameter in [0,1] (discretized into 10 bins and mapped to a learnable embedding) as joint prompts. It outputs a single binary segmentation mask at the requested granularity level, where values near 1.0 yield whole-object masks and values near 0 yield fine-grained part masks. Unlike multi-granularity methods that output multiple candidate masks requiring post-hoc selection, GraCo produces exactly one mask per inference, eliminating redundancy.

## Method

GraCo is built on SimpleClick (ViT backbone initialized with MAE) and introduces a two-stage training pipeline. The first stage, the Any-Granularity Mask Generator (AGG), automatically produces abundant mask-granularity pairs without manual annotation: a multi-granularity loop simulation iteratively adds negative clicks to a pre-trained IS model to generate diverse part proposals, while a granularity estimator quantifies each proposal using a linear combination of scale granularity (part area / object area) and semantic granularity (peak-difference ratio of the model's probability map within the part vs. the whole object). The second stage, Granularity-Controllable Learning (GCL), discretizes granularity into 10 bins, creates a lookup table of learnable embeddings, and fine-tunes the pre-trained model using LoRA (rank 8, applied to Q and K projection layers) with Normalized Focal Loss and iterative click sampling. This allows the model to acquire granularity controllability while preserving its original object-level segmentation performance at minimal computational cost.

## Results

GraCo w/ GT+AGG with ViT-L backbone achieves the best NoC@85 across all six benchmarks: 1.18 on GrabCut, 1.17 on Berkeley, 2.69 on SBD, 3.87 on DAVIS, 6.00 on PascalPart, and 4.92 on PartImageNet. On the part-level benchmarks, GraCo reduces NoC@85 by over 40% compared to the best single-granularity method (SimpleClick ViT-L: 10.23 on PascalPart, 8.14 on PartImageNet) and by over 55% compared to SAM ViT-L (13.15 and 11.94 respectively). On object-level benchmarks, GraCo also outperforms SAM* ViT-L (best multi-granularity baseline) by 0.54 clicks on GrabCut NoC@85 and 1.17 clicks on SBD NoC@85.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraCo w/ GT+AGG (ViT-L) outperforms all 11 baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest single-granularity method SimpleClick ViT-L, GraCo reduces NoC@85 from 1.38→1.18 on GrabCut, 1.40→1.17 on Berkeley, 4.12→3.87 on DAVIS, and dramatically from 10.23→6.00 on PascalPart and 8.14→4.92 on PartImageNet. Against the multi-granularity SAM ViT-L, GraCo wins by large margins on SBD (2.69 vs 5.99 NoC@85), PascalPart (6.00 vs 13.15), and PartImageNet (4.92 vs 11.94), and by smaller margins on GrabCut (1.18 vs 1.86) and Berkeley (1.17 vs 1.84). Even the best multi-granularity variant SAM* ViT-L is surpassed by GraCo on all benchmarks, with the largest gaps on SBD (2.69 vs 5.74) and part-level datasets. Earlier single-granularity methods (DIOS, LD, BRS, FocalClick, FocusCut, PseudoClick, CDNet, RITM, GPCIS) are all substantially outperformed, with NoC@85 gaps of 1–4 clicks on object-level and 5–9 clicks on part-level benchmarks. On the SA-1B subset (Table 2), GraCo ViT-L achieves NoC@90 of 3.96, comparable to SAM* ViT-L (2.97) while significantly outperforming SimpleClick ViT-L (6.74), confirming strong generalization to multi-granularity scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SimpleClick, a ViT-based interactive segmentation model, and extends it with a granularity control parameter that neither single-granularity IS (which ignores spatial ambiguity) nor multi-granularity IS (SAM, which produces redundant outputs) addresses. Conceptually, it departs from the multi-granularity paradigm by replacing the 'output many masks and let the user pick' strategy with an explicit input parameter that constrains the prediction to a desired level of detail. The Any-Granularity Mask Generator (AGG) is a novel data-engineering contribution that exploits the semantic property of pre-trained IS models to auto-generate training pairs, while the Granularity-Controllable Learning strategy borrows LoRA from NLP to efficiently inject controllability without degrading base performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GraCo offers a practical, low-cost path to flexible pixel-level annotation tools that adapt to both object and part-level tasks without requiring expensive multi-granularity annotations. The finding that AGG-generated proposals outperform manual part annotations for granularity learning suggests that data diversity may matter more than annotation precision for this type of controllability. Open questions include whether the granularity parameter generalizes to video segmentation or 3D settings, how the approach scales to very fine-grained part hierarchies (e.g., sub-part levels), and whether the reliance on a strong pre-trained IS model (SimpleClick) limits applicability to domains where such models are unavailable.
