## Problem

Existing prompt inversion methods for text-to-image diffusion models either rely on manual prompt engineering or optimize soft embeddings (pseudo-tokens) that are uninterpretable and lack semantic grounding in the model's vocabulary. Hard prompt inversion—recovering actual vocabulary tokens—is fundamentally discrete and exponentially large, and standard SGD-based optimization fails in practice due to vanishing gradients through the two-stage CLIP+U-Net pipeline and high variance in the stochastic diffusion trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target RGB image (encoded to a latent representation via the LDM encoder) and a frozen pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) as input, and produces a hard prompt of up to L tokens drawn strictly from the CLIP text encoder's existing vocabulary. The recovered prompt is human-readable and can be fed back into the same diffusion model to synthesize diverse images sharing the target's visual semantics, or composed with other inverted prompts for multi-concept generation and concept removal.

## Method

PH2P (Prompting Hard or Hardly Prompting) optimizes the diffusion loss (Eq. 2) over a restricted range of later, noisier timesteps (t ≥ 500 out of T), where the conditioning signal is empirically strongest and semantic information is encoded, rather than over all timesteps. To handle the discrete token constraint, the method employs delayed projected gradient descent: the embedding vector is updated freely via L-BFGS (a quasi-Newton method) at each iteration, then projected onto the nearest vocabulary embedding, balancing optimization efficiency with vocabulary feasibility. This restricted-timestep, L-BFGS-based projected descent yields better gradient flow and convergence than full-timestep optimization with Adam or SGD, producing crisp, interpretable prompts that outperform the concurrent PEZ baseline (which uses CLIP similarity matching) in both generation fidelity and prompt quality as measured by CLIP/LPIPS similarity and BertScore.

## Results

On COCO, PH2P achieves 0.77 CLIP similarity and 0.462 LPIPS similarity (lower is better) for generated images, outperforming the PEZ baseline (0.72 and 0.477 respectively). On LSUN, PH2P reaches 0.77 CLIP similarity and 0.463 LPIPS similarity versus PEZ's 0.70 and 0.480. In terms of prompt interpretability (BertScore against ground-truth captions on COCO), PH2P attains a precision of 0.803 and F1 of 0.820, compared to PEZ's 0.772 and 0.802. PH2P also yields higher LPIPS diversity (0.435 on COCO, 0.422 on LSUN), indicating more varied yet semantically consistent generated images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is PEZ [49], a concurrent (unpublished) method that optimizes hard prompts via CLIP image-text similarity matching with delayed projection and standard gradient descent. PH2P outperforms PEZ on every reported metric across both datasets: CLIP similarity improves by 5 points on COCO (0.77 vs 0.72) and 7 points on LSUN (0.77 vs 0.70); LPIPS similarity (lower is better) improves from 0.477 to 0.462 on COCO and from 0.480 to 0.463 on LSUN; and LPIPS diversity increases from 0.417 to 0.435 on COCO. On prompt interpretability (BertScore vs. ground-truth captions), PH2P achieves higher precision (0.803 vs 0.772) and F1 (0.820 vs 0.802), while recall is nearly identical (0.838 vs 0.835). Qualitatively, PH2P prompts are described as more crisp and free of the special/uninterpretable characters that frequently appear in PEZ outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion model inversion paradigm from continuous embedding-space optimization (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, P+) to discrete hard-prompt recovery within the model's existing vocabulary. It builds directly on the concurrent PEZ work [49], which introduced delayed projected gradient descent for hard prompt optimization, but replaces the external CLIP image-text similarity loss with the diffusion model's own denoising objective, making the approach agnostic to the choice of encoder. The key methodological departure is restricting optimization to later (noisier) timesteps where semantic conditioning is strongest, combined with L-BFGS quasi-Newton updates rather than standard SGD or Adam.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Recovering interpretable, editable hard prompts removes the need for domain-specific prompt engineering expertise and enables downstream applications like evolutionary multi-concept composition, concept removal via negative prompting, and unsupervised segmentation without auxiliary models. However, the evaluation is limited to Stable Diffusion v1.5 on 500 images with a single external baseline (PEZ), leaving open whether the timestep-restriction insight generalizes to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) or larger prompt lengths. The reliance on the CLIP text encoder's vocabulary also constrains the expressiveness of recovered prompts to what was seen during pre-training.
