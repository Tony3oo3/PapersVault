## Problem

When applying In-Context Learning (ICL) with Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for Visual Question Answering, practitioners typically resort to the simplest configuration—random sampling of demonstrations—yielding suboptimal performance. No prior work has systematically explored how to configure in-context sequences for VQA, nor has ICL been leveraged to probe the inner properties of LVLMs in this task. The paper addresses both gaps: identifying effective demonstration configuration strategies and uncovering fundamental properties of LVLMs during ICL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241574/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image and question along with n-shot (4, 8, or 16) image-question-answer triplets retrieved from the training set of the target VQA dataset, forming a multi-modal in-context sequence fed to Open-Flamingo (v1/v2) or IDEFICS. The output is a free-form answer text generated autoregressively. A key practical detail is that the SQA retrieval strategy (using ground-truth answers for retrieval) serves only as an upper-bound analysis tool, while SQPA substitutes a pseudo-answer generated by a first-round ICL pass to make answer-informed retrieval feasible at inference time.

## Method

The paper designs a systematic experimental framework combining five retrieval strategies (Random Sampling, Similar Image, Similar Question, Similar Question&Answer, Similar Question&Pseudo-Answer) with manipulation operations (mismatching images/answers/QA pairs, cross-modal reordering, and prepending instructions) to configure in-context sequences. It refines the NLP-derived Task Recognition / Task Learning decomposition for the VL domain by further splitting TR into format TR, visual TR, and linguistic TR, then uses targeted perturbations (e.g., mismatching triplets, varying shot counts, identical-input probes) to isolate and measure each component. Through exhaustive experiments on VQAv2, VizWiz, and OK-VQA, the study uncovers three properties of LVLMs: TR dominates TL (mismatched demonstrations cause minimal degradation), a short-cut inference effect causes the model to copy answers from question-similar demonstrations (copy rates up to ~90% for SQA), and the vision and language modules are only partially aligned (linguistic TR outweighs visual TR, and NLP-effective strategies like reordering or instructions lose efficacy in the VL setting). Based on these findings, the paper identifies that similar-image retrieval, cross-modal reordering, instructions (for linguistically stronger models like OFv2), and pseudo-answer retrieval at low shot counts are the strategies that consistently improve ICL VQA performance.

## Results

On VQAv2 (OFv2, 4-shot), Similar Image (SI) retrieval achieves 50.36% accuracy, outperforming Random Sampling (RS) at 48.82% by 1.54 points; on VizWiz the gap widens dramatically to 14.23 points (36.30% vs. 22.07%). The upper-bound SQA strategy (using ground-truth answers for retrieval) reaches 61.50% on VQAv2 and 41.46% on VizWiz (OFv2), far exceeding all practical methods. Mismatching experiments confirm that Task Recognition dominates Task Learning: replacing all images in RS demonstrations (RS(MI)) drops VQAv2 accuracy by only 0.21 points (49.94→49.73), while replacing all answers (RS(MA)) causes a much larger 1.00-point drop, and on VizWiz the answer mismatch causes a catastrophic 15.70-point collapse (27.21→11.51).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is across retrieval strategies (RS, SI, SQ, SQA, SQPA) rather than against external baselines, with IDEFICS serving as a complementary LVLM to validate the three discovered properties. SI consistently outperforms RS across all three datasets on both OFv1 and OFv2, with the largest gain on VizWiz (+11.21 points for OFv1, +11.89 for OFv2) due to low image quality. SQ shows mixed results: it beats RS on VizWiz and OK-VQA but underperforms on VQAv2 (48.83 vs. 49.94 for OFv2), attributed to the short-cut inference effect where the model copies answers from similar-question demonstrations. IDEFICS (Table 2) confirms the same patterns: SI (54.23) marginally beats RS (53.70) on VQAv2, SQ (52.34) underperforms RS, and the copy rate for SQ (75.34%) is far higher than RS (44.10%), validating that the short-cut effect is not specific to Open-Flamingo. The SQA upper bound (64.05 on IDEFICS) substantially exceeds all practical strategies, indicating significant room for improvement in demonstration retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TR/TL decomposition of in-context learning from Pan (2023) [34]—originally formulated for NLP LLMs—into the vision-language domain, further splitting TR into format, visual, and linguistic sub-components. It also draws on the NLP ICL configuration literature (similarity-based retrieval, ordering, label-space manipulation) and is the first to systematically apply these techniques to VQA, where prior work [45] had only explored image captioning. The experimental platform is Open-Flamingo (v1/v2) and IDEFICS, chosen specifically because they support ICL without having seen the evaluation VQA datasets during pre-training, avoiding the confound present in models like MMICL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three discovered properties—dominance of TR over TL, a strong short-cut/copying effect triggered by similar questions, and partial vision-language module incompatibility—provide a diagnostic framework for evaluating and debugging LVLM ICL behavior beyond simple accuracy. Practically, the paper identifies concrete strategies (similar-image retrieval, cross-modal reordering, pseudo-answer retrieval, and instructions for linguistically stronger backbones) that consistently improve VQA accuracy. However, findings are primarily validated on the Open-Flamingo family with only partial corroboration on IDEFICS, leaving open whether the short-cut effect and TR/TL imbalance generalize to other LVLM architectures (e.g., LLaVA, Otter) or whether architectural changes can mitigate the copying behavior at the model level.
