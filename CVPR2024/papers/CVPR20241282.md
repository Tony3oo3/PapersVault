## Problem

Universal image restoration requires a single model to handle multiple degradation types simultaneously, but existing methods rely on multi-partite mapping—using task-specific prompts or multi-encoder architectures that force the model to learn each degradation distribution in isolation. This approach ignores shared information across tasks (e.g., rain and fog co-occurring in real scenes) and typically demands large-scale pretrained models with hundreds of millions of parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241282/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded RGB image as input and produces the corresponding clean restored image. During training, 256×256 patches are randomly cropped from images across five tasks (deraining, low-light enhancement, desnowing, dehazing, deblurring) with task-specific batch weights (0.4/0.1/0.2/0.2/0.1). At inference, full-resolution images are processed using only 3 DDIM sampling steps, and the degraded image serves dual roles: it is explicitly injected into the diffusion algorithm as a condition and implicitly concatenated with the diffusing target as network input.

## Method

DiffUIR introduces a selective hourglass mapping strategy within a conditional diffusion framework built on a U-Net backbone. The forward process modifies RDDM's residual-based noising by subtracting a Shared Distribution Term (δ_t · I_in) that progressively reduces the condition's influence, mapping all task-specific distributions toward a shared impure Gaussian endpoint (0.1·I_in + β̄_T·ε) rather than pure noise. The reverse process then uses DDIM sampling (3 steps) to iteratively recover from this shared distribution back to the task-specific clean image, guided by both the explicit condition in the algorithm and the implicit concatenated condition. Training minimizes L1 loss on residual prediction (I_res = I_in − I_0) with the Adam optimizer over 300k iterations. The key insight is that the SDT coefficient (set to 0.9) creates a synergistic effect: strong enough condition guidance for accurate generation direction, yet sufficient distribution sharing to capture cross-task complementary information.

## Results

DiffUIR-L (36.26M params) achieves PSNR of 31.03, 25.12, 32.65, 32.94, and 29.17 dB on deraining, low-light enhancement, desnowing, dehazing, and deblurring respectively, outperforming the best universal baselines by 0.36–1.85 dB PSNR across tasks. The tiny variant DiffUIR-T (0.89M params, 1.72G FLOPS) still achieves 28.60/23.01/30.34/30.83/27.25 dB PSNR, surpassing the 8.93M-parameter AirNet on all five tasks. In zero-shot unknown-task generalization on TOLED, DiffUIR reaches 29.55 dB PSNR and 0.887 SSIM, beating the best universal baseline IDR (27.91/0.795) by 1.64 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffUIR-L outperforms all six universal baselines on every task where they report results: it beats Prompt-IR (35.59M) by +1.47 dB on deraining and +2.23 dB on enhancement, and DA-CLIP (174.1M) by +2.07 dB on deraining and +3.75 dB on dehazing, while using 5–43× fewer parameters. Against the same-parameter-count task-specific RDDM (36.26M), DiffUIR-L gains +0.29 to +2.16 dB PSNR across the five tasks, demonstrating that the shared-distribution mapping strategy is more effective than task-specific residual diffusion. The 0.89M DiffUIR-T surpasses the 8.93M AirNet on all five tasks (e.g., +3.73 dB on deraining, +8.18 dB on enhancement) and even exceeds Prompt-IR on deblurring (27.25 vs 27.21 dB). On zero-shot unknown-task generalization (TOLED), DiffUIR achieves 29.55 dB PSNR, beating the best universal method IDR (27.91 dB) by 1.64 dB and the best task-specific method NAFNet (26.89 dB) by 2.66 dB. The only task-specific methods that exceed DiffUIR-L are Restomer on deraining (33.96 vs 31.03) and MAXIM on desnowing (34.19 vs 32.65), but these require separate models per task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the residual denoising diffusion model (RDDM) framework, which introduced explicit condition fusion into the diffusion forward process, by adding a Shared Distribution Term (SDT) that progressively reduces the condition's weight toward an impure Gaussian endpoint. It departs from existing universal image restoration methods (AirNet, Painter, ProRes, DA-CLIP, Prompt-IR) that rely on multi-partite mapping—using task-specific prompts or encoders to learn separate distributions—by instead mapping all degradation distributions toward a single shared distribution during the forward pass and recovering task-specific structure during the reverse pass. The training objective and deterministic sampling are derived from DDPM and DDIM respectively, but the core novelty lies in the modified forward process (Eq. 4) and the resulting hourglass-shaped distribution trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight U-Net (as small as 0.89M parameters) with only 3 DDIM sampling steps can outperform large-scale transformer-based universal restoration methods by a substantial margin, suggesting that the mapping strategy matters more than model scale for this task. The zero-shot generalization to unknown mixed degradations (under-display camera) validates the shared-distribution hypothesis in practical settings. Open questions include whether the SDT approach generalizes to tasks with fundamentally different input-output structures (e.g., super-resolution vs. inpainting), whether the 0.9 coefficient is robust across domains, and whether the shared distribution concept can be formalized beyond the empirical 'impure Gaussian' framing.
