## Problem

Unsupervised action segmentation on long, untrimmed videos requires decoding temporally consistent per-frame labels from noisy frame-to-action affinities, but prior OT-based methods (TOT, UFSA) impose a balanced assignment assumption that is unreasonable for long-tailed action distributions and do not encode temporal consistency into the transport formulation itself. Existing HMM-based post-processing further requires knowing the action ordering a priori, failing on videos with order variations or repeated actions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed video frame embeddings (e.g., Fisher vectors) and learnable action class centroids, along with the number of action classes K, to produce a soft coupling matrix T ∈ R^{N×K} whose argmax per row yields temporally consistent per-frame action labels. The KOT cost matrix is derived from cosine distance between frame and action embeddings, optionally augmented with a temporal prior R that encourages banded-diagonal couplings across videos. The solver operates on full-length videos at test time (up to N=16k frames) while training uses 256 randomly sampled frames per video.

## Method

ASOT formulates segmentation as a fused, unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein (FUGW) OT problem: the KOT component captures visual similarity between frames and actions, the GW component penalizes assigning temporally adjacent frames (within radius r) to different actions via a structure-aware cost matrix C^v, and the unbalanced formulation replaces the column marginal constraint with a KL-divergence penalty weighted by λ, allowing only a subset of actions to appear in a video. The problem is solved efficiently on GPUs via projected mirror descent with entropy regularization (−εH(T)), converging in ~25 iterations at O(NK) per iteration by exploiting the sparsity of C^v and C^a. In the unsupervised pipeline, ASOT generates soft pseudo-labels that supervise an MLP frame encoder through a cross-entropy loss with stop-gradient on the pseudo-labels, while action embeddings are initialized via k-means and jointly optimized. The GW structural prior is applied at both training time (shaping pseudo-labels) and test time (refining final segmentations), and the method handles order variations and repeated actions without any fixed or estimated action ordering.

## Results

In the primary Unsupervised (full) evaluation setting, ASOT achieves 56.1/38.3/18.6 (MoF/F1/mIoU) on Breakfast, 52.9/35.1/24.7 on YouTube Instructions, 46.2/37.4/24.9 on 50 Salads (Mid), 59.3/53.6/30.1 on 50 Salads (Eval), and 70.4/68.0/45.9 on Desktop Assembly. These results are state-of-the-art for unsupervised action segmentation, with the largest margins over the strongest prior method UFSA(T) on 50 Salads (Mid) (+9.5 MoF) and Desktop Assembly (+5.0 MoF). In the Per Video setting, ASOT achieves 71.2/63.3/47.8 on YouTube Instructions and 73.4/68.0/47.6 on Desktop Assembly, outperforming TWF and ABD on MoF and F1 for Breakfast and YTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASOT outperforms all prior unsupervised methods in the Full evaluation setting across all four datasets. Against the strongest prior method UFSA(T) (transformer encoder), ASOT gains +9.5 MoF on 50 Salads (Mid) (46.2 vs 36.7), +3.5 MoF on 50 Salads (Eval) (59.3 vs 55.8), and +5.0 MoF on Desktop Assembly (70.4 vs 65.4), while using a simpler MLP encoder. Against UFSA(M) (comparable MLP encoder), ASOT improves by +9.7 MoF on YouTube Instructions (52.9 vs 43.2) and +11.5 MoF on 50 Salads (Eval) (59.3 vs 47.8). Against TOT [23], ASOT improves by up to +14.1 MoF on Desktop Assembly (70.4 vs 56.3). In the Per Video setting, ASOT beats TWF and ABD on MoF and F1 for Breakfast and YouTube Instructions, but underperforms on 50 Salads (Mid/Eval) where TWF and ABD achieve higher MoF (e.g., 71.8 vs 64.3 on FS Mid), likely because per-video Hungarian matching favors methods that find boundaries for dominant classes at the expense of rare actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASOT extends the line of unsupervised action segmentation methods (CTE, UDE, VTE, ASAL, TOT, UFSA) that rely on HMM-based post-processing to decode temporally consistent segmentations, by replacing the HMM with a fused, unbalanced Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport problem. The key conceptual departure is that temporal consistency is encoded as a structural prior in the GW component rather than as a transition matrix requiring known action ordering, and the unbalanced formulation relaxes the balanced assignment assumption that prior OT-based methods (TOT, UFSA) imposed. The method also draws on the broader OT-for-structured-prediction literature (FGW for graphs, unbalanced OT for fMRI alignment) and repurposes these tools for the first time in a video segmentation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASOT provides a principled, order-agnostic post-processing step that can be plugged into both unsupervised and supervised action segmentation pipelines, removing the restrictive assumption that action ordering is known or must be estimated. The demonstration that a simple MLP encoder with OT-based decoding outperforms a transformer encoder with additional learned modules (UFSA) suggests that the OT formulation itself carries substantial of the temporal reasoning burden. Open questions include whether the approach can be backpropagated through for semi/fully-supervised settings, how to handle learning across all activity categories simultaneously (rather than per-category), and whether the per-video evaluation gap can be closed by estimating clusters from a single video.
