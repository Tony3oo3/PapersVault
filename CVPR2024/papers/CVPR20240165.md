## Problem

Plane-based hybrid NeRF representations (e.g., TensoRF, HexPlane) achieve high rendering quality but require tens to hundreds of megabytes of storage, and existing compression methods either rely on parameter-efficient data structures that still leave ~50MB footprints or apply only quantization and entropy coding while overlooking transform coding. The key unexplored question is whether the non-linear transforms proven effective in neural image compression can be adapted to compress NeRF feature planes, given that training such transforms from scratch on millions of 3D scenes is infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained 2D feature planes from a plane-based hybrid NeRF (TensoRF-VM or HexPlane) as input, where each plane has a channel dimension that may differ from the 3-channel assumption of standard image codecs. The output is a single multiplexed bitstream (~0.5 MB) containing entropy-coded latent codes, the content-adaptive decoder head parameters, CP-decomposed feature residual vectors, quantized tiny MLPs, and metadata. At the receiver, the bitstream is demultiplexed into substreams, each independently entropy-decoded, and the reconstructed feature planes are queried once to enable subsequent volume rendering for novel view synthesis.

## Method

NeRFCodec reuses a pre-trained 2D neural image codec (Cheng et al., CVPR 2020) as the non-linear transform backbone, replacing only the encoder's first layer and the decoder's last layer to match the feature plane channel dimension, then fine-tunes the full encoder and decoder head per scene while keeping the decoder backbone frozen. A hyperprior network estimates the probability distribution of quantized latent codes for entropy coding, and a feature compensation module adds a CP-decomposed residual matrix (outer product of two low-rank vectors) to recover high-frequency details lost during lossy compression. Training is multi-stage: pre-train the base NeRF, warm up the codec with feature-plane reconstruction loss, jointly optimize the codec and NeRF with a combined rendering loss and entropy loss, then perform quantization-aware training on transmittable parameters. This transfer-learning strategy avoids the infeasibility of training a neural codec on millions of 3D scenes while the non-linear transform outperforms the linear wavelet transform used in Masked Wavelet NeRF, achieving a 159.7× overall compression ratio with 0.5 MB per scene.

## Results

NeRFCodec compresses a single scene to 0.453 MB (≈0.5 MB) while achieving 35.08 dB PSNR and 0.981 SSNR on the Chair scene (NeRF-Synthetic). The overall compression ratio is ×159.7 (72.342 MB → 0.453 MB), with the feature planes alone compressed by ×1345.3 (69.953 MB → 0.052 MB). The method is evaluated across three datasets (NeRF-Synthetic, NSVF-Synthetic, Tanks and Temples) and achieves superior rate-distortion performance compared to all baselines at every bitrate point tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRFCodec outperforms all 10 baselines across the three datasets in the memory–quality scatter plot (Fig. 4). Against parameter-compression baselines, it beats Masked Wavelet NeRF [32] (which uses a linear wavelet transform), VQRF [24] (vector quantization + entropy coding), and BiRF [33] (binary quantization), with the improvement over MWNeRF attributed to the non-linear transform replacing the wavelet transform. Against parameter-efficient data-structure baselines (DVGO [36], Plenoxels [43], TensoRF [9], CCNeRF [39], Re:NeRF [15], Instant-NGP [30], K-Planes [17]), NeRFCodec achieves substantially lower memory footprints at equal or higher PSNR, since those methods still require tens of megabytes for raw parameters. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that simply reusing a 2D image codec without architectural modification yields 1.786 MB / 29.96 dB, while the tuned codec reaches 1.150 MB / 33.25 dB, confirming that the content-adaptive encoder/decoder head design is critical for the 0.453 MB / 35.08 dB result.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRFCodec sits at the intersection of two mature research threads: neural image compression (autoencoder + hyperprior + entropy coding, as in Ballé et al. and Cheng et al.) and NeRF parameter compression (VQRF, Masked Wavelet NeRF, BiRF). The key conceptual departure from prior NeRF compression is the use of a learned non-linear transform rather than linear wavelet transforms or direct quantization, and the key practical insight is that a pretrained 2D image codec backbone transfers effectively to 3D feature-plane compression with only the encoder/decoder heads re-initialized per scene. This repurposes the image compression literature's non-linear basis as a 3D scene compression tool, building on TensoRF's plane-based representation and its CP decomposition for the residual compensation module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving a single-scene representation in ~0.5 MB while maintaining high PSNR is practically significant for bandwidth-constrained 3D media delivery and storage. However, the per-scene fine-tuning of the codec (100k+ iterations) is a substantial practical bottleneck that limits deployment to offline or pre-computed settings, and the ablation showing that a codec trained on only 8 scenes fails to generalize to unseen objects highlights that a truly generalizable neural feature codec remains an open problem. Future work on large-scale feature-plane datasets (e.g., from Objaverse or OmniObject3D) to train a universal codec would be the natural next step to remove the per-scene training requirement.
