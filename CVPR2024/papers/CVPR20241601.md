## Problem

Pseudo-label-based semi-supervised learning on class-imbalanced data suffers from cascading bias: the classifier favors majority classes, and biased pseudo-labels further entrench this bias during training. Existing CISSL methods either assume the unlabeled set's class distribution is known and matches the labeled set, or rely on post-hoc rebalancing (cRT, logit adjustment) that only accounts for the labeled distribution, failing when the two distributions mismatch.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/STL-10, 64×64 for Small-ImageNet) as both labeled and unlabeled training inputs, plus a single patternless probe image (solid color or out-of-range non-image input) fed through the classifier at each training iteration and at test time. The output is a refined class label obtained by taking argmax over logits that have had the probe-image bias logits subtracted, effectively decoupling feature-based evidence from class-frequency bias.

## Method

CDMAD measures the classifier's per-class bias by computing logits on a solid color image (assumed irrelevant to learned features) at every training iteration, then subtracts these bias logits from the logits of weakly augmented unlabeled samples to produce debiased pseudo-labels used in the base SSL algorithm's consistency loss. At test time, the same subtraction is applied to test-sample logits before argmax. The key insight is that the probe-image logits implicitly encode the combined class distributions of both labeled and unlabeled sets, so no explicit distribution estimation is needed and the method remains effective under severe distribution mismatch. Theoretically, CDMAD is a CISSL extension of post-hoc logit adjustment where the fixed class prior π is replaced by the learned classifier prior P_θ(y|I), and it is proven Fisher consistent for minimizing balanced error. No additional parameters, auxiliary classifiers, or extra training stages are required beyond the base SSL algorithm (FixMatch or ReMixMatch).

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT with matched class distributions (γ_l=γ_u), ReMixMatch+CDMAD achieves 88.3% bACC / 88.1% GM at γ=50, 85.5% / 85.3% at γ=100, and 82.5% / 82.0% at γ=150, outperforming all baselines. Under the more challenging mismatched setting (γ_l=100, γ_u=1, γ_u unknown), ReMixMatch+CDMAD reaches 83.1% bACC / 82.7% GM while FixMatch+CDMAD reaches 87.5% / 87.1%. On CIFAR-100-LT (100 classes), ReMixMatch+CDMAD achieves 57.0% bACC at γ=20 and 44.9% at γ=100, and on Small-ImageNet-127, FixMatch+CDMAD reaches 59.3% bACC at 64×64 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDMAD consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across all four datasets and both matched and mismatched class distribution settings. On CIFAR-10-LT (γ=100), ReMixMatch+CDMAD (85.5% bACC) beats the strongest baselines ReMixMatch+SAW+cRT (85.4%) and ReMixMatch+ABC (84.5%) by 0.1 and 1.0 points respectively, while FixMatch+CDMAD (83.6%) surpasses FixMatch+CoSSL (83.2%) and FixMatch+DARP+cRT (82.4%). Under the mismatched setting (γ_l=100, γ_u=1), CDMAD's advantage widens: ReMixMatch+CDMAD reaches 83.1% bACC versus 80.9% for ReMixMatch*+SAW+cRT and 80.6% for ReMixMatch*+DARP+LA, demonstrating that LA and cRT degrade when the unlabeled distribution is unknown. On CIFAR-100-LT (γ=50), ReMixMatch+CDMAD (51.1%) leads ReMixMatch+CoSSL (48.9%) by 2.2 points, and on Small-ImageNet-127 (64×64), FixMatch+CDMAD (59.3%) exceeds FixMatch+ABC (56.1%) by 3.2 points. CDMAD also outperforms the recent ACR method (81.8% vs. 83.6% bACC at γ=100) and FreeMatch+SAW+cRT (82.8% vs. 84.8%), confirming its compatibility with newer SSL backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDMAD extends post-hoc logit adjustment (LA) from fully supervised class-imbalanced learning to the semi-supervised setting, where the unlabeled set's class distribution is unknown and may differ from the labeled set. While prior CISSL methods (DARP, CReST, ABC, CoSSL, SAW) either assume the unlabeled distribution matches the labeled one or rely on post-hoc corrections (cRT, LA) that cannot account for the unknown unlabeled distribution, CDMAD replaces LA's fixed class-frequency prior with a data-driven estimate obtained by probing the classifier on a patternless (solid-color) image. This probe implicitly encodes the combined influence of both labeled and unlabeled class distributions on the classifier's bias, positioning CDMAD as a minimal, plug-in refinement of base SSL algorithms (FixMatch, ReMixMatch) rather than a new training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in real-world SSL deployments, the unlabeled pool's class distribution is rarely known and often mismatches the labeled set, yet most CISSL methods either ignore this or require expensive distribution estimation. CDMAD's simplicity (a few lines of code, no extra parameters or training stages) and its Fisher-consistency guarantee for balanced error make it an attractive drop-in improvement. The authors acknowledge that the use of a solid-color image as a bias probe 'lacks a firm theoretical basis,' leaving open the question of a rigorous justification for why this particular input isolates the classifier's prior. Broader applicability beyond CNN-based image classification (e.g., transformers, non-image modalities) remains unexplored.
