## Problem

Existing semantic correspondence methods struggle with three interrelated challenges: semantically irrelevant regions with similar appearances get confused, inconsistent object scales between image pairs hinder detail-level matching, and nearby pixels remain indistinguishable. These issues cause matching results to violate geometric layouts and lose fine-grained detail, particularly under large appearance variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source-target RGB image pair at 256×256 resolution and produces a dense semantic matching flow at 1/2 scale (128×128 offset field), which is the finest end-to-end correspondence resolution reported in the literature. The ViT-B/16 backbone initially encodes at 1/16 scale (16×16 patches), the PFSR module upscales features to 1/8, and the MMFI module refines the flow to 1/2, yielding pixel-level correspondences in units of 2×2 pixels.

## Method

LPMFlow is a three-module pipeline built on a ViT-B/16 (iBOT-pretrained) backbone. The Layout-aware Representation Learning (LARL) module uses cascaded transformer blocks with region-based position embeddings and a novel Referenced Patch Token Correction (RPTC) task—where foreground source tokens are replaced with [CLS] tokens and recovered via a self-contrastive InfoNCE loss against 3×3 neighborhood embeddings—to enforce shared geometric layout in the representation. The Progressive Feature Super-Resolution (PFSR) module progressively upscales features from 1/16 to 1/8 using feature super-resolution blocks (cascaded window/shifted-window attention) and scale-asymmetric cross-attention between source and target, then constructs four cross-scaled 4D correlation tensors (1/16×1/16, 1/16×1/8, 1/8×1/16, 1/8×1/8). The Multi-scale Matching Flow Integration (MMFI) module converts these tensors to 2D flows via soft-argmax and refines them through a coarse-to-fine Swin-attention structure with progressively smaller windows (16×16, 8×8, 4×4) to produce the final 1/2-scale dense flow. Training is end-to-end, supervised by endpoint-error loss on pseudo-flows derived from sparse keypoint annotations, combined with the RPTC contrastive loss whose guidance intensity λ decays gradually during training.

## Results

On SPair-71K, LPMFlow (iBOT backbone) achieves 65.6% PCK@0.1 and 46.7% PCK@0.05, outperforming the closest competitor ACTR (62.1% and 42.0%) by 3.5% and 4.7% respectively. On PF-PASCAL, LPMFlow reaches 94.3% PCK@0.1 and 82.4% PCK@0.05, edging out ACTR (94.0% and 81.2%). On PF-WILLOW (generalizability test, trained on PF-PASCAL), LPMFlow scores 87.6% PCK@bbox and 81.0% PCK@bkp, surpassing ACTR by 0.4% and 1.1%. The method uses 93.9M parameters with 3.8 GB memory and 85.7 ms inference time on a TITAN RTX, comparable to ACTR (87.8M, 3.9 GB, 84.1 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPMFlow outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor ACTR (iBOT backbone, 2D flow, no multi-scale), LPMFlow gains 4.7 points on SPair-71K PCK@0.05 (46.7 vs. 42.0), 3.5 points on PCK@0.1 (65.6 vs. 62.1), 1.2 points on PF-PASCAL PCK@0.05 (82.4 vs. 81.2), and 1.1 points on PF-WILLOW PCK@bkp (81.0 vs. 79.9), while matching or slightly exceeding ACTR on the looser PF-PASCAL thresholds (94.3 vs. 94.0 at α=0.1). Against ResNet-101-based methods, the margins are substantially larger: LPMFlow exceeds CATs (55.2) and TransMatcher (57.9) by 10.4 and 7.7 points respectively on SPair-71K PCK@0.1, and surpasses MMNet-FCN (50.4) by 15.2 points. Even when comparing against the iBOT-backbone variants of CATs* (55.2) and TransMatcher* (57.9), LPMFlow leads by 10.4 and 7.7 points on SPair-71K PCK@0.1, confirming that the gains come from the pipeline design rather than backbone quality. On generalizability (PF-WILLOW, trained on PF-PASCAL), LPMFlow achieves the best PCK@bbox (87.6) and PCK@bkp (81.0), beating ACTR by 0.4 and 1.1 points. In terms of efficiency, LPMFlow (178.9M params, 3.8 GB, 85.7 ms) is comparable to ACTR (172.8M, 3.9 GB, 84.1 ms) and far lighter than MMNet-FCN (64.7M head but 5.4 GB, 258.6 ms) or NCNet (1.2 GB but 222.9 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

LPMFlow extends the transformer-based semantic correspondence line initiated by CATs (2D matching flow representation) and ACTR (asymmetric feature learning with flow super-resolution), both from the same research group. It departs from prior multi-scale approaches (MMNet, DHPF) that either fuse lower-scale matching results into larger scales or align features before uniform correspondence construction, instead proposing a progressive feature super-resolution pipeline that upscales features before computing cross-scale 4D correlation tensors. The layout-aware representation learning module draws on BERT-style token replacement/recovery tasks (RPTC) and region-based position embeddings from tracking literature (OSTrack), repurposing them to enforce shared geometric layout consistency between source and target tokens. The coarse-to-fine Swin-attention refinement for multi-scale flow integration builds on ACTR's multi-path flow fusion but generalizes it to a hierarchical window-size schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LPMFlow sets a new SOTA on SPair-71K and PF-PASCAL with the most notable gains at stricter thresholds (α:0.05), suggesting genuinely finer-grained pixel-level matching rather than just coarser-scale accuracy. The progressive feature super-resolution strategy—upsampling features before computing correspondence rather than upscaling similarity matrices—offers a practical recipe for handling large intra-class scale variations that could transfer to other dense prediction tasks. However, the authors acknowledge limitations: heavy reliance on [CLS] tokens, dependence on sparse key-point annotations for supervision, and inapplicability to multi-instance correspondence settings, leaving open whether the approach scales to scenes with multiple objects of the same category or benefits from denser supervision signals.
