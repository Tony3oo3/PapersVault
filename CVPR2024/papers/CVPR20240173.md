## Problem

Existing satellite image pre-training methods either ignore the multi-scale information inherent in remote sensing data or, like ScaleMAE, rely on GSD-based positional encodings that are restricted to single-modality (RGB) imagery and cannot handle multi-spectral data where different channels have different ground sample distances. The paper asks whether multi-scale representations can be learned effectively without such modality-specific encodings, using a simpler and more general mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240173/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method takes a satellite image (224×224 RGB or 96×96 multi-spectral with 10 Sentinel-2 bands grouped by GSD) and produces reconstructed images at up to three spatial resolutions (H×W, 2H×2W, 4H×4W) via a cascade of upsampling blocks. The base MAE encoder operates only on the lowest-resolution version, while higher-resolution targets are generated by downsampling the original. During fine-tuning, the same ViT-Large backbone outputs class labels for single-label land cover classification or multi-label soft-margin predictions on BigEarthNet.

## Method

SatMAE++ extends the SatMAE masked autoencoder by appending a chain of convolution-based upsampling blocks (transpose convolution → normalization → leaky ReLU → residual conv block → linear projection) after the standard MAE decoder, each doubling spatial resolution and producing a reconstruction target at a higher scale. The key design choice is to retain standard sinusoidal positional encodings rather than ScaleMAE's GSD-based encoding, making the approach modality-agnostic and directly applicable to multi-spectral data with heterogeneous channel resolutions. The training loss is a weighted sum of MSE at the base scale and L1 at each higher scale, encouraging the encoder to capture fine-grained structural details. The framework is trivially extensible to additional scale levels by stacking more upsampling blocks, and ablations show diminishing returns beyond three scales (63.23% vs. 62.86% vs. 61.61% top-1 on fMoW-Sentinel).

## Results

On fMoW-RGB, SatMAE++ achieves 78.14% top-1 accuracy, surpassing ScaleMAE (77.9%) and SatMAE (77.84%). On fMoW-Sentinel, it reaches 63.23% top-1 accuracy with ViT-Large, a 1.75% gain over SatMAE. On downstream tasks, SatMAE++ sets new state-of-the-art across all six datasets: 99.04% on EuroSAT, 97.48% on RESISC-45, 97.62% on UC-Merced, and 85.11% mAP on BigEarthNet (a 2.5% mAP gain over the previous best).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SatMAE++ outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the closest competitor ScaleMAE [20], SatMAE++ wins on fMoW-RGB (78.14% vs 77.9%) and RESISC-45 (97.48% vs 95.7%), while ScaleMAE is inapplicable to multi-spectral data due to its GSD-based positional encoding requiring uniform channel resolution. Compared to the primary baseline SatMAE [5], SatMAE++ gains 0.3% on fMoW-RGB, 1.75% on fMoW-Sentinel, 3.3% on EuroSAT, 2.68% on RESISC-45, 3.57% on UC-Merced, and 2.98% mAP on BigEarthNet. Against earlier self-supervised methods (GASSL, SeCo, MoCo-V2), the improvements are substantially larger (e.g., 85.11% vs 80.20% mAP on BigEarthNet vs GASSL). On BigEarthNet, SatMAE++ also surpasses SeCo [16] (85.11% vs 82.62% mAP), which was previously the strongest multi-label classifier. The ablation confirms that multi-scale pre-training drives these gains: going from 1 to 2 scales adds 1.25% and from 2 to 3 scales adds 0.37% on fMoW-Sentinel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SatMAE (NeurIPS 2022), which adapted the MAE framework for multi-spectral and temporal satellite imagery, by adding multi-scale reconstruction through convolution-based upsampling blocks. It deliberately departs from ScaleMAE's approach of encoding scale via GSD-based positional encodings and a Laplacian pyramid decoder, arguing that standard sinusoidal positional encodings combined with multi-scale reconstruction are sufficient and, crucially, extensible to multi-spectral data where different channels have different GSD resolutions. The architectural lineage traces from vanilla MAE → SatMAE (channel grouping, spectral positional encodings) → SatMAE++ (multi-scale reconstruction head), while the conceptual contribution is showing that scale information can be learned implicitly through reconstruction at multiple resolutions rather than explicitly injected into token representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SatMAE++ provides a unified pre-training recipe that works for both RGB and multi-spectral satellite imagery without modality-specific architectural changes, filling a gap left by ScaleMAE's RGB-only GSDPE. The finding that multi-scale pre-training accelerates finetuning convergence (12th vs. 20th epoch peak) is practically valuable for practitioners with limited compute budgets. Limitations include occasional failure to reconstruct fine-grained structural details and the observation that gains diminish beyond two scale levels, suggesting a ceiling on this approach. The authors note future work on extending multi-scale pre-training to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), which remains an open question for the satellite imagery community.
