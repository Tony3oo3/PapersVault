## Problem

In video super-resolution, motion compensation during frame alignment requires resampling sub-pixel values, yet existing works uniformly default to bilinear interpolation without examining its impact. The paper shows that bilinear (and bicubic) interpolation imposes a low-pass filtering effect that attenuates high-frequency content essential for super-resolution, while nearest-neighbor avoids smoothing but introduces spatial distortions. No prior work has systematically studied or addressed the resampling step in alignment for VSR.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video sequence and produces a 4× super-resolved high-resolution sequence. The proposed alignment module is a plug-in component that operates on intermediate feature maps (not raw pixels): it receives a reference feature map, a current-frame feature map, and a motion displacement field (from optical flow or learned offsets), and outputs a motion-compensated reference feature map. It is designed to be inserted into existing VSR backbones (BasicVSR for CNN, PSRT-recurrent for Transformer) without altering their overall architecture, adding only ~0.2% parameters.

## Method

The core contribution is an implicit resampling-based alignment that replaces explicit interpolation kernels with a coordinate network and window-based cross-attention. The motion offset is decomposed into an integer part (which selects a w×w window in the reference frame) and a decimal part (which is encoded via sinusoidal positional encoding to capture sub-pixel position). Both the query (current-frame pixel) and the key/value (reference window pixels) are processed through PE-MLP coordinate networks that jointly model feature values and positional information. Alignment is then performed via cross-attention: the affinity matrix is computed from the similarity of encoded features and positional encodings, so no explicit interpolation kernel is applied. This avoids the low-pass filtering of bilinear/bicubic interpolation and the quantization distortion of nearest-neighbor, while the window-based formulation keeps computation at O(w²·HW) rather than full global attention. The module is trained end-to-end with the VSR backbone and generalizes across feature scales and alignment configurations without retraining.

## Results

On REDS4 (BI degradation), IA-RT achieves 32.90 PSNR / 0.9138 SSIM, outperforming PSRT-recurrent (32.72 / 0.9106) by +0.18 PSNR with identical parameter count (13.4M). On Vid4 (BI), IA-RT reaches 28.26 PSNR, surpassing RVRT (27.99) and VRT (27.93). Under BD degradation on Vid4, IA-RT attains 29.68 PSNR / 0.8884 SSIM, the best among all compared methods. The CNN variant IA-CNN improves over BasicVSR by +0.26 PSNR on REDS4 BI with only 2.2M additional parameters (8.5M vs 6.3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IA-RT outperforms all eight compared methods on REDS4 BI degradation, with the closest competitor being RVRT (32.75 PSNR) and its direct baseline PSRT-recurrent (32.72 PSNR), both trailed by 0.15–0.18 PSNR. On Vid4 BI, IA-RT leads RVRT by +0.27 PSNR and VRT by +0.33 PSNR, establishing SOTA among second-order propagation methods. Under BD degradation on Vid4, IA-RT achieves 29.68 PSNR, surpassing RVRT (29.54) and VRT (29.42) by 0.14 and 0.26 PSNR respectively. The CNN variant IA-CNN improves over BasicVSR by +0.26 PSNR on REDS4 BI and +0.18 on Vid4 BI, while remaining below BasicVSR++ and RVRT as expected for a first-order model. The one notable exception is Vimeo90K-T, where IA-RT (38.14 PSNR) falls slightly below PSRT-recurrent (38.27) and VRT (38.20), which the authors attribute to difficulty in estimating accurate optical flow on that dataset, limiting the benefit of sub-pixel sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of video super-resolution alignment and implicit neural representations. It builds directly on the alignment paradigms established by EDVR (deformable convolution), RVRT (deformable attention), and PSRT-recurrent (patch alignment), all of which perform motion compensation via explicit resampling (bilinear or nearest-neighbor). The key conceptual departure is borrowing the coordinate-network / positional-encoding machinery from implicit image representations (NeRF, LIF) and scene-flow priors to replace explicit interpolation with a learned, implicit resampling through window-based cross-attention. The paper also draws on the frequency-response analysis of classical interpolation kernels to motivate why the default bilinear choice is suboptimal for super-resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that bilinear interpolation — the near-universal default in VSR alignment — acts as an implicit low-pass filter that attenuates the very high-frequency content super-resolution aims to recover is a useful diagnostic for the field. The proposed implicit resampling module is lightweight (≈0.01–0.2 M extra parameters) and plug-and-play across CNN and Transformer backbones, making it an easy upgrade for existing VSR pipelines. However, the absolute gains are modest (≈0.1–0.2 dB PSNR), the method's interpretability is reduced, and its benefit diminishes when optical flow is inaccurate (e.g., on Vimeo90K). Open questions include whether the coordinate-network resampling generalizes to other alignment-heavy tasks (frame interpolation, video deblurring) and whether the window-based attention can be made more efficient at larger window sizes.
