## Problem

Real-time relighting of animatable human head avatars remains unsolved because existing geometry representations (meshes, volumetric primitives) cannot capture sub-millimeter structures like hair strands, and existing relighting models either lack all-frequency reflection fidelity or are too slow for real-time rendering under high-resolution continuous illumination. No prior method achieves photorealistic, real-time relighting that simultaneously handles the diverse scattering behaviors of skin, hair, and eyes in a unified, drivable representation.

## Input / Output

Training uses multiview video from a calibrated light-stage (110 cameras at 4096×2668, 460 LEDs, 90 Hz, ~144k frames per subject) with time-multiplexed illumination. At inference, the model consumes a 256-dim expression latent code (from a CVAE or headset tracking), per-eye gaze vectors, a view direction, and an illumination source (prefiltered environment map or point lights), producing a splatted RGB image of the relit head in real time. The same latent code also decodes a coarse mesh for headset-driven animation, and the per-Gaussian radiance transfer coefficients enable arbitrary relighting without retraining.

## Method

The geometry is a set of 3D anisotropic Gaussians anchored on a shared UV texture map of a template head, decoded by 2D CNNs conditioned on a CVAE latent code and gaze, with positions guided by barycentric interpolation of a coarse mesh to avoid local minima. For appearance, the paper introduces a learnable radiance transfer that factorizes each Gaussian's color into a view-independent diffuse term (RGB spherical harmonics up to 3rd order plus monochrome SH from 4th to 8th order, preconvolving occlusion, subsurface scattering, and multibounce) and a view-dependent specular term (a normalized angle-based spherical Gaussian lobe aligned with the reflection vector, modulated by a learned view-dependent visibility that absorbs Fresnel and geometric attenuation). The key architectural insight is integrating the target illumination at the output of the neural decoder rather than feeding it as input, which preserves linearity of light transport and enables real-time all-frequency reflection via a single mipmap lookup per Gaussian. An explicit two-sphere eye model with frozen Gaussian positions and fixed surface normals is added to capture mirror-like corneal glints and support refraction through a view-conditioned albedo.

## Results

On held-out expression segments, the full method (3D Gaussians + explicit eye model + learned radiance transfer) achieves PSNR 36.50, SSIM 0.943, and LPIPS 0.110. On held-out light patterns, the same configuration reaches PSNR 34.04, SSIM 0.858, and LPIPS 0.148. The method outperforms the strongest appearance baseline (EyeNeRF) by ~1.4 dB PSNR on held-out segments and ~3.2 dB on held-out lights, and outperforms the linear appearance model [87] by ~2.7 dB PSNR on held-out segments while reducing LPIPS from 0.184 to 0.110.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EyeNeRF [33] (spherical-harmonics-based appearance), the proposed learned radiance transfer wins by 1.4 dB PSNR (36.53 vs 35.11) and 0.005 SSIM on held-out segments, with a smaller LPIPS gap (0.111 vs 0.113); on held-out lights the gap widens to 3.0 dB PSNR (33.85 vs 30.84). Against the linear appearance model of Yang et al. [87], the proposed method gains 2.7 dB PSNR (36.53 vs 33.83) and substantially reduces LPIPS (0.111 vs 0.184) on held-out segments, though on held-out lights the linear model achieves slightly higher SSIM (0.870 vs 0.831) while still trailing on PSNR (32.83 vs 33.85) and LPIPS (0.202 vs 0.148). For geometry, replacing 3D Gaussians with MVP [41] voxels costs 0.7 dB PSNR (36.53 vs 35.79) and 0.023 LPIPS (0.111 vs 0.134) when paired with the same appearance model, confirming that Gaussian splatting is critical for strand-level hair detail. The explicit eye model (EEM) provides a modest additional gain of ~0.03 dB PSNR over the Gaussian-only geometry (Row B vs E) but is essential for convincing corneal glints qualitatively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting for efficient geometry representation, Precomputed Radiance Transfer (PRT) for separating intrinsic scene properties from extrinsic illumination, and explicit parametric eye models for gaze control. The key conceptual departure from prior neural relighting work (Bi et al., Yang et al., EyeNeRF) is that instead of learning a direct illumination-to-radiance mapping, the authors learn the intrinsic radiance transfer function and integrate it with the target illumination at the output, preserving the linearity of light transport while gaining the expressiveness of spherical Gaussians for specular lobes. The view-dependent normal that can rotate along fiber tangents for hair while remaining fixed for skin surfaces is a novel unification that avoids predefining per-Gaussian material types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that real-time, all-frequency relighting of dynamic head avatars—including strand-level hair detail and mirror-like eye reflections—is achievable on consumer VR hardware, a milestone for telepresence and interactive applications. The learned PRT framework (SH for diffuse + spherical Gaussians for specular) is a clean, generalizable recipe that could be adopted beyond heads. Key limitations remain: the method requires light-stage capture with known illumination, coarse mesh and gaze tracking as preprocessing, and scales linearly with the number of avatars rendered simultaneously. Extending to in-the-wild inputs with unknown illumination and enabling scalable end-to-end training without explicit tracking are the most pressing open questions.
