## Problem

Event-based video frame interpolation networks suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in domains with different camera settings, lighting conditions, or event trigger thresholds, unlike frame-based VFI which generalizes reasonably across domains. Existing adaptation strategies (meta-learning, cyclic self-supervision) fail to bridge this gap effectively, and practical deployment requires online adaptation without access to source data or high-frame-rate ground truth in the target domain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LFR video frame triplets (I_0, I_1, I_2) and inter-frame event streams converted to voxel grids (E_{0→1}, E_{2→1}), producing interpolated intermediate frames at arbitrary times t_0 ∈ [0,1] and t_1 ∈ [1,2]. During test-time adaptation, only LFR frames are available in the target domain—no HFR ground truth—so the network must self-supervise from the generated outputs. For efficiency, images are cropped to 320×320 during online updates, and the network is updated only once per triplet regardless of how many intermediate frames are generated.

## Method

TTA-EVF introduces three key components for online adaptation. First, a Norm-Residual (NR) Block replaces conventional residual blocks in encoders, splitting features into a skip path and a group-normalized path that are fused via concatenation and convolution with a residual connection, enabling selective parameter updates that balance adaptability with statistical stability. Second, Reliable Pixel Sampling (RPS) employs a teacher-student framework where pixels are deemed reliable only when both networks agree within an adaptive threshold ρ, computed per-scene from LFR reconstruction error while excluding static regions via optical-flow-based motion masking. Third, Patch-Mixed Sampling (PMS) maintains a memory bank of reliable generated samples and blends them with current outputs using a beta-distributed mixing ratio (0.7–1.0) to prevent overfitting and catastrophic forgetting during continuous online learning. The total training objective combines a self-supervised LFR reconstruction loss, the RPS consistency loss, and the PMS regularization loss.

## Results

On cross-domain event-based VFI (Table 1), TTA-EVF achieves the highest PSNR across all four source-target pairs and all three base networks. The largest gains appear on the most challenging R→B transfer (HighREV→BS-ERGB), where TimeLens improves from 22.32 dB (No Adapt) to 26.91 dB (+4.59 dB) and CBMNet from 21.89 to 27.16 dB (+5.27 dB). On the continuous domain-shifting ERDS dataset (Table 2), TTA-EVF with CBMNet reaches 33.53 dB total PSNR versus 31.42 dB without adaptation. Notably, TTA-EVF also surpasses supervised and unsupervised methods trained directly on the target domain (Table 3), e.g., 34.24 dB vs. 32.81 dB supervised on HighREV with TimeLens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTA-EVF is compared against two baselines: MetaVFI [11], a meta-learning approach for frame-based VFI adapted here to the event setting, and Cycle [16], which incorporates TimeReplayer's cyclic self-supervised loss into the TTA framework. MetaVFI yields only marginal gains over No Adapt (e.g., +0.39 dB on B→R with TimeLens), confirming that per-scene meta-learning is insufficient for large event-domain shifts. Cycle [16] provides moderate improvements (e.g., +0.97 dB on B→R with TimeLens) but still trails TTA-EVF by 1.2–2.8 dB across all settings, because cyclic consistency alone cannot filter unreliable pixels in shifted domains. TTA-EVF's advantage is most pronounced on the hardest transfer (R→B), where it gains 2.8–5.3 dB over No Adapt versus only 1.0–2.5 dB for Cycle. Additionally, TTA-EVF outperforms fully supervised and unsupervised methods trained on the target domain itself (Table 3), achieving 1.43 dB higher PSNR than supervised TimeLens on HighREV, demonstrating that online test-time adaptation can bridge domain gaps better than offline target-domain training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the teacher-student pseudo-labeling framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of generating reliable pseudo-labels for classification, it identifies spatially reliable pixels in generated intermediate frames to enable self-supervised VFI training without ground truth. It extends the TTA paradigm—previously applied to image classification and segmentation (TENT, Ev-TTA)—to the low-level vision task of event-based video frame interpolation, where the absence of HFR ground truth in the target domain makes standard self-supervised losses (e.g., cyclic consistency from TimeReplayer) insufficient. The Norm-Residual Block departs from the common TTA practice of updating only normalization layers, instead introducing a hybrid architecture that balances parameter flexibility with statistical modulation, and the ERDS dataset fills a gap by providing the first high-quality RGB-event benchmark with continuously shifting distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that an online TTA method can outperform supervised training on the target domain (Table 3) is practically significant for deployment scenarios where retraining on new data is infeasible, suggesting that careful self-training with reliable pixel estimation can bridge domain gaps more effectively than direct supervision. The ERDS dataset and the continuous domain-shift evaluation protocol address a realistic but previously untested challenge for event-based VFI, where lighting and trigger-threshold changes occur continuously in the field. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other event-based tasks (deblurring, depth estimation), whether the memory bank and patch-mixing strategy scales to longer sequences or more diverse scenes, and whether the NR Block design transfers to non-VFI architectures. The method's reliance on optical flow for motion masking and its sensitivity to the reliability threshold η may limit robustness in extremely low-motion or high-noise regimes.
