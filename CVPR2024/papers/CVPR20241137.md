## Problem

Existing multimodal models struggle to jointly process video, audio, and text because these modalities have vastly different sampling rates, information volumes, and temporal alignment properties. Video and audio are time-synchronized and high-volume, while text arrives as global context, yet prior architectures either allocate too few parameters to media inputs (e.g., Flamingo dedicates ~1% to video) or can only handle a limited number of frames, preventing effective modeling of long-range temporal dependencies.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video sequence of N frames (128–512 frames, partitioned into T=16 chunks of K=8 or 32 frames), a synchronized audio waveform converted to a spectrogram with time bands matched to 25 fps, and a tokenized text sequence (question, description, or prompt). Output is a free-form text response generated autoregressively for VideoQA tasks, or a class name for audio-video classification, evaluated by exact match against ground truth.

## Method

Mirasol3B decouples autoregressive modeling into two components: a time-aligned model for video+audio and a separate autoregressive model for unaligned text, coordinated via cross-attention. Video features are extracted using sparse 3D tubes and 2D patches through a ViT encoder, while audio spectrograms are processed by a shared ViT backbone; a Combiner module (either a causal Transformer or a Token Turing Machine variant) then fuses per-chunk video and audio features into compact m=32 representations while respecting causality. The TTM Combiner maintains an external memory updated via differentiable read/write operations, reducing per-step complexity to O(1) versus O(t²) for a standard Transformer and saving ~30% memory. An autoregressive latent causal model processes the compressed chunk representations sequentially in time, and the text model attends to all latent video/audio representations via cross-attention. This partitioning-and-combining design allows scaling from 128 to 512 frames without increasing model parameters (3B total), with over half allocated to the media autoregressive component.

## Results

On MSRVTT-QA (open-ended generation), Mirasol3B achieves 50.42% accuracy, outperforming the previous best M-PLUG2 (48.0%) by 2.4 points and the 80B-parameter Flamingo (47.4%) by 3.0 points, while using only 3B parameters. On long-video benchmarks, Mirasol3B-512 frames reaches 51.13% on ActivityNet-QA and 73.2% (TTM variant) on NExT-QA, surpassing HiTeA (63.1%) by over 10 points. On audio-video classification treated as open-ended generation, Mirasol3B(A+V) achieves 90.1% on Kinetics-Sound, 69.8% on VGG-Sound, and 78.2% on Epic-Sound, all setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mirasol3B outperforms all 26 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On MSRVTT-QA, it beats the closest competitor M-PLUG2 [58] by 2.4 points (50.42% vs. 48.0%) and the 80B Flamingo [2] by 3.0 points, while using roughly 27x fewer parameters. On NExT-QA (long video), the 512-frame TTM variant achieves 73.2%, exceeding HiTeA [64] (63.1%) by 10.1 points and MIST-CLIP [13] (57.18%) by 16 points. On ActivityNet-QA, Mirasol3B-512 frames (51.13%) surpasses UMT-L [27] (47.9%) and VindLU [9] (44.7%), though VideoCoca [60] reports 56.1% under a classification setting that is not directly comparable. On audio-video tasks, Mirasol3B(A+V) leads Kinetics-Sound by 5.1 points over MBT [30] (90.1% vs. 85.0%), VGG-Sound by 1.6 points over ONE-PEACE [48] (69.8% vs. 68.2%), and Epic-Sound by 25.7 points over ASF [17] (78.2% vs. 53.75%), with the TTM variant further improving Epic-Sound to 79.4%. The consistent margins across video-only, long-video, and audio-video settings demonstrate that the decoupled autoregressive architecture with the Combiner generalizes broadly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of feeding visual features as compressed embeddings into a text-centric autoregressive model (as in Flamingo, GIT, or VideoCoca) by decoupling autoregressive modeling into a dedicated time-aligned component for video+audio and a separate component for text, coordinated via cross-attention. The Combiner mechanism draws directly on Token Turing Machines (Ryoo et al., CVPR 2023) and TokenLearner (Ryoo et al., 2021) to achieve constant-time sequential fusion of modality features, while video feature extraction builds on sparse 3D tubes (Piergiovanni et al., CVPR 2023). Unlike prior multimodal models that process 8–32 frames, the architecture's chunked partitioning and compact Combiner representations enable scaling to 512 frames without parameter growth, addressing a key limitation of both embedding-based and per-frame-encoding approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled autoregressive design offers a practical recipe for handling heterogeneous modality rates (high-rate video/audio vs. low-rate text) without the parameter imbalance that plagues unified models, and the TTM-based Combiner's constant-time complexity makes streaming and very long video processing feasible. The 3B-parameter model outperforming 80B Flamingo on VideoQA suggests that architectural alignment with modality structure can substitute for raw scale. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to even longer videos (beyond 512 frames), whether the Combiner's fixed 32-feature bottleneck limits expressiveness for highly dynamic scenes, and how the method extends to additional modalities or generative tasks beyond QA and classification.
