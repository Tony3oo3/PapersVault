## Problem

Zero-1-to-3 and One-2-3-45, despite being SOTA for single-view 3D object reconstruction, produce results that fall significantly short of SOTA clothed human reconstruction methods (e.g., IntegratedPIFu) when applied to human data. The core issues are view inconsistency among generated novel views, an overly unconstrained output space, and the absence of any mechanism to incorporate human-specific priors such as SMPL-X body meshes. This paper addresses these gaps by adapting the latent diffusion approach in a structured way specifically for clothed human reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB image of a clothed human (512×512) and optionally a SMPL-X mesh. The input image is first transformed by a Transformative Diffuser (M) into an 'ideal' image with weak-perspective projection, even lighting, and camera at the subject's center height. The Sequential Diffuser (F) then produces four novel views at 90° azimuth intervals, which are fused by a multi-view pixel-aligned implicit model (PIFu-style) to yield a 3D clothed human mesh. When SMPL-X is available, its blendweight vectors are projected into 4 three-channel images (via random 3×55 projection) and concatenated as additional conditioning for F.

## Method

R-Cyclic Diffuser uses two latent diffusion models (M and F), both pretrained on LAION-5B and finetuned on THuman2.0. The Cyclic Conditioning Mechanism has two parts: Ideal Subset Transformation (M maps any in-the-wild image onto a constrained 'ideal subset' ring of viewpoints, drastically reducing F's output space from a full sphere to a ring) and Sequential Conditioning (F generates each view conditioned on the previous three views via cross-attention and channel-concatenation, ensuring inter-view consistency). Two regularization features further constrain F: the Lateral Inversion Constraint exploits weak-perspective geometry to laterally flip the image 180° away, providing pixel-aligned priors that reduce prediction ambiguity; Cyclic Noise Selection (test-time only) generates multiple view sequences with different Gaussian noises, completes the cycle to predict I₀, selects the noise set with the best cycle-consistency score (MSE/PSNR/SSIM), and regenerates with full DDIM steps. Finally, SMPL-X Blendweight Priors extract per-vertex blendweight vectors from the SMPL-X mesh, apply random projection to compress 55 channels to 3, and produce k=4 interpretable blendweight images that serve as a soft human-parsing template for F to fill in.

## Results

On THuman2.0, R-Cyclic Diffuser without SMPL-X achieves CD 5.410×10⁻⁴ and P2S 4.879×10⁻⁴, outperforming IntegratedPIFu (5.925/5.777) and One-2-3-45 (8.613/7.556). With SMPL-X priors, the method achieves CD 0.9409×10⁻⁴ and P2S 0.8806×10⁻⁴ on THuman2.0, and CD 0.7119×10⁻³ and P2S 0.6091×10⁻³ on BUFF, surpassing all SMPL-X-based baselines (ICON, ECON, D-IF) on every metric. The SMPL-X Blendweight Priors yield a roughly 5.8× reduction in Chamfer Distance on THuman2.0 compared to the no-prior variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the no-SMPL-X setting, R-Cyclic Diffuser outperforms IntegratedPIFu by 8.7% in CD (5.410 vs 5.925) and 15.5% in P2S (4.879 vs 5.777) on THuman2.0, and beats One-2-3-45 by 37% in CD (5.410 vs 8.613). On BUFF, the no-prior variant improves over IntegratedPIFu by 6% in CD (2.040 vs 2.171) and 11.5% in P2S (1.852 vs 2.092). In the SMPL-X setting, R-Cyclic Diffuser achieves the best CD on both datasets: 0.9409×10⁻⁴ on THuman2.0 (beating ICON's 0.9934 by 5.3% and ECON's 1.108 by 15.1%) and 0.7119×10⁻³ on BUFF (beating ICON's 0.8668 by 17.9% and D-IF's 0.9116 by 21.9%). The P2S gains with SMPL-X are even more pronounced on BUFF, where R-Cyclic Diffuser reaches 0.6091×10⁻³ versus D-IF's 0.7696×10⁻³ (20.9% improvement). Notably, Zero-1-to-3 and One-2-3-45, despite being pretrained on Internet-scale data, perform worse than the pixel-aligned implicit baseline IntegratedPIFu in the no-prior setting, underscoring the difficulty of directly transferring object-reconstruction diffusion models to clothed humans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper fuses two previously separate paradigms: the latent diffusion-based novel view synthesis introduced by Zero-1-to-3 (and extended by One-2-3-45) and the pixel-aligned implicit surface reconstruction lineage (PIFu, IntegratedPIFu, ICON). It departs from Zero-1-to-3 by restricting the output space from an arbitrary viewpoint sphere to a fixed ring of ideal-condition views, replacing independent per-view predictions with sequential conditioning, and adding geometric constraints (Lateral Inversion Constraint) and noise selection to curb propagation errors. It departs from pixel-aligned implicit models by using a pretrained diffusion model to generate the multi-view inputs rather than relying solely on the single input image, thereby leveraging Internet-scale visual priors that these models had not previously exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a general-purpose latent diffusion model, when properly constrained with cyclic conditioning and domain-specific priors (SMPL-X blendweights), can outperform specialized single-view human reconstruction methods on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks. This suggests a broader path for adapting large pretrained generative models to structured 3D reconstruction tasks beyond objects. Limitations include the fixed 4-view (90°) generation schedule, the need for paired ideal/non-ideal training data, and the significant performance gap between the with-SMPL-X and without-SMPL-X variants, raising open questions about robustness in settings where body priors are unavailable or inaccurate.
