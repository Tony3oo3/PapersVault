## Problem

ViT-based face recognition models rely heavily on accurate geometric alignment, but alignment frequently fails on low-quality images (poor lighting, low resolution), causing sharp performance drops. Standard Relative Position Encoding (RPE) in ViTs only encodes pixel proximity and cannot adapt its spatial understanding when the image undergoes unseen affine transformations, leaving the model blind to how semantic structure (e.g., facial layout) shifts under scale, rotation, or translation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240513/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 112×112 face image (or gait silhouette) together with a set of normalized keypoints (5 facial landmarks for faces, 17 body joints for gait) predicted by a lightweight RetinaFace (MobileNetV2 backbone). The keypoints are not used for geometric warping; instead they modulate the attention mechanism internally. The output is a standard face or gait embedding vector used for verification (cosine similarity) or retrieval (rank-based metrics).

## Method

KP-RPE modifies the iRPE bias table so that the attention offset B_ij becomes a function of the image's keypoints: B_ij = f(P)[d(i,j)], where d(i,j) is the quantized product distance between query i and key j, and f(P) is a learnable mapping from keypoint geometry to the bias table. The strongest variant (Multihead Relative f(P)) computes D = M − P (the broadcasted difference between the patch-location grid M and keypoints P), reshapes it, and projects it through a per-head, per-layer weight matrix W_L, yielding a unique offset for every query location relative to every keypoint. Because f(P) is computed once per forward pass, the overhead is negligible (0.02 GFLOP for ViT-Small) compared to iRPE's 0.71 GFLOP. The model is trained end-to-end with the AdaFace loss on aligned data only, yet learns to maintain spatial coherence under misalignment at test time, effectively decoupling recognition quality from alignment accuracy.

## Results

On the low-quality TinyFace dataset, AdaFace with ViT+KP-RPE trained on WebFace4M achieves 75.80% Rank-1 accuracy, outperforming the same model without KP-RPE (74.81%) and the best ResNet101 baseline (72.02%). On IJB-S, KP-RPE reaches 72.78% Rank-1 vs. 71.90% for ViT alone. Scaling to WebFace12M, ViT+KP-RPE further improves to 76.18% on TinyFace and 97.82% TAR@FAR=0.01% on IJB-C. On Gait3D, SwinGait-2D+KP-RPE improves Rank-1 by 1.1% and mAP by 2.05% over the SwinGait-2D baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-backbone ViT baseline (AdaFace, WebFace4M), KP-RPE consistently improves low-quality metrics: +0.99% on TinyFace Rank-1 (75.80 vs. 74.81) and +0.88% on IJB-S Rank-1 (72.78 vs. 71.90), while high-quality metrics (AgeDB, CFPFP, IJB-C) remain within 0.5%. Compared to iRPE (the standard content-independent RPE), KP-RPE gains +0.88% on TinyFace Rank-1 and +0.85% on IJB-S Rank-5, and is an order of magnitude cheaper in FLOPs (+0.07 vs. +1.42 GFLOP for ViT-Base). Against the strongest ResNet101 baseline (AdaFace, WebFace4M), ViT+KP-RPE leads by +3.78% on TinyFace Rank-1 and +2.36% on IJB-S Rank-1, confirming that the ViT+KP-RPE combination surpasses CNN-based SoTA on low-quality data. On Gait3D, SwinGait-2D+KP-RPE outperforms the SwinGait-2D baseline by +1.1% Rank-1, +0.7% Rank-5, +2.05% mAP, and +1.23% mINP, and also surpasses prior SoTA methods like DyGait (66.3% Rank-1) and GaitBase (64.6% Rank-1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Relative Position Encoding (RPE) lineage from Shaw et al. (2018) and iRPE (Wu et al., ICCV 2021) by making the RPE bias table a function of image-specific keypoints rather than a fixed lookup indexed only by pixel distance. Standard RPE methods encode static spatial relationships that remain unchanged under affine transformations, which the paper identifies as a fundamental limitation for recognition tasks where alignment can fail. KP-RPE departs from prior keypoint-based recognition work (e.g., keypoint pooling for action recognition) by injecting keypoint information directly into the attention mechanism's position encoding rather than through a separate pooling or alignment step, making it a drop-in modification to any ViT-based model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KP-RPE offers a model-level solution to alignment failures in face recognition, which is practically important for surveillance and low-quality video scenarios where landmark detection is unreliable. The method is notably efficient (0.02 GFLOP overhead vs. 0.71 for iRPE) and generalizes beyond faces to gait recognition, suggesting broad applicability to any recognition task with definable keypoints. A key limitation is the dependency on external keypoint supervision, which constrains use to objects with consistent topology; the authors identify self-discovery of keypoints as an important future direction. The finding that noisy keypoints are more tolerable in KP-RPE than in rigid alignment is a useful conceptual insight for practitioners designing robust pipelines.
