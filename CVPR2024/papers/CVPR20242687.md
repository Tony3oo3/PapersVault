## Problem

Object detection systems deployed on resource-constrained edge devices are vulnerable to latency attacks that exploit the quadratic time complexity of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), yet the precise mechanism driving NMS latency and the most efficient way to exploit it remain underexplored. Existing attacks (Phantom Sponge, Daedalus) rely on complex composite objectives involving pairwise IoU minimization and box-area reduction, incurring high memory and compute costs without clear performance gains over simply maximizing the number of objects fed into NMS.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard 640×640 RGB image and produces a perturbed adversarial image (L2-norm ε=15) that causes the target YOLOv5 detector to output up to ~25,000 ghost objects into NMS, inflating single-image inference time by roughly 10× on Nvidia Jetson NX. The spatial attention mechanism internally partitions the image into an m×m grid to modulate per-cell perturbation magnitudes, but the final output is a single adversarial image of the same spatial dimensions as the input.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that NMS elapsed time is dominated by |C|² (the number of candidate objects) rather than the number of surviving boxes, so the attack objective reduces to simply maximizing the sum of per-object confidences (Equation 5), eliminating the need for pairwise IoU gradient tracking or box-area minimization used by prior work. A novel spatial attention technique divides the image into an m×m grid, assigns lower weights to cells already dense with predicted objects, and scales the gradient-based perturbation per cell (Equation 7), steering adversarial energy toward low-density regions to generate more distinct ghost objects. This design keeps the optimization differentiable and memory-light (7.5 GB peak, batch size 16 on V100), whereas adding IoU or area losses causes out-of-memory failures or yields no measurable latency gain. The attack is NMS-agnostic: it targets only object confidence, so any NMS variant (DIoU, CIoU, etc.) that preserves the O(|C|²) pairwise structure remains vulnerable.

## Results

On Nvidia Jetson NX with YOLOv5s, Overload increases inference time from ~16.4 ms (original, 50th percentile) to ~217.8 ms (adversarial), a ~13× slowdown, while generating ~24,000 objects out of a maximum of 25,200. For YOLOv5n, the increase is from ~11.5 ms to ~128.9 ms (~11×). In a pipeline simulation processing 1,000 images, FPS drops from 43.5 to 4.3 (YOLOv5s) and from 50.0 to 7.4 (YOLOv5n) when all inputs are adversarial. The attack requires only ε=15 perturbation budget to produce ~20,000 objects, compared to ε=70 needed by Phantom Sponge for ~19,000 objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Overload outperforms both baselines on the primary metric of objects generated per image. Against Phantom Sponge, Overload produces ~20,000 objects with ε=15, exceeding Phantom Sponge's ~19,000 objects at ε=70 (a 4.7× larger perturbation budget) and ~9,000 objects at ε=30. Against Daedalus, Overload generates roughly 13× more objects (20,000 vs. 1,500). In terms of resource efficiency, Overload processes 16 images in a batch in ~3 minutes with 7.5 GB peak memory, while Daedalus takes ~145 minutes for 10 sequential images with 8.5 GB peak memory, and Phantom Sponge requires multiple NMS executions during the attack loop. The IoU-minimization loss (Overload+L_IoU) causes out-of-memory failures on a V100 32 GB GPU, confirming that the simpler confidence-only objective is both more effective and more practical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the emerging line of latency attacks on deep learning models, building conceptually on the Sponge attack (for NLP) and two prior object-detection-specific attacks: Phantom Sponge [33] and Daedalus [35]. It departs from both by arguing that their composite objectives (maximizing confidence, minimizing pairwise IoU, minimizing box area) are unnecessarily complex, and instead shows that a single-term objective—maximizing per-object confidence—suffices to saturate NMS input. The spatial attention mechanism is a novel gradient-guided weighting scheme that directs perturbations toward low-density regions, distinguishing it from the uniform or IoU-aware perturbation strategies in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NMS-agnostic nature of the attack means any object detection pipeline relying on NMS (which, per a cited benchmark, includes 11 of 12 popular underwater detection models on edge devices) is potentially vulnerable, making this a broadly relevant security concern for embedded and real-time systems. The 10× latency inflation on Jetson NX translates directly into DoS risk in shared-resource or safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, unmanned retail). Key open questions remain: the attack is evaluated only under white-box settings with YOLOv5 on a single dataset, black-box transferability is untested, and no defense is validated—leaving practitioners without a clear mitigation path.
