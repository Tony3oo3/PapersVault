## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image generation methods either require computationally expensive subject-specific fine-tuning (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) or, in tuning-free variants, suffer from identity distortion and an inability to edit facial expressions independently of the reference image. No prior method simultaneously achieves single-image inference without test-time tuning, robust identity preservation, and text-driven expression editing at low training cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image (no multi-image set required) and a free-form text prompt that may include emotion words (e.g., 'happy', 'angry') to control expression. It outputs a 512×512 personalized portrait via 50-step Euler sampling with classifier-free guidance scale 5. For multi-subject generation, multiple reference faces are combined through MultiDiffusion, producing a single image containing all subjects with their respective identities preserved.

## Method

PortraitBooth builds on Stable Diffusion v1-5 and introduces three modules. First, Subject Text Embedding Augmentation (STEA) extracts a face embedding from a pre-trained TFace recognition model (41.5M params), concatenates it with the identity token's text embedding, and projects the result through an MLP into the U-Net's cross-attention context space, replacing the need for a trained image encoder. Second, Dynamic Identity Preservation (DIP) computes an identity loss at early timesteps (t ≤ R_t = 250) by performing a one-step reverse to estimate ẑ₀, decoding it via the VAE, and penalizing cosine dissimilarity between the reference face and the reconstructed face region. Third, Emotion-aware Cross-Attention Control (ECAC) applies a truncation mechanism to cross-attention maps so that both the identity token and expression token can simultaneously attend to the face region, with hyperparameters β=0.8 and γ=0.1 capping their respective maximum response intensities. Only the U-Net and MLP are trained (150k steps, 6 V100s ≈ 3 A100s) with a composite loss L_total = L_loc + L_noise + L_id, and at inference only the STEA module is needed—no fine-tuning or optimization.

## Results

On single-subject image generation (CelebV-T test set), PortraitBooth achieves the highest identity preservation score of 0.657, surpassing Subject-Diffusion (0.605), FastComposer (0.514), and Custom Diffusion (0.434). Its CLIP text-image consistency (0.245) is competitive with fine-tuning methods and slightly below the text-only Stable Diffusion baseline (0.268). Inference time is approximately 2 seconds per image, matching other one-shot methods, while training requires only 3 A100 GPUs—far less than Subject-Diffusion (24 A100) or FastComposer (8 A6000). For expression editing, PortraitBooth achieves the best expression coefficient of 0.193, outperforming Custom Diffusion (0.182) and FastComposer (0.133).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PortraitBooth outperforms all seven baselines on identity preservation, achieving 0.657 vs. 0.605 (Subject-Diffusion), 0.514 (FastComposer), and 0.434 (Custom Diffusion). On CLIP text-image consistency, PortraitBooth (0.245) slightly trails the text-only Stable Diffusion baseline (0.268) but edges out FastComposer (0.243) and DreamBooth (0.239), while fine-tuning methods like Textual Inversion (0.219) and Subject-Diffusion (0.228) score lower. The key efficiency advantage is stark: PortraitBooth requires only 3 A100 GPUs for training versus 24 A100 for Subject-Diffusion and 8 A6000 for FastComposer, while maintaining the same ≈2 s inference time as other one-shot methods. Fine-tuning baselines (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) incur 789–2500 s of per-subject optimization time and need 5 reference images, whereas PortraitBooth needs a single image and no test-time tuning. For expression editing, PortraitBooth (0.193) beats all baselines including Custom Diffusion (0.182) and FastComposer (0.133), and even its ablated version without expression control (0.177) surpasses FastComposer, demonstrating effective identity–attribute decoupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

PortraitBooth builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture (VAE + U-Net + text encoder) and departs from the dominant test-time fine-tuning paradigm (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion) by amortizing subject adaptation into a single training phase. Among tuning-free methods, it distinguishes itself from FastComposer, Face0, and Subject-Diffusion by replacing their purpose-trained image encoders with a pre-trained face recognition model (TFace) projected through a lightweight MLP, arguing that discriminative identity features generalize better for generation than coarse-grained encoder representations. The Emotion-aware Cross-Attention Control (ECAC) truncation mechanism extends prior attention-masking approaches (e.g., FastComposer's subject-region masking) by allowing multiple tokens to co-attend to the same spatial region with bounded response intensities, enabling expression editing without sacrificing identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PortraitBooth requires only 3 A100 GPUs for training (vs. 24 A100 for Subject-Diffusion or 64 TPU for Face0) and a single reference image at inference with ~2s latency, making identity-preserved personalization accessible to most research labs. However, multi-subject generation still depends on the external MultiDiffusion framework, and expression editing is limited to a fixed set of 11 emotion words, leaving open the question of whether the truncation mechanism generalizes to arbitrary attribute editing (e.g., age, accessories, pose). The finding that a 41.5M-parameter face recognition model outperforms much larger purpose-built image encoders for identity preservation suggests a broader principle—discriminative features may be more transferable to generative conditioning than generative features—which could inform future personalization architectures beyond faces.
