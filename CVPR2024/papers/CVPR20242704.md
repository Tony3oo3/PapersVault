## Problem

Existing reference-based super-resolution (RefSR) methods for remote sensing suffer from two coupled limitations: land cover changes between the reference and low-resolution images cause underuse or misuse of reference texture, and GAN-based architectures struggle to reconstruct realistic details at large scaling factors (8×–16×). Prior diffusion-model-based RefSR approaches implicitly model the LR–Ref relationship during denoising, leaving reference information ambiguously utilized and content fidelity unguaranteed in changed regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242704/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs: a low-resolution remote sensing image (synthesized with multi-stage degradation including anisotropic/motion blur, Gaussian noise, and JPEG compression), a geographically co-located high-resolution reference image acquired at a different time, and a pixel-level multi-category land cover change mask (either ground-truth or predicted by a ChangeFormer-based CD model with ~85–88% F1). The output is a single high-resolution super-resolved image at 8× or 16× the LR resolution, evaluated on 256×256 test crops from the SECOND and CNAM-CD datasets.

## Method

Ref-Diff builds on the EDM framework and introduces a change-aware denoising model whose encoder concatenates the LR image, Ref image, and land cover change mask with the noisy input (avoiding the block-wise injection used by HSR-Diff). In the decoder, the land cover change priors enable a decoupled denoising strategy: a semantics-guided SFT module (conditioned on change mask + LR features) reconstructs content in changed areas, while a reference texture-guided SFT module (conditioned on change mask + Ref features) transfers texture in unchanged areas. Both SFT modules are modified to combine guidance features with the current denoising features when learning spatially adaptive weights and biases, making them robust to the 60–80% accuracy of practical CD predictions. The two denoising paths are applied iteratively so that changed and unchanged regions reinforce each other, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with the standard EDM loss over 500k iterations.

## Results

On the SECOND dataset at 8× scaling, Ref-Diff achieves LPIPS 0.2642 and FID 32.60, outperforming the best diffusion baseline HSR-Diff (LPIPS 0.2689, FID 45.47) and EDM* (LPIPS 0.2886, FID 34.58). At 16× scaling on SECOND, Ref-Diff reaches LPIPS 0.3433 and FID 33.97, while on CNAM-CD it achieves LPIPS 0.2791/FID 43.02 (8×) and LPIPS 0.3519/FID 45.65 (16×). Ref-Diff attains the best LPIPS and FID across all four dataset–scaling-factor combinations, with the largest FID margins over HSR-Diff on CNAM-CD (up to 22.5 points at 16×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ref-Diff outperforms all seven baselines on both LPIPS and FID across all four evaluation settings (SECOND/CNAM-CD at 8×/16×). Against the closest diffusion-based competitor HSR-Diff [38], Ref-Diff improves FID by 12.9 points on SECOND 8× (32.60 vs 45.47) and by 22.5 points on CNAM-CD 16× (45.65 vs 68.20), while LPIPS gains are smaller (0.005–0.025). Compared to EDM* [15] (the unconditional diffusion baseline adapted for RefSR), Ref-Diff reduces FID by 2.0 points on SECOND 8× and by 13.8 points on CNAM-CD 8×, showing that explicit land-cover-change priors add substantial value beyond simply concatenating LR and Ref images. All five GAN-based methods (TTSR, WTRN, C²-Matching, AMSA, DATSR) are substantially outperformed, with FID gaps exceeding 100 points at 16× scaling, confirming that diffusion models are far more effective for large-factor remote sensing super-resolution. The largest absolute advantage of Ref-Diff over HSR-Diff appears on CNAM-CD, suggesting the change-prior guidance is particularly beneficial when land cover changes are more diverse and dramatic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional diffusion framework (EDM) to reference-based super-resolution for remote sensing by introducing land cover change priors as explicit denoising guidance, departing from prior diffusion-based RefSR methods (e.g., HSR-Diff) that implicitly model LR–Ref relationships via cross-attention. The change-aware decoder builds on the spatial feature transform (SFT) and SPADE normalization ideas but modifies them to combine guidance features with denoising features, addressing the mislabel robustness problem. The method also leverages off-the-shelf change detection (ChangeFormer) as a plug-and-play prior, connecting the RefSR and change detection literatures that had previously been treated independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly separating changed and unchanged regions via change priors improves both content fidelity and texture transfer at large scaling factors (8×, 16×) where GAN-based RefSR methods struggle. A key open question is the sensitivity to change detection accuracy: using predicted masks (F1 ≈ 85–88%) degrades performance noticeably, especially at 16× on CNAM-CD (FID 45.7 → 56.8), suggesting that tighter integration of CD into the RefSR pipeline is needed for practical deployment. The authors also note the potential for mutual reinforcement between RefSR and CD tasks, but this remains speculative and unexplored experimentally.
