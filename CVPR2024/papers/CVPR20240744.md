## Problem

Existing multi-stage Adaptive Compressive Sensing (ACS) methods require image reconstruction at every sampling stage to analyze texture or saliency and steer the next adaptive allocation. This in-loop reconstruction is performed n times for an n-stage process, bottlenecking sampling speed and preventing deployment in time-sensitive scenarios such as ultrafast photography or real-time medical imaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240744/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sub-Nyquist CS measurements of an image (Y channel in YUV space) as input, where the image is partitioned into non-overlapping 8×8 blocks and each block is sampled at a potentially different rate determined adaptively. A single 5-stage model (5% per stage) produces a reconstructed image at any of 10%, 15%, 20%, or 25% total sampling rates. During inference, no intermediate reconstruction is performed—only the final reconstruction from all accumulated measurements is computed.

## Method

The core contribution is a reconstruction-free cascaded ACS framework where a lightweight ScoreNet (two ConvNeXt blocks with a Softmax output) directly scores each image block from raw CS measurements, eliminating the need for in-loop image reconstruction. A Score-to-Mask (STM) module, formulated as an integer linear programming problem and made differentiable via a perturbed optimizer, converts scores into a binary sampling mask that selects rows from the measurement matrix, enabling end-to-end backpropagation through the discrete sampling decision. For reconstruction, a Multi-Grid Spatial-Attention Network (MGSANet) with four parallel horizontal branches at progressively coarser scales, dense residual blocks, and spatial attention modules provides high parallelism for fast GPU inference. Training proceeds stage-by-stage with out-of-loop reconstruction-fidelity supervision (L1 + SSIM loss) applied outside the sampling loop, followed by a two-phase protocol: end-to-end training with linearly decaying perturbation noise, then fine-tuning with frozen ScoreNet parameters.

## Results

On Set11, the proposed reconstruction-free cascaded ACS achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 34.10/0.9439 across 10%–25% sampling rates, outperforming the best non-adaptive baseline OCTUF+ (33.59/0.9369) by 0.51 dB and the best adaptive baseline AMS-Net (33.96/0.9248) by 0.14 dB in PSNR. On the more challenging Urban100 dataset, the advantage widens substantially: the method reaches 31.86/0.9286, surpassing OCTUF+ by 1.20 dB and AMS-Net by 1.01 dB in PSNR, demonstrating that adaptive sampling yields larger gains on images with highly non-uniform texture distributions. At 25% sampling rate on Urban100, the method achieves 34.27 PSNR vs. 33.08 for OCTUF+ and 33.23 for AMS-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 14 baselines on both Set11 and Urban100 across every sampling rate tested. On Set11, the closest competitor is AMS-Net (33.96/0.9248 avg), which the method surpasses by 0.14 dB PSNR and 0.0191 SSIM; the best non-adaptive method OCTUF+ (33.59/0.9369) trails by 0.51 dB PSNR. On Urban100, where texture is highly non-uniform, the advantage of adaptive sampling is much more pronounced: the method beats OCTUF+ by 1.20 dB PSNR and AMS-Net by 1.01 dB PSNR, with SSIM margins of 0.0173 and 0.0350 respectively. Among adaptive baselines, CASNet (33.17/0.9348 on Set11; 30.00/0.9063 on Urban100) is consistently outperformed by over 0.9 dB on Set11 and 1.86 dB on Urban100. Notably, AMS-Net requires ground-truth access for its adaptive scheme, limiting practical applicability, whereas the proposed method operates purely from prior CS measurements without any in-loop reconstruction. The gap between adaptive and non-adaptive methods widens with sampling rate and is largest on Urban100, confirming that scene-aware allocation is most beneficial for complex, texture-rich scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-stage cascaded adaptive compressive sensing (ACS) framework introduced by Qiu et al. (2022), which required iterative in-loop reconstruction to steer subsequent sampling stages. The key departure is replacing that reconstruction step with a lightweight ScoreNet that scores image blocks directly from raw CS measurements, combined with an LP-based differentiable sampling module built on the perturbed-optimizer technique of Berthet et al. (NeurIPS 2020). For the reconstruction side, the proposed MGSANet draws on multi-branch parallel architectures (ShuffleNet v2) and spatial attention (CBAM lineage) but is tailored for multi-stage CS training with out-of-loop fidelity supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating reconstruction from the adaptive sampling loop, the method removes a major speed bottleneck that has limited ACS to offline or non-time-critical settings, making it more viable for applications like ultrafast photography, medical imaging, and THz imaging where sampling latency is critical. The out-of-loop supervision strategy and the differentiable LP formulation for discrete measurement allocation are practical techniques that could transfer to other sequential decision-making problems in sensing. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions and more stages, generalization beyond 128×128 block-based sampling, and whether the lightweight ScoreNet's capacity becomes a limiting factor on highly complex or out-of-distribution scenes.
