## Problem

Existing monocular HumanNeRF methods learn deformation blending weights from scratch or fine-tune SMPL weights, which overfit to limited training data and fail to generalize to novel poses significantly different from the input. Additionally, prior methods rely on frame-level features (frame index, body pose) to condition non-rigid deformation, further entangling the representation to specific training poses and degrading pose generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241945/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video (as few as ~30 frames in the few-shot setting) along with per-frame estimated SMPL pose parameters and vertices, which can be obtained from off-the-shelf estimators. It produces photorealistic renderings of the same person in arbitrary novel poses and viewpoints via differentiable volumetric rendering. No multi-camera setup, additional texture maps, or explicit 3D geometry recovery is required; the only external dependency is the SMPL fit.

## Method

HumanNeRF-SE combines explicit SMPL prior knowledge with an implicit NeRF representation through three key mechanisms. First, SMPL vertices are voxelized into a sparse volume and channel-by-channel sparse convolution is applied to simultaneously extract point-level spatial-aware features and filter out sampling points unrelated to the body (reducing points from ~300K to <100K). Second, rigid deformation to canonical space uses frozen SMPL blending weights directly (never learned), which avoids the overfitting that plagues methods that optimize weights per subject. Third, a small network refines the rigidly-deformed coordinates using only point-level spatial-aware features (not frame-level features), enabling non-rigid correction without pose-specific overfitting. The entire pipeline uses less than 1% of the learnable parameters of comparable methods, trains in ~2.5 hours versus 56–70 hours for baselines, and renders 15× faster without any acceleration module.

## Results

On ZJU-MOCAP (full input), HumanNeRF-SE achieves PSNR 31.09, SSIM 0.9740, and LPIPS 24.085, matching HumanNeRF's PSNR (31.15) while improving LPIPS by ~0.7. In the more challenging few-shot setting (~30 frames), it leads all baselines with PSNR 30.11 and LPIPS 32.084, outperforming MonoHuman (LPIPS 36.494) by 4.4 points. On the in-the-wild dataset (single rotation video for training, diverse poses for evaluation), it achieves the best LPIPS in both full (46.308) and few-shot (47.161) settings. Training time is reduced to 2.5 hours (vs. 56h for HumanNeRF, 70h for MonoHuman) and rendering speed reaches 0.16 s/it (vs. 2.37 s/it for HumanNeRF), a ~15× speedup without any acceleration module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HumanNeRF [56], HumanNeRF-SE trades a negligible 0.06 dB PSNR on ZJU-MOCAP full input for a 0.7-point LPIPS improvement, and clearly wins in the few-shot regime (PSNR +0.21, LPIPS −0.97). On the in-the-wild data, it surpasses HumanNeRF on every metric in both full and few-shot settings, with the largest gap in few-shot LPIPS (47.16 vs. 50.24). Against MonoHuman [62], the advantage is more pronounced: on ZJU-MOCAP few-shot, LPIPS improves from 36.49 to 32.08, and on in-the-wild few-shot, LPIPS drops from 56.22 to 47.16, indicating substantially better pose generalization. Ani-NeRF [40] is far behind on ZJU-MOCAP (PSNR ~21–22 vs. ~30–31) and produced no reasonable results on the in-the-wild data, as it was designed for multi-camera input. In terms of efficiency, HumanNeRF-SE trains in 2.5 h (22× faster than HumanNeRF's 56 h, 28× faster than MonoHuman's 70 h) and renders at 0.16 s/it (15× faster than HumanNeRF), using fewer than 1% of the learnable parameters of comparable methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HumanNeRF line of work (Weng et al., CVPR 2022) and its successor MonoHuman (Yu et al., CVPR 2023), which decompose human deformation into learned rigid (LBS) and non-rigid (neural) components. The key departure is replacing the per-subject learned or fine-tuned blending weights with frozen, pre-trained SMPL weights for rigid deformation, and introducing a sparse-convolution-based filter on voxelized SMPL vertices to discard irrelevant sampling points and extract point-level spatial-aware features. This draws on NeuralBody's (Peng et al., CVPR 2021) use of structured vertex features but applies them in a simpler, monocular few-shot setting without frame-level conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing training from ~56h to 2.5h and rendering from ~2.4s to 0.16s per iteration without any acceleration module makes animatable human NeRF feasible on modest hardware. The conceptual finding that frozen SMPL weights plus a lightweight point filter suffice—eliminating the need for large learned deformation networks—challenges the field's default assumption that per-subject deformation must be optimized. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to hand and facial articulation (explicitly noted as a limitation), how sensitive results are to SMPL fitting errors in the wild, and whether the voxelization-based filtering can be replaced by differentiable alternatives to avoid edge serrations.
