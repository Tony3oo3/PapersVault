## Problem

NeRF-based human rendering methods achieve photorealistic quality but require seconds per frame due to volumetric sampling, while existing real-time methods like DDC produce blurry, low-detail results. 3D Gaussian Splatting offers both high quality and real-time speed, but was designed exclusively for static scenes, making it non-trivial to extend to dynamic, pose-controllable clothed humans with motion-dependent appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240020/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ASH takes a skeletal motion sequence (a sliding window of D-DoF poses with root translation normalized to the current frame) and an arbitrary virtual camera projection matrix, producing a photorealistic RGB rendering at ~30 fps. Training requires multi-view RGB videos from calibrated cameras (120 cameras at 25 fps in their custom setup) annotated with 3D skeletal poses and foreground segmentations; evaluation is conducted at 1K resolution. The rendered output captures motion-dependent details such as clothing wrinkles and view-dependent appearances that vary with both pose and camera angle.

## Method

ASH attaches a fixed set of 3D Gaussians onto a person-specific deformable template mesh, parameterizing each Gaussian as a texel in the mesh's 2D UV texture space, thereby converting the skeletal-motion-to-Gaussian-parameters mapping into a 2D-to-2D image translation problem solvable with efficient 2D convolutional networks. Two motion-aware U-Net decoders consume normal and position textures rendered from the posed template mesh: a geometry decoder predicts per-texel canonical offsets, scales, rotation quaternions, and opacities, while a separate appearance decoder predicts spherical harmonics coefficients conditioned on global root-translation features. Gaussians are posed from canonical to world space via dual quaternion skinning, then splatted into the target view using the standard 3D Gaussian Splatting formulation. Training uses a two-stage strategy: a warmup phase learns per-frame pseudo ground-truth Gaussian parameters (with fixed positions and no splitting/merging) via L2 loss, followed by end-to-end fine-tuning with L1 + SSIM pixel losses on rendered multi-view images.

## Results

On the DynaCap dataset, ASH achieves 35.84 PSNR and 11.92 LPIPS for novel-view synthesis on the tight-outfit subject, and 35.47 PSNR and 8.30 LPIPS on the loose-outfit subject, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. For novel-pose synthesis, ASH attains the highest PSNR (28.90 tight, 27.12 loose) and the best LPIPS on the loose-outfit subject (20.22), while ranking second on LPIPS for the tight-outfit subject (22.83 vs. HDHumans' 20.69). ASH renders at ~30 fps in real time, whereas HDHumans, Neural Actor, and TAVA require seconds per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASH decisively outperforms all four baselines on novel-view synthesis. Against DDC (the only other real-time method), ASH gains +4.63 PSNR and −10.64 LPIPS on the tight-outfit subject, and +7.37 PSNR and −23.38 LPIPS on the loose-outfit subject. Against the strongest offline method HDHumans, ASH still leads by +4.86 PSNR and −3.17 LPIPS (tight) and +6.23 PSNR and −7.49 LPIPS (loose). On novel-pose synthesis, ASH achieves the best PSNR for both subjects (28.90 vs. 28.78 for Neural Actor on tight; 27.12 vs. 26.71 for HDHumans on loose) and the best LPIPS on the loose-outfit subject (20.22 vs. 22.75 for HDHumans), though HDHumans edges out ASH on LPIPS for the tight-outfit subject (20.69 vs. 22.83). TAVA and Neural Actor struggle most with loose clothing, producing blurry or artifact-laden renderings, while ASH handles both apparel types robustly in real time.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASH extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), originally designed for static scenes, to the dynamic, pose-controllable human domain by attaching Gaussians to a deformable template mesh (from DDC, Habermann et al., 2021) and parameterizing them in 2D UV texture space. This departs from the dominant hybrid NeRF-based approaches (HDHumans, Neural Actor, TAVA) that require expensive per-ray volume rendering, and from mesh-based methods (DDC) whose quality is bounded by mesh resolution. The key conceptual move is reframing the skeletal-motion-to-Gaussian-parameters mapping as a 2D image-to-image translation problem solvable with standard convolutional networks, circumventing the need for 3D architectures that do not scale to the ~20,000 Gaussians required for high-fidelity human rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASH demonstrates that real-time (~30 fps) photorealistic human rendering can match or exceed the quality of offline NeRF-based methods, removing a key barrier to interactive applications in gaming, film, and AR/VR. The 2D texture-space parameterization is a broadly applicable architectural pattern for scaling Gaussian-based representations to large, structured scenes. A stated limitation is that the underlying deformable mesh geometry is not updated during training, leaving open whether Gaussian splatting can directly refine 3D mesh shape. The approach also requires multi-view studio capture with synchronized cameras and motion capture, limiting immediate applicability to in-the-wild single-video settings.
