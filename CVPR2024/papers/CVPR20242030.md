## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods require synchronized multi-view cameras, and neither dynamic GS nor controllable NeRF approaches (e.g., CoNeRF) support real-time manipulation of scene elements without pre-computed or manually labeled control signals. This paper addresses both limitations by enabling monocular dynamic scene reconstruction with direct, per-Gaussian attribute control that does not depend on pre-defined control signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence (single camera) plus 2D masks that can be as minimal as a single manually labeled frame or fully automatic (e.g., facial keypoint detection). It produces a dynamic 3D Gaussian scene representation where each Gaussian carries a learned mask vector and a PCA-derived scalar control signal; at inference time, adjusting these control signals (e.g., 0 to 1 for a door opening) yields real-time renderings of novel configurations not present in the original video, such as independently opening a car's hood, trunk, and doors.

## Method

CoGS operates in two stages. First, a Dynamic GS model augments standard 3D Gaussian Splatting with per-parameter deformation MLPs (for position, color, rotation, scale) conditioned on time, trained with four regularization losses: a normalization loss that localizes position offsets, a local-difference loss enforcing trajectory consistency among k-nearest neighbors, and a local-rigidity plus rotational loss (adapted from Dynamic 3D Gaussians) that constrains neighboring Gaussians to move as a coherent rigid body. Second, the Controllable GS extension learns a per-Gaussian mask tensor (softmax-normalized over L attributes) by rendering it through the same splatting pipeline with the mask as opacity and supervising against 2D masks; it then extracts a scalar control signal per attribute via PCA on the centroid trajectory of masked Gaussians, and trains per-signal MLPs to output attribute offsets that are masked to the relevant region before an end-to-end fine-tuning pass.

## Results

On synthetic dynamic scenes (D-NeRF dataset), CoGS achieves PSNR 37.90 dB, SSIM 0.983, and LPIPS 1.74, surpassing the strongest baseline TiNeuVox-B (PSNR 32.67, SSIM 0.972, LPIPS 4.25) by over 5 dB in PSNR and nearly halving the LPIPS. On real dynamic scenes (HyperNeRF dataset), CoGS reaches PSNR 29.6, SSIM 0.950, and LPIPS 17.1, essentially matching HyperNeRF (PSNR 29.8, SSIM 0.954, LPIPS 17.2) while dramatically outperforming static 3D GS (PSNR 22.1) and NeRF (PSNR 22.3). Ablation studies confirm that each regularization loss (L^norm, L^diff, L^rigid) contributes to performance, with the largest drop in SSIM (0.950→0.905) when removing L^norm on real scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On synthetic dynamic scenes, CoGS decisively outperforms all ten baselines, with the largest margins over static 3D GS (+14.83 dB PSNR) and NeRF (+18.92 dB PSNR), and a clear +5.23 dB PSNR advantage over the strongest dynamic baseline TiNeuVox-B. On real dynamic scenes, CoGS (PSNR 29.6) is within 0.2 dB of HyperNeRF (29.8) and slightly ahead of Nerfies (29.3), while TiNeuVox-B lags at 28.0 and static 3D GS drops to 22.1, confirming that explicit Gaussian representations with learned deformation fields are critical for dynamic reconstruction. The static 3D GS baseline performs poorly on both dynamic benchmarks (PSNR 23.07 synthetic, 22.1 real), validating the necessity of the deformation network. Notably, CoGS matches or exceeds the best NeRF-based dynamic methods (HyperNeRF, Nerfies) on real scenes while offering real-time rendering via rasterization rather than ray-marching, and it does so without requiring pre-computed control signals or multi-view synchronized cameras.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from static to dynamic scenes by learning per-Gaussian deformation offsets via MLPs, and borrows the local-rigidity and rotational regularization losses from Dynamic 3D Gaussians (Luiten et al., 2023). It departs from the NeRF-based dynamic and controllable line of work (D-NeRF, HyperNeRF, CoNeRF, CoNFies) by exploiting the explicit Gaussian representation to enable direct per-Gaussian manipulation, eliminating the need for pre-computed or manually labeled control signals that implicit hyperspace methods require. The PCA-based control signal extraction from the learned dynamic model is a novel mechanism not present in prior controllable NeRF approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on pre-computed control signals and operating from monocular video, CoGS lowers the practical barrier to creating controllable dynamic 3D content, with implications for VR/AR and interactive media. However, the method still struggles with shiny objects, non-rigid deformations, and large-scale movements in monocular settings, and the PCA-based trajectory model may be insufficient for highly complex articulations. Open questions include whether the explicit Gaussian representation can scale to more complex multi-object interactions and whether the control signal extraction generalizes beyond simple linear motion patterns.
