## Problem

Existing VI-ReID methods focus exclusively on extracting modality-shared features, overlooking the identity-aware discriminative information (e.g., color, contrast, saturation) that is implicitly encoded in modality-specific features. This reliance on shared cues alone limits the upper bound of discriminative power, and the modality discrepancies within specific features prevent their direct exploitation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes paired visible and infrared pedestrian images resized to 3×384×128 as input, processed through a dual-stream ResNet-50 that shares the first three residual blocks and splits into separate deep branches for specific and shared features. At inference, only the modality-shared feature embedding (obtained via GeM pooling) is used for cross-modal person matching, while the modality-specific branch and all auxiliary losses are discarded.

## Method

IDKL employs a dual-stream ResNet-50 where a modality discriminator (standard classifier) shapes the specific branch and a GRL-based modality confuser shapes the shared branch, both trained with standard ReID losses. An IN-guided Information Purifier applies Instance Normalization to the specific feature, then uses two SE-Net-style channel masks—one enhancing discriminative characteristics on the raw feature, one reducing modality discrepancy on the normalized feature—combined via an alternating integration strategy to yield a purified specific feature that retains identity cues while suppressing style. This purified knowledge is distilled into the shared feature at two levels: Triplet Graph Structure Alignment (TGSA) aligns pairwise Euclidean-distance affinity matrices across branches via KL divergence at the feature level, and Class Semantic Alignment (CSA) aligns classifier logits via KL divergence at the semantic level. A Modality Discrepancy Reduction (MDR) loss further applies TGSA and CSA within the shared branch itself to minimize the visible-infrared gap on the final shared representation.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (the primary benchmark), IDKL achieves 81.42% rank-1 and 79.85% mAP under the all-search single-shot protocol, and 87.14% rank-1 and 89.37% mAP under the indoor-search single-shot protocol, setting new state-of-the-art results. On RegDB, IDKL reaches 94.72% rank-1 / 90.19% mAP (visible→infrared) and 94.22% rank-1 / 90.43% mAP (infrared→visible). On the large-scale LLCM dataset, IDKL attains 72.22% rank-1 (V→IR) and 70.72% rank-1 (IR→V), substantially outperforming the next-best method DEEN by over 9 percentage points in rank-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SYSU-MM01 all-search single-shot, IDKL (81.42% rank-1, 79.85% mAP) outperforms the strongest prior methods PartMix (77.78%, 74.62%), MSCLNet (76.99%, 71.64%), and SAAI (75.90%, 77.03%) by 3.6–5.5 points in rank-1 and 2.8–8.2 points in mAP. On RegDB, IDKL achieves 94.72% rank-1 (V→IR) and 94.22% (IR→V), slightly trailing CMT (95.17%) and MUN (95.19%) on V→IR rank-1 by ~0.5 points, but surpassing them by 2.9–3.2 points in mAP (90.19% vs. 87.3%/87.15%) and by 2.3–2.4 points in IR→V rank-1 (94.22% vs. 91.97%/91.86%). On LLCM, IDKL dominates with 72.22% rank-1 (V→IR) and 70.72% (IR→V), exceeding the next-best DEEN (62.5%, 54.9%) by 9.7 and 15.8 points respectively, and CAJ (56.5%, 48.8%) by 15.7 and 21.9 points. The consistent gains across all three datasets and both search directions indicate that the implicit discriminative knowledge distillation strategy generalizes well across dataset scale and modality direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VI-ReID line of work by reframing the role of modality-specific features: rather than treating them as noise to be discarded (as in direct shared-feature learning methods like NFS, MCLNet) or as a gap to be bridged via generation (CMT, AlignGAN), it argues they contain 'implicit discriminative knowledge' (color, contrast, saturation cues) that can be distilled into shared features. The dual-stream architecture with a GRL-based modality confuser and a standard modality discriminator draws directly on domain-adaptation techniques (Ganin & Lempitsky, 2015), while the Information Purifier repurposes SE-Net-style channel attention (Hu et al., 2018) combined with Instance Normalization to selectively preserve identity-relevant content. The TGSA loss is a novel contribution that applies KL-divergence alignment to triplet graph-structure affinity matrices, extending the graph-structure ideas from DDAG and counterfactual feature transfer into a cross-branch distillation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that modality-specific features are not merely a source of domain discrepancy but also a reservoir of discriminative signal—offers a principled alternative to generation-based compensation, which the authors note introduces noise. The method achieves strong SOTA on SYSU-MM01 (81.42% rank-1, 79.85% mAP single-shot all-search) without requiring paired generation or external data, making it attractive for practical deployment. However, the approach adds considerable architectural and loss-function complexity (dual-stream backbone, purifier, four auxiliary losses), and the evaluation is limited to three datasets with a single ResNet-50 backbone. Open questions include whether the implicit-knowledge-distillation principle generalizes to other cross-modal retrieval tasks (e.g., RGB-X, thermal-visible) and whether the purification step is truly necessary beyond standard IN, as the ablation gap between L_b+L_tgsa and L_b+L_ip+L_tgsa is modest.
