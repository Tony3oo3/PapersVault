## Problem

Existing universal 3D hand models rely on a separate tracking-then-modeling pipeline, where tracking errors accumulate and cannot be recovered during modeling. Additionally, prior hand avatar creation from phone scans (e.g., HARP) produces plausible but inauthentic results due to limited geometric expressiveness of MANO and a single-point-light shadow assumption that fails under real-world multi-source lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240501/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adaptation pipeline consumes a ~15-second iPhone 12 phone scan (RGB + depth) of a single hand in neutral pose with varying global rotations, preprocessed into 2D keypoints, foreground masks, and MANO parameters. It outputs a 32-dimensional personalized ID code (driving geometry via the frozen UHM), a UV-space albedo texture, and a learned shadow map. For animation, off-the-shelf 3D pose estimators supply per-frame 3D joint angles that, combined with the fixed ID code, produce a 16K-vertex / 32K-face posed mesh renderable with standard graphics pipelines or Phong reflection with environment maps.

## Method

UHM parameterizes hand geometry via linear blend skinning with three corrective terms (ID-dependent skeleton, ID-dependent vertex, and pose-and-ID-dependent vertex) decoded from a 32-dim ID code and 6D joint rotations through VAE and MLP networks. The key architectural insight is performing tracking and modeling simultaneously in a single end-to-end stage, eliminating the error accumulation inherent in separate ICP-based tracking followed by mesh fitting. A novel image matching loss computes optical flow between rendered images (using a static neutral-pose reference texture) and captured images, then minimizes the L1 distance between rasterized vertex positions and their optically-flowed targets, enforcing semantic vertex consistency and preventing skin sliding. For adaptation, the pre-trained UHM is frozen while the ID code, per-frame global rotation/translation, and a fully convolutional ShadowNet (which models shadow as a UV-space darkness difference rather than assuming a single point light) are jointly optimized against the phone scan using L1, VGG, and total-variation losses.

## Results

On the phone scan adaptation task (Table 3), UHM achieves PSNR 31.82, SSIM 0.962, LPIPS 0.076, and P2S 0.45 mm, substantially outperforming HARP (PSNR 29.89, P2S 2.04 mm) and Handy (PSNR 26.02, P2S 2.21 mm). For the universal hand model (Table 1), UHM achieves the lowest P2S error across all three test sets: 0.72 mm (Ours), 0.75 mm (MANO test), and 0.59 mm (DHM test), compared to 1.20/0.78/1.11 mm for Handy and 1.44/0.94/1.36 mm for MANO. On the DHM dataset with varying view counts (Table 2), UHM reduces P2S error from 3.68 mm (LISA, 1 view) to 1.63 mm, a more than 2× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UHM outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the universal hand models (Table 1), UHM reduces P2S error by 48–57% over MANO (0.72 vs 1.44 mm on the Ours test set), 41–52% over NIMBLE, and 40–47% over Handy. On the DHM dataset (Table 2), UHM achieves 1.63 mm P2S with a single view versus LISA's 3.68 mm, a 2.25× improvement. For the end-to-end hand avatar task (Tables 3–4), UHM beats HARP by 1.93 dB PSNR and 4.4× lower P2S (0.45 vs 2.04 mm), and beats Handy by 5.8 dB PSNR and 4.9× lower P2S. The P2S gap is particularly striking: UHM's 0.45 mm is within the noise floor of the depth sensor, while HARP and Handy remain above 2 mm, indicating that the high-fidelity universal geometry of UHM is the key differentiator for authentic shape reproduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal 3D hand modeling line (MANO, NIMBLE, Handy) by unifying tracking and modeling into a single end-to-end stage, departing from the conventional two-stage pipeline where ICP-based tracking errors accumulate irrecoverably into the model. For the adaptation-to-phone-scan setting, it builds on HARP's personalized hand reconstruction but replaces HARP's single-point-light shadow assumption with a learned ShadowNet and leverages UHM's learned priors (ID code, pose correctives) rather than optimizing from a subdivided MANO template. The sparse vertex corrective estimation follows STAR, and the VAE-based ID encoding follows the MANO formulation, but the image matching loss using RAFT optical flow is a novel addition not present in prior hand modeling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the capture requirement from tens of synchronized studio cameras to a 15-second phone scan with a depth sensor, this work makes authentic, animatable 3D hand avatars accessible for everyday AR/VR use, with a 4–5× reduction in P2S error over the prior best phone-scan method (HARP). The simultaneous tracking-and-modeling formulation and the optical-flow-based image matching loss address two previously overlooked failure modes (error accumulation and skin sliding) that could inform other articulated-body modeling tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to hands with accessories or extreme skin conditions, whether the 2-hour adaptation time can be reduced toward real-time, and whether the depth-sensor requirement can be relaxed to pure RGB.
