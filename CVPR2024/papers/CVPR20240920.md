## Problem

Existing monocular 3D human reconstruction methods struggle to recover fine geometric details (face, hands, cloth wrinkles) and are prone to depth ambiguities that distort geometry along the camera optical axis. While consumer-grade RGB-D sensors are now ubiquitous, prior approaches either ignore depth information, estimate it from RGB (introducing noise), or lack pixel-alignment with the input, limiting reconstruction fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240920/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB-D image as input, from which surface normals are derived via neighboring-pixel cross-product and a sparse voxel grid is constructed from the depth map. At inference, the SDF is evaluated on a non-uniform 3D grid concentrated around the input point cloud (resolution m×H, m×W, m×D with m=∛(M³/L), M=256) rather than a random grid, and the final watertight mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes at the zero level-set.

## Method

ANIM combines three components: (1) a multi-resolution 2D feature extractor with two stacked hourglass networks (LR-FE for holistic context, HR-FE for local detail) processing concatenated RGB and surface normals; (2) a novel SparseConvNet U-Net (VFE) that extracts voxel-aligned geometric features from depth-derived voxels linked with LR image features, exploiting sparsity for efficiency; and (3) an MLP that fuses pixel-aligned 2D features with voxel-aligned 3D features to predict the SDF. A depth-supervision strategy adds a second Huber loss that forces the SDF to zero at input point-cloud locations (which lie on the visible surface), refining surface estimation without additional parameters. Semantic-aware sampling augments point density on face and hand regions using body-part segmentation masks, enabling finer detail learning in those areas. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end and can be fine-tuned on the introduced ANIM-Real dataset to handle consumer-grade sensor noise.

## Results

On RenderPeople, ANIM achieves a Chamfer Distance of 2.075 cm, Normal reprojection error of 0.4990, and P2S of 1.936 cm, outperforming all 15 compared methods. On THuman2.0, ANIM reaches CD=0.913 cm, Normal=0.2545, and P2S=0.878 cm, again setting the best scores across all metrics. The closest RGB-D competitor (PIFu+VFE+SN) trails ANIM by 0.011 cm in CD on THuman2.0, while the best RGB-only method (ECON) is 0.426 cm worse in CD on RenderPeople. ANIM also substantially outperforms other RGB-D methods: NormalGAN (CD=3.924) and OcPlanes (CD=5.619) on RenderPeople.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANIM outperforms all 15 baselines on both RenderPeople and THuman2.0 across all three metrics (CD, Normal, P2S). Among RGB-only methods, ECON is the strongest competitor (CD=2.261 on RenderPeople, 1.339 on THuman2.0), yet ANIM still leads by 0.186 cm and 0.426 cm respectively, demonstrating the clear advantage of incorporating depth. Among RGB-D methods, the adapted PIFu+VFE+SN is the closest rival (CD=0.924 on THuman2.0 vs. ANIM's 0.913), but ANIM still wins on all three metrics. The original RGB-D baselines NormalGAN and OcPlanes are significantly outperformed, with OcPlanes showing the worst CD (5.619 on RenderPeople, 3.734 on THuman2.0), confirming that global plane-aligned occupancy is inferior to the proposed pixel-voxel-aligned implicit representation. Adding the VFE or HR-FE modules to PIFu and IF-Net substantially improves those baselines (e.g., PIFu+VFE+SN drops from CD=4.650 to 2.242 on RenderPeople), but neither reaches ANIM's accuracy, validating the full multi-resolution pixel-voxel-aligned architecture combined with depth supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANIM unifies two previously separate alignment strategies in implicit human reconstruction: the pixel-alignment introduced by PIFu (projecting 3D queries into 2D image features) and the voxel-alignment proposed by PaMIR (leveraging spatial structure from a parametric model). Rather than relying on a parametric model for voxel structure, ANIM derives voxels directly from the input depth map and processes them with a SparseConvNet U-Net, departing from the dense 3D convolutions used in prior implicit-function methods. The depth-supervision strategy—using the input point cloud to explicitly constrain SDF values on the visible surface—is a new training signal not present in PIFu, PIFuHD, or IF-Net. The ANIM-Real dataset also fills a gap, as no prior benchmark paired high-resolution multi-view 3D ground truth with synchronized consumer-grade RGB-D captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making high-fidelity 3D human reconstruction accessible with ubiquitous consumer-grade sensors (e.g., Azure Kinect) rather than requiring expensive multi-view scanning rigs, which is critical for AR/VR, gaming, and e-commerce applications. The ANIM-Real dataset and fine-tuning protocol address a real deployment gap: models trained on clean synthetic data degrade severely on noisy real captures, and no prior dataset bridged this divide. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view or video settings (the authors note temporal fusion as future work), how it performs under occlusion or extreme poses, and whether the depth-supervision strategy generalizes to other implicit-reconstruction tasks beyond humans.
