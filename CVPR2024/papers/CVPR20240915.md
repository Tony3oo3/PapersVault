## Problem

Existing facial reflectance reconstruction methods rely on large-scale Light Stage captures (typically <300 subjects) and are trained in UV space, making them unable to generalize across diverse real-world identities. The high cost of reflectance capture and the lack of subject diversity limit the practical applicability of these models to in-the-wild faces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240915/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB face image (resized to 512×512) and a pre-built reflectance template library of 12 multi-view images (4 domains × 3 fixed views: left, frontal, right). The identity is extracted via a pre-trained ArcFace ResNet-100 at 112×112 resolution. The output is a complete set of UV-space reflectance maps (diffuse albedo, specular albedo, roughness, surface normal) obtained by synthesizing three-view identity-conditioned reflectance images and stitching them via dense correspondences estimated with Deep3D, yielding renderable PBR assets in 3.8 seconds per face on an A6000 GPU.

## Method

The core insight is that facial reflectance data shares structural priors with RGB faces in image space, enabling large-scale RGB data (FFHQ) to supplement limited reflectance captures. The architecture is a VQGAN with five domain-specific codebooks (diffuse, specular, roughness, normal, texture) trained in two stages: first a shared codebook on mixed RGB+reflectance data, then per-domain fine-tuning with a Swin Transformer fusion module that learns per-pixel weighted combinations of the five quantized representations. For identity conditioning, a lightweight swapper module with AdaIN-based injection branches is inserted at each decoder upsampling stage and trained exclusively in the RGB domain using VGGFace2HQ; because the reflectance and RGB codebooks are aligned during joint training, the swapping capacity transfers automatically to the reflectance domain. At inference, the closest reflectance template is selected by identity similarity, the ArcFace embedding drives multi-view identity-conditioned decoding, and the three resulting views are stitched into UV maps using YUV-space color matching and face parsing to exclude non-facial regions.

## Results

On albedo estimation (Table 1), ID2Reflectance achieves PSNR 28.47, SSIM 0.923, LPIPS 0.1248, and identity similarity 0.735, substantially outperforming TRUST (PSNR 21.63, ID 0.478) and ID2Albedo (PSNR 23.72, ID 0.532) across all four metrics. The multi-domain codebook ablation (Table 2) shows that domain-specific codebooks improve diffuse albedo PSNR from 24.87 (joint codebook) to 31.62, and using the closest template over a fixed template raises diffuse PSNR from 25.26 to 28.47. Total inference time is 3.8 seconds per face on an A6000 GPU.

**Table 1: Albedo estimation comparison on CelebAMask-HQ and captured test set.**

| Method | PSNR ↑ | SSIM ↑ | LPIPS ↓ | ID Similarity ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRUST [16] | 21.63 | 0.852 | 0.2014 | 0.478 |
| ID2Albedo [53] | 23.72 | 0.884 | 0.1549 | 0.532 |
| **Ours** | **28.47** | **0.923** | **0.1248** | **0.735** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Quantitatively, ID2Reflectance dominates both TRUST [16] and ID2Albedo [53] on all four albedo metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, ID similarity), with the largest gap in identity preservation (0.735 vs. 0.532 for ID2Albedo and 0.478 for TRUST). Visually, the paper compares against Dib et al. [13], AvatarMe++ [33], Relightify [50], ReflectanceMM [24], AlbedoMM [58], AvatarMe [32], and FitMe [34] on Digital Emily and in-the-wild images; ID2Reflectance produces more identity-consistent and illumination-invariant diffuse/specular albedos, while competitors show color shifts, over-smoothing, or identity drift. For cross-domain face swapping (Fig. 13), SimSwap [7] and InfoSwap [18] generate obvious noise and artifacts in the reflectance domain, and StyleGAN-based methods E4S [46] and 3dSwap [40] can only operate in the texture/diffuse domain due to insufficient pattern coverage in StyleGAN/EG3D latent spaces. ID2Reflectance's codebook-based swapper is the only method that achieves consistent, artifact-free swapping across both RGB and all four reflectance domains simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant UV-space formulation of facial reflectance models (AvatarMe, FitMe, Relightify) by learning reflectance priors in image space, where facial structure is shared with RGB data. The generative backbone is VQGAN with a novel multi-domain codebook scheme (Swin Transformer fusion) that aligns reflectance and RGB latent spaces, while the identity-conditioned swapping module adapts AdaIN-based face swapping (SimSwap, FaceShifter) to transfer identity from RGB to reflectance domains. The approach also extends the authors' own ID2Albedo framework from albedo-only to full multi-component reflectance reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the dependency on expensive Light Stage captures (only 135 subjects needed vs. hundreds in prior work), this method makes high-quality facial reflectance reconstruction more accessible and generalizable to diverse real-world identities. However, the reconstructed PBR maps are not physically plausible since they lack ground-truth PBR supervision, and the method still requires a curated reflectance template library for inference. Open questions include whether the image-space formulation can be extended to full BRDF parameters, whether the 135-subject training set is sufficient for truly diverse populations, and whether the non-physical PBR outputs limit downstream rendering fidelity.
