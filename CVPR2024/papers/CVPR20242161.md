## Problem

3D CNNs for EM neuron segmentation suffer from high computational cost and limited 2D-section input size, making them impractical for large-scale volumes where sliding-window patching introduces stitching errors. While lightweight 2D networks avoid these issues, they inherently lack inter-section spatial context along the axial direction, leading to ambiguous affinity predictions at section boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The 2D Y-shaped network consumes pairs of adjacent 2D EM sections (e.g., 1024×1024 pixels at 3×3×29 nm or 8×8×35 nm resolution) sequentially through the volume, rather than processing 3D patches. It outputs a 3-channel 3D affinity map (Z, H, W directions) computed as cosine distances between C-dimensional embedding vectors, which is then converted to instance masks via Waterz or LMC post-processing. The 3D teacher network operates on the full 3D volume during training but is discarded at inference.

## Method

A 2D Y-shaped network (shared encoder, two decoders) extracts embedding maps from two adjacent sections; 3D affinities are derived by measuring cosine distances between corresponding and neighboring embeddings. Cross-dimension Affinity Distillation (CAD) performs online knowledge distillation by minimizing MSE between the 2D student's and 3D teacher's predicted affinity maps, transferring inter-section dependency knowledge in real time. A Feature Grafting Interaction (FGI) module further enhances transfer by inserting 2D embeddings into the 3D embedding map and computing long-range affinities across all section pairs, supervised by ground-truth labels. Training jointly optimizes L = L_2D + L_3D + λ₁·L_CAD + λ₂·L_FGI with MSE losses, introducing the distillation terms after 10k iterations to balance convergence rates. At inference, only the lightweight 2D network is retained, processing the full volume section-by-section without patching, achieving 1/20 the latency of 3D baselines.

## Results

On AC3/4 with Waterz post-processing, the proposed method achieves VOI of 0.8835 and ARAND of 0.0808, outperforming the second-best APViT (VOI 0.9764) by 9.5%. On the newly introduced Wafer4 dataset (125×1250×1250 voxels), it achieves VOI of 0.5587 (Waterz) and 0.7289 (LMC), the best across all methods. In terms of efficiency, the 2D-based method requires only 0.88M parameters and 7,509 GMACs with 17.29 s inference latency on the AC3/4 test set, compared to 113.83–374.02 s for 3D baselines—a roughly 20× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the lowest VOI across all three datasets (CREMI, AC3/4, Wafer4) and both post-processing algorithms (Waterz, LMC), outperforming all four baselines. Against Superhuman [21] and MALA [10], the gains are consistent and substantial (e.g., VOI 0.8835 vs. 1.0305 and 1.1338 on AC3/4 Waterz). Against PEA [15], the advantage is smaller but still clear (VOI 0.8835 vs. 1.0502 on AC3/4 Waterz; 0.5587 vs. 0.5930 on Wafer4 Waterz). Against APViT [33], the closest competitor, the method wins on VOI in every setting (e.g., 0.8835 vs. 0.9764 on AC3/4 Waterz, a 9.5% reduction), though APViT occasionally achieves a marginally lower ARAND on CREMI-C (0.1102 vs. 0.1487). The most striking advantage is efficiency: the 2D-based method runs in 17.29 s with 0.88M parameters, versus 113.83 s (Superhuman), 160.94 s (MALA), 374.02 s (PEA), and 345.50 s (APViT), representing a ~20× latency reduction while using an order of magnitude fewer FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the explicit affinity generation paradigm from PEA (Huang et al., AAAI 2022) and Lee et al. (IEEE TMI 2021), which compute voxel affinities via cosine distance between learned embeddings, by replacing the 3D CNN backbone with a lightweight 2D Y-shaped network that processes adjacent 2D sections. The key conceptual departure is the cross-dimension affinity distillation (CAD) strategy, which adapts the online distillation paradigm from Deep Mutual Learning (Zhang et al., CVPR 2018) to a cross-dimensional setting: a 3D teacher network transfers inter-section dependency knowledge to a 2D student in real-time during training. The Feature Grafting Interaction (FGI) module further distinguishes this work by enabling the 2D student's embeddings to be grafted into the 3D teacher's feature space for long-range affinity computation, a mechanism not present in prior distillation or affinity-based segmentation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× inference speedup while simultaneously improving segmentation accuracy over all 3D-CNN baselines addresses a critical bottleneck for practical deployment on large-scale EM volumes, where 3D networks require sliding-window patching that introduces stitching errors. The newly released Wafer4 dataset (125×1250×1250 voxels, >1.9×10⁸ annotated voxels) provides a much larger and more challenging benchmark than existing CREMI/AC3/4 datasets, enabling more reliable evaluation of scalability. Open questions include whether the cross-dimension distillation strategy generalizes to other 3D segmentation tasks beyond EM, whether the 3D teacher can be eliminated entirely (offline distillation), and whether the approach extends to anisotropic or higher-dimensional imaging modalities.
