## Problem

Existing super-resolution methods either recover a single solution, are trained for specific scale factors and image classes, or require graphical/semantic-map inputs for exploration. No prior work enables zero-shot, open-domain exploration of diverse super-resolution solutions through natural language text prompts without explicit training for the target degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240727/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution image (produced by bicubic downsampling at ×8 or ×16), a free-form text prompt specifying desired attributes (e.g., age, expression, hair style), and the known linear degradation operator A (with pseudoinverse computed via SVD), and produces multiple 256×256 high-resolution reconstructions that simultaneously satisfy exact data consistency (Aŷ = y) and semantic alignment with the prompt. The same LR input can yield diverse outputs by varying only the text, enabling interactive exploration of the solution space.

## Method

The paper proposes two zero-shot paradigms. First, it adapts multi-stage text-to-image diffusion models (unCLIP/DALL-E 2 and Imagen) for super-resolution by inserting zero-shot restoration guidance—DPS gradient guidance, IIGDM pseudoinverse guidance, or DDNM null-space rectification—at both the 64×64 down-sampled stage and the 256×256 upsampling stage, using a modified measurement operator A_LR for the first stage and the full A for the second. Second, it augments DDNM with CLIP-based text guidance: at each reverse step, the clean estimate is first rectified for null-space data consistency, then the intermediate previous-step sample is further shifted by the gradient of a CLIP cosine-similarity energy between the image and text embeddings. The key insight is that null-space projection guarantees exact measurement consistency regardless of what the generative model hallucinates, while text conditioning (either native to the T2I model or via CLIP) steers the null-space component toward semantically meaningful variations.

## Results

On the NoCaps open-domain dataset at 16× SR, Imagen+DDNM achieves the best NIQE (4.72) and unCLIP+DDNM achieves the best CLIP score (0.3381), substantially outperforming vanilla DDNM (NIQE 13.24, CLIP 0.2162) and DPS (NIQE 4.81, CLIP 0.2418). On CelebA-HQ faces at 16× SR, unCLIP+DDNM achieves the best NIQE (5.43) while DDNM achieves the highest LR PSNR (80.91 dB) due to exact null-space projection. All proposed methods maintain LR PSNR > 45 dB, confirming data consistency with the low-resolution input. CLIP-guided DDNM improves both NIQE and CLIP score over vanilla DDNM on NoCaps (NIQE 5.71 vs. 13.24; CLIP 0.2517 vs. 0.2162 at 16×) at a small cost in LR PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to DPS [12], the T2I-based methods (Imagen+DDNM, unCLIP+DDNM) achieve significantly better image quality on NoCaps (NIQE 4.62–5.21 vs. 9.66 at 8×) and much higher CLIP scores for text matching (0.31–0.34 vs. 0.26), while DPS maintains slightly better LR PSNR. Compared to vanilla DDNM [81], the T2I+DDNM variants dramatically reduce NIQE on NoCaps (4.62–5.21 vs. 10.27–13.24) and improve CLIP scores (0.30–0.34 vs. 0.22–0.23), at the cost of lower LR PSNR (50–70 dB vs. 73–78 dB) since DDNM's exact projection guarantees perfect consistency but produces blurry outputs. CLIP-guided DDNM sits between the two: it improves NIQE and CLIP over vanilla DDNM on NoCaps but does not match the T2I models in either metric. On faces, unCLIP+DDNM achieves the best NIQE (5.43 at 16×) among all methods, while DDNM's LR PSNR advantage (80.91 dB) reflects its exact null-space projection rather than superior perceptual quality. In the user study, Imagen+DDNM received the highest preference for both text similarity (90.89%) and photo-realism (69.35%), confirming that the T2I-based approaches outperform both DPS and CLIP-guided restoration in perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of zero-shot diffusion-based restoration (DPS, DDNM, IIGDM) and text-to-image generative models (DALL-E 2/unCLIP, Imagen), repurposing the latter's multi-stage cascaded diffusion architecture for image recovery by injecting measurement consistency at each resolution stage. It departs from prior explorable super-resolution work [2, 5], which relied on graphical inputs or semantic maps and was restricted to specific image classes or fixed SR factors, by introducing natural language as the exploration interface in a fully zero-shot, open-domain setting. The CLIP-guided DDNM variant extends the training-free guidance paradigm [3, 84] to the restoration context, while the T2I-based approaches adapt the range-null space decomposition and gradient-based guidance of [12, 74, 81] to handle the multi-stage generation process inherent in models like Imagen and unCLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical framework for text-guided exploration of the super-resolution solution space without task-specific training, which is particularly valuable for extreme SR factors where the problem is severely ill-posed. A key practical finding is that classifier-free guidance, while improving text adherence, conflicts with gradient-based measurement guidance and can drastically reduce data consistency—a trade-off that practitioners combining T2I models with inverse-problem constraints will encounter. The limitation that T2I-DDNM can produce structurally unrealistic yet measurement-consistent images (via null-space rectification of hallucinated content) highlights an open question about how to enforce structural plausibility beyond pixel-level consistency. Future work could address efficiency (multiple diffusion stages are expensive), extend to arbitrary degradations beyond bicubic downsampling, and develop better mechanisms to prevent the model from hallucinating content incompatible with the observation.
