## Problem

Existing video visual relation detection datasets (e.g., VidVRD, Action Genome) barely involve human-human interactions and define semantically simple relations (spatial, atomic actions, visual comparisons) that can be recognized from appearance or category priors alone, without requiring spatio-temporal context reasoning. This limits research on understanding complex multi-person interactions in sports and surveillance videos, where high-level interactions like tactical coordination and confrontation demand detailed spatio-temporal reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip centered at a keyframe (720P, 25FPS source, annotated at 5FPS) and produces human bounding boxes plus interaction triplets ⟨S, I, O⟩, where S and O are bounding boxes of two distinct people and I is one of 34 high-level interaction classes (16 basketball, 18 volleyball). Two evaluation modes are defined: HHICls uses ground-truth human boxes and predicts only interaction classes, while HHIDet performs full detection of both humans and interactions. The dataset contains 11,398 keyframes with 118,075 human boxes and 50,649 interaction instances, averaging 10.36 people per frame.

## Method

The baseline adopts a two-stage pipeline: an offline Faster-RCNN (ResNeXt-101-FPN) detects person boxes, and all box pairs are enumerated as interaction proposals. For each proposal, the representation concatenates three components: (1) motion features extracted via RoIAlign on a 3D video backbone (SlowFast-R50 or VideoMAE ViT-B) applied separately to subject and object boxes, (2) context features from RoIAlign on the union box, and (3) relative position encoding generated by creating separate binary spatial masks for subject and object (flattened and projected to 256-d), preserving the ordered nature of the triplet. A multi-head self-attention layer then enables information exchange across all proposals in a frame before an FCN head classifies each into an interaction class or background. Ablations confirm that motion features are most critical, position encoding is complementary to visual features, and inter-proposal attention consistently improves performance regardless of representation choice.

## Results

On the SportsHHI validation set, the proposed two-stage baseline with a SlowFast-R50 backbone achieves mAP 7.52 and R@50 59.53 in HHICls mode (ground-truth person boxes), outperforming all tested baselines. With the stronger VideoMAE-pretrained ViT-B backbone, performance improves substantially to mAP 10.69 and R@50 68.13 in HHICls mode, and mAP 4.93 and R@50 42.99 in the end-to-end HHIDet mode. In HHIDet mode, the SlowFast-R50 baseline reaches mAP 3.36 and R@50 37.72, still leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed baseline outperforms all four compared methods in both HHICls and HHIDet modes across every reported metric. In HHICls mode with the same SlowFast-R50 backbone, the baseline achieves mAP 7.52 vs. 5.44 for ACARN and 5.00 for SlowFast, a 2.1–2.5 mAP gain, and R@50 of 59.53 vs. 56.53 (ACARN) and 52.73 (SlowFast). The video scene graph generation methods STTran (mAP 3.31) and HORT (mAP 3.75) perform substantially worse, confirming that appearance-based image backbones and category-prior-dependent designs are insufficient for high-level human-human interaction recognition. In HHIDet mode, the gap narrows somewhat (mAP 3.36 vs. 2.53 for ACARN), but the baseline still leads by 0.8 mAP. The largest gains come from swapping to the VideoMAE-pretrained ViT-B backbone, which boosts HHICls mAP from 7.52 to 10.69 and HHIDet mAP from 3.36 to 4.93, highlighting the importance of strong spatio-temporal representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the video visual relation detection line (AG, VidVRD, VidOR) by arguing that existing relation categories are semantically shallow—recognizable from appearance or category priors—and instead introduces high-level human-human interactions (tactical coordination, confrontation) that demand spatio-temporal context reasoning. Methodologically, it builds on the two-stage Faster-RCNN pipeline and video backbones (SlowFast, VideoMAE) from action detection, combining RoIAlign-based motion/context features with relative position encoding borrowed from scene graph parsing and multi-head self-attention for inter-proposal information exchange. The dataset is constructed on MultiSports videos, extending that resource from implicit interaction modeling in action detection to explicit interaction triplet annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SportsHHI fills a clear gap: no prior video relation detection dataset provides explicit, high-level human-human interaction annotations in complex multi-person scenes, making it the first benchmark where spatio-temporal context reasoning is genuinely required for relation recognition. The error analysis reveals a conceptual distinction from action detection—localization and classification of interacting pairs are inseparable, and the dominant error is false positives on non-interacting pairs in crowd scenes—pointing to open challenges in distinguishing sparse interactions from dense background pairs. Limitations include coverage of only two sports, frame-level (not tube-level) evaluation, and relatively low absolute mAP (~7.5), suggesting substantial room for methodological progress. Future work could extend to more sports, incorporate temporal tube prediction, and explore whether the spatio-temporal reasoning gains transfer to other multi-person domains like surveillance.
