## Problem

Existing multi-view representation learning methods produce view-consistent and view-specific representations with high mutual information between them, creating substantial redundancy that degrades downstream task performance. The joint (end-to-end) learning paradigm forms a Min-Max game over mutual information bounds that risks suboptimal solutions, and the disparity in information density between consistency (requiring multi-view integration) and specificity (per-view) is largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view image data (32×32 grayscale for E-MNIST/E-FMNIST, grayscale or RGB for COIL-20/COIL-100, RGB for Office-31) where each sample has 2–3 views. During training, a random mask (default 70% ratio) is applied to all views simultaneously, and the single consistent encoder processes all visible blocks at once rather than per-view encoders. The output is a pair of representations: a shared view-consistent vector c (dimension 10 by default) and per-view specific vectors s^i, which are concatenated as z^i = [c, s^i] for downstream k-means clustering or SVC classification.

## Method

MRDD is a two-stage pipeline. Stage I (Masked Cross-View Prediction) randomly masks portions of all views, feeds the visible blocks to a single VAE-based consistent encoder, and trains multiple view-specific decoders to reconstruct the masked content from the shared representation, learning consistency without per-view encoder overhead. Stage II (Distilled Disentangling) freezes the consistent encoder, extracts coarse view-specific representations via per-view encoders, then applies the CLUB estimator to minimize the mutual information upper bound between c and each s^i, effectively filtering out consistency-related information from the specific representations. A reconstruction loss on the concatenated z^i = [c, s^i] prevents trivial solutions in the disentangling module. The key insight is treating the already-learned consistent representation as prior knowledge to 'distill' away from specific representations, rather than learning both simultaneously in a joint Min-Max game.

## Results

On the clustering task across five datasets, MRDD-cs achieves the best ACC_clu on all five: 75.93% (E-MNIST), 58.25% (E-FMNIST), 69.18% (COIL-20), 62.00% (COIL-100), and 37.14% (Office-31), with average improvements of +1.64 to +8.83 over the previous state-of-the-art. On the classification task (three datasets), MRDD-cs reaches 98.37% ACC_cls on E-MNIST, 91.17% on COIL-100, and 73.51% on Office-31, outperforming the best baseline by +4.16, +4.44, and +4.18 respectively. The consistency-only variant (MRDD-c) also surpasses all baselines on E-MNIST (70.34%) and COIL-100 (65.29%) clustering, demonstrating that the masked cross-view prediction stage alone yields strong representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRDD-cs achieves the best clustering accuracy on all five datasets, with the largest margin on E-MNIST (+8.83 over GCFAgg at 67.10%) and Office-31 (+2.74 over UNITER at 34.40%). In classification, MRDD-cs outperforms UNITER (the strongest baseline) by +4.16 on E-MNIST (98.37 vs. 94.21), +4.44 on COIL-100 (91.17 vs. 86.73 Joint-VAE), and +4.18 on Office-31 (73.51 vs. 69.33). Among information-theory-based baselines, Multi-VAE and MIB are the closest competitors on E-FMNIST clustering (53.16% and 53.71% vs. MRDD-cs's 58.25%), while model-based methods like CMC and EAMC are competitive on COIL-100 but fall 2–5 points behind. Notably, the consistency-only variant MRDD-c already surpasses all baselines on E-MNIST and COIL-100 clustering, indicating that the masked cross-view prediction stage alone provides substantial gains. Single-view methods (Joint-VAE, β-VAE) are consistently the weakest, confirming the benefit of multi-view fusion, though Joint-VAE surprisingly outperforms several multi-view methods on COIL-20, suggesting that view quality can degrade fused representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends information-theoretic multi-view disentangling methods (DVIB, Multi-VAE, MIB) by identifying and addressing a previously overlooked issue: redundancy between view-consistent and view-specific representations. It repurposes the masked modeling paradigm (from BERT, MAE, denoising autoencoders) into a novel Masked Cross-View Prediction (MCP) strategy for extracting cross-view consistency with a single encoder, and introduces a 'distilled disentangling' module that uses the frozen consistency encoder as prior knowledge to filter consistency-related information from view-specific representations via the CLUB mutual information upper bound. The two-stage pipeline departs from the joint end-to-end training paradigm common in prior MvRL work, which the authors argue leads to suboptimal Min-Max solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic (mutual information estimation via MINE) showing that existing MvRL methods produce highly correlated consistency and specificity representations, and offers a principled two-stage remedy. Two empirical findings have broader implications: high mask ratios (80%) improve consistency quality, and reducing the dimensionality of consistent representations relative to specific ones refines combined representations. However, the two-stage dependency introduces training instability in MCP, and the authors acknowledge that in complex scenarios the consistent representations struggle to reconstruct original data, leaving open the question of how to decouple the stages and improve robustness.
