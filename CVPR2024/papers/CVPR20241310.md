## Problem

Most video scene detection methods rely on ordinary shot-to-shot similarity to determine scene boundaries, which can incorrectly link visually similar shots from different scenes while failing to connect dissimilar shots within the same scene. The key gap is that context information from neighboring shots is neglected when measuring shot-to-shot affinity, leading to both false merges across scene boundaries and false splits within a single scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates at shot level: N=21 temporally adjacent shots are fed in, each represented by a single randomly selected frame encoded by a pre-trained ResNet-50 (or ViT-S/16) into a fixed-length feature vector. The model outputs a binary label per shot indicating whether it is a scene boundary shot (Class 1) or not (Class 0). A local sliding window of length l=10 constrains neighbor selection, and the model is robust to varying input shot counts (performance fluctuates <1% across N values).

## Method

NeighborNet performs cascaded graph reasoning in two dimensions. First, a feature graph connects semantically similar shots within a local time window, with edge weights computed as the sum of three similarity terms: direct cosine similarity (IRS), neighbor-node similarity (RNS, comparing shot j against neighbors of shot i), and neighbor-neighbor similarity (RRS, comparing neighbors of shot i against neighbors of shot j); a one-layer GCN then propagates messages to produce semantic neighbor-aware features that suppress cross-scene similarity. Second, a temporal graph is built by connecting each pair of feature-graph-similar shots to all shots temporally between them, with edge weights combining in-context similarity (ICS) and temporal context similarity (TCS, comparing the temporal neighborhoods surrounding two shots); a second one-layer GCN propagates to yield temporal neighbor-aware features that strengthen intra-scene relations among dissimilar shots. Training supports three paradigms: self-supervised (pseudo-boundary prediction via Modified Dynamic Warping), fully supervised (ground-truth boundary labels), and self-supervised transfer (pre-train then fine-tune), all using binary cross-entropy on the final temporal neighbor-aware features.

## Results

On MovieScenes (MovieNet test split), NeighborNet achieves 71.9 AP / 64.5 mIoU / 62.7 F1 under self-supervised transfer learning, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art TranS4mer (60.8 AP) by 11.1 AP points. Under fully supervised learning, it reaches 64.0 AP / 61.2 mIoU / 57.8 F1, outperforming MHRT (54.8 AP) by 9.2 AP. In the self-supervised-only setting, NeighborNet attains 51.2 AP versus TranS4mer's 34.5 AP (a 16.7-point margin). On transfer evaluation to OVSD and BBC, NeighborNet leads all baselines by at least 5.4 AP (47.3 vs. 41.9 on OVSD; 50.6 vs. 44.0 on BBC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeighborNet outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every learning setting and dataset. Against the strongest prior method TranS4mer [23] (CVPR'23), it gains 16.7 AP in self-supervised learning (51.2 vs. 34.5), 11.1 AP in self-supervised transfer (71.9 vs. 60.8), and 16.7 AP in fully supervised learning (64.0 vs. 47.3 for the best supervised baseline MHRT [41]). The paper also ablates its graph architecture against Transformer [38], GATv2 [6], and HopGNN [10] as alternative backbones; NeighborNet's own neighbor-relation modules consistently beat all three, with the largest gap over HopGNN (which also models neighbor interactions) suggesting the specific RNS/RRS/TCS design is critical. On zero-shot transfer to OVSD and BBC, NeighborNet leads the next-best method (HopGNN) by 5.4 AP and 6.6 AP respectively, confirming generalization beyond the training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in video scene detection that relies on raw shot-to-shot similarity (as in BaSSL, TranS4mer, and SCRL) by introducing neighbor-aware similarity, which measures shot relations through the lens of their semantic and temporal neighbors within a local time window. Conceptually, it repurposes graph convolutional message passing (previously applied to shot sequences in GATv2 or HopGNN baselines) into a two-stage cascaded architecture: a feature graph that suppresses false connections between similar shots from different scenes, followed by a temporal graph that strengthens connections between dissimilar shots within the same scene. The key insight—that similar shots from different scenes will have different semantic neighbors while dissimilar shots in the same scene will share similar temporal neighbors—reframes the similarity computation from a pairwise operation to a context-dependent one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11% AP improvement over the prior state-of-the-art (TranS4mer) is substantial and well-supported by component-wise ablations, suggesting that neighbor-aware context is a genuinely underexploited signal in scene detection. The method's encoder-agnostic design (validated with both ResNet-50 and ViT-S/16) and strong cross-dataset generalization (BBC, OVSD without fine-tuning) indicate broad practical utility for video understanding pipelines. Open questions include whether the local time window constraint (l=10, r=5) limits performance on very long or fast-cut videos, and whether the neighbor-aware similarity principle transfers to other temporal segmentation tasks beyond scene boundaries.
