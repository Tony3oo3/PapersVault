## Problem

Existing multi-object tracking methods treat data association as a single global optimization problem, which retains numerous confusions (ID switches, misassignments) among detections, trajectories, and associations. Additionally, when multiple cues (motion, appearance) are combined, methods either linearly weight them or use heuristic fusion rules, making trackers sensitive to hyperparameters and potentially allowing unreliable appearance cues to interfere with motion cues. Standard NMS also discards heavily occluded detections that could otherwise aid association.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241791/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes per-frame detection boxes (center, width, height, confidence) from a YOLOX detector, Kalman-filter predicted positions for active trajectories, and appearance embeddings from a pre-trained SBS-50 ReID model. Detections are split into two groups using two confidence thresholds and two NMS thresholds (η_nms^first=0.7, η_nms^second=0.95), producing a 'reliable' set for first-stage association and an 'unreliable' set for second-stage association. The output is a set of trajectories with persistent IDs, where each trajectory is a sequence of per-frame bounding boxes.

## Method

The core contribution is Decomposed Data Association (DDA), which replaces the single global Hungarian assignment with three sequential sub-problem solvers: DDM resolves one-trajectory-to-many-detections confusion using only motion cues (finding better-fitting unreliable detections for matched trajectories), TDM resolves many-trajectories-to-one-detection confusion using appearance cosine distance, and ADM resolves two-to-two association confusion by comparing cross-assignment appearance distances. Appearance cues are applied only when motion cues are insufficient (gated by a confusion reduction factor κ), avoiding the hyperparameter sensitivity of linear fusion. Complementary to DDA, Occlusion-aware NMS (ONMS) uses a second, looser NMS threshold to retain heavily occluded detections that standard NMS would discard, increasing association opportunities. The entire pipeline is non-learnable and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying tracker.

## Results

On the MOT17 test set (private detection protocol), DeconfuseTrack achieves HOTA 64.9, IDF1 80.6, MOTA 80.4, AssA 65.1, and DetA 65.0, setting new state-of-the-art on HOTA, IDF1, AssA, and DetA. On the MOT20 test set, it reaches HOTA 63.3, IDF1 77.6, MOTA 78.1, AssA 62.7, and DetA 64.1, again leading in HOTA, IDF1, and DetA. Compared to the ByteTrack baseline, DeconfuseTrack improves HOTA by +1.8 (MOT17) and +2.0 (MOT20), IDF1 by +3.3 and +2.4, and AssA by +3.1 on both datasets, confirming that the decomposed association and occlusion-aware NMS effectively reduce tracking confusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeconfuseTrack outperforms the ByteTrack baseline by +1.8 HOTA, +3.3 IDF1, and +3.1 AssA on MOT17, and by +2.0 HOTA, +2.4 IDF1, and +3.1 AssA on MOT20, validating the decomposed association design. Among non-learnable matchers, it surpasses the closest competitors SAT (64.4 HOTA) and StrongSORT++ (64.4 HOTA) by 0.5 HOTA on MOT17, while C-BIOU achieves a slightly higher MOTA (81.1 vs. 80.4) but trails by 0.8 HOTA and 0.9 IDF1. On MOT20, DeconfuseTrack leads all non-learnable methods in HOTA (63.3 vs. 62.8 for RTU++) and IDF1 (77.6 vs. 77.0 for StrongSORT++), with MOTA only 0.1% below UTM (78.2). Against learnable matchers, it exceeds MOTRv2 by +2.9 HOTA and +5.6 IDF1 on MOT17, and by +3.0 HOTA and +5.4 IDF1 on MOT20, demonstrating that a simple non-learnable decomposed approach can rival or surpass Transformer- and GNN-based association. The plug-and-play nature is confirmed by Table 5: adding ONMS+DDA to SORT yields +1.1 HOTA and +2.8 IDF1, to Tracktor +1.9 HOTA and +4.1 IDF1, and to OC-SORT +0.1 HOTA and +0.7 IDF1, with only DeepSORT showing a marginal −0.1 IDF1 drop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ByteTrack's two-stage reliable/unreliable detection framework but departs from the global Hungarian matching paradigm shared by most tracking-by-detection methods (SORT, DeepSORT, OC-SORT, StrongSORT). Rather than treating data association as a single global optimization or linearly fusing multiple cues, DeconfuseTrack decomposes the assignment problem into three targeted sub-problems (detection, trajectory, and association disambiguation) and selectively invokes appearance cues only when motion cues are ambiguous. It also introduces ONMS as a post-detection refinement over ByteTrack's single NMS threshold, positioning the work as a non-learnable, interpretable alternative to the growing family of GNN- and Transformer-based learnable matchers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a simple, non-learnable, plug-and-play decomposition of the association step can match or exceed complex learnable matchers (GNN, Transformer, RNN) on standard MOT benchmarks, offering MOT practitioners a low-cost, interpretable upgrade path without retraining. The single hyperparameter κ governing all three disambiguation modules simplifies deployment, though its sensitivity (optimal at 0.3) and the reliance on a ReID model for appearance cues limit fully end-to-end simplicity. Open questions include whether the decomposed approach generalizes beyond pedestrian tracking to domains with very different motion dynamics (vehicles, drones), whether it scales to scenes with hundreds of objects, and whether the decoupled appearance-cue strategy remains effective under severe appearance variation (e.g., clothing changes).
