## Problem

Astronaut photography from the ISS spans 4.5M+ images, but only ~300K have been manually localized (minutes to hours per photo by experts), and the only prior automated method (pairwise image matching) would require over 20 compute years to process the full archive. This paper reframes Astronaut Photography Localization (APL) as a worldwide image retrieval task against a satellite imagery database—a formulation that had never been attempted and was previously believed infeasible—enabling low-latency, scalable localization for both the existing archive and incoming disaster-response imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an astronaut RGB photo (variable focal length, oblique view, no canonical orientation due to zero-gravity) along with the ISS nadir point derived from the image timestamp, which constrains the search to a ~2500 km radius. The database consists of 700k Sentinel-2 satellite tiles at 1024×1024 resolution (zoom levels 9–11, covering latitudes 60°N to 60°S), with four temporal snapshots per region (2018–2021). At inference, 4×90° test-time augmentation generates four feature embeddings per database image to handle arbitrary query orientation, and kNN retrieval returns the geographic coordinates of the best-matching tile plus an estimated image orientation relative to North.

## Method

EarthLoc uses a MixVPR-style architecture (ResNet50 backbone + MLP-Mixer, 4096-dim output) trained with a multi-similarity metric learning loss over regional quadruplets—four images of the same geographic region, one per year—so that temporal variation is handled natively rather than through synthetic augmentation. Three key training innovations drive performance: (1) clustered batches, where the model periodically re-clusters regions by feature-space similarity and forms batches from within-cluster samples to force discrimination between visually similar but geographically distinct areas; (2) year-wise data augmentation, where the same random augmentation is applied to all images from a given year across all regions, pushing same-year images closer in feature space and forcing the model to ignore such shared perturbations; and (3) a Neutral-Aware Multi-Similarity Loss that introduces a third 'neutral' category for partially overlapping image pairs (e.g., different zoom levels of the same area), avoiding incorrect positive/negative supervision. Inference is a single kNN lookup against pre-computed database features, taking ~0.05 s per query with 4×90° TTA.

## Results

EarthLoc achieves an average Recall@1 of 57.1% across six evaluation sets (Texas, Alps, California, Gobi Desert, Amazon, Toshka Lakes), with Recall@10 of 74.4% and Recall@100 of 88.9%. The best-performing baseline, AnyLoc (DINOv2+NetVLAD), reaches 42.8% R@1 on average, while the strongest UAV method (OGCL with ViT-L/14) achieves only 19.1% R@1. EarthLoc localizes a query in 0.05 seconds with 4×90° TTA, compared to ~1 minute per query for the previous pairwise-matching approach (FMAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EarthLoc outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluation set and metric. The closest competitor is AnyLoc (DINOv2+NetVLAD), a universal VPR model, which achieves 42.8% average R@1 — EarthLoc surpasses it by 14.3 points in R@1 and 4.4 points in R@100, while being 50× faster in feature extraction and producing 10× smaller feature vectors (4096-D vs. 49152-D). Among UAV-domain methods, OGCL with ViT-L/14 is the strongest at 19.1% R@1, still 38 points below EarthLoc. Urban VPR methods (NetVLAD, SFRS, EigenPlaces, etc.) and satellite self-supervised pre-training methods (TorchGeo with MOCO/ScCo/GASSL) all remain below 10% R@1, confirming that neither street-view place recognition nor generic satellite representation learning transfers well to the astronaut photography domain. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that each of the three proposed training innovations — Clustered Batches, Year-Wise Data Augmentation, and the Neutral-Aware Multi-Similarity Loss — contributes additively, lifting R@1 from 25.5% (baseline multi-similarity) to 57.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes Astronaut Photography Localization (APL) from the pairwise-matching paradigm used by FMAP [58] into a standard image-retrieval problem against a worldwide Sentinel-2 satellite database. Architecturally, it adopts the MixVPR backbone (ResNet50 + MLP-Mixer) from the urban VPR literature, and builds on the Multi-Similarity Loss for metric learning, extending it with a neutral-aware term to handle partial-overlap pairs that are neither positives nor negatives. The training strategy draws on hard-negative mining (clustered batches) and contrastive learning, but introduces year-wise data augmentation as a novel mechanism to enforce temporal invariance across regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With 4.5M astronaut photos growing by tens of thousands per month and manual localization taking minutes to hours per image, an efficient automated method directly accelerates Earth-science research and disaster response (e.g., Cyclone Haiyan protocol). The six evaluation sets and comprehensive benchmark fill a gap that previously had no standardized testbed, and the neutral-aware loss is applicable to any retrieval task with partial-overlap semantics. Key limitations remain: daytime-only queries, no nighttime or panoramic imagery, and a zoom range bounded by the database (5,000–900,000 sq km), leaving open questions about pixel-wise geolocation, seasonal adaptation, and whether training directly on the 300k+ already-localized queries would further close the gap.
