## Problem

Transformer-based VPR methods either derive attention queries dynamically from the input (self-attention), yielding inconsistent information aggregation across images, or rely on computationally expensive two-stage reranking with geometric verification to achieve competitive performance. Meanwhile, the global retrieval stage of Transformer-based approaches has lagged behind strong CNN-based aggregators like MixVPR and CosPlace. The paper seeks a single-stage, efficient aggregation mechanism that closes this gap while remaining interpretable and backbone-agnostic.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 320×320 RGB image, processes it through a pre-trained backbone (ResNet-50 cropped at the second-to-last residual block, or a ViT with linear projection), and produces a single L2-normalized global descriptor of dimension 4096 or 16384. The backbone output is treated as a sequence of N local feature vectors of dimension d, which are then aggregated by the BoQ blocks. The final descriptor lives on the unit hypersphere, optimized for cosine-similarity-based nearest-neighbor search over a database of reference places.

## Method

BoQ introduces a fixed set of M learnable global queries per block that are independent of the input, in contrast to self-attention where queries are derived from the input itself. Each BoQ block first applies self-attention among the queries to let them share context, then applies cross-attention (queries attend to the backbone's local features as keys and values) to selectively aggregate relevant information. Multiple BoQ blocks are stacked in a cascade with Transformer encoder units between them, and the outputs of all blocks are concatenated before a two-layer linear projection reduces dimensionality. The key architectural insight is that the learnable queries act as universal, input-agnostic 'lenses' that consistently probe for the same place-specific attributes across all images, and they do not contribute directly to the output (no residual connection), unlike DETR-style object queries. Training uses Multi-Similarity loss on GSV-Cities with AdamW, and the technique integrates seamlessly with both CNN and ViT backbones.

## Results

BoQ (16384-dim) achieves 95.0% R@1 on Pitts250k-test (the first global retrieval method to cross the 95% threshold), 91.4% R@1 on MSLS-val, 86.2% R@1 on SPED, and 74.4% R@1 on the highly challenging Nordland* benchmark. On the SVOX Night subset, BoQ reaches 85.2% R@1, outperforming the second-best method (MixVPR at 64.4%) by over 20 percentage points. In terms of efficiency, BoQ's feature extraction takes only 7 ms per image with no reranking step, compared to 31–1300 ms extraction plus 400–35200 ms reranking for two-stage methods like R2Former and DELG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoQ outperforms all 14 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest one-stage global retrieval methods, BoQ (16384-dim) leads MixVPR by 0.8 pp on Pitts250k (95.0 vs 94.2), by 3.4 pp on MSLS (91.4 vs 88.0), and by a substantial 16 pp on Nordland* (74.4 vs 58.4), where extreme seasonal variation is the dominant challenge. Against EigenPlaces, the margin is 0.9 pp on Pitts250k, 2.2 pp on MSLS, and 20.2 pp on Nordland*. On the SVOX Night subset (Table 3), BoQ achieves 85.2% R@1 versus 64.4% for MixVPR and 58.9% for EigenPlaces, a gap of over 20 pp under low-light conditions. Notably, BoQ also surpasses two-stage retrieval methods that employ geometric reranking: on MSLS-val it beats R2Former (89.7% R@1) by 1.7 pp and TransVPR (86.8%) by 4.6 pp, while on Pitts30k it exceeds R2Former (91.1%) by 1.3 pp—all without any reranking step and with 30×+ faster inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

BoQ carves new ground in VPR aggregation by repurposing the concept of learnable queries (analogous to DETR's object queries) for feature aggregation rather than detection, using cross-attention to probe backbone features with a fixed set of global queries. It departs from self-attention-based Transformer VPR methods (TransVPR, ETR) where queries are dynamically derived from the input, and from NetVLAD's fixed cluster centers, by introducing trainable queries that are input-independent yet contextually refined via inter-query self-attention. Architecturally, it sits between the MLP-based MixVPR and the attention-based Transformer approaches, being agnostic to both CNN and ViT backbones while maintaining a single-stage global retrieval pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple, interpretable, single-stage method that outperforms two-stage reranking pipelines by 30× in inference speed while achieving higher recall, making VPR viable on resource-constrained platforms. The finding that fixed learnable queries outperform input-derived self-attention queries for aggregation challenges the prevailing assumption that input-adaptive attention is necessary for discriminative feature pooling. Open questions include whether the spatial information in the final encoder output (X^L) can be exploited for lightweight reranking, and why deeper backbones (ResNet-101) did not yield gains, possibly due to training batch-size constraints.
