## Problem

Existing VIO methods lack the ability to generalize across different environments and sensor conditions: classic optimization-based systems rely on hand-crafted visual features and random-walk IMU bias modeling that fail under challenging scenarios, while end-to-end learning-based VIO methods suffer from poor generalization in complex motion. No prior system simultaneously integrates learned visual association and IMU bias estimation into a VIO pipeline with the ability to adapt online to unseen environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240506/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes monocular RGB image frames and synchronized IMU measurements (accelerometer and gyroscope), with IMU preintegration intervals aligned to image frame timestamps and bias assumed fixed within each interval. It outputs a metric-scale camera pose trajectory, per-feature depth estimates, and per-timestep IMU bias predictions. The visual network requires pre-training on TartanAir, while the IMU bias network is trained from scratch during online continual learning, and the factor graph maintains a sliding window of 10 keyframes with a maximum 3-frame gap between consecutive keyframes.

## Method

Adaptive VIO replaces traditional optical flow matching with a CNN-based visual correspondence predictor (borrowing architecture from DPVO but without hidden-state composition) and replaces random-walk IMU bias modeling with a GRU-based bias predictor, while retaining classic visual-inertial bundle adjustment (VIBA) solved via Gauss-Newton for state estimation. A key feedback mechanism feeds the optimized poses and velocities back to construct self-supervised losses: a photometric reprojection loss for the visual predictor and preintegration residual losses (position, velocity, rotation) for the IMU bias predictor, enabling gradient backpropagation through the differentiable preintegration chain. Online continual learning is embedded within the VIO loop itself—the system acts as an automatic dataloader, performing tracking and gradient updates simultaneously on each incoming frame, with the visual predictor updated every 100 iterations and the IMU predictor every iteration, allowing the system to start/stop adaptation without interrupting odometry.

## Results

On the EuRoC dataset, Adaptive VIO achieves an average RMSE ATE of 0.07 m (SE(3)-aligned), outperforming all learning-based VIO methods (SelfVIO: 0.15 m, iSLAM: 0.58 m) and most classic optimization-based methods (VINS-Mono: 0.17 m, VI-DSO: 0.08 m), while remaining comparable to the best classic method DM-VIO (0.06 m). On the more challenging TUM-VI handheld dataset, the method achieves 0.867 m average RMSE ATE, beating VINS-Mono (1.099 m) and OKVIS stereo (0.949 m) but trailing DM-VIO (0.634 m). Online continual learning on a single sequence (MH_01 / room1) yields over 10% ATE improvement across all test sequences when both visual and IMU networks are jointly adapted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adaptive VIO (0.07 m avg) outperforms all learning-based baselines on EuRoC: SelfVIO (0.15 m) by 2.1×, iSLAM (0.58 m) by 8.3×, and DPVO (used as reference in the online-adaptation ablation, 0.114 m avg on MH sequences) by ~40%. Among classic methods, it beats VINS-Mono (0.17 m), OKVIS (0.23 m), ROVIO (0.23 m), MSCKF (0.34 m), Kimera stereo (0.13 m), Online VIO (0.13 m), and VI-DSO (0.08 m). The only method it does not surpass is DM-VIO (0.06 m on EuRoC, 0.634 m on TUM-VI), which benefits from more robust initialization and long-term scale refinement. On TUM-VI, Adaptive VIO (0.867 m) still beats VINS-Mono (1.099 m) and OKVIS stereo (0.949 m) but trails DM-VIO by ~27%. Notably, the method uses only monocular + IMU sensors yet outperforms stereo-inertial systems Kimera and iSLAM on EuRoC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two VIO paradigms: classic optimization-based systems (VINS-Mono, DM-VIO) that use hand-crafted features and random-walk bias models, and end-to-end learning-based VIO (SelfVIO, DeepVIO) that replace the entire pipeline with networks. It borrows the visual correspondence architecture from DPVO and the preintegration/factor-graph formulation from VINS-Mono, but departs from both by using neural networks only for targeted sub-tasks (visual matching and IMU bias prediction) while retaining a full visual-inertial bundle adjustment backbone. The key conceptual departure from prior learning-optimization hybrids (DROID-SLAM, iSLAM) is the simultaneous integration of learned visual association and learned IMU bias, plus the use of optimization residuals as a self-supervised feedback signal for online continual learning—something not done in prior online-adaptation VO/VIO work (Li et al., Vödisch et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: VIO systems that are pre-trained on one dataset often degrade on unseen environments, and the online continual learning mechanism here (optimization feedback → self-supervised loss → network update) offers a deployment-friendly adaptation path without requiring separate training infrastructure. However, the performance gains are modest (comparable to but not clearly exceeding DM-VIO on TUM-VI), the IMU bias network requires 30 epochs of visual-only BA before it can be trained, and the method has only been validated on two relatively small indoor/semi-outdoor benchmarks. Open questions include whether the feedback mechanism scales to longer sequences or more diverse sensor configurations, and whether the computational overhead of per-frame gradient backpropagation is acceptable for real-time UAV or handheld deployment.
