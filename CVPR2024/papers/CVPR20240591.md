## Problem

Existing action anticipation methods treat the (verb, noun) prediction as a single classification task, so if either component is difficult to predict due to limited visual cues, the entire action prediction fails. The underlying dependencies between verbs and nouns—where knowing one makes the other substantially easier to predict—are not explicitly exploited, and the predictive uncertainty inherent in future action estimation is largely unmodeled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240591/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video frame sequence up to time t, processed by a pretrained backbone (MViT-b for EK100, TSN for EGTEA Gaze+, I3D for 50-Salads) into per-frame feature vectors; optical flow and object detection features can be concatenated at each time step as additional modalities. The output is a joint (verb, noun) action prediction at a future time t + t_f, produced by separately predicting verb and noun through two parallel streams and then fusing them with uncertainty-weighted coefficients, followed by a post-processing step that selects the most probable valid (verb, noun) pair from the dataset's known combinations.

## Method

UADT decouples action anticipation into a verb-to-noun (VtN) model and a noun-to-verb (NtV) model, each comprising a probabilistic Transformer encoder and a decoder with causal self-attention and cross-attention layers. The encoders replace their final feed-forward layers with Gaussian probabilistic layers (using the reparameterization trick) to sample N parameter sets and quantify epistemic uncertainty via the variance of prediction entropies. An uncertainty mask inversely proportional to epistemic uncertainty is applied to encoder embeddings before cross-attention, filtering unreliable or redundant information. The two streams are fused dynamically: each stream's prediction is weighted by the sigmoid of the other stream's uncertainty, so the more confident prediction dominates. A novel top-K cross-entropy loss (K=5) is used for encoder training to tolerate erroneous top-1 predictions, and a two-stage training strategy first trains encoders with a combined top-K and feature-anticipation loss, then trains decoders with a combined next-action, feature, and historical verb/noun loss.

## Results

On EPIC-KITCHENS-100 (EK100) validation with K700 RGB features, UADT achieves 38.2% verb, 41.4% noun, and 20.3% action Top-5 Recall, outperforming the previous SOTA RAFTformer (33.7/37.1/18.0) by 4.5/4.3/2.3 points respectively. With additional optical flow and object modalities, UADT further improves to 43.5/46.6/23.0. On EGTEA Gaze+, UADT reaches 68.4% Top-5 Recall (vs. 63.5% for RAFTformer), and on 50-Salads it achieves 62.7% Top-1 Accuracy (vs. 59.6% for Latent-goal).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UADT outperforms all 13 baselines across all three benchmarks. On EK100, UADT (K700, RGB) surpasses RAFTformer [25] by 2.3 points in action Top-5 Recall (20.3 vs. 18.0) and MeMViT [61] by 2.6 points (20.3 vs. 17.7), while also exceeding the multi-modal RAFTformer-2B (19.1) using only RGB. On EGTEA Gaze+, UADT (68.4%) beats RAFTformer (63.5%) by 4.9 points, RED [23] (54.6%) by 13.8 points, and RULSTM [20] (58.6%) by 9.8 points. On 50-Salads, UADT (62.7%) exceeds Latent-goal [48] (59.6%) by 3.1 points, AVT [26] (48.0%) by 14.7 points, and ActionBanks/TempAgg [50] (40.7%) by 22.0 points. The consistent margin over RAFTformer across all three datasets (2.3, 4.9, and 9.5 points) highlights the benefit of the verb-noun decoupling and uncertainty-aware fusion over the single-stream transformer design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transformer-based action anticipation lineage (AVT, RAFTformer, MeMViT) but departs from the standard formulation of treating action prediction as a single (verb, noun) classification. Instead, it decouples the task into two complementary sub-tasks—verb-to-noun and noun-to-verb—each using a Transformer encoder-decoder with cross-attention, and introduces a probabilistic extension (Gaussian layers with reparameterization, following Kendall & Gal) to quantify epistemic uncertainty for feature selection and prediction fusion. The top-K cross-entropy loss is a novel training objective designed to make encoders robust to top-1 errors before the decoder stage, and the uncertainty-based dynamic fusion replaces the post-hoc decomposition used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The verb-noun decoupling addresses a fundamental fragility in action anticipation: a single mispredicted component invalidates the entire action label, and the decoupled formulation converts a joint prediction into a conditional one that is inherently easier. The uncertainty-aware mechanism provides a principled, input-dependent way to weight which stream's prediction to trust, which is particularly relevant given the intra-class ambiguity inherent in anticipating unseen future actions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to long-term (multi-step) anticipation, whether LLM-based verb-noun dependency modeling (mentioned as future work) can replace the learned cross-attention, and whether the two-stage training cost is justified over end-to-end training in resource-constrained settings.
