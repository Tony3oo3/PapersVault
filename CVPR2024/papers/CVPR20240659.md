## Problem

Existing renal pathology segmentation methods focus on single tissue types or structures at similar scales, requiring architecture modifications when new classes are added and failing to model the intricate spatial relationships (subset/superset, mutual exclusion) among objects spanning regions, functional units, and cells. No prior deep learning approach achieves comprehensive panoramic segmentation across all three anatomical levels simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240659/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes partially labeled renal pathology image patches at varying resolutions (1024×1024 at 5× for regions, 256×256 at 5×/10× for functional units, 512×512 at 20× for cells), each conditioned on a class index and magnification level. Because annotations are partial—only one tissue type is labeled per image—the model must infer spatial constraints for unlabeled classes. Output is a set of 8 binary segmentation masks (multi-label), one per anatomical class, enabling simultaneous prediction of overlapping structures like glomerular capsule and tuft.

## Method

PrPSeg combines three components: (1) a universal proposition matrix (n×n) encoding anatomical relationships via Aristotle's Universal Affirmation (subset) and Universal Negation (mutual exclusion), which is extendable as new classes are added; (2) a token-based dynamic head built on a Residual-U-Net backbone, where dimensionally-stable class-aware and scale-aware tokens are injected into each encoder block and a 2D convolutional fusion block maps the combined GAP feature plus token slices to parameters of a lightweight 162-parameter dynamic segmentation head, keeping the architecture fixed during class expansion; (3) an anatomy loss that operates as online semi-supervised learning—using the proposition matrix to penalize violations of spatial constraints between the supervised label for class i and the unsupervised prediction for class j (e.g., Dice loss on the complement when i ⊇ j, Dice loss on overlap when i ∩ j = ∅). Training proceeds in two phases: 50 epochs of pure supervised learning, followed by joint supervised + anatomy-loss optimization with λ_upl = 0.1.

## Results

On the 8-class panoramic renal pathology segmentation benchmark (spanning regions, functional units, and cells), PrPSeg achieves a mean Dice of 78.66%, outperforming the strongest baseline Omni-Seg (76.66%) by 2.0 points. The largest gains appear at the functional-unit level (e.g., PT: 85.27% vs. 81.48% for Omni-Seg; Tufts: 94.40% vs. 92.36%) and the cell level (Podocyte: 70.98% vs. 67.28%). PrPSeg also substantially surpasses Transformer-based baselines such as SegFormer (65.19%) and Swin-Unetr (62.38%), and CNN-based multi-class methods like DeepLabV3 (60.84%) and U-Nets (47.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrPSeg outperforms all nine baselines on the mean Dice across 8 classes. Against the closest competitor Omni-Seg (76.66%), PrPSeg wins on 7 of 8 individual classes, with the only loss being a marginal 0.58-point deficit on Medulla (72.38 vs. 72.96); the largest per-class margins are on PT (+3.79), Podocyte (+3.70), and Mesangial cell (+1.65). Among Transformer-based methods, SegFormer (65.19%) and Swin-Unetr (62.38%) trail PrPSeg by 13.5 and 16.3 points respectively, while Unetr (57.79%) and Segmenter (60.18%) are even further behind. CNN-based multi-class baselines show the weakest performance: U-Nets (47.58%) and DeepLabV3 (60.84%) struggle particularly at the region level (Medulla scores of 23.86% and 41.70%), and Multi-kidney (60.98%) and Residual-U-Net (63.54%) also underperform on Medulla (13.13% and 13.21%), highlighting the difficulty of capturing spatial subset/superset relationships without the proposed anatomy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

PrPSeg builds directly on Omni-Seg [11], reusing its Residual-U-Net backbone and dynamic-head paradigm, but departs from it in two key ways: replacing the triple outer-product controller with a token-based fusion mechanism (class-aware and scale-aware tokens inspired by ViT) and introducing a universal proposition matrix derived from Aristotle's categorical logic to encode anatomical subset/superset/mutual-exclusion relationships. It also extends the line of dynamic neural network work (DoDNet, Omni-Seg) by showing that partially labeled, multi-scale pathology data can be handled within a single stable architecture that accommodates class growth without structural modification. Conceptually, the paper repurposes formal logic (universal affirmation/negation) as a loss-function design tool, a framing not seen in prior pathology segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly segment renal pathology across three anatomical levels (regions, functional units, cells) in a single network, addressing a practical gap where pathologists must currently run separate pipelines per scale. The proposition-matrix-to-loss translation offers a generalizable template for injecting hierarchical domain knowledge into segmentation training, potentially applicable to other organs with known anatomical taxonomies. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baseline (Omni-Seg) are modest (~2 Dice points average), the evaluation is confined to a single 8-class renal dataset, and it remains unclear whether the anatomy loss generalizes to non-hierarchical or more complex anatomical relationships. Open questions include scalability to gigapixel WSI-level inference and applicability beyond kidney pathology.
