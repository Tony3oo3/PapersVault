## Problem

Existing perception-enhanced vision-language models (P-VLMs) represent continuous visual perception signals (bounding boxes, segmentation masks) as sequences of discrete tokens, which introduces unavoidable precision errors, requires many redundant tokens (e.g., 30+ for a mask contour), and forces full-parameter training with enormous computational cost. No prior end-to-end approach could simultaneously achieve accurate perception, flexible signal handling, and efficient training within a single LLM framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242166/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed by a ViT encoder) and a text instruction, and can additionally receive a bounding box or segmentation mask as an input perception signal. Outputs include free-form text responses, bounding boxes, and pixel-level segmentation masks. The key structural detail is that each perception signal—whether box or mask of arbitrary shape—is encoded into and decoded from a single dynamic token embedding (<vis>) rather than a sequence of discrete coordinate tokens, making the I/O representation compact and continuous.

## Method

PerceptionGPT introduces a special <vis> token whose embedding is dynamic (context-dependent) rather than static, allowing one token to carry arbitrary perception information. Lightweight task-specific encoders (3-layer MLP for boxes; ResNet + linear layer for masks) map perception signals into the LLM's embedding space, while corresponding decoders (MLP for boxes; 2-layer bidirectional transformer for masks) reconstruct them. Training combines standard autoregressive language modeling loss with task-specific objectives (L1 + GIoU for boxes, BCE + DICE for masks), enabling the LLM to learn when to emit <vis> while the decoders learn precise signal reconstruction. A multi-layer ViT feature fusion strategy learns adaptive per-layer weights to balance semantic and fine-grained visual information. The entire system is trained with LoRA (rank 32) on only ~4% of parameters, achieving competitive performance with less than 25% of the training time required by prior methods like Shikra.

## Results

On referring expression comprehension (REC), PerceptionGPT-13B achieves 89.17/93.20/85.96 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 83.72/89.19/75.31 on RefCOCO+, and 84.13/85.20 on RefCOCOg, outperforming Shikra-13B (87.83/91.11/81.81) with only 4% trainable parameters. On referring expression segmentation (RES), PerceptionGPT-13B reaches 75.3/79.1/72.1 on RefCOCO and 70.7/71.9 on RefCOCOg, surpassing the two-stage LISA (74.9/79.1/72.3) that relies on SAM as an external expert. On conventional VQA and captioning tasks, PerceptionGPT-7B scores 85.1 on VQAv2 and 123.2 CIDEr on COCO, exceeding Shikra (83.3 and 117.5 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest generalist competitor Shikra, PerceptionGPT-7B outperforms Shikra-7B by 1.58/1.90/4.36 points on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, and PerceptionGPT-13B beats Shikra-13B by 1.34/2.09/4.15 on the same splits, while using only 4% trainable parameters (LoRA rank 32 vs. full fine-tuning). Compared to the specialist SOTA G-DINO-L, PerceptionGPT-13B remains within 1.4 points on RefCOCO val (89.17 vs. 90.56) and 2.3 points on RefCOCO testB (85.96 vs. 88.24), demonstrating competitive grounding without a dedicated detection head. On RES, PerceptionGPT-13B (75.3/79.1/72.1 on RefCOCO) marginally surpasses the two-stage LISA (74.9/79.1/72.3) that depends on SAM, and significantly outperforms all end-to-end baselines such as ReLA (73.8/76.5/70.2) and LAVT (72.7/75.8/68.8) by 1.5–3.3 points. On conventional VQA and captioning, PerceptionGPT-7B exceeds Shikra (85.1 vs. 83.3 on VQAv2; 123.2 vs. 117.5 CIDEr on COCO) and BLIP-2 (85.1 vs. 65.2 on VQAv2), confirming that the parameter-efficient training with dynamic token embeddings preserves the LLM's original language capabilities better than full fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in perception-enhanced vision-language models (P-VLMs) such as Shikra, Kosmos-2, and VisionLLM, which encode perception signals (bounding boxes, masks) as sequences of discrete tokens within the LLM vocabulary. Instead, PerceptionGPT repurposes the LLM's high-dimensional token embedding space as a continuous representation channel, using a single dynamic <vis> token whose embedding is populated by lightweight perception encoders and decoded by task-specific decoders. The multi-layer ViT feature fusion draws on the FPN-inspired layer-wise aggregation idea from detection literature, and the parameter-efficient training (LoRA) follows the broader trend of reducing fine-tuning cost in VLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that PerceptionGPT achieves competitive REC/RES performance with only 4% trainable parameters and ~25% of Shikra's training time, making perception-capable VLLMs substantially more accessible to resource-constrained groups. The single-token representation also yields a 20–200× inference speedup over discrete formulations for mask decoding, which matters for real-time applications. Open questions include whether the dynamic embedding approach generalizes cleanly to higher-dimensional perception signals (depth maps, 3D pose) as the authors claim, and whether the performance gap to specialist detectors (e.g., G-DINO-L) can be closed without abandoning the end-to-end formulation.
