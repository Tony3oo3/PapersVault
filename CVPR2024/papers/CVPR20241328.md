## Problem

Existing floorplan localization methods (LaLaLoc, LaLaLoc++, LASER, PF-net) assume upright camera poses or panorama images, limiting deployment on handheld and head-mounted devices. Additionally, the repetitive structures in floorplans (corners, walls) create severe localization ambiguity that single-frame methods cannot resolve, and integrating accurate single-frame observations into a high-frequency sequential filter remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241328/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a short window of perspective RGB images (108° horizontal FOV) with known relative poses, camera intrinsics, and gravity direction from an IMU, alongside a 2D occupancy floorplan (no semantic labels). Internally, it predicts a 1D equiangular ray scan representing depth to the floorplan boundary, which is compared against precomputed ray features for each candidate pose. The output is a full SE(2) posterior over the floorplan (x, y, orientation at 0.1 m × 0.1 m × 10° resolution), from which the best pose and its uncertainty are extracted by lookup.

## Method

The observation model predicts floorplan depth via two complementary branches: a monocular ResNet+Attention network outputting a depth-hypothesis probability distribution, and a multiview module that extracts column features, gathers them via plane sweeping into a reference frame, computes cross-view feature variance as a 2D cost volume, and filters it with a U-Net-like network to yield a depth distribution. A learned MLP softly fuses the two distributions based on relative poses and mean depths, exploiting monocular robustness at small baselines and multiview scale accuracy at larger baselines. A virtual roll-pitch augmentation (homography-based masking during training) eliminates the upright-camera assumption. The sequential filter is an SE(2) histogram filter where the ego-motion transition is decomposed into per-orientation 2D Gaussian translation filters applied as grouped convolution (one group per orientation bin) followed by a 1D rotation filter along the orientation axis, enabling efficient GPU implementation at 27 Hz on consumer hardware.

## Results

On the sequential localization task (Gibson(t)), the full system (Ours_f) achieves a 94.6% success rate at 1 m and an RMSE of 0.12 m on succeeded runs, compared to 59.5% and 0.39 m for LASER. For single-frame observation on Gibson(g), the fused monocular+multiview model (Ours_f) reaches 44.5% recall at 1 m, nearly 4× the 11.8% of LASER and 8× the 5.6% of PF-net. The histogram filter runs at 0.037 s per iteration (27 Hz on an RTX 3070 Ti), which is 45% faster than a particle filter (0.067 s) and 6.5× faster than LASER with a histogram filter (0.241 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art floorplan localization methods: PF-net [20] and LASER [28]. Against PF-net, the proposed method wins decisively on every metric: on Gibson(f) the monocular model achieves 36.6% R@1 m versus PF-net's 6.9%, and on Gibson(g) the fused model reaches 44.5% versus 5.6%. PF-net is also impractically slow for filtering (2.375 s per matching step), so it cannot be integrated into a sequential filter at all. Against LASER, the advantage is similarly large: 44.5% vs 11.8% R@1 m on Gibson(g) for single-frame, and 94.6% vs 59.5% success rate for sequential localization. In the sequential setting, the full system reduces RMSE from 1.96 m (LASER, all runs) to 0.51 m, a 74% improvement. The timing comparison shows the proposed histogram filter iterates in 0.037 s versus 0.241 s for LASER with the same filter, a 6.5× speedup, while the particle filter variant of the proposed method (0.067 s) is still 45% faster than LASER's particle filter (0.287 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the floorplan localization line (LaLaLoc, LaLaLoc++, LASER, PF-net) by replacing their 2D-depth, point-cloud, or map-patch representations with a compact 1D equiangular ray scan that is invariant to rotation and translation. For sequential filtering, it departs from PF-net's particle filter by formulating the SE(2) histogram filter's transition as grouped convolution over orientation bins, building on the histogram filter concept of Jonschkowski & Brock but generalizing it from R² to SE(2). The multiview depth module adapts the plane-sweeping cost-volume architecture of P-MVSNet/DeepTAM to column-level features, and a learned selection network fuses monocular and multiview predictions based on relative pose degeneracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The system is notable for practical deployability: it runs at 27 Hz on a laptop GPU, requires only consumer RGB cameras and IMU data, handles non-upright poses via virtual roll-pitch augmentation, and needs no per-map retraining or large image database. Key limitations include the absence of a real-world dataset with both sequential observations and floorplans (the main evaluation is in iGibson simulation), the lack of semantic cues (doors, windows) that could further disambiguate repetitive layouts, and the domain gap between simulated and real scenes. Open questions include whether the 1D ray representation and SE(2) histogram filter generalize to multi-floor or outdoor floorplan settings, and whether incorporating semantic information from both image and map would close the remaining ambiguity gap.
