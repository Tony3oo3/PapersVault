## Problem

High-resolution fisheye images from automotive cameras are highly distorted when projected onto flat rectangular grids, causing projection and distortion losses that degrade performance in safety-critical tasks like autonomous driving. Existing spherical models relying on Driscoll-Healy grids suffer from non-uniform sampling, cubic scaling of Fourier-domain tensors, and inefficiency when data covers only half the sphere. No prior work treats automotive fisheye images as native spherical signals with an efficient transformer architecture.

## Input / Output

The model consumes fisheye RGB images (or RGB-D for indoor scenes) along with camera calibration data, which are projected onto a HEALPix grid restricted to 8 of 12 base quadrilaterals to cover approximately half the sphere. The input is represented as a one-dimensional list in nested HEALPix ordering rather than a 2D pixel array, and the UNet-like architecture produces outputs at the same spatial resolution as the input: per-pixel segmentation masks, per-pixel depth values, or a single class label. For depth estimation, predictions are converted to 3D point clouds using spherical angles from HEALPix pixel positions and evaluated via Chamfer distance against ground-truth point clouds.

## Method

HEAL-SWIN exploits the nested hierarchical structure of the HEALPix grid to implement SWIN transformer operations with minimal overhead: patching joins n_patch consecutive pixels in nested ordering, windowing groups n_win consecutive patches, and patch merging/expansion operates on blocks of 4^k consecutive entries. Two shifting strategies distribute information globally—grid shifting (translating along quadrilateral axes with boundary masking at base-pixel collisions) and spiral shifting (rolling the ring-ordered list by n_shift pixels, introducing mild polar distortion but no interior boundary effects). A UNet-like encoder-decoder with skip connections is used for dense prediction tasks, and a relative position bias is computed from the approximately rectangular sub-grid within each base quadrilateral. The architecture requires no Fourier transforms, enabling efficient handling of high-resolution data and partial-sphere coverage at the same inference speed as the flat SWIN baseline.

## Results

On the Stanford 2D-3D-S indoor fisheye segmentation benchmark, HEAL-SWIN achieves 44.3 mIoU and 61.9 mAcc, outperforming all compared spherical models (best competitor: HexRUNet at 43.3 mIoU / 58.6 mAcc). On real-world WoodScape automotive fisheye images, HEAL-SWIN reaches 0.628 spherical mIoU versus 0.617 for the flat SWIN baseline. On the synthetic SynWoodScape Large+AD subset (12 classes including pedestrians and traffic signs), HEAL-SWIN scores 0.841 mIoU versus 0.809 for SWIN. For depth estimation on SynWoodScape, HEAL-SWIN produces point clouds with lower Chamfer distance to ground truth than SWIN, confirming superior 3D representation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEAL-SWIN is compared against the flat SWIN transformer (same architecture, rectangular input) and five spherical CNN baselines on Stanford 2D-3D-S. Against the flat SWIN baseline, HEAL-SWIN wins on every segmentation dataset tested: 0.628 vs 0.617 mIoU on real WoodScape, 0.841 vs 0.809 on SynWoodScape Large+AD, and 0.947 vs 0.918 on Large SynWoodScape, with inference time essentially identical (~297 ns/pixel). On the Stanford 2D-3D-S benchmark, HEAL-SWIN (44.3 mIoU) outperforms all five spherical CNN baselines: Gauge CNN (39.4), UGSCNN (38.8), SphCNN (40.2), Spin-SphCNN (41.9), and HexRUNet (43.3), with the largest margin over UGSCNN (+5.5 mIoU) and the smallest over HexRUNet (+1.0 mIoU). For depth estimation, HEAL-SWIN achieves lower Chamfer distance to ground-truth point clouds than SWIN, indicating better learned 3D geometry. The paper also notes that HEAL-SWIN benefits more from larger training sets than SWIN, with the performance gap widening as dataset size increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines the SWIN transformer's hierarchical windowed self-attention with the HEALPix spherical grid from astrophysics, exploiting the structural alignment between HEALPix's nested quadrilateral subdivisions and SWIN's patch-merging hierarchy to perform patching, windowing, and shifting natively on the sphere. It departs from prior spherical CNNs (SphCNN, Spin-SphCNN, UGSCNN) that rely on Fourier transforms over the Driscoll-Healy grid or icosahedral sampling, which scale poorly with resolution and require full-sphere coverage. The two novel shifting strategies (grid-aligned and spiral) are specific to the HEALPix structure and have no direct analogue in flat SWIN or prior spherical transformer work such as the Spherical Transformer [7] or SWPT [25].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that treating fisheye automotive images as distortion-free spherical signals yields consistent gains in both 2D segmentation and 3D depth estimation over flat representations, with the added practical benefit of handling half-sphere data without wasting computation on unused grid regions. Key open questions remain: the model is not rotationally equivariant (a significant gap for segmentation/depth tasks), the grid boundary at base-pixel edges leaves image corners uncovered, and the UNet-style decoder is acknowledged as suboptimal. The finding that HEAL-SWIN scales better with dataset size than flat SWIN suggests the spherical inductive bias becomes more valuable as data grows, which is relevant for the growing availability of large-scale fisheye driving datasets.
