## Problem

3D diffusion models produce geometrically consistent 3D assets but suffer from limited quality and generalization because trainable 3D data is scarce and expensive. Conversely, 2D diffusion models excel at fine detail and diverse prompt handling but struggle to maintain 3D consistency across views. The paper asks how to combine the strengths of both model families into a single fast pipeline that yields high-fidelity, 3D-consistent assets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241449/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (positions, covariances, colors, opacities) that is directly renderable in real time at 512×512 without conversion to mesh. For the text-to-3D path, the prompt drives both Shape-E/Point-E (initialization) and Stable Diffusion 2.1 (refinement); for the avatar path, the prompt is simplified to motion-relevant phrases to drive MDM, and the resulting SMPL pose is textured via the 2D diffusion model. Rendering is performed at 1024×1024 but the SDS optimization operates at 512×512, and the full 1200-iteration training completes in ~15 minutes on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

GaussianDreamer bridges 3D and 2D diffusion models through 3D Gaussian Splatting as the shared explicit representation. A 3D diffusion model (Shape-E for objects, MDM for avatars) generates a coarse mesh or SMPL pose, which is converted to a point cloud; two augmentation operations—noisy point growing (uniform sampling within the bounding box, retaining points within a 0.01 normalized distance via KDTree) and color perturbation (adding random offsets in [0, 0.2])—densify and diversify the initial Gaussians. These augmented points initialize the 3D Gaussians (opacity 0.1, covariance from nearest-neighbor distance), which are then refined by 1200 iterations of Score Distillation Sampling against a frozen 2D diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2.1, guidance scale 100). The key insight is that the geometry priors inherited from both the 3D diffusion model's output and the point-cloud-like structure of 3D Gaussians dramatically accelerate convergence, reducing generation time from hours (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer) to 15 minutes while achieving the best T³-Bench average score (45.7) among compared methods.

## Results

On T³ Bench, GaussianDreamer achieves the best average score of 45.7, outperforming ProlificDreamer (43.3), Magic3D (32.7), and DreamFusion (21.7). It leads on Single Object (54.0) and Single with Surroundings (48.6) categories, while ProlificDreamer retains a slight edge on Multi Object (35.8 vs. 34.5). Critically, GaussianDreamer completes generation in 15 minutes on a single GPU, compared to 6–10 hours for DreamFusion, Fantasia3D, and ProlificDreamer, and 5.3 hours for Magic3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianDreamer outperforms all six T³ Bench baselines on the average score, with the closest competitor being ProlificDreamer (45.7 vs. 43.3 average). On Single Object and Single with Surroundings, GaussianDreamer leads ProlificDreamer by +4.6 and +3.8 points respectively, but trails by 1.3 points on Multi Object (34.5 vs. 35.8), suggesting the 3D diffusion initialization is less effective for complex multi-object scenes. Compared to Magic3D and DreamFusion, the gains are substantial (+13.0 and +24.0 average points) while reducing generation time by 21× and 24× respectively. Against LatentNeRF, which shares the same 15-minute runtime, GaussianDreamer improves the average by 17.6 points, demonstrating that the 3D diffusion initialization is the key differentiator. For 3D avatar generation (Figure 6), GaussianDreamer achieves comparable visual quality to DreamAvatar, DreamWaltz, and AvatarVerse while being 4–24× faster, and uniquely supports arbitrary body poses via the text-to-motion initialization pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianDreamer sits at the intersection of two established text-to-3D streams: 3D pretrained diffusion models (Shape-E, Point-E, MDM) that provide geometric priors but lack fine detail, and 2D diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) that produce rich appearance but struggle with 3D consistency. The paper's core architectural choice is to use 3D Gaussian Splatting as the shared representation that both model families can interact with, adopting DreamFusion's SDS loss for the 2D-diffusion refinement stage. The specific contributions over concurrent 3DGS-based text-to-3D work (DreamGaussian, GS-GEN) are the explicit 3D-diffusion-model initialization step and the noisy point growing / color perturbation operations that densify and diversify the initial Gaussian set before SDS optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15-minute single-GPU generation time (21–24× faster than DreamFusion-class methods) combined with competitive T³-Bench scores makes high-quality text-to-3D generation practically accessible, and the real-time renderability of the 3DGS output removes the mesh-conversion bottleneck. However, the authors acknowledge that edges remain soft, multi-face artifacts can still appear in front-back asymmetric objects, and large-scale scene generation is out of scope. The broader open question is whether 3D-aware diffusion models can fully replace the SDS refinement loop, potentially eliminating the remaining consistency gaps while preserving the speed advantage of explicit representations.
