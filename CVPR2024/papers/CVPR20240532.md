## Problem

Existing text-to-video diffusion models such as AnimateDiff cannot directly produce satisfactory 360° panoramic videos because equirectangular projections (ERPs) differ from standard videos in three fundamental ways: they require a 360°×180° field of view, their motion patterns follow curved trajectories rather than straight lines, and their left and right edges must be spatially continuous. No prior diffusion-based method specifically targets panoramic video generation, and no large-scale captioned panoramic video dataset exists to train such a model.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt and an optional 5D optical flow tensor (batch × channels × frames × height × width) computed by PanoFlow as motion guidance; during training, the motion input is zeroed with probability 0.2 so the model can also generate videos without explicit motion conditioning. Output is a 16-frame 360° panoramic video in ERP format at 512×1024 resolution, decoded from latent space via a frozen VAE. The framework is plug-and-play across multiple personalized Stable Diffusion models (e.g., Realistic Vision, Lyriel, ToonYou, RCNZ Cartoon), inheriting each model's style while adding panoramic video capability.

## Method

360DVD freezes a pre-trained AnimateDiff U-Net (Stable Diffusion v1.5 + Motion Module v14) and inserts a lightweight 360-Adapter that downsamples the motion condition via pixel unshuffle, extracts multi-scale features through four blocks of 2D convolutions and pseudo-3D residual blocks (1×3×3 spatial + 3×1×1 temporal convolutions), and adds the resulting features to the U-Net encoder at four resolution levels. Three 360 Enhancement Techniques enforce panoramic structure: a latitude-aware loss that up-weights low-latitude pixels via a cosine weight matrix to counter ERP polar distortion, a latent rotation mechanism that cyclically shifts the left edge to the right at each denoising step (θ=π/2) for macroscopic wraparound coherence, and a circular padding mechanism applied only in the final half of DDIM steps for pixel-level edge continuity. Training uses the newly constructed WEB360 dataset (~2,114 ERP video clips at 720p) with captions generated by a 360 Text Fusion pipeline that projects each ERP frame into four 90° perspective views, captions them with BLIP, and fuses the four descriptions via ChatGPT into a single detailed scene summary.

## Results

The paper evaluates 360DVD through a user preference study with 31 participants rating 8 sets of generated videos across five criteria. 360DVD achieves the highest preference on all metrics: 51.6% for graphics quality, 64.5% for frame consistency, 71.8% for end continuity, 67.0% for content distribution, and 74.6% for motion pattern. The largest margins are on panorama-specific criteria, where 360DVD leads native AnimateDiff by over 67 percentage points on end continuity (71.8% vs. 5.3%) and motion pattern (74.6% vs. 4.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 360DVD against three baselines: native AnimateDiff, AnimateDiff augmented with a panoramic LoRA (LatentLabs360), and the LoRA-augmented model further equipped with the proposed 360 Enhancement Techniques (360ET). 360DVD wins decisively on all five user-preference criteria. Against native AnimateDiff, the gap is most dramatic on panorama-specific metrics: end continuity improves from 5.3% to 71.8%, content distribution from 4.8% to 67.0%, and motion pattern from 4.4% to 74.6%. The LoRA baseline (B) slightly improves content distribution (12.1%) but degrades frame consistency (10.5% vs. 15.3%), indicating that simply fine-tuning with panoramic images introduces temporal instability. Adding 360ET to the LoRA model (C) recovers some continuity (16.9%) and content distribution (16.1%) but still lags far behind 360DVD, confirming that the 360-Adapter's learned motion modeling is essential for coherent panoramic video generation rather than just post-hoc enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AnimateDiff + Stable Diffusion pipeline for text-to-video generation by introducing a lightweight 360-Adapter that injects motion-conditioned features into a frozen U-Net, specifically targeting the equirectangular projection (ERP) format of panoramic videos. It departs from prior panoramic generation work—which has focused almost exclusively on static images (OmniDreamer, PanoGen, PanoDiff, StitchDiffusion)—by being the first to address panoramic *video* generation with diffusion models. The 360 Enhancement Techniques (latitude-aware loss, latent rotation, circular padding) are novel contributions that address ERP-specific artifacts (polar distortion, left-right discontinuity) not encountered in standard T2V, and the WEB360 dataset with its 360 Text Fusion captioning pipeline fills a gap in captioned panoramic video data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first practical pipeline for generating 360° panoramic videos from text prompts and optional motion conditions, addressing a real cost barrier in VR content creation. The plug-and-play 360-Adapter design is attractive because it preserves the generative priors of any personalized Stable Diffusion model without retraining the base architecture. However, limitations are notable: generation is restricted to 16 frames at 512×1024, the training set is only ~2,000 clips, and the evaluation relies on a single user study with 31 participants against relatively weak baselines (native AnimateDiff, AnimateDiff+LoRA). Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences and higher resolutions, whether it generalizes beyond ERP to other projection formats (cubemap, pyramidal), and whether the 360-Adapter can be combined with newer T2V backbones beyond AnimateDiff.
