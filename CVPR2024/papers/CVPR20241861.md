## Problem

Existing RGB-D 6DoF pose estimation methods either discard RGB appearance information, rely on computationally expensive rendering-based refinement (ICP, CIR, PFA), or fail to exploit the hierarchical structure of binary surface encodings for iterative outlier removal. HiPose addresses this by establishing 3D-3D correspondences directly from RGB-D input and using a coarse-to-fine hierarchical pruning strategy that eliminates the need for both RANSAC and rendering-based refinement while maintaining state-of-the-art accuracy.

## Input / Output

The network receives a cropped Region of Interest from both the RGB image and the depth map, where the depth is back-projected into a uniformly sampled point cloud. For each 3D point, the network predicts a d-bit binary code (determined by the object mesh vertex count N=2^d) along with a visibility mask. The binary code is split into m coarse bits (selecting an initial sub-surface) and n fine bits (used iteratively for sub-surface partitioning), and the final output is a 6DoF pose (rotation matrix R ∈ SO(3) and translation vector t ∈ R³) obtained through successive Kabsch solves with progressive outlier rejection.

## Method

HiPose extends ZebraPose's hierarchical binary surface encoding from 2D-3D to 3D-3D correspondences by processing RGB-D input through a bidirectional fusion network (FFB6D architecture with ConvNext backbone). The key innovation is a RANSAC-free hierarchical correspondence pruning scheme: the first m bits yield a coarse point-to-surface correspondence whose centroid provides an initial pose via Kabsch; then, for each of the remaining n bits, the current pose transforms the sub-surface, and point-to-surface distances (Eq. 2) are thresholded by their median to reject outliers before the next finer partition. A trust-bit mechanism uses per-bit correctness probabilities (Eq. 1) to adaptively select the starting surface per point, prioritizing finer correspondences where the network is more confident. The entire pipeline is render-free and RANSAC-free, achieving ~0.075s inference per image on an RTX 3090, and is trained exclusively on synthetic PBR data with L1 loss on both the visibility mask and binary code.

## Results

On the BOP Challenge benchmark (synthetic PBR training only), HiPose achieves a mean BOP score of 84.6 across LM-O (79.9), YCB-V (90.7), and T-LESS (83.3), surpassing all refinement-based methods including GDRNPP (84.4) and RADet+PFA (80.8). Crucially, HiPose requires no pose refinement step, running at 0.16 s per image versus 6.37 s for GDRNPP (~40× faster) and 9.04 s for SurfEmb. On LM-O specifically, HiPose outperforms the best RGB-D method DFTr by 11.9% in ADD(-S) recall (89.6% vs 77.7%), and on YCB-V it leads all methods in AUC of ADD(-S) at 95.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiPose wins on the mean BOP score against all six BOP Challenge baselines, most notably beating GDRNPP (84.4) by 0.2 points while being ~40× faster (0.16 s vs 6.37 s). On LM-O, HiPose (79.9) edges out RADet+PFA (79.7) and GDRNPP (77.5), and on YCB-V it matches GDRNPP (90.7 vs 90.6) while substantially outperforming SurfEmb (79.9) and RCVPose (84.3). On T-LESS, GDRNPP (85.2) slightly outperforms HiPose (83.3), the only per-dataset loss. In the per-object LM-O comparison (Table 3), HiPose dominates all RGB-only methods (ZebraPose 76.9, GDR-Net 62.2) and RGB-D methods (DFTr 77.7, FFB6D 66.2, RCVPose 70.2, PR-GCN 65.0) by 11.9–27.4% in ADD(-S) recall. On YCB-V (Table 4), HiPose leads all RGB-D baselines in AUC of ADD(-S) (95.5 vs DFTr 94.4, RCVPose 95.2, FFB6D 92.7) and RGB-only baselines (ZebraPose 85.3, GDR-Net 84.4, SO-Pose 83.9). The ablation confirms that the hierarchical pruning strategy (89.62%) outperforms both raw Kabsch (86.65%) and RANSAC+Kabsch (89.12%), validating the RANSAC-free design.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiPose extends ZebraPose's hierarchical binary surface encoding from the RGB-only 2D-3D correspondence setting to the RGB-D 3D-3D correspondence setting, using FFB6D's bidirectional fusion network as the backbone. The key conceptual departure is replacing the standard RANSAC+Kabsch pipeline with a hierarchical correspondence pruning strategy that iteratively exploits the coarse-to-fine structure of the binary code for outlier removal, and eliminating the rendering-based refinement step (ICP, CIR, PFA) that most prior RGB-D methods rely on. This positions HiPose against the dominant paradigm of 'RGB-only initial pose + expensive depth-based refinement' by arguing that direct RGB-D processing with proper hierarchical decoding is both more accurate and far more efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving SOTA-level accuracy on LM-O and YCB-V without any rendering-based refinement while running ~40× faster than the closest refinement-based competitor (GDRNPP) makes real-time 6DoF pose estimation feasible for robotics and AR applications that previously required multi-second inference. The finding that iterative point-to-surface pruning can analytically replace both RANSAC and rendering-based refinement challenges the field's working assumption that some form of iterative geometric refinement is necessary for high-accuracy pose. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to novel/unseen objects, whether performance holds under more severe real-world sensor noise, and whether the hierarchical pruning can be further accelerated for even tighter real-time constraints.
