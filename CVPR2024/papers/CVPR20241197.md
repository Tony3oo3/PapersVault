## Problem

Ultra-fine-grained visual categorization (Ultra-FGVC) requires distinguishing sub-categories within a single fine-grained class (e.g., soybean cultivars), where inter-class differences are nearly imperceptible and intra-class variation is large. Existing novel class discovery (NCD) methods, designed for generic or fine-grained benchmarks, struggle to transfer discriminative knowledge to such ultra-fine-grained unlabeled classes, and fully annotating UFG images is impractical. This paper introduces UFG-NCD as a new task and proposes a tailored framework to address it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241197/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB leaf images resized to 448×448, split into a labeled set of 99 old classes (5 images per class for NCD, 8 for GCD) and an unlabeled set of disjoint new classes whose count is known a priori. Two augmented views of each image are generated for contrastive learning. The output is a 2048-dim feature embedding per image, from which class labels for unlabeled images are assigned via k-means clustering; evaluation uses classification accuracy for old classes and clustering accuracy (with Hungarian-algorithm permutation) for new classes.

## Method

RAPL combines a Channel-wise Region Alignment (CRA) module with a Semi-supervised Proxy Learning (SemiPL) strategy on a ResNet-50 backbone. CRA partitions the 2048 feature-map channels into H×W spatial groups and applies a per-channel cross-entropy loss that forces each channel to encode its assigned local region, enabling transferable local discriminative features across labeled and unlabeled classes. SemiPL replaces the linear classifier with learnable class-level proxies: proxy-guided supervised learning (PSL) uses a top-k negative mask to suppress spurious high-similarity negative relations inherent to UFG data, plus a proxy regularization loss that maximizes inter-proxy separation; proxy-guided contrastive learning (PCL) augments the contrastive set with both old and new proxies so that unlabeled instances are pulled toward and pushed away from class centers using global distribution guidance. Training proceeds in two phases—pre-training on labeled data with CRA+PSL, then joint fine-tuning on labeled and unlabeled data with CRA+PSL+PCL—followed by k-means for final label assignment.

## Results

On the five SoyAgeing UFG-NCD datasets (R1, R3, R4, R5, R6), RAPL achieves the best overall clustering accuracy (ACC) across all splits, ranging from 50.20% (R6) to 61.01% (R5) on the 'All' metric. The method outperforms the strongest baselines (IIC and rKD) by a minimum of 3.94% (R6) and a maximum of 8.38% (R5) on 'All' ACC. Under the task-aware protocol on unlabeled training splits, gains are even larger, reaching up to 14.19% on SoyAgeing-R4. RAPL also improves on both 'Old' and 'New' class accuracy simultaneously, whereas rKD trades off 'New' performance for 'Old' accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPL outperforms all five NCD baselines on every dataset for the 'All' ACC metric, with the largest margins over the strongest competitors IIC [16] and rKD [8]. Specifically, on SoyAgeing-R5, RAPL reaches 61.01% 'All' ACC versus 52.63% for rKD and 49.60% for IIC, a gap of 8.38% and 11.41% respectively. For 'New' class accuracy, RAPL leads by a wide margin (e.g., 47.27% vs. 37.37% for IIC on R5), while rKD's explicit knowledge distillation of old classes causes it to underperform on 'New' classes despite competitive 'Old' scores (e.g., rKD achieves 75.15% 'Old' on R3 vs. RAPL's 78.59%). The earlier baselines—RankStats IL [10], UNO [7], and ComEx [28]—lag significantly behind, with 'All' ACC typically 10–20 points lower than RAPL, indicating that instance-level or feature-level clustering objectives struggle with the extremely small inter-class variation in UFG images. Under the task-aware protocol (Table 2), RAPL's advantage widens further, gaining up to 14.19% over IIC on SoyAgeing-R4, confirming that the proxy-guided contrastive learning and channel-wise region alignment are particularly effective for discovering novel UFG categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining two previously separate research threads: ultra-fine-grained visual categorization (Ultra-FGVC), which focuses on distinguishing nearly identical sub-categories via local feature learning (e.g., MaskCOV, SPARE), and novel class discovery (NCD), which discovers unlabeled classes from labeled ones of disjoint categories. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes proxy-based deep metric learning (Proxy Anchor Loss, multi-proxy frameworks) as the mechanism for knowledge transfer between labeled and unlabeled ultra-fine-grained classes, and adapts channel-wise local feature alignment from Ultra-FGVC into a transferable representation module. The two-stage pre-train/discover paradigm follows the standard NCD protocol, but the proxy-guided contrastive learning for unlabeled data is a departure from prior NCD methods that rely on instance-level clustering or soft pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: ultra-fine-grained categorization (e.g., soybean cultivar identification for precision agriculture) is infeasible to fully annotate, making semi-supervised discovery a natural and needed paradigm. However, the experimental validation is confined to five soybean leaf datasets from a single domain and a single backbone (ResNet-50), leaving open whether the channel-wise region alignment and proxy learning strategy generalize to other ultra-fine-grained domains (e.g., medical imaging, industrial inspection) or to transformer-based backbones. A key open question is whether the proxy-guided contrastive learning component remains beneficial at larger scale or with more diverse class distributions, and whether the task-aware protocol gains (up to 14%) reflect genuine representation learning or are partly an artifact of the small dataset sizes (5 images per class).
