## Problem

Existing text-driven video editing methods operate under a paradigm where motion and appearance are intricately intertwined, causing the network to either overfit appearance content (failing to capture underlying motion patterns) or focus on motion at the expense of generalizing appearance to diverse creative textual prompts. The paper addresses this by seeking a principled way to disentangle motion dynamics from appearance content within video templates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240074/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video template (uniformly sampled at 32 frames, 480×480 resolution) and an edited text prompt as input, and produces a 32-frame edited video that preserves the template's motion patterns while rendering new appearance content. It is built on Stable Diffusion v1.4 inflated with temporal self-attention layers, fine-tuned per video template for 500 steps on a single A100 GPU. At inference, DDIM inversion of the source video provides structural guidance, and classifier-free guidance (scale w) jointly conditions on both the textual and learned motion prompts.

## Method

CAMEL introduces two synergistic components. First, learnable motion prompts (a single unconditional embedding projected to a 32-token sequence matching CLIP text-embedding dimensions) are optimized via direct gradient descent to summarize motion concepts from the video template, then injected into the diffusion latent space as a motion condition. Second, the Causal Motion-enhanced Attention (CAM-Attn) mechanism employs a learnable causal motion filter (window width σ=11, inspired by Haar wavelet but with optimizable coefficients and a broader receptive field) to decompose intermediate UNet representations along the temporal dimension into high-frequency (motion) and low-frequency (appearance) components. The high-frequency components are enhanced through cross-attention with the motion prompts, then recombined with the low-frequency components to form reweighted latents. During fine-tuning, only the query projections W_Q and the CAMEL module are optimized while key/value projections remain frozen, preserving the pre-trained T2I model's appearance knowledge.

## Results

On the TGVE-DAVIS dataset, CAMEL achieves the highest UMTScore across all four editing tasks (Object: 36.52, Background: 36.60, Style: 34.08, Multiple: 33.92), outperforming the strongest baseline Tune-A-Video by 1.50/1.62/0.77/1.16 points respectively. On TGVE-Videvo, CAMEL similarly leads with UMTScores of 37.03/37.54/35.26/37.36. For frame consistency, CAMEL also achieves the best scores on both datasets (e.g., 95.16 on Background editing for TGVE-DAVIS vs. 92.13 for Tune-A-Video), confirming superior temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMEL consistently outperforms all three baselines on both video-text alignment (UMTScore) and frame consistency across both datasets and all four editing tasks. Against Tune-A-Video [33], the closest competitor, CAMEL gains 1.50–1.62 UMTScore points on TGVE-DAVIS and 1.54–2.39 points on TGVE-Videvo, with the largest margin on Background editing (37.54 vs. 36.15 on Videvo). Text2Video-Zero [14] and ControlVideo [41] trail by a wider margin (3–6 UMTScore points), with ControlVideo being the weakest overall. On frame consistency, CAMEL leads Tune-A-Video by up to 3.03 points (95.16 vs. 92.13 on DAVIS Background) and Text2Video-Zero by up to 2.94 points (97.56 vs. 97.06 on Videvo Background), indicating that the causal motion filter and motion prompts jointly improve temporal coherence beyond what existing methods achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMEL builds directly on Stable Diffusion v1.4 as its base T2I model, following the established paradigm of inflating a 2D UNet with temporal self-attention layers for video generation. It departs from prior text-driven video editing methods (Tune-A-Video, Text2Video-Zero, ControlVideo) that treat motion and appearance as an inseparable joint signal, instead introducing a frequency-based decomposition via a learnable causal motion filter to explicitly disentangle high-frequency motion patterns from low-frequency appearance content. The motion prompts mechanism draws an analogy to how CLIP text embeddings condition the denoising process for static images, extending this conditioning paradigm to the temporal/motion domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-based decoupling-then-reweighting framework offers a principled way to address the core tension in text-driven video editing: preserving motion fidelity while allowing appearance to generalize to new textual prompts. However, the evaluation is limited to 53 short (32-frame, 480×480) videos on a single A100, and the method is tied to Stable Diffusion v1.4, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, higher resolutions, and newer diffusion backbones. The fixed window size (σ=11) of the causal motion filter and the single unconditional motion prompt may limit expressiveness for complex or multi-scale motion patterns.
