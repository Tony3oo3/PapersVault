## Problem

Existing slice-to-volume reconstruction (SVR) methods for MRI require multiple orthogonal slice stacks to cross-regularize motion estimation, making them too slow for time-sensitive applications such as fetal fMRI. Learning-based alternatives either process slices independently without 3D constraints or rely on non-convolutional architectures that scale poorly. The paper asks whether a fully convolutional network can predict dense slice motion from a single stack alone, achieving state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction without multi-stack acquisition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240319/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single stack of 2D MRI slices (sagittal, axial, or coronal) as input, internally represented as 3D slabs with a 4-voxel thickness-to-spacing ratio to handle variable slice spacings. It outputs a dense 3D motion field per slice (sub-voxel accuracy) and a 3D volume reconstruction produced by splatting slice intensities with the predicted motion, followed by a separate interpolation network that fills holes from missed regions. For fetal brains, a single network trained with random Euler-angle rotations handles all slicing directions simultaneously.

## Method

The Splat-Slice U-Net is a 3D fully convolutional network that reinterprets the alternating optimization of classical SVR as a series of splat and slice operations within a U-Net architecture. The downward path extracts 2D features from the slice stack, while the upward path synthesizes 3D volume features by splatting 2D skip features with the current motion estimate; at each resolution level, the sliced 3D features and 2D slice features are jointly convolved to predict residual motion, which is added to the coarser-level motion. Training uses a rigid motion-compensating loss that first aligns predicted and ground-truth motion point clouds via a global rigid transform (solved in closed form through polar decomposition of the unconstrained minimizer) before computing MSE, ensuring only relative inter-slice motion is penalized. A separate fully convolutional interpolation network, trained on (splat volume, true volume) pairs, post-processes the raw reconstruction to fill intensity holes and remove slicing artifacts.

## Results

In the single-stack fetal SVR setting (the paper's primary contribution), the proposed Splat-Slice U-Net achieves a motion endpoint error (EPE) of 1.81±0.40 mm, reducing error by 77.6% relative to SVRnet (8.08±2.35 mm) and 44.6% relative to SVoRT v2 (3.27±0.71 mm). Volume PSNR reaches 23.43±1.40 dB, surpassing SVRnet (16.03 dB) and SVoRT v2 (18.49 dB), while slice PSNR is 23.69±1.39 dB. Inference time is 0.224 s per volume on an RTX 8000, still orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based methods. On adult brain SVR, predicted motion is accurate to less than 1 mm on average across 500 held-out scans from 9 public datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three learning-based SVR baselines: SVRnet [20] (2D CNN predicting per-slice anchor points), PlaneInVol [24] (joint slice prediction with inter-slice attention), and SVoRT [25] (vision transformer for iterative slice-to-volume registration). In the single-stack setting, the proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines: it achieves 1.81 mm EPE versus 8.08 mm for SVRnet (77.6% reduction) and 3.27 mm for SVoRT v2 (44.6% reduction), and its volume PSNR of 23.43 dB exceeds SVoRT v2 (18.49 dB) by nearly 5 dB. SVRnet fails to align slices in most fetal cases, while SVoRT v2 introduces visible spatial distortion from inaccurate slice alignment. The proposed method is slightly slower (0.224 s vs. 0.142 s for SVoRT v2) but still runs in real time. In the 3-stack setting, SVoRT* achieves the best EPE (4.35 mm), but the proposed method's single-stack EPE (1.81 mm) already surpasses even the 3-stack SVoRT result, demonstrating that the FCN formulation eliminates the need for multiple orthogonal stacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the alternating optimization of classical SVR (Rousseau et al. [19]) as a series of splat-and-slice operations, then replaces the iterative numerical procedure with a fully convolutional U-Net that predicts dense slice motion fields in a single forward pass. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to monocular depth estimation—predicting disparity from a single view—while architecturally it borrows the multi-scale residual motion refinement strategy from FCN-based image registration [28–32]. It departs from prior learning-based SVR methods (SVRnet [20], PlaneInVol [24], SVoRT [25]) by predicting dense motion fields rather than per-slice position parameters, and by operating within a convolutional framework that avoids the quadratic scaling of transformer-based inter-slice attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling accurate single-stack SVR, this work removes the multi-stack requirement that has limited SVR to long, time-prohibitive scans, opening the door to time-sensitive applications like fetal fMRI where the subject cannot hold still for multiple acquisition passes. The rigid motion-compensating loss (via polar decomposition) is a practical contribution that addresses a common confound in MRI slice positioning. Key limitations remain: the method is currently restricted to rigid motion (precluding placental or torso reconstruction), is trained on pre-segmented slices, and the interpolation network is a standard U-Net whose design is left largely unexplored. Extending to deformable motion and equivariant interpolation are natural next steps.
