## Problem

Diffusion-based image enhancement methods produce high-quality restorations but require dozens of iterative denoising steps, making them impractical for real-time or industrial use. Conventional predictive and GAN-based approaches are more efficient but lack the robust generative prior needed to handle diverse, unpredictable real-world degradations. The paper addresses how to retain the rich knowledge of a pretrained diffusion model while reducing inference to a handful of steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240477/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image (e.g., blurred, noisy, downsampled, JPEG-compressed face at 512×512 or a general image for super-resolution) and optionally a binary mask for inpainting. All processing occurs in the VQGAN latent space rather than pixel space. The output is a single enhanced high-quality RGB image decoded from the latent representation. A key detail is that the input is first passed through a lightweight initial-stage model (SwinIR) to produce a coarse restoration, which is then concatenated with the noisy intermediate latent to form the spatial condition guiding the flow.

## Method

FlowIE replaces the curved ODE trajectories of diffusion sampling with straight-line paths via conditioned rectified flow, initialized from a Stable Diffusion U-Net and fine-tuned with LoRA on cross-attention layers. Instead of the standard one-to-one noise-to-image mapping, it constructs a many-to-one transport where any Gaussian noise point maps to a fixed HQ image, eliminating the need for expensive diffusion-sampled training pairs. A ControlNet-style branch injects spatial guidance from the coarse restoration, and a zero-convolution layer ensures stable training. For inference, the authors devise Mean Value Sampling: rather than stepping forward with Euler, they identify a midpoint on the transport curve (empirically k=3 out of N=5) where the predicted velocity is parallel to the chord connecting endpoints, yielding visually superior results in only 4 steps—roughly 10× faster than diffusion-based baselines.

## Results

On the synthetic CelebA-Test benchmark for blind face restoration, FlowIE achieves FID 19.81 and IDS 0.69, setting new state-of-the-art on both metrics while maintaining competitive PSNR (21.92), SSIM (0.6005), and LPIPS (0.4367). On real-world datasets, FlowIE obtains FID 38.66 on LFW-Test and 32.41 on WIDER-Test, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based competitor DiffBIR (39.61 and 33.51 respectively). For blind image super-resolution, FlowIE reaches MANIQA 0.5953 on RealSRSet and 0.6087 on Collect-100, surpassing DiffBIR (0.5906 and 0.6022) while running at 2.85 FPS versus DiffBIR's 0.286 FPS — approximately 10× faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowIE outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary quality metrics. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor DiffBIR [23], FlowIE improves CelebA FID from 20.19 to 19.81, IDS from 0.52 to 0.69, LFW FID from 39.61 to 38.66, and WIDER FID from 33.51 to 32.41, while running ~10× faster (2.85 vs 0.29 FPS). Among GAN-based and predictive methods, FlowIE achieves the best FID on all four face datasets (e.g., 38.66 on LFW vs 43.38 for DMDNet [21], 48.41 for RestoreFormer [39], 51.95 for GPEN [41]) and the highest IDS (0.69 vs 0.66 for DMDNet, 0.55 for RestoreFormer). On the BSR task (Table 2), FlowIE surpasses DiffBIR [23] in MANIQA (0.5953 vs 0.5906 on RealSRSet; 0.6087 vs 0.6022 on Collect-100) and all GAN-based baselines (Real-ESRGAN+ [35]: 0.5373, BSRGAN [46]: 0.5638, SwinIR-GAN [22]: 0.5296, FeMaSR [2]: 0.5250), while also outperforming zero-shot diffusion methods DDNM [36] (0.4539) and GDP [10] (0.4583) by a wide margin. FlowIE's inference speed (2.85 FPS) is comparable to one-step GAN methods (1.7–6.0 FPS) and roughly 10× faster than any diffusion-based baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowIE sits at the intersection of rectified flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2022) and latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), repurposing the straight-line transport framework originally designed for one-to-one image synthesis into a many-to-one mapping for image enhancement. It departs from diffusion-based restoration methods (DiffBIR, DDNM, GDP) that require dozens of iterative denoising steps, and from GAN-based approaches (GFPGAN, CodeFormer, Real-ESRGAN) that are task-specific and difficult to tune, by leveraging a pretrained Stable Diffusion U-Net as a velocity predictor conditioned via a ControlNet-style branch on a coarse restoration output. The mean value sampling strategy, inspired by Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem, is a novel inference-time trick that replaces the standard forward Euler integration with a single midpoint velocity evaluation, reducing the required steps to four.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving near-diffusion quality at roughly 10× the inference speed, making generative-prior-based restoration viable for real-time or resource-constrained deployment. The framework's generality—handling BFR, BSR, inpainting, and color enhancement with only 5K steps of task-specific fine-tuning—suggests a reusable recipe for adapting pretrained diffusion knowledge to new restoration tasks without expensive paired-data preparation. Open questions include whether the many-to-one mapping and midpoint sampling generalize to video restoration or higher-resolution settings, and whether the reliance on a separate coarse-restoration model (SwinIR) as the conditioning source limits the upper bound of achievable quality.
