## Problem

Self-attention in Vision Transformers is computationally expensive, and existing token pruning methods reduce FLOPs but still require loading the full network, leaving the memory bound unchanged. This paper asks whether uninformative attention layers can be entirely removed and their function absorbed into subsequent MLP layers, guided by entropy analysis of information content across transformer blocks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241838/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT (DeiT-B) and standard RGB images as input, producing a structurally simplified ViT where a subset of transformer blocks contain only MLP layers (no self-attention). The simplified model then outputs class labels (ImageNet-1k, CIFAR-100) or pixel-level segmentation masks (ADE20K). The key structural change is that removed attention layers are replaced by identity mappings folded into the residual connection, reducing parameter count by 13.7–16.4% and increasing the maximum batch size under a fixed 10 GB memory budget by over 20%.

## Method

The approach has three components. First, entropy is quantified per layer using a Gaussian approximation of feature distributions, revealing that bottom-block attention layers carry less information than their accompanying MLP layers, which are under-exploited. Second, the NOSE (Entropy-based Selection Strategy) uses transfer entropy between candidate attention layers and the final output layer to greedily select the combination of layers whose removal causes minimal performance impact, accounting for inter-layer interactions rather than treating layers independently. Third, a dilution learning technique gradually degenerates selected attention layers into identity mappings by applying a sparse mask M that decays from 1 to 0 over training, with a feature compensation term (1−M)⊙x that counteracts the vanishing backward gradient and stabilizes training. Once M reaches 0, the attention layer plus its residual connection collapses into a simple 2x identity, which is absorbed into the subsequent MLP layer, yielding MLP-only blocks at inference.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, removing 40% of DeiT-B's attention layers (5 out of 12) preserves the original 81.8% Top-1 accuracy while reducing parameters by 13.7% (86.6M → 74.7M) and improving the memory bound by 20.5% (606 → 730 images per 10 GB). Removing 50% of attention layers (6 out of 12) yields 81.5% Top-1 with a 16.4% parameter reduction and 36.5% throughput improvement (408 vs. 299 images/s). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the 40%-pruned model achieves 24.6% mIoU from scratch, slightly exceeding the full DeiT-B (24.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method matches DeiT-B exactly on ImageNet-1k Top-1 (81.8%) while removing 40% of attention layers, and outperforms all token-pruning baselines: TPS (81.4%), EViT (81.3%), DynamicViT (81.3%), Evo-ViT (81.3%), and DiffRate (81.5%) by 0.3–0.5%. The most striking advantage is in memory bound: all token-pruning methods retain the full network and thus share the same ~606 images/10 GB limit, whereas the proposed method reaches 730 images/10 GB (+20.5%) because attention layers are physically removed. On CIFAR-100 linear probing, the 40%-pruned model scores 81.3%, surpassing DeiT-B (80.6%) by 0.7% and TPS (76.5%) by 4.8%, indicating superior feature generalization. On ADE20K from scratch, the 40%-pruned model achieves 24.6% mIoU vs. DeiT-B's 24.4%, EViT's 24.0%, and TPS's 23.5%, confirming that removing attention layers does not harm dense prediction where global context matters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of ViT efficiency work (token pruning/merging: DynamicViT, EViT, TPS, Evo-ViT, ToMe) that reduces computation by selectively discarding or combining tokens while retaining the full network architecture. Instead, it removes entire self-attention layers from specific transformer blocks, replacing them with identity mappings absorbed into the subsequent MLP. The dilution mechanism adapts sparse-mask regularization from L0-pruning literature (Louizos et al., Guo et al.), while the layer-selection strategy (NOSE) repurposes transfer entropy from information-theoretic time-series analysis to measure inter-layer dependency in a ViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing memory bound, which token-pruning methods cannot solve since they still require loading the full network; removing attention layers yields a 13.7% parameter reduction and 20.5% increase in batch capacity under a fixed GPU budget. The DFT analysis suggesting the simplified model encodes stronger high-frequency components offers a mechanistic explanation for why performance is preserved, though this remains correlational. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to DeiT-B, a 300-epoch dilution training cost, and the greedy NOSE search scaling poorly to deeper architectures; whether the 40% removal ratio generalizes to ViT-L, Swin, or non-DeiT initializations remains open.
