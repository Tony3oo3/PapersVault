## Problem

Existing CLIP-based person ReID methods like CLIP-ReID rely on ID-specific learnable soft prompts optimized in a two-stage pipeline, which are inadequate for expressing the full visual context of a pedestrian and fail to generalize to unseen identities at inference. Moreover, prior attention-based ReID methods highlight locally discriminative regions without explicit semantic control, and the visual representation from CLIP alone lacks the fine-grained detail needed to distinguish between similar-looking individuals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240685/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image resized to 256×128 and a text prompt as input. During training, the prompt is composed as 'A photo of a s* person' where s* is a learned pseudo-token; at inference, a simplified fixed prompt 'A photo of a person' can be used to avoid the text encoder overhead. The output is a person feature embedding (from the final and preceding two ViT layer states) used for triplet and classification losses, enabling standard mAP/Rank-1 retrieval evaluation.

## Method

PromptSG introduces two key mechanisms: (1) a lightweight 3-layer MLP inversion network that maps the global CLIP visual embedding to a pseudo-token s* in the token embedding space, trained with a symmetric supervised contrastive loss to ensure same-identity images share appearance details; and (2) a Multimodal Interaction Module (MIM) that performs patch-to-prompt cross-attention, where the text embedding acts as query and patch embeddings as key/value, explicitly directing which image regions align with the language prompt semantics. Two additional self-attention transformer blocks follow the cross-attention layer to produce the final re-weighted representation. The entire framework is trained end-to-end (unlike CLIP-ReID's two-stage approach) with triplet loss, ID classification loss, and the supervised contrastive loss, while the CLIP text encoder remains frozen. This design achieves ~2× faster training than CLIP-ReID and allows the language prompt to guide attention even for unseen classes at inference.

## Results

With a ViT-B/16 backbone, PromptSG achieves 94.6% mAP / 97.0% R-1 on Market-1501, 87.2% mAP / 92.6% R-1 on MSMT17, 81.6% mAP / 91.0% R-1 on DukeMTMC, and 83.1% mAP / 85.1% R-1 on CUHK03-NP, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. The most striking gain is over 13.8% mAP on MSMT17 compared to the previous CLIP-based SOTA (CLIP-ReID), and nearly 5% mAP over the previous ViT-based SOTA (PHA) on Market-1501. With a ResNet-50 backbone, the method still leads on MSMT17 (68.5% mAP) and Market-1501 (91.8% mAP), demonstrating robustness across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest CLIP-based competitor CLIP-ReID [21] (ViT-B/16), PromptSG gains +5.0% mAP and +1.5% R-1 on Market-1501 (94.6 vs 89.6) and a substantial +13.8% mAP and +3.9% R-1 on MSMT17 (87.2 vs 73.4), attributed to the end-to-end pseudo-token and cross-attention guidance that CLIP-ReID's two-stage, ID-bound prompts cannot provide at inference. Versus the previous ViT-based SOTA PHA [46], PromptSG improves Market-1501 mAP by +4.4% (94.6 vs 90.2) and MSMT17 mAP by +18.3% (87.2 vs 68.9), while also matching or exceeding PHA on CUHK03-NP (83.1 vs 83.0 mAP). Among CNN-based methods, PromptSG with ResNet-50 surpasses the strongest baseline LTReID [39] by +9.9% mAP on MSMT17 (68.5 vs 58.6) and +2.9% mAP on Market-1501 (91.8 vs 86.9). On DukeMTMC, PromptSG (ViT) leads TransReID [14] by +0.4% mAP and +0.3% R-1, and CLIP-ReID by +0.9% mAP and +1.0% R-1. The paper also reports that training is ~2× faster than CLIP-ReID (2417 s vs 4689 s on Market-1501) with only a marginal increase in parameter count (94 M vs 89 M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP-ReID (Li et al., AAAAI 2023), the first work to adapt CLIP for person ReID via ID-specific learnable prompt vectors, but departs from its two-stage training pipeline in favor of an end-to-end one-stage framework. It replaces CLIP-ReID's per-identity soft prompts with a fixed-size inversion network that learns a single pseudo-token per image via textual inversion (Gal et al., ICLR 2022), and introduces a cross-attention module that uses the text embedding as a query to explicitly re-weight visual patch tokens. The broader lineage includes ViT-based ReID methods (TransReID, PHA) that rely solely on uni-modal self-attention, which this paper shows can be improved by injecting cross-modal semantic guidance without requiring additional annotations like masks or skeletons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that natural-language prompts can serve as an effective, annotation-free mechanism to steer visual attention in ReID, achieving over 10% mAP gains on MSMT17 and ~5% on Market-1501 while training roughly 2× faster than the two-stage CLIP-ReID baseline. A practical limitation is that fine-tuning the CLIP visual encoder for supervised ReID may degrade its zero-shot generalization, and the composed-prompt inference path doubles latency (though a simplified fixed prompt recovers most accuracy at full speed). The authors note that prompt learning in the visual branch remains largely unexplored, suggesting future work could further exploit foundation-model priors without sacrificing open-vocabulary capability.
