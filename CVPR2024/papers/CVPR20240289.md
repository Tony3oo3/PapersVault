## Problem

Existing data-driven motion reconstruction methods from sparse IMUs are locked to a single fixed sensor configuration (typically six IMUs at predetermined body locations), forcing users to retrain or discard sensors when their activity demands different coverage. This paper asks whether a single generative model can reconstruct whole-body motion in real time from any arbitrary combination of one to thirteen IMUs and/or pressure insoles, without retraining, while maintaining accuracy comparable to dedicated regressive baselines.

## Input / Output

At each 20 Hz timestep the model receives orientation and linear-acceleration signals from whichever subset of 13 body locations is instrumented (pelvis, thighs, shanks, feet, arms, wrists, torso, head), optionally binary heel/toe contact labels from pressure insoles, and a scalar body-height condition. The output is a 190-dimensional per-frame feature vector comprising 24 segment orientations (6-DOF parameterization), 13×3 world-frame linear accelerations, 2D root position delta plus vertical root position, and 4 binary foot-contact flags; only the unobserved features of the current frame are predicted autoregressively while observed features are held fixed. Sequences of 61 frames are processed, with the autoregressive loop shifting one new frame into history per timestep.

## Method

DiffusionPoser is a DDPM-style diffusion model with a transformer-decoder denoiser (8 layers, 512-dim) trained to denoise 61-frame motion sequences, conditioned on body height via cross-attention. The key architectural choice is to use inpainting denoising (à la Repaint) rather than conditional generation: sensor measurements are treated as 'observed' features that are re-imposed at every denoising step via a binary mask, so the model never needs to know which sensors are present during training. At inference, an autoregressive four-step loop runs at 20 Hz: the motion history and current sensor reading form the inpainting input, the denoiser (accelerated with DDIM, 30 steps) generates the full sequence, and only the last frame's unobserved features are extracted before shifting into history. A post-hoc root correction subtracts the mean horizontal displacement of predicted-contact foot points from the root delta to suppress drift and foot sliding. Training uses a composite loss (simple L2, velocity smoothness, forward-kinematics consistency, accumulated root drift, and contact-consistent foot sliding) with energy-weighted sampling to encourage diverse motion.

## Results

On TotalCaptureReal with six IMUs (pelvis, head, wrists, shanks), DiffusionPoser achieves 14.4° global angular error (GA), 13.0° local angular error (LA), 6.1 cm joint position error (JPE), 2.8 jitter ratio, and 0.25 m root translation error at 10 s (RE10). These numbers are within 1.1° and 1 cm of the best-performing baseline (PIP) on angular and position metrics, while DiffusionPoser achieves the lowest RE10 (0.25 m) among all four systems. The method also demonstrates that reducing sensors from six to two (e.g., pelvis + shanks for legs) degrades GA from 14.4° to 36.4° on real data, and that adding ground-truth insole contact labels improves GA by up to 1.1° and RE10 by up to 0.29 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionPoser is compared against three regression-based baselines—Transpose, PIP, and TIP—all of which are restricted to a fixed six-IMU configuration. On global angular error (GA), DiffusionPoser (14.4°) ties PIP (14.4°) and TIP (14.3°) and clearly beats Transpose (16.1°). On local angular error (LA) and joint position error (JPE), PIP leads (11.9° LA, 5.3 cm JPE) with DiffusionPoser trailing by ~1.1° and ~0.8 cm respectively. On root translation error at 10 s, DiffusionPoser achieves the best result (0.25 m), outperforming PIP (0.27 m), Transpose (0.29 m), and TIP (0.32 m). On the jitter metric, PIP reports the lowest value (1.1), but the authors note this likely reflects oversmoothing; DiffusionPoser (2.8) is substantially better than Transpose (5.0) and TIP (5.3). The key practical advantage is that DiffusionPoser matches or approaches these baselines while supporting arbitrary sensor counts and placements without retraining, and it gracefully handles signal dropout—a capability none of the regression baselines possess.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of sparse-IMU motion reconstruction (DIP, Transpose, PIP, TIP) by replacing the fixed six-sensor configuration with an arbitrary combination of up to thirteen IMUs and pressure insoles. The key methodological departure is the use of inpainting denoising (from Repaint and FLAME) rather than conditional generation to incorporate sensor measurements, which is what enables configuration flexibility without retraining. The autoregressive inference scheme, combined with DDIM-accelerated sampling, adapts the offline diffusion framework to real-time frame-by-frame reconstruction, a setting not previously addressed by diffusion-based motion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to select sensor configurations on-the-fly without retraining is practically significant for health monitoring, rehabilitation, and biomechanics applications where different body regions require different accuracy trade-offs. The generative nature provides robustness to signal dropout and corruption that regressive baselines like PIP cannot handle, making the system more deployable in real-world settings. Limitations include ~130ms total latency (filter + reconstruction) that precludes applications requiring sub-50ms response, and the sim-to-real gap (roughly 2× in angular error) remains a practical concern. Future work on estimating joint torques and muscle forces could extend the system toward full musculoskeletal analysis.
