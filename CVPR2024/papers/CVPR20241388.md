## Problem

Existing human-centric point cloud video understanding methods rely on generic point cloud backbones and fully supervised training, which overfit to specific datasets and fail to exploit the structural semantics and motion dynamics unique to the human body. No unified framework existed that could leverage human prior knowledge to enable generalized representation learning across diverse downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241388/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point cloud video sequences of L=30 frames (L=32 for LIP), each frame normalized to N=384 points via Farthest Point Sampling, with optional IMU measurements. Points are partitioned into 9 body-part patches of N'=48 points each using pre-trained HBSeg labels, and point-wise motion flow vectors (D'=3) are extracted by pre-trained HMFlow. Outputs are 12-class action labels (HuCenLife) or 3D root-relative joint positions (LIP), evaluated by class mean accuracy and MPJPE respectively.

## Method

UniPVU-Human is a three-stage framework that uses human body-part semantics as the fundamental tokenization unit rather than arbitrary FPS-sampled neighborhoods. Stage 1 pre-trains HBSeg (PointNeXt-L) and HMFlow (FLOT) on ~1M synthetic LiDAR scans of AMASS/SURREAL meshes to obtain part labels and motion flow on real data. Stage 2 performs masked autoencoder self-learning where body-part tokens are masked temporally (r_t=0.8) and spatially (r_s=0.6), encoded by interleaved spatial and temporal transformer layers (4+4+4 layers, 6 heads, C=384), and decoded to reconstruct masked 3D coordinates via Chamfer Distance loss. Stage 3 fine-tunes the pre-trained encoder with hierarchical features: a global token from the full human instance, part-level tokens enriched with motion flow vectors fused in the tokenizer, and a class token for classification, enabling transfer to action recognition and pose estimation.

## Results

On HuCenLife action recognition, UniPVU-Human achieves 61.8% class-mean accuracy (mAcc), surpassing the best self-supervised baseline PointMAE (58.0%) by 3.8 points and the supervised HuCenLife baseline (57.4%) by 4.4 points. On LIP 3D pose estimation, the method achieves 58.8 mm MPJPE with point clouds only (vs. 60.1 mm for LIP) and 47.2 mm with PC+IMU (vs. 48.9 mm for LIP), establishing state-of-the-art in both settings. In semi-supervised settings with only 20% of labeled data, UniPVU-Human retains 51.0% mAcc, showing the smallest performance drop (−10.8) compared to MaST-Pre (−14.3) and its own no-self-learning variant (−10.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPVU-Human outperforms all ten baselines on both tasks. On HuCenLife action recognition, it beats the strongest static point-cloud baselines (PointMLP 58.1%, PCT 57.6%) by 3.7–4.2 mAcc points, and the supervised HuCenLife baseline (57.4%) by 4.4 points. Against self-supervised video methods, it surpasses PointMAE† (58.0%) by 3.8 points and MaST-Pre† (54.1%) by 7.7 points, with particularly large gains on motion-dependent categories like Bend-Over (93.8% vs. 57.9% for PointMAE) and Walk-Stand (76.1% vs. 32.5% for MaST-Pre). On LIP 3D pose estimation, UniPVU-Human reduces MPJPE by 1.3 mm over LIP in the PC-only setting and by 1.7 mm in the PC+IMU setting, while LiDARCap lags by 10.6 mm. The semi-supervised ablation (Table 4) further confirms that the self-learning stage provides the most robust performance under label scarcity, with UniPVU-Human losing only 4.5 points at 50% data versus 5.3 for MaST-Pre.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked autoencoder paradigm for point clouds (PointMAE) and spatio-temporal transformer architectures (P4Transformer, PST-Transformer), but departs from them by replacing generic FPS-based local patches with semantically meaningful body-part patches obtained via a pre-trained Human Body Segmentation network. It also extends the self-supervised learning line for point cloud videos (contrastive methods, MaST-Pre's tube-based masking) by incorporating human motion flow estimated via optimal transport (FLOT) as an explicit dynamic prior. The key conceptual departure from prior LiDAR human-centric work (LiDARCap, LIP, HuCenLife) is the shift from fully supervised generic backbones to a three-stage pipeline that first extracts human-specific geometric and dynamic priors, then self-learns representations, and finally fine-tunes with hierarchical features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework explicitly designed for human-centric point cloud video understanding, addressing the annotation bottleneck through self-supervised pre-training on unlabelled data and the lack of human-specific inductive bias through body-part-structured tokens. The synthetic-data strategy for HBSeg and HMFlow (simulated LiDAR scanning of SMPL meshes with noise and occlusion) is a practical recipe that could be adapted to other non-rigid object domains. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets, the framework's specificity to human anatomy (9 fixed body parts), and the reliance on pre-trained auxiliary networks whose quality bounds the main pipeline. Open questions include whether the body-part masking strategy generalizes to other articulated objects and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or multi-person scenarios.
