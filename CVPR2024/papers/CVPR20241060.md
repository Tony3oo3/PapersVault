## Problem

Most domain generalization methods (DANN, SagNet, CSVPT, DART) require modifying the architecture or fine-tuning the parameters of the objective network, which compromises its privacy and integrity. This paper asks whether domain generalization can be achieved entirely through input-side perturbations—adding learned prompts to images—without ever touching the frozen network's weights or structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241060/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and produces a class label prediction from a frozen CLIP-pretrained ViT. The key detail is that before the image enters the network, a learned prompt (a 30×30 pixel perturbation, following VP conventions) is added to the input. This prompt is the only trainable artifact; the ViT backbone and its classification head remain completely fixed. During inference, the prompt is computed per-image as an attention-weighted mixture of N source-domain expert prompts, so the effective input is image-specific.

## Method

A2XP decomposes domain generalization into two phases. In Expert Adaptation, one prompt per source domain is optimized via adversarial reprogramming (gradient descent on the prompt only), initialized from a shared meta prompt and L2-normalized to equalize scales. In Attention-based Generalization, a shared ResNet-18 encoder with two separate linear heads produces query embeddings (from target images) and key/value embeddings (from expert prompts); the per-image prompt is then the sum of all expert prompts weighted by tanh(QKᵀ), deliberately avoiding softmax to prevent suppression of low-weight experts. The theoretical framing casts the target prompt as a linear combination of source experts whose coefficients maximize the likelihood of the target distribution, reducing domain generalization to a direction-regression problem. Because only the input prompt and two small embedder heads are trained, the objective network's architecture and parameters are never exposed or modified, preserving full privacy.

## Results

On the PACS dataset, A2XP achieves 95.07% average leave-one-domain-out accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline MIRO (90.33%) by 4.74% and DANN (88.78%) by 6.29%. On VLCS, A2XP reaches 83.15% average accuracy, outperforming DANN (79.24%) by 3.91% and MIRO (78.56%) by 4.59%. Notably, A2XP is the only method that does not require DART ensemble augmentation, meaning it preserves the privacy of the objective network while still achieving state-of-the-art performance with only 1,000 generalization updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2XP outperforms all five baselines on every target domain across both PACS and VLCS. On PACS, the largest margin is over MIRO (90.33% → 95.07%, +4.74%) and DANN (88.78% → 95.07%, +6.29%), with the most dramatic per-domain gain on Cartoon (90.46% → 98.07%, +7.61%). On VLCS, A2XP leads DANN by 3.91% on average, with the biggest per-domain advantage on SUN09 (73.53% → 80.19%, +6.66%) and VOC 2007 (77.86% → 84.07%, +6.21%). SagNet and SAM are substantially weaker (66.45% and 18.51% on PACS respectively), indicating that adversarial domain discrimination and sharpness-aware minimization alone are insufficient for cross-domain generalization. ERM (88.22% on PACS, 78.66% on VLCS) serves as a strong non-DG baseline but still trails A2XP by 6.85% and 4.49% respectively. Crucially, all baselines rely on DART ensemble augmentation (which requires modifying the objective network), whereas A2XP achieves superior accuracy without any such modification, preserving network privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

A2XP builds directly on Visual Prompting (VP) [Bahng et al., 2022], which demonstrated that a frozen network can be adapted by optimizing input-space prompts via adversarial reprogramming, and on the adversarial reprogramming framework of Elsayed et al. [2018] as the optimization engine for expert adaptation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant domain generalization paradigm (DANN, SagNet, MIRO, DART) that requires modifying the backbone network's parameters or architecture, instead reframing the problem as a 'direction regression' where per-domain expert prompts are mixed via cross-attention to approximate the optimal target-domain prompt. This positions A2XP at the intersection of prompt tuning, domain adaptation, and ensemble learning, while introducing a privacy-preserving constraint absent from prior domain generalization methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The privacy-preserving framing is a genuinely new constraint for domain generalization: most existing methods (DANN, SagNet, CSVPT, DART) require full access to and modification of the target network, which is impractical when the network is proprietary. A2XP achieves SOTA on PACS and VLCS with far fewer parameters than DART-based ensembles, making it attractive for settings where the backbone cannot be touched. However, the method's effectiveness hinges on successful expert adaptation, and the authors acknowledge that some domains are difficult to adapt via input prompts alone, leaving open the question of how A2XP scales to more challenging or higher-dimensional prompt spaces. The finding that full tuning combined with A2XP is counterproductive also raises open questions about the interaction between prompt-based and parameter-based adaptation.
