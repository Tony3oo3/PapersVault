## Problem

Existing multimodal visual object trackers face a trade-off: symmetric frameworks that fully fine-tune pre-trained RGB trackers overfit due to scarce multimodal data, while asymmetric prompt-tuning frameworks (e.g., ViPT) treat RGB as dominant and other modalities as auxiliary, causing severe performance degradation when RGB is occluded or missing. No prior work combines symmetric multimodal fusion with parameter-efficient adapter-based fine-tuning to address both data scarcity and modality dependence simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a pair of template and search frames in two modalities (RGB plus X, where X is Depth, Thermal, or Event), each projected into patch embeddings of dimension D. During training, both clean and randomly masked versions of the patch embeddings are processed through the network; during inference, only the clean path is used. The prediction head (a frozen FCN from OSTrack) takes the element-wise sum of RGB and X search features—rather than RGB alone as in prior methods—and outputs a tracking score map from which the bounding box is extracted.

## Method

SDSTrack freezes a pre-trained OSTrack ViT backbone and trains only lightweight adapters (bottleneck FC-GELU-FC modules with residual connections) in two roles: Cross Modal Adaptation (CMA) inserts adapters after MSA and parallel to MLP in each ViT block to bridge the RGB-to-X modality gap, while Multimodal Fusion Adaptation (MFA) reuses the last ViT block of each encoder stage as a fusion stage where an attention mask zeros out same-modality self-attention, forcing tokens to attend only across modalities. For robustness, a Random Complementary Patch Mask (RCPM) strategy masks patches in one modality and correspondingly masks the complementary regions in the other, guaranteeing at least one modality remains valid. A self-distillation loss (MSE between clean-path and masked-path fusion features at each stage) supervises the masked path to recover information from the surviving modality. The total training objective combines clean tracking loss, masked tracking loss (weighted by α=0.3), and the self-distillation loss (weighted by β=0.25).

## Results

On DepthTrack, SDSTrack achieves state-of-the-art performance with 61.9% precision, 60.9% recall, and 61.4% F-score, surpassing the previous best (ViPT) by 2.7% in precision and 2.0% in F-score. On VOT-RGBD2022, it obtains 72.8% EAO and 88.3% robustness (1.2% improvement over ViPT in robustness). On RGBT234, SDSTrack reaches 84.8% MPR and 62.5% MSR, and on VisEvent it achieves 76.7% precision and 59.7% success, all setting new records across RGB-D, RGB-T, and RGB-E tracking benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDSTrack outperforms all 22 compared baselines across every main benchmark. On DepthTrack, it surpasses the closest competitor ViPT [71] by 2.7% in precision (0.619 vs. 0.592) and 2.0% in F-score (0.614 vs. 0.594), and leads ProTrack [59] by 3.6% in F-score. On VOT-RGBD2022, SDSTrack achieves the best EAO (0.728 vs. ViPT's 0.721) and robustness (0.883 vs. 0.871), though it trails ViPT by 0.3% in accuracy (0.812 vs. 0.815). On RGBT234, it beats ViPT by 1.3% MPR (0.848 vs. 0.835) and 0.8% MSR (0.625 vs. 0.617), and leads APFNet [54] by 2.1% MPR. On LasHeR and VisEvent, SDSTrack sets new records of 66.5%/53.1% and 76.7%/59.7% (precision/success), exceeding ViPT by 1.4% and 0.9% in precision respectively. In robustness experiments (modality drop and occlusion), SDSTrack shows substantially larger gains over ViPT when RGB is dropped (e.g., +23.2% precision on LasHeR), confirming the advantage of its symmetric design over asymmetric prompt-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDSTrack builds on OSTrack, a one-stream ViT-based RGB tracker, as its frozen pre-trained backbone, and departs from two prior lines of multimodal tracking: symmetric frameworks (e.g., DeT) that fully fine-tune all parameters and risk overfitting on scarce multimodal data, and asymmetric prompt-tuning frameworks (e.g., ViPT, ProTrack) that treat RGB as dominant and create modality dependency. The paper's key positioning is that adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning had not been applied to multimodal VOT, and it combines this PEFT strategy with a symmetric (modality-agnostic) architecture to address both data scarcity and modality dominance simultaneously. The complementary masked patch distillation draws on masking strategies from NLP (BERT, RoBERTa) and vision pre-training (MAE, SimMIM) but repurposes them for multimodal tracking robustness via self-distillation between clean and masked paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical applications like autonomous driving, the demonstrated robustness gains under modality drop (e.g., 23.2% precision improvement on LasHeR when RGB is lost) are practically significant, showing that symmetric adapter-based tracking can maintain performance when a sensor fails. The approach offers a practical recipe—freeze a strong RGB backbone, insert lightweight adapters symmetrically, and use complementary masking with self-distillation—that is broadly applicable to any new modality combination without re-architecting the tracker. The main limitation is increased training complexity from the dual-path self-distillation, and it remains open whether the approach generalizes to more than two modalities or to fundamentally different backbone architectures beyond ViT-based one-stream trackers.
