## Problem

First-order deep unrolling networks for sparse-view CT reconstruction suffer from slow convergence requiring many iterations (18–30), high computational cost, and limited ability to capture long-range dependencies due to locally-focused CNN regularization. While second-order quasi-Newton methods offer superlinear convergence, their full Hessian matrix approximation is prohibitively memory-intensive, restricting prior work to small-scale problems. This paper bridges that gap by combining a quasi-Newton unrolling framework with a memory-efficient latent-space Hessian approximation and a non-local regularization architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse CT sinogram (32, 64, or 128 projection views uniformly sampled from 512 full views in a 2D fan-beam geometry with 512 detector pixels) as input, optionally corrupted with mixed Gaussian (5%) and Poisson noise. It produces a reconstructed 256×256 CT image after 14 unrolling iterations. The forward model is the discrete Radon transform implemented via the ODL library, and the pseudo-inverse A† is approximated by the FBP algorithm.

## Method

QN-Mixer unrolls a quasi-Newton optimization (BFGS update) into a 14-iteration deep recurrent network, where each step computes x_{t+1} = x_t + D(−H_t · E(∇J(x_t))). The key memory-saving mechanism is a latent BFGS update: an encoder E downsamples the full gradient to a 64×64 latent representation, so the inverse Hessian H_t is only 4096×4096 rather than 65536×65536, and a decoder D maps the latent search direction back to image space. The learned regularization term G is Incept-Mixer, which first extracts multi-scale local features via an Inception block, then applies patchification (p=4) and two MLP-Mixer layers (token MLPs over height/width, channel MLP over features) to capture long-range dependencies before patch-expanding back to image size. Unlike prior learned-optimizer approaches that regress the Hessian directly from data, QN-Mixer explicitly applies the BFGS rank-one update equations at each iteration, preserving the mathematical structure of the quasi-Newton method while remaining differentiable and trainable end-to-end with supervised MSE loss.

## Results

On the AAPM dataset (no noise, 32 views), QN-Mixer achieves 39.51 dB PSNR and 96.11% SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline RegFormer (38.71 dB / 95.42%) by +0.80 dB and +0.69%. On DeepLesion (32 views, Poisson noise N₁=10⁶), QN-Mixer reaches 39.39 dB / 95.67% SSIM versus RegFormer's 37.38 dB / 93.89%, an average improvement of +2.23 dB PSNR and +1.02% SSIM across all view counts. QN-Mixer requires only 14 unrolling iterations compared to 30 for LEARN and 18 for RegFormer, while also reducing memory usage by 1.3× relative to RegFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QN-Mixer outperforms all six baselines across both AAPM and DeepLesion datasets in the no-noise and low-noise settings. Against the strongest first-order unrolling baseline RegFormer [54], QN-Mixer gains +1.65 dB PSNR and +0.58% SSIM on average (AAPM, no noise) and +2.23 dB / +1.02% on DeepLesion. First-order unrolling methods LEARN [7] and Learned PD [1] trail QN-Mixer by 1.9–2.0 dB and 3.6–3.7 dB respectively at 32 views on AAPM. Post-processing (FBPConvNet [19]) and dual-domain (DuDoTrans [49]) methods are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 9+ dB PSNR at 32 views. Under high noise (N₂=5×10⁵), QN-Mixer narrows to near-parity with RegFormer (−0.02 dB at 32 views), which the authors attribute to compressed gradient information in the latent BFGS. On the OOD test (randomly overlaid circle), QN-Mixer degrades only −2.67 dB at 32 views versus −3.1 dB (LEARN) and −4.22 dB (RegFormer), indicating better generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unrolling network paradigm for sparse-view CT reconstruction by replacing the standard first-order gradient descent iteration with a quasi-Newton (BFGS) update, building directly on the BFGS rank-one Hessian approximation and the quasi-Newton convergence theory. The regularization term, Incept-Mixer, fuses the Inception multi-scale feature extraction with the MLP-Mixer token/channel mixing mechanism, drawing on both architectures independently. The memory-efficient latent BFGS update is inspired by the encoder-decoder structure of Latent Diffusion Models, repurposing it to project gradient information into a lower-dimensional space before Hessian approximation. Unlike prior second-order unrolling work (Gartner et al., 2023) that learns the inverse Hessian directly from data, QN-Mixer retains the explicit BFGS update equations, preserving the mathematical structure of the optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that second-order unrolling can match or exceed first-order methods with fewer iterations (14 vs. 18–30), offering a practical efficiency gain for iterative reconstruction pipelines. The latent BFGS approach makes quasi-Newton methods feasible at image scale by reducing the Hessian dimensionality, though the authors acknowledge that the 64×64 Hessian size is a hard memory constraint limiting generalizability to larger problems. A notable advantage is improved OOD robustness compared to first-order unrolling networks, suggesting the curvature information provides better generalization. Open questions include whether the latent BFGS strategy transfers to other inverse problems (MRI, limited-angle CT) and whether larger Hessian approximations can be achieved with further memory optimizations.
