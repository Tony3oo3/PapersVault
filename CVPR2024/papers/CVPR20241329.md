## Problem

NeRF-based 3D reconstruction requires tens to hundreds of input images for artifact-free results, and existing few-view regularizers (depth, frequency, visibility-based) still degrade significantly when only 3–9 views are available. While diffusion models can generate realistic novel views, they do not produce a single consistent 3D shape, and prior 3D generative approaches are either category-specific or restricted to single-image inputs, preventing their use as a general scene reconstruction prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241329/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of posed RGB images (typically 3–9, but demonstrated up to 81) at 512×512 resolution along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics. For each sampled novel view during reconstruction, only the 3 nearest observed camera positions are selected to condition the diffusion model, enabling scaling to arbitrarily many input images. The output is a Zip-NeRF 3D scene representation that renders photorealistic novel views; the diffusion model itself operates in a 64×64×8 latent space decoded back to 512×512 pixels.

## Method

ReconFusion combines two components: (1) a latent diffusion model fine-tuned from a pretrained text-to-image LDM for multiview-conditioned novel view synthesis, and (2) a Zip-NeRF reconstruction pipeline regularized by this diffusion prior. The diffusion model receives two conditioning signals: CLIP embeddings of input images via cross-attention for high-level semantics, and a PixelNeRF-rendered feature map (64×64×128) concatenated along channels to the noisy latent, which implicitly encodes the relative camera pose. It is trained jointly with the PixelNeRF encoder on a mixture of Objaverse, CO3D, MVImgNet, and RealEstate10K using a standard denoising loss plus a photometric loss on the PixelNeRF output. During 3D reconstruction, a random novel pose is sampled along a fitted elliptical or B-spline path; the current NeRF rendering is encoded to a noisy latent at an annealed noise level, then denoised via 10-step DDIM sampling to produce a target image, which supervises the NeRF rendering through an L1 + LPIPS loss. This multistep sampling approach (starting from intermediate noise rather than pure noise) was found empirically superior to score distillation sampling, reducing blotchy artifacts while preserving observed-region fidelity.

## Results

ReconFusion achieves state-of-the-art few-view 3D reconstruction across five real-world datasets (RealEstate10K, LLFF, DTU, CO3D, mip-NeRF 360) with 3, 6, and 9 input views. On the hardest 360-degree scenes, it leads by large margins: on CO3D (9-view) it reaches PSNR 22.95 / SSIM 0.736 / LPIPS 0.318, and on mip-NeRF 360 (9-view) PSNR 18.19 / SSIM 0.432 / LPIPS 0.511, outperforming the next-best baseline (ZeroNVS*) by over 2 dB in PSNR and ~0.08 in SSIM. On forward-facing scenes (LLFF, DTU), gains over the strongest few-view regularizer (FreeNeRF) are more modest but consistent in LPIPS (e.g., 0.134 vs 0.193 on LLFF 9-view). The method also scales gracefully, improving over the Zip-NeRF backbone up to 81 input views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReconFusion outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of dataset–view-count combinations. Against the dense-view backbone Zip-NeRF*, it wins on every dataset and view count, with the largest gaps on 360-degree scenes (e.g., +2.7 dB PSNR on CO3D 9-view, +3.9 dB on mip-NeRF 360 9-view). Among few-view regularizers, FreeNeRF is the closest competitor on forward-facing DTU (9-view PSNR 25.56 vs. 24.62), but ReconFusion still leads on SSIM (0.921 vs. 0.902) and LPIPS (0.094 vs. 0.102); on all other datasets ReconFusion surpasses FreeNeRF by 1–5 dB in PSNR. ZeroNVS*, the strongest single-image-based baseline, is clearly beaten on 360-degree scenes (CO3D: +2.45 dB PSNR at 9-view; mip-NeRF 360: +2.2 dB) but remains competitive on forward-facing LLFF where it still trails by ~6 dB. DiffusioNeRF, SimpleNeRF, and RegNeRF are consistently outperformed across all settings, with ReconFusion gaining 2–12 dB in PSNR depending on dataset difficulty. SparseFusion (evaluated only on CO3D in a category-specific setup) is surpassed by ReconFusion by 3.4 dB PSNR at 9-view, confirming the advantage of a general-purpose multiview diffusion prior over category-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReconFusion sits at the intersection of two active research threads: few-view NeRF regularization (RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SimpleNeRF, DiffusioNeRF) and diffusion-based novel view synthesis (Zero-1-to-3, GeNVS, SparseFusion). It departs from prior diffusion-for-3D work by training a general-purpose, multiview-conditioned latent diffusion model on a mixture of real and synthetic datasets, rather than relying on category-specific or single-image models. The reconstruction pipeline uses a multistep sampling loss (closer to SparseFusion) rather than the score distillation sampling of DreamFusion, and conditions the diffusion model via a PixelNeRF-rendered feature map rather than direct pose embeddings, enabling arbitrary numbers of input views with precise geometric grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReconFusion demonstrates that a learned 2D diffusion prior can serve as a broadly effective, drop-in regularizer for NeRF optimization across diverse scene types (forward-facing, 360-degree, unstructured) and view counts (3 to 81), reducing the capture burden for practical 3D reconstruction. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the computational cost of the diffusion model during optimization, limited 3D outpainting capability relative to the 2D model's generative capacity, and the tediousness of balancing reconstruction and sample losses. Open questions include whether more efficient distillation or amortized approaches can reduce the per-scene optimization cost, and whether the diffusion prior can be made to produce more geometrically consistent hallucinations in severely underconstrained regions.
