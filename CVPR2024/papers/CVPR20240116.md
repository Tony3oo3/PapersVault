## Problem

Monocular depth estimators struggle with zero-shot generalization to unfamiliar scenes because their visual knowledge is bounded by training data. The paper asks whether the extensive scene-understanding priors captured in internet-scale pretrained diffusion models can be repurposed to produce a more generalizable depth estimator without requiring large, diverse real depth datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces an affine-invariant depth map (depth up to a global scale and shift). During training, depth maps are normalized to [-1, 1] via 2nd/98th percentile clipping to match the VAE's expected input range, and the single-channel depth is replicated to three channels to feed the RGB-trained VAE. At inference, the decoded depth latent is averaged across its three channels to yield the final single-channel depth estimate.

## Method

Marigold fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v2 by keeping the VAE and latent space entirely frozen and modifying only the denoising U-Net: its first convolutional layer is expanded to accept concatenated image and depth latent codes (input channels doubled, weights halved to preserve activation magnitudes). Training uses only ~74K synthetic RGB-D pairs from Hypersim and Virtual KITTI with the standard DDPM noise objective, augmented by a multi-resolution noise schedule that anneals toward standard Gaussian noise for faster convergence. The key design principle is preserving the pretrained latent space so that the rich visual priors from LAION-5B training remain intact. At inference, DDIM sampling with 50 re-spaced steps is used, and a test-time ensembling scheme aggregates N independent denoising passes by jointly optimizing per-prediction scale and shift to minimize pairwise distances, yielding a final merged depth map.

## Results

Marigold achieves the best average rank (1.4) across five zero-shot benchmarks using only 74K synthetic training samples and no real depth data. With test-time ensembling (10 runs), it attains AbsRel of 5.5% on NYUv2, 9.9% on KITTI, 6.5% on ETH3D, 6.4% on ScanNet, and 30.8% on DIODE, with corresponding δ1 accuracies of 96.4%, 91.6%, 96.0%, 95.1%, and 77.3%. Even without ensembling, it already outperforms all baselines on four of five datasets. Training requires only ~2.5 GPU-days on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Marigold outperforms all six baselines on four of five benchmarks (NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, ScanNet) and achieves the best overall average rank (1.4 vs. 3.2 for the next-best HDN). Against the strongest competitor HDN, Marigold reduces AbsRel by 1.4–2.1 points across the four shared wins (e.g., 5.5 vs. 6.9 on NYUv2, 6.5 vs. 12.1 on ETH3D). The sole exception is DIODE, where DPT achieves 18.2% AbsRel versus Marigold's 30.8%, likely due to DPT's training on 1.2M real samples spanning diverse outdoor scenes. Notably, Marigold uses only 74K synthetic samples (Hypersim + Virtual KITTI) with no real depth data, whereas competitors like Omnidata (11.9M real) and MiDaS (2M real) rely on orders of magnitude more annotated data. Even without test-time ensembling, Marigold already beats HDN on NYUv2 (6.0 vs. 6.9) and ETH3D (7.1 vs. 12.1), confirming that the diffusion prior itself drives most of the gain rather than the ensembling step.

## Relation to Prior Work

Marigold repurposes the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion model (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and its DDPM/DDIM training framework (Ho et al., 2020) for a fundamentally different task: instead of generating images from text, it generates depth maps conditioned on an input image. This departs sharply from the dominant monocular depth estimation lineage (MiDaS, DPT, Omnidata, HDN, LeReS), which trains dedicated encoder-decoder or transformer architectures on millions of real RGB-D pairs, and also from earlier diffusion-for-depth attempts (DDP, DiffusionDepth, DepthGen, DDVM) that used diffusion as a training objective within purpose-built depth architectures rather than inheriting a pretrained image prior. The key conceptual move is preserving the VAE latent space and fine-tuning only the denoising U-Net, so that the rich scene-understanding priors distilled from LAION-5B remain intact while the model learns to map image latents to depth latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that 74K synthetic depth samples suffice to outperform methods trained on millions of real depth pairs challenges the field's assumption that large, diverse, real annotated datasets are the primary driver of generalizable depth estimation, pointing instead to the value of generative image priors. This opens a broader question: can other dense prediction tasks (surface normals, optical flow, segmentation) be similarly unlocked by fine-tuning a pretrained image generator on small synthetic sets? Practical limitations remain—50 DDIM steps plus 10 ensembling runs per image make inference expensive, outputs are stochastic, and distant scene parts are still poorly handled—so efficiency and consistency improvements are important open directions.
