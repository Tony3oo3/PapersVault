## Problem

Existing motion diffusion models achieve strong generation quality but lack a unified, training-free mechanism to serve as versatile motion priors for diverse downstream tasks such as editing, denoising, and completion. Prior approaches either require task-specific retraining (e.g., AGRoL, AvatarPoser), limit conditioning to a fixed set of signals (e.g., OmniControl), or approximate the optimization criterion at intermediate denoising steps, introducing error when the posterior variance is large (e.g., GMD). The paper asks whether a pretrained motion diffusion model can be repurposed as a universal prior for any differentiable motion criterion without architectural modification or fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained text-to-motion diffusion model (MDM, MLD, or GMD) operating on the relative-root representation (263-dimensional joint features per frame) and a task-specific differentiable criterion defined on the output motion. For editing, the input is a reference motion plus target joint locations or obstacle SDFs; for refinement, it is a noisy or partially observed motion. The output is a full 3D human motion sequence in the same relative-root format. Optimization runs at inference time for 300–500 steps (~3 min on an RTX 3090, 18 GB VRAM with DDIM-10 and batch 16), with no text conditioning applied during the optimization loop.

## Method

DNO frames motion tasks as latent optimization over the diffusion noise x_T: it backpropagates a task-specific loss L(x_0) through the full DDIM-ODE solver to obtain ∇_{x_T} L, then updates x_T via gradient descent with unit-normalized gradients for stability. For editing, x_T is initialized by inverting the reference motion through the ODE (diffusion inversion), and a content-preservation penalty ‖x_T_ref − x_T‖₂ keeps the result close to the original. For denoising/completion, a multi-scale latent decorrelation loss (inspired by StyleGAN2) is added to prevent correlated noise from producing foot-skating artifacts. Unlike guided diffusion methods that evaluate the criterion on the expected x̂_0 at each intermediate step, DNO computes the loss exactly on the fully denoised output, eliminating approximation error. The approach requires no invertible ODE, no additional networks, and no retraining—only the standard ODE solver and an optimizer.

## Results

On the motion editing task (Table 1), DNO-MDM Edit achieves a perfect 0.00 m Objective Error across all four actions (jumping, long jump, walking with raised hand, crawling), while GMD achieves 0.15–0.22 m. DNO also substantially outperforms GMD on Content Preservation (0.92–0.95 vs. 0.59–0.79) and Jitter (0.32–1.34 vs. 0.51–4.10), indicating it retains the original motion's character while fulfilling the spatial constraint. On motion refinement (Table 2), DNO-GMD achieves the best FID (0.10–0.13) and MPJPE (7.0–7.5 cm) across all joint configurations, while the GMD baseline struggles with fine pose signals (MPJPE > 25 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DNO against two baselines: GMD (Guided Motion Diffusion, ICCV 2023) and HuMoR (ICCV 2021). On motion editing, DNO-MDM Edit decisively outperforms GMD on all metrics except Foot Skating on crawling (0.08 vs. 0.04): DNO achieves 0.00 m Objective Error (vs. GMD's 0.15–0.22 m), 0.92–0.95 Content Preservation (vs. GMD's 0.59–0.79), and substantially lower Jitter (e.g., 1.31 vs. 3.07 for jumping). On motion refinement, DNO-GMD achieves the best FID (0.10–0.13) and MPJPE (7.0–7.5 cm) across all joint configurations, outperforming both HuMoR* (FID 0.87–1.40, MPJPE 7.2–9.3 cm) and the GMD baseline (FID 6.88–7.07, MPJPE 25.7–31.2 cm). The GMD baseline, while producing smooth motions (low Jitter ~0.8), fails to satisfy fine pose constraints and can only follow trajectory guidance, resulting in MPJPE > 25 cm. DNO's advantage stems from computing the criterion exactly on the fully denoised output rather than on an intermediate expected value, eliminating the approximation error inherent in guided diffusion methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DNO extends the latent-optimization paradigm from GANs (StyleGAN2) and the image-editing work DOODL to the motion domain, but with a key simplification: it shows that an invertible ODE is not required, only standard backpropagation through a DDIM-ODE solver. It departs from guided diffusion methods like GMD, which compute the criterion on an expected x̂₀ at each intermediate denoising step (introducing approximation error), by instead computing the loss exactly on the fully denoised output. The method is model-agnostic and builds directly on pre-trained text-to-motion diffusion models (MDM, MLD, GMD) without any fine-tuning, positioning itself as a training-free alternative to task-specific architectures like EgoEgo, AGRoL, and AvatarPoser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DNO offers a genuinely unified, plug-and-play framework where any new motion task reduces to writing a differentiable loss function, eliminating the need to train a separate model per task. This is practically valuable for motion researchers who need to handle diverse constraints (trajectories, obstacles, partial observations) without retraining. Key limitations include the ~3-minute optimization time per edit (limiting interactivity), dependence on base-model quality, and degraded performance when body coverage is sparse. Open questions include whether the normalized-gradient and decorrelation-loss tricks transfer to other diffusion domains (image, video, 3D), and whether faster solvers (distilled or high-order) can make the approach interactive.
