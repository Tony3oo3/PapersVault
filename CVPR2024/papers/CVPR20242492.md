## Problem

Quantifying epistemic uncertainty in pre-trained Neural Radiance Fields remains an open challenge: existing approaches are either heuristic proxies without theoretical guarantees or probabilistic methods (ensembles, variational Bayesian NeRFs) that demand costly additional training or intricate modifications to the training pipeline. No prior work provides a post-hoc, architecture-independent framework that derives uncertainty from first principles on an already-trained NeRF.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242492/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained NeRF of any architecture (treated as a differentiable black box) and the ground-truth camera parameters of the training views; it does not require the training images themselves or any additional training. The output is a spatial uncertainty field U: R³ → R⁺ stored on a uniform M³ grid (default M=256) with trilinear interpolation, occupying ~64 MB (25% of the original model size) and computable in ~90 seconds on an RTX 6000. The field renders as an additional volumetric color channel with no overhead on rendering speed.

## Method

Bayes' Rays reparametrizes the NeRF by introducing a spatial deformation field D_θ stored on a grid of M³ vertices, which perturbs input coordinates (x → x + D_θ(x)) before querying the frozen NeRF network. A Bayesian Laplace approximation is then performed around θ* = 0 (the mode, since zero deformation recovers the original NeRF), yielding a posterior covariance Σ = −H(0)⁻¹. The Hessian is approximated via the Fisher information identity, reducing second derivatives to products of first-order Jacobians J_θ(r) computed by backpropagation over R sampled rays, plus a Gaussian prior term. Because each grid vertex only affects its local cells, H is sparse, so only diagonal entries are retained (following Ritter et al.), giving per-vertex marginal variances that define spatial ellipsoids of allowable deformation. The resulting uncertainty field is computed entirely from the pre-trained model and camera parameters, requiring no training images, no ensemble, and no architecture-specific knowledge.

## Results

On the uncertainty evaluation task (AUSE, lower is better), Bayes' Rays achieves values of 0.17–0.35 across 8 ScanNet and Light Field scenes, closely matching a 10-model ensemble (0.15–0.36) while requiring no additional training. In the NeRF clean-up application, Bayes' Rays-best achieves PSNR 18.27, SSIM 0.60, and LPIPS 0.27 with 0.70 coverage, outperforming Nerfbusters (PSNR 17.99, coverage 0.63) in under 2 minutes versus 20 minutes. The uncertainty field is computed in ~90 seconds on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 with only 64 MB of additional storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CF-NeRF [1], the previous state-of-the-art for NeRF uncertainty quantification, Bayes' Rays achieves substantially lower AUSE across all 8 scenes (e.g., 0.22 vs. 0.97 on 'torch', 0.17 vs. 0.46 on 'statue'), indicating far better calibration of the uncertainty field to actual depth error. Compared to a 10-model ensemble (the gold standard for epistemic uncertainty), Bayes' Rays matches or nearly matches AUSE values (e.g., 0.28 vs. 0.28 on #0000, 0.35 vs. 0.36 on #0079) while requiring 10× less time and memory since no additional NeRFs need to be trained. In the NeRF clean-up task, Bayes' Rays-best surpasses Nerfbusters [52] on PSNR (18.27 vs. 17.99) and SSIM (0.60 vs. 0.60, tied) while maintaining higher pixel coverage (0.70 vs. 0.63) and running ~12× faster (1.6 min vs. 20 min). Against the unmodified Nerfacto baseline [49], the clean-up improves PSNR from 16.83 to 18.27 and LPIPS from 0.39 to 0.27, confirming that thresholding the uncertainty field effectively removes floater artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the diagonal Laplace approximation framework of Ritter et al. [38] to the NeRF setting, but departs from prior NeRF uncertainty methods (CF-NeRF, Stochastic NeRF, density-aware ensembles) that all require modifying the training pipeline or training multiple models. The key conceptual move is reparametrizing the problem onto a spatial deformation field rather than network weights, drawing on photogrammetry's tradition of modeling geometric uncertainty as spatial Gaussians and on deformation fields from computer graphics. This reparametrization makes the method architecture-independent and post-hoc, requiring only the trained NeRF and camera parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Bayes' Rays provides the first architecture-agnostic, training-free uncertainty field for NeRFs, enabling practical applications like real-time floater removal without retraining. However, it captures only epistemic uncertainty (missing/occluded data), not aleatoric effects like lighting changes or transient objects, and the uniform grid discretization incurs memory costs in geometrically sparse regions. Open questions include extending the framework to 3D Gaussian Splatting, incorporating off-diagonal Hessian entries for correlated parameters, and combining with aleatoric uncertainty estimators for a complete picture.
