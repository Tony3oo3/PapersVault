## Problem

Estimating physical properties (mass, friction, hardness) of objects from visual appearance alone remains challenging because labeled ground-truth data is expensive to acquire and the interior of objects is inherently unobservable from surface views. Prior work either requires task-specific training data for a single property type or relies on dynamic interaction, leaving no general-purpose, annotation-free method for dense multi-property prediction from static images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of posed multi-view RGB images of a single object (optionally with segmentation masks) and produces a continuous 3D physical property field queryable at any occupied point, yielding per-point values for mass density, kinetic friction coefficient, or Shore hardness. For volumetric properties like total mass, it additionally outputs a scalar object-level estimate obtained by integrating density over LLM-estimated surface thickness. The entire pipeline is training-free: no task-specific weights are learned, and the same feature field supports querying arbitrary physical properties by simply changing the LLM prompt.

## Method

NeRF2Physics decomposes physical property estimation into object-level semantic reasoning and point-level appearance reasoning. First, a NeRF (Nerfacto) is trained on the posed images to recover 3D geometry, from which source points are extracted via median depth rendering and voxel downsampling. Per-patch CLIP features (56×56) are fused into each source point through visibility-aware averaging across all input views, creating a language-embedded point cloud. A BLIP-2 caption of a canonical view is then passed to an LLM (GPT-3.5 or GPT-4) to produce a dictionary of K candidate materials with their physical property values. For each source point, a zero-shot CLIP-based kernel regression computes the property as a softmax-weighted average over the dictionary, where weights are cosine similarities between the point's fused CLIP embedding and each material name's text embedding, scaled by a temperature parameter. For mass estimation, the LLM additionally predicts per-material thickness, and the total mass is computed by integrating density over surface-anchored cuboids of size d×d×τ, with a validation-tuned scaling factor and depth-carving upper bound to correct for geometric bias.

## Results

On the ABO-500 mass estimation benchmark, NeRF2Physics achieves an ADE of 8.73 kg, ALDE of 0.771, and MnRE of 0.552, outperforming all three baselines on these metrics. For per-point friction estimation on the in-house dataset, the method achieves the best ADE (0.155), ALDE (0.321), MnRE (0.736), and PRA (0.800) across all baselines. For per-point Shore hardness, NeRF2Physics achieves the highest PRA (0.710) while remaining competitive on absolute error metrics with GPT-4V and CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRF2Physics outperforms Image2mass [35] by a wide margin on ADE (8.73 vs. 12.50 kg), ALDE (0.771 vs. 1.792), and MnRE (0.552 vs. 0.341), though Image2mass has a slightly lower APE (0.976 vs. 1.061) due to its tendency to underestimate small objects. The 2D CNN baseline fails to learn meaningful patterns, producing an extreme APE of 14.459 and the worst ADE (15.43 kg). LLaVA [22] is the weakest baseline overall (ADE 17.33, MnRE 0.306), as it tends to give imprecise round-number answers despite prompt engineering. For friction estimation, NeRF2Physics beats GPT-4V [41] on every metric (e.g., PRA 0.800 vs. 0.467, ADE 0.155 vs. 0.209), while for hardness it achieves the best PRA (0.710 vs. 0.609 for GPT-4V) but is slightly behind on ADE (34.30 vs. 32.75). Notably, GPT-4V must be queried per point, making it computationally expensive, whereas NeRF2Physics queries thousands of points at negligible cost once the feature field is built.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes three established components—NeRF for 3D geometry, CLIP for appearance-to-semantics mapping, and LLMs for open-vocabulary material reasoning—into a unified training-free pipeline for dense physical property prediction. It departs from prior visual physics reasoning work (e.g., Image2mass, Galileo) that either requires task-specific labeled data, observes object dynamics, or interacts with a physics engine, by instead leveraging grounded LLM knowledge about materials to enable zero-shot estimation of arbitrary properties. The language-embedded point cloud construction draws on CLIP-fields and LERF for 3D feature fusion, but replaces object-level region proposals with per-patch averaging to support intra-object material discrimination, a requirement absent in those prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to estimate diverse physical properties (mass, friction, hardness) in a zero-shot, annotation-free manner from posed images opens the door to physically grounded digital twins, robot simulation, and agricultural biomass estimation without expensive manual measurement. Key limitations include reliance on visible surfaces only (interior geometry is approximated via LLM-estimated thickness), dependence on LLM accuracy for material property values, and evaluation on relatively small datasets (100 test objects for mass, 31 points for hardness, 6 points for friction). Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes, whether more powerful VQA models can replace the two-stage captioning-plus-LLM pipeline, and how to reason about occluded internal structures.
