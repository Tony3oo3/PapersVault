## Problem

Existing protective perturbation methods (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, Glaze) claim to prevent Stable Diffusion from learning protected images during fine-tuning, but their effectiveness has not been systematically evaluated under realistic threat conditions. This paper demonstrates that these protections are fragile in practice—sensitive to the choice of fine-tuning method, the proportion of protected vs. unprotected images, and common natural transformations like JPEG compression and Gaussian blur—and further shows that adversarial purification can effectively remove the perturbations entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241071/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GrIDPure takes a high-resolution (512×512) RGB image bearing an imperceptible adversarial perturbation (from AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, or Glaze) and produces a purified image at the same resolution with the perturbation removed while preserving structural details. Because the underlying unconditional diffusion model operates at 256×256, the method employs grid-based cropping to handle the resolution mismatch, dividing the input into overlapping 256×256 patches. The purified output is evaluated for both structural fidelity (SSIM, PSNR against the clean original) and downstream learnability (FID, CLIP-Score after Stable Diffusion fine-tuning).

## Method

GrIDPure extends DiffPure with two key mechanisms: (1) iterative small-step SDEdit purification, where a single large-step denoising process is replaced by multiple iterations of small-step forward-noise-then-denoise cycles, which better preserves high-frequency image details while still removing the perturbation; and (2) grid-based cropping, where the high-resolution image is divided into overlapping 256×256 grids (with 128-pixel overlaps ensuring every pixel belongs to at least two grids), each purified independently by the small-step SDEdit process, then merged back via averaging in overlap regions to eliminate seam artifacts. The full grid-purify-merge pipeline is iterated multiple times, with each iteration blending the purified result back toward the original image using a weight γ to regulate the purification rate and further protect structural integrity. This design allows effective purification at 512×512 resolution using only a 256×256 unconditional diffusion model, and the paper shows it outperforms standard DiffPure in both SSIM/PSNR (e.g., 0.92 vs. 0.64 SSIM for AdvDM) and downstream generative quality after Stable Diffusion fine-tuning.

## Results

GrIDPure achieves CLIP-Score of 0.8526 (CelebA-HQ + AdvDM) and 0.8758 (WikiArt + AdvDM) for purified images, substantially outperforming DiffPure at t=100 (0.7400 and 0.7843 respectively). In terms of structural preservation, GrIDPure reaches PSNR of 30.60 and SSIM of 0.9199, compared to DiffPure(t=100) at 22.24 and 0.6378, indicating far better retention of original image details. On the evaluation side, natural transformations (JPEG compression, Gaussian blur) reduce the FID of protected CelebA-HQ images from 424.7 to 129.8 under LoRA fine-tuning, effectively breaking the protective perturbation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GrIDPure is compared against DiffPure (the state-of-the-art diffusion-based purification) across three protective perturbation methods (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, Glaze) on two datasets. GrIDPure consistently outperforms DiffPure on CLIP-Score: e.g., 0.8526 vs. 0.7400 (CelebA-HQ + AdvDM) and 0.8758 vs. 0.7843 (WikiArt + AdvDM), indicating that purified images retain more of the original semantic content for Stable Diffusion to learn. On structural fidelity (PSNR/SSIM), GrIDPure achieves 30.60/0.9199 versus DiffPure(t=100) at 22.24/0.6378, a substantial gap showing that DiffPure's large timesteps destroy image structure while GrIDPure's small-step iterative approach preserves it. On the evaluation side, the paper shows that all four protective perturbation baselines (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, Glaze, ImprovedAdvDM) are vulnerable: natural transformations like JPEG and Gaussian blur reduce FID by 60–70% (e.g., 424.7→129.8 for AdvDM+LoRA on CelebA-HQ), and even EoT-optimized perturbations fail to maintain robustness. The paper also demonstrates that adaptive attacks (Diff-PGD-style) against DiffPure do not restore protection, confirming that the inherent instability of generative-model adversarial perturbations makes them fundamentally fragile against purification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: (1) protective adversarial perturbations for Stable Diffusion (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth, Glaze, ImprovedAdvDM) that aim to prevent fine-tuning from learning protected concepts, and (2) diffusion-based adversarial purification (DiffPure, SDEdit) originally developed for classification. The paper departs from prior protection work by reframing the problem as a practical security evaluation under a realistic threat model (multiple fine-tuning methods, mixed clean/protected data, natural internet transformations) rather than a controlled method proposal. On the purification side, GrIDPure extends DiffPure from low-resolution (256×256) classification images to high-resolution (512×512) generative-model training images via grid-based cropping and iterative small-step SDEdit, addressing the structure-preservation requirement that classification purification does not face.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that protective perturbations are fragile under realistic conditions—natural transformations, mixed data ratios, and diffusion-based purification—challenges the assumed robustness of the entire adversarial-protection paradigm for generative AI and suggests that image owners may have a false sense of security. GrIDPure provides a practical, open tool that image exploiters (or security researchers auditing protections) can use to test whether a given perturbation method actually holds. Open questions include whether future protection methods can be made robust to purification, whether the grid-based approach generalizes to even higher resolutions or video, and how the arms race evolves as both protection and purification techniques improve. The paper also notes that GrIDPure works better for photographs and modern art than oil paintings, where oil texture is entangled with the perturbation.
