## Problem

Existing diffusion-based methods generate video and audio in isolation, leaving a gap for open-domain joint visual-audio generation where both modalities are created simultaneously and remain semantically aligned. Prior joint generation work (e.g., MM-Diffusion) is unconditional, restricted to a narrow training domain, and lacks text-based semantic control, while separate V2A/A2V pipelines suffer from limited generation quality and poor cross-modal alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240270/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Depending on the task, the method accepts a text prompt (for joint or text-conditioned generation), a video clip (for V2A), an audio clip (for A2V), or a single image frame (for I2A) as the conditioning signal. The output is a generated audio waveform, a generated video clip, or a synchronized video-audio pair. Crucially, the conditioning modality is not fed directly into the generative network; instead, it is encoded by ImageBind and used only as a gradient target during the denoising loop, so no architectural modification to the base diffusion models is required.

## Method

The core mechanism is a training-free diffusion latent aligner that, at each denoising timestep, predicts the clean latent z̃₀ from the current noisy latent z_t, encodes it (and the conditioning modality) through ImageBind's shared embedding space, and computes a cross-modal distance loss. This loss is backpropagated to obtain a gradient on z_t, which is subtracted from the latent to nudge the sampling trajectory toward the condition—mirroring classifier guidance but operating in a multimodal semantic space rather than a single-class logit space. For joint video-audio generation the guidance is bidirectional, and a triangle loss (video–audio, video–text, audio–text) is used to compensate for the often sparse semantics of audio. A guided prompt tuning step additionally optimizes the text embedding vector shared across all timesteps to improve temporal coherence in audio-to-video generation. The entire procedure requires no dataset, no fine-tuning of the base models (AudioLDM, AnimateDiff), and only a small number of extra optimization steps per denoising step.

## Results

On the A2V task, the proposed method achieves FVD of 402.4 and KVD of 34.8, dramatically outperforming TempoTokens (FVD 1866.3, KVD 389.1) while also improving AV-align from 0.423 to 0.522. On V2A, the method achieves the best KL (2.619 vs. 3.290 for SpecVQGAN) and FD (32.92 vs. 37.27), with competitive FAD (7.32 vs. 7.74). For open-domain Joint-VA generation, the aligner improves AV-align from 0.226 (vanilla) to 0.283 and AT-alignbind from 0.081 to 0.138, demonstrating effective bidirectional cross-modal guidance without any training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On A2V, the proposed method overwhelmingly outperforms TempoTokens [48], reducing FVD from 1866.3 to 402.4 (4.6× improvement) and KVD from 389.1 to 34.8 (11× improvement), while also improving AV-align from 0.423 to 0.522. On V2A, the method beats SpecVQGAN [26] on KL (2.619 vs. 3.290) and FD (32.92 vs. 37.27), though SpecVQGAN retains a slight FAD advantage (7.736 vs. 7.316 is actually Ours winning; SpecVQGAN's 7.736 is worse). On I2A, the method achieves the best FAD (6.869 vs. 7.576 for Im2Wav [37]) but trails Im2Wav on KL (2.691 vs. 2.612), ISc (6.149 vs. 7.055), and FD (20.958 vs. 19.627), indicating Im2Wav still produces more semantically diverse audio from images. For open-domain Joint-VA, no direct external baseline exists; the method improves over its own vanilla pipeline on all alignment metrics (AV-align 0.283 vs. 0.226, AT-alignbind 0.138 vs. 0.081), and when plugged into MM-Diffusion [36] on the Landscape domain, it maintains video quality while boosting audio fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classifier-guidance mechanism from diffusion models [10] by replacing the trained classifier with ImageBind's multimodal embedding space, creating a training-free 'latent aligner' that steers denoising at inference time. It builds directly on pre-trained single-modality generators (AudioLDM for audio, AnimateDiff for video) and the contrastive multimodal representation learned by ImageBind [17], rather than training a joint model from scratch. Conceptually, it departs from prior joint generation work like MM-Diffusion [36], which learns a unified diffusion process over paired audio-video data in a fixed domain, by instead bridging two independent diffusion models through a shared semantic space at test time. The guided prompt tuning component extends the standard text-conditioning paradigm by making the prompt embedding itself an optimization variable, a technique borrowed from prompt-tuning literature but applied here to enforce cross-modal consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, optimization-based formulation makes multimodal generation accessible without large-scale paired datasets or expensive co-training, which is practically significant for industry adoption. However, the approach inherits limitations: inference is slower due to per-timestep optimization, the quality ceiling is bounded by the individual base models, and the reliance on ImageBind's embedding quality means alignment is only as good as that model's cross-modal understanding. Open questions include whether the method scales to longer videos or more complex multi-modal compositions (e.g., music + speech + video), whether the prompt-tuning component can be replaced by learned adapters for efficiency, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the audio-visual pair to other modality combinations. The paper also reveals that audio often lacks sufficient semantic content to guide visual generation alone, suggesting that text remains a necessary anchor for robust cross-modal alignment.
