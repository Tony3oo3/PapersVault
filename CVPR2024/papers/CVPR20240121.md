## Problem

Existing monocular human avatar methods face a fundamental trade-off: neural field approaches (NeRF-based) achieve photorealism but lack explicit geometry, are slow to render, and require ill-posed backward mapping for articulation, while mesh-based methods support fast rendering and forward kinematics but cannot model rich appearance or topological changes. No prior representation simultaneously delivers high-fidelity rendering, real-time speed, compact storage, and seamless graphics-pipeline compatibility for animatable humans.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular RGB video along with per-frame SMPL pose estimates (from PARE) and subject masks (from MediaPipe), plus camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It produces a canonical Gaussians-on-Mesh representation (3.63 MB per subject) that, given any target pose and camera, renders an RGB image, alpha mask, and normal map at 43 FPS on an A100 GPU. The rendered image is factorized into a pseudo albedo map (from Gaussian splatting) multiplied by a pseudo shading map (predicted from the mesh normal map), enabling view-dependent appearance without explicit lighting estimation.

## Method

GoMAvatar introduces the Gaussians-on-Mesh (GoM) representation, which attaches one 3D Gaussian to each triangle face of a skeleton-driven deformable mesh. Each Gaussian's rotation, scale, and color are learned in the triangle's local coordinate frame and transformed to world coordinates via a face-dependent matrix, so the Gaussian shape dynamically adapts as the mesh deforms across poses. Articulation proceeds in the forward direction (canonical → observation) using linear blend skinning plus a pose-conditioned non-rigid deformation MLP, avoiding the ill-posed backward mapping required by NeRF-based methods. Rendering combines Gaussian splatting (for the pseudo albedo and alpha mask) with mesh rasterization (for the normal map), from which a 1×1 convolutional shading network predicts the pseudo shading map. Training is supervised with L1, LPIPS, and mask losses plus mesh regularizations (Laplacian smoothing, normal consistency, color smoothness), and a GoM subdivision step refines geometry by splitting each face into four sub-faces.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap, GoMAvatar achieves 30.37 dB PSNR / 0.9689 SSIM / 32.53 LPIPS* for novel view synthesis and 30.34 dB PSNR / 0.9688 SSIM / 32.39 LPIPS* for novel pose synthesis, matching or surpassing the best competing method (MonoHuman). Inference runs at 23.2 ms/frame (≈43 FPS) on an A100 GPU, making it 257× faster than MonoHuman and 76× faster than HumanNeRF, while using only 3.63 MB of parameters per subject. On PeopleSnapshot, GoMAvatar reaches 30.68 dB PSNR and 0.9767 SSIM at 25.82 ms/frame, outperforming InstantAvatar (28.61 dB / 71.26 ms) and Anim-NeRF (28.89 dB / 217 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MonoHuman (the strongest baseline), GoMAvatar wins on PSNR for both novel view (30.37 vs 30.26) and novel pose (30.34 vs 30.05) synthesis, and is 257× faster (23.2 ms vs 5970 ms) with 77× less memory (3.63 vs 280.67 MB), though MonoHuman has a slightly lower LPIPS* (30.92 vs 32.53). Against HumanNeRF, GoMAvatar improves PSNR by ~0.76 dB on novel view and ~0.6 dB on novel pose while being 76× faster and using 68× less memory. Against Neural Body, GoMAvatar gains ~1.65 dB PSNR on novel view and is ~9× faster with 4.6× less memory. Against NeuMan, GoMAvatar achieves ~1.4 dB higher PSNR and is 147× faster, though NeuMan uses slightly less memory (2.27 vs 3.63 MB). On PeopleSnapshot, GoMAvatar outperforms InstantAvatar by 2.07 dB PSNR and 8.4× faster inference, and beats Anim-NeRF by 1.79 dB PSNR at 8.4× lower latency, while matching Anim-NeRF's LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoMAvatar sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: 3D Gaussian splatting for fast radiance field rendering (Kerbl et al., 2023) and SMPL-based articulated human modeling (HumanNeRF, MonoHuman, Neural Body). It departs from prior NeRF-based human avatars by replacing volume rendering with a hybrid Gaussians-on-Mesh representation that attaches one Gaussian per triangle face in local coordinates, enabling forward-kinematics articulation via linear blend skinning rather than the ill-posed backward mapping used by implicit methods. The pseudo albedo / pseudo shading color decomposition draws on the view-dependent appearance modeling in NeRF but implements it through a lightweight 1×1 convolution on mesh normals rather than a full neural radiance field. The pose refinement module is directly adapted from HumanNeRF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 257× speedup over MonoHuman (23.2 ms vs. 5970 ms per frame) and 77× memory reduction (3.63 MB vs. 280 MB) while matching or exceeding rendering quality make real-time, memory-constrained deployment of animatable human avatars practical for AR/VR, gaming, and interactive applications. The explicit mesh geometry also resolves a key limitation of NeRF-based avatars: inability to handle self-penetration poses (sitting, leg-crossing) and incompatibility with standard graphics pipelines like OpenGL. Open questions include whether the GoM representation generalizes to scene-agnostic (multi-identity) settings, whether the 'pseudo' albedo/shading decomposition can be made physically meaningful, and how the approach scales to more complex clothed or multi-person scenes.
