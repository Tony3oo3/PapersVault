## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods produce irregular triangle meshes with sliver faces that are unsuitable for artist workflows, animation, and subdivision, while implicit representations cannot be directly used in production rendering pipelines. Quad-dominant meshes solve these issues but prior re-meshing techniques are non-differentiable, preventing end-to-end correction of topology errors during optimization. This paper bridges the gap by making field-aligned quad re-meshing differentiable and integrating it into an inverse rendering framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images with known camera extrinsics and foreground masks, assuming a single unknown environment light. It outputs a lightweight quad-dominant base mesh (typically ~32k vertices) plus a neural displacement field, spatially-varying PBR materials (diffuse and specular ORM), and an environment light probe. The quad mesh is subdivided via Catmull-Clark to pixel resolution at render time, so the stored geometry remains editable while the rendered surface achieves pixel-level fidelity. After convergence, materials are re-baked into 2D textures for direct use in production renderers and game engines.

## Method

The surface is represented by an MLP-based SDF with multi-resolution hash grid encoding, extracted to a triangle mesh via differentiable Marching Cubes at each iteration. A novel self-learning mechanism uses an o-MLP and p-MLP to predict initial orientation and position fields, which are then smoothed using the standard field-aligned re-meshing procedure; the MLPs are supervised by von-Mises-based losses (L_o) and tangent-space L2 losses (L_p) against the smoothed fields with stop-gradient, enabling stable topology learning across iterations. A vector-quantization-style gradient skip (Eq. 11) copies gradients from the smoothed fields back to the initial predictions, bypassing the non-differentiable smoothing steps while still supervising the SDF. Pixel-level details below the quad face size are captured by differentiable Catmull-Clark subdivision followed by a bounded displacement field (tanh-scaled by half the remeshing edge length), implicitly decomposing the surface into low-frequency geometry and high-frequency displacement. The entire pipeline—SDF, orientation/position fields, displacement, PBR materials, and light probe—is optimized end-to-end via differentiable rendering with image-space, mask, field, and regularization losses.

## Results

On the Myles dataset (79 objects), QUADify with displacement (Our Disp.) achieves the best Chamfer Distance (4.63×10⁻⁵) and F1 score (0.70), while the base quad mesh (Our Quad) reduces Aspect Ratio and Radius Ratio outliers above threshold 4 to 0.00% and 0.24% respectively, compared to 6.69–16.89% for triangle-based baselines. On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, the quad-dominant mesh achieves a visible Chamfer Distance of 3.2×10⁻² (3.0 with displacement), substantially outperforming Nvdiffrec (7.8×10⁻²) while using fewer base vertices (32k vs. 40k). View interpolation PSNR is competitive at 27.96 dB, slightly below Nvdiffrec (29.06 dB) but with far superior geometric regularity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Marching Cubes and DMTet, QUADify dramatically reduces irregular face outliers: AR>4 drops from 12.02%/16.89% to 0.00% and RR>4 from 12.01%/16.32% to 0.24%, while matching or slightly exceeding their Chamfer Distance. Compared to the concurrent FlexiCubes, QUADify's base quad mesh achieves comparable CD (4.96 vs. 4.87) with fewer vertices (10.66k vs. 11.87k) and near-zero AR/RR outliers (0.00%/0.24% vs. 6.69%/8.26%), and with displacement surpasses FlexiCubes on both CD (4.63 vs. 4.87) and F1 (0.70 vs. 0.69). On the NeRF Synthetic view interpolation task, Nvdiffrec retains a small PSNR advantage (29.06 vs. 27.96 dB), but QUADify's visible Chamfer Distance is more than 2× better (3.2 vs. 7.8 ×10⁻²), reflecting the benefit of quad faces aligned to surface features. On the real-world NeRD dataset, QUADify produces smoother base geometry and recovers fine details (e.g., grooves in the Gold Cape scene) as displacement that Nvdiffrec's triangle mesh cannot represent without artifacts. The key differentiator is that QUADify is the only method producing quad-dominant topology that supports artifact-free Catmull-Clark subdivision and displacement, as demonstrated in the DMTet-vs-Quad comparison (Fig. 10) where subdivided triangle meshes exhibit visible shading artifacts absent in the quad mesh.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: neural implicit surface reconstruction (NeuS, Neuralangelo), differentiable inverse rendering for material/lighting decomposition (Nvdiffrec), and field-aligned quad re-meshing (InstantMeshes, QuadriFlow). The key departure is making the entire pipeline from SDF through re-meshing, Catmull-Clark subdivision, and displacement fully differentiable, whereas prior quad re-meshing methods are non-differentiable post-processing steps and prior differentiable mesh extractors (DMTet, FlexiCubes) produce triangle meshes. The self-learning of orientation and position fields via MLPs, combined with a vector-quantization-style gradient skip to backpropagate through the discrete smoothing iterations, is the central technical novelty that enables end-to-end optimization of topology alongside geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for production 3D pipelines: the output is a lightweight, editable quad-dominant mesh with displacement and PBR textures directly compatible with Blender and game engines, eliminating the manual cleanup artists currently perform on triangle-mesh photogrammetry or NeRF extractions. The main limitation acknowledged is that the continuous MLP-based SDF struggles with concave regions and topology changes (e.g., holes), as seen in the GOLD CAPE failure. Open questions include whether the approach can handle scenes with complex topology (genus > 0), dynamic objects, or single-image inputs, and whether the progressive subdivision strategy generalizes to higher-frequency details beyond two subdivision levels.
