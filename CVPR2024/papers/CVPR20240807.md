## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language reasoning methods lack explicit situational awareness: they fail to ground an embodied agent's self-location and orientation from a textual description before performing question answering. The SQA3D baseline's situational estimation is nearly at random level, and a pilot study shows that even ground-truth situational vectors, when fed directly into existing architectures, contribute negligibly to QA performance. This reveals a missing registration between situational descriptions and 3D scene embeddings that prevents generalizable 3D reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (voxelized at 0.02 m resolution and projected to a bird's-eye-view x-y plane yielding 256 visual tokens), a free-form situational description (e.g., 'I am facing a trash can, while there is a door on my right'), and a question. It outputs a 6-DoF situational vector — a 3D position (x, y, z) and a 6D rotation representation (with pitch fixed to 0, i.e., parallel to the ground plane) — plus a textual answer produced either by classification over candidate answers (SQA3D) or auto-regressive generation (ScanQA).

## Method

SIG3D tokenizes the scene with a CLIP-distilled Minkowski sparse 3D convolutional network (OpenScene) and encodes text with a fine-tuned SBERT-MPNet. The key architectural insight is an anchor-based situational estimation: each visual token (voxel) serves as an anchor point that predicts a position likelihood (trained with a Gaussian soft label and CenterPoint-style peak enlarging) and a 6D rotation vector, converting the high-dimensional 3D localization problem into a per-token classification. After selecting the peak-likelihood token as the estimated position, the model re-encodes all visual tokens under a repositioned and reoriented coordinate frame (origin at the estimated position, y-axis aligned with the estimated heading) using new situational positional embeddings followed by self- and cross-attention layers that fuse situation and question context. A large transformer decoder then fuses the situation-guided visual tokens with question tokens to produce the final answer.

## Results

On SQA3D, SIG3D achieves 52.6% EM@1 overall, outperforming the previous best 3D-VisTA (48.5%) by 4.1 points and 3D-LLM (48.1%) by 4.5 points. On the situational estimation task, SIG3D achieves 27.4% Acc@0.5m and 28.7% Acc@15°, surpassing 3D-VisTA (11.7% / 16.9%) by more than 2x and the original SQA3D baseline (9.5% / 8.7%) by over 3x. On ScanQA (general 3D QA without situational descriptions), SIG3D achieves BLEU-1 39.5, BLEU-4 12.4, ROUGE 35.9, and CIDEr 68.8, matching or slightly exceeding 3D-LLM (39.3 / 12.0 / 35.7 / 69.4) without large-scale 3D-text pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIG3D outperforms all 11 baselines on SQA3D overall EM@1 (52.6%), with the largest margins over GPT-3 (+11.6), ClipBERT (+9.3), and the original SQA3D baseline (+5.4). Against the strongest 3D-specific competitors, SIG3D beats 3D-VisTA (48.5%) by 4.1 points and 3D-LLM (48.1%) by 4.5 points, notably without relying on large-scale 3D-text pretraining (3D-VisTA) or LLM backbones (3D-LLM, GPT-3). On situational estimation, SIG3D's anchor-based approach yields 27.4% Acc@0.5m, more than doubling 3D-VisTA (11.7%) and tripling the SQA3D baseline (9.5%), confirming that the prior methods' direct regression strategy fails to ground situational descriptions in 3D space. On ScanQA general QA, SIG3D is essentially on par with 3D-LLM: it edges ahead on BLEU-1 (39.5 vs. 39.3), BLEU-4 (12.4 vs. 12.0), and ROUGE (35.9 vs. 35.7), but trails slightly on METEOR (13.4 vs. 14.5) and CIDEr (68.8 vs. 69.4), indicating that the situational-awareness gains transfer to general 3D QA without degrading open-ended generation quality. The GPT-3 baseline remains the strongest on the 'What' question category (39.7 vs. 35.6), suggesting that a stronger language encoder could further complement SIG3D's situational grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on OpenScene's pretrained sparse voxel encoder for 3D visual tokenization, CenterPoint's anchor-based position likelihood formulation for narrowing the 3D search space, and the 6D rotation representation from Zhou et al. for orientation estimation. It departs from SQA3D's direct-regression approach to situational estimation by reframing it as an anchor-based classification problem, and from 3D-VisTA's reliance on large-scale 3D-text pretraining by showing that architectural design (situation-guided re-encoding) matters more than pretraining scale for situational awareness. The key conceptual contribution is the two-stage pipeline: first ground the textual situation description into a 3D vector, then re-encode visual tokens from that grounded perspective before multi-modal fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pilot study revealing that existing methods' situational estimation loss is essentially inert—corrupting or removing GT situational vectors barely changes QA accuracy—exposes a fundamental architectural gap in how 3D VL models integrate spatial self-localization. The oracle experiment further shows that even providing ground-truth situation directly as input fails to improve performance, underscoring that the problem is not estimation accuracy but the lack of a mechanism to interpret and propagate situational context into visual features. Limitations include evaluation only on indoor ScanNet-derived scenes, modest QA gains (~3-4%) relative to the large situational estimation gains, and no integration with LLM backbones, leaving open whether the approach transfers to outdoor or robotic navigation settings.
