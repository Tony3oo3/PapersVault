## Problem

Prior embodied question answering benchmarks were limited to closed-vocabulary answers, synthetic or single-frame scenes, and non-interactive settings, making them poorly suited to evaluating modern foundation models in realistic deployment scenarios. No existing benchmark simultaneously supported both episodic-memory (smart glasses) and active-exploration (mobile robot) settings with open-vocabulary, human-generated questions from real-world environments and an automated evaluation protocol.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242183/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For EM-EQA, the agent receives a natural language question and an episode history of 50–75 uniformly sampled RGB-D frames from real-world scans (ScanNet, HM3D); for A-EQA, the agent is spawned in a Habitat simulator and must generate its own observations via frontier exploration before answering. The output is a free-form natural language answer scored on a 1–5 scale by GPT-4 (LLM-Match), with object-localization questions carrying five ground-truth answers to accommodate spatially equivalent phrasings. A-EQA additionally reports an efficiency score that penalizes longer exploration paths relative to a scripted ground-truth trajectory.

## Method

OpenEQA is a zero-shot benchmark of 1,636 crowd-sourced questions across seven categories (object/attribute/state recognition, localization, spatial reasoning, functional reasoning, world knowledge) drawn from 180+ real-world environments, validated by independent annotators. Evaluation uses an LLM-Match metric where GPT-4 scores model answers against human ground truth on a 1–5 similarity scale, validated to correlate with human judgements at Spearman ρ = 0.909. Baseline agents span four families—blind LLMs (GPT-4, LLaMA-2-70B), Socratic LLMs augmented with LLaVA frame captions or object-centric scene-graphs (ConceptGraph, Sparse Voxel Map), and multi-frame VLMs (GPT-4V processing 50 frames directly)—all used without fine-tuning. A force-a-guess protocol substitutes the corresponding blind LLM's answer when a perceptual agent abstains, preventing conservative refusal from inflating failure rates. The best model (GPT-4V) achieves 49.6% LLM-Match versus 86.8% for humans, with the largest gaps on spatial and localization questions.

## Results

On the OpenEQA benchmark, GPT-4V (Multi-Frame VLM) achieves the best model score of 49.6% LLM-Match on EM-EQA (ALL), significantly outperforming the next-best agent (GPT-4 w/ LLaVA-1.5 at 43.6%) and blind GPT-4 (33.5%). Human performance reaches 86.8% on EM-EQA, revealing a 37.2-point gap between the best model and humans. On A-EQA, GPT-4V scores 41.8% LLM-Match with 7.5% efficiency, while all agents show degraded performance compared to EM-EQA due to exhaustive frontier exploration. Blind LLMs (GPT-4: 33.5%, LLaMA-2: 28.3%) serve as surprisingly strong baselines, indicating substantial world-knowledge priors in current LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V (49.6% EM-EQA ALL) outperforms all other model baselines, beating the best Socratic agent (GPT-4 w/ LLaVA-1.5 at 43.6%) by 6.0 points and blind GPT-4 (33.5%) by 16.1 points, suggesting tight perception-language integration is critical for EQA. Among Socratic agents, GPT-4 consistently outperforms LLaMA-2 by 5–9 points across all perceptual backends (frame captions, ConceptGraph, SVM), indicating LLM capacity is a key factor. Notably, scene-graph representations (ConceptGraph: 36.5%, SVM: 38.9%) do not surpass simple frame captions (43.6%) for GPT-4, and in some cases perform worse, suggesting current scene-graph pipelines lose information relative to raw visual frames. On A-EQA, all agents degrade substantially (e.g., GPT-4 w/ ConceptGraph drops from 36.5% to 34.4%), and efficiency scores remain very low (4.3–7.5%), highlighting that frontier exploration is far from question-targeted. The 37.2-point gap between GPT-4V and humans (86.8%) underscores that no current foundation model approaches human-level embodied understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper modernizes the Embodied Question Answering (EQA) task first introduced by Das et al. (2018) and extended by MT-EQA, MP3D-EQA, and IQA, which relied on closed-vocabulary answers, synthetic or limited real scenes, and single-setting formulations. The key conceptual departure is unifying episodic-memory EQA (smart-glasses-style) and active-exploration EQA (robot-style) under a single open-vocabulary framework grounded in real-world scanned environments (ScanNet, HM3D). For evaluation, it adapts the LLM-as-judge protocol from MMBench to handle open-ended natural language answers, replacing the closed-set matching used in prior EQA and VQA benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenEQA exposes a stark 37-point gap between the best model (GPT-4V at 49.6%) and human performance (86.8%), with spatial understanding and object localization being the primary failure modes across all agent families. The finding that structured scene-graph representations (ConceptGraph, SVM) do not outperform simple frame captions—even on spatial reasoning questions—challenges the assumption that explicit 3D structure helps LLMs reason about space. Open questions include designing question-targeted (rather than exhaustive) exploration for A-EQA, improving spatial grounding in VLMs, and whether the LLM-Match metric generalizes beyond GPT-4 as the judge.
