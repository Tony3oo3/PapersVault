## Problem

Existing Pruning-at-Initialization (PaI) methods that leverage Neural Tangent Kernel theory either neglect or loosely approximate the data-dependent component of the NTK spectrum, assuming data has minimal impact on identifying lottery tickets. Additionally, it remains unclear whether PaI can be applied to large pre-trained models without damaging their downstream transferability, a critical question as foundation models continue to grow in size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242699/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a neural network with its current parameters (randomly initialized or pre-trained) and a set of training data mini-batches to compute saliency scores. It produces a binary pruning mask of the same dimensionality as the parameter vector, which when applied yields a sparse subnetwork. The saliency is computed via two auxiliary networks of identical architecture—one with squared parameters and unit activations, the other with squared inputs and copied activation states—making the procedure compatible with standard automatic differentiation frameworks without requiring explicit NTK matrix construction.

## Method

PX derives a new analytical upper bound on the NTK trace by decomposing it into the product of the Frobenius norms of the Path Activation Matrix (data-dependent) and the Path Kernel (architecture- and initialization-dependent), then defines the saliency score as the gradient of their composite function R(x, θ, a) with respect to squared parameters. This gradient is computed efficiently via backpropagation through two auxiliary networks h and g that share the original architecture but use simplified inputs (squared data or unit vectors) and modified parameters (squared weights or ones). Because the resulting scores are strictly positive and the pruning is applied iteratively, PX satisfies the conditions of the Maximal Critical Compression theorem, guaranteeing avoidance of layer collapse. Unlike prior NTK-based methods that preserve only the data-independent Path Kernel trace, PX jointly accounts for data statistics, neuron activations, and layer connectivity in each parameter's relevance score.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, PX achieves 68.11% top-1 accuracy at 89.26% sparsity and 60.28% at 95.60% sparsity, outperforming all baselines at both levels. At extreme sparsity (98.20%) on CIFAR-100 with VGG-16, PX shows a considerable accuracy gap over all competitors including NTK-SAP. PX also preserves layer output width across iterative pruning rounds (unlike SynFlow and NTK-SAP) and avoids layer collapse in segmentation tasks where NTK-SAP fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PX outperforms NTK-SAP (the closest NTK-based competitor) by 0.13% at 89.26% and 0.44% at 95.60% sparsity on ImageNet, with the gap widening substantially at extreme sparsity on CIFAR-100 where NTK-SAP degrades sharply. Against GraSP, PX leads by 0.90% and 0.27% at the two ImageNet sparsity levels, and surpasses GraSP in execution time at T=2 pruning rounds (0.28s vs 0.39s at T=1). SNIP, a data-driven single-shot method, trails PX by 7.61% at 95.60% sparsity on ImageNet, though it remains competitive on smaller datasets. SynFlow (data-agnostic) is outperformed by 1.63% and 0.87% on ImageNet and suffers from exploding gradients when applied to pre-trained models. PX uniquely avoids layer collapse in segmentation (where NTK-SAP fails) and preserves output layer width across iterative rounds, whereas SynFlow and NTK-SAP exhibit progressive narrowing of output units.

## Relation to Prior Work

PX builds directly on the NTK-based pruning lineage initiated by SynFlow-L2 and refined by NTK-SAP, which decompose the NTK into a data-independent Path Kernel and a data-dependent Path Activation Matrix but either neglect or loosely approximate the latter. The key departure is providing an analytical upper bound on the NTK trace that explicitly couples both terms, yielding a saliency score that is jointly data-, architecture-, and initialization-dependent. This positions PX between the data-agnostic NTK family (SynFlow, NTK-SAP, PHEW) and the data-driven family (SNIP, GraSP, ProsPR), and extends the PaI setting to pre-trained models (supervised ImageNet, MoCo v2, CLIP), a context the authors identify as an open question in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By guaranteeing positive saliency scores and iterative application, PX provides a theoretical safeguard against layer collapse that data-agnostic NTK methods lack, and the spectral analysis confirms that the data-dependent term is essential for preserving the NTK eigenspectrum at high sparsity. The demonstration that PaI can be applied to large pre-trained vision models while retaining near-dense transferability on classification and segmentation is practically significant as foundation models continue to grow. However, the paper itself notes that for complex tasks like semantic segmentation, PaI methods still only approximate dense performance at trivial sparsity, and the NTK-SAP failure with pre-trained models (attributed to batch-normalization interference) highlights that data-free pruning remains fragile in the transfer-learning regime.
