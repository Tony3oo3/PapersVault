## Problem

While empirical methods for learning compatible representations exist to avoid reprocessing gallery images upon model updates, there is a lack of comprehensive theoretical understanding of what makes representations compatible. Specifically, no prior work establishes a formal relationship between stationarity (induced by fixed classifiers), neural collapse, and the formal compatibility definition, nor addresses the higher-order alignment challenge that arises when stationary models are sequentially fine-tuned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 from CIFAR-100R for fine-tuning, 32×32 from CIFAR-10 for evaluation) and produces d-dimensional feature vectors (d=1023, corresponding to K=1024 pre-allocated d-Simplex classes). The retrieval protocol is 1:N search: 50,000 query images are matched against 10,000 gallery images using a distance function, with the critical constraint that query and gallery features may come from different model versions (different fine-tuning steps or entirely different architectures) and must remain directly comparable without re-extraction.

## Method

The paper proves (Theorem 1) that stationary representations learned under a d-Simplex fixed classifier optimally approximate both compatibility inequality constraints in expectation, by modeling class features as hyperballs centered at fixed prototypes and analyzing expected inter-hyperball distances via the Ball Line Picking problem. To address the higher-order alignment gap that emerges when stationary models are sequentially fine-tuned (where first-order statistics already align), the authors introduce the Higher-Order Compatibility (HOC) loss: a convex combination of the d-Simplex cross-entropy loss and an infoNCE contrastive loss between consecutive model versions φ_t and φ_{t-1}. This combined loss is shown to be equivalent to solving the constrained optimization problem under the same-class compatibility inequality while capturing mutual information between old and new representations. The practical scenario (IAM-CL²R) demonstrates that a model undergoing sequential fine-tuning can be asynchronously replaced by a better pre-trained model—potentially with a different architecture (ResNet18→SENet18→RegNetY-400MF)—while maintaining seamless retrieval service without gallery reprocessing.

## Results

On CIFAR100R/10 with model replacements, d-Simplex-HOC achieves an Average Compatibility (AC) of 0.95 on the 7-task sequence and 0.65 on the 31-task sequence, far exceeding all baselines. In terms of Average multi-model Accuracy (AA), d-Simplex-HOC reaches 68.13% (AA₇) and 67.40% (AA₃₁), outperforming the next-best method d-Simplex-FD (56.58% and 56.27%) by over 11 points. All other baselines (ER, FAN, BCT-ER, LCE-ER, AdvBCT-ER, CVS) fail to achieve meaningful compatibility (AC ≈ 0) and score between 29–36% AA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

d-Simplex-HOC decisively outperforms all six baselines on both compatibility (AC) and accuracy (AA) metrics. The closest competitor, d-Simplex-FD [39], also uses a d-Simplex fixed classifier but achieves only AC = 0.05 (7 tasks) and 0.21 (31 tasks) versus 0.95 and 0.65 for d-Simplex-HOC, with AA roughly 11 points lower; the gap is attributed to the HOC loss capturing higher-order dependencies that cross-entropy alone misses. Among the non-stationary baselines, FAN [37], CVS [38], BCT-ER [27], LCE-ER [28], and AdvBCT-ER [36] all fail to achieve compatibility (AC = × or <0.01) and score between 29–36% AA, with CVS [38] and FAN [37] showing near-zero cross-test accuracy after model replacements. Notably, after asynchronous model replacement, methods like CVS and BCT-ER suffer a clear performance decay below the no-replacement baseline, whereas d-Simplex-HOC consistently improves with each replacement, even when the replacement involves a different architecture (SENet18 → RegNetY_400MF).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the missing theoretical bridge between two previously separate lines of work: the d-Simplex fixed classifier / regular polytope networks (Pernici et al.) that produce stationary representations, and the formal compatibility definition from Backward Compatible Training (Shen et al., CVPR 2020). The central theorem proves that stationarity under a d-Simplex classifier optimally approximates both inequality constraints of compatibility, grounding what was previously only empirical evidence. The HOC loss further connects to the constrained-optimization-to-convex-combination transformation from Jiang et al. (ICLR 2021) and to the Neural Collapse / UFM framework, unifying these threads into a single training objective. The paper departs from prior compatible-representation methods (BCT, LCE, CVS, FAN) by showing they fail under asynchronous model replacement, whereas the stationary approach handles this scenario natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IAM-CL²R scenario—where a production retrieval system asynchronously swaps in a better pretrained model without reprocessing the gallery—addresses a real operational bottleneck in large-scale search (social media, robotics, automotive). The theoretical result gives practitioners confidence that the d-Simplex fixed classifier is not just a heuristic but the optimal stationary solution, which should guide future architecture design. Limitations include reliance on the UFM expressiveness assumption, the theorem holding only in expectation, and experiments conducted on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-100R, CIFAR-10) with modest architectures; scaling to ImageNet-scale galleries and transformer backbones remains open. The 31-task AC dropping to 0.65 also suggests degradation over very long sequences, an area needing further investigation.
