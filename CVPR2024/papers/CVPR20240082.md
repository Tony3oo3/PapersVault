## Problem

General image fusion must handle tasks with fundamentally different fusion mechanisms—balanced fusion (multi-exposure, visible-infrared) versus polarized selection (multi-focus)—yet existing unified models either suffer from dominant-task bias or sacrifice task-specific individuality for multi-task commonality. The paper asks whether a single parameter-efficient framework can adaptively customize its fusion behavior per task while sharing a common backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240082/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a pair of aligned multi-source images (visible+infrared, under/over-exposed pair, or partially-focused pair), cropped to 448×448 during training but supporting arbitrary resolution at inference, and outputs a single fused RGB image. A distinctive feature is post-hoc controllability: the learned prompt can be linearly rescaled (α) and shifted (β) after training to adjust the dominant-intensity bias toward either source, enabling zero-shot adaptation to unseen fusion tasks such as medical or pan-sharpening fusion.

## Method

TC-MoA inserts lightweight adapter modules into every τ=4 transformer blocks of a frozen MAE-large ViT backbone, adding only 2.8% learnable parameters. Each TC-MoA module contains a task-specific router (TopK-softmax over N=4 shared adapters) that selects which adapters to activate per token pair, and the weighted adapter outputs are globally pooled into a spatial prompt (prompt_x, prompt_y ∈ (0,1)) that scales the respective source features. A mutual information regularization (|prompt_x + prompt_y − 1|) enforces complementarity, while input-independent source embeddings provide modality-specific bias. Task-customized unsupervised losses (MaxPixel/MaxGrad for VIF, AvgPixel/MaxGrad for MEF, MaskPixel/MaskGrad for MFF) are trained jointly across all three tasks with EMA on routers and adapters, and a learnable residual coefficient λ_f gradually blends the fused feature back into each source stream for progressive refinement.

## Results

On the LLVIP VIF benchmark, TC-MoA achieves the best VIF score of 0.726, MS-SSIM of 0.949, and the lowest Qcv of 423.773, outperforming all 14 competing methods. On MEFB, TC-MoA attains the highest MEF-SSIM (0.964) and PSNR (57.213) among all general fusion methods. On the MFF benchmark, TC-MoA leads in NMI (0.875), MI (6.695), and Qcb (0.718) while achieving the lowest Qcv (36.512), surpassing even the supervised IFCNN baseline. The model adds only 3.87M trainable parameters (2.8%) on top of the frozen MAE-Large backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TC-MoA outperforms all four general image fusion baselines (IFCNN, U2Fusion, DeFusion, FusionDN) across every metric on all three tasks. On VIF, TC-MoA surpasses the strongest task-specific method CDDFuse by 0.032 in VIF score (0.726 vs. 0.694) and by 0.016 in MS-SSIM (0.949 vs. 0.933), while also achieving the best Qcv (423.773 vs. 495.473). On MEF, TC-MoA achieves the highest MEF-SSIM (0.964) and PSNR (57.213), though MEFNet retains a lead on Qc (0.656 vs. 0.578) and Qp (0.595 vs. 0.598 is a near-tie). On MFF, TC-MoA beats the supervised IFCNN on NMI (0.875 vs. 0.847), MI (6.695 vs. 6.495), and Qcv (36.512 vs. 44.373) without requiring a decision map. In the fine-tuning ablation (Table 5), TC-MoA consistently outperforms AdaptFormer [4] on both ViT-Base and ViT-Large across all three tasks, confirming the advantage of the task-customized routing over a single shared adapter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts routing mechanism (Shazeer et al., 2017) not for capacity scaling but as a task-customized adapter selection strategy applied to a frozen MAE/ViT backbone, bridging the PEFT literature (AdaptFormer, prompt tuning) with the general image fusion line (U2Fusion, DeFusion, IFCNN). Unlike prior general fusion methods that either enforce a single dominant fusion paradigm across tasks or sacrifice task-specificity for cross-task commonality, TC-MoA introduces per-task routers that dynamically select from a shared adapter bank, with a mutual information regularization (prompt_x + prompt_y ≈ 1) that is novel to the fusion setting. The approach departs from task-specific fusion networks (MEFNet, RFN-Nest, CDDFuse) by treating the backbone as a fixed feature extractor and confining all task adaptation to lightweight adapter modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single model with only ~2.8% additional learnable parameters can match or exceed task-specific fusion methods across VIF, MEF, and MFF, while offering a practical controllability knob (linear prompt manipulation) for adjusting dominant-source bias and brightness—features absent from prior general fusion frameworks. The zero-shot transfer to medical image fusion and pan-sharpening, though yielding only 'reasonable' rather than state-of-the-art results, suggests the adapter-prompt paradigm may generalize to fusion tasks beyond the three studied. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two source images, whether the mutual information constraint generalizes to asymmetric multi-source settings, and whether the controllability can be learned end-to-end rather than applied as a post-hoc linear transformation.
