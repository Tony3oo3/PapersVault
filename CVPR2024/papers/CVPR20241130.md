## Problem

SAM achieves strong generalizable segmentation but lacks any semantic understanding—it cannot describe what it segments. Compounding this, regional captioning data is extremely scarce (Visual Genome has ~100K images versus SAM's 11M+ training images), making it difficult to train captioning models that generalize beyond a narrow set of visual concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241130/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a visual prompt (point, box, or mask) and simultaneously outputs a binary segmentation mask and a natural language caption for the prompted region. The image encoder produces a single-level 64×64 feature map at 256 dimensions; the frozen text decoder (GPT2-large or LLaMA-3B) generates captions conditioned on 8 caption query tokens and 6 optimizable task tokens prepended to the token sequence.

## Method

The core contribution is a 12-layer bidirectional transformer feature mixer (~19M parameters) stacked above SAM's existing 2-layer mixer, which is the sole trainable module in the entire system. It introduces new text query tokens that, through self-attention with encoded prompt tokens and cross-attention with global image tokens, progressively extract region-specific features aligned to the language model's embedding space. To address data scarcity, the mixer is first pretrained with weak supervision on Objects365 detection and COCO-Panoptic segmentation data (1.8M images with category labels only), then fine-tuned on Visual Genome's regional captions. SAM's ViT encoder, SAM's feature mixer, and the language model all remain frozen, keeping training fast, memory-efficient, and scalable across nodes.

## Results

On the Visual Genome test split, SCA with GPT2-large and weak-supervision pretraining achieves 149.8 CIDEr-D, 17.5 METEOR, and 31.4 SPICE, setting a new state-of-the-art for region captioning. The variant using LLaMA-3B as the text decoder reaches 149.8 CIDEr-D and 31.3 SPICE, demonstrating that scaling the frozen language model further improves performance. The model only optimizes ~19M parameters (the 12-layer text feature mixer), while keeping the SAM encoder, SAM feature mixer, and language model frozen, making training both fast and scalable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCA outperforms the strongest trained baseline, GRiT [89], by +7.6 CIDEr-D (149.8 vs. 142.2), +0.3 METEOR, and +0.9 SPICE, while also achieving higher BLEU and ROUGE scores across all n-gram orders. The SAM+captioner baselines (SAM+BLIP, SAM+GIT, SAM+BLIP2) from the training-free Caption Anything pipeline [86] perform substantially worse, with the best variant (SAM+GIT-large-coco) reaching only 71.8 CIDEr-D — roughly half of SCA's score — because cropped image patches lose global context and deviate from the captioner's training distribution. Compared to GRiT, SCA has a key architectural advantage: it only trains the lightweight 19M-parameter text feature mixer while keeping the SAM encoder, SAM feature mixer, and language model frozen, whereas GRiT optimizes its entire pipeline (image encoder, region proposal network, and text decoder from scratch). The paper also shows that swapping the frozen language model from GPT2-large (774M) to LLaMA-3B yields a small but consistent improvement, confirming that the method benefits from stronger language backbones without any additional training cost on the language side.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM [35], repurposing its category-agnostic segmentation architecture as a semantic feature extractor for regional captioning. It departs from prior regional captioning systems (GRiT [89], Dense-Cap [33]) that train full pipelines end-to-end, instead freezing SAM's encoder and language model and optimizing only a lightweight 12-layer query-based feature mixer (~19M parameters). The weak supervision pretraining strategy, leveraging object detection and segmentation datasets with only category labels, is inspired by the broader weak-supervision literature [44, 103, 111] but applied specifically to bridge the gap between segmentation-scale data and the scarce regional captioning data in Visual Genome.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that SAM's implicit semantic knowledge—acquired from annotators drawing masks over 'whatever things or stuff they recognized' without explicit labels—is sufficient to ground regional captions with minimal additional training, a conceptual insight that could inform how future foundation models are augmented with language capabilities. The lightweight adapter design (tens of millions of trainable parameters) makes the approach practical for rapid iteration and scaling. Key limitations include wrong attribute prediction, confusion between similar visual concepts, and unenforced alignment between mask and caption outputs. The authors identify self-training and larger-scale weak supervision as the natural next steps toward truly scaling regional captioning data beyond Visual Genome's ~100K images.
