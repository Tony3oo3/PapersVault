## Problem

In DETR-based detectors, multiple queries compete for each object, and the leading query must dramatically enlarge its score gap over trailing competitors across decoder layers. This competition is inherently difficult—queries start with very close scores and positions—slowing convergence and limiting detection accuracy. The paper asks whether explicitly biasing the decoder toward the leading query can ease this competition and improve both training efficiency and final performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241736/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in module within existing DETR decoders, consuming RGB images (typically 1333×800 for COCO) and producing per-query bounding boxes, class labels, and confidence scores. Unusually, it leverages intermediate decoder-layer predictions (scores and boxes from the preceding layer) to compute pairwise query relationships, which then modulate attention in the current layer. The module is backbone-agnostic (ResNet-50, Swin-T, Swin-L) and compatible with various DETR variants including Deformable DETR, DINO, and H-DETR, adding only ~0.1 GFLOPs for 300 queries.

## Method

EASE-DETR introduces a binary 'leading/trailing' relationship (R_rank) between every pair of queries based on their previous-layer prediction scores, optionally combined with spatial overlap (R_spt = IoU of predicted boxes) for locality. In the modified self-attention (MSelf-attention), the product R_rank·R_spt is encoded through a small MLP (16-dim hidden layer) and sigmoid to produce a per-head decay weight D^s that modulates attention scores before softmax, protecting the leading query from mutual suppression. In the modified cross-attention (MCross-attention), a suppression level L_i = max_j(−R_rank(i,j)·R_spt(i,j)) is similarly encoded into a decay D^c that scales the cross-attention output, reducing object-feature absorption by trailing queries. All additional parameters are learned end-to-end with no hand-crafted hyperparameters, and the module adds virtually no computational overhead. Combined with the one-to-many supervision strategy from DAC-DETR (where the one-to-one branch uses EASE attention and the one-to-many branch uses standard attention), the method achieves 50.8 AP on R50-DINO and 57.8 AP on Swin-L-DINO, surpassing prior SOTA.

## Results

On COCO 2017 val, EASE-DETR (DINO + one-to-many) achieves 50.8 AP with ResNet-50 in 12 epochs (+1.8 over DINO baseline) and 51.6 AP in 24 epochs (+1.2). With Swin-Large backbone, it reaches 57.8 AP in 12 epochs (+1.0), surpassing the previous SOTA Stable-DINO (57.7 AP). The EASE module adds only 0.1 GFLOPs (300 queries) or 0.7 GFLOPs (900 queries) with virtually no parameter increase, and also improves 3D detection on PETR by +1.6 NDS and +0.9 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASE-DETR consistently outperforms all compared baselines under matched settings. Against DINO [39], it gains +1.8 AP (R50, 12ep) and +1.0 AP (Swin-L, 12ep). Against Stable-DINO [22], it wins by +0.4 AP on R50-12ep (50.8 vs 50.4) and +0.1 AP on Swin-L-12ep (57.8 vs 57.7), while also beating the 24-epoch Stable-DINO on R50 (51.6 vs 51.5). Against DAC-DETR [14], it leads by +0.8 AP (R50-12ep) and +0.5 AP (Swin-L-12ep). Against Group-DETR [5], it wins by +1.0 AP on R50-12ep (50.8 vs 49.8); on Swin-L, Group-DETR uses 36 epochs (58.4 AP) vs EASE-DETR's 12 epochs (57.8 AP), so EASE-DETR is competitive with 3× fewer training iterations. On Deformable++/H-DETR [16] baselines (Table 1), EASE adds +0.7 to +1.3 AP. On 3D detection, EASE-DETR improves PETR [24] by +1.6 NDS and +0.9 mAP. DAB-DETR [21] is used only for efficiency comparison, where EASE adds negligible overhead (0.1–0.7 GFLOPs, 0 extra parameters).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DETR family (Deformable DETR, DINO, H-DETR, DAC-DETR) by reframing the one-to-one label assignment's non-duplicate detection property as an explicit competition among queries, then intervening in that competition through modulated attention. Conceptually, it extends the modulated-attention line (SMCA, DAB-DETR, Deformable DETR) but shifts the modulation target from query-to-feature similarity to inter-query relationships (ranking and spatial overlap). Unlike prior DETR improvements that modify the training objective (denoising, hybrid matching) or the attention structure (deformable, conditional), EASE-DETR is a lightweight plugin that injects a binary leading/trailing signal into existing self- and cross-attention layers, making it composable with one-to-many strategies like DAC-DETR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The competition framing provides a useful mechanistic lens for understanding why DETR's one-to-one assignment produces non-duplicate predictions, and the resulting plugin is practically attractive: it adds negligible compute (~0.1 GFLOPs at 300 queries), requires no hand-crafted hyperparameters, and consistently improves multiple popular DETR variants by 0.5–1.8 AP. However, the gains are modest and the method is inherently tied to DETR-style query-based decoders, limiting broader applicability. An open question is whether the explicit inter-query bias becomes redundant as decoder depth or query count increases, and whether the competition framing can inform more principled query initialization or pruning strategies.
