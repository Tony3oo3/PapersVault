## Problem

Existing Next-Best-View (NBV) policies for active 3D reconstruction rely on hand-crafted view-selection criteria, limited action spaces (e.g., a hemisphere or a small candidate set), or per-scene optimized representations like NeRF, all of which prevent generalization to unseen building-scale geometries. The paper asks how to design an NBV policy that supports free-space exploration and generalizes across diverse, unseen objects without per-scene optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent (a CrazyFlie drone in Isaac Gym) receives a 400×400 RGB image, a depth map, and a 5D camera state (x, y, z, yaw, pitch); the full observation is the concatenation of all historical captures and actions within the episode. The policy outputs a 5D action vector — a 3D position within a ~20 m × 20 m × 10 m workspace plus 2D heading (yaw and pitch) — parameterized as a normal distribution. Roll-axis rotation is excluded because it is not supported on all drone platforms.

## Method

GenNBV formulates NBV as an MDP solved with PPO, but its key contributions are the action space and state representation. The action space is extended from a hemisphere or 2D plane to a 5D free space, letting the drone scan from any viewpoint and avoiding scale-shift issues at evaluation. The state embedding fuses three sources: (1) a probabilistic 3D occupancy grid with three voxel states (occupied, free, unknown) updated via Bresenham's line algorithm and a log-odds formulation, (2) a 2-layer CNN semantic feature extracted from grayscale RGB frames, and (3) a linear encoding of the historical viewpoint sequence. These are concatenated and passed through a 3-layer MLP that parameterizes a Gaussian policy over the 5D action space. The reward is the per-step increase in coverage ratio (occupied voxels / ground-truth surface voxels), augmented with collision and over-capture penalties, and training runs for ~32 M iterations on 256 Houses3K objects in Isaac Gym.

## Results

On the Houses3K test set (30 views), GenNBV achieves 91.19% Mean AUC and 98.26% Final Coverage Ratio with 0.37 cm reconstruction accuracy. On the cross-dataset OmniObject3D house category (20 views), it reaches 88.63% Mean AUC and 97.12% Final Coverage Ratio with 0.33 cm accuracy. For non-house categories (Table 2), GenNBV outperforms Scan-RL by 5–19 percentage points in AUC across animals, trucks, dinosaurs, and the Replica Room 0 scene, reaching 99.16% Final Coverage Ratio on the indoor scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenNBV outperforms all three main baselines on both in-distribution (Houses3K) and out-of-distribution (OmniObject3D) evaluations. Against the strongest RL baseline Scan-RL [26], GenNBV gains +6.70 pp in Mean AUC on Houses3K (91.19% vs 84.49%) and +6.46 pp on OmniObject3D (88.63% vs 82.17%), while also improving Final Coverage Ratio by +6.65 pp and +4.59 pp respectively. The per-scene NeRF-based methods (Uncertainty-Guided [14] and ActiveRMap [46]) perform competitively on Houses3K (~83–85% AUC) but collapse to ~69% AUC on OmniObject3D, revealing their inability to generalize without per-scene optimization. Ablations confirm that the 5D free-space action space contributes +2.91 pp AUC over Scan-RL's hemisphere (87.39% vs 84.49%), and the multi-source state embedding adds a further +3.80 pp over the geometric-only representation (91.19% vs 87.39%). On non-house categories (Table 2), GenNBV consistently beats Scan-RL by 5–19 pp in AUC, with the largest gap on Replica Room 0 (88.53% vs 69.21%), demonstrating that free-space exploration is critical for complex indoor scenes with multi-scale occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based NBV policy line initiated by Scan-RL [26] and SCONE [9], which were constrained to hemisphere or 2D action spaces and relied on per-scene optimized representations (NeRF) or RGB-only features. GenNBV departs from this lineage in two key ways: it replaces the constrained action space with a 5D free-space formulation (3D position + 2D heading), and it substitutes per-scene NeRF optimization with a multi-source state embedding combining a probabilistic 3D occupancy grid, a lightweight semantic CNN, and an action sequence encoder. The probabilistic grid drawing on classical occupancy grid mapping [40] and the PPO training loop follow established RL practice, while the multi-source embedding design is the paper's primary architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train a single NBV policy on building-scale objects and generalize to entirely different categories (animals, trucks, dinosaurs, indoor rooms) without retraining addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous 3D scanning, where manual view planning is expensive and error-prone. The free-space action space is a meaningful step toward realistic drone deployment, removing the artificial constraint of hemisphere-based sampling. However, the work remains entirely simulation-based (Isaac Gym), uses a single CrazyFlie agent, and focuses on static surface reconstruction; open questions include real-world transfer, multi-drone coordination, dynamic scenes, and whether the probabilistic grid scales to city-level environments with millions of voxels.
