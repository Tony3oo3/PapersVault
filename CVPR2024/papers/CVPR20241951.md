## Problem

Existing Category-Agnostic Pose Estimation methods (POMNet, CapeFormer) rely primarily on similarity matching and query-side iterative refinement, largely neglecting the rich category-specific semantic and structural information embedded in support samples. This leads to vulnerability under complex occlusions, similar appearances, and wide pose variations, as the initial matching is easily disrupted and averaging support keypoint features ignores complex inter- and intra-sample dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241951/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single query RGB image and N support RGB images (1-shot or 5-shot) with 2D keypoint annotations, where the number of keypoints K ranges from 8 to 68 across 100 sub-categories. A shared backbone extracts both high-resolution (H/4 × W/4) and low-resolution (H/32 × W/32) feature maps from the query, while support images yield feature maps from which initial keypoint features are sampled via heatmaps. The output is a set of 2D keypoint coordinates for the query image, predicted iteratively as offsets over T refinement stages.

## Method

SDPNet deeply integrates support information into both the matching and refinement stages. A cross-instance interaction module (intra-instance then inter-instance self-attention with instance/keypoint encodings) constructs semantic-aware and structure-aware support keypoint features with category consensus. The semantic-aware features generate dynamic depth-wise convolution kernels (7×7) that selectively activate category-relevant channels in the high-resolution query feature map. The structure-aware features drive a multi-head attention mechanism to predict a category-specific adjacency matrix (trained via mask reconstruction with sparsity constraint), which parameterizes a dynamic GCN with per-keypoint modulation matrices for directional information passing among query keypoints. Finally, a modulated deformable attention mechanism uses the difference between query and support keypoint embeddings to predict per-keypoint scaling coefficients that widen the sampling range for low-confidence keypoints, preventing local optima during iterative refinement.

## Results

On the MP-100 dataset, SDPNet with HRNet-32 backbone achieves an average PCK of 87.36% under 1-shot and 90.42% under 5-shot settings, outperforming the previous SOTA CapeFormer (85.31% and 89.30%) by 2.05% and 1.12% respectively. With a ResNet-50 backbone (for fair comparison with prior methods that do not use high-resolution features), SDPNet still achieves 85.96% and 89.81%, surpassing CapeFormer by 0.65% and 0.51%. The method also substantially outperforms POMNet (79.70%/80.71%) and generic few-shot baselines such as ProtoNet (44.78%/58.56%), MAML (61.50%/62.50%), and Fine-tune (63.58%/64.61%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDPNet outperforms all five baselines across both 1-shot and 5-shot settings on all five splits of MP-100. Against the strongest prior CAPE method CapeFormer [33], SDPNet (HRNet-32) gains +2.05% PCK in 1-shot (87.36 vs. 85.31) and +1.12% in 5-shot (90.42 vs. 89.30); even with the weaker ResNet-50 backbone, SDPNet still leads by +0.65% and +0.51%. Against POMNet [45], the first dedicated CAPE method, SDPNet improves by a large margin of +7.66% (1-shot) and +9.71% (5-shot), demonstrating the benefit of iterative refinement over single-shot similarity matching. The generic few-shot baselines—ProtoNet [34], MAML [13], and Fine-tune [25]—trail by 20–43 PCK points, confirming that task-specific CAPE architectures are far superior to general-purpose meta-learning or fine-tuning approaches for pose estimation. The paper also notes that SDPNet's advantage is most pronounced under occlusion and appearance variation, where the dynamic graph convolution and modulated deformable attention provide robustness that CapeFormer lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDPNet extends the CAPE pipeline established by POMNet (ECCV 2022) and CapeFormer (CVPR 2023), which both rely on similarity matching followed by iterative refinement of query keypoints. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods treat support samples primarily as a source for initializing query keypoint features via averaging, whereas SDPNet deeply integrates support information into both the matching and refinement stages through cross-instance attention, dynamic convolution, and a dynamically predicted category-specific graph structure. The dynamic GCN with mask-reconstruction-based structure learning draws on data-driven adjacency prediction from skeleton-based action recognition, but adapts it to the category-agnostic setting where no fixed topology is available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the rich semantic and structural information in support samples—previously underexploited—can substantially improve robustness to occlusion, symmetric confusion, and appearance variation in CAPE. However, the evaluation is confined to MP-100, the sole public CAPE benchmark, and the method does not model 3D object structure, leaving occluded keypoints in complex poses still challenging. Open questions include whether the dynamically predicted 2D prior structures generalize to categories with highly variable topology, and whether the approach extends to 3D category-agnostic pose estimation or video-based settings.
