## Problem

Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) methods for LiDAR localization treat all input points equally, ignoring that dynamic objects (pedestrians, vehicles) and repetitive structures (road, trees) degrade pose estimation. No prior method effectively incorporates semantic awareness into SCR to down-weight distracting points while preserving salient static geometry, and naive point filtering based on segmentation labels introduces hard thresholds and inference overhead that limit practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242198/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single LiDAR scan in local coordinates (scanner at origin) and outputs a 6-DoF pose (position in meters, orientation in degrees) relative to a pre-built global map. The SCR backbone down-samples the input by a factor of 8 before regressing world coordinates, and RANSAC over the predicted correspondences yields the final pose. Critically, at inference time only the SCR module runs (38 ms on Oxford, 75 ms on NCLT at batch size 1), with no segmentation or distillation modules present.

## Method

LiSA augments the SGLoc SCR architecture with an additional semantic feature regression head that outputs per-point features F_stu ∈ R^{N'×d}. During training, a frozen 3D semantic segmentation model (SphereFormer or SPVNAS) provides teacher features F_tea, and a diffusion-based knowledge distillation loss (DDPM) is used to transfer semantic knowledge: the student feature is treated as a noisy version of the teacher feature, a diffusion model denoises it, and an L1 loss is applied between the denoised student and the clean teacher. The total training loss combines the standard SCR L1 coordinate loss with the distillation loss. After training, both the segmentation and distillation modules are discarded, so inference incurs zero extra computation or parameters over the baseline SCR. This diffusion-based distillation avoids the pitfalls of hard point filtering (which sacrifices orientation accuracy) and feature concatenation (which requires the segmentation model at inference), while being robust to noisy segmentation labels.

## Results

On QEOxford, LiSA achieves a mean position error of 0.95 m and mean orientation error of 1.14°, improving over the previous state-of-the-art SGLoc (1.53 m, 1.60°) by 38% in position and 29% in orientation. On NCLT, LiSA reaches 1.51 m / 2.34° versus SGLoc's 1.83 m / 3.54° (17% and 34% improvement). On the non-enhanced Oxford dataset, LiSA attains 2.74 m / 1.32°, surpassing all methods in position accuracy while remaining competitive in orientation. Inference time is 38 ms on Oxford and 75 ms on NCLT, satisfying real-time requirements without any extra modules at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiSA outperforms all seven baselines on both position and orientation on QEOxford, with the largest gap against retrieval (PNVLAD: 11.45 m) and matching (DCP: 10.62 m) methods, and a 38% relative improvement over the closest SCR baseline SGLoc (1.53 m → 0.95 m). Among APR methods, HypLiLoc is the strongest competitor (3.89 m, 1.27°), yet LiSA still halves its position error and reduces orientation by 10%. On the non-enhanced Oxford dataset, LiSA wins on position (2.74 m vs. HypLiLoc's 5.74 m) but trails HypLiLoc slightly on orientation (1.32° vs. 1.05°), which the authors attribute to ground-truth trajectory noise in the original Oxford data. On NCLT, LiSA achieves sub-meter position accuracy on 3 of 4 trajectories and beats SGLoc by 17% in position and 34% in orientation, even though the teacher segmentation model performs worse on NCLT than on Oxford. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that knowledge distillation (KD) is superior to both point filtering (1.20 m / 1.97°) and feature concatenation (1.14 m / 1.60°), which also require the segmentation network at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiSA builds directly on SGLoc (CVPR 2023), the first SCR-based LiDAR localization method, adopting its multi-scale feature extractor and regressor architecture. It departs from prior SCR work by introducing semantic awareness through diffusion-based knowledge distillation (Huang et al., 2024), transferring per-point semantic features from a frozen segmentation teacher (SphereFormer or SPVNAS) into the SCR network during training. This contrasts with earlier uses of semantic information in localization, which were limited to retrieval-based descriptor enhancement or matching-based label attachment, and with regression-based approaches that relied on object detection (AD-PoseNet) or graph-based object relations (ORGPoseNet) rather than full-scene semantic segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiSA demonstrates that semantic awareness can be embedded into SCR-based LiDAR localization at zero inference cost, achieving sub-meter accuracy without pose graph optimization—a practical milestone for real-time deployment. The finding that naive point filtering with predicted semantic labels degrades orientation accuracy while knowledge distillation improves both position and orientation highlights the importance of soft, feature-level integration over hard label-based decisions. Open questions include whether the per-scene training paradigm can be relaxed, whether the approach transfers to camera-based SCR localization, and how much further gains are possible with stronger segmentation teachers.
