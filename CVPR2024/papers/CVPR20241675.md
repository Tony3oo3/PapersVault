## Problem

Existing video frame interpolation methods rely on linear or unambiguous motion assumptions and produce blurry, artifact-ridden outputs when the start and end frames are significantly different or the underlying motion is complex and nonlinear. The paper argues that such cases are inherently ambiguous and should be treated as a conditional generative problem rather than a regression problem, since a generative model can produce plausible samples rather than collapsing to a blurry mean.

## Input / Output

VIDIM takes a pair of 256×256 RGB frames (start and end) and generates 7 intermediate 256×256 frames, producing a 9-frame video. The cascaded pipeline first generates 7 frames at 64×64 resolution, then upsamples to 256×256; the super-resolution stage additionally conditions on the original high-resolution start and end frames to preserve fine details like text and textures. An odd number of output frames is chosen so the middle frame can be evaluated independently, matching prior video interpolation benchmarks.

## Method

VIDIM is a cascaded diffusion model with a base UNet (generating 7 64×64 frames) and a super-resolution UNet (generating 7 256×256 frames), both using shared convolutional and self-attention blocks over frames with temporal attention for cross-frame mixing. The key architectural innovation is parameter-free frame conditioning: the start and end frames are simply concatenated along the frame axis into the UNet with their noise levels set to the minimum, allowing information to propagate through temporal attention without any extra cross-attention layers. Classifier-free guidance is applied to the conditioning frames by replacing them with isotropic Gaussian noise at maximum noise level for the unconditioned path, with a learned null token for timestamps. Training uses the v-parametrization objective with L1 loss and a cosine log-SNR schedule, while the super-resolution model employs noise-conditioning augmentation on the low-resolution input frames.

## Results

On the curated Davis-7 and UCF101-7 benchmarks (400 videos each with large/ambiguous motion), VIDIM achieves the best FVD scores by a significant margin: 199.32 vs. 214.8–245.02 on Davis-7 and 278 vs. 316.3–344.5 on UCF101-7 (all 7 frames). On the middle-frame FID (the hardest frame), VIDIM leads on Davis-7 (53.38 vs. 56.28–69.34) but trails RIFE (42.33) and LDMVFI (42.96) on UCF101-7 (53.71). In a 31-participant user study with 1,334 ratings, VIDIM is strongly preferred over all four baselines, with inter-rater agreement varying by at most 0.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIDIM is compared against four baselines: AMT (all-pairs multi-field transforms), RIFE (real-time intermediate flow estimation), FILM (frame interpolation for large motion), and LDMVFI (latent diffusion VFI). On FVD—the metric capturing temporal consistency—VIDIM wins decisively on both datasets, beating the best baseline (FILM at 214.8) by 15.5 points on Davis-7 and LDMVFI (316.3) by 38.3 points on UCF101-7. On FID, the picture is mixed: VIDIM wins on Davis-7 middle-frame FID (53.38 vs. 56.28 for LDMVFI) but loses to RIFE (42.33) and LDMVFI (42.96) on UCF101-7, and also trails RIFE and LDMVFI on all-7-frame FID for both datasets. On reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM), all baselines score higher than VIDIM, which the authors attribute to the well-known tendency of regression-based methods to produce blurrier but pixel-accurate outputs. In the human evaluation, VIDIM is preferred over every baseline across all 400 examples, with the gap being most pronounced on high-motion and ambiguous-motion clips where the baselines produce visible artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIDIM extends the cascaded diffusion framework of Ho et al. [16] to video frame interpolation, adapting the Imagen Video [17] UNet architecture with temporal attention and FiLM-based positional encodings. It departs fundamentally from the dominant VFI paradigm (RIFE, FILM, AMT) that relies on optical flow estimation and regression objectives, instead framing interpolation as a conditional generative problem using the continuous-time diffusion formulation of Kingma et al. [22]. The key architectural departure is parameter-free frame conditioning—simply concatenating conditioning frames along the frame axis with minimal noise levels—rather than the cross-attention layers used in prior diffusion-based conditioning (e.g., [27, 63]), which the authors show scales better and avoids quality degradation from misalignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that generative diffusion models can handle large, nonlinear, and ambiguous motion in video interpolation where regression-based methods produce blurry or artifact-laden outputs, with strong human-evaluation support. The parameter-free conditioning mechanism and the finding that explicit frame conditioning at training time substantially outperforms imputation-based conditioning (even with reconstruction guidance) offer a reusable recipe for other conditional video generation tasks. Open questions remain around arbitrary aspect ratios, variable frame counts, and improving super-resolution fidelity, and the fixed-resolution, fixed-frame-count constraint limits immediate practical deployment.
