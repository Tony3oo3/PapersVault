## Problem

Generating images that combine multiple personalized concepts simultaneously remains challenging: existing approaches either require joint training or weight merging that fails for semantically related concepts (e.g., cat and dog), struggle to scale beyond three concepts, and produce blended or missing concept appearances. The paper addresses this by proposing a tuning-free inference-time composition method that avoids any additional optimization beyond per-concept fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt with placeholder tokens marking each custom concept (e.g., 'A [C1] dog and a [C2] cat, [C3] mountain background'), a template image (generated at 1024×1024 via SDXL or a real photograph), and a bank of pre-trained single-concept models (fine-tuned on cross-attention K/V weights via Custom Diffusion or LoRA). It produces a 768×768 image where each concept's appearance is injected into its designated spatial region while preserving the template's overall structure. The entire inference pipeline (inversion + 50-step denoising) runs in approximately 60 seconds on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

Concept Weaver decomposes multi-concept generation into a cascading process: DDIM inversion of the template image to obtain a noisy latent and intermediate U-Net features (ResNet outputs at l=4, self-attention maps at l=4,7,9), followed by region mask extraction via SAM and Grounding DINO. During the 50-step denoising, the method fuses N single-concept models without weight merging by: (1) injecting pre-calculated structural features into residual and self-attention layers to preserve template geometry, (2) applying concept-aware text conditioning so each model sees only its own concept token, (3) spatially mixing cross-attention output features using the region masks, and (4) subtracting a concept-free baseline feature (scaled by λ) to suppress generic appearance leakage. This clean separation of structural guidance (pre-computed features) from semantic guidance (fused cross-attention features) eliminates the need for joint optimization and prevents concept blending even for closely related subjects.

## Results

On the Custom Concept 101 dataset with 5 multi-concept combinations (each containing >3 concepts), Concept Weaver achieves the highest CLIP Text-similarity (0.3804) and Image-similarity (0.8124) scores, outperforming the strongest baseline Mix-of-show (0.3634 / 0.7984) by 4.7% and 1.7% respectively. In a 20-participant human preference study, Concept Weaver scores 4.70/5 on text match, 4.64/5 on concept match, and 4.43/5 on realism, substantially exceeding the next-best method Mix-of-show (3.44 / 3.39 / 3.78). The method also scales to 4+ concepts where Mix-of-show exhibits severe concept-missing failures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Concept Weaver outperforms all four baselines on both CLIP metrics. Against the closest competitor Mix-of-show [4], it gains +0.017 in Text-sim and +0.014 in Image-sim; Mix-of-show's weight-merging approach degrades sharply with more than 3 concepts and mixes appearances when subjects are spatially close (e.g., 'kissing' prompts). Custom Diffusion [11] and Textual Inversion [3] both fail to preserve individual concept identities in multi-concept scenes, with Textual Inversion scoring lowest on Image-sim (0.7256). Perfusion [25] is the weakest overall (0.3182 Text-sim), indicating its key-locked rank-one editing is insufficient for multi-concept composition. In the human study, Concept Weaver leads by a wide margin (4.70 vs. 3.44 text match; 4.64 vs. 3.39 concept match), confirming that the CLIP-score advantage translates to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Custom Diffusion's cross-attention fine-tuning for single-concept personalization and Plug-and-Play diffusion's DDIM inversion and intermediate feature extraction, but departs fundamentally from the weight-merging paradigm used by Mix-of-Show and Perfusion. Rather than optimizing a unified set of merged weights (which causes concept blending, especially for semantically close concepts), Concept Weaver composes pre-trained single-concept models at inference time through a cascading pipeline: template image generation, structural feature injection, region-specific cross-attention feature fusion, and concept-aware text conditioning. The key conceptual departure is treating multi-concept generation as a spatially-guided feature-space composition problem rather than a parameter-space optimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in personalized content creation: the inability of existing approaches to reliably compose more than three concepts or to handle semantically related subjects (e.g., cat and dog) without appearance blending. By eliminating the weight-merging optimization step, the approach scales more gracefully to additional concepts and enables plug-and-play composition from a bank of independently trained models. Limitations include dependence on template image quality and segmentation accuracy, a ~60-second generation pipeline, and evaluation restricted to SD2.1 with 15 concepts. Open questions include whether the feature-fusion strategy generalizes to architectures without explicit cross-attention layers, how it behaves with 10+ concepts, and whether the template-image requirement can be relaxed for fully open-ended generation.
