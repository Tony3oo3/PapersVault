## Problem

Generating realistic 3D human motions for the 'last mile' of object interaction (approaching and initiating contact) is hindered by two gaps: existing motion models are either scene-unaware or require expensive per-frame annotations for contact, action class, and phase, and paired human-object interaction data is scarce and costly to capture. Prior guidance approaches rely on hand-designed constraints rather than learned, data-driven objectives that capture both physical and semantic interaction plausibility.

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes a 5000-point canonicalized object point cloud, a 4×4 rigid object pose relative to the person, 10-dim SMPL body shape parameters, a starting pose, and random noise, and produces a full N-frame 3D motion sequence in a canonicalized coordinate frame (joint positions, 6D rotations, velocities, angular velocities for 22 SMPL joints, plus global translation and velocity). The interaction field operates on a simplified pose representation (joint positions + global translation only) and outputs a per-joint offset vector that projects the query pose onto the valid interaction manifold for that object.

## Method

NIFTY combines an object-conditioned transformer diffusion model (x0-prediction formulation) with a learned object interaction field that provides gradient-based guidance at every denoising step. The interaction field is an encoder-only transformer that takes a simplified human pose and the canonical object point cloud as tokens and regresses an offset vector projecting the pose to the valid interaction manifold; its L2 norm serves as the guidance objective whose gradient perturbs the predicted clean trajectory. To overcome data scarcity, the paper re-trains HuMoR as a time-reversed motion model and uses tree-based branching rollouts from a small set of manually selected anchor poses (174 for sitting, 72 for lifting) to generate 200K+ synthetic interaction sequences, which are then used to train both the diffusion model and the interaction field.

## Results

NIFTY achieves 99.6% of sitting motions within 2 cm of the object (D2O) and 77.7% for lifting, substantially outperforming SAMP (80.3%/59.4%) and cVAE (88.8%/57.4%). In user studies, NIFTY is preferred over SAMP 87.2% of the time and over cVAE 89.4% of the time across both actions. NIFTY also achieves the lowest skeleton distance (0.54 for sitting, 0.42 for lifting) and highest contact IoU (0.54/0.17), indicating generated interaction poses closely match the training data distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NIFTY outperforms SAMP [12] on every metric for both sitting and lifting, with the largest gaps in D2O (99.6% vs. 80.3% for sitting) and penetration (65.0% vs. 31.0% within 2 cm). SAMP's autoregressive design produces the highest foot skating (0.94/0.85) and its GoalNet often generates poorly suited goals, leading to object penetrations. Against cVAE [53], NIFTY wins on D2O, skeleton distance, contact IoU, and penetration, though cVAE achieves slightly lower foot skating for sitting (0.77 vs. 0.47). The unguided cMDM baseline [45] produces the most natural-looking individual motions (lowest foot skating at 0.36/0.28) but fails to approach the object (37.9%/36.3% D2O), confirming the critical role of the learned interaction field guidance. In user studies, NIFTY is preferred over all three baselines by at least 86% of the time, and its synthetic training data scores 4.39/5 on realism, close to AMASS mocap at 4.87/5.

## Relation to Prior Work

NIFTY extends the Human Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) by adding object geometry conditioning and a learned neural interaction field that guides denoising at every step, departing from prior scene-conditioned approaches (SceneDiffuser, HUMANISE) that rely on hand-designed guidance constraints or full-scene point clouds. The interaction field concept draws from neural distance fields for pose (PoseNDF) and robotic grasping (NGDF, Grasping Fields), but is the first to apply such a field to full-body human-object interaction and use it as a differentiable guidance signal during diffusion sampling. For data, the paper repurposes HuMoR as a time-reversed motion prior with tree-based branching rollout, avoiding the expensive per-frame annotations (contacts, phases, action labels) required by object-centric models like SAMP and COUCH.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automated synthetic data pipeline addresses a critical bottleneck in human-object interaction research: the scarcity of high-quality paired mocap data. The finding that generated motions score 4.39/5 in realism (vs. 4.87 for AMASS mocap) suggests this approach could substantially reduce data collection costs for new interaction types. Key limitations remain: the method does not handle object manipulation or chaining multiple interactions, struggles with harder approach geometries (e.g., from behind a chair), and generalization to unseen objects is not yet demonstrated. Extending the interaction field to multi-stage interactions and combining with physics-based object dynamics are natural next steps.
