## Problem

Diffusion models achieve high image quality for semantic image synthesis but require hundreds to thousands of iterative denoising steps, making inference two orders of magnitude slower than GANs, and they exhibit lower consistency with input segmentation maps. GAN-based approaches are fast but have historically underperformed in image quality on large, diverse datasets. Additionally, while pre-trained image backbones have been leveraged in classification, segmentation, and even class-conditional GANs, their use in semantic image synthesis discriminators remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a semantic label map of shape W×H×C (where C is the number of classes) and a random noise vector z ~ N(0,I) as input, and produces a photorealistic RGB image of shape W×H×3. For COCO-Stuff and Cityscapes, instance-level annotations are incorporated by concatenating vertical and horizontal offset maps (relative to each object's center of mass) to the semantic label map. Generation resolution is 256×256 for ADE-20K and COCO-Stuff, and 256×512 for Cityscapes. The label map is encoded through a multi-scale pyramid that produces conditioning maps at each resolution level for the SPADE blocks in the generator.

## Method

DP-SIMS introduces a UNet-style discriminator whose encoder is a frozen pre-trained backbone (ConvNeXt-L) followed by a feature conditioning module that applies a sigmoid nonlinearity and batch normalization to equalize the contribution of all feature channels, preventing dominant features from overwhelming the trainable decoder. The generator builds on DP-GAN's dual-pyramid architecture but replaces simple noise concatenation with cross-attention: the noise vector is mapped through an MLP and injected as keys/values into cross-attention blocks at multiple scales, while self-attention in the label map encoding pyramid captures long-range spatial dependencies. Training combines a weighted focal pixel-wise GAN loss (replacing weighted cross-entropy to better handle rare classes), a patch-wise InfoNCE contrastive loss computed on VGG features to enforce global coherence, and a diversity loss that penalizes insufficient variation between images generated from the same mask with different noise vectors. This combination achieves state-of-the-art FID and mIoU on three benchmarks while generating images in a single forward pass (~0.04 s) versus 4.6–260 s for diffusion baselines.

## Results

DP-SIMS achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmark datasets. On COCO-Stuff, it reaches FID 13.6 and mIoU_MF 65.2; on ADE-20K, FID 22.7 and mIoU_MF 67.8; and on Cityscapes, FID 38.2 and mIoU_MF 78.5. Critically, inference takes only 0.04 seconds per image (single forward pass), compared to 17.4 s for PITI and 260 s for SDM — a two-orders-of-magnitude speedup over diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-SIMS outperforms all GAN-based baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. On COCO-Stuff, it improves FID from 14.4 (FLIS) to 13.6, mIoU_MF from 52.1 (OASIS) to 65.2 (+13.1), and mIoU from 47.9 (ECGAN++) to 57.7 (+9.8). On ADE-20K, it beats ECGAN++ by 2.0 FID points and OASIS by 14.3 mIoU_MF points. On Cityscapes, it surpasses SDM by 3.9 FID points, 5.7 mIoU_MF points, and 3.0 mIoU points. Against diffusion-based methods (SDM, PITI, FLIS), DP-SIMS matches or exceeds image quality (FID) while substantially improving segmentation consistency (mIoU_MF), and does so with 2–3 orders of magnitude faster inference (0.04 s vs. 17.4–260 s per image). The largest single-metric gap is on mIoU_MF, where the pre-trained discriminator backbone provides a clear advantage over both GAN and diffusion competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-pyramid GAN framework of DP-GAN [34] and the pixel-wise adversarial supervision of OASIS [53] by introducing pre-trained image backbones (e.g., ConvNeXt-L) as frozen encoders in the UNet discriminator, a strategy previously explored only for class-conditional GANs via Projected GANs [52]. The key methodological departure is a simple feature conditioning scheme (sigmoid nonlinearity followed by batch normalization) that outperforms the cross-channel/scale mixing of Projected GANs, along with cross-attention-based noise injection in the generator—a mechanism not previously used in semantic image synthesis. The work thus bridges the gap between discriminative pre-training (classification backbones) and generative modeling for layout-conditioned image synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DP-SIMS matches or exceeds recent diffusion models (SDM, PITI, FLIS) on both FID and mIoU across three benchmarks while requiring roughly 100× less inference time, making high-quality semantic image synthesis feasible in latency-sensitive applications like interactive editing and data augmentation. A notable open question is the instability observed with transformer-based backbones (Swin) as discriminator encoders despite their superior ImageNet accuracy, suggesting that the interaction between attention-based feature spaces and adversarial training is not yet well understood. The finding that a simple sigmoid + batch-norm conditioning outperforms more elaborate mixing strategies also raises questions about what properties of pre-trained features are actually useful for adversarial discrimination.
