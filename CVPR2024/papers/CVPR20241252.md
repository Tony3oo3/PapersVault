## Problem

Current state-of-the-art GBC detection methods rely on individual ultrasound images, which exhibit limited generalization and may lack sufficient information to capture malignancy. Video-based GBC detection from ultrasound has not been previously attempted, and the random masking strategy in standard video MAEs is suboptimal when the disease region occupies only a tiny fraction of the frame, causing the network to learn redundant background representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241252/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes ultrasound videos (360×480 frames, resized to 224×224) that are sub-sampled at stride 4, split into 16-frame clips, with 4 clips randomly sampled per video during pretraining. Tokens are 2×3×16×16 cubic patches (1568 tokens per 16-frame clip). At inference, if any of 5 uniformly sampled clips is predicted malignant, the entire video is labeled malignant. The method is also applied to CT scan slices (512×512) treated analogously to video frames for a cross-modality validation on COVID detection.

## Method

FocusMAE introduces region-prior-guided masking into the video MAE pipeline: a Faster-RCNN-based RPN (trained on the GBCU dataset) generates candidate gallbladder bounding boxes, and an auxiliary MHA network produces per-token masking probabilities that are inflated by a factor π for tokens whose spatial center falls within these candidate regions. This forces the encoder (ViT-S with space-time attention, 12 layers, 384-dim) to reconstruct high-information malignant tokens rather than redundant background, yielding a more refined malignancy representation. Training combines an MSE reconstruction loss over masked tokens with a token-sampling loss (adapted from AdaMAE's REINFORCE-style formulation) that maximizes expected reconstruction error, with the key difference that token probabilities are augmented by region priors rather than learned purely from reconstruction signal. The decoder is deliberately deepened to 10 layers (vs. the typical 4–6), and the model is pretrained with Kinetics-400 initialization before fine-tuning with soft-target cross-entropy at a denser sub-sampling rate of 3.

## Results

On the curated US video dataset for GBC detection, FocusMAE achieves 96.4% accuracy, 91.0% specificity, and 100% sensitivity (5-fold cross-validation), surpassing the video-based SOTA AdaMAE (94.7% acc, 91.3% sens) and the image-based SOTA GBCNet/RadFormer (84.0% acc). The perfect sensitivity is particularly notable for a cancer detection task where missed malignancies are critical. On the public COVID-CT-MD dataset, FocusMAE achieves 88.5% accuracy and 86.9% sensitivity, outperforming VideoMAE (85.2% acc, 78.9% sens) and all image-based baselines (≤77.0% acc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FocusMAE outperforms all 15 baselines on accuracy for GBC detection from US videos, achieving 96.4% versus 94.7% for the closest video-based competitor AdaMAE and 84.0% for the image-based SOTA (GBCNet and RadFormer). The most striking result is FocusMAE's perfect sensitivity (100%), meaning zero missed malignancies across all 5 folds, compared to 91.3% for AdaMAE, 94.0% for VideoMAEv2, and 87.7% for RadFormer — a critical advantage for a cancer screening task. The trade-off is slightly lower specificity (91.0%) compared to Video-Swin and VidTr (both 100%), though this is offset by the substantially higher accuracy. On the COVID-CT-MD generalization task, FocusMAE (88.5% acc, 86.9% sens) beats VideoMAE (85.2% acc, 78.9% sens) by 3.3% in accuracy and 8% in sensitivity, while all image-based methods (ResNet50, InceptionV3, ViT, DEIT) remain below 77% accuracy. The paper also reports that FocusMAE surpasses both expert radiologists (78.6% and 87.4% accuracy) in detecting GBC from US videos, with the radiologists' performance consistent with published literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

FocusMAE extends the video masked autoencoder lineage (MAE-ST [14], VideoMAE [45]) by replacing random tube-based masking with a region-prior-guided sampling strategy. It builds directly on AdaMAE's [4] MHA-based auxiliary sampling network and REINFORCE-style token sampling loss, but augments the learned token probabilities with external object localization priors from a Faster-RCNN RPN, rather than relying solely on reconstruction error to drive adaptive masking. The paper also positions itself as a paradigm shift from the established image-based GBC detection literature (GBCNet [5], RadFormer [6], US-UCL [8]) to a video-based formulation, which it claims has not been previously attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a simple region-prior boost to masking probabilities outperforms both random masking and the more complex AdaMAE adaptive strategy, the paper suggests that incorporating lightweight anatomical priors into self-supervised pretraining can be more effective than purely data-driven adaptive masking for small, domain-specific medical datasets. The cross-modality validation on CT-based Covid detection supports the generality of the approach, though the evidence base remains limited to two tasks. Key limitations include the very small single-center dataset (91 videos), reliance on an externally trained RPN for region priors, and the absence of multi-center or external validation, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse clinical cohorts.
