## Problem

Existing NeRF-based X-ray reconstruction methods rely on simple MLPs that treat all sampled points along a ray equally, failing to model the complex internal 3D structures that X-rays penetrate. Additionally, their naive pixel-level ray sampling ignores 2D contextual and geometric information in projections, and prior work is largely confined to medical scenes, leaving performance on biology, security, and industry applications unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240979/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 50 sparse-view X-ray projections (circular cone beam, 0°–180°) of a single scene as input and produces two outputs: novel X-ray projections at unseen viewpoints (NVS) and a full 3D CT volume (CT reconstruction). No paired CT data is required for training; the model is supervised solely by the Beer-Lambert law relating predicted radiodensity along each ray to the observed projection intensity. The X3D dataset spans 15 scenes across four application domains (medicine, biology, security, industry) with 3% noise added to projections.

## Method

SAX-NeRF replaces the standard MLP backbone with Lineformer, a Line Segment-based Transformer that partitions each X-ray into M line segments and computes multi-head self-attention (LS-MSA) within each segment, capturing internal structural dependencies at linear O(NC²/k) complexity rather than the quadratic O(N²C) cost of vanilla Transformer. Point positions are first mapped through a multiresolution hash encoding, then processed by four Line Segment-based Attention Blocks (LSABs) with skip connections to predict per-point radiodensity. A Masked Local-Global (MLG) ray sampling strategy complements the architecture: a binary foreground mask segments informative regions, from which non-overlapping S×S patches are sampled for local 2D context while remaining foreground pixels are sampled for global geometry, avoiding uninformative background rays. Training minimizes the squared error between Beer-Lambert-law-predicted intensities and ground-truth projection pixel values over 3000 iterations with Adam.

## Results

On the X3D dataset (15 scenes across medicine, biology, security, and industry), SAX-NeRF achieves an average PSNR of 51.37 dB and SSIM of 0.9994 on novel view synthesis, surpassing the best NeRF-based baseline (NAF) by 12.56 dB and the best general RGB NeRF method (TensoRF) by 13.70 dB. On CT reconstruction, SAX-NeRF reaches 37.25 dB PSNR and 0.9753 SSIM, outperforming the best NeRF-based method (NAF) by 2.49 dB, the best optimization-based method (SART) by 4.92 dB, and the analytical FDK method by 12.13 dB. Per-domain NVS gains over the second-best method are 10.91 dB (medicine), 15.03 dB (biology), 5.13 dB (security), and 13.76 dB (industry).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAX-NeRF wins decisively on all 15 scenes for both NVS and CT reconstruction against all 8 baselines. On NVS, the largest gap is against InTomo (31.96 dB) and NeRF (32.49 dB), both of which use simple MLP backbones; the closest competitor is NAF (38.81 dB), a medical-specific NeRF variant, which SAX-NeRF still exceeds by 12.56 dB. On CT reconstruction, SAX-NeRF outperforms the analytical FDK method by 12.13 dB, the optimization-based SART and ASD-POCS by ~4.9 dB, and the best NeRF-based method NAF by 2.49 dB. The per-domain analysis shows the advantage is consistent across all four application areas (medicine, biology, security, industry), with the largest relative gain in biology (15.03 dB over second-best). The robustness analysis further shows SAX-NeRF maintains its lead even when only 60% of training projections are used, whereas other methods degrade more sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRF paradigm from visible-light rendering to X-ray imaging, departing from prior X-ray NeRF methods (NAF, InTomo, NeAT) that rely on simple MLPs to model radiodensity. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that X-ray attenuation encodes internal 3D structure along each ray, and designing a Transformer backbone (Lineformer) with line-segment-based self-attention to explicitly model dependencies among points within each segment, achieving linear rather than quadratic complexity. The MLG ray sampling strategy further distinguishes the work by incorporating 2D geometric and contextual information from the projection, going beyond the naive pixel-level sampling used in all prior NeRF-based X-ray methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The X3D dataset covering four application domains (medicine, biology, security, industry) fills a gap where prior benchmarks were limited to a handful of medical scenes, enabling more general evaluation of X-ray 3D reconstruction. The Lineformer's linear-complexity attention mechanism makes Transformer-based neural rendering practically feasible for X-ray, a modality where the number of sampled points per ray is large. Open questions include whether the fixed line-segment partitioning is optimal versus learned boundaries, whether the threshold-based foreground mask in MLG generalizes to noisier or lower-dose projections, and whether the approach extends to multi-energy or spectral X-ray reconstruction.
