## Problem

Color spike cameras achieve ultra-high temporal resolution (e.g., 20,000 Hz) but at the cost of limited spatial resolution, making them unsuitable for applications requiring both high-speed and high-resolution imaging. While grayscale spike camera super-resolution methods exist, no approach has addressed the super-resolution of color images from Bayer-pattern spike streams, where the interleaved color layout, high-speed motion, and quantization noise pose unique challenges that cannot be solved by simply chaining a CSC reconstruction method with a standard image SR network.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clip of N=41 binary Bayer-pattern spike frames of spatial size H×W (e.g., 96×96 patches), where each pixel is 0 or 1 and is assigned to one of four sub-channels (R, G1, G2, B) according to the RGGB Bayer layout. It produces a single high-resolution RGB image at the middle time point of the clip, upscaled by a factor s ∈ {2, 3, 4} to resolution sH×sW. The input is inherently binary and temporal (a short clip rather than a single frame), and the output is a full-color image, requiring the network to jointly handle demosaicing, motion compensation, and spatial upscaling.

## Method

CSCSR is a CNN-based network with three key modules. First, the Bayer-pattern Spike Stream Representation (BSSR) splits the binary clip into four color-channel sequences via Bayer masks, then encodes each with a sliding temporal window plus a global perception branch to capture local temporal dynamics while mitigating quantization errors. Second, the Motion-Guided Super-Resolution (MSR) module jointly estimates sub-pixel optical flow from all four channels using Spike2Flow (averaging per-channel flows for robustness), then warps features to sub-pixel positions at neighboring time points and interpolates from the four nearest known pixels defined by the upsampled color mask to fill spatial gaps; a residual-based Feature Refinement module subtracts a temporal-average reference to suppress motion-estimation errors. Third, the Color Correlation-based Reconstruction (CCR) module fuses multi-stage features across the three color channels through iterative Color Fusion blocks that condition each stage on the previous stage's output, followed by residual blocks to produce the final HR image. The network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss on synthetic data generated by a purpose-built CSC simulator that accumulates intensity from interpolated video frames and fires spikes according to the integrate-and-fire model.

## Results

On the primary REDS-based evaluation dataset, CSCSR achieves 33.39 dB PSNR / 0.9121 SSIM / 0.1764 LPIPS at ×2, 29.92 dB / 0.8513 / 0.2653 at ×3, and 28.77 dB / 0.7963 / 0.3253 at ×4, outperforming all baselines on every metric and scale. On the Vid4-based dataset, CSCSR reaches 28.77 dB (×2), 25.68 dB (×3), and 23.79 dB (×4) PSNR, again leading all competitors. On the Vimeo-based dataset (×4 only), CSCSR attains 25.34 dB PSNR and 0.2967 LPIPS, the best among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSCSR outperforms the strongest grayscale spike-camera SR baseline, SpikeSR-Net, by +1.01 dB PSNR at ×2 (33.39 vs 32.38), +0.13 dB at ×3 (29.92 vs 29.79), and +0.49 dB at ×4 (28.77 vs 28.28) on REDS, while also achieving lower LPIPS across all scales. Against the two-stage pipeline 3DRI+BasicVSR (the best reconstruction-then-SR combination), CSCSR gains +1.79 dB at ×2, +1.27 dB at ×3, and +1.83 dB at ×4, confirming that joint processing of the spike stream avoids the information loss inherent in separate reconstruction and SR stages. The per-channel variants (VidarSR*, SpikeSR-Net*) that handle each color independently degrade substantially (e.g., SpikeSR-Net* drops to 25.93 dB at ×4 vs 28.28 dB for the end-to-end version), highlighting the importance of the proposed color-correlation-based reconstruction. CSCSR also surpasses the raw-image-then-JDSR pipelines (TFI/TFP + TSCNN or Real-RawVSR) by 1.5–2.5 dB across scales, and leads on the cross-dataset generalization tests (Vid4, Vimeo) by similar margins. Overall, CSCSR achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on every dataset and scale tested, with the largest absolute gains at higher SR scales where temporal-pixel collection and motion-guided search become more critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grayscale spike camera super-resolution line (VidarSR, SpikeSR-Net) to the color spike camera setting with a Bayer-pattern CFA, which the authors identify as the first attempt at this specific task. It builds directly on Spike2Flow for sub-pixel optical flow estimation from spike streams, adapting it to the four-channel Bayer layout via joint motion estimation across color channels. Conceptually, it departs from the natural two-stage pipeline (reconstruct raw images, then apply standard SR) by operating directly on the binary spike stream, preserving temporal information that would be lost in a first reconstruction stage. The motion-guided temporal pixel search and color-correlation-based reconstruction modules draw on ideas from video SR (BasicVSR-style feature propagation) and joint demosaicing-SR (TSCNN, Real-RawVSR), but reframe them within the spike-stream and CFA constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental trade-off in neuromorphic vision: color spike cameras sacrifice spatial resolution for ultra-high temporal resolution, and this paper shows that sub-pixel motion across a short temporal window can partially recover the lost spatial detail. The finding that per-channel processing (ignoring color correlation) degrades performance substantially validates the need for joint multi-channel modeling in CFA-based sensors. Limitations include reliance on a simulator for training (real-world HR ground truth is unavailable for high-speed scenes), evaluation on relatively small synthetic datasets, and the assumption of linear motion within the short clip. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other CFA patterns (e.g., X-Trans), scales to higher SR factors, and can be deployed in real-time on embedded neuromorphic hardware.
