## Problem

Semantic segmentation of satellite imagery requires extensive manual annotation that is costly and difficult to obtain, and conventional augmentation (flipping, rotating) cannot capture the vast scene diversity of aerial data. No prior work has used generative diffusion models to jointly synthesize both satellite images and their corresponding pixel-level semantic masks as additional training data for downstream segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242529/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model G takes a random noise vector z ∈ R^L (L = THWC) and produces a joint 128×128 image-mask pair, where the image is 3-channel RGB normalized to [0,1] and the mask is encoded in ⌈log₂K⌉ binary channels (e.g., 4 channels for K=16 in iSAID). A conditional super-resolution variant G_SR additionally accepts a low-resolution 128×128 pair as input and outputs a 256×256 pair. The generated continuous mask values are thresholded and mapped back to discrete class indices {0,…,K−1} via nearest-neighbor assignment to the valid binary codes.

## Method

The core contribution is learning the joint distribution p(x, y) of satellite images and semantic labels via an unconditional DDPM, where discrete labels are encoded in bit-space (binary) rather than one-hot, reducing channel count from K to ⌈log₂K⌉ and enabling direct concatenation with RGB channels. The U-Net backbone predicts the noise vector ε over T=1000 denoising steps with a linear schedule, and the generated pairs are used to augment the original training set D ∪ D' for downstream segmentation with standard architectures (FPN, SegFormer, PSPNet). A two-stage super-resolution pipeline first generates 128×128 samples via G, then upsamples to 256×256 via a conditional DDPM that concatenates the low-resolution pair at each denoising step. Ablations confirm that bit-space encoding and ε-prediction are both critical for optimal segmentation performance, with the best configuration achieving 52.13 mIoU on iSAID at R=3 resampling ratio.

## Results

On the semantic segmentation comparison (Table 1b), the proposed method achieves 52.13 mIoU / 66.13 F1 on iSAID, 48.97 / 64.83 on LoveDA, and 62.24 / 76.10 on OpenEarthMap, outperforming both SemGAN and SegDiff across all three benchmarks. In terms of visual sample quality (Table 1a), the method attains the best FID scores (8.66 on iSAID, 13.87 on LoveDA, 12.09 on OpenEarthMap) and the highest Inception Score on iSAID (1.17). When used as data augmentation on iSAID (Table 2), integrating synthetic pairs improves mIoU by up to 7.59% (PSPNet: 48.95→56.54) and consistently benefits all tested architectures including PFSegNet (+2.78) and FarSeg (+0.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines. Against SemGAN [32] (a GAN-based joint image-mask generator using test-time optimization), the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics: on iSAID, mIoU jumps from 13.01 to 52.13 (+39.12) and F1 from 18.08 to 66.13; on OpenEarthMap, IoU improves from 40.43 to 62.24. Against SegDiff [1] (a diffusion-based image-to-image segmentation model), the proposed method also wins on every benchmark, with the largest gap on iSAID (52.13 vs. 41.25 mIoU, +10.88) and OpenEarthMap (62.24 vs. 51.23, +11.01). Against vanilla DDPM [27] (unconditional image-only generation), the proposed method achieves substantially better visual quality, e.g., FID of 8.66 vs. 17.50 on iSAID and 13.87 vs. 22.70 on LoveDA, confirming that jointly modeling images and labels in bit space yields higher-fidelity samples. The method does not lose to any baseline on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDPM [27] as the generative backbone and on the bit-space encoding of discrete data introduced in AnalogBits [14], extending both to the joint image-mask domain for satellite imagery. It departs from SemGAN [32], which also fits a joint image-mask distribution but via GANs and test-time optimization for label extraction, and from SegDiff [1], which uses diffusion for direct image-to-image segmentation rather than data synthesis. The cascaded super-resolution step draws on Ho et al. [29] for 2× upsampling. The key conceptual departure is treating diffusion purely as a data-augmentation engine—sampling joint (x, y) pairs to expand the training set—rather than as a segmentation predictor itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe for alleviating the annotation bottleneck in satellite semantic segmentation, with consistent mIoU gains across five different segmentation backbones and three benchmarks. The bit-space encoding of labels (⌈log₂K⌉ channels vs. K for one-hot) is a lightweight but important design choice that keeps the joint generation well-balanced. Limitations include the reliance on relatively low resolutions (128×128, optionally 256×256 via SR), the diminishing returns at higher resampling ratios, and evaluation restricted to three datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions without the cascaded step, whether it transfers to other earth-observation tasks (change detection, cloud inpainting), and how it interacts with self-supervised pre-training methods like SatMAE.
