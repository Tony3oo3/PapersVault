## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods either optimize 3D representations via score distillation from pretrained T2I models (yielding non-photorealistic objects without backgrounds) or train diffusion models from scratch on small real-world 3D datasets (yielding realistic but less diverse results). No prior approach combines the large 2D prior of a pretrained text-to-image model with real-world multi-view training data to produce images that are simultaneously diverse, photorealistic, 3D-consistent, and include authentic surroundings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt or any number of posed input images (with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics) and produces a batch of N multi-view consistent RGB images at 256×256 resolution, each showing the same 3D object in a realistic background from a specified camera pose. At inference, N can be increased up to 30 per batch, and an autoregressive scheme allows conditioning on previously generated images to render smooth trajectories around the object from arbitrary novel viewpoints. Camera conditioning is provided as a 4×4 pose matrix, focal length/principal point, and per-image RGB mean/variance, injected via LoRA adapters into the attention projections.

## Method

ViewDiff fine-tunes a pretrained latent-diffusion U-Net on CO3Dv2 real-world multi-view data by inserting two novel layers into every U-Net block: (1) a cross-frame-attention layer that replaces self-attention to compute attention across spatial features of all N images in the batch simultaneously, enabling shared object identity and global style; and (2) a projection layer that unprojects compressed 2D features into a 3D voxel grid, aggregates multi-view contributions via an IBRNet-style weighted MLP, refines with a small 3D CNN, and volume-renders back to 2D features using NeRF-style ray marching with a MERF background model. All N images are denoised jointly in a single forward pass with a shared network, and per-image timesteps enable autoregressive generation where previously rendered images (set to t=0) condition the denoising of new views. The model is trained with the standard DDPM noise-prediction L2 loss, alternating between unconditional and image-conditional generation, while a prior dataset preserves the original 2D generation quality.

## Results

On unconditional generation (Table 1), ViewDiff achieves FID scores of 49.39 (Teddybear), 46.45 (Hydrant), 68.86 (Donut), and 56.85 (Apple), improving by ~30% over HoloFusion and ~60% over ViewSetDiffusion. KID scores range from 0.033 to 0.054, roughly halving the best baseline. On single-image reconstruction (Table 2), ViewDiff achieves average PSNR of 21.98/22.49/21.50/25.94 and SSIM of 0.84/0.85/0.85/0.91 across the four categories, matching or exceeding DFM while producing higher-resolution images with backgrounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewDiff outperforms both HoloFusion (HF) and ViewSetDiffusion (VD) on all FID and KID metrics across all four categories for unconditional generation, with FID improvements of 30–60% and KID improvements of ~37–78%. Against DFM on single-image reconstruction, ViewDiff is competitive: it slightly beats DFM on Teddybear (PSNR 21.98 vs 21.81) and Apple (PSNR 25.94 vs 25.79), is on par on Hydrant (PSNR 22.49 vs 22.67), but trails on Donut (PSNR 21.50 vs 23.91, LPIPS 0.18 vs 0.10). The paper notes that DFM operates at 128×128 resolution while ViewDiff produces 256×256 images with backgrounds, making the comparison not entirely apples-to-apples. ViewSetDiffusion is consistently the weakest baseline, producing blurry renderings without backgrounds. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing the projection layer drops PSNR from 22.24 to 16.55 and removing cross-frame-attention drops it to 18.15, while FID/KID remain nearly unchanged, showing these layers are critical for 3D consistency rather than image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) methods that fine-tune pretrained T2I diffusion models for 3D consistency (Zero-1-to-3, One-2-3-45), which preserve diversity but rely on synthetic data and lack backgrounds, and (2) methods trained from scratch on real multi-view 3D datasets (HoloDiffusion, ViewSetDiffusion), which produce photorealistic results but sacrifice diversity due to limited data scale. ViewDiff departs from both by fine-tuning a large pretrained T2I U-Net on real CO3Dv2 data while injecting explicit 3D geometry through a novel projection layer (borrowing volume rendering from NeRF and multi-view aggregation from IBRNet) and cross-frame attention (inspired by video diffusion). The autoregressive generation scheme further distinguishes it from concurrent work like SyncDreamer by showing that a separate 3D reconstruction stage is unnecessary for consistent novel-view rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the 2D prior of billion-scale pretrained T2I models can be preserved while training on small real-world 3D datasets, offering a practical path to diverse, photorealistic multi-view generation without the compute cost of training from scratch or the quality loss of synthetic-data fine-tuning. The ablation revealing that FID/KID remain nearly unchanged when removing the 3D-consistency layers (while PSNR drops sharply) is a useful diagnostic: image quality and geometric consistency are partially decoupled, suggesting future work should target both independently. Limitations include evaluation on only four object categories at 256×256 resolution, view-dependent lighting artifacts inherited from the training data, and object-scale scope; extending to scene-level generation and adding explicit lighting control (e.g., via ControlNet) are natural next steps.
