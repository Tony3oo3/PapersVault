## Problem

In multi-plane scenes, each individual homography can have an extremely low inlier ratio among all correspondences, forcing RANSAC to perform many iterations with the standard 4-point minimal solver. Prior work reduced the sample size to 2 or 3 points by incorporating keypoint scale, orientation, or affine information, but it remained unclear whether pushing to a single point—and accepting the resulting model noise from reusing the same measurements in multiple approximate constraints—could still yield competitive or superior robust estimation performance.

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a single keypoint correspondence (2D positions in both images) together with its associated SIFT scale and orientation, plus known camera intrinsics (focal length and principal point). It outputs a 3×3 Euclidean homography matrix (up to two solutions per sample). Notably, the same scale and orientation values are reused across three distinct constraint families (relative depth, line orientation, and affine correspondence), so the input is deliberately over-constrained relative to the number of geometric degrees of freedom, which is what makes the estimates noisy but the sampling extremely efficient.

## Method

The proposed 1-SIFT solver constructs an 8×10 linear system by combining (i) three relative-depth constraints from the SIFT scale ratio, (ii) one line-orientation constraint from the SIFT angle, and (iii) four affine-correspondence constraints approximated from scale and orientation via a similarity-transform LAF. Although eight constraints are written, only seven are linearly independent; the system is made full-rank by treating the line normal as a point, yielding a 2D null space parameterized by a single free variable. Substituting this parameterization into the Euclidean homography singular-value constraint (median singular value equals 1) produces a quadratic equation with up to two solutions. The key insight is that the constraints, while sharing the same underlying measurements, are algebraically independent because they encode different geometric approximations (depth ratio vs. local linearization of the plane-induced mapping), so the resulting noisy homographies are still sufficient for GC-RANSAC's local optimization to converge to accurate models—demonstrating that sample efficiency outweighs per-sample accuracy in low-inlier-ratio regimes.

## Results

On the HEB benchmark (226,260 homographies, ~4M correspondences), the proposed 1-SIFT solver achieves the best mAA@5° (14.7%), mAA@10° (29.4%), and mAA@5m (43.1%) among all tested solvers, while also being the fastest at 10.3 ms average runtime. This represents a ~30% speedup over the previous state-of-the-art 2-AC solver (15.3 ms) with marginally higher accuracy. The 1-SIFT solver also outperforms the standard 4-point solver by 7.8 points in mAA@10° (29.4 vs. 21.6), demonstrating that extreme sample efficiency compensates for the noisy individual homography estimates when combined with GC-RANSAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 1-SIFT solver outperforms all five baselines on the HEB dataset. Against the closest competitor, 2-AC [4], it achieves slightly higher mAA@10° (29.4 vs. 29.1) and mAA@5m (43.1 vs. 43.0) while running ~33% faster (10.3 vs. 15.3 ms). Compared to the standard 4-point solver [30], the 1-SIFT solver gains 7.8 points in mAA@10° (29.4 vs. 21.6) and 5.2 points in mAA@5m (43.1 vs. 37.9), showing that sample efficiency is more valuable than per-sample accuracy in low-inlier-ratio scenarios. The 2-SIFT solver from [7] is the slowest baseline (52.1 ms) and underperforms the proposed 1-SIFT on all accuracy metrics. The 3-SIFT solver [3] is intermediate in both speed and accuracy. On HPatches, the 1-SIFT solver with SIFT scale/orientation achieves 67.3% AUC at 5px threshold (full dataset), outperforming the 4-point solver (71.3% at 5px on the full set but only 21.6% mAA@10° on HEB), and the single-affine-correspondence solver HSolo [26] (51.0% at 5px).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on reducing minimal sample size for homography estimation, which progressed from the classical 4-point solver through 2-AC (Barath & Hajder, 2016) and 2-SIFT/3-SIFT solvers (Barath et al., 2018/2019) that exploit SIFT scale and orientation. The key conceptual departure is combining the same keypoint scale/orientation measurements in two algebraically independent but approximately redundant constraint sets (relative depth and local affine transformation) to derive a one-point solver, rather than seeking additional independent measurements. The affine constraint formulation is inherited from Oskarsson (2014), and the robust estimation framework is GC-RANSAC (Barath & Matas, 2018), whose local optimization step is what makes the noisy one-point estimates viable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that extreme sample efficiency (one point per hypothesis) can outperform more accurate multi-point solvers in RANSAC for homography estimation—challenges the implicit assumption that better per-sample model accuracy is always preferable. This insight is broadly applicable to other geometric estimation tasks where inlier ratios are very low. However, the method requires known camera intrinsics and per-keypoint scale/orientation, which limits applicability to modern learned detectors that typically output only positions; the authors note that Self-Sca-Ori predictions remain coarse. Open questions include whether improved learned scale/orientation predictors or end-to-end training could close the remaining gap with 4-point solvers in the most challenging scenarios.
