## Problem

Model-based RL methods like DreamerV2 learn latent representations from reconstruction losses that fail to separate task-relevant features from environment-specific appearance, leading to poor out-of-distribution generalization. Naively adding contrastive learning to world models causes feature collapse because the encoder is trained separately from the policy, leaving no correct supervisory signal to anchor the representations. The paper addresses both gaps by combining contrastive invariant feature learning with an intervention-invariant auxiliary regularizer within a world model.

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB images plus a task observation vector (goal location, current position, linear and angular velocities) at each timestep and outputs continuous control actions (rotation in radians and forward distance in meters for a TurtleBot). Depth maps are consumed only during training as a regularizer signal and are not required at deployment, making the perception module transferable to real robots without depth sensors. The world model additionally predicts stochastic latent states and scalar rewards in the imagined latent space for policy optimization.

## Method

ReCoRe augments a DreamerV2-style world model with two key additions: (1) an invariant representation learning module that applies style interventions (spatial jitter, Gaussian blur, color jitter, grayscale, cutout) to RGB observations and trains the encoder via InfoNCE contrastive loss with EMA regularization (MoCo-style momentum 0.999), and (2) an intervention-invariant regularizer that forces the latent state to predict pixel-wise depth (or segmentation/denoising when depth is unavailable), which is invariant to the applied augmentations. The critical insight is that in model-based RL the encoder is decoupled from the policy, so contrastive loss alone collapses the encoder; the auxiliary depth task provides the grounding signal that prevents this collapse while contrastive loss enforces invariance. The latent dynamics use a recurrent network with deterministic hidden states to predict stochastic latent priors, and an actor-critic controller is trained in the imagined latent space. The full world model loss jointly optimizes contrastive loss, depth reconstruction, reward prediction, and KL divergence between posterior and prior latent distributions.

## Results

On the iGibson 1.0 PointGoal navigation OoD generalization task (500k steps), ReCoRe achieves 59.7% average Success Rate (SR) and 0.50 SPL, outperforming the strongest baseline DV2+G DINO (50.0% SR, 0.45 SPL) by ~10 points in SR. At 100k steps, ReCoRe (36.0% SR) is on par with DV2+G DINO (37.1% SR), while model-free baselines RAD (0.5% SR) and CURL (4.7% SR) and model-based DreamerV2 (1.3% SR) and MWM (1.7% SR) all fail to generalize. On DMControl (500k steps), ReCoRe with segmentation as regularizer achieves SOTA total rewards on 8 out of 12 tasks compared to CURL, the closest contrastive-learning baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCoRe outperforms all model-based baselines (DreamerV2, DreamerV2+DA, MWM) by a wide margin on OoD navigation: at 500k steps, ReCoRe achieves 59.7% SR versus 1.5% (DreamerV2), 13.0% (DreamerV2+DA), and 2.5% (MWM). Against model-free baselines, ReCoRe also dominates: 59.7% SR vs. 36.3% (RAD) and 31.4% (CURL) at 500k steps. The closest competitor is DV2+G DINO (DreamerV2 with frozen Grounding DINO features), which slightly edges out ReCoRe at 100k steps (37.1% vs. 36.0% SR) but is clearly surpassed at 500k steps (50.0% vs. 59.7% SR, 0.45 vs. 0.50 SPL). On DMControl, ReCoRe with segmentation regularizer beats CURL on 8 of 12 tasks at 500k steps (e.g., Walker walk: 960 vs. 902; Ball in cup: 984 vs. 959), while RAD and SAC+AE achieve higher rewards on some tasks but do not optimize features for downstream generalization. The ablation confirms that removing the intervention-invariant regularizer (ReCoRe-D) causes near-total collapse (0.8% SR at 500k), validating the necessity of the auxiliary depth task.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCoRe builds directly on the DreamerV2 architecture for its latent dynamics model and actor-critic controller, but departs from it by replacing the standard VAE reconstruction objective with a contrastive (InfoNCE) representation learning module augmented by an intervention-invariant auxiliary task. The contrastive learning component draws from the model-free RL literature (CURL, MoCo, BYOL) and the self-supervised vision literature, but the key conceptual departure is identifying that naive integration of contrastive loss into a world model causes complete feature collapse, and proposing an auxiliary task (depth, segmentation, denoising) that is invariant to the same augmentations used in the contrastive objective. The paper also positions itself against frozen foundation-model features (Grounding DINO) as a computationally cheaper alternative for embodied agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intervention-invariant regularizer provides a practical recipe for making world-model-based RL robust to appearance shifts without requiring large-scale pretraining, which is important for deployment on resource-constrained robots. However, the method's reliance on auxiliary signals (depth, segmentation) during training limits its generality, and the DMControl results show only modest gains over CURL when no natural invariant auxiliary task exists. Open questions include how to automatically discover appropriate intervention-invariant tasks for arbitrary domains, whether the approach composes with DreamerV3's scaling techniques, and whether the perception module's sim-to-real transfer can extend to the full controller in real-world deployment.
