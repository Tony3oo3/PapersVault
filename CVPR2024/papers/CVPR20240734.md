## Problem

Self-training for 3D UDA inevitably produces unreliable pseudo boxes in the confidence threshold interval, which previous methods either reweight or partially select but still use to supervise training, poisoning the process. Additionally, cross-dataset Instance-level Point Number Inconsistency (IPNI)—where 64-beam datasets contain far more points per instance than 32-beam datasets—causes proposals to cluster around source-like density regions and confuses RoI features across categories, further degrading pseudo label quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain LiDAR point clouds (NuScenes 32-beam, KITTI 64-beam, or Waymo 64-beam) and unlabeled target-domain point clouds, operating within a fixed detection range of [-75.2, 75.2] m in X/Y and [-2, 4] m in Z with 0.1×0.1×0.15 m voxels. It outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, size, heading angle) with category labels for Car, Pedestrian, and Cyclist, evaluated via AP_BEV and AP_3D at IoU thresholds of 0.7/0.5/0.5 respectively. The detector is a two-stage voxel-based architecture (PVRCNN or SECOND-IOU) generating 512 proposals during training and 100 during testing per sample.

## Method

PERE introduces three mechanisms within the iterative self-training loop. First, Complementary Augmentation (CA) handles unreliable boxes (confidence in [T_neg, T_pos]) by weighted sampling between PointRemove (deleting all interior points) and BoxReplace (swapping the box with a same-category high-confidence box, rescaling and re-rotating its points to the unreliable box's location), then discarding the original unreliable box from training. Second, Additional Proposal Generation via Interpolation and Extrapolation (I&E) produces two extra proposals per high-confidence proposal—one interpolating toward its nearest remaining proposal and one extrapolating away—to push detection boundaries into sparse-point regions and counter IPNI-induced proposal clustering. Third, Cross-Domain RoI Feature Alignment reformulates the triplet loss into intra-domain and inter-domain components that pull same-category RoI features together and push different-category features apart across both source and target domains, mitigating the feature confusion caused by IPNI. The total loss combines the standard detection loss with a weighted triplet loss (η=0.1), and pseudo labels are updated every 2 epochs.

## Results

PERE achieves average AP_BEV of 52.29 and AP_3D of 44.85 on N→K (PVRCNN), 62.12 and 52.63 on W→K (PVRCNN), 46.03 and 40.57 on N→K (SECOND-IOU), and 54.37 and 43.30 on W→K (SECOND-IOU). For Car detection with PVRCNN, PERE surpasses the second-best method by 3.68%, 4.15%, and 1.21% in AP_BEV (12.25%, 7.84%, and 5.60% in AP_3D) across N→K, W→K, and W→N respectively. PERE closes the AP_3D gap to the oracle model by up to 82.09% (N→K, SECOND-IOU, Car) and 70.77% (N→K, PVRCNN, Car).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PERE consistently outperforms all four baselines (ST3D, ST3D++, DTS, and LD) across every benchmark where it is reported. Against ST3D [42], the closest self-training baseline, PERE gains 9.70 AP_3D points on Car for N→K (PVRCNN: 68.34 vs. 58.64) and 17.18 points on N→K (SECOND: 66.84 vs. 49.66). Against ST3D++ [43], which adds class-balanced pseudo-labeling, PERE still leads by 7.46 AP_3D on Car (N→K, PVRCNN) and 16.30 on Car (N→K, SECOND). DTS [11], which addresses density inconsistency via upsampling/downsampling, is surpassed by PERE by 10.52 AP_3D on Car (N→K, PVRCNN) and 8.45 on Car (N→K, SECOND). LD [39] is only evaluated on W→N (high-beam to low-beam) transitions where it achieves 20.12/22.03 AP_3D on Car (PVRCNN/SECOND), and the paper reports PERE surpasses it by 5.60% AP_3D on W→N (PVRCNN). Notably, PERE achieves the best Pedestrian AP_BEV on all tasks and the best Cyclist AP_3D on all reported benchmarks, indicating broad category-level gains rather than improvements limited to a single class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ST3D self-training framework for 3D UDA, which established the iterative selection-training loop with confidence-threshold-based pseudo box filtering. It departs from prior handling of unreliable boxes (CL3D's soft reweighting, ST3D's voting) by entirely removing or replacing them via a complementary augmentation that exploits the editability of point clouds. The cross-domain RoI feature alignment reformulates triplet losses from 2D person re-identification and image retrieval for 3D voxel-based detection, while the additional proposal generation (interpolation/extrapolation) addresses the instance-level point number inconsistency (IPNI) that prior density-matching approaches (LD, DTS) tackle through lossy point beam resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PERE identifies and addresses two concrete failure modes in 3D UDA self-training—unreliable pseudo boxes poisoning training and IPNI causing RoI feature confusion across beam-count domains—that prior methods only partially mitigate. The practical impact is meaningful for autonomous driving pipelines that must transfer across sensor configurations (32-beam vs 64-beam LiDAR). Limitations include reliance on two-stage voxel-based detectors, the need for a non-trivial database of high-confidence boxes for BoxReplace, and evaluation restricted to three datasets with three object categories. Open questions include whether the IPNI formulation generalizes to one-stage detectors, whether the triplet alignment scales to more categories, and whether the complementary augmentation strategy transfers to other 3D tasks like segmentation.
