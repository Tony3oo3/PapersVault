## Problem

Existing point cloud upsampling methods rely on suboptimal local shape representations (single feature vectors or raw k-NN patches) and point generation strategies (folding, feature expansion, or learned distance functions) that produce outliers, shrinkage artifacts, or struggle to predict accurate 3D coordinates from sparse, noisy inputs. The paper identifies that neither the representation nor the generation mechanism has been jointly optimized for upsampling quality and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240660/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse point cloud (256 points per patch during training; 2,048 points for full shapes at test time) and produces a dense point cloud at a target upsampling rate (4× or 16× by default). Ball query with a fixed radius makes neighbor selection density-sensitive, so the number of local points varies with input sparsity. A parameter-free Fixed-to-Arbitrary (F2A) strategy extends the fixed 4× model to any target rate by midpoint interpolation followed by one 4× upsampling pass and random point dropping.

## Method

RepKPU reformulates upsampling as the deformation of kernel points via a Kernel-to-Displacement paradigm. The RepKPoints Extraction Module (REM) projects local features onto 15 deformable kernel points on a sphere using KPConv-style weight averaging, yielding a density-sensitive, large-receptive-field, position-adaptive local representation that generalizes both feature vectors and k-NN patches. The KP-Queries Generation Module (KGM) creates r kernel point queries (one per output point) with predefined spherical positions and learned features. A three-layer multi-head cross-attention transformer then uses KP-Queries as Q and RepKPoints as K/V to convert queries into displacement features, which a final MLP maps to 3D offsets relative to the center point. Training is supervised with Chamfer Distance plus fitting and repulsive losses on RepKPoint offsets, and the encoder uses vector attention with dense connections.

## Results

On PU-GAN (4× upsampling), RepKPU achieves the best CD (0.248×10⁻³) and P2F (1.906×10⁻³), while being second only to Grad-PU on HD (2.880 vs. 2.623 ×10⁻³). On the larger PU1K dataset (4×), RepKPU leads all baselines on every metric: CD 0.327×10⁻³, HD 0.268×10⁻², P2F 0.938×10⁻³. In terms of efficiency, RepKPU is 29% faster than the second-fastest method (MPU) at inference (0.215 vs. 0.303 s/pc) and uses 17% less GPU memory during training (5.860G vs. 7.050G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepKPU outperforms all eight baselines on CD and P2F for 4× upsampling on PU-GAN, with the closest competitor being Grad-PU (CD 0.264 vs. 0.248; P2F 1.982 vs. 1.906). The sole metric where RepKPU trails is HD on PU-GAN 4×, where Grad-PU achieves 2.623×10⁻³ versus RepKPU's 2.880×10⁻³. On PU1K (4×), RepKPU dominates every baseline across all three metrics, beating the next-best PU-Flow by 8.1% on CD (0.327 vs. 0.356), 16.5% on HD (0.268 vs. 0.321), and 28.5% on P2F (0.938 vs. 1.315). In the robustness test (Table 3), RepKPU achieves the best average CD (0.732) and P2F (5.556), while Dis-PU is closest on CD (0.745) but lags significantly on HD (8.891 vs. 8.204). For arbitrary-scale upsampling (Table 4), RepKPU consistently beats Grad-PU on CD and P2F at every tested rate (5×–19×), with the gap widening at higher rates (e.g., at 19×: CD 0.102 vs. 0.278, P2F 2.030 vs. 7.701). RepKPU is also the fastest method in inference across all settings, being 29% faster than MPU and 30% faster than Grad-PU on PU-GAN 4×.

## Relation to Prior Work

RepKPU repurposes the kernel point mechanism from KPConv (originally a convolution operator for point cloud classification/segmentation) as both a local shape representation (RepKPoints) and a generation scaffold (KP-Queries), unifying representation and point generation under a single geometric primitive. This departs from the two dominant upsampling paradigms—feature-expansion/folding-based generation (PU-Net, MPU, PU-GCN) and denoising-based refinement (Grad-PU, Dis-PU, PU-Flow)—by reformulating upsampling as the deformation of predefined kernel points guided by cross-attention with RepKPoints. The encoder leverages vector attention (Point Transformer) for feature extraction, and the F2A strategy for arbitrary-scale upsampling builds on midpoint interpolation from Grad-PU, making it applicable to any fixed-scale method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RepKPU offers a practical efficiency-accuracy tradeoff: it is 29% faster than Grad-PU while achieving lower CD and P2F on both PU-GAN and PU1K, and uses 17% less training memory than MPU. The parameter-free F2A strategy is broadly applicable to other fixed-scale upsamplers, and the robustness across diverse input patterns (uniform, noisy, random, mixed) addresses a real deployment concern. Open questions include whether the 4×-trained model with F2A can match dedicated high-rate training at very large upsampling factors, and whether the RepKPoints representation transfers to other point generation tasks (completion, synthesis) beyond upsampling.
