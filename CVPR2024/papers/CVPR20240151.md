## Problem

Transferring appearance from one 3D scene representation to another with different geometry in a semantically meaningful way remains unsolved. Existing 2D image-analogy and style-transfer methods cannot be naively lifted to 3D due to non-differentiable operations and lack of multi-view consistency, while text-based NeRF editing methods often fail to capture the precise intended appearance. The paper asks whether one can generalize classic image analogies to NeRFs so that semantically related regions between source and target geometries receive the correct appearance transfer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source NeRF (encoding both geometry and view-dependent RGB appearance) and a target NeRF (encoding geometry only, with no meaningful appearance) as input. Both are rendered from 100 random views, and 5,000 non-background pixels are sampled per view to extract per-pixel features, positions, normals, and viewing directions. The output is a new NeRF analogy trained as a 3D-consistent radiance field that predicts view-dependent appearance at the target's known geometry, with the density collapsing to a Dirac delta so no volume rendering is needed. Objects are assumed roughly aligned in orientation and pose, and the target geometry should be textured for best DiNO-ViT correspondence quality.

## Method

The core mechanism uses DiNO-ViT features as dense semantic descriptors: per-pixel ViT activations are extracted from renderings of both source and target, and a discrete mapping φ is established by argmax cosine similarity between target and source feature vectors, linking each target location to its most semantically similar source location. This mapping is intentionally non-bijective and not enforced to be 3D-consistent, allowing 1:n transfers (e.g., one chair leg to four table legs). A NeRF analogy L_θ is then trained via direct supervised learning (no differentiable rendering) to predict the source's view-dependent radiance at each target position under the corresponding source viewing direction, with the target's surface normal fed as additional high-frequency input. A difference-of-Gaussians edge loss (σ₁=1.0, σ₂=1.6, weight λ=50 after 15% of training) regularizes high-frequency detail around edges and silhouettes. For efficiency, the cosine-similarity search is restricted to the 5 closest source views via importance sampling.

## Results

The proposed NeRF Analogies method achieves the highest scores across all quantitative metrics: BPSNR of 36.16, BSSIM of 0.984, and CLIP direction consistency of 0.992, outperforming the next-best baseline DIA (33.06 BPSNR, 0.968 BSSIM). In the user study (42 participants), the method is preferred by 58.5% of users for transfer quality, 76.7% for multi-view consistency, and 84.8% for overall quality, with a combined preference of 68.4% — all statistically significant (p < 0.001). Results are demonstrated on object pairs, multi-object scenes, and real-world scenes from MiP-NeRF 360 and Tanks and Temples datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Neural Style Transfer (ST), WCT, Deep Image Analogies (DIA), and SNeRF. The proposed method wins decisively on all metrics against every baseline. The closest quantitative competitor is DIA, which achieves 33.06 BPSNR and 0.968 BSSIM (vs. 36.16 and 0.984 for the proposed method), but DIA lacks 3D information and is not multi-view-consistent, resulting in only 20.5% user preference for MVC versus 76.7% for the proposed method. SNeRF, the only other 3D-consistent baseline, scores 32.41 BPSNR and 0.947 BSSIM, with its VGG-based style transfer struggling on the highly non-stationary source appearances used in experiments. ST and WCT perform worst overall (BPSNR 25.14 and 28.64 respectively), producing blurry, semantically incoherent results with severe multi-view inconsistencies. The user study confirms the quantitative gap: the proposed method receives 68.4% combined preference versus 23.0% for DIA, 4.8% for SNeRF, and 1.9% each for ST and WCT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classic 2D Image Analogies formulation [Hertzmann et al., 2023] to 3D by leveraging DiNO-ViT features for dense semantic correspondence between rendered views of a source NeRF and a target geometry. It departs from the NeRF editing literature, which has largely focused on either shape editing, text-driven appearance changes, or non-semantic stylization, by introducing a geometry-preserving, semantically-guided appearance transfer that explicitly matches corresponding parts across objects. The key technical departure from prior 2D analogy methods is that the non-differentiable nearest-neighbor search is replaced by a ViT-feature-based mapping, and the 3D consistency is enforced by training a NeRF on the resulting position-appearance pairs rather than naively lifting 2D outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method opens a practical 'mix-and-match' space for 3D content creation, allowing users to combine captured geometry with appearance from reference objects in a semantically meaningful way, without the imprecision of text prompts or the non-semantic nature of style transfer. Key limitations include the inability to transfer fine-grained texture (point-based transfer), sensitivity to rotational symmetry, and the assumption of roughly aligned objects. Open questions raised by the authors include 3D-consistent texture transfer, intrinsic scene parameter transfer (roughness, specular albedo), and automatic selection of optimal views for training the NeRF analogy.
