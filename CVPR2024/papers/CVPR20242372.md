## Problem

Existing μ-mesh construction methods rely on stage-based pipelines (decimation → remeshing → displacement baking) where each stage independently minimizes a local objective, leading to error accumulation, suboptimal bounding shell volumes, and no mechanism to jointly optimize geometry and displacements toward the ultimate rendering-quality goal. No prior approach treats the full construction as a single end-to-end differentiable problem guided by rendering consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242372/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution polygon mesh (0.1M–4M triangles) and initializes a low-polygon base mesh via GPU-based QEM decimation; the base mesh topology is then frozen while vertex positions remain optimizable. The output is a compact μ-mesh consisting of the optimized base mesh (vertex offsets applied to the initial base vertices) and per-μ-vertex scalar displacements at multiple LOD levels. Only the updated base mesh and displacement array are saved to disk; subdivision topology and μ-vertex positions are regenerated on-the-fly during rendering, keeping storage minimal.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully differentiable end-to-end framework that jointly optimizes base-vertex offsets and μ-vertex displacements under a rendering-consistency loss computed via nvdiffrast on pre-tessellated μ-meshes. To stabilize optimization and prevent mesh tangling, the authors introduce a cotangent-Laplacian diffusion reparameterization (adapted from Nicolet et al.) applied to both the vector-valued offsets and scalar displacements, yielding update rules with an (I + λL)⁻² damping factor. Training follows a curriculum: a warmup phase at level 0, then alternating 1-to-4 Loop subdivision and optimization up to a maximum level, with new μ-vertex displacements initialized by interpolation and Laplacian operators recomputed after each subdivision. Three novel regularizers are integrated: a shell-volume term (sum of displacement variances per base vertex), an isotropy term (variance of edge lengths per base triangle), and a visual-guided adaptive LOD stopping criterion that measures per-face rendering improvement between adjacent subdivision levels to decide where to stop subdividing.

## Results

On 121 high-resolution meshes (0.1M–4M triangles), the proposed method achieves the highest rendering PSNR across all three size subsets: 42.93 dB (medium), 44.18 dB (large), and 44.89 dB (extreme), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art staged method [28] by 1.3–2.3 dB. Shell volume is also minimized in all subsets (e.g., 0.083 vs. 0.089 on extreme meshes), while construction time remains competitive (4.8 min vs. 5.3 min on extreme meshes). Geometry error and isotropy are comparable to the staged baseline, confirming that the end-to-end optimization does not sacrifice base-mesh quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the commercial Simplygon pipeline [29] and the recent staged method by Maggiordomo et al. [28]. Against Simplygon, the proposed method achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 44.89 vs. 35.02 dB on extreme meshes, a ~10 dB gap) and lower shell volume (0.083 vs. 0.124), at the cost of longer construction time (4.8 vs. 3.1 min). Against the state-of-the-art staged method [28], the proposed method wins on PSNR by 1.3–2.3 dB across all subsets, reduces shell volume by 5–7% (e.g., 0.083 vs. 0.089 on extreme), and is slightly faster on extreme meshes (4.8 vs. 5.3 min). On base-mesh geometry error, the two methods are nearly tied (1.80 vs. 1.84 ×10⁻⁵ on extreme), while isotropy is marginally lower for the proposed method (0.747 vs. 0.751), reflecting the trade-off of optimizing global rendering objectives rather than enforcing strict equilateral triangles. Overall, the end-to-end approach consistently outperforms both staged baselines on the metrics that directly determine μ-mesh rendering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established staged μ-mesh construction pipeline (decimation → remeshing → subdivision → displacement baking) used by NVIDIA's whitepaper, Simplygon, and Maggiordomo et al. [28], replacing it with a single end-to-end differentiable optimization. The key technical lineage is Nicolet et al. [34], whose diffusion reparameterization and UNIFORMADAM optimizer for standard mesh inverse rendering are adapted here to the multi-level μ-mesh setting with both vector-valued offsets and scalar displacements. The differentiable rasterization backbone (nvdiffrast) and the progressive/curriculum learning structure are borrowed from the broader differentiable rendering literature, while the shell-volume and isotropy regularizers are novel objectives specific to the μ-mesh primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As μ-mesh becomes a first-class GPU primitive (NVIDIA Ada architecture), a principled construction method that jointly optimizes geometry fidelity, shell volume, and adaptive LOD is practically important for content pipelines. The end-to-end formulation eliminates error accumulation inherent in staged pipelines and makes it straightforward to plug in additional objectives, which is a meaningful architectural advantage. Limitations include evaluation only under fixed diffuse shading and a single environment map, no treatment of textures or materials, and construction times that are comparable to or slightly longer than the staged baseline. Open questions include scalability to very large scenes, handling of non-manifold or open meshes, and whether the visual-guided LOD criterion generalizes to dynamic or animated content.
