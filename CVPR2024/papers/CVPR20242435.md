## Problem

Slot-based autoencoders for unsupervised object-centric learning struggle with complex real-world scenes due to two intertwined issues: (1) the encoder's slot-attention module produces inferior object segmentation masks compared to the decoder's cross-attention masks, yet this gap has not been exploited to improve slot generation; and (2) autoregressive transformer decoders overfit to teacher-forced tokens, progressively neglecting slot vectors for later positions and thereby weakening the supervisory signal that drives object-centric learning in the encoder.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, encodes it via a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 into n patch-level features, and groups them into k slot vectors (7 for COCO, 6 for PASCAL, 11 for MOVi-C, 24 for MOVi-E). The reconstruction target is not the raw image but the self-supervised DINO feature map, and the primary evaluation outputs are slot-attention masks (n×k probability distributions over patches and slots) derived from both the encoder's slot-attention module and the decoder's cross-attention layers. The autoregressive decoder operates on a permuted sequence of target features with a permutation-specific [BOS] token, producing predictions that are inverse-permuted before the reconstruction loss is applied.

## Method

SPOT introduces a two-stage training procedure. In stage 1, the model is trained solely with a reconstruction loss in DINO feature space. In stage 2, a teacher-student self-training scheme distills the teacher's decoder cross-attention masks (converted to hard assignments via argmax/one-hot, aligned with Hungarian matching) into the student's encoder slot-attention masks via a cross-entropy loss, improving object-specific slot generation. Simultaneously, the autoregressive transformer decoder is augmented with nine predefined patch-order permutations (eight directional sweeps plus one spiral), from which one is randomly sampled per training step; each permutation uses a distinct [BOS] token so the decoder knows the current ordering. This forces every token position—regardless of its location in the default reading order—to rely on slot information rather than accumulated context, equalizing the gradient signal back to the encoder across all positions. The ViT image encoder is frozen in stage 1 but fine-tuned in stage 2, where the self-training loss acts as a stabilizing factor that makes this fine-tuning feasible.

## Results

On the challenging COCO dataset, SPOT achieves 34.7±0.1 MBO^i and 44.3±0.3 MBO^c (without test-time ensembling), surpassing the prior state-of-the-art DINOSAUR by 2.4 and 5.5 points respectively. On PASCAL VOC 2012, SPOT reaches 48.1±0.4 MBO^i and 55.3±0.4 MBO^c, ranking second only to SlotDiffusion on MBO^i (50.4) while matching it on MBO^c. On the synthetic MOVi-C and MOVi-E benchmarks, SPOT achieves 47.0±1.2 and 39.9±1.1 MBO^i respectively, outperforming all prior methods including (Stable-)LSD and DINOSAUR. Test-time ensembling of nine permutations yields a small additional gain (e.g., 35.0 MBO^i on COCO) at the cost of increased inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPOT outperforms all baselines on COCO, the most challenging real-world dataset, achieving 34.7 MBO^i vs. DINOSAUR's 32.3, SlotDiffusion's 31.0, and (Stable-)LSD's 30.4. On PASCAL, SPOT (48.1 MBO^i) is the only method to come within 2.3 points of SlotDiffusion (50.4), while clearly beating DINOSAUR (44.0) and Rotating Features (40.7); on MBO^c, SPOT (55.3) ties SlotDiffusion (55.3) and surpasses DINOSAUR (51.2) by over 4 points. On MOVi-C, SPOT (47.0 MBO^i) beats (Stable-)LSD (45.6) and DINOSAUR (42.4) by 1.4 and 4.6 points respectively. On MOVi-E, SPOT (39.9 MBO^i) surpasses (Stable-)LSD (39.0) and DINOSAUR-MLP (35.5), with a notable 9.7-point margin over SlotDiffusion (30.2). The only scenario where SPOT does not rank first is PASCAL MBO^i, where SlotDiffusion leads by 2.3 points, though SPOT matches it on the class-level MBO^c metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPOT builds directly on the slot-based autoencoder paradigm introduced by Slot Attention [34], the DINOSAUR framework [41] that established self-supervised pre-trained features (DINO) as effective reconstruction targets and revealed that decoder-side attention masks outperform encoder-side masks for object segmentation, and the SLATE autoregressive transformer decoder [44]. The paper's two contributions—self-training via slot-attention distillation and patch-order sequence permutations—target complementary weaknesses: the former refines the encoder's slot-attention module by distilling superior decoder masks, while the latter addresses the autoregressive decoder's tendency to over-rely on past ground-truth tokens at the expense of slot vectors. Unlike prior work that improves either the encoder (e.g., bi-level optimization, architectural modifications) or the decoder (e.g., diffusion-based methods) in isolation, SPOT simultaneously strengthens both components within a unified two-stage training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPOT sets a new state of the art on COCO—the most challenging real-world benchmark for unsupervised object-centric learning—surpassing DINOSAUR by 2.7 MBO^i and 5.9 MBO^c, which is meaningful given the difficulty of decomposing complex multi-object scenes without labels. The sequence permutation strategy is architecturally trivial (only permuting input/output order and adding multiple [BOS] tokens) yet addresses a fundamental training-dynamics issue in autoregressive decoders, making it a low-cost technique potentially transferable to other vision tasks using autoregressive models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based or multi-modal object-centric learning, whether the self-training distillation generalizes beyond slot-attention to other bottleneck structures, and whether the gains on COCO translate to even more complex or out-of-distribution scenes.
