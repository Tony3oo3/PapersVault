## Problem

Encoder-decoder networks (ED-Nets) are the standard architecture for depth completion, but their internal working mechanism has remained ambiguous. Through feature visualization, the authors reveal that decoder features are inherently sparse (complementing encoder features to produce dense fused features), yet they are derived from dense fused features at the previous stage via a 'dense → sparse' process that destroys feature completeness and loses information. Existing ED-Nets therefore fail to fully exploit their own internal dense features, capping their performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse LiDAR depth map (roughly 4% of pixels populated) and a corresponding RGB image, and outputs a dense depth map at full resolution. The DFU module operates as a plug-and-play add-on: it extracts a low-resolution depth feature at 1/8 spatial resolution (H/8 × W/8 × 64 channels) from the sparse depth via a small 8-block residual network, then progressively upsamples it to full resolution using multi-scale fused encoder-decoder features from the host ED-Net as guidance. The module adds approximately 1.7 M parameters and 40 G FLOPs per layer, making it lightweight relative to the base network.

## Method

The Depth Feature Upsampling Network (DFU) explicitly leverages the dense fused encoder-decoder features of an existing ED-Net to guide the progressive upsampling of a low-resolution depth feature to full resolution across four stages, preserving feature completeness throughout. Each upsampling stage employs a Confidence-aware Guidance Module (CGM) with two components: (1) a confidence map predicted via a 3×3 convolution over the concatenated depth and guidance features, which filters unreliable values and directs the guidance to prioritize uncertain regions; and (2) Guidance with Adaptive Receptive Fields (GARF), which decomposes spatially-variant depthwise kernels over pre-generated multi-scale Fourier-Bessel bases (3×3, 5×5, 7×7) and predicts per-pixel weights to select an appropriate receptive field, handling uneven depth distributions. The refined feature is combined with the original via a confidence-weighted blend. DFU can be stacked to multiple layers (up to three) for incremental gains, and is trained in a two-stage supervised procedure (first the ED-Net, then DFU with L1+L2 loss) on KITTI and NYUv2.

## Results

On the KITTI online benchmark, the improved LRRU-Base (SPN-based) with three-layer DFU achieves a new SOTA RMSE of 686.46 mm and MAE of 187.95 mm, outperforming the previous best BEV@DC (697.44 mm RMSE) by 10.98 mm. The improved dual-branch ED-Net reaches 706.23 mm RMSE and 199.14 mm MAE, surpassing PointDC (736.07 mm) and GraphCSPN (738.41 mm). On NYUv2, the improved dual-branch ED-Net achieves 96.27 mm RMSE and 12.92×10⁻³ REL, while the improved LRRU-Base maintains 90.77 mm RMSE. The DFU module adds only ~3.1 M parameters and ~76 G FLOPs for the three-layer variant, a modest overhead relative to the base ED-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The improved LRRU-Base (686.46 mm RMSE) achieves a new SOTA on KITTI, beating the previous best BEV@DC (697.44 mm) by 10.98 mm and PointDC (736.07 mm) by 49.61 mm. The improved dual-branch ED-Net (706.23 mm RMSE) outperforms CompletionFormer (708.30 mm), RigNet (712.66 mm), GFormer (721.48 mm), GraphCSPN (738.41 mm), and PointDC (736.07 mm) on KITTI RMSE, though it trails BEV@DC (697.44 mm) and DySPN (709.12 mm) by a small margin. On NYUv2, methods that explicitly fuse 3D point-cloud information (BEV@DC at 89 mm RMSE, PointDC at 89 mm) still outperform the proposed ED-Net variants (96–98 mm), while the improved LRRU-Base matches PointFusion and GraphCSPN at 91 mm. The single-branch ED-Net, despite being the simplest architecture, improves from 745.16 to 719.65 mm RMSE with DFU, closing the gap to multi-branch methods. Overall, DFU consistently reduces RMSE by 10–25 mm across all base architectures tested, with the largest relative gains observed on sparser depth inputs (fewer LiDAR lines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard encoder-decoder (ED-Net) architecture that underpins most depth completion methods (S2D, GuideNet, LRRU, etc.) and on the decomposed guided dynamic filter approach for multi-modal feature fusion. Its key conceptual departure is a diagnostic observation: by visualizing internal feature maps, the authors show that ED-Net decoders perform repeated 'dense → sparse' transformations that destroy feature completeness, meaning the fused encoder-decoder features already contain rich dense information that existing methods underutilize. The proposed DFU repurposes these existing dense features as guidance for a separate upsampling pathway, while the GARF module extends guided convolution by decomposing adaptors over multi-scale Fourier-Bessel bases (borrowed from dynamic convolution literature) to achieve spatially adaptive receptive fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DFU (adding ~1.7M parameters and ~40G FLOPs per layer) makes it immediately applicable to any existing ED-Net or SPN-based method without architectural redesign, and it achieves Top-1 on the KITTI benchmark. The feature-completeness analysis provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why ED-Nets plateau, potentially guiding future decoder designs. Limitations include performance saturation beyond three DFU layers, overfitting on smaller datasets (NYUv2) with large backbones, and the fact that the method still depends on the quality of the underlying ED-Net's internal features. An open question is whether the 'dense → sparse' destruction can be eliminated at the source (in the decoder itself) rather than compensated downstream.
