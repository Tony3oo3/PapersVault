## Problem

Transformer-based human pose estimators achieve state-of-the-art accuracy but are too large for edge deployment, while smaller variants under-fit and lose significant performance. Existing knowledge distillation approaches require training a separate teacher network or performing costly feature-space alignment, and no prior method increases the effective depth of a small transformer without adding parameters. This paper asks whether a small transformer can be made to behave like a deeper one at training time while retaining single-pass inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (256×192 for MSCOCO experiments) and produces either 17-channel keypoint heatmaps (heatmap-based variants) or direct 2D keypoint coordinates (regression-based SDPose-Reg variant). Internally, the backbone (StemNet) extracts a feature map that is split into patches and linearly projected into visual tokens, which are concatenated with K learnable keypoint tokens before entering the transformer encoder. At inference, only one forward pass through the transformer is performed, so the computational footprint matches the base TokenPose architecture.

## Method

SDPose introduces a Multi-Cycled Transformer (MCT) module that feeds the same token sequence through the transformer layers N times during training, using each cycle's output as the next cycle's input, thereby increasing the model's 'latent depth' without adding parameters. A self-distillation loss then transfers knowledge from later cycles to earlier ones: keypoint-token and visual-token MSE losses align cycle i's output with cycle i+1's output, while a pose prediction loss (MSE against ground-truth heatmaps) is applied to every cycle's prediction head output. Because the transformer's input and output tokens share the same vector space, no additional projection or alignment is needed for cross-cycle distillation. At inference, the model performs a single forward pass, so the extra training-time computation of multiple cycles does not affect deployment cost. The approach is validated on MSCOCO and CrowdPose, and extended to a regression-based head and to ImageNet classification with DeiT-Tiny.

## Results

On MSCOCO validation (256×192 input), SDPose-S-V2 achieves 73.5% AP with only 6.2M parameters and 4.7 GFLOPs, setting a new state-of-the-art among tiny transformer-based pose estimators. SDPose-S-V1 reaches 72.3% AP at 6.6M params / 2.4 GFLOPs, and the smallest variant SDPose-T attains 69.7% AP with just 4.4M params and 1.8 GFLOPs. On the regression-based track, SDPose-Reg scores 73.0% AP at 5.4M params / 2.4 GFLOPs, and on the more challenging Crowdpose benchmark, SDPose-S-V2 improves over its baseline by 2.2% AP (64.5% vs 62.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct baselines (TokenPose variants), SDPose consistently wins at identical parameter and FLOP budgets: SDPose-S-V1 gains +2.8% AP over TokenPose-S-V1 (72.3 vs 69.5), SDPose-S-V2 gains +1.7% over TokenPose-S-V2 (73.5 vs 71.8), and SDPose-B gains +0.5% over TokenPose-B (73.7 vs 73.2). The smallest model SDPose-T matches TokenPose-S-V1's AP (69.7 vs 69.5) while cutting parameters by 33% and FLOPs by 25%. Compared to PPT-S (69.8% AP, 6.6M params, 2.0 GFLOPs), SDPose-T achieves nearly the same AP with 33% fewer parameters and 11% fewer FLOPs; SDPose-S-V2 (73.5%) also edges out PPT-B (73.4%) while using half the parameters (6.2M vs 13.2M). On the regression track, SDPose-Reg (73.0% AP) beats DistilPose-S (71.6%) by 1.4% at the same 5.4M/2.4 GFLOPs budget, and surpasses Poseur-MobileNetV2 (71.9%) by 1.1% with fewer parameters. On Crowdpose, both SDPose variants outperform their TokenPose counterparts by 1.6–2.2% AP, confirming generalizability to crowded scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TokenPose's tokenized keypoint representation and DistilPose's regression-based head, introducing the Multi-Cycled Transformer (MCT) module that loops tokens through the same transformer layers multiple times to increase 'latent depth' without adding parameters. It departs from prior knowledge distillation approaches (OKDHP, DistilPose) by eliminating the need for a separate teacher model and avoiding vector-space alignment, instead leveraging the fact that MCT cycles operate in the same token space to enable direct self-distillation. The self-distillation paradigm draws from 'Be Your Own Teacher' and 'Born-Again Neural Networks' but is, to the authors' claim, the first application to transformer-based HPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDPose addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying transformer-based HPE on edge devices by showing that small models can be substantially improved without increasing inference cost, achieving SOTA among tiny networks (73.5% AP at 6.2M params). However, the gains are modest (0.5–2.8% AP over baselines), the benefit plateaus and even reverses beyond two cycles, and the method adds training complexity (multiple forward passes per iteration). Open questions include whether the MCT concept generalizes beyond HPE to other token-based transformer tasks, and whether the 'latent depth' framing can be formalized more rigorously to predict optimal cycle counts.
