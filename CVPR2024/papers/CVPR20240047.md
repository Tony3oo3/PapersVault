## Problem

Existing NeRF-based inverse rendering methods represent illumination as 2D environmental maps, implicitly assuming all light comes from infinity. This oversimplification fails for glossy objects surrounded by nearby objects that contribute substantial local radiance, leading to degraded geometry and material reconstruction. The paper addresses this by formulating lighting as a full 5D function that captures position-dependent light interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240047/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view RGB images of a single glossy object (10 real-world and 10 synthetic scenes, each with multiple viewpoints) and produces a watertight 3D triangle mesh extracted via marching cubes from the learned SDF, along with per-vertex PBR material maps (metalness, roughness, albedo). The outputs are directly compatible with conventional rendering engines for relighting, which is a key practical advantage over raw NeRF representations.

## Method

The pipeline is two-stage. In the fields learning stage, a NeuS-based SDF object field and a Mip-NeRF environmental field are jointly trained; the object color is decoupled into albedo and lighting-modulated components, and a dynamic weighting loss (inversely proportional to the albedo–final-color discrepancy) down-weights uncertain reflective regions to stabilize geometry. In the material learning stage, a 5D Neural Plenoptic Function (NeP) is constructed from the frozen environmental field via ray tracing against the extracted mesh, evaluating the rendering equation recursively over N bounces. A material-aware cone sampling strategy derives the cone apex angle directly from the predicted roughness through the GGX microfacet CDF, so that Mip-NeRF's pre-integrated Gaussian samples provide pre-filtered radiance for each BRDF lobe integral, reducing the required samples from ~256 rays to 8 diffuse and 4 specular cones.

## Results

On the proposed 10-scene synthetic benchmark, the method achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.00164, roughly 2× lower than NeRO (0.00322) and 9× lower than NeuS (0.01541). For material estimation, the method attains average MSE of 0.003 (roughness), 0.020 (metallic), and 0.036 (albedo), outperforming NeRO's 0.007/0.051/0.039 on all three channels. The method also requires only 8 diffuse and 4 specular cones per shading point versus ~256 diffuse and 128 specular rays for standard GGX importance sampling, yielding a substantial efficiency gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRO (SIGGRAPH 2023), the closest competitor, the proposed method wins on every metric: CD is halved (0.00164 vs 0.00322), roughness MSE is more than halved (0.003 vs 0.007), and metallic MSE is reduced by 61% (0.020 vs 0.051), with albedo MSE also slightly lower (0.036 vs 0.039). The advantage is most pronounced on Nefertiti, where NeRO's metallic MSE is 0.103 versus 0.021 for the proposed method, reflecting NeRO's difficulty with complex local lighting from nearby objects. Against NeuS (CVPR 2021), the gap is even larger—CD is 9.4× lower (0.00164 vs 0.01541)—since NeuS was not designed for glossy-object inverse rendering. Nerfacto (CVPR 2022) performs worst on geometry (CD 0.05523) and is not evaluated on materials, as it does not produce PBR material outputs. The ablation study further confirms that removing the dynamic weighting loss degrades surface smoothness, while removing the environmental field or cone sampling degrades material fidelity, validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NeRF-based inverse rendering by replacing the 2D environmental map (or sphere-defined directional function) used by NeRO, NeILF, and NeRFactor with a 5D Neural Plenoptic Function (NeP) constructed from a pre-trained Mip-NeRF environmental field, enabling explicit modeling of local light interactions from nearby objects. The geometry stage builds on NeuS's SDF formulation but introduces a dynamic weighting loss derived from albedo–final-color discrepancy to handle glossy surfaces, while the material stage leverages Mip-NeRF's Integral Positional Encoding as a natural pre-filterer for BRDF-lobe importance sampling via a roughness-derived cone angle. The method thus sits at the intersection of implicit surface reconstruction, multi-scale radiance field representation, and physically-based rendering, departing from prior work primarily in its lighting representation and sampling strategy rather than in the overall two-stage decomposition framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: glossy objects in scenes with nearby light-emitting or reflecting objects, a setting where 2D environment-map assumptions break down and prior methods produce visibly incorrect geometry and materials. The output (triangle mesh + PBR parameters) is directly compatible with conventional rendering engines, making it relevant for game production and AR/VR pipelines. However, the material stage's dependence on first-stage geometry quality and the residual ambiguity in lighting–albedo decomposition remain open problems, and the evaluation is limited to 20 scenes (10 synthetic, 10 real) with only one strong baseline (NeRO) for material comparison, leaving questions about robustness to more complex multi-bounce scenarios and larger scene scales.
