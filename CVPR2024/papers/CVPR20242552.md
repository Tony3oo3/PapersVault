## Problem

Oriented object detection (OOD) typically requires costly rotated bounding box (RBox) annotations, and existing weakly-supervised alternatives either rely on HBox annotations or use multi-stage pipelines (Point-to-HBox-to-RBox, Point-to-Mask-to-RBox) that are slower and less accurate. End-to-end detection directly from single point annotations for oriented objects remains an unexplored setting in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242552/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images with one point annotation per object (no size or angle information), augmented with synthetic patterns overlaid on the image and a transformed copy (rotated/flipped/scaled) fed through a parameter-shared second branch. At inference, only the original RGB image is processed through a single forward pass of the YOLOF-A1 backbone (single-level feature, fixed anchor size of 64×64 for DOTA or 128×128 for DIOR/HRSC), producing rotated bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, w, h, θ) with class scores. DOTA images are pre-split into 1024×1024 patches with 200-pixel overlap; HRSC images are scaled to 800×800.

## Method

Point2RBox combines two training principles on an FPN-free YOLOF-A1 backbone. First, synthetic pattern knowledge combination: for each labeled point, local color features (face and edge) are sampled and mapped onto basic patterns (rectangles/circles with curve textures, or one manually-cropped sketch per category), which are randomly augmented, recolored, and overlaid on the input image; their known bounding boxes supervise the regression head. Second, transform self-supervision: a randomly transformed copy (rotate 95%, flip 5%, or scale 30%) of the image is fed through a parameter-shared branch, and the two branches' outputs are penalized for not following the same geometric transformation (angle flip/rotation loss or GIoU on scaled circumscribed HBoxes). A classification-score-based label assignment replaces the standard IoU-based FPN/anchor assignment since point annotations carry no size information. The total loss weights classification, center regression (from points), box regression (from synthetic patterns), and transform self-supervision, while inference requires only the single forward pass of the backbone and heads.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, Point2RBox-SK (ResNet50) achieves AP50 of 40.27%, surpassing the best point-supervised alternative P2BNet+H2RBox-v2 (21.87%) by 18.4 points. On DIOR, it reaches 27.34% AP50 vs. 23.61% for P2BNet+H2RBox-v2. On HRSC, Point2RBox-SK attains 79.40% AP50, outperforming P2BNet+H2RBox-v2 (14.60%) by a large margin and KCR (79.10%) by 0.3%. With a stronger CSPNeXt backbone, the best results are 41.05%/27.62%/80.01% on DOTA/DIOR/HRSC, and a two-stage variant further pushes DOTA to 44.90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Point2RBox-SK (40.27% AP50 on DOTA) dramatically outperforms all existing point-supervised alternatives: P2BNet+H2RBox-v2 (21.87%), P2BNet+H2RBox (19.63%), and Point2Mask-RBox (9.72%), with margins of 18.4, 20.6, and 30.6 points respectively. On HRSC, the advantage is even more pronounced—79.40% vs. 14.60% for P2BNet+H2RBox-v2—and Point2RBox also edges out KCR (79.10%), which uses HBox supervision plus cross-dataset knowledge transfer, by 0.3% under the more challenging point-only setting. Against the fully RBox-supervised YOLOF-A1 baseline (49.94% on DOTA, 81.14% on HRSC), Point2RBox-SK trails by 9.67% and 1.74% respectively, indicating that the synthetic pattern knowledge combination recovers most of the information lost by replacing RBox labels with single points. On DIOR, Point2RBox-SK (27.34%) beats P2BNet+H2RBox-v2 (23.61%) by 3.73 points, confirming consistent gains across datasets. The two-stage variant (44.90% on DOTA) narrows the gap to the RBox-supervised YOLOF-A1 to 5.04 points, suggesting that pseudo-label bootstrapping can further close the supervision gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised oriented detection line (H2RBox, H2RBox-v2) one step further in the supervision hierarchy, replacing HBox annotations with single point annotations. Architecturally, it builds on YOLOF's FPN-free single-level design because point annotations cannot be assigned to different FPN layers or anchor scales by size. The transform self-supervision module (rotation, flip, scale consistency) is directly inherited from H2RBox-v2, while the core novelty—the synthetic pattern knowledge combination for regression supervision—repurposes the idea of copy-paste augmentation into a structured, color-sampled pattern generation pipeline that provides known-box supervision without any RBox labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Point annotations are roughly 105% cheaper than RBox annotations, making this setting practically relevant for large-scale aerial/retail datasets where re-annotation is prohibitive. However, the performance gap to full RBox supervision remains substantial (~10% AP50 on DOTA), and the method is architecturally constrained to FPN-free detectors, limiting its applicability. Open questions include whether the synthetic pattern approach generalizes to more complex or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the non-unique boundary problem (e.g., basketball courts) can be addressed without additional supervision.
