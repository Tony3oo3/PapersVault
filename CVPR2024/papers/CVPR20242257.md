## Problem

Existing text-driven video editing methods either preserve the original structure too rigidly (preventing shape changes like car-to-bike) or lose motion fidelity when structural deviation is allowed. Prior motion transfer works are restricted to same or closely related object categories with strong geometric priors (e.g., humans), leaving the general case of transferring motion across drastically different object shapes and fine-grained motion traits (e.g., jumping dog to dolphin) unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a driving video (any resolution, processed through a VAE encoder into latent space) and a free-form text prompt describing the target objects and scene. It produces a new video at the same temporal length as the input, where the overall motion, camera trajectory, and semantic scene layout of the driving video are preserved while the objects and appearance conform to the text prompt. No training data, fine-tuning, or auxiliary inputs (depth maps, masks, pose estimates) are required.

## Method

The method operates entirely within a fixed, pre-trained ZeroScope latent text-to-video diffusion model in a zero-shot guided denoising loop. The key insight is an empirical analysis showing that the Spatial Marginal Mean (SMM) of intermediate space-time features—obtained by averaging over spatial dimensions—acts as a per-frame global descriptor that encodes object pose, position, and scene layout while being robust to pixel-level appearance and shape variations. Rather than matching absolute SMM values (which would lock in the source appearance), the method minimizes a pairwise SMM differences loss that preserves the relative temporal evolution of features across frames, thereby transferring motion structure while freeing the generator to produce the target object's unique shape and fine-grained motion traits. A low-frequency filtered initialization (downsample-then-upsample the DDIM-inverted noise, adding back high-frequency random noise) prevents convergence to undesired local minima in object placement while retaining editability.

## Results

The method is evaluated on 54 video-edit text pairs (21 unique videos) using two metrics: CLIP text-image similarity for edit fidelity and a novel Motion Fidelity Score based on tracklet displacement correlation. The method achieves the best balance between both metrics, outperforming all baselines that exhibit a trade-off (high motion but low edit fidelity, or vice versa). In a user study with 7,000 judgments from 150 participants, the method is preferred over TokenFlow (72.57%), Tune-A-Video (77.80%), and Control-A-Video (84.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines spanning layered video editing, T2I-based video editing, and video-to-video diffusion. TokenFlow [19] and Control-A-Video [13] both preserve original motion well (high Motion Fidelity Score) but fail to deviate structurally from the input, resulting in low CLIP edit fidelity; the proposed method wins the user study against TokenFlow by 72.57% and Control-A-Video by 84.50%. Tune-A-Video [67] satisfies the text prompt but loses motion alignment (objects in wrong pose/position), and the proposed method is preferred 77.80% of the time. Gen-1 [17] could not be quantitatively compared due to differing output frame counts, but qualitatively it struggles to deviate from input depth-conditioned shapes. SA-NLA [30] matches motion fidelity but requires ~10 hours of per-video training and cannot handle large structural deviations (e.g., swan→duck). SDEdit [36] applied to the same T2V model at various noise levels fails to resolve the motion–edit trade-off at any setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior motion transfer methods (which require same-category objects and explicit pose models like OpenPose) and from text-driven video editing methods that manipulate spatial features of T2I diffusion models (TokenFlow, Tune-A-Video, Control-A-Video, Gen-1). The key conceptual leap is repurposing the intermediate space-time features of a pre-trained T2V diffusion model (ZeroScope) as a motion-preserving signal, rather than relying on per-frame spatial features or depth/structure conditioning. The Spatial Marginal Mean (SMM) descriptor and the pairwise-differences loss are novel constructs derived from empirical feature-inversion experiments on the T2V model, extending the feature-manipulation paradigm from the T2I literature (Prompt-to-Prompt, PnP) into the temporal domain for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen T2V model's internal space-time features can be exploited zero-shot to transfer motion across drastically different object categories (car→bike, dog→dolphin), a setting where prior methods fundamentally fail. The SMM-as-per-frame-global-descriptor insight is a useful and generalizable observation about T2V feature geometry. However, the approach is bounded by the quality and coverage of current T2V models: out-of-distribution motion-object combinations degrade gracefully but visibly, and publicly available models remain limited in resolution, length, and training scale. Open questions include whether the SMM/pairwise-differences formulation transfers to other T2V architectures, whether it scales to longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the motion-fidelity metric (trajectory correlation) generalizes beyond the 54-pair evaluation set.
