## Problem

Zero-shot referring expression comprehension requires fine-grained disentanglement of visual scenes and textual contexts, plus understanding of relationships among entities. Existing VLA models like CLIP behave as 'bags-of-words' that fail at compositional reasoning, and prior zero-shot REC methods rely on fragile hand-crafted language parsers that break on long, complex captions with rich interactions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an image with pre-extracted bounding box proposals (from MAttNet on RefCOCO/+/g, or dataset-provided boxes on Who's Waldo) and a free-form text referring expression. The caption is decomposed into (subject, predicate, object) triplets via ChatGPT in-context learning, while image entities are paired via Cartesian product with the union bounding-box area serving as the visual predicate. The output is a single grounded bounding box selected by maximum instance-level structural similarity score; on Who's Waldo the direction reverses to image→text, linking masked [NAME] tokens to person boxes.

## Method

The core pipeline decomposes both the caption (via ChatGPT) and the image (via Cartesian product of box proposals) into (subject, predicate, object) triplets, then computes a triplet-level structural similarity as the sum of three cosine similarities between separately encoded subject, predicate, and object embeddings from a VLA model. Triplet-level matches are propagated to instance level by aggregating similarity scores across all triplets in which a given entity appears as either subject or object, yielding a structure-aware instance similarity matrix. To compensate for VLA models' weak relational understanding, the paper introduces a triplet-level contrastive loss (symmetric InfoNCE over triplet pairs) and fine-tunes CLIP or FLAVA with LoRA on curated relational datasets (HICO-det, SWiG, Visual Genome), producing a VR-VLA model that scores entity relationships more accurately while preserving generic representations.

## Results

On RefCOCO/+/g, the full method (Ours+VR-CLIP) achieves up to 19.53% improvement over the SOTA zero-shot baseline ReCLIP (e.g., 66.52% vs 46.99% on RefCOCO TestA), with an average gain of 9.74% across all splits. Even without fine-tuning the VLA backbone, the triplet-based structural similarity pipeline alone surpasses ReCLIP by up to 3.91%. On the more challenging Who's Waldo dataset (long captions, ~30 words), the zero-shot method reaches 61.3% accuracy, closing the gap to the fully supervised Who's Waldo model (63.5%) to just 2.2%, while ReCLIP drops to 29.4%—barely above random.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReCLIP [54], the primary zero-shot SOTA, the full method (Ours+VR-CLIP) wins on every RefCOCO/+/g split, with the largest margin of 19.53 points on RefCOCO TestA (66.52 vs 46.99) and 14.11 on RefCOCO+ TestA (62.56 vs 48.45). On Who's Waldo, ReCLIP collapses to 29.4% because its dependency parser fails on long, complex captions, while the proposed method reaches 61.3%. Compared to GradCAM [48], the method gains 10.36 points on RefCOCOg Val (59.87 vs 49.51) and 17.48 on RefCOCO TestA (66.52 vs 49.04). Against CPT variants [62], the improvement is even more dramatic (e.g., 59.87 vs 36.70 on RefCOCOg Val for CPT-Seg). On Who's Waldo, the method outperforms all grounding-data-pretrained baselines—SL-CCRF [39] (46.4%), Gupta et al. [13] (38.2%), UNITER [4] (34.2%), and MAttNet [64] (24.1%)—by 14.9 to 37.2 points, and trails only the supervised Who's Waldo model [6] by 2.2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot referring expression comprehension (REC) line initiated by ReCLIP (Subramanian et al., ACL 2022), from which it inherits the test-time augmentation strategy, heuristic spatial filters, and MAttNet box proposals. The key departure is replacing ReCLIP's hand-crafted dependency parser with ChatGPT's in-context learning to decompose captions into (subject, predicate, object) triplets, and introducing a triplet-level structural similarity that is then propagated to instance-level grounding. Additionally, the paper introduces VR-VLA, a parameter-efficient (LoRA) fine-tuning recipe that uses contrastive learning on relational datasets (HICO-det, SWiG, Visual Genome) to address the well-documented 'bag-of-words' limitation of VLA models like CLIP and FLAVA, connecting to the compositional reasoning literature (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that zero-shot grounding can approach supervised performance on complex, long-caption benchmarks (Who's Waldo: 61.3% vs. 63.5% supervised), reducing the need for expensive grounding annotations. The triplet decomposition + structural similarity pipeline is a general recipe applicable to any VLA backbone, and the VR-VLA fine-tuning strategy offers a reusable recipe for improving relational understanding in vision-language models. A notable limitation is that fine-tuning degrades the VLA model's generalist capabilities (CLIP zero-shot classification drops from 0.63 to 0.50), and the authors suggest that large-scale unlabeled image-caption pairs with SAM-generated pseudo-triplets could mitigate this. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even more complex multi-entity scenes and whether the ChatGPT parsing step introduces systematic biases that limit robustness.
