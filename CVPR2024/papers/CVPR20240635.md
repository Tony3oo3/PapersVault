## Problem

Existing garment manipulation methods design task-specific policies that cannot generalize across diverse garment geometries within a category, and typically require large-scale human-annotated data. The paper addresses how to learn a unified, category-level representation that captures the shared topological structure of garments (e.g., a body with two sleeves and a collar) despite varying shapes, sizes, and deformations, enabling one or few-shot generalization to novel garments across multiple manipulation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240635/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an N-point (N=10,000) 3D partial point cloud observation of a garment as input, processed by a PointNet++ backbone to produce per-point 512-dimensional unit-norm feature vectors. Given a demonstration garment observation and its action sequence (ordered pick and place point pairs, single- or dual-arm), the system computes dense per-point correspondence scores (normalized to [-1,1]) between the novel garment and the demonstration, then selects the highest-scoring points as manipulation targets. The final output is a sequence of pick-place actions executed by the robot arm(s).

## Method

The framework learns dense visual correspondence in two self-supervised stages: (1) cross-deformation correspondence, where the same garment in different deformation states (generated via self-play in simulation) is used to enforce invariance to self-deformations, and (2) cross-object correspondence, where skeleton points (generated by Skeleton Merger on flat garments and projected to arbitrary deformations via point tracing) serve as ordered structural anchors to align different garments in the same category. Both stages use InfoNCE contrastive loss with 150 negative samples per positive pair. A coarse-to-fine refinement step then identifies online prediction failures (points with high correspondence scores but large canonical-state distance from the true match) and applies a distance-weighted InfoNCE loss to sharpen boundaries. Finally, few-shot adaptation (≤5 annotated functional points per task) fine-tunes the topological representation into a task-specific functional one, enabling accurate pick-point selection for unfolding, folding, and hanging with minimal human annotation.

## Results

Across three garment manipulation tasks (unfolding, folding, hanging) on three categories (tops, dresses, trousers), the proposed method achieves 83.6/86.9/81.6% success on Unfold-RAND, 83.5/84.0/83.3% on Fold-FLAT, and 81.9/77.4/83.3% on Hang-RAND. The most dramatic gains appear in the more realistic deformed-state settings: on Fold-FLING the method reaches 77.9/82.5/81.3% versus 61.7/63.5/60.3% for ClothFunnels, and on Hang-FLING it reaches 83.8/89.6/81.5% versus 81.2/80.9/78.7% for GCSR. In real-world folding with two Franka Panda arms, the method achieves 11/15 successes, outperforming ClothFunnel (8/15) and UniFolding (10/15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On folding, the proposed method dramatically outperforms ClothFunnels in the realistic Fold-FLING setting (avg ~80.6% vs ~61.8%), because ClothFunnels' keypoint detector is trained only on fully flat garments and fails when garments retain residual deformations; on Fold-FLAT the two are comparable (~83.6% vs ~81.9% average). Against UniFolding, the method is competitive on Fold-FLAT (83.5/84.0/83.3 vs 83.5/82.9/81.6) and slightly ahead on Fold-FLING for dresses and trousers (82.5/81.3 vs 81.5/78.6) while marginally behind on tops (77.9 vs 78.7). On unfolding, the method beats FlingBot by ~3–4 points on both Unfold-RAND and Unfold-DROP, and outperforms DefoAfford by 10–20 points, since DefoAfford uses only one gripper and cannot execute the two-point fling action effectively. On hanging, the method surpasses GCSR by 3–9 points on Hang-FLING (where occluded collars hurt GCSR) and by 3–8 points on Hang-RAND, while also beating DefoAfford by 4–13 points across both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense visual correspondence line of work (Ganapathi et al., ICRA 2021) from square-shaped fabrics and near-identical T-shirts to diverse garments across a category, and repurposes Skeleton Merger (Shi et al., CVPR 2021) as a structural bridge to build cross-object correspondence without per-pair supervision. It departs from prior garment manipulation methods (ClothFunnels, FlingBot, UniFolding, GCSR) that each design task-specific policies on limited garment shapes, by proposing a single topological representation that is adapted to task-specific functional correspondence via few-shot fine-tuning. The decoupling of cross-deformation and cross-object correspondence learning, combined with coarse-to-fine refinement, is a novel architectural choice not present in either parent line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified correspondence framework reduces the annotation burden for garment manipulation from large-scale task-specific datasets to one or two demonstrations per task, which is practically significant for deploying robots in home-assistant settings where garment diversity is unbounded. The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer (11/15 real-world folds) validates the approach beyond simulation, though the gap from simulation success rates (~83%) to real-world (73%) remains a notable limitation. Open questions include whether the skeleton-based structural bridge generalizes to categories with more topological variation (e.g., garments with pockets, zippers, or asymmetric designs), whether the approach scales to long-horizon multi-step manipulation beyond the 3-step fling or sequential fold, and whether the few-shot adaptation can be made fully automatic without any human-annotated functional points.
