## Problem

Existing diffusion-based test-time adaptation methods, such as DDA and Diffpure, rely on structural guidance (e.g., low-pass filtering) that only handles high-frequency pixel-level corruptions and fails to generalize to broader OOD types like style shifts (sketch, painting, sculpture). Moreover, the diffusion model trained solely on source-domain data carries learning biases that prevent it from restoring diverse distribution shifts. The paper asks how to design a more general structural guidance that accounts for both the sample's semantic content and the downstream classifier's output behavior.

## Input / Output

At test time, GDA takes a 256×256 OOD image, a short text prompt describing the source-domain style (e.g., 'photo-realistic'), a frozen pretrained classifier (ResNet-50, ConvNeXt-T, Swin-T, or CLIP-B/16), and a pretrained unconditional DDPM. It produces an adapted 256×256 sample shifted back toward the source domain and a class prediction from the classifier on that sample. No model weights are modified; the entire adaptation occurs in 10 DDIM reverse steps per sample, with 16 AugMix augmentations applied at each step for the marginal entropy computation.

## Method

GDA introduces a three-component structural guidance applied iteratively during the DDIM reverse sampling process. At each reverse step t, the predicted denoised image x_{0,t}^g is used to compute (1) a marginal entropy loss over 16 AugMix-augmented versions passed through the frozen classifier, which pushes the sample away from decision boundaries; (2) a CLIP-based style loss measuring cosine similarity between the image embedding and a source-domain text prompt; and (3) a patch-wise contrastive loss on spatial features extracted from the UNet backbone to preserve content. The gradient of the combined loss is backpropagated to update the next-step sample x_{t-1}^g. A final confidence filter retains the adapted sample only if its classifier entropy is lower than the original's, preventing degradation on already-clean inputs.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 3), GDA achieves 41.70% (ResNet50), 65.24% (ConvNext-T), and 59.35% (Swin-T) top-1 accuracy, improving over standard models by 4.4–5.64%. On the more challenging style-shift benchmarks, GDA gains 2.5–7.4% over standard models on Rendition, 2.5–6.9% on Sketch, and 6.4% on average on Stylized-ImageNet. GDA also outperforms the strongest diffusion-based baseline DDA by 2.7–5% on Stylized and by 0.6–5.5% on Rendition, while DDA and Diffpure show no improvement (or even degradation) on Sketch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDA is compared against two diffusion-based TTA baselines: DDA [5] (which uses a low-pass filter as structural guidance) and Diffpure [30] (which solves the SDE for adversarial purification). GDA consistently outperforms both across all four OOD benchmarks and all four classifier architectures. On Rendition, GDA beats DDA₅₀ by 2.5% (ResNet50: 44.5 vs 42.0) and Diffpure by 14.7% (44.5 vs 29.8). On Sketch, the gap is most dramatic: GDA achieves 25.5% (ResNet50) while DDA₅₀ barely exceeds the standard model (23.5 vs 23.0) and Diffpure actually degrades performance (13.9%). On Stylized-ImageNet, GDA leads DDA by 3.8% (ResNet50: 23.0 vs 19.2) and Diffpure by 16.9% (23.0 vs 6.1). The ablation 'w/o marginal' (removing the marginal entropy loss) shows that the entropy component contributes 1.5–5.1% additional accuracy over style+content guidance alone, confirming that model-output consistency is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

GDA extends the diffusion-based test-time adaptation line initiated by DDA (Gao et al., 2022), which introduced structural guidance (low-pass filtering) into the DDPM reverse process but was limited to high-frequency noise corruptions. The key departure is the addition of a marginal entropy loss that conditions the diffusion on the downstream classifier's output consistency, complementing CLIP-based style transfer and patch-wise contrastive content preservation losses borrowed from Yang et al. (2023). Unlike DDA's purely signal-processing guidance, GDA's guidance is target-domain-agnostic and explicitly accounts for the classifier's decision behavior, enabling generalization to style-shifted OOD data (sketch, painting, sculpture) where DDA and Diffpure actually degrade performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDA demonstrates that diffusion-based TTA can be made broadly applicable across corruption types and style shifts, not just noise, by incorporating model-output-aware guidance—a simple but effective principle that could transfer to other generative adaptation settings. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ImageNet-based classification benchmarks, dependence on a pretrained unconditional diffusion model, and the need for per-sample reverse sampling (albeit faster than DDA). Open questions raised by the authors include extending GDA to detection tasks, incorporating text-prompt guidance, and exploring alternative guidance mechanisms for further efficiency gains.
