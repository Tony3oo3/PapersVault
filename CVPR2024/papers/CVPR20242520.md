## Problem

Existing rolling shutter correction methods estimate an undistortion flow (RS→GS) by first predicting inter-frame optical flow between RS frames and then linearly rescaling it under a constant-velocity assumption. This two-stage pipeline is inaccurate under complex non-linear and large motions, and the subsequent forward warping operation introduces unavoidable artifacts such as black holes. The paper asks whether directly estimating the distortion flow (GS→RS) in a single stage can overcome both limitations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts N consecutive RS frames (N=2 or N=3) in RGB format as input and outputs a single corrected GS image corresponding to the timestamp of the middle scanline of the middle input frame. During training, input frames are randomly cropped to a width of 256 pixels (height preserved) with random horizontal flip; at test time no augmentation is applied. Experiments are conducted at 640×480 resolution, and the model is trained for 150k iterations with an Adam optimizer at an initial learning rate of 4×10⁻⁴.

## Method

The key insight is to directly predict the distortion flow from the latent GS image to the RS frames, enabling backward warping (pixel sampling with interpolation) instead of the artifact-prone forward warping used by prior methods. A global correlation-based flow attention mechanism computes an attention map between a predicted GS feature (query) and RS frame features (key) at the lowest resolution, simultaneously yielding warped RS features and an initial distortion flow by aggregating the RS features and a 2D coordinate grid as values. A coarse-to-fine decoder then progressively refines both the flow and GS features across L=4 scales by fusing backward-warped RS features at each level. At the finest resolution, a multi-distortion flow decoding strategy predicts G groups of displacement fields to handle occlusions, and the final GS image is synthesized from the refined GS features and multiple warped RS feature groups. Training combines Charbonnier loss, VGG-19 perceptual loss, and an indirect flow supervision loss that enforces consistency between backward-warped RS images and the ground-truth GS image across all scales.

## Results

On the synthetic Fastec-RS dataset, the 3-frame model achieves 30.00 dB PSNR, 0.882 SSIM, and 0.0665 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline QRSC(5F) (29.49 dB, 0.872, 0.0814) by 0.51 dB in PSNR while using only 3 input frames versus 5. On Carla-RS, it reaches 32.10 dB PSNR and 0.0218 LPIPS, again surpassing QRSC(5F) (32.01 dB, 0.0253). On the real-world BS-RSC dataset, the method achieves 34.48 dB PSNR and 0.954 SSIM, exceeding QRSC(5F) by approximately 1 dB in PSNR. The 2-frame variant also outperforms the most efficient prior method JAMNet (28.88 vs. 28.70 dB on Fastec-RS) with 40% fewer parameters (2.87M vs. 4.73M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 8 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitor QRSC [27], the 3-frame model surpasses QRSC(5F) by 0.51 dB PSNR on Fastec-RS (30.00 vs. 29.49) and 0.09 dB on Carla-RS (32.10 vs. 32.01), while using only 3 input frames versus 5 and running 34× faster (34 ms vs. 1149 ms). On the real-world BS-RSC dataset, the gap widens to ~1 dB PSNR (34.48 vs. 33.50), confirming the advantage of direct distortion flow estimation under complex non-linear motion. The 2-frame variant also beats JAMNet [10] (the most efficient prior 2-frame method) by 0.18 dB PSNR on Fastec-RS while using 40% fewer parameters (2.87M vs. 4.73M) and slightly lower runtime (26 ms vs. 28 ms). Against older methods like DSUN [24], SUNet [7], and AdaRSC [2], the improvements are substantial (3–4 dB PSNR on Fastec-RS), reflecting the limitations of linear motion scaling and forward warping. The method also leads on the ACC subset (34.35 dB vs. 33.36 dB for QRSC(5F)), which specifically isolates non-constant-motion frames, further validating the non-linear motion modeling capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in deep rolling shutter correction, where methods like DSUN, SUNet, JAMNet, and QRSC estimate inter-frame optical flow between RS frames and then linearly or quadratically rescale it to obtain an undistortion flow (RS→GS) for forward warping. Instead, it directly estimates a distortion flow (GS→RS) and applies backward warping, drawing on the multi-distortion flow prediction strategy from AdaRSC [2] and the global correlation matching idea from GMFlow [38] for its flow attention mechanism. The coarse-to-fine joint appearance-motion decoder is inspired by JAMNet [10], but the key architectural shift is predicting the correction field in the opposite direction and at a single stage rather than a two-stage flow-then-scale pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The direct distortion flow formulation sidesteps the fundamental limitation of linear/quadratic motion models for non-linear real-world motion, and backward warping eliminates the black-hole artifacts inherent to forward warping. The 30× speedup over QRSC while achieving ~1 dB higher PSNR makes this a practically attractive baseline for RSC. However, the authors acknowledge that current datasets (synthetic or limited real-world) constrain generalization, and integrating explicit camera exposure parameters remains an open direction for truly robust real-world deployment.
