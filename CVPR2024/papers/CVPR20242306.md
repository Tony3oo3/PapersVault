## Problem

Training a versatile medical image segmentation model across diverse imaging equipment and protocols typically demands a large, fully annotated dataset, which is prohibitively expensive to curate. Existing methods for partially labeled data cannot handle sparsely labeled data (where only a fraction of slices carry annotations), and none address the combined challenges of label ambiguity, modality/dataset/class imbalance, and the absence of background annotations in multi-source settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242306/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D CT or MRI volumes resampled to a uniform 2×2×2 mm³ spacing with intensities normalized to [0,1], where each volume may carry annotations for only a subset of 16 abdominal structures and/or only a fraction of its slices (partial or sparse labeling). A single forward pass through the network produces a per-voxel probability map over all 16 structures simultaneously, eliminating the need for multiple conditional forward passes required by dynamic baselines like DoDNet or CLIP-driven.

## Method

The framework uses a 3D TransUNet backbone with a segmentation head, trained under a weakly-supervised objective that combines three strategies. First, model self-disambiguation is achieved through ambiguity-aware focal cross-entropy and dice losses computed slice-wise: for unannotated voxels, all unannotated structure predictions are aggregated into a pseudo-background class so the model learns to disambiguate without explicit supervision. Second, a class-agnostic Shannon entropy minimization regularizer encourages confident predictions on challenging unannotated voxels, accelerating convergence. Third, a hierarchical sampling strategy (class → modality → dataset) ensures balanced representation across anatomical structures, imaging modalities, and source datasets during training. The overall loss weights the entropy term at λ=3 for entirely unannotated slices and λ=1 otherwise.

## Results

On a multi-source abdominal segmentation benchmark of 2,960 scans from 8 datasets (CT and MRI), the proposed method with 3D TransUNet as the base network achieves 88.7±7.0% DSC, outperforming DoDNet (83.5±15.9%) and CLIP-driven (83.3±16.3%) by 5.2 and 5.4 percentage points respectively. The method also shows substantially lower variance (±7.0 vs ±15.9/±16.3), indicating more consistent performance across subjects. With alternative backbones, MedNeXt reaches 88.4±7.3% and UNet++ reaches 87.4±8.5%, confirming the approach's robustness to architecture choice. Per-structure evaluation (Table 4) shows the proposed method averaging 85.7% DSC across 16 abdominal organs, versus 80.0% for DoDNet and 80.7% for CLIP-driven.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art dynamic segmentation methods: DoDNet (CVPR 2021) and CLIP-driven (ICCV 2023), both adapted to use 3D TransUNet as the shared base network and the same hierarchical sampling strategy for fair comparison. The proposed method decisively outperforms both baselines: 88.7% vs 83.5% DSC over DoDNet (+5.2 pp) and 88.7% vs 83.3% over CLIP-driven (+5.4 pp), with roughly half the standard deviation, indicating far more consistent per-subject performance. Beyond accuracy, the proposed method requires only a single forward pass to predict all 16 anatomical structures, whereas DoDNet and CLIP-driven each require 16 separate forward passes (one per structure), making inference 16× faster. Additionally, DoDNet and CLIP-driven cannot utilize images that lack annotations for any target structure (e.g., TotalSegmentator volumes with no relevant organs in the ROI), whereas the proposed method can leverage all 2,960 images. On a per-structure basis (Table 4), the gap is consistent across all 16 organs, with the largest margins on small structures like the adrenal glands (RAG: 75.4% vs 69.6%/70.7%) and the prostate/uterus (79.2% vs 59.2%/68.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the background-modeling line of work (Fang & Yan 2020, Shi et al. 2021) that computes adaptive losses for unannotated voxels in partially labeled data, but generalizes it to handle sparsely labeled data where only a fraction of slices carry any annotation — a setting the authors explicitly note no prior method addresses. It departs from dynamic models (DoDNet, CLIP-driven) that require multiple forward passes and cannot exploit fine-grained multi-structure annotations simultaneously, and from semi-supervised co-training approaches that demand fully annotated pretraining data. The core conceptual contribution is the 'self-disambiguation' framing: rather than treating unannotated regions as background or ignoring them, the ambiguity-aware focal CE and dice losses let the model infer correct labels for all voxels in a single forward pass, while hierarchical sampling and Shannon entropy regularization address multi-source imbalance and prediction uncertainty respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for clinical deployment: by accepting sparsely labeled data (as few as 20% of slices) and multi-source partial annotations, the method dramatically reduces the annotation burden for building versatile segmentation models, and the single-forward-pass inference is far more efficient than the 16 passes required by dynamic baselines. An open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond the 16 abdominal structures and CT/MRI modalities studied here to whole-body or multi-organ tasks with greater class imbalance. The paper does not explore how performance degrades as the fraction of annotated slices drops below 20%, nor does it test on truly unseen modalities (e.g., ultrasound), leaving the boundary of the method's robustness unclear.
