## Problem

Previous embodied navigation methods are task-specific specialists that struggle to generalize to unseen scenarios or transfer across different navigation tasks. This paper asks whether a single generalist model can be trained to handle a wide spectrum of embodied navigation tasks—ranging from vision-language navigation and object localization to 3D question answering and embodied question answering—while also generalizing to tasks not seen during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240288/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images at the agent's current location (encoded by a frozen EVA-CLIP ViT into per-view features, then fused by a 2-layer transformer encoder into scene representations), a text instruction or dialog, and a sequence of past scene representations as navigation history. All outputs are generated as token sequences by the LLM: a numeric viewpoint ID for navigation steps, a numeric object ID for localization, or free-form text for question answers and trajectory summaries. The schema-based instruction format prepends learnable ID embeddings to each view or step representation, making the input structure uniform across all tasks.

## Method

NaviLLM couples a scene encoder (frozen EVA-CLIP-02-Large ViT + 2-layer transformer encoder for multi-view fusion) with a Vicuna-7B LLM, and unifies all embodied tasks through a schema-based instruction format comprising four components: Task (the instruction or question), Observation (scene or object representations with view IDs), History (past observations with step IDs), and Output Hint (a prompt specifying the expected output type). This design casts every task—VLN, object localization, 3D-QA, trajectory summarization, and EQA—into a generation problem optimized with a single cross-entropy objective. Training follows a two-stage protocol: pre-training with teacher forcing on a combined dataset (CVDN, SOON, R2R, REVERIE, ScanQA, and augmented data), followed by multi-task fine-tuning that alternates between teacher forcing and student forcing while incorporating LLaVA-23k for general visual-language grounding. The LLM's pretrained linguistic knowledge is critical, as ablation shows a significant performance drop when the LLM is randomly initialized, and the unified multi-task training improves performance on all individual tasks compared to single-task training.

## Results

NaviLLM achieves state-of-the-art performance on CVDN (7.90 GP on test, a 29% relative improvement over VLN-PETL's 6.13), SOON (26.26 SPL on test, surpassing Meta-Explore's 25.80), and ScanQA (26.3 EM on test, a 7.2% absolute gain over 3D-LLM's 19.1). On R2R, NaviLLM matches the best baselines (60 SPL on test, tied with AZHP and BEV-BERT), while on REVERIE it trails DUET (32.33 vs. 36.06 SPL). In held-out generalization experiments, NaviLLM improves SOON SPL by 136% and CVDN GP by 32% over the best DUET variant, and achieves 47.78% SR / 35.60% SPL / 44.5% ACC on zero-shot MP3D-EQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaviLLM decisively outperforms all task-specific baselines on CVDN, achieving 7.90 GP on test versus 6.13 for VLN-PETL (the previous best) and 5.83 for VLN-SIG, a 29% relative gain attributed to LLM dialog comprehension and multi-task data sharing. On SOON, NaviLLM (26.26 SPL test) surpasses Meta-Explore (25.80) and DUET (21.42), and on ScanQA it leads 3D-LLM by 7.2 EM points (26.3 vs. 19.1) and 11.2 CIDER points (80.8 vs. 69.6). On R2R, NaviLLM ties the best baselines (AZHP, BEV-BERT) at 60 SPL on test, while on REVERIE it trails DUET (36.06) and AZHP (35.85) by roughly 3–4 SPL points, likely because REVERIE instructions are simpler and more similar to R2R. In held-out generalization tests, NaviLLM dramatically outperforms DUET variants: 14.29 vs. 2.84 SPL on SOON (+136%) and 4.46 vs. 3.38 GP on CVDN (+32%). On zero-shot MP3D-EQA, NaviLLM achieves 35.60 SPL (vs. DUET-R2R's 30.51) and 44.5% QA accuracy, while DUET models cannot perform question answering at all; with ground-truth trajectories, NaviLLM (47.4% ACC) even surpasses the fully-supervised EQA model (46.0% ACC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the schema-based instruction paradigm from multi-turn dialog systems (Chen et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2021) to the multimodal embodied navigation setting, using it to unify heterogeneous tasks (VLN, object localization, 3D-QA, trajectory summarization) into a single generative formulation. It builds on the LLM-for-vision-language line (LLaVA, InstructBLIP) and 3D-LLM, but departs from both by addressing sequential decision-making in 3D environments rather than single-shot understanding. Unlike pipeline approaches that chain independent foundation models (e.g., NavGPT), NaviLLM is an end-to-end unified learner, and unlike prior multi-task VLN models (MT-RCM), it leverages pretrained LLM weights to inherit broad linguistic and reasoning capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single LLM-based model can simultaneously achieve SOTA across multiple embodied navigation benchmarks and generalize zero-shot to unseen tasks (EQA, 3D captioning), suggesting a viable path toward generalist embodied agents. The schema-based instruction design is a clean, extensible mechanism that could be adopted by future work to incorporate new task types without architectural changes. Limitations include a persistent gap on REVERIE compared to task-specific DUET, modest object localization performance relative to methods that fuse object and image features, and reliance on a 7B model that may not scale to more complex or real-time deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether this unification approach transfers to outdoor or multi-agent settings and how to handle the compute cost of multi-view scene encoding at inference time.
