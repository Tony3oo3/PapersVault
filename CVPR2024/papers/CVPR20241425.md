## Problem

Instructional how-to videos are disconnected: each shows a single execution path with fixed ingredients, tools, and techniques, so users whose constraints differ (no grill, dietary restriction, different skill level) must manually search for an alternative video. Existing text-to-video retrieval, temporal localization, and video QA methods fail because they ignore the source video's viewing history, which is essential to interpret context-poor queries like 'can I do this without an electric grill?' and identify the correct alternative step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241425/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video watched up to timestamp t_s (encoded at one spatio-temporal feature per second via InternVideo), a short natural language query that often omits explicit recipe or step context, and a repository of ~3,873 candidate instructional videos. It outputs two things: the identity of the correct detour video from the repository, and a temporal window (start and end times) within that video showing the alternative execution of the relevant step.

## Method

VidDetours encodes both the source video (up to t_s) and candidate detour videos as visual tokens via InternVideo features mapped through a trainable linear layer, concatenates them with the query's text tokens into a single sequence, and passes this through a trainable LLAMA2-13B as a multimodal sequence encoder that captures cross-modal interactions between viewing history, query, and candidate video. Two task-specific heads are trained on top: a transformer classifier for detour video retrieval (binary cross-entropy with hard negatives from the same task) and a VSLNet-based head for temporal window localization (cross-entropy on start/end time distributions). Training data is weakly supervised: LLAMA2 summarizes HowTo100M narrations into timestamped step descriptions, similar video pairs are identified via MPNet cosine similarity (>0.75), and the LLM is prompted to generate detour queries and target timestamps, yielding ~587K training tuples. The key architectural insight is early fusion of source video context and query within the LLM's token sequence, which outperforms late fusion of separate video and text embeddings by a large margin.

## Results

On the detour video retrieval task (3,873 candidate videos), VidDetours achieves R@5=17.6, R@10=27.8, R@50=62.4, and median rank of 30, outperforming the best baseline InternVideo (R@5=9.7, R@50=27.2, MedR=313) by 7.9 points at R@5 and 35.2 points at R@50. On detour window localization, the method achieves mean R@1=12.8 (IoU=0.3: 16.7, IoU=0.5: 7.7, IoU=0.7: 2.8), surpassing the second-best method STALE (mean R@1=9.6) by 3.2 points. The evaluation is conducted on a manually annotated test set of 16,207 detour instances across 3,873 videos and 1,080 recipes from HowTo100M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidDetours substantially outperforms all 10 baselines on both subtasks. For retrieval, the gap over the strongest baseline InternVideo [72] is 7.9 points at R@5 and 35.2 points at R@50, with median rank improving from 313 to 30. CLIP [63] and CLIP-Hitchhiker [7] perform similarly (R@5 ≈ 8), confirming that simple late-fusion of video and text embeddings is insufficient. CoVR [69], a composed video retrieval method, performs worst among video-based baselines (R@5=4.3, MedR=473), likely because it samples only a few frames and cannot leverage full viewing history. For localization, VidDetours (mean R@1=12.8) beats STALE [58] (9.6), UMT [47] (9.4), and VSLNet [84] (9.4) by 3+ points, with the advantage growing at stricter IoU thresholds (IoU=0.7: 2.8 vs. 1.7 for STALE). The paper attributes gains to early multimodal fusion of source video context with the query via an LLM, rather than the late-fusion approach used by all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from standard text-to-video retrieval (CLIP, InternVideo, VideoCLIP) and temporal grounding (VSLNet, 2D-TAN, UMT) by introducing a task that conditions on the *viewing history* of a source video alongside a natural language query, rather than treating the query in isolation. Architecturally, it repurposes VSLNet for the localization head and borrows the composed-retrieval idea from CoVR (reference + modification), but elevates both to full instructional-video scale using an LLM (LLAMA2) as a multimodal sequence encoder that fuses source video tokens, query tokens, and candidate video tokens in a single forward pass. The data pipeline is novel in using LLMs to generate weakly-supervised detour annotations from HowTo100M narrations, a strategy not previously applied to cross-video navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first formal benchmark for cross-video instructional navigation (16K human-annotated detour instances over 4K videos), opening a research direction toward interconnected how-to knowledge bases rather than isolated lessons. A key open question is generalization beyond the cooking domain, where recipes have well-structured, highly overlapping steps; domains like repair, sports, or music may have less predictable detour structure. The reliance on LLM-generated training labels (85% satisfactory) and the single-domain evaluation limit confidence in broader applicability, and multi-turn or sequential detour navigation remains unexplored.
