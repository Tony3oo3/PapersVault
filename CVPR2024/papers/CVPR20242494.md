## Problem

Real driving-video dehazing is hindered by the near-impossibility of acquiring precisely aligned hazy/clear video pairs in dynamic driving scenarios with unpredictable weather, inconsistent speeds, and moving objects. Existing video dehazing methods either rely on strictly aligned ground truth (unavailable in the wild) or are trained on indoor smoke datasets that do not generalize to outdoor driving haze. This paper introduces a non-aligned regularization paradigm that trains a video dehazing network using clear reference frames that are only approximately matched in time and space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242494/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous hazy driving video sequences (captured at 1920×1080, 30 FPS) and produces temporally consistent dehazed video frames. Before entering the video dehazing network, each hazy frame is pre-processed by a frozen image dehazing model (NSDNet) to partially remove haze; training crops are 256×256. The supervision signal is not a pixel-aligned ground truth but rather a pair of non-aligned clear reference frames (one at the matched index, one at the next index) selected from a separate clear video of the same scene, which may differ in driving speed and lateral position.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, a Non-aligned Reference Frame Matching (NRFM) module iteratively slides a window over the clear video, using VGG-16 feature cosine similarity to select the two best-matching clear frames for each hazy frame, thereby resolving temporal misalignment. Second, the video dehazing network aligns and fuses multi-frame features via a Flow-guided Cosine Attention Sampler (FCAS), which uses coarse SpyNet optical flow to guide a learnable multi-scale cosine attention sampling grid (default 7×7) for offset estimation, and a Deformable Cosine Attention Fusion (DCAF) module that applies deformable convolution to expand the receptive field before computing cosine-similarity-based feature aggregation. Training combines adversarial loss, a multi-frame contextual reference loss against the two matched clear frames, an L1 alignment loss, and a flow-warped temporal consistency regularization, all without requiring pixel-aligned ground truth.

## Results

On the GoProHazy dataset, DVD achieves the best FADE of 0.7598 and NIQE of 3.7753, outperforming the strongest image baseline RIDCP (FADE 0.8010, NIQE 4.6640) and the best video baseline PM-Net (FADE 1.1011, NIQE 4.1211). On the higher-speed DrivingHazy dataset, DVD reaches FADE 0.8207 and NIQE 3.5825, improving FADE by 0.1227 and NIQE by 0.3119 over PM-Net. On the out-of-distribution InternetHazy dataset (no retraining), DVD attains FADE 0.8745 and NIQE 3.7480, surpassing all baselines including RIDCP (FADE 0.9391, NIQE 4.6610) and PM-Net (FADE 1.1517, NIQE 4.0590). In the user study, DVD receives the highest preference votes (0.440 on DrivingHazy, 0.555 on InternetHazy) among the four methods evaluated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVD outperforms all nine baselines across all three datasets on both FADE and NIQE. Against the strongest image dehazing baseline RIDCP (CVPR'23), DVD improves FADE by 0.0412 on GoProHazy (0.7598 vs. 0.8010) and by 0.0646 on InternetHazy (0.8745 vs. 0.9391), while also reducing NIQE by 0.8887 and 0.9130 respectively. Against the best video dehazing baseline PM-Net (ACM MM'22), DVD achieves a FADE gain of 0.3413 on GoProHazy (0.7598 vs. 1.1011) and 0.1227 on DrivingHazy (0.8207 vs. 0.9434), with corresponding NIQE improvements of 0.3458 and 0.3119. The non-aligned image method NSDNet (the pre-dehazing component) is the closest competitor on GoProHazy (FADE 0.7996), but DVD still leads by 0.0398 FADE and 0.3794 NIQE, demonstrating the benefit of the video-level temporal fusion. On the out-of-distribution InternetHazy set, DVD's advantage over RIDCP widens (FADE 0.8745 vs. 0.9391), and it receives 0.555 user-study votes versus RIDCP's 0.265, confirming superior perceptual quality without any retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work on non-aligned supervision for image dehazing (NSDNet) to the video domain, specifically targeting real driving scenarios where perfectly aligned hazy/clear ground truth is infeasible to collect. It departs from prior video dehazing methods (PM-Net, MAP-Net, CG-IDN on REVIDE) that require pixel-aligned GT pairs, and from standard video alignment techniques that rely on precise optical flow warping or deformable convolution, by introducing cosine-attention-based alignment (FCAS, DCAF) that tolerates coarse flow and large motion. The non-aligned reference frame matching (NRFM) module draws on contextual loss ideas for non-aligned data but adapts them to a temporal sliding-window search over clear video frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical barrier of collecting aligned hazy/clear driving video pairs is a real bottleneck for deploying dehazing in autonomous driving safety systems, and this paper demonstrates that non-aligned supervision can close much of the gap to fully supervised methods. However, the dataset is small (27 video pairs, ~4,256 frames), the method still requires a clear video of the same scene (just not temporally/spatially aligned), and generalization to other adverse weather (rain, fog of different density) remains untested. Open questions include whether the non-aligned paradigm scales to longer videos, more diverse driving conditions, and whether the cosine-attention alignment modules transfer to other video restoration tasks beyond dehazing.
