## Problem

Radar heatmaps for autonomous driving are extremely difficult to annotate because they appear as uninterpretable blobs with no sharp object boundaries, and mmWave specularity means most of a car's surface is invisible. Only ~10% of available radar frames are labeled, and prior cross-modal supervision (using camera or lidar labels) introduces errors from viewpoint misalignment and modality-specific visibility mismatches. This paper tackles the annotation bottleneck by learning radar representations self-supervisedly from large-scale unlabeled radar-vision pairs.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes paired BEV radar heatmaps (L range bins × A azimuth bins, derived from a 3D complex tensor of M×N MIMO antenna-pair signals) alongside corresponding RGB camera images. During inference, only the radar heatmap is required, and the network outputs 2D rotated bounding boxes for cars in the BEV plane. The radar backbone operates on both high- and low-resolution heatmaps via a two-stream architecture, while the vision branch uses a frozen CLIP encoder on the paired camera image.

## Method

Radical pre-trains a Radatron FPN-based radar backbone using a composite contrastive loss with two terms: an intra-modal radar-to-radar term that enforces invariance to radar-specific augmentations, and a cross-modal radar-to-vision term that distills semantic priors from a frozen CLIP encoder. The key novel augmentation is RMM (Radar MIMO Mask), which operates on the raw 3D complex antenna-pair tensor before non-coherent integration, combining random antenna dropout (Bernoulli masking of virtual elements) and random phase noise (uniform phase shifts mimicking Doppler-induced distortions) to generate positive views that preserve geometric structure while simulating real-world RF variability. Repurposed vision augmentations (horizontal flip, polar-coordinate rotation, polar-coordinate center cropping) complement RMM. After pre-training on 32K unlabeled frames, the radar backbone is fine-tuned with a task-specific detection head on 13K labeled frames, yielding a 5.8% mAP improvement over the supervised Radatron baseline.

## Results

On the Radatron dataset, Radical achieves 62.3% mAP for 2D rotated bounding box car detection from radar heatmaps, outperforming the supervised Radatron baseline (56.5% mAP) by 5.8%. The composite intra- and cross-modal contrastive loss also improves AP75 by 5.2 points (69.7 vs. 64.5) and AP50 by 0.7 points (89.6 vs. 88.9) over the supervised baseline. Under reduced label budgets, the advantage grows: Radical gains +7.4% mAP at 10% labeled data and +11.5% at 1% labeled data compared to Radatron.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Radical against three baselines: the supervised Radatron detector (random initialization), intra-modal-only contrastive learning (radar-to-radar), and cross-modal-only contrastive learning (radar-to-vision). Radical wins on all three metrics (mAP, AP75, AP50) against every baseline. The largest gap is in mAP: Radical's 62.3% exceeds Radatron's 56.5% by 5.8 points, intra-modal CL's 59.4% by 2.9 points, and cross-modal CL's 59.7% by 2.4 points. The AP75 metric shows the most discriminative separation (69.7 vs. 64.5/66.8/67.1), indicating that Radical's advantage is primarily in precise box localization rather than coarse detection. The composite loss clearly outperforms either modality alone, confirming that both radar-specific structure (sparsity, specularity) and visual priors (object semantics) are necessary for optimal radar embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the contrastive learning frameworks of SimCLR and MoCo to the radar domain, extending them with a composite loss that combines intra-modal (radar-to-radar) and cross-modal (radar-to-vision) terms, drawing the prototype-vector technique from cross-modal point cloud SSL. It departs from prior cross-modal radar supervision work (which relies on explicit camera or lidar labels) by operating at the mutual-information level without any annotated radar data. The novel RMM augmentation is specifically designed for cascaded MIMO radar hardware, filling a gap left by prior radio-SSL work that lacked mature augmentation recipes for automotive radar formats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that self-supervised pre-training can improve radar-only 2D bounding box detection in autonomous driving, directly addressing the annotation bottleneck that limits radar perception research. The RMM augmentation (antenna dropout + random phase noise) offers a practical, hardware-aware data augmentation strategy that could transfer to other MIMO radar systems. However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (Radatron), a single object class (cars), and 2D rotated boxes; generalization to multi-class detection, 3D localization, or different radar hardware remains open. The label-efficiency gains (11.5% at 1% labels) suggest the approach could reduce annotation costs for future radar deployments, but broader validation is needed.
