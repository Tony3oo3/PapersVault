## Problem

Online Continual Learning (OCL) faces a unique overfitting-underfitting dilemma absent in standard CL: new tasks are underfit because each sample is seen only once, while old tasks in the memory buffer are overfit because buffered samples are replayed repeatedly across subsequent tasks. Existing replay-based and contrastive methods partially address this but suffer from sub-optimal convergence and high computational overhead, leaving a significant performance gap between OCL and offline CL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) that are augmented via random horizontal flip, random grayscale, and random resized crop, with the augmented view concatenated to the original for dual-view training. At inference, the final expert's feature map is classified via a Nearest Class Mean (NCM) classifier that compares the encoded feature against stored class prototypes, producing a single class label. The model processes a one-pass stream of 10 or 100 sequential tasks with a fixed memory buffer (1k–10k samples) and batch sizes of B=10 (incoming) and B^M=64 (buffer).

## Method

MOSE splits a ResNet18 backbone into 4 sequential latent experts (E₁–E₄), each equipped with a dimension-alignment module and dual output heads for cross-entropy and supervised contrastive losses, forming a Multi-Level Supervision (MLS) signal that forces every depth to learn a functional classifier. To prevent gradient bias from repeatedly computing cross-entropy over buffer samples, the new-task loss uses a separated softmax restricted to current-task classes. The second component, Reverse Self-Distillation (RSD), treats shallower experts as teachers and the full network (E₄) as student, minimizing L2 distance between their normalized feature vectors so that the final predictor absorbs the complementary strengths of all experts without any extra inference cost. Empirically, shallower experts generalize better on early tasks while deeper ones capture more semantic features for later tasks, and RSD consolidates this diagonal pattern of expertise into a single strong final predictor, yielding up to 7.3% ACC gains over SOTA on Split CIFAR-100.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), MOSE achieves 54.8% average accuracy with M=5k memory, outperforming the best OCL baseline GSA (49.7%) by 5.1% and the best offline CL baseline BiC (42.5%) by 12.3%. On Split Tiny-ImageNet (100 tasks), MOSE reaches 38.7% ACC with M=10k, surpassing GSA (33.2%) by 5.5%. The MOE variant (averaging all expert outputs) pushes gains further to 55.6% and 39.3% respectively. Average forgetting (AF) for MOSE is 13.5% on CIFAR-100 (M=5k) and 15.5% on Tiny-ImageNet (M=10k), competitive with most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOSE outperforms all 11 OCL baselines and 7 offline CL baselines on both datasets across all memory sizes. The strongest OCL competitor is GSA (2023), which MOSE surpasses by 3.7% ACC on CIFAR-100 (M=1k: 35.1 vs 31.4) and 5.5% on Tiny-ImageNet (M=10k: 38.7 vs 33.2). Among offline CL methods run under the one-epoch constraint, BiC (2019) is the closest competitor on CIFAR-100 (42.5% at M=5k), yet MOSE still leads by 12.3%. On the forgetting metric, MOSE's AF (13.5% on CIFAR-100 M=5k) is higher than OCM (4.5%) and OnPro (5.3%), but its substantially higher ACC more than compensates, yielding the best overall performance. The MOE variant, which averages logits from all four experts at test time, further extends the lead to 7.3% over GSA on CIFAR-100 and 6.1% on Tiny-ImageNet, confirming that multi-level expertise aggregation is the key driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOSE extends the replay-based OCL paradigm (ER, SCR, OCM, OnPro, GSA) by introducing an architectural modification—multi-level supervision that treats each ResNet block as a latent expert—rather than relying solely on memory buffer strategies or loss modifications. It departs from conventional knowledge distillation in CL (LwF, DER, iCaRL), which requires a frozen copy of the previous model, by performing distillation internally via Reverse Self-Distillation: shallower experts serve as teachers for the final predictor, eliminating the need for stored model snapshots. The separated-softmax trick from SSIL [1] is adapted to handle gradient imbalance between new-task and buffer batches, and the overall design is modular, compatible with existing replay-based losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically characterizes the overfitting-underfitting dilemma as the core gap between offline CL and OCL, providing a useful diagnostic (BOF metric) and a principled mechanism (multi-level experts at different convergence points) to address it. The modular design means MOSE can be layered onto existing replay-based methods without architectural overhaul, making it practically adoptable. However, validation is limited to ResNet-18 on two small-scale datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) in the class-incremental setting; whether the multi-level expert decomposition generalizes to ViT backbones, larger datasets, or task-incremental scenarios remains open. The auxiliary modules (alignment layers, extra heads) add ~13% parameters, raising questions about efficiency at scale.
