## Problem

Vanilla vision-transformer-based patch-word alignment suffers from two inherent defects: (1) a large proportion of visual patches are redundant to the textual content, overshadowing crucial patches and accumulating misalignment, and (2) tiny non-overlapping patches lack semantic integrity, producing ambiguous representations that yield moderate alignment scores with many distinct texts. Previous fine-grained methods relied on expensive pre-trained object detectors, and the shift to pure ViT patch features introduced these redundancy and ambiguity problems that had not been explicitly addressed.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image (224×224 or 384×384) and a text sentence as input. The image is partitioned into non-overlapping patches (14×14 for ViT-224, 24×24 for ViT-384, 7×7 for Swin-224, 12×12 for Swin-384) and encoded by a ViT or Swin Transformer, while the sentence is tokenized and encoded by BERT. The output is a scalar alignment score S(I,T) computed via sparse max-correspondence between slimmed patches and words, along with intermediate artifacts: a binary patch-selection decision matrix, a set of aggregated informative patches, and a single fused redundant-patch token.

## Method

LAPS comprises three sequential modules. First, the Language-Context Patch Selection (LPS) module estimates per-patch significance by combining a score-aware MLP prediction with cross-attention scores against the text global embedding and self-attention scores against the image global embedding, then converts these scores into a differentiable binary decision matrix via Gumbel-Softmax sampling. Second, the Semantic-Spatial Patch Calibration (SPC) module aggregates the selected significant patches into fewer informative patches using a learned weight matrix (MLP + softmax), and fuses all redundant patches into a single token to preserve residual semantics. Third, the Sparse Patch-Word Alignment (SPA) module computes cosine similarities between the slimmed patch set and word tokens, then aggregates via bidirectional max-correspondence (patch-to-word and word-to-patch) to produce the final alignment score. Training uses a bi-directional triplet loss with hard negative mining plus a mean-squared ratio-constraint loss that enforces a predefined selection ratio ρ for stable optimization.

## Results

On Flickr30K with ViT-Base-224, LAPS achieves rSum 507.3, outperforming the best fine-grained baseline CHAN (489.9) by 17.4 points and the coarse-grained VSE++ (496.1) by 11.2 points. With Swin-Base-384, LAPS reaches rSum 545.3 on Flickr30K and 470.1 on MS-COCO 5K, surpassing the strongest detector-based method HREM (540.6 / 444.1) and all transformer-based fine-grained methods. On MS-COCO 5K with ViT-Base-384, LAPS achieves rSum 442.2 versus 431.1 for CHAN and 425.8 for VSE++. The paper reports 5%–15% rSum gains over state-of-the-art fine-grained alignment methods across all tested backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAPS consistently outperforms all four transformer-based fine-grained baselines (SCAN, SGR, CHAN) and the coarse-grained VSE++ across every encoder setting. The most striking gap appears with ViT-Base-224 on Flickr30K, where LAPS (rSum 507.3) beats CHAN (489.9) by 17.4 points and SCAN (485.6) by 21.7 points, confirming that vanilla patch-word alignment is fundamentally incompatible with fine-grained methods without patch slimming. Against the detector-based HREM (rSum 520.4 on Flickr30K), LAPS with Swin-Base-384 (545.3) surpasses it by 24.9 points while eliminating the need for a pre-trained object detector. On the VLP backbone (CLIP-ViT-Base-224, Table 2), LAPS achieves 92.9/80.6 R@1 (I2T/T2I) on Flickr30K, beating VSE++ (92.2/80.5) and SCAN (88.2/75.3), and approaching the zero-shot CLIP baseline (81.4/61.1) with a large margin. On visual grounding (Table 3), LAPS improves over the best prior method (SGR) by 2.6–4.9 points across RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, e.g., 51.3 vs 45.5 on RefCOCOg Val.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAPS departs from the detector-based fine-grained alignment paradigm (SCAN, SGR, CHAN) that relied on pre-trained object detectors like Faster R-CNN to extract region features, instead operating on ViT/Swin Transformer patches in a fully end-to-end framework. It borrows the differentiable discrete sampling idea from Gumbel-Softmax (used in token pruning for efficient ViTs like DynamicViT and AdaViT) but repurposes it for cross-modal patch selection guided by linguistic context, which prior token-pruning methods did not consider. The patch aggregation step draws on Self-Slimmed Vision Transformer's adaptive aggregation network, and the overall alignment objective follows the bi-directional triplet loss with hard negative mining established by VSE++.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a previously underexplored failure mode: when fine-grained alignment methods are naively transferred from detector-based regions to ViT patches, they actually underperform simple coarse-grained baselines, revealing that patch-level granularity introduces redundancy and semantic ambiguity that degrade alignment quality. This insight is practically important for anyone building transformer-based cross-modal systems, as it shows that patch selection and calibration are necessary prerequisites for fine-grained alignment to work. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or longer-text settings, whether the fixed selection/aggregation ratios can be made fully adaptive per sample, and whether the framework generalizes to other modalities beyond RGB images and short captions.
