## Problem

Previous pan-sharpening methods that leverage frequency-domain information only perform global Fourier fusion on the entire image, neglecting the fact that frequency relationships between PAN and LRMS images vary significantly across different local regions. This paper identifies that local Fourier information exhibits distinct complementarity patterns in different image regions, and that spatial fusion alone cannot capture these frequency relationships, motivating a hierarchical integration of spatial, global Fourier, and local Fourier information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240131/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution panchromatic (PAN) image and a low-resolution multi-spectral (LRMS) image as input, where the LRMS is bicubically upsampled to match the PAN resolution before processing. Training follows the Wald protocol with 128×128 PAN crops and 32×32 LRMS crops (4× downsample ratio), producing a high-resolution multi-spectral (HRMS) image with both high spatial and spectral resolution. The framework also generalizes to visible-infrared image fusion (RoadScene) and depth image super-resolution (NYU v2 at ×4, ×8, ×16).

## Method

HFIN is built from stacked SGLI (Spatial and Global-Local Fourier information Integration) modules, each performing two stages: information stratification and information integration. For stratification, three parallel blocks extract hierarchical features: a spatial block (3×3 CNNs), a global Fourier block (DFT on the full image, fusing magnitude and phase spectra via 1×1 convolutions before IDFT), and a local Fourier block (DFT applied to 4 non-overlapping regions with 50% overlap, where overlapping pixels are averaged). For integration, an SF fusion step uses sigmoid-gated concatenation to inject spatial details into the frequency branches, followed by a GL fusion step with cross-add convolutions to enable interaction between global and local Fourier features. The network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss using Adam (lr=1e-3, halved at epoch 200) on a single GTX 3090, and the final output combines the global and local Fourier branches with a residual connection.

## Results

On the three reduced-resolution pan-sharpening benchmarks (WV2, GF2, WV3), HFIN achieves PSNR improvements of 0.4 dB, 1.0 dB, and 0.1 dB over the second-best method, respectively, while also leading in SSIM, SAM, and ERGAS. On the real-world full-resolution GF2 scene (no-reference evaluation), HFIN attains the best D_λ (0.1098) and QNR (0.8261), though PanFlowNet edges it on D_s (0.0665 vs. 0.0710). The method also generalizes strongly: on visible-infrared fusion (RoadScene) it achieves MI=4.81, VIF=0.867, FMI=0.878 (all best), and on depth super-resolution (NYU v2) it reaches RMSE of 1.53/3.19/6.44 at ×4/×8/×16, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFIN outperforms all seven deep-learning baselines (PNN, PANNET, MSDCNN, SRPPNN, GPPNN, SFIINET, PanFlowNet) and five traditional methods (SFIM, Brovey, GS, IHS, GFPCA) on the reduced-resolution WV2/GF2/WV3 benchmarks in nearly every metric, with the largest PSNR gap (1.0 dB) on GF2. On the real-world full-resolution GF2 test, HFIN achieves the best spectral fidelity (D_λ = 0.1098) and overall quality (QNR = 0.8261), surpassing PanFlowNet (D_λ = 0.1113, QNR = 0.8257) and SFIINET (D_λ = 0.1230, QNR = 0.8146); however, PanFlowNet retains a slight edge on spatial distortion (D_s = 0.0665 vs. 0.0710). Among traditional methods, all are substantially outperformed, confirming the advantage of learned hierarchical frequency integration. In cross-task generalization (RoadScene visible-infrared fusion and NYU v2 depth SR), HFIN sets new state-of-the-art on every reported metric, beating the strongest prior methods (e.g., TarDAL on MI, PacNet on depth RMSE) by clear margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the spatial-frequency integration framework of Zhou et al. (ECCV 2022, SFIINET), which first demonstrated that global Fourier information from PAN and LRMS images is complementary and beneficial for pan-sharpening. The key conceptual departure is introducing a third hierarchy level—local Fourier information—by partitioning the image into overlapping regions and applying DFT to each, arguing that frequency complementarity between PAN and LRMS varies spatially across the scene. The SGLI module then integrates all three hierarchies (spatial, global Fourier, local Fourier) through dedicated SF and GL fusion sub-modules, positioning the work as a natural hierarchical completion of the prior spatial + global Fourier paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that frequency-domain complementarity between PAN and LRMS images is not uniform across the scene, motivating region-aware Fourier processing as a principled middle ground between purely spatial and purely global frequency fusion. The demonstrated generalization to infrared-visible fusion and depth super-resolution suggests the hierarchical frequency integration framework could serve as a reusable building block beyond pan-sharpening. However, the fixed 4-region partition with 50% overlap is a heuristic choice that may not adapt to scenes with highly heterogeneous frequency content, and the modest gains over SFIINET (0.1–1.0 dB PSNR) leave open whether more adaptive or learned region partitioning could yield substantially larger improvements.
