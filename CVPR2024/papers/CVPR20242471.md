## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) activation calibration methods rely on pointwise distance metrics (MSE, cosine distance, KL divergence) between full-precision and quantized activations, but none account for the information-theoretic degradation that occurs during quantization. This paper argues that calibration should instead maximize the mutual information between pre- and post-quantized activations, a well-defined distributional metric from information theory that has been overlooked in the PTQ literature.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained full-precision network and 128 unlabeled calibration images (randomly sampled from ImageNet training set) as input, and produces per-layer activation quantization parameters (scaling factor S_a^k and zero point Z_a^k) that are optimized jointly with weight rounding values over 20,000 iterations. The quantized activations are stored in dequantized float form (X_Q ≈ X_F) rather than integer form, facilitating alignment in the contrastive space. CL-Calib is designed as a plug-and-play add-on module that can be layered on top of existing SoTA PTQ methods such as QDrop and PD-Quant without modifying their core quantization logic.

## Method

CL-Calib formulates activation calibration as a contrastive learning proxy task: for a batch of M+1 calibration samples, positive pairs are (quantized, FP) activations from the same input, while negative pairs are activations from different inputs. The key architectural choice is using the frozen downstream FP network f_F^[k:] as the mapping function g in the discriminator, rather than training a new encoder from scratch—this avoids the collision solution that plagues CL under limited calibration data and aligns with PTQ's rapid-deployment goal. The CL-Calib loss (Eq. 8) is a binary cross-entropy on a sigmoid discriminator d that classifies activation pairs as positive or negative, and the overall calibration objective combines it with the standard quantization reconstruction loss via a weighting coefficient λ. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Eqs. 14–15) showing that minimizing this combined loss is equivalent to maximizing a variational lower bound on the mutual information I(A_Q^k, A_F^k), thereby ensuring quantized activations retain maximal information from their FP counterparts.

## Results

On ImageNet, CL-Calib serves as a plug-and-play add-on to existing PTQ methods (QDrop, PD-Quant), consistently improving top-1 accuracy across all tested architectures and bit settings. At the most challenging W2A2 setting, CL-Calib boosts MobileNetV2 from 14.17% to 17.70% (+3.53%) and MNasx2 from 28.03% to 30.34% (+2.31%) over PD-Quant. At W4A4, gains are more modest (e.g., ResNet-18: 69.41% vs. 69.30% for PD-Quant, +0.11%), but the advantage grows substantially as bit-width decreases, reaching +1.37% on ResNet-18 and +1.32% on ResNet-50 at W2A2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-Calib is evaluated as an add-on to two recent SoTA PTQ methods: QDrop [40] and PD-Quant [20]. Against PD-Quant, CL-Calib wins on nearly all architecture-bit combinations, with the largest margins at W2A2 (e.g., +3.53% on MobileNetV2, +2.31% on MNasx2) and W4A2 (e.g., +2.37% on MobileNetV2, +1.91% on MNasx2); the sole exception is RegNetX-3.2GF at W4A4 where it drops 0.17%. Against QDrop, CL-Calib consistently outperforms at all bit settings, with the most dramatic gains at W2A2 (e.g., +3.21% on ResNet-18, +5.30% on MNasx2). Older baselines—ACIQ-Mix [2], LAPQ [24], Bit-Split [39], AdaRound [21], BRECQ [19], and AdaQuant [15]—are substantially outperformed; for instance, at W2A2 on ResNet-18, CL-Calib (54.45%) exceeds BRECQ (42.54%) by over 11 points and AdaQuant (0.11%) by a wide margin. The paper also demonstrates that CL-Calib generalizes to Vision Transformers and object detection tasks (reported in the appendix), confirming its applicability beyond the CNN classification setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the contrastive learning framework (rooted in NCE/InfoNCE and mutual information estimation) from self-supervised representation learning into the PTQ activation calibration problem, where prior methods (QDrop, PD-Quant, BRECQ, AdaRound) rely on pointwise distance metrics like MSE or cosine similarity between FP and quantized activations. The key conceptual departure is framing calibration as mutual information maximization between pre- and post-quantized activations rather than minimizing a reconstruction error, and the practical departure is using the frozen full-precision network's downstream layers as the critic function to avoid the collision solution that plagues CL under limited calibration data. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play add-on to existing PTQ pipelines rather than a standalone quantization algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual information perspective provides a principled, information-theoretic justification for PTQ calibration that goes beyond ad-hoc distance metrics, and the plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt it without modifying their existing PTQ pipeline. The most significant gains appear at extreme low-bit settings (W2A2), where information preservation is most critical, suggesting the MI objective is particularly well-suited to the regime where quantization noise is most destructive. Limitations include evaluation primarily on ImageNet classification with CNN architectures (ViT and detection results are relegated to the appendix), modest gains at W4A4, and the method's dependence on having a well-trained FP model available for the critic function. Open questions include whether the MI calibration objective transfers to non-visual modalities, whether it helps with non-uniform or mixed-precision quantization schemes, and whether the regularization effect generalizes beyond the specific calibration data sizes tested.
