## Problem

Video diffusion models suffer from enormous parameter counts and computational demands that make both training and inference prohibitively expensive, while different deployment scenarios require different model sizes and target resolutions. Training separate architectures for each cost-resolution combination is wasteful, and no prior NAS framework has been designed specifically for video diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242374/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded by a frozen T5 text encoder) and produces a generated video clip. A single trained supernet yields subnets spanning 640M–1.6B parameters (pixel-space) or 274M–548M parameters (latent-space) at resolutions from 64×64 to 256×256. The pixel-space pipeline uses a cascading architecture where a base model generates a low-resolution video that is recursively upscaled by a spatial super-resolution (SSR) model, while the latent-space pipeline compresses video into a 3D latent space via an autoencoder before denoising.

## Method

SNED trains a single over-parameterized supernet via one-shot NAS with weight sharing: at each iteration, a random subnet architecture and target resolution are sampled, only the corresponding weights are updated while the rest of the supernet is frozen. The search space spans two dimensions—dynamic channels (each layer independently assigned 40%–100% of its full channel count) and fine-grained dynamic blocks (individual attention or ResBlock components within each diffusion block can be dropped). A 'super-position training' mechanism samples different input resolutions per iteration so one supernet simultaneously covers multiple resolution targets, with per-resolution model-size caps to balance memory. A sampling warmup strategy gradually expands the search space from 100% to 40% over 30,000 iterations to stabilize early training. The framework is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both an Imagen-style 2D UNet (pixel-space) and a 3D U-Net (latent-space, building on LVDM).

## Results

For the pixel-space video diffusion base model, SNED subnets ranging from 640M to 1.6B parameters all achieve stable FVD₁₂ and KVD₁₂ scores, with SNED-M (960M) attaining the best FVD of 452.2 and SNED-L (1.28B) the best KVD of 13.0. The smallest subnet SNED-S (640M) achieves FVD 472.3 and KVD 16.8 with 16.0s latency, yielding a 1.53× speedup over the full 1.6B supernet (24.4s). For the latent-space model at 256×256, SNED at 548M parameters matches the LVDM baseline (FVD₁₆ 298.3 vs. 295.1, KVD₁₆ 20.8 vs. 20.9) with identical 86.8s inference time, while smaller subnets (411M, 274M) trade modest quality for 14–23% latency reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline is LVDM [10], compared on the Sky Timelapse dataset at 256×256 resolution with 16-frame unconditional video generation. At the same 548M parameter count, SNED essentially matches LVDM: FVD₁₆ is 298.3 vs. 295.1 (a negligible 1.1% gap) and KVD₁₆ is 20.8 vs. 20.9 (SNED is marginally better), with identical 86.8s inference latency. When the model is pruned to 411M parameters, SNED incurs a moderate quality drop (FVD 348.2, KVD 23.5) but reduces inference time to 74.2s (14.6% faster). At 274M parameters, the quality gap widens (FVD 472.3, KVD 28.7) while latency drops to 66.7s (23.2% faster). The paper does not report a direct pixel-space baseline comparison, as Imagen Video's model and dataset are not publicly available; instead, the pixel-space results (Table 1) are presented as a self-comparison across SNED subnet sizes, demonstrating that even the smallest 640M subnet outperforms the 1.6B supernet on both FVD and KVD.

## Relation to Prior Work

SNED adapts the one-shot weight-sharing NAS paradigm (as in Single Path One-Shot NAS [8]) to the video diffusion model setting, a combination not previously explored. It inherits the cascading base-model-plus-spatial-super-resolution architecture from Imagen Video [12] and uses LVDM [10] as the latent-space baseline. The key conceptual departure is the 'superposition training' mechanism, where a single supernet simultaneously handles multiple input resolutions (64×64 through 256×256) via shared weights, extending the typical single-resolution NAS formulation. The dynamic channel and fine-grained dynamic block search spaces, along with the sampling warmup schedule, are practical extensions of standard NAS search dimensions tailored to diffusion model blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first NAS framework targeting video diffusion models, SNED offers a practical path to deploying video diffusion at varying compute budgets without retraining from scratch, which is significant given the months-long training costs of these models. However, the pixel-space experiments rely on a non-public 19M-pair internal dataset, and the latent-space evaluation is confined to the small Sky Timelapse benchmark, limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the superposition multi-resolution training scales to longer video sequences or higher resolutions, whether the search space can incorporate architectural choices beyond channel width and block dropout, and whether the modest FVD/KVD gains (and the smaller-subnet-beats-supernet phenomenon) persist at larger scales or with more diverse data.
