## Problem

Current audio-visual segmentation (AVS) methods process labeled and unlabeled frames equally via global temporal modeling, yet experiments show this yields only marginal gains (0.14–0.58 mIoU) over training on labeled frames alone, revealing a systematic underutilization of the abundant unlabeled frames in sparsely-annotated video datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241287/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three modalities per frame: a 224×224 RGB image, a 2-channel optical flow map computed via the Farneback algorithm between the target frame and its temporally-adjacent unlabeled neighbor, and a VGGish audio embedding. The optical flow is encoded by a modified ResNet-18 (first conv layer adapted to 2 channels) and fused with multi-scale visual features before audio-visual fusion. The output is a per-pixel binary segmentation mask for the sounding object, and the framework is architecture-agnostic, plugging into both FCN-based (TPAVI) and transformer-based (AVSegFormer) pipelines.

## Method

The UFE framework partitions unlabeled frames into two categories by temporal distance: neighboring frames (NFs) and distant frames (DFs). For NFs, optical flow is extracted and fed as an auxiliary input, with a refinement network fusing flow features into multi-scale visual features via upsampling and summation, providing motion cues that sharpen objectness localization. For DFs, a teacher-student network with weak-to-strong consistency (FixMatch-style) treats them as natural semantic augmentations: the teacher (stop-gradient) predicts pseudo-masks on weakly-augmented views, while the student is regularized to match on strongly-augmented views (cutmix, color jitter, grayscale). Training proceeds in two phases—a 10-epoch burn-in on labeled data only, followed by joint optimization with supervised loss (BCE or Dice) on labeled frames and an unsupervised consistency loss (weighted λ=0.5) on unlabeled distant frames.

## Results

On AVSBench-S4, the best configuration (Ours w/ AVSegFormer, PVT backbone) achieves 83.15 mIoU and 0.904 F-score, setting a new state-of-the-art. On the more challenging AVSBench-MS3 subset, the same model reaches 61.95 mIoU and 0.709 F-score. With the lighter ResNet backbone, the method still achieves 78.96 mIoU (S4) and 55.88 mIoU (MS3), outperforming all prior methods using the same backbone. The framework also shows strong data efficiency: with only 10% of labeled data, it gains nearly 10 mIoU points over the TPAVI baseline on S4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method (Ours w/ AVSegFormer, PVT) achieves the best mIoU on both S4 (83.15) and MS3 (61.95) among all methods using the same PVT backbone, outperforming AVSegFormer [19] by +1.09 (S4) and +3.59 (MS3), ECMVAE [54] by +1.41 (S4) and +4.11 (MS3), CATR [44] by +1.75 (S4) and +2.95 (MS3), AVSC [47] by +2.58 (S4) and +3.73 (MS3), and AuTR [49] by +2.75 (S4) and +5.75 (MS3). Against TPAVI [81], the gains are even larger: +4.45 mIoU on S4 and +7.95 on MS3 with PVT. The only method that surpasses Ours on MS3 is SAMA-AVS [50] (63.14 vs 61.95 mIoU), but it uses a much heavier ViT-H backbone rather than PVT, making it not a directly comparable setting. On S4, Ours (PVT) also beats SAMA-AVS (ViT-H) by 1.62 mIoU. The framework's versatility is highlighted by the fact that even when applied to the weaker TPAVI baseline, it surpasses AVSegFormer, CATR, AVSC, and AuTR on nearly all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two representative audio-visual segmentation methods — TPAVI (ECCV 2022, FCN-based) and AVSegFormer (AAAI 2024, transformer-based) — by proposing a plug-and-play framework that restructures how unlabeled frames are exploited. Prior methods apply global temporal modeling (GTM) uniformly across all frames, treating labeled and unlabeled ones identically; this paper departs from that by explicitly partitioning unlabeled frames into temporally-adjacent neighboring frames (NFs, used for optical-flow-based motion guidance) and temporally-distant frames (DFs, used as semantic augmentations via teacher-student pseudo-labeling). The teacher-student weak-to-strong consistency mechanism draws from the semi-supervised segmentation literature (FixMatch, Unbiased Teacher), but its application to the audio-visual setting with flow-augmented inputs is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete underutilization problem in AVS — that GTM yields only 0.14–0.58 mIoU from unlabeled frames — and shows that structured exploitation (motion from NFs + augmentation from DFs) recovers 3–6+ mIoU, a substantial gap. The framework's versatility (applicable to both FCN and transformer backbones, effective at 5–10% labeled data) makes it a practical recipe for any AVS pipeline. Open questions include whether the NF/DF partition boundary is optimal, whether the approach transfers to other sparsely-annotated video tasks (e.g., temporal action detection), and whether the teacher-student component could be replaced by simpler consistency or contrastive objectives.
