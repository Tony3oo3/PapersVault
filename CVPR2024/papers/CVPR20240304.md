## Problem

Multi-view photometric stereo (MVPS) methods that jointly solve MVS and PS within a multi-objective optimization suffer from potentially conflicting objectives, leading to loss of fine geometric details. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a single-objective formulation that seamlessly integrates PS-derived reflectance and normal maps into a standard neural volume rendering pipeline without requiring multiple competing loss terms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240304/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-view reflectance (scalar albedo) and normal maps produced by a photometric stereo network (SDM-UniPS), along with known camera poses. In practice, each map is computed as the median over 100 random trials of 10 images (from 96 available per view), and pixels with angular uncertainty exceeding 15° are discarded. Reflectance maps are rescaled across viewpoints to resolve the per-view scale ambiguity inherent in uncalibrated PS. The output is a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from the learned SDF, together with a continuous albedo field.

## Method

The core contribution is a pixel-wise re-parameterization that converts each (reflectance, normal) pair into a 3-dimensional vector of radiances simulated under an arbitrary, per-pixel lighting triplet using the linear Lambertian model, making the inhomogeneous inputs homogeneous quantities amenable to a single L1 loss. The volume renderer is adapted from NeuS by replacing the apparent color with ρ(x)∇f(x)ᵀl_{k,l}, where ρ is an optimized albedo MLP and l_{k,l} are the same illumination vectors used in the input re-parameterization, thereby removing the viewing-direction dependency and enforcing consistency with the Lambertian model. The lighting triplet is chosen per pixel to be optimal (120° apart in tilt, 54.74° slant) to guarantee bijectivity of the re-parameterization. The full objective is a single data-fidelity term (L1 between input-simulated and volume-rendered radiances) plus the standard eikonal regularization on the SDF, optimized jointly over the SDF and albedo MLPs for 300k iterations.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT-MV benchmark (5 objects, 20 views each), RNb-NeuS achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.23 and a mean normal MAE of 4.95°, outperforming all fully automated baselines. The CD is 17.4% lower than the second-best fully automated method (MVPSNet, 0.27), and the MAE is 0.2° lower than the closest competitor (Kaya23, 5.14°). On high-curvature regions, the CD error increases by only 4% (vs. 36% for PS-NeRF and 96% for MVPSNet), and on low-visibility regions by only 13% (vs. 78–81% for PS-NeRF and MVPSNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNb-NeuS outperforms all fully automated MVPS baselines on both Chamfer distance and normal MAE. Against the closest competitor Kaya23 (WACV 2023), it improves CD from 0.28 to 0.23 (18% reduction) and MAE from 5.14° to 4.95°. Against MVPSNet (2023), the CD advantage is 17.4% (0.23 vs 0.27) and the MAE advantage is substantial (4.95° vs 8.18°). PS-NeRF (ECCV 2022) and Kaya22 (CVPR 2022) are clearly outperformed on both metrics (CD 0.28/0.37, MAE 7.88°/7.41°). The classical Park16 method (TPAMI 2016) is far behind (CD 0.62, MAE 10.1°). The only method with a marginally lower CD is the semi-automated Li19 (TIP 2020, 0.22), which requires manual intervention and is not ranked; RNb-NeuS still beats it on MAE (4.95° vs 5.97°). The mono-view PS reference SDM-UniPS achieves 7.12° MAE without producing a full 3D reconstruction, confirming that the 3D integration step adds meaningful geometric accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NeuS volume-rendering framework for SDF-based surface reconstruction, adapting it to handle per-pixel varying illumination by replacing the apparent color with an explicit albedo-times-shading term. It departs from the dominant multi-objective MVPS paradigm (Kaya22, Kaya23, PS-NeRF) by introducing a pixel-wise re-parameterization that converts reflectance and normal inputs into a vector of simulated Lambertian radiances, collapsing the problem into a single data-fidelity objective. The PS front-end (SDM-UniPS) is treated as a black box, making the approach agnostic to the specific photometric stereo method used, which contrasts with prior MVPS methods that tightly couple PS and geometry estimation within a joint optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-objective formulation eliminates the hyperparameter balancing and objective conflicts that plague multi-objective MVPS methods, and the re-parameterization trick is broadly applicable to any PS pipeline producing reflectance and normals. The particularly strong gains in high-curvature and low-visibility regions (4% vs. 36–96% error inflation for competitors) suggest the approach is well-suited for fine-detail reconstruction in cultural heritage and medical imaging. Key limitations include the 8–16 hour runtime (mitigable via NeuS2), heavy dependence on PS normal quality, and the restriction to a scalar albedo model; extending to full PBR (specularity, roughness, anisotropy) and incorporating reflectance uncertainty remain open directions.
