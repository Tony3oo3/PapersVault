## Problem

Long-term 3D scene understanding from sparse, irregular observations has been largely overlooked: existing methods either track objects in short-term high-frequency sequences or address relocalization and reconstruction as separate, disconnected tasks. No prior work jointly solves instance matching, 6DoF registration, and shape reconstruction for evolving indoor environments, and combining separate methods for each subtask compounds errors.

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple 3D point cloud scans acquired at irregular time intervals, each pre-segmented into per-instance point clouds (via GT or predicted masks from Mask3D). It outputs 6DoF rigid transformations aligning matched instances across scans, instance correspondences, and per-instance 3D reconstructions extracted as zero level-sets from a 64³ SDF voxel grid via MISE. A key design choice is that the method accumulates point clouds from all temporal scans per instance, so reconstruction quality improves as more scans are added rather than being limited to a single pair.

## Method

MORE² uses a single SE(3)-equivariant encoder-decoder (VN Transformer + DeepSDF) trained only on ShapeNet reconstruction, producing a compact representation with invariant, equivariant, scale, and centroid components that serves all three subtasks. Matching combines cosine similarity on invariant embeddings with an alignment residual computed via Kabsch on equivariant embeddings, solved by Hungarian assignment. Registration initializes with Kabsch on SE(3)-equivariant embeddings, then refines via back-propagation on the SE(3) manifold (Torch-Lie) minimizing a joint SDF-to-zero-level-set loss and Chamfer distance, followed by ICP. For multi-scan accumulation, a joint optimization simultaneously refines a shared equivariant embedding and a pose graph across all temporal scans, enabling progressive improvement in both registration accuracy and geometric completeness.

## Results

On the end-to-end multi-object relocalization and reconstruction task, MORE² achieves 74.39% MR recall and 62.00% MRR recall on FlyingShape, and 49.07% MR recall and 40.74% MRR recall on 3RScan, outperforming the combined baseline (67.32/54.30 and 44.02/30.77 respectively). For point cloud registration, the full method reaches 83.83% registration recall with 0.74° median rotation error on FlyingShape and 61.11% RR with 4.74 mm RMSE on 3RScan. For instance reconstruction, joint optimization yields 66.73% volumetric IoU and 83.33% SDF recall on FlyingShape, and 6.16×10⁻³ L1-Chamfer distance on 3RScan.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MORE² outperforms all six baselines across every subtask and dataset. In instance matching, it surpasses VN-ONet (86.63% vs. 88.75% instance-level recall on FlyingShape) and MendNet (83.69%), with the largest gap on 3RScan where dynamic-object recall reaches 87.50% vs. 72.05% for VN-ONet. In registration, MORE² (full) achieves 83.83% RR and 0.74° MedRE on FlyingShape, beating GeoTransformer (77.67% RR, 1.36° MedRE) and FreeReg (47.50% RR); on 3RScan it reaches 61.11% RR vs. GeoTransformer's 51.71% and RPMNet's 9.40%. In reconstruction, joint optimization lifts IoU to 66.73% on FlyingShape (vs. ConvONet's 36.99% and VN-ONet's 34.47%) and reduces L1-Chamfer to 6.16×10⁻³ on 3RScan (vs. ConvONet's 7.61×10⁻³). The end-to-end gap widens relative to individual-task gaps, confirming that the unified equivariant representation and joint optimization provide compounding benefits over the pipeline of separate networks (VN-ONet + GeoTransformer + ConvONet) used as the combined baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate subtasks—instance matching, point cloud registration, and neural implicit reconstruction—under a single SE(3)-equivariant encoder-decoder architecture built on Vector Neurons and DeepSDF. It departs from prior long-term change detection work (3RScan, ReScan, NSS) that addressed individual tasks in isolation, and from short-term dynamic scene understanding (scene flow, multi-object tracking) that assumed dense, regular observations. The key conceptual departure is using one frozen network trained solely on reconstruction to flexibly solve all three tasks via different adaptations of the same equivariant embedding, combined with a joint shape-pose optimization for multi-scan accumulation that no prior indoor long-term method attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'living scene' formulation addresses a practical gap: cumulative 3D understanding of indoor environments over irregular, sparse observations, which is relevant for mixed reality, robotics, and digital twin maintenance. Limitations include the assumption of rigid objects, dependence on instance segmentation quality, and inability to handle elastic deformations or multiple identical objects—challenges the authors explicitly flag as future work. The accumulation benefit saturating around four scans suggests diminishing returns for very long temporal baselines, and the domain gap between synthetic training and real-world 3RScan remains significant (performance drops ~25 points across datasets).
