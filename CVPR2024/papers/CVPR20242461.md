## Problem

Existing self-supervised video alignment methods (e.g., TCC) assume monotonic action ordering and single occurrences of each action, making them unable to handle in-the-wild videos with repeated actions, background frames, or non-monotonic step sequences. Additionally, prior approaches train a separate model per activity, requiring the activity label at inference time and incurring substantial computational overhead. This paper addresses both limitations to enable robust alignment and online progress prediction without fine-grained annotations or task-specific models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of video frame sequences (each encoded by a ResNet-50 or MCN encoder into D-dimensional embeddings) and produces three outputs: aligned frame embeddings, a per-frame drop probability (via a logistic regression conditioned on the other video's context vector), and an online progress estimate in [0,1] computed as cumulative inter-frame geodesic distance normalized by the mean total geodesic distance of training videos. Progress is estimated frame-by-frame at inference without access to future frames, and the drop probability is asymmetric—computed for frames in U given context from V.

## Method

GTCC generalizes TCC by replacing the single soft nearest neighbor with K soft nearest neighbors obtained from a discretized Gaussian Mixture Model fit to the forward similarity distribution, enabling multi-neighbor cycle consistency for repeated actions. A multi-cycle-back regression loss weights each component's cycle-back loss by its GMM mixture weight, and a local temporal window constrains the backward distribution to enforce only a local peak around the query frame. A context-dependent drop function (a feedforward network producing a context vector from the other video, followed by logistic regression) assigns each frame a drop probability; the final loss minimizes cycle-back regression for alignable frames (low drop probability) and maximizes it for droppable frames (high drop probability). For multi-task learning, the MCN architecture replaces the single encoder with a shared base network, L task-specialized head networks, and an attention mechanism that selects the relevant head at each time step, eliminating the need for activity labels at inference.

## Results

On in-the-wild datasets, GTCC achieves EAE of 13.3 on EgoProceL and 18.9 on COIN, outperforming the best baselines (LAV: 20.8/26.7, VAVA: 23.2/32.0) by at least 7.5%. For online progress estimation, GTCC reduces OPE from 34.0% (TCC) to 13.6% on EgoProceL and from 22.7% (LAV) to 21.4% on COIN. On monotonic datasets, GTCC achieves the best EAE (7.9 on Pouring, 16.7 on Penn Action) and OPE (22.5 on Pouring, 14.5 on Penn Action), with KT reaching 88.1 and 88.3 respectively. GTCC also improves PC(1.0) substantially on in-the-wild data (80.6 on EgoProceL vs. 68.8 for LAV; 56.3 on COIN vs. 47.3 for VAVA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTCC outperforms all five baselines (SAL, TCN, TCC, LAV, VAVA) on EAE, OPE, and KT/KTW across all four datasets. On in-the-wild data, the gains are most dramatic: GTCC reduces EAE by 7.5–13.1 points over LAV and VAVA on EgoProceL/COIN, and cuts OPE by roughly 20 points on EgoProceL (13.6 vs. 34.0–36.2). VAVA, the only prior method explicitly designed for in-the-wild alignment, is consistently outperformed by GTCC on every metric in both single-task and multi-task settings, including drop accuracy (62.0% vs. 44.7% on COIN). On monotonic datasets (Pouring, Penn Action), GTCC wins on EAE, OPE, KT, and PHP but does not improve PC over LAV/VAVA, since PC measures action-segment clustering rather than precise temporal alignment. SAL and TCN, which lack any alignment mechanism, are the weakest baselines, trailing GTCC by 10+ points on EAE and 15+ points on OPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes Temporal Cycle Consistency (TCC) [12] by replacing its single-neighbor assumption with a GMM-based multi-neighbor cycle consistency to handle repeated actions, and by adding a context-dependent drop function to handle background and redundant frames. It departs from the optimal-transport formulation of VAVA [39] (the most recent in-the-wild alignment method) by avoiding temporal priors that are ill-suited for unconstrained videos, and from all prior alignment methods (TCC, LAV, VAVA) by introducing a Multi-head Crosstask Network (MCN) that jointly learns embeddings across multiple activities without requiring the activity label at inference time. The progress estimation via cumulative geodesic distance on learned embeddings extends the linear-regression-on-embeddings approach used in prior work to an online, non-linear setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: prior self-supervised alignment methods assume monotonic, repetition-free, background-free videos, which fails for real-world procedural tasks (cooking, medical procedures, assembly) where actions repeat, ordering varies, and irrelevant frames are common. The MCN architecture's ability to embed and estimate progress without knowing the activity label is important for AR assistants and robots that must operate at the onset of a task. Open questions include whether the geodesic-distance progress estimate generalizes when the test execution style differs substantially from training videos, and whether the GMM component count K and drop-context function scale gracefully to very long or highly variable procedural videos.
