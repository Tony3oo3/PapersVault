## Problem

Instant-NGP-based NeRFs store over 99% of their parameters in multi-resolution hash feature embeddings, making them impractical for large-scale or numerous scenes. Existing NeRF compression methods rely on pruning, quantization, or binarization but overlook the structured spatial redundancy in these embeddings that could be exploited via entropy coding. This paper is the first to introduce context models—long established in image and video compression—into the NeRF compression setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a trained Instant-NGP model comprising 12 levels of 3D hash embeddings (resolutions 16–512, up to 2^19 vectors per level), 4 levels of 2D tri-plane embeddings (resolutions 128–1024, up to 2^17 vectors per level), a 128-resolution binary occupancy grid, and a rendering MLP. The output is a compressed bitstream where feature embeddings are entropy-coded via Arithmetic Coding using context-predicted Bernoulli probabilities, the MLP is quantized to 13 bits, and the occupancy grid is binary-coded; decoding takes ~1 second and subsequent rendering is identical to standard INGP with no additional overhead.

## Method

CNC binarizes all hash embeddings to {−1, +1} (following BiRF) and then applies two complementary context models to predict per-element Bernoulli probabilities for entropy coding. The level-wise context model works coarse-to-fine: for each vertex at level l, it interpolates features from the previous Lc=3 levels plus the level's occurrence frequency, feeding them into a tiny 2-layer MLP (Context Fuser) to estimate the probability. The dimension-wise context model projects the finest 3D voxel level onto xy, xz, and yz planes to produce 2D Projected Voxel Features (PVF), which serve as cross-dimensional context for decoding the 2D tri-plane embeddings. A Hash Fusion module resolves hash collisions by weighting each collided vertex's probability by its Area of Effect (intersection with occupied cells from the occupancy grid) and discarding entirely invalid vectors. Training minimizes an MSE reconstruction loss plus a λ-weighted entropy loss, with λ varied from 0.7e-3 to 8e-3 to sweep the rate-distortion curve, achieving 100× and 70× size reduction over INGP while improving fidelity.

## Results

On Synthetic-NeRF, CNC achieves a 100× size reduction over the Instant-NGP baseline while simultaneously improving fidelity, and attains an 86.7% BD-rate reduction over the SOTA compression method BiRF. On Tanks and Temples, CNC achieves a 70× size reduction over INGP and an 82.3% BD-rate reduction over BiRF. The total bitstream size on Synthetic-NeRF (λ=4e-3) is approximately 0.42 MB (0.220 MB 3D embeddings + 0.148 MB 2D embeddings + 0.011 MB MLP + 0.039 MB occupancy grid). CNC closely approaches the fidelity upper-bound on Synthetic-NeRF and even surpasses it on Tanks and Temples, suggesting the entropy constraint acts as a regularizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CNC dominates all 10 baselines on rate-distortion performance. Against the SOTA compression method BiRF [36], CNC achieves 86.7% and 82.3% BD-rate reduction on Synthetic-NeRF and Tanks and Temples respectively, meaning CNC stores the same fidelity in roughly 13% and 18% of the bits BiRF requires. Against other compression methods (VQRF [20], MaskDWT [32], Re:NeRF [6]), CNC's RD curves are consistently upper-left (better), indicating superior compression at every bitrate. Against uncompressed NeRF variants (INGP [29], DVGO [38], Plenoxels [7], TensoRF [4], CCNeRF [44], K-Planes [8]), CNC achieves 70–100× size reduction while matching or exceeding their fidelity. Notably, on Tanks and Temples CNC even surpasses its own fidelity upper-bound (λ=0), implying the context-model entropy constraint provides beneficial regularization that the baselines lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Instant-NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding and BiRF's binarization of feature embeddings, adopting their hybrid 2D-3D (tri-plane + voxel) architecture as the base model. It departs from prior NeRF compression methods (VQRF, BiRF, MaskDWT, Re:NeRF), which rely on pruning, quantization, or binarization to reduce parameter count, by instead framing compression as an entropy-coding problem: the authors introduce context models—borrowed from image and video compression—to predict per-element probabilities for arithmetic coding of binarized embeddings. This is the first work to exploit spatial and cross-dimensional contextual dependencies in multi-resolution hash embeddings for NeRF compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× size reduction on Synthetic-NeRF (and 70× on Tanks and Temples) while simultaneously improving fidelity over the uncompressed baseline is practically significant for deploying NeRFs in storage-constrained or large-scale settings. The observation that the entropy constraint from context models acts as an implicit regularizer—causing the compressed model to surpass the uncompressed upper bound on Tanks and Temples—suggests a broader principle that rate-distortion tradeoffs in NeRF can yield quality gains, not just losses. The main limitation is a 1.3× training slowdown, and the approach is validated only on static scenes; extending context-based entropy coding to dynamic or city-scale NeRFs remains an open question.
