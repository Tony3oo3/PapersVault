## Problem

Robustness verification of MaxPool-based CNNs is hindered by the multi-variate nature of MaxPool, which existing single-neuron relaxation methods approximate too loosely, yielding small certified lower bounds. Multi-neuron relaxation methods are precise but scale poorly due to exponential constraint growth. The paper addresses the gap between these two extremes by proposing a tighter yet computationally efficient linear approximation specifically for MaxPool.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241586/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The verifier takes a trained CNN or PointNet model, a single input image (normalized to [0,1] pixel values), a perturbation radius ε under l1, l2, or l∞ norm, and the true label, and outputs a certified lower bound ε_l — the largest radius within which no adversarial example can flip the classification. Internally, it computes layer-by-layer global linear upper and lower bounds (L^K(x⁰) and U^K(x⁰)) via back-substitution, then uses a 15-iteration binary search to maximize ε_l. The method supports networks with ReLU, Sigmoid, Tanh, or Arctan activations and works on both 2D images and 3D point clouds.

## Method

MaxLin tightens the linear approximation of MaxPool by selecting the first and second maximum values among the upper bounds of the pooled inputs to construct the upper linear bound, and using the maximum midpoint of the input intervals as the lower bound. The key theoretical contribution is a block-wise tightest property: the volume of the over-approximation zone of the combined Activation+MaxPool block is minimized, which is provably superior to neuron-wise tightness when MaxPool follows a non-linear activation. For ReLU+MaxPool specifically, the upper bound is proven to be block-wise tightest. The method uses single-neuron relaxation (O(K² × max n_k³) complexity) and integrates directly into existing verifiers like CNN-Cert and 3DCertify, requiring only a swap of the MaxPool linear bounds while keeping all other components identical for fair comparison.

## Results

On MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny ImageNet, MaxLin achieves up to 110.60% improvement in certified lower bound over CNN-Cert (e.g., 2712×10⁻⁵ vs. 1288×10⁻⁵ on 5-layer CNN, l∞) and up to 49.26% over Ti-Lin. On the ERAN framework, MaxLin outperforms DeepPoly by up to 62.17% and 3DCertify by up to 39.94% in certified bound tightness, while delivering up to 5.13× speedup over 3DCertify. Runtime is comparable to CNN-Cert and DeepPoly (e.g., ~1.4 min vs. ~1.4 min on 4-layer MNIST CNN), confirming that the tighter bounds come at negligible computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaxLin consistently outperforms all back-substitution baselines. Against CNN-Cert [4], MaxLin improves certified bounds by 36–110.60% across all network depths and datasets, with the largest gains on deeper 5-layer MNIST CNNs. Against Ti-Lin [45] (neuron-wise tightest), MaxLin achieves 4–49.26% improvement, demonstrating that block-wise tightness (accounting for activation+MaxPool interleaving) is superior to neuron-wise tightness when MaxPool follows a non-linear activation. Against DeepPoly [37] on the ERAN framework, MaxLin improves bounds by 14–62.17%, and against 3DCertify [27] by 1.77–39.94%, while also being up to 5.13× faster than 3DCertify due to direct computation of linear bounds versus the Double Description Method. In comparisons with branch-and-bound methods (α,β-CROWN [41,48,50] and MN-BaB [12]) on ERAN benchmarks, MaxLin achieves much higher certified accuracy with less time cost across different perturbation ranges, though these BaB methods can in principle be complete. The only case where MaxLin underperforms is MNIST_LeNet_Sigmoid vs. 3DCertify (−1.43%), which the authors attribute to network-specific weight/bias differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-neuron relaxation / linear approximation framework for CNN robustness verification (DeepPoly, CNN-Cert, 3DCertify, Ti-Lin) by replacing the neuron-wise tightest MaxPool bounds with a block-wise tightest formulation that jointly considers the preceding activation layer and the MaxPool layer. The key conceptual departure from Ti-Lin's neuron-wise tightest over-approximation is the introduction of block-wise tightness, which minimizes the over-approximation volume of the combined Activation+MaxPool block rather than the MaxPool layer in isolation. The method is instantiated within existing back-substitution verifiers (CNN-Cert, 3DCertify) and inherits their support for l1, l2, l∞ perturbations and diverse activation functions, while the MaxPool-specific bounds are the sole change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaxPool is ubiquitous in practical CNNs yet has been a persistent weak point in certified verification, and this paper provides the first provably block-wise tightest upper bound for the ReLU+MaxPool block, closing a gap that neuron-wise methods cannot address. The practical impact is significant: up to 110% larger certified bounds and 5× speedup over 3DCertify make tighter certificates achievable at scale. However, the approach remains an incomplete verifier with inherent precision loss, the block-wise tightness guarantee is proven only for ReLU+MaxPool (not Sigmoid/Tanh+MaxPool), and the single-neuron relaxation framework fundamentally limits how tight the bounds can become for deeper or more complex architectures. Open questions include whether the block-wise notion generalizes to multi-layer non-linear blocks or to other multi-variate functions beyond MaxPool.
