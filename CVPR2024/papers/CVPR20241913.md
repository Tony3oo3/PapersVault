## Problem

Despite rapid progress in large multimodal models (LMMs), it remains unclear which architectural and data design choices actually drive performance, as existing systems differ in too many dimensions to isolate causes. This paper presents the first systematic, controlled study of LMM design choices within the LLaVA framework, asking what minimal modifications to LLaVA are needed to achieve state-of-the-art across both conversational and academic-task benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 336×336 resolution (or arbitrary higher resolutions via grid splitting in LLaVA-1.5-HD) paired with free-form text instructions, and produces natural-language text responses. A key I/O detail is the response format prompting mechanism: appending phrases like 'Answer the question using a single word or phrase' to the instruction steers the model between short-form and long-form outputs without requiring separate model variants or post-processing. For high-resolution inputs, the image is divided into 336×336 patches encoded independently, then merged with a downsampled 224×224 global context feature before feeding the LLM.

## Method

LLaVA-1.5 makes three orthogonal modifications to the original LLaVA: (1) replacing the single linear vision-language projection with a two-layer MLP connector to increase cross-modal representation capacity, (2) incorporating academic-task-oriented VQA datasets (VQA-v2, OKVQA, A-OKVQA, OCRVQA, TextCaps, Visual Genome, RefCOCO, GQA) alongside the original LLaVA-Instruct data, and (3) upgrading the vision encoder to CLIP-ViT-L-336px. A critical insight is the use of explicit response formatting prompts appended to VQA questions, which resolves the short-form/long-form answer imbalance that plagues InstructBLIP without requiring ChatGPT-based data conversion. Training follows a two-stage protocol—vision-language alignment pretraining on ~558K image-text pairs, then visual instruction tuning on ~665K samples—completing in roughly one day on a single 8×A100 node. For higher resolutions, LLaVA-1.5-HD splits images into 336×336 grids, encodes each patch independently with the frozen ViT, concatenates the merged features with a downsampled global context, and feeds the combined sequence to the LLM, avoiding positional embedding interpolation entirely.

## Results

LLaVA-1.5-13B achieves state-of-the-art on 11 out of 12 benchmarks using only 1.2M publicly available training samples (558K pretrain + 665K finetune). On instruction-following benchmarks, it scores 1531.3 on MME, 67.7 on MMBench, 61.6 on SEED-Bench, 72.5 on LLaVA-Wild, and 36.1 on MM-Vet. On academic benchmarks, it reaches 80.0 on VQAv2, 63.3 on GQA, 53.6 on VizWiz, 71.6 on ScienceQA, and 61.3 on TextVQA. The high-resolution variant LLaVA-1.5-13B-HD further improves MM-Vet to 39.4 and SEED-Bench (image) to 70.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-1.5-13B outperforms InstructBLIP-14B by a wide margin on MME (1531.3 vs. 1212.8), LLaVA-Wild (72.5 vs. 58.2), and MM-Vet (36.1 vs. 25.6), while using only 1.2M training samples versus InstructBLIP's 129M pretraining + 1.2M finetuning. Against Qwen-VL-Chat, LLaVA-1.5-13B leads on MMBench (67.7 vs. 60.6), MMBench-CN (63.6 vs. 56.7), and SEED-Bench (61.6 vs. 58.2), despite Qwen-VL being trained on 1.4B in-house pretraining samples. On academic benchmarks (Table 3), LLaVA-1.5-13B surpasses InstructBLIP-14B on GQA (63.3 vs. 49.5), VizWiz (53.6 vs. 33.4), ScienceQA (71.6 vs. 63.1), and TextVQA (61.3 vs. 50.7), and edges out Qwen-VL on GQA (63.3 vs. 59.3) and VizWiz (53.6 vs. 35.2), though it trails Qwen-VL on TextVQA (61.3 vs. 63.8). The 13B model also outperforms the 80B IDEFICS on MMBench (67.7 vs. 54.5) and MMBench-CN (63.6 vs. 38.1) with a fraction of the parameters. Compared to the original LLaVA-7B, LLaVA-1.5-13B improves MME by 721.7 points, MMBench by 29.0 points, and MM-Vet by 10.6 points, demonstrating the cumulative effect of the MLP connector, response format prompting, and academic-task data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA (Visual Instruction Tuning, Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023), which established the two-stage protocol of vision-language alignment pretraining followed by visual instruction tuning with a simple linear projection connector. It departs from the InstructBLIP and Qwen-VL line of work, which rely on complex visual resamplers (QFormer) trained on hundreds of millions to billions of image-text pairs, by demonstrating that a two-layer MLP connector with only ~1.2M publicly available data achieves comparable or superior performance. The paper also introduces response format prompting as a targeted fix for the multitask balancing problem (short-form vs. long-form answers) identified in prior work on InstructBLIP, and proposes grid-based high-resolution encoding as an alternative to positional embedding interpolation used by Qwen-VL and BLIP-2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that a simple architecture with modest public data can match or exceed systems requiring orders of magnitude more pretraining data, this paper challenges the prevailing assumption that large-scale vision-language alignment pretraining is necessary for strong multimodal instruction-following, making SOTA LMM research accessible to academic labs with a single 8-A100 node. The findings on data efficiency (75% downsampling with minimal performance loss) and hallucination reduction through higher resolution open concrete directions for future work on dataset compression and the interplay between input granularity and model capability. Limitations include the lack of multi-image understanding, restricted problem-solving in specialized domains, and persistent hallucination, leaving open questions about how far compositional generalization can be pushed without explicit joint training.
