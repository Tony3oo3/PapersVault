## Problem

Reconstructing 4D dynamic surfaces from sparse, noisy, or partial point cloud sequences is an ill-posed problem where multiple reconstructions can match the same input. Existing feed-forward methods relying on single global latent codes struggle to capture local shape and deformation patterns, leading to poor generalization to unseen identities and motions, and producing implausible outputs under ambiguous observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T=17 frames of point clouds (L=300 points for D-FAUST, L=512 for DT4D-A) that may be sparse, corrupted with Gaussian noise (σ=0.05), or partial (e.g., monocular depth from a fixed camera, or upper-body-only scans). It outputs a continuous mesh sequence {M^t}_{t=1}^T with explicit vertices and faces, where the first frame is reconstructed as a reference surface and subsequent frames are generated via dense deformation correspondences from that reference, enabling temporally coherent tracking.

## Method

Motion2VecSets represents 4D dynamics as a shape latent set S (for the reference frame) plus a sequence of deformation latent sets {D^t}_{t=2}^T, each encoding local deformation from the reference via a transformer-based autoencoder with FPS-based correspondence. A two-stage training first learns these autoencoders, then trains EDM-style diffusion models: a shape diffusion denoises S conditioned on the first input frame, while a synchronized deformation diffusion applies a single uniform noise ε across all frames and denoises them jointly. The denoiser uses an Interleaved Spatio-Temporal Attention (ISTA) block that alternates Space Self-Attention (within each frame), Conditional Cross-Attention (injecting sparse observation codes), and Time Self-Attention (across frames at the same spatial index), reducing complexity from O(T²N²) to O(TN²)+O(NT²). KL-regularization in the latent space preserves coherent global structure while the set-based representation captures local geometry, enabling diverse and plausible outputs for one-to-many ambiguous inputs.

## Results

On the DT4D-A dataset (unseen individual), Motion2VecSets achieves 83.7% IoU, 0.058 Chamfer distance, and 0.074 correspondence error, substantially outperforming the previous best CaDeX (64.7% IoU, 0.127 CD, 0.239 Corr). On D-FAUST (unseen individual), it reaches 83.7% IoU, 0.045 CD, and 0.064 Corr versus CaDeX's 80.7% IoU, 0.055 CD, and 0.087 Corr. The method reduces Chamfer distance and correspondence error to less than half of prior state-of-the-art across both datasets, with the largest gains observed on the more topologically diverse animal dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Motion2VecSets outperforms all three baselines (OFlow, LPDC, CaDeX) on every metric across both datasets. The most dramatic improvement is on DT4D-A unseen individuals, where the method gains ~19 IoU points over CaDeX (83.7% vs. 64.7%) and nearly halves the correspondence error (0.074 vs. 0.239), reflecting the advantage of latent set representation over global codes for diverse animal topologies. On D-FAUST, gains over CaDeX are more modest (83.7% vs. 80.7% IoU for unseen individuals) but still consistent across all metrics. OFlow and LPDC, which rely on Neural-ODE or single global latent codes respectively, are consistently the weakest baselines, particularly on the animal dataset where their global representations struggle with varied topologies. The paper attributes the superiority to the 4D latent set diffusion capturing local geometry and deformation patterns, which generalizes better to unseen identities than the global-code approaches used by all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DShape2VecSet latent vector set representation from static 3D shapes to 4D dynamics by introducing a separate deformation latent set that encodes correspondences between a reference frame and subsequent frames. It adapts the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al.) and the EDM training paradigm (Karras et al.) to the 4D reconstruction setting, departing from prior feed-forward 4D methods (OFlow, LPDC, CaDeX) that rely on single global latent codes or Neural-ODE solvers. The key architectural departure is the Interleaved Spatio-Temporal Attention block, which enables synchronized denoising of deformation latent sets across frames while reducing complexity from O(T²N²) to O(TN²) + O(NT²).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-based priors over latent vector sets can effectively handle the ill-posed nature of 4D reconstruction from ambiguous observations (sparse, noisy, partial point clouds), producing not just a single reconstruction but multiple plausible outputs for highly underdetermined inputs like half-body scans. The large generalization gap on unseen animal identities (83.7% vs 64.7% IoU over CaDeX) suggests that local latent set representations are substantially more transferable than global codes for diverse topologies. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, more complex multi-object scenes, or real-world unstructured captures, and the authors note potential extensions to text-driven 4D generation and RGB video-based reconstruction.
