## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods rely on static CLIP text embeddings that cannot adapt to the large intra-class variance inherent in food ingredients, where the same ingredient (e.g., egg) appears drastically different across cooking methods. Additionally, the only available ingredient-level food segmentation dataset (FoodSeg103) covers just 103 classes, far too few to represent real-world dietary diversity, making open-vocabulary segmentation essential but unaddressed for food images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241345/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a food image (processed at 640×640 for visual embedding extraction and 320×320 for mask prediction) along with a set of ingredient class names as text queries. It produces pixel-level segmentation masks with per-class probability distributions, where novel classes (those absent from training) are segmented without any class-specific supervision. The text input is not used directly but is first transformed into image-informed text embeddings that incorporate visual context from the specific input image before classification.

## Method

OVFoodSeg introduces a two-stage training pipeline built on a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone. In Stage I, a FoodLearner module (a Q-Former-style transformer initialized from BLIP2 weights) is pre-trained on ~1M image-recipe pairs from Recipe-1M+ using three joint losses (image-text contrastive, image-text matching, and language modeling) to learn extracting food-relevant visual knowledge via 32 learnable query tokens with cross-attention. In Stage II, the pre-trained FoodLearner extracts image-specific visual representations that are average-pooled and added element-wise to CLIP text embeddings to form image-informed text embeddings, which are then fed into the SAN segmentation framework for supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy and Dice losses. The key mechanism is that the text embedding for each ingredient class is dynamically adjusted by the visual content of the current image, allowing the model to distinguish, for example, scrambled egg from boiled egg by conditioning the 'egg' text representation on what is actually visible.

## Results

On FoodSeg103, OVFoodSeg achieves an average mIoU of 33.6% on novel classes, 44.8% on base classes, and 42.6% overall, outperforming the SOTA method SAN by 4.9% on novel-class mIoU. On the more challenging FoodSeg195 (195 ingredient classes), OVFoodSeg reaches 21.6% mIoU on novel classes, a 3.5% improvement over SAN. The variant without Recipe-1M+ pre-training (OVFoodSeg*) still gains 4.0% and 2.4% on novel classes over SAN on FoodSeg103 and FoodSeg195 respectively, confirming the core benefit of image-informed text embeddings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVFoodSeg outperforms all six baselines on novel-class mIoU across both FoodSeg103 and FoodSeg195. Against the strongest baseline SAN [34], OVFoodSeg gains 4.9% mIoU on novel classes (33.6% vs. 28.7%) on FoodSeg103 and 3.5% on FoodSeg195 (21.6% vs. 18.2%), while also improving base-class mIoU by ~4.4% and ~2.4% respectively. Compared to SimSeg [33], OVFoodSeg leads on novel classes by ~10% on FoodSeg103, though SimSeg achieves a slightly higher overall mIoU on FoodSeg195 (31.5% vs. 28.1% in Split 1) due to its heavily trained mask generator; however, SimSeg is ~30× slower (0.3 fps vs. 9.8 fps). Against MaskCLIP variants [36, 37], OVSeg [18], and FreeSeg [25], OVFoodSeg leads by 10–18% on novel-class mIoU, demonstrating that image-informed text embeddings are particularly effective for the high intra-class variance characteristic of food ingredients.

## Relation to Prior Work

OVFoodSeg builds on the frozen CLIP model as its vision-language backbone and follows the SAN pipeline for the segmentation head, but departs from all prior open-vocabulary segmentation methods (MaskCLIP, SimSeg, SAN, OVSeg, FreeSeg) by replacing their static CLIP text embeddings with image-informed text embeddings. The key architectural novelty is the FoodLearner, a Q-Former-style module inspired by BLIP-2 that extracts visual knowledge from food images to conditionally enrich text representations, addressing the large intra-class variance unique to food ingredients (e.g., boiled vs. scrambled egg). This positions the work at the intersection of VLM-based open-vocabulary segmentation and domain-specific visual-text alignment, extending the HIPIE idea of integrating visual features into text embeddings but with a more structured cross-attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that enriching text embeddings with image-specific visual context is a practical and effective strategy for handling high intra-class variance in open-vocabulary segmentation, a challenge that extends beyond food to any domain with visually diverse instances of the same category. The finding that simple or no prompt templates outperform elaborate prompt engineering strategies (ViLD, ImageNet) is a useful counterpoint to the prevailing emphasis on prompt design in VLM-based methods. However, the approach is validated only in the food domain on relatively small datasets (103 and 195 classes), and its generalizability to other domains with similar intra-class variance remains an open question. The reliance on a frozen CLIP encoder also limits the ceiling of performance, and the two-stage training pipeline adds practical complexity.
