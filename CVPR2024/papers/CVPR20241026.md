## Problem

Existing vision-language pre-training methods for OCR (MIM and MLM) fail to achieve effective text-image alignment: MIM masks can obscure the small text regions entirely, while MLM does not explicitly exploit text location information, leading to weak correspondence between text tokens and their spatial positions in the image. This misalignment limits the transferability of pre-trained features to scene text detection and spotting tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 scene text image and an OCR-Text prompt (up to 32 text instances, each with content and location, text length capped at 25 tokens) as dual-branch inputs. It produces a single-channel destylization binary image (not a 3-channel RGB reconstruction) where text glyphs are rendered in a uniform font style, along with pre-trained ResNet50 backbone weights that are subsequently transferred to downstream detection or spotting networks. The binary output format is key: it strips away diverse visual styles, forcing the model to learn text-agnostic structural features.

## Method

ODM pairs a ResNet50 image encoder with a 6-layer transformer text encoder, linked by cross-attention to produce aligned features that a lightweight FPN + 1×1 convolution decoder maps to a destylized binary glyph image. A Text-Controller module introduces two regularization strategies: Drop-Text randomly selects 0–100% of text instances as the prompt (forcing the model to reconstruct only the corresponding region), and Noise-Text injects non-existent text tokens to improve robustness to imperfect labels. Training combines three losses: pixel-level binary cross-entropy, a feature-level OCR LPIPS loss computed via a VGG-based detector, and a CLIP-style batch contrastive loss to pull text-image pairs into a shared semantic space. Pixel-level labels are auto-generated by rendering text in a uniform font (e.g., NotoSans-Regular) at estimated character positions derived from bounding-box or Bezier-curve annotations, eliminating the need for manual pixel annotations and enabling weakly supervised pre-training on pseudo-labeled data.

## Results

On the PSENet backbone pre-trained with ODM on SynthText, the method achieves H-mean scores of 86.90 on ICDAR15, 85.62 on CTW1500, and 85.94 on TotalText, improving over the no-pre-training baseline by +6.92, +7.62, and +7.64 respectively. For detection, DBNet++ with ODM pre-training reaches 88.81 H on ICDAR15 (+2.57) and 85.03 H on TotalText (+4.01). For spotting, ABCNet+ODM achieves 87.56 D-H and 80.87 E-S on ICDAR15, while DeepSolo+ODM reaches 88.08 D-H and 85.83 E-S on the same dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two pre-training baselines, ODM clearly outperforms STKM on all three detection datasets (e.g., 86.90 vs. 85.88 H on ICDAR15, 85.62 vs. 81.51 on CTW1500, 85.94 vs. 81.51 on TotalText) and also beats oCLIP trained on SynthText (86.90 vs. 84.78 on ICDAR15, 85.62 vs. 82.8 on CTW1500). Notably, ODM pre-trained solely on SynthText surpasses oCLIP pre-trained on 40 million web images on CTW1500 (85.62 vs. 83.5 H), though oCLIP-Web retains a slight edge on TotalText (87.0 vs. 85.94). For detection backbones, ODM improves FCENet by +1.26 to +0.98 H across datasets and DBNet++ by +2.57 to +4.01 H, with the largest gain on TotalText. For spotting, ODM consistently improves ABCNet (+2.34 D-H, +2.86 E-S on ICDAR15), DeepSolo (+1.64 D-H, +2.3 E-S on ICDAR15), and SPTS (+1.3 to +2.0 across E-S/E-W/E-G), confirming that the pre-trained weights transfer effectively across diverse architectures. In the weakly-supervised LSVT experiment, ODM with pseudo-labels yields +1.55 H for PSENet and +1.46 H for DBNet++ over their no-pre-training counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODM carves out a distinct niche within vision-language pre-training for OCR by departing from both MIM (which ignores text semantics) and MLM (which masks text tokens without explicit spatial grounding). It draws conceptual inspiration from MaskFeat's feature-reconstruction paradigm and glyph-based OCR methods, but repurposes them into a pixel-level binary destylization objective that explicitly aligns text prompts with OCR-Text locations via cross-attention. The Text-Controller module with Drop-Text and Noise-Text strategies is a novel regularization mechanism not seen in prior OCR pre-training work (STKM, oCLIP, StrucTexTv2, VLPT), and the font-based label generation method addresses a practical gap that prior methods sidestepped by relying on fully annotated pixel-level ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling large-scale pre-training with weakly supervised pseudo-labels (via PPOCRv3), which directly reduces the annotation bottleneck that has limited OCR pre-training to curated datasets. The finding that domain-specific pre-training on ~800K SynthText images can match or exceed web-scale pre-training (40M images via oCLIP) on CTW1500 suggests that objective design may matter more than data scale for OCR-specific tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to handwriting, multilingual scripts, or document-level understanding, and whether the ResNet50 backbone is optimal compared to modern ViT-based encoders. The method's reliance on a well-trained VGG detector for the OCR LPIPS loss also introduces a dependency that could limit applicability in low-resource settings.
