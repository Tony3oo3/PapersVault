## Problem

Existing multimodal learning methods either employ separate encoders per modality (making them computationally expensive and hard to scale), use unified tokenizers that underperform on diverse data types, or are restricted to a small set of modalities (e.g., text, image, video). There is no practical framework that jointly learns robust representations across ~12 heterogeneous modalities with multiple tasks per modality in a single shared architecture, while enabling cross-modal knowledge transfer during both pretraining and supervised fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242467/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 13 input types (RGB images, video clips, audio waveforms, text passages, 3D point clouds, depth maps, time series, tabular rows, graph structures, X-ray/infrared/hyperspectral images, IMU sensor streams) is first converted into a fixed-length token sequence by a modality-specific tokenizer, so the shared transformer backbone always operates on uniform token representations regardless of the source modality. During training, the network processes pairs of modalities in a two-stream configuration with cross-attention fusion in the middle; at inference it collapses to a single stream without cross-attention. Outputs are task-dependent: class labels, pixel-wise segmentation masks, text summaries, retrieval rankings, molecular property values, time-series forecasts, or tabular predictions, each produced by a dedicated ViT-Tiny head.

## Method

The architecture consists of modality-specific tokenizers feeding into a shared BERT-based transformer (f), followed by a cross-attention fusion module that merges features from two modality streams, a second shared transformer (g), and modality-task-specific ViT-Tiny prediction heads. Training proceeds in three stages: (i) unimodal masked pretraining where one modality at a time is masked and predicted by a decoder, (ii) multimodal masked pretraining where pairs of modalities are randomly sampled and jointly decoded in a two-stream setup, and (iii) supervised multitask training where pairs of modality-task combinations are sampled per batch and optimized with standard task losses. The key design choice is pairwise modality sampling rather than full joint training over all modalities simultaneously, which enables stochastic cross-modal knowledge sharing while keeping batch construction tractable. Task balancing via loss magnitude adjustment based on convergence rate allows random pairing of modalities without needing to hand-select compatible pairs, as required by prior work.

## Results

On iNaturalist-2018, OmniVec2 achieves 94.6% top-1 accuracy, surpassing OmniVec (93.8%), MetaFormer (87.5%), and MAE (86.8%). On Kinetics-400 video action recognition, it reaches 93.6% top-1 accuracy (vs. OmniVec 91.1%), and on ESC50 audio classification it achieves 99.1% (vs. OmniVec 98.4%). For 3D point cloud segmentation on S3DIS, it obtains 77.1 mIoU (vs. OmniVec 75.9%), and on ModelNet40-C classification it achieves a 0.142 error rate (vs. OmniVec 0.156). On the GLUE text benchmark, it achieves second-best performance on 3 of 5 tasks, with scores of 95.6 (SST-2), 85.8 (MRPC), 82.2 (QQP), 87.9 (MNLI), and 84.2 (QNLI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniVec2 consistently outperforms its predecessor OmniVec [68] across nearly all evaluated modalities: +0.8% on iNaturalist-2018, +1.6% on Kinetics-400, +0.7% on ESC50, +1.2 mIoU on S3DIS, and +0.7 ROGUE R-L on DialogueSUM. Against MetaFormer [16], which uses a unified tokenizer, OmniVec2 gains approximately 5–7% on image/video/audio tasks and dramatically outperforms it on unseen modalities (e.g., 90.6% vs. 67.6% OA on Indian Pine hyperspectral; 0.1397 vs. 0.8863 MAE on PCQM4M-LSC graph data). On the GLUE benchmark, OmniVec2 trails RoBERTa [52] on SST-2 (95.6 vs. 96.0) and QQP (82.2 vs. 84.0) but surpasses it on MNLI (87.9 vs. 84.0) and QNLI (84.2 vs. 92.0 is actually RoBERTa winning; OmniVec2 wins on MNLI). The one clear loss is on UCF-101 where OmniVec2 scores 99.1% vs. OmniVec's 99.6% 3-fold accuracy. On cross-domain retrieval (YouCook2), OmniVec2 achieves 69.9% Recall@10 zero-shot, surpassing OmniVec's pretrained 64.2% and approaching its fine-tuned 70.8%, while on MSR-VTT it reaches 89.4% fine-tuned vs. Socratic Models' 80.0% pretrained (though SM uses internet-scale data). On time-series forecasting, OmniVec2 outperforms Pyraformer [51], Informer [103], LogTrans [47], MetaFormer [94], and Reformer [40] across ETTh1, Traffic, Weather, and Exchange datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own OmniVec framework by replacing per-modality encoders with modality-specific tokenizers feeding into a shared transformer backbone, reducing computational cost while preserving cross-modal knowledge sharing. It draws tokenizer design from Uni-Perceiver but departs by processing one modality at a time through the transformer rather than combining multi-modality tokens, and by using task-specific heads (ViT-Tiny) instead of a unified task head. The key methodological departure from prior multimodal work (VATT, Omnivore, MetaFormer) is the pairwise modality sampling strategy for both masked pretraining and supervised training, which stochastically minimizes the full multi-task loss by optimizing pairs of modality-task losses at a time rather than requiring all modalities simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single transformer-based architecture with modality-specific tokenizers can achieve competitive or state-of-the-art performance across 12 modalities and 25 datasets, including previously underexplored modalities like hyperspectral, X-ray, IMU, and graph data. The pairwise training strategy is a practical simplification over complex modality-integration schemes in prior work, enabling random modality pairing without hand-crafted groupings. Open questions include whether the pairwise approach scales gracefully to 20+ modalities, whether the ~16% multimodal pretraining gain persists at larger scale, and whether the shared backbone truly captures cross-modal structure or merely benefits from increased effective training data. The evaluation on unseen modalities uses frozen embeddings with simple linear probes, leaving open whether full fine-tuning would close or widen the gap to modality-specific SOTA.
