## Problem

Diffusion models, unlike GANs, possess highly unstructured latent spaces where naive interpolation between two image latents produces abrupt content flickering and semantically incoherent intermediates. This prevents diffusion models from performing smooth image morphing—a capability that GANs achieve naturally through latent code interpolation—despite their superior generation quality. The paper asks whether smooth, GAN-comparable image morphing can be achieved using only the prior knowledge of a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images (real photographs or diffusion-generated) along with optional text prompts describing each image, and produces a user-specified sequence of n+1 intermediate frames forming a smooth morphing video. Images are encoded into the latent space of Stable Diffusion v2.1-base's VAE, and the number of output frames is controlled by the user. No correspondence annotations are required; the method automatically discovers dense correspondences between the two inputs through the learned LoRA parameters and attention mechanisms.

## Method

DiffMorpher trains two LoRAs on the Stable Diffusion UNet, each fitted to one input image, so that the low-rank parameter space encodes the semantic identity of each image. During generation, it linearly interpolates between the two LoRA parameter sets and applies spherical linear interpolation (slerp) between DDIM-inverted latent noises, producing semantically coherent intermediates. To eliminate residual low-level texture flickering, it linearly interpolates and injects the self-attention K and V matrices from both input images into the early denoising steps (controlled by a hyperparameter λ ≈ 0.4–0.6), while an AdaIN adjustment enforces color and brightness consistency. Finally, a histogram-equalization-inspired reschedule redistributes the interpolation ratio α across frames to minimize variance in perceptual distance between consecutive images, yielding a uniform transition speed.

## Results

On the newly introduced MorphBench, DiffMorpher achieves the best overall FID of 54.69 (vs. 67.57 for Warp&Blend, 101.68 for DDIM, and 146.66 for Diff.Interp.), demonstrating superior image fidelity. Among all deep interpolation methods, DiffMorpher also attains the lowest PPL (21.10) and PDV (21.42), indicating the smoothest and most uniform transition video. In the animation scenario specifically, DiffMorpher reaches FID 43.15, PPL 5.14, and PDV 3.55, substantially outperforming the next-best deep method (DDIM: FID 174.31, PPL 18.70, PDV 249.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMorpher outperforms all five baselines on FID across both metamorphosis and animation scenarios, with the largest margin over Diff.Interp. (54.69 vs. 146.66 overall FID) and DGP (54.69 vs. 138.20). Among deep interpolation methods, DiffMorpher achieves the lowest PPL (21.10 vs. 25.38 for DDIM, 105.23 for Diff.Interp.) and PDV (21.42 vs. 288.51 for DDIM, 112.84 for Diff.Interp.), confirming substantially smoother and more uniform transitions. The classic Warp&Blend method reports lower PPL (14.27) and PDV (3.67) than DiffMorpher, but the paper attributes this to the inherently linear warping-and-blending operation that produces ghosting artifacts rather than semantically meaningful intermediates. StyleGAN-XL and DGP, both GAN-based, show the worst smoothness metrics (PPL 39.67 and 29.08; PDV 143.22 and 67.35), reflecting the difficulty of GAN inversion for real-world images. Overall, DiffMorpher is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best fidelity and competitive smoothness without the ghosting artifacts of classic warping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-studied lines: GAN-based latent interpolation for morphing (which achieves smoothness but fails on arbitrary real images due to GAN inversion) and diffusion-model-based interpolation (which handles real images but produces abrupt content changes due to the unstructured noise latent space). The key departure is repurposing LoRA — originally a parameter-efficient fine-tuning tool — as a semantic bridge: by fitting two LoRAs to the input images and linearly interpolating their parameters, the authors create a smooth semantic transition path that the raw diffusion latent space cannot provide. This builds directly on Stable Diffusion's architecture, DDIM inversion for latent recovery, and the deep network interpolation idea of Wang et al. (2018), but is the first to apply parameter-space interpolation specifically for image morphing with diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: it closes a long-standing functional gap between diffusion models and GANs (smooth morphing), and it introduces MorphBench, the first benchmark for evaluating general-object image morphing, enabling future quantitative comparison. The LoRA-as-semantic-bridge idea is broadly applicable beyond morphing — it suggests a general mechanism for continuous semantic manipulation in pretrained diffusion models, potentially enabling new editing and animation workflows. Limitations include the per-image-pair LoRA training cost, restriction to topologically similar objects, and the slight FID increase from rescheduling; open questions include extending to multi-image morphing, video morphing, and whether the approach transfers to newer diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models).
