## Problem

Dense video captioning requires precise event boundary annotations that are rare and expensive to produce, creating a fundamental data bottleneck. While Vid2Seq attempted to address this by pretraining on large-scale unlabeled narrated videos, it directly converts raw subtitle text and timestamps into pseudo captions and boundaries, introducing substantial noise from dialogues, personal reflections, and temporal misalignment that degrades event learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240387/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video sampled at 1 FPS with a fixed frame count (F=200 for YouCook2, F=100 for ActivityNet) and produces a set of timestamped event boundaries (start, end) paired with natural language captions for each localized event. For pretraining, the input is a video paired with its subtitle or speech-to-text transcription; the LLM pipeline transforms these raw narrations into structured, event-centric caption sequences before boundary optimization. The final model uses a PDVC-style architecture with a transformer encoder, transformer decoder, and three prediction heads (localization, caption, event counter) that jointly predict boundaries, captions, and event count without NMS.

## Method

DIBS generates high-quality pseudo training data from unlabeled videos through three stages. First, diverse LLMs (LLAMA2, InternLM, ChatGPT, Claude) are prompted with iteratively refined instructions to extract concise, action-oriented, event-centric captions from raw subtitles, replacing noisy dialogue with structured descriptions. Second, a caption-aware pseudo boundary generation algorithm computes a vision-language similarity matrix (aggregating CLIP and UniVL scores) and iteratively refines boundaries using soft time constraints: it initializes boundaries by dividing the video into N equal segments, then in each iteration collects top-k most similar frames locally, selects the most concentrated frame as the new center, and determines boundary extent via standard deviation, choosing the iteration with minimum similarity-weighted distance loss. Third, an online boundary refinement strategy augments each pseudo boundary with jitter, links augmented proposals to model queries via Hungarian matching, scores them using PDVC's event classification and caption scoring heads, and replaces the original boundary with a weighted average of the top-K highest-scoring proposals, iterated over two stages during training.

## Results

On YouCook2, DIBS with the UniVL backbone achieves the best captioning scores (METEOR 9.41, CIDEr 59.35, SODA_c 7.97) using only 56k pretraining videos, surpassing Vid2Seq (METEOR 9.30, CIDEr 47.10, SODA_c 7.90) which uses 15M videos. On ActivityNet, DIBS with the CLIP backbone achieves the best results (METEOR 8.93, CIDEr 31.89, SODA_c 5.85), outperforming Vid2Seq (METEOR 8.50, CIDEr 30.10, SODA_c 5.80). For event localization on YouCook2, DIBS with UniVL reaches 30.80% recall and 45.13% precision at IoU 0.5, exceeding Vid2Seq's 27.90% recall and 27.80% precision. The method also demonstrates strong few-shot capability, matching full-data performance with only 50% of the fine-tuning set on YouCook2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vid2Seq [27], the primary large-scale pretraining competitor, DIBS wins on YouCook2 with the UniVL backbone (CIDEr 59.35 vs 47.10, SODA_c 7.97 vs 7.90) and on ActivityNet with the CLIP backbone (METEOR 8.93 vs 8.50, CIDEr 31.89 vs 30.10, SODA_c 5.85 vs 5.80), while losing on YouCook2 with CLIP (METEOR 7.51 vs 9.30) and ActivityNet with UniVL (CIDEr 28.85 vs 30.10). Against PDVC [25] (same architecture, no pretraining), DIBS consistently improves: on YouCook2 with UniVL, METEOR rises from 7.87 to 9.41 and CIDEr from 46.02 to 59.35; on ActivityNet with CLIP, METEOR rises from 8.31 to 8.93 and CIDEr from 30.11 to 31.89. Against UEDVC [29], DIBS substantially outperforms on YouCook2 (METEOR 9.41 vs 2.18) but UEDVC's ActivityNet SODA_c (5.49) is competitive with DIBS's UniVL result (5.35). Against E2ESG [33], DIBS dominates on all reported YouCook2 metrics (METEOR 9.41 vs 3.49, CIDEr 59.35 vs 25.00). Against the weakly-supervised baselines WLT [20], WS-DEC [6], and EC-SL [3], DIBS in its weakly-supervised setting (no ground-truth boundaries) achieves comparable or better ActivityNet captioning (METEOR 7.33/6.76 vs 4.93/6.30/7.49) while also reporting YouCook2 results that these methods do not provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIBS builds directly on the PDVC architecture (parallel decoding with Hungarian matching and set prediction) as its training backbone, while departing from Vid2Seq's approach to large-scale DVC pretraining. Where Vid2Seq naively converts raw subtitle text and timestamps into pseudo captions and boundaries, DIBS introduces an LLM-based caption enrichment pipeline and a principled pseudo-boundary optimization algorithm with soft temporal constraints. The work also positions itself against the weakly-supervised DVC line (WS-DEC, WLT, EC-SL), arguing that those methods conservatively redesign training frameworks rather than directly addressing the fundamental data scarcity through better pseudo-label generation at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that 56K carefully processed unlabeled videos (0.4% of Vid2Seq's 15M) can match or exceed Vid2Seq's performance, suggesting that pseudo-label quality—driven by LLM caption enrichment and boundary optimization—matters more than raw data volume. However, the domain gap between instructional cooking videos (HowTo100M) and general activity videos (ActivityNet) significantly limits transfer, with the UniVL backbone actually degrading on ActivityNet after pretraining. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly diverse video domains, how to close the remaining gap to ground-truth boundaries, and whether the online refinement strategy can be extended to multi-stage or curriculum-based training.
