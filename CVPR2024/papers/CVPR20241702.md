## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods based on Score Distillation Sampling with implicit representations (NeRF, DMTET) suffer from the Janus problem—collapsed, multi-faced geometry—because 3D priors cannot be explicitly integrated into implicit density functions. Additionally, these methods are slow to produce elaborate models with rich texture details. The paper asks whether the explicit, object-centric nature of 3D Gaussian Splatting can be leveraged to directly incorporate 3D geometric priors and accelerate high-fidelity generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as primary input, which is consumed by two separate diffusion models: StableDiffusion (for 2D image score guidance) and Point-E (for 3D point cloud initialization and geometry guidance). Optionally, a user-provided 3D shape (mesh or point cloud) can replace Point-E initialization, downsampled via farthest point sampling or uniform surface sampling. The output is a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians (positions, covariances, SH colors, opacities) rendered via GPU tile-based rasterization, producing real-time-renderable 3D assets in approximately 40 minutes.

## Method

GSGEN employs a two-stage progressive optimization over a set of 3D Gaussians. In the geometry optimization stage, it jointly minimizes a 2D SDS loss (StableDiffusion) and a novel 3D SDS loss (Point-E) that directly guides Gaussian positions through a point cloud diffusion prior, establishing a 3D-consistent rough shape and mitigating the Janus problem. In the appearance refinement stage, only the 2D SDS loss is applied while a compactness-based densification strategy—using KD-Tree nearest neighbors to insert new Gaussians between close pairs whose radii overlap—fills geometric gaps and enriches high-frequency details. Additional regularizations include an opacity penalty weighted by distance to center and a position-deviation loss that anchors Gaussians to their geometry-optimized locations, preventing degeneration during refinement.

## Results

The paper's evaluation is entirely qualitative, with no quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, CLIP score, or user study scores) reported in the main text. The single quantitative detail provided is generation time: GSGEN produces a 3D asset in approximately 40 minutes, which is on par with Magic3D and Fantasia3D. Qualitatively, GSGEN demonstrates superior geometry consistency (reduced Janus problem) and richer high-frequency detail capture (feathers, intricate textures, animal fur) compared to all four baselines across multiple text prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSGEN is compared qualitatively against four state-of-the-art text-to-3D methods. Against DreamFusion [50], GSGEN produces significantly more 3D-consistent geometry, as DreamFusion's NeRF-based representation is prone to the Janus problem and collapsed multi-face structures. Against Magic3D [34] and Fantasia3D [12], GSGEN avoids the over-smoothed geometry inherent to mesh-based representations while matching their ~40-minute generation time and delivering sharper high-frequency details (e.g., thatched roof textures, sushi patterns, peacock feathers). Against ProlificDreamer [77], which uses variational score distillation (VSD) to improve appearance diversity, GSGEN still produces more geometrically accurate results; ProlificDreamer's VSD guidance improves visual richness but remains susceptible to the multi-face problem, whereas GSGEN's explicit 3D point-cloud prior (via Point-E) directly corrects geometric deviations during the geometry optimization stage. The ablation studies further confirm that removing the 3D prior reintroduces the Janus problem, and that compactness-based densification is necessary to avoid the over-smoothing or over-densification trade-off of the original gradient-based split under SDS.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSGEN extends the DreamFusion-style Score Distillation Sampling paradigm by replacing the implicit NeRF representation with explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting, which enables direct incorporation of a 3D point cloud diffusion prior (Point-E) into the optimization loop — something infeasible with implicit density fields. The two-stage progressive strategy (geometry optimization with joint 2D+3D guidance, followed by appearance refinement with compactness-based densification) departs from prior single-stage SDS methods like Magic3D, Fantasia3D, and ProlificDreamer, and is conceptually distinct from the concurrent DreamGaussian, which focuses on fast image-to-3D rather than 3D prior integration. The compactness-based densification is a novel supplement to the original view-space gradient splitting in 3D Gaussian Splatting, motivated by the stochastic nature of SDS gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that the explicit geometry of Gaussian Splatting can be directly guided by a 3D diffusion prior, GSGEN offers a practical pathway to mitigate the persistent Janus problem in SDS-based text-to-3D generation, producing more 3D-consistent assets with high-frequency detail (feathers, fur, intricate textures) at roughly 40 minutes per asset. However, the approach remains limited by Point-E's language understanding and the CLIP text encoder, and does not fully eliminate geometric degenerations for highly biased prompts. Open questions include whether richer 3D priors (e.g., mesh- or SDF-based diffusion) could further improve geometric fidelity, and whether the two-stage strategy generalizes to multi-object or scene-level generation.
