## Problem

Existing debiasing methods require full supervision on either spurious attributes or target labels, leaving the practical scenario of large unlabeled datasets with unknown spurious correlations unaddressed. Moreover, self-supervised learning alone has been shown to be insufficient for out-of-distribution generalization when dataset bias persists after augmentation, and most supervised debiasing frameworks degrade in low-label regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242690/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes batches of unlabeled RGB or X-ray images during the self-supervised pretraining stage (no labels required), and a small set of labeled samples during the downstream debiasing stage. The biased encoder outputs low-rank latent representations whose auto-correlation structure encodes spurious attributes; the main encoder outputs debiased representations. The final output is a class prediction from a linear probe (linear evaluation) or a fine-tuned network (semi-supervised setting), with the key intermediate artifact being an error set E of bias-conflicting sample indices used for upweighting.

## Method

The paper first establishes via spectral analysis that spurious correlations suppress the effective rank (spectral entropy) of latent representations, concentrating singular value energy on few directions and degrading feature discriminability. It then introduces a rank regularization loss defined as the negative sum of squared off-diagonal entries of the batch auto-correlation matrix, which intentionally amplifies feature redundancy and forces the encoder into a semantic bottleneck that entangles invariant and spurious attributes. The DeFund framework operates in two stages: (1) a biased encoder is pretrained self-supervised (e.g., SimCLR) with the rank loss, and a linear classifier on top of it identifies bias-conflicting samples via its error set; (2) the main encoder, pretrained with standard self-supervised learning, is debiased by upweighting those identified bias-conflicting samples in the cross-entropy loss during linear evaluation or fine-tuning. This design requires no bias labels, no target labels during pretraining, and is compatible with both contrastive and non-contrastive self-supervised objectives.

## Results

In the linear evaluation protocol (Table 4), DeFund achieves 59.5% bias-conflict accuracy on UTKFace(age), 63.7% on UTKFace(gender), and 58.4% on CelebA(makeup), outperforming the best self-supervised baseline SimCLR (36.4%, 56.3%, 46.9%) by 23.1, 7.4, and 11.5 points respectively. In the semi-supervised setting with only 10% labels (Table 5), DeFund reaches 60.5% conflict / 75.6% unbiased accuracy on CelebA(makeup) and 75.1% / 85.8% on CelebA(blonde), surpassing the best supervised debiasing method (LNL: 55.7% / 75.6%) on conflict accuracy. On MIMIC-CXR+NIH (Table 3), DeFund achieves 56.8% conflict accuracy, exceeding the best supervised baseline EnD (50.4%) by 6.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeFund decisively outperforms all three self-supervised baselines (SimCLR, SimSiam, VICReg) on every dataset and metric in the linear evaluation, with the largest gap on UTKFace(age) conflict accuracy (59.5% vs. 36.4% for SimCLR, a 23.1-point margin). Against supervised debiasing methods, the picture is mixed: DeFund beats LNL, EnD, and CVaR DRO on UTKFace(age) conflict accuracy (59.5% vs. 45.8/45.3/45.7%) but trails JTT (63.8%) and ERM (45.4%→actually ERM is 45.4, so DeFund wins). On UTKFace(gender), DeFund (63.7% conflict) underperforms LNL (73.1%), EnD (75.5%), and JTT (71.2%), indicating that when bias is weaker, supervised methods with explicit bias annotations retain an advantage. In the semi-supervised setting (10% labels), DeFund clearly surpasses all supervised baselines including JTT (60.5% vs. 51.5% conflict on CelebA-makeup) and LNL (60.5% vs. 55.7%), demonstrating that leveraging unlabeled data via self-supervised pretraining of the biased encoder is the key differentiator. The LfF baseline (Table 2) fails to amplify less-salient bias attributes on MultiCMNIST, while DeFund's rank regularization handles multiple bias attributes simultaneously, achieving 73.6% average debiased accuracy vs. LfF's 52.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the debiasing literature (LNL, EnD, JTT, LfF, CVaR DRO) by introducing a label-free mechanism—rank regularization on the auto-correlation matrix of latent features—to intentionally amplify spurious correlations in a self-supervised encoder. It builds on the self-supervised learning paradigm (SimCLR, VICReg, SimSiam) and the spectral analysis of neural network representations (effective rank, SVD), but departs from prior debiasing methods by requiring neither bias annotations nor target labels during pretraining. The two-stage design (biased encoder for sample identification + main encoder for debiased learning) is conceptually related to LfF and JTT, but the rank-regularization-based bias amplification and the compatibility with fully unlabeled pretraining are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: large-scale unlabeled datasets are often biased (e.g., by ethnicity, gender, data source), yet existing debiasing frameworks cannot operate without labels. The empirical discovery that spurious correlations systematically reduce the effective rank of representations provides a new diagnostic lens for understanding and exploiting dataset bias. Limitations include reliance on a simple linear classifier for bias-conflicting sample identification, sensitivity to the rank regularization hyperparameter, and the assumption that a single biased encoder suffices for multi-attribute bias. Open questions include whether the rank-bias relationship holds for more complex or compositional biases, and whether the upweighting strategy can be replaced by more principled reweighting schemes.
