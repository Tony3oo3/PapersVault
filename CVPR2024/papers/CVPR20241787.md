## Problem

Previous multi-modal object ReID methods extract global features from all image regions and aggregate them, making them vulnerable to irrelevant background noise within each modality and amplifying modality gaps during cross-modal fusion. No prior work explicitly selects object-centric tokens to preserve diverse multi-modal information while suppressing background interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241787/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a triplet of co-registered images (RGB, NIR, TIR) resized to 256×128 (person ReID) or 128×256 (vehicle ReID), processed through a shared ViT-B/16 backbone with per-modality learnable class tokens. The output is a 3D feature vector formed by concatenating the three modality-specific class tokens after hierarchical aggregation, used for cosine-similarity-based retrieval across non-overlapping camera views.

## Method

EDITOR first extracts tokenized features from all three modalities via a shared ViT-B/16. A Spatial-Frequency Token Selection (SFTS) module then selects object-centric tokens using two complementary strategies: spatial-based selection that multiplies attention weights across all ViT layers and unions top-s tokens across heads and modalities, and frequency-based selection that applies Discrete Haar Wavelet Transform, sums decomposition results across modalities, inverse-transforms to highlight salient regions, and selects top-f patches. A Hierarchical Masked Aggregation (HMA) module then performs independent per-modality masked encoding followed by cross-modality collaborative encoding to fuse selected tokens. Two auxiliary losses further refine features: Background Consistency Constraint (BCC) enforces MSE alignment of non-selected (background) tokens across modalities to stabilize selection, and Object-Centric Feature Refinement (OCFR) pulls same-ID class tokens toward an exponentially-decayed ID center for tighter intra-class compactness.

## Results

On RGBNT201 (person ReID), EDITOR(B) achieves 65.7% mAP and 68.8% R-1, outperforming the strongest prior multi-modal method TOP-ReID(B) by 1.1% mAP and 4.2% R-1. On RGBNT100 (vehicle ReID), EDITOR(B) reaches 82.1% mAP and 96.4% R-1, surpassing TOP-ReID(B) (81.2% mAP) and CCNet (77.2% mAP). On the smaller, more challenging MSVR310, EDITOR(B) achieves 39.0% mAP and 49.3% R-1, improving over TOP-ReID(B) by 3.1% mAP and over CCNet by 2.6% mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDITOR(B) outperforms the closest competitor TOP-ReID(B) on all three datasets: +1.1% mAP on RGBNT201 (65.7 vs 64.6), +0.9% mAP on RGBNT100 (82.1 vs 81.2), and +3.1% mAP on MSVR310 (39.0 vs 35.9). In the AL setting, EDITOR(A) trails TOP-ReID(A) by 5.8% mAP on RGBNT201 (66.5 vs 72.3) and 6.1% on RGBNT100 (79.8 vs 73.7 is actually a win; correction: 79.8 vs 73.7, so EDITOR(A) wins by 6.1% on RGBNT100), but the paper notes EDITOR(A) is more robust across settings. Against earlier multi-modal methods, EDITOR(B) leads CCNet by 4.9% mAP on RGBNT100 (82.1 vs 77.2) and by 2.6% on MSVR310 (39.0 vs 36.4), and surpasses PHT by 2.2% mAP on RGBNT100 (82.1 vs 79.9). On MSVR310, EDITOR(B) also substantially outperforms the single-modal TransReID (39.0 vs 18.4 mAP), highlighting the benefit of multi-modal fusion in complex visual scenarios. Overall, EDITOR achieves the best mAP in the BL setting across all three benchmarks while using fewer parameters than TOP-ReID.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDITOR builds directly on the TransFG [11] mechanism for spatial-based token selection, reusing its layer-wise attention-weight multiplication to identify object-centric patches, but extends it with a modality-level union and a frequency-based (Haar wavelet) selection channel that prior token-selection work in ReID (e.g., TVTR [46]) did not explore. In the multi-modal ReID lineage (PFNet → IEEE → TOP-ReID), previous methods aggregate global features from all image regions across modalities; EDITOR departs by explicitly selecting and masking object-centric tokens before aggregation, and by introducing two new losses (BCC, OCFR) to suppress background interference during both selection and refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The instance-level, dynamic token selection (variable number of retained tokens per image) is a practical advantage over fixed top-K schemes, and the modality-level union provides a simple yet effective mechanism for cross-modal collaboration that prior multi-modal ReID methods lacked. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (TOP-ReID) are modest (≈1% mAP), and the framework is validated only on three RGB+NIR+TIR benchmarks, leaving open whether the SFTS/HMA pipeline generalizes to other modality combinations (e.g., RGB+depth, RGB+thermal-only) or to larger-scale datasets. The reliance on a shared ViT backbone and the fixed Haar wavelet for frequency selection also raise questions about scalability and whether learned frequency decompositions could further improve selection quality.
