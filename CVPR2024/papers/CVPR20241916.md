## Problem

Traditional Knowledge Distillation methods assume teacher and student operate in the same modality, but Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation (CMKD) requires transferring knowledge from one modality to a different one with inference only on the student modality. The paper empirically identifies two failure factors: modality imbalance (performance disparities between modalities cause the student to lose confidence when imitating a weaker teacher) and soft label misalignment (non-target class rank orders conflict across modalities, making direct KL-divergence distillation counterproductive).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241916/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes paired multimodal samples (e.g., audio clips + video frames, images + text, RGB + depth maps) with shared class labels, but at inference only the distilled student modality is used. For classification tasks (CREMA-D, AVE, VGGSound, CrisisMMD), the output is a single class label; for the NYU-Depth V2 segmentation task, it produces a per-pixel 40-class mask. The teacher modality is fully discarded at test time, which is the practical motivation for the method in sensor-failure or computation-constrained settings.

## Method

C²KD replaces the fixed-teacher paradigm with bidirectional distillation where both teacher and student are jointly updated. The On-the-Fly Selection Distillation (OFSD) strategy gates cross-modal KD using the Kendall Rank Correlation (KRC) between teacher and student logits: samples with KRC below a threshold ω are excluded, and only non-target class distributions are distilled to avoid modality imbalance. Proxy teacher and student heads (a Conv-BN-ReLU adaptation layer plus a classification head) are added to each modality, serving as intermediate bridges that inherit both unimodal and cross-modal knowledge before the final cross-modal transfer occurs. The total loss combines supervision for both modalities, unimodal KD between each modality and its proxy, and KRC-gated cross-modal KD between the two proxies, with all balancing weights set to 1.

## Results

On the four multimodal classification datasets, C²KD (fully updated teacher) achieves top-1 accuracy of 62.8% (Visual) and 61.4% (Audio) on CREMA-D, 34.7% (Visual) and 54.9% (Audio) on AVE, 40.9% (Visual) and 61.9% (Audio) on VGGSound, and 68.8% (Image) and 70.1% (Text) on CrisisMMD. On NYU-Depth V2 semantic segmentation, C²KD reaches 38.1% mIoU for RGB (ResNet-18) and 33.7% mIoU for Depth (MobileNetV2) when combined with DIST loss, surpassing the strongest baseline CIRKD by 0.8 and 0.3 mIoU respectively. The largest gains over the no-KD baseline appear on AVE audio (54.9% vs. 52.8%) and VGGSound audio (61.9% vs. 59.4%), where modality imbalance is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²KD consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across all four classification datasets and the segmentation benchmark. The most dramatic failures of existing methods occur on AVE and VGGSound audio (V→A direction): vanilla KD [16] drops audio accuracy from 52.8% to 46.6% on AVE, and RKD [28] collapses to 44.5%, confirming that relation-based distillation is particularly harmful under soft-label misalignment. Feature-based methods (FitNet [33], Review [7]) also degrade performance, attributed to significant cross-modal feature divergence. Among the strongest baselines, NKD [47] and DIST [17] achieve the best single-modality results (e.g., NKD: 52.2% AVE audio, 59.3% VGGSound audio) but still trail C²KD by 2.7 and 2.6 points respectively. Online KD methods (DML [53], SHAKE [25]) partially mitigate the gap by updating the teacher but remain 1.5–3.0 points behind C²KD on the imbalanced audio direction. Finally, Self-KD methods (DLB [35], ZipfKD [26], USKD [47]) improve over no-KD by ~0.5–1.0 points but are outperformed by C²KD by 1.4–2.6 points, validating that cross-modal transfer provides information beyond what a single modality can self-distill.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the foundational KD framework of Hinton et al. [16] and the target/non-target class decoupling of DKD [54] into the cross-modal setting, where teacher and student operate on different modalities. The proxy (shadow head) architecture is borrowed from SHAKE [25], but repurposed here as a bidirectional bridge that progressively transfers cross-modal knowledge rather than merely bridging offline-to-online distillation. The paper departs from prior cross-modal distillation work (Gupta et al., Roheda et al., Xue et al.) by providing a unified diagnosis of *why* standard KD fails across modalities—modality imbalance and soft label rank misalignment—and a single plug-and-play solution (OFSD + proxies) applicable to arbitrary modality pairs, rather than task-specific heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in sensor-failure or compute-constrained deployments, one must infer on a single modality while training on multimodal data, and the paper shows that naively applying standard KD in this regime can *degrade* the student by 3–6 points. The KRC-based filtering criterion offers a simple, interpretable diagnostic for distillability that could inform broader multimodal learning. Open questions remain: feature-based CMKD is left unexplored (CKA analysis shows severe cross-modal feature divergence), the efficacy of multimodal teachers is inconclusive, and the paper acknowledges that modality-general decisive features (per Xue et al.'s MFH) may be a more fundamental solution. The method is validated on classification and segmentation but not on generative or retrieval tasks.
