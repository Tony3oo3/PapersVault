## Problem

Existing sketch representations each have a critical flaw: raster sketches discard temporal stroke order, raw vector sketches grow linearly with sketch complexity, and learned autoregressive models (e.g., SketchRNN) become slow and inaccurate for complex multi-stroke sketches. No prior representation can compactly encode complex vector sketches at high fidelity while supporting fast parallel decoding and controllable abstraction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-length vector sketch (sequence of xy-coordinates in [0,1] and binary pen states) and compresses it into a fixed-size latent code (e.g., 64- or 512-dimensional). Decoding produces a vector sketch at arbitrary resolution by sampling J timestamps across K strokes, where both J and K are user-controllable at inference time. For conditional generation, a raster sketch is encoded via a ResNet-18 encoder into the latent space; for completion, a partial vector sketch (e.g., the first quarter of timestamps) is used to invert the latent code. Pen states are not directly predicted but derived implicitly from strokestamp boundaries.

## Method

SketchINR represents a sketch as a continuous implicit function f_θ(t, s) that maps a 2D input of normalized time t∈[0,1) and strokestamp s∈[0,1) to (x, y, δ) coordinates, implemented as an 8-layer MLP auto-decoder conditioned on a learned latent code ν_i (following the DeepSDF paradigm). Positional embeddings are applied to both time and stroke inputs to counteract the spectral bias of MLPs toward low-frequency functions. Training uses a combined loss: a visual loss that compares exponential intensity maps (grounding vector output to raster appearance) and a weighted MSE loss on coordinates, with the smoothing parameter γ=150 balancing optimization stability against reconstruction fidelity. For multi-sketch generalization, a single shared decoder is trained on per-sketch latent codes; for generation, a VAE encoder (ResNet-18) maps raster sketches to the latent space with KL regularization. The strokestamp mechanism explicitly controls pen-up/pen-down transitions, enabling reconstruction at arbitrary abstraction levels by varying K at inference without retraining.

## Results

On FS-COCO (complex scene sketches, ~3000 waypoints), SketchINR achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.011 and R@10 of 15.61, dramatically outperforming SketchODE (CD 0.41, R@10 5.17) and BézierSketch (CD 0.57, R@10 2.96), while SketchRNN and CoSE fail entirely. On Sketchy (object-level sketches), SketchINR reaches CD 0.008 and R@10 57.29, surpassing the next-best CoSE (CD 0.14, R@10 45.82) by ~17× in reconstruction fidelity. Decoding speed is also substantially faster: 0.0007 s per sketch on Sketchy versus 0.08–0.13 s for all baselines (~100–180× speedup), and 0.3 s on FS-COCO versus 1.22–1.68 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchINR wins decisively on every reported metric against all four baselines. On FS-COCO, where sketches have ~3000 waypoints and 64 strokes, SketchINR achieves CD 0.011 versus 0.41 (SketchODE) and 0.57 (BézierSketch) — a 37× and 52× improvement in reconstruction fidelity — while SketchRNN and CoSE cannot handle this complexity at all. On Sketchy, SketchINR's CD of 0.008 is 17.5× lower than CoSE (0.14), 20× lower than SketchRNN (0.16), and 29× lower than BézierSketch (0.23). In retrieval (R@10), SketchINR leads with 57.29 on Sketchy, beating CoSE (45.82) by 11.5 points and SketchRNN (41.65) by 15.6 points. Decoding speed is the most dramatic advantage: SketchINR decodes in 0.0007 s on Sketchy, roughly 140–180× faster than the autoregressive baselines (0.10–0.13 s), and 4–5× faster on FS-COCO (0.3 s vs. 1.22–1.68 s). No baseline matches SketchINR on any metric; the closest competitor is CoSE on Sketchy, which still trails by a wide margin in both fidelity and speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

SketchINR transplants the implicit neural representation paradigm (established for 3D shapes via DeepSDF and for images) into the vector sketch domain, where prior work has relied on autoregressive sequence models (SketchRNN), per-stroke Gaussian mixtures (CoSE), Neural ODEs (SketchODE), or parametric curve fitting (BezierSketch). The key architectural departure is conditioning a single MLP decoder on a fixed-size latent code plus positional embeddings over both time and stroke indices, replacing sequential decoding with parallel evaluation. For generative modelling, the paper wraps this decoder in a standard VAE whose encoder maps raster sketches to the latent space, directly adapting the Kingma & Welling framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a compact 64-dim latent plus a shared 8-layer MLP can faithfully encode complex scene sketches (FS-COCO, ~3000 waypoints) with 60× raster and 10× vector compression, the paper opens a practical path toward sketch-specific codecs for large-scale datasets. The ability to control abstraction via stroke count and to perform a-temporal completion (reconstructing the first half from the second) are capabilities unavailable to autoregressive baselines. However, slow convergence from pixel-space optimisation, poor cross-category generalisation (fails beyond 2–3 categories), and the reliance on per-dataset decoder training limit immediate broad adoption; faster implicit function engineering and amortised cross-category encoders are the most pressing open questions.
