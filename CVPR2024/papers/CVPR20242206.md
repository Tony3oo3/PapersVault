## Problem

Transformer-based skeletal action recognition models concentrate attention on a small set of discriminative joints, causing significant accuracy drops under minor skeleton perturbations and when the pose estimator changes between training and testing. Existing methods neither exploit the semantic information carried by unactivated joints nor capture high-level action semantics shared across same-class sequences, limiting both robustness and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D skeleton sequence of T=243 frames and J joints as input and produces an action class label. During training, a second masked pathway receives the same skeleton with the most attention-activated joints replaced by learnable mask tokens (fraction δ=0.7), guided by the attention scores from the last MHSA block. At inference, only the standard pathway is used, so there is no change in model size or inference time relative to the base transformer.

## Method

MaskCLR introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of a shared transformer backbone. First, Attention-Guided Probabilistic Masking (AGPM) converts the last-layer attention scores into a probability distribution via a sharpened softmax (τ_prop<1), then applies the Gumbel-max trick to stochastically select the top-K most activated joints for replacement with learnable mask tokens, forcing the model to rely on a broader set of joints. Second, Multi-Level Contrastive Learning (MLCL) imposes two losses: a sample-level contrastive loss that pulls the standard and masked representations of the same sequence together while pushing apart representations of different sequences, and a class-level contrastive loss that contrasts EMA-updated class prototypes against batch means of True Positive, False Negative, and False Positive samples, with a SegFormer-inspired penalty term that explicitly penalizes confusion between semantically similar classes. Training proceeds in two stages: 300 epochs of standard cross-entropy on the unmasked pathway, followed by 300 epochs with the masked pathway active and the full combined loss (L_ce + α·L_sc + β·L_cc).

## Results

On the DSTFormer backbone, MaskCLR achieves 93.9% top-1 accuracy on NTU60-XSub, 97.3% on NTU60-XView, 87.4% on NTU120-XSub, 89.5% on NTU120-XSet, and 44.7% on Kinetics400, surpassing MotionBERT (same backbone) by up to 5.8 points on Kinetics400. With the vanilla Transformer backbone, MaskCLR reaches 93.5% on NTU60-XSub and 97.5% on NTU60-XView, matching or exceeding MAMP. Under pose-estimator generalization (training on HRNet, testing on ViTPose), MaskCLR improves over MotionBERT by 20.5 points on NTU60-XSub and 26.6 points on NTU120-XSub.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskCLR consistently outperforms MotionBERT (same DSTFormer backbone) across all five benchmarks, with the largest gain of 5.8 points on Kinetics400 (44.7 vs. 38.8) and 3.1 points on NTU120-XSet (89.5 vs. 86.4). Against the strongest GCN-based methods (PoseConv3D, InfoGCN, MS-G3D), MaskCLR with the vanilla Transformer backbone matches or exceeds them on NTU60 (93.5 vs. 93.7 for PoseConv3D on XSub; 97.5 vs. 97.1 for InfoGCN on XView) and clearly leads on NTU120 (90.5 vs. 87.2 for MS-G3D on XSub). On the STTFormer backbone, MaskCLR improves over SkeletonMAE by 3.5 points on NTU60-XSub and 2.5 on NTU60-XView. Under pose-estimator generalization (HRNet→ViTPose), MaskCLR beats MotionBERT by 20.5 points on NTU60-XSub and 26.6 on NTU120-XSub, while all GCN baselines drop to 50–80% range. Under Gaussian noise (σ=0.002), MaskCLR shows only 0.1–1.1% accuracy drop versus 2.9–8.3% for MotionBERT and 9.2–15.3% for PoseConv3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskCLR sits at the intersection of two lines of work: transformer-based skeleton action recognition (MotionBERT, STTFormer, DSTFormer) and contrastive representation learning. It departs from prior contrastive approaches in skeleton recognition (CrossCLR, AimCLR, ActCLR) in three key ways: (1) it uses an adaptive, attention-guided masking strategy rather than fixed sample-wide augmentations, (2) it introduces a class-level contrastive loss that exploits cross-sequence semantic structure beyond individual sample pairs, and (3) it operates in a fully supervised setting rather than as a pre-training stage. The attention-guided probabilistic masking also contrasts with the random masking used in MotionBERT and MAMP, targeting the most activated joints specifically to force exploration of underutilized body parts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment concern that prior work largely ignored: skeleton-based models are brittle to pose estimator changes and small perturbations, yet real-world systems inevitably face distribution shifts in pose quality. The 26.6-point generalization gap on NTU120-XSub (HRNet→ViTPose) reveals that prior SOTA methods were heavily overfitting to a specific pose estimator's error distribution, a finding that should prompt re-evaluation of how robustness is reported in the field. Open questions include whether the class-level contrastive loss (which depends on confident TP samples) remains effective with very small batch sizes or highly imbalanced class distributions, and whether the attention-guided masking strategy transfers to non-transformer backbones or other modalities (e.g., point clouds, video).
