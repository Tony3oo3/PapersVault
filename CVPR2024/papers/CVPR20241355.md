## Problem

Neural Parametric Head Models (NPHMs) can represent high-fidelity full-head geometries with hair, but their over-parametrized latent spaces are severely underconstrained when fitting to noisy, partial monocular depth observations, leading to implausible reconstructions. No existing method effectively regularizes the identity and expression latent optimization to keep solutions on the manifold of plausible head shapes during test-time fitting to commodity depth sensor data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241355/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of monocular depth maps from a single Kinect Azure sensor, back-projected into 3D point clouds with surface normals per frame. It outputs a continuous signed distance field for a full-head avatar (including hair) parameterized by one shared identity latent vector and one expression latent vector per frame, where the expression space uses backward deformations from arbitrary expression poses to canonical space, enabling topology changes (e.g., open mouth) that forward-deformation models cannot capture.

## Method

DPHMs couple a modified NPHM (with backward-deformation expression space) to two latent diffusion models (DDPM with UNet-1D denoisers) trained on over-parametrized identity and expression latent codes from 4,760 high-fidelity head scans. At test time, the diffusion noise prediction ε_φ(x_t, t) is proportional to the negative score function −∇_x log p(x), so subtracting it from the corrupted latent effectively pushes the identity and expression codes back onto the learned data manifold. The full optimization minimizes a composite loss combining SDF zero-crossing and normal-consistency data terms, the diffusion prior regularizers for both identity and expression (applied at a fixed noise level t∈[0.4,0.6]), and a temporal smoothness penalty on consecutive expression latents, solved via three-phase stochastic gradient descent (first-frame joint fit, incremental per-frame expression tracking, then joint fine-tuning).

## Results

On the DPHM-Kinect dataset, DPHMs achieve the best performance across all four metrics: ℓ₂ distance of 1.465 mm, normal consistency of 86.80%, Recall@1.5mm of 70.79%, and Recall@3mm of 90.98%. On the NerSemble single-view depth sequences, DPHMs similarly lead with ℓ₂ = 1.103 mm, NC = 88.73%, RC = 89.96%, and RC2 = 97.20%. The closest competitor is the improved NPHM (with backward deformations), which DPHMs outperform by 0.11 mm in ℓ₂ and 4.84 points in RC on DPHM-Kinect, and by 0.10 mm in ℓ₂ and 5.23 points in RC on NerSemble.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPHMs outperform all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the closest competitor NPHM (improved with backward deformations), DPHMs reduce ℓ₂ error from 1.579 to 1.465 mm and improve Recall@1.5mm from 65.95% to 70.79% on DPHM-Kinect, and from 84.73% to 89.96% on NerSemble. FLAME, a template-based PCA model, achieves moderate ℓ₂ (5.251 mm) but very poor recall (15.80% RC), reflecting its limited capacity for hair and complex expressions. IMAvatar performs worst overall (ℓ₂ = 5.581 mm, RC = 11.30%), producing over-smooth results due to lack of high-quality geometry priors. ImFace and ImFace* (trained on NPHM data) show high ℓ₂ errors (11.0–11.2 mm) because they only model the front face region, though their recall scores are intermediate. The consistent margin over NPHM across both datasets confirms that the diffusion prior regularizer is the key differentiator, constraining latents to the plausible manifold and preventing overfitting to noisy, partial depth observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neural Parametric Head Models (NPHMs) by coupling their over-parameterized SDF-based identity and expression latents with latent diffusion models (DDPM-style) trained on those latents. The key conceptual departure is using the diffusion noise estimator as a score-function regularizer during test-time optimization, pulling identity and expression codes back onto the learned data manifold when fitting to noisy, partial depth observations. It also replaces NPHMs' forward deformation expression space with backward deformations to support topology changes (e.g., open mouth), and contrasts its diffusion prior against VAE-based regularization (à la V-Poser), showing the latter is insufficient for plausible reconstructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: over-parameterized neural implicit head models overfit severely to the sparse, noisy observations from commodity depth sensors, producing implausible geometries. The diffusion-prior regularizer is a clean, generalizable recipe (score-as-gradient) that could transfer to other parametric neural models fitted to incomplete data. However, the method still requires per-sequence test-time optimization (hundreds of iterations per frame), limiting real-time applicability, and the performance gains over the improved NPHM baseline are modest (e.g., 1.579→1.465 mm l2 on Kinect). The DPHM-Kinect dataset with rapid expression transitions is a useful new benchmark, but the broader question of whether diffusion priors can enable real-time, single-pass head tracking from depth remains open.
