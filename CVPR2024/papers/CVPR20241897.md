## Problem

SAM's transformer architecture incurs prohibitive memory and computation costs for edge deployment, yet existing PTQ methods fail on SAM due to two unique challenges: a bimodal distribution in post-Key-Linear activations that inflates quantization error by over 5×, and substantially different post-Softmax distributions across SAM's three attention types (self-attention, token-to-image, and image-to-token cross-attention) that prior uniform quantizers cannot handle. This is the first post-training quantization framework specifically designed for SAM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained SAM variant (B, L, or H) and a minimal calibration set of 32 images (only the first sample is needed for bimodal detection) and outputs a 4-bit or 6-bit quantized SAM model. The quantized SAM then consumes RGB images with box or point prompts to produce binary segmentation masks, which feed downstream tasks: instance segmentation (masks from detector boxes), semantic segmentation (refining SegFormer boundaries), and oriented object detection (rotated bounding boxes via minimum circumscribed rectangle). The framework is plug-and-play, integrating into both statistic-based (OMSE) and learning-based (QDrop) PTQ pipelines without architectural changes.

## Method

PTQ4SAM introduces two complementary components. First, Bimodal Integration (BIG) detects the bimodal distribution in post-Key-Linear activations via Gaussian KDE, computes a per-channel sign factor γ from each channel's mean, and equivalently absorbs γ into both the query and key linear weights offline (W′ = W ⊙ γ), collapsing the two peaks into a single normal distribution at zero runtime cost. Second, Adaptive Granularity Quantization (AGQ) replaces the fixed-base Log2 quantizer for post-Softmax activations with an adaptive base 2^(1/τ), where τ ∈ {1, 2, 4} is selected by minimizing the Frobenius-norm error of the attention-value matrix product (AV − Â_τ V) across the calibration set, rather than the local attention-score error. Hardware efficiency is preserved by restricting τ to powers of two, enabling bit-shift operations plus a single 4KB lookup table for the residual fractional exponent, making the scheme deployable on FPGA and similar accelerators.

## Results

On COCO instance segmentation with the DINO detector, PTQ4SAM-L at W6A6 achieves 40.4% mAP on SAM-B (vs. 44.5% FP), 48.3% on SAM-L (vs. 48.6% FP), and 48.7% on SAM-H (vs. 49.1% FP), with only 0.1–0.4% degradation. At the more challenging W4A4 setting, PTQ4SAM-L still reaches 43.9% mAP on SAM-L, surpassing the next-best method (QDrop at 41.7%) by 2.2%. The framework delivers 3.9× theoretical FLOPs acceleration and 4.9× storage reduction at 6-bit while maintaining near-lossless accuracy on SAM-L and SAM-H.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTQ4SAM-L (learning-based) is nearly lossless at W6A6, dropping only 0.1–0.4% mAP versus full precision across all SAM variants with DINO, while QDrop—the strongest prior method—loses 0.3–0.8% and AdaRound/BRECQ lose 2.0–2.6%. At W4A4, PTQ4SAM-L achieves 43.9% mAP on SAM-L with DINO, outperforming QDrop (41.7%) by 2.2% and AdaRound (18.2%) by 25.7%; AdaRound and BRECQ become essentially infeasible (mAP < 1) on SAM-B at W4A4. The statistic-based variant PTQ4SAM-S also surpasses QDrop at W6A6 on SAM-L (47.7 vs. 47.5 with DINO; 35.7 vs. 35.2 with Faster R-CNN), demonstrating that the BIG+AGQ components alone provide substantial gains even without learned reconstruction. MinMax, Percentile, and OMSE suffer catastrophic degradation (mAP drops of 28–33 points at W6A6 on SAM-B), confirming that standard calibration strategies are inadequate for SAM's bimodal and diverse post-Softmax distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Log2 quantization framework (Miyashita et al., 2016) by introducing an adaptive base parameter τ, and builds on the general PTQ pipeline (statistic-based OMSE, learning-based QDrop/AdaRound/BRECQ) by identifying two SAM-specific distributional challenges absent in prior ViT quantization work. It departs from prior ViT softmax quantization methods (RepQViT, FQ-ViT, Log-Int-Softmax) that treated all attention mechanisms uniformly, whereas SAM's self-attention and two-way cross-attention produce markedly different post-Softmax distributions. The Bimodal Integration strategy draws inspiration from channel-wise asymmetry handling in RepQViT and Outlier Suppression+ but applies it to a different location (post-Key-Linear rather than post-LayerNorm) with a mathematically equivalent sign absorption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM's deployment on edge devices has been a major bottleneck, and this paper demonstrates that 6-bit quantization can achieve lossless accuracy with 3.9× theoretical acceleration, making practical SAM deployment significantly more feasible. The plug-and-play design (compatible with both statistic-based and learning-based PTQ backends) broadens its applicability. A key open question the authors explicitly flag is why the bimodal distribution arises in post-Key-Linear activations in the first place, which remains unexplained. The method's generalizability beyond SAM to other multi-attention transformer architectures is not explored, leaving an open direction for future work.
