## Problem

Existing Shape-from-Template methods for cloth reconstruction either rely on isometric or conformal deformation models that produce noisy, unphysical results (since real cloth stretches and shears), or use classical physics simulation that is too slow for practical use (φ-SfT requires 17–24 hours per scene). No prior work has employed an unsupervised neural surrogate model to accelerate the physics-based SfT optimization loop while retaining physical plausibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242626/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video sequence, a template mesh remeshed to a regular 32×32 grid, known camera intrinsics, and a texture extracted from the first video frame. It produces a temporally coherent 3D cloth mesh sequence, per-scene physical parameters (stretching stiffness Y, shearing stiffness S, bending stiffness B), spatially and temporally varying external forces (constant component w plus turbulent component T), and an optimized UV-map that corrects for perspective and occlusion artifacts in the initial texture.

## Method

The core contribution is a UNet-based CNN that replaces the classical backward Euler cloth solver: it maps a rectangular grid of vertex positions, velocities, stiffness parameters (Y, S, B), and external forces to the next-step acceleration, making the simulation both fast and naturally differentiable via backpropagation through time. The network is trained self-supervised with a physics-based loss (internal Hookean stretching/shearing/bending energy + external-force alignment + inertia penalty) whose gradient condition implicitly enforces Newton's second law, eliminating the need for ground-truth simulation data. At inference, the pre-trained surrogate unrolls the cloth simulation, nvdiffrast renders textured images and silhouettes, and a combined image + silhouette + force-regularization loss drives gradient-based optimization of all physical parameters, external forces, and UV-coordinates. A successive frame-adding schedule (starting with 10 frames, adding one every 5 optimization cycles) stabilizes convergence, yielding a 400–500× speedup over φ-SfT with comparable Chamfer distance.

## Results

On the φ-SfT dataset (6 scenes), the method achieves a symmetric Chamfer distance (×10⁴) ranging from 6.9 (R7) to 14.5 (R4), with φ-SfT winning on 4 of 6 scenes (e.g., 7.9 vs. 12.5 on R3; 2.9 vs. 8.6 on R9) while the neural surrogate wins on R5 (11.7 vs. 14.4) and R7 (6.9 vs. 9.1). The headline advantage is runtime: optimization converges in 2.5–3.1 minutes per scene versus 17.7–24.2 hours for φ-SfT, yielding a 380×–585× speedup. Stability testing over 10,000 epochs confirms the reconstruction does not diverge, whereas φ-SfT often becomes unstable under extended optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is φ-SfT [Kairanda et al., CVPR 2022], the state-of-the-art physics-based SfT method that uses a classical differentiable cloth simulation. On reconstruction accuracy (symmetric Chamfer distance), φ-SfT outperforms the neural surrogate on 4 of 6 scenes (R3, R4, R8, R9), with the largest gaps on R8 (3.7 vs. 10.1) and R9 (2.9 vs. 8.6, ×10⁴). The neural surrogate wins on R5 (11.7 vs. 14.4) and R7 (6.9 vs. 9.1), where complex folding and manual movement are present. The decisive advantage is runtime: the neural surrogate completes optimization in 2.5–3.1 minutes per scene compared to 17.7–24.2 hours for φ-SfT, a 380×–585× speedup, while maintaining comparable visual quality and superior long-term stability (no divergence after 10,000 epochs, whereas φ-SfT often collapses under extended optimization).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the φ-SfT framework (Kairanda et al., CVPR 2022), which established the pipeline of differentiable physics simulation + differentiable rendering + gradient-based optimization for cloth reconstruction from monocular video. The key departure is replacing the computationally expensive classical cloth solver with a pre-trained neural surrogate (a UNet-based CNN) that learns to solve the backward-Euler implicit scheme, drawing on self-supervised physics-based training strategies from PBNS [3] and SNUG [40] and the iterative pool-based training cycle from HOOD [14]. The authors also swap PyTorch3D for nvdiffrast as the differentiable rasterizer and introduce UV-coordinate optimization to reduce texture-mismatch artifacts, neither of which were part of φ-SfT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400–500× runtime reduction (from ~20 hours to ~3 minutes per scene) makes physics-regularized SfT practically usable for iterative or interactive applications, while maintaining reconstruction quality comparable to the state-of-the-art classical approach. However, the method does not surpass φ-SfT in Chamfer distance on most scenes (losing in 4 of 6), and it struggles with fine wrinkles and high-frequency wind-driven motion due to the fixed 32×32 grid resolution. Open questions include whether higher-resolution grids or alternative energy formulations can close the accuracy gap, and whether the UV-warping artifacts can be mitigated without sacrificing the precision gains that UV optimization provides.
