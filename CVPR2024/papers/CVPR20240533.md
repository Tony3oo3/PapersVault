## Problem

Absolute pose regression (APR) networks encode scene geometry implicitly in their weights, requiring days of novel view synthesis per scene to generate sufficient training data, and still achieve lower accuracy than geometry-based relocalization methods. The paper asks whether a pose regressor can be made scene-agnostic—trained once across hundreds of scenes—while retaining the explicit 3D geometric reasoning that gives correspondence-based methods their centimeter-level accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240533/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method consumes only an RGB image (shortest side 480 px) and the 3×3 camera intrinsic matrix K; no depth, point cloud, or pre-built map is required. The scene-specific geometry network G_S outputs a dense per-pixel 3D scene coordinate map at 8× downsampled resolution, which is then fed to the transformer. The final output is a 10-dimensional vector unpacked into a 3×3 rotation matrix (from a 6D two-axis representation) and a 3D translation (from 4 homogeneous coordinates), yielding a full 6-DoF metric pose in the scene's coordinate frame.

## Method

The architecture couples a fast-training scene-specific CNN (ACE) that regresses per-pixel 3D scene coordinates with a scene-agnostic 12-block linear-attention transformer (d_model=256, 8 heads) that maps those coordinates to a camera pose. The key innovation is a Dynamic Positional Encoding that fuses a camera-aware 2D embedding—computed from ray directions derived from the intrinsics, making the transformer invariant to calibration—with a 3D sinusoidal embedding of the scene coordinates, so the network learns a generic image-to-pose relationship independent of any particular scene. A Re-Attention mechanism injects residual connections every four transformer blocks to counteract information degradation in deep stacks. Training uses an L1 pose loss with auxiliary supervision at intermediate transformer blocks, plus online augmentation (±1 m jitter, up to 180° rotation) to prevent the regressor from memorizing absolute poses. The transformer is trained once on ~500 K frames from 450 outdoor scenes, and can optionally be fine-tuned per scene in 1–10 minutes for further accuracy gains.

## Results

On the indoor 7-Scenes dataset, marepo achieves a median translation/rotation error of 3.9 cm / 1.68° on average, outperforming the best prior APR method (LENS at 8 cm / 3.00°) by approximately 50%, while requiring only 5 minutes of per-scene training versus days for competing methods. The fine-tuned variant marepo_S further improves to 3.2 cm / 1.54° in under 15 minutes. On the outdoor Wayspots dataset, marepo reaches 47.2% / 63.4% of frames within 10 cm / 5° and 0.5 m / 5° respectively, matching or exceeding geometry-based SCR methods (ACE: 52.2% / 62.6%) while running at 55.6 fps versus 17.9 fps for SCR baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 7-Scenes, marepo (3.9 cm avg) outperforms all prior APR methods by a wide margin: LENS (8 cm), DFNet-EB0 (8 cm), MS-Transformer (18 cm), MapNet (21 cm), and PoseNet (44 cm), representing roughly a 2× improvement over the best prior APR. The fine-tuned marepo_S (3.2 cm) approaches the accuracy of the best SCR method ACE (2.8 cm) while remaining a single feed-forward pass without RANSAC. On Wayspots, marepo (47.2% within 10 cm/5°) is competitive with ACE (52.2%) and DSAC* (50.7%) at the 0.5 m threshold (63.4% vs 62.6%), while dramatically outperforming PoseNet (5.3%) and MS-Transformer (1.9%). Inference throughput favors marepo at 55.6 fps versus 17.9 fps for both SCR baselines, since no iterative pose solver is needed. The per-scene training ablation (Table 5) confirms that training the full network from scratch per scene yields only 0.7% within 10 cm/5°, validating the scene-agnostic pre-training strategy as essential to the method's success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces map-relative pose regression (marepo) as a new formulation within the absolute pose regression (APR) family, departing from both standard APR (which trains a dedicated per-scene network over hours or days) and scene coordinate regression (SCR) methods (which require a separate RANSAC/PnP stage). It builds directly on ACE [6] for the fast-training scene-specific geometry predictor G_S and adapts LoFTR's [50] sinusoidal positional encoding into a camera-aware dynamic positional encoding that embeds intrinsics into the transformer input. Conceptually, the key departure is conditioning a scene-agnostic transformer on scene-specific 3D coordinates rather than raw pixels, effectively injecting explicit geometric reasoning into what was previously a purely 2D feed-forward regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: mapping time drops from hours or days (required by prior APR methods) to approximately 5 minutes per scene, while achieving accuracy comparable to geometry-based methods that rely on iterative solvers. This makes real-time, end-to-end relocalization feasible in rapidly changing environments where re-mapping is frequent. Limitations include the continued need for a scene-specific G_S (though fast), and on the challenging Wayspots outdoor dataset, marepo does not uniformly outperform ACE or DSAC* on every scene. Open questions include scalability to much larger or more structurally complex scenes, and whether the approach degrades under significant appearance changes beyond what the 5-minute G_S training can capture.
