## Problem

Existing super-resolution models adopt a bottom-up, pixel-level approach that focuses on local texture restoration while neglecting global semantic understanding of the scene. This leads to omission of semantically critical details or introduction of erroneous textures, particularly in severely degraded real-world images. The paper argues that SR should mimic the top-down cognitive process of human experts who first comprehend the scene before restoring fine details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241863/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (128×128 for 4× super-resolution) and produces a 512×512 high-resolution image. Internally, it generates an intermediate semantically aligned reference image via Stable Diffusion, which is then encoded into latent control features alongside the LR input. The cognitive embedding (50 learnable tokens in CLIP language space) serves as a bridge between the image appearance and the text-to-image diffusion prior, enabling both implicit and explicit conditioning without requiring user-provided text prompts.

## Method

CoSeR introduces a three-component pipeline built on Stable Diffusion 2.1-base. First, a cognitive encoder (CLIP image encoder + a Q-Former-inspired adapter with 50 learnable queries) extracts a multi-token cognitive embedding from the LR image, supervised by CLIP language embeddings from BLIP2-generated captions to align image and language representations while retaining fine-grained visual features. Second, this embedding is fed directly into Stable Diffusion to generate a high-fidelity reference image, which is encoded via VQGAN and a shared ControlNet encoder into multiscale control features. Third, a novel All-in-Attention (AiA) module integrates all three conditional sources—LR control, reference control, and cognitive embedding—into every attention layer of the denoising U-Net's middle and decoder blocks, using dedicated LR attention (Q from U-Net, K/V from LR control), reference attention (Q from LR control, K/V from reference control), and standard cross-attention (K/V from cognitive embedding), with a one-hot attention variant to counteract blurring in reference-based SR.

## Results

CoSeR achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks (ImageNet Test2000, RealSR, DRealSR) on nearly all six perceptual metrics. On ImageNet Test2000, CoSeR attains FID 19.41, DISTS 0.1482, LPIPS 0.2863, CLIP-Score 0.8755, MANIQA 0.2133, and MUSIQ 65.51. The FID advantage over the second-best method (StableSR) is 13.8% on ImageNet Test2000, 3.8% on RealSR, and 4.7% on DRealSR. The only metric where CoSeR does not lead is MUSIQ on ImageNet Test2000, where FeMaSR scores 67.20 vs. CoSeR's 65.51, though the authors attribute this to FeMaSR's unrealistic artifacts not being penalized by the non-reference metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSeR outperforms all seven baselines on FID, DISTS, LPIPS, CLIP-Score, and MANIQA across all three datasets. The closest competitor is StableSR [53], which is the strongest diffusion-based SR method; CoSeR improves FID by 13.8% (19.41 vs. 22.53) on ImageNet Test2000 and by 3.8–4.7% on the real-world benchmarks. FeMaSR [7] is the only method that beats CoSeR on any metric, achieving a higher MUSIQ (67.20 vs. 65.51) on ImageNet Test2000, but the authors note this is due to unrealistic artifacts that the non-reference metric fails to penalize. Among GAN-based methods, Real-ESRGAN+ [59] and BSRGAN [73] are substantially outperformed, with CoSeR reducing FID from 32.68/41.11 to 19.41 on ImageNet Test2000. The raw LDM (Stable Diffusion) [48] baseline shows that simply applying a diffusion model without SR-specific conditioning yields poor fidelity (FID 34.54), highlighting the importance of the All-in-Attention design. A user study with 23 subjects on 20 real-world images confirmed that ~80% of participants preferred CoSeR's output over Real-ESRGAN+, FeMaSR, and StableSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSeR sits at the intersection of diffusion-based SR (StableSR, DiffBIR) and reference-based SR (MASA-SR, TTSR), but departs from both by autonomously generating reference images via a cognitive embedding rather than relying on manual references or purely implicit diffusion priors. The cognitive adapter draws on the Q-Former architecture from BLIP2 and CLIP's image-language alignment, but replaces full captioning with a lightweight multi-token embedding (3% of BLIP2's parameters) that preserves fine-grained spatial information. The All-in-Attention module extends ControlNet-style conditioning by unifying LR, reference, and cognitive controls in a single attention mechanism, moving beyond the SFT-based conditioning used in StableSR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoSeR demonstrates that a lightweight cognitive bridge between image appearance and language understanding can simultaneously activate diffusion priors and produce high-fidelity reference images, reducing the need for manual reference curation in real-world SR. The approach is computationally heavier than GAN-based SR due to the diffusion sampling step (200 timesteps), which limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the cognitive embedding generalizes to higher magnification factors (beyond 4×), whether the reference generation step can be distilled for efficiency, and whether the All-in-Attention design transfers to other conditional generation tasks beyond SR.
