## Problem

Pretrained diffusion models inherit and amplify demographic biases from their training data, generating imbalanced content across attributes like gender and race. Existing debiasing approaches either require costly model retraining or a balanced reference dataset, making them impractical for large text-to-image models. This paper asks whether a pretrained diffusion model can be steered at inference time to produce images matching a user-specified attribute distribution, without any retraining of the generative model itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained diffusion model (unconditional P2 or text-conditional Stable Diffusion v1.5), a user-defined reference attribute distribution p_ref^a (e.g., 50/50 gender split), and a batch of N noise samples (N=100 optimal) as input, optionally conditioned on a text prompt. It outputs a batch of generated images whose aggregate attribute distribution closely matches p_ref^a. The guidance operates on intermediate U-Net bottleneck features (h-space) rather than pixel space, and the Attribute Distribution Predictor is a single linear layer conditioned on the diffusion timestep, making it lightweight and fast to train on as few as 2K pseudo-labeled examples.

## Method

The core contribution is Distribution Guidance, which replaces per-sample classifier guidance with batch-level distribution matching: during reverse diffusion, the h-space features of an entire batch are passed through a linear Attribute Distribution Predictor (ADP) to estimate the batch's attribute distribution, and a chi-square loss against the reference distribution is backpropagated to update those features before the U-Net decoder produces the noise prediction. The key insight is that h-space features are semantically rich enough that a simple linear head achieves high attribute classification accuracy, eliminating the need for expensive image-space classifiers at each noise level. Unlike sample guidance, which forces each individual sample toward a preset attribute (causing distortion for samples far from the decision boundary), distribution guidance only constrains the aggregate batch distribution, allowing the model to preferentially transform samples near the decision boundary and preserving generation quality. The ADP is trained with pseudo-labels from pretrained attribute classifiers applied to DDIM-inverted h-space representations, requiring no access to ground-truth labels or model retraining.

## Results

On single-attribute debiasing (CelebA-HQ, P2 diffusion model), H-Distribution Guidance achieves FD of 0.049 (Gender), 0.113 (Race), and 0.014 (Eyeglasses) with FIDs of 50.27, 52.38, and 51.78 respectively, substantially outperforming random sampling (FD 0.178/0.334/0.251) across all three attributes. On Stable Diffusion v1.5, the method reduces gender FD from 0.317 (random) to 0.024, and achieves the best FD for profession-biased prompts (Doctor: 0.015, Firefighter: 0.093) while maintaining competitive FIDs (~67–70). For multi-attribute debiasing, H-Distribution Guidance achieves FD of 0.075 (Gender+Race), 0.101 (Eyeglasses+Race), and 0.057 (Gender+Eyeglasses), consistently the best among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Universal Guidance [1], H-Distribution Guidance wins on FD for all three attributes (e.g., Gender: 0.049 vs. 0.127 with 30k training data; Eyeglasses: 0.014 vs. 0.051) and on FID for Eyeglasses (51.78 vs. 78.57), though Universal Guidance (30k) achieves a marginally lower FID for Gender (48.94 vs. 50.27). Against Latent Editing [21], the proposed method trades a small FD/FID gap on Gender (0.049/50.27 vs. 0.001/37.40) for decisive wins on Race (FD 0.113 vs. 0.214) and Eyeglasses (FD 0.014 vs. 0.330, FID 51.78 vs. 75.04); the paper notes Latent Editing produces visible artifacts and collapsed images qualitatively. Against MAGNET [13] (StyleGAN2-based), H-Distribution Guidance dominates on both FD and FID across all attributes, though the comparison is not directly apples-to-apples due to different base architectures. On Stable Diffusion, H-Distribution Guidance outperforms ITI-Gen [57] on FD for all three prompts (Gender: 0.024 vs. 0.049; Doctor: 0.015 vs. 0.072; Firefighter: 0.093 vs. 0.184) with comparable FIDs, and beats Fair Diffusion [9] on FD for Gender (0.024 vs. 0.227) and Doctor (0.015 vs. 0.035), though Fair Diffusion achieves a lower FD for Firefighter (0.036 vs. 0.093).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier guidance framework of Dhariwal & Nichol (NeurIPS 2021) from per-sample attribute enforcement to batch-level distribution matching, introducing 'distribution guidance' as a more flexible alternative to 'sample guidance.' It repurposes the semantically rich h-space bottleneck features identified by Kwon et al. (2022) for attribute classification, replacing the need for image-space classifiers trained at each noise level with a single linear head. The approach departs from prior debiasing methods that require model retraining or reference datasets by operating entirely at inference time on a frozen pretrained diffusion model, positioning itself as a practical plug-in for large text-to-image systems where fine-tuning is infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a lightweight, retraining-free debiasing pipeline that is particularly relevant for large text-to-image models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) where retraining is computationally prohibitive. The conceptual distinction between distribution guidance and sample guidance—showing that batch-level flexibility preserves generation quality while still achieving fairness—provides a useful design principle for future guidance-based conditioning. Key limitations include the need to train h-space classifiers (albeit with minimal data) and reliance on accurate attribute classifiers for pseudo-labels; extending the approach to settings without any labeled data or attribute classifiers remains an open question, as does generalizing beyond demographic attributes to broader controlled generation tasks.
