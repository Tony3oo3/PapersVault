## Problem

Existing monocular 3D object detectors are designed for either indoor or outdoor scenes, and the sole prior attempt at unified detection (Cube RCNN) suffers from severe convergence instability and training collapse. The BEV detection paradigm, which explicitly aligns image features with 3D space, has not been successfully generalized to handle the diverse geometry properties (close-range small objects vs. far-range sparse objects) and heterogeneous domain distributions (real/synthetic, varying label spaces) present in unified detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240567/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image (640×512 or 1280×1024 depending on compute budget) from any of six sub-datasets spanning indoor, outdoor, real, and synthetic domains, and outputs 3D bounding boxes with center, dimensions, and orientation. The BEV feature covers a perception range of (−30, 30) × (−40, 40) × (0, 80) meters with a (60, 80) grid resolution, and the detector uses M=100 proposal queries plus N randomly initialized queries for the second-stage Transformer decoding.

## Method

UniMODE employs a two-stage BEV detection architecture: a CenterNet-style proposal head first estimates rough 3D target positions, which are encoded as proposal queries to initialize the 6 Transformer decoders, preventing the reference-point collapse that plagues single-stage BEV detectors on mixed indoor/outdoor data. An uneven BEV grid design uses a quadratic depth-bin split (Eq. 1) so that grids near the camera are finer for small indoor objects while grids far away are coarser for the 100 m outdoor range, without increasing total grid count. A sparse BEV feature projection discards 82.6% of projection points whose depth confidence falls below a threshold τ=1e-3, cutting projection cost while preserving accuracy. For domain heterogeneity, a Domain Adaptive Layer Normalization (DALN) module computes input-dependent affine parameters from a shared domain head to align feature distributions across domains, and a class alignment loss down-weights penalties for classes absent from a given sub-dataset's label space.

## Results

On the Omni3D benchmark (unified indoor+outdoor detection), UniMODE with ConvNext-Base backbone achieves 28.2% AP₃D, surpassing the previous SOTA Cube RCNN (23.3%) by 4.9% absolute. In the indoor-only setting, UniMODE* reaches 26.9% AP₃D_in vs. Cube RCNN's 15.0% (+11.9%), and in the outdoor-only setting it achieves 41.0% AP₃D_out vs. 31.9% (+9.1%). With the lighter DLA34 backbone, UniMODE still leads all baselines at 25.5% AP₃D. Inference speed is 21.41 FPS (ConvNext-Base) and 43.48 FPS (DLA34) on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMODE outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Cube RCNN [5] (the only prior unified monocular 3D detector), UniMODE* gains +4.9% AP₃D overall, +11.9% AP₃D_in (indoor), and +9.1% AP₃D_out (outdoor). The two popular BEV detectors, BEVFormer [15] and PETR [18], fail to converge at all in the unified indoor+outdoor setting (marked '×' in Table 1), despite achieving 25.9% and 27.8% AP₃D_out when trained on outdoor data alone—highlighting the difficulty of unifying diverse geometry properties. Among camera-view baselines, PGD [33] (22.9% AP₃D_out) and SMOKE [19] (19.5% AP₃D_out) are the strongest outdoor-only methods but do not report indoor results. ImVoxelNet [28] is the only other method reporting both indoor (30.6% AP₃D_sun) and outdoor (21.5% AP₃D_out) scores, yet its overall AP₃D is only 9.4%, far below UniMODE. The ablation (Table 3) confirms the proposal head contributes the largest single gain (+3.6% AP₃D), while the sparse BEV feature projection reduces computation by 82.6% with negligible accuracy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BEV detection paradigm (BEVFormer, PETR) to the unified indoor-outdoor monocular 3D detection setting, where the sole prior work Cube RCNN (Omni3D) operates in the camera view. It departs from the classical single-stage Deformable DETR architecture by introducing a two-stage design with a CenterNet-style proposal head to stabilize convergence across heterogeneous geometry. The domain alignment strategy (DALN) is conceptually related to spatially-adaptive normalization but differs by sharing a single domain head across all layers rather than regressing per-layer parameters, and the uneven BEV grid is mathematically similar to CaDDN's depth bin discretization but serves the opposite purpose (balancing feature density rather than concentrating it).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a BEV detector can successfully generalize to unified 3D object detection, opening the door to applying the broader BEV ecosystem (planning, tracking, multi-sensor fusion) to cross-scene robotics. The finding that strong outdoor BEV detectors (BEVFormer, PETR) fail entirely to converge when indoor data is added highlights a non-trivial barrier that the proposed two-stage architecture and uneven grid specifically address. The 82.6% projection cost reduction is practically significant for deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that zero-shot generalization to truly unseen scenarios remains limited, and the cross-domain evaluation (Table 7) shows substantial performance drops on unseen sub-datasets, leaving open the question of whether unified detection can scale to genuinely novel environments.
