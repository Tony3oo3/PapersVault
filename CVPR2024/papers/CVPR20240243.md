## Problem

Existing concept attribution methods for generative AI rely on visual correlation (e.g., contrastive embeddings) between synthetic and training images, but similarity does not establish causation and can falsely match images absent from the training set. No prior work provides a proactive, causative mechanism to trace a generated image back to the specific training concepts that influenced its creation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240243/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single synthetic RGB image (256×256) produced by the watermarked diffusion model and a lookup table of N predefined 160-bit concept sequences. A pretrained RoSteALS secret decoder extracts a predicted 160-bit sequence from the image, which is matched by Hamming distance to the nearest concept sequence, yielding one or two attribution labels. For multi-concept attribution, the image is split horizontally into two halves, each carrying an independent watermark, so two bit-sequences are predicted simultaneously.

## Method

ProMark proactively embeds orthogonal, content-independent spatial watermarks (derived from 160-bit secrets via RoSteALS, averaged over 100 random images to remove content bias) into training images before diffusion model training. The LDM denoising module is trained with a combined objective: the standard denoising loss plus an auxiliary binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss that forces the decoded generated image to preserve the correct watermark bit-sequence, with α=2 weighting. This BCE supervision is critical—removing it drops attribution accuracy from 100% to 2%. At inference, the generated image is passed through the LDM autoencoder decoder and then the frozen RoSteALS secret decoder to recover the bit-sequence, which is matched to the closest concept by counting identical bits. The approach supports up to 2^16 orthogonal concepts and multi-concept attribution by partitioning the image into halves, each carrying a distinct watermark.

## Results

On the unconditional diffusion model evaluation across five datasets, ProMark at 100% watermark strength achieves 100% attribution accuracy on Stock, LSUN, Wiki-A, and Wiki-S, and 91.07% on ImageNet. At a more practical 30% watermark strength (to preserve image quality), ProMark still reaches 100%, 95.12%, 97.45%, 98.12%, and 83.06% respectively, outperforming all five baselines on every dataset. For multi-concept attribution on the BAM dataset, ProMark achieves 90.12% combined accuracy (media + content) at full strength, compared to 46.23% for the best baseline (AbC-CLIP). On the conditional model (ImageNet), ProMark attains 90.13% accuracy on newly sampled images, versus 50.19% for AbC-CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMark outperforms all five baselines (ALADIN, CLIP, AbC-CLIP, SSCD, EKILA) on every dataset and at both watermark strengths. The margins are most dramatic on style and artist attribution: on Wiki-S (styles), ProMark at 30% strength reaches 98.12% versus 60.84% for CLIP and 60.43% for AbC-CLIP, a gap of roughly 37 percentage points. On Wiki-A (artists), ProMark achieves 97.45% versus 77.58% for CLIP, a ~20-point advantage. On ImageNet (objects), ProMark at 30% strength scores 83.06% versus 62.83% for AbC-CLIP, the strongest baseline there. On Stock (near-duplicate templates), the gap is smaller (100% vs. 99.86% for ALADIN) because the passive methods already perform well on highly similar images. For multi-concept attribution on BAM, ProMark's 90.12% combined accuracy nearly doubles the best baseline (AbC-CLIP at 46.23%), demonstrating that the causal watermark approach scales to simultaneous attribution of multiple concepts where correlation-based methods struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProMark departs from the dominant passive attribution paradigm (EKILA, AbC, ALADIN, SSCD, CLIP), which approximates causation through visual correlation in contrastively trained embedding spaces, by instead embedding orthogonal watermarks directly into training data and training the diffusion model to propagate them into generated images. The method repurposes the proactive watermarking transformation framework from prior works on manipulation detection and deepfake tagging (Asnani et al.) and the RoSteALS steganographic encoder/decoder as the watermark mechanism, while the generative backbone is standard LDM. The key architectural novelty is the auxiliary BCE loss that explicitly supervises the denoising network to retain the correct bit-sequence watermark, a supervision signal absent from both prior attribution methods and standard diffusion training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal-vs-correlation distinction is practically significant: correlation-based methods can attribute a generated image to training data that was never actually used, undermining legal or compensation decisions. ProMark's near-perfect accuracy (100% at full strength, 83–98% at 30% strength) across five diverse datasets, combined with support for up to 2^16 concepts and multi-concept attribution, makes it a credible tool for creative provenance. Key limitations include the quality-accuracy trade-off (FID rises from 13.28 to 17.63), the requirement for predefined concepts (no mechanism for introducing new ones post-training), and evaluation restricted to LDM. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other generative architectures, how to handle interrelated concepts that violate the orthogonality assumption, and whether learned (rather than fixed) watermarks could close the quality gap.
