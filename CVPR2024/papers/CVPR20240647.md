## Problem

Real-world low-light images suffer from both underexposure and sensor-specific noise, yet existing enhancement methods either depend on carefully curated paired/unpaired training data (impractical given the diversity of noise across devices and scenes) or are dataset-independent but neglect noise reduction. No prior method achieves joint denoising and enhancement of noisy low-light images in a truly zero-shot manner without requiring any training data or prior knowledge of the noise distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single noisy low-light RGB image (no clean reference, no paired data, no noise statistics) and produces a denoised, brightness-normalized, and contrast-enhanced RGB image. Training is performed per-image: the input is downsampled via a 2×2 diagonal/anti-diagonal averaging operation into two half-resolution views that serve as self-supervised training pairs, so no external dataset is needed. The network is a compact 11-layer CNN (48 or 64 kernels, 3×3) trained end-to-end for 2000 epochs with batch size 1.

## Method

ZERO-IG decomposes the problem into three sequentially connected subnetworks—LD-Net (preliminary denoising), IE-Net (illumination estimation), and RD-Net (reflection denoising)—all trained end-to-end via ZS-N2N-style self-supervision. The IE-Net estimates illumination from the preliminarily denoised image, constraining it by both a global brightness coefficient (scaling to a target mean luminance of 0.5) and a per-pixel gamma-like term that adaptively amplifies darker pixels more than brighter ones, preventing under-enhancement and overexposure. The key architectural insight is that the RD-Net receives the concatenation of the noisy reflection R = I ∘ S̃⁻¹ with the illumination S̃, and is trained to output a concatenation of the clean reflection R̂ with the same S̃; an illumination-consistency loss (‖S − S̃‖²) enforces that the illumination remains unchanged, which algebraically guarantees that denoising R in the brightened domain is equivalent to denoising the original low-light image I while preserving its original noise characteristics. Additional losses (interactive denoising via structural-similarity-guided local averaging, local variance minimization, and Gaussian-blur color fidelity) further refine the RD-Net output.

## Results

On SIDD-Small, ZERO-IG achieves the best PSNR of 18.98 dB and SSIM of 0.6253, outperforming the strongest supervised method (KinD++, 16.55 dB) and the best unsupervised method (PairLIE, 17.03 dB). On LOL, ZERO-IG reaches 22.18 dB PSNR and 0.7719 SSIM, surpassing all unsupervised baselines (best: PairLIE at 18.47 dB) though trailing the top supervised methods (SNR-aware at 24.70 dB). On LSRW (Huawei and Nikon), ZERO-IG achieves 19.84 dB and 16.62 dB PSNR respectively, beating all competing methods across both camera brands, demonstrating cross-device generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZERO-IG outperforms all eight LLIE baselines (four supervised: URetinexNet, LLFlow, SNR-aware, KinD++; four unsupervised: SCI, ZeroDCE++, PairLIE, RUAS) on SIDD and both LSRW subsets in PSNR and SSIM. On SIDD, ZERO-IG leads the best supervised method KinD++ by 2.43 dB PSNR and the best unsupervised method PairLIE by 1.95 dB. On LOL, ZERO-IG (22.18 dB) trails the top supervised methods SNR-aware (24.70 dB) and LLFlow (24.06 dB) by ~2.5 dB, but exceeds all unsupervised methods by at least 3.7 dB. On LSRW-Huawei, ZERO-IG beats the closest competitor PairLIE by 0.85 dB; on LSRW-Nikon it leads SNR-aware by 0.68 dB. Cascade denoising baselines (ZS-N2N and BM3D applied before or after enhancement) fail to match ZERO-IG's joint approach, either over-smoothing details or leaving residual noise, as shown in Figure 7.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZERO-IG sits at the intersection of Retinex-based low-light image enhancement (building on Land & McCann's decomposition into reflection and illumination) and zero-shot denoising (adopting ZS-N2N's downsampling-based training paradigm). It departs from the dominant cascade paradigm—where denoising and enhancement are treated as separate sequential stages—by using illumination as a computational bridge that couples the two tasks end-to-end. Unlike prior zero-shot or unsupervised LLIE methods (ZeroDCE++, SCI, PairLIE, RUAS) that largely ignore noise, and unlike supervised methods that require paired training data covering all noise distributions, ZERO-IG leverages the smoothness of illumination to estimate a near-authentic illumination map from a preliminarily denoised image, then uses that illumination both to adaptively enhance and to preserve original noise characteristics during the final denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ZERO-IG requires no training data, no noise distribution knowledge, and no camera-specific calibration, making it applicable to arbitrary real-world low-light images from unknown devices—a setting where dataset-based methods degrade. The concatenation trick (keeping illumination constant across the denoising step) is a clean conceptual resolution to the well-known problem that enhancement alters noise statistics, making subsequent denoising suboptimal. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or video, whether the 2000-epoch-per-image training cost is acceptable in practice, and whether the simple 11-layer CNN architecture is optimal or whether deeper/more expressive backbones would further improve results. The VILNC dataset, while a contribution, is small and its long-term utility is uncertain.
