## Problem

Existing real-world vehicle-infrastructure cooperation (VIC) datasets such as DAIR-V2X and V2X-seq rely on a single Camera+Lidar pair at the roadside, which severely limits perception due to occlusion and blind spots in complex intersection traffic. No prior dataset provides multi-viewpoint, multi-sensor overlapping coverage at intersections with synchronized data from both road-side and vehicle-side, making it impossible to study how diverse sensor layouts affect cooperative perception quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240297/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 100k+ synchronized frames at 10 fps (100 ms NTP-aligned timestamps) from up to 18 sensors per intersection, including RGB cameras (1920×1080, 25 Hz, FOV 31°–120°), fisheye cameras (2048×2048, 180° FOV), and 150-beam Lidar (1.6M pps, 100°×40° FOV) on the road side, plus 2 cameras and 2 Lidars (40/64-beam) on the vehicle side. Annotations consist of 11.47M 3D bounding boxes [x,y,z,l,w,h,θ,η] in camera, lidar, intersection, and ego-vehicle coordinate systems, with per-sensor Track IDs, cross-sensor Global IDs, and matched vehicle-roadside object associations forming global BEV trajectories for three categories (Vehicle, Cyclist, Pedestrian).

## Method

The core contribution is the construction of five holographic intersections using four sensor layouts (4C+2L, 8C+2L, 12C+4F+2L, 4C+2F+2L) tailored to intersection geometry, where overlapping sensor fields of view eliminate blind spots. A multi-coordinate alignment pipeline (Global ENU via RTK/WGS84, Intersection, Ego-Vehicle, Lidar via Kabsch, Camera via PnP, Pixel via intrinsics) unifies all sensor data into a common spatial frame. The annotation pipeline assigns 3D boxes per sensor, links them into per-sensor trajectories via Track IDs, associates the same object across sensors at a timestamp via Global IDs, and matches vehicle-side and road-side detections to form cooperative ground truth. Five benchmark tasks are formulated—Mono3D, Lidar 3D Detection, 2D/3D MOT, Multi-Sensor MOT, and VIC Perception—evaluated with mAP/mAOS and MOTA/IDF1. Baselines employ a late-fusion strategy using the Hungarian algorithm to associate local tracklets into global trajectories for multi-sensor and VIC fusion, with detection backbones spanning FCOS3D, PGD, SECOND, PointPillars, PV-RCNN, and TransFusion-L.

## Results

On the HoloVIC validation set, the best Mono3D detector (PGD) achieves 52.54 3D AP and 43.21 BEV AP for Vehicle, while the best Lidar 3D detector (PVRCNN) reaches 72.78 3D AP and 82.16 BEV AP for Vehicle. For Pedestrian detection, Transfusion-L substantially outperforms other Lidar methods with 45.51 3D AP versus 26.25 (SECOND) and 27.43 (PointPillars). In the VIC perception task, upgrading roadside sensors from 1C+1L to 4C+2L improves BEV AP in the 50–70 m range from 59.82 to 85.75 and AOS from 63.00 to 90.43, demonstrating the critical benefit of multi-sensor holographic intersections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For Mono3D detection, PGD [23] consistently outperforms FCOS3D [22] across all three categories, gaining +5.33 3D AP on Vehicle and +5.63 on Cyclist, though FCOS3D retains a slight edge on Pedestrian 3D AP (11.31 vs 8.67). Among Lidar 3D detectors, PVRCNN [20] leads on Vehicle (72.78 3D AP) and Cyclist BEV AP (82.22), while Transfusion-L [1] dominates Pedestrian detection with 45.51 3D AP—nearly 19 points above SECOND [31] (26.25) and 18 points above PointPillars [14] (27.43), highlighting the transformer-based architecture's advantage for small, sparse targets. For 2D MOT, FairMOT [35] achieves the best IDF1 (68.17) and MOTA (63.35), substantially outperforming DeepSort [26] (IDF1 53.72) and Tracktor [3] (IDF1 59.34). In 3D MOT, AB3DMOT [25] with PointPillars yields IDF1 of 57.62, while the same tracker with FCOS3D drops to 42.85, confirming that detection quality is the bottleneck for 3D tracking. In the VIC perception task (Table 6), the 4C+2L roadside configuration improves BEV AP in the 50–70 m range by +38.8 points over vehicle-only (85.75 vs 46.95) and +25.9 points over 1C+1L (85.75 vs 59.82), with MOTA rising from 75.64 to 90.19, demonstrating that multi-sensor holographic intersections provide substantial cooperative perception gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloVIC extends the V2X/VIC dataset lineage (DAIR-V2X, V2X-seq) by replacing the single-viewpoint roadside sensor (1C+1L) with multi-sensor overlapping layouts (up to 12C+4F+2L) at holographic intersections, drawing conceptually from multi-camera tracking datasets like AICITY22 and WildTrack but applying them to the cooperative perception setting. It also departs from simulated V2X benchmarks (V2X-sim, DeepAccident, V2XSet) by providing real-world synchronized data with RTK-based global alignment. The individual tasks (Mono3D, Lidar 3D detection, MOT, VIC perception) follow established formulations, but the multi-sensor multi-viewpoint tracking (MSMOT) and the multi-layout VIC perception are new task instantiations that require cross-device ID association and intersection-coordinate fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first large-scale real-world multi-viewpoint, multi-sensor holographic intersection dataset (100k+ frames, 11.47M 3D boxes, 5 intersections, 4 layouts), HoloVIC fills a critical gap: prior VIC datasets could not evaluate how overlapping sensor coverage at intersections affects perception quality or how multi-sensor fusion benefits ego-vehicle perception. The demonstrated 38.8% AP and 40.76% AOS gains at 50–70 m range quantify the practical value of roadside cooperation for long-range perception. Open questions include whether the late-fusion baselines (Hungarian matching + AB3DMOT) are near-optimal, how results generalize to denser urban intersections, and whether the dataset supports trajectory prediction and end-to-end cooperative planning tasks the authors mention as future work.
