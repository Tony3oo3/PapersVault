## Problem

Existing test-time augmentation methods for vision-language models (e.g., TPT, DiffTPT) rely on intensive prompt-tuning procedures and ad hoc confidence thresholds to filter degenerated augmented views, making them incompatible with black-box or API-based applications where gradients and internal model states are inaccessible. The paper asks whether one can improve the image representation directly in the embedding space—without any prompt learning or model retraining—to achieve superior zero-shot generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240789/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single test image and a set of class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [class_k]'). At test time, N augmented views of the image (via RandomCrop or diffusion) are encoded by the frozen vision encoder, and the text prompts are encoded by the frozen language encoder. The output is a single class label obtained by cosine similarity between the converged density mode and each text embedding. Crucially, the method only requires access to final embedding vectors, not model internals or gradients, making it deployable on top of any CLIP-like model or proprietary API.

## Method

MTA formulates a robust multi-modal extension of MeanShift that jointly optimizes a density mode m and per-view inlierness scores y_p over the set of augmented-view embeddings. The objective combines a weighted kernel density estimate (where y_p down-weights outlier views), a text-knowledge-guided quadratic term that couples inlierness scores of views sharing similar zero-shot text predictions, and a Shannon entropy barrier keeping y on the probability simplex. Optimization proceeds via block coordinate descent: inlierness scores are updated through the Concave-Convex Procedure (yielding a closed-form softmax-like update), and the mode is updated via weighted fixed-point iterations. The entire procedure is training-free, hyperparameter-free (fixed λ=4, λ_y=0.2, variable bandwidth with ρ=0.3), and runs roughly three times faster than TPT since it avoids a second forward pass through the vision encoder.

## Results

On the 5 ImageNet variants (ImageNet, -A, -V2, -R, -Sketch), MTA achieves 63.16% average top-1 accuracy in a fully training-free, black-box setting, outperforming TPT (62.40%) which requires prompt optimization. When combined with CoOp's pretrained prompt, MTA+CoOp reaches 65.68% average, surpassing TPT+CoOp (65.14%) while remaining training-free at test time. MTA runs nearly 3× faster than TPT per image across all tested CLIP backbones (ResNet-50 through ViT-L/14), and the same fixed hyperparameters generalize across all five architectures without tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTA consistently outperforms TPT [38] on the 5 ImageNet variants (63.16% vs. 62.40% average) while being training-free and ~3× faster, and the gap widens to +0.54% when combined with CoOp (65.68% vs. 65.14%). Against the CLIP baseline [46], MTA yields a +4.05% average gain without any prompt training. On the 10 fine-grained datasets (Table 2), MTA+E. achieves 65.58% average vs. TPT's 64.76% and CLIP+E.'s 65.16%, with the largest gains on Aircraft (+1.65% over CLIP) and Cars (+2.57%). In the few-shot setting (Table 5), MTA adds +2.1–2.4% on top of CoOp and ProGrad across 1–16 shots, and +1.5–2.1% on top of Tip-Adapter, whereas TPT generally degrades CoOp performance on fine-grained tasks (Figure 3). Against DiffTPT [14] with diffusion-based augmentation (Table 3), MTA achieves 63.28% vs. 62.70% average, confirming robustness across augmentation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical MeanShift mode-seeking algorithm (Comaniciu & Meer, 1999) and its variable-bandwidth variant (Comaniciu et al., 2001) into a robust, multi-modal formulation for test-time augmentation of vision-language models. It departs from the dominant test-time prompt tuning paradigm (TPT, DiffTPT) by operating exclusively on final image embeddings rather than optimizing learnable prompt tokens, thereby eliminating the need for gradient access to model internals. The key conceptual shift is replacing hard confidence-based filtering of augmented views with a soft inlierness scoring mechanism jointly optimized with density mode seeking, and incorporating text-prediction-guided pairwise affinities to regularize which views are considered inliers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MTA offers a practical, training-free, and hyperparameter-free alternative to prompt tuning that is compatible with black-box and API-based vision-language models where gradient access is unavailable — a setting of growing importance as proprietary models dominate deployment. The finding that TPT degrades performance when stacked on top of few-shot methods while MTA consistently improves them suggests that prompt optimization and image representation refinement address complementary failure modes. Open questions include whether the inlierness formulation generalizes to non-classification tasks (e.g., retrieval, segmentation), whether the approach benefits from more sophisticated augmentation pipelines beyond RandomCrop and diffusion, and how it scales to very large numbers of augmented views or multi-image contexts.
