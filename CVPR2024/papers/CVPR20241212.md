## Problem

While score distillation has enabled high-quality text-to-3D synthesis, extending this to dynamic 4D scenes with an additional temporal dimension remains largely unexplored. The only prior work (MAV3D) relies on a NeRF-based 4D representation that cannot compose multiple animated objects into larger scenes and produces only short, fixed-length sequences, limiting practical content creation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241212/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a dynamic 4D scene parameterized by a set of 3D Gaussians (positions, isotropic scales, opacities, spherical-harmonic colors) plus an MLP deformation field Δ(x,y,z,τ) that displaces each Gaussian over a normalized time interval τ∈[0,1]. The resulting 4D scene is fully renderable from arbitrary camera positions and times, and can be autoregressively extended with new text prompts or composed with independently generated 4D objects. The video diffusion model is conditioned on frame rate (fps ∈ {4, 8, 12}), allowing the same 4D sequence to be rendered at different temporal resolutions.

## Method

AYG uses a disentangled 4D representation: static 3D Gaussians capture geometry and appearance, while a separate MLP deformation field models temporal dynamics, enabling trivial composition of multiple animated objects. Optimization proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 synthesizes a static 3D Gaussian scene by composing gradients from MVDream (3D-aware multiview prior) and Stable Diffusion (general image prior) with view-guidance for canonical pose; Stage 2 freezes the Gaussians and optimizes only the deformation field by composing gradients from a custom text-to-video model (trained on HDVG130M + Webvid-10M with fps conditioning) and Stable Diffusion, using classifier score distillation (CSD) rather than standard SDS. A novel Jensen-Shannon divergence regularization constrains the mean and diagonal covariance of the Gaussian point cloud to remain stable over time, preventing degenerate global translations. A motion amplification mechanism scales the per-frame video score deviations from their temporal mean to encourage larger, more vivid motion, and an autoregressive scheme interpolates deformation fields across an overlap interval to extend sequences beyond the video model's native clip length.

## Results

In a user study on 28 text prompts, AYG is preferred over MAV3D on all six evaluated metrics. The largest margins appear in Overall Quality (53.6% vs. 38.8% preference) and Motion Text Alignment (47.4% vs. 33.7%), while Motion Realism is nearly tied (44.4% vs. 43.9%). The authors also report R-Precision on a larger prompt set, performing on par with MAV3D, though they note R-Precision is not a meaningful metric for dynamic scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AYG is compared against MAV3D [79], the only prior work generating dynamic 4D scenes via score distillation. AYG wins on all six user-study metrics, with the most decisive advantages in Overall Quality (53.6% vs. 38.8%) and Motion Text Alignment (47.4% vs. 33.7%), indicating that the compositional generation framework (combining text-to-image, text-to-video, and multiview diffusion models) produces both higher-fidelity geometry and better text-aligned motion. Motion Realism is essentially a tie (44.4% vs. 43.9%), suggesting MAV3D's NeRF+HexPlane representation captures motion plausibility comparably. Qualitatively, AYG produces more detailed 4D outputs and uniquely supports autoregressive sequence extension, varying text prompts mid-sequence, and composition of multiple animated objects into large scenes—capabilities MAV3D's representation does not permit. On R-Precision (a static text-alignment metric), the two methods perform on par.

## Relation to Prior Work

AYG extends the score distillation framework (DreamFusion) from static 3D to dynamic 4D generation, replacing the NeRF+HexPlane representation of the only prior text-to-4D work (MAV3D) with dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting plus a deformation field. Its key conceptual departure is a compositional generation framework that simultaneously distills gradients from a text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion), a 3D-aware multiview model (MVDream), and a custom text-to-video model, whereas prior 3D/4D synthesis typically relied on a single diffusion prior. The idea of composing multiple DM gradients has appeared in controllable 2D image generation but had not been applied to 3D or 4D synthesis. AYG also adopts the concurrent classifier score distillation (CSD) formulation, which it independently derived by observing that ProlificDreamer's control variates vanish at initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AYG demonstrates two capabilities absent from prior text-to-4D work: autoregressive temporal extension of 4D sequences (with optional changing text prompts) and trivial composition of multiple independently-generated 4D objects into a single large scene, enabled by the explicit Gaussian representation. These open practical pathways for animation pipelines and synthetic data generation with exact tracking labels. Key limitations remain: the method cannot produce topological changes, is object-centric rather than scene-level, and the deformation field is a single global MLP that may struggle with highly articulated or multi-body dynamics. The JSD-based distribution regularization and motion amplification are lightweight but effective techniques that could transfer to other optimization-based 4D generation systems.
