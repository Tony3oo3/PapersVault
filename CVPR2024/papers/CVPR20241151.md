## Problem

Existing panoramic scene generation methods either treat the 360° panorama as a single equirectangular image (failing at loop closure) or inpaint multiple perspective views with the same text prompt, causing duplicate objects (e.g., multiple beds in a bedroom). No prior mechanism encodes global scene layout into individual view generation, resulting in incoherent panoramic scenes with repeated salient objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241151/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single perspective image (real or synthesized via conditional diffusion models from text, depth maps, sketches, or colored scripts) and produces a high-resolution 360° equirectangular panorama. Each intermediate perspective view is generated at 512×512 by Stable Diffusion v2 and upscaled to 2048×2048 via super-resolution before multi-view fusion. The pipeline also supports downstream 3D point cloud generation (via monocular depth estimation) and immersive video rendering with camera rotation and translation.

## Method

L-MAGIC performs iterative warping-and-inpainting: completed views are projected onto a unit sphere, warped to novel camera poses (100° FoV, ~40° rotation between views, expanding alternately from both sides), and the missing regions are inpainted using Stable Diffusion v2. The key innovation is using pre-trained language models (ChatGPT) and vision-language models (BLIP-2) without fine-tuning to generate per-view layout descriptions, a scene-level style prompt, and a repetition-avoidance list that together form the inpainting prompt for each view. A VLM-based verification loop re-runs inpainting (up to 20 times) if unwanted repeated objects are detected in the output. Final quality is enhanced by super-resolution, weighted multi-view fusion (weights based on distance to image boundary), and equirectangular projection to produce the seamless panorama.

## Results

On image-to-panorama, L-MAGIC achieves an Inception Score of 2.66, outperforming Stable Diffusion v2 (1.93), Text2room (2.11), and MVDiffusion (2.08). On text-to-panorama, L-MAGIC reaches IS 2.26, surpassing Text2light (1.67), Stable Diffusion v2 (1.58), LDM3D (1.86), Text2room (1.89), and MVDiffusion (1.86). In human evaluations with 15 anonymous voters, L-MAGIC receives >70% preference over all baselines on both rendered view quality and 360° scene layout coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-MAGIC consistently outperforms all five baselines on both tasks. In image-to-panorama, it leads Text2room (IS 2.11) by 0.55 and MVDiffusion (2.08) by 0.58, with the largest gap over Stable Diffusion v2 (1.93) at 0.73. In text-to-panorama, the advantage is even more pronounced: L-MAGIC (2.26) exceeds the strongest baseline Text2room (1.89) by 0.37 and the weakest Stable Diffusion v2 (1.58) by 0.68. The paper attributes baseline failures to two recurring issues: single-image methods (Stable Diffusion v2, Text2light, LDM3D) cannot close the 360° loop due to equirectangular projection splitting, while multi-view methods (Text2room, MVDiffusion) lack a mechanism to avoid duplicating salient objects (e.g., multiple beds) across views. Human evaluation confirms these quantitative gaps, with L-MAGIC preferred in >70% of side-by-side comparisons on both quality and layout dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

L-MAGIC sits at the intersection of multi-view diffusion inpainting (Text2room, MVDiffusion) and language-model-guided generation, but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than fine-tuning multi-view attention modules or applying a single global text prompt to all views, it uses a frozen LLM (ChatGPT) as a per-view scene planner that generates diverse, non-overlapping layout descriptions and a vision-language model (BLIP-2) as a consistency checker. The warping-and-inpainting loop itself follows the geometric pipeline of Text2room, and the inpainting engine is the off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion v2 model, but the LLM-mediated prompt decomposition is the novel architectural contribution. The paper also repurposes conditional diffusion (ControlNet) and monocular depth estimation (Metric3D, DPT-hybrid) as plug-in front- and back-ends, making the core pipeline modality-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, no-fine-tuning design makes L-MAGIC immediately applicable to in-the-wild images and extensible to new input modalities without retraining, which is practically valuable for design and VR pipelines. However, the method remains dependent on the reliability of LLM outputs (format errors, training-data biases in diffusion models that re-introduce 'expected' objects) and on monocular depth estimation for 3D outputs, both of which introduce artifacts. Open questions include whether the iterative warping strategy is optimal compared to learned multi-view generation, whether the LLM planning step can be distilled into a lightweight learned module, and how the approach scales to scenes with many salient objects or complex spatial relationships.
