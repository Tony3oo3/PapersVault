## Problem

Existing neural acoustic field models (NAF, INRAS, NACF, AV-NeRF) were developed and evaluated exclusively on synthetic or highly simplified datasets with restrictive assumptions such as fixed emitters, 2D-only coverage, or planar geometry. No real-world benchmark existed to assess these methods under realistic room conditions with complex geometry, material variations, and source directivity, making it impossible to determine their true performance or the actual value of visual cues in acoustic modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models take a speaker 3D position, a 2D speaker orientation (azimuth/elevation from the robotic stand's 120° rotations), and a receiver 3D position as primary inputs; audio-visual variants additionally consume RGB and depth images rendered from a trained NeRF at either fixed bounce points (NACF) or the listener position (AV-NeRF). The output is a time-domain room impulse response, captured at 48 kHz (or 16 kHz) and truncated to 0.32 s for training and evaluation based on the room's average reverberation time. The dataset provides 47K RIRs for the empty room and 39K for the furnished room, each paired with 6DoF pose annotations from OptiTrack motion capture and multi-view images (8,030 and 3,388 respectively) captured via the Eyeful Tower rig.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the RAF dataset, captured using a custom Earful Tower (36 omni-directional microphones in an inverted pine-cone arrangement on three phase-locked RME 12Mic-D units) and a robotic Genelec 8030C speaker stand, with logarithmic sine-sweep excitation and OptiTrack 6DoF tracking. For modeling, the authors extend four existing 2D neural field architectures (NAF, INRAS, NACF, AV-NeRF) to 3D by adding a vertical dimension to bounce-point sampling and position encoding, and introduce NAF++ and INRAS++ by augmenting their losses with an energy decay term that penalizes L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth log decay curves. A key empirical finding is that 3D bounce-point sampling and the decay loss substantially improve C50, EDT, and T60 accuracy over vanilla models. The paper also proposes a sim2real pipeline: pretraining INRAS on dense Pyroomacoustics shoebox-simulator RIRs (using the room's bounding box and average T60), then fine-tuning on as few as ~100 real samples, which yields significant few-shot gains over training from scratch on the same sparse real data.

## Results

On the RAF dataset (48 kHz RIRs, averaged across two scenes), INRAS++ achieves the best overall performance with STFT error 0.39 dB, C50 error 0.57 dB, EDT error 0.017 s, and T60 error 6.17%, while using only 1.33M parameters, 5.31 MB storage, and 3.36 ms inference time. NACF is competitive on C50 (0.59 dB) and EDT (0.017 s) but slightly worse on T60 (6.62%). Notably, the classical Nearest-Neighbor + Opus baseline achieves STFT error 0.49 dB, approaching the neural methods, reflecting the high density of the RAF captures. Adding the energy decay loss improves INRAS from T60 8.01% to 6.17% and NAF from 10.08% to 8.19%, confirming its importance for modeling reverberation decay.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INRAS++ (the paper's improved INRAS with energy decay loss) outperforms NAF on every metric, reducing T60 error from 10.08% to 6.17% and C50 error from 0.71 dB to 0.57 dB, while also being 4x smaller (1.33M vs 5.51M params) and 3.6x faster (3.36 ms vs 11.98 ms). Against NACF, INRAS++ wins on T60 (6.17% vs 6.62%) and is marginally better on C50 (0.57 vs 0.59 dB), while NACF edges ahead on EDT (0.017 s tied) and inference speed (3.17 vs 3.36 ms). AV-NeRF, despite having 10x more parameters (12.99M) and 10x more storage (51.98 MB), is outperformed by INRAS++ on all four acoustic metrics, suggesting that position-dependent visual context does not help over fixed bounce-point representations in this setting. The classical Nearest-Neighbor + Opus baseline (STFT 0.49 dB) is competitive with the neural methods on STFT error, indicating that the dense spatial sampling in RAF makes interpolation effective. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that 3D bounce-point sampling improves over 2D, and that audio-only INRAS++ with 2D bounce points (C50 0.57 dB) matches audio-visual NACF (C50 0.58 dB), suggesting visual cues provide limited additional benefit for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural acoustic field line of work (NAF, INRAS, NACF, AV-NeRF) from 2D synthetic settings to 3D real-world scenes, introducing the RAF dataset as the first dense 3D multimodal RIR benchmark. Prior real-world datasets (MeshRIR, Liang et al., Chen et al.) suffered from severe limitations—single fixed emitters, 2D-only coverage, or sparse receivers—making them unsuitable for evaluating arbitrary speaker-receiver pairs in 3D. The paper also repurposes the energy decay loss from Majumder et al. into NAF++ and INRAS++ variants, and introduces a sim2real pretraining strategy using Pyroomacoustics that had not been applied to neural acoustic fields before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAF fills a critical gap by providing the first densely sampled, real-world 3D RIR dataset paired with high-fidelity visual reconstructions, enabling the field to move beyond synthetic benchmarks that lack material variation, source directivity, and complex geometry. The finding that a simple Opus-encoded nearest-neighbor baseline is competitive with neural methods, and that audio-only INRAS++ with 2D bounce points matches audio-visual NACF, raises open questions about whether visual context and 3D bounce sampling are truly necessary or whether dense real data simply makes the problem easier. The sim2real approach offers a practical path for few-shot deployment, though its reliance on a basic shoebox simulator limits generalizability. A key limitation is that RAF covers only one physical room (two configurations), constraining cross-scene generalization studies.
