## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding models suffer from three intertwined limitations: poor object recognition on sparse, unordered point clouds (exacerbated by the scarcity of 3D training data), suboptimal spatial encoding that relies on absolute world coordinates rather than relative anchor-based relationships, and a black-box fusion process that offers no explainability for diagnosing errors. View-dependent descriptions—where the correct answer changes with the observer's viewpoint—are particularly challenging for rotationally invariant encoders like PointNet++ that lose directional information.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud scene (unordered, variable sparsity, from ScanNet) and a natural language referring expression as input. Rather than producing a single logit vector, it outputs two distinct scores per candidate object: a category score (how well the object matches the described target class) and a spatial score (how well the object's relative position to anchors matches the spatial description). These are combined via a weighted sum of normalized logits to yield the final grounding decision, making the source of any error (category vs. spatial) directly inspectable.

## Method

MiKASA introduces three key mechanisms. First, a scene-aware object encoder applies self-attention across all N objects in the room after PointNet++ encoding, enriching each object's feature with contextual information from neighbors (improving object classification from 63.8% to 70.8%). Second, a multi-key-anchor spatial module generates N relative-position maps—each treating one object as the target and all others as anchors—augmented with rotation matrices for viewpoint invariance, then fused with BERT text features via cross-attention. Third, a late-fusion architecture with a 3-layer fusion module (each layer performing text-object fusion, object-spatial fusion, attention-based spatial aggregation, and view aggregation) progressively refines anchor weightings, culminating in the dual-scoring output. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a composite cross-entropy loss combining reference, text-categorization, object-categorization, and scene-aware-object-categorization terms.

## Results

MiKASA achieves the highest overall accuracy on both Sr3D (75.2%) and Nr3D (64.4%) in the ReferIt3D benchmark, surpassing the previous best (BUTD-DETR at 67.0% Sr3D / 54.6% Nr3D) by +8.2% and +9.8% respectively. The gains are especially pronounced in view-dependent (VD) scenarios, where MiKASA reaches 70.4% on Sr3D and 65.4% on Nr3D, outperforming the next-best method by +17.4% and +10.3% over BUTD-DETR and ViewRefer. On the harder subset (more distractors), MiKASA scores 67.3% (Sr3D) and 59.4% (Nr3D), again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiKASA outperforms all eight compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the two strongest prior methods, BUTD-DETR (ECCV'22) and ViewRefer (ICCV'23), MiKASA gains +8.2% overall on Sr3D and +9.8%/+8.4% on Nr3D, with the largest margins in view-dependent settings (+17.4% and +18.2% over BUTD-DETR and ViewRefer respectively on Sr3D-VD). Compared to MVT (CVPR'22), which shares the multi-view augmentation strategy, MiKASA improves overall accuracy by +10.7% (Sr3D) and +9.3% (Nr3D). Against earlier transformer-based methods (SAT, 3DVG-Transformer, InstanceRefer), the gap widens to 17–27 percentage points, reflecting the cumulative benefit of the scene-aware encoder and multi-key-anchor spatial module. NS3D (CVPR'23) reports only Sr3D results (62.7%), where MiKASA leads by +12.5%. Notably, MiKASA's advantage is most concentrated in the VD and Hard categories, suggesting the multi-key-anchor mechanism specifically addresses the directional ambiguity that plagues rotationally invariant encoders like PointNet++.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiKASA extends the 3D visual grounding line established by ReferIt3D, MVT, ViewRefer, and BUTD-DETR, but departs from the dominant early-fusion paradigm by adopting a late-fusion dual-scoring architecture that separately evaluates object category and spatial alignment. Its scene-aware object encoder draws on context-aware recognition literature (e.g., Structure Inference Net, context-based categorization) and replaces graph-based neighborhood aggregation with self-attention, while the multi-key-anchor spatial encoding is a conceptual departure from prior methods that encode absolute world coordinates. The approach is architecturally inspired by human object-searching behavior (identify category first, then localize), which prior 3DVG models treat as a single monolithic prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantially larger gains in view-dependent scenarios (+17–19% over prior SOTA) validate the hypothesis that relative-to-anchor encoding resolves directional ambiguity inherent in rotationally invariant encoders like PointNet++, offering a principled explanation for why view-dependent grounding has been a persistent weak point. The dual-scoring late-fusion design improves explainability for error diagnosis, a practical need in deployed systems. However, the model still relies on PointNet++ for base point encoding and does not explicitly preserve object orientation post-encoding (acknowledged as future work), and all results are confined to ScanNet-based ReferIt3D scenes, leaving open whether the multi-key-anchor strategy generalizes to outdoor or large-scale 3D environments.
