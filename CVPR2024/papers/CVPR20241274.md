## Problem

Existing 3D representation learning for robotics is limited to single-object, complete point clouds, ignoring self-occlusions and clutter present in real scenes. Moreover, prior 3D pre-training focuses on geometry and semantics while neglecting object affordances (e.g., grasping poses) that are critical for manipulation. 2D pre-trained representations, while generalizable, remain sub-optimal for handling occlusions and predicting 3D robotic actions.

## Input / Output

The primary input is a 3D point cloud of 4096 points (each with 3D coordinates and RGB color), processed via farthest point sampling into 256 key points with 32 nearest neighbors each. Task-specific prompt tokens (e.g., [img], [txt], [mask], [obj1]–[objK], [ref], [act]) and encoded text sequences are fed to the decoder. Outputs are task-dependent: reconstructed point coordinates and colors (MPM), image/text-space feature vectors (CML), per-point binary validity and 4×4 grasping pose matrices (GPS), per-point instance masks (INS/REG), or downstream class labels, 3D segmentation masks, and 7-DoF robot actions.

## Method

SUGAR employs a versatile transformer architecture comprising a point cloud encoder (shared PointNet local encoding followed by 12-layer self-attention) and a prompt-based decoder (12 layers of self-attention + cross-attention that layer-wisely query point embeddings). Five pre-training tasks are jointly optimized: masked point modeling (60% token masking with extended Chamfer distance including color), cross-modal knowledge distillation from OpenCLIP ViT-bigG-14 image/text features, per-point grasping pose synthesis (binary validity + 6D rotation via Gram-Schmidt), DETR-style 3D instance segmentation with Hungarian matching, and referring expression grounding. Multi-object cluttered scenes are automatically constructed via BlenderProc physics simulation (48.9K ShapeNet + 62.8K Objaverse scenes), providing free supervision for semantics, locations, and grasping poses. A curriculum learning strategy first trains on single-object data with MPM and CML, then jointly trains all five tasks on both single- and multi-object data.

## Results

On the RLBench 10-task manipulation benchmark, SUGAR(Ens_m) achieves 93.0% average success rate, outperforming PolarNet (89.8%) and Hiveformer with R3M pre-training (88.5%). On referring expression grounding, SUGAR(Ens_m) reaches 97.74% Acc@0.25 on OCID-Ref (vs. 90.77% for V-Cond) and 65.04% Acc@0.5 on the challenging RoboRefit testB split (vs. 54.12% for RefTR-r50). For zero-shot 3D object recognition, SUGAR(single) with ensembled pre-training data achieves 68.0% Top-1 on ScanObjectNN OBJ_BG, surpassing OpenShape by 15.4%, and 42.1% Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS (vs. 39.1% for OpenShape).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUGAR consistently outperforms all 2D pre-trained representations (R3M, MVP, CLIP, V-Cond) on referring expression grounding, achieving 97.74% Acc@0.25 on OCID-Ref versus 90.77% for the strongest 2D baseline V-Cond. On RoboRefit testB (unseen scenes), SUGAR(Ens_m) beats the best transformer baseline RefTR-r50 by over 10 points in Acc@0.5 (65.04% vs. 54.12%). For zero-shot 3D object recognition, SUGAR(single) with ensembled data surpasses OpenShape-PointBERT by 15.4% on ScanObjectNN OBJ_BG (68.0% vs. 52.6%) and by 3.0% on Objaverse-LVIS Top-1 (42.1% vs. 39.1%), while ReCon, CLIP2Point, and ULIP-PointBERT are all clearly outperformed. On RLBench manipulation, SUGAR(Ens_m) (93.0%) exceeds PolarNet (89.8%), Hiveformer+R3M (88.5%), and Hiveformer+CLIP (87.2%), with the largest gap over Auto-λ (69.3%). Notably, the grasping pose synthesis pre-training task contributes ~1% improvement on RLBench (93.0% vs. 92.0% without grasp), and multi-object scene pre-training adds ~4% over single-object pre-training on RoboRefit testB, confirming both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUGAR extends the 3D point cloud pre-training line (Point-BERT, ReCon, OpenShape) in two key directions: from single-object complete point clouds to cluttered multi-object scenes, and from geometry/semantics-only to joint affordance learning via grasping pose synthesis. Architecturally, it combines PointNet-based local encoding with a transformer encoder and a DETR-style prompt-based decoder, borrowing Hungarian matching from object detection for instance segmentation. The multi-object pre-training data is automatically constructed via BlenderProc with physically simulated object placement, providing free supervision for semantics, geometry, and grasping poses without manual annotation. This positions SUGAR as a bridge between the 2D pre-training-for-robotics paradigm (R3M, Voltron, MVP) and 3D representation learning, arguing that 3D cluttered scenes are the natural pre-training domain for robotic manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D pre-training in cluttered scenes with affordance learning yields representations that outperform both 2D and prior 3D pre-trained models across recognition, grounding, and manipulation, establishing a practical recipe for robotics-oriented 3D representation learning. A notable finding is that grasping pose synthesis (an affordance task) improves referring expression grounding, suggesting that manipulation-relevant features are not orthogonal to scene understanding. Limitations include the small model size (d=384, L=12) which appears to bottleneck performance when scaling pre-training data, and the evaluation remains primarily in simulation (RLBench) with limited real-robot validation. Open questions include whether larger models resolve the capacity issue, how the approach handles real-world sensor noise beyond synthetic depth, and whether the curriculum from single to multi-object scenes is optimal versus joint training.
