## Problem

Extending instruction-guided scene editing from 2D images to 4D dynamic scenes is fundamentally difficult: 2D diffusion models like IP2P edit frames independently, producing temporal inconsistency, and 3D editing pipelines like IN2N fail in 4D because 4D NeRFs model each absolute 3D location per frame rather than tracking individual objects, so fitting inconsistent multi-view renderings cannot recover spatial consistency. No prior work addresses general instruction-guided 4D scene editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242100/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4D NeRF scene (rendered via NeRFPlayer) and a natural language editing instruction (e.g., 'make it look like a Van Gogh painting'). It renders frames from the current NeRF, edits them with IP2P, and refits the NeRF on the edited dataset iteratively. Scenes range from monocular (single moving camera, one pseudo-view) to multi-camera indoor setups with 20+ views and hundreds of frames per view, and the entire pipeline completes in hours using two GPUs in parallel.

## Method

The core insight is to treat a 4D scene as a pseudo-3D scene where each pseudo-view is a video, decoupling the problem into temporal-consistent video editing and spatial-consistent 3D propagation. For temporal consistency, the authors augment IP2P with 3D convolutions and an anchor-aware cross-attention module (replacing self-attention) that references a shared anchor frame across batches, combined with a RAFT-based optical flow sliding window that warps edits between adjacent frames and edits in windows of B frames. For spatial consistency, depth-based warping from a small set of key pseudo-views (n=5 out of V>20) propagates edits to all other views, combined with temporal warping from the previous frame. The full pipeline iterates between generating a consistently edited dataset and refitting the NeRF, with parallelized NeRF training and dataset generation on separate GPUs and a noise-level annealing schedule for fast convergence.

## Results

On the multi-camera coffee_martini scene (DyNeRF), Instruct 4D-to-4D achieves an average PSNR of 19.67, SSIM of 0.635, LPIPS-Alex of 0.323, and LPIPS-VGG of 0.405, consistently outperforming the IN2N-4D baseline (PSNR 14.11, SSIM 0.457, LPIPS-Alex 0.512, LPIPS-VGG 0.587) across all four style-transfer instructions (Van Gogh, Hopper, Munch, Fauvism). The largest gap appears on the Hopper instruction, where PSNR improves from 11.96 to 17.47. Qualitatively, Instruct 4D-to-4D completes editing in roughly 2 hours while IN2N-4D requires 24× longer and still fails to converge to the target style.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is IN2N-4D, a naive extension of Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (IN2N) to 4D that iteratively generates one edited frame at a time and adds it to the training dataset. Instruct 4D-to-4D wins on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS-Alex, LPIPS-VGG) across all four style-transfer instructions, with the average PSNR gap reaching 5.56 dB (19.67 vs. 14.11) and LPIPS-VGG nearly halved (0.405 vs. 0.587). The advantage is most pronounced on the Hopper instruction, where IN2N-4D produces heavily blurred, artifact-laden results (PSNR 11.96) while Instruct 4D-to-4D yields coherent, photo-realistic edits (PSNR 17.47). Beyond quality, Instruct 4D-to-4D is dramatically faster: it completes the coffee_martini editing in approximately 2 hours, whereas IN2N-4D requires 24× more time and still fails to converge to the target style. The paper notes that no other published method addresses instruction-guided 4D scene editing, so no additional baselines are available for comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-guided 3D scene editing line (IN2N, Instruct 3D-to-3D) to the 4D setting by introducing a pseudo-3D decomposition: a 4D scene is treated as a collection of pseudo-views (videos), decoupling the problem into temporal-consistent video editing and spatial-consistent 3D editing. The video-editing sub-problem draws on Tune-a-Video's anchor-frame cross-attention and RAFT optical flow, while the spatial sub-problem inherits ViCA-NeRF's key-view depth-based warping and IN2N's iterative dataset update. The key departure from IN2N is recognizing that 4D NeRFs model absolute 3D locations rather than object identities across time, making frame-by-frame distillation fundamentally insufficient and motivating the pseudo-view formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first framework for general instruction-guided 4D scene editing, this work establishes a viable pipeline and validates that 2D diffusion models can be distilled into temporally and spatially consistent 4D edits, opening a direction that was previously unaddressed. Limitations include reliance on a specific NeRF backbone (NeRFPlayer), evaluation on only one scene for quantitative comparison, and a weak baseline (naive IN2N extension) that may overstate the gap. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences and more complex dynamics, whether the pseudo-3D decomposition generalizes to non-NeRF 4D representations (e.g., 4D Gaussian splatting), and whether the iterative fitting can be replaced by direct optimization for faster convergence.
