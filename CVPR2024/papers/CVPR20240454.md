## Problem

Existing autonomous driving datasets (KITTI, Waymo, nuScenes) either lack cold-weather conditions entirely or fail to capture the same physical locations across multiple seasons, making it impossible to isolate the effect of weather on perception performance from changes in scene content. The Ithica365 dataset partially addresses this but provides very limited 3D bounding box annotations and no radar or CAN data. MSU-4S fills this gap by recording the same diverse routes across four seasons with full multimodal sensor coverage and per-frame weather/season labels.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 100,000+ time-synchronized frames from three front-facing FLIR cameras (2048×1536, ~150° combined FOV), a 64-line Ouster OS-1 and a 32-line Velodyne VLP-32C lidar, six Continental ARS430 radars returning RCS and SNR per detection, RTK GNSS, IMU, and CAN bus signals (wheel speed, steering angle, throttle, brake). Outputs include 2D bounding boxes with an expanded class taxonomy (nine legally-defined sign subcategories, specific vehicle types, traffic lights), 3D bounding boxes for vehicles/pedestrians/cyclists in PCD point clouds, and per-frame YAML annotations for weather, time-of-day, and time-of-year. Camera images are stored as native JPEG to avoid re-encoding loss, and all frames carry nanosecond-precision Unix timestamps synchronized via PTP.

## Method

The core contribution is a data collection and labeling pipeline designed to enable controlled domain-shift analysis: fixed 11.2 km and 14.8 km routes through five distinct zones (central hub, thoroughfare, industrial, forest, neighborhood) are traversed repeatedly across clear, rainy, snowy, and autumn-foliage conditions, keeping static background geometry constant while varying weather. Sensor calibration uses PNP for intrinsics, NDT registration for the two lidars, and hand-measured plus automated refinement for camera-lidar extrinsics; temporal synchronization is verified at ~63 ns (PTP) and ~7.6 ms average (camera-lidar). Labeling is performed independently in 2D (camera) and 3D (lidar) by trained personnel using two separate tools, then cross-checked for consistency. The empirical study trains PV-RCNN++ on clear/rain/snow 3D segments and YOLOv5 on four 2D seasonal segments, then evaluates cross-condition performance, revealing an 18+ point AP drop for clear→snow 3D detection and a significant 2D mAP drop between summer and fall despite similar weather, confirming strong domain shift attributable to seasonal appearance changes.

## Results

For 3D object detection using PV-RCNN++, the clear-weather-trained model achieves 67.99 AP (IoU=0.5) on clear test data but drops to 62.44 on rain (−5.55) and 55.50 on snow (−12.49), demonstrating a strong domain shift. At IoU=0.7, the clear-to-snow drop widens to −18.45 points. For 2D detection with YOLOv5, the winter-snow-trained model achieves 47.3 mAP on snow but suffers a −19.30 mAP drop when tested on fall-clear data, revealing that even similar-weather cross-season shifts (e.g., summer→fall) cause significant performance degradation (−7.30 to −9.20 mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a dataset paper, so no external method baselines are compared; instead, PV-RCNN++ (3D) and YOLOv5 (2D) serve as fixed detectors to quantify the domain shift inherent in MSU-4S. The key finding is asymmetric robustness: models trained on challenging conditions (snow, rain) degrade less when tested on easier conditions (clear) than vice versa — the clear-trained model loses 12.49 AP on snow, while the snow-trained model loses only 3.86 AP on clear. The rain-to-snow cross-domain gap (−7.29 AP) is notably smaller than clear-to-snow (−12.49 AP), suggesting that wet-weather conditions share more visual/point-cloud characteristics. For 2D detection, the largest cross-domain drop is winter-snow→fall-clear (−19.30 mAP), indicating that autumn foliage introduces a domain shift comparable to or exceeding that of snow, even when weather and lighting are similar.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSU-4S carves new ground in the autonomous driving dataset landscape by combining same-location multi-season capture with radar data, a combination absent from all major prior datasets. The closest analogue, Ithica365, also traverses a fixed route across seasons but lacks radar, CAN data, and has far fewer 3D-annotated frames. The empirical domain-shift analysis repurposes standard detectors (PV-RCNN++ for 3D, YOLOv5 for 2D) rather than introducing new architectures, positioning the contribution squarely as a benchmark resource rather than a methodological advance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controlled same-location design enables rigorous quantification of domain shift that no prior dataset supports, and the 18+ point AP degradation from clear to snow conditions (3D) and the significant mAP drops from autumn foliage (2D) provide concrete evidence that current detectors are far from season-robust. The inclusion of six radars with RCS and SNR measurements opens a path for radar-inclusive fusion research that remains underexplored. Open questions include whether domain adaptation methods can close the observed gaps, whether radar signals remain reliable in snow/rain, and whether the single-geographic-location constraint limits generalizability of the domain-shift findings.
