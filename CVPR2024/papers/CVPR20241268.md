## Problem

Existing industrial anomaly detection datasets (MVTec AD, VisA) have reached performance saturation above 99% AUROC with limited scale (~5K–10K images) and single-view captures, making it difficult to distinguish method quality or evaluate multi-view scenarios that reflect real production lines. Moreover, the standard unsupervised setting assumes perfectly clean training data, which is unrealistic given that production yield rates are typically 60–90%, meaning a non-trivial fraction of training samples will be defective. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a large-scale, multi-view, real-world dataset and a new fully unsupervised evaluation setting that relaxes the clean-training assumption.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 150K+ high-resolution RGB images (2000–5000 px, captured at 3648×5472) across 30 object categories, each with 5 synchronized views (1 top-down + 4 at 45°) and pixel-level segmentation masks. Outputs are evaluated at three granularities: image-level AUROC, a novel sample-level S-AUROC that integrates all 5 views per object, and pixel-level AUPRO. Under the FUIAD setting, training sets contain 10–40% anomalous samples mixed with normal ones, with the noise ratio α controlling the proportion while keeping total training size constant.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction and benchmarking framework rather than a new detection architecture. The pipeline involves capturing 30 objects with manually created defects (pits, cracks, scratches, deformation, missing parts) using 5 synchronized cameras with ring lighting, then iteratively cleaning annotations via a cascade RCNN (HRNet-32w backbone) for confident learning until prediction-annotation consistency stabilizes. Two evaluation settings are defined: standard UIAD (clean normal training) and FUIAD (0–40% noisy training), where FUIAD is constructed by fixing a 100-sample test set per category and adaptively adjusting training composition to maintain constant total size. A sample-level S-AUROC metric integrates multi-view predictions to better reflect production-line quality inspection. Eight representative methods (PatchCore, PaDiM, CFlow, SimpleNet, DeSTSeg, RD, UniAD, SoftPatch) are benchmarked across both settings, revealing significant performance drops relative to MVTec/VisA and exposing method-specific vulnerabilities to noisy training data.

## Results

On the proposed Real-IAD dataset (multi-view), the best-performing methods achieve only 94.9% S-AUROC (SimpleNet), 89.4% I-AUROC (PatchCore), and 93.8% P-AUPRO (RD), representing a significant drop from MVTec AD (97.9% I-AUROC) and VisA (91.8% I-AUROC). Under the Fully Unsupervised IAD (FUIAD) setting with 40% noise, all methods degrade substantially, with SoftPatch (90.6% S-AUROC) and PatchCore (88.9% S-AUROC) showing the most robustness, while PaDim drops to 76.5% S-AUROC and DeSTSeg to 74.5%. The mean I-AUROC across all methods on multi-view Real-IAD is 85.0±3.6, confirming the dataset's higher difficulty compared to existing benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the multi-view Real-IAD UIAD setting, SimpleNet leads in sample-level detection (94.9% S-AUROC) while PatchCore leads in image-level detection (89.4% I-AUROC); RD achieves the best pixel-level localization (93.8% P-AUPRO) thanks to its multi-level feature reconstruction. Under the FUIAD setting with increasing noise (α=0.1 to 0.4), SoftPatch and PatchCore demonstrate the most robustness, with SoftPatch achieving the best S-AUROC at α=0.4 (90.6%) by filtering noisy features before memory-bank construction. In contrast, PaDiM, SimpleNet, DeSTSeg, and RD suffer severe degradation: PaDiM drops from 92.7% to 76.5% S-AUROC, and DeSTSeg from 94.6% to 74.5% as noise increases. The paper highlights that no single method dominates all metrics, and the performance gap between methods is much larger on Real-IAD (e.g., 11.2-point spread in I-AUROC) than on MVTec AD (1.8-point spread), making Real-IAD a more discriminative benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of 2D industrial anomaly detection benchmarks established by MVTec AD and VisA, which standardized the unsupervised IAD task but have become saturated (methods exceed 99% I-AUROC on MVTec) and limited in scale (~5K–10K images), category diversity (12–15 classes), and single-view acquisition. Real-IAD departs from this lineage on three axes: it scales to 150K high-resolution images across 30 objects, introduces multi-view (5-angle) capture as the first 2D IAD dataset to do so, and formulates a Fully Unsupervised IAD (FUIAD) setting that injects 0–40% anomalous noise into the training set based on the practical observation that production yield rates exceed 60%. The benchmark evaluation draws on the full spectrum of existing IAD method families—memory-bank (PatchCore, SoftPatch), normalizing-flow (CFlow), data-augmentation (SimpleNet, DeSTSeg), and reconstruction/distillation (RD, UniAD)—positioning the dataset as a common testbed rather than proposing a new detection algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-IAD addresses a critical bottleneck: the inability of current benchmarks to differentiate methods or support practical deployment scenarios, since MVTec/VisA are saturated and too small to construct noisy-training experiments (Table 3 shows VisA drops to 0 valid categories at α=0.2 with 100 test samples). The FUIAD setting is the most practically relevant contribution, as it mirrors real production lines where perfectly clean training data is unavailable, yet the results reveal that even the most robust method (PatchCore's memory bank) degrades substantially (94.4→88.9 S-AUROC from α=0 to α=0.4), and SoftPatch's denoising offers only marginal gains—indicating that noise-robust IAD remains an open problem. The multi-view and sample-level evaluation protocol also opens questions about how to fuse cross-view evidence and whether unified multi-class models can scale to 30+ categories without per-class retraining. The authors acknowledge limitations: only a subset of methods is benchmarked, and zero-shot, few-shot, and semi-supervised settings are left for future work.
