## Problem

Existing human motion generation models treat actors and reactors symmetrically or focus on single-person synthesis, neglecting the causal, asymmetric nature of human-human interactions. No prior dataset annotated actor-reactor roles, and no method simultaneously addressed the asymmetric, dynamic, synchronous (online), and detailed (hand-level) characteristics of real interactions. This paper introduces the first multi-setting benchmark and a dedicated model for generating a person's reaction conditioned on another person's action in an online, intention-agnostic manner.

## Input / Output

The model takes an actor's motion sequence in SMPL-X format (54 joints including articulated hands, jaw, and eyeballs, with 6D rotation representation) as the primary condition, plus an optional action category label for constrained settings. It produces the reactor's full-body motion sequence in the same SMPL-X parameterization (pose θ∈R^{3K}, global orientation q∈R^3, root translation γ∈R^3). Critically, generation is causal: at each timestep the reactor only observes the actor's motion up to that point, enforced by a directional attention mask, and inference proceeds auto-regressively with 5 DDIM steps yielding ~0.76 ms per frame.

## Method

ReGenNet is a conditional diffusion model whose denoising network F is a stack of 8 Transformer decoder layers with masked multi-head attention, where the directional mask prevents the reactor from attending to future actor frames, enabling online synthesis. Actor and noised-reactor features are linearly projected and concatenated along the feature dimension to form input tokens, while the diffusion timestep and optional action label are injected via separate feed-forward networks summed into a conditioning token. The network directly predicts clean poses (x̂₀) rather than noise residuals, allowing an explicit distance-based interaction loss that measures relative joint positions (via forward kinematics), relative orientations (via rotation matrices), and relative translations between actor and reactor. Training uses a cosine noise schedule over 1000 timesteps with classifier-free guidance, and the total loss combines the diffusion reconstruction term with the interaction loss (λ_inter=1). At inference, DDIM with 5 steps provides near-real-time latency while maintaining quality close to 1000-step sampling.

## Results

On the primary NTU120-AS dataset (online, unconstrained setting), ReGenNet achieves a test-conditioned FID of 11.00, substantially outperforming MDM-GRU (24.25), MDM (54.54), AGRoL (44.94), and cVAE (70.10), while matching the real-data FID of 0.09 in the train-conditioned setting (0.90). ReGenNet also attains the best action recognition accuracy (1.000 train, 0.749 test) and multimodality (22.90 test) among all baselines. On InterHuman-AS, ReGenNet achieves the best R-Precision (0.407), FID (2.265), and MModality (2.391) over all compared methods including RAIG and InterGen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGenNet decisively outperforms all baselines on FID across all three datasets. On NTU120-AS (test-conditioned), ReGenNet's FID of 11.00 is more than 2× better than MDM-GRU (24.25) and over 4× better than MDM (54.54), AGRoL (44.94), and cVAE (70.10). On InterHuman-AS, ReGenNet achieves FID 2.265 vs. RAIG's 2.915 and InterGen's 13.237, while also leading in R-Precision (0.407 vs. 0.363 for RAIG and 0.374 for InterGen). The closest competitor is MDM-GRU, which benefits from a GRU backbone for temporal modeling but still lags ReGenNet by 13.25 FID points on NTU120-AS test and lacks the explicit interaction loss and directional attention mask. On Chi3D-AS, ReGenNet achieves FID 0.27 (train) and 13.76 (test), again the best among all methods, though the smaller test set (75 sequences) introduces higher variance. ReGenNet also generalizes best to unseen viewpoints (camera 2), achieving FID 8.16 vs. MDM-GRU's 25.57 and cVAE's 63.23.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReGenNet builds on the denoising diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the architectural choices of MDM (Tevet et al., 2022) for human motion synthesis, but departs from prior work in a fundamental way: instead of generating motion from static scene/object contexts or treating multi-person interactions symmetrically, it formulates an asymmetric, online, and unconstrained action-reaction synthesis problem. The Transformer decoder with masked multi-head attention enforces causal (leftward) generation so that reactors cannot access future actor states, and the explicit distance-based interaction loss draws from human-scene/object interaction literature (PLACE, GOAL) but adapts it to dynamic person-person relative geometry. The use of SMPL-X with detailed hand and facial articulation also distinguishes it from prior multi-person works that rely on sparse skeletons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark and set of annotated datasets (NTU120-AS, InterHuman-AS, Chi3D-AS) that explicitly encode actor-reactor asymmetry, filling a gap that all prior multi-person interaction datasets overlooked. The online, unconstrained setting directly targets practical AR/VR and game applications where reactions must be generated instantaneously without knowing the actor's intention. Key limitations remain: the benchmark only covers atomic action periods (not longer interactions with role transitions), NTU120's algorithmically extracted SMPL-X poses are noisy with inter-penetrations, and facial expressions are unnatural. Open questions include extending to longer multi-phase interactions, handling actor-reactor role switches, and collecting higher-quality MoCap data with natural expressions.
