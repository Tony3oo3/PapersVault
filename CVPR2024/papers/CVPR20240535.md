## Problem

Existing virtual IHC staining methods either rely on paired H&E-IHC images that are difficult to obtain due to inherent misalignment between adjacent tissue slices, or operate in a fully unsupervised manner that preserves image style but fails to maintain pathological consistency (i.e., whether a region is protein-positive or negative). This paper addresses the gap by introducing weakly-supervised learning to guide unpaired virtual staining while ensuring accurate transmission of pathological information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240535/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 H&E-stained histopathology patches at 20× magnification as input and produces corresponding virtual IHC-stained patches (HER2, ER, or GPC3) as output. Only WSI-level pos/neg labels are available as supervision (no patch-level annotations), making this a weakly-supervised setting. The framework operates bidirectionally (H&E→IHC and IHC→H&E) within a CycleGAN-style architecture, and the generated patches can be assembled into full-slide virtual IHC images for downstream diagnosis.

## Method

Confusion-GAN builds on CycleGAN (unet256 generators, cycle and identity losses) and introduces two key modules. First, a Patch-level Pathology Information Extractor (PPIE) uses top-k selection from MIL attention scores to identify candidate positive patches, then employs a diffusion model (BBDM) to synthesize pseudo-positive patches from negative ones; a dual-spherical loss pushes positive features farther from a center than pseudo-positive features, enabling anomaly-detection-style patch classification without manual annotations. Second, a Multi-branch Discriminator (MBD) adds a confusion discriminator that must identify the single generated image among N−1 real images in a reference pool (concatenated along the channel dimension), forcing the generator to produce outputs indistinguishable from the real domain distribution. A Pathology Loss constrains the IHC classifier's prediction on the generated image to match the PPIE's prediction on the input H&E patch, ensuring pathological consistency during translation.

## Results

On the HCI dataset (unsupervised setting), confusion-GAN achieves FID 77.3, LPIPS 0.570, and SSIM 0.2300, outperforming CycleGAN (FID 90.9–105.4), UGATIT (FID 95.3), and DCD (FID 91.3). On the BCI dataset, it reaches FID 49.2 and LPIPS 0.478, surpassing the best supervised baseline ASP (FID 54.3, LPIPS 0.503). On MIST, confusion-GAN achieves FID 39.8, LPIPS 0.516, and SSIM 0.2174, all best among the five compared methods. In downstream HCC diagnosis, generated GPC3 images yield an AUC of 88.9% versus 55.6% for original H&E images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Confusion-GAN is compared against three unsupervised methods (CycleGAN, UGATIT, DCD) on HCI and four supervised methods (Pix2pix, Pix2pixHD, PyramidP2P, ASP) on BCI and MIST. On HCI, it wins all three metrics (FID, LPIPS, SSIM) over all unsupervised baselines, with the largest gap over CycleGAN-unet (FID 77.3 vs. 105.4). On BCI, it achieves the best FID (49.2 vs. ASP's 54.3) and LPIPS (0.478 vs. 0.503), but trails ASP on SSIM (0.4579 vs. 0.4923), suggesting slightly lower pixel-level structural similarity despite better perceptual quality. On MIST, it dominates all three metrics, beating ASP by 1.4 FID points and 0.016 LPIPS. The supervised baselines Pix2pix, Pix2pixHD, and PyramidP2P show significant texture artifacts due to misalignment in their paired training data, while ASP (contrastive-learning-based) is the closest competitor but still lags on FID and LPIPS. In the downstream HCC classification task, generated GPC3 images improve ABMIL AUC from 55.6% (H&E input) to 88.9%, demonstrating that the virtual staining preserves diagnostically relevant pathological information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CycleGAN's unpaired image-to-image translation framework, adopting its generator architecture, cycle-consistency loss, and identity loss as the base. It departs from prior unsupervised virtual IHC staining methods (CycleGAN, UGATIT, DCD) by introducing a weakly-supervised pathology consistency constraint via a patch-level pathology information extractor (PPIE) that leverages multiple instance learning and anomaly detection principles. The PPIE itself repurposes a diffusion model (BBDM) to synthesize positive-like samples from negative patches, and the multi-branch discriminator introduces a one-to-many 'confusion' discrimination paradigm not previously applied to histopathology translation. The work also extends the MIL literature by proposing PPIE as a plug-and-play module that improves top-k selection across multiple MIL backbones (ABMIL, CLAM-MB, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Virtual IHC staining addresses a real clinical bottleneck—actual IHC is expensive, time-consuming, and requires specialized reagents—making a method that avoids the need for hard-to-obtain paired H&E-IHC images practically valuable. The downstream result (AUC jumping from 55.6% to 88.9% when using generated GPC3 images versus H&E for HCC diagnosis) suggests virtual staining could serve as a low-cost diagnostic proxy, though this is demonstrated on a single dataset. Open questions include whether the patch-level approach generalizes to full WSI-scale translation, whether the color-analysis-based IHC labeling is robust across staining protocols, and whether the PPIE's reliance on a diffusion model for synthetic positive generation scales to rarer pathological patterns. The HCI dataset (first GPC3 H&E-IHC collection) also fills a gap in publicly available resources for hepatocellular carcinoma research.
