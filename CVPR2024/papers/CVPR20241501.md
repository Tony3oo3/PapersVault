## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods are designed as specialized architectures for one of five task variants (stochastic, deterministic, momentary observation, domain adaptation, few-shot), and state-of-the-art models for one task suffer significant performance drops when transferred to another. The paper asks whether a single unified predictor can achieve competitive performance across all five tasks without task-specific architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241501/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes historical pedestrian trajectories as 2D coordinate sequences of variable length (T_hist=2 for momentary observation, T_hist=8 for standard tasks) plus a scene image converted to a binary traversability map via off-the-shelf semantic segmentation. It produces future 2D coordinate paths of T_fut=12 frames, either a single deterministic path or S=20 multimodal samples. Unusually, all trajectories are first projected into a fixed-dimensional Singular space (K=4) via SVD-derived motion vectors and Cardinal B-spline interpolation, so the diffusion model operates on low-dimensional motion-flow coefficients rather than raw frame-by-frame coordinates.

## Method

SingularTrajectory unifies all five tasks through three components. First, a Singular space is constructed by applying truncated SVD (K=4) to the entire training dataset to extract orthogonal spatio-temporal motion vectors; trajectories of any length are projected into this space via Cardinal B-spline interpolation of the basis vectors, yielding a length-agnostic representation. Second, an adaptive anchor is formed by clustering future trajectories in Singular space into S centroids (prototype paths), then deforming them using a vector field computed from the traversability map to relocate misplaced prototypes toward walkable regions. Third, a diffusion-based predictor (DDIM scheduler, M=10 steps) performs cascaded denoising of residuals added to the adaptive anchor, conditioned on historical trajectories, environmental prototype paths, and agent interactions encoded by a one-layer transformer (256-dim, 4-head attention), with MSE loss against Gaussian noise for end-to-end training.

## Results

On the standard stochastic prediction benchmark (ETH/UCY, 8-frame input, 12-frame output, 20 samples), SingularTrajectory achieves 0.21/0.32 ADE/FDE, matching the best ADE (NPSN, EqMotion, EigenTrajectory) while delivering the lowest FDE. On the deterministic task, it achieves 0.47/0.90 ADE/FDE, a ~10% improvement over the next best (Trajectron+ at 0.52/1.11). In domain adaptation, it reaches 0.48/0.94 ADE/FDE average, outperforming the best specialized method (Adaptive meta-learning at 0.59/1.22) by ~19% on ADE. For momentary observation (2-frame input) and few-shot (10% data), it achieves 0.25/0.40 and 0.23/0.35 ADE/FDE respectively, both state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SingularTrajectory achieves the best or tied-best ADE/FDE across all five tasks. In stochastic prediction, it ties the top ADE (0.21) shared by NPSN, EqMotion, EigenTrajectory, SocialVAE, and LED, but delivers the lowest FDE (0.32 vs. 0.33–0.36), indicating more accurate endpoint predictions. In deterministic prediction, it clearly outperforms all baselines, beating Trajectron+ (0.52/1.11) and STAR-D (0.53/1.14) by ~10% on both metrics. In domain adaptation, it surpasses the best specialized method (Adaptive meta-learning [24] at 0.59/1.22) by 19% on ADE and 23% on FDE, and dominates in the hardest cross-domain pairs (e.g., B2A: 1.16 vs. 1.85 FDE for Adaptive). For momentary observation, it beats MOE-Traj++ [77] (0.31/0.56) by 19% on ADE and 29% on FDE, and matches EigenTrajectory [6] on ADE while improving FDE (0.40 vs. 0.42). In few-shot (10% data), it ties EigenTrajectory on ADE (0.23) but improves FDE (0.35 vs. 0.39), and outperforms the diffusion-based MID [18] by 29% on ADE (0.23 vs. 0.32). Notably, it does not lose to any baseline on any task, and its advantage is most pronounced in deterministic and domain adaptation settings where task-specific architectures struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EigenTrajectory line of work [6], which introduced SVD-based low-rank descriptors and fixed anchor paths for multimodal trajectory prediction, by replacing the fixed anchor with an environment-adaptive anchor that corrects prototype paths using a traversability map. It also departs from prior diffusion-based predictors (MID [18], LED [48]) that generate trajectories from pure Gaussian noise, instead performing cascaded denoising of residuals relative to the adaptive anchor. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'Singular space'—a unified embedding via truncated SVD with Cardinal B-spline interpolation—that projects trajectories of arbitrary lengths (from 2-frame momentary to 8-frame stochastic) into a common coordinate system, enabling a single architecture to serve all five task variants without task-specific modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real fragmentation problem in trajectory prediction where state-of-the-art models for one task variant (e.g., stochastic) degrade significantly on others (e.g., momentary or domain adaptation), and demonstrates that a unified SVD-based representation plus diffusion refinement can close much of this gap. However, all experiments are confined to the small ETH/UCY pedestrian datasets (5 scenes, ~1,500 trajectories), leaving open whether the Singular space approach generalizes to larger-scale, more diverse agent populations (autonomous driving, multi-modal agents) or 3D settings. A practical limitation is the reliance on an off-the-shelf semantic segmentation model for the traversability map, which introduces a dependency and potential failure mode in unseen environments. The finding that only K=4 singular vectors suffice across all tasks suggests the human motion manifold is remarkably low-dimensional, which could inform more efficient representations in related fields.
