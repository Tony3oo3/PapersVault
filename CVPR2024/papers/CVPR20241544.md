## Problem

High-resolution image generation is increasingly centralised behind paywalls because training at higher resolutions demands prohibitive hardware investments (e.g., 256 A100 GPUs for 20 days for SD 1.5 at 512²). Existing open-source LDMs are capped at their training resolution (1024² for SDXL), and naive approaches—direct prompting at higher resolution, super-resolution cascades, or simple MultiDiffusion patch fusion—fail to produce coherent, detail-rich high-resolution images. The paper asks whether existing LDMs already contain untapped prior knowledge for higher-resolution generation that can be unlocked without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image at 4×, 16×, or higher magnification relative to the base LDM's native resolution (e.g., 4096² from SDXL's 1024²). It is a plug-and-play inference-time modification requiring no fine-tuning and runs on a single consumer-grade RTX 3090 GPU. A practical side effect of the progressive design is that intermediate low-resolution results are available within seconds as previews, enabling rapid prompt iteration before committing to the full high-resolution generation (which takes ~25 min at 4096²).

## Method

DemoFusion is a tuning-free, inference-time extension to pre-trained LDMs (SDXL, SD 1.5, SD 2.1) that builds on MultiDiffusion's overlapped patch denoising but introduces three complementary mechanisms. (1) Progressive Upscaling replaces direct high-resolution synthesis with an iterative 'upsample–diffuse–denoise' loop: the base-resolution image is interpolated, re-noised via a forward diffusion step, and denoised at the next scale, gradually filling in local detail. (2) Skip Residual injects noise-inversed intermediate latents from the preceding phase as a weighted guidance signal during denoising, with a scaled cosine decay (α₁) that fades the global constraint over time so later steps can refine local detail. (3) Dilated Sampling augments MultiDiffusion's local patch paths with global denoising paths obtained by dilated (strided) sampling of the full latent at factor s, fused with local paths via a second cosine decay weight (α₂); a Gaussian filter with time-varying standard deviation is applied before dilated sampling to suppress graininess from independent global paths. Together these three components provide both global semantic coherence and rich local detail without any parameter updates.

## Results

On LAION-5B (1K captions), DemoFusion achieves the best overall performance across FID, IS, FIDcrop, IScrop, and CLIP score at all three tested resolutions (2048×2048, 2048×4096, 4096×4096). At 4096×4096, DemoFusion scores FID 74.11, IS 16.11, FIDcrop 70.34, IScrop 24.28, and CLIP 29.57, outperforming all baselines on local-detail metrics (FIDcrop, IScrop) while remaining competitive on global metrics. Inference time on a single RTX 3090 ranges from 3 min (2048×2048) to 25 min (4096×4096), compared to 1 min for SDXL+BSRGAN but with substantially richer local detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DemoFusion outperforms SDXL Direct Inference on every metric at all three resolutions, with the gap widening as resolution increases (e.g., FID drops from 105.65 to 74.11 at 4096²). Against MultiDiffusion, DemoFusion consistently wins on both global (FID, IS) and local (FIDcrop, IScrop) metrics, confirming that dilated sampling and skip residual resolve the semantic incoherence and repetition issues of naive patch fusion. Compared to SDXL+BSRGAN, DemoFusion achieves substantially better local-detail scores (FIDcrop 70.34 vs 77.20, IScrop 24.28 vs 22.42 at 4096²) but trails on global FID/IS because BSRGAN's output is inherently consistent with the low-res input that these downsampled metrics favour; BSRGAN is also ~25× faster. Against the concurrent SCALECRAFTER, DemoFusion wins on FIDcrop and IScrop at all resolutions (e.g., 70.34 vs 84.36 at 4096²) and on global FID at 4096² (74.11 vs 87.50), while SCALECRAFTER is faster at 2048² (1 min vs 3 min) but slower at 4096² (19 min vs 25 min). Overall, DemoFusion is the only method that simultaneously achieves top or near-top scores on both global coherence and local detail metrics across all resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DemoFusion builds directly on MultiDiffusion's idea of fusing multiple overlapped denoising paths, but departs from it by addressing the critical weakness of semantic incoherence and repetitive content in object-centric generation. It positions itself against two alternative strategies: super-resolution cascading (e.g., SDXL+BSRGAN), which the authors argue cannot synthesize genuinely new high-resolution detail, and concurrent tuning-free methods like SCALECRAFTER that dilate convolutional kernels. The key conceptual departure is the observation that LDMs trained on cropped images already encode high-resolution priors, and that these can be unlocked through a progressive 'upsample-diffuse-denoise' loop augmented with skip residuals and dilated sampling—requiring zero additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pushing the practical frontier from 1024² to 4096²+ on a single consumer GPU with no fine-tuning, DemoFusion directly challenges the centralisation of high-resolution GenAI behind corporate paywalls. However, the approach inherits MultiDiffusion's exponential time cost (25 min for 4096²), and its quality is bounded by the underlying LDM's training data—local irrationality in close-ups and small-object repetition in sparse backgrounds remain unresolved. An open question the authors raise is whether training a bespoke LDM explicitly for patch-fusion inference could further improve results, and whether the progressive-upscaling + dilated-sampling recipe generalises cleanly to video or 3D generation.
