## Problem

Existing 3D face reconstruction methods struggle with extreme expressions because landmarks become sparse or inaccurate and photometric texture losses cannot directly constrain shape. While facial part segmentation offers dense, lighting-robust geometric supervision, prior attempts to exploit it via differentiable silhouette renderers suffer from local optima, gradient instability, and rendering error propagation, leaving the segmentation geometry underutilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240440/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a single 224x224 RGB face image (cropped and aligned via RetinaFace) and predicts 3DMM parameters (80-dim identity, 64-dim expression, 3-dim rotation, 3-dim translation, 9-dim spherical-harmonic illumination) that define a 35,709-vertex mesh. During training, 2D facial part segmentation masks from DML-CSR (covering left/right eye, eyebrow, upper/lower lip, nose, and skin) serve as auxiliary supervision signals for PRDL rather than as network inputs; occluded or invisible parts are handled by zeroing their loss weight.

## Method

The core contribution is Part Re-projection Distance Loss (PRDL), which replaces differentiable renderers with a point-set distance formulation. For each facial part, the 2D segmentation mask is converted into a target point set, while the re-projected 3D mesh vertices are split into predicted point sets using new semantically-aligned 3D mesh annotations (provided for BFM and FaceVerse). A fixed HxW grid of anchors and three statistical distance functions (nearest, furthest, average) construct geometry descriptor tensors for both sets; PRDL minimizes the L2 difference between these tensors, yielding a clear, interpretable gradient that pulls each predicted vertex toward the correct distance from every anchor. The full training objective combines PRDL with landmark, photometric, perceptual, and regularization losses, using a ResNet-50 backbone. The paper additionally synthesizes 200K+ emotional expression images (closed-eye, open-mouth, frown) via MaskGAN to address data scarcity for extreme expressions, and introduces a Part IoU benchmark that measures 2D overlap of reconstructed parts against ground-truth segmentation.

## Results

On the novel Part IoU benchmark (MEAD, 500 images), the full method achieves 73.82% average overlap IoU across all facial parts, outperforming the strongest baseline HRN (71.69%) by 2.13 points. On the REALY benchmark (100 scanned faces), it attains the best average 3D reconstruction error of 1.436 mm (frontal-view) and 1.442 mm (side-view), compared to HRN's 1.537 mm and 1.468 mm. Ablations confirm that PRDL alone (without synthetic data) already surpasses all baselines (72.22% Part IoU, 1.468 mm frontal), and adding the synthetic expression dataset yields the final gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six 3DMM-based baselines on both benchmarks. Against the closest competitor HRN [25], it gains +2.13 points in Part IoU (73.82 vs 71.69) and reduces REALY frontal error by 0.10 mm (1.436 vs 1.537). Against Deep3D [11], the margin is larger: +4.31 Part IoU and −0.22 mm frontal error. DECA [14] and PRNet [13] trail by over 10 and 10 Part IoU points respectively, with DECA also showing the worst REALY side-view error (2.107 mm). Against renderer-based geometric guidance (SoftRas [33], DIB-R [8], ReDA [56]), PRDL produces visibly sharper part alignment in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 8), attributed to its clear per-anchor gradients versus the renderers' gradient instability. Against point-driven alternatives (ICP [54], Chamfer Distance [53]), PRDL's use of multiple statistical distances (min, max, average) and a full grid of anchors avoids the local-optima trap of nearest-neighbor-only formulations (Fig. 9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the direct successor to 3DDFA-v2 [17], inheriting its 3DMM fitting framework, ResNet-50 backbone, and overall training pipeline, while also drawing on the photometric and perceptual loss formulations from Deep3D [11] and DECA [14]. The key conceptual departure is in how facial part segmentation is exploited: prior renderer-based approaches (SoftRas [33], DIB-R [8], ReDA [56]) use differentiable rasterization with IoU loss, which the authors argue suffers from gradient instability and local optima. Instead, PRDL reframes the problem as matching statistical distance descriptors (min, max, mean) from a fixed grid of anchors to two point sets, sidestepping the renderer entirely. The synthetic data pipeline repurposes MaskGAN [24] for expression augmentation, and the segmentation backbone is DML-CSR [55].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Part IoU benchmark addresses a genuine gap: 3D error metrics (e.g., REALY) can disagree with 2D part alignment, which is what matters for VR/AR applications like emoji and makeup. The 200K+ synthetic expression dataset (closed-eye, open-mouth, frown) fills a data scarcity that limits extreme-expression reconstruction across the field. However, the method is tightly coupled to 3DMM parameterization and a specific segmentation model (DML-CSR), and the performance gains over strong baselines like HRN are modest (73.82% vs 71.69% Part IoU; 1.436mm vs 1.537mm REALY). Open questions include whether the anchor-based statistical distance formulation generalizes to non-parametric mesh representations or to video-based reconstruction where temporal consistency matters.
