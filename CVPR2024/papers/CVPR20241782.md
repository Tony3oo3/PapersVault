## Problem

Existing monocular metric depth estimation (MMDE) methods achieve high accuracy only within their training domains and fail to generalize even under moderate domain shifts. Prior generalizable MMDE approaches still require known camera intrinsics at test time, limiting their applicability to uncontrolled, in-the-wild scenarios. UniDepth addresses the more demanding 'universal MMDE' task: predicting metric 3D points from a single image with no external information about the scene or camera.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as its sole input and produces, for every pixel, a metric 3D point expressed in a pseudo-spherical coordinate system (azimuth θ, elevation φ, log-depth z_log). The camera module internally predicts a dense H×W×2 angular camera representation (azimuth and elevation per pixel), which is embedded via 81-term Spherical Harmonic Encoding to condition the depth module. The final 3D output is the concatenation of the predicted camera rays and the exponentiated depth, yielding an H×W×3 tensor. Optionally, external dense camera representations can substitute the self-predicted ones for improved peak accuracy.

## Method

UniDepth comprises an encoder backbone (ViT-L or ConvNext-L), a self-promptable Camera Module, and a Depth Module. The Camera Module predicts multiplicative residuals to a pinhole initialization (Δf_x, Δf_y, Δc_x, Δc_y), backprojects pixel coordinates to obtain dense azimuth/elevation angles, and embeds them via Laplace Spherical Harmonic Encoding (degree ≤ 8, 81 harmonics) to produce conditioning tokens. These embeddings condition the depth features through a cross-attention layer, injecting scale prior knowledge and avoiding mode collapse. A geometric invariance loss enforces consistency between camera-conditioned depth features from two geometric augmentations (random rescale + translation) of the same image, using a stop-gradient pseudo-GT formulation. The optimization uses a per-dimension λMSE loss in the pseudo-spherical space (λ_θ=λ_φ=1, λ_z=0.15), which decouples camera and depth gradients by design, plus the invariance loss weighted by α=0.1. A stop-gradient on encoder features flowing to the Camera Module prevents camera supervision from dominating backbone gradients.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation across 8 unseen datasets, UniDepth-V (ViT-L backbone) achieves an average 34.0% improvement in SI_log and 12.3% improvement in δ₁ and F_A over the best prior method. On the standard benchmarks, UniDepth-V attains δ₀.₅ = 98.4 and SI_log = 5.27 on NYU, and δ₀.₅ = 98.6 and SI_log = 5.84 on KITTI Eigen-split, surpassing all in-domain trained methods. The model ranks first on the official KITTI Depth Prediction Benchmark with a 15.5% SI_log improvement over the second-best entry, despite being trained on only ~3M images versus 8M (Metric3D) or 17M (ZeroDepth).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDepth outperforms all seven baselines across all 8 zero-shot test datasets. Against the closest zero-shot competitors, Metric3D [48] and ZeroDepth [20], UniDepth-V improves δ₁ by 5.8% and 7.3% on NYU, and by 1.1% and 9.4% on KITTI, while also achieving substantially lower SI_log (5.27 vs. 9.13 and N/A on NYU; 5.84 vs. 7.28 and N/A on KITTI). On in-domain trained baselines, UniDepth-V surpasses ZoeDepth [3] (the strongest in-domain method on NYU with δ₀.₅=95.2) by 3.2 points and iDisc [34] (best in-domain on KITTI with δ₀.₅=97.5) by 1.1 points, despite never seeing those datasets during training. Traditional MMDE methods (BTS, AdaBins, NeWCRF) show severe degradation in zero-shot settings, with SI_log values 2–3× higher than UniDepth. The only notable weakness is on ETH-3D and IBims-1, where scale-dependent F_A drops (11.8% and 31.4% below best) due to out-of-domain camera types, though scale-invariant SI_log still improves by 36.9% and 28.5% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDepth carves new ground by defining 'universal MMDE'—predicting metric 3D points from a single image with no external information whatsoever—departing from both traditional MMDE methods (AdaBins, iDisc, NeWCRF) that assume matched training/testing domains and known intrinsics, and generalizable MMDE methods (Metric3D, ZeroDepth) that still require camera intrinsics at test time. Architecturally, it repurposes standard ViT/ConvNext backbones with DINO or ImageNet pretraining but introduces a novel pseudo-spherical output space (azimuth, elevation, log-depth) that orthogonally disentangles camera and depth optimization, a self-promptable camera module using spherical harmonic encoding, and a geometric invariance loss enforcing cross-view feature consistency. The paper does not build on a specific prior architecture but rather rethinks the output representation and conditioning mechanism from first principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve universal MMDE without any test-time assumptions, and its zero-shot performance surpasses in-domain trained SotA on both KITTI and NYU, suggesting that architectural design (pseudo-spherical space, camera self-prompting) can compensate for domain diversity more effectively than simply scaling training data. The self-prompting camera mechanism provides a practical dual-mode capability: robust camera-free inference when intrinsics are unknown or noisy, and peak performance when clean intrinsics are available. Limitations include degraded scale-dependent metrics on certain out-of-domain cameras (ETH3D, IBims-1), suggesting domain-specific fine-tuning would still help, and the approach has not been validated on video or dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the pseudo-spherical representation generalizes to other 3D perception tasks and whether the geometric invariance loss can be extended to multi-view or temporal settings.
