## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models produce high-quality images but require dozens of iterative sampling steps, making them impractical for real-time or consumer deployment. Existing one-step distillation methods either depend on large text-image datasets for training or employ complex multi-stage schedules, and none achieve quality comparable to the multi-step teacher without any image supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242581/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model takes a random Gaussian noise vector z (in the latent space of Stable Diffusion's autoencoder, corresponding to 512×512 images) and a text prompt y, and outputs a single clean latent x̂₀ in one forward pass. Training requires only 2M deduplicated text captions from JourneyDB—no image data at all. The student is re-parameterized so that its output is the predicted clean latent rather than the noise, bridging the domain gap with the pretrained noise-prediction architecture.

## Method

SwiftBrush repurposes Variational Score Distillation (VSD) from ProlificDreamer's text-to-3D NeRF optimization by replacing the NeRF rendering function with a one-step text-to-image student model f_θ(z,y). Two teachers operate in parallel: a frozen pretrained Stable Diffusion teacher ε_ψ and a trainable LoRA-adapted teacher ε_φ (rank 64). The student is updated via the VSD gradient—proportional to the difference between the two teachers' noise predictions on a noised version of the student's output—while the LoRA teacher is alternately fine-tuned with standard diffusion loss on the student's outputs to track the evolving student distribution. This alternating scheme, combined with classifier-free guidance (scale 4.5) on both teachers and a re-parameterization that converts the student's noise-prediction output into an x̂₀ prediction, enables stable one-step distillation using only text prompts over ~65K iterations.

## Results

On the COCO-2014 30K benchmark, SwiftBrush achieves an FID of 16.67 and a CLIP score of 0.29 in a single inference step, substantially outperforming Guided Distillation (FID 37.3), LCM (FID 35.56), and BOOT (FID 48.20). On the HPSv2 human-preference benchmark, SwiftBrush attains the best scores across all four categories (Anime 26.91, Photo 27.21, Concept Art 26.32, Paintings 26.37), approaching the 25-step Stable Diffusion teacher (e.g., Photo 26.89). Notably, SwiftBrush is the first one-step distilled model to be trained entirely without image data, using only 2M text prompts from JourneyDB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwiftBrush decisively outperforms Guided Distillation (FID 37.3→16.67, CLIP 0.27→0.29), LCM (FID 35.56→16.67, CLIP 0.24→0.29), and BOOT (FID 48.20→16.67, CLIP 0.26→0.29) on COCO-30K, with the largest margin over BOOT. Against InstaFlow, SwiftBrush trades a slightly higher FID (16.67 vs. 13.10) for a better CLIP score (0.29 vs. 0.28) and superior HPSv2 scores in all four categories (e.g., Photo 27.21 vs. 26.32), while requiring a single end-to-end training stage rather than InstaFlow's 4-stage pipeline. On HPSv2, SwiftBrush leads all one-step baselines by 0.5–1.0 points, closing the gap to the 25-step SD teacher to within ~0.3–1.1 points. The user study (400 raters, 30 prompts) confirms SwiftBrush is preferred over InstaFlow for both image fidelity and text-to-image accuracy. A key differentiator is that SwiftBrush requires no training images—only text prompts—whereas Guided Distillation, LCM, and InstaFlow all depend on large text-image datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwiftBrush directly repurposes Variational Score Distillation (VSD) from ProlificDreamer's text-to-3D pipeline, replacing the NeRF rendering function g(θ,c) with a one-step text-to-image generator f_θ(z,y) and dropping the camera-view conditioning. This positions it as a conceptual bridge between the text-to-3D literature (DreamFusion, Score Jacobian Chaining, ProlificDreamer) and the one-step diffusion distillation line (Guided Distillation, LCM, InstaFlow, BOOT), from which it departs by eliminating the need for any training images. The auxiliary LoRA teacher and the student re-parameterization (converting noise-prediction output to x̂₀ form) are practical adaptations borrowed from and extended beyond the 3D setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a one-step text-to-image generator can be distilled without a single training image, SwiftBrush opens a path for distillation in data-scarce or data-restricted settings where prior methods (InstaFlow, LCM, Guided Distillation) are inapplicable. The main limitations are that the current design supports only single-step generation (no few-step trade-off), quality still trails the 25-step teacher, and the method requires two teacher models (frozen SD + trainable LoRA), increasing training cost. Open questions include whether the VSD-based distillation extends to few-step students, whether a single-teacher variant is feasible, and whether the approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion to other diffusion backbones or modalities.
