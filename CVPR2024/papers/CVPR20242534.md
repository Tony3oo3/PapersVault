## Problem

Neural Radiance Fields excel at interpolating between dense input views but degrade severely when extrapolating beyond them, producing blurry and detail-deficient results in disoccluded regions and areas outside the original viewing volume. Prior NeRF-based view extrapolation work is largely confined to object-centric settings, leaving scene-level extrapolation from a small number of (typically six or fewer) sparse views as an open and practically important problem for live capture scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242534/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse set of RGB images (typically 3–6), their camera poses, and dense depth maps computed via SPARF (with early stopping at 30K iterations) for geometric supervision. It outputs a fully trained neural radiance field (Instant-NGP backbone with scene contraction) that renders photo-realistic novel views from extrapolated viewpoints, including inpainted disoccluded regions within the original volume and outpainted content beyond it, along with completed depth maps for each virtual view produced by a MiDaS-v3-based depth completion network.

## Method

ExtraNeRF is a three-stage pipeline that couples NeRF with two scene-specific diffusion models. First, a BaseNeRF is trained on the sparse inputs with RGB and depth losses plus distortion and hash-decay regularization. Second, a visibility-aware inpainting stage iteratively renders virtual views from the NeRF, computes a per-pixel visibility mask by ray-marching transmittance toward each training view (requiring ≥2 views for reliable geometry), and uses a DreamBooth-fine-tuned latent diffusion inpainter to fill only the unobserved regions, backpropagating the inpainted supervision into the NeRF. Third, a second fine-tuned diffusion enhancement model—trained on deliberately corrupted NeRF renderings to mimic blur and color drift—sharpen and corrects the inpainted regions without masking intact pixels. Both diffusion models are fine-tuned per-scene on the input imagery using LoRA-adapted text encoders, and the iterative virtual-view optimization bootstraps each diffusion step from the continuously improving NeRF to ensure cross-view consistency.

## Results

On the LLFF view extrapolation benchmark (6 training views, 8 test views), ExtraNeRF achieves the best score across all five metrics: PSNR 20.76, SSIM 0.688, LPIPS 0.269, KID 0.0154, and MUSIQ 54.13. In the few-shot view synthesis protocol (3 training views), it reaches PSNR 21.17, SSIM 0.719, and LPIPS 0.264, outperforming the next-best method (Sparf) by ~1 dB in PSNR and ~0.09 in SSIM. On Tanks & Temples, ExtraNeRF leads in LPIPS (0.312 vs. 0.518 for Sparf) and MUSIQ (40.85 vs. 36.07), though it trails *SPIn-NeRF on KID (0.065 vs. 0.042), which the authors attribute to the small dataset size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExtraNeRF outperforms all six baselines on every metric in the primary LLFF view extrapolation table (Table 1). The closest competitor is *SDS (DreamFusion score distillation), which trails by 0.20 dB in PSNR and 2.78 points in MUSIQ; *SPIn-NeRF is second on KID (0.0156 vs. 0.0154) and MUSIQ (51.84 vs. 54.13). Sparf and FreeNeRF, the leading sparse-view NeRF methods, are competitive on PSNR (~20.2–20.4) but fall significantly behind on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.324–0.329 vs. 0.269; MUSIQ ~40 vs. 54.13), reflecting their inability to synthesize coherent content in disoccluded regions. DiffusioNeRF, which also uses a diffusion prior, is limited by its patch-based receptive field and scores lower on LPIPS (0.296) and MUSIQ (50.03). On Tanks & Temples, ExtraNeRF wins on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and MUSIQ but loses to *SPIn-NeRF on KID (0.065 vs. 0.042), a gap the authors attribute to the small test set making KID estimates unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExtraNeRF builds on NeRF as the underlying 3D representation and latent diffusion models as the generative prior, combining them in a visibility-aware multi-stage pipeline. It departs from prior NeRF extrapolation work (largely object-centric) by tackling scene-level view extrapolation, and from diffusion+NeRF methods like DiffusioNeRF by using full-image diffusion with explicit 3D visibility tracking rather than patch-based supervision. The per-scene fine-tuning strategy follows DreamBooth, and the depth supervision leverages Sparf's sparse-view depth estimation, but the key conceptual contribution is the two-stage inpaint-then-enhance design grounded in NeRF-computed visibility masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in NeRF-based reconstruction: the inability to render convincingly outside the observed viewing volume, which is critical for live-capture settings with only a handful of input views. The visibility-aware masking provides a principled way to separate observed from unobserved 3D content, grounding the generative process in geometry rather than relying purely on 2D priors. Limitations include the per-scene fine-tuning overhead for both diffusion models, the acknowledged KID underperformance on Tanks & Temples (attributed to small test sets), and the method's focus on static scenes. Open questions include whether the two-stage design can be unified, whether per-scene fine-tuning can be replaced by a more general prior, and how the approach scales to larger or dynamic scenes.
