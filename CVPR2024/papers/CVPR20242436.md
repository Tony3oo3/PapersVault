## Problem

Existing LiDAR-based human pose and shape estimation methods (LiDARCap, MOVIN, LIP) cannot predict the full set of SMPL parameters (pose, shape, and global translation) simultaneously, and they are fragile to the distribution variations, self-occlusion, and noise inherent in real-world LiDAR point clouds captured at varying distances and in uncontrolled environments. This limits their applicability to large-scale, in-the-wild scenarios where optical and inertial methods fail due to lighting sensitivity or the need for wearable devices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a consecutive sequence of T single-person LiDAR point clouds as input, with each frame resampled to a fixed 256 points via farthest point sampling and centered by subtracting the mean location. It outputs full SMPL parameters: 6D-rotation-based pose (θ ∈ R^{6N_J}), shape (β ∈ R^{10}), and global translation (Tr ∈ R³), from which the SMPL model generates joint positions and mesh vertices. The pipeline achieves real-time performance at 45 fps on a single LiDAR sensor.

## Method

LiveHPS comprises three sequential stages: (1) a Point-based Body Tracker that extracts point-wise features via a PointNet-GRU backbone and predicts joint positions, augmented by a Vertex-guided Adaptive Distillation (VAD) module that aligns diverse LiDAR point distributions with regular SMPL mesh vertex distributions in high-level feature space using a pose-prior consistency loss; (2) a Consecutive Pose Optimizer (CPO) that refines joint positions through temporal attention across frames and spatial attention across joints to exploit motion coherence and geometric constraints; (3) a Multi-head SMPL Solver with attention-based pose/shape heads (sharing CPO's architecture) and a Skeleton-aware Translation Solver that uses cross-attention between refined joints and the raw point cloud to predict a translation bias added to the point cloud centroid, avoiding error accumulation from velocity integration. A novel Scene-level Unidirectional Chamfer Distance (SUCD) loss provides end-to-end geometric supervision from the raw point cloud to predicted mesh vertices.

## Results

On the FreeMotion dataset, LiveHPS achieves a joint/vertex error (PST) of 119.27/128.61 mm, substantially outperforming LiDARCap (186.55/196.06 mm), LIP (129.88/141.77 mm), and MOVIN (2853.87/2863.89 mm). The scene-level unidirectional Chamfer distance (SUCD) is 2.85 mm for LiveHPS versus 5.00 mm (LiDARCap), 4.24 mm (LIP), and 9252.86 mm (MOVIN). On SURREAL, LiveHPS achieves the lowest pose-only joint/vertex error of 23.99/30.81 mm and SUCD of 0.67 mm, compared to LIP's 31.72/42.40 mm and 0.95 mm. Cross-dataset evaluation on LiDARHuman26M, LIPD, CIMI4D, SemanticKITTI, and HuCenLife further confirms consistent SOTA performance without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiveHPS outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. Against LiDARCap, LiveHPS reduces the FreeMotion J/V Err(PST) by 36% (119.27 vs. 186.55 mm) and SUCD by 43% (2.85 vs. 5.00 mm), while also improving Ang Err from 16.79° to 15.79°. Against LIP, the gains are more modest but consistent: J/V Err(PST) improves from 129.88 to 119.27 mm (8% reduction) and SUCD from 4.24 to 2.85 mm (33% reduction) on FreeMotion; on SURREAL, LiveHPS achieves 34.45/40.14 mm J/V Err(PST) versus LIP's 45.22/53.18 mm. MOVIN is the weakest baseline, with catastrophic translation errors (SUCD of 9252.86 mm on FreeMotion and 3.3M mm on Sloper4D) due to its velocity-regression-based translation approach failing on synthetic data and noisy real scenes. Notably, LiveHPS is the only method that estimates full SMPL parameters (pose, shape, and translation) from a single LiDAR, whereas LiDARCap and LIP only predict pose, and MOVIN predicts pose and translation but not shape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LiDAR-based human pose estimation line initiated by LiDARCap (GRU-based, pose-only) and MOVIN (CVAE, pose+translation) by being the first to recover full SMPL parameters (pose, shape, and global translation) from a single LiDAR in unconstrained environments. Architecturally, it builds on the PointNet-GRU backbone from LIP [42] but replaces its GRU-based pose-guided corrector with an attention-based Consecutive Pose Optimizer that jointly models temporal motion consistency and spatial joint-to-joint geometric constraints. The key conceptual departure is the Vertex-guided Adaptive Distillation (VAD) mechanism, which uses SMPL mesh vertices as a structural prior to regularize point cloud feature extraction against distribution shifts across capture distances and devices — a problem prior LiDAR-HPS methods largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FreeMotion dataset (578k frames, multi-person, multi-modal LiDAR/RGB/IMU, full SMPL annotations in real outdoor scenes) fills a critical gap: no prior LiDAR motion dataset provided shape and translation annotations, and none captured multi-person interactions with natural occlusions. The SUCD metric offers a more holistic evaluation than joint-position error alone by measuring scene-level geometric fidelity. The stated limitation — misjudged global orientation for static humans — highlights an open problem in leveraging temporal dynamics when motion is absent, and the 45 fps real-time performance makes the approach immediately deployable for robotics and autonomous driving. Broader open questions include whether the distillation strategy generalizes to other sparse-sensor modalities and whether multi-person interactions can be handled without per-person segmentation.
