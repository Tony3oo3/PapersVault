## Problem

Existing human free-viewpoint rendering methods face a fundamental trade-off: real-time approaches like DDC produce blurred details due to the ambiguous pose-to-appearance mapping, while higher-fidelity methods like HDHumans hallucinate plausible but incorrect details and are too slow for interactive use. No prior method simultaneously achieves real-time 4K rendering, sparse input (few cameras), faithful reproduction of fine dynamic details (wrinkles, expressions, hand gestures), and support for loose clothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241551/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes exactly four sparse RGB camera views, the corresponding 3D skeletal pose, and the target virtual camera parameters, producing a full 4112×3008 (4K) novel-view rendering in real-time (~22–25 FPS on dual A100 GPUs). At training, it requires dense multi-view video (120 synchronized 4K cameras) and a rigged static 3D surface scan of the actor. The output is a complete high-resolution image (not just a character cutout), with the SRNet explicitly trained to recover border details by omitting the matting mask on the super-resolved output.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages operating on an explicit deformable mesh. Stage 1 uses a structure-aware GNN (embedded graph for coarse rotation/translation + per-vertex displacement network) to pose a template mesh from skeletal motion, improved with a Chamfer loss against neural SDF reconstructions and hand-specific parameter zeroing to fix high-articulation artifacts. Stage 2 projects the four sparse views onto the mesh UV space via inverse texture mapping with visibility and angle-based validity masks, then a TexFeatNet (texture-to-texture CNN) fuses the partial texture with temporally stacked posed normals and per-texel camera direction to produce a view-dependent dynamic texture plus 72 auxiliary feature channels. Stage 3 renders the textured mesh at low resolution and applies a shallow 2D CNN (SRNet) to super-resolve to 4K, removing texel-discretization noise and mesh boundary artifacts. The key design insight is learning appearance in 2D texture/image space rather than 3D, using the explicit mesh as a 3D-consistency proxy while exploiting 2D convolutions for real-time efficiency, and using the partial texture to disambiguate the one-to-many skeletal-pose-to-surface-appearance mapping.

## Results

On the DynaCap dataset, Holoported Characters achieves PSNR 33.93 / LPIPS 12.98 / FID 6.28 (novel view) and PSNR 30.24 / LPIPS 23.74 / FID 11.62 (novel pose) on subject S1 (tight clothing), and PSNR 31.45 / LPIPS 21.86 / FID 5.95 (novel view) and PSNR 28.03 / LPIPS 28.49 / FID 13.26 (novel pose) on subject S2 (loose clothing). All metrics are reported at 1K resolution for fair comparison; the method runs in real-time at 4K (4112×3008) on dual A100 GPUs (~22–25 FPS per stage). The advantage over real-time baselines is most pronounced on the loose-clothing subject S2, where FID drops from 47–144 (baselines) to 5.95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDC [6], the closest real-time animatable baseline, Holoported Characters wins on all three metrics for both subjects; the gap is largest on S2 (loose clothing) where FID improves from 47.23 to 5.95 and LPIPS from 36.33 to 21.86, reflecting DDC's inability to capture high-frequency wrinkles. Against ENeRF [17], a general-scene image-driven method, the method achieves +3.2 dB PSNR and roughly 2× lower LPIPS on S1, with ENeRF additionally suffering multi-view consistency artifacts at far views. Against DVA [28], the most closely related image-driven human method, the advantage is dramatic on S2 (FID 5.95 vs. 144.21), as DVA's volume-primitive formulation degrades on loose apparel and large pose variety. Against HDHumans [7], the non-real-time state-of-the-art in photorealism, Holoported Characters matches or exceeds LPIPS on both subjects (12.98 vs. 14.61 on S1; 21.86 vs. 26.28 on S2) and wins on PSNR by ~3 dB, while HDHumans retains a slight FID edge on S1 (4.93 vs. 6.28) due to its generative (adversarial) formulation producing plausible but not ground-truth-aligned details. Critically, Holoported Characters achieves these results in real-time, whereas HDHumans does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deformable character model of DDC (Habermann et al., 2021) by adding Chamfer-distance supervision from neural SDF reconstructions and hand-specific parameter freezing, improving geometric fidelity for projective texturing. It departs from the dominant neural-implicit (NeRF-based) paradigm for human free-viewpoint rendering (e.g., HDHumans, Neural Actor) in favor of an explicit mesh-and-texture pipeline, arguing that explicit structures enable real-time inference and truthful detail reproduction rather than plausible hallucination. The three-stage architecture—skeleton-driven deformation, projective texturing with a TexFeatNet, and an image-space SRNet—repurposes standard 2D CNN super-resolution in a novel context, combining it with a view-dependent texture generation network that has not been previously integrated into a real-time human rendering pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving real-time 4K free-viewpoint rendering from only four cameras with truthful (not hallucinated) fine details like wrinkles, facial expressions, and hand gestures is a significant practical milestone for immersive telepresence and merged-reality applications. The key limitation is the inability to handle topological changes (e.g., opening a jacket), which the authors identify as requiring multi-layered human representations. An open question is whether the explicit mesh-and-texture formulation can scale to even sparser inputs (e.g., 1–2 cameras) or to multi-person scenes without the per-identity training cost.
