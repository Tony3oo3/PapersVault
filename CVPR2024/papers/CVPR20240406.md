## Problem

Existing fast ODE solvers for diffusion models suffer from significant truncation errors at extremely low NFE budgets (around 5), causing rapid sample quality degradation. Single-step solvers lack the flexibility of multi-step methods to adjust directions, while multi-step solvers require history records. The paper identifies that this limitation stems from heuristic choices of intermediate evaluation points and gradient directions rather than fundamental solver-order constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on text prompts or class labels) and produces RGB images at resolutions from 32×32 to 512×512. The AMED predictor itself is a lightweight 9k-parameter network that consumes the U-Net's bottleneck feature (via channel-wise mean pooling) concatenated with time embeddings to output a scalar r_n and c_n per step. Total sampling cost is 2(N−1) NFE, or N−1 NFE when the analytical first step (AFS) trick is applied, making it practical at 3–9 NFE.

## Method

AMED-Solver exploits the geometric observation that each sampling trajectory lies almost entirely in a two-dimensional subspace of the high-dimensional image space (validated via PCA showing <8% projection error with 2 principal components). This justifies applying the mean value theorem to the PF-ODE integral, yielding a single-step update x_{t_n} ≈ x_{t_{n+1}} + c_n(t_n − t_{n+1}) ε_θ(x_{s_n}, s_n) where the intermediate time s_n and scaling factor c_n are learned rather than fixed heuristically. A small predictor network g_φ is trained via knowledge distillation against teacher trajectories generated with doubled NFE, minimizing L2 distance between student and teacher samples at each time step. The method generalizes to AMED-Plugin, which can be attached to any existing ODE solver (iPNDM, DPM-Solver++, DDIM, etc.) by predicting the same (s_n, c_n) pair to replace the solver's default intermediate evaluation, requiring only 2–8 minutes of additional training on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

At 5 NFE, AMED-Solver achieves 10.74 FID on ImageNet 64×64 and 13.20 FID on LSUN Bedroom 256×256, while AMED-Plugin (applied on iPNDM) achieves 6.61 FID on CIFAR-10 32×32 and 12.49 FID on FFHQ 64×64. On Stable Diffusion (512×512), AMED-Plugin on DPM-Solver++(2M) reduces FID from 14.58 to 13.24 at 20 NFE. These results represent state-of-the-art among solver-based methods at extremely low NFE budgets (3–9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMED-Solver and AMED-Plugin consistently outperform all six baselines at 5 NFE across all four datasets. Against the strongest multi-step baseline iPNDM, AMED-Plugin on iPNDM improves FID by 6.98 on CIFAR-10 (6.61 vs 13.59), 4.68 on ImageNet (10.74 vs 18.99 via AMED-Solver), and 4.68 on FFHQ (12.49 vs 17.17). Against DPM-Solver++(3M) and UniPC, the gains are even larger (e.g., 18.36 FID improvement on CIFAR-10 over DPM-Solver++(3M)). Single-step baselines DDIM, EDM, and DPM-Solver-2 suffer severe quality degradation at low NFE (FID > 40 at 5 NFE on most datasets), while AMED-Solver maintains FID below 15. On LSUN Bedroom 256×256, AMED-Solver (13.20) substantially outperforms AMED-Plugin on DPM-Solver-2 (25.68), suggesting the plugin is less effective when combined with multi-step solvers at high resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PF-ODE sampling framework (Song et al., 2021) and the fast ODE solver line (DPM-Solver, Lu et al., 2022) by replacing numerical discretization with a learned mean-direction approximation justified by the mean value theorem. The key conceptual departure from prior solvers (DDIM, EDM, DPM-Solver, PNDM, DEIS, UniPC) is that instead of approximating the integral via Euler steps, Taylor expansions, or polynomial extrapolation, the method learns a single intermediate time step and scaling factor per step that directly targets the integral's mean value. The geometric observation that sampling trajectories lie in a ~2D subspace (extending Chen et al., 2023) provides the theoretical justification for why the vector-valued mean value theorem can be applied here. The AMED-Plugin further generalizes this idea to be orthogonal to any existing solver, making it a composable improvement rather than a standalone replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time and resource-constrained applications: achieving 6.61 FID on CIFAR-10 and 10.74 on ImageNet 64×64 at only 5 NFE with a 9k-parameter network trained in minutes makes near-photorealistic generation feasible in settings where even 10-step sampling was previously the practical floor. The plugin formulation means existing deployments can adopt the improvement without retraining the base diffusion model. However, the authors acknowledge that fast ODE solvers remain highly sensitive to time schedule choice, and the AMED-Plugin only partially alleviates this by adjusting half the schedule; a principled, geometry-aware time schedule design remains an open problem. The 2D subspace property, while empirically validated, lacks a formal proof, leaving open whether it holds universally across architectures and conditioning regimes.
