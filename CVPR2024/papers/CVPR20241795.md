## Problem

Existing point cloud sampling methods face a fundamental trade-off: task-agnostic methods like FPS rely solely on 3D coordinates and ignore semantic meaning, while learnable methods (S-Net, SampleNet) incorporate semantic information but require task-specific losses and labels, making them infeasible when downstream tasks are unknown at collection time. The paper asks how to design a learnable, task-agnostic, label-free sampling strategy that still captures semantically meaningful points.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense point cloud of n points in R³ and outputs a simplified point cloud of m points (m ≤ n) that is guaranteed to be a subset of the original via nearest-neighbor matching at inference. Notably, the frozen CLIP 2D feature extractor is used only during training for loss computation and is entirely absent at inference, where only the lightweight MLP-based sampling network is deployed. The simplified point clouds are stored as a new dataset and later fed to task-specific backbones (e.g., PointNet) when a downstream task is specified.

## Method

The core insight is to leverage a frozen pretrained 2D feature extractor (CLIP vision branch) as a semantic teacher during training, circumventing the lack of large-scale pretrained 3D models. Dense and simplified point clouds are projected to 10 multi-view depth maps, which are fed through the frozen CLIP encoder to extract semantic features. A novel semantic loss combines an intra-view term (MSE between features of dense vs. simplified clouds under the same view) and an inter-view term (MSE between Gram matrices of concatenated multi-view features) to compensate for projection-induced information loss. This semantic loss is combined with the standard geometric loss (average, worst-case, and spread Chamfer-style terms from S-Net) to train the MLP-based sampling network end-to-end. The approach is task-agnostic because no task-specific labels or losses are needed, yet it outperforms task-specific baselines by transferring rich 2D semantic knowledge into the sampling process.

## Results

On ModelNet40 point cloud classification, LTA-PCS achieves 90.0% instance accuracy at 256 sampled points, matching the oracle (90.3%) at 512 points, while outperforming the best task-agnostic baseline SampleNet* (87.9%) by 2.1% at 256 points. On ShapeNet, LTA-PCS reaches 83.5% at 256 points versus 82.4% for FPS and 82.7% for task-specific S-Net. For point cloud retrieval (mAP on ModelNet40), LTA-PCS achieves 74.8% at 256 points, surpassing SampleNet* (72.6%) and task-specific S-Net (74.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTA-PCS consistently outperforms both task-agnostic baselines (FPS, S-Net*, SampleNet*) and task-specific baselines (S-Net, SampleNet) across all sampled point counts on both ModelNet40 and ShapeNet. On ModelNet40 at 256 points, LTA-PCS (90.0%) beats the strongest task-agnostic baseline SampleNet* (87.9%) by 2.1% and the strongest task-specific baseline S-Net (88.4%) by 1.6%. On ShapeNet at 256 points, LTA-PCS (83.5%) exceeds S-Net (82.7%) and SampleNet (82.7%) by 0.8%. For point cloud retrieval (mAP), LTA-PCS leads all baselines at every point count, e.g., 74.8% vs. 74.0% (S-Net) and 72.6% (SampleNet*) at 256 points. Notably, LTA-PCS is task-agnostic (no task-specific labels needed) yet surpasses task-specific methods, which the authors attribute to the semantic prior injected by the frozen CLIP 2D feature extractor during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on S-Net (Dovrat et al., CVPR 2019) for the sampling network architecture and geometric loss formulation, and on SampleNet (Lang et al., CVPR 2020) as a differentiable sampling baseline. It departs from both by removing the task-specific loss that makes prior learnable samplers task-bound, and instead introduces a frozen pretrained 2D feature extractor (CLIP vision encoder) as a semantic supervision signal via multi-view depth-map projection—an idea borrowed from PointCLIP [55] but repurposed here for training a sampler rather than classifying shapes. The key conceptual shift is treating 2D pretrained knowledge as a task-agnostic proxy for 3D semantics, sidestepping the absence of a large-scale pretrained 3D feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important and underexplored scenario: point clouds are collected and stored before the downstream task is known, so sampling must be task-agnostic yet semantically meaningful. The finding that a task-agnostic sampler can match or exceed task-specific ones (attributed to the richer 2D pretrained prior) challenges the assumption that task-specific supervision is necessary for good sampling. Limitations include the inherent information loss from 3D-to-2D projection, evaluation on relatively small synthetic datasets (ModelNet40, ShapeNet), and the reliance on a specific 2D backbone; whether the approach transfers to large-scale real-world LiDAR scenes or benefits from a future strong 3D pretrained model remain open questions.
