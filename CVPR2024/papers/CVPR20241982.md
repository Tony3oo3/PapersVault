## Problem

Ground-truth optical flow labels are scarce, and while data augmentation and semi-supervised learning have been explored, existing Video Frame Interpolation (VFI) models are restricted to interpolating only the exact middle frame and lack generalizability across domains. Prior forward-warping approaches like RealFlow's RIPR require a depth estimation network with over 300M parameters and computationally expensive EM iterations, limiting their practicality for augmenting optical flow training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241982/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of consecutive video frames (I_0, I_1) and their bidirectional optical flows (V_{0→1}, V_{1→0}) predicted by a pre-trained RAFT network (5.3M parameters). It produces an intermediate frame I_t at any arbitrary time step t∈(0,1), two intermediate optical flows (V_{t→0}, V_{t→1}), and corresponding confidence maps. Unlike standard VFI that only generates the midpoint frame, OCAI can interpolate at any t value, enabling diverse motion ranges for training. The intermediate flows serve dual purposes: they provide supervision signals for the student model and enable confidence-based fusion of warped images.

## Method

OCAI performs occlusion-aware forward warping to generate intermediate frames and flows without depth estimation. It derives an occlusion-aware weighting mask M_0 from forward-backward consistency (replacing RealFlow's depth-based weighting) to resolve pixel ambiguity by selecting foreground pixels closer to the camera. Missing values (holes) in the forward-warped flow maps are filled using the linear motion assumption, where the opposite-direction flow is scaled by the ratio t/(1−t). The two backward-warped images are then fused using confidence maps computed from forward-backward consistency. For training, a teacher-student semi-supervised scheme is employed: the teacher generates interpolated frame-flow pairs at random t values, the student trains on these pairs with confidence-masked loss and semantic distraction augmentation, and the teacher is updated via Exponential Moving Average of the student weights.

## Results

On the KITTI test set, OCAI achieves a Fl-all of 4.13, reducing the error by 0.5 over RealFlow (4.63) and 0.58 over DistractFlow (4.71), while on Sintel test it reaches a Final-epe of 2.63 (warm-start), outperforming DistractFlow (2.71) and FlowSupervisor (2.79). On the semi-supervised train-set evaluation (Table 2), OCAI achieves 2.07 Fl-epe / 7.6 Fl-all on KITTI and 1.20 Clean-epe / 2.32 Final-epe on Sintel, setting new bests across all three benchmarks (SlowFlow, Sintel, KITTI). For video frame interpolation, OCAI attains the best LPIPS (AlexNet) scores on both Sintel (0.078) and KITTI (0.112) with only 5.3M parameters, matching or exceeding SOTA backward-warping methods that require 12–66M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCAI outperforms all six compared semi-supervised optical flow methods on both Sintel and KITTI test sets. Against the closest competitor DistractFlow (CVPR 2023), OCAI reduces Sintel Final-epe from 2.71 to 2.63 and KITTI Fl-all from 4.71 to 4.13 (a 0.58-point gain). Compared to RealFlow (ECCV 2022), which uses an expensive EM framework with depth estimation, OCAI improves KITTI Fl-all from 4.63 to 4.13 while requiring no depth network and fewer training iterations (100k vs. 800k for 4 EM cycles). On the train-set evaluation, OCAI also dominates: it beats DistractFlow by ~1.0 Fl-epe on KITTI (2.07 vs. 3.01) and by ~0.6 Fl-all (7.6 vs. 11.7), and beats RealFlow by 0.09 Fl-epe (2.07 vs. 2.16) and 0.9 Fl-all (7.6 vs. 8.5). For VFI quality, OCAI achieves the best LPIPS (AlexNet) on Sintel 12→24 FPS (0.078 vs. 0.084 for RIPR and 0.087 for AMT-L) and ties RIPR on KITTI (0.112), while using only 5.3M parameters versus 349.5M for RIPR. OCAI's PSNR/SSIM scores are competitive but not always the highest (e.g., EMA-VFI achieves 30.51 PSNR on Sintel vs. OCAI's 29.47), though the authors note PSNR/SSIM do not always reflect perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

OCAI extends the forward-warping VFI lineage initiated by Softmax Splatting (Niklaus & Liu, 2020) and RealFlow's RIPR (Han et al., 2022), replacing the depth-based pixel weighting with an occlusion-aware mask derived from forward-backward consistency (borrowed from UnFlow). It departs from RealFlow's computationally expensive EM training loop by adopting a teacher-student semi-supervised scheme with EMA, and from backward-warping VFI methods (RIFE, IFRNet, AMT) that must predict intermediate flows without access to the inter-frame. The key conceptual shift is treating VFI not as a standalone task but as a data-generation engine for optical flow training, where the intermediate flows themselves provide supervision signals and confidence maps for the student network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for a 300M+ parameter depth estimation network and the iterative EM loop, OCAI makes semi-supervised optical flow training substantially more efficient while achieving the best reported Fl-all on KITTI test (4.13 vs. 4.63 for RealFlow). The occlusion-aware forward warping also produces fewer holes and sharper details than depth-based alternatives, suggesting a more robust path for VFI in the absence of reliable depth. Open questions include whether the linear-motion assumption degrades under very large or non-rigid displacements, how well the approach generalizes to domains beyond Sintel/KITTI without retraining, and whether the teacher-student framework can be extended to other flow architectures beyond RAFT.
