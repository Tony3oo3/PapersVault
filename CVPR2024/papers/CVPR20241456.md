## Problem

No existing method can directly estimate 3D human pose and shape from lensless measurements, which encode scene information as overlapping global patterns rather than spatially resolved images. The natural two-stage pipeline—first reconstructing an RGB image, then running a standard pose estimator—suffers from severe quality degradation in the reconstructed image, producing incomplete local features and large positional deviations that make accurate pose estimation unreliable and computationally expensive for edge deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a single raw lensless measurement (1280×1024×3, pre-processed to 224×224×3) captured through a diffuser-based mask-modulated system, where local scene information is optically encoded into overlapping global patterns on the sensor. It outputs SMPL parameters (pose θ, shape β, and pseudo-camera parameters π̃) that generate a 6890-vertex 3D mesh, sparse 3D keypoints, and projected 2D keypoints. The pseudo-camera parameters are learned jointly to enable projection-based supervision without requiring explicit camera calibration.

## Method

LPSNet is a CNN-based end-to-end framework with three core components. The Multi-Scale Lensless Feature Decoder (MSFDecoder) uses a global perception layer inspired by HRNet, maintaining parallel high- and low-resolution convolution branches to decode the optically encoded global information into multi-scale spatial features at 7×7, 14×14, 28×28, and 56×56 resolutions. A PyMAF-style iterative regressor then projects downsampled SMPL mesh vertices (6890→431) onto these feature maps, extracts point-wise features via bilinear sampling and MLPs, and updates SMPL parameters through residual corrections across scales. During training only, a Double-Head Auxiliary Supervision (DHAS) mechanism concatenates all-scale features into a single map and applies two auxiliary losses: a SimCC-based 2D keypoint classification loss and a DensePose IUV dense correspondence loss (body-part classification + UV regression), which together sharpen limb-end estimation accuracy without affecting inference.

## Results

On the LenslessHuman3.6M dataset, LPSNet achieves an MPJPE of 119.20 mm, PA-MPJPE of 81.52 mm, and PVE of 134.74 mm. Compared to the fine-tuned two-stage baseline (PyMAF†), LPSNet reduces MPJPE by 7.08 mm and PVE by 16.86 mm, while PA-MPJPE is essentially tied (81.52 vs. 81.37 mm). The original unfine-tuned baseline (PyMAF) performs substantially worse, with MPJPE of 257.07 mm and PVE of 277.18 mm, highlighting the difficulty of the two-stage reconstruction-then-estimation pipeline on low-quality recovered images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Since LPSNet is the first end-to-end method for human pose and shape estimation from lensless measurements, the only available comparison is a two-stage baseline that first reconstructs an RGB image from the lensless measurement (using Rego et al.'s PSF-estimation method) and then applies PyMAF for pose/shape regression. Against the unfine-tuned baseline (PyMAF), LPSNet reduces MPJPE from 257.07 to 119.20 mm (−53.6%) and PVE from 277.18 to 134.74 mm (−51.4%), a dramatic improvement driven by avoiding the quality loss of intermediate RGB reconstruction. Against the fine-tuned baseline (PyMAF†, trained on train-LenslessHuman3.6M), LPSNet still wins on MPJPE (119.20 vs. 126.28 mm, −7.08 mm) and PVE (134.74 vs. 151.60 mm, −16.86 mm), while PA-MPJPE is essentially tied (81.52 vs. 81.37 mm). The authors note that PA-MPJPE, being computed after rigid alignment, may understate the true advantage of LPSNet in mesh-image alignment quality. Qualitative results (Fig. 6) confirm that LPSNet produces noticeably better limb alignment than both baselines, particularly for extremities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the iterative mesh-alignment feedback loop from PyMAF and the parallel multi-resolution architecture of HRNet to the novel input modality of lensless measurements, replacing the standard CNN backbone with a purpose-built Multi-Scale Lensless Feature Decoder. The auxiliary supervision heads borrow SimCC's coordinate-classification formulation for 2D keypoints and DensePose's IUV mapping for dense body-part correspondence, repurposing both as training-time regularizers rather than primary outputs. The key conceptual departure from the entire HPS literature (HMR, SPIN, PyMAF, HMR2.0, etc.) is that all prior methods operate on RGB images, whereas LPSNet consumes raw optically-encoded sensor measurements directly, bypassing the conventional two-stage pipeline of image reconstruction followed by pose regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that 3D human pose and shape can be estimated end-to-end from lensless measurements without intermediate RGB reconstruction, opening a path toward ultra-compact, privacy-preserving body-sensing hardware. The introduced lensless HPS datasets (LenslessHuman3.6M, LenslessMPII, LenslessCOCO, Lensless3DPW, LenslessMIP-INF-3DHP) are a necessary resource for a field that previously had no such benchmarks. However, the authors acknowledge that results remain less robust than RGB-based methods, the scarcity of lensless data prevents large-scale pre-training of the decoder, and the quantitative evaluation is concentrated on a single dataset (LenslessHuman3.6M), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to diverse real-world conditions at scale.
