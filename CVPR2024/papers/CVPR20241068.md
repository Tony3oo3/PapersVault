## Problem

Despite the apparent success of diffusion models at scale, their training dynamics suffer from uncontrolled drifts in activation magnitudes, weight magnitudes, and update magnitudes that degrade final image quality and make the architecture fragile to hyperparameter changes. The paper identifies these imbalances in the widely used ADM U-Net and shows they stem from residual signal paths without normalization, the interaction between weight normalization and gradient geometry, and unbalanced contributions from merging branches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241068/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is conditioned on a class label and a noise level, and outputs a 64×64×4 latent tensor that is decoded to a 512×512 RGB image via a pre-trained 8× spatial upsampling decoder (following the latent diffusion paradigm of Stable Diffusion). For ImageNet-64 experiments, the model operates directly in RGB space at 64×64×3. Sampling uses 63 deterministic second-order function evaluations (NFE), substantially fewer than the 250–1000 NFE typical of stochastic samplers in prior work.

## Method

The paper redesigns the ADM U-Net layer-by-layer to preserve activation, weight, and update magnitudes on expectation: (1) learned layers (convolutions, linear) are weight-normalized by dividing each output channel by the L2 norm of its weight vector, eliminating learned magnitude control; (2) a forced weight normalization step resets all weight vectors to unit variance before each optimizer update, preventing the effective learning rate from decaying as weights grow; (3) group normalizations are replaced with much weaker pixel normalizations, and residual connections use fixed weighted sums (30% residual path) with output rescaling; (4) fixed-function layers (SiLU, Fourier features, concatenation) are rescaled to maintain unit-variance activations. An independent contribution introduces a post-hoc EMA method that stores two power-function-averaged parameter snapshots during training, enabling arbitrary EMA-length reconstruction after the run via least-squares fitting, which reveals that optimal EMA length depends strongly on architecture, training duration, and guidance strength.

## Results

On ImageNet-512, EDM2-XXL achieves a new record FID of 1.81 with classifier-free guidance (guidance strength 1.2) and 1.91 without guidance, surpassing the previous best of 2.41 (StyleGAN-XL) and 2.99 (VDM++) respectively. All EDM2 models use fast deterministic sampling with only 63 NFE, compared to 250–1000 NFE for prior methods. On ImageNet-64 (RGB-space diffusion), EDM2-L reaches FID 1.33 with 63 NFE, improving the previous deterministic-sampling record of 2.22 (EDM) by a wide margin. The smallest model EDM2-S (280M params, 102 Gflops) already beats all prior diffusion baselines on ImageNet-512 without guidance (FID 2.56 vs. 2.99 for VDM++).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDM2 dominates all prior diffusion baselines on ImageNet-512. The smallest EDM2-S (280M params) already outperforms VDM++ (FID 2.56 vs. 2.99 no-CFG; 2.23 vs. 2.65 w/ CFG), U-ViT,L (2.56 vs. 3.54), RIN (2.56 vs. 3.95, with 63 vs. 1000 NFE), DiT-XL/2 (2.56 vs. 12.03), and ADM (2.56 vs. 23.24), while using roughly 5× fewer Gflops than most competitors. Scaling to EDM2-XXL sets a new ImageNet-512 record of FID 1.81 (w/ CFG), beating the previous SOTA StyleGAN-XL (2.41) by a 0.60 FID margin, and 1.91 without guidance versus VDM++'s 2.99. On ImageNet-64, EDM2-S improves the deterministic-sampling record from 2.22 (EDM) to 1.58, and EDM2-L reaches 1.33, approaching RIN's stochastic-sampling FID of 1.23 (which requires 1000 NFE vs. 63). Notably, all EDM2 results use deterministic sampling, whereas every prior diffusion baseline relies on stochastic sampling, and the paper notes that stochastic sampling could potentially improve EDM2 results further.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper systematically redesigns the ADM U-Net architecture (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) within the EDM training framework (Karras et al., 2022), drawing on a broad lineage of magnitude-control techniques: weight normalization (Salimans & Kingma, 2016), self-normalizing networks (Klambauer et al., 2017), normalization propagation (Arpit et al., 2016), and StyleGAN2's pixel normalization (Karras et al., 2020). Rather than introducing a new architecture from scratch, it applies a unified magnitude-preservation philosophy to every operation—convolutions, activations, concatenations, and residual sums—while keeping the U-Net structure intact. The post-hoc EMA contribution is independent of the architectural changes and builds on the well-known practice of exponential moving average in generative model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that uncontrolled magnitude drift in activations and weights was silently degrading diffusion model training, and that a principled fix yields a 5× more efficient model that surpasses prior SOTA (FID 1.81 vs. 2.41 on ImageNet-512). The post-hoc EMA technique is broadly practical, enabling dense exploration of EMA hyperparameters at a fraction of the compute cost, and reveals non-obvious interactions with guidance strength and model capacity. Open questions include whether the magnitude-preserving philosophy transfers to transformer-based diffusion architectures (DiT, RIN) and whether stochastic sampling could further close the gap to the best stochastic-sampling results. As a drop-in replacement for the widely adopted ADM, this work is likely to become a new standard baseline for latent diffusion training.
