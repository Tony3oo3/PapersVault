## Problem

While GANs have well-understood latent spaces enabling disentangled editing, diffusion models like Stable Diffusion lack comparable interpretability. Existing methods for exploring diffusion model latent spaces rely on user-provided text prompts to target specific semantics, which is restrictive in domains like art, fashion, or medicine where appropriate prompts are difficult to conceive. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first unsupervised method to discover disentangled latent directions in large text-to-image diffusion models without any text prompts or labeled data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of ~100 unlabeled images from a target domain (e.g., FFHQ faces, AFHQ-Cats, Stanford Cars) and a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 model as input, requiring no text prompts or annotations. It outputs K learned latent directions (100 for faces/paintings, 50 for cats/cars) formulated as conditional embeddings matching the dimensionality of CLIP text embeddings. These directions can be applied simultaneously—within a single domain or across different domains in the same image—to produce disentangled edits on both generated and real images (via DDIM Inversion), with each edit taking ~5 seconds on a single NVIDIA L40 GPU.

## Method

NoiseCLR formulates each latent direction as a learnable conditional embedding and defines feature divergences Δε_k^n = ε_θ(x_t^n, d_k) − ε_θ(x_t^n, φ) capturing how direction d_k alters the denoising network's noise prediction on sample x_n. The contrastive objective (Eq. 5) attracts feature divergences produced by the same direction across different samples (positives) while repelling divergences from different directions on the same sample (negatives), using cosine similarity with temperature τ=0.5. For editing, the method augments the classifier-free-guided noise prediction with a scaled difference term λ_e(ε_θ(x_t, d_e) − ε_θ(x_t, φ)), and multiple directions are composed by summing their respective difference terms (Eq. 8), enabling disentangled multi-direction edits without semantic masks. Training uses AdamW (lr=10⁻³, batch=6) for ~7 hours per domain on a single GPU, and real-image editing is enabled by first inverting the image to x_T via DDIM Inversion before applying the direction-conditioned denoising.

## Results

In a 50-participant user study, NoiseCLR achieves the highest scores in both edit quality (2.65/5) and disentanglement (3.05/5), outperforming Cycle-Diffusion (1.87/2.73), SEGA (1.52/1.81), Composable Diffusion (2.19/2.15), and Unsupervised Concept Discovery (1.20/1.28). On LPIPS (lower is better), NoiseCLR achieves the lowest scores for Mustache (0.17), Gender (0.20), and Race (0.13) edits, though Cycle-Diffusion (0.10) and SEGA (0.11) are slightly lower on the Age edit. A CLIP re-scoring analysis confirms strong disentanglement, with the target attribute showing large positive shifts (e.g., +48.9 for Mustache, +32.8 for Child) while unrelated attributes remain near zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoiseCLR outperforms all four baselines on both user-study metrics. Against Cycle-Diffusion [39], the closest competitor on disentanglement (2.73), NoiseCLR leads by 0.32 points while also surpassing it on edit quality (2.65 vs. 1.87); the paper notes Cycle-Diffusion struggles when multiple semantics are modified simultaneously. Against SEGA [2], NoiseCLR wins by a wide margin on both quality (+1.13) and disentanglement (+1.24), with SEGA also showing higher LPIPS across most edit types. Against Composable Diffusion [20], NoiseCLR improves edit quality by 0.46 and disentanglement by 0.90, and achieves substantially lower LPIPS (e.g., 0.17 vs. 0.40 for Mustache), indicating better content preservation. Against Unsupervised Concept Discovery [21], NoiseCLR dominates on both metrics (2.65 vs. 1.20 quality; 3.05 vs. 1.28 disentanglement), reflecting that concept-level energy functions are coarser than fine-grained latent directions. On LPIPS, NoiseCLR is not uniformly best—Cycle-Diffusion and SEGA achieve slightly lower Age-edit LPIPS (0.10/0.11 vs. 0.17)—but NoiseCLR is clearly superior on the remaining three edit types.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoiseCLR adapts the contrastive learning framework of LatentCLR [43]—originally designed for GAN latent direction discovery—to the diffusion model setting by formulating the contrastive objective over noise estimation differences across multiple diffusion timesteps rather than over GAN latent vectors. It departs from prior diffusion editing methods (Cycle-Diffusion, SEGA, Composable Diffusion) that require user-supplied text prompts to target specific semantics, and from earlier unsupervised diffusion direction discovery work [26] that only succeeded on small single-domain DDPMs. For real image editing, it incorporates DDIM Inversion [24] to initialize the reverse process, extending the editing pipeline beyond generated images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to discover disentangled, composable latent directions in a large-scale text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) without any text prompts or labeled data, opening the door to editing in domains where appropriate prompts are hard to construct (art, fashion, medicine). The ability to apply multiple directions simultaneously—within and across domains—without semantic masks or per-image optimization is practically significant for real-time editing workflows. Key limitations include dependence on Stable Diffusion's training data and CLIP biases (e.g., entanglement between race and age), and the approach has only been validated on SD 1.5 with small per-domain datasets (~100 images), leaving open whether it generalizes to newer architectures or larger direction sets.
