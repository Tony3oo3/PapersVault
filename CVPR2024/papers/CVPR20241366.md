## Problem

Learning unsigned distance fields (UDFs) from multi-view images via volume rendering requires a density function that is simultaneously unbiased (weights peak at zero distance), occlusion-aware (accumulated density diverges at the surface), and bounded. Existing UDF methods (NeuralUDF, NeUDF) cannot satisfy all three conditions at once, producing either choppy surfaces with artifacts or nearly-closed models that fail to represent open boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241366/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (e.g., 72 views at 1024×1024 for DeepFashion3D, studio-captured sequences for DTU) and produces a neural UDF from which a 3D non-watertight mesh is extracted via MeshUDF. Open models (garments, plant, rack) are trained without mask supervision, while watertight models (DTU) use mask supervision. The UDF is a smooth, differentiable scalar field whose zero level-set defines the surface, with non-negative values everywhere.

## Method

2S-UDF decouples the density function and weight function across two stages to sidestep the conflicting requirements. Stage 1 learns a coarse UDF using a simple bell-shaped density σ₁ (inspired by HF-NeuS) with a fixed parameter c=5 that trades slight bias for practical opacity. Stage 2 bypasses the density function entirely, directly learning a weight function w₂ (the derivative of a sigmoid scaled by |cos θ|) that is provably unbiased, while ray truncation—triggered at the first local UDF maximum where accumulated weight exceeds δ_thres=0.5—provides occlusion-awareness without requiring unbounded density. The UDF is represented by an MLP with a softplus output activation (β=100) to ensure differentiable non-negativity, and is trained with color loss, Eikonal loss, an iso-surface regularizer, and an optional penalty on the learnable sharpness parameter s.

## Results

On the DeepFashion3D open-model benchmark (DF3D#Ours, textureless), 2S-UDF achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 5.02×10⁻³, substantially outperforming the best UDF baseline NeUDF (7.36×10⁻³) and SDF baselines NeuS (11.09×10⁻³) and VolSDF (11.64×10⁻³). On the texture-rich DF3D#NeuralUDF split, 2S-UDF reaches 1.96×10⁻³ versus NeUDF's 2.26×10⁻³ and NeuralUDF's 2.34×10⁻³. On the watertight DTU dataset, 2S-UDF (0.71×10⁻³) improves over NeUDF (0.78×10⁻³) by ~9% and is competitive with NeuralUDF (0.69×10⁻³) and NeAT (0.74×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDF-based methods (NeuS, VolSDF), 2S-UDF wins decisively on open models because SDFs inherently close boundaries, inflating Chamfer distances by 2–3× (e.g., 5.02 vs. 11.09 on DF3D#Ours). Among UDF methods, 2S-UDF outperforms NeuralUDF by ~41% on textureless DF3D (5.02 vs. 8.53) and by ~16% on textured DF3D (1.96 vs. 2.34), with the gap attributed to NeuralUDF's complex indicator-function density causing double-layered surfaces and training instability. Versus NeUDF, 2S-UDF improves by ~32% on DF3D#Ours (5.02 vs. 7.36) and ~13% on DF3D#NeuralUDF (1.96 vs. 2.26), and by ~9% on DTU (0.71 vs. 0.78). On the watertight DTU benchmark, NeuralUDF edges out 2S-UDF slightly (0.69 vs. 0.71), which the authors attribute to SDF learning being inherently simpler for closed surfaces; NeAT (0.74) also trails 2S-UDF on DTU. Overall, 2S-UDF achieves the best or near-best Chamfer distance across all three datasets while requiring no mask supervision for open models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UDF-based volume rendering line initiated by NeuralUDF and NeUDF, which attempt to learn unsigned distance fields for non-watertight model reconstruction but must sacrifice at least one of three critical properties (boundedness, unbiasedness, occlusion-awareness) in their density functions. Conceptually, it departs from the single-stage paradigm by borrowing the sigmoid-derivative weight formulation from Neural RGB-D Surface Reconstruction [1] for Stage 2 refinement, while adapting the bell-shaped density from HF-NeuS [36] for Stage 1 coarse learning. The key architectural departure is the explicit decoupling of density (Stage 1) and weight (Stage 2), sidestepping the impossibility of a single UDF-induced density satisfying all three requirements simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage decoupling provides a practical resolution to a recognized open problem in UDF-based rendering, making non-watertight model reconstruction from multi-view images more stable and accurate without requiring mask supervision. However, the method cannot model transparent surfaces due to ray truncation, and the authors themselves recommend SDF-based methods (NeuS, HF-NeuS) for watertight models, limiting the scope to genuinely open geometries. Open questions include whether the two-stage paradigm generalizes to dynamic scenes or sparse-view settings, and whether more robust mesh extraction (e.g., DoubleCoverUDF) would further close the gap with SDF-based reconstruction quality.
