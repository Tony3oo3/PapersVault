## Problem

Existing Free-Viewpoint Video (FVV) construction methods face a fundamental trade-off: NeRF-based approaches require complete video sequences for offline training and cannot stream, while methods that do support online training (e.g., StreamRF) fail to achieve real-time rendering. No prior method simultaneously achieves fast on-the-fly per-frame reconstruction, real-time rendering at megapixel resolution, and competitive image quality for real-world dynamic scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video streams from known-pose cameras (21 cameras at 2704×2028 on N3DV; 13 cameras at 1280×720 on Meet Room, both at 30 FPS) and produces a real-time renderable FVV stream at ~200 FPS. Crucially, the pipeline processes frames sequentially in an online fashion: each new frame triggers a ~12-second two-stage training step, and only the compact NTC parameters and a small set of frame-specific additional 3DGs are stored per frame rather than the full 3DG set, keeping per-frame storage to ~7.6 MB on N3DV.

## Method

3DGStream represents the scene with 3D Gaussians and processes each new timestep through a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1, a Neural Transformation Cache (NTC) — a multi-resolution hash encoding feeding a shallow fully-fused MLP — predicts per-Gaussian translations (3D vector) and rotations (quaternion) from the previous frame's frozen 3DGs, requiring only 150 training iterations and storing a compact model instead of all Gaussians. In Stage 2, an adaptive 3DG addition strategy spawns a limited number of frame-specific Gaussians near high view-space positional gradient regions to capture emerging objects (e.g., flames, poured liquid), with periodic splitting and opacity-based pruning to control their count. A warm-up phase initializes the NTC to near-identity transformations for training stability, and SH coefficients are rotated via spherical-harmonics rotation invariance when Gaussians are reoriented. This design reduces per-frame training to ~12 s and per-frame storage to a few MB while maintaining PSNR competitive with offline methods.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset, 3DGStream achieves 31.67 dB PSNR with 0.20 min/frame training time, 7.6 MB storage, and 215 FPS rendering, outperforming the closest online competitor StreamRF (30.68 dB, 0.25 min, 17.7 MB, 8.3 FPS) across all four metrics. On the Meet Room dataset, the gap widens: 3DGStream reaches 30.79 dB PSNR at 288 FPS with 4.0 MB storage, versus StreamRF's 26.72 dB at 10 FPS with 5.7 MB. The method's PSNR is within 0.4 dB of the best offline methods (HexPlane 31.70, K-Planes 31.63) while requiring only 0.20 min per frame instead of 0.8–2.4 min for the full video, and it renders 25× faster than StreamRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StreamRF [29], the primary online competitor, 3DGStream wins on every metric: +1.0 dB PSNR (31.67 vs 30.68) on N3DV and +4.1 dB (30.79 vs 26.72) on Meet Room, 20% faster per-frame training (0.20 vs 0.25 min), 57% less storage (7.6 vs 17.7 MB), and 26× faster rendering (215 vs 8.3 FPS). Against static per-frame baselines (3DG-S, I-NGP, Plenoxels), 3DGStream trades a small PSNR gap (31.67 vs 32.08 for 3DG-S) for a 41× reduction in per-frame training time (0.20 vs 8.3 min) and 6× less storage (7.6 vs 47.1 MB), while still rendering at 215 FPS. Against offline dynamic methods (HexPlane, K-Planes, HyperReel, MixVoxels), 3DGStream matches or exceeds their PSNR (31.67 vs 31.70/31.63/31.10/30.80) while being the only method that supports real-time streaming at 215 FPS; all offline methods render below 17 FPS. On the Meet Room dataset, 3DG-S per-frame still leads in PSNR (31.31 vs 30.79) but requires 2.6 min training per frame and 21.1 MB storage, whereas 3DGStream trains in 0.10 min with 4.0 MB and renders at 288 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DG-S) from static to dynamic scenes by introducing a Neural Transformation Cache (NTC) that models per-frame 3DG translations and rotations using multi-resolution hash encoding and a shallow fully-fused MLP, drawing directly on I-NGP's hash-grid architecture and the Neural Radiance Caching paradigm. It positions itself as a direct competitor to StreamRF, the prior state-of-the-art for online FVV streaming, but replaces StreamRF's Plenoxel-based representation with 3DGs to inherit their real-time rasterization advantage. The adaptive 3DG addition strategy for emerging objects is a novel component not present in prior streaming methods, which typically rely on residual grids or decomposition modules to handle scene changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving ~12-second per-frame training and 200 FPS rendering at megapixel resolution, 3DGStream makes real-time free-viewpoint video streaming of real-world dynamic scenes practically viable for VR/AR/XR applications, a capability that was previously limited to offline-trained models or sub-real-time streaming methods. The paper acknowledges that it inherits 3DG-S's sensitivity to initial point cloud quality and that the limited per-frame iteration budget constrains modeling of drastic motion and complex emerging objects. Open questions include whether the NTC approach generalizes to longer sequences without error accumulation, how it handles topological changes beyond what adaptive spawning can capture, and whether the method scales to unbounded or very large dynamic scenes.
