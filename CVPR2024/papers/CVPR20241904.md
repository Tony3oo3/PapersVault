## Problem

Large language models achieve remarkable scaling and flexible task specification through prompting, but no equivalent Large Vision Model exists that operates purely from pixels without linguistic data. Prior vision pretraining approaches (MAE, BEiT) struggle to scale to very large datasets, and the heterogeneous structure of visual data (images, videos, diverse annotations) lacks a unified sequential format amenable to autoregressive modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241904/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the model consumes 'visual sentences'—sequences of 1 to 16 images (each 256×256), where each image is tokenized into a 16×16 grid of 256 discrete VQGAN tokens (codebook size 8192), yielding a 4096-token context window. At test time, a partial visual sentence (e.g., 7 video frames, or 7 input-annotation pairs plus a query image) is provided as a prompt, and the model autoregressively generates the next 256 tokens, which are decoded back into a 256×256 image. No special task tokens or format indicators are used; the model infers the task purely from the visual context.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline: (1) a VQGAN tokenizer (f=16 downsampling, 8192-entry codebook, trained on 1.5B LAION images) converts each image independently into 256 discrete semantic tokens; (2) a LLaMA-style causal Transformer (300M–3B parameters) is trained with standard next-token cross-entropy prediction over concatenated token sequences from all visual sentences. The key design choice is the 'visual sentence' abstraction: raw images, video clips, 3D multi-view renderings, and all annotation types (segmentation, depth, edges, keypoints, detection boxes, pose skeletons) are uniformly represented as image sequences with no task-specific tokens, enabling a single autoregressive objective across 420 billion tokens from 1.64 billion images. Inference for downstream tasks is performed by constructing a suitable partial visual sentence prompt and sampling the model's next-token distribution to generate the output image.

## Results

On the main comparison against Visual Prompting [8], LVM achieves mIOU of ~48–51 on foreground segmentation and ~48–50 on single object detection (5-shot, ImageNet val), dramatically outperforming all MAE/MAE-VQGAN-based baselines which score below 31 mIOU. On colorization, LVM achieves MSE 0.51 and LPIPS 0.46, competitive with the best baseline (MAE CVF: MSE 0.43). On Pascal 3D+ keypoint detection (an unseen task), LVM achieves 81.2 PCK without any task-specific training, surpassing dedicated models. Video frame prediction on Kinetics-700 yields a perplexity of 49.8, improving from 62.1 with 1-frame context to 48.4 with 11+ frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Visual Prompting [8] (the closest prior method for arbitrary task specification), LVM wins decisively on foreground segmentation (mIOU ~49.7 vs. 4.3–27.2) and single object detection (mIOU ~49.2 vs. 1.7–25.0) across all four MAE/MAE-VQGAN configurations, with the largest gap over ImageNet-pretrained baselines. On colorization, LVM is competitive: it matches or beats MAE-VQGAN variants on LPIPS (0.46 vs. 0.75) but trails MAE (CVF) slightly on MSE (0.51 vs. 0.43). On Pascal 3D+ keypoint detection, LVM (81.2 PCK) outperforms three dedicated task-specific models: StackedHourglass [60] (68.0), MSS-Net [42] (68.9), and StarMap [101] (78.6), despite never being trained on that dataset. The scaling experiments further show that the 3B model consistently achieves lower perplexity than 300M/600M/1B variants across all four evaluated downstream tasks, confirming that the advantage over baselines grows with model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the LLM recipe—VQGAN tokenization (Esser et al., 2021; Chang et al., 2022), LLaMA-style autoregressive transformer (Touvron et al., 2023), and next-token cross-entropy training (Brown et al., 2020)—entirely into the visual domain, eliminating all linguistic data. It departs from the dominant masked-image-modeling line (MAE, BEiT, SimMIM) which the authors argue struggles to scale to very large datasets, and from inpainting-based Visual Prompting (Bar et al., 2022) which inherits those same scaling limitations. The key conceptual contribution is the 'visual sentence' format, which unifies raw images, video sequences, diverse annotations (segmentation, depth, keypoints, 3D multi-view), and synthetic renderings into a single autoregressive training objective without task-specific tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first credible demonstration that the LLM scaling-and-prompting paradigm transfers to a purely visual, language-free setting, and the UVDv1 dataset (1.64B images, 420B tokens) is a substantial open resource for the community. The clean scaling behavior across 300M–3B parameters and the ability to handle unseen tasks (e.g., 81.2 PCK on Pascal 3D+ without training on it) suggest a viable path toward a single general-purpose vision model. However, performance still lags task-specific models on most benchmarks, the tokenizer introduces quality bottlenecks on out-of-distribution content, and the video training data is limited in quality and diversity—open questions include whether continued scaling closes the gap and whether the autoregressive formulation is truly superior to parallel decoding at this scale.
