## Problem

The most widely used training set for instance-level image retrieval, GLDv2-clean, contains landmark categories that overlap with the standard evaluation sets (ROxford, RParis), causing artificially inflated and inconsistent performance across methods. Additionally, existing detect-to-retrieve (D2R) approaches require complex two-stage training and indexing pipelines with separate location supervision, making them inefficient and difficult to train end-to-end.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240510/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a 2048-dimensional global embedding via GeM pooling for retrieval, along with an intermediate spatial attention map (w×h, normalized to [0,1]) and a sequence of T binary localization masks. The attention map is computed from the backbone feature tensor through a 1×1 convolution followed by softplus and min-max normalization; the masks are generated by thresholding this map at T learnable levels, with background values drawn dynamically from a clipped normal distribution rather than fixed to zero.

## Method

CiDeR is a single-stage, end-to-end pipeline that replaces explicit object detection with an attentional localization (AL) module, eliminating the need for location supervision and a separate detection training set. The architecture composes a ResNet101 backbone, backbone enhancement (e.g., ECNet), selective context (e.g., ASPP or SKNet), the AL module, and GeM spatial pooling, all trained jointly with ArcFace loss. The AL module computes a spatial attention map, applies T thresholding operations to produce binary masks with randomized background values (sampled from N(0.1, 0.9) clipped to [0,1]), and fuses the independently masked feature tensors via a learnable convex combination whose weights are parameterized by softplus of learnable scalars. This design allows the network to implicitly learn which spatial regions correspond to objects of interest purely from retrieval supervision, achieving single-stage training, single-stage indexing, and no location annotation requirement.

## Results

On the new RGLDv2-clean training set, CiDeR-FT achieves 77.8% mAP on ROxf Medium and 87.4% on RPar Medium, outperforming the previous best (DOLG) by 7.0% and 4.2% respectively. On the Hard protocol, CiDeR-FT reaches 61.9% and 75.3% mAP on ROxf and RPar, improving over DOLG by 14.5% and 7.4%. On the established SfM-120k set, CiDeR-FT attains a mean mAP of 79.4%, surpassing GLAM (69.7%) by 9.7% and AGeM (67.0% ROxf Med) by 9.2%. The paper also demonstrates that removing just 1,565 overlapping images (0.1%) from GLDv2-clean causes a dramatic 4–18% mAP drop across all reproduced SOTA methods, confirming that class overlap inflates reported performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CiDeR-FT outperforms all 14 baselines on the RGLDv2-clean evaluation. Against DOLG [59], the closest competitor, CiDeR-FT gains 7.0% mAP on ROxf Medium, 4.2% on RPar Medium, 14.5% on ROxf Hard, and 7.4% on RPar Hard. Against GLAM [46], CiDeR-FT improves by 5.6% (ROxf Med) and 12.3% (ROxf Hard). Against SOLAR [27], gains are 7.0% (ROxf Med) and 13.9% (ROxf Hard). The Token-based method [58] is the weakest baseline, trailing CiDeR-FT by 17.0% on ROxf Medium and 24.6% on ROxf Hard. On SfM-120k, CiDeR-FT beats DIR+RPN [8] by 7.4% on Ox5k and 1.1% on Par6k, and surpasses AGeM [9] by 9.2% on ROxf Medium. Notably, CiDeR (without fine-tuning) already exceeds DOLG on ROxf Medium (73.7 vs 70.8) and RPar Medium (84.6 vs 83.2), confirming that the single-stage end-to-end design is competitive even without the two-stage fine-tuning protocol used by most D2R methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

The paper operates at the intersection of two threads in instance-level image retrieval: the detect-to-retrieve (D2R) paradigm introduced by Teichmann et al. [48], which typically requires two-stage training and location supervision, and the spatial attention mechanisms used in methods like DELF [28] and GLAM [46]. CiDeR departs from prior D2R work by replacing explicit object detection with a thresholded spatial attention map, enabling single-stage end-to-end training without any location annotations. On the data side, the paper revisits the widely adopted GLDv2-clean [56] training set and identifies a critical flaw—landmark overlap with the standard ROxford/RParis [34] evaluation sets—that prior work [55] had noted but not systematically corrected. The method itself is deliberately assembled from off-the-shelf components (ECA-Net, SKNet, ASPP, GeM pooling) rather than proposing new architectural primitives, positioning it as a streamlined integration rather than a novel architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that removing only 1,565 images (≈0.1%) from GLDv2-clean causes performance drops of up to 30% and reshuffles the ranking of SOTA methods has immediate practical consequences: many published results on landmark retrieval are not directly comparable, and the corrected RGLDv2-clean should become the standard training set for fair benchmarking. The single-stage, supervision-free D2R approach also lowers the barrier to entry for detect-to-retrieve methods, which previously required separate detection training sets and multi-stage pipelines. Open questions include whether the attentional localization generalizes beyond landmark retrieval to other instance-level domains (e.g., products, faces), and whether the dramatic overlap effect extends to other large-scale training sets that may share similar curation issues.
