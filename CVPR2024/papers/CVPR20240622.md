## Problem

Existing 3D segmentation methods are either restricted to predefined categories due to limited 3D annotation, limited to single-object interaction, or unable to capture the hierarchical part-whole structure of scenes. A key unresolved challenge is that 2D segmentations from different viewpoints are inherently inconsistent, and naively lifting them into 3D destroys the hierarchical relationships embedded in overlapping masks (e.g., a chair leg mask is contained within a chair mask).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses as input; a 32×32 grid of prompts per image is fed into a pretrained SAM to generate 500+ binary masks per view. At inference, a single 2D click (query point) on any rendered view produces a dense 3D score field, and by adjusting a binarization threshold the user can traverse the scene hierarchy from atomic parts to whole objects or select multiple objects simultaneously via multiple clicks.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical 2D representation that avoids the information loss of overlapping SAM masks: each image is partitioned into disjoint patches defined by unique one-hot mask-membership vectors, and a correlation matrix C_hi is built by counting how many masks jointly contain each patch pair (a voting strategy), yielding a per-anchor hierarchical tree. During training, a NeRF-based 3D feature field (InstantNGP backbone) is differentially rendered onto 2D image planes, and rendered features are clustered by patch ID. A hierarchical contrastive loss then pulls features of highly-correlated patches (smaller tree depth d) closer to the anchor while pushing less-correlated patches apart, using a per-level decay factor λ^{d-1} and a max-loss ordering constraint to enforce monotonic similarity across hierarchy levels. Training proceeds in two stages—geometry (color + ray-saturation regularization) followed by semantic (hierarchical contrastive + L2 normalization)—for 50k iterations each on a single GPU.

## Results

On the Replica hierarchical segmentation benchmark, OmniSeg3D achieves 90.1% average mIoU (91.3% Level-1, 88.9% Level-2), substantially outperforming SAM (86.5% avg) and the non-hierarchical variant (86.7% avg), with the largest gain at Level-2 (+8.7% over SAM). On 3D instance segmentation, OmniSeg3D achieves 91.7% mIoU / 98.4% Acc on NVOS, 94.3% / 99.3% on MVSeg, and 84.4% on Replica, surpassing all baselines on every dataset. The method requires only a single scene-level training pass (30–40 min on one RTX 3090) and reuses the same feature field for all instance queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On hierarchical segmentation (Replica), OmniSeg3D (90.1% avg mIoU) beats SAM (86.5%), DINO (66.1%), and LSeg (66.9%), with the most dramatic improvement at Level-2 (88.9% vs. SAM's 80.2%), though SAM retains a slight edge at Level-1 (92.8% vs. 91.3%). On instance segmentation, OmniSeg3D outperforms SA3D by +1.4 mIoU on NVOS (91.7 vs. 90.3), +1.9 on MVSeg (94.3 vs. 92.4), and +1.4 on Replica (84.4 vs. 83.0), while also exceeding SPIn-NeRF/MVSeg by +3.4 on MVSeg and a large +52.0 on Replica. ISRF and the original NVOS method trail by 7.9 and 21.6 mIoU respectively on NVOS. A key practical advantage is that OmniSeg3D trains one feature field per scene and reuses it for all instances, whereas SA3D and SPIn-NeRF require per-instance or per-reference-view retraining, making OmniSeg3D more efficient for multi-object scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniSeg3D sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) lifting 2D segmentations into 3D via differentiable rendering (Semantic-NeRF, SPIn-NeRF, SA3D), (2) contrastive learning for 3D feature fields (ContrastiveLift), and (3) hierarchical contrastive learning (Zhang et al. 2022). It departs from SPIn-NeRF and SA3D by eliminating the single-object, reference-view dependency and instead learning a globally consistent feature field in one pass. It extends ContrastiveLift from closed-set instance segmentation to category-agnostic, multi-object, and hierarchical segmentation by introducing a patch-based 2D representation with a voting strategy that preserves inclusion relationships destroyed by SAM's overlapping masks, and by incorporating per-level decay into the contrastive loss to encode hierarchy into feature distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 3D feature field, trained once per scene, can support hierarchical segmentation, multi-object selection, and global discretization interactively—addressing a practical gap where prior methods require retraining or careful view selection for each query. The hierarchical contrastive learning framework is a generalizable technique applicable beyond NeRF (the authors note compatibility with meshes, point clouds, and Gaussian splatting). Key open questions remain: the lack of a principled definition of hierarchy levels means threshold-based segmentation may not consistently recover the same semantic level, and single-image contrastive learning can conflate objects never co-observed in a frame. The interactive GUI also positions this as a potential 3D annotation tool, which could help alleviate the 3D data scarcity problem.
