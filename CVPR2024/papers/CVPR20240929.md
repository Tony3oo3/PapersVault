## Problem

Generating long, continuous human motion sequences driven by multiple text descriptions (Human Motion Composition) remains unsolved: autoregressive methods accumulate errors over long sequences and require annotated transitions, while existing diffusion-based approaches (DoubleTake, DiffCollage, MultiDiffusion) generate subsequences independently and rely on redundant denoising steps or predefined transition durations to stitch them together. No prior diffusion model produces a single seamless composition in one pass without postprocessing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240929/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a series of non-overlapping text descriptions, each paired with a duration, defining intervals [0, τ₁), [τ₁, τ₂), …, [τⱼ, N) over a total sequence of N frames. It outputs a single continuous 3D motion sequence of N frames with no postprocessing. Notably, the model is trained with only one condition per motion sequence yet generalizes to multiple conditions at inference; motion representations differ by dataset (263D pose vectors for HumanML3D, SMPL joint positions/orientations for Babel), and the sequence length can exceed the maximum training subsequence length L (up to 200 frames) via extrapolation.

## Method

FlowMDM is a bidirectional diffusion Transformer that introduces two key mechanisms. First, Blended Positional Encodings (BPE) schedule a transition from sinusoidal absolute positional encoding (APE) in early denoising steps—recovering global intra-subsequence coherence—to Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE) in later steps—enforcing translation invariance for smooth inter-subsequence transitions; during training, APE and RPE are randomly alternated so the model learns both. Second, Pose-Centric Cross-Attention (PCCAT) injects the text condition only into the query projection while using noisy poses alone for keys and values, minimizing entanglement between conditions and poses so that each frame is denoised based on its own condition and neighboring poses, enabling robust multi-condition inference from single-condition training. The model uses x₀ parameterization with L2 loss, classifier-free guidance, and a binary-step BPE scheduler (125/60 initial APE steps for Babel/HumanML3D), achieving 47–84% fewer pose-wise denoising steps than competing diffusion baselines.

## Results

On Babel, FlowMDM achieves the best subsequence FID (0.99 vs. 1.33–3.16 for baselines) and the best transition smoothness metrics: Peak Jerk of 0.06 (vs. 0.07–1.09) and Area Under the Jerk of 0.13 (vs. 0.44–2.35), while matching state-of-the-art R-precision (0.702). On HumanML3D, FlowMDM leads in subsequence R-precision (0.685 vs. 0.615–0.643), FID (0.29 vs. 0.80–1.56), and transition FID (1.38 vs. 1.71–4.59), with the lowest AUJ (0.51 vs. 1.06–2.85). The method also requires 16.5–47.1% fewer pose-wise denoising steps than competing diffusion-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowMDM outperforms all four baselines on transition quality metrics across both datasets. On Babel, it reduces transition FID from 3.01–6.56 (TEACH_B, DoubleTake, MultiDiffusion, DiffCollage) to 2.61, and cuts AUJ by 3–18× (0.13 vs. 0.44–2.35). On HumanML3D, it improves subsequence R-precision from 0.615–0.643 to 0.685 and FID from 0.80–1.56 to 0.29, while also achieving the best transition FID (1.38 vs. 1.71–4.59). TEACH (autoregressive) is the weakest on transition FID in Babel (3.27) and cannot be evaluated on HumanML3D due to missing transition annotations. Among diffusion-based baselines, MultiDiffusion and DiffCollage produce the worst transition FID (6.56 and 4.36 on Babel), while DoubleTake with PCCAT is the closest competitor but still trails FlowMDM by 0.54 in transition FID and 5× in AUJ. FlowMDM also requires no redundant denoising steps, saving 16.5–47.1% of pose-wise computation compared to the diffusion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowMDM builds directly on the bidirectional Transformer diffusion architecture of MDM (Tevet et al., ICLR 2022) but departs from all prior HMC approaches in how they handle multi-condition sequences. Autoregressive methods (TEACH, MultiAct) generate motions sequentially and accumulate errors, while diffusion-based composition methods (DoubleTake, DiffCollage, MultiDiffusion) generate subsequences independently and merge them via redundant denoising steps. FlowMDM's key architectural departures are Blended Positional Encodings—switching from absolute to relative positional encodings across the denoising chain—and Pose-Centric Cross-Attention, which decouples conditions from keys/values to handle the training-inference mismatch of multiple conditions per sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BPE technique offers a general principle for diffusion Transformers: early denoising stages benefit from absolute positions (global structure recovery) while later stages benefit from relative positions (local smoothness and extrapolation), a trade-off that could apply beyond motion generation. The paper also exposes a real evaluation gap—FID is insensitive to abrupt jerk spikes in transitions—by introducing Peak Jerk and Area Under the Jerk metrics. Limitations include the inability of the APE stage to model inter-subsequence low-frequency dependencies (requiring a future intention-planning module), and the method is restricted to single-human motion. An open question the authors raise is whether the learned motion prior could be shared across different control modalities (text, music, objects) under a unified framework.
