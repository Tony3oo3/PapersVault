## Problem

Decentralized federated learning (DFL) lacks a central server to coordinate Byzantine defense, making it more vulnerable than centralized FL. Existing Byzantine-robust DFL schemes have not explicitly addressed the practical and critical setting where Byzantine clients dominate a benign client's neighborhood (more than half of its neighbors are malicious) while data distributions across clients are highly non-IID, a combination that causes single-metric defenses to fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240765/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benign client receives pre-aggregation local model vectors from its connected neighbors in a fixed communication topology, along with the number of local training epochs E and mini-batch size B used by each neighbor. The method additionally performs a privacy-respecting model inversion (adapted DLG) on each neighbor's model to synthesize a small dummy dataset (10 samples per class for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 5 for CIFAR-10), which is used only for computing Wasserstein distances and never exposes pixel-level private data. The output is a post-aggregation local model per client, computed by averaging only the pre-aggregation models from the identified benign group.

## Method

DFL-Dual combines dual-domain distance metrics with a two-stage clustering and trust bootstrapping procedure. In the first stage, for each candidate client j, the remaining neighbors are split into two groups via 2-Median clustering on a clipped weighted sum of Euclidean distance (model domain) and Wasserstein distance (data domain, computed over inverted dummy datasets); the group with the higher average cosine similarity (trust score) to the evaluating client is selected, yielding a per-client divergence rate. In the second stage, all clients are re-clustered by their divergence rates via 2-Median, and the group with the higher trust score is aggregated. The model-inversion component adapts DLG by sharing equivalent gradients (model deltas scaled by epochs and batch size) rather than raw gradients, introducing a round-dependent scaling factor to amplify inter-client differences, and optimizing only dummy features while sampling labels uniformly at random, thereby preserving privacy while revealing distributional discrepancies.

## Results

Under targeted attacks with 60% Byzantine clients and non-IID degree 0.8, DFL-Dual achieves near-clean accuracy with very low attack success rates: 96.21%/0.50% (Scaling), 96.54%/0.48% (DBA), and 95.59%/0.55% (A Little is Enough) on MNIST-CNN. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, DFL-Dual maintains 49.01%/4.44%, 48.79%/4.35%, and 50.01%/4.78% ACC/ASR respectively, compared to clean accuracy of 49.96%. Under untargeted attacks (e.g., Back-Gradient), DFL-Dual preserves 95.14% accuracy on MNIST-CNN while all baselines collapse to 10–26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFL-Dual dominates all six baselines on CIFAR-10 across every targeted attack, e.g., under Scaling it achieves 49.01%/4.44% ACC/ASR versus the best baseline Multi-Krum at 31.62%/53.49% (a ~17-point accuracy gap and ~49-point ASR gap). On MNIST under Scaling, DFL-Dual (96.21%/0.50%) matches Multi-Krum's accuracy (96.92%) but reduces ASR from 99.99% to 0.50%, a near-complete elimination of the backdoor. Under DBA on MNIST, DFL-Dual (96.54%/0.48%) is within 0.5% accuracy of IOS (97.04%/0.29%) and Multi-Krum (96.89%/0.43%), with comparable ASR. Under A Little is Enough on MNIST, IOS edges out DFL-Dual slightly on accuracy (96.95% vs 95.59%) but DFL-Dual achieves a lower ASR (0.55% vs 0.53%—essentially tied). DFLTrust and vanilla DFL are consistently the weakest baselines, often collapsing to ~10% accuracy under untargeted attacks, while DFL-Dual maintains near-clean performance in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFL-Dual extends the Byzantine-robust federated learning literature from centralized to decentralized settings, adapting FLTrust's trust-score mechanism (cosine-similarity-based group selection) to a peer-to-peer topology where no central server exists. It departs from prior DFL defenses (BridgeM, IOS, Bristle) that rely on single-domain model-space distances by introducing a dual-domain metric combining Euclidean distance in model space with Wasserstein distance in a data space recovered via a privacy-respecting adaptation of DLG-style model inversion. The two-stage 2-Median clustering with trust bootstrapping is a novel orchestration of these components, explicitly targeting the underexplored regime where over 50% of a benign client's neighbors are Byzantine and data is highly non-IID.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic and under-studied threat model in DFL—Byzantine clients strategically clustering around and outnumbering benign neighbors—where existing defenses degrade sharply. The dual-domain distance idea (model-space + data-space) offers a principled way to overcome the curse of dimensionality that limits pure model-space metrics on deep networks. Open questions include the computational cost of per-round model inversion and Wasserstein distance computation at scale, formal privacy guarantees of the dummy-data generation step, and whether the approach generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities or larger topologies (experiments use only 10 clients).
