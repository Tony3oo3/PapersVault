## Problem

Cross-modal associations between touch and other senses (vision, language, sound) remain underexplored because tactile data is expensive to collect and touch sensors are not standardized, producing large domain gaps between different hardware. Existing tactile representations are typically constrained to a single sensor, preventing unified zero-shot reasoning across diverse vision-based tactile sensors and their connections to other modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240966/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes tactile images (RGB frames from vision-based sensors such as GelSight, DIGIT, Taxim, TACTO, or GelSlim) paired with corresponding RGB images during training, and at inference accepts text prompts, vision images, or audio signals as conditioning inputs. The primary output is a 1024-dimensional tactile embedding in ImageBind's shared multimodal space, which can be further decoded into class labels, retrieved cross-modal items, generated images via diffusion, or natural-language responses through an LLM. Sensor-specific tokens (5 learnable vectors per sensor) are prepended to the ViT patch-token sequence, and for unseen sensors the nearest-neighbor token is retrieved via L1 distance to per-sensor pixel prototypes.

## Method

UniTouch aligns a ViT-based tactile encoder (24 layers, 16 heads) to ImageBind's frozen pretrained image embedding using bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive loss, so that touch inherits connections to language, audio, and other modalities without any paired text or audio data. To handle heterogeneous sensors simultaneously, the model introduces learnable sensor-specific tokens (L=5 per sensor, K=3 sensors) that are concatenated as prefixes to the touch patch tokens, absorbing sensor-specific artifacts like calibration and background color while the shared backbone learns common texture and geometry. A structured in-batch sampling strategy allocates σ=75% of each batch to a single randomly chosen dataset (weighted by dataset size) to emphasize intra-sensor hard negatives, with the remaining 25% drawn uniformly from other datasets for inter-sensor discrimination. At inference on an unseen sensor, the model retrieves the nearest sensor token by computing L1 distance between the input tactile image and stored per-sensor pixel prototypes. The resulting embedding plugs directly into off-the-shelf CLIP-style zero-shot classification, cross-modal retrieval, text-to-image diffusion conditioning, and LLM-based touch question answering without additional training.

## Results

On tactile material classification (linear probing), UniTouch achieves 61.3% on Touch and Go, 85.4% on ObjectFolder 2.0, and 78.1% on YCB-Slide, outperforming the best prior self-supervised baselines (VT CMC: 49.2/70.3/69.5%) by 12–15 points. For grasping stability prediction, UniTouch reaches 82.3% on Feeling of Success and 75.8% on out-of-domain ObjectFolder 1.0, surpassing supervised ImageNet features (75.9/68.9%). In zero-shot cross-modal retrieval on ObjectFolder 2.0, UniTouch achieves 41.9 mAP for touch→vision, 37.9 for touch→audio, and 38.0 for touch→text, exceeding the best fully-supervised baseline (DAR: 32.3/27.8/31.9) by 9–10 mAP without any paired training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTouch outperforms both prior self-supervised tactile methods—VT CMC [128] and SSVTP [64]—by 12–15 points on in-domain material classification and by 7–13 points on out-of-domain sensors (e.g., 61.2% vs. 48.1% on ObjectFolder Real), confirming that alignment to a large-scale multimodal embedding space yields stronger generalization than sensor-specific contrastive pretraining. It also beats supervised ImageNet features [24] on most datasets (e.g., 85.4% vs. 70.3% on OF 2.0), though ImageNet retains a slight edge on YCB-Slide (72.3% vs. 78.1% for UniTouch) and SSVTP (73.4% vs. 77.4%). In cross-modal retrieval, UniTouch's zero-shot mAP (41.9/37.9/38.0) surpasses all four supervised baselines—CCA [50], PLSCA [23], DSCMR [144], and DAR [3]—by 9–37 mAP, demonstrating that no explicit paired touch-audio or touch-text data is needed. For touch-to-image generation, UniTouch achieves higher CVTP (0.56 vs. 0.35) and material consistency (0.31 vs. 0.25) than the supervised Vision-from-touch [129], though Vision-from-touch has a lower FID (81.2 vs. 103.1), suggesting UniTouch generates more semantically aligned but slightly less photorealistic images. In touch image captioning, UniTouch's Touch-LLM scores 3.54/5 (GPT-4 rated), substantially outperforming BLIP-2 [78] (1.01), InstructBLIP [21] (1.93), LLaVA-1.5 [84] (2.33), and ImageBind-LLM [47] (1.89), indicating that aligning touch to the ImageBind space enables far better language interpretation of tactile signals than generic vision-language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ImageBind paradigm [41]—which aligned six modalities through image-only contrastive learning—to the sense of touch, aligning tactile embeddings to ImageBind's pretrained joint image space so that touch inherits connections to text and audio without paired data. Prior tactile representation learning (VT CMC [128], SSVTP [64]) was restricted to a single sensor and did not connect touch to other modalities; UniTouch departs from this by introducing learnable sensor-specific tokens and a structured in-batch sampling strategy to handle heterogeneous vision-based sensors (GelSight, DIGIT, Taxim) simultaneously. For downstream applications, the paper repurposes LLaMA-Adapter [34, 143] for Touch-LLM and Stable Diffusion [104] for touch-conditioned image synthesis, following the same 'swap the encoder' recipe used in ImageBind-LLM [47].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniTouch is the first model to unify multiple vision-based tactile sensors in a single representation and demonstrate zero-shot cross-modal capabilities (retrieval, image synthesis, LLM-based QA) for touch, which could substantially reduce the per-sensor engineering burden in robotics and assistive technology. Key limitations include restriction to vision-based sensors (excluding force, barometric, or array-based sensors), a small training set of only three sensor types, and the black-box nature of the learned embedding that limits interpretability. Open questions include whether the sensor-token approach scales to fundamentally different sensor modalities, whether the zero-shot gap to supervised methods narrows with more diverse training data, and how the representation behaves under temporal/dynamic touch sequences rather than single-frame images.
