## Problem

Semi-supervised 3D Lidar object detection relies on heuristic, hand-crafted clustering pipelines (e.g., DBSCAN with Kalman tracking) to pseudo-label moving objects, but these methods cannot improve with more data and are brittle to noise and sparse point clouds. The paper asks whether the pseudo-labeling process itself can be made fully data-driven, learning to group points into object instances from labeled examples rather than relying on fixed geometric heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T Lidar point clouds, from which static points are removed and ego-motion-compensated 24-step trajectories are predicted per point via a self-supervised trajectory network. The graph is built over the filtered points using k-nearest-neighbor connectivity in Euclidean space. Output is a per-point instance segmentation (connected components of the learned graph), which is converted to axis-aligned 3D bounding boxes with heading, then inflated to minimum dimensions to approximate amodal ground-truth boxes for training a PointPillars detector.

## Method

SeMoLi revisits correlation clustering within a Message Passing Network: a graph is constructed over filtered Lidar points where node features encode spatial coordinates plus velocity statistics (mean, min, max) from predicted trajectories, and edge features encode relative spatial offsets between k-nearest neighbors. Over L message-passing layers, node and edge embeddings are iteratively updated with shared-weight linear-normalization-dropout functions and mean aggregation, allowing each edge to accumulate global context beyond local proximity. A binary edge classifier on the final edge features scores whether two points belong to the same object, operationalizing the Gestalt principle of common fate (what moves together belongs together) as a learned signal. At inference, edges below a 0.5 threshold are cut and correlation clustering is applied to the remaining graph to extract connected components as object instances. The network is trained on a small labeled subset of Waymo, then applied zero-shot to unlabeled sequences (and even cross-dataset to Argoverse2) to generate pseudo-labels for a class-agnostic PointPillars detector.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset (moving objects, 100×40 m region), SeMoLi trained with 90% pseudo-labels and 0% ground-truth labels achieves 57.5 AP@0.4, outperforming the DBSCAN++† baseline (100% pseudo-labels) by +14.3 AP. The fully supervised moving-only GT baseline reaches 89.0 AP@0.4, meaning SeMoLi recovers 64.6% of the supervised performance without any manual labels. For pseudo-label quality (3DIoU F1@0.4), SeMoLi90 reaches 57.6 versus 16.2 for the best DBSCAN variant, and cross-dataset evaluation on Argoverse2 (no retraining) yields 57.6 AP@0.4 for moving objects versus 82.4 for the supervised detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeMoLi is compared against two families of baselines: vanilla DBSCAN and DBSCAN++ (the heuristic pipeline from Najibi et al., ECCV 2022), including variants with outlier filtering (†) and long-term trajectory features (l). In the pseudo-label quality evaluation (Table 3, 3DIoU F1@0.4), all DBSCAN variants collapse to ≤16.2 when using estimated (noisy) scene flow, whereas SeMoLi10 maintains 53.1 and SeMoLi90 reaches 57.6 — a 3–4× improvement in F1. In the final object detection task (Table 6, moving objects, AP@0.4), SeMoLi with 90% pseudo-labels and no GT labels achieves 57.5, beating DBSCAN++† (100% pseudo, 0% GT) by +14.3 AP and the originally reported DBSCAN++ result (40.4 mAP) by +17.1. With 10% GT labels added, SeMoLi reaches 54.6 AP@0.7, still surpassing DBSCAN++† (61.0 AP@0.4 with 90% pseudo + 10% GT is actually higher at 0.4 threshold — but at 0.7 threshold SeMoLi's 31.8 AP vs DBSCAN's 29.8 AP shows a modest edge). Cross-dataset on Argoverse2, SeMoLi (trained only on Waymo) achieves 57.6 AP@0.4 for moving objects versus 43.2 for DBSCAN++†, confirming that the data-driven approach generalizes better to unseen sensors and class distributions than the hand-crafted heuristic pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-frames motion-inspired pseudo-labeling for 3D Lidar object detection as a learnable problem rather than a hand-crafted pipeline. Prior auto-labeling systems (Najibi et al. 2022, Osep et al. 2019, Zhang et al. 2023) rely on heuristic density-based clustering (DBSCAN) combined with Kalman filtering to segment moving objects, making them inherently unable to improve with more data. SeMoLi departs from this by revisiting correlation clustering within a Message Passing Network framework, learning edge scores from labeled data to group points based on both spatial proximity and common-fate motion cues, and leveraging self-supervised scene flow estimation for trajectory prediction. The student detector (PointPillars) is standard; the novelty is entirely in the teacher pseudo-labeling network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a data-driven pseudo-labeler that generalizes across datasets (Waymo → Argoverse2) and sensor types without retraining, and that improves monotonically with more labeled data, addresses a key scalability bottleneck in 3D perception. The release of previously proprietary baselines and full experimental data is a meaningful contribution to reproducibility in a field where prior results were difficult to verify. However, the gap to fully supervised detection remains substantial (57.5 vs. 89.0 AP on moving objects), and the method still depends on the quality of upstream scene flow estimation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex dynamic scenes, whether the class-agnostic formulation can be made class-aware without losing generality, and how performance scales with sensor diversity beyond two datasets.
