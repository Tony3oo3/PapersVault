## Problem

Faithfully modeling the space of plausible articulations as a data-driven prior remains a notorious challenge: GMM/VAE-based priors suffer from Gaussian assumptions that bias toward mean poses, and GAN-based priors are training-unstable. The closest prior work, Pose-NDF, uses Euclidean gradient descent for projection (requiring costly re-projection onto SO(3) at every step) and a naive noise-corruption sampling scheme that produces a chi-like distance distribution rather than the dense-near-surface distribution needed for well-behaved distance field learning.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a K-joint articulated pose represented as a tuple of unit quaternions on the product manifold H_1^K and outputs a single scalar: the unsigned geodesic distance to the nearest plausible pose in the training set. For downstream optimization tasks (IK from sparse markers, image-based pose refinement), the distance value and its Riemannian gradient (via backprop + egrad2rgrad) serve as a differentiable prior term in a joint optimization with a data-fitting loss, yielding complete SMPL/MANO/SMALR parameters. The key structural constraint is that all intermediate iterates during projection must remain on the product quaternion manifold, not in ambient R^{4K}.

## Method

NRDF is a hierarchical neural network with an MLP decoder trained in a supervised fashion to predict the geodesic distance d_q from any input pose to its nearest neighbor in the training set, using the L_1 product metric over per-joint quaternion geodesic distances. A novel sampling algorithm (Alg. 1) generates training pairs by first drawing a target distance h from a user-chosen distribution (e.g., half-Gaussian or exponential), then sampling a random unit direction in the tangent space T_θ H_1^K, and mapping back via the exponential map Exp_θ(h·v), which provably preserves the sampled distance (Prop. 3). For projection, RDFGrad performs adaptive-step Riemannian gradient descent: at each iteration the Riemannian gradient (backprop gradient projected onto the tangent space via a newly derived quaternion egrad2rgrad operator, Prop. 2) is scaled by the predicted distance and applied through the exponential map, keeping all iterates on the manifold without re-projection. The paper further establishes a formal connection to Riemannian Flow Matching, noting that NRDF obtains the vector field toward the data manifold via backpropagation on the distance field rather than through explicit flow prediction.

## Results

On 3D pose estimation from images (3DPW), NRDF-based optimization of SMPLer-X predictions reduces PA-MPJPE from 77.82 mm to 68.47 mm, PA-PVE from 62.77 mm to 53.29 mm, and improves PCK@50 from 59.30% to 66.17%. On pose denoising, NRDF (α=1.0) achieves the lowest Δq+m2m error (0.170) and m2m error (14.32 cm) while converging in only 8 steps, compared to 40 steps for Pose-NDF (0.349 / 25.04 cm). For pose generation, NRDF (α=0.01) achieves an APD of 23.12 cm with FID 0.636 and d_NN 0.177 rad, striking a balance between diversity and realism that neither VPoser (low diversity) nor Pose-NDF (high FID 3.92) achieves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NRDF outperforms Pose-NDF [63] on every metric across all tasks: on pose denoising it halves the Δq+m2m error (0.170 vs 0.349) and converges 5× faster (8 vs 40 steps); on pose estimation it reduces PA-MPJPE by 1.72 mm (68.47 vs 70.19) and improves PCK@50 by 4.11 points (66.17 vs 62.06). Against VPoser [51], NRDF wins decisively on pose estimation (PA-MPJPE 68.47 vs 73.44 mm; PCK@50 66.17 vs 60.49%) and on pose diversity (APD 23.12 vs 14.68 cm), though VPoser retains a lower FID (0.048 vs 0.636) due to its tendency to generate near-mean poses. Compared to GAN-S [27], NRDF achieves substantially higher APD (23.12 vs 10.91 cm) at the cost of a slightly higher FID (0.636 vs 0.201). Against the score-based GFPose-Q [25], NRDF achieves lower denoising error (0.170 vs 0.359 Δq+m2m) and lower pose estimation error (68.47 vs N/A, but GFPose-Q is not used in the optimization pipeline). The RFM-based FM-Dis baseline, which is the closest methodologically, is outperformed by NRDF on denoising (0.170 vs 0.230) and pose estimation (68.47 vs 71.53 PA-MPJPE), confirming the advantage of the distance-field formulation over direct vector-field prediction. Against the GMM baseline [13], NRDF achieves much higher diversity (APD 23.12 vs 21.94 cm) and lower FID (0.636 vs 0.435), though the GMM's d_NN is slightly lower (0.159 vs 0.177 rad).

## Relation to Prior Work

NRDF extends Pose-NDF's neural distance field formulation for pose manifolds by replacing its Euclidean gradient descent (with costly re-projection onto SO(3)) with a principled Riemannian gradient descent (RDFGrad) that stays on the quaternion product manifold at every step. The paper also establishes a formal connection to Riemannian Flow Matching, showing that NRDF's backpropagated gradients serve the same role as RFM's predicted vector fields, while its sampling algorithm (Alg. 1) mirrors the scheduled optimal transport construction in RFM. Conceptually, NRDF departs from the broader family of VAE/GAN/diffusion pose priors by framing the problem as learning a zero-level-set in a high-dimensional Riemannian space rather than a latent distribution, avoiding Gaussian assumptions and adversarial training instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The principled Riemannian formulation provides a cleaner and faster projection mechanism for pose priors, with the adaptive-step RDFGrad converging in ~8 steps versus 40+ for Pose-NDF, which is practically significant for optimization-based downstream tasks like IK and pose refinement. The connection to Riemannian Flow Matching opens a path for unifying distance-field and flow-matching generative paradigms on manifolds, though the paper notes that NRDF's iterative sampling is less efficient than direct latent-to-pose mapping. Open questions include modeling uncertainty over the manifold (e.g., as a distribution over implicit surfaces) and whether injecting noise during projection could yield a sequential geodesic MCMC sampler for diverse generation around a given pose.
