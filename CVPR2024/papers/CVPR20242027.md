## Problem

Existing multimodal intent recognition (MIR) methods model each video independently, ignoring global contextual information that spans across videos. This independent learning introduces perception biases that are exacerbated by inconsistencies in multimodal representations, amplifying intent uncertainty—particularly when individual modalities provide ambiguous or conflicting intent cues. The paper tackles two specific gaps: (1) how to capture refined cross-video context dependencies without interference from irrelevant videos, and (2) how to reduce modality discrepancies by incorporating global context into contrastive alignment beyond the mini-batch level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities per video: a text transcript (BERT token embeddings, 768-dim), video frames (ResNet frame-level features, 256-dim on MIntRec / 47-dim on CMU-MOSI), and an audio signal (wav2vec signal-level features, 768-dim on MIntRec / 74-dim on CMU-MOSI). These are aligned to a common word-level sequence via a CTC module before fusion. On MIntRec the output is a 20-class fine-grained intent label or a binary intent polarity; on CMU-MOSI it is a 7-class sentiment score (−3 to +3). A cross-video bank retrieves top-7 scene-similar videos per sample, and the maximum sequence lengths are 30 tokens (text), 230 frames (video), and 180 steps (audio).

## Method

CAGC comprises two core components. First, a Context-Augmented Transformer (CAT) module learns long-range cross-video context by using the current video's visual feature as Query and progressively incorporating retrieved cross-video features as Key/Value in an iterative attention loop (Algorithm 1); a cross-video bank pre-filters sources using L2 scene similarity and an intent-tendency voting principle to avoid error accumulation from irrelevant videos. Second, a Global Context-guided Contrastive Learning (GCCL) scheme performs two levels of InfoNCE contrast: a local contrast within the mini-batch aligning text with same/different-intent audio and visual features, and a global contrast across the entire dataset that aligns text features with global multimodal fusion features from cross-videos, using those global features as supervisory signals. The multimodal intent decoder fuses local (text + current video/audio) and global (text + cross-video context) representations before classification. Training minimizes a weighted sum of cross-entropy task loss, local contrast loss (α=0.02), and global contrast loss (β=0.02) with temperature τ=0.7.

## Results

On the MIntRec dataset (primary task), CAGC achieves 73.39% accuracy and 70.09% F1 on the 20-class intent recognition task, and 90.11% accuracy on the binary intent polarity task, outperforming all baselines. On the secondary CMU-MOSI sentiment analysis benchmark, CAGC attains 85.70% binary accuracy (ACC-2) and 85.60% F1, ranking first on these two metrics, though it trails Self-MM (MAE 0.708) and HyCon (MAE 0.713) on regression-based metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MIntRec, CAGC outperforms all three MIR baselines across every metric. The largest gains are over MISA [15] (+1.10% ACC, +0.77% F1 on 20-class) and MulT [43] (+0.87% ACC, +0.84% F1 on 20-class), with a +0.87% ACC improvement over MAG-BERT [40] on the binary task. On CMU-MOSI, CAGC wins on ACC-2 (85.70%) and F1 (85.60%) over ConFEDE [53] (85.52%, 85.52%) and Self-MM [55] (85.46%, 85.43%), but trails Self-MM and HyCon [34] on MAE (0.775 vs. 0.708/0.713), Pearson correlation (0.774 vs. 0.796/0.790), and 7-class accuracy (44.80% vs. 46.67%/46.60%). The paper positions CAGC as achieving comparable rather than superior performance on sentiment analysis, with its primary advantage demonstrated on the intent recognition task.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAGC builds on the recently introduced multimodal intent recognition (MIR) task (Zhang et al., 2022) and departs from prior MIR methods (MAG-BERT, MulT, MISA) that model each video in isolation by repurposing cross-video context learning—previously explored in video object segmentation, correspondence learning, and surgical scene understanding—for intent disambiguation. On the contrastive learning side, the paper elevates the standard mini-batch InfoNCE formulation used in multimodal sentiment analysis (HyCon, ConFEDE, UniMSE) to a global, dataset-wide scope by introducing cross-video global fusion features as supervisory anchors. The cross-video bank with intent-consistency voting is a novel filtering mechanism not present in prior cross-video retrieval work, which typically relies solely on visual similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that global cross-video context can meaningfully reduce intent ambiguity in multimodal settings, offering a practical recipe (similarity-based retrieval + intent-consistency filtering + progressive attention) that could transfer to other ambiguous multimodal classification tasks. However, the cross-video bank construction depends on ground-truth intent labels for the voting-based denoising step, limiting its applicability to unsupervised or few-label scenarios. The evaluation is confined to two small datasets (MIntRec: 2,224 samples; CMU-MOSI: 2,199 samples), and on CMU-MOSI the method does not achieve SOTA on MAE or correlation, leaving open whether the global contrast mechanism scales to larger, more diverse corpora or whether the modest gains (~1% ACC) generalize beyond the specific intent taxonomy used here.
