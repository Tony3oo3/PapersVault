## Problem

Pose ambiguity in 6D object pose estimation—caused by object symmetries and occlusions—transforms the image-to-pose mapping from one-to-one to one-to-many, degrading methods that assume a single ground-truth pose. Existing probabilistic approaches either require impractical symmetry annotations or model only the rotation distribution on SO(3), leaving the joint rotation-translation distribution on SE(3) unexplored. This paper is the first to apply score-based diffusion models to the SE(3) group in the image domain for pose estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image cropped to the object's bounding box, along with a segmentation mask isolating visible parts, as input. A ResNet34/50 encoder extracts a condition vector from the image once, which is then reused across all denoising steps. The output is a set of pose samples (1,000 by default) drawn from the conditional distribution p(X|I) on SE(3), represented as vectors in the se(3) Lie algebra, capturing the full multi-modal pose uncertainty rather than a single point estimate.

## Method

The core contribution is a score-based generative model operating directly on the SE(3) Lie group, where both rotation and translation are coupled through the group composition rule (R₂,T₂)(R₁,T₁) = (R₂R₁, T₂+R₂T₁), enabling the model to capture perspective-induced correlations between rotation and translation. A key technical challenge is that SE(3) does not satisfy the Jacobian symmetry property J_l(z) = J_r^T(z) that allows closed-form score simplification on SO(3) and R³SO(3); to address this, the authors propose a surrogate Stein score s̃_X(X̃,σ) = −z/σ² that avoids expensive Jacobian inversion while improving denoising convergence. The score network is an MLP with a novel Fourier-based conditioning mechanism (cos/sin terms modulated by the image condition) that better captures the periodic structure of score fields on SO(3). Training uses the standard Denoising Score Matching objective on SE(3), and sampling proceeds via a Geodesic Random Walk variant on the Lie group. No 3D object models or symmetry annotations are required at training or inference time.

## Results

On SYMSOL (SO(3)), the SE(3) diffusion model with ResNet50 achieves 0.37° average angular error, outperforming Normalizing Flows (0.70°), HyperPosePDF (1.94°), and Implicit-PDF (3.96°). On the real-world T-LESS benchmark, the SE(3) model reaches 93.16% MSPD and 47.21% R@2, surpassing the SOTA regression method GDRNPP (90.17% MSPD, 21.60% R@2) by 3.0 and 25.6 percentage points respectively. On SYMSOL-T (SE(3) space), the method reduces rotation error to 0.41–0.64° across all five symmetric shapes, compared to 1.84–2.92° for the regression baseline. Inference reaches 250 FPS at 5 denoising steps on T-LESS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SYMSOL, the proposed SE(3) diffusion model (ResNet50) achieves 0.37° average angular error, halving the error of the closest probabilistic baseline Normalizing Flows (0.70°) and outperforming HyperPosePDF (1.94°), Implicit-PDF (3.96°), and DBN (22.44°) by wide margins. On T-LESS, the SE(3) model decisively outperforms GDRNPP on rotation metrics—R@2 improves from 21.60% to 47.21% (+25.6 pp) and R@5 from 71.18% to 86.94% (+15.8 pp)—while GDRNPP retains a translation advantage (T@2: 90.31% vs. 71.72%), which the authors attribute to GDRNPP's use of 3D model geometry for depth estimation. On SYMSOL-T, the SE(3) model reduces rotation error by 4–8× compared to the symmetry-aware regression baseline (e.g., 0.59° vs. 2.92° on tetrahedron), while the R³SO(3) variant struggles with the icosahedron (29.35°) due to its inability to model the joint rotation-translation distribution. The SE(3) parametrization consistently outperforms R³SO(3) on rotation across all datasets, validating the paper's hypothesis that coupling rotation and translation in a unified group structure captures perspective-induced correlations that separate diffusion processes miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based generative modeling on Lie groups from SO(3) (Leach et al., Jagvaral et al.) and R³SO(3) (Urain et al., Yim et al.) to the full SE(3) group, and is the first to apply such models in the image domain for 6D pose estimation. Conceptually, it departs from prior probabilistic pose estimators (Implicit-PDF, HyperPose-PDF, Bingham networks) that model rotation-only densities on SO(3) by jointly capturing the rotation-translation distribution, motivated by the perspective-induced correlation between the two. The surrogate Stein score formulation (simply −z/σ²) is a practical departure from the automatic-differentiation-based scores used in prior SE(3) diffusion work, exploiting a higher-order sub-stepping argument to avoid Jacobian inversions on SE(3) where the left/right Jacobian symmetry property fails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled diffusion-based framework for joint 6D pose distribution estimation on SE(3) from RGB images, eliminating the need for symmetry annotations that plague prior ambiguity-aware methods. However, it still requires ground-truth bounding boxes and segmentation masks, limiting its applicability to fully end-to-end detection-and-pose pipelines. The translation accuracy trade-off on T-LESS (T@2: 71.7% vs. GDRNPP's 90.3%) and the cross-shape interference observed when training a single model across all objects (e.g., ResNet50 underperforming ResNet34 on cones) highlight open questions about scalability to diverse object categories and the removal of auxiliary supervision. The surrogate score's empirical advantage over autograd-based scores on SE(3) is a useful practical finding that could inform future Lie-group diffusion work beyond pose estimation.
