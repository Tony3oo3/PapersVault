## Problem

Learning-based stereo matching methods inevitably lose geometric edge structure in certain feature channels due to imbalanced nonlinear transformations during deep network training, causing edge detail mismatches in the resulting disparity maps. No prior work has exploited the repeated geometric contours (motifs) that persist across channels to restore this lost edge information for more accurate matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of left and right RGB stereo images and produces a dense disparity map. Disparity is first estimated at 1/4 resolution through iterative refinement (up to 16 iterations), then upsampled to full resolution via the REMP refinement module. The architecture operates at four scales (1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32) with an EfficientNet backbone pretrained on ImageNet serving as a frozen feature extractor.

## Method

MoCha-Stereo introduces Motif Channel Attention (MCA), which applies adaptive-weight sliding windows in the frequency domain (Fourier transform followed by Gaussian high-pass filtering) to capture repeated geometric contours across feature channels, producing motif channels that encode invariant edge structure. These motif channels are projected onto normal channels via a Channel Affinity Matrix Profile (CAMP), which modulates the Group-Wise Correlation volume to form the Motif Channel Correlation Volume (MCCV), yielding a more accurate matching cost for edges. For full-resolution refinement, the Reconstruction Error Motif Penalty (REMP) module uses a UNet on the photometric reconstruction error to separate low-frequency and high-frequency error components, with a latent motif channel branch guiding the network to learn representative geometric error patterns that penalize the disparity map. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a Smooth-L1 loss on Scene Flow, using an iterative update operator to progressively refine disparity at 1/4 resolution.

## Results

On the Scene Flow test set, MoCha-Stereo achieves a new SOTA EPE of 0.41 px, surpassing IGEV-Stereo (0.47 px) by 12.77% and DLNR/ACVNet (0.48 px) by 14.6%, while maintaining competitive inference time (0.34 s). On the KITTI-2015 leaderboard, MoCha-Stereo ranks 1st with 1.53% all-pixel error and 1.44% non-occluded error, outperforming IGEV-Stereo (1.59%/1.49%) and DLNR (1.76%/1.61%). On KITTI-2012 Reflective, it achieves 3.83% Out-Noc and 0.8 px Avg-Noc, the first method to keep Avg-Noc below 0.8 px at the 5 px threshold. Zero-shot on Middlebury (Full: 12.4) and ETH3D (3.2) also set new SOTA, and the MVS extension (MoCha-MVS) achieves 0.319 overall error on DTU, beating DispMVS (0.339) by 5.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCha-Stereo outperforms all 23 compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On Scene Flow, it beats the closest competitor IGEV-Stereo by 0.06 px EPE (12.77% relative improvement) and is faster (0.34 s vs. 0.37 s). On KITTI-2012, it surpasses IGEV-Stereo by 5.36% in Out-Noc (1.06% vs. 1.12%) and by 11.95% in Reflective Out-Noc (3.83% vs. 4.35%), while also improving over RAFT-Stereo, GwcNet, CREStereo, and Lac-GwcNet. On KITTI-2015, it leads IGEV-Stereo by 0.06% in all-pixel error (1.53% vs. 1.59%) and 0.05% in non-occluded error (1.44% vs. 1.49%), and notably beats DLNR by 1.23% in all-pixel error despite DLNR's stronger foreground performance. In zero-shot settings, MoCha-Stereo improves over IGEV-Stereo by 14.5% on Middlebury Full (12.4 vs. 15.2) and 11.1% on ETH3D (3.2 vs. 3.6). In the MVS domain on DTU, MoCha-MVS achieves the best overall score (0.319) and accuracy (0.314), edging out DispMVS (0.339/0.354) and Miper-MVS (0.345/0.364), though its completeness (0.325) is slightly behind PatchmatchNet (0.277).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCha-Stereo is architecturally grounded in IGEV-Stereo's combined GWC+APC cost volume and iterative update framework, while borrowing the iterative refinement operator from DLNR. Its key departure is the introduction of Motif Channel Attention (MCA), which repurposes the time-series motif mining concept (sliding-window pattern discovery) to identify repeated geometric contours across feature channels, and the Channel Affinity Matrix Profile (CAMP) that projects these motif channels onto the correlation volume. The REMP module extends the reconstruction-error refinement line (iResNet, DLNR, DRNet) by explicitly decomposing error into high- and low-frequency branches guided by learned motif features, a separation none of those prior works attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode—geometric edge blurring in individual feature channels due to imbalanced nonlinearity—and shows that a lightweight attention mechanism over motif channels can recover enough structure to yield meaningful accuracy and efficiency gains (12.77% EPE reduction on Scene Flow, 4 iterations beating IGEV-Stereo's 16). The zero-shot transfer to Middlebury and ETH3D, plus the MVS extension, suggest the motif-channel idea is not dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the fixed 3×3 sliding-window motif extraction generalizes to higher-resolution or multi-scale settings, and whether the Fourier-domain preprocessing is truly necessary or merely a convenient initialization.
