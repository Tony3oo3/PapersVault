## Problem

Pretrained diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion memorize and reproduce training images during inference, exposing users to copyright litigation risks. Existing mitigation strategies either require costly retraining, rely on manually curated prompt lists, or only partially reduce memorization at the expense of image quality and text-alignment, and none provide an automated mechanism to detect and selectively address memorization cases during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AMG operates as an inference-time modification to any pretrained diffusion model (DDPM, iDDPM, or Stable Diffusion) without retraining. It consumes the user's text prompt or class label as conditioning, plus access to the training dataset for real-time nearest-neighbor lookup via CLIP or SSCD embeddings at each denoising step. The output is a generated image guaranteed to fall below a user-specified similarity threshold (pixel-level nL2 or object-level SSCD) relative to all training samples, with the guidance strength adjustable between a 'main' and 'strong' mode.

## Method

AMG introduces three complementary guidance strategies that modify the epsilon prediction during the reverse diffusion process: (1) Despecification Guidance (G_spe) attenuates the classifier-free guidance scale proportionally to the current similarity score, steering the prediction away from the overly specific prompt that acts as a memorization 'key'; (2) Caption Deduplication Guidance (G_dup) uses the caption of the nearest training image as a negative conditional prompt, exploiting duplicated captions as precise retrieval keys to push generation away from replicated images; (3) Dissimilarity Guidance (G_sim) directly computes the gradient of the similarity metric (nL2 or SSCD) with respect to x_t and guides in the direction that minimizes similarity, providing a definitive guarantee against memorization. An indicator function with a parabolic threshold schedule activates guidance only when the current similarity exceeds a dynamic, timestep-dependent threshold, ensuring selective intervention that preserves the original sampling process in non-memorized cases. The three strategies are synergistic: G_spe and G_dup maintain quality within the CFG plane while reducing the required G_sim scale, and G_sim provides the theoretical guarantee that the generation exits the memorization region.

## Results

On text-conditional generation over LAION-5B, AMG (Main) reduces the percentage of generated images exceeding the SSCD memorization threshold of 0.5 from 44.85% (Stable Diffusion baseline) to 0.00%, while maintaining a FID of 99.12 and CLIP score of 26.98. The Strong variant further eliminates all images above the stricter 0.4 threshold (0.00% vs. 59.23% baseline) at a marginal cost of FID (100.45) and CLIP (26.72). On CIFAR-10 unconditional and class-conditional generation, AMG reduces pixel-level memorization (nL2 < 1.4) from 0.93% and 1.53% to 0.00% respectively, with FID scores of 7.25 and 11.54 that match or slightly exceed the original iDDPM model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMG decisively outperforms all baselines on memorization: it achieves 0.00% of images above the SSCD 0.5 threshold, compared to 44.85% for Stable Diffusion, 42.75% for GNI, 26.45% for CWR, 17.78% for RNA, and 15.07% for RT. On quality (FID), AMG (99.12) is competitive with CWR (96.25) and GNI (97.81), and slightly better than the unmitigated SD baseline (106.41). On text-alignment (CLIP), AMG (26.98) is the second-best among all mitigation strategies, trailing only GNI (27.79) but substantially outperforming RT (22.63) and RNA (23.37). On CIFAR-10, AMG eliminates all pixel-level memorization (nL2 < 1.4 drops from 0.93% to 0.00% unconditional, 1.53% to 0.00% class-conditional) while matching or slightly improving FID over iDDPM (7.25 vs. 7.44; 11.54 vs. 11.81). The Ablation baseline [19] reports only a partial metric (0.30% > 0.5) and no FID/CLIP, making direct comparison limited, but AMG still achieves a strictly lower memorization rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMG is built directly on the classifier-free guidance (CFG) and classifier guidance frameworks, repurposing their linear epsilon-prediction adjustments to steer generations *away* from memorized training images rather than toward desired conditions. It departs from prior memorization-mitigation work—training-data deduplication with retraining [4], concept ablation via fine-tuning [19], and randomization of text conditioning [36]—by operating entirely at inference time without any model modification, and by decomposing the problem into three targeted guidance strategies (desspecification, caption deduplication, dissimilarity) each addressing a distinct root cause. The paper also extends the memorization problem to class-conditional generation, a setting not examined in prior studies that focused on unconditional and text-conditional tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMG's claim of complete memorization elimination (0% above threshold) with near-unchanged FID and CLIP scores is practically significant for companies deploying diffusion models under copyright risk, and the inference-only design avoids the computational cost of retraining. However, the approach requires access to the training set for nearest-neighbor similarity computation at every denoising step, which is a substantial practical barrier at LAION-5B scale and raises questions about scalability to video or 3D diffusion. Open questions include whether the parabolic scheduling and three-strategy decomposition generalize to architectures beyond DDPM/LDM, whether adversarially crafted prompts can circumvent the detection mechanism, and whether the method remains effective when the training set is partially unknown or continuously updated.
