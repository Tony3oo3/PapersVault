## Problem

Diffusion-based trajectory planners struggle with long-range composition tasks that require executing multiple sequential skills from abstract language instructions. Existing approaches either rely on predefined skill libraries that cannot be learned end-to-end or lack explicit skill decomposition, producing fragmented trajectories that fail to capture inter-step dependencies. The paper asks whether a model can autonomously learn interpretable, reusable skill abstractions from visual and linguistic inputs and use them to condition diffusion-based planning in a fully end-to-end fashion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only single-frame RGB images (encoded via R3M) and a natural language instruction (encoded via frozen distilBERT), explicitly excluding robot proprioception. The diffusion model generates a sequence of state embeddings (not raw pixel states or actions) over a 20-step horizon, which are then decoded into an action sequence by a task-specific inverse dynamics MLP that also receives the current observation for closed-loop control. Discrete skill codes from a fixed-size codebook (K=20 on LOReL) are applied at regular horizon intervals rather than with predicted durations.

## Method

SkillDiffuser is a two-level hierarchical planner. At the high level, a GPT-2-based skill predictor fuses visual and language embeddings to produce a continuous skill vector, which is discretized via vector quantization (VQ-VAE-style) into one of K learned skill codes; a straight-through estimator enables end-to-end training through the non-differentiable quantization. At the low level, a U-Net diffusion model with classifier-free guidance generates state trajectories conditioned on the learned skill embedding (projected through an MLP and injected into residual blocks), avoiding the need for a separate reward or Q-function. A separate inverse dynamics MLP decodes actions from consecutive generated state pairs plus the current observation, decoupling state planning from motion execution so that only this final module needs to change for a new embodiment. Training uses two optimizers: one for the inverse dynamics (behavior cloning loss) and one for the skill predictor and diffusion model jointly (VQ loss + diffusion loss with classifier-free dropout).

## Results

On the LOReL Sawyer benchmark (6 tasks, 20-step horizon, 10 runs), SkillDiffuser achieves a 43±1.1% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline LISA (40%) and all other methods. On unseen compositional tasks (40-step horizon), SkillDiffuser reaches 25.21±2.7% success, roughly 2× the non-hierarchical baseline and ~25% higher than LISA (20.89%). On Meta-World MT10 (visual-only, single-frame input), SkillDiffuser attains 23.3% success versus 16.7% for a language-conditioned diffuser without skill abstraction and 13.8% for LISA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkillDiffuser outperforms all five baselines on the LOReL Sawyer average: it beats LISA (the closest competitor) by 3 percentage points (43% vs. 40%), LCBC by 14 points (43% vs. 29%), Lang DT by 28 points (43% vs. 15%), LCRL by 30 points (43% vs. 13%), and the random policy by 23 points. On rephrasal robustness (Table 2), SkillDiffuser leads LISA by 9.6% on average across all five rephrasal types, with the largest gap on unseen noun+verb instructions (42.02% vs. 20%). On multi-step compositional tasks (Table 3), SkillDiffuser achieves 25.21% versus LISA's 20.89% and LOReL's MPC-based planner at 18.18%, confirming the advantage of learned skill abstractions over fixed reward-based planning. On Meta-World MT10 (Table 4), SkillDiffuser (23.3%) surpasses the language-conditioned diffuser without skills (16.7%) by 6.6 points and LISA (13.8%) by 9.5 points, validating that the VQ-based skill set combined with conditional diffusion is more effective than either component alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkillDiffuser sits at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) skill-based hierarchical planning, exemplified by LISA [13] and Decision Diffuser [2], and (2) diffusion-based trajectory generation, descended from Diffuser [19] and Diffusion Policy [5]. Its key departure from Decision Diffuser is replacing a hand-defined skill library with end-to-end learned discrete skills via vector quantization, and its departure from LISA is substituting the low-level transformer policy with a classifier-free diffusion model conditioned on those learned skills. The framework also inherits the visual-only (no proprioception) constraint from R3M [30], positioning it as a more generalizable multi-task planner than state-based alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is a modular architecture where the diffusion planner is task-agnostic and only the inverse-dynamics head needs swapping for a new embodiment, which could reduce the cost of deploying language-conditioned manipulation across robot platforms. However, absolute success rates remain modest (43% average on LOReL, 23% on Meta-World MT10), and the skill set size is fixed at training time, raising open questions about scalability to open-vocabulary or long-horizon tasks. The interpretability of learned skills—where discrete codes map to human-readable phrases—is a promising direction for human-robot collaboration but has not yet been validated in real-world, non-simulated settings.
