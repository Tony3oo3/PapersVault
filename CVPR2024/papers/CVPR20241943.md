## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP exhibit severely imbalanced zero-shot performance across visual concepts, with accuracy dropping below 10% for rare classes (e.g., night snake) while exceeding 80% for common ones. The root cause—long-tailed concept distributions in large-scale pretraining data like LAION—had never been directly measured because no tool existed to estimate concept frequency in free-form pretraining captions with significant lexical variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241943/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For zero-shot recognition, the method takes an RGB image and a set of concept names (class labels from benchmarks like ImageNet's 1K classes) and outputs a class prediction. For frequency estimation, it consumes pretraining text captions (~60 GB for LAION-400M) and produces per-concept frequency counts and ranked synonym lists. The retrieval step selects exactly 500 images per concept from pretraining data to form a balanced training set for the linear classifier, requiring only 50 GB of storage across all nine benchmark datasets.

## Method

REAL comprises two complementary variants. REAL-Prompt queries ChatGPT to enumerate synonyms for each concept, counts their frequencies in pretraining texts via efficient string matching, and replaces the original concept name in the prompt template with its most frequent synonym (e.g., 'cash machine' → 'ATM'), with OpenCLIP's text encoder filtering out ambiguous synonyms. REAL-Linear retrieves a class-balanced subset of 500 pretraining images per concept using the same synonym-based string matching (avoiding costly VLM feature extraction), then trains a linear classifier via cross-modal WiSE-FT adaptation atop the frozen VLM and ensembles it with the zero-shot classifier. This design achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot accuracy using 400× less storage and 10,000× less training time than the prior retrieval-augmented SOTA REACT, which requires 250 GPU hours for feature extraction and 256 GPU hours for contrastive fine-tuning.

## Results

On the average of eight zero-shot recognition benchmarks (ImageNet, Flowers, Cars, Aircraft, Pets, Food-101, DTD, EuroSAT), REAL-Prompt achieves 66.0% mean per-class accuracy, surpassing OpenAI's hand-engineered prompt templates (62.6%) and LLM-enriched prompts like CuPL (63.7% on ImageNet). REAL-Linear (500 retrieved images per concept) reaches 67.8% average accuracy, matching REACT's Gated-Image variant (67.8%) while using only 5% of its retrieved images and ~1% of its compute (2 minutes vs. 256 GPU hours for training). On ImageNet specifically, REAL-Linear achieves 65.9% top-1 accuracy, outperforming REACT(10K) at 65.7% and the OpenAI template baseline at 62.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenAI's hand-engineered prompt templates [35], REAL-Prompt improves average accuracy from 62.6% to 66.0% (+3.4%) simply by replacing concept names with their most frequent synonyms, with the largest gains on Flowers (+8.6%) and DTD (+5.4%). Against LLM-enriched prompting methods, REAL-Prompt (66.0% avg) outperforms DCLIP [28] (62.1% on ImageNet) and CuPL [34] (63.7% on ImageNet) while requiring only 10K ChatGPT tokens ($0.1) versus 50K–500K tokens ($0.5–$5) for the competing methods. In the retrieval-augmented setting, REAL-Linear (500 images/concept) matches REACT's Gated-Image variant [24] at 67.8% average accuracy but uses 5% of the retrieved images (0.5M vs. 10M) and ~1% of the compute (2 minutes training vs. 256 GPU hours; 25 GB vs. 10 TB storage). REAL-Linear also outperforms REACT(10K) [24] by 2.3% on average (67.8% vs. 65.5%). The paper additionally reports that REAL-Linear outperforms NeuralPriming [44] under its experimental setup (Table 10 in the supplement), though specific numbers are not in the main text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's zero-shot recognition framework and the cross-modal adaptation (WiSE-FT) technique for training linear classifiers atop frozen VLMs. It departs from prior prompting methods (DCLIP, CuPL) that invest in LLM-generated visual descriptions by instead identifying the most frequent synonym of each concept in pretraining texts—a simpler, cheaper strategy. It also diverges from retrieval-augmented approaches like REACT, which perform costly VLM-based feature extraction for data retrieval, by using fast string matching on pretraining captions. Conceptually, the paper carves new ground by being the first to systematically measure concept frequency in VLM pretraining data using LLMs, exposing a long-tailed distribution that prior bias analyses (focused on gender, race, geography) had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical diagnostic tool for identifying which visual concepts VLMs have under-learned, with direct implications for fairness and reliability in downstream applications like visual chatbots and text-to-image generation. The efficiency gains of REAL-Linear over REACT (400× less storage, 10,000× less training time) make retrieval-augmented adaptation accessible to academic labs. Key limitations remain: the frequency estimation cannot be validated against ground-truth labels in pretraining data, relies solely on textual captions (missing visual-only concepts), and the LLM-based filtering step is computationally expensive at scale. Open questions include whether the long-tail issue can be addressed at the data curation stage and whether synonym-based prompting generalizes to non-English or low-resource settings.
