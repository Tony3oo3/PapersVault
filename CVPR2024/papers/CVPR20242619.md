## Problem

Existing formula-driven supervised learning (FDSL) datasets for pre-training vision models are limited to 2D mathematical objects and/or grayscale images, and no single FDSL dataset has been demonstrated to effectively pre-train both CNNs and ViTs for classification while also producing CNNs usable as frozen feature extractors for cold-start anomaly detection. Additionally, widely used natural image datasets like ImageNet carry copyright, privacy, and licensing restrictions that limit their commercial deployment.

## Input / Output

The proposed datasets consist of 512×512 RGB images that are perspective projections of 3D Mandelbulb Variation fractals, each augmented with orbit-trap-based coloring and external-light-source shading to simulate realistic depth and brightness variation. During pre-training and downstream evaluation, images are randomly cropped and resized to 224×224. The pre-trained models (ResNet-50, WideResNet-50, ViT-T, ViT-B) produce class labels for supervised classification, per-pixel anomaly score maps via PatchCore for anomaly detection, or frozen intermediate feature representations for cold-start anomaly detection.

## Method

The paper generates two FDSL datasets from Mandelbulb Variations, 3D fractals parameterized by a power n ∈ [2,18] and a 9-bit boolean vector b that controls sign flips, angle swaps, and sin/cos choices in the iterative map. MandelbulbVAR-1k contains 1037 classes (61 valid b-vectors × 17 n-values) with 1000 rendered images per class (~1M total), while MandelbulbVAR-Hybrid-21k combines ~20k pairs of base variations via sequential application of two iteration functions to increase shape diversity, yielding 21k classes with 50 images each (~1M total). Rendering is implemented in GLSL with random orbit-trap coloring patterns, a simulated external light source for shading, and a virtual camera at a random viewpoint, producing RGB projections that mimic the 3D, colored, shaded appearance of real-world objects. The key design insight is that modeling 3D colored objects with shading better matches the statistical properties of target RGB datasets than the 2D grayscale contours used in prior FDSL work, enabling a single dataset to serve CNN pre-training (classification + anomaly detection) and ViT pre-training simultaneously.

## Results

For CNN pre-training, MandelbulbVAR-1k achieves 84.5% average top-1 accuracy across ImageNet-1k, CIFAR-10/100, Flowers, and ImageNet-100, outperforming all existing FDSL datasets by at least 1.0% (vs. FractalDB-10k at 83.5%). For anomaly detection on MVTec AD, PatchCore with a WideResNet-50 backbone pre-trained on MandelbulbVAR-1k reaches 97.2% image-level AUROC, only 1.9 points below ImageNet-1k pre-training (99.1%) and 4.5 points above the best FDSL baseline VisualAtom-1k (92.7%). For ViT pre-training, MandelbulbVAR-Hybrid-21k enables ViT-Base to reach 82.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, exceeding ImageNet-21k pre-training (81.8%) by 0.4 points and trailing only VisualAtom-1k (82.3%) by 0.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FractalDB (Kataoka et al., IJCV 2022), MandelbulbVAR-1k wins on average CNN classification accuracy (84.5% vs. 83.5% for FractalDB-10k, which used ~3× more pre-training images) and on anomaly detection image-level AUROC (97.2% vs. 90.6% for FractalDB-1k). Against VisualAtom (Takashima et al., CVPR 2023), MandelbulbVAR-1k clearly wins on CNN classification (84.5% vs. 83.1%) and anomaly detection (97.2% vs. 92.7% AUROC, a 4.5-point gap), but MandelbulbVAR-Hybrid-21k trails VisualAtom-1k by 0.1 points on ViT-Base ImageNet-1k accuracy (82.2% vs. 82.3%). Against RCDB and ExFractalDB (Kataoka et al., CVPR 2022), MandelbulbVAR-1k dominates on CNN pre-training (84.5% vs. 82.1%/82.4%) and anomaly detection (97.2% vs. 77.7%/87.4% AUROC), while on ViT pre-training MandelbulbVAR-Hybrid-21k is marginally below RCDB-1k (82.2% vs. 82.3%) but above ExFractalDB-1k (82.2% vs. 80.4%). Compared to ImageNet pre-training, MandelbulbVAR-1k narrows the gap to 1.9 points on anomaly detection AUROC and MandelbulbVAR-Hybrid-21k actually surpasses ImageNet-21k by 0.4 points on ViT-Base ImageNet-1k accuracy, demonstrating that formula-driven 3D fractal pre-training can match or exceed natural-image pre-training for these architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Formula-Driven Supervised Learning (FDSL) paradigm introduced by Kataoka et al. [12] (FractalDB) and subsequently applied to ViTs [17, 11, 24], by replacing 2D grayscale fractal/contour images with colored, shaded projections of 3D Mandelbulb Variations [19, 26]. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 3D geometry, orbit-trap coloring, and external-light shading to better match the statistical properties of real RGB images, and the explicit evaluation of FDSL pre-training for frozen-feature-extractor anomaly detection—a setting no prior FDSL work had addressed. The hybrid variation mechanism (composing two Mandelbulb Variation functions) is a new generative trick to increase shape diversity without increasing per-class image count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial anomaly detection: a legally clean, bias-free, zero-annotation-cost pre-training dataset that narrows the gap to ImageNet-1k to just 1.9% in image-level AUROC (97.2% vs. 99.1%) removes a major licensing barrier for deploying PatchCore-style systems in production. A notable limitation is the absence of semantic content, which likely caps performance on tasks requiring object-level understanding; the ViT results also show that contour-based datasets (VisualAtom) still hold a slight edge, suggesting shape diversity alone does not fully replicate the benefits of natural-image pre-training. Open questions include whether scaling the 3D fractal approach (more variations, higher resolution, multi-view) can close the remaining gap, and whether the anomaly-detection advantage generalizes beyond MVTec AD to other industrial domains.
