## Problem

CLIP-based open-vocabulary recognition suffers a ~40% accuracy gap between optimal and random viewpoints in embodied settings, yet existing active recognition methods are confined to closed vocabularies and cannot generalize to unseen classes. This paper tackles the under-explored task of active open-vocabulary recognition, where an embodied agent must both navigate to informative viewpoints and classify arbitrary objects specified by text, without any fine-tuning of the underlying CLIP model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241528/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a sequence of T RGB observations (T=6 on ShapeNet, T=10 on Habitat) starting from a random viewpoint, along with text embeddings for all candidate classes (base classes during training, the full open vocabulary during testing) and its own proprioceptive state (relative displacement and last executed action). At each timestep it outputs a discrete movement action from a predefined space (e.g., move_forward, turn_left, look_up), and after T steps it outputs a single class label. The CLIP image encoder is frozen throughout; only the attention fusion module and the RL policy are trained.

## Method

The agent is trained as a POMDP with PPO, where the reward is the classification score of the ground-truth class computed from a fused global feature. The key architectural choice is an attention-based fusion module that assigns per-frame weights via a self-attention layer whose input is a concatenation of inter-concept similarity (top-k cosine similarity between the CLIP image feature and text embeddings), inter-frame similarity (semantic distance to prior frames), and proprioceptive state — deliberately avoiding raw CLIP features to prevent class-specific knowledge from entering the fusion. The policy module similarly uses a lightweight 3-layer image encoder rather than CLIP, plus the full |C_B|-dimensional inter-concept similarity against base classes, so that the navigation strategy encodes only relative semantic proximity rather than absolute category identity. This design allows the agent to generalize to novel classes at test time without retraining, since both the policy and the fusion operate on similarity statistics that are invariant to the specific class set.

## Results

On ShapeNet with a 10/45/55 base/novel/open class split, the proposed agent achieves 53.3% top-1 accuracy on open classes, a 23.7-point absolute improvement over the passive CLIP (ViT-B/32) baseline (29.6%). On the 45 novel classes specifically, the agent reaches 36.6% top-1 versus CLIP's 21.6%, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen categories. On the Habitat simulator (10 base / 15 novel classes), the agent attains 28.0% top-1 on open classes compared to CLIP's 24.9%. The agent also outperforms heuristic movement policies (Random: 35.4%, Largest-step: 36.7% on ShapeNet open classes), confirming that intelligent navigation is essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CLIP (ViT-B/32) used as a passive single-frame recognizer. The proposed agent substantially outperforms CLIP across all class splits: on ShapeNet open classes (10/45/55), the agent achieves 53.3% top-1 versus CLIP's 29.6% (+23.7 points); on novel classes the gap is even wider at 36.6% vs. 21.6% (+15.0 points). On Habitat, the agent improves over CLIP by 3.1 points on open classes (28.0% vs. 24.9%) and 3.1 points on novel classes (35.4% vs. 32.3%). The paper also compares against two heuristic movement policies (Random and Largest-step) that share the same architecture but lack learned navigation; both are clearly inferior to the learned policy (e.g., 35.4% and 36.7% vs. 53.3% on ShapeNet open classes), demonstrating that random or greedy exploration is insufficient. A 'Last-prediction' variant (no evidence fusion, only the final frame) reaches 47.4%, confirming that the attention-based multi-frame integration contributes an additional ~6 points beyond simply finding a good viewpoint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: active recognition (Jayaraman & Grauman 2016; Fan et al. 2021; Yang et al. 2019), which uses RL to navigate for better classification but is confined to closed-vocabulary settings, and open-vocabulary recognition via CLIP (Radford et al. 2021), which handles arbitrary text prompts but is passive and single-frame. The key departure is formulating their combination as a new task—active open-vocabulary recognition—and designing both the policy and the feature-fusion module around inter-concept and inter-frame similarities rather than class-specific representations, so that the agent generalizes to unseen categories without retraining. The RL framework (POMDP + PPO) follows prior active recognition work, but the self-attention-based evidence fusion that preserves CLIP's zero-shot discriminative power is a novel architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantification of CLIP's viewpoint and occlusion sensitivity in embodied settings (≈40% accuracy gap between random and optimal views), giving a concrete motivation for pairing open-vocabulary models with active perception. The 23.7-point absolute gain over passive CLIP on ShapeNet is substantial, but the Habitat results (24.9% → 28.0% top-1) remain low in absolute terms, and the authors acknowledge that real-world challenges like varying lighting are unaddressed. Open questions include whether the similarity-based policy generalizes to more complex 3D environments, whether the approach scales to larger open vocabularies, and whether the modest Habitat gains are sufficient for practical deployment in household robotics.
