## Problem

Vision Transformers have demonstrated strong performance in supervised segmentation and other semi-supervised tasks, yet they remain unexplored for semi-supervised semantic segmentation (S4), where ConvNet-based methods appear to have reached a performance plateau. Simply replacing ConvNet backbones with Transformers is insufficient; the paper identifies that tailored regularization strategies aligned with Transformer-specific characteristics (patch-based input, self-attention) are critical to fully exploit the architecture's global contextual modeling in the S4 setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242042/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images split into an L×L patch grid (e.g., 6×6) for the Transformer encoder, with a small fraction of images paired with dense pixel-level one-hot masks (K classes) for supervised training. During inference, a single RGB image is processed at its native resolution (center-cropping for Pascal VOC/COCO, sliding window for Cityscapes) to produce a per-pixel class probability map. The teacher-student pipeline internally generates two augmented views of each unlabeled image: a weakly augmented version for the teacher and a strongly augmented version (PatchShuffle + CutMix) for the student.

## Method

S4Former embeds a Vision Transformer (DeiT or MiT) within a standard teacher-student framework and introduces three parameter-free regularization modules targeting the image, feature, and output dimensions. PatchShuffle randomly permutes M×M blocks of image patches before the Transformer encoder and restores them before the decoder, forcing the model to rely on global semantic context rather than spatial layout. Patch-Adaptive Self-Attention (PASA) adds a confidence-based mask to the self-attention matrix that amplifies attention among low-confidence patches, directing the model's focus to ambiguous regions. The Negative Class Ranking (NCR) loss extends the consistency objective beyond the positive pseudo-label by matching the softmax-normalized probability distribution over all negative classes between teacher and student, using an L2 distance. Together, these components require no additional trainable parameters or memory banks and are compatible with existing S4 frameworks such as UniMatch and AugSeg.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012 (classic setting, SETR DeiT-Base backbone), S⁴Former achieves 80.1% mIoU with only 92 labeled images (1/16 split), surpassing the previous best (UniMatch, 75.2%) by 4.9 points. With the full 1,464 labeled images, it reaches 85.0% mIoU. On COCO (81 classes), the method achieves 46.9% mIoU with 925 labeled images, outperforming UniMatch (44.4%) by 2.5 points. On Cityscapes (SegFormer MiT-B4), it reaches 80.9% mIoU with 1,488 labeled images, exceeding UniMatch (79.5%) by 1.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S⁴Former outperforms all eight baselines across all three datasets. On Pascal VOC 2012 (classic, 1/16 split), it beats UniMatch [53] by 4.9 points (80.1 vs. 75.2), iMAS [55] by 10.1 points (80.1 vs. 70.0), AugSeg [56] by 9.0 points (80.1 vs. 71.1), U²PL [45] by 12.1 points (80.1 vs. 68.0), PS-MT [31] by 14.3 points (80.1 vs. 65.8), and SemiCVT [20] by 11.5 points (80.1 vs. 68.6). On COCO (1/128 split), it surpasses UniMatch [53] by 2.5 points (46.9 vs. 44.4), PC2Seg [59] by 6.8 points (46.9 vs. 40.1), and PseudoSeg [61] by 7.8 points (46.9 vs. 39.1). On Cityscapes (1/2 split), it leads UniMatch [53] by 1.4 points (80.9 vs. 79.5), AugSeg [56] by 0.5 points (80.9 vs. 80.4), and SemiCVT [20] by 1.3 points (80.9 vs. 79.6). The gains are most pronounced in the low-label regime (1/16 split), where the Transformer's global context modeling combined with the proposed regularization components provides the largest advantage over ConvNet-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

S4Former extends the FixMatch/Mean Teacher weak-to-strong consistency paradigm to Vision Transformers for the first time as the primary segmentation backbone, departing from prior S4 methods that relied on ConvNet encoders (DeepLabV3+, ResNet) or used Transformers only as auxiliary parallel branches (SemiCVT, Transformer-CNN Cohort). Its three regularization components—PatchShuffle, PASA, and NCR—are explicitly designed around Transformer-specific properties (patch-based input, self-attention, global context modeling) rather than being generic augmentations transferable to any architecture. The NCR loss extends standard cross-entropy consistency by additionally constraining the relative distribution of negative-class probabilities, a refinement not present in prior pseudo-labeling or consistency frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that Vision Transformers, when paired with architecture-aware regularization, can break the performance plateau observed in ConvNet-based S4 methods, and that the choice of regularization strategy matters more than simply scaling unlabeled data (Figure 2). The parameter-free nature of all three components makes them drop-in additions to existing teacher-student pipelines, lowering the barrier for adoption. Open questions include whether these techniques transfer to larger ViT backbones (e.g., Swin, ViT-H), whether the block-level shuffling granularity and attention-mask design generalize beyond the tested architectures, and whether the approach remains effective in extremely low-label regimes (e.g., 1/512 splits on COCO where gains shrink).
