## Problem

Existing depth sensors (stereo, d-ToF, i-ToF) produce inaccurate or missing measurements on transparent, reflective, and textureless surfaces, and prior polarization-based depth enhancement methods are either physics-based and restricted to a single sensor type or deep-learning approaches limited to specific sensing modalities. No general framework exists that leverages polarization as a cross-sensor geometry cue, and the small size of available polarization datasets (e.g., HAMMER with ~7k frames) makes training a strong model from scratch impractical compared to large-scale RGB-D datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 4-channel polarization representation P = [I; φ; ρ; V] (intensity, angle of linear polarization, degree of linear polarization, and per-pixel viewing direction) from a DoFP polarization camera, together with a raw sensor depth map from one of three commodity devices (Intel D435 stereo, L515 d-ToF, or i-ToF). Because the depth sensors capture scene-level data, the method operates under a perspective projection assumption rather than the orthographic approximation common in object-level polarization work, and the viewing direction is explicitly included as an input channel. The output is a single dense, refined depth map at the same resolution as the input sensor depth.

## Method

The core contribution is Polarization Prompt Fusion Tuning (PPFT), a cross-modal transfer learning strategy that loads CompletionFormer weights pre-trained on NYU-Depth V2 as a foundation and injects polarization cues through a stack of Polarization Prompt Fusion Blocks (PPFB) at every encoder layer. Each PPFB contains two parallel branches: a spatial attention-like branch that concatenates the polarization prompt M with the input feature X, projects them via an MLP to produce key/query/value vectors, and computes a fused feature X*; and a channel fovea branch that learns channel-wise statistics s_q, s_k via global average pooling over the summed features to produce an updated polarization prompt M*. This sequential prompt-update mechanism allows polarization information to persist and be progressively fused across all encoder depths, addressing the modality misalignment between polarization and RGB that causes naive concatenation to underperform. The full network is trained end-to-end with a combined L1+L2 depth loss on the HAMMER dataset, and the same PPFT strategy is shown to transfer to Shape-from-Polarization by plugging it into the iDisc backbone.

## Results

On the HAMMER dataset (all sensors averaged), the proposed PPFT method achieves RMSE 28.63 mm, MAE 14.17 mm, and δ₁ 0.992, outperforming the strongest RGB-based baseline CompletionFormer (RMSE 38.88, MAE 19.94) by 26.4% in RMSE and 28.9% in MAE. Per-sensor, the method reduces RMSE by 24.4% (stereo: 24.78 vs 32.78), 29.7% (d-ToF: 23.84 vs 33.93), and 25.3% (i-ToF: 37.27 vs 49.95) relative to CompletionFormer. On the Shape-from-Polarization task (SPW dataset), PPFT applied to iDisc achieves a mean angular error of 10.91°, improving over the naive polarization-concatenation iDisc (12.99°) and the dedicated SfP-Wild method (18.59°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines across two tasks. On depth enhancement (HAMMER), the physics-based Polarized 3D [22] fails catastrophically (RMSE 772.49 mm vs. 28.63 mm), confirming its strict assumptions (specular reflection, closed surfaces) do not generalize to commodity sensors. Among learning-based RGB-guided baselines, CompletionFormer [65] is the strongest, achieving RMSE 38.88 mm; the proposed PPFT reduces this to 28.63 mm (−26.4% RMSE, −28.9% MAE). The RGB-fine-tuned CompletionFormer† (41.73 mm) is actually worse than the pre-trained version, suggesting RGB fine-tuning on the small HAMMER set is counterproductive. SemAttNet [40] and DySPN [34] lag further behind (RMSE 64.01 and 58.77 mm respectively), indicating that attention/propagation architectures without polarization-specific fusion struggle with the modality gap. On Shape-from-Polarization (SPW), PPFT+iDisc (10.91° MAE) outperforms both the dedicated SfP-Wild [29] (18.59°) and the naive polarization-concatenation iDisc (12.99°), demonstrating the transfer strategy's generality beyond depth completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CompletionFormer (CVPR 2023) as a pre-trained RGB-D backbone and extends it with a polarization-specific fusion pathway. It departs from prior polarization-based depth enhancement methods (Kadambi et al. 2015, Yoshida et al. 2018) that relied on physics-based formulas tied to specific reflection models and single sensor types, and from deep learning approaches (Jeon et al. 2023, Cui et al. 2017) that addressed only one sensor modality. The key conceptual departure is the Polarization Prompt Fusion Tuning (PPFT) strategy, which treats polarization as a 'prompt' injected into a pre-trained foundation model via a dedicated PPFB module, rather than simply concatenating polarization channels at the input. The channel fovea and spatial attention operations in PPFB draw from Selective Kernel Networks and visual prompt tracking literature, repurposed for cross-modal polarization fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in polarization vision: existing polarization-depth datasets (e.g., HAMMER with ~7k frames) are orders of magnitude smaller than RGB-D datasets, making from-scratch training infeasible. The PPFT strategy offers a reusable recipe for injecting a scarce modality into a pre-trained model, validated on both depth completion and shape-from-polarization. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (HAMMER, 13 scenes) and reliance on the quality of the RGB-D pre-training; whether PPFT scales to larger polarization corpora or other modalities (e.g., event cameras, thermal) remains open. The generalization claim across stereo, d-ToF, and i-ToF is supported but within one dataset's scene distribution.
