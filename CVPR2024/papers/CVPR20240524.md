## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods rely on architecture-dependent bi-level optimization (Synthesis-Time Matching), which biases the output space toward a fixed network and forces re-distillation for every new architecture. Moreover, the nested optimization loop becomes computationally infeasible on large-scale datasets like ImageNet-1K. The paper argues that constraining consistency between the real and synthetic image spaces—rather than matching within a discriminative architecture—would yield distilled datasets with superior cross-architecture generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes original dataset images (up to 512×512 for ImageNet-1K, 32×32 for CIFAR) along with their textual label prompts. Images are first encoded into a compact latent space by a pre-trained LDM autoencoder, then clustered into per-category prototypes; the label texts are mapped into the same feature space by a text encoder. The output is a set of synthetic images (configurable IPC) decoded back to pixel space at up to 512×512 resolution, accompanied by soft labels generated by a teacher network at each training epoch. Because synthesis is architecture-free, a single distilled dataset serves all student architectures without regeneration.

## Method

D⁴M disentangles feature extraction from image generation within a Latent Diffusion Model. First, the pre-trained LDM encoder compresses all original images into latent vectors, and Mini-Batch k-Means clustering computes per-category prototypes that aggregate class-level information while reducing redundancy. Second, the LDM's denoising U-Net is conditioned on both the noisy prototype latents and text label embeddings (fused via cross-attention) to synthesize high-fidelity images, ensuring the output remains in the same pixel space as the input. Third, the Synthesis-Time Matching step is entirely eliminated and replaced by Training-Time Matching: a teacher network produces soft labels on the distilled images, and the student is trained via KL-divergence minimization at each epoch (following the FKD protocol). This decoupling makes synthesis cost scale linearly with dataset size and removes any dependency on a specific matching architecture, while the multi-modal conditioning (prototypes + text) preserves semantic richness that pure gradient or distribution matching would discard.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with IPC-100, D⁴M achieves 66.5% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-101, surpassing SRe²L (65.8%) and approaching the full-dataset performance of 81.9%. On Tiny-ImageNet with IPC-100, D⁴M reaches 55.3% (R101) versus SRe²L's 54.2%. On CIFAR-100 with IPC-10, D⁴M attains 45.0% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline TESLA (41.7%) by 3.3 points. Synthesis time is 2.7s per image at 512×512 resolution with only 6.1 GB GPU memory, compared to 5.2s and 34.8 GB for SRe²L at 224×224.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On large-scale datasets, D⁴M consistently outperforms SRe²L across all IPC settings and architectures: on ImageNet-1K IPC-50, D⁴M gains +8.4 points over SRe²L with R18 (55.2% vs 46.8%) and +2.6 points with R101 (63.4% vs 60.8%). TESLA is only competitive on CIFAR-10 IPC-10 (66.4% vs D⁴M's 56.2%), but D⁴M surpasses it on CIFAR-100 IPC-10 (45.0% vs 41.7%) and IPC-50 (48.8% vs 47.9%). Among meta-learning baselines, FRePO is the strongest on CIFAR-10 IPC-10 (65.5%) but D⁴M still trails it there; however, D⁴M dominates on CIFAR-100 where FRePO reaches only 42.5% at IPC-10 versus D⁴M's 45.0%. DSA and CAFE are the weakest baselines, with DSA scoring 52.1% and CAFE 50.9% on CIFAR-10 IPC-10, both well below D⁴M. The cross-architecture versatility is a unique advantage: D⁴M's distilled ImageNet can be directly subsetted to Tiny-ImageNet (D⁴M-G) without re-distillation, achieving 53.2% (R101, IPC-50) that exceeds SRe²L's dedicated Tiny-ImageNet distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant Synthesis-Time Matching (STM) paradigm in dataset distillation, where a fixed discriminative architecture guides image generation through bi-level optimization (as in MTT, TESLA, DSA, CAFE, SRe²L). Instead, D⁴M introduces Training-Time Matching (TTM), decoupling synthesis entirely from any matching network by leveraging a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model conditioned on learned prototypes (via Mini-Batch k-Means) and text label embeddings. The TTM strategy borrows the soft-label matching mechanism from FKD [37], applying KL-divergence alignment between teacher and student predictions at training time rather than during synthesis. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the LDM's latent-space compression as a natural analogue to DD's goal of condensing information, while the cross-attention text conditioning provides a multi-modal signal absent from prior single-modality DD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the architecture dependency at synthesis time, D⁴M produces a single distilled dataset that generalizes across ResNets, ViTs, Swin Transformers, and MobileNets without re-distillation—a practical advantage for teams deploying multiple model families. The 512×512 resolution at 6.1 GB GPU memory (vs. 224×224 at 34.8 GB for SRe²L) makes large-scale DD substantially more accessible. However, the authors acknowledge significant performance degradation at extreme distillation ratios (IPC-1/10), and the method's reliance on a pre-trained LDM (Stable Diffusion) introduces a dependency on the quality and coverage of that generative prior. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal or video datasets, and whether the prototype-based aggregation can be made adaptive to handle long-tail class distributions.
