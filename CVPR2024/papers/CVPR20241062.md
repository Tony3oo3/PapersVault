## Problem

Active domain adaptation (ADA) methods for object detection are largely absent; existing ADA approaches designed for classification or segmentation rely on uncertainty of predicted objects and cannot account for undetected objects (false negatives). The paper identifies through empirical analysis that under domain shift, false negative errors are the dominant cause of performance degradation in object detection, yet conventional active learning strategies are structurally unable to estimate uncertainty for objects that never appear in detection proposals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain RGB images, a pool of unlabeled target-domain RGB images (resized to 600 px on the shorter side), and a growing labeled target subset selected via active sampling. Beyond standard detection outputs (bounding boxes with class labels), the system produces per-image acquisition scores that combine four normalized metrics—predicted FN count, localization variance, classification entropy, and domain-discriminator diversity—into a single multiplicative score used to rank unlabeled images for annotation. The FNPM additionally outputs a scalar regression value representing the predicted number of false negatives per image.

## Method

The core contribution is a False Negative Prediction Module (FNPM), a lightweight DNN regression branch (GAP + FC layers) attached to the domain-adapted backbone that predicts the count of FN errors per image, trained with MSE loss against ground-truth FN counts computed from the frozen detection model. The acquisition function multiplies four z-normalized metrics—undetectability (FNPM output), localization uncertainty (variance of MCDropout-sampled box coordinates), classification uncertainty (entropy of class probabilities), and diversity (inverse domain-discriminator confidence)—so that images scoring high on all four are preferentially selected for labeling. Training proceeds in five alternating rounds: UDA initialization with adversarial feature alignment (GRL + domain discriminator), FNPM training with the detection model frozen, active sampling and annotation, and semi-supervised DA training that combines supervised loss on source and labeled target data with uncertainty-guided pseudo-labeling (only boxes whose localization variance falls below a threshold γ contribute to the unsupervised loss) and mean-teacher EMA updates.

## Results

With only 1% of the labeling budget, the proposed method achieves mAP of 52.2 (C→F), 45.1 (C→B), 61.8 (S→C), and 64.2 (K→C), outperforming all UDA baselines in three of four scenarios and the sole ADA baseline (AADA) by 10–17 mAP points. At 5% budget, performance reaches 51.8, 50.9, 65.6, and 67.9 mAP respectively, approaching the Oracle (fully supervised) values of 46.3, 52.3, 68.9, and 68.9 — notably exceeding Oracle in C→F. In the K→C scenario, the method improves over Source-only (31.3) by over 33 mAP points with just 1% labeling, and the accuracy difference ratio to Oracle at 5% budget is 98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest UDA baseline NSA-UDA [61], the proposed method at 1% budget wins in C→B (45.1 vs 35.5), S→C (61.8 vs 56.3), and K→C (64.2 vs 55.6) but trails slightly in C→F (52.2 vs 52.7). Against the only prior ADA method for detection, AADA [40], the proposed method dominates across all four scenarios at both 1% and 5% budgets, with the largest gap in K→C (64.2 vs 47.7 at 1%, a 16.5 mAP improvement). At 5% budget, the method exceeds Oracle in C→F (51.8 vs 46.3) and comes within 1.0 mAP in K→C (67.9 vs 68.9) and 3.3 mAP in S→C (65.6 vs 68.9). In the pure UDA setting (0% budget), the method is competitive with state-of-the-art UDA, achieving 52.0 in C→F (vs 52.7 NSA-UDA) and 36.3 in C→B (vs 35.5 NSA-UDA), confirming it also serves as a strong UDA baseline. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that adding the undetectability metric s^fn to MeanEntropy and AADA strategies yields consistent gains at low budgets (≤1%), confirming the FNPM's contribution to the overall advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active domain adaptation (ADA) from its established domain in classification and semantic segmentation to object detection, where the core challenge is fundamentally different: undetected objects (false negatives) have no predicted output from which to estimate uncertainty. It builds on adversarial feature alignment (Ganin & Lempitsky), the Probabilistic Teacher UDA framework for pseudo-labeling and augmentation, and Monte Carlo Dropout for uncertainty quantification, but introduces the False Negative Prediction Module (FNPM) as a new acquisition signal that directly estimates per-image undetectability. The diversity metric is borrowed from AADA (Su et al.), but the key departure is the product-form acquisition function that multiplies undetectability, localization uncertainty, classification entropy, and diversity—addressing a gap that prior ADA methods, designed for pixel- or image-level classification, cannot fill for bounding-box prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying false negative errors as the dominant source of performance degradation under domain shift (more than double the gap of false positives), the paper reframes the active sampling problem for detection: the most informative samples are those containing objects the model cannot see, not merely those it is uncertain about. The practical impact is significant—achieving near-Oracle mAP with 1–5% labeling budget across four in-vehicle camera scenarios—making domain-adaptive deployment far more annotation-efficient. Limitations include evaluation on a single detector architecture (Faster R-CNN with VGG16), a focus on driving scenes, and the FNPM's reliance on a frozen teacher model for stable training, which may not transfer cleanly to iterative or online settings. Open questions include whether the FNPM generalizes to modern transformer-based detectors, 3D detection, or scenarios with open-vocabulary classes.
