## Problem

Existing multi-modal face anti-spoofing methods fail to generalize to unseen attacks and deployment conditions due to two intertwined issues: modality unreliability, where domain shifts in depth and infrared sensors cause unreliable features to propagate through cross-modal fusion, and modality imbalance, where training over-relies on a dominant fast-converging modality at the expense of slower ones. No prior work has systematically addressed both problems in a multi-source domain generalization setting for multi-modal FAS.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three co-registered images per sample—RGB, depth, and infrared—each resized to 224×224×3 and tokenized into 14×14 patch tokens plus a class token (197 tokens total, hidden size 768). A single-layer fully-connected head reduces the final class token to a 2-dim live/spoof logit. The framework is designed to gracefully handle test-time missing modalities (depth, infrared, or both absent) by simply omitting the corresponding U-Adapter fusion terms, making it applicable to flexible-modal deployment.

## Method

MMDG fine-tunes a pretrained ViT-B backbone via lightweight U-Adapters inserted at each transformer block's MLP output, where each adapter performs cross-modal attention fusion (query from one modality, key/value from another) weighted by per-token uncertainty estimated through Monte Carlo Dropout over the MHSA layers; unreliable tokens are suppressed via an exponential penalty in the attention scores, and central difference convolution integrates fine-grained local details. To counter modality imbalance, ReGrad adaptively modulates backward gradients on U-Adapter parameters by decomposing each modality's gradient into orthogonal and non-orthogonal components relative to the other, then selectively retaining or dropping components based on gradient conflict and relative convergence speed (monitored via a Single-Side Prototypical loss that clusters features by domain), with an additional uncertainty-based scaling that further constrains fast-converging but unreliable modalities.

## Results

On the proposed multi-source DG benchmark (Protocol 1, fixed modalities), MMDG achieves an average HTER of 19.25% and AUC of 87.96% across four leave-one-out sub-protocols (CPS→W, CPW→S, CSW→P, PSW→C). This represents a 6.2% HTER and 6.9% AUC improvement over the strongest flexible-modal baseline (VP-FAS: 25.45% HTER, 81.08% AUC), and a 9.9% HTER improvement over the best multi-modal method (CMFL: 28.26% HTER). Under missing-modality testing (Protocol 2), MMDG maintains 24.51% average HTER, outperforming VP-FAS by 5.2% HTER. Under limited source domains (Protocol 3), MMDG achieves 20.12% HTER on CW→PS and 36.60% HTER on PS→CW, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMDG outperforms all 11 baselines across all three protocols. Against the strongest flexible-modal method VP-FAS [60], MMDG reduces average HTER from 25.45% to 19.25% (−6.2%) and improves AUC from 81.08% to 87.96% (+6.9%) on Protocol 1; the gap widens to 5.2% HTER under missing-modality testing (Protocol 2). Against multi-modal baselines, MMDG beats CMFL [13] by 9.0% HTER and MM-CDCN [58] by 23.6% HTER, indicating that existing multi-modal fusion strategies are highly vulnerable to domain shifts. Among domain-generalized unimodal methods (SSDG [20], SSAN [50], IADG [71], ViTAF [18]), MMDG achieves 9.9–15.0% lower HTER, confirming that simply concatenating modalities into a DG pipeline is insufficient. In the ablation (Table 4), ReGrad outperforms both OGM-GE [43] (29.32% HTER) and PMR [11] (24.54% HTER) when paired with ViT+U-Adapter, achieving 19.48% HTER, demonstrating that gradient modulation adapted for hybrid cross-modal fusion is more effective than existing modality-balancing strategies designed for late-fusion architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: domain generalization for FAS (SSDG, SSAN, IADG, ViTAF), multi-modal FAS with cross-modal fusion (CMFL, VP-FAS, ViT+AMA), and modality balancing via gradient modulation (OGM-GE, PMR). It departs from prior DG-FAS work, which has been exclusively unimodal (RGB), by identifying two failure modes specific to multi-modal DG—modality unreliability (domain-shifted depth/infrared misleading RGB via cross-modal fusion) and modality imbalance (over-reliance on a fast-converging modality). The U-Adapter repurposes Monte Carlo Dropout uncertainty and central difference convolution within a parameter-efficient ViT fine-tuning framework, while ReGrad extends OGM-GE's orthogonal gradient decomposition with uncertainty-aware suppression and an SSP loss adapted from SSDG's asymmetric prototype learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first large-scale multi-source benchmark for multi-modal FAS under DG (four datasets, three protocols including missing-modal and limited-source settings) fills a clear evaluation gap and will likely become a standard reference. The finding that adding modalities can degrade DG performance (Fig. 1) challenges the field's default assumption that more sensors always help, and the observation that some methods perform better with more modalities missing than fewer highlights how unreliable cross-modal fusion actively harms detection. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond three modalities or four datasets, whether more efficient uncertainty estimation (e.g., evidential deep learning) could replace the costly MCD sampling, and whether the gradient modulation strategy generalizes to other multi-modal DG tasks beyond FAS.
