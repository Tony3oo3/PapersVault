## Problem

General-purpose text-to-image foundation models (SD, SDXL, DALLE, MidJourney) produce inferior quality and suffer from text-image misalignment when generating humans, especially with dense descriptions covering multiple body parts and outfit attributes. No specialized human-centric foundation model exists that simultaneously delivers high-fidelity generation, precise alignment with dense concepts, and easy integration into downstream 2D/3D tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes dense structured text prompts organized into two fixed groups — a body-level group (overall appearance: age, gender, ethnicity, hair, etc.) and an outfit-level group (fine-grained clothing/accessory attributes per body region) — and outputs photo-realistic single-person human images. Built on SD-1.5 or SDXL, it inherits their latent diffusion pipeline and 512×512 or 1024×1024 generation resolution, but the text conditioning is discretized into sub-captions aligned with human parsing semantic masks rather than fed as a single free-form caption.

## Method

The Daring (Decomposed-Attention-Refocusing) framework modifies only the training objective of an existing SD/SDXL model without adding extra modules. It decomposes the cross-attention key matrix into sub-keys corresponding to each body-region group, then introduces the HOLA loss that supervises each group's attention maps to concentrate in the spatial region defined by the corresponding human parsing mask — a per-concept term pushes individual token attention toward its semantic region, while a group-mean term ensures harmonious arrangement of concepts within the same region. This is paired with the Annotate Anyone data production paradigm, a human-AI cooperation flywheel that iteratively refines VLM-based (InstructBLIP) captions using targeted human annotation on tail categories, yielding the CosmicMan-HQ 1.0 dataset of 6M images at mean 1488×1255 resolution with 115M structured attributes across 70 categories.

## Results

CosmicMan-SDXL achieves the best FID of 35.42 and the highest overall fine-grained alignment (Acc_all) of 83.6%, outperforming all compared models including SDXL (FID 48.61, Acc_all 78.1%), DALLE3 (FID 66.36, Acc_all 77.8%), and MidJourney (FID 53.89, Acc_all 74.7%). The FID improvement over the corresponding SDXL backbone is 27.13% relative, and over SD-1.5 it is 23.52%. On fine-grained shape alignment, CosmicMan-SDXL reaches 69.7% Acc_shape, a 15.97% relative gain over DALLE3 (60.1%). In human preference studies, CosmicMan is preferred over SDXL, DALLE3, and MidJourney in 82.93%, 88.56%, and 81.68% of pairwise comparisons for text-image alignment, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CosmicMan-SDXL wins decisively on FID (35.42) against all seven baselines, with the largest margins over DALLE3 (66.36) and MidJourney (53.89). On fine-grained alignment metrics (Acc_obj, Acc_tex, Acc_shape, Acc_all), CosmicMan-SDXL leads every competitor: it surpasses SDXL by 5.5 points in Acc_all (83.6 vs 78.1), DALLE3 by 5.8 points (83.6 vs 77.8), and MidJourney by 8.9 points (83.6 vs 74.7). The shape alignment gap is particularly notable—CosmicMan-SDXL achieves 69.7% Acc_shape versus 63.2% for SDXL and 60.1% for DALLE3. The one metric where CosmicMan trails is CLIP score (27.31 vs 30.78 for SDXL), which the authors attribute to CLIP's inability to capture fine-grained dense-concept alignment, consistent with findings from DSG and GenEval. In downstream applications (Table 5), replacing SDXL with CosmicMan-SDXL in T2I-Adapter improves FID from 47.73 to 37.62 and Acc_all from 76.6 to 82.9, and replacing SD with CosmicMan-SD in Magic123 raises CLIP-Similarity from 0.83 to 0.88 and Acc_all from 67.6 to 70.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

CosmicMan builds directly on Stable Diffusion and SDXL as its backbone, applying minimal architectural modification through the Daring training framework. It departs from general-purpose text-to-image models (DALLE, Imagen, SD) by explicitly specializing for human subjects, addressing what the authors identify as a gap in the literature. The HOLA loss draws on the cross-attention alignment literature (Prompt-to-Prompt, FastComposer) but introduces a human-specific structural prior by decomposing attention maps according to body-part semantic regions. The Annotate Anyone data production paradigm is novel in combining flowing data sourcing with human-in-the-loop VLM fine-tuning, positioning itself between purely manual annotation and purely automated captioning approaches used in prior datasets like LAION-5B and SHHQ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first text-to-image foundation model explicitly designed for human-centric generation, and the 6M-image CosmicMan-HQ 1.0 dataset with 115M structured attributes is a substantial resource for the community. The Annotate Anyone data flywheel offers a scalable, cost-effective paradigm for continuously updating training data, addressing a practical bottleneck in foundation model development. However, the model is limited to single-person images, and the notably lower CLIP score compared to some baselines (while fine-grained metrics improve) raises questions about whether the HOLA loss trades holistic semantic alignment for local structural precision. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes, whether the data flywheel maintains quality at scale, and whether the body-part decomposition generalizes to non-human structured domains.
