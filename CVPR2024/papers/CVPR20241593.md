## Problem

Transformer-based architectures dominate human motion understanding but suffer from three practical limitations: they are slow and memory-inefficient for real-time sequential prediction on continuous frame streams, they do not generalize to unseen or variable frame rates, and their O(F²) attention complexity makes training on long sequences prohibitively expensive. This paper asks whether attention-free State Space Models can match Transformer accuracy while resolving these efficiency and generalization gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241593/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of 2D joint positions with scalar confidence scores (shape B×F×J×3) and produces a per-frame, per-joint spatiotemporal representation (B×F×J×D_rep) that feeds task-specific heads for 3D pose, SMPL mesh parameters, or action labels. A fully causal variant restricts each time-step's prediction to past and current frames only, enabling streaming inference with constant memory. The continuous-time SSM formulation allows the discretization parameter Δ to be rescaled at inference time, so the same trained model handles arbitrary input frame rates without retraining.

## Method

HumMUSS stacks spatiotemporal blocks, each containing two parallel streams that alternate spatial and temporal Gated Diagonal State Space Model (GDSSM) blocks. Each GDSSM block applies LayerNorm, routes the signal through an independent pathway and a DSSM pathway (forward-only for causal, forward+backward for bidirectional), then combines them via multiplicative gating with a skip connection. The two streams (spatial-then-temporal and temporal-then-spatial) are fused with learnable softmax weights to capture complementary spatio-temporal interactions. Pretraining uses a 2D-to-3D lifting pretext task on AMASS and MPI-INF-3DHP with a 3D position+velocity loss for 30 epochs, followed by a 2D re-projection loss on PoseTrack and InstaVariety for 60 epochs with random joint masking and noise augmentation. The diagonal SSM parameterization (complex-conjugate-paired eigenvalues with HiPPO-inspired initialization) yields O(F log F) training complexity and O(1) per-step sequential inference, while the continuous-time formulation provides inherent frame-rate generalization.

## Results

On MPI-INF-3DHP (3D pose estimation), HumMUSS(f) achieves 21.0 mm MPJPE in the causal setting, marginally outperforming MotionBERT(f) (21.1 mm), and 16.3 mm MPJPE in the bidirectional setting, closely matching MotionBERT(f) (16.0 mm). On 3DPW (mesh recovery), HumMUSS(f) reaches 82.0 mm MPJPE and 50.2 mm PA-MPJPE (causal), slightly beating MotionBERT(f) (82.3 / 50.9). On NTU-RGB+D (action recognition), HumMUSS achieves 92.0% X-Sub and 97.4% X-View accuracy, competitive with MotionBERT (93.0 / 97.2) but below SkeleTR (94.8 / 97.7). The key practical advantage is that HumMUSS is 3.8× more memory-efficient and 11.1× faster during sequential inference at 243 frames, with O(1) memory complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary Transformer baseline MotionBERT [109], HumMUSS is essentially on par: in the causal 3D pose setting, HumMUSS(f) edges out MotionBERT(f) by 0.1 mm on MPJPE (21.0 vs 21.1) but trails by 0.3 PCK and 0.7 AUC; in the bidirectional setting, MotionBERT(f) leads by 0.3 mm MPJPE and 0.7 AUC. On mesh recovery (3DPW), HumMUSS(f) slightly outperforms MotionBERT(f) in the causal setting (82.0 vs 82.3 mm MPJPE; 50.2 vs 50.9 mm PA-MPJPE) but is marginally behind in the bidirectional setting (77.4 vs 76.9 mm MPJPE). On action recognition (NTU-RGB+D), HumMUSS (92.0% X-Sub) trails both MotionBERT (93.0%) and SkeleTR (94.8%), though it matches MotionBERT on X-View (97.4% vs 97.2%). The decisive advantage of HumMUSS is efficiency: it is 3.8× more memory-efficient and 11.1× faster for sequential inference at 243 frames, and it generalizes to unseen frame rates with minimal degradation, whereas MotionBERT's accuracy drops sharply under sub-sampling. HumMUSS also trains faster than MotionBERT for long sequences due to O(F log F) vs O(F²) complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HumMUSS transplants the Gated Diagonal State Space Model (GDSSM) architecture from NLP (Mehta et al. [62], Wang et al. [91]) into the human motion understanding domain, replacing the transformer blocks that dominate the field (MotionBERT [109], PoseFormer [106], MHFormer [47]). It retains MotionBERT's dual-stream spatiotemporal design and pretraining recipe (2D-to-3D lifting on AMASS + MPI-INF-3DHP, then 2D reprojection on PoseTrack/InstaVariety) but substitutes attention with diagonal SSMs parameterized via the HiPPO-inspired scheme of Gu et al. [25]. The key conceptual departure is that SSMs' continuous-time formulation (with a learnable discretization Δ) grants frame-rate generalization that discrete token-based transformers fundamentally lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical payoff is substantial for real-time and on-device deployment: O(1) per-step sequential inference (11.1× faster, 3.8× less memory than MotionBERT at 243 frames) and graceful degradation under variable sampling rates make HumMUSS a drop-in replacement for transformer backbones in latency-sensitive pipelines. However, accuracy remains roughly on par with MotionBERT rather than clearly surpassing it (e.g., 16.3 vs 16.0 MPJPE bidirectional 3D pose), and mesh recovery from sparse 2D keypoints alone is still inherently limited, requiring hybrid RGB refinement. Open questions include whether SSM-based motion backbones can close the remaining gap on more complex or longer-horizon tasks, and whether the causal variant's efficiency advantage persists at much larger model scales or with multi-person inputs.
