## Problem

Learning-based 6-DoF grasp detection methods suffer a significant performance drop on objects whose shapes and structures deviate substantially from the training distribution. Prior generalization efforts rely on data augmentation to broaden the training set, but this only helps objects near the training distribution and offers little benefit for truly out-of-distribution novel objects, where predictions become either completely wrong or unstable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240763/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB-D images (256 views per scene from RealSense/Kinect) and reconstructs them into a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) via KinectFusion, producing a more complete fusion point cloud than single-view depth alone. This is critical for novel objects where occluded geometry cannot be inferred from partial point clouds. The network outputs 6-DoF grasp poses (translation, rotation, gripper width) with associated grasp scores, which are then refined at test time by the C-SJO module within a local SE(3) neighborhood.

## Method

The framework has two components. During training, Physical Constraint Regularization (PCR) enforces the antipodal rule (a simplified force-closure condition) as a differentiable loss term, using a 3D SDF grid with trilinear interpolation to compute contact distances and surface normals end-to-end; the regularization combines antipodal, collision, and surface constraints weighted by predicted grasp scores. At test time, Contact-Score Joint Optimization (C-SJO) refines unstable predictions by minimizing a joint objective that aligns a projection contact map (which highlights the true contact region even when contacts are off-surface) with a ContactNet prediction while preserving grasp scores from a ScoreNet, using Adam gradient descent in the pose neighborhood. The backbone is a sparse UNet (Minkowski Engine) with multi-scale cylinder grouping that computes per-point graspness to generate candidates. The key insight is that physics-based constraints are object-independent, making them a distribution-agnostic inductive bias that generalizes to novel shapes where augmentation-based methods fail.

## Results

On the GraspNet-1billion benchmark (RealSense scenes, multi-view TSDF input), the proposed method achieves 36.67% AP on the novel object set, 65.37% AP on the similar set, and 66.61% AP on the seen set. The novel-set improvement is the headline result: a 8.61-point gain over the strongest prior method (GSNet, 28.06%) and a 19.58-point gain over Scale-balanced Grasp (17.09%). In real-world experiments on a UR-10 robot with 20 novel objects, the method reaches 68.33% success rate on isolated grasping and 96% scene completion rate on cluttered grasping, outperforming its own baseline by 10 and 10 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper re-implements three representative 6-DoF grasp detection methods on the same multi-view TSDF input for fair comparison. Against GraspNet-baseline [10], the proposed method wins by a large margin on all three splits (e.g., 36.67% vs 7.48% AP on novel). Against Scale-balanced Grasp [19], the gain is most dramatic on the novel set (36.67% vs 17.09%, +19.58 points), reflecting the method's focus on out-of-distribution generalization. Against GSNet [35], the closest competitor, the proposed method improves novel-set AP by 8.61 points (36.67% vs 28.06%) and novel-set AP₀.₈ by 8.89 points (45.08% vs 36.19%), while maintaining comparable performance on seen (66.61% vs 60.47%) and similar (65.37% vs 58.55%) sets. The paper also shows that its domain-prior approach is complementary to object augmentation: combining both yields 37.00% novel AP, slightly above either alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the scale-balanced 6-DoF grasp detection network (Ma & Huang, CoRL 2022) and GSNet (Wang et al., ICCV 2021) as its architectural backbone, but departs from the dominant generalization strategy in the field—data augmentation via procedural or generative object synthesis—by instead injecting domain prior knowledge from analytical robotics (antipodal rule, force closure) directly into the learning pipeline. The Physical Constraint Regularization draws on classical force-closure and antipodal-point grasping theory (Nguyen 1988, Chen & Burdick 1993), while the Contact-Score Joint Optimization borrows the contact-map concept from dexterous hand-object synthesis (Brahmbhatt et al. 2019, Jiang et al. 2021) and adapts it to the noisier, incomplete point clouds of cluttered 6-DoF detection. Unlike prior physics-informed grasping methods (Liu et al. 2020, 2022) that require accurate watertight object models and complex differentiable force-closure solvers, this work makes the physics constraints tractable for real-time detection by encoding them through a differentiable SDF grid and a lightweight regularization term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physics-informed regularization can outperform data augmentation for out-of-distribution generalization in grasp detection (4.37% AP gain on novel objects without extra data), offering a complementary and potentially more scalable path than ever-larger synthetic object libraries. The projection contact map is a practical insight for handling the singularity problem when contact points drift off the surface in cluttered scenes, and the real-robot validation on a UR-10 with 20 novel objects strengthens the practical credibility. Limitations include the reliance on multi-view TSDF reconstruction (via KinectFusion) which adds a preprocessing step, the local nature of the C-SJO optimization (neighborhood search in SE(3)), and the restriction to two-finger parallel grippers; extending the physics priors to dexterous hands or single-view settings remains open.
