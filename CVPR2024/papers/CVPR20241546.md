## Problem

Existing pathology vision-language models (PLIP, MI-Zero, BiomedCLIP, CONCH) fine-tune using one-to-one image-text alignment with simple noun-based phrases, limiting zero-shot classification because a single disease can be described in many ways by different pathologists and manifest in diverse morphologies. No prior histology VL model incorporates heterogeneous, comprehensive textual prompts during training or inference, leaving a gap in how well these models generalize across unseen tissue categories and cancer subtypes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241546/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes unlabeled histology image tiles (224×224) from the ARCH dataset (~180K images) without any ground-truth annotations, pairing them with bags of up to 17 textual descriptions and 21 retrieved visual concepts. At inference, it accepts either individual histology tiles or gigapixel whole-slide images (binarized via Otsu and tiled at 224×224) along with pathology-specific text prompts, producing class labels via cosine similarity or pixel-level segmentation masks. For WSI-level tasks, tile embeddings are aggregated with top-K pooling (K ∈ {1,5,10,50,100}) to yield slide-level predictions.

## Method

CPLIP replaces the standard one-to-one contrastive objective with a many-to-many bag-based alignment. For each unlabeled histology image, it constructs a textual bag B^t by (i) matching against a curated 200-prompt pathology dictionary via MI-Zero, (ii) expanding the top match into 5 GPT-3 paraphrases plus 3 causes and 3 symptoms, and (iii) retrieving 5 additional descriptions from Medical Twitter via PLIP's text encoder. A corresponding visual bag B^v is built by using PLIP to retrieve images matching each textual description and performing image-to-image retrieval, capped at 21 images. The bags are pruned by a cosine-similarity threshold δ_t, then aligned using an adapted MIL-NCE loss that sums exp-similarities over all positive text-image pairs in the bag while normalizing over all in-batch negatives. This unpaired, annotation-free fine-tuning of PLIP (or other VL backbones) yields a model that transfers zero-shot to tile classification, WSI subtyping, and segmentation across nine independent datasets.

## Results

On tile-level zero-shot classification across five datasets, CPLIP achieves the highest weighted F1 on all five: 0.681 (CRC100K), 0.856 (DigestPath), 0.388 (SICAP), 0.791 (WSSS4LUAD), and 0.757 (PanNuke). The largest gains are on CRC100K (13.5% balanced accuracy and 13.9% weighted F1 over CONCH) and SICAP (14.3% weighted F1 over CONCH). At the WSI level, in-domain CPLIP (CPLIP₂) reaches 82.4% balanced accuracy on TCGA-BRCA subtyping (vs. 64.3% for CONCH) and 92.6% on TCGA-NSCLC (vs. 80.7% for CONCH), setting new state-of-the-art zero-shot cancer subtyping results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPLIP outperforms all five baselines (CLIP, BiomedCLIP, PLIP, MI-Zero, CONCH) on every dataset and every metric in the tile-level comparison. The most dramatic margin is on CRC100K, where CPLIP's 0.681 weighted F1 exceeds CONCH (0.542) by 13.9 points and MI-Zero (0.536) by 14.5 points. On SICAP, CPLIP's 0.388 weighted F1 surpasses CONCH (0.245) by 14.3 points, though absolute scores remain low due to the fine-grained Gleason grading task. On WSSS4LUAD, CPLIP gains 5.6% balanced accuracy and 4.9% weighted F1 over MI-Zero, and substantially outperforms CONCH (which used a different split). At the WSI level, in-domain CPLIP₂ leads CONCH by up to 18.1% balanced accuracy on TCGA-BRCA (82.4% vs. 64.3%) and by 11.9% on TCGA-NSCLC (92.6% vs. 80.7%), while also exceeding MI-Zero by 4.3–7.5% across all four WSI datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPLIP extends the pathology vision-language model lineage (PLIP, MI-Zero, BiomedCLIP, CONCH) by replacing their standard one-to-one image-text contrastive alignment with a many-to-many bag-based objective. It builds directly on PLIP as the base encoder, adapts the MIL-NCE loss from video-text pretraining (Miech et al.) to connect bags of textual descriptions with bags of visual concepts, and inherits CLIP's prompt-based zero-shot inference pipeline. The key conceptual departure is the 'comprehensiveness' principle: rather than aligning a single caption to a single image, CPLIP constructs heterogeneous bags of 17 textual descriptions (sourced from a curated pathology dictionary, GPT-3 paraphrases, and PLIP retrieval) and 21 visual concepts per training instance, arguing that a disease's full semantic footprint requires multi-source, multi-view alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real limitation in pathology VL models—reliance on a single textual prompt per image that may underrepresent the morphological and clinical diversity of a condition—and demonstrates that enriching the training signal with heterogeneous text and image bags yields consistent zero-shot gains across nine datasets. However, the ablation showing that ground-truth paired text (GT+GPT-3+PLIP) outperforms the fully unsupervised dictionary approach (D200+GPT-3+PLIP) on all five tile-level datasets somewhat undermines the 'unsupervised' framing, suggesting the method's gains partly depend on access to paired annotations. Open questions include whether the bag-size caps (17 texts, 21 images) are near-optimal, whether the approach transfers to other medical imaging domains beyond H&E histopathology, and whether the reliance on GPT-3 for text augmentation introduces systematic biases that could limit generalization to rare or emerging cancer subtypes.
