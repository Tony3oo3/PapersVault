## Problem

Plain Vision Transformers underperform on dense prediction tasks (object detection, instance/semantic segmentation) because they lack inner-patch spatial interaction and produce only a single-scale feature representation. Existing remedies—vision-specific backbones like Swin or PVT—redesign the ViT architecture, forcing retraining of pre-trained weights from scratch on large-scale datasets, while adapter-based approaches do not fully exploit multi-scale convolutional features. The paper asks whether one can keep the ViT structure intact (preserving access to all open-source pre-trained weights) while injecting the multi-scale, local-receptive-field strengths of CNNs.

## Input / Output

The backbone consumes a standard RGB image and produces a set of multi-scale feature maps at 1/8, 1/16, and 1/32 spatial resolutions (each D-dimensional), which are then fed into downstream heads (Mask R-CNN, Co-DETR, UperNet, Mask2Former). The ViT branch operates at 1/16 resolution via 16×16 patch embedding, while the CNN branch generates a three-level feature pyramid (C₃, C₄, C₅). Depending on the task head, the final outputs are bounding boxes with class scores, per-instance segmentation masks, or pixel-wise semantic class maps.

## Method

ViT-CoMer is a two-branch architecture that keeps the plain ViT completely unchanged (so any ViT pre-trained weights—ImageNet-1K, 22K, DINOv2, BEiT—can be loaded directly) and adds a parallel CNN branch. The CNN branch employs a Multi-Receptive Field Feature Pyramid (MRFP) that splits channels into groups, applies depth-wise convolutions with different kernel sizes (3×3, 5×5) to each group, and recombines them, yielding multi-scale features with expanded receptive fields. A CNN-Transformer Bidirectional Fusion Interaction (CTI) module then performs N rounds of cross-branch fusion: at each stage, the ViT feature and the 1/16 CNN feature are added, passed through multi-scale deformable attention and an FFN to unify the two modalities, and the result is injected back into both branches (with a learnable α initialized to zero to protect the ViT during early training). This bidirectional, multi-scale interaction is repeated at N=4 stages, after which the two branches' features are summed at each scale for the downstream prediction head.

## Results

On COCO val2017 with Mask R-CNN (3× schedule), ViT-CoMer-L with DINOv2 pre-training achieves 55.9% AP^b and 49.1% AP^m, outperforming ViT-Adapter-L (52.1%/46.0%) and Swin-B (48.5%/43.3%) at comparable or smaller parameter counts. With BEiTv2* pre-training in the Co-DETR framework, ViT-CoMer-L reaches 64.3% AP^b on COCO val2017, the best record without extra detection data. On ADE20K val with UperNet, ViT-CoMer-L attains 55.6% mIoU (+MS) with ImageNet-22K pre-training and 56.8% with multi-modal pre-training, surpassing Swin-L (53.5%) and ViT-Adapter-L (54.4%/55.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against plain ViT backbones, ViT-CoMer-S delivers a substantial +5.6% AP^b and +3.4% AP^m gain over ViT-S under the 1× Mask R-CNN schedule (45.8 vs. 40.2), and +4.8% AP^b under 3× (48.8 vs. 44.0), while using only 1/6 the parameters of ViT-L. Compared to adapted backbones, ViT-CoMer-S edges out ViT-Adapter-S by +1.1% AP^b (45.8 vs. 44.7) at 1× and +0.6% at 3× (48.8 vs. 48.2); at the L scale with DINOv2, ViT-CoMer-L surpasses ViT-Adapter-L† by +3.8% AP^b (55.9 vs. 52.1). Against vision-specific backbones, ViT-CoMer-S outperforms Swin-S (+1.0% AP^b), PVT-S (+5.4%), and MixFormer-B4 (+0.7%) at 1×, though it trails ConvNeXt-T (−2.1%) and Focal-T (−2.7%) at the T scale. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, ViT-CoMer-L achieves 55.6% mIoU+MS, beating Swin-L by +2.1% and ViT-Adapter-L by +1.2% with ImageNet-22K pre-training, and reaching 56.8% with multi-modal pre-training. In the SOTA comparison (Table 4), ViT-CoMer-L with Co-DETR and BEiTv2* pre-training attains 64.3% AP^b, surpassing HTC++ with ViT-Adapter-L (60.5% without Objects365) and Co-DETR with Swin-L (60.7%), while in Table 7 it matches Mask2Former with ViT-Adapter-G (62.1 vs. 62.8 mIoU+MS) using 604M vs. 1.9B parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViT-CoMer positions itself in the 'adapted backbone' paradigm for dense prediction, building directly on plain ViT while adding a parallel CNN branch — a design philosophy shared with ViT-Adapter but departing in key ways. Where ViT-Adapter injects spatial priors without inter-scale interaction, ViT-CoMer introduces a Multi-Receptive Field Feature Pyramid (drawing on FPN and multi-kernel depthwise convolution ideas) and a bidirectional fusion module using multi-scale deformable attention (borrowed from Deformable DETR) to unify CNN and Transformer features across resolutions. Unlike vision-specific backbones (Swin, PVT, MixFormer) that redesign the transformer and forfeit access to existing ViT pre-trained weights, ViT-CoMer preserves the original ViT architecture intact, enabling direct loading of DINOv2, BEiT, or ImageNet-22K checkpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling backbone design from pre-training: any future ViT pre-trained weight can be immediately plugged into ViT-CoMer for dense prediction without retraining from scratch, a constraint that vision-specific backbones cannot satisfy. The 64.3% AP on COCO val2017 without extra detection data (e.g., Objects365) and 62.1% mIoU on ADE20K demonstrate that the CNN-Transformer interaction is sufficient to close the gap with purpose-built hierarchical transformers. Open questions include whether the bidirectional fusion generalizes to video or 3D tasks, whether the approach remains effective at very large scales (beyond ViT-L), and whether the full-finetuning requirement can be relaxed to parameter-efficient adaptation without significant performance loss.
