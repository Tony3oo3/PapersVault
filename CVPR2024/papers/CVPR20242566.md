## Problem

Multi-modal Language Models (MLMs) frequently produce hallucinated or incorrect VQA answers, especially when relevant visual details are small or hard to perceive. While contextual information (e.g., image descriptions) can help answer such questions, it is not uniformly beneficial and can introduce noise that degrades performance. The paper addresses the gap of determining *when* generated context is actually helpful for a given sample and how to best aggregate multiple context-conditioned answers.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a single-choice question as input and produces a final VQA answer selected from the option likelihood distribution. At inference time, the same MLM generates N context descriptions (40 for LLaVA, 20 for LLaVA-v1.5) using a simple prompt, and each candidate's answer likelihood is computed by averaging log-probabilities over the answer tokens. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates as a plug-and-play decoding strategy on off-the-shelf MLMs.

## Method

Causal-CoG is a training-free prompting strategy with three components: (1) Context Generation, where the MLM is prompted to produce multiple image descriptions; (2) a Causality Filter that computes the Natural Direct Effect (NDE) and Total Indirect Effect (TIE) of the image on the answer distribution using Jensen-Shannon Divergence, applying CoG only when TIE > NDE (i.e., the indirect path through context dominates); and (3) Top-k Aggregation, where candidates are weighted by their individual TIE^c values (the JSD between the context-conditioned and direct answer distributions) and the top-k candidates are combined via weighted majority vote. The key insight is framing the context's contribution as a causal mediation effect, enabling principled per-sample filtering and candidate ranking without any retraining.

## Results

Causal-CoG applied to LLaVA achieves consistent improvements across all 10 benchmarks, with headline gains of +6.30% on POPE (70.85 vs 64.55), +13.69% on Vizwiz* (42.23 vs 28.54), and +6.43% on VQAv2* (49.44 vs 43.10). On the stronger LLaVA-v1.5 backbone, Causal-CoG still yields +7.43% on Vizwiz* and +3.60% on VSR, though it slightly underperforms on MME (−0.43) and GQA* (−0.87). The method also outperforms Naive-CoG (single-candidate context generation without causality filtering or aggregation) on most benchmarks, e.g., +4.18% on Vizwiz* and +1.63% on VQAv2*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-CoG is compared against two decoding-stage baselines on LLaVA: Ensemble (averaging likelihood distributions from 5 different system prompts, based on CLIP-style prompting [Radford et al., ICML 2021]) and One-shot in-context learning (following Flamingo [Alayrac et al., NeurIPS 2022]). Causal-CoG outperforms both baselines on all 10 benchmarks. Against Ensemble, the largest margins are on Vizwiz* (+12.76), VQAv2* (+6.58), and POPE (+4.39); against One-shot, the largest margins are on Vizwiz* (+9.98), VQAv2* (+6.53), and VSR (+7.37). Notably, both baselines show inconsistent behavior — Ensemble drops below the base LLaVA on VSR (54.42 vs 55.56) and GQA* (37.23 vs 37.71), while One-shot underperforms LLaVA on MME (54.72 vs 57.49) and VSR (51.55 vs 55.56) — whereas Causal-CoG improves or matches the base model on 9 out of 10 benchmarks for LLaVA and 8 out of 10 for LLaVA-v1.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes causal inference concepts (Natural Direct Effect, Total Indirect Effect) from the statistics literature into a practical decoding strategy for multi-modal language models, drawing an analogy between the image→context→answer pathway and a mediated causal graph. It extends the prompting paradigm well-established in LLMs (chain-of-thought, self-consistency) to MLMs, but departs from prior context-aware VQA work (ScienceQA, MM-CoT, LLaMA-Adapter-V2) by having the MLM itself generate the context via a simple 'describe this image' prompt rather than relying on external rationales or retrained models. The Jensen-Shannon Divergence serves as a tractable proxy for comparing potential outcomes, making the causal framework computationally feasible at inference time without any training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Causal-CoG offers a training-free, plug-and-play decoding strategy that consistently improves off-the-shelf MLMs across 10 benchmarks, which is practically valuable given the difficulty of retraining large multimodal models. The causality filter's ability to identify when context is harmful (NDE > TIE) and skip CoG in those cases is a useful safeguard, though the method's effectiveness diminishes on stronger models like LLaVA-v1.5 (some negative deltas), raising questions about whether the approach scales as base models improve. Open questions include whether richer context types (multi-step reasoning, object-level descriptions) would yield larger gains, whether the 40-candidate generation cost is prohibitive for real-time deployment, and whether the causal framework can be extended to other decoding decisions beyond context selection.
