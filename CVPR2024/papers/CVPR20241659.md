## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors rely on frequency-domain artifacts or model-specific fingerprints that fail to generalize to unseen generation sources. While up-sampling layers are ubiquitous in both GAN and diffusion pipelines, prior work has only examined their global spectral impact, leaving the local spatial-domain artifacts they imprint on neighboring pixels unexplored as a source-invariant detection cue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241659/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and computes Neighboring Pixel Relationships (NPR) by partitioning each color channel into non-overlapping 2×2 grids and subtracting a reference pixel (w₁) from every other pixel in the grid, yielding a 3-channel × 3-difference artifact map of the same spatial resolution. This NPR representation is fed into a lightweight CNN (convolutional layers + ResNet blocks, 1.44M parameters) that outputs a single real/fake probability score. No pre-processing, frequency transforms, or external model access is required.

## Method

The key insight is that up-sampling operations (nearest-neighbor, bilinear, etc.) create strong local correlations among pixels in the up-scaled feature map, and because subsequent convolutional layers are translation-invariant, this local interdependence persists in the final generated image regardless of the specific generator. NPR formalizes this by dividing the image into l×l patches (l=2) and computing pairwise differences wᵢ − wⱼ within each patch, capturing a local, relative, and source-agnostic structural signature of the up-sampling trace. This representation is then used as the sole input to a lightweight binary classifier trained with standard supervised cross-entropy loss on ProGAN (car, cat, chair, horse) images. The approach generalizes across 28 generation models—including GANs, DDPM, LDM, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, and DALL-E—because it exploits the shared architectural property of up-sampling rather than model-specific frequency patterns, achieving 93.3% mean accuracy across all test sources.

## Results

Across 28 generation models (17 GANs + 11 Diffusions), NPR achieves a mean accuracy of 93.3% over 38 sub-testsets, outperforming the strongest baselines LGrad (80.5%) and Ojha (79.8%) by 12.8% and 13.5% respectively. On the ForenSynths GAN test set, NPR reaches 92.5% mean accuracy and 96.1% mean A.P.; on the DiffusionForensics diffusion test set, it reaches 95.3% mean accuracy and 99.8% mean A.P. The detector is trained solely on ProGAN (car, cat, chair, horse) images yet generalizes to all unseen GAN and diffusion sources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPR outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluation setting. On the ForenSynths GAN test set, NPR (92.5% mean Acc.) surpasses the two strongest baselines LGrad (86.1%) and Ojha (89.1%) by 6.4% and 3.4% respectively. On the DiffusionForensics test set, the gap widens dramatically: NPR (95.3%) beats LGrad (88.2%) by 7.1% and Ojha (74.4%) by 20.9%, indicating that frequency-based and gradient-based representations degrade more severely on diffusion-generated images. On the more challenging 1000-step diffusion set (including Midjourney and DALL-E from Discord), NPR (80.1%) still leads LGrad (64.2%) by 15.9% and Ojha (66.4%) by 13.7%. Frequency-domain methods (Frank, Durall, F3Net, BiHPF, FrePGAN) and image-domain methods (CNNDetection, SelfBland, GANDetection) all fall well below NPR, with mean accuracies ranging from 46% to 81% across the 28 models, confirming that local spatial up-sampling artifacts captured by NPR provide a more source-invariant signal than global frequency or patch-level features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions the up-sampling artifact literature by shifting from global frequency-domain analysis (Frank, Durall, F3Net, BiHPF) to local spatial-domain pixel relationships. While prior work established that up-sampling leaves spectral fingerprints in generated images, and FrePGAN noted that frequency artifacts overfit to specific GAN architectures, this paper argues that the *local interdependence* among neighboring pixels induced by up-sampling combined with CNN translation invariance is a more source-invariant signal. The NPR representation is a novel, minimal construction (pixel subtraction within 2×2 blocks) that repurposes the well-known up-sampling observation without relying on any specific frequency decomposition or complex feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.8% mean-accuracy gain over the strongest baselines (Ojha, LGrad) across 28 generation models, with a detector trained solely on ProGAN generalizing to diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, DALL-E), demonstrates that a trivially simple spatial representation can outperform elaborate frequency-domain pipelines. This suggests the field may have over-invested in frequency analysis at the expense of simpler spatial cues. Open questions include whether NPR extends to non-CNN generators (e.g., transformer-based diffusion decoders), video deepfakes, and whether the 2×2 block size remains optimal as generator architectures evolve. The method's simplicity also raises the question of whether further gains can be extracted from even more local or higher-order pixel relationships.
