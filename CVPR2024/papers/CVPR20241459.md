## Problem

Reconstructing parametric CAD construction sequences from point clouds is critical for bridging real-world geometry and reusable digital designs, yet prior methods either rely on fixed sketch-extrusion pairs with heuristic primitive fitting, or treat CAD sequences as language without incorporating the top-down design paradigm that human designers follow (outlines first, details later). No existing work integrates this design methodology into the generative reconstruction process, resulting in sequences that are geometrically accurate but less likely to be reused by human designers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a CAD point cloud as input, which is compressed into 64 discrete tokens via a pretrained VQ-VAE codebook. The output is a hierarchical CAD construction sequence represented as a depth-first traversal of a primitive token tree, where each sketch-extrusion pair is encoded with SOL loop markers, geometric primitive tokens (type + parameters), and extrusion tokens (plane location, axis direction, scale, Boolean operation). The sequence format is a modification of DeepCAD's representation with unnecessary tokens removed.

## Method

CAD-Diffuser unifies point cloud tokens and CAD construction sequence tokens at the discrete token level, modeling the forward diffusion process as a random walk between each token and a [MASK] token (absorbing state for target CAD tokens, reflective state for source point cloud tokens), and the reverse process as masked token prediction by a DistilBERT denoiser. A volume-based noise schedule orders masking by each token's contributed volume—detail tokens are masked first and outline tokens last—so the learned reverse process generates outlines before details, mimicking the top-down design paradigm. The point cloud tokenizer is a discrete VAE trained with a self-supervised face segmentation pretext task that uses vMF Mean Shift clustering to pull same-face features together and separate different-face features in embedding space. Training proceeds in two stages: the tokenizer is pretrained for 200 epochs, then the multimodal diffuser is trained for 350 epochs with batch size 512, where at each timestep only the tokens masked at that specific step contribute to the loss.

## Results

On DeepCAD, CAD-Diffuser achieves 88.55% command accuracy and 82.92% parameter accuracy, with a median Chamfer Distance of 0.302 and IoU of 0.743, while maintaining the lowest invalid rate (1.48%) and smallest primitive count difference (#ΔP = 2.20) among all methods. On Fusion 360 Gallery, the method reaches 85.56% command accuracy, 80.48% parameter accuracy, 0.385 median CD, and 0.632 IoU with only 1.65% invalid rate. In generation diversity (Table 2), CAD-Diffuser also leads with 0.45 distinct N-grams, 0.082 embedding similarity, and 0.73 edit distance, outperforming DeepCAD, SkexGen, and HNC-CAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-Diffuser outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across both DeepCAD and Fusion 360 Gallery. The closest competitor, HNC-CAD [49], trails by 5.86 points in command accuracy (82.69% vs. 88.55%) and 8.34 points in parameter accuracy (74.58% vs. 82.92%) on DeepCAD, and by 10.10 and 15.96 points respectively on Fusion 360. Reconstruction-based methods (Point2cyl, ExtrudeNet, SECAD-Net) are far behind on sequence fidelity, with command accuracies below 37%, though they match or exceed CAD-Diffuser on geometric IoU (e.g., Point2cyl reaches 0.738 IoU vs. 0.743). The search-based method (Lambourne's [21]) achieves 72.96% command accuracy on DeepCAD but suffers a 16.16% invalid rate, nearly 11× higher than CAD-Diffuser's 1.48%. On generation diversity, CAD-Diffuser leads all baselines in distinct N-grams (0.45 vs. 0.35 for HNC-CAD) and embedding similarity (0.082 vs. 0.138), confirming that the volume-based noise schedule produces both more accurate and more diverse CAD sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CAD reconstruction from point clouds via heuristic or learned sketch-extrusion fitting (Point2cyl, ExtrudeNet, SECAD-Net), (2) language-model-based CAD sequence generation (DeepCAD, HNC-CAD), and (3) discrete diffusion for token generation (DiffusionBERT, Diffuseq, Diffusion-NAT). It departs from prior CAD reconstruction by reframing the problem as a multimodal discrete diffusion between point cloud tokens and CAD sequence tokens, and from prior text diffusion by introducing a volume-based noise schedule that encodes the top-down design paradigm (outlines before details) into the denoising order. The point cloud tokenizer, trained with a self-supervised face segmentation pretext task via vMF Mean Shift, extends VQ-VAE-based point cloud encoding with geometric structure awareness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that encoding design methodology (top-down, outline-first) into the noise schedule of a diffusion model yields CAD sequences that are not only geometrically accurate but also more aligned with human design workflows, as validated by a user study showing near-ground-truth reusability ratings. The generation diversity results—substantially outperforming autoregressive baselines on N-gram, edit distance, and coverage metrics—suggest that the stepwise denoising process naturally explores the design space more broadly than one-pass generation. Open questions include whether the volume-based schedule generalizes to more complex CAD operations beyond sketch-extrusion (e.g., revolve, sweep, fillet), whether the approach scales to larger pretrained language models, and whether the separate point cloud tokenizer training can be unified with the diffusion training in an end-to-end fashion.
