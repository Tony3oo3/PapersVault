## Problem

Vision Transformers are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations comparable to CNNs, and while adversarial training transfers well to ViTs, existing randomization defenses designed for CNNs (e.g., randomized normalization layers) do not directly apply due to architectural differences such as the use of Layer Normalization and the self-attention mechanism. The paper addresses how to design a randomization strategy that is native to the ViT self-attention structure to further reduce adversarial transferability.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet/ImageNette) and outputs class labels. During inference, a random token sampled from a standard Gaussian distribution (scaled by intensity s=0.1) is concatenated to the patch-embedded input tokens at every self-attention layer, and the final output is computed via a modified Local SoftMax that preserves the original attention weights for input tokens while adding a random additive term. The extra random output row is truncated to maintain consistent output dimensions.

## Method

ReiT introduces a Random Self-Attention module that concatenates a random token to input tokens and replaces the global SoftMax with a Local SoftMax (LSM) that only normalizes over the original input-token block, yielding an output Y_out = Y + R where R is an additive random perturbation. At inference, the Input-Independent Random entangled Self-Attention (II-ReSA) module samples a first random token r₁, then iteratively optimizes a second token r₂ via gradient sign descent to minimize the dot-product similarity between ∂R₁/∂X and ∂R₂/∂X, making the two attention outputs maximally dissimilar; the optimization depends only on the linear projection parameters (W^Q, W^K, W^V) and r₁, not on the input, so it is input-independent and computationally cheap. A final random token is sampled uniformly from {r₁, r₂} and concatenated to the input, creating a large random search space (e.g., 2^96 for a 12-block, 8-head ViT) that hampers adversarial perturbation optimization. During training, random tokens are concatenated to inputs alongside standard adversarial training (PGD, FGSM, or TRADES), teaching the model to tolerate the random additive term and avoiding clean-accuracy degradation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PGD-trained ViT-S, ReiT achieves 52.86% adversarial accuracy under PGD attack (+1.35% over vanilla PGD's 51.51%) and 54.95% under TRADES training (+0.60% over vanilla TRADES's 54.35%). On CIFAR-100 with TRADES-trained ViT-S, ReiT reaches 34.06% PGD accuracy versus 31.56% for vanilla TRADES. On ImageNet with TRADES-trained DeiT-T, ReiT attains 23.55% PGD accuracy versus 22.90% for vanilla TRADES. ReiT also substantially improves defense against unseen attacks (e.g., DeepFool and CW), where vanilla models often drop to near-zero accuracy while ReiT maintains 20–27% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole external baseline is ARD & PRM (Attention Random Dropping + Perturbation Random Masking) from Mo et al. [38, NeurIPS 2022]. On CIFAR-10, ReiT outperforms ARD & PRM on every metric for both ViT-S and DeiT-T: for ViT-S, ReiT gains +0.95% on PGD-20 (52.68 vs 51.73), +1.04% on PGD-100 (52.61 vs 51.57), and +1.72% on AutoAttack (49.70 vs 47.98); for DeiT-T, the gains are +0.55% on PGD-20, +0.60% on PGD-100, and +3.56% on AutoAttack (50.39 vs 46.83). ReiT also preserves clean accuracy better than ARD & PRM (86.55% vs 85.18% for ViT-S), whereas ARD & PRM sacrifices ~1.4% clean accuracy. Internally, ReiT consistently improves over vanilla adversarial training (PGD, FGSM, TRADES) across all six ViT variants and four datasets, with the largest gains appearing on unseen attacks (DeepFool, CW) where vanilla models often collapse to near-zero accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized defense line of work (random normalization, input noise, random discretization) from CNNs to ViTs, arguing that existing CNN-oriented randomization strategies (e.g., randomizing BatchNorm) are architecturally mismatched with ViTs that rely on LayerNorm and self-attention. It builds on the adversarial training framework (PGD, TRADES) already validated for ViTs, but departs from prior ViT robustness work by targeting the self-attention mechanism itself: concatenating random tokens to input tokens and introducing a Local SoftMax operation that preserves the original attention computation while injecting a controlled random additive term. The input-independence of the II-ReSA module—optimizing the second random token using only linear projection parameters rather than the input—distinguishes it from input-dependent randomization approaches and keeps inference overhead minimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReiT offers a practical, architecture-aware randomized defense for ViTs that can be layered on top of standard adversarial training (PGD, TRADES) with modest inference cost, and the dual randomization (two entangled tokens plus random sampling across blocks and heads) creates a theoretically large random space that hampers adaptive attack search. However, the clean-accuracy drop (~1%) and the iterative optimization of r₂ at inference introduce a small but non-trivial overhead, and the method has only been validated on small/tiny ViT variants (ViT-S/T, DeiT-S/T, Swin-S/T) on relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger ViTs (ViT-L, ViT-H) and whether the randomization provides meaningful protection against adaptive white-box attacks that can query the model multiple times to average out the randomness.
