## Problem

Existing visual odometry methods rely on two-view point tracking, ignoring the rich temporal context in image sequences and providing no assessment of trajectory reliability. This leads to failures under occlusion, dynamic objects, and low-texture regions. Moreover, prior long-term point trackers like PIPs process each query independently, overlooking global motion patterns and lacking a reliability metric needed for quality-sensitive downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of S consecutive RGB images (S=8 model window, extendable to S_LP=12 via sequential prediction) along with N query point coordinates (N=256 during training). Beyond standard 2D trajectories, it outputs a full multivariate Cauchy distribution over the trajectory (location and scale matrices for X and Y coordinates separately), per-frame binary visibility labels, a per-track dynamic/static label, and a scalar per-point uncertainty derived from the diagonal of the combined scale matrices. In the LEAP-VO system, these outputs feed into a sliding-window bundle adjustment (S_BA=15) that produces camera poses and 3D scene point positions.

## Method

LEAP builds on CoTracker's iterative refinement with local cost volumes and adds two key modules. First, an anchor-based dynamic track estimation module samples 64 well-distributed anchors via Sobel-gradient-based grid sampling, then applies inter-track attention between query and anchor features/trajectories through a shallow MLP with temporal average pooling to predict a dynamic track label. Second, a temporal probabilistic formulation models the trajectory as a product of two multivariate Cauchy distributions (one per coordinate axis), with scale matrices constructed via linear-kernel Gram matrices of projected point features plus a small regularization term, trained with a weighted negative log-likelihood loss. The LEAP-VO pipeline uses LEAP as the front-end, applies multi-criteria track filtering (visibility threshold, dynamic label threshold, uncertainty quantile, minimum track length), and performs sliding-window bundle adjustment with Huber loss optimized via Gauss-Newton and Schur decomposition.

## Results

On the dynamic MPI Sintel benchmark, LEAP-VO achieves an ATE of 0.037 m, RPE trans of 0.055 m, and RPE rot of 1.263°, substantially outperforming the next-best method DPVO (0.076 m ATE) by 2×. On the static Replica dataset, LEAP-VO records 0.204 m ATE, 0.030 m RPE trans, and 1.992° RPE rot, beating DPVO (0.257 m) and DytanVO (0.289 m). On TartanAir-Shibuya, LEAP-VO achieves an average ATE of 0.0290 m across seven sequences, nearly halving the error of DytanVO (0.0607 m) and outperforming DPVO (0.1514 m) by over 5×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAP-VO outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. On MPI Sintel, traditional feature-based methods ORB-SLAM2 and DynaSLAM completely fail to track (marked 'X'), while LEAP-VO achieves 0.037 m ATE—nearly 2× better than DPVO (0.076 m) and 3.5× better than DytanVO (0.131 m). On the static Replica benchmark, LEAP-VO (0.204 m ATE) beats DPVO (0.257 m), DytanVO (0.289 m), and TartanVO (0.406 m), though classical ORB-SLAM2 (0.086 m) and DynaSLAM (0.039 m) remain stronger in purely static settings due to re-localization. On TartanAir-Shibuya, LEAP-VO's average ATE of 0.0290 m is 2× better than DytanVO (0.0607 m) and 5× better than DPVO (0.1514 m), with the largest margins on the hardest sequences (RoadCrossing 06: 0.0689 m vs. 0.1119 m for DPVO; RoadCrossing 07: 0.0246 m vs. 0.1498 m for DPVO). The front-end ablation (Table 6) confirms that the LEAP module itself—rather than the bundle adjustment—drives the gains: replacing LEAP with the pretrained CoTracker front-end degrades ATE from 0.037 m to 0.126 m on MPI Sintel and from 0.029 m to 0.160 m on Shibuya.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PIPs/CoTracker line of iterative-refinement long-term point tracking (TAP) by adding two components: an anchor-based inter-track attention module for dynamic track estimation, and a temporal probabilistic formulation using multivariate Cauchy distributions for per-point uncertainty. It departs from prior VO front-ends (ORB-SLAM2, DROID-SLAM, DPVO) that rely on two-view feature matching or dense optical flow, instead using multi-frame point trajectories as the correspondence source. The semi-supervised training scheme for anchors (no ground truth for anchor trajectories) is a practical departure from fully supervised TAP training, and the use of long-term tracking as a VO front-end is framed as a novel integration rather than a new tracking algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that long-term point tracking can serve as a more informative VO front-end than two-view matching, particularly in dynamic scenes where it achieves roughly 2× lower ATE than the best baselines (DPVO, DytanVO) on MPI Sintel and TartanAir-Shibuya. The probabilistic uncertainty output enables a principled track-filtering step that replaces RANSAC, and the ablation showing a 130% ATE increase without confidence filtering underscores its importance. However, all evaluations are on synthetic datasets (Replica, MPI Sintel, TartanAir-Shibuya), leaving open questions about real-world robustness, computational cost for real-time deployment, and whether the Cauchy distribution choice generalizes beyond the tested scenarios. The approach is also monocular and tied to the CoTracker architecture, so its transferability to other TAP front-ends or stereo/RGB-D settings remains unexplored.
