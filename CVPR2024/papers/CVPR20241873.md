## Problem

Class-agnostic object counting (few-shot via example boxes, zero-shot via class names) remains challenging because density-based methods lack interpretability, while detection-based approaches overfit to training categories. A natural solution—using SAM to segment all objects and CLIP to classify them—fails in practice: uniform grid point prompts miss small crowded objects, and CLIP's image-level representations are not discriminative enough for small regions. The paper addresses how to synergize SAM and CLIP for counting without compromising their zero-shot generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 1024×1024) plus either three example bounding boxes (few-shot) or a class name string (zero-shot). It produces a final object count (number of detected boxes above a score threshold after NMS), along with per-object bounding boxes and segmentation masks. Internally, the point decoder outputs a 256×256 class-agnostic heatmap from which the top K=1000 peak points are extracted (averaging only ~378–388 points per image), and SAM generates hierarchical mask proposals (whole/part/subpart) per point that are then scored and filtered.

## Method

PseCo follows three stages—point, segment, count—built on frozen SAM (ViT-H) and CLIP (ViT-B) backbones. The point decoder, initialized from SAM's mask decoder, is trained as a keypoint-estimation task to predict a class-agnostic heatmap; its supervision combines SAM-detected contour centers with ground-truth points to avoid overfitting to training categories, using Gaussian-kernel splatting and MSE loss. SAM then segments all objects conditioned on the predicted point prompts, producing hierarchical mask proposals. A lightweight two-layer MLP classifier scores each proposal against CLIP image/text embeddings (serving as classification weights W), trained with binary cross-entropy plus a hierarchical knowledge distillation loss that aligns region features with CLIP embeddings of nested proposals from the same point (IoU > 0.5 defines positives), making the classifier discriminative among small, overlapping masks. Only the point decoder and classifier are trained (50k iterations, batch 32, Adam lr=1e-4); SAM's image encoder and mask decoder remain frozen, keeping inference cost comparable to a 32×32 grid while covering a 256×256 search space.

## Results

On FSC-147 few-shot object counting (test set), PseCo achieves MAE 13.05 and RMSE 112.86, outperforming the best detection-based baseline C-DETR (MAE 16.79, RMSE 123.56) and approaching the best density-based method CounTR (MAE 11.95, RMSE 91.23). For zero-shot counting, PseCo reaches MAE 16.58 and RMSE 129.77, surpassing SAM-Free (MAE 24.79) and SAM (MAE 42.48). On object detection, PseCo nearly doubles C-DETR's AP on FSC-147 (43.53 vs. 22.66) and achieves 4.5× improvement on FSCD-LVIS (22.37 vs. 4.92). On large-scale COCO and LVIS, PseCo outperforms Detic by +5.1 AP50n on COCO and +3.8 APm on LVIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PseCo outperforms all detection-based counting baselines: it reduces MAE by 3.74 over C-DETR (13.05 vs. 16.79), 6.90 over SAM-Free (13.05 vs. 19.95), and 14.92 over vanilla SAM (13.05 vs. 27.97), while also improving RMSE over all of them. Against the strongest density-based methods, PseCo is competitive on MAE (13.05 vs. CounTR's 11.95) but lags on RMSE (112.86 vs. 91.23), which the authors attribute to the inherent difficulty of detection-based counting versus density regression. In zero-shot counting, PseCo (MAE 16.58) beats SAM-Free (24.79) and SAM (42.48) but remains behind CounTR (14.12), with the gap attributed to ambiguous class names in FSC-147. For object detection, PseCo achieves roughly 2× AP improvement over C-DETR on FSC-147 (43.53 vs. 22.66) and 4.5× on FSCD-LVIS (22.37 vs. 4.92), and surpasses CAL+ViLD by +2.97 AP on FSC-147 few-shot. On large-scale open-vocabulary detection, PseCo leads Detic by +5.1 AP50n on COCO (32.9 vs. 27.8) and +4.0 APm on LVIS (30.8 vs. 26.8), while also outperforming ViLD and OVR-CNN on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PseCo departs from the dominant density-map paradigm in class-agnostic counting (CounTR, BMNet+, FamNet) by formulating counting as detection, and from prior detection-based counting (C-DETR, FR/FSOD) by replacing the RPN with a learned class-agnostic point decoder and the classifier with CLIP embeddings. It builds directly on SAM's zero-shot segmentation (using its image encoder and mask decoder as frozen components) and CLIP's vision-language alignment (using text/image embeddings as classification weights), extending ViLD's knowledge distillation into a hierarchical variant that discriminates among SAM's multi-scale mask proposals. The key conceptual departure is treating object localization as keypoint estimation (following HigherHRNet's splatting formulation) rather than box regression, which naturally handles small and crowded objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PseCo provides an interpretable, detection-based alternative to density-map counting that matches or approaches SOTA density methods while producing verifiable per-object detections, addressing a long-standing interpretability gap in the field. The class-agnostic object localization via keypoint heatmaps is a practical solution to the small-object miss problem inherent in uniform grid prompting, and the hierarchical knowledge distillation offers a reusable recipe for making CLIP-based classifiers discriminative on fine-grained proposals. Limitations include sensitivity to occlusion (inherited from SAM) and a notable performance gap between few-shot and zero-shot settings, particularly on ambiguous class names. The authors point toward fine-grained counting with multi-modal LLMs as a natural next step, and the framework's reliance on frozen SAM/CLIP leaves open how much further performance could improve with end-to-end fine-tuning.
