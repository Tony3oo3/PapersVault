## Problem

Existing fingerprinting mechanisms for open-source text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) rely on independent post-hoc modules that end-users can trivially bypass by commenting out a single line of code. This paper asks whether user attribution can be achieved by embedding fingerprints directly into the model weights themselves, making circumvention infeasible without structural model modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241536/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt for image generation and a user-specific binary fingerprint vector φ of length d_φ (default 32 bits, drawn from Bernoulli(0.5)) as inputs. The fingerprint is first mapped through a two-layer fully connected network into an intermediate representation, then used to modulate decoder weights via per-channel scaling. The output is a fingerprinted image (indistinguishable from a standard SD output) and, when decoded by a ResNet-50 fingerprint decoder, the recovered binary fingerprint bits. The fingerprinted decoder accepts only latent-space input, meaning the user receives a modified model rather than a watermarking overlay.

## Method

WOUAF embeds user fingerprints by applying per-channel multiplicative weight modulation exclusively to the autoencoder decoder D of Stable Diffusion: W^φ_{i,j,k} = u_j · W_{i,j,k}, where u_j is derived from an affine transformation of the mapped fingerprint. A key design choice is modulating only the decoder (not the U-Net diffusion model), as jointly modulating both—mirroring GAN-based approaches—forces a trade-off between attribution accuracy and image quality that cannot be simultaneously optimized. Training uses two objectives: a binary cross-entropy fingerprint decoding loss (via a ResNet-50 decoder) and a perceptual quality regularization loss, both weighted equally. The system requires a single one-time training pass; generating a user-specific model then costs only one forward pass through the mapping network, in contrast to per-user fine-tuning required by Stable Signature. Robustness is further enhanced by incorporating differentiable post-processing augmentations (erasing, rotation, blurring, cropping, brightness, noise, JPEG) during training via the Kornia library.

## Results

On MS-COCO, WOUAF-all achieves 0.99 attribution accuracy with a FID of 24.42 and CLIP-score of 0.73, matching the original Stable Diffusion baseline (FID 24.48, CLIP 0.73) while DAG drops to 0.70 accuracy and FID 26.54. On LAION-Aesthetics, WOUAF-all reaches 0.99 attribution accuracy with FID 19.91 and CLIP 0.51, slightly outperforming Stable Signature (0.98 accuracy, FID 20.02). Fingerprinting time is under 1 second per user, compared to 8.4 hours for DAG and under 1 minute for Stable Signature. Against image post-processes, WOUAF achieves an average 11% improvement in attribution accuracy over Stable Signature and 29% over DAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAG [16], WOUAF dominates on every metric: attribution accuracy jumps from 0.70–0.71 to 0.99, FID improves from 26.54 to 24.42 (MS-COCO), and fingerprinting time drops from 8.4 hours to under 1 second. Against Stable Signature [5], WOUAF matches the 0.99 attribution accuracy on MS-COCO and edges ahead on LAION (0.99 vs. 0.98), while also achieving a marginally better FID (19.91 vs. 20.02) and a faster fingerprinting time (<1 sec vs. <1 min). The most significant advantage over Stable Signature appears in robustness: under eight types of image post-processing (erasing, rotation, blurring, cropping, brightness, noise, JPEG, and combinations), WOUAF maintains an average 11% higher attribution accuracy than Stable Signature and 29% higher than DAG. Compared to the original Stable Diffusion baseline, WOUAF-all introduces negligible quality degradation (FID 24.42 vs. 24.48 on MS-COCO; 19.91 vs. 19.67 on LAION), confirming that the fingerprint is effectively imperceptible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the weight modulation fingerprinting technique originally developed for GANs (Yu et al. 2020) to the Stable Diffusion architecture, shifting from inventor-oriented (train-from-scratch) to distributor-oriented (fine-tune-at-distribution) fingerprinting. It departs from the closest prior work, Stable Signature (Fernandez et al. 2023), which also fine-tunes SD per user but requires fixed-time fine-tuning for each fingerprint, whereas WOUAF needs only a single forward pass through a learned mapping network. A key design departure from the GAN analogy is modulating only the autoencoder decoder rather than both the diffusion U-Net and decoder, a choice empirically justified in the paper.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, computationally cheap mechanism for model distributors (e.g., HuggingFace) to attribute generated images to specific users, addressing a growing accountability gap as T2I models proliferate. However, the approach is validated only on Stable Diffusion with 32-bit fingerprints, and the auto-encoder attack experiments reveal that aggressive compression can degrade attribution to near-random levels—albeit at the cost of image quality. Open questions include generalization to other diffusion architectures, longer fingerprint lengths for larger user bases, and extension to non-image modalities (text, audio, video) as the authors note.
