## Problem

Existing event-based trackers either fuse RGB and event data at inference (incurring high computational cost) or rely solely on event signals (suffering from sparse spatial resolution and noisy events in slow-motion scenes). Additionally, all prior event-based tracking datasets are limited to 346×260 resolution, leaving it unexplored whether event representation and tracking methods generalize to high-resolution event streams. The paper asks whether multi-modal or multi-view knowledge acquired during training can be transferred to enable robust, low-latency event-only tracking at test time.

## Input / Output

At inference, the student tracker consumes only event data—either event images (stacked to match RGB exposure timing) or event voxels (split along spatial W×H and temporal T dimensions into grids of scale a×b×c)—at 1280×720 resolution from a Prophesee EVK4-HD camera. The output is a response map over the search region from which the target bounding box is extracted. The teacher network, used only during training, additionally ingests the corresponding RGB frame alongside the event stream (or a second event representation such as voxels when images are used for the student).

## Method

HDETrack employs a teacher-student Transformer architecture where the teacher is trained on RGB+event (or multi-view event: image+voxel) pairs using a unified Transformer backbone (following OSTrack/CEUTrack), while the student receives only event data. Three hierarchical distillation losses guide the student: (1) similarity-matrix KD aligns the multi-head self-attention attention maps between teacher and student layers via L2 loss after dimension matching; (2) feature KD minimizes the Frobenius-norm distance between teacher and student token representations; (3) response-map KD applies a Gaussian Weighted Focal (GWF) loss between temperature-scaled (τ=2) teacher and student response maps. The overall objective combines standard tracking losses (focal, L1, GIoU) with the three distillation terms, enabling the student to inherit cross-modal relational knowledge while remaining a lightweight, single-modality tracker at 105 FPS.

## Results

On the newly proposed high-resolution EventVOT dataset (1280×720), HDETrack achieves 57.8 SR, 62.2 PR, and 73.5 NPR, outperforming the base OSTrack architecture (55.4/60.4/71.1) by +2.4/+1.8/+2.4 points respectively. On the low-resolution FE240hz dataset, the method reaches 59.8 SR / 92.2 PR, surpassing STNet (58.5/89.6) and TransT (56.7/89.0). On COESOT, HDETrack scores 53.1/64.1/64.5 (SR/PR/NPR), and on VisEvent (event-only input) it achieves 37.3/54.6/44.5, leading all event-only baselines on PR and NPR. The tracker runs at 105 FPS with 92.1M parameters, matching the speed of the base OSTrack while delivering consistent accuracy gains across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EventVOT, HDETrack outperforms all 12 compared trackers on all three metrics (SR, PR, NPR). The closest competitor is AiATrack (57.4 SR), which HDETrack edges out by 0.4 SR but leads by 2.5 PR and 0.7 NPR; against the base OSTrack architecture, the knowledge distillation adds +2.4 SR and +2.4 NPR. On FE240hz, HDETrack (59.8/92.2 SR/PR) beats STNet (58.5/89.6) and TransT (56.7/89.0), confirming gains on low-resolution event data as well. On VisEvent with event-only input, HDETrack leads on PR (54.6) and NPR (44.5) over STNet (49.2) and TransT (47.1), though STNet and TransT achieve slightly higher SR (39.7, 39.5 vs. 37.3). On COESOT, DiMP50 (53.8/64.8/65.1) marginally surpasses HDETrack (53.1/64.1/64.5) on all three metrics, while HDETrack clearly outperforms OSTrack (+2.2 SR), TrDiMP (+2.4 SR), and SimTrack (+4.8 SR). Overall, the hierarchical knowledge distillation consistently improves the base OSTrack backbone across all four datasets, with the largest absolute gains observed on the high-resolution EventVOT benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the one-stream Transformer tracking architecture of OSTrack (Ye et al., ECCV 2022) for both teacher and student networks, and on the unified multi-modal backbone design of CEUTrack (Tang et al., 2022) for the RGB-Event teacher. Its key departure is applying a hierarchical knowledge distillation strategy—simultaneously distilling similarity matrices, feature representations, and response maps—from a multi-modal (RGB+Event) or multi-view (event image + voxel) teacher to a unimodal event-only student, rather than requiring multi-modal input at inference. The paper also introduces EventVOT, the first high-resolution (1280×720) event-based tracking dataset, departing from all prior event tracking benchmarks (FE240hz, VisEvent, COESOT) that are limited to 346×260 resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EventVOT dataset addresses a critical gap: whether event representation methods designed for low-resolution streams remain effective at high resolution, a question that cannot be answered without high-res data. The KD framework offers a practical path to event-only tracking at test time (105 FPS) while leveraging richer multi-modal supervision during training, though the improvement over the OSTrack baseline is modest (~2.4 SR points). A notable open question is why event voxels collapse to 8.6 SR on high-resolution data versus 57.8 for event images, suggesting the voxel representation problem at high resolution remains fundamentally unsolved. The authors note future work on self-supervised pre-training and larger high-res collections.
