## Problem

Existing multi-view consistent diffusion models (e.g., SyncDreamer, Zero-1-to-3) can generate novel views from a single image but cannot be explicitly controlled by a 3D human model for expression or pose animation. Prior attempts at controllable avatar generation either rely on 2D rasterizations of meshes that fail to achieve true 3D consistency, or require multi-view/video input. The paper addresses how to integrate a 3D morphable model into a diffusion pipeline to enable both high-fidelity novel view synthesis and explicit animation from a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240318/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image of a human face or full body, along with a 3D morphable mesh (FLAME, SMPL, or bilinear topology) parameterized by identity β and expression/pose Θ. It produces N multi-view consistent novel view images from predefined camera viewpoints (azimuth constrained to [-90°, 90°] for faces), which can be re-generated under different morphable model parameters to animate the avatar. The generated image set can additionally be fed to NeuS2 for coarse 3D surface reconstruction.

## Method

The core contribution is a morphable conditioning mechanism that lifts 2D noisy image features into 3D space: pixel-aligned noise features from all target views are unprojected onto mesh vertices, fused via channel-wise mean pooling for view-order invariance, processed by a SparseConvNet to produce a 3DMM-aware feature volume, and then trilinearly interpolated into each target view's frustum. This 3D feature volume is injected into a pre-trained UNet (initialized from SyncDreamer/Zero-1-to-3) at every layer via depth-wise cross-attention, where the frustum features serve as keys and values. Training uses a shuffled cross-expression scheme: at each step, the model is conditioned on a single input image with one expression and asked to denoise a set of novel views with a *different* expression, disentangling identity reconstruction (from the image) from animation (from the morphable model) while acting as data augmentation. The pipeline is finetuned from models pretrained on Objaverse, with input-view parameter embeddings zeroed out to improve generalization.

## Results

On the FaceScape novel view synthesis task, Morphable Diffusion achieves LPIPS 0.1653, SSIM 0.8064, PCK@0.2 95.80%, and Re-ID 99.86%, outperforming the strongest baseline SyncDreamer (LPIPS 0.1854, SSIM 0.7732, PCK 94.07%, Re-ID 99.60%) on all metrics except FID (6.73 vs. 6.05). On full-body synthesis (THuman 2.0), the method achieves LPIPS 0.0625, SSIM 0.9181, and FID 30.25, substantially beating SyncDreamer (0.0960, 0.8826, 41.33). For novel facial expression synthesis, the method dominates all baselines with LPIPS 0.1693, SSIM 0.8026, FID 14.34, PCK 95.46%, and Re-ID 99.89%, compared to the best baseline DiffusionRig* (0.2534, 0.7438, 42.93, 89.74%, 97.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SyncDreamer [45], the closest competitor and the method this work builds upon, Morphable Diffusion wins on LPIPS (0.1653 vs. 0.1854), SSIM (0.8064 vs. 0.7732), PCK (95.80 vs. 94.07), and Re-ID (99.86 vs. 99.60), but trails slightly on FID (6.73 vs. 6.05), indicating marginally lower distributional realism. Zero-1-to-3 [44] performs poorly on structural metrics (PCK 4.69%) due to its assumption of uniform camera intrinsics, producing misaligned and distorted views. pixelNeRF [91] and SSD-NeRF [10] preserve identity (Re-ID >97%) but produce overly blurry results, reflected in their higher LPIPS scores (~0.22) and lower SSIM. For novel expression synthesis, DiffusionRig* [16] (which requires per-subject finetuning) is outperformed on every metric, with the largest gap in FID (14.34 vs. 42.93) and PCK (95.46 vs. 89.74). DECA [18] fails dramatically on photorealism (FID 182.25, Re-ID 25.23%) because it renders predicted albedo maps onto meshes rather than generating images. MoFaNeRF [97] loses resemblance and produces artifacts under non-neutral expressions, scoring lower on all five metrics compared to the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SyncDreamer's multi-view consistent diffusion framework by replacing its generic 3D-aware feature attention with explicit conditioning on a 3D morphable model (SMPL, FLAME, or bilinear), where noisy image features are unprojected onto mesh vertices and processed by a SparseConvNet before being interpolated into view frustums. It departs from prior diffusion-based avatar methods like DiffusionRig, which condition on 2D rasterizations of meshes and thus fail to achieve true multi-view consistency, by performing the conditioning entirely in 3D space. The work also builds on Zero-1-to-3's camera-controlled diffusion as a pretrained starting point, but removes its uniform-intrinsics assumption and adds the morphable model as an additional driving signal for expression and pose control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based framework to simultaneously achieve 3D-consistent novel view synthesis and facial expression animation from a single image of an unseen subject, bridging the gap between reconstruction-based avatar methods (which require video or multi-view input) and generative methods (which lack 3D consistency). Key limitations include the 256×256 resolution inherited from prior work, strong bias toward Asian subjects with red caps due to the FaceScape training set, and inability to generalize to out-of-distribution camera parameters, necessitating an external NeRF-based system for free-path rendering. Open questions include whether the morphable-model conditioning strategy transfers to higher resolutions, more diverse populations, and full-body animation with clothing.
