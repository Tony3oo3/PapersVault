## Problem

Existing object-level LVLMs exhibit a pronounced self-consistency gap: they cannot reliably generate a discriminative caption for a given bounding box and then use that same caption to re-locate the object, with performance dropping sharply on out-of-domain images. Conventional multi-task instruction tuning treats referring expression generation (REG) and comprehension (REC) as independent objectives, neglecting their inherent cyclic correlation and leaving the bidirectional alignment between visual grounding and linguistic description unsatisfactory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241637/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images resized to 448×448 paired with bounding boxes whose coordinates are discretized into integers (range [0,1000] for Qwen-VL, [0,100] for MiniGPT-v2) and rendered as text tokens. In the describer stage, the input is an image plus a ground-truth bbox and the output is a free-form referring expression; in the locator stage, the input is the image plus that generated expression and the output is a predicted bbox parsed from the model's text response. Training requires only ~166K images with bbox annotations from Object365—no text annotations are needed.

## Method

SC-Tune treats a single pre-trained LVLM as a dual-component system with two roles—describer and locator—that are iteratively fine-tuned in alternating cycles with parameter synchronization every 200 steps, inspired by the target-network mechanism in DQN. The describer is optimized via PPO reinforcement learning where the sentence-level reward is the IoU between the input bbox and the bbox reconstructed by the frozen locator from the generated caption, augmented by a token-level KL penalty against the initial model to prevent linguistic degradation; the advantage is computed using a REINFORCE-with-baseline formulation that avoids a separate value network. The locator is trained with standard MLE loss on pseudo-captions produced by the frozen describer, enabling gradient-based updates without RL. This cyclic bbox→caption→bbox pipeline forces the generated expressions to be simultaneously descriptive and discriminative, and the alternating freeze-and-sync schedule ensures both capabilities co-improve rather than one degrading the other.

## Results

SC-Tune substantially improves self-consistency (Pr@0.5) on out-of-domain data: Qwen-VL rises from 76.9% to 94.1% on Object365 and from 52.9% to 68.8% on OpenImages, while MiniGPT-v2 improves from 65.4% to 76.6% on Object365. On zero-shot REC, Qwen-VL+SC-Tune reaches 69.28% accuracy on ReferItGame and 77.49% on Flickr30K Entities, surpassing the fully supervised specialist CLIP-VG (70.89% / 81.99%) within a few points. On out-of-domain REG, Qwen-VL+SC-Tune gains over 20 CIDEr points on ReferItGame (43.2 vs 19.2 on testA) and 23.3 points on Flickr30K (80.9 vs 57.6 on test). Image-level benchmarks (Nocaps, Flickr30K, VQAv2, GQA) show maintained or slightly improved performance, confirming no degradation from the fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two baseline LVLMs (Qwen-VL and MiniGPT-v2), SC-Tune delivers large zero-shot REC gains: +7.8% on ReferItGame and +13.3% on Flickr30K for Qwen-VL, and +7.6% / +4.6% for MiniGPT-v2. In the zero-shot setting, Qwen-VL+SC-Tune (69.28% on ReferItGame) nearly matches the fully supervised specialist SeqTR (69.66%) and approaches CLIP-VG (70.89%), while remaining well below VLTVG (71.98%). On out-of-domain REG, Qwen-VL+SC-Tune (43.2 CIDEr on ReferIt testA) far exceeds all specialist baselines (SLR 10.9, EU 9.5, DisCLIP 8.8), and MiniGPT-v2+SC-Tune (93.5 CIDEr on Flickr30K test) surpasses the specialist PFOS (87.7 on RefCOCO TestA) in the in-domain setting. On in-domain REC (RefCOCO), SC-Tune causes a slight drop (e.g., Qwen-VL 88.88→88.04 on val), but adding in-domain supervised data (L_sup) recovers and exceeds the baseline (89.61). On image-level tasks, SC-Tune maintains or slightly improves performance (e.g., Flickr30K CIDEr 81.0→86.0 for Qwen-VL), confirming no collateral degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard multi-task instruction tuning paradigm used by object-level LVLMs (Qwen-VL, MiniGPT-v2, Shikra, Kosmos-2), which trains REG and REC independently and ignores their inherent cyclic consistency. It repurposes two ideas from adjacent fields: the PPO-based RLHF pipeline (Ouyang et al., Nakano et al.) for the describer's caption generation, and the DQN target-network concept (Mnih et al.) for the alternating freeze-and-synchronize training loop between the describer and locator roles. The key conceptual departure is framing referential comprehension as a self-consistency problem—where the model must generate a caption that its own localization head can use to recover the original bbox—rather than treating REG and REC as separate supervised objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that current LVLMs exhibit a pronounced self-consistency gap between in-domain (RefCOCO) and out-of-domain (Object365, OpenImages) images highlights a practical reliability concern for deployment in embodied agents, accessibility tools, and interactive systems. SC-Tune's data efficiency (166K images, no text annotations, ~15 hours on 8 A100s) and model-agnostic applicability make it a practical fine-tuning recipe that practitioners can adopt. Open questions include whether the slight in-domain REC degradation is acceptable in safety-critical settings, whether the approach extends to video or 3D grounding, and whether the PPO-based describer training can be replaced by more sample-efficient alternatives as LVLMs scale.
