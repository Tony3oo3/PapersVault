## Problem

Existing zero-shot skeleton-based action recognition methods align only globally averaged visual features with label-level semantics, failing to capture locally consistent movements (e.g., similar hand or arm motions) that are shared across globally dissimilar actions. This limits the transfer of fine-grained visual knowledge from seen to unseen classes, as the model cannot exploit part-level or temporal-phase similarities that humans naturally leverage to recognize new actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D skeleton sequences of shape L×J×M×3 (e.g., 300 frames × 25 joints × 2 performers × 3 coordinates for NTU-RGB+D) and action label names as input. GPT-3 generates enriched textual descriptions at three granularities—global, four body-part-based (head, hands, torso, legs), and three temporal-interval-based (start, middle, end)—which are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. At test time, only the global visual representation is projected and matched against unseen-class label embeddings to predict the action class, while local representations are used exclusively during training.

## Method

PURLS introduces a prompting module that queries GPT-3 to produce refined descriptions of global, spatially local (per body part), and temporally local (per interval) movements from each action label, and a cross-attention-based adaptive partitioning module that learns to weight all joint-level features from a pre-trained Shift-GCN backbone to form visual representations semantically aligned with each description. The key mechanism is a cross-attention layer where text embeddings serve as queries and skeleton features as keys, producing an attention matrix that adaptively samples description-relevant nodes rather than relying on fixed spatial or temporal partitions. Training uses a weighted sum of contrastive losses (InfoNCE-style) between projected visual representations and their corresponding text embeddings across all P+Z+1 scales, with learnable or uniform aggregation weights. At inference, the projection layer—having distilled part-aware knowledge during multi-scale training—maps only the global visual feature to label semantics, enabling efficient single-pass prediction over unseen classes.

## Results

PURLS achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot action recognition accuracy across all three datasets and all split configurations. On NTU-RGB+D 60, PURLS reaches 79.23% (55/5 split) and 23.52% (30/30 split), outperforming the strongest prior method SMIE by 1.25 and 5.07 points respectively. On NTU-RGB+D 120, PURLS scores 71.95% (110/10) and 19.63% (60/60), surpassing SMIE by 6.21 and 7.44 points. On the newly introduced Kinetics-skeleton 200, PURLS achieves 32.22% (180/20) and 11.75% (120/80), exceeding the best baseline ReViSE by 7.27 and 5.52 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PURLS outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated split and dataset. Against the strongest prior skeleton-based ZSL method SMIE [48], PURLS gains +1.25% on NTU-60 55/5 (79.23 vs 77.98) and +6.21% on NTU-120 110/10 (71.95 vs 65.74), with the gap widening to +7.44% on the hardest NTU-120 60/60 split (19.63 vs 12.19). Compared to the generative approach SynSE [15], PURLS leads by +3.42% on NTU-60 48/12 (40.99 vs 33.30) and +8.37% on NTU-120 96/24 (52.01 vs 38.70). The common-space embedding ReViSE [36] and visual-to-language DeViSE [14] are the strongest baselines on Kinetics-skeleton 200, where PURLS exceeds them by +7.27% (32.22 vs 24.95) and +10.00% (32.22 vs 22.22) respectively on the 180/20 split. The margin over all baselines grows consistently as the number of unseen classes increases, indicating that PURLS's part-aware local alignment provides substantially better knowledge transfer under data-scarce conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PURLS extends the skeleton-based zero-shot action recognition line (JPoSE, SynSE, SMIE) by shifting from global-only or verb-level semantic alignment to part-aware local alignment across both spatial (body-part) and temporal (interval) dimensions. It repurposes CLIP's contrastive alignment framework and GPT-3's description generation—previously applied to supervised skeleton learning [41] or RGB-based ZSL—into a unified cross-attention partitioning mechanism that adaptively weights joint features per description. The adaptive partitioning module departs from the static, manually-defined body-part groupings used in prior skeleton ZSL work, replacing them with learned cross-attention weights that can capture combinative context across body parts and time intervals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PURLS demonstrates that local visual-semantic alignment is a critical missing component for skeleton ZSL generalization, with consistent gains of 5–15% over global-only baselines across all splits and datasets. The backbone-agnostic design (validated on four different GCN architectures) makes the approach broadly applicable, and the introduction of Kinetics-skeleton 200 as a ZSL benchmark addresses a gap in evaluation diversity. Open questions include whether the GPT-3 description step is truly necessary (vs. simpler templates), how the method scales beyond 200 unseen classes, and whether the adaptive partitioning can be distilled into a lighter-weight inference-time mechanism.
