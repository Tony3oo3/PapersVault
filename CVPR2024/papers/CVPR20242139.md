## Problem

Existing domain adaptation methods rely on either CNN or ViT as the feature encoder, failing to exploit the complementary strengths of both architectures. CNN excels at local spatial representations but lacks global context, while ViT captures global relationships but can overfit on limited data. Prior multi-model DA approaches (e.g., DECOTA, FixBi) still use CNN as the encoder, leaving ViT's ability to identify general class-specific boundaries unexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a labeled source domain, a small set of labeled target samples (1-shot or 3-shot in SSDA; none in UDA), and a large unlabeled target set. Both branches are pre-trained on ImageNet-1K, and unlabeled target samples undergo weak augmentation (random flip/crop) and strong augmentation (RandAugment with 14 transformations) to generate two views for co-training. At test time, only the CNN branch (ResNet-50 for UDA, ResNet-34 for SSDA) is used for inference, producing a single class label with no additional computational overhead over standard CNN-based DA methods.

## Method

ECB employs a hybrid architecture with a ViT/B-16 encoder (E₁) and a ResNet encoder (E₂), each paired with a shared pair of MLP classifiers (F₁, F₂). Training proceeds in three stages: (1) supervised cross-entropy training of both branches on labeled data; (2) a Finding-to-Conquering (FTC) strategy where the ViT encoder is frozen and both classifiers maximize their output discrepancy (mean absolute probability difference) to explicitly expand class-specific decision boundaries and estimate worst-case hyperspaces, after which the CNN encoder minimizes the same discrepancy to cluster target features within those ViT-defined boundaries; (3) bidirectional co-training where each branch generates confidence-thresholded pseudo labels on weakly augmented samples to supervise the other branch on strongly augmented samples, reducing residual knowledge gaps. The key insight is that ViT's global self-attention identifies broader, more general class boundaries, while CNN's local inductive bias refines feature clustering within those boundaries, and the max-min discrepancy mechanism explicitly bridges the two roles.

## Results

On Office-Home under the UDA setting (ResNet-50), ECB(CNN) achieves a mean accuracy of 81.2%, surpassing the nearest competitor EIDCo (75.8%) by +5.4% and the second-best ATDOC (73.2%) by +8.0%. On DomainNet under the SSDA setting (ResNet-34), ECB(CNN) reaches 83.8% mean accuracy in the 1-shot setting and 85.5% in the 3-shot setting, outperforming the best prior method G-ABC (77.2% / 78.4%) by +6.6% and +7.1% respectively. The largest per-task gains are +8.1% on C→R (Office-Home) and +9.3% on skt→pnt 3-shot (DomainNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECB(CNN) wins against all 23 baselines on both benchmarks. On Office-Home (UDA), it outperforms the strongest prior method EIDCo [38] by +5.4% in mean accuracy (81.2% vs. 75.8%), with the largest per-task margins on C→A (+7.7%), C→R (+8.1%), and P→A (+7.2%) over EIDCo. On DomainNet (SSDA), ECB(CNN) beats the best competitor G-ABC [17] by +6.6% in 1-shot (83.8% vs. 77.2%) and +7.1% in 3-shot (85.5% vs. 78.4%), with a peak gain of +9.3% on skt→pnt 3-shot. Earlier UDA baselines such as DANN [8] (57.6%), MCD [30] (64.1%), and MDD [37] (68.1%) trail by 13–24 points in mean accuracy on Office-Home. Among SSDA baselines, DECOTA [34] (73.9%/75.6%) and MVCL [23] (76.2%/78.1%) are the closest prior methods on DomainNet but still fall 7–10 points short of ECB. The paper notes that ECB uses only the CNN branch at test time, so no additional inference cost is incurred relative to these baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Maximum Classifier Discrepancy (MCD) framework and the dual-branch co-training paradigm of DECOTA by replacing one CNN encoder with a ViT encoder, creating the first hybrid ViT+CNN architecture for domain adaptation. Prior DA methods (MME, MDD, UODA, FixBi, MVCL, DECOTA) uniformly use CNN encoders with MLP classifiers, leaving ViT's global representation capability unexploited in DA. The key conceptual departure is the asymmetric 'Finding to Conquering' strategy: ViT maximizes classifier discrepancy to define broad class-specific boundaries (exploiting its global receptive field), while CNN minimizes discrepancy to cluster target features within those boundaries (exploiting its local inductive bias).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the ViT+CNN hybrid substantially outperforms both CNN+CNN and ViT+ViT variants, suggesting that local and global representations are complementary rather than redundant for DA. The co-training mechanism significantly improves pseudo-label quality (from ~8,000 to ~18,000 labels), directly addressing the data bias problem that plagues single-encoder DA methods. A notable limitation is the reliance on fixed confidence thresholds (τ_vit=0.6, τ_cnn=0.9) that the authors acknowledge are time-consuming to tune, leaving dynamic thresholding as an open problem. The method's practical appeal is strengthened by the fact that inference uses only the lightweight CNN branch, incurring no additional testing cost over prior methods.
