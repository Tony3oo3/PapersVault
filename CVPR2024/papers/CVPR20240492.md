## Problem

Neural fields for dynamic scenes are constrained by the absence of large-scale, real-world, multi-view datasets: existing collections are short (typically <15 s), captured with monocular or forward-facing cameras, or restricted to specific categories like humans. This limits the ability to develop and evaluate methods for long-duration, 360° immersive reconstruction of table-scale dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240492/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized 1280×720, 120 FPS video from 53 RGB cameras arranged around a 1 m³ capture volume, along with COLMAP+I-NGP-refined camera poses, multi-view-consistent foreground-background segmentation masks, synchronized audio, and text descriptions. For benchmarking, sequences are downsampled to 30 FPS, split into 5-second chunks, undistorted and cropped to 1160×550, with 35 cameras used for training and 6 held out for testing. The output is novel-view renderings evaluated via PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and JOD metrics.

## Method

The paper introduces BRICS, a low-cost capture system with 53 synchronized RGB cameras in a 3×3 grid on each of six sides of a 1 m³ aluminum frame, using network-based synchronization (2–3 ms accuracy) and ArUco-marker calibration refined with I-NGP dense photometric loss. DiVa-360 contains 46 dynamic sequences (21 object-centric across five motion types, 25 hand-object interaction, 8 long-duration ≥120 s) totaling 17.4 M frames over 2738 s. Foreground masks are generated by fitting per-frame I-NGP models with progressively reduced bounding boxes, yielding multi-view-consistent segmentation. The benchmarking of PF I-NGP, MixVoxels, and K-Planes reveals that per-frame reconstruction outperforms dynamic methods in quality, and that dynamic methods are biased toward capturing motion at the expense of high-frequency spatial detail, with K-Planes benefiting from more temporal context while MixVoxels prefers less.

## Results

On DiVa-360, PF I-NGP (per-frame, no temporal modeling) achieves the best reconstruction quality with PSNR 28.31, SSIM 0.94, and LPIPS 0.08, outperforming both dynamic methods MixVoxels (PSNR 27.68, LPIPS 0.09) and K-Planes (PSNR 26.39, LPIPS 0.19). PF I-NGP also trains faster (48.7 s/frame) than MixVoxels (57.6 s/frame) and renders faster (0.94 s/frame) than both MixVoxels (1.48 s/frame) and K-Planes (3.03 s/frame). However, PF I-NGP requires 29 MB storage per time step versus 2.7–4.7 MB for MixVoxels and 2 MB for K-Planes, and suffers from temporal inconsistency in static regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PF I-NGP, which trains independently on each frame without exploiting temporal information, outperforms both state-of-the-art dynamic methods on all quality metrics: it beats MixVoxels by 0.63 dB PSNR and K-Planes by 1.92 dB PSNR, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.08 vs. 0.09 and 0.19). MixVoxels is slower to train (57.6 vs. 48.7 s/frame) and renders slower than PF I-NGP, and it struggles with complex dynamic content such as hands, producing blurry and noisy reconstructions. K-Planes has comparable training speed to PF I-NGP but renders 3.2× slower (3.03 vs. 0.94 s/frame) and suffers from background floaters due to overfitting from incorrect space-time decomposition. In the segmentation comparison (Table 7), I-NGP-based segmentation achieves mIoU 0.926 with lower cross-view variance (std 0.048) compared to SAM (mIoU 0.919, std 0.086), confirming multi-view consistency advantages. The temporal sensitivity analysis reveals that K-Planes benefits from more temporal context (fewer chunks), while MixVoxels performs best with less temporal information, suggesting the two methods have fundamentally different capacity trade-offs for dynamic content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of multi-camera capture systems and dynamic neural field research. Prior dynamic datasets (DyNeRF, NDSD, ILFV, NeRF-DS, Deep3DMV) are limited to short durations (~15s), forward-facing or monocular cameras, or lack 360° coverage, while room-scale systems (Panoptic Studio, Eyeful Tower) target human capture rather than table-scale object interactions. DiVa-360 carves new ground by combining a purpose-built 53-camera 360° capture rig (BRICS) with long-duration (up to 3 min) table-scale sequences and multi-view-consistent segmentation masks, filling a gap that no prior dataset addresses. The benchmarking component repurposes existing methods (PF I-NGP, MixVoxels, K-Planes) under a standardized protocol rather than proposing a new reconstruction algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset is likely to become a standard benchmark for long-duration dynamic neural fields, as it is the largest (17.4M frames) real-world 360° multi-view dynamic dataset with segmentation masks, directly enabling research that prior short-duration datasets could not support. The finding that per-frame I-NGP outperforms purpose-built dynamic methods in both quality and speed raises a fundamental open question about whether explicit temporal modeling is currently beneficial or whether capacity allocation between static and dynamic components is the real bottleneck. Limitations include the table-scale constraint, limited baseline coverage (only 3 methods due to training cost), and the absence of audio/text evaluation metrics, all of which the authors flag as future work.
