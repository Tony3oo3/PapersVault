## Problem

Existing post-hoc OOD detection methods rely on signals such as softmax probabilities, feature norms, and gradient norms that were originally designed for in-distribution categorization, making them suboptimal for the fundamentally different task of distinguishing ID from OOD samples. This paper identifies that convolutional kernel responses are inherently more pronounced for ID inputs yet have not been explored as an ID-free OOD detection signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR benchmarks, 224×224 for ImageNet) and a pre-trained CNN classifier as input, requiring no access to training data. It produces a scalar OOD detection score computed from convolutional responses across 4–5 selected layers, which is thresholded to yield a binary ID/OOD decision. The thresholds τ+ and τ− are calibrated on Gaussian and uniform noise to minimize false positives.

## Method

CORES exploits the observation that convolutional kernels produce stronger positive peaks and deeper negative troughs for ID samples than for OOD ones. For each kernel, it extracts extreme values and computes four metrics: response magnitude (RM+, RM−) capturing how far extremes exceed thresholds, and response frequency (RF+, RF−) capturing how many kernels exhibit significant extremes. To focus on informative kernels, CORES backtracks from the highest and lowest confidence predictions (c_max, c_min) through the network's weight matrices, selecting the top/bottom 20% of kernels at each layer that most strongly influence those extreme outputs. The final score is the product of the four metrics raised to weighting exponents (λ1=10, λ2=1), computed per layer and averaged across 4–5 convolutional layers, yielding a single scalar that is higher for ID and lower for OOD samples.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (small-scale common OOD), CORES achieves average FPR95 of 5.09% (ResNet-18), 6.03% (DenseNet-101), and 3.25% (WRN-28), with corresponding AUROC values of 98.91%, 98.63%, and 99.29%. On CIFAR-100, CORES reduces average FPR95 to 25.40% (ResNet-18), 33.61% (DenseNet-101), and 46.26% (WRN-28). On the large-scale ImageNet-1k setting, CORES attains average FPR95 of 31.89% (VGG-16) and 45.69% (SqueezeNet), and on the challenging near-OOD ImageNet-O task, achieves FPR95 of 72.20% (VGG-16) and 77.70% (SqueezeNet) with AUROC of 79.10% and 78.10% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORES outperforms all seven baselines across every model and dataset configuration. On CIFAR-10, the closest competitor is FeatureNorm (CVPR 2023), which CORES surpasses by 19.97% FPR95 on ResNet-18 (5.09% vs 25.06%), 10.01% on DenseNet-101 (6.03% vs 16.04%), and 5.37% on WRN-28 (3.25% vs 8.62%). On the harder CIFAR-100 setting, CORES beats the next-best method (TFEM on ResNet-18 at 53.39%, DICE on DenseNet-101 at 52.28%, TFEM on WRN-28 at 71.46%) by 27.99%, 18.67%, and 25.20% FPR95 respectively. On the large-scale ImageNet-1k setting, CORES leads FeatureNorm by 5.74% FPR95 on VGG-16 (31.89% vs 37.63%) and 2.74% on SqueezeNet (45.69% vs 48.43%). In the near-OOD ImageNet-O scenario where MSP collapses to >95% FPR95, CORES still achieves 72.20% (VGG-16) and 77.70% (SqueezeNet), outperforming FeatureNorm (the best baseline) by 8.00% and 3.70% FPR95 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the post-hoc, ID-free OOD detection literature by repurposing convolutional kernel responses—a signal previously studied only for interpretability and pruning—as the basis for an OOD score. It explicitly departs from prior methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy, ReAct, DICE, FeatureNorm, GradientNorm) that rely on signals 'primarily designed for ID categorization,' arguing these are ill-suited for the fundamentally different task of ID-vs-OOD discrimination. The kernel-selection mechanism, which traces backward from extreme predictions through inter-layer weight matrices, is a novel procedural contribution not present in any prior OOD detection method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORES offers a practical, training-free, and ID-data-free OOD detection tool that plugs into any pre-trained CNN without architectural modification, addressing a key deployment constraint. The substantial FPR95 reductions over the closest competitor (FeatureNorm) across five architectures and four ID/OOD settings—including challenging near-OOD scenarios—suggest the convolutional response signal is a robust and underexploited indicator. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-convolutional architectures (e.g., ViTs), whether the threshold calibration step (τ+, τ−) introduces dataset-specific fragility, and how the method behaves under distribution shifts that preserve low-level texture statistics. The authors note future application to adversarial attack detection as a natural extension.
