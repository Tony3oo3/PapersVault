## Problem

Procedure planning in instructional videos requires an agent to produce a logical sequence of action steps from an initial visual state to a goal state, but existing methods overlook the implicit causal constraints in step sequencing, the varied transition probabilities between steps, and the existence of multiple viable plans. Prior approaches compensate by relying on costly annotations (intermediate visual states, task labels, or natural language instructions), leaving the structured procedural knowledge underlying step ordering unexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241506/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two short video clips (initial state v_s and goal state v_g) extracted from an instructional video, along with a planning horizon T (typically 3–6 action steps), and outputs a sequence of T action step labels. Supervision requires only step class annotations from the training procedure plans—no intermediate visual states, task labels, or step text are needed. At inference, the model receives only the two visual clips and T, with no access to ground-truth steps or task identity.

## Method

KEPP decomposes procedure planning into two sub-problems: a Step Model (Conditioned Projected Diffusion Model) predicts the first and last action steps from the visual states, and a Planning Model (also a Conditioned Projected Diffusion Model) generates the full action sequence conditioned on those endpoints plus a plan recommendation retrieved from a Probabilistic Procedural Knowledge Graph (P²KG). The P²KG is a directed weighted graph built from training procedure plans, where nodes are action steps and edge weights are normalized transition frequencies, encoding step-ordering constraints and transition likelihoods. At inference, the graph is queried with the predicted first and last steps to find the top-R most probable paths (no longer than T), which are aggregated into a single recommendation and fed as an additional conditioning row into the Planning Model's diffusion array. This design requires only minimal supervision (step class labels) while injecting rich, structured procedural knowledge that acts as a domain 'textbook' for the planning model.

## Results

On CrossTask (conventional setting), KEPP with P²KG(R=1) achieves 33.34% SR and 61.36% mAcc at T=3, and 20.38% SR and 55.54% mAcc at T=4, outperforming the previous best (SkipPlan: 28.85% SR at T=3) by over 4.5 points in success rate. Under PDPP's task setting, KEPP reaches 38.12% SR at T=3 and 24.15% SR at T=4, surpassing PDPP† by 0.9% and 2.7% respectively. On NIV, KEPP achieves the best mIoU (86.67 at T=3; 91.49 at T=4) and best SR at T=4 (22.71%). On COIN, KEPP does not rank first at T=3 or T=4 but achieves the best SR at T=5 (16.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KEPP outperforms all baselines on CrossTask in the conventional setting: it beats SkipPlan (28.85% SR) by 4.5 points and E3P (26.40% SR) by 7.0 points at T=3, and beats PDPP (18.69% SR) by 1.7 points at T=4. Under PDPP's task setting, KEPP (38.12% SR at T=3, 24.15% at T=4) surpasses PDPP† (37.20%, 21.48%) by 0.9% and 2.7% respectively, while requiring only step-class annotations versus PDPP's task-class labels. On NIV, KEPP achieves the best mIoU at both T=3 (86.67 vs. PDPP's 86.66) and T=4 (91.49 vs. E3P's 74.90), and the best SR at T=4 (22.71% vs. E3P's 21.37%). On COIN, KEPP does not rank first at T=3 (20.25% SR vs. SkipPlan's 23.65%) or T=4 (15.63% vs. SkipPlan's 16.04%), but achieves the best SR at T=5 (16.06% vs. PDPP's 13.02%), suggesting the knowledge graph provides greater benefit for longer-horizon planning. A key differentiator is annotation efficiency: KEPP requires only step-class labels, whereas P³IV needs step text, E3P needs both step text and task class, and PDPP needs task class.

## Relation to Prior Work

KEPP builds directly on PDPP's Conditioned Projected Diffusion Model as its generative backbone for both step prediction and plan generation, and adopts SkipPlan's decomposition strategy of predicting first/last steps before filling in intermediates. The key departure from the entire prior line of work (DDN, PlaTe, Ext-GAIL, P³IV, PDPP, E3P, SkipPlan) is the introduction of a Probabilistic Procedural Knowledge Graph (P²KG) extracted from training procedure plans, which replaces the need for expensive intermediate visual state annotations, natural-language step descriptions, or task-level labels that all prior methods require. The P²KG concept draws on procedural knowledge graphs from video understanding [5, 62] but repurposes them as a retrieval-based 'textbook' that conditions the diffusion model at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the dramatic reduction in annotation requirements: KEPP needs only step class labels, whereas competing SOTA methods require intermediate visual observations, step text, or task labels. This makes the approach more scalable to new domains where such annotations are costly or unavailable. However, the P²KG is inherently constrained to the training domain and cannot generalize to unseen procedural activities, a limitation the authors acknowledge by comparing against LLM-generated plans. Open questions include improving the step model's accuracy (a clear bottleneck per Table 9), extending the knowledge graph to handle out-of-domain tasks, and adapting the framework for error detection in instructional videos.
