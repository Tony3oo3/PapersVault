## Problem

Existing medical visual representation learning methods overlook the multi-granularity nature of radiograph-report pairs and rely on standard contrastive learning that treats semantically similar samples from different batches as negatives (false negatives). Additionally, because pathological features occupy only a small fraction of a chest X-ray, much of the image-text information goes underutilized, limiting the generalizability of learned representations across downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241063/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes frontal-view chest radiographs paired with free-text radiology reports (impression and finding sections extracted from MIMIC-CXR, yielding ~217,000 pairs) along with a UMLS-derived knowledge graph of medical entity-relation triples. The image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) and text encoder produce global and local (patch-level and token-level) feature vectors, which are the transferable visual representations used as frozen backbones for downstream detection, segmentation, and classification heads.

## Method

MLIP employs a three-level contrastive learning scheme guided by UMLS domain knowledge. At the global level, a divergence encoder—whose parameters are manually updated via cosine-similarity-weighted interpolation rather than backpropagation—generates augmented features to increase sample diversity and mitigate false negatives. At the local level, cross-modal attention aligns image patches and text tokens with TransE+GAT-derived knowledge embeddings, using weighted InfoNCE losses to pull relevant patch-knowledge pairs together. At the category level, tucker fusion integrates visual, textual, and knowledge features, which are then clustered via iterative Sinkhorn-Knopp with trainable cross-modal prototypes to perform prototype-based contrastive learning that groups semantically similar samples across batches. Two proxy tasks (image-text matching with hinge loss and probabilistic text swapping) further enforce correct cross-modal alignment.

## Results

On medical object detection (RSNA), MLIP achieves 12.3 mAP zero-shot and 17.2 mAP with 1% data, surpassing the prior best (PRIOR: 10.7 and 15.6 mAP). On semantic segmentation (RSNA), MLIP reaches 44.3 Dice zero-shot and 67.7 Dice at 1%, outperforming PRIOR (41.8 and 66.4). On image classification (CheXpert, ViT-B/16), MLIP attains 57.0 AUC zero-shot and 90.0 AUC at 100% data, edging out MGCA (50.0 and 89.7) and PRIOR (56.3 and 88.8). Across all three downstream tasks, MLIP consistently achieves the best or near-best results even with only 1% of annotated training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLIP outperforms all ten baselines across the three downstream tasks. The closest competitor is PRIOR [16], which MLIP surpasses by 1.6 mAP (zero-shot, RSNA detection), 2.5 Dice (zero-shot, RSNA segmentation), and 0.7 AUC (zero-shot, CheXpert classification with ViT-B/16). Against MGCA [56], the gains are more pronounced in zero-shot settings: +7.0 AUC on CheXpert and +13.8 AUC on RSNA (ViT-B/16), while at 100% data the gap narrows to ~0.3 AUC. M-FLAG [40] and ConVIRT [64] trail by 1–4 points depending on the task and data regime. Notably, with only 1% of training data, MLIP's advantage over PRIOR widens (e.g., 17.2 vs. 15.6 mAP on RSNA detection; 67.7 vs. 66.4 Dice on RSNA segmentation), confirming the data-efficiency claim. On the Object CXR detection benchmark, MLIP achieves 17.4 mAP at 10% data versus 15.2 for PRIOR and 12.1 for MGCA, the largest margin observed across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MLIP extends the multi-granularity medical image-text contrastive learning paradigm established by MGCA [56] and the knowledge-guided alignment concept from ARL [15], but departs from both in key ways. Rather than relying solely on image-text similarity for local alignment, MLIP inserts UMLS knowledge graph embeddings as an intermediate mediator between visual patches and text tokens, and introduces a divergence encoder whose parameters are manually updated (not backpropagated) based on cosine similarity to the main encoder. At the category level, it replaces standard batch-level InfoNCE negatives with Sinkhorn-Knopp prototype clustering guided by knowledge-fused features, explicitly targeting the false-negative problem where semantically similar samples from different batches are incorrectly treated as negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The false-negative problem in medical contrastive learning—where different symptoms of the same disease cause semantically close samples to be pushed apart—is a genuine and under-addressed limitation of prior medical VLP methods, and MLIP's knowledge-guided clustering offers a principled mitigation without requiring object detectors or scene graph parsers. However, the approach is tightly coupled to chest radiographs and the UMLS knowledge graph, raising open questions about generalizability to other modalities (CT, MRI) and to settings where curated medical ontologies are less mature. The divergence encoder's manual parameter-update strategy, while effective, lacks theoretical grounding and its interaction with standard gradient-based training remains underexplored.
