## Problem

Manga is largely inaccessible to visually impaired readers because its narrative relies on visual layout, character appearance, and speech balloon placement. No prior work provides a unified, fully automatic pipeline that detects all page components, clusters characters by identity without knowing the number of identities a priori, associates dialogue to speakers, and generates a correctly ordered transcript in a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240571/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests a single high-resolution manga page (resized to 800px on the shorter side, capped at 1333px on the longer) and produces a structured graph: bounding boxes for panels, text blocks, and characters as nodes, plus character-character identity edges and text-character speaker edges. From this graph, a dialogue transcript is generated in manga reading order (top-to-bottom, right-to-left), with low-confidence speaker predictions replaced by a placeholder token. Unlike prior re-identification methods that operate on character crops, Magi processes the entire page in context, preserving surrounding visual cues for grouping.

## Method

Magi formulates detection and association as a graph generation problem within a DETR-style architecture: a ResNet-50 backbone feeds a 6-layer transformer encoder-decoder that processes 300 [OBJ] query tokens plus two special tokens ([C2C], [T2C]). The [OBJ] tokens yield bounding boxes and class labels via standard detection heads, while pairwise combinations of [OBJ] tokens are scored by dedicated MLP matching modules (conditioned on [C2C] or [T2C] and a MAE-initialized ViT crop embedding) to produce character identity clusters and speaker associations. Training is two-stage—pretraining on 1.5M pseudo-annotated pages from Mangadex, then supervised fine-tuning on 55K human-annotated PopManga pages—using detection loss, binary cross-entropy for matching heads, and supervised contrastive loss on crop embeddings. For transcript generation, panels are ordered via topological sorting of a directed acyclic graph (robust to overlapping panels unlike recursive-cutting methods), and text within each panel is sorted by distance from the top-right corner before OCR.

## Results

On character clustering (the paper's core contribution), Magi achieves 0.655 AMI and 0.851 NMI on PopManga Test-S, outperforming the best baseline AMFR (0.155 AMI, 0.536 NMI) by a large margin. For detection, Magi reaches 0.923 AP on text blocks and 0.936 AP on panels, substantially exceeding Grounding-DINO's zero-shot results (0.792 and 0.513 AP respectively). For speaker association, Magi achieves 0.845 Recall@#text on PopManga Test-S versus 0.776 for the nearest-neighbor baseline. On Manga109 body clustering, Magi scores 0.635 AMI compared to 0.262 for AMFR, confirming generalisation across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Magi decisively outperforms all six baselines on character clustering across every metric and test set. The largest gap is on AMI (the clustering-specific metric): Magi scores 0.655 vs. AMFR's 0.155 on PopManga Test-S, a 4.2× improvement, and 0.635 vs. 0.262 on Manga109 body. General-purpose vision encoders (CLIP, DINOv2) and the zero-shot anime model ZACI all score below 0.25 AMI, confirming that generic features are poorly suited to manga character identity. On detection, Magi matches or slightly exceeds DASS on characters (0.849 vs. 0.841 AP on Test-S) but substantially surpasses Grounding-DINO on text (0.923 vs. 0.792 AP) and panels (0.936 vs. 0.513 AP). For speaker association, Magi improves over the nearest-neighbor heuristic by ~7 points (0.845 vs. 0.776 Recall@#text on Test-S). The crop-only ablation (0.434 AMI) vs. full Magi (0.655 AMI) validates the paper's key design choice that page-level context is essential for forming hard cluster boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented manga-understanding pipeline (panel detection, text detection, character detection, character re-identification, speaker association) into a single graph-generation model built on Conditional-DETR, departing from prior crop-based metric-learning approaches (AMFR, ZACI, DASS) that operate on isolated character crops. The in-context formulation, where the network ingests the full page and simultaneously produces detections and association edges, is inspired by RelationFormer's image-to-graph paradigm but adapted to the manga domain. For transcript generation, the paper replaces recursive 'cut'-based panel ordering with a DAG + topological sort approach, and leverages a finetuned TrOCR for OCR, building on the Manga109 dataset's annotation schema while extending it to English manga.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PopManga benchmark (57K+ pages, 80+ series, English) fills a critical gap: prior manga datasets like Manga109 are Japanese-only, making English-manga diarisation previously unevaluable. The significant AMI gap (0.65 vs ≤0.28 for crop-based baselines) validates that full-page context is essential for character clustering in manga, a finding that could inform other in-context recognition tasks. However, the paper explicitly scopes itself to diarisation only, leaving scene captioning, action recognition, and conversation-context-aware speaker disambiguation as open problems; the authors suggest LLM integration as a natural next step. The accessibility motivation (serving visually impaired readers) gives the work broader societal relevance beyond the CV community.
