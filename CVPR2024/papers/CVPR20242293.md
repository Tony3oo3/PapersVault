## Problem

3D GANs using neural volume rendering cannot scale to the native resolution of 2D training images because dense sampling at 512×512 requires evaluating tens of millions of depth samples per image, making full-resolution rendering infeasible in memory and compute. Existing workforces a trade-off: patch-based training limits receptive fields, while low-resolution rendering with 2D super-resolution sacrifices multiview consistency and the fidelity of the resolved 3D geometry, leaving high-frequency geometric detail unresolved.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a 512-dimensional latent noise vector and a camera pose (following EG3D's conditioning) and produces a full-resolution 512×512 RGB image rendered entirely via neural volume rendering—no post-processing super-resolution. The 3D representation is a triplane (3×32×512×512) mapped through an MLP to an SDF value, a spatially-varying surface-tightness parameter β, and geometry features. From the SDF, the method extracts explicit surface geometry and renders normal maps; the entire pipeline operates with only ~30 depth samples per ray (12 from a coarse probe plus 18 adaptively sampled), compared to 96–192 in prior methods.

## Method

The method combines three key components to enable full-resolution rendering with few samples. First, an SDF-based NeRF parametrization (building on VolSDF) uses a spatially-varying Laplacian variance β that is regularized to tighten the surface throughout training, concentrating density near the true surface and reducing the number of samples needed for accurate integration. Second, a lightweight CNN proposal network takes a cheap low-resolution probe (192 uniform samples at 128×128, equivalent to only 12 samples at 512×512) and predicts high-resolution volume-rendering weight distributions at 512×512, supervised by cross-entropy against ground-truth weights from a small 64×64 patch. Third, a robust stratified sampling strategy applies nucleus sampling (selecting the smallest set of bins whose cumulative probability exceeds τ=0.98) to the predicted PDFs, then performs stratified inverse-CDF sampling within those bins, making the renderer resilient to small prediction errors in the high-frequency integrand. Training proceeds in stages: a low-resolution GAN is first converged, then the sampler is introduced, and the rendering resolution is progressively increased to 512×512 while all losses (adversarial, sampler, surface-tightness, and SDF decision-boundary) are jointly optimized.

## Results

On FFHQ-512, the method achieves FID 4.97 and FID-N 60.76 without super resolution, outperforming all other neural-rendering-only baselines (Mimic3D: FID 5.37, FID-N 64.97; Epigraf: FID 9.92, FID-N 67.33) and matching the SR-based EG3D (FID 4.70, FID-N 63.02) while using only 30 samples per ray versus 96+. On AFHQv2 Cats-512, the method achieves FID 4.23 and NFS 21.89 without SR, beating Mimic3D (FID 4.29, NFS 12.67) and substantially outperforming EG3D on NFS (21.89 vs. 14.14). The method also achieves the highest NFS among SR-free methods on FFHQ (29.35), indicating more pronounced 3D geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EG3D (the strongest SR-based baseline), the method achieves a slightly higher FID (4.97 vs. 4.70 on FFHQ) but a notably better FID-N (60.76 vs. 63.02), indicating superior geometry quality without relying on 2D super resolution to repair undersampling artifacts. Against Mimic3D, the method wins on both FID (4.97 vs. 5.37) and FID-N (60.76 vs. 64.97) on FFHQ, and on FID (4.23 vs. 4.29) and NFS (21.89 vs. 12.67) on AFHQ, while also avoiding the patch-based geometry distortions visible in Mimic3D outputs. Against Epigraf, the method achieves substantially better FID (4.97 vs. 9.92) and FID-N (60.76 vs. 67.33), though Epigraf reports a higher NFS (33.95 vs. 29.35). Against StyleSDF, the method dominates on FID (4.97 vs. 11.19) and FID-N (60.76 vs. 87.42), with the gap attributable to StyleSDF's lack of spatially-varying surface tightness and lower-resolution rendering. On AFHQ, the method also outperforms GRAM-HD (FID 4.23 vs. 7.67) and GMPI (FID 4.23 vs. 7.79) without SR, and beats EG3D on NFS (21.89 vs. 14.14) despite EG3D's lower FID (2.77), confirming that the geometry is more three-dimensional even when image quality is slightly lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D GAN lineage (EG3D, StyleSDF, Mimic3D, Epigraf) by addressing the core bottleneck that has prevented these methods from rendering at native 2D resolution: the prohibitive cost of dense volume sampling. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing per-scene or category-specific acceleration structures with a generalizable learned proposal network (a lightweight CNN) that predicts high-resolution sampling distributions from cheap low-resolution probes, and (2) combining a spatially-varying SDF tightness parameter (inspired by VolSDF and Adaptive Shells) with a surface regularization that progressively tightens geometry during training. The robust stratified sampling strategy, analogous to nucleus sampling in NLP, is a novel application to volume rendering that tolerates imperfect proposal distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D GAN to render every pixel at full resolution without 2D super-resolution post-processing, closing the long-standing gap between 2D image quality and 3D geometric fidelity. The 5× reduction in depth samples (30 vs. 96–192) makes high-resolution 3D GAN training substantially more tractable, and the learned sampler's scene-generalizability means it can be applied to other NeRF-based systems beyond this specific architecture. Open questions remain around handling specularities (which cause dents), transparent objects, and the frontal bias inherited from casual camera labels; extending to 360° generation and incorporating more advanced material models are natural next steps.
