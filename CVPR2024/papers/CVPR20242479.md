## Problem

Existing data valuation methods in Federated Learning rely on Shapley values with exponential complexity O(2^N), require post-hoc model training to assess contributions, and lack per-datum granularity. There is no established privacy-preserving framework that can evaluate both client-level contributions and individual data point quality before training, without access to raw data or a validation dataset, and without incurring prohibitive computational costs at scale.

## Input / Output

Each client provides only local interpolating measures and scalar Wasserstein distances to the server—no raw samples are shared. The input data is transformed into augmented stacked vectors of the form [x; m_y; vec(Σ_y^{1/2})], where m_y and Σ_y are the conditional feature mean and covariance for label y, enabling a unified Euclidean distance over the joint feature-label space. Outputs include a per-client Wasserstein distance (inverted as a contribution score), a per-datum calibrated gradient indicating whether each sample increases or decreases the distance to the target distribution, and the iteratively updated Wasserstein barycenter when no validation set is available.

## Method

FedBary decomposes the Wasserstein distance W_p(P_i, Q) via the triangle inequality into four terms computed through interpolating measures (geodesics) between P_i and Q, so that clients compute local terms W_p(P_i, η_{P_i}) and W_p(η_{P_i}, γ_i) while the server computes W_p(γ_i, η_{Q_i}) and W_p(η_{Q_i}, Q), exchanging only the interpolating measures η_{P_i} and η_{Q_i}. When no validation set exists, the target Q is approximated as the Wasserstein barycenter of all client distributions via a K-round iterative update that simultaneously refines each γ_i and the barycenter support. For per-datum detection, the paper applies the OT duality theorem to extract dual variables f*, then computes a calibrated gradient (f*_l minus the mean of other f*_j) whose sign identifies whether a datum pulls the local distribution toward or away from the target. The overall complexity is O(3NK(Sm²+S²)log(m+S)), linear in the number of clients N, making it practical for large-scale FL with hundreds of participants.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 5 clients, FedBary's contribution scores closely track exactFed (ground-truth Shapley values) across all five evaluation cases, correctly identifying that higher feature noise reduces contribution (Case 5) where GTG-SV and other Shapley approximations produce inverted rankings. For noisy feature detection, FedBary achieves a 100% success rate (all noisy points receive positive calibrated gradients, no clean points do), matching Lava which requires raw data access. In terms of scalability, FedBary evaluates 100 clients in 21 min (S=100) or 1 h (S=500), while ExactFed, GTG-Shapley, MR, and DataSV all fail to complete at N≥50. The Wasserstein barycenter converges within ~10 iterations, and the distance metric is robust to dataset duplication (39.14 → 39.23 → 39.18 across 1×, 2×, 3× replication).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DataSV [8] and GTG-Shapley [20], FedBary is substantially faster (1 min vs. 25 min and 33 s at N=5, respectively) and, critically, does not require a pre-trained model or validation set; at N=100, both baselines are infeasible while FedBary completes in 21 min. Compared to MR/OR [34], FedBary achieves similar contribution rankings in most cases (especially Cases 1–3) but additionally provides per-datum detection and noisy-data identification, which MR does not offer. Against Lava [13], FedBary matches the 100% noisy-feature detection success rate and performs comparably on mislabeled-data detection, but does so without accessing raw data samples, preserving privacy. KNNShapley [10] degrades sharply in the point-removal experiment (accuracy plateaus after ~55% removal), whereas FedBary's calibrated gradients continue to identify harmful points effectively. FedShapley [41] produces contribution scores in a similar range to FedBary but requires full federated model training, making it post-hoc and computationally heavier.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedBary sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) optimal-transport-based data valuation (Alvarez-Melis & Fusi [2], Lava [13]), which measures dataset distance without a pre-specified learning algorithm, and (2) federated computation of Wasserstein quantities via interpolating measures (Rakotomamonjy et al. [28]). The key departure is that prior OT-based valuation methods (notably Lava) require direct access to raw data samples, while Shapley-based FL methods (DataSV, GTG-Shapley) require full model training and scale exponentially in the number of clients. FedBary is the first to combine the duality-theorem-based per-datum detection of Lava with the federated interpolating-measure machinery of [28], and to introduce the Wasserstein barycenter as a validation-free target distribution, thereby achieving privacy-preserving, pre-training client evaluation and noisy-data detection simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for data-marketplace FL deployments: a server can rank and filter clients before any model training, with linear (not exponential) cost in the number of clients, and without ever receiving raw samples. The 100% detection rate for corrupted features (matching Lava, which requires data access) is a strong practical signal. However, the experimental scope is narrow—primarily CIFAR-10 with 5 clients—and label-noise detection is noticeably weaker than feature-noise detection. Open questions include the choice of barycenter weights λ, scalability beyond a few hundred clients, and whether the approach generalizes to non-i.i.d. or highly heterogeneous FL settings beyond the five synthetic cases tested.
