## Problem

Open-set novel view synthesis from a single image remains challenging because existing 3D-free methods like Zero-1-to-3 encode camera pose as global language-like tokens, leading to poor pose accuracy, and generate each view independently, producing inconsistent multi-view results. Methods that achieve consistency by building explicit 3D representations (e.g., NeRF, volumetric grids) are slow and memory-intensive, requiring per-scene optimization or additional 3D reconstruction networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB source image and a sequence of N target camera poses (each specified by intrinsic matrix K, rotation R, and translation T) and jointly generates all N novel views in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. Views are produced simultaneously rather than independently, enabling the network to enforce cross-view consistency during a single denoising pass. For 360° video rendering, 50 azimuth angles are uniformly sampled at a fixed elevation, with a 7.2° step between consecutive frames.

## Method

Free3D fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion UNet with three key modifications. First, a Ray Conditioning Normalization (RCN) layer computes per-pixel Plücker coordinates from the target camera pose and uses them to predict scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters via a small MLP, modulating each UNet sub-module's activations through an adaptive layer norm—this distributed per-pixel ray encoding replaces the concentrated global pose tokens used in Zero-1-to-3. Second, a pseudo-3D cross-view attention module reshapes the 5D latent (batch × views × channels × height × width) so that attention operates across views at each spatial location, exchanging information between views at low computational cost. Third, all target views share the same initial noise vector during sampling, reducing aleatoric variation between views. The model is trained with a standard denoising diffusion objective on Objaverse (772,870 objects), concatenating the source image latent and CLIP encoding as additional conditioning channels.

## Results

On the full Objaverse test set (7,729 objects), Free3D achieves SSIM 0.8620, LPIPS 0.0784, FID 1.21, and PPLC 10.82, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The most striking gain is in multi-view consistency (PPLC), where Free3D scores 10.82 versus 18.84 for Zero-1-to-3 (43% reduction) and 25.61 for Zero123-XL (58% reduction). On unseen datasets, Free3D generalizes strongly: on OmniObject3D it reaches PSNR 18.23 / LPIPS 0.0996 / PPLC 8.67, and on GSO it reaches PSNR 21.13 / LPIPS 0.0619 / PPLC 9.10, again leading all compared methods. Inference is fast at 3 s per target view and 52 s for a full 360° video, comparable to Zero-1-to-3 and much faster than SyncDreamer (25 s / 77 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Free3D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. Against Zero-1-to-3 [38] (the closest 3D-free baseline), Free3D improves LPIPS by 16% (0.0784 vs. 0.0938) and PPLC by 43% (10.82 vs. 18.84), showing that the RCN layer and multi-view attention/noise sharing address the two main weaknesses of the original design. Against Zero123-XL [14], which is trained on a much larger dataset (Objaverse-XL, 10M+ objects), Free3D still leads on all metrics, including a 58% PPLC reduction (10.82 vs. 25.61), demonstrating that better pose encoding matters more than raw data scale. Against SyncDreamer [39], which uses an explicit 3D volumetric representation, Free3D is dramatically faster (3 s vs. 25 s per view) while achieving substantially better LPIPS (0.0784 vs. 0.1910) and FID (1.21 vs. 7.57), though SyncDreamer's PPLC (16.32) is closer to Free3D's than the other baselines. Against Consistent123 [69], which also uses multi-view diffusion with cross-view attention, Free3D achieves a 39% PPLC improvement (10.82 vs. 17.89) and is faster per view (3 s vs. 4 s), with the paper attributing the gap to the RCN layer's superior pose control. On the unseen OmniObject3D and GSO datasets, Free3D extends these lead margins, confirming that the gains are not dataset-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

Free3D extends the 3D-free NVS line initiated by Zero-1-to-3 (fine-tuning Stable Diffusion with camera pose tokens) and 3DiM (ray-conditioned diffusion for NVS), but departs from both by replacing the concentrated pose-token encoding with a per-pixel Ray Conditioning Normalization (RCN) layer inspired by NeRF/LFN Plücker-coordinate ray representations. It also borrows the cross-view attention mechanism from video diffusion models and introduces multi-view noise sharing, positioning itself against concurrent methods (SyncDreamer, Consistent123) that achieve consistency through explicit 3D volumetric or NeRF-based representations. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that these 3D-free components, combined, can match or exceed the consistency of 3D-representation-based approaches without the associated compute and memory overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a lightweight RCN layer plus cross-view attention and shared noise can replace expensive 3D reconstruction pipelines for consistent multi-view synthesis, lowering the barrier for open-set NVS in production settings. The proposed PPLC metric also offers a scalable consistency evaluation that avoids per-instance 3D model training, which prior methods required. Limitations include dependence on 3D training data (Objaverse) for supervision, the fixed 50-frame circular trajectory in evaluation, and the fact that the multi-view attention operates only at the UNet level without explicit geometric reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unbounded scenes, whether the noise-sharing trick generalizes to longer sequences or non-circular trajectories, and whether the RCN layer can be combined with text conditioning for controllable NVS.
