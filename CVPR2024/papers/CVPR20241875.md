## Problem

Existing prompt-based UDA methods (e.g., DAPrompt) learn domain-specific textual prompts for source and target separately, which encodes source knowledge into non-transferable prompts and limits cross-domain alignment. Moreover, prompting only the language branch of a VLM cannot dynamically adapt both modalities, leaving the visual embeddings domain-biased and misaligned with the target distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241875/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from both a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain during training, and a single target-domain image at inference. Classification is performed by computing cosine similarity between a prompted visual embedding and K prompted textual embeddings in the frozen CLIP joint space, with a learned temperature τ=0.01. Only the learnable context tokens p_{1:N} (N=32) and the cross-attention prompting module G (2 decoder layers) are updated; both CLIP encoders remain frozen, keeping the total tunable parameter count very small (~11.9% of PADCLIP's).

## Method

DAMP introduces a mutual prompting framework where a shared, domain-agnostic textual prompt template guides the generation of visual prompts via a cross-attention module G, while spatial visual context (the discarded token-level embeddings from CLIP's attention pooling) reciprocally adjusts the textual embeddings on a per-instance basis. The cross-attention module uses L Transformer decoder layers to enable bidirectional information flow between the two modalities, producing domain-invariant visual embeddings v′ and instance-conditioned textual embeddings s′_k that are combined with residual connections weighted by learnable coefficients γ_v and γ_s. Two auxiliary regularizations enforce the desired properties: an instance-discrimination contrastive loss that penalizes domain-specific cues leaking into the prompted text embeddings, and a semantic-consistency loss (FixMatch-style) that enforces correct classification under strong augmentation for both source and confident target samples. An information-maximization entropy loss further regularizes unconfident target predictions to avoid category collapse. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a combined objective of supervised loss, semantic-consistency loss, contrastive loss, and information-maximization loss.

## Results

On Office-Home (12 domain transfer tasks), DAMP achieves 78.2% average accuracy with ResNet-50 and 87.1% with ViT-B/16, outperforming the strongest prompt-based baselines DAPrompt (74.5%/84.4%) and AD-CLIP (75.9%/86.1%) by 3.7% and 3.1% respectively. On VisDA-17, DAMP reaches 88.4% (ResNet-101) and 90.9% (ViT-B/16), matching or exceeding PADCLIP. On Mini-DomainNet, DAMP sets new state-of-the-art with 77.5% (ResNet-50) and 87.6% (ViT-B/16), improving over CLIP by 6.3% and 4.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMP consistently outperforms all prompt-based UDA baselines. On Office-Home, it surpasses DAPrompt by 3.7% (ResNet-50) and 3.1% (ViT-B/16), and AD-CLIP by 2.3% and 1.0% respectively, with the largest gains on challenging tasks like Ar→Cl (+5.6% over DAPrompt with ResNet-50). Against PADCLIP (which fine-tunes the full CLIP visual backbone), DAMP improves by 1.6% with ResNet-50 while using only 11.9% of the tunable parameters. On VisDA-17, DAMP slightly trails PADCLIP by 0.1% with ResNet-101 (88.4% vs 88.5%) but matches it exactly with ViT-B/16 (90.9%), while clearly beating DAPrompt (86.9%/89.5%) and AD-CLIP (87.7%/90.7%). On Mini-DomainNet, DAMP achieves the best results across all 12 transfer tasks with both backbones, improving over AD-CLIP by 2.3% (ResNet-50) and 0.7% (ViT-B/16), with particularly large gains on difficult pairs like Cl→Sk (+5.7% over DAPrompt with ResNet-50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning-for-CLIP line (CoOp, CoCoOp) into the UDA setting, departing from DAPrompt and AD-CLIP which learn domain-specific textual prompts separately for source and target and only adapt the language branch. DAMP's key conceptual shift is making prompts domain-agnostic (shared across domains) and introducing mutual prompting: a cross-attention module lets domain-agnostic textual semantics guide visual embedding refinement while instance-level visual context adjusts textual embeddings, creating a bidirectional synergy absent in prior single-modality or unidirectional approaches. The auxiliary losses (instance-discrimination contrastive, semantic-consistency, information maximization) borrow from FixMatch-style semi-supervised UDA and contrastive learning literature but are repurposed to regularize the prompted embeddings toward domain invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAMP offers a parameter-efficient (only ~12% of PADCLIP's tunable parameters) and conceptually clean recipe for adapting frozen VLMs to UDA, which is practically attractive as VLMs become the default backbone. The mutual prompting framework is modular and could transfer to other VLM-based adaptation tasks (segmentation, detection) or to domain generalization settings. Open questions include whether the domain-agnostic prompt assumption holds under very large or out-of-distribution domain shifts, whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP to newer VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2+text), and whether the cross-attention module's depth and the learnable γ coefficients scale gracefully to larger class sets or multi-source settings.
