## Problem

Vision-language models like ALBEF can localize objects via GradCAM without any box annotations, but they produce inconsistent attention maps for semantically equivalent phrases (e.g., 'frisbee' vs. 'disc'), limiting both their working vocabulary and localization accuracy. No prior weakly-supervised method explicitly enforces self-consistency across paraphrased inputs, leaving a gap in how to improve grounding without bounding boxes or object detectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240641/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB image and a short text phrase as input and produces a GradCAM-based spatial relevance heatmap (not a bounding box) indicating where the referenced object appears. During training, each image-text pair is augmented with an LLM-generated paraphrase, so the model simultaneously processes two text inputs for the same image and is penalized if their resulting heatmaps diverge. The paraphrases are generated by Vicuna-13B via a two-level prompting strategy: first chunking global captions into object-centric phrases, then replacing the primary noun with synonyms, hypernyms, or related terms while preserving other attributes.

## Method

SelfEQ (Self-consistency EQuivalence Tuning) finetunes ALBEF (ViT-B + BERT-base) by adding a self-consistency loss on top of the standard vision-language objectives (ITM, MLM, ITC). For each image-text pair and its LLM-generated paraphrase, GradCAM heatmaps are extracted from the multimodal fusion encoder; a pixel-wise MSE loss (L_sim) minimizes the L2 distance between the two heatmaps, while a consistency loss (L_cst) uses a thresholded RoI mask (where the sum of both heatmaps exceeds k=0.8) to encourage consistently high attention scores and low variance within the overlapping region, preventing trivial collapse to uniform maps. The composite training objective blends the vision-language loss with SelfEQ via a scheduled weight α that transitions from 0 to 1 after the second epoch. The key insight is that mutual supervision between a known phrase and its paraphrase allows the model to extrapolate localization for less common vocabulary through weak supervision alone, without any box or segment annotations at any stage.

## Results

On Flickr30k (pointing game accuracy), SelfEQ achieves 84.07% when trained on MS-COCO and 81.90% when trained on Visual Genome, representing absolute improvements of 4.69% and 2.52% over the ALBEF baseline respectively. On ReferIt, SelfEQ reaches 67.40% (VG) and 62.75% (MS-COCO), a 7.68% absolute gain over ALBEF in the VG setting. On RefCOCO+, SelfEQ attains 75.10% (TestA) and 55.49% (TestB), improving over off-the-shelf ALBEF by 5.75% and 1.72% respectively, while remaining competitive with box-supervised methods like AMC (80.34%/64.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among weakly-supervised methods (no box annotations), SelfEQ achieves the best Flickr30k score under both training settings: 81.90% (VG) and 84.07% (MS-COCO), outperforming the strongest prior method g++ [44] by 1.95% and 5.97% respectively. On ReferIt, SelfEQ (VG) trails g++ [44] by 2.85% (67.40 vs. 70.25), but surpasses it when trained on MS-COCO (62.75 vs. 61.53). Compared to box-supervised methods, SelfEQ (MS-COCO) reaches 84.07% on Flickr30k, narrowly behind AMC [51] (86.59%) and well ahead of VMRM [12] (81.11%), InfoGround [19] (76.74%), and 12-in-1 [35] (76.40%). On RefCOCO+, SelfEQ (75.10% TestA, 55.49% TestB) substantially outperforms the ALBEF baseline (69.35%/53.77%) and approaches AMC [51] (80.34%/64.55%) without any box supervision, while significantly exceeding VMRM [12] (58.87%/50.32%) and InfoGround [19] (39.80%/41.11%). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that simply adding paraphrases as extra image-text pairs without the SelfEQ objective does not improve performance, validating that the self-consistency loss is the key driver of gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ALBEF's off-the-shelf GradCAM-based grounding capability and extends the weakly-supervised visual grounding line of work (GbS, g, g++) by introducing a self-consistency objective over gradient-based explanation maps. Unlike prior methods that either modify the network architecture (g++ uses layerwise relevance propagation) or rely on pseudo-labels from external models (CLIP in g++), SelfEQ operates purely on the existing GradCAM pipeline without architectural changes. The use of LLM-generated paraphrases as a data augmentation strategy for visual grounding is novel; prior weakly-supervised methods either augment the image side (GbS's alpha-blending) or rely on external detectors, whereas this work augments the text side with semantically equivalent phrases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that enforcing self-consistency across paraphrased prompts is a practical, annotation-free lever for improving visual grounding, and that LLM-based paraphrase generation is a viable data augmentation strategy for vision-language models. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the ALBEF + GradCAM pipeline, and the paraphrase strategy (primarily synonym substitution via WordNet) is relatively simple, leaving open whether more diverse paraphrase types (structural rewrites, hypernym/meronym shifts) would yield further gains. A key open question is whether this self-consistency principle generalizes to other VLMs (CLIP, BLIP-2) or to more complex referring expressions where a single paraphrase pair may not capture the full semantic equivalence.
