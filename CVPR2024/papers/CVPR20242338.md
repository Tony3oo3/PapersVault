## Problem

Existing text-to-shape generation methods, such as SDFusion, treat text and 3D shapes as flat representations, ignoring that both are inherently hierarchical: text has a syntactic tree structure, and 3D shapes exhibit part-to-whole compositionality. This paper argues that jointly embedding these hierarchical structures in hyperbolic space—whose exponential expansion naturally accommodates trees—can significantly improve generation fidelity and specificity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a 3D shape represented as a 64×64×64×1 Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF). The text is consumed through two parallel pathways: a transformer-based encoder producing sequential/multi-modal features (C₁) and a graph convolution module operating on a spaCy-parsed syntax tree producing hierarchical syntactic features (C₂). The 3D shape is first compressed by a VQ-VAE into a 16×16×16×3 discrete latent code, which the diffusion model generates before decoding back to TSDF.

## Method

HyperSDFusion is a dual-branch latent diffusion model that operates in hyperbolic (Poincaré ball) space. A VQ-VAE compresses 64³ TSDFs into 16³×3 latent codes; the diffusion denoiser is a dual-branch 3D U-Net where each branch receives one text condition (C₁ from a hyperbolic text-image encoder pre-trained via MERU, C₂ from a hyperbolic graph convolution over the syntax tree) through separate cross-attention layers, preventing feature interference. A novel hyperbolic hierarchical loss regularizes the denoiser's multi-scale features by enforcing that geodesic distances from the Poincaré ball origin satisfy d_shallow > d_medium > d_deep, thereby preserving the part-to-whole hierarchy of generated shapes. The VQ-VAE is trained on ShapeNet (13 categories) and frozen, while the diffusion model is trained on Text2Shape with AdamW (lr=1e-5) and sampled via DDIM (T=100).

## Results

On the Text2Shape test set (1000 random samples), HyperSDFusion achieves IoU of 16.21, Chamfer Distance of 0.7433, F-score of 15.16, and FID of 0.70, outperforming SDFusion (IoU 13.98, CD 1.246, F-score 12.67, FID 5.47) by 23% in IoU, 40% reduction in CD, 19.7% in F-score, and 87% reduction in FID. The method also outperforms Liu et al. (IoU 12.21, CD 1.41, F-score 13.34, FID 1.00) across all four metrics. On Text2Shape++ with texts of varying lengths (≤8, 8–16, >16 words), HyperSDFusion consistently surpasses SDFusion, with the largest gains on short texts (IoU 15.41 vs. 8.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperSDFusion is compared against two baselines: SDFusion [7] (the primary diffusion-based text-to-shape method) and Liu et al. [28] (an implicit text-guided 3D shape generation method). Against SDFusion, HyperSDFusion wins decisively on all four metrics: IoU improves from 13.98 to 16.21, CD drops from 1.246 to 0.7433, F-score rises from 12.67 to 15.16, and FID plummets from 5.47 to 0.70, indicating substantially more faithful and visually coherent shape generation. Against Liu et al., HyperSDFusion also leads on every metric (IoU 16.21 vs. 12.21, CD 0.7433 vs. 1.41, F-score 15.16 vs. 13.34, FID 0.70 vs. 1.00). The paper further shows that HyperSDFusion maintains its advantage across text lengths (short, medium, long) on Text2Shape++, and achieves lower Hierarchical Mutual Difference (HMD), confirming better preservation of the text-shape hierarchical structure compared to SDFusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperSDFusion extends the SDFusion pipeline (VQ-VAE compression + latent diffusion denoiser) by replacing Euclidean text encoders and adding a hyperbolic geometric layer throughout. It draws on MERU's hyperbolic text-image contrastive pretraining for the sequential/multi-modal branch and introduces a novel hyperbolic graph convolution over syntax trees for the hierarchical branch, a combination not previously applied to 3D shape generation. The dual-branch denoiser architecture adapts the latent diffusion U-Net design from Rombach et al. to handle two distinct text condition streams in parallel, and the hyperbolic hierarchical loss is a new regularization that has no direct precedent in prior text-to-shape work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that hyperbolic geometry can meaningfully encode the part-whole hierarchy of 3D shapes and the syntactic hierarchy of language simultaneously, offering a principled geometric inductive bias for text-to-shape generation. However, evaluation is confined to the Text2Shape dataset (chairs and tables only), and absolute quality metrics remain modest (IoU ~16%), leaving open whether the hierarchical inductive bias generalizes to more complex, multi-category shape spaces. The authors themselves note the need for 'more direct links to bridge language and 3D geometry,' suggesting the current approach is a first step rather than a definitive solution.
