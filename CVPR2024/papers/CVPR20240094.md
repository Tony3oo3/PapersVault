## Problem

Cross-modal adaptation from images to events for object recognition has typically required paired data from both modalities or access to the source image dataset. This paper tackles the more restrictive and practically important setting where only a pre-trained source image model is available (due to privacy or commercial constraints) and no source images can be accessed, while the significant modality gap between RGB images and sparse, asynchronous event streams makes direct knowledge transfer non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240094/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw event streams (x, y, t, p tuples) and a white-box pre-trained source image model (ResNet-18/34/50 trained on ImageNet). During training, events are converted into three representations—stack images (H×W×1), voxel grids (H×W×C with B temporal bins), and event spike tensors (EST)—each feeding a separate target model. At inference, a single target model (typically the voxel-grid one) produces a class label; the reconstruction model and source model are discarded.

## Method

EventDance comprises two mutually-updated modules. The Reconstruction-based Modality Bridging (RMB) module uses a pre-trained E2VID+EvFlowNet pipeline to reconstruct intensity frames from events, creating surrogate images that are fed to the frozen source model for pseudo-label extraction; the reconstruction model is fine-tuned by minimizing the entropy of source predictions (L_EN) and enforcing temporal consistency across reconstructed frames (L_TC), so the surrogate images are optimized for knowledge extraction rather than visual fidelity. The Multi-representation Knowledge Adaptation (MKA) module trains three target ResNets on the three event representations simultaneously, supervised by pseudo-labels (L_Sup), with cross-representation consistency loss (L_PC) among the three target models and a bidirectional cross-modal consistency loss (L_CM) that co-updates the source and target models. The entire framework is optimized end-to-end, and the key insight is that multiple event representations are jointly beneficial for source-free cross-modal adaptation, as each captures complementary spatiotemporal information lost in any single representation.

## Results

On the novel image-to-events cross-modal adaptation task, EventDance achieves 92.30% top-1 accuracy on N-MNIST, 85.69% on CIFAR10-DVS, and 92.35% on N-CALTECH101 with a ResNet-50 backbone, outperforming all source-free UDA baselines by 2.9–22.3 percentage points. The gains over the pre-trained source model baseline are +15.20% (N-MNIST), +24.81% (CIFAR10-DVS), and +21.62% (N-CALTECH101). With R-50, EventDance reaches 92.35% on N-CALTECH101, approaching the 94.56% of DSAN which has access to source data, while remaining fully source-free and unsupervised.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventDance decisively outperforms all three source-free UDA baselines (SHOT, SHOT++, Zhao et al./CTN) across all three datasets and backbones; the largest margin is on CIFAR10-DVS where EventDance (R-18: 62.13%) surpasses SHOT++ (37.26%) by 24.87 points. Against the label-free methods (E2VID+CLIP at 9.40%, Ev-LaFOR+CLIP at 82.61%, Wang et al.+CLIP at 43.50% on N-CALTECH101), EventDance (92.35% with R-50) leads by 9.74–82.95 points. The closest competitor is DSAN [77], a UDA method that uses source data, achieving 94.56% on N-CALTECH101 (R-50) versus EventDance's 92.35%—a 2.21-point gap despite EventDance having no source data access. On N-MNIST, EventDance (92.30%, R-50) trails supervised event-based methods (EV-VGCNN 99.10%, Deep SNN 98.70%, Phased LSTM 97.30%, PointNet++ 95.50%) by 3.2–6.8 points, which is expected given the unsupervised and source-free constraints. In the extended edge-map-to-voxel-grid setting (Table 6), EventDance also outperforms CTN [68] by 1.8–2.5 points across backbones, confirming generality beyond the primary cross-modal task.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventDance sits at the intersection of three research threads: source-free UDA (SHOT, SHOT++), cross-modal knowledge transfer (SOCKET, CTN), and event-to-video reconstruction (Paredes-Vallés & de Croon, CVPR 2021). It departs from prior cross-modal adaptation methods that require paired data or source data access by introducing a reconstruction-based surrogate domain to bridge the image-event modality gap without any source images. The multi-representation consistency training in the MKA module extends the single-representation target models used in CTN [68] to simultaneously exploit stack images, voxel grids, and event spike tensors, drawing on the observation that no single representation captures all spatiotemporal information in events.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically important constraint—privacy and commercial restrictions that prevent sharing labeled source data—while tackling the particularly challenging cross-modal gap between images and events. The finding that optimizing surrogate images for knowledge extraction (via entropy minimization) rather than visual fidelity yields a 17.83% gain challenges the intuitive assumption that better-looking reconstructions are more useful for transfer. Key limitations include the increased computational cost of training three target models simultaneously, and the method is currently validated only on object recognition; extending to other downstream tasks (detection, segmentation) and reducing the multi-model overhead remain open questions.
