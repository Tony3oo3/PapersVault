## Problem

Existing feature tracking methods only predict 2D trajectories in the image plane, while real-world objects move in 3D space. No established method or dataset exists for tracking 3D feature trajectories of high-speed moving objects, as traditional frame-based cameras cannot capture sufficient motion detail and 3D vision sensors lack the temporal resolution to record ground truth 3D trajectories at high speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240452/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes stereo event streams from two event cameras and a 31×31 grayscale template patch (captured by camera 1 at the initial moment) as input. Events are binned into 4 ms time windows and converted to grid-based event patches; camera 2 contributes a full-width event row patch for disparity estimation. The output is a continuous 3D feature trajectory in camera 1's coordinate system, updated at 250 FPS (one 3D position per 4 ms step).

## Method

The core contribution is a joint framework that simultaneously predicts 2D feature motion offsets and 3D spatial position at each timestep, rather than decoupling 2D tracking from depth estimation. A motion compensation module predicts per-step affine parameters (scale, rotation, shear) to correct template deformation caused by depth changes and rotation. The offset estimation module uses a two-branch FPN (adapted from DeepEvT) with a ConvLSTM for temporal fusion. For 3D position, a patch matching module constructs bi-polarity hypergraphs over candidate disparity positions: a positive hypergraph connects semantically similar and spatially close patches (to be enhanced) while a negative hypergraph connects semantically similar but spatially distant patches (to be suppressed), eliminating mismatching in local patches. A stereo motion consistency loss enforces the geometric constraint that the difference in 2D horizontal offsets between the two cameras equals the disparity change, tying the offset and disparity predictions together. The full model is trained end-to-end with Manhattan distance losses on 3D trajectory, 2D offsets, disparity, and the stereo motion consistency term.

## Results

On the E-3DTrack dataset, the proposed method achieves an RMSE of 0.1181 m, reducing error by 37.5% over the second-best baseline (DeepEvT+SDE, RMSE 0.1889 m). The Tracked Feature Ratio at 0.1 m threshold reaches 0.6928, compared to 0.5782 for DeepEvT+SDE, and Feature Age at 0.1 m improves from 0.2314 to 0.2601. Inference time is 40.30 ms/step, a 56.8% reduction over DeepEvT+SDE (93.22 ms/step), enabling 250 FPS tracking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baseline combinations of 2D event-based tracking (E-RAFT, EKLT, DeepEvT) with stereo depth estimation (TSES, SDE). The proposed method outperforms all baselines on every metric. Against the strongest baseline DeepEvT+SDE, it reduces RMSE from 0.1889 to 0.1181 m (−37.5%) and improves TFR at 0.1 m from 0.5782 to 0.6928 (+19.8%). The gap widens substantially against weaker combinations: EKLT+TSES achieves RMSE 0.4806 m and TFR(0.1m) 0.1180, while E-RAFT+TSES reaches RMSE 0.4726 m and TFR(0.1m) 0.1701. Notably, SDE consistently outperforms TSES as the depth estimation partner across all 2D trackers, confirming that the joint 3D tracking framework (rather than a better depth estimator alone) is the key to the large performance gains. The method also runs 2.3× faster than DeepEvT+SDE (40.30 vs. 93.22 ms/step).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established paradigm of treating event-based 2D feature tracking (DeepEvT, EKLT, E-RAFT) and event-based stereo depth estimation (SDE, TSES) as independent problems, arguing that their naive composition fails for high-speed 3D motion. It builds on DeepEvT's FPN-based offset estimation architecture and adapts the hypergraph message-passing formulation from HGNN+ (Gao et al., 2023) into a bi-polarity hypergraph for local patch matching. The key conceptual departure is a joint framework that simultaneously predicts 2D motion offsets and 3D spatial position, coupled by a stereo motion consistency constraint derived from the geometric relationship between disparity change and inter-camera offset difference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first method and dataset for high-speed 3D feature tracking with event cameras, this work establishes a benchmark (E-3DTrack at 250 FPS) that was previously unattainable due to the limited capture frequency of conventional 3D sensors. The demonstration that separate 2D tracking and depth estimation pipelines degrade significantly under 3D motion (vs. the joint approach) provides a clear design principle for future event-based 3D perception. Limitations include reliance on a specific stereo event camera setup, a relatively small dataset (40 scenarios), and the 250 FPS rate being tied to the Optitrack hardware; open questions include generalization to monocular or multi-view configurations, real-time deployment on embedded hardware, and scaling to cluttered scenes with occlusion.
