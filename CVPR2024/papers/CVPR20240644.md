## Problem

Regression-based scene flow networks suffer from unreliable point correspondence due to KNN's limited search range and from accumulated errors in coarse-to-fine refinement, where each layer is constrained to a small spatial neighborhood around a potentially inaccurate initialization. Additionally, prior work provides no mechanism to assess the per-point reliability of estimated flow, leaving users unable to distinguish confident matches from erroneous ones in challenging scenarios such as dynamics, noise, and repetitive patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes two consecutive LiDAR point cloud frames (PC₁ ∈ ℝ^{N×3}, PC₂ ∈ ℝ^{M×3}) and outputs a dense per-point 3D displacement vector (sf ∈ ℝ^{N×3}) for every point in PC₁, together with a per-point binary uncertainty flag indicating whether the estimated correspondence is trustworthy. The flow is produced hierarchically across three diffusion-based refinement layers, each operating at progressively higher resolution, and the uncertainty is propagated layer-to-layer via the same coarse-to-fine feature propagation used for the flow itself.

## Method

DifFlow3D replaces the conventional iterative refinement stage with a conditional denoising diffusion process (DDIM, T=1000) that generates flow *residuals* rather than the full flow vector, starting from a coarse sparse initialization at the lowest resolution. To suppress the inherent generation diversity of diffusion models, three flow-related condition signals—geometry features from PC₁, a GRU-augmented cross-frame cost volume, and up-sampled coarse dense flow embeddings—are concatenated and fed to the denoising network at every timestep. A per-point uncertainty residual is jointly predicted by the same denoising network and supervised with a binary ground truth whose thresholds (E₁, E₂) decay with training progress, progressively tightening the acceptable error band. The overall loss combines multi-scale scene flow L1 loss, diffusion residual loss, and uncertainty residual loss across all refinement layers, and the resulting diffusion-based refinement module can be dropped into existing networks (3DFlow, BPF, MSBRN) as a plug-and-play replacement for their original refinement stages with only ~3% additional runtime.

## Results

DifFlow3D achieves state-of-the-art scene flow estimation with EPE3D of 0.0114 m on FlyingThings3D (FT3Ds) and 0.0078 m on KITTI (KITTIs), representing 24.0% and 29.1% reductions over the previous best method MSBRN. Notably, the 0.0078 m EPE3D on KITTI is the first millimeter-level accuracy reported for this dataset. The method also leads on all secondary metrics: Acc3DS of 0.9836/0.9817, Acc3DR of 0.9949/0.9924, and Outliers of 0.0350/0.0795 on FT3Ds/KITTIs respectively. On the occluded variants (FT3D_o, KITTI_o), DifFlow3D reduces EPE3D by 18.9% and 29.5% over MSBRN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DifFlow3D outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every metric across both datasets, with no losses or ties. Against the closest competitor MSBRN [5], it reduces EPE3D by 24.0% (0.0114 vs 0.0150) on FT3Ds and 29.1% (0.0078 vs 0.0110) on KITTIs, while also cutting outliers from 0.0560 to 0.0350 on FT3Ds. The margin over earlier SOTA methods is even larger: IHNet [47] is beaten by 40.3% on FT3Ds EPE3D, and 3DFlow [44] by 59.4% on FT3Ds and 74.8% on KITTIs. On the occluded KITTI_o setting, DifFlow3D achieves 0.031 m EPE3D versus 0.044 m for MSBRN (29.5% reduction) and 0.063 m for the self-supervised SCOOP [17]. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 3) further show that adding the diffusion refinement module to 3DFlow, BPF, and MSBRN yields 6.3–24.0% EPE3D improvements, confirming the generality of the approach. Runtime is competitive at 228.3 ms, only 3.3% slower than its MSBRN baseline, while methods like RCP [10] require 2854.6 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al., 2020) and DDIM sampling (Song et al., 2020) for a deterministic regression task—scene flow estimation—rather than their conventional generative role. It builds directly on the MSBRN (Cheng et al., 2023) architecture for flow initialization and GRU-augmented cost volumes, and on FlowNet3D's (Liu et al., 2019) hierarchical set-convolution feature extraction. The key conceptual departure is treating the flow residual as a diffusion latent variable conditioned on geometry, cost volume, and coarse flow embeddings, thereby converting a coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline into an iterative denoising process. This contrasts with prior bi-directional or recurrent refinement strategies (3DFlow, BPF, IHNet) that address correlation reliability through architectural complexity rather than probabilistic modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the diffusion-based refinement module is practically significant: it can be dropped into existing scene flow networks (3DFlow, BPF, MSBRN) and consistently yields 5–24% EPE3D reductions without architectural redesign, making it an accessible upgrade for the field. The introduction of per-point uncertainty estimation within the diffusion framework opens a path toward confidence-aware 3D motion perception, which is critical for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large outdoor scenes with millions of points, whether the T=1000 DDIM steps can be further compressed for real-time use, and whether the uncertainty signal can be leveraged downstream (e.g., for selective flow application in SLAM or motion segmentation).
