## Problem

Existing music generation models are conditioned exclusively on text, yet musicians naturally compose from both visual and verbal cues. No prior work synthesizes music jointly conditioned on an image and a text prompt, and the obvious workaround of captioning the image first and feeding the caption to a text-to-music model loses fine-grained visual semantics and degrades audio quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241114/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form text prompt (up to ~3 sentences describing genre, mood, instrumentation, etc.) as joint conditioning. The image is first DDIM-inverted into a noisy latent of a frozen Stable Diffusion model, while the text is encoded by FLAN-T5. The output is a 10-second music waveform, produced by decoding a latent representation back to a mel-spectrogram via AudioVAE and then synthesizing the waveform with a HiFi-GAN vocoder.

## Method

MELFUSION introduces a 'visual synapse' that bridges a frozen text-to-image diffusion model and a trainable text-to-music diffusion model. At each denoising step, the self-attention key and value features (K_l^I, V_l^I) extracted from the decoder blocks of the image LDM (consuming the DDIM-inverted image latent) are convexly combined with the cross-attention key and value features (K_l^M, V_l^M) of the music LDM's corresponding decoder blocks, modulated by a single learned scalar α_l per layer. This per-layer mixing coefficient controls how much visual semantic guidance each block receives, analogous to synaptic strength in a nervous system. The music LDM's UNet architecture mirrors the text-to-image UNet, and training optimizes the music LDM parameters θ and all α_l end-to-end under the standard diffusion denoising loss, while the image LDM remains frozen. The approach is parameter-efficient: only the α scalars and the music LDM weights are learned, with no additional cross-modal projection network.

## Results

On MeLBench, MELFUSION achieves a FAD of 1.05, KL divergence of 0.72, and IMSM of 0.83, outperforming the best text-to-music baseline MusicGen (FAD 3.28, KL 1.21) by 67.98% on FAD and 40.49% on KL. On Extended MusicCaps, MELFUSION reaches FAD 1.12 and KL 0.89, a 67.05% FAD improvement over MusicGen. Subjectively, MELFUSION scores 88.45 OVL and 87.39 REL on MeLBench, surpassing the closest multi-modal baseline TANGO++ (85.52 OVL, 84.81 REL) by ~3 points on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MELFUSION outperforms all seven text-to-music baselines (Riffusion, Mubert, MusicLM, Mousai, Noise2Music, MeLoDy, MusicGen) on every reported objective metric, with the largest gap against MusicGen: 67.98% FAD reduction on MeLBench (1.05 vs. 3.28) and 67.05% on MusicCaps (1.12 vs. 3.40). Against the two multi-modal baselines, MELFUSION also wins decisively: over TANGO++ it improves FAD from 2.93→1.05 (MeLBench) and 3.05→1.12 (MusicCaps), and over MusicLM+InstructBLIP from 3.88→1.05 and 4.12→1.12 respectively. On the proposed IMSM metric (image–music similarity), MELFUSION achieves 0.83 on MeLBench and 0.76 on MusicCaps, beating TANGO++ (0.71/0.68) and MusicLM+InstructBLIP (0.63/0.55) by a wide margin. Subjectively, MELFUSION leads on both OVL and REL across both datasets, with the largest subjective gap over MusicLM+InstructBLIP (~6–7 points on OVL/REL on MeLBench). The paper attributes TANGO++'s relative weakness to its use of simple addition for fusing CLIP image and FLAN-T5 text embeddings, and MusicLM+InstructBLIP's drop to a distributional shift between InstructBLIP captions and the expert-annotated MusicCaps captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MELFUSION builds on two key pillars: the Stable Diffusion architecture (Rombach et al.) for its text-to-image UNet whose self-attention features serve as the visual guidance source, and AudioLDM (Liu et al.) for the AudioVAE encoder/decoder that maps music spectrograms to latent space. The paper departs from prior text-to-music methods (MusicLM, MusicGen, Riffusion, Mousai) that condition solely on text, and from the naive alternative of captioning images with InstructBLIP before feeding text to a music model. Its core conceptual departure is the 'visual synapse'—a learned convex combination that injects frozen text-to-image self-attention features into the cross-attention layers of a text-to-music diffusion UNet, rather than concatenating or aligning modality embeddings in a shared space as TANGO++ does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of multi-modal (image + text) music generation, accompanied by the first dataset (MeLBench, 11,250 triplets) and a purpose-built evaluation metric (IMSM) for image-music correspondence, filling a clear gap in the music generation literature. The finding that a short text prompt plus image conditioning outperforms verbose text-only prompts (Table 6) suggests visual semantics carry compositional information that is difficult to verbalize, which has implications for how we think about cross-modal conditioning in creative generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-conditioned music, whether the visual synapse generalizes to other audio types beyond music, and whether the reliance on DDIM inversion of a frozen Stable Diffusion model limits scalability and diversity of generated outputs.
