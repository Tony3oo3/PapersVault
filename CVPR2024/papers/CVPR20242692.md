## Problem

While Stable Diffusion's UNet has been shown to produce semantically meaningful feature maps for keypoint matching (DIFT, SD+DINO), the textual prompt conditioning the UNet has been handcrafted or left as an empty string, leaving the model's full representational capacity untapped. The paper asks whether directly optimizing the prompt in latent space can substantially improve SD-based semantic matching without modifying the diffusion model itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242692/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images resized to 768×768 and, for each annotated query point on the source image, produces a matching probability map over the entire target image via a softmax-normalized correlation map. At inference, Kernel-Softmax localizes the predicted correspondence. The entire UNet and VAE remain frozen; only the prompt embedding (75 tokens for Single/Class, 25 global + 50 conditional tokens for CPM) is learned, making the approach parameter-efficient relative to the full diffusion model.

## Method

SD4Match learns a prompt embedding θ that conditions a frozen Stable Diffusion 2-1 UNet to extract better matching features, trained with a cross-entropy loss between the softmax correlation map and Gaussian-smoothed Dirac ground-truth correspondences. Three prompt variants are proposed: a single universal prompt (SD4Match-Single), one prompt per object category (SD4Match-Class), and a Conditional Prompting Module (SD4Match-CPM). The CPM extracts local feature patches from both images via DINOv2, concatenates them along the feature dimension, projects through two linear layers (one feature-wise, one patch-wise for inter-patch information exchange), applies adaptive MaxPooling to fit the SD token budget, and combines the result with a learnable global prompt via a conditioning weight and positional embedding. A key design choice is conditioning on the image pair jointly rather than on individual images, and on local patches rather than global descriptors, which ablations show are both critical for matching accuracy.

## Results

On SPair-71k (α=0.1), SD4Match-CPM achieves 75.5% PCK overall, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art SimSC-iBOT (63.5%) by 12 percentage points and the best zero-shot SD-based method SD+DINO (59.3%) by 16.2 points. SD4Match-Class also reaches 75.5%, while the simplest variant SD4Match-Single (72.6%) already surpasses all prior methods by 9.1 points. On the generalizability test (tuned on PF-Pascal), SD4Match-CPM achieves 95.2% PCK on PF-Pascal and 80.4% on PF-Willow, matching or exceeding SimSC-iBOT across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD4Match-CPM (75.5% PCK on SPair-71k) outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every category. Against the strongest prior method SimSC-iBOT [30] (63.5%), it gains 12 percentage points, a substantial margin in a task where typical improvements are 1–3 points. Among zero-shot SD-based methods, SD4Match-Single (72.6%) already surpasses SD+DINO [55] (59.3%) by 13.3 points and DIFT [50] (52.9%) by 19.7 points, demonstrating that prompt tuning alone unlocks significant latent capacity. Compared to zero-shot ViT-based extractors DINOv2 [41] (53.9%) and DINO [3] (35.9%), the improvement is even more pronounced (21.6 and 39.6 points respectively). Among keypoint-supervised methods, SD4Match-CPM beats SCorrSAN [18] (55.3%) by 20.2 points, Trans.Mat. [23] (53.7%) by 21.8 points, and CATs [4] (49.9%) by 25.6 points. Image-level annotation methods (NCNet 20.1%, SFNet 26.3%, PMD 26.5%) are far behind, confirming that keypoint-level supervision remains essential for competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of SD-based semantic matching initiated by DIFT and SD+DINO, which demonstrated that Stable Diffusion's UNet can serve as a feature extractor for correspondence. The key departure is the introduction of prompt tuning: rather than using fixed textual templates or implicit captioners as prompts, the authors directly optimize prompt embeddings in latent space, and further propose a conditional prompting module (CPM) that conditions the prompt on local DINOv2 features of the image pair rather than global descriptors of individual images. The CPM design draws on COCOOP-style conditional prompting and the implicit captioner from open-vocabulary segmentation, but restructures them around the pair-level, local-feature rationale specific to matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12-percentage-point improvement on SPair-71k over the previous SOTA (SimSC-iBOT) is substantial for a task that has seen incremental gains for years, and the finding that a single learned universal prompt already yields a 37% relative improvement over DIFT suggests that prior SD-based matchers were significantly underutilizing the model's capacity. The CPM's pair-level, local-feature conditioning is a design principle that could transfer to other pair-wise perception tasks. A noted limitation is that prompts tuned on smaller datasets (PF-Pascal) overfit and lose generalizability on SPair-71k, pointing to an open question about data efficiency and domain coverage for learned prompts. Whether prompt tuning on diffusion models generalizes beyond semantic matching to broader visual perception tasks remains to be explored.
