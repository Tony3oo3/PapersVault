## Problem

Existing CAD reconstruction methods from point clouds either handle simple parametric primitives well but cannot recover freeform surfaces, or can fit freeform surfaces but fail to assemble them into complete boundary representations with consistent topology. Geometrically complex freeform surfaces and topologically complex assemblies of simple surfaces have largely been studied in isolation, leaving no unified pipeline that recovers a full B-rep CAD model from a raw scan.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242126/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud (no mesh required) and a pretrained segmentation backbone (e.g., ParSeNet or HPNet) that partitions points into surface clusters. It outputs a complete B-rep CAD model comprising analytical surface primitives (planes, spheres, cylinders, cones) and INR-parameterized freeform surfaces, pairwise-intersection edges, corner points, and adjacency matrices encoding face-edge-corner topology. The INR surfaces are extended 10% beyond their supporting points to provide margin for robust intersection computation.

## Method

Point2CAD is a modular pipeline that uses learning only for point cloud segmentation, then relies on test-time optimization and classical geometry for all subsequent stages. The key novelty is a 1-layer MLP autoencoder with a 2D uv bottleneck for freeform surface fitting, where a single hidden layer mixes SiLU and sinusoidal activations: SiLU neurons provide smooth extrapolation beyond the data while sinusoidal neurons capture high-frequency geometric detail, and this complementary behavior emerges without explicit coordination mechanisms. Each surface is fitted independently via 1000 steps of Adam on the autoencoder loss, taking only seconds on a single GPU. Topology is then recovered deterministically by converting all surfaces to triangle meshes, computing pairwise mesh intersections for edges, and intersecting adjacent edge polylines for corners, followed by clipping to remove unsupported regions. The authors argue that this hybrid analytic-neural design outperforms end-to-end learned approaches because it guarantees geometric-topological consistency by construction.

## Results

On the ABC dataset, Point2CAD with ParseNet segmentation achieves a residual error of 0.018 and Chamfer distance of 0.017 for full CAD reconstruction, outperforming ComplexGen (Res-err 0.020, Chamfer 0.042). For the core freeform surface fitting task, the proposed INR achieves a residual error of 0.002 (open) and 0.003 (closed) with P-coverage of 0.999/0.998, substantially beating ComplexGen (0.021/0.023, 0.938/0.900) and ParseNet (0.006/0.008, 0.930/0.902). With ground-truth segmentation, Point2CAD reaches Res-err 0.011 and Chamfer 0.016, indicating the pipeline's upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ComplexGen, Point2CAD (with ParseNet segmentation) wins on residual error (0.018 vs 0.020) and Chamfer distance (0.017 vs 0.042, a 2.5× improvement), while slightly trailing on P-coverage (0.942 vs 0.950) due to the deliberate surface clipping step that removes spurious margins. For the freeform surface fitting sub-task (Table 1), Point2CAD's INR dramatically outperforms ComplexGen on both residual error (0.002 vs 0.021 for open surfaces, ~10× lower) and P-coverage (0.999 vs 0.938). Against ParseNet, Point2CAD's INR also wins clearly on residual error (0.002 vs 0.006) and P-coverage (0.999 vs 0.930) for open surfaces, confirming that the neural fitting stage is the key differentiator. In the full topology evaluation (Table 5), Point2CAD with HPNet achieves the highest surface F-score (0.788 at θ=0.08) and edge F-score (0.611 at θ=0.05) among learned-segmentation variants, while the GT-segmentation variant reaches 0.947 surface F-score, far exceeding ComplexGen's 0.731.

## Relation to Prior Work

Point2CAD departs from the end-to-end learning paradigm of ComplexGen by decomposing CAD reconstruction into a modular pipeline: pretrained neural segmentation (ParSeNet, HPNet), test-time-optimized surface fitting, and classical geometric intersection for topology. The freeform surface fitting draws on implicit neural representation research (SIREN, positional encoding, SiLU) and the autoencoder framework of Kramer (1991), but introduces a novel mixed-activation design (SiLU + sinusoidal in the same layer) specifically to balance data fidelity with smooth extrapolation. The paper explicitly positions itself against the trend of replacing well-understood analytical operations with data-driven feedforward models, arguing that learning is most valuable for the ill-posed segmentation step rather than for surface fitting or topology recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design means Point2CAD can be immediately upgraded as better segmentation backbones emerge, and the INR fitting scheme is a reusable component for any pipeline requiring parametric freeform surfaces with controlled extrapolation. The main limitation is sensitivity to segmentation quality—outliers in cluster assignment propagate into surface fitting and topology errors. An open direction the authors identify is using the INR fitting in a feedback loop to drive point cloud partitioning, which would remove the dependence on external segmentation. More broadly, the paper's empirical argument that classical geometric operations outperform learned topology prediction (edges must lie on surfaces, corners must be consistent) is a useful counterpoint to the field's push toward fully end-to-end CAD generation.
