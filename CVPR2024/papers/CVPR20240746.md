## Problem

Semi-supervised medical image segmentation typically assumes labeled and unlabeled data share the same distribution, while unsupervised domain adaptation requires abundant labeled source data. In practice, clinicians often have only a small number of annotated images from one center alongside large volumes of unlabeled images from multiple centers with different imaging protocols. This paper introduces the Mixed Domain Semi-supervised Segmentation (MiDSS) setting, where both limited annotation and multi-domain shift coexist, and shows that neither SSMS nor UDA methods alone can handle this scenario effectively.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D medical image slices (256×256 for Fundus, 384×384 for Prostate, 288×288 for M&Ms) with a small set of labeled samples from a single domain and a larger pool of unlabeled samples from multiple domains. It produces pixel-wise segmentation masks (binary for Prostate, two-class for Fundus optic cup/disc, three-class for M&Ms LV/MYO/RV). At inference, only the student U-Net is used, so the model has the same parameter count and computational cost as a standard U-Net.

## Method

Built on the FixMatch mean-teacher framework with a U-Net backbone, the method constructs intermediate domains between labeled and unlabeled data via Unified Copy-Paste (UCP), which simultaneously applies both embedding directions (labeled→unlabeled and unlabeled→labeled) to generate diverse intermediate samples with known pseudo-labels. A Symmetric Guidance (SymGD) strategy then trains the student from two perspectives: guiding intermediate sample predictions with merged pseudo-labels, and guiding unlabeled sample predictions with pseudo-labels derived by merging the unlabeled regions of intermediate predictions, with a confidence-weighted XOR consistency mask. To address stylistic domain differences that local semantic mixing cannot capture, a Training Process aware Random Amplitude MixUp (TP-RAM) module progressively blends low-frequency FFT amplitude components from unlabeled data into labeled data, with the mixing ratio Φ(t)=t/t_total increasing linearly over training to ensure stable intermediate domain construction. The total loss combines supervised cross-entropy/Dice on labeled data with weighted consistency losses on intermediate and symmetric-guidance branches, where the symmetric loss weight grows via a time-dependent Gaussian warm-up.

## Results

On the Prostate dataset (40 labeled samples), the method achieves 87.98% average Dice coefficient, a 13.57% improvement over the best prior method (FixMatch at 74.41%), and is within 0.34% of the supervised upper bound (88.32%). On Fundus (20 labeled), it reaches 87.66% average DC vs. 83.39% for FixMatch and 83.05% for BCP. On M&Ms (20 labeled), it achieves 84.31% average DC, outperforming FixMatch (82.96%) and BCP (71.65%), with HD of 5.15 and ASD of 2.42.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 11 baselines across all three datasets. The most relevant SSMS baseline, FixMatch, is surpassed by 13.57% average DC on Prostate (87.98 vs. 74.41), 4.27% on Fundus (87.66 vs. 83.39), and 1.35% on M&Ms (84.31 vs. 82.96). BCP, the closest CP-based competitor, is beaten by 23.17% on Prostate (87.98 vs. 64.81) and 12.66% on M&Ms (84.31 vs. 71.65), though the gap on Fundus is smaller (87.66 vs. 83.05). UDA methods (FDA, SIFA, UDA-VAE++) all underperform, with UDA-VAE++ reaching only 66.54% DC on Prostate, confirming that domain adaptation alone cannot compensate for limited labels. Several SSMS methods (CPS, SS-Net, CauSSL, UA-MT) suffer severe error accumulation under domain shift, dropping below even the supervised U-Net baseline on Prostate. The method approaches the supervised upper bound within 0.34% DC on Prostate and 0.80% on Fundus, indicating near-optimal utilization of the available labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FixMatch mean-teacher framework for semi-supervised segmentation and extends the Copy-Paste (CutMix) data augmentation technique into a unified bidirectional form (UCP) that simultaneously applies both embedding directions between labeled and unlabeled samples, going beyond BCP's random single-direction selection. The TP-RAM module repurposes frequency-domain MixUp (amplitude interpolation via FFT) to introduce gradual stylistic transitions into intermediate samples, addressing a gap that local semantic mixing alone cannot fill. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of semi-supervised learning and unsupervised domain adaptation, arguing that neither paradigm alone suffices when both label scarcity and multi-domain shift coexist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MiDSS formulation captures a realistic clinical deployment scenario—multiple imaging centers with limited annotations at one site and abundant unlabeled data elsewhere—that prior SSMS and UDA methods do not jointly address. The finding that standard SSMS methods can degrade below supervised-only baselines under domain shift (due to error accumulation in pseudo-labels) highlights a practical risk that practitioners may underestimate. Open questions include whether the intermediate-domain construction strategy generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation, whether the approach extends to more than two unlabeled domains with very large distributional gaps, and whether the training complexity (dual models, multiple loss terms, FFT operations) remains tractable at scale.
