## Problem

Existing few-shot object detection (FSOD) methods rely on ImageNet-pretrained backbones and elaborate human-designed metric-learning networks (GCNs, cross-transformers, feature alignment modules) to classify noisy proposals, yet still achieve inferior accuracy compared to data-abundant detectors. Although recent works have begun incorporating foundation models like CLIP or DINOv2, they still design complex few-shot classification heads and do not comprehensively study how to leverage the in-context learning capabilities of large language models for proposal classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240156/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query RGB image and an N-way K-shot support set (K cropped bounding-box images per novel class) as input. The frozen DINOv2 ViT extracts patch-level features from the query image, while support features are pre-computed via RoIAlign and averaged into per-class prototypes. The LLM receives a single structured instruction containing all 300 proposal features (projected to token space) and all class prototypes in a fixed order, and outputs one ordered class token per proposal; final detections are obtained by fusing these class assignments with the Deformable DETR bounding box predictions.

## Method

FM-FSOD combines three submodules: (1) a frozen DINOv2 ViT backbone for patch-level visual features, (2) a two-stage Deformable DETR with an additional cross-attention layer between class prototypes and encoder features to generate support-class-aware proposals, and (3) a Vicuna-7B LLM that performs contextualized few-shot proposal classification via in-context learning. The key design choice is to feed all 300 proposals and the full category-to-prototype mapping table into a single LLM instruction, so the model automatically exploits proposal-proposal, proposal-class, and class-class relations without any hand-crafted metric-learning network. The LLM is fine-tuned with meta-learning (N-way K-shot episodes over base classes) using next-token prediction loss, and the backbone remains frozen throughout, enabling pre-computation of support features and scaling to many classes. Ablations confirm that the Transformer-based detection head compensates for the frozen backbone, and that the contextualized LLM classification outperforms per-proposal or per-class LLM inference while being ~30× faster.

## Results

On MSCOCO (novel classes only), FM-FSOD with ViT-L achieves 27.7 AP at 10-shot and 37.0 AP at 30-shot, outperforming the previous best DETR-based method Meta-DETR by 8.7 and 14.8 AP points respectively. On the generalized few-shot setting (both base and novel classes), FM-FSOD (ViT-L) reaches 40.0 AP at 10-shot and 43.1 AP at 30-shot, surpassing DE-VIT by over 9 AP points on both settings. On PASCAL VOC, FM-FSOD (ViT-L) achieves 72.0 AP50 at 10-shot (Novel Set 1), matching or exceeding all prior methods across the three novel splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FM-FSOD substantially outperforms Faster R-CNN-based methods: it beats FCT [17] by 10.6 AP at 10-shot and 15.6 AP at 30-shot, and DiGeo [36] by 17.4 AP at 10-shot and 22.8 AP at 30-shot. Against DETR-based competitors, FM-FSOD surpasses Meta-DETR [66] by 8.7 AP at 10-shot and 14.8 AP at 30-shot, and FS-DETR [3] by 16.4 AP at 10-shot; however, FS-DETR retains a small edge on 1-shot and 2-shot AP50 (13.6 vs. 7.8 and 17.5 vs. 15.1), which the authors attribute to the LLM being harder to tune with very few training examples. Against the closest competitor DE-VIT [69] (also using DINOv2), FM-FSOD wins on 30-shot novel-only AP (37.0 vs. 34.0) but trails on 10-shot novel-only AP (27.7 vs. 34.0). Crucially, on the generalized few-shot setting (Table 2), FM-FSOD dominates DE-VIT by over 9 AP on both 10-shot (40.0 vs. 30.6) and 30-shot (43.1 vs. 30.6), because DE-VIT's shared binary classifier sacrifices base-class accuracy. On PASCAL VOC, FM-FSOD (ViT-L) matches or exceeds FCT and Meta Faster R-CNN across all three novel splits at 5- and 10-shot, with the largest margin at 10-shot Novel Set 1 (72.0 vs. 67.1 AP50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant FSOD paradigm of ImageNet-pretrained backbones paired with hand-designed metric-learning networks (e.g., FCT, Meta Faster R-CNN, QA-FewDet) by replacing both components with frozen foundation models: DINOv2 for visual features and a Vicuna LLM for in-context proposal classification. It builds directly on Deformable DETR as the detection backbone and on the in-context learning capabilities of LLMs demonstrated in NLP, but repurposes the LLM not for bounding-box generation (as in Shikra, Pix2Seq, or VisionLLM) but solely for ordered proposal classification with a visual lookup table. Unlike concurrent foundation-model FSOD efforts like DE-VIT and MM-FSOD, which still require heavily engineered few-shot classification heads, this work argues that the LLM's contextual reasoning over all proposals and classes simultaneously obviates the need for such bespoke architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that freezing DINOv2 and pairing it with a Transformer-based detection head (Deformable DETR) eliminates the need for backbone fine-tuning, enabling pre-computed support features and broader class coverage in a single forward pass — a significant simplification over prior FSOD pipelines. The LLM-based classification module reduces architectural complexity while automatically exploiting proposal-proposal, proposal-class, and class-class relations, though it underperforms at 1-shot and 2-shot settings where the LLM struggles with limited training data. Open questions include whether incorporating external detection datasets or more efficient LLM training strategies (e.g., LoRA, distillation) can close the low-shot gap, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two standard FSOD benchmarks to truly open-world or long-tail detection scenarios.
