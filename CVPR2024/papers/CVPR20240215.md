## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for VLMs (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe) are designed for natural images and lack domain-specific knowledge, leading to significant performance degradation on specialized domains like remote sensing and medical imaging. While large-scale domain-specific foundation models (e.g., ViTAE, MedSAM) offer strong domain representations, they operate only at the vision level and cannot natively support joint vision-language contrastive prompting. This paper bridges the gap by transferring domain-specific knowledge from these foundation models into the VLM's prompt learning framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes domain-specific RGB images (remote sensing scenes or medical scans) and a text template 'a photo of [category]' for each class name, processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone. Domain-specific vision features are additionally extracted from a separate foundation model (ViTAE/ViT for remote sensing, MedSAM for medical) and fed into quaternion layers as guidance. Training uses 16-shot samples per class with a batch size of 4, and the output is a softmax-normalized class probability computed from cosine similarity between the final vision embedding and per-class language embeddings, scaled by a learnable temperature.

## Method

The core mechanism places domain-specific vision features (from the foundation model) and learnable context embeddings (from the language branch) on two orthogonal axes of a quaternion hidden space, where a quaternion network extracts cross-modal domain knowledge and projects the generalized context into the specialized domain. Random Gaussian noise, scaled by the mean of the domain features, is injected into the language-branch quaternion space to counteract overfitting from limited few-shot data. Leveraging the stable vision-language alignment in pre-trained CLIP, the method then hierarchically forwards domain-specific information from the language branch to the vision branch: at each of k=9 prompt layers, a quaternion layer combines the current language prompt feature with the domain vision features to produce a vision prompt feature. Both branches are prompted simultaneously, enabling domain-specific vision-language contrastive learning that minimizes the domain gap. The domain-project layer (Linear-ReLU-Linear) maps raw foundation-model features into the quaternion-compatible space before the orthogonal decomposition.

## Results

On the average of 8 remote sensing datasets (base-to-novel generalization, 16-shot), the proposed method with ViT backbone achieves 97.90% base accuracy, 73.30% novel accuracy, and 83.83% harmonic mean (HM), surpassing the previous best (MaPLe) by 3.41% in HM. On medical image classification (3 datasets), the method reaches 74.36% base accuracy and 53.36% HM, a 4.35% HM improvement over MaPLe. In cross-dataset generalization (MLRSNet → 7 targets), the method achieves 73.83% average accuracy, outperforming MaPLe by 1.41%. The largest single-dataset gain is on RSICD, where HM improves from 70.38% (MaPLe) to 77.29% (+6.91%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against CLIP (zero-shot, no prompt tuning), the improvement is dramatic: HM rises from 70.63% to 83.83% on remote sensing and from 45.18% to 53.36% on medical images. Compared to CoOp (78.03% HM) and CoCoOp (78.43% HM), the method gains 5.8% and 5.4% HM respectively on remote sensing, primarily by simultaneously prompting both vision and language branches with domain-specific quaternion-guided features. The closest competitor is MaPLe (80.42% HM on remote sensing, 49.01% on medical), which the method surpasses by 3.41% and 4.35% HM respectively. The only notable exception is on the MLRSNet dataset specifically, where the gain over MaPLe is modest (70.34% vs. 70.15% HM), attributed to limited domain-specific knowledge in the foundation model for that particular scene type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt learning lineage (CoOp → CoCoOp → MaPLe) by addressing a gap those methods leave open: adaptation to specialized domains like remote sensing and medical imaging. The key architectural departure is the introduction of quaternion networks (originally from Parcollet et al. for recurrent architectures) as a mechanism to model orthogonal cross-modal relationships between domain-specific vision features (extracted from foundation models like ViTAE or MedSAM) and generalized language contextual embeddings. Rather than treating domain adaptation as a separate fine-tuning problem, the paper repurposes the quaternion hidden space as a structured interface where domain knowledge is injected into the prompt learning pipeline, then hierarchically propagated from the language branch to the vision branch via the pre-trained VLM's established vision-language alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled recipe for transferring VLM recognition ability into specialized domains without full fine-tuning, which is practically important for fields where domain-specific foundation models exist but vision-language contrastive frameworks do not. However, the improvements are modest (0.19% HM on MLRSNet, ~3% average on remote sensing), and the method's dependence on a pre-trained domain-specific foundation model limits its applicability to domains where such models are available. Open questions include whether the quaternion formulation is truly necessary (the ablation shows meaningful but not decisive gains over a non-quaternion variant), whether deeper domain-specific backbones (ViTAE) can be better exploited, and why domain-specific medical models improve base accuracy but not novel-category generalization.
