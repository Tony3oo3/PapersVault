## Problem

Class-agnostic motion prediction from LiDAR point clouds typically requires expensive manual annotations, and existing annotation-efficient methods either depend on weak semantic labels or additional image data. The paper identifies that self-supervised learning from coarse matching-based pseudo labels produces spatially inconsistent predictions within rigid objects and temporally inconsistent predictions across future timestamps, a failure mode that prior self-supervised approaches have not addressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of 5 consecutive LiDAR sweeps (current + 4 past, at 0.2 s intervals) cropped to a 64 m × 64 m × 5 m region and voxelized at 0.25 m × 0.25 m × 0.4 m resolution. The 3D voxel grid is collapsed into a 2D BEV pseudo-image (Z-axis becomes the channel dimension), and the network predicts a 2D displacement vector per BEV cell for each of 5 future timestamps (1 s horizon). Ground points are removed via Patchwork++ before pseudo-label matching, and their motion labels are forced to zero.

## Method

The approach uses MotionNet as a model-agnostic backbone and generates pseudo motion labels by solving an optimal transport problem (Sinkhorn algorithm) between current and future BEV cells, with a pre-warping step that iteratively improves label quality as predictions sharpen. To counter the spatial and temporal inconsistencies inherent in these noisy pseudo labels, three regularization losses are introduced: (1) a cluster consistency loss that enforces uniform motion within BFS-clustered groups of nearby BEV cells, (2) a forward consistency loss that smooths predictions across adjacent future timestamps via a scaled smooth-L1 penalty, and (3) a backward consistency loss that feeds a reversed frame sequence through the same network and penalizes the divergence between forward and backward predictions, weighted by an exponential decay in time horizon. The backward loss acts as an implicit confidence signal: cells with large forward-backward divergence (indicating unreliable pseudo labels) receive stronger penalty, while consistently predicted cells are barely affected.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, the proposed self-supervised method (LiDAR only) achieves mean L2 errors of 0.0419 m (static), 0.3213 m (slow, ≤5 m/s), and 2.2943 m (fast, ≥5 m/s). These results substantially outperform the previous best self-supervised method PillarMotion (which additionally uses images) by 74.1%, 53.9%, and 35.4% at static, slow, and fast speed levels respectively. On the Waymo dataset, the method achieves 0.0515 m, 0.4440 m, and 2.3692 m mean errors at the three speed levels, again surpassing self-supervised WeakMotionNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest self-supervised baseline PillarMotion (which uses both LiDAR and images), the proposed method wins by large margins: 0.0419 vs 0.1620 m (static), 0.3213 vs 0.6972 m (slow), and 2.2943 vs 3.5504 m (fast), while using only LiDAR. Against self-supervised WeakMotionNet (LiDAR only), the gap is even wider: 0.0419 vs 0.1919 m (static) and 2.2943 vs 2.9715 m (fast). Compared to fully-supervised MotionNet, the method narrows the gap to 0.0419 vs 0.0256 m (static) and 0.3213 vs 0.2565 m (slow), though a notable gap remains at fast speeds (2.2943 vs 1.0744 m). The method also outperforms scene flow estimation baselines (FlowNet3D, HPLFlowNet, PointPWC, RigidFlow) at both slow and fast speed levels, and surpasses weakly-supervised WeakMotionNet at static and slow levels. On Waymo, the method (0.0515/0.4440/2.3692 m) outperforms self-supervised WeakMotionNet (0.1345/0.5833/3.0180 m) across all speed levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MotionNet as the backbone for class-agnostic motion prediction, but departs from prior self-supervised approaches (PillarMotion, which requires paired LiDAR+Image data, and WeakMotionNet, which needs foreground/background masks) by training exclusively from unlabeled LiDAR sequences. The pseudo-label generation via Sinkhorn optimal transport borrows from self-supervised scene flow estimation (PointPWC, RigidFlow), but the three proposed spatial-temporal consistency regularizations—cluster, forward, and backward—are novel to the motion prediction setting and exploit the temporal structure of the input sequence rather than cross-modal cues. The work positions itself as closing the gap between weakly-supervised and fully-supervised methods without any annotation or multi-modal dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: removing both annotation requirements and the need for synchronized camera data simplifies the data pipeline for autonomous driving systems and makes self-supervised motion prediction more deployable. However, a non-trivial gap with fully-supervised methods persists (e.g., ~3× error at fast speed), and the BFS clustering step introduces ~10% mis-clustering that limits spatial consistency. Open questions include whether instance-aware clustering or learned correspondences could further close the gap, and whether the approach extends to higher-frequency or multi-sensor LiDAR configurations.
