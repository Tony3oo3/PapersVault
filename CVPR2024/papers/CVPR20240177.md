## Problem

Blind Spot Denoising (BSD) and related self-supervised single-image denoising methods assume independent and identically distributed (iid) noise, yet real-world sensor noise is often spatially correlated, which significantly degrades denoising performance. No prior work systematically characterizes how the masking ratio in BSD interacts with noise correlation, nor provides a mechanism to automatically adapt to unknown noise correlation levels in a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240177/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (e.g., 256×256×3 patches from SIDD, 512×512×3 from FMDD/PolyU) with no clean reference or supporting dataset. It trains a denoising CNN from scratch at test time and outputs a denoised RGB image computed as the average of K=10 predictions, each generated by applying a different random Bernoulli mask at the selected optimal masking ratio to the input.

## Method

MASH extends BSD with two key mechanisms. First, it introduces adaptive masking: by training with both a high (τ=0.8) and low (τ=0.2) masking ratio and computing the noise-level estimation gap ε between them, it automatically selects the optimal masking ratio (low, medium, or high) based on the detected noise correlation level. Second, when high correlation is detected, it applies Local Pixel Shuffling (LPS): an intermediate denoised prediction serves as a pseudo-clean image to identify flat regions (where local 4×4 tile standard deviation falls below a threshold λ), and pixels within those tiles are randomly permuted to break spatial noise correlation before being used as the training target. The denoising network (same architecture as Noise2Noise) is trained with a masked reconstruction loss where the target is either the original noisy image (low correlation) or the locally shuffled image (high correlation), and the final output is an ensemble of K masked predictions.

## Results

MASH achieves 35.06 dB PSNR / 0.851 SSIM on SIDD Validation, 34.78 / 0.900 on SIDD Benchmark, 33.71 / 0.882 on FMDD, and 37.62 / 0.932 on PolyU. Compared to the fixed-masking BSD baseline (τ=0.5), MASH improves by approximately 2 dB on both SIDD splits and 1.5 dB on FMDD. Among single-image test-time training methods, MASH attains the best PSNR on SIDD Validation, SIDD Benchmark, and FMDD, while ScoreDVI retains a slight edge on PolyU (37.77 vs. 37.62 dB). The adaptive masking scheme selects the correct masking ratio in ~90% of cases (88.7% SIDD, 92.4% FMDD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASH outperforms all single-image test-time training baselines on SIDD Validation (35.06 vs. 34.75 for ScoreDVI, 33.97 for PD-denoising, 32.11 for DIP) and SIDD Benchmark (34.78 vs. 34.60 for ScoreDVI, 33.61 for PD-denoising). On FMDD, MASH leads by a clear margin (33.71 vs. 33.10 for ScoreDVI, 33.01 for PD-denoising, 30.76 for Self2Self), and also surpasses dataset-based methods APBSN (31.99) and CVF-SID (32.73). On PolyU, ScoreDVI edges out MASH slightly (37.77 vs. 37.62 dB), while NN+denoiser is comparable (37.66). Against the fixed-masking BSD baseline, MASH gains ~2 dB on SIDD and ~1.5 dB on FMDD, confirming the value of adaptive masking and local pixel shuffling. MASH remains competitive with dataset-based methods (CVF-SID, LUD-VAE) on SIDD while requiring no external training data, and its inference time (75.3 s) is substantially lower than Self2Self (3546.5 s) and NN+denoiser (897.6 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

MASH extends the Blind Spot Denoising (BSD) framework of Krull et al. and the generalized Bernoulli masking formulation of Self2Self by introducing two key departures: an adaptive masking ratio that is automatically selected based on estimated noise correlation, and a local pixel shuffling technique that directly decorrelates noise in flat image regions. While prior BSD variants (Noise2Void, Self2Self, APBSN) treat the masking ratio as a fixed hyperparameter or use pixel-shuffle downsampling as a proxy for decorrelation, MASH explicitly models the interaction between masking level and spatial noise correlation through systematic empirical analysis. The method remains within the test-time training paradigm, training a CNN from scratch on a single noisy image, but adds a two-phase training procedure where the first phase estimates noise correlation and the second phase applies shuffling-aware loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most self-supervised denoising methods implicitly assume iid noise, yet real-world sensor noise is frequently spatially correlated, degrading performance. The adaptive masking mechanism provides a lightweight, data-driven way to handle unknown noise characteristics without requiring a noise model, which is valuable for single-image settings where no supporting dataset is available. Limitations include the test-time training cost (~75s per 256×256 image), the reliance on a pseudo-clean intermediate estimate for the shuffling partition (which can be inaccurate in early training), and the fact that the method still trails supervised approaches by ~2.5 dB. Open questions include whether the shuffling strategy generalizes to structured sensor-specific noise patterns (e.g., PRNU, fixed-pattern noise) and whether the correlation estimation gap ε remains reliable under very low or very high noise levels.
