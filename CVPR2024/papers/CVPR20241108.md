## Problem

Cross-Domain Few-Shot Segmentation (CD-FSS) requires segmenting novel categories in a target domain using only a few annotated exemplars, but meta-learned FSS models suffer severe performance drops when the target domain differs from the source domain due to domain-biased category correspondence. Naive fine-tuning on the target domain fails to close this gap because the extreme scarcity of novel-category examples causes overfitting, leaving a critical unresolved tension between domain adaptation and data efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a support image with its binary mask and a query image, both resized to 400×400, processed through a ResNet-50 backbone (last stage and ReLU discarded) pre-trained on ImageNet. During target-domain fine-tuning, the query image and its label are not independently available but are derived from the support image via paired augmentations (horizontal/vertical flip, 90° rotation, brightness and hue variation), ensuring spatial correspondence between support and query regions. The output is a binary segmentation mask for the query image, predicted via cosine similarity between query features and a support prototype generated by masked average pooling.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage fine-tuning strategy built on the Self-Support Prototype (SSP) module. First, Bi-directional Few-shot Prediction (BFP) introduces a Support-to-Query-to-Support (S2Q2S) stream alongside the standard S2Q stream, using the support ground-truth mask as additional supervision to establish richer category correspondence and reduce overfitting risk. Second, Iterative Few-shot Adaptor (IFA) recursively applies BFP for T iterations during target-domain fine-tuning, where each iteration generates new prototype predictions and corresponding BCE losses on both support and query sides, progressively mining support-query correspondence from the same sparse exemplars. The total loss combines base S2Q losses, S2Q2S losses, and per-iteration losses with fixed weights (λ_bs=0.2, λ_bq=1.0, λ_s'=0.4, λ_i=0.1), and at test time only the direct S2Q prediction is used since the support prototype is assumed representative after sufficient adaptation.

## Results

On the primary CD-FSS benchmark (Pascal VOC 2012 → four target domains), IFA (T=3) achieves an average mIoU of 67.8% (1-shot) and 71.4% (5-shot), surpassing the previous best method SSP by +7.8% (1-shot) and +3.4% (5-shot). The largest gains appear on ISIC (+17.7% 1-shot, +4.4% 5-shot over SSP) and Deepglobe (+9.3% 1-shot). On the DS-FSS benchmark (COCO-20i → Pascal-5i), IFA reaches 71.0% mIoU (1-shot, Res-50) and 79.6% (1-shot, Res-101), outperforming Meta-Memory by +5.4% and +13.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IFA (T=3) outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every target domain and shot setting. Against the strongest prior method SSP [13] (reproduced by the authors), IFA gains +9.3% mIoU on Deepglobe (1-shot), +17.7% on ISIC (1-shot), +1.4% on Chest X-Ray (1-shot), and +3.1% on FSS-1000 (1-shot). On the DS-FSS benchmark (COCO-20i → Pascal-5i), IFA surpasses Meta-Memory [54] by +5.4% (Res-50, 1-shot) and +13.0% (Res-101, 1-shot), and beats HSNet [40] by +9.5% (Res-50) and +16.4% (Res-101). IFA also substantially outperforms the zero-shot SAM [29] on medical imaging: 66.3% vs. 36.1% mIoU on ISIC and 74.0% vs. 27.8% on Chest X-Ray, despite using far fewer parameters and training data. Among fine-tuning strategies (Table 3), IFA (50.6/66.3) clearly dominates TAFT [33] (43.2/64.3) and CPFT [14] (44.6/54.5) on Deepglobe and ISIC respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SSP (Fan et al., ECCV 2022) for its prototype-based prediction mechanism and on PATNet (Lei et al., ECCV 2022) for the CD-FSS task formulation and episode-based training protocol. It departs from prior CD-FSS work by identifying that the standard fine-tuning stage is both necessary and prone to overfitting, then proposing a bidirectional prediction strategy (S2Q + S2Q2S) that creates augmented supervision from the single available support mask. The iterative extension (IFA) further distinguishes this work by recursively re-mining support-query correspondence across multiple passes, a design not present in prior prototype- or affinity-based FSS methods, which the authors argue are both ill-suited to the cross-domain, data-scarce setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated fine-tuning recipe for CD-FSS that addresses the core overfitting bottleneck, with consistent gains across four diverse target domains (satellite, medical, everyday objects). The finding that a simple iterative bidirectional prediction loop outperforms more elaborate adaptation strategies (TAFT, CPFT) and even zero-shot SAM on medical tasks suggests that careful exploitation of the few available labels can substitute for model scale. Open questions include whether the iterative scheme generalizes beyond prototype-based backbones, whether the augmentation-based query derivation introduces domain-specific biases, and whether the approach extends to other few-shot tasks (detection, classification) with similar data scarcity.
