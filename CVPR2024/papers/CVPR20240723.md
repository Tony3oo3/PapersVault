## Problem

Vehicle re-identification research has focused almost exclusively on day-to-day matching under favorable lighting, leaving the day-night cross-domain scenario unaddressed. Nighttime vehicle images suffer from headlight glare, low-light degradation, and domain discrepancies that existing single-domain or visible-infrared cross-modality methods do not handle, and no dedicated benchmark or method existed for this problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240723/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired daytime and nighttime RGB vehicle images (resized to 256×256) as input, with an additional pre-computed binary glare mask (derived from a brightness threshold of 220 on the grayscale nighttime image) fed into the NGS module as a prompt channel. It produces 2048-dimensional feature vectors per image, which are used for cross-domain retrieval ranking (Rank-k and mAP). During training, the CCA module exchanges class activation maps between day and night samples of the same identity, but this exchange is disabled at test time.

## Method

The DNDM framework builds on a ResNet-50 backbone and inserts three specialized modules: (1) a Night-domain Glare Suppression (NGS) module that concatenates the initial feature tensor with a binary glare mask, projects through a learnable vector to produce a suppression mask, and subtracts the masked feature to attenuate headlight-affected regions; (2) a Dual-domain Structure Enhancement (DSE) module that computes non-negative pixel-wise gradients in 5×5 local windows, aggregates neighbor features via gradient-weighted pooling to form structural descriptors, and adds them back to appearance features; (3) a Cross-domain Class Awareness (CCA) module that projects features to the class dimension, computes class activation maps, and exchanges them between day and night domains (training only) to align discriminative regions across domains. DSE and CCA are applied at all four ResNet-50 stages for multi-scale modulation. The network is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss plus a cross-domain triplet loss where positive pairs span day-night domains, using an ImageNet-pretrained backbone and Adam optimizer with warmup.

## Results

On the DN-348 dataset, DNDM achieves 70.7% Rank-1 and 47.5% mAP for Day-to-Night retrieval, and 80.3% Rank-1 and 46.2% mAP for Night-to-Day retrieval, outperforming all VI-ReID baselines by 2.6–7.2% in Rank-1. On the larger DN-Wild dataset, DNDM reaches 51.2% Rank-1 and 40.5% mAP (Day-to-Night) and 49.5% Rank-1 and 40.0% mAP (Night-to-Day), surpassing the strongest ReID baseline BOT by 0.9% and 2.4% in Rank-1 respectively. Compared to the paper's own baseline (ResNet-50 + cross-entropy + triplet loss), DNDM improves Rank-1 by 8.0% (Day-to-Night) and 8.0% (Night-to-Day) on DN-348.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNDM outperforms all seven baselines across both datasets and both retrieval directions. On DN-348 Day-to-Night, the closest competitor is AGW (Rank-1: 68.1%, mAP: 46.5%), which DNDM surpasses by 2.6% in Rank-1 and 1.0% in mAP; PMT leads in mAP among baselines (47.0%) but trails DNDM by 0.5%. On DN-348 Night-to-Day, DCL achieves the highest baseline Rank-1 (78.9%) yet DNDM still leads by 1.4%, while PMT has the best baseline mAP (46.1%) but is beaten by 0.1%. On DN-Wild, BOT is the strongest baseline in both directions (Rank-1: 50.3% D→N, 47.1% N→D), and DNDM improves upon it by 0.9% and 2.4% respectively. Notably, PMT, which performs competitively on the balanced DN-348, drops significantly on the imbalanced DN-Wild (Rank-1: 49.1% D→N, 44.4% N→D), indicating that domain-gap modeling alone is insufficient under severe sample imbalance. LbA and DDAG show the weakest performance overall, with LbA dropping to 26.3% mAP on DN-Wild Day-to-Night, suggesting poor generalization to large-scale, imbalanced settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining the Day-Night cross-domain vehicle ReID (DN-ReID) problem, which sits between standard day-to-day vehicle ReID and visible-infrared cross-modality person ReID (VI-ReID). Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes concepts from visual prompting (for glare suppression), class activation maps (for cross-domain class awareness), and local gradient-based structure learning, integrating them into a unified DNDM framework on a ResNet-50 backbone. The authors explicitly note that thermal-infrared cameras are impractical for vehicle ReID due to cost, resolution, and light interference, motivating the day-night visible-domain formulation as a practical alternative to the VI-ReID paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first formal treatment of DN-ReID, this paper provides two new benchmark datasets (DN-Wild with 2,286 identities and DN-348 with balanced day-night samples) that fill a clear gap in the vehicle ReID literature, where prior benchmarks had 0% day-night identity overlap. The practical relevance is high: nighttime vehicle surveillance is a core requirement for intelligent transportation and urban security, yet prior methods were never designed for it. Key limitations include the reliance on a fixed brightness threshold (220) for glare detection, which may not generalize across camera systems, and the fact that DN-Wild inherits sample imbalance from VERI-Wild 2.0. Open questions include label-free DN-ReID, whether the approach extends to other cross-domain settings (e.g., seasonal, weather), and whether more sophisticated glare estimation could replace the threshold-based mask.
