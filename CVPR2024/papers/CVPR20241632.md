## Problem

Current video inpainting methods rely on manually annotated binary masks to specify restoration regions, a process that is tedious, labor-intensive, and impractical for long videos. This paper introduces language-driven video inpainting, where natural language instructions (simple referring expressions or chat-style conversations) replace pixel-level masks to guide the inpainting process, and provides the first dataset and end-to-end baseline for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241632/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 24-frame video clip at 512×320 resolution along with either a simple referring expression (e.g., 'the woman on the left') or a multi-turn chat-style conversation as the language input. The original video is encoded via a pre-trained VAE and concatenated channel-wise with the noised target latent as a control signal. For the interactive variant (LGVI-I), the MLLM additionally receives an image frame and the full conversation, producing both a natural-language response and a set of prompt tokens that condition the diffusion U-Net.

## Method

LGVI inflates a Stable Diffusion U-Net to video by transposing the batch dimension to treat frames as a batch and inserting a parameter-efficient Temporal Attention module (complexity O(CT²)) between cross-attention and FFN layers to enforce temporal consistency. The original video latent is concatenated with the noised target latent in the channel dimension, and a mask decoder head provides explicit spatial supervision on the inpainting region. For the interactive task, LGVI-I integrates LLaVA-7B as an MLLM that processes the image frame and chat conversation, generating a language response and extracting hidden states between special <PROMPT> tokens via an MM head; these vision-aware prompt tokens are fed into the U-Net cross-attention. The full system is trained end-to-end with a combined objective of diffusion noise-prediction loss, mask cross-entropy loss, and language modeling loss, with the U-Net initialized from MagicBrush weights.

## Results

On the referring video inpainting task (Table 2), LGVI achieves PSNR 22.85, SSIM 0.756, VFID 0.308, and E*warp 0.901, outperforming MagicBrush on all metrics and matching Inpaint Anything* (PSNR 22.84) while being a one-stage model. On the interactive video inpainting task (Table 3), the MLLM-enhanced LGVI-I achieves the best overall performance with PSNR 22.24, SSIM 0.732, VFID 0.299, and E*warp 0.867, surpassing the strongest two-stage baseline IA*+MLLM (PSNR 21.37, E*warp 0.875) by +0.87 PSNR and +0.008 E*warp. The end-to-end MLLM integration yields a consistent +1.54 PSNR improvement over the non-interactive LGVI baseline on the harder interactive task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGVI outperforms all three image-based baselines (InstructPix2Pix, Inst-Inpaint, MagicBrush) on every metric for both referring and interactive video inpainting, confirming that frame-level image editing models cannot handle temporal coherence. Against the multi-stage Inpaint Anything* baseline, LGVI matches PSNR (22.85 vs. 22.84) and SSIM (0.756 vs. 0.728) on the referring task while being a single-stage model, though Inpaint Anything* edges ahead on VFID (0.283 vs. 0.308) and E*warp (0.874 vs. 0.901). On the interactive task, LGVI-I clearly wins over all baselines: it beats the strongest two-stage MLLM-enhanced baseline IA*+MLLM by +0.87 PSNR and +0.008 E*warp, and surpasses the zero-shot LGVI+MLLM combination by +0.79 PSNR, demonstrating that end-to-end MLLM integration is more effective than simply prompting an MLLM to convert chat into a short referring expression. The qualitative results further show that Inpaint Anything* fails on multi-object and nonexistent-object references due to its fixed one-mask-per-sentence design, while LGVI handles these cases correctly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) to video via temporal inflation techniques from Make-A-Video (Singer et al., 2022) and Tune-A-Video (Kondratyuk et al., 2023), adding a lightweight Temporal Attention module for inter-frame consistency. It departs from the dominant mask-based video inpainting paradigm (E2FGVI, ProPainter) by replacing binary masks with natural language instructions, and from general-purpose image editing models (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush) by specializing for the inpainting objective with explicit mask supervision. The interactive sub-task further integrates LLaVA (Liu et al., 2023) as an end-to-end language controller, a combination not previously applied to low-level video editing tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first dataset (ROVI) and end-to-end baseline for language-driven video inpainting, this work provides a concrete benchmark that the field can build upon, reducing the labor-intensive mask annotation bottleneck in video inpainting applications. The interactive sub-task—where users issue implicit, conversational requests—opens a path toward more natural human-AI interaction for video editing. However, the dataset is relatively small (5,650 videos, 24 frames at 512×320), ground truth is synthesized by a single inpainting model (E2FGVI) rather than human-verified, and the paper itself flags scalability and generalization as open challenges, leaving questions about performance on longer, higher-resolution, or more diverse video content.
