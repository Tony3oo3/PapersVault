## Problem

Existing deep-learning-based infrared small target detection (IRSTD) methods focus on designing complex model architectures for feature extraction, while the loss functions remain under-explored. Widely used IoU and Dice losses are insensitive to target scales and locations—targets with different sizes or positions can yield identical loss values—making it difficult for detectors to distinguish and precisely localize targets of varying characteristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242689/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 single-frame infrared images as input and produces a pixel-level binary detection mask of the same spatial resolution. Internally, the multi-scale head generates four intermediate predictions at progressively coarser resolutions (1/16, 1/8, 1/4, and 1/1 of input size), which are upsampled and concatenated through a final convolution to yield the full-resolution output. During training, the SLS loss is applied independently to each of the four scale-specific predictions as well as the final concatenated prediction.

## Method

The paper proposes a Scale and Location Sensitive (SLS) loss composed of two components: a scale-sensitive term that reweights the standard IoU loss by a factor w derived from the variance of predicted and ground-truth pixel counts (penalizing larger scale mismatches more heavily), and a location-sensitive term that measures the discrepancy between predicted and ground-truth target center points in polar coordinates (distance ratio plus squared angular difference), which distinguishes more location error patterns than L1/L2 distances. This loss is paired with MSHNet, a plain U-Net augmented only with a lightweight multi-scale head that attaches a dedicated 1×1 convolution + sigmoid to each of the four decoder feature maps, producing scale-specific predictions. The final prediction is obtained by upsampling and concatenating all four scale predictions through a shared convolution. Training applies SLS loss to all five predictions (four scale-specific plus the final one), so that targets at different scales receive differentiated supervision signals, enabling the simple architecture to achieve state-of-the-art detection performance with fewer FLOPs and lower inference time than complex-structure competitors.

## Results

On IRSTD-1k, MSHNet achieves 67.16% IoU, 93.88% Pd, and 15.03×10⁻⁶ Fa, outperforming the previous best deep-learning method DNANet (65.71% IoU, 91.84% Pd, 17.61×10⁻⁶ Fa) by 1.45% IoU and 2.04% Pd. On NUDT-SIRST, MSHNet reaches 80.55% IoU, 97.99% Pd, and 11.77×10⁻⁶ Fa, surpassing DNANet (79.98% IoU, 96.93% Pd, 12.78×10⁻⁶ Fa) by 0.57% IoU and 1.06% Pd. MSHNet also achieves the lowest inference time and among the lowest FLOPs among all evaluated deep-learning methods, demonstrating a better performance–efficiency trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSHNet outperforms all 13 baselines on both datasets across all three metrics (IoU, Pd, Fa). Against the strongest prior deep-learning method DNANet [14], MSHNet gains +1.45% IoU and +2.04% Pd on IRSTD-1k while reducing Fa from 17.61 to 15.03 (×10⁻⁶); on NUDT-SIRST the margins are +0.57% IoU, +1.06% Pd, and Fa drops from 12.78 to 11.77. Compared to ISNet [30], the gap is larger: +4.28% IoU and +1.29% Pd on IRSTD-1k, and +12.69% IoU and +5.40% Pd on NUDT-SIRST, with Fa reduced from 27.92 to 15.03 and from 34.65 to 11.77 respectively. Against ALCNet [4], MSHNet improves IoU by +1.48% (IRSTD-1k) and +7.66% (NUDT-SIRST) while cutting Fa substantially. All traditional methods (filtering, local-contrast, low-rank) are far behind, with the best traditional method (IPI [8]) reaching only 27.92% IoU on IRSTD-1k versus MSHNet's 67.16%. The paper also shows that applying the SLS loss to DNANet and ISNet boosts their IoU by 1.38% and 1.54% respectively on IRSTD-1k, confirming the loss's generalizability beyond MSHNet's architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant trend in deep-learning-based IRSTD of designing increasingly complex architectures (dense nested attention in DNANet, Taylor finite difference in ISNet) by arguing that the loss function, not the model structure, is the under-explored bottleneck. It extends the standard IoU loss with a scale-dependent weight and introduces a polar-coordinate-based location penalty, drawing on ideas from GIoU/CIoU for bounding-box regression but adapting them to pixel-level segmentation. The multi-scale head is a straightforward addition to a plain U-Net, deliberately avoiding the architectural complexity that prior SOTA methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple U-Net with a well-designed loss can match or exceed complex architectures while using fewer FLOPs and less inference time, offering a practical efficiency win for IRSTD practitioners. The SLS loss's generalizability—improving DNANet and ISNet when swapped in—suggests it could be adopted broadly across IRSTD pipelines. However, the acknowledged trade-off of increased false alarms (Fa) from the location-sensitive component remains an open problem, and the performance margins over strong baselines (1.45% IoU on IRSTD-1k) are modest, leaving room for whether the conceptual framing (loss > architecture) generalizes beyond this specific task.
