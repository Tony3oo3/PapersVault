## Problem

3D motion generation has been dominated by methods requiring expensive motion-capture datasets, leaving entire domains (professional basketball, horse jumping, rhythmic gymnastics with objects) underexplored due to 3D data scarcity. While 2D pose estimates from in-the-wild videos are abundant, existing lifting approaches produce jittery or invalid poses, and score-distillation methods suffer from slow convergence, mode collapse, and out-of-distribution sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242502/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MAS takes random 3D Gaussian noise (ε_3d ∈ R^{L×J×3}) as input, projects it orthographically to V=5 fixed camera views to initialize 2D noisy sequences, and outputs a 3D motion sequence X ∈ R^{L×J×3} (joint positions over L frames). The 2D diffusion prior is trained on pose estimates extracted from videos using off-the-shelf trackers (AlphaPose for humans, APT-36K for horses, COCO+ball for gymnastics), with variable-length sequences and per-joint confidence masking. A full 3D sample is generated in under 10 seconds on a single RTX 2080 Ti using 100 diffusion steps.

## Method

MAS extends ancestral sampling to simultaneously denoise V 2D views of the same 3D motion, enforcing multi-view consistency at every step via a Consistency Block that triangulates predicted 2D motions into a single 3D sequence (minimizing reprojection error) and projects it back to each view. A key design choice is sampling 3D noise and projecting it orthographically to each view, preserving the Gaussian distribution while maintaining inter-view correlation—this prevents mode collapse and significantly improves diversity. The 2D diffusion model is an unconditioned MDM with a 6-layer transformer encoder trained on 2D pose sequences from in-the-wild videos. Unlike SDS-based methods that iteratively optimize a 3D representation with small corrections, MAS follows the standard denoising loop, keeping all views at the same timestep and applying full triangulation at each step, which avoids out-of-distribution sampling and timestep-scheduling heuristics.

## Results

On the NBA dataset, MAS achieves a FID of 5.38, closely matching the 2D diffusion model upper bound (FID 5.23) while substantially outperforming ElePose (FID 10.76) and MotionBERT (FID 30.22). MAS also leads in Precision (0.50 vs. 0.28 for ElePose and 0.04 for MotionBERT) and Recall (0.60 vs. 0.58 and 0.34). Under side-view evaluation, lifting methods degrade sharply (ElePose FID jumps to 18.28, MotionBERT to 36.89) while MAS remains unaffected, confirming its advantage as a generative rather than lifting-based approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAS clearly outperforms both pose-lifting baselines on all four metrics. Against ElePose (unsupervised lifting), MAS reduces FID from 10.76 to 5.38 and more than doubles Precision (0.50 vs. 0.28); ElePose also suffers a severe Recall drop from 0.58 to 0.17 under side-view evaluation, while MAS remains stable. Against MotionBERT (supervised lifting, applied zero-shot), the gap is even larger: MAS achieves FID 5.38 vs. 30.22 and Precision 0.50 vs. 0.04. Compared to the DreamFusion/SDS adaptation (Table 3), MAS reduces FID from 66.38 to 5.38 and Recall from 0.17 to 0.60, demonstrating that the ancestral-sampling formulation avoids the mode-collapse and out-of-distribution issues inherent to score distillation. MAS also slightly exceeds the 2D diffusion model's Precision (0.50 vs. 0.44) while matching its FID, suggesting the multi-view consistency constraint adds a mild regularizing effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAS builds directly on the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) architecture as its 2D diffusion backbone, but departs fundamentally from the SDS-based text-to-3D paradigm (DreamFusion, SJC) by replacing iterative optimization with a multi-view ancestral sampling loop. Where SDS methods render a 3D representation, noise it, and back-propagate corrections from a 2D model, MAS instead denoises V correlated 2D views simultaneously and enforces 3D consistency through triangulation at each step. This also distinguishes MAS from pose lifting methods (ElePose, MotionBERT), which require a specific 2D input and produce a single 3D estimate, whereas MAS performs unconditional generation from pure noise. The multi-view consistency mechanism echoes ideas from MVDream and related 3D-consistent diffusion work, but is applied here in a sampling (rather than optimization) context and to articulated motion rather than static geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAS opens 3D motion generation to domains where motion capture is impractical or nonexistent (horses, professional basketball, rhythmic gymnastics with object interaction), demonstrated by three newly collected in-the-wild datasets. The ancestral-sampling formulation avoids well-known SDS pathologies (mode collapse, out-of-distribution sampling, slow convergence) by design, and the 3D noise ablation reveals that multi-view-consistent noise is critical—its removal causes complete mode collapse (recall drops to ~0.01). Key limitations remain: the method cannot predict global position, lacks text conditioning, occasionally produces self-intersecting or scale-drifting characters, and requires 2D data with diverse viewpoints. Extending the pipeline to multi-person interactions, hand/face motions, and arbitrary object tracking is identified as promising future work.
