## Problem

Existing text-to-texture methods face a trade-off between quality and efficiency: SDS-based approaches (e.g., DreamFusion, Latent-Paint) require hours of optimization and produce over-saturated, blurred textures, while inpainting-based methods (e.g., Text2Tex, TEXTure) are sensitive to predefined view ordering and introduce artifacts at mask boundaries. The paper seeks a zero-shot approach that generates clean, naturally colored textures in minutes while maintaining both global style and low-level geometric consistency across multiple viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241715/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (UV-parameterized via xatlas) and a text prompt as input, and produces a single UV-space texture map as output. During sampling, the mesh is rendered from N sparse viewpoints to obtain depth maps that condition ControlNet, and the texture map is maintained in the latent space of Stable Diffusion's autoencoder. Because memory limits the number of concurrent viewpoints, a denser set of views is used in post-processing (inpainting and img2img) to fill unobserved regions and refine details.

## Method

The core contribution is texture space sampling: instead of denoising rendered images and projecting gradients back (as in SDS), the method maintains a set of N latent texture maps and iteratively renders them to image space for noise prediction, then inverse-renders the predicted clean latent back to texture space at each DDIM step. Multi-view consistency is enforced through two mechanisms: (1) style consistency, achieved by converting self-attention to cross-view attention and group normalization to 3D (across all views) in the U-Net and decoder, and (2) dynamic alignment, which blends each viewpoint's latent texture with a softmax-weighted uniform latent using a time-dependent signal c(t) that is strong mid-denoising and weak at the extremes. After sampling, reference-based inpainting fills blank regions using a denser view set, and an img2img pass with color histogram matching repairs residual artifacts and enhances detail.

## Results

GenesisTex achieves a FID of 74.58 and KID of 2.89×10⁻³ on the 35-mesh evaluation set, substantially outperforming the best baseline (TEXTure: FID 100.47, KID 9.22×10⁻³). In the user study (350 comparison groups), GenesisTex is preferred for visual quality 50.00% of the time and for prompt alignment 46.28% of the time, roughly double the next-best method (TEXTure at 23.42% and 24.86%). Texture space sampling alone takes approximately 3 minutes per object on a single NVIDIA A10 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenesisTex outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor TEXTure (FID 100.47, KID 9.22), GenesisTex reduces FID by ~26 points and KID by ~6.3×10⁻³, and nearly doubles the user-study preference rates. The SDS-based methods (Latent-Paint, Text2Mesh) lag furthest behind, with FID scores above 110 and user-study preference below 5%, reflecting their known over-saturation and blurriness issues. Inpainting-based methods (Text2Tex, TEXTure) perform better than SDS approaches but still trail GenesisTex by a wide margin, primarily due to boundary artifacts and sensitivity to view ordering. TexFusion, compared qualitatively only (no public code), produces blurry details from repeated noise addition during sequential sampling, whereas GenesisTex's concurrent multi-view denoising avoids this degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenesisTex sits at the intersection of two lines of work: SDS-based text-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, Latent-Paint) that iteratively optimize a 3D representation using 2D diffusion gradients, and inpainting-based multi-view texture synthesis (Text2Tex, TEXTure, TexFusion) that sequentially fill in views. It departs from both by reformulating the problem as concurrent denoising in a latent texture space, adapting Stable Diffusion's U-Net and DDIM sampler to operate on per-viewpoint latent texture maps rather than rendered images. The multi-view consistency mechanisms—cross-view self-attention and 3D group normalization—borrow from video diffusion and multi-view diffusion models (MVDream, SyncDreamer) but are applied here without any additional training, and the dynamic alignment with a time-varying consistency signal is a novel practical contribution specific to the texture-space setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: generating a full texture map in ~3 minutes with zero additional training makes text-to-texture generation viable for interactive or production pipelines, a substantial improvement over the hours required by SDS methods. The concurrent multi-view formulation avoids the sequential-ordering sensitivity and boundary artifacts inherent to inpainting-based approaches. The primary open limitation is the memory cost of cross-view attention, which caps the number of sampling viewpoints and necessitates post-processing (inpainting, img2img); the authors suggest hierarchical style consistency as a path forward. Broader implications include whether the texture-space sampling paradigm can be extended to other 3D representations (SDFs, NeRFs) or to video texture generation.
