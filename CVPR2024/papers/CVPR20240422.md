## Problem

Generating diverse, high-quality 3D objects from single images on large multi-category datasets like ImageNet remains an ill-posed problem: without multi-view supervision or domain-specific alignment (camera poses, key points, cropping), naive triplane training suffers from volume collapse, producing disconnected semi-transparent clouds that explain the input view but yield blurry or skewed novel views. Existing 3D generative methods either require curated multi-view data, per-scene optimization, or incur massive computational costs, leaving a gap for efficient, scalable single-view 3D generation on unaligned datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning latent (class label, text prompt, or single RGB image) and produces a 3D triplane representation renderable from arbitrary viewpoints. A latent diffusion model first generates a 128×128 RGBD image from the conditioning signal, which is then fed to the triplane generator; pseudo-depth from an off-the-shelf monocular estimator provides geometric supervision without requiring ground-truth depth. The final output is upscaled to 256×256 via a super-resolution network, and the model generalizes across modalities (class, text, image) and even out-of-domain content like cartoons without retraining.

## Method

G3DR combines a latent diffusion model for RGBD image generation with a U-net triplane generator (64 channels) rendered via small 2-layer MLPs, but its core contribution is a depth regularization kernel k(x, t_i) that rescales the gradients of NeRF density and color during backpropagation based on the distance between each sampled point and the estimated surface, preventing volume collapse by encouraging high density near the surface while suppressing it elsewhere. For novel-view supervision, the method replaces adversarial training with a CLIP feature-matching loss that enforces semantic consistency between rendered novel views and the ground-truth image, yielding more stable convergence. A multi-resolution triplane sampling strategy resamples the same triplane at three decreasing resolutions and aggregates features via summation, improving texture quality without adding parameters. Training alternates between canonical-view losses (reconstruction, depth L1, VGG perceptual) and novel-view losses (CLIP, TV on depth, VGG perceptual) under a probabilistic schedule that gradually increases novel-view sampling probability.

## Results

On ImageNet 256², G3DR achieves FID 13.1 and Inception Score 151.7, outperforming the previous best 3D method 3DGP (FID 19.7, IS 124.8) by 22% in FID and 21.5% in IS. On geometry, G3DR reaches NFS 36.5 and depth accuracy 0.39, nearly doubling the NFS of 3DGP (18.5) and IVID (19.2) while achieving the best depth accuracy. On fine-grained datasets (Dogs, Horses, Elephants), G3DR achieves the best NFS scores (36.89, 36.2, 35.6) while remaining competitive in FID, and trains in 14.5 A100 days—half the 28 A100 days required by 3DGP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 3D methods, G3DR outperforms all baselines on both FID and IS. The closest competitor is VQ3D (FID 16.8), which G3DR beats by 3.7 FID points; 3DGP (FID 19.7, IS 124.8) is surpassed by 22% in FID and 21.5% in IS. IVID (FID 14.1 at 128×) is outperformed by G3DR even at the lower resolution (G3DR at 128× achieves FID 13.0, IS 136.4). On geometry, G3DR's NFS of 36.5 nearly doubles 3DGP (18.5) and IVID (19.2), and its depth accuracy of 0.39 is substantially better than 3DGP (0.47) and IVID (1.23). On fine-grained datasets, G3DR achieves the best NFS across all three (Dogs 36.89, Horses 36.2, Elephants 35.6) while matching or slightly trailing 3DGP in FID on Horses (5.64 vs 4.86) and Elephants (5.30 vs 5.79). The 2D baselines (BigGAN, StyleGAN-XL, ADM) achieve lower FID scores but are not directly comparable since they produce no 3D geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

G3DR sits in the lineage of 3D generation from single views on large multi-category datasets, directly extending the problem formulation of 3DGP (ICLR 2023) but replacing its adversarial training and knowledge distillation with a CLIP-based novel-view loss and a novel depth regularization kernel that rescales NeRF volumetric rendering gradients. The triplane representation is inherited from EG3D, but G3DR removes the requirement for per-image camera transformations by using a fixed canonical camera. The RGBD input images are generated by a pretrained LDM3D latent diffusion model, and the multi-resolution triplane sampling strategy draws on prior work on level-of-detail rendering, though it resamples a single triplane rather than learning separate latents at each resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The depth regularization kernel is a simple, theoretically compatible gradient-scaling technique that could be adopted by other NeRF-based reconstruction and generation pipelines to prevent volume collapse without multi-view supervision. The CLIP-based novel-view loss offers a stable, adversarial-training-free alternative that may generalize to other single-view 3D tasks. Limitations include reliance on pseudo-ground-truth depth from a monocular estimator, fixed camera parameters, and moderate resolution (128×128 upscaled to 256×256); open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or more extreme novel-view angles, and whether the depth regularization transfers to other implicit representations beyond triplanes.
