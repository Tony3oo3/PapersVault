## Problem

Generating adversarial attacks for multi-label learning (MLL) models is challenging because attacking a target label set often flips unrelated labels, producing semantically inconsistent predictions that trivially reveal the attack. Naively adding a loss to preserve non-target labels introduces highly negatively correlated gradients that counteract the attack, and even fixing all non-target labels does not guarantee semantic consistency (e.g., turning off 'vehicle' without also turning off 'car'). This paper addresses both the semantic consistency and gradient conflict problems for targeted MLL attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a user-specified target label set Ω, and a directed acyclic knowledge graph G built from WordNet hypernym/hyponym relations (using maximum WUP similarity to select a single parent per node). It outputs an l_p-bounded adversarial perturbation e, the resulting adversarial image, and a consistent target set Ψ — the minimal superset of Ω whose modification yields semantically consistent MLL predictions. The knowledge graph scales to 9,600 labels (OpenImages) and can be replaced by other sources such as ConceptNet or the OpenImages class hierarchy.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, an efficient O(|Ω|·|C|) search algorithm (Algorithm 1) traverses the knowledge graph from each target node toward leaves and toward the root, expanding the target set to include children that lack alternative active parents and parents that lack alternative active children, yielding the minimal consistent target set Ψ. Second, rather than optimizing a sum of target and non-target losses (which produces opposing gradients), the paper formulates the non-target preservation as a first-order linear constraint g_{x,Ψ̄}ᵀe = 0 and projects the target-set gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the non-target gradient via P = I − ggᵀ/‖g‖². The resulting projected gradient is solved iteratively in the AutoPGD style, yielding a closed-form l_∞ update e = ε·Pν/‖Pν‖_∞ where ν = sgn(g_{x,Ψ}). An additional direction is used when target and non-target gradients are positively correlated, combining normalized gradients to find a compromise direction.

## Results

On PASCAL-VOC with ε=0.01, GMLA achieves near-perfect naive fooling rate (FR_N ≈ 99–100%) across all three MLL models while dramatically outperforming baselines on semantic-based fooling rate (FR_S): 99.4% vs. 75.5% (MLA-U) and 68.9% (MLA-C) on ML-GCN, 98.8% vs. 52.8% and 39.7% on ASL, and 96.2% vs. 66.2% and 50.6% on ML-Decoder for |Ω|=1. Non-target flip rate (NTR) remains low (2.2–2.7%), comparable to MLA-C and well below MLA-U (4.6–5.3%). On OpenImages with 9,600 labels, GMLA maintains reasonable NTR (0.06–0.32) as target set size grows to 5, while MLA-C's fooling rate collapses to 0% for |Ω|≥3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MLA-U [71], GMLA wins decisively on FR_S across all models and datasets (e.g., 99.4% vs. 75.5% on ML-GCN, 98.8% vs. 52.8% on ASL for PASCAL-VOC |Ω|=1), while also reducing NTR from 4.6–5.3% to 2.2–2.7%. Against MLA-C [71], GMLA again dominates FR_S (99.4% vs. 68.9%, 98.8% vs. 39.7%, 96.2% vs. 50.6%) with comparable NTR, confirming that the projected-gradient formulation resolves the negative gradient correlation that cripples the sum-of-losses approach. Against MLA-LP [95], GMLA is overwhelmingly superior in attack success (FR_N of 99–100% vs. 16–56%) while MLA-LP's LP formulation fails to converge on OpenImages entirely. On the large-scale OpenImages setting (Table 2), GMLA maintains NTR of 0.06–0.32 as target set size increases to 5, whereas MLA-C's fooling rate drops to 0% for |Ω|≥3, and MLA-U's NTR grows to 0.87 for |Ω|=5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-label adversarial attack framework of Song et al. [71] (MLA-U and MLA-C) by addressing two key limitations: semantic inconsistency in predictions and the negative gradient correlation between target and non-target losses. The optimization follows the iterative linearized scheme of AutoPGD [17], but introduces a projected gradient step that constrains the perturbation to the orthogonal complement of the non-target loss gradient rather than summing both losses in the objective. The consistent target set construction via a WordNet-based knowledge graph departs from prior MLL attacks that treat labels as independent, incorporating hierarchical label dependencies (hypernym/hyponym) to ensure semantically coherent outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical vulnerability in deployed multi-label systems (autonomous driving, surveillance) where semantically inconsistent predictions after an attack could serve as a simple detection signal, undermining the attack's stealth. The finding that MLA-C fails entirely on large-scale datasets (OpenImages, 9600 labels) for target sets larger than 2 labels underscores that naive constraint-based approaches do not scale. Open questions include whether the knowledge graph construction (currently WordNet-based) can be replaced with learned label relationships, whether the approach extends to black-box settings, and whether the semantic consistency guarantee holds under model retraining or defense mechanisms.
