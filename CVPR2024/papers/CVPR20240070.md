## Problem

Existing image vectorization methods store a single color per closed shape, making them fundamentally unable to capture fine texture details within regions. Raster images, while texture-rich, tightly couple geometry and texture in pixel values, making decoupled editing difficult. The paper addresses how to build a compact vectorized representation that simultaneously encodes shape geometry and local texture in a decoupled, editable form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240070/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB raster image (H×W×3) and produces a parameterized representation consisting of n closed Bezier shapes (each with m=12 control points), a d-dimensional latent texture code (d=16) attached to every control point, and a shared lightweight MLP (a single linear layer mapping 18-dim input to 3-dim RGB). The number of shapes is task-dependent (n=5 for Emoji/Icon, n=128 for Clipart), and the representation is resolution-agnostic: the same parameters can be rasterized at arbitrary resolutions by querying the MLP at different coordinate densities.

## Method

The core idea is to distribute latent texture codes onto Bezier shape control points rather than fixed spatial grids, so that texture resources are allocated adaptively to geometrically complex regions. At any query point, a shape-aware inverse-distance-weighting interpolation retrieves a local latent code from the enclosing shape's control points, and overlapping shapes are fused via IDW-Fusion; the latent code is concatenated with the 2D coordinate and fed through a single linear-layer MLP to predict RGB. For geometry optimization, the paper introduces a geometric field (a probability map derived from Canny edge points) and a chamfer distance loss that directly constrain control-point coordinates, avoiding the gradient sparsity problem of differentiable rasterization. The entire representation is optimized in a zero-shot, self-supervised manner per image by minimizing pixel MSE plus the geometric field and chamfer losses, requiring no training dataset or image-to-vector supervision.

## Results

On the Clipart dataset (n=128 shapes), the proposed method achieves MSE 0.0009, LPIPS 0.2492, and SSIM 0.8803, outperforming DiffVG (MSE 0.0089) and LIVE (MSE 0.0090) by roughly 10× in pixel error. On Emoji and Icon datasets with only 5 shapes, the method reaches MSE 0.0007 and 0.0009 respectively, compared to 0.0212/0.0573 for DiffVG and 0.0019/0.0026 for LIVE. Against implicit representation baselines (SIREN, LIIF-grid), the method achieves comparable or better reconstruction (MSE 0.0009 vs. 0.0010 for Grid-/4) while using only 1,536 latent codes versus 16,384, yielding a ~10× parameter compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines across three datasets. Against DiffVG [15] and LIVE [17] on the Clipart dataset, the proposed method achieves approximately 10× lower MSE (0.0009 vs. 0.0089/0.0090 at n=128) and substantially better perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.2492 vs. 0.3741/0.3424), demonstrating that texture-encapsulated shapes capture fine texture details that flat-color vectorization cannot. Against NPA [7] on Emoji/Icon (n=5), the method reduces MSE from 0.0049 to 0.0007 (Emoji) and from 0.0093 to 0.0009 (Icon), showing superior shape efficiency. Against SIREN [29], the method achieves better reconstruction (MSE 0.0009 vs. 0.0020 for SIREN-3) with far fewer parameters (27,702 vs. 49,920). Against the LIIF-style grid-based latent code approach [6], the method matches or exceeds reconstruction quality (MSE 0.0009 vs. 0.0010 for Grid-/4) while using only 1,536 latent codes versus 16,384, confirming the efficiency of shape-anchored distributed texture codes over fixed-grid storage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: differentiable vector graphics rasterization (DiffVG, Im2Vec, LIVE, NPA) and implicit neural image representations (SIREN, LIIF, Deep Image Prior). It departs from prior vectorization methods that assign a single flat color per Bezier shape by anchoring per-control-point latent texture codes to the geometric primitives, and departs from grid-based implicit representations (LIIF) by distributing those codes adaptively along shape boundaries rather than on a fixed spatial lattice. The geometric-field optimization strategy (Canny edges + chamfer distance) replaces the gradient-sparse differentiable-rasterization loop that plagues prior shape-fitting approaches, drawing on a simpler but more direct geometric supervision signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled shape-and-texture parameterization enables genuinely independent editing of geometry and appearance in vectorized artistic images, a capability that neither pure raster nor pure flat-color vector formats support. The shape-anchored latent code distribution achieves roughly 10× fewer parameters than grid-based implicit representations at comparable reconstruction quality, suggesting a content-adaptive allocation principle that could transfer to other 2D/3D representation tasks. Limitations include the per-image zero-shot optimization (no learned generalization across images), the very lightweight linear MLP that may cap expressiveness for highly complex textures, and evaluation confined to relatively small-scale artistic benchmarks (Emojis, Icons, 200 cliparts) rather than natural photographs.
