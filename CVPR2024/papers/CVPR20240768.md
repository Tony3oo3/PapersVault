## Problem

High-accuracy dichotomous image segmentation on high-resolution images requires simultaneously capturing global object context and fine-grained boundary details, but existing methods rely on multiple cascaded encoder-decoder streams or multi-resolution pyramids that introduce parameter redundancy, computational overhead, and optimization difficulty. The paper addresses this by reframing DIS as a multi-view object perception problem, inspired by how the human visual system captures regions of interest from complementary distant and close-up perspectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240768/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution RGB image (2K–4K or larger, resized to 1024×1024) and decomposes it into a low-resolution global view (512×512, simulating a distant view) and four non-overlapping close-up patches (512×512 each, simulating close-up views), which are processed jointly through a single encoder-decoder stream. The output is a full-resolution binary segmentation mask, reconstructed by a view rearrangement module that merges the four patch predictions with the global prediction and applies a three-layer convolutional head to smooth patch-boundary misalignments.

## Method

MVANet is a single-stream, single-stage encoder-decoder using a Swin-B backbone that processes the multi-view patch sequence in parallel. The Multi-view Complementary Localization Module (MCLM) applies multi-granularity average pooling (receptive fields 4, 8, 16) to generate multi-sensory tokens as K/V, while global tokens serve as Q in cross-attention to enhance object localization; the updated global tokens are then rearranged per-patch and used as K/V to guide local tokens via a second cross-attention, establishing long-range interactions across views. The Multi-view Complementary Refinement Module (MCRM) is embedded at every decoder stage, where a token attention map (1×1 conv + sigmoid) filters background noise from local features, and local tokens attend to multi-sensory global tokens to inject fine-grained details into the global representation. A simple view rearrangement module with a three-layer convolutional head merges the four aligned patch features back into the global feature to produce the final high-resolution prediction. Training uses BCE + weighted IoU loss with multi-level supervision at each decoder output, and the model achieves 4.6 FPS on an RTX 3090, roughly twice the speed of the prior best method.

## Results

On the DIS-5K benchmark (Overall), MVANet (Swin-B) achieves F_β^max = 0.916, F_β^ω = 0.855, E_φ^m = 0.938, S_m = 0.905, and MAE = 0.035, outperforming the second-best method InSPyReNet (Swin-B) by 2.5%, 2.1%, 0.5%, and 0.4% on F_β^max, E_φ^m, S_m, and MAE respectively. MVANet also achieves 4.6 FPS inference speed on an RTX 3090, more than 2× faster than InSPyReNet (2.2 FPS), while maintaining a single-stream, single-stage architecture. The gains are consistent across all four test subsets (DIS-TE1 through DIS-TE4) with increasing shape complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVANet outperforms all 11 baselines across every metric on all four DIS-TE subsets. Among CNN-based methods, the strongest are FP-DIS and UDUN (both R-50), which achieve F_β^max ≈ 0.831 and MAE ≈ 0.057; MVANet surpasses them by roughly 8.5% in F_β^max and 39% in MAE reduction. The transformer-based PGNet (Swin-B + ResNet-50) reaches F_β^max = 0.809 but is still 10.7% below MVANet. The closest competitor is InSPyReNet (Swin-B, ACCV 2022), which uses a multi-resolution pyramid blending strategy; MVANet leads it by 2.5% in F_β^max (0.916 vs. 0.891), 2.1% in E_φ^m (0.938 vs. 0.917), 0.5% in S_m, and 0.4% in MAE (0.035 vs. 0.039), while running at 4.6 FPS versus 2.2 FPS — more than 2× faster. Notably, MVANet achieves these gains with a single-stream, single-stage encoder-decoder, whereas InSPyReNet and PGNet rely on multi-scale or multi-stream architectures that increase parameter count and inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-stream, multi-stage paradigm in dichotomous image segmentation (IS-Net, UDUN) and the multi-resolution pyramid blending approach of transformer-based methods (InSPyReNet, PGNet), which both rely on cascading encoder-decoder branches to reconcile global context with local detail. Instead, it repurposes the multi-view learning concept—previously applied to 3D shape recognition and reconstruction—by splitting a single HR image into a downscaled 'distant view' and non-overlapping 'close-up view' patches, then unifying them in a single encoder-decoder stream. The cross-attention mechanisms draw on the multi-granularity pooling idea from P2T to reduce attention cost, and the overall architecture is a single-stream, single-stage design that avoids the parameter redundancy and optimization difficulty of prior multi-branch DIS networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-stream, single-stage design that simultaneously improves accuracy and doubles inference speed over the prior best method is practically significant for real-time HR segmentation applications (autonomous driving, video processing). However, the approach is validated only on the DIS-5K benchmark with a single backbone (Swin-B), leaving open whether the multi-view patch-splitting strategy generalizes to other high-resolution segmentation tasks or scales to even larger resolutions. The finding that increasing patch count from 4 to 16 causes dramatic performance degradation suggests the method is sensitive to the global-local resolution balance, and the fixed 2×2 patch grid may limit applicability to non-square or extremely elongated objects.
