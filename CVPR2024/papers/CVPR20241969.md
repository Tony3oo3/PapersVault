## Problem

Existing input transformation-based adversarial attacks apply fixed or pre-computed augmentation strategies across all iterations, limiting the diversity of transformed inputs and capping adversarial transferability. The paper identifies that an optimal transformation trajectory exists per image and per iteration, but the search space grows exponentially (e.g., 5^10 for 5 operations over 10 iterations), making exhaustive enumeration infeasible. No prior method dynamically adapts which transformations to apply at each iteration in response to the evolving adversarial example.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241969/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign RGB image (224×224 from ImageNet validation), its ground-truth class label, and a surrogate classifier (e.g., ResNet-18, Inception-v4, ViT, Swin). At each of T=10 iterations, it samples L=10 transformation sequences, each composed of K=2 operations drawn from a pool of 10 transformation categories (crop, rotation, shuffle, scaling, mix-up, translation, etc., each with 10 parameter variants). The output is a single adversarial example with L∞ perturbation budget ε=16, produced without any additional trained network—only a scalar probability vector over the operation pool is maintained and updated online.

## Method

L2T formulates optimal transformation selection as a trajectory optimization problem and solves it with a policy-gradient-style reinforcement learning loop. At each iteration, it samples L transformation sequences from a categorical probability distribution p over M candidate operations, applies them to the current adversarial example, and computes the average gradient to update the perturbation via momentum-based MI-FGSM. After updating the adversarial example, it performs one step of gradient ascent on p to maximize the expected classification loss over the sampled transformations, effectively learning which operations are most effective at the current stage of the attack. This bi-level optimization (inner: adversarial example; outer: sampling policy) is solved with a one-step approximation, avoiding the need for any separately trained augmentation network. The key distinction from prior learned-transformation methods (AutoMA, ATTA, AITL) is that L2T requires no pre-trained generative or prediction model—only a lightweight probability vector updated per iteration—making it plug-and-play and more computationally efficient.

## Results

On ImageNet (1,000 validation images), L2T achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) that outperforms the strongest baseline by 22.9% across ten target models (ResNet-18/101, ResNeXt-50, DenseNet-121, Inception-v3/v4, ViT, PiT, Visformer, Swin). Against defense mechanisms using an ensemble surrogate, L2T reaches 47.9% ASR on adversarial training (AT), 98.5% on HGD, 87.2% on NRP, and 46.7% on randomized smoothing (RS), exceeding the best baseline (AITL) by 4.6% on RS. On real-world vision APIs, L2T outperforms the strongest baseline by 8.7% (Google Vision) and 12.6% (Azure AI), and achieves nearly 100% ASR on both GPT-4V and Gemini.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2T is compared against 12 baselines spanning fixed-transformation attacks (TIM, SIM, Admix, DEM, IDE, Mask, S²IM, BSR, SIA) and learned-transformation attacks (AutoMA, ATTA, AITL), all integrated with MI-FGSM. L2T consistently outperforms every baseline across all ten surrogate models, with a 22.9% average ASR improvement; even in the worst-case surrogate (Inception-v3), it beats the strongest baseline (S²IM) by 2.1%. Against the four defense mechanisms, L2T dominates all baselines, with the largest margin (4.6%) on the certified defense RS where AITL is the closest competitor. On vision-only APIs (Google Vision, Azure AI), L2T leads the strongest baseline by 8.7% and 12.6% respectively, and on foundation-model APIs (GPT-4V, Gemini) it reaches nearly 100% ASR, a clear separation from all baselines. The random-sampling ablation (Table 1) confirms that the learned policy is essential: random transformation selection yields 43–65% ASR versus 79–96% for L2T, a gap of up to 31.12%.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2T extends the input transformation-based adversarial attack lineage, which progressed from fixed augmentation schemes (DIM, TIM, Admix, SIA, BSR) to learned per-image transformation prediction (AutoMA, ATTA, AITL). The key departure is from AITL and AutoMA, which compute a single transformation strategy per image via a pre-trained network; L2T instead treats the transformation sequence across iterations as a trajectory and dynamically updates a sampling distribution over operation combinations at every step via a policy-gradient (bi-level optimization) update. This shifts the problem from 'predict the best augmentation once' to 'learn which combination of operations is optimal at each iteration,' explicitly accounting for the distribution shift between benign inputs and evolving adversarial examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (no additional trainable modules, integrates directly with MI-FGSM) makes L2T immediately deployable alongside existing attack pipelines, and the near-100% ASR on GPT-4V and Gemini underscores a practical AI-safety concern for foundation-model APIs. A notable limitation is that the per-iteration RL update adds L forward passes (L=10–20) per step, increasing computational cost relative to fixed-transformation baselines, and the approach has only been validated on ImageNet-scale classification. Open questions include whether the learned per-iteration dynamics generalize to video, multimodal, or detection tasks, and whether the policy can be distilled into a lightweight predictor to reduce overhead.
