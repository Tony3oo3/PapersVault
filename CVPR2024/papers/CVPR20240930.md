## Problem

In adversarial distillation, the pretrained robust teacher is trained against its own adversarial examples, but adversarial examples are parameter-dependent, so the teacher's robustness degrades against the student-targeted adversarial examples encountered during distillation. This distribution shift makes the teacher's guidance increasingly unreliable as the student trains, yet prior methods only mitigate the effect of bad guidance rather than improving the teacher's actual robustness against the student's attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240930/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) and outputs class labels, operating under an l∞ perturbation budget of ε=8/255. Unusually, the training loop simultaneously optimizes two networks (peer and student) using adversarial examples generated from the student via PGD, with the peer's predictions on natural images serving as soft targets in both the inner maximization and outer minimization steps.

## Method

PeerAiD replaces the fixed pretrained robust teacher with a peer network trained from scratch alongside the student, where the peer is adversarially trained on examples generated from the student (x_S*) rather than from itself. This specialization makes the peer highly robust against student-targeted attacks (69.19% on CIFAR-100) while having 0% robustness against its own attacks, yet it retains near-natural-level clean accuracy (75.63%), avoiding the robustness-accuracy trade-off that plagues pretrained robust teachers. The inner maximization uses KL divergence between the peer's prediction on natural images and the student's prediction on adversarial examples to generate x_S*, while the outer minimization jointly optimizes a peer loss (CE + KL distillation from student) and a student loss (CE + KL distillation from peer + a regularization term penalizing prediction discrepancy between natural and adversarial inputs). The peer's soft labels thus provide reliable, evolving guidance that tracks the student's changing vulnerability profile throughout training.

## Results

PeerAiD achieves the highest AutoAttack (AA) robust accuracy across all dataset–model combinations, improving over the best baseline by 0.73–1.66 percentage points (e.g., 52.57% vs. 50.12% for AKD² on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18; 21.67% vs. 20.01% on TinyImageNet ResNet-18). It also attains the best clean accuracy in most settings, surpassing the strongest adversarial-distillation baselines by up to 4.72 pp (55.19% vs. 50.47% on TinyImageNet ResNet-18). The gains are more pronounced for the smaller ResNet-18 architecture than for WideResNet34-10, which is favorable for the typical edge-deployment motivation of adversarial distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeerAiD outperforms all seven baselines on AutoAttack accuracy in every dataset–model setting, with the largest margin of +1.66 pp over AKD² on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18 (52.57 vs. 50.12) and +4.77 pp over TRADES on TinyImageNet ResNet-18 (21.67 vs. 16.90). Against the strongest adversarial-distillation baselines (AKD², RSLAD, IAD, CAT, AdaAD), PeerAiD consistently leads on AA by 0.7–2.5 pp while also achieving the highest clean accuracy in most configurations. The only setting where PeerAiD does not lead on PGD-20 is CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 (29.69 vs. RSLAD's 30.78 and CAT's 31.44), yet its AA score (27.33) still exceeds all baselines, indicating the PGD-20 gap is within gradient-based attack noise. Compared to pure adversarial training (PGD-AT, TRADES), PeerAiD gains 2.4–4.8 pp on AA and 3.5–6.3 pp on clean accuracy, demonstrating that the peer-tutoring distillation framework adds value beyond standalone adversarial training. The advantage is most pronounced for the smaller ResNet-18 architecture, aligning with the practical goal of boosting robustness on resource-constrained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PeerAiD extends the adversarial distillation framework (PGD-AT, TRADES, RSLAD, IAD, AdaAD) by replacing the fixed pretrained robust teacher with a simultaneously-trained peer network, drawing a conceptual parallel to online knowledge distillation and collaborative adversarial training (CAT). The key departure is from the widely-held assumption that a robust teacher's guidance remains reliable throughout student training; prior methods (IAD, AKD², MTARD) mitigate unreliable teacher guidance by down-weighting or blending predictions, whereas PeerAiD instead builds a teacher that is inherently specialized for the student's adversarial distribution. The loss function combines KL-divergence-based inner maximization (as in RSLAD) with a dual CE+KL outer minimization for both peer and student, making it a new-loss contribution within the established adversarial distillation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a network can be 0% robust against its own PGD-20 attacks yet substantially more robust against a peer's adversarial examples than a fully pretrained robust teacher challenges the implicit assumption that robustness must be a general, self-consistent property. Practically, this offers a path to improve small-model robustness on edge devices without requiring a large pretrained teacher, and the peer avoids the well-known robustness-accuracy tradeoff (75.63% clean vs. 57.30% for the robust teacher). Limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet (no ImageNet-scale results), the added compute cost of training two networks simultaneously, and the open question of whether the peer's specialization transfers across architectures or requires retraining for each new student.
