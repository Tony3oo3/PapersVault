## Problem

State-of-the-art few-shot adapters for vision-language models (CLIP-Adapter, TIP-Adapter, TaskRes) rely on per-task hyperparameter tuning via validation or test sets to control the trade-off between zero-shot knowledge and support-set adaptation. The paper shows that without this unrealistic model selection, these methods can underperform a simple zero-shot-initialized Linear Probing baseline by up to 21%, and fail to generalize under domain shifts. The gap is a principled, validation-free mechanism for balancing prototype deviation that generalizes across tasks with a fixed configuration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted CLIP visual features (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) of a few-shot support set (K images per class, K ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}), augmented 20× per sample with random zoom, crops, and flips, along with text prompts per class used to compute ℓ₂-normalized zero-shot prototypes. It outputs softmax class scores computed as temperature-scaled cosine similarity between a query image's visual embedding and the learned class prototypes. Notably, no validation or test samples are accessed during adaptation; all hyperparameters (300 epochs, SGD momentum 0.9, LR 0.1 with cosine decay) are fixed identically across all 11 benchmark datasets and both backbones.

## Method

CLAP (CLass-Adaptive linear Probe) formulates few-shot adaptation as a constrained Linear Probing problem: minimize cross-entropy on the support set subject to each learned class prototype w_c remaining close to its zero-shot text prototype t_c. The constraint is solved via an Augmented Lagrangian Multiplier (ALM) with class-wise penalty weights λ_c and the PHR penalty function, replacing the uniform scalar penalty used in prior work. Two critical adaptations distinguish this ALM from the standard formulation: (i) sample-wise multipliers are relaxed to class-wise ones because mini-batch gradient descent averages prototypes and data augmentation creates inconsistent per-sample penalties; (ii) only a single outer-step update of λ is performed to prevent overfitting on the small support set, with λ_c initialized as the mean zero-shot softmax score over that class's support samples. The inner loop trains prototypes with SGD while enforcing ℓ₂-normalization after each update and scaling cosine similarity by CLIP's pre-trained temperature τ, matching the pre-training geometry.

## Results

On the 11-dataset few-shot benchmark (ResNet-50 backbone, validation-free protocol), CLAP achieves 62.79% top-1 accuracy at K=1, 69.13% at K=4, and 74.57% at K=16, consistently outperforming all adapter-based baselines. In the domain generalization setting (adapt on ImageNet, evaluate on 4 shifted variants), CLAP with ViT-B/16 reaches 60.04% average target accuracy, surpassing zero-shot (57.17%) by +2.87 points, while all other adapter methods either match or fall below zero-shot. CLAP also outperforms full fine-tuning methods (FLYP: 60.98%, WiSE: 60.59%) in the low-data regime (8 shots) on average target accuracy (59.21% vs. 60.98% for FLYP, but CLAP uses only 8 shots vs. 16 total for FT methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAP outperforms all adapter-based competitors across every shot setting: at K=1 it leads CLIP-Adapter by +4.36 points (62.79 vs. 58.43), TIP-Adapter by +3.93 points, and TaskRes(e) by +1.35 points. Against the strongest adapter baseline CrossModal-LP [24], CLAP gains +0.55 at K=1 and +0.92 at K=4, with the gap narrowing to +0.92 at K=16. The paper's own ZS-LP baseline (zero-shot-initialized linear probing with data augmentation and temperature scaling) is already competitive, reaching 61.28% at K=1, but CLAP's class-adaptive penalty consistently adds 1.3–1.5 points in the low-shot regime. In domain generalization (Table 2), CLAP is the only method that beats zero-shot on the average target domain for both backbones (+2.30 for ResNet-50, +2.87 for ViT-B/16), whereas CLIP-Adapter drops by 9.40 points (ResNet-50) and TIP-Adapter(f) drops by 21.6 points (ViT-B/16) relative to zero-shot. Against full fine-tuning methods (Table 3), CLAP with 8 shots achieves 59.21% average target accuracy, competitive with FLYP (60.98%) and WiSE (60.59%) which require 16 total shots (8 train + 8 validation) and access to the full backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits Linear Probing for CLIP-based few-shot adaptation, which prior ETL literature (CLIP-Adapter, TIP-Adapter, TaskRes) largely dismissed in favor of more complex adapter architectures. It departs from these methods by showing that a properly configured LP—initialized with zero-shot prototypes, using CLIP's temperature scaling, explicit L2-normalization, and data augmentation—serves as a surprisingly strong baseline that matches or exceeds SoTA adapters when hyperparameters are held fixed across tasks. The proposed CLAP objective builds on the Augmented Lagrangian Multiplier framework from constrained optimization (Bertsekas, 1996), adapting it to learn class-wise penalty weights without a validation set, in contrast to prior methods that rely on a single scalar penalty or per-task grid search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widespread assumption in the few-shot VLM adaptation literature: that complex adapter architectures with task-specific hyperparameter tuning are necessary to outperform simple baselines. By demonstrating that prior SoTA results are substantially inflated by per-task model selection (up to 21% degradation under cross-task transfer), it reframes what constitutes a fair and realistic evaluation protocol. The practical impact is significant—CLAP provides a validation-free, homogeneous-configuration method that is easy to deploy in real-world settings where large validation sets are unavailable. A noted limitation is that CLAP's advantage over ZS-LP diminishes as the number of shots increases, leaving open the question of whether other strategies (e.g., full fine-tuning or prompt learning) become preferable in the higher-data regime.
