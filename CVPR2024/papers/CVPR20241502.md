## Problem

No effective generative model existed for producing high-quality handwritten mathematical expression (HME) images from symbolic sequences. Existing G2I pipelines (e.g., Sg2im) require real segmentation masks to supervise layout refinement, but HMEs have no well-defined pixel boundaries (white pixels around strokes are essential), and the space of possible symbol layouts is infinite, making complete training data infeasible to collect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241502/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a LaTeX sequence, converts it to a symbol graph with nodes from 126 symbol categories and directed edges encoding 8 positional relations (superscript, subscript, left, right, etc.), and produces a handwritten HME image at three resolutions (64×64, 128×128, 256×256) via a Cascaded Refinement Network. Intermediate outputs include per-symbol bounding boxes in normalized [0,1] coordinates and one-channel soft masks indicating pixel-level symbol membership probability, all produced differentiably within a single forward pass.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully differentiable end-to-end pipeline (graph → layout → mask → image) that eliminates the need for real masks or non-differentiable layout-mask alignment. The layout predictor uses a GEM-based GCN trained adversarially with a layout discriminator, and employs a Less-is-More (LiM) strategy that trains only on 1-degree subgraphs to focus on local symbol relations while generalizing to arbitrary graph structures at test time. A Sequential BBox-to-Mask (B2M) module differentially maps bounding boxes to pixel-level soft masks by subtracting box coordinates from coordinate matrices to form 4×w×w grid tensors, then projecting through an FCN with Sigmoid output. The image decoder (CRN) consumes the mask-embedding product plus Gaussian noise, and the entire system is jointly optimized with pixel loss, image/symbol adversarial losses, and an auxiliary symbol classification loss.

## Results

On CROHME2019, the proposed method achieves SSIM 0.793, FID 10.98, WER 0.326, and ExpRate 0.316. The headline result is a 6.5% absolute reduction in WER and a 9.7% absolute increase in ExpRate over the closest baseline Sg2im (WER 0.393, ExpRate 0.219), while matching or slightly exceeding Sg2im on image quality (SSIM 0.793 vs 0.787; FID 10.98 vs 10.02). When used for synthetic data augmentation of the CAN HMER model, the generated images yield 5–11% absolute ExpRate improvement over the baseline across CROHME2014/2016/2019 (e.g., 44.7→50.2 on CROHME14, 42.8→53.9 on CROHME16, 39.3→49.6 on CROHME19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CycleGAN and FormulaGAN (I2I translation baselines), the proposed method dominates on every metric: FID drops from 84.14/74.68 to 10.98, WER drops from 0.671/0.601 to 0.326, and ExpRate rises from 0.026/0.066 to 0.316, confirming that graph-conditioned generation is far superior to unpaired image translation for structured formula synthesis. Against the closest structural baseline Sg2im, the method wins decisively on recognition-oriented metrics (WER −6.5%, ExpRate +9.7% absolute) and marginally on SSIM (+0.006), while Sg2im retains a small FID advantage (10.02 vs 10.98), indicating comparable perceptual realism but markedly better layout clarity and symbol recognizability in the proposed method. Qualitative comparisons with four diffusion-based models (ControlNet, SD+LoRA, DiffSketcher, DiffAE) show that none can produce realistic handwritten styles or recognizable multi-symbol formulas, and all incur substantially higher computational cost. The ablation (Table 2) further confirms that removing the LiM strategy, layout discriminator, or B2M module each degrades mIoU and recognition metrics, with the combined removal (Sg2im baseline) dropping ExpRate from 0.316 to 0.219.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sg2im graph-to-image generation pipeline (Johnson et al., CVPR 2018) from a two-stage graph→layout→image formulation to a three-stage end-to-end graph→layout→mask→image pipeline, using GEM (Li et al., ICML 2019) for graph encoding in the layout predictor. It departs from prior G2I works (Sg2im, COLoR, Migs) that require real segmentation masks for supervision, instead introducing a differentiable BBox-to-Mask module trained with image-level losses. The LiM training strategy—restricting training to 1-degree sub-graphs—repurposes the 'less is more' principle from network science to address the combinatorial explosion of possible symbol graph structures, a problem absent in natural-image scene graph generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end generative pipeline for HME synthesis from symbolic structure without requiring real masks or non-differentiable alignment, filling a gap that has limited synthetic data augmentation for HMER. The 5–11% absolute HMER improvement via 6,000 generated samples demonstrates practical utility for a data-scarce recognition task. Limitations include reliance on naive CRN decoders due to compute constraints (occasional unrecognizable symbols in complex formulas) and two-stage training rather than true joint optimization. Open questions include whether diffusion-based decoders (VQ-GAN, latent diffusion) can close the remaining quality gap, and whether the LiM strategy generalizes to other structured graph-to-image domains beyond mathematics.
