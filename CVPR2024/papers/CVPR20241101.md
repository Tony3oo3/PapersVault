## Problem

Weakly supervised 3D medical image segmentation with only volume-level labels (e.g., tumour present/absent) remains challenging because existing CAM-based methods produce low-resolution heatmaps and are limited to fixed viewing directions. No prior work has combined deep learning with the inverse Radon transform to perform dense 3D prediction using arbitrary 2D encoders, leaving the large ecosystem of pretrained 2D networks underutilized for volumetric tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D medical volume (CT, MRI, or PET) and a single binary volume-level label indicating presence or absence of a region of interest. During inference, 224×224 slices are extracted at L=2000 arbitrary 3D orientations (Fibonacci lattice) with M=100 slices per orientation, each fed to a 2D classifier. The output is a continuous 3D heatmap matching the input volume's shape, which is binarized via connected-component analysis to yield a segmentation mask; for the Duke dataset, a MaxIoU metric on bounding boxes is used instead of Dice since only one bounding box per patient is available.

## Method

ToNNO replaces the pixel-sum in the 3D-2D Radon transform with a trained 2D neural network (ResNet-10-T, ImageNet-pretrained, frozen batch normalization), then applies the inverse Radon transform (filtered backprojection) to reconstruct a 3D heatmap from the network's slice logits. The classifier is trained on randomly oriented 224×224 slices sampled from 3D volumes, each assigned the volume-level label (introducing inherent label noise), minimizing binary cross-entropy. The second derivative required by the filtered backprojection formula is estimated via finite differences across the M+2 regularly spaced slices per angle, and all interpolations use trilinear sampling via PyTorch's grid_sample. Two extensions are proposed: Averaged CAM, which averages LayerCAM/GradCAM heatmaps across all L angles, and Tomographic CAM, which additionally applies the second-derivative filter per spatial location to correct the blurriness of deep-layer CAM maps, yielding sharper reconstructions at deeper network layers.

## Results

On four 3D medical imaging datasets (Multiple Sclerosis MRI, AutoPET-II, MosMedData COVID-19 CT, Duke breast MRI), Tomographic LayerCAM achieves the best F1-score across all datasets, with an average improvement of +0.14 over the LayerCAM baseline. ToNNO alone outperforms LayerCAM on F1 in 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., +0.27 on Duke, +0.11 on MosMed) but underperforms on AutoPET precision (0.52 vs 0.59). Averaged LayerCAM and Tomographic LayerCAM further boost Dice scores, reaching 0.58 on Multiple Sclerosis (vs 0.41 for LayerCAM) and 0.48 on MosMed (vs 0.35).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LayerCAM (applied slice-wise along the axial axis), which ToNNO outperforms on F1 in 3 of 4 datasets (MS: 0.82 vs 0.73; MosMed: 0.50 vs 0.39; Duke: 0.51 vs 0.24) but loses on AutoPET precision (0.52 vs 0.59) and balanced accuracy (0.74 vs 0.80). GradCAM is consistently outperformed by LayerCAM across all datasets and is not the focus of the main comparison. The paper's key contribution over baselines is the Averaged and Tomographic CAM variants: Tomographic LayerCAM achieves the best F1 on all four datasets (average +0.14 over LayerCAM) and the best Dice on MS (0.58 vs 0.41) and MosMed (0.41 vs 0.35). On Duke, where only bounding-box annotations exist, ToNNO and Tomographic LayerCAM both more than double the F1-score of LayerCAM (0.51 vs 0.24) and improve MaxIoU by +0.08 to +0.13. GradCAM++ and ScoreCAM (reported in the appendix) are also outperformed by the proposed methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical 3D-2D Radon transform and its inverse (filtered backprojection) from computed tomography to reconstruct a 3D heatmap from 2D neural network predictions, a combination the authors note has not been previously explored for dense prediction. It is positioned as orthogonal to the dominant CAM-based family of weakly supervised segmentation methods (GradCAM, LayerCAM), then extends them by averaging CAM heatmaps across many slice orientations and incorporating the tomographic second-derivative filter to correct blurriness at deeper layers. Unlike prior 3D segmentation approaches that use full 3D encoder-decoders or three fixed 2D models (axial/sagittal/coronal), ToNNO trains a single 2D encoder on arbitrarily oriented slices, leveraging the large ecosystem of pretrained 2D backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical way to perform 3D dense prediction with any off-the-shelf 2D encoder, lowering the barrier to entry for 3D medical image segmentation and enabling transfer of ImageNet-pretrained weights. The finding that the filtered backprojection step (second derivative) corrects the inherent blurriness of CAM at deeper layers is a useful practical insight for anyone working with class activation maps. Key limitations include the ~40-second reconstruction time (vs. <1 s for slice-wise CAM), the unresolved intermediate-resolution case between Averaged and Tomographic CAM, and the fact that the method still relies on noisy volume-level labels that introduce label noise during training. Open questions include whether iterative reconstruction techniques can handle the intermediate cases and whether self-supervised pretraining on 3D slices could further improve the 2D encoder.
