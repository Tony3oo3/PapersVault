## Problem

Existing 6-DoF pose estimation methods that combine pose classification with residual regression train both tasks in parallel via shared top-level features, preventing the regression branch from receiving direct guidance from the classification outcome. This design leads to suboptimal performance, particularly in challenging scenarios involving object symmetry, lack of texture, or heavy occlusion, where the regression task cannot effectively correct classification errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241032/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image crop (expanded and padded to square) around the object of interest, concatenated with a dilated binary bounding-box encoding to model detection inaccuracies, along with the object's 3D CAD model. It produces a full 6-DoF pose (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t) in a single non-iterative pass, decomposed internally into a coarse classification prediction and a fine-grained residual correction. The image crop is typically generated by an off-the-shelf detector such as Mask R-CNN, and the method operates on 256×256 crops at inference.

## Method

MRC-Net is a two-stage sequential Siamese pipeline using a shared-weight ResNet34-UNet backbone. Stage 1 classifies the pose into discrete buckets (4608 rotation prototypes via uniform SO(3) partitioning, a 64×64 translation grid, and 1000 depth bins) using soft probabilistic labels to handle symmetry ambiguity, then renders the 3D CAD model in the predicted pose. Stage 2 regresses the residual pose by processing the rendered image through a novel MultiScale Residual Correlation (MRC) layer that computes feature correlations between the real and rendered images at three scales, progressively aggregating correlation features with downsampled context and the object visibility mask. The MRC layer replaces explicit optical-flow or correspondence prediction, directly feeding discriminative correlation features to the regression head in an end-to-end trainable manner. Perspective correction via bounding-box geometry and test-time augmentation (4 rotations) further improve accuracy, and the entire system requires no iterative refinement or post-processing.

## Results

On the BOP benchmark with purely synthetic training, MRC-Net achieves an average AR of 63.3% across four datasets, outperforming the next-best method SurfEmb (60.9%) by 2.4% on average. The largest margin is on T-LESS (77.1% vs. 74.1% for SurfEmb, +3.0%) and ITODD (39.3% vs. 38.7%). When fine-tuned with real images on T-LESS and YCB-V, MRC-Net reaches 80.8% average AR, surpassing SC6D (78.4%) and CosyPose (77.4%). On YCB-V non-BOP metrics, MRC-Net attains 95.0% AUC ADD-S and 92.3% AUC ADD(-S), improving over CheckerPose by 3.7% and 5.9% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRC-Net outperforms all 15+ competing RGB-based methods on the four-dataset average (63.3% vs. 60.9% for SurfEmb, the runner-up). On T-LESS, the gain over SurfEmb is 3.0% AR and over SC6D is 3.2%; on ITODD it leads SurfEmb by 0.6% and SC6D by 9.0%. On YCB-V, MRC-Net edges out NCF (67.3%) by 0.8% and SCFlow (65.1%) by 3.0%. On LM-O, it surpasses SCFlow (68.2%) by 0.3% and CRT-6D (66.0%) by 2.5%. When fine-tuned with real data (Table 2b), MRC-Net leads SC6D by 2.4% average AR (80.8 vs. 78.4) but trails CosyPose (82.1) and SCFlow (82.6) on YCB-V by 0.4–0.9%, which the authors attribute to YCB-V's rich textures favoring iterative refinement methods and known CAD/annotation noise in that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRC-Net departs from the dominant parallel multitask paradigm (e.g., SC6D, Mousavian et al.) where classification and residual regression share a common backbone, instead proposing a strictly sequential pipeline in which the classifier's output conditions the regressor via a rendered image. It builds on the render-and-compare lineage (DeepIM, CosyPose, CIR, SCFlow) but eliminates iterative refinement and PnP solvers by introducing the MultiScale Residual Correlation (MRC) layer that directly feeds multi-scale feature correlations into a regression head in a single pass. The method also draws on CDPN's SITE formulation for translation quantization and Zhou et al.'s 6D rotation representation, while introducing soft probabilistic labels to handle symmetry-induced classification ambiguity—a concept related to but distinct from the continuous pose distributions in EPro-PnP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-shot, end-to-end design without iterative refinement or post-processing makes MRC-Net practically attractive for real-time deployment (16 FPS), and its strong performance under pure synthetic training suggests applicability in data-scarce domains like underwater or aerospace pose estimation. The ablation revealing that a naively added parallel regression head actually degrades performance relative to classification-only is a useful negative result that validates the sequential conditioning hypothesis. Key limitations include dependence on accurate CAD models (inherited from render-and-compare) and RGB-only input; extending to depth or developing model-agnostic variants remain open directions.
