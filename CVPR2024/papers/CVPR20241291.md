## Problem

Existing 3D human pose estimation datasets are predominantly collected in controlled laboratory settings with fixed cameras, uniform backgrounds, and limited action diversity, causing models trained on them to degrade significantly in real-world environments. The absence of a large-scale multi-view dataset capturing variable lighting, complex backgrounds, diverse scenes, and varying human body scales has stalled progress toward practical 3D pose estimation in the wild.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241291/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FreeMan provides 11M frames at 1920×1080 resolution (30/60 FPS) from 8 movable smartphone cameras arranged in a circle (2–5.5 m from the subject center), yielding 8000 sequences across 40 subjects and 10 scene types. The four benchmarks consume different input formats: a single RGB image or video for monocular 3D HPE, pre-computed 2D keypoints (17 joints, COCO format) for 2D-to-3D lifting, multi-view RGB images with calibrated camera parameters for multi-view 3D HPE, and monocular video for neural rendering. Outputs include 3D joint coordinates in camera or world coordinates, SMPL mesh parameters, and novel-view rendered images evaluated by PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-automated annotation pipeline that replaces expensive manual labeling: 8 Mi11 phones are wirelessly synchronized via NTP (sub-frame error), calibrated with a chessboard and refined using LightGlue dense cross-view matching, and annotated by triangulating HRNet-w48 2D keypoints into 3D pose with smoothness and bone-length constraints, followed by SMPLify mesh fitting and re-projection to correct occluded 2D poses. A novel erroneous-pose detector feeds estimated 2D poses into Stable Diffusion 1.5 with ControlNet to generate human images, then compares SAM segment masks of original vs. generated images via IoU to flag frames for manual correction. The paper benchmarks eight representative methods (HMR, PARE, SimpleBaseline, VideoPose3D, PoseFormer, MHFormer, VoxelPose, HumanNeRF) across four tasks, demonstrating that FreeMan-trained models substantially outperform Human3.6M- and HuMMan-trained counterparts on cross-domain transfer to 3DPW, AIST++, and Human3.6M.

## Results

On the 3DPW test set, HMR trained on FreeMan achieves MPJPE of 151.85 mm and PA-MPJPE of 88.85 mm (with 2D+SMPL supervision), substantially outperforming Human3.6M (279.92 / 133.13 mm) and HuMMan (407.57 / 192.75 mm). PARE trained on FreeMan reaches 94.27 / 60.39 mm, beating both Human3.6M (118.54 / 81.22 mm) and HuMMan (110.99 / 63.11 mm). In 2D-to-3D lifting on AIST++, SimpleBaseline trained on the full FreeMan training set achieves 126.23 mm MPJPE (24.64% improvement over HuMMan), and in multi-view estimation, VoxelPose trained on FreeMan generalizes to Human3.6M with 61.29 mm MPJPE@500mm while the reverse transfer (Human3.6M→FreeMan) yields 0% AP@25mm, demonstrating severe domain gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeMan consistently outperforms both Human3.6M and HuMMan as a training set across all four benchmark tasks. For monocular 3D HPE on 3DPW, HMR trained on FreeMan reduces PA-MPJPE by 33.95% over Human3.6M and 51.75% over HuMMan; PARE shows similar gains (8.76–15.39% improvement). In 2D-to-3D lifting on AIST++, SimpleBaseline and MHFormer trained on the full FreeMan training set achieve 24.64% and 21.97% PA-MPJPE improvements over HuMMan respectively, despite FreeMan's training set being smaller than Human3.6M's. The multi-view results are particularly striking: VoxelPose trained on Human3.6M completely fails on FreeMan (0% AP@25mm, 89.85 mm MPJPE@500mm), while the reverse transfer (FreeMan→Human3.6M) retains 96.68% Recall@500mm and 61.29 mm MPJPE@500mm, confirming that FreeMan's real-world diversity provides far superior generalization. For neural rendering, HumanNeRF achieves PSNR ranging from 23.86 dB (Park) to 30.11 dB (Dance Room) across 10 FreeMan scenes, demonstrating both the learnable and challenging content within the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of 3D human pose datasets collected in controlled laboratory settings (Human3.6M, HuMMan, AIST++) by constructing a large-scale multi-view benchmark under real-world conditions with movable cameras. It builds directly on the HuMMan annotation pipeline (smoothness and bone-length constraints) and extends it with a semi-automated error-detection stage leveraging Stable Diffusion/ControlNet and SAM for pose verification. The evaluation framework reuses established baselines (HMR, PARE, VoxelPose, HumanNeRF, PoseFormer, MHFormer) to position FreeMan as a drop-in benchmark rather than proposing new estimation architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeMan addresses a critical gap: models trained on lab datasets degrade sharply in the wild, yet no prior multi-view dataset offered the scene, lighting, and scale diversity needed to close that gap. The demonstrated transferability (e.g., FreeMan-trained HMR achieving 87.93 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW vs. 133–193 mm for Human3.6M/HuMMan-trained counterparts) suggests that scene diversity, not raw frame count, is the key driver of real-world generalization. Open questions include whether the semi-automated annotation pipeline scales beyond 17-joint COCO format, how annotation quality at 60 FPS compares to 30 FPS, and whether the error-detection threshold is robust across all occlusion severities. The paper acknowledges that prompt tuning for the generation-based error detector remains a practical bottleneck.
