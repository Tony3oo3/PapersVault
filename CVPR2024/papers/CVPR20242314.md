## Problem

Existing activation pruning methods for memory-efficient fine-tuning, such as BackRazor, use element-wise top-k sparsification that introduces substantial computational overhead from sorting and element-wise comparisons, sometimes making training slower than full fine-tuning. The paper identifies that this memory-computation trade-off is unjustified and asks whether alternative sparsity patterns and adaptive pruning strategies can reduce both memory and compute simultaneously without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242314/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained Transformer checkpoints (ViT-B/16 on ImageNet-22K, BERT-base-uncased) and fine-tunes them on standard downstream tasks: CIFAR-10/100 (32×32 images), CUB-200, and RTE from GLUE (text entailment pairs). Beyond the final fine-tuned weights, the method maintains a per-coordinate probability distribution over activation columns that is updated each iteration via a bandit rule, and produces column-wise binary masks that gate which activations are stored and which gradients are computed during backpropagation. The forward pass remains fully dense; only the activations saved for the backward pass are sparsified.

## Method

PreBackRazor replaces BackRazor's element-wise self-sparsified top-k pruning with a column-wise joint-sparse scheme that simultaneously prunes activations and their corresponding parameter gradients. At each iteration, a Bernoulli variable is sampled per column coordinate using a probability distribution maintained by a bandit update (adapted from JointSpar), which prioritizes coordinates with large gradient norms and freezes the rest. Frozen coordinates skip both activation storage and gradient computation, eliminating the sorting and comparison overhead of element-wise pruning. The bandit update uses the squared gradient norm of selected coordinates to adjust future selection probabilities, with a KL-divergence projection onto the simplex constrained by a sparsity budget. The method further integrates a customized FlashAttention-2 kernel (PreBackRazor++) to accelerate attention computation on consumer GPUs, and provides a convergence proof showing the algorithm converges to a bounded neighborhood of a stationary point under standard smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions.

## Results

On ViT-B/16 fine-tuning, PreBackRazor++JS@70% achieves 92.4% accuracy on CIFAR-100, 98.8% on CIFAR-10, and 85.0% on CUB-200, while reducing memory to 7440 MB (2.7× reduction vs. full fine-tuning's 19980 MB) and cutting epoch time from 6 min 19 s (BackRazor) to 4 min 37 s (1.4× speedup). At the more aggressive 50% JointSpar setting, PreBackRazor++JS further reduces memory to 6880 MB (2.9×) with a 1.6× speedup (4 min 02 s) while maintaining 92.1% CIFAR-100 accuracy. With the integrated FlashAttention kernel, the method reaches a 1.7× speedup (3 min 40 s) at 92.2% CIFAR-100 accuracy with 7960 MB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BackRazor [Jiang et al., NeurIPS 2022], which uses element-wise top-k self-sparsified activation pruning. PreBackRazor wins decisively on computational efficiency: it achieves a 1.4× speedup at 70% JointSpar and 1.6× at 50%, reducing epoch time from 6 min 19 s to 4 min 37 s and 4 min 02 s respectively, by replacing the expensive element-wise sorting and comparison with a cheaper column-wise joint-sparse mask. On accuracy, PreBackRazor++JS@70% slightly edges out BackRazor on CIFAR-100 (92.4% vs. 92.1%) and CIFAR-10 (98.8% vs. 98.6%), while BackRazor retains a small advantage on CUB-200 (86.6% vs. 85.0%). Memory usage is comparable, with PreBackRazor using marginally less (7440 MB vs. 7540 MB at 70%). On BERT fine-tuning for RTE, PreBackRazor matches or slightly exceeds BackRazor's accuracy (e.g., 72.92% vs. 69.31% at 80% pruning, batch size 8) while being up to 9.7% faster, confirming the speed advantage generalizes beyond vision models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PreBackRazor directly extends BackRazor's asymmetric self-sparsified backpropagation pipeline, replacing its computationally expensive element-wise top-k activation pruning with a column-wise joint-sparse pattern. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the bandit-based probability update mechanism from JointSpar (originally designed for communication-efficient distributed gradient sparsification) and repurposing it to decide which activation columns to retain for backpropagation, thereby avoiding unnecessary pruning and gradient computation entirely. This shifts the paradigm from 'prune activations then compute full gradients on the pruned set' to 'selectively compute only the gradients that matter, guided by learned importance scores.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making memory-efficient fine-tuning actually faster rather than slower: BackRazor's element-wise pruning paradoxically doubles training time relative to full fine-tuning, and PreBackRazor recovers that overhead while preserving the memory savings, which is critical for consumer-GPU users (RTX 4090, RTX 2080 Ti). However, validation is limited to ViT-B/16 and BERT-base on a single GPU, and the theoretical convergence proof is restricted to linear networks, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger LLMs or deeper non-linear architectures. The integration with FlashAttention-2 kernels is a practical engineering contribution that may be independently useful, but the paper does not explore interaction with other PEFT methods (LoRA, adapters) or multi-GPU settings.
