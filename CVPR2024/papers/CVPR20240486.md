## Problem

LUT-based image restoration models achieve high inference efficiency by caching learned LQ-HQ patch pairs, but incorporating multiple LUTs to improve performance causes storage to grow rapidly, often exceeding the allocatable on-device cache size of mainstream smartphone chips. No prior work addresses how to compress these spatial-wise LUTs while preserving their representation capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240486/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, a local LQ patch (e.g., a 2×2 pixel block for ×4 super-resolution) serves as the coordinate index into the compressed LUT structure, and the corresponding HQ patch (e.g., 4×4 pixels) is retrieved as the prediction. The compression framework itself takes a trained 4D spatial-wise LUT of shape L×L×L×L (where L = 2^(8−q)+1) and outputs two smaller LUTs: a lower-dimensional diagonal LUT of shape K×L×L and a sparser non-diagonal LUT of shape D×D×D×D, whose combined storage is a small fraction of the original.

## Method

The key observation is a diagonal-dominance property: occurrence statistics of LQ patches show that most cached indices cluster along the LUT diagonal because adjacent pixels in natural images have similar values. DFC exploits this by splitting the LUT into diagonal cells (|I₀−I₁| ≤ λ) and non-diagonal cells. Diagonal cells are preserved and re-indexed via an Index Mapper that collapses two dimensions into one (reducing a 4D LUT to 3D), while non-diagonal cells are aggressively subsampled at a larger interval σ > q to produce a much sparser fallback LUT. This converts one LUT into two smaller LUTs with a configurable compression ratio. Additionally, the paper introduces SPF-LUT, a new multi-LUT architecture that splits output channels for progressive receptive-field enlargement and multi-scale feature fusion, extending the scaling law beyond MuLUT's single-channel bottleneck.

## Results

On the primary ×4 super-resolution task, SPF-LUT+DFC achieves 31.05 PSNR / 0.8755 SSIM on Set5 with only 2.018 MB storage (11.7% compression ratio), losing just 0.06 dB compared to the uncompressed SPF-LUT (31.11 dB, 17.284 MB). The compressed model outperforms MuLUT (30.60 dB, 4.062 MB) in PSNR while using half the storage. Across all four restoration tasks (SR, denoising, deblocking, deblurring), DFC reduces storage by ~90% with negligible performance drops (≤0.1 dB PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LUT-based methods, SPF-LUT+DFC (31.05 dB on Set5, 2.018 MB) outperforms both MuLUT (30.60 dB, 4.062 MB) and SR-LUT (29.94 dB, 1.274 MB) in PSNR, achieving the best quality-to-storage ratio in the LUT category. The +DFC variants of each model lose only 0.04–0.06 dB PSNR relative to their uncompressed counterparts while reducing storage by ~90% (e.g., SPF-LUT: 17.284→2.018 MB; MuLUT: 4.062→0.407 MB; SR-LUT: 1.274→0.128 MB). Against classical methods (NE+LLE, ANR, A+), all LUT-based models achieve higher PSNR on every dataset, with SPF-LUT+DFC surpassing A+ by 0.78 dB on Set5 despite using 7.5× less storage. Compared to DNN baselines (RRDB at 63.9 MB, EDSR at 164.4 MB), LUT methods trail by 1.4–1.6 dB PSNR on Set5 but require no GPU and fit within smartphone L2/L3 cache budgets. On denoising (Table 2), MuLUT+DFC (31.38 dB on Set12, 49 KB) beats the original SR-LUT (30.42 dB, 81.6 KB) in both quality and storage, while still trailing DnCNN (32.86 dB) and SwinIR (33.36 dB). On deblocking and deblurring, the same pattern holds: DFC-compressed LUTs maintain near-original performance at ~10% storage, consistently outperforming classical baselines but remaining below the strongest DNN methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial-wise LUT line of work initiated by SR-LUT (Jo & Kim, CVPR 2021) and MuLUT (Li et al., ECCV 2022), which established the 'space for time' paradigm of caching DNN outputs into lookup tables for efficient image restoration. The key conceptual departure is identifying and exploiting the diagonal-dominance property of LUT cell access patterns, motivated by the low-dimensional manifold structure of natural images, to compress LUTs without retraining the underlying DNN. The proposed SPF-LUT structure further extends the multi-LUT scaling law by incorporating multi-scale feature fusion inspired by IMDN, moving beyond MuLUT's single-channel bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling advanced LUT-based restoration models to fit within the L2/L3 cache of mainstream smartphone chips (e.g., Snapdragon 888 Plus), which was previously infeasible for multi-LUT designs. The finding that ~90% of LUT storage is redundant for ×4 super-resolution, with the critical information concentrated in a narrow diagonal band, provides a clear and actionable compression strategy. Open questions include whether the diagonal-dominance property holds for higher-order LUTs or different degradation types, whether the compression ratio can be pushed further with learned (rather than uniform) subsampling, and whether the approach transfers to other LUT-based applications beyond image restoration.
