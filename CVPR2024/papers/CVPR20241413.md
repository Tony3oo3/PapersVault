## Problem

Prior multi-modal 3D object detection methods fuse LiDAR and radar without accounting for each sensor's weather-specific strengths or weaknesses, and they perform fusion in BEV or pseudo-image domains that discard the height information essential for accurate 3D localization. This paper addresses both gaps by fusing LiDAR and 4D radar directly in 3D space while adaptively modulating the fusion based on weather conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241413/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud (N₀×3), a 4D radar tensor (M₀×3, preprocessed to retain only the top 10% highest-power points), and an RGB image (H×W×3). The image is not used directly for detection; instead, it feeds a pre-trained lightweight 2D CNN weather classifier that produces a single 1D feature vector encoding the current weather condition. The network outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, rotation) and class labels for the Sedan category, evaluated at IoU thresholds of 0.3 and 0.5 across seven weather conditions on the K-Radar dataset.

## Method

The framework uses a three-layer sparse 3D convolution backbone for both LiDAR and 4D radar to preserve 3D structure. At each layer, the 3D-LRF module queries each non-empty LiDAR voxel against its K_l nearest-neighbor radar voxels (within radius r_l, both halved per layer) via an attention mechanism: abundant radar neighbors activate the LiDAR feature (confirming a real object), while sparse neighbors suppress it (flagging weather-induced noise). The WRGNet then gates the resulting fusion feature using a 1D weather-conditioned image feature concatenated with radar voxel features, controlling how much radar information flows into the LiDAR stream depending on the current weather. Both the gated enhanced LiDAR feature and the original LiDAR feature are compressed through separate BEV encoders, concatenated across layers, and passed to a detection head trained with focal loss and smooth L1 loss. The weather classifier is pre-trained for 43 epochs before the full network is trained end-to-end for 11 epochs.

## Results

On the K-Radar dataset, the proposed method achieves 84.0 AP_BEV and 74.8 AP_3D at IoU=0.3, and 73.6 AP_BEV and 45.2 AP_3D at IoU=0.5 for the Sedan class. It surpasses the second-best LiDAR-only baseline (RTNH*) by approximately 19% in AP_3D at IoU=0.5 (45.2 vs. 37.8). Under heavy snow, the method shows performance improvements of 10–43% over the second-best scores across metrics, and exhibits the least degradation when increasing the IoU threshold from 0.3 to 0.5, indicating superior 3D localization accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines across all metrics. Against RTNH (4D radar only), it gains +37.4 AP_3D at IoU=0.3 (74.8 vs. 37.4), demonstrating that LiDAR's precise localization is essential even when radar provides weather robustness. Against RTNH* (LiDAR only, the strongest single-modality baseline), it improves AP_3D by +2.1 at IoU=0.3 and +7.4 at IoU=0.5, with the larger gap at stricter IoU confirming better 3D box placement. Against InterFusion (the only prior 4D radar + LiDAR fusion method), it achieves +21.8 AP_3D at IoU=0.3 (74.8 vs. 53.0), showing that fusing in the 3D domain with sensor-aware attention substantially outperforms pseudo-image-based fusion. The paper notes a minor exception: under sleet and fog, RTNH* slightly edges out the proposed method on a few metrics, attributed to the model's heavier reliance on radar in those conditions increasing false positives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RTNH [28], which established sparse 3D convolution as the backbone for encoding 4D radar tensors on the K-Radar dataset, and extends it with two novel fusion modules. It departs from InterFusion [43], the prior LiDAR–4D radar fusion method, by rejecting the pseudo-image/BEV-based fusion paradigm in favor of native 3D-domain fusion that preserves height information. The weather-conditional gating (WRGNet) introduces a new design principle absent from prior multi-modal fusion work: rather than treating all modalities symmetrically, the model explicitly modulates how much radar information flows into LiDAR based on a pre-trained weather classifier, acknowledging that sensor strengths are weather-dependent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to fuse LiDAR and 4D radar in the 3D domain while explicitly conditioning on weather, addressing a gap where prior multi-modal methods either ignored sensor-specific weather robustness or lost critical height information through BEV/pseudo-image compression. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (K-Radar) and a single object class (Sedan), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to diverse object categories, larger-scale benchmarks, or real-time deployment constraints. The reliance on a separate pre-trained weather classifier as a gating signal also raises questions about robustness when weather classification itself is uncertain or when the image modality is severely degraded.
