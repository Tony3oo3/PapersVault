## Problem

Generative non-exemplar class incremental learning methods synthesize old-class images via model inversion for knowledge distillation, but these synthetic samples suffer a significant domain gap from real images, producing abstract patterns that degrade old-class recognition. Prior approaches enforce only semantic consistency (alignment with class prototypes) while ignoring domain consistency, leaving the synthetic distribution visually and content-wise dissimilar to real data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240376/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes new-class RGB images, the frozen old model θ_{t-1}, and old class prototypes as input; no old-class data is stored or accessed. During training it produces synthetic old-class images (via a conditional generator) that are used transiently for knowledge distillation and then discarded. At inference, a single RGB image is mapped to a class label through a task-routed classifier, with the generator and embedding vectors no longer present.

## Method

DCMI employs a conditional GAN with learnable per-class embedding vectors, where the generator input is a linear combination of old-class embeddings weighted by prototype cosine similarities. Semantic consistency is enforced by minimizing cross-entropy between the prototype-similarity distribution of generated features and the target distribution derived from class prototypes, applied to both old-class and new-class synthetic samples. Domain consistency is achieved through adversarial training where a discriminator distinguishes synthetic from real images of the *new* classes, effectively disentangling the real domain distribution from the semantic shift between old and new classes. At inference, Prototypical Routing partitions the classifier into task-specific heads and uses nearest-prototype matching to select the correct head, eliminating task-recency bias without requiring a joint prediction space.

## Results

DCMI achieves average accuracy of 67.9%, 66.8%, and 64.0% on CIFAR-100 (5, 10, 20 phases), 54.8%, 53.9%, and 52.5% on Tiny-ImageNet, 70.5%, 70.0%, and 65.5% on ImageNet-Subset, and 61.9% on ImageNet-Full (10 phases). It also attains the lowest average forgetting among all compared methods, e.g., 7.8% and 7.3% on CIFAR-100 (5 and 10 phases) versus 21.0–45.7% for other non-exemplar baselines. The method surpasses the previous non-exemplar state-of-the-art (SOPE) by 1.3%, 1.0%, and 2.2% on CIFAR-100 and by 1.1%, 1.0%, and 0.6% on Tiny-ImageNet across the three phase settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCMI outperforms all non-exemplar baselines on CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-Subset across all phase settings. On CIFAR-100, it beats the closest competitor SOPE [57] by 1.3/1.0/2.2% (5/10/20 phases) and SSRE [56] by 2.0/1.8/2.3%. On Tiny-ImageNet, DCMI leads SOPE by 1.1/1.0/0.6% and FeTrIL [39] by 1.9/2.2/2.8%. On ImageNet-Subset, it surpasses FeTrIL by 0.9/1.1/3.0% and SOPE by 0.8% at P=10. However, on ImageNet-Full (P=10), DCMI (61.9%) trails FeTrIL (64.4%) by 2.5%, though it still exceeds SOPE (60.2%) and SSRE (58.1%). In terms of forgetting, DCMI achieves dramatically lower values (7.3–9.8% on CIFAR-100) compared to all other methods (15.9–73.8%), confirming its superior retention of old-class knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-inversion-based NECIL line initiated by ABD (Always Be Dreaming) [42], which identified the domain gap between synthetic and real images as a critical limitation but addressed it only through local cross-entropy and importance-weighted feature distillation. DCMI departs by explicitly enforcing dual consistency—semantic alignment via prototype-matching cross-entropy and domain alignment via adversarial training—on the generated samples, leveraging new-class real data as a domain anchor. The Prototypical Routing component draws from the prototype-matching literature [44, 50] to provide an unbiased task prior, complementing the linear classifier's intra-task discriminative power. ## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-consistency framework offers a principled recipe for generating synthetic samples that are both semantically faithful and domain-realistic, addressing a gap that prior generative NECIL methods acknowledged but did not directly close. Prototypical Routing is a lightweight, easily adoptable technique for mitigating task-recency bias that could benefit other incremental learning settings beyond NECIL. However, the method adds substantial training complexity (a GAN generator, discriminator, and multi-stage optimization), and the gains over strong baselines like SOPE and FeTrIL are modest (1–2%), leaving open whether the added cost is justified in practice. An open question is whether the adversarial domain-alignment strategy generalizes to other generative paradigms (e.g., diffusion-based inversion) or to very long task sequences where the domain distribution itself may drift.
