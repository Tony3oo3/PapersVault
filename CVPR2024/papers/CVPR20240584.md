## Problem

Long-tail class incremental learning (LT-CIL) requires a model to sequentially acquire knowledge from an imbalanced, evolving data stream while retaining previously learned classes. Existing methods only re-balance data distribution but ignore the shared semantic structure between different classes, resulting in non-robust representations and severe forgetting in minority classes. The paper identifies two compounding obstacles: intra-task imbalance (unequal sample sizes within a task) and inter-task imbalance (catastrophic forgetting across tasks).

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, processed by a ResNet18 feature extractor that maps each image to a D-dimensional feature vector. A unified classifier then produces a probability distribution over all classes seen so far. The method operates in two sequential phases per task: a construction phase (121 epochs) where the sub-prototype space is built alongside the model, and a re-sampling phase (81 epochs) where the feature extractor and classifier are fine-tuned using augmented features generated in the sub-prototype space. No replay data is required; the reminiscence space stores only class-level Gaussian statistics and correlation matrices.

## Method

The core contribution is two parallel spaces that jointly address intra- and inter-task imbalance. The sub-prototype space consists of independently learned basis vectors (n per new class) that capture shared semantic sub-structures across classes; features are projected into this space via cosine-similarity-based top-k selection with a frequency-based controlling factor to prevent dimensional bias. In the re-sampling phase, augmented features are synthesized by combining the top-2 correlated basis vectors with random combinations of h remaining vectors, with the number of augmentations per class dynamically scaled to compensate for sample scarcity. The reminiscence space stores per-class Gaussian distributions (mean and covariance) and mean correlation matrices, which are used to impose distillation losses on both the basis vectors and the classifier during subsequent tasks, preventing the previously constructed space from being corrupted by new knowledge. Training alternates between a construction loss (cross-entropy + L2 consistency + basis-vector distillation) and a re-sampling loss (cross-entropy on augmented features + classifier distillation).

## Results

On Shuffled LT-CIL (CIFAR-100, ρ=0.01, 5 tasks), the proposed method achieves 40.2% average accuracy without any replay data, outperforming the best replay-based method LUCIR+LWS (37.2%, memory=1000) by 3.0% and the best replay-free method IL2A (35.1%) by 5.1%. On ImageNet-Subset under the same setting, it reaches 45.3% vs. 43.1% for LUCIR+LWS. Across all 12 evaluation settings (2 datasets × 3 imbalance rates × 2 task counts), the method consistently achieves the highest average accuracy while using zero memory, with improvements of 2.0–3.0% over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (0 memory) outperforms all 11 baselines across every setting in both Shuffled and Ordered LT-CIL. Against the strongest replay-based competitor LUCIR+LWS [22] (1000 memory), it gains 3.0% on CIFAR-100 (ρ=0.01, 5 tasks: 40.2 vs. 37.2) and 2.2% on ImageNet-Subset (ρ=0.01, 5 tasks: 45.3 vs. 43.1). Among replay-free methods, it surpasses IL2A [48] by 5.1% (CIFAR-100, ρ=0.01, 5 tasks) and SAVC [34] by 5.8%, while also beating prototype-based methods SDC [44] and PASS [49] by 7.5% and 6.6% respectively under the same setting. Long-tail-specific methods (LDAM [4], BalPoE [1], MDCS [47]) perform poorly in the incremental setting (≤26%), confirming that static re-balancing alone cannot address inter-task forgetting. The Ordered LT-CIL results (Table 2) show similar margins: 2.5% over LUCIR+LWS on CIFAR-100 (ρ=0.01, 5 tasks: 44.8 vs. 42.3) and 2.5% on ImageNet-Subset (52.7 vs. 50.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LT-CIL formulation introduced by Liu et al. (ECCV 2022), which first combined long-tailed data distribution with class-incremental learning. It departs from prior continual learning methods (EWC, LwF, iCaRL, IL2A) that assume balanced per-class sample counts, and from long-tailed recognition methods (LDAM, BalPoE, MDCS) that operate on a single static dataset. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a learnable sub-prototype space with shared basis vectors across classes, combined with a reminiscence space that stores per-class feature distributions for anti-forgetting, enabling the method to operate without any replay buffer (memory size 0).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing a realistic deployment scenario where data is both imbalanced and arriving incrementally, without requiring storage of past samples (a privacy and storage constraint). The no-replay SOTA over methods using 1000 replay samples is a useful result for practitioners. However, the evaluation is limited to 100-class datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-Subset) with a single backbone (ResNet-18), leaving open whether the sub-prototype space scales to larger class counts or deeper architectures. The two-phase training (construction then re-sampling) and multiple loss terms add implementation complexity that may limit adoption.
