## Problem

ViT-based semantic segmentation suffers from quadratic attention cost at high resolutions, and existing token reduction methods face a fundamental trade-off: pruning or pausing tokens loses information needed for per-pixel predictions, while single-stage merging methods (e.g., CTS, ELViT) offer limited efficiency gains due to computational overhead or one-time application. The paper asks whether a parameter-free, two-stage merging strategy can simultaneously improve throughput and segmentation quality by exploiting when and where token similarity reliably reflects semantic redundancy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images processed by plain ViT backbones (ViT-T/S/B/L) with patch-based tokenization, and produces per-pixel segmentation masks of shape C×H×W. A key design detail is the token unmerging step: merged tokens are duplicated back to their original spatial indices before (for CNN decoders) or after (for transformer decoders) the decoder, so the output resolution always matches the input. The approach is parameter-free and can be applied to pre-trained models without fine-tuning, though training with ALGM yields additional mIoU gains.

## Method

ALGM integrates two merging stages at different network depths. In the first transformer layer, the CLAP module groups tokens into small k×k windows and merges all tokens in a window into their average if the mean pairwise cosine similarity exceeds an automatically computed threshold τ = μ_sim + σ_sim (derived from training-set statistics). In an intermediate layer (layer 5 for ViT-S/B, layer 7 for ViT-L), the GBM module applies a modified bipartite matching (adapted from ToMe's BSM) that splits tokens into two sets, retains only the highest-similarity edge per token, and merges pairs whose similarity exceeds τ rather than a fixed count. This adaptive threshold means the number of merged tokens varies per image based on content complexity. The key empirical insight motivating this design is that intra-class token similarities within small local windows are already well-separated from inter-class similarities in the first layer, while global intra-class vs. inter-class separation only emerges in intermediate layers, making early global merging harmful.

## Results

On ADE20K, ALGM applied to Segmenter with ViT-S improves mIoU from 45.3% to 46.4% (+1.1) while increasing throughput from 134 to 192 im/sec (+43%) and reducing GFLOPs from 38.6 to 26.3 (−32%). The efficiency-optimized variant ALGM* further boosts throughput to 235 im/sec (+75%) while maintaining mIoU at 45.5%. Across all tested backbones (ViT-T/S/B/L) and architectures (Segmenter, SegViT, SETR), ALGM achieves an average throughput improvement of 39% with a +0.7 mIoU gain, and ALGM* reaches up to +100% throughput improvement (Seg-L: 10→20 im/sec) with no mIoU drop. Similar gains are observed on Cityscapes (+90% throughput for Seg-S), COCO-Stuff (+72% for Seg-L), and Pascal-Context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALGM consistently outperforms all six compared token reduction methods on both mIoU and efficiency metrics. Against CTS [29], ALGM achieves higher mIoU (e.g., 46.4% vs. 45.1% for Seg-S on ADE20K) and higher throughput (192 vs. 174 im/sec), while CTS requires a separate CNN policy network for pre-processing. Against ToMe [2], ALGM improves mIoU by +0.2 to +1.1 points across backbones while also achieving higher throughput (e.g., 192 vs. 170 im/sec for Seg-S), whereas ToMe degrades segmentation quality. Against ELViT [23] and AiluRus [21], ALGM matches or exceeds mIoU (52.7% vs. 51.4%/52.2% for Seg-L) while delivering substantially higher throughput (16 vs. 12 im/sec) and lower GFLOPs (438 vs. 539/479). In the direct comparison with DToP [39] on SETR-B, ALGM* reduces GFLOPs by 30% (108→75) versus DToP's 25% (108→81) while maintaining mIoU. Against DoViT [26] on Seg-S, ALGM improves mIoU by +1.1 (45.3→46.4) and reduces GFLOPs by 32% (38.6→26.3), whereas DoViT drops mIoU by 0.4 and only reduces GFLOPs by 18%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALGM builds directly on ToMe's bipartite matching algorithm for global token merging, but replaces its fixed merge count with an adaptive similarity threshold, and crucially restricts global merging to intermediate layers where cosine similarity reliably reflects category correspondence. It also draws on CTS's observation that local token sharing in early layers preserves segmentation quality, but eliminates CTS's CNN-based policy network by using simple cosine-similarity-based window averaging (CLAP). The key conceptual departure from prior work is the two-stage local-then-global strategy, motivated by a systematic analysis showing that local similarities are informative in layer 1 while global similarities only become meaningful in deeper layers — a finding that explains why single-stage merging methods (ELViT, AiluRus) or global-only merging (ToMe) underperform for segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that token merging can *improve* segmentation quality (up to +1.1 mIoU) rather than merely preserve it while gaining efficiency is a useful conceptual insight for the ViT-segmentation community, with the paper attributing the gain to attention balancing across class frequencies and token denoising. The parameter-free, adaptive-threshold design makes ALGM a drop-in module for any plain ViT segmentation backbone without retraining, which has practical appeal. Open questions include whether the quality-improvement mechanism generalizes to panoptic or video segmentation, and whether the attention-balancing effect persists in architectures with different decoder designs or pre-training regimes.
