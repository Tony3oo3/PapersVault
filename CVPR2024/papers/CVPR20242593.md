## Problem

Existing dynamic view synthesis methods based on neural radiance fields or image-based rendering achieve high quality but render at only 1–2 FPS at 4K resolution, far from real-time. The core challenge is that their appearance models require per-ray network evaluations during inference, making high-resolution rendering prohibitively slow. This paper targets closing the gap between rendering quality and speed for dynamic 3D scenes at 4K resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242593/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB videos (e.g., 60 views at 4K/2K for DNA-Rendering, 18 views at 1080p for ENeRF-Outdoor) and produces a compact 4D neural point cloud representation (~2 MB per frame) that can be rendered as novel-view images at up to 4K (2160×3840) resolution. Rendering speed reaches 400+ FPS at 1080p and 80+ FPS at 4K on an RTX 4090, with the representation stored as pre-computed point attributes (position, radius, density, SH coefficients, blending weights) streamed asynchronously to the GPU.

## Method

4K4D represents dynamic scenes as a point cloud initialized via space carving, where each point's position is a learnable vector and its radius, density, and SH coefficients are predicted by MLPs from a 4D feature grid (six planes in the K-Planes style) that provides spatial regularization. The key architectural insight is a hybrid appearance model combining a discrete image-blending term (whose network is made view-direction-independent so its output can be pre-computed) with a continuous spherical-harmonics term, achieving both high fidelity and pre-computability. Rendering uses a differentiable depth-peeling algorithm with K passes through the hardware rasterizer to obtain ordered points per pixel, followed by analytic volume rendering. During inference, all network outputs are pre-computed and stored in 16-bit format, reducing runtime computation to depth peeling and SH evaluation only, enabling real-time 4K rendering.

## Results

On the DNA-Rendering dataset (1024×1224 / 1125×1536), 4K4D achieves PSNR 31.17, SSIM 0.976, LPIPS 0.055, and 203.6 FPS on an RTX 3090, outperforming the best prior method (Im4D: PSNR 28.99, 15.4 FPS) by 2.2 dB in PSNR and 13× in speed. On the more challenging ENeRF-Outdoor dataset (960×540), 4K4D reaches PSNR 25.82, SSIM 0.898, LPIPS 0.147 at 141.7 FPS, surpassing ENeRF (PSNR 25.45, 11.3 FPS) in both quality and a 12.5× speedup. On an RTX 4090, the method renders 4K (2160p) images at 85 FPS on ENeRF-Outdoor and 289 FPS on DNA-Rendering, confirming real-time 4K synthesis on commodity hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4K4D wins on every metric against all five baselines on both DNA-Rendering and ENeRF-Outdoor. Against the closest competitor Im4D [48] (concurrent work), 4K4D gains +2.18 dB PSNR and 13× speedup (203.6 vs. 15.4 FPS) on DNA-Rendering. Against ENeRF [49], the prior SOTA real-time method, 4K4D improves PSNR by +3.07 dB (DNA-Rendering) and +0.36 dB (ENeRF-Outdoor) while rendering 30× and 12.5× faster respectively. IBRNet [90] and K-Planes [19] are far behind in both quality (PSNR 27.8 and 27.5 on DNA-Rendering) and speed (0.1 and 0.6 FPS). Compared to 3DGS [33] on the first frame of each sequence, 4K4D achieves comparable or better PSNR (31.87 vs. 31.16 on DNA-Rendering; 26.54 vs. 21.63 on ENeRF-Outdoor) with 50× and 45× lower storage (4.7 MB vs. 224 MB; 16 MB vs. 715 MB) and higher FPS (241.7 vs. 113.2; 148.6 vs. 88.4), while being less prone to overfitting due to its simpler point-based representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

4K4D sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: K-Planes' 4D feature grid for spatial-temporal regularization, ENeRF's image blending model for view-dependent appearance, and 3D Gaussian Splatting's point-based forward rendering. The key departure is making the image blending network view-direction independent so its outputs can be pre-computed, then compensating for the resulting discreteness with a continuous SH term — a hybrid that no prior method combines. Architecturally, it replaces NeRF-style backward ray-marching with a differentiable depth-peeling algorithm that exploits the hardware rasterizer, shifting the computational bottleneck from network evaluation to GPU rasterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 30× speedup over prior SOTA while simultaneously improving rendering quality (31.2 vs 28.1 PSNR on DNA-Rendering) shifts the practical ceiling for dynamic view synthesis from interactive 1080p to real-time 4K on commodity GPUs, opening the door to consumer-facing volumetric video applications. The 50× storage reduction over 3DGS for dynamic scenes is also practically significant. Open questions remain: the method cannot produce cross-frame point correspondences (limiting downstream tasks like motion analysis), storage grows linearly with frame count (hindering long-sequence modeling), and rendering quality is bounded by input image resolution.
