## Problem

Multiway point cloud mosaicking—globally aligning unordered, partially overlapping point clouds into a unified coordinate system—remains challenging when overlap is low or scenes undergo spatial and temporal changes. Existing pairwise methods fail under low overlap, and end-to-end learning-based multiway pipelines sacrifice inference accuracy for trainability, while classical optimization approaches remain sensitive to pairwise registration errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242550/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered set of n partially overlapping 3D point clouds (from LiDAR or RGB-D scanners) with no assumed trajectory or ordering, including pairs with as little as 10% overlap and scenes with large geometric and temporal changes. It outputs globally consistent poses (rotations in SO(3) and translations in R³) for each point cloud, effectively producing a unified mosaic. The pipeline operates on superpoint-level features extracted via KPConv-FPN, with dense correspondences resolved at half the original point resolution.

## Method

The Wednesday pipeline combines a novel pairwise matcher (ODIN) with a decoupled global optimization sequence. ODIN uses a two-stream attention architecture (explicit one-way self/cross-attention plus direct cross-attention) with iteratively updated overlap-aware attention masks, and applies a diffusion-based UNet to denoise the predicted correlation matrix before Sinkhorn-based optimal transport matching. For multiway alignment, the pipeline first performs global rotation averaging, then re-estimates relative translations via a globally optimal maximum-sphere-overlap (branch-and-bound) consensus maximization, followed by Levenberg-Marquardt translation optimization, and finally a diffusion-based Transformer that jointly refines all rotations and translations conditioned on point cloud features and pairwise edge constraints.

## Results

On the multiway registration task, the full ODIN+Wednesday pipeline achieves 2.01° rotation error and 0.42 m translation error on NSS, 2.32°/8.4 cm on 3DMatch, 6.44°/26.5 cm on 3DLoMatch, and 2.18°/4.76 cm on KITTI — reducing rotation error by up to 80% (NSS) and translation error by up to 27% over the best prior method (RMPR). For pairwise registration, ODIN reaches 69.73% recall on NSS (vs. 58.72% for PEAL), 95.8% on 3DMatch, 81.2% on 3DLoMatch, and 99.8% on KITTI, consistently outperforming all baselines in both rotation and translation error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ODIN+Wednesday pipeline dominates all 12 baselines across all four datasets in both rotation and translation error. On the most challenging NSS dataset (spatiotemporal changes), Wednesday reduces rotation error from 10.87° (RMPR, best prior) to 2.24° with Predator, and further to 2.01° with ODIN — an 80% reduction. On 3DLoMatch (low-overlap pairs), the translation error drops from 34.4 cm (RMPR) to 26.5 cm, and rotation error from 10.18° to 6.44°. On KITTI, rotation error is halved (4.69° → 2.18°) and translation error reduced by 25% (6.38 → 4.76 cm). For pairwise registration, ODIN outperforms PEAL by 11% recall on NSS (69.73% vs. 58.72%), by 10° rotation error on 3DLoMatch (12.61° vs. 23.15° for GeoTransformer), and nearly halves both rotation and translation errors on KITTI (0.14°/3.6 cm vs. 0.23°/6.8 cm for PEAL). Classical baselines (FPFH, FCGF, D3Feat) are far behind on NSS, with recall below 25% versus ODIN's 69.73%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODIN extends the transformer-based pairwise registration lineage (GeoTransformer, PEAL) by adding two key ingredients: iterative overlap-aware attention masking that feeds predicted 3D overlap scores back into the attention learning loop, and a diffusion-based UNet that denoises the predicted correlation matrix before matching. For the multiway stage, Wednesday deliberately departs from the recent trend toward end-to-end learnable pose graph optimization (LMPR, LIRTS, RMPR, DeepMapping2) in favor of a decoupled classical pipeline—rotation averaging, a novel globally optimal sphere-overlap translation re-estimation, and Levenberg-Marquardt position averaging—capped by a diffusion-based joint refinement adapted from PoseDiffusion. The paper's positioning is explicitly anti-end-to-end: it argues that representational and algorithmic choices made for trainability (e.g., differentiable rotation manifolds, IRLS) sacrifice inference accuracy, and instead selects each module for its best possible performance at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 82% rotation error reduction on the NSS benchmark (a dataset specifically designed with large spatiotemporal changes) and consistent 20–50% improvements across 3DMatch, 3DLoMatch, and KITTI establish a new practical benchmark for multiway point cloud mosaicking, particularly in scenarios where scenes change between scans. The globally optimal robust translation re-estimation via maximum sphere overlap is a clean algorithmic contribution that outperforms exhaustive RANSAC in both speed and inlier count, and could be reused in other geometric estimation pipelines. Open questions include scalability to very large point cloud sets (hundreds or thousands of scans), real-time applicability given the multi-stage pipeline, and whether the diffusion-based final optimization generalizes beyond the training distribution of pose configurations. The reliance on ground-truth poses for training the diffusion denoiser and the KPConv feature extractor also limits deployment in settings where such supervision is unavailable.
