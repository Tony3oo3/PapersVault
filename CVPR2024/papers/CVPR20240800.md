## Problem

State-of-the-art multi-person multi-view 3D pose estimation methods rely on expensive 3D ground-truth poses from dense camera systems, while optimization-based alternatives that avoid such labels underperform significantly. This paper asks whether a learning-based model can be trained entirely without 2D or 3D ground-truth poses, using only calibrated multi-view images and noisy 2D pseudo poses from an off-the-shelf detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240800/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images from C calibrated cameras (5 HD views in Panoptic) and 2D pseudo poses generated offline by Mask R-CNN + HRNet as supervision signals. It outputs 3D joint positions (P×J×3) for all detected persons and 3D root (mid-hip) locations. Notably, the root localization network consumes only single-channel root-heatmaps rather than the full 15-channel joint heatmap stack used by VoxelPose, reducing input dimensionality and inference cost.

## Method

Built on the VoxelPose architecture (heatmap_net_2d → root_net → pose_net_3d), SelfPose3d replaces all supervised losses with two self-supervised objectives. For 3D root localization, it trains on a synthetic dataset of randomly placed 3D points projected into multi-view root-heatmaps, regularized by a cross-affine-view consistency loss that enforces invariance under two random affine augmentations. For 3D pose estimation, it applies two random affine transforms to the multi-view input, obtains bottleneck 3D poses from each, then projects them cross-wise (pose from view-set 1 rendered in view-set 2's space and vice versa) using differentiable Gaussian heatmap rendering, and compares the rendered 2D joints/heatmaps against affine-transformed pseudo 2D poses via L1 and L2 losses. An adaptive supervision attention mechanism mitigates pseudo-label noise: a hard attention drops the worst-performing view for L1 joint loss, while a soft attention (ResNet-18 backbone producing per-pixel attention maps with an all-ones regularization) reweights the L2 heatmap loss.

## Results

On the Panoptic dataset, SelfPose3d achieves AP50=96.4, AP100=98.5, Recall@500=99.6, and MPJPE=24.5 mm without any 2D or 3D ground-truth poses, compared to fully-supervised VoxelPose (AP50=98.3, MPJPE=17.7 mm) and TEMPO (AP50=99.1, MPJPE=14.7 mm). On Shelf and Campus, SelfPose3d (trained with pseudo 3D poses from Panoptic) reaches average PCP of 95.1 and 87.9 respectively, approaching fully-supervised VoxelPose (97.0 and 96.7) and MvP (97.4 and 96.6). The method substantially outperforms optimization-based baselines: MvPose achieves only AP50=2.97 and MPJPE=84.2 mm on Panoptic, while ACTOR reports MPJPE=168.4 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfPose3d is closest in performance to the fully-supervised VoxelPose [57], trailing by 1.9 points in AP50 (96.4 vs. 98.3) and 6.8 mm in MPJPE (24.5 vs. 17.7), while achieving a higher Recall@500 (99.6 vs. 98.8). Against the strongest fully-supervised methods TEMPO [15] (MPJPE=14.7) and MvP [63] (MPJPE=15.8), the gap widens to roughly 10 mm in MPJPE, reflecting the inherent noise in pseudo 2D labels. The advantage over optimization-based methods is dramatic: MvPose [18] achieves only AP50=2.97 and MPJPE=84.2 mm on Panoptic, and ACTOR [49] reports MPJPE=168.4 mm, indicating that non-learning approaches struggle with multi-person interactions from sparse cameras. On Shelf and Campus, SelfPose3d (95.1 / 87.9 average PCP) narrows the gap to fully-supervised VoxelPose (97.0 / 96.7) and MvP (97.4 / 96.6) to within 2–9 points, while still substantially outperforming the optimization-based 3DPS [2] (77.5 / 84.5). Notably, SelfPose3d uses only root-heatmaps (1 input channel) for 3D localization versus VoxelPose's 15-channel full-joint heatmaps, making it computationally lighter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the VoxelPose architecture (Tu et al., ECCV 2020) — a voxel-based multi-person multi-view 3D pose estimator — by replacing its supervised training with self-supervised objectives, while adapting the differentiable heatmap rendering technique from Zhang et al. (CVPR 2020) to an online, cross-affine-view setting. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate paradigms: learning-based methods that require expensive 3D ground truth from dense camera rigs, and optimization-based methods that enforce geometric consistency but lack learned representations. The key departure is introducing cross-affine-view consistency (projecting bottleneck 3D poses across two differently augmented views) and adaptive supervision attention as the supervisory signals that substitute for 3D labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is reducing the barrier to training multi-person multi-view 3D pose estimators: instead of requiring a dense camera system to capture 3D ground truth, one only needs a calibrated sparse camera setup and an off-the-shelf 2D pose detector. However, a measurable gap remains versus fully-supervised methods (AP50: 96.4 vs. 98.3; MPJPE: 24.5 vs. 17.7 mm on Panoptic), and the ablation showing that ground-truth 2D poses in the same framework nearly close this gap (AP50: 98.8) suggests the bottleneck is pseudo-label quality rather than the self-supervised objective itself. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the VoxelPose architecture, whether it transfers to truly unconstrained/wild multi-view settings, and whether the adaptive attention mechanism can be replaced by more principled uncertainty estimation.
