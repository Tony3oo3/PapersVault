## Problem

Existing uncertainty estimation techniques for deep networks each excel in narrow benchmark settings (e.g., failure prediction or OOD detection) but no single method performs uniformly well across diverse real-world challenges such as data corruption, label noise, and long-tailed class distributions. This paper identifies that the lack of a unified, robust uncertainty estimation recipe limits the deployment of reliable AI in safety-critical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240468/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs a class label together with a softmax confidence score that serves as the uncertainty estimate. For downstream applications like long-tailed classification, the per-sample maximum softmax confidence from a first-stage SURE training is reused as a re-weighting signal in a second-stage fine-tuning, requiring no architectural modification to the backbone.

## Method

SURE integrates three complementary recipes for increasing entropy on hard samples—RegMixup regularization (heavy Mixup with β=10 as an additional loss term), a correctness ranking loss that aligns confidence with historical per-sample correctness proportions, and a cosine similarity classifier that replaces the final linear layer to emphasize directional alignment over magnitude—with two flat-minima optimization techniques, Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA). The total training objective is L_ce + λ_mix·L_mix + λ_crl·L_crl, optimized via SAM-SGD with cosine annealing and SWA in the final epochs. The key insight is that the synergistic combination across the regularization, classifier, and optimization dimensions yields substantially better uncertainty calibration than any individual component, as confirmed by ablation studies showing each addition incrementally improves AURC and AUROC. The approach is backbone-agnostic, validated across ResNet, VGG, DenseNet, WideResNet, and DeiT architectures without task-specific modifications.

## Results

On failure prediction with ResNet-18, SURE achieves 96.14% accuracy and 2.97 AURC on CIFAR-10, 80.49% accuracy and 45.81 AURC on CIFAR-100, and 69.55% accuracy and 93.46 AURC on Tiny-ImageNet, consistently outperforming all individual techniques and the combined FMFP baseline. With the stronger WRNet backbone, SURE reaches 97.02% accuracy and 1.79 AURC on CIFAR-10, surpassing OpenMix (which uses additional OOD data) by 0.53 AURC. On the noisy-label benchmark Food-101N (20% noise), SURE attains 88.0% top-1 accuracy, exceeding the previous SOTA Jigsaw-ViT (86.7%) by 1.3% without any task-specific adjustments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURE outperforms every individual technique (MSP, RegMixup, CRL, SAM, SWA) and their combination FMFP across all three datasets and all four metrics with ResNet-18, with the largest gains in AURC (e.g., 45.81 vs. 55.03 for FMFP on CIFAR-100). SURE also surpasses OpenMix, which leverages additional OOD training data, on both CIFAR-10 (AURC 2.97 vs. 4.68) and CIFAR-100 (AURC 45.81 vs. 53.83) without using any extra data. On the noisy-label task, SURE achieves 88.0% on Food-101N, beating Jigsaw-ViT (86.7%) by 1.3% and WarPI (85.9%) by 2.1%, while tying Jigsaw-ViT at 89.0% on Animal-10N. For long-tailed classification, SURE with uncertainty-aware re-weighting reaches 73.24% on CIFAR100-LT (IF=10), exceeding GLMC+MaxNorm (72.33%) by 0.91%, though it trails slightly on CIFAR10-LT (IF=100: 86.93% vs. 87.57%). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that RegMixup and SWA contribute the most individual gains, while the full combination yields the best AURC of 45.81 on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

SURE is positioned as a consolidation of existing uncertainty estimation techniques into a unified training recipe, rather than a fundamentally new method. It draws on RegMixup [59] and CRL [54] for loss-level regularization, the cosine similarity classifier [23, 33] for the output layer, and SAM [19] + SWA [35] for optimization (a pairing first recommended by FMFP [81]). The key conceptual departure from prior work is the argument that no single technique dominates across diverse real-world challenges (corruption, label noise, long-tailed distribution), and that their synergistic combination consistently outperforms any individual component—a finding the authors validate through extensive ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of SURE lies in providing a single, task-agnostic training recipe that matches or exceeds specialized methods for noisy labels (Food-101N: 88.0% vs. 86.7% for Jigsaw-ViT, which uses extra self-supervised pre-training) and long-tailed classification without any task-specific modifications. This suggests that much of the benefit attributed to specialized architectures or losses in these settings may actually stem from improved uncertainty calibration during training. Open questions include whether the recipe transfers to non-image modalities, whether the specific hyperparameter choices (β=10, SWA start epoch) generalize to larger-scale training, and whether the synergy is truly additive or whether one or two components (RegMixup + SWA appear most impactful in ablation) could suffice with better tuning.
