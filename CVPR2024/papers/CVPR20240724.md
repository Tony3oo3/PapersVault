## Problem

Current text-to-4D generation methods face a three-way tradeoff: text-to-image models produce realistic appearance but no motion and suffer from the Janus problem (inconsistent 3D structure across viewpoints); 3D-aware text-to-image models fix structure but sacrifice appearance; and text-to-video models add motion but degrade both appearance and 3D structure. No existing technique combines these complementary strengths into a single pipeline that simultaneously achieves high-quality appearance, consistent 3D geometry, and plausible motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt (e.g., 'a space shuttle launching') and produces a parametric 4D scene represented as a neural radiance field with separate static and dynamic multi-resolution hash tables, which can be rendered from arbitrary camera positions and time steps. During optimization, the scene is rendered at different resolutions for each diffusion model: 256×256 for the 3D-aware model (4 views), 512×512 for the VSD update (1 view), and 320×576 for the video model (16 frames). The final output is a fully navigable dynamic 3D scene, not merely a fixed video.

## Method

The core contribution is hybrid score distillation sampling (SDS), a three-stage alternating optimization that blends gradient signals from three pre-trained diffusion models. Stage 1 optimizes a static scene using 3D-aware SDS with MVDream (dynamic hash table frozen) to establish consistent geometry without the Janus problem. Stage 2 alternates between 3D-aware SDS and variational SDS (VSD) with Stable Diffusion (finetuned via LoRA with camera conditioning) to improve photorealistic appearance while preserving structure. Stage 3 introduces video SDS with Zeroscope to add temporal motion, alternating among all three gradient sources with the dynamic hash table unfrozen only during video updates and the static hash table learning rate reduced to protect appearance. The 4D representation uses a hash-encoding-based neural radiance field that decomposes the scene into static and dynamic feature grids queried via trilinear (spatial) and quadrilinear (spatiotemporal) interpolation, decoded by small MLPs into density and color.

## Results

4D-fy achieves a CLIP Score of 34.2 compared to MAV3D's 33.9 on 28 text prompts. In a user study with 26 evaluators, 67% of users prefer 4D-fy overall, with strong margins in appearance quality (92%) and 3D structure quality (89%). Motion quality is roughly on par (41% preference for 4D-fy), and text alignment is slightly favored (52%). Ablations confirm that removing image guidance drops CLIP to 29.3, while removing 3D-aware guidance degrades 3D structure despite maintaining high CLIP (35.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is MAV3D [Singer et al., ICML 2023], a two-stage text-to-4D method that first optimizes with a text-to-image model and then fine-tunes with a text-to-video model. 4D-fy wins on CLIP Score (34.2 vs. 33.9) and shows statistically significant user preference in appearance quality (92%), 3D structure quality (89%), text alignment (52%), and overall preference (67%). Motion quality is essentially a tie (41% preference for 4D-fy), which the authors attribute to MAV3D using a proprietary text-to-video model. The key qualitative advantage is that 4D-fy avoids the Janus problem (multiple faces/heads from inconsistent viewpoints) that plagues MAV3D, thanks to the 3D-aware SDS stage. The ablation 'w/o hybrid SDS' (which mimics MAV3D's sequential training) drops to 33.8 CLIP and loses 5% in overall preference, confirming that the alternating blending of all three gradient sources is what gives 4D-fy its edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score distillation sampling (SDS) paradigm from DreamFusion by blending gradients from three distinct diffusion models—3D-aware text-to-image (MVDream), standard text-to-image (Stable Diffusion via VSD from ProlificDreamer), and text-to-video (Zeroscope)—in an alternating optimization scheme. The closest prior work, MAV3D, uses a strictly sequential two-stage approach (T2I then T2V), whereas 4D-fy's key departure is the hybrid alternating schedule that keeps all three supervision signals active throughout training. The 4D representation itself is a straightforward extension of Instant NGP's multi-resolution hash encoding with an additional time-varying dynamic hash table, following the static/dynamic decomposition of SUDS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first text-to-4D method built entirely on open-source pretrained models, 4D-fy lowers the barrier for academic research in dynamic scene generation and provides a reproducible baseline. The three-way tradeoff framing (appearance vs. 3D structure vs. motion) and the ablation studies give the community a clearer map of which supervision signal drives which quality dimension. However, motion remains limited to simple movements, generation is slow (120k total iterations), and evaluation relies on CLIP score and a small user study—more sophisticated 4D metrics and faster generation (e.g., via large-scale pre-training or dynamic 3D Gaussians) remain open problems.
