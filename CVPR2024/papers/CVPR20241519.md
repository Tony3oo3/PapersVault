## Problem

State-of-the-art text-based video editing relies on diffusion models to generate every frame, which is computationally expensive (minutes per clip) and often requires scarce paired text-video datasets for training. The paper asks whether diffusion models are truly necessary for all frames, or whether the task can be decomposed to achieve comparable quality at a fraction of the cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241519/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (default 16 frames, resized to 384×672) and a text prompt describing the desired edit. HED edge maps are extracted from each frame as structural conditions. The output is a full set of edited video frames that match the prompt while preserving the original motion and layout. The framework extends to longer videos by selecting multiple keyframes and interpolating between consecutive pairs.

## Method

MaskINT disentangles video editing into two stages. Stage 1 uses ControlNet with extended cross-frame self-attention to jointly edit only the first and last keyframes in a zero-shot manner, ensuring appearance consistency between them. Stage 2 introduces a structure-aware frame interpolation module built on a non-autoregressive masked generative transformer: both RGB frames and HED edge maps are tokenized with a 2D Retina-VQ tokenizer, embedded through separate learned projections plus spatial and temporal positional encodings, and processed by a window-restricted attention transformer (spatial 1×h×w and spatial-temporal N×h_w×w_w windows). The model is trained with Masked Token Modeling on video-only datasets, conditioning on the edge-map tokens and the first/last frame tokens while masking intermediate color tokens. At inference, all intermediate frames are generated in parallel via iterative non-autoregressive decoding (32 steps by default), yielding 5–7× faster inference than pure diffusion baselines while matching their temporal consistency and prompt alignment.

## Results

On DAVIS, MaskINT achieves a prompt consistency (P.C.) of 0.311, temporal consistency (T.C.) of 0.952, warping error (W.E.) of 9.5×10⁻³, and long-term consistency (L.C.) of 27.7×10⁻³, while generating a 16-frame clip in 22 seconds. On ShutterStock, the corresponding values are 0.304, 0.971, 8.6×10⁻³, and 22.3×10⁻³. The headline advantage is speed: MaskINT is ~5.5× faster than ControlVideo (120 s) and ~7× faster than TokenFlow (150 s), while remaining within a small margin on quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskINT trades a modest quality gap for a large speed gain. On DAVIS, it lags TokenFlow (T.C. 0.977, W.E. 7.0, L.C. 19.6) and ControlVideo (T.C. 0.975, W.E. 6.9, L.C. 23.9) by roughly 0.02 in temporal consistency and 2–8×10⁻³ in warping/long-term error, while matching their prompt consistency (0.311 vs. 0.314–0.317). Against the per-frame ControlNet baseline, MaskINT substantially improves temporal consistency (0.952 vs. 0.914) and warping error (9.5 vs. 36.6), confirming that joint keyframe editing plus structure-guided interpolation is far superior to independent frame editing. Tune-a-Video achieves comparable temporal consistency but requires 20 minutes per clip, making it impractical. The key differentiator is inference time: MaskINT's 22 s is 5.5× faster than ControlVideo, 7× faster than TokenFlow, and ~55× faster than Tune-a-Video, while staying within a small margin on all four quality metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskINT sits at the intersection of two research threads: non-autoregressive masked generative transformers (MaskGiT, MaskViT, MAGVIT) and diffusion-based video editing (ControlNet, TokenFlow, ControlVideo, Tune-a-Video). Its key conceptual departure is disentangling video editing into a diffusion-based keyframe editing stage and a non-autoregressive interpolation stage, rather than applying diffusion to every frame as prior zero-shot video editing methods do. The window-restricted attention mechanism is borrowed from MaskViT, the iterative masked decoding from MaskGiT, and the spatial conditioning from ControlNet, but the explicit structural (edge-map) conditioning of a non-autoregressive transformer for frame interpolation is, to the authors' knowledge, novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–7× inference speedup over diffusion-based video editing while maintaining comparable temporal and prompt consistency offers a practical path toward real-time or near-real-time text-based video editing, a setting where iterative denoising across all frames is prohibitive. The ablation showing MaskINT's interpolation module substantially outperforming dedicated VFI methods (FILM, RIFE) by ~5 dB PSNR suggests that generative models with structural guidance can handle large-motion interpolation better than flow-based regression. However, the method is limited to structure-preserving edits (style, appearance) and cannot handle structural changes like object identity swaps, and its quality is bottlenecked by the upstream diffusion editor and edge detector. An open question is whether replacing the diffusion stage with a token-based editor (e.g., Muse) could further close the efficiency gap end-to-end.
