## Problem

Conventional prompt learning methods for CLIP overfit to the training distribution and degrade significantly under distribution shifts at test time. While prior work introduces uncertainty into prompts or performs test-time fine-tuning, none explicitly models the relationships between training and test distributions, leaving the method unable to handle arbitrary or combined shifts (covariate, label, concept, conditional, and joint) in a unified way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241884/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes a single RGB test image and the full set of class-name strings for the task; no test labels or additional test samples are required. The image is encoded by the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder and the class names by the frozen text encoder, and these features—along with a sampled training prompt—are fed into a 2-layer transformer that outputs a Gaussian-distributed test prompt. This prompt is injected into both the image and text encoders, and the final output is a softmax class prediction averaged over multiple prompt samples (N_t × N_s Monte Carlo draws).

## Method

Any-shift prompting introduces a hierarchical Bayesian model where a training prompt v_s (a Gaussian learned on downstream data) conditions the generation of a test prompt v_t, explicitly linking training and test distributions in latent space. A shared 2-layer transformer inference network generates both the prior (conditioned on one test image + all class names) and the variational posterior (conditioned on a batch of images + their ground-truth labels) of the test prompt, with the KL divergence between them driving the prior to encode batch-level distributional information. A pseudo-shift training mechanism simulates distribution shifts by treating the current mini-batch as pseudo-test data and previous mini-batches as pseudo-training data, so the model learns to bridge distributions without ever seeing actual test data. At inference, the test prompt is sampled from the prior in a single feedforward pass—no backpropagation or fine-tuning—and is injected into both CLIP encoders to generalize features and classifiers to the specific test distribution.

## Results

On covariate shift (8 datasets), any-shift prompting achieves the best accuracy without test-time optimization: 98.16% on PACS, 86.54% on VLCS, 85.16% on Office-Home, and 60.93% on DomainNet, outperforming CoCoOp by 1.16–2.77 points. On label shift (11 datasets), it achieves the best harmonic mean of 79.21 (Base 82.36, New 76.30), surpassing MaPLe (78.55) and BPL (77.43). On concept shift, it reaches 71.12% on ImageNet-Superclass (+1.35 over CoCoOp), and on conditional shift, 81.74% on Entity-30 (+2.22 over CoCoOp). On the joint shift benchmark Open-Office-Home, it achieves 84.50% mean accuracy, a 2.31-point improvement over CoCoOp (82.19%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (frozen zero-shot), any-shift prompting improves accuracy by 2.03 points on PACS, 5.11 on VLCS, 4.81 on Office-Home, and 6.85 on DomainNet, confirming that the learned prompts add substantial value over raw CLIP. Against CoOp and CoCoOp (the standard prompt-learning baselines), the method wins on all eight covariate-shift datasets by 1.16–2.77 points without any test-time optimization, and by 0.52–3.53 points when combined with TPT. Against DPL (domain prompt learning), it leads by 1.09–2.55 points on the four shared domain-generalization benchmarks. Against BPL (Bayesian prompt learning), it outperforms on all four ImageNet variants by 0.30–0.52 points. Against TPT (test-time prompt tuning), it matches or exceeds on 7 of 8 datasets without any test-time optimization, and when combined with TPT it surpasses CoCoOp+TPT on all 8 datasets. On label shift, it beats MaPLe (the strongest baseline) by 0.66 in harmonic mean and leads on 7 of 11 datasets in new-class accuracy; on concept and conditional shift it consistently outperforms CoCoOp by 1.35–2.22 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt learning line for CLIP (CoOp, CoCoOp) by introducing a hierarchical probabilistic framework that explicitly models the relationship between training and test distributions through a learned test prompt. It departs from Bayesian prompt learning (BPL), which only captures uncertainty in the training prompt, and from test-time prompt tuning (TPT), which requires per-sample optimization, by generating a distribution-aware test prompt in a single feedforward pass via a transformer inference network. The pseudo-shift training mechanism, which treats different mini-batches as pseudo-training and pseudo-test distributions, draws conceptual parallels to meta-learning for domain generalization but is applied specifically to prompt generation rather than model parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a unified, optimization-free approach to handling multiple types of distribution shifts (covariate, label, concept, conditional, and joint) simultaneously, which is more realistic than prior work that typically addresses one shift type at a time. Its practical advantage—requiring only a single forward pass at test time while matching or exceeding methods that perform test-time optimization—makes it attractive for deployment scenarios where per-sample adaptation is infeasible. Open questions include whether the pseudo-shift approximation remains valid under extreme or novel shifts not seen during training, how the approach generalizes beyond CLIP to other vision-language architectures, and whether the 2-layer transformer inference network is sufficient for more complex multi-modal tasks.
