## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation relies on the static scene assumption, causing moving objects to receive inaccurate depth supervision from the reprojection loss. Existing remedies such as automasking or explicit motion estimation introduce their own ambiguities and require auxiliary networks. This paper exploits the observation that most dynamic objects in outdoor scenes (cars, pedestrians, bicycles) contact the ground, providing a reliable depth anchor that can serve as self-supervision without ground-truth depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single monocular RGB image and outputs a per-pixel depth map, with no additional inputs required. During training, it additionally consumes pre-computed instance segmentation masks (from Mask2Former with Swin-S backbone) to identify dynamic-class objects, but these masks are not needed at test time. Experiments use resolutions of 128×416, 192×640, and 256×832, and predicted depths are normalized via median scaling within a 0–80 m range for evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a coarse-to-fine two-stage training strategy that can be plugged into any existing self-supervised DE network without architectural changes. In the coarse stage, dynamic-class objects are masked out of the reprojection loss, and a novel Ground-contacting-prior Disparity Smoothness Loss (GDS-Loss) is applied: it extends the standard edge-aware smoothness loss by applying a large vertical-gradient weight (γ=100) between object pixels and their ground-contacting neighbors, forcing object depth to align with the reliably predicted ground depth. In the fine stage, the full unmasked reprojection loss is re-enabled to recover detailed object-surface depth, while a regularization loss L_REG with a cost-volume-based weighting factor λ_cv penalizes divergence from the frozen coarse-stage network specifically in regions where the cost-volume-induced depth disagrees with the reliable coarse prediction, thereby preventing the reprojection loss from corrupting moving-object depth. The strategy requires only pre-computed segmentation masks at training time and adds roughly 11% training overhead.

## Results

On Cityscapes at 192×640, Ours-MonoViT achieves the best absrel of 0.088, outperforming the original MonoViT (0.106) by 17.0% and the multi-frame SOTA DynamicDepth (0.103 at 128×416) without requiring test-time multi-frame processing. The coarse-to-fine strategy yields consistent improvements across all base architectures: Monodepth2 (0.125→0.102), HR-Depth (0.120→0.100), CADepth (0.124→0.097), and MonoViT (0.106→0.088) in absrel. On KITTI at 192×640, Ours-MonoViT achieves absrel 0.096 and a1 0.904, surpassing DynamicDepth (absrel 0.096, a1 0.897) and all other single-frame methods. The strategy adds only ~11.1% training time (13.5h→15h on RTX 3090) while requiring no architectural modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's coarse-to-fine strategy is applied as a plug-in to four base architectures (Monodepth2, HR-Depth, CADepth, MonoViT), each showing substantial absrel improvements on Cityscapes 192×640: 18.4%, 16.7%, 21.8%, and 17.0% respectively. Against the multi-frame SOTA DynamicDepth [9], Ours-MonoViT wins on Cityscapes 128×416 (absrel 0.096 vs 0.103) using only single-frame inference, and on KITTI 192×640 it matches absrel (0.096) while leading in sqrel (0.696 vs 0.720), rmse (4.327 vs 4.458), and a1 (0.904 vs 0.897). Ours-CADepth and Ours-MonoViT both outperform RM-Depth [22] on Cityscapes (absrel 0.097/0.088 vs 0.100) without requiring an additional object motion estimation network. Against methods that use semantic information at test time (DynamicDepth, SGDepth [27], FSRE-Depth [25]), Ours-MonoViT achieves superior results using semantic masks only during training. On KITTI, Ours-MonoViT also outperforms Petrovai et al. [40] (absrel 0.096 vs 0.106) and PackNet [17] (0.096 vs 0.111), while the improvements over Struct2Depth [3], Gordon et al. [16], Li et al. [32], Lee et al. [31], and InstaDM [30] are all >10% in absrel on Cityscapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conventional self-supervised monocular depth estimation pipeline (reprojection loss + edge-aware disparity smoothness loss from Monodepth2) by introducing a coarse-to-fine training strategy that addresses the moving-object problem. It departs from prior approaches that estimate 3D object motions or disentangle object motion for cost-volume construction by instead exploiting the simple geometric observation that dynamic objects in outdoor scenes contact the ground, formalized as the GDS-Loss. The cost-volume-based weighting factor in the fine stage draws on multi-frame cost-volume construction (Watson et al.), but repurposes it as a regularization signal rather than a primary depth estimation mechanism. The strategy is deliberately architecture-agnostic, demonstrated as a plug-and-play addition to CNN-based (Monodepth2, HR-Depth, CADepth) and Transformer-based (MonoViT) backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ground-contacting prior offers a lightweight, supervision-free mechanism to resolve depth ambiguity for dynamic objects without requiring auxiliary motion estimation networks or multi-frame inference, making it broadly applicable to existing self-supervised DE pipelines. The plug-and-play nature (only requiring pre-computed instance segmentation masks during training) lowers the barrier for adoption. Limitations include the assumption that dynamic objects contact the ground (failing for flying objects or elevated structures), dependence on external segmentation quality, and evaluation restricted to outdoor driving scenes. Open questions include whether the prior generalizes to indoor or aerial settings, whether the segmentation dependency can be eliminated, and whether the coarse-to-fine schedule is optimal versus joint training with the GDS-Loss.
