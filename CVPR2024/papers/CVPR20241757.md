## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF methods assume sharp input images and degrade significantly when motion blur is present, as blur corrupts inter-frame relationships, depth, and optical flow priors needed to constrain scene geometry. No NeRF-based method previously addressed the combined challenge of deblurring and dynamic scene representation, leaving a gap for real-world monocular video where extended exposure inevitably introduces motion blur from both camera and object movement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241757/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video of 24 frames with motion blur (left blurry sequences from the Stereo Blur Dataset) along with camera poses estimated via COLMAP, and produces a dynamic radiance field that synthesizes sharp novel views from arbitrary camera positions. The exposure time of each frame is discretized into n=7 sub-timestamps, and the model must jointly recover camera trajectory and object motion within each exposure window. Evaluation uses the corresponding right sharp stereo sequences as ground truth for PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

## Method

DyBluRF models motion blur by discretizing each frame's exposure into n timestamps and jointly learning camera poses (via SE(3) Lie-algebra interpolation, following BAD-NeRF) and object DCT trajectories predicted by a dynamic MLP. A key insight is using DCT coefficients to represent global object motion trajectories, enabling the model to simulate object-induced blur within an exposure window and to warp rays across non-adjacent frames for cross-time rendering, which enforces temporal consistency over the entire video rather than only between neighbors. A static MLP is blended with the dynamic one via a learned weight, and Extreme Value Constraints (EVC) replace naive averaging when applying depth and optical flow priors from blurry images, taking the minimum depth and maximum flow across sub-timestamps to avoid translucent artifacts at blurry edges. The DCT basis itself is made learnable, initialized with standard cosine values, and jointly optimized with the network.

## Results

On the curated 6-scene subset of the Stereo Blur Dataset, DyBluRF achieves a mean PSNR of 25.66 dB, SSIM of 0.895, and LPIPS of 0.079 for novel view synthesis from motion-blurred monocular video. This represents a 2.33 dB PSNR improvement over the strongest direct dynamic NeRF baseline (NSFF, 23.33 dB) and a 1.87 dB gain over the best 2D-deblurring-then-NeRF pipeline (JCD+NSFF, 23.79 dB). The LPIPS gap is particularly large: 0.079 vs. 0.181 for NSFF, indicating substantially better perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyBluRF outperforms all four direct dynamic NeRF baselines by a wide margin: it surpasses NSFF (the strongest baseline) by 2.33 dB in PSNR and 0.102 in LPIPS, and beats RoDynRF by 6.38 dB in PSNR. Against the 2D-deblurring-then-NeRF pipelines, DyBluRF still leads by 1.87 dB PSNR over the best combination (JCD+NSFF at 23.79 dB), demonstrating that modeling blur in 3D space preserves spatial-temporal scene geometry better than pixel-space deblurring followed by NeRF training. The 2D deblurring pipelines also show inconsistent behavior: JCD+NSFF (23.79 dB) outperforms Restormer+NSFF (23.17 dB), while for RoDynRF the two deblurring methods yield nearly identical results (~20.8 dB), suggesting that 2D preprocessing introduces geometry artifacts that degrade downstream 3D reconstruction. Qualitatively, existing dynamic NeRF methods produce visibly blurry and artifact-laden novel views in both static and dynamic regions when fed motion-blurred inputs, whereas DyBluRF generates sharp, temporally consistent results.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyBluRF sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: static-scene deblurring NeRF (BAD-NeRF, Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF) and dynamic NeRF (NSFF, DVS, RoDynRF, HyperNeRF). It borrows the exposure-time camera trajectory modeling from BAD-NeRF, the DCT-based trajectory prior from non-rigid reconstruction (Valmadre & Lucey), and the disocclusion-weight and scene-flow-loss machinery from NSFF, but repurposes them jointly for the first time to handle motion blur in dynamic scenes. The key conceptual departure is the cross-time rendering strategy, which extends temporal consistency enforcement beyond adjacent frames to the entire temporal range via DCT trajectories, and the Extreme Value Constraints that address the specific failure mode of depth/flow priors under blur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first NeRF-based method to explicitly model and remove motion blur in dynamic scenes, filling a gap that becomes critical for real-world video capture where long exposures are unavoidable. The finding that 2D deblurring followed by dynamic NeRF produces inferior results compared to modeling blur in 3D space is a useful practical insight for the community. However, the experimental scope is narrow (6 scenes, 24 frames each, single dataset), training takes ~2 days per scene, and the method relies on COLMAP-estimated poses, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, real-time applications, and more complex multi-object dynamic scenes.
