## Problem

Building video-based dialogue systems is constrained by GPU memory: the LLM and visual encoder already consume most of it, leaving little budget for the temporal modeling needed to understand video dynamics. Existing temporal modeling approaches face a trade-off—joint or separate space-time modeling requires full encoder fine-tuning (high cost, knowledge loss), while parameter-efficient methods like temporal pooling or position embeddings offer only limited temporal sensitivity. The paper asks whether effective temporal modeling can be added to frozen image-language models without video instruction tuning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip of T frames (each split into N non-overlapping patches) and a text prompt. Each frame is independently encoded by frozen CLIP, while the BT-Adapter branch processes all T×N tokens with divided space-time attention. For LLM input, the combined patch tokens are pooled along the time dimension, yielding only N tokens—the fewest among all compared temporal modeling strategies. Outputs include conversational text responses (video dialogue), video-text retrieval rankings (R@K), and action class predictions (Top-K accuracy).

## Method

BT-Adapter is a branching separate space-time network that runs alongside (not inside) the frozen CLIP backbone, using divided space-time attention: tokens at the same spatial position across frames attend temporally, then each frame's tokens attend spatially. A multi-level backbone-branch interaction module uses sigmoid-gated learnable weights to selectively combine CLIP and branch outputs at each of the last K+1 layers, with a zero-initialized temporal projection and a learnable video [CLS] token for stable training. Pretraining employs an asymmetric tube-token masking strategy (masking ~70% of tokens only in the branch, leaving CLIP unmasked) combined with three objectives: Video-Text Contrastive (VTC), Masked Branching Token Alignment (MBTA, aligning unmasked branch tokens to CLIP via MSE), and Masked Branching Cross-Modal Alignment (MBCA, contrastively aligning branch patch tokens with text). This design preserves pretrained multimodal knowledge while enabling strong temporal modeling, and the pretrained adapter can be plugged into any image conversation model using the same CLIP version to enable zero-shot video conversation.

## Results

On the VideoChatGPT generative benchmark, BT-Adapter-LLaVA without instruction tuning (ZS) achieves a mean score of 2.46, surpassing all instruction-tuned baselines including VideoChatGPT (2.38), VideoChat (2.29), and VideoLLaMA (1.98). With instruction tuning (FT), BT-Adapter-LLaVA reaches 2.69 mean score, outperforming the previous SOTA by 0.31. On zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, BT-Adapter achieves R@1 of 40.9 on MSR-VTT, 35.6 on DiDeMo, and 37.0 on ActivityNet using only 2M pretraining samples and 8 V100 GPUs for 3 hours — 130× fewer GPU hours than UMT, 560× fewer than TVTSv2, and 2687× fewer than InternVideo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In video conversation, BT-Adapter (ZS) outperforms all instruction-tuned baselines on average (2.46 vs. VideoChatGPT's 2.38), with the largest margin on ActivityNet-QA (46.1% vs. 35.2% accuracy, +10.9 points). After instruction tuning, BT-Adapter (FT) extends its lead to 2.69 mean score, beating VideoChatGPT by 0.31 and VideoLLaMA by 0.71. On zero-shot retrieval, BT-Adapter surpasses UMT-L by 7.6 points on MSR-VTT R@1 (40.9 vs. 33.3) and by 5.1 points on ActivityNet R@1 (37.0 vs. 31.9), while slightly trailing UMT-L on LSMDC R@1 (19.5 vs. 20.0). Compared to TVTSv2, BT-Adapter wins on MSR-VTT (40.9 vs. 38.2) and DiDeMo (35.6 vs. 34.6) with 2M pretraining samples versus 8.5M. Against InternVideo, BT-Adapter matches MSR-VTT R@1 (40.9 vs. 40.7) while using 2687× fewer GPU hours. The zero-shot plug-and-play setting (Table 4) shows BT-Adapter consistently improves LLaVA, MiniGPT4, and InstructBLIP by 0.25–0.68 points on temporal understanding without any additional instruction tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

BT-Adapter builds directly on CLIP's frozen spatial encoder and Bertasius et al.'s divided space-time attention, but departs from both joint-ST and interpolate-style temporal modeling (which require full encoder finetuning) by introducing a parallel branching network that operates alongside the backbone without disrupting its forward pass. This branching design is conceptually closest to STAN [20], but differs in its zero-initialized temporal projection, learnable video CLS token, multi-level selective combination, and the novel asymmetric masking with MBTA/MBCA training objectives. In the video conversation space, the paper positions against VideoLLaMA, VideoChat, VideoChatGPT, and LLaMA-Adapter, which rely on limited temporal modules (position embeddings, pooling) and require video instruction tuning, whereas BT-Adapter achieves effective temporal modeling while remaining parameter-efficient and pluggable into any CLIP-based image chatbot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single 3-hour pretraining run on 8 V100 GPUs produces a temporal adapter that can be dropped into LLaVA, MiniGPT4, or InstructBLIP to enable video conversation without collecting or tuning on video instruction data, dramatically lowering the barrier to video dialogue. The finding that zero-shot transfer from image to video conversation is competitive with instruction-tuned SOTAs challenges the prevailing assumption that video-specific instruction data is necessary. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos (beyond the 8-frame setting), whether it works with non-CLIP backbones, and whether the zero-shot advantage persists as video conversation benchmarks grow in complexity and length.
