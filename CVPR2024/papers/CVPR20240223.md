## Problem

Reconstructing HDR video from alternating-exposure LDR sequences remains difficult under large camera or object motion: existing flow-based methods produce ghosting artifacts in occluded or textureless regions, while attention-based methods aggregate irrelevant content causing local inconsistencies. Moreover, these alignment modules are computationally expensive, precluding real-time processing. Off-the-shelf optical flow networks are sub-optimal for HDR fusion because they assume photometric consistency between frames, which is violated when inputs have different exposures.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3 LDR frames with two alternating exposures (e.g., EV-3, EV+0, EV-3) or 5 frames with three alternating exposures, along with per-frame exposure times, at resolutions up to 1920×1080. The middle frame serves as the reference, and the method outputs a single reconstructed HDR frame for that reference. The entire pipeline (flow estimation + fusion) runs in 25 ms at 720p on a single GPU, making it the first real-time HDR video reconstruction method for alternating-exposure sequences.

## Method

HDRFlow consists of a lightweight feed-forward flow network and a U-Net fusion network. The flow network uses multi-size large kernel depth-wise convolutions (7×7, 9×9, 11×11) at 1/16 resolution to expand the receptive field for large-motion modeling at negligible cost, predicting bidirectional optical flows in ~10 ms. A novel HDR-domain alignment loss (HALoss) supervises the flow by computing photometric consistency between tonemapped clean HDR frames (via a differentiable μ-law) rather than LDR frames, making it robust to exposure differences; a luminance mask restricts the loss to over/under-exposed regions where alignment matters most. Training incorporates Sintel synthetic data with ground-truth forward flow and RAFT-generated backward flow to improve robustness under large motions. The fusion network blends five linear HDR frames (reference, two warped neighbors, two original neighbors) using learned per-pixel weights to produce the final ghost-free HDR output.

## Results

On the DeepHDRVideo dataset (1536×813), HDRFlow (Vimeo+Sintel) achieves 43.25 dB PSNR_T and 77.29 HDR-VDP-2 for 2-exposure, and 40.56 dB PSNR_T and 72.42 HDR-VDP-2 for 3-exposure, outperforming all baselines on PSNR_T and HDR-VDP-2. On the Cinematic Video dataset (1920×1080), it reaches 39.30 dB PSNR_T (2-exp) and 36.65 dB PSNR_T (3-exp), surpassing the second-best by up to 1.08 dB and 1.58 dB respectively. Inference time is 35 ms (2-exp) and 50 ms (3-exp) on DeepHDRVideo, approximately 10× faster than the next fastest method (Chen et al. at 522 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDRFlow outperforms all seven baselines on PSNR_T and HDR-VDP-2 across both exposure settings on DeepHDRVideo, with the largest margin over Chen et al. [2] (+0.77 dB PSNR_T, +2.49 HDR-VDP-2 in 2-exp) and LAN-HDR [6] (+2.08 dB PSNR_T, +3.68 HDR-VDP-2 in 3-exp). However, Chen et al. [2] retains a slight edge on SSIM_T (0.9620 vs. 0.9520 in 2-exp; 0.9569 vs. 0.9535 in 3-exp), suggesting marginally better local structural fidelity despite lower overall quality. On the Cinematic Video dataset, HDRFlow wins on all three metrics for both 2- and 3-exposure, with up to 1.58 dB PSNR_T improvement over LAN-HDR in the 3-exposure case. The speed advantage is dramatic: 35 ms vs. 522 ms (Chen) and 415 ms (LAN-HDR), making HDRFlow the first real-time alternating-exposure HDR video method. Compared to the off-the-shelf RAFT+fusion baseline, HDRFlow gains +0.65 dB PSNR_T and is ~15× faster (35 ms vs. 532 ms), confirming that task-specific flow training is essential for HDR fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-based HDR video reconstruction line (Kalantari et al. 2019, Chen et al. 2021) by replacing general-purpose optical flow estimators (SPyNet, RAFT) with a purpose-built flow network that incorporates multi-size large-kernel depth-wise convolutions borrowed from the segmentation/detection literature (Ding et al. 2022, Chen et al. 2023). It departs from both flow-based and attention-based alignment (LAN-HDR, Chung et al. 2023) by introducing an HDR-domain alignment loss (HALoss) that supervises flow in the tonemapped HDR space rather than relying on LDR photometric consistency, which is violated by alternating exposures. The training scheme also diverges from prior work by incorporating Sintel synthetic data with ground-truth flow to improve large-motion robustness, whereas previous methods trained exclusively on real videos like Vimeo-90K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HDRFlow is the first method to achieve real-time (25 ms at 720p) HDR video reconstruction from alternating-exposure sequences, making it practically deployable on mobile cameras where prior methods (500–900 ms) are infeasible. The conceptual finding that sharp flow boundaries are not necessary for high-quality HDR fusion—only accurate alignment in under/over-exposed regions—opens the door to simpler, faster alignment strategies beyond this specific architecture. Limitations include the reliance on a fixed two- or three-exposure setting, the use of a pre-trained RAFT to generate backward flow for Sintel (introducing potential bias), and evaluation limited to synthetic benchmarks; real-world deployment on consumer hardware and extension to arbitrary exposure counts remain open.
