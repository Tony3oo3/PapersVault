## Problem

Existing customized video generation methods can only optimize one aspect of a video—either the spatial subject identity or the temporal motion pattern—but not both simultaneously. Dreamix and TuneA-Video each fine-tune a single dimension, while AnimateDiff produces generalized animations lacking motion diversity. This paper tackles the underexplored task of concurrently customizing both subject appearance and motion pattern in generated videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–5 static images of a target subject, 1–3 reference videos demonstrating a desired motion, and a free-form text prompt describing the scene context. At inference, a randomly selected training image is additionally fed through a CLIP encoder to provide appearance guidance to the motion adapter. The output is a 32-frame video at 8 fps generated via 50-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance, where the subject identity and motion pattern are independently swappable across different text prompts.

## Method

DreamVideo decouples customization into two independently trainable adapters on a frozen ModelScopeT2V 3D UNet. For subject learning, it first optimizes a pseudo-word embedding via Textual Inversion to capture the coarse concept, then trains a lightweight identity adapter (a bottleneck with skip connection using GELU activation) in the cross-attention layers to recover fine appearance details. For motion learning, it trains a structurally similar motion adapter in all temporal transformer layers, augmented with a condition linear layer that injects a CLIP image embedding of a randomly sampled training frame as appearance guidance—forcing the adapter to encode pure motion rather than spatial appearance. The two adapters (85M parameters total) are combined at inference, enabling any subject to be paired with any motion without retraining, and each can also be used independently for single-aspect customization.

## Results

On the joint subject+motion customization task, DreamVideo achieves the best CLIP-T (0.314), CLIP-I (0.665), and DINO-I (0.452) scores among all methods, while using only 85M trainable parameters compared to 1.21B (AnimateDiff) and 1.31B (ModelScopeT2V). For subject-only customization, DreamVideo reaches DINO-I 0.475 and CLIP-T 0.295 with just 11M parameters, outperforming Dreamix (823M params) on DINO-I and CLIP-T. For motion-only customization, DreamVideo attains the highest CLIP-T (0.309) and Temporal Consistency (0.975), surpassing both ModelScopeT2V and Tune-A-Video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnimateDiff, DreamVideo wins on all three content metrics (CLIP-T +0.016, CLIP-I +0.008, DINO-I +0.020) but trails on Temporal Consistency (0.971 vs. 0.982), which the authors attribute to AnimateDiff's tendency to produce small, camera-like motions. Against ModelScopeT2V (full fine-tuning), DreamVideo leads on CLIP-T (+0.009), CLIP-I (+0.045), and DINO-I (+0.087) while using roughly 15× fewer parameters, though it is slightly behind on Temporal Consistency (0.971 vs. 0.976). Against LoRA fine-tuning, DreamVideo dominates on every metric (CLIP-T +0.028, CLIP-I +0.021, DINO-I +0.043, T. Cons. +0.007) despite using ~14× more parameters, indicating that the decoupled adapter design is more effective than generic low-rank adaptation for this task. In the subject-only setting, DreamVideo matches Dreamix on CLIP-I (0.701 vs. 0.705) and Temporal Consistency while surpassing it on DINO-I (0.475 vs. 0.459) and CLIP-T (0.295 vs. 0.284) with 75× fewer parameters. In the motion-only setting, DreamVideo outperforms both ModelScopeT2V and Tune-A-Video on CLIP-T and Temporal Consistency, confirming that the appearance-guidance mechanism successfully decouples motion from subject identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamVideo builds on the ModelScopeT2V pre-trained video diffusion model (3D UNet with spatiotemporal transformers) and incorporates Textual Inversion as the initialization step for subject learning. It departs from prior customized video generation work—Dreamix (subject-only via full fine-tuning) and Tune-A-Video (motion-only via temporal attention tuning)—by introducing two lightweight bottleneck adapters (identity and motion) that are trained independently and composed at inference. The parameter-efficiency design draws on PEFT techniques from NLP and vision, while the appearance-guidance mechanism in the motion adapter is a novel trick to prevent spatial-temporal entanglement during motion learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly customize both subject identity and motion pattern in video generation, addressing a gap where prior approaches handled only one aspect and suffered from fusion conflicts when naively combined. The decoupled adapter design offers practical flexibility (any subject × any motion) at a fraction of the parameter cost (85M vs. 1.21B for AnimateDiff), making it accessible for real-world applications. A key open limitation is the inability to handle multiple subjects with multiple motions simultaneously, which the authors suggest would require training a general customized video model. The finding that cross-attention layers dominate subject learning while all temporal layers contribute equally to motion learning provides useful architectural guidance for future customization work.
