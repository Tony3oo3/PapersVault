## Problem

Traditional anisotropic Laplace-Beltrami operators (ALBOs) used in shape analysis are heuristically designed without theoretical grounding, and Finsler manifolds—which naturally encode asymmetric anisotropy beyond what Riemannian geometry allows—have remained largely unexplored in computer vision. This paper derives a theoretically justified Finsler-Laplace-Beltrami operator (FLBO) from the Finsler heat equation, providing a principled generalization of ALBOs that captures direction-dependent, non-symmetric diffusion on shapes.

## Input / Output

The method operates on triangular mesh representations of 3D shapes, with SHOT descriptors as per-vertex input features. The Finsler metric is parameterized per face by a Randers pair (M_{αθ}, ω_θ), where M encodes anisotropic Riemannian diffusivity along principal curvature directions and ω introduces an asymmetric component scaled by hyperparameter τ. The output is a dense correspondence map between a source shape and target shapes, evaluated by geodesic accuracy at radii r ∈ {0, 0.01} on full-mesh and partial-mesh benchmarks.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is showing that the Finsler heat equation on a Randers manifold, under short-time and small-ω assumptions, reduces to a homogeneous anisotropic heat equation with diffusivity D_{F*} = M* − ω*ω*ᵀ, from which the FLBO is defined as −div_X(D_{F*} ∇_X u). This operator is a special case of ALBO but is derived from Finsler geometry rather than heuristic design, and it inherits the full spectral decomposition machinery. For discretization, a generalized cotangent weight scheme computes stiffness matrix entries using the Finsler diffusivity. For shape correspondence, the FLBO's eigendecomposition defines θ-oriented spectral convolution kernels with Chebyshev polynomial filters (S=16 terms, learnable coefficients), which are summed over 8 uniformly spaced angles and fed into a SplineCNN trained with Adam for 100 epochs. Setting τ=0 recovers the standard ALBO of ACSCNN, making the Finsler extension a strict generalization.

## Results

On FAUST Remeshed, the FLBO-based method (τ=0.1) achieves 62.61% accuracy at r=0 and 82.90% at r=0.01, outperforming ACSCNN (61.95% / 82.46%) and FieldConv (53.39% / 75.01%). On SCAPE Remeshed, it reaches 46.94% at r=0 and 72.44% at r=0.01, again surpassing ACSCNN (46.13% / 71.70%). On the partial shape benchmarks (SHREC'16 Cuts and Holes), the method is competitive with ACSCNN, winning on several categories (e.g., David: 35.85 vs 32.80 on Holes; Centaur: 40.13 vs 38.10 on Cuts) while remaining within 1–2 points on others. The best τ value varies slightly per dataset (τ=0.1–0.3), but performance is robust across the tested range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: FieldConv [33] and ACSCNN [29]. Against FieldConv, the FLBO method achieves a large and consistent margin across all four datasets (e.g., +9.2 points on FAUST r=0, +9.5 on SCAPE r=0), confirming that the anisotropic Finsler-based spectral convolution substantially improves over the simpler field-convolution approach. Against ACSCNN (which is the direct predecessor and coincides with the proposed method when τ=0), the FLBO method yields a modest but consistent improvement: +0.66 points on FAUST (r=0), +0.81 on SCAPE (r=0), and wins on 5 out of 8 categories on SHREC'16 Cuts and 5 out of 8 on Holes. The gains are small (typically <1 point) but systematic, and the authors note that the additional preprocessing cost for computing the Finsler metric and FLBO is negligible relative to neural network training time. The method is not highly sensitive to the τ hyperparameter, with τ=0.1–0.3 all performing comparably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives a theoretically grounded anisotropic Laplace-Beltrami operator (FLBO) from Finsler geometry, specifically from the heat equation on Randers metric manifolds, in contrast to prior ALBOs (Andreux et al. 2014; Boscaini et al. 2016; Li et al. 2020) where anisotropy was injected heuristically along curvature directions. The FLBO is a special case of the existing ALBO family but is justified by the Finsler heat kernel rather than empirical design, and it introduces a new asymmetric anisotropy degree of freedom via the linear component ω of the Randers metric. For the shape correspondence application, the paper directly builds on the ACSCNN pipeline (Li et al. 2020), replacing its ALBO eigendecomposition with the FLBO eigendecomposition while retaining the SplineCNN architecture and Chebyshev spectral filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main value is conceptual: it provides the first rigorous derivation of an anisotropic surface operator from Finsler geometry, opening a previously unexplored mathematical framework to the CV/shape-analysis community and suggesting that the asymmetric anisotropy inherent to Finsler metrics could capture directional asymmetries (e.g., wind, current) that Riemannian ALBOs cannot. However, the empirical gains over ACSCNN are modest (≤1% accuracy), and the derivation relies on strong simplifying assumptions (small ‖ω‖, short-time analysis, specific initial condition) that limit generality. Open questions include whether the assumptions can be relaxed, whether non-Randers Finsler metrics yield meaningfully different operators, and whether the asymmetric component provides practical benefits on tasks where directional asymmetry is physically meaningful.
