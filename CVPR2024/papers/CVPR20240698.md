## Problem

Machine understanding of abstract freehand scene sketches remains largely unexplored due to a severe lack of annotated data and the fundamental statistical differences between sketches and photographs (sparsity, absence of color and texture, multi-level abstraction). Existing scene sketch segmentation methods rely on pixel-level annotations or semi-synthetic datasets that do not generalize to genuine freehand sketches, and no prior work has demonstrated that brief language captions alone can supervise open-vocabulary scene sketch segmentation.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes 224×224 bitmap scene sketches paired with brief natural-language captions from FS-COCO, requiring no pixel-level annotations. At inference, given a bitmap sketch and a user-specified set of category text prompts (formatted as 'A sketch of *'), the model produces a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask by computing cosine similarity between category text embeddings and patch-level visual features, upscaling via bi-cubic interpolation, and assigning each pixel to the highest-scoring category. A learnable threshold can additionally isolate individual categories by filtering low-confidence pixels.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level hierarchical training strategy on a CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder that shares weights across both levels. Level 1 (Holistic) aligns a Visual Scene Token with caption embeddings via triplet loss, while Level 2 (Category-level) introduces cross-attention between frozen text category tokens and visual patch features in the 7th, 10th, and 12th transformer layers to sharpen per-category disentanglement. A key architectural modification is a dual-path ViT where the second path replaces standard q-k self-attention with v-v self-attention (softmax(VV^T/√d)·V), incorporated directly into training rather than only at inference as in CLIP Surgery. Fine-tuning is parameter-efficient: only Layer Normalization weights, three learnable visual prompt tokens, and cross-attention linear projections are updated, while all other CLIP weights remain frozen. A learnable threshold that increases over training epochs forces progressively stronger category separation, and multi-layer features (l7, l10, l12) are used in the category-level triplet loss for richer semantic signals.

## Results

On the FS-COCO test set of 500 freehand scene sketches, the method achieves 73.48 mIoU, 85.54% pixel accuracy (Acc@P), and 87.02% stroke accuracy (Acc@S). This represents a 37-point improvement over zero-shot CLIP segmentation (48.54% Acc@P) and a 13.5-point mIoU gain over the strongest fine-tuned baseline, CLIP Surgery** (59.98 mIoU). On the independent Ge et al. freehand sketch dataset, the method also outperforms the fully-supervised LDP baseline by 16.8 mIoU points (53.94 vs. 37.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all eight baselines across every metric. Against fully-supervised image segmentation methods (ZegCLIP, DenseCLIP, Grounded-SAM) and the fully-supervised sketch method LDP, the gap is largest: LDP achieves only 33.04 mIoU / 56.23% Acc@P versus 73.48 / 85.54% for the proposed method, confirming that pixel-level supervision on semi-synthetic sketches does not transfer to freehand scene sketches. Among language-supervised baselines, zero-shot CLIP Surgery (52.63 mIoU) is the strongest, but the proposed method still leads by 20.85 mIoU points. After fine-tuning all baselines on the FS-COCO training set, CLIP Surgery** (v-v attention at both training and inference) narrows the gap to 13.5 mIoU points (59.98 vs. 73.48), while SegCLIP★ and GroupViT★ trail further behind at 49.26 and 45.71 mIoU respectively. On the independent Ge et al. dataset, the method again beats LDP by 16.8 mIoU points, demonstrating generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation line (GroupViT, SegCLIP, CLIP Surgery) to the underexplored domain of freehand scene sketches, where prior work has been limited to fully supervised methods on semi-synthetic data (Ge et al., SketchSeger) or single-category sketches. It adopts the v-v self-attention mechanism from CLIP Surgery but departs by incorporating it directly into training rather than only at inference, and introduces a two-level hierarchical training scheme (holistic scene encoding + category-level disentanglement) with cross-attention between text and vision branches that has no direct precedent in the sketch literature. The use of triplet loss with hard mining and learnable visual prompts (from Jia et al.) for efficient fine-tuning of the frozen CLIP ViT encoder positions the work at the intersection of prompt-tuning and contrastive alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that brief language captions alone—without any pixel-level annotations—can drive competitive scene sketch segmentation, opening a path to scalable sketch understanding where per-pixel labeling is prohibitively expensive. The user study reveals concrete failure modes (ambiguous sketches, semantically interchangeable categories, correlated co-occurring categories, high category counts) that define a clear research agenda for aligning machine and human sketch interpretation. A key open question is whether the two-level hierarchical training and v-v-in-training strategy transfer to other sparse/abstract visual domains beyond sketches, and whether the inherent limitation of language supervision for correlated categories can be overcome without reverting to pixel-level supervision.
