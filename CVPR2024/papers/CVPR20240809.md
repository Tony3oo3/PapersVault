## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D human pose and shape estimators (e.g., HMR2.0, PARE) still produce body self-intersections in a large fraction of frames (up to 39.2% on 3DPW-test). Existing remedies—iterative collision penalization, BVH-based optimization, or training on self-contact data—are non-differentiable, do not guarantee complete elimination of self-intersections at inference, and cannot be integrated into an end-to-end training pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240809/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

CLOAF takes an initial self-intersection-free SMPL pose Θ₀ and a target pose Θ₁ (which may contain self-intersections) and produces a collision-free SMPL trajectory between them, with the final pose as the corrected output. In video settings, Θ₀ is the corrected pose from the previous frame; for single images, alternatives include random jittering of intersecting limbs or retrieval from a precomputed keypose dictionary. The motion field is either a learned 6-layer MLP (with Fourier features, n_f=20) or a user-defined simplified field, and the ODE is integrated with an adaptive Dormand-Prince solver over S=1000 surface-sampled points.

## Method

The key insight is that ODE solutions are diffeomorphisms (Picard-Lindelöf theorem), so trajectories of initially distinct points can never cross, guaranteeing zero self-intersections. The authors reformulate the spatial ODE in SMPL parameter space by computing the Jacobian J of the SMPL transformation and solving the over-constrained system dΘ/dt = J† f_ω(X(Θ), t | Θ₀, Θ₁) via Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse, which acts as an exact inverse-kinematics projection that preserves the parametric body shape prior at every integration step. The motion field f_ω is a ShapeFlow-style 6-layer MLP trained on AMASS pose pairs to minimize endpoint distance plus a trajectory loss that prevents the model from stalling. Because the entire pipeline is differentiable, CLOAF can be appended to any pose estimator (e.g., EFT) for end-to-end fine-tuning with a self-intersection-aware loss, and it also accepts arbitrary user-defined motion fields (blended via Bézier functions) to model interactions with objects or edit motion.

## Results

On the 3DPW-test set, CLOAF achieves a 0.0% collision rate (Col.Rate@0), completely eliminating self-intersections, compared to 5.7–39.2% for all baselines. It also delivers the best temporal smoothness with an acceleration error of 9.4 mm/s², substantially lower than the 14.3–16.1 mm/s² range of competing methods. In terms of pose accuracy, CLOAF reports MPJPE of 82.3 mm and P-MPJPE of 53.2 mm, which are within ~1 mm of the best baselines (e.g., HMR2.0 at 82.0/52.7), indicating that collision elimination comes at negligible accuracy cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLOAF decisively wins on the primary metric (collision rate) against all seven baselines, reducing Col.Rate@0 from 39.2% (HMR2.0), 36.1% (PARE), 23.7% (TUCH), 17.8% (EFT), 15.5% (SPIN), 9.2% (SMPLify-X/Opt.Cones), and 5.7% (COAP) down to 0.0%. On temporal smoothness (Accel.Err), CLOAF (9.4 mm/s²) outperforms every baseline by 4–7 mm/s², with the largest gap over HMR2.0 (16.1) and Opt.Cones+ (16.4). On pose accuracy, CLOAF is competitive but slightly behind: P-MPJPE of 53.2 mm vs. 52.4 mm for Opt.Cones and 52.7 mm for HMR2.0, a gap the authors attribute to ground-truth data containing self-intersections that their method correctly removes. In the fine-tuning experiment (Table 3), differentiable CLOAF improves EFT's P-MPJPE from 58.1 to 55.4 mm while halving its collision rate from 6.5% to 3.4%, outperforming the non-differentiable Opt.Cones baseline (57.5/6.1). Overall, CLOAF trades a sub-millimetre accuracy loss for a guaranteed zero-collision guarantee and markedly smoother trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLOAF sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: ODE-based diffeomorphic shape deformation (ShapeFlow, MeshODE, NMF) and parametric human body modeling (SMPL). Prior collision-avoidance methods (SMPLify, SMPLify-X, COAP, TUCH) rely on iterative optimization with explicit collision detection and soft penalty losses, making them non-differentiable and unable to guarantee zero self-intersections. CLOAF departs from both lines by reparameterizing the ODE in SMPL parameter space via a Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse projection (an exact, non-iterative inverse-kinematics step), thereby inheriting the diffeomorphism guarantee of ODEs while enforcing a learned body-shape prior at every integration step. The network architecture for the velocity field is adapted from ShapeFlow, and the Bézier blending for user-defined local fields follows von Funck et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a hard guarantee of zero self-intersections (not merely a reduction) combined with full differentiability, enabling end-to-end fine-tuning of pose estimators without separate collision-detection modules. This is particularly relevant for downstream applications in robotics and animation where self-penetration is unacceptable. Limitations include the requirement for a valid (collision-free) initial pose, dependence on the SMPL parametric model, and evaluation on a single dataset (3DPW). The authors note that ground-truth annotations themselves contain self-intersections, which slightly inflates their error metrics. Open questions include extension to motion generation with temporal priors, multi-person scenes, and non-SMPL body representations.
