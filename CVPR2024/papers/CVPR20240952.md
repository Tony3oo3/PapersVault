## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning (pFL) methods face a fundamental trade-off: full model personalization incurs high computation and communication costs by training two full models per client, while partial model personalization overfits to limited local data and generalizes poorly under test-time distribution shifts. Additionally, no prior work provides generalization bounds or convergence guarantees for pFL methods that employ server-side ensemble knowledge distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240952/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives natural RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, Office-Home at 256×256) or medical chest X-ray images (CheXpert) and produces single-label class predictions or multi-label AUC scores, respectively. Only the adapter parameters (1.41M, 12.6% of the 11.18M ResNet-18 backbone) are trained and communicated per client, while the pretrained backbone remains frozen. The server additionally consumes an unlabeled public distillation dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100 for CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10 for Office-Home/CheXpert) to train the global adapter via knowledge distillation.

## Method

PERADA freezes a pretrained backbone and inserts small adapter modules (with skip connections that reduce to identity at zero parameters) as the sole trainable and communicable components. Each client trains a personalized adapter regularized toward a global adapter (ℓ₂ penalty) and a local adapter initialized from the global adapter; the local adapter is sent to the server. The server then performs ensemble knowledge distillation: it uses the averaged softmax logits of all received local adapters (teachers) on an unlabeled public dataset as pseudo-labels to train the global adapter (student), avoiding direct parameter averaging under heterogeneous data. This bi-level optimization—local adapter training on the client and server-side KD on public data—yields a global adapter that encodes ensemble knowledge while the personalized adapter captures local specifics. The paper derives generalization bounds under FL covariate shift showing that small distillation error and a distillation distribution close to the target tighten the bound, and proves O(1/√T) convergence to stationary points under non-convex settings.

## Results

PERADA achieves 91.82% local-test accuracy on CIFAR-10, 83.58% on Office-Home, and 76.98% AUC on CheXpert, while training only 2.82M parameters (12.6% of the full ResNet-18) and communicating just 1.41M parameters per round. On out-of-distribution generalization, PERADA reaches 59.05% on CIFAR-10 global-test, 48.53% on CIFAR-10-C, and 77.88% on CheXpert global-test. The method outperforms the best full-model pFL baseline (DITTO) by +1.61% on CIFAR-10 local-test and +5.23% on CIFAR-10 global-test, while using roughly 4× fewer trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PERADA outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and evaluation metric. On CIFAR-10, it beats the strongest full-model pFL method DITTO by +1.61% local-test (91.82 vs 90.21) and +5.23% global-test (59.05 vs 53.82), while training only 2.82M vs 22.36M parameters. Against the closest partial-model adapter baselines (FEDALT/FEDSIM with adapters), PERADA gains +4.06% on CIFAR-10 local-test and +27.08% on CIFAR-10 global-test, demonstrating that server-side KD is critical for generalization. On Office-Home (feature non-IID), PERADA leads by +0.34% local-test and +6.58% global-test over the best baseline (FEDALT output-layer). On CheXpert (medical X-ray), PERADA achieves +4.85% local-test and +3.21% global-test over FEDALT (adapter), the strongest medical-domain baseline. The ablation PERADA w/o KD already surpasses all baselines, confirming that the adapter architecture itself is effective, while KD provides an additional +5.24% boost on CIFAR-10 global-test and +4.08% on CIFAR-10-C.

## Relation to Prior Work

PERADA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) personalized federated learning, where full-model methods (DITTO, PFEDME, APFL) train two complete models per client and partial-model methods (FEDALT, FEDSIM, FEDBN) split parameters but suffer from poor generalization; (2) parameter-efficient fine-tuning via adapters, which the paper repurposes from transfer learning to make pFL communication and computation lightweight; and (3) server-side ensemble knowledge distillation in FL (FEDDF, FedMD), which the paper adapts to train only adapter parameters rather than full models. The key departure is combining all three: using adapters as the personalization mechanism while leveraging server-side KD to build a well-generalized global adapter that regularizes local adapters, and providing the first convergence guarantees for FL under server-side distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical tension in pFL deployment: clients with limited resources need lightweight personalization that still generalizes under test-time distribution shifts (e.g., varying imaging conditions in medical domains). The theoretical contributions—generalization bounds showing how KD and distillation data distribution affect both global and personalized model performance, plus convergence rates under non-convex bi-level optimization—fill a notable gap in the FL theory literature. Open questions include whether the adapter architecture scales to much larger foundation models, how the method performs under more complex heterogeneity patterns (e.g., compound label and feature shifts), and whether the reliance on an unlabeled public distillation dataset limits applicability in privacy-sensitive settings where no public data is available.
