## Problem

Face restoration is inherently ill-posed: under heavy degradation, multiple high-quality outputs are possible, and generative-prior methods (GANs, diffusion models) frequently produce realistic but identity-unfaithful results because they lack personal context. Existing reference-based methods struggle when reference and input images differ in pose or illumination, and no prior work has shown how to personalize a restoration model with a few images while preserving the base model's generative priors.

## Input / Output

At training time the model consumes a synthesized low-quality face image and a randomly sampled high-quality reference image (3–5 references per identity, no alignment required); at inference only the LQ image is needed since identity is encoded in the personalization blocks. The base model operates at 512×512 with random crops, and super-resolution beyond 512×512 is achieved via a tiling strategy. Personalization fine-tuning takes ~500 iterations (~10 min on a single A100) per identity, after which the model is reusable for arbitrary LQ inputs of that person.

## Method

PFStorer freezes a base restoration model (StableSR fine-tuned on FFHQ with SFT blocks) and inserts trainable Personalization Blocks—cross-attention layers between reference-image features (extracted by a frozen StableDiffusion encoder) and intermediate UNet features—so that existing priors are never modified. A per-layer learnable scalar γ (initialized to zero) scales each adapter's contribution, giving fine-grained control over how much personalization is injected at each resolution. A generative regularizer (L_Gen) is added to the diffusion loss: it removes the LQ conditioning and forces the model to reconstruct the identity from reference images alone, preventing the model from taking shortcuts through residual identity information in the LQ input. The full training loss combines the standard diffusion denoising loss, the generative regularizer (weighted by λ_Gen), and a cross-attention map regularizer for the learnable text-embedding token, while the alignment-free training pipeline uses random crops and a second-order degradation model to improve robustness and enable tiling-based super-resolution.

## Results

On the Celeb-Ref heavy-degradation benchmark, PFStorer achieves an ID score of 57.18% (cosine similarity with ArcFace), nearly 20 percentage points higher than the next best method (CodeFormer at 38.33%), and reduces landmark MSE to 18.37, roughly half that of CodeFormer (33.34). It also attains the best LPIPS (0.414) and MUSIQ (64.04) among all compared methods. In a user study with 40 participants and 8,000 total votes, PFStorer is voted best 61% of the time for perceived identity, compared to 25% for the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFStorer decisively outperforms all baselines on the identity metric (ID), scoring 57.18% versus CodeFormer's 38.33%, DR2+SPAR's 30.01%, and DMDNet's 29.51%—a gap of roughly 19–28 percentage points. On landmark alignment (LMSE), PFStorer achieves 18.37, nearly halving CodeFormer's 33.34 and dramatically improving over DMDNet (89.26) and DR2+SPAR (40.82). CodeFormer remains competitive on pixel-level fidelity (PSNR 22.26 vs. 22.62) and SSIM (0.642 vs. 0.679), while DR2+SPAR edges out PFStorer on SSIM (0.701 vs. 0.679), but PFStorer leads on perceptual quality (MUSIQ 64.04 vs. 60.92) and perceptual distance (LPIPS 0.414 vs. 0.422). DMDNet, despite using the same 5 reference images, lags substantially on both identity and landmark metrics, confirming that explicit alignment-based fusion is less robust than learning a neural identity representation. The user study further validates these quantitative findings, with PFStorer receiving 61% of votes for identity fidelity versus 25% for CodeFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFStorer sits at the intersection of two research threads: diffusion-based face restoration (building on StableSR as a base model) and few-shot personalization (drawing on ViCo's cross-attention adapter architecture and textual inversion for the learnable identity token). It departs from DreamBooth-style full fine-tuning, which the authors show degrades the base model's restoration priors, and from reference-based restoration methods (DMDNet, ASFFNet) that require pose/illumination alignment at inference. The key conceptual move is inverting the personalization direction: rather than personalizing a generative model and then adapting it for restoration (as in MyStyle or IdentityEncoder), PFStorer personalizes a restoration model directly, using frozen base weights plus lightweight adapter blocks with a learnable γ to control the degree of identity injection per layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20-point ID similarity gap over CodeFormer (57.18% vs 38.33%) and the 61% user-study win rate demonstrate that identity faithfulness, not just perceptual quality, is a critical and previously under-addressed dimension of face restoration. The practical cost of personalization (~10 minutes on a single A100, 500 iterations) makes this approach deployable for individual users. Open questions include handling appearance drift between reference images and the target (e.g., aging, hairstyle changes), mitigating Stable Diffusion's inherent stochasticity and slow sampling, and whether the generative regularizer's optimal λ_Gen can be made adaptive rather than fixed. The alignment-free training strategy also opens the door to more robust real-world deployment where landmark detection fails.
