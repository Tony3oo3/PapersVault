## Problem

Existing multi-view clustering (MVC) methods assume all views are informative, but in practical scenarios some views can be noisy, causing MVC to underperform even single-view clustering on the best view. The paper identifies this as the Noisy-View Drawback (NVD) and traces it to two design choices in prior methods: shared network parameters that get dominated by noisy views' irreducible clustering loss, and enforced consistent clustering predictions that force informative views to conform to noisy ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes V feature matrices {X^v ∈ R^{N×D_v}} as input, one per view, and produces per-sample cluster assignments (argmax of the final robust soft labels Y_(t)), view-specific representations Z^v, and view-specific soft labels Y^v. Unlike prior MVC methods that fuse views into a single shared representation, MVCAN maintains fully separate encoder-decoder autoencoders per view and only couples them through a block-diagonal scaling matrix W(t) whose entries are updated via normalized mutual information between each view's soft labels and the robust multi-view soft labels.

## Method

MVCAN introduces a parameter-decoupled multi-view clustering objective with two key constraints: (1) all network parameters Θ^v are un-shared across views, and (2) per-view permutation matrices A^v (solved via the Hungarian algorithm) allow each view to have a different cluster-label ordering, so noisy views are not forced to match informative ones. Training proceeds via a two-level iterative scheme: the T-level alternates between computing a scaled multi-view representation Z_(t) = [Z^1 ... Z^V]·W_(t), generating robust soft labels Y_(t) via K-means centroids, and updating the scaling factors w^v_(t+1) = exp(2·NMI(Y^v, Y_(t))) to down-weight noisy views; the R-level then trains each view's autoencoder with a combined reconstruction loss L_r^v and clustering loss L_c^v = ‖T·A^{v*} − Y^v‖_F². Theoretical analysis (Theorems 2–5) proves that the resulting robust soft labels Y_(t) inherit consistency from informative views, exploit complementarity across views with differing cluster assignments, and are robust to views where individual samples are ambiguous.

## Results

On four normal multi-view datasets, MVCAN achieves 98.4% ACC / 95.3% NMI on BDGP, 99.5% / 98.8% on DIGIT, 99.6% / 99.1% on COIL, and 82.6% / 86.7% on Amazon, outperforming the best SVC baseline (DEC-BestV) by 5.8–35.6% ACC. On four real-world datasets, MVCAN reaches 84.8% ACC on DHA, 48.0% on RGB-D, 93.6% on Caltech, and 24.2% on YoutubeVideo, surpassing the best MVC baselines by 1–4% ACC. On noise-simulated datasets, MVCAN maintains 98.0–99.2% ACC (vs. 54.7–98.8% for the best baselines), demonstrating robustness to extreme noise where most MVC methods degrade severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVCAN outperforms all 10 baselines on Amazon (82.6% ACC vs. 77.1% for DIMVC, the next best) and on all four real-world datasets (e.g., 84.8% on DHA vs. 80.2% for SDMVC; 93.6% on Caltech vs. 90.5% for DSMVC). On the easy normal datasets, MVCAN is competitive with the top methods: it slightly trails SDMVC on DIGIT (99.5 vs. 99.8 ACC) and DSIMVC on COIL (99.6 vs. 99.7 ACC), but leads on BDGP NMI (95.3 vs. 95.0) and Amazon by a large margin. The most dramatic advantage appears on noise-simulated datasets: MVCAN achieves 98.0% ACC on NoisyBDGP while CPSPAN collapses to 12.4% and DSMVC to 36.6%; on NoisyAmazon, MVCAN reaches 72.8% ACC versus 54.7% for DSIMVC and 12.4% for CPSPAN. DEC-WorstV (SVC on the worst view) shows that individual noisy views can be extremely uninformative (e.g., 22.2% ACC on NoisyBDGP), confirming the Noisy-View Drawback that MVCAN is designed to address. Overall, MVCAN is the only method that consistently beats DEC-BestV across all 12 datasets without any performance degeneration.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVCAN builds directly on the DEC (Deep Embedded Clustering) paradigm, adopting its soft-label formulation and self-training learning target as the per-view foundation. It departs from the dominant MVC design convention—shared network parameters across views and enforced consistent clustering predictions—by arguing that both assumptions are harmful when views differ in quality. The two-level iterative optimization (T-level for robust target generation via NMI-based view scaling, R-level for per-view parameter training with Hungarian-matched targets) is a new training strategy that replaces the shared-fusion modules used in prior methods like DMJC, DIMC-net, and GP-MVC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes the Noisy-View Drawback (NVD), showing empirically that standard MVC methods can underperform the best single-view baseline when one or more views are noisy—a practical concern in sensor-fusion and multi-modal settings where view quality is not guaranteed. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–5) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why shared parameters and forced consistency fail, and why parameter decoupling plus per-view cluster matching restores consistency, complementarity, and noise robustness. Open questions include sensitivity to initialization and the fixed cluster number K, as well as whether the block-diagonal scaling matrix generalizes to more complex cross-view interactions or incomplete views.
