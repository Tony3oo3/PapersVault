## Problem

MegaPortraits, the state-of-the-art one-shot cross-driving head avatar model, fails to faithfully transfer intense and asymmetric facial expressions due to dimensionality collapse in its 512-dimensional latent expression space (only 18 components capture 99% of variance) and expression leakage into the canonical volume. Additionally, no public multi-view dataset captures the full range of extreme, asymmetric, and nuanced facial motions needed to train and evaluate such capabilities, and no existing model unifies visual-driven and speech-driven animation in a single architecture.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single source image for identity and appearance, combined with either a driver image (visual mode), an audio waveform (speech mode), or both simultaneously. Internally it operates on a 3D canonical volume representation of the source face, and the speech mode additionally generates plausible head rotations and eye blinks that are absent from the audio signal. The FEED dataset used for training and evaluation is captured at 4K resolution across 3 synchronized camera views, and the model is evaluated on 100 FFHQ source images paired with 20 driving images each from MEAD and FEED.

## Method

EMOPortraits modifies MegaPortraits' architecture and training in three key ways: (1) it reduces the expression latent vector dimension from 512 to 128 and introduces a source-driver mismatch loss (L_sdm) with identity-dependent margins to prevent identity leakage into the expression code under imbalanced sampling of extreme expressions; (2) it enforces canonical volume neutrality via a matching loss (L_CV^n) that penalizes expression-dependent variation in the 3D volume across views of the same person, ensuring the volume carries only geometry; and (3) it disentangles head pose from expression by augmenting the CosFace contrastive loss with an additional cross-pose positive pair, enabling the latent expression vector to be predicted from audio alone. For speech-driven mode, an audio encoder is trained with E_motion as a teacher, and a mouth-specific PCA loss (L_PCA) is computed over principal components extracted from a curated mouth-only video, isolating lip-movement directions in the latent space to achieve accurate lip synchronization without requiring full facial motion from the audio signal.

## Results

In image-driven cross-driving synthesis (Table 3), EMOPortraits achieves FID of 59.6 on MEAD and 60.2 on FEED, outperforming MegaPortraits (61.1 / 62.8) and all other baselines. The user preference for motion/expression transfer (UMTN) is dramatically higher: 70.3% on MEAD and 74.6% on FEED versus 22.7% and 16.7% for MegaPortraits, the next-best method. In speech-driven mode (Table 5), EMOPortraits achieves the best FID (28.5) and best SyncNet offset (0.07), substantially outperforming SadTalker (FID 55.6, offset 0.27) and StyleHEAT (FID 62.4, offset 0.41), though it trails slightly on mouth landmark position error (MP 2.53 vs. 2.12 for SadTalker).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMOPortraits decisively outperforms MegaPortraits [9] (its direct predecessor) on expression transfer: UMTN jumps from 22.7% to 70.3% on MEAD and from 16.7% to 74.6% on FEED, while FID improves modestly (61.1→59.6, 62.8→60.2). Identity preservation (CSIM) is comparable on MEAD (0.74 vs. 0.73) but slightly lower on FEED (0.70 vs. 0.73), which the authors attribute to EMOPortraits actually transferring asymmetric expressions rather than leaving the source largely unchanged. Against StyleHEAT [31], EMOPortraits wins on FID (59.6 vs. 71.9) and UMTN (70.3 vs. 3.7) but trails on UAPP (43.4 vs. 3.3 on MEAD; 34.7 vs. 3.8 on FEED). NOFA [32] and UVA [17] require per-source optimization and score far worse on UMTN (3.8 and 0.6 on FEED). FOMM [22] and MetaPortrait [35] are the weakest baselines, with FOMM scoring FID 84.4 and UMTN 2.4 on MEAD. In speech-driven mode, EMOPortraits beats SadTalker [36] on FID (28.5 vs. 55.6) and SyncNet offset (0.07 vs. 0.27) but loses on mouth landmark position (MP 2.53 vs. 2.12) and SyncNet confidence (5.77 vs. 7.58); it also outperforms PC-AVS [39] and MakeItTalk [40] on FID and facial geometry metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of the authors' own MegaPortraits model, which established state-of-the-art one-shot cross-driving head avatar synthesis using a learned latent expression space. The key departures are: (1) a restructured latent expression space (reduced from 512 to 128 dimensions, with PCA-guided analysis revealing dimensionality collapse in the original), (2) new losses enforcing expression-neutrality of the canonical volume and identity disentanglement of expression vectors, and (3) an added speech-driven mode via an audio encoder trained with a mouth-specific PCA loss. For speech-driven avatars, it positions against StyleHEAT, SadTalker, MakeItTalk, and PC-AVS, while for image-driven cross-driving it compares against NOFA, FOMM, UVA, MetaPortrait, and StyleHEAT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FEED dataset (520 multi-view 4K videos of extreme, asymmetric, and uncategorizable expressions) fills a genuine gap in publicly available facial expression data, which is limited to basic emotion categories. The discovery that MegaPortraits' canonical volumes—designed to be expression-free—actually retain significant expression information (43% relative difference) provides a concrete architectural diagnosis for why prior methods fail on intense expressions, and the proposed fix is simple and broadly applicable. Limitations include the absence of body/shoulder generation, degraded performance under extensive head rotation, and a minor identity-preservation trade-off on extreme expressions. Open questions include whether the 128-dimensional latent space generalizes to even more diverse motion vocabularies and whether the speech-driven mode can be extended to longer sequences or multi-speaker scenarios.
