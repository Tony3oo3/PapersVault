## Problem

Neural compression codecs achieve strong rate-distortion performance but require large, expressive decoders that are impractical on hardware-constrained devices. While overfitting a small network per image (as in COIN and Cool-chic) drastically reduces decoding complexity, prior methods fall well short of the RD performance of classical codecs like VTM. This paper closes that gap, matching VTM on images with less than 3k MACs/pixel for decoding and extending the approach to video for the first time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resolutions from 439×720 to 1370×2048) or a single HD video (1080×1920, split into spatial-temporal patches of (30,180,240) to (75,270,320)) and produces a compressed bitstream containing quantized multi-resolution latent grids plus the quantized parameters of a small synthesis and entropy network, all entropy-coded via a range coder. Decoding is autoregressive: the entropy network sequentially predicts each latent entry from its causal neighborhood, and the synthesis network then upsamples and maps the latent grids to pixel values. Because the networks are transmitted as part of the bitstream, the decoder is fully self-contained and can begin streaming frames immediately after receiving the small network parameters.

## Method

C3 overfits a small synthesis network (depth ≤4, width ≤40) and an autoregressive entropy network to each image or video patch jointly with multi-resolution latent grids, using a two-stage quantization-aware optimization. The key improvements over Cool-chic are: (1) soft-rounding with annealed temperature and Kumaraswamy-distributed noise in Stage 1 to better approximate quantization, (2) GELU activations, smaller quantization steps, and a shifted log-scale parameterization for the entropy model, and (3) a conditional entropy model with FiLM layers for resolution-dependent prediction. For video, the method extends to 3D latent grids with a wider spatial context (up to 65 latent pixels) and a learned custom mask to capture fast motion without inflating the entropy model's parameter count. An adaptive variant sweeps architecture choices per instance to find the best RD trade-off, yielding <3k MACs/pixel decoding for images and ~4.4k MACs/pixel for video—less than 0.1% of VCT's decoding complexity.

## Results

On the CLIC2020 image benchmark, C3 matches VTM (the H.266 reference codec) with a BD-rate of −0.1%, and C3 adaptive outperforms VTM by −2.9% BD-rate, while requiring less than 3k MACs/pixel for decoding. On Kodak, C3 performs slightly worse than VTM. On the UVG video benchmark, C3 matches the RD performance of VCT (a competitive neural video codec) while requiring only 4.4k MACs/pixel, less than 0.1% of VCT's decoding complexity. Decoding a 768×512 image takes <100 ms on a CPU (unoptimized research code).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C3 matches VTM on CLIC2020 (−0.1% BD-rate) and outperforms it with per-image adaptation (−2.9%), making it the first neural codec to reach VTM-level RD at <3k MACs/pixel decoding. Against Cool-chic v2, C3 improves BD-rate by 20.8% on CLIC2020 while maintaining similar decoding complexity. On video (UVG), C3 matches VCT in RD performance but uses less than 0.1% of VCT's decoding MACs/pixel (4.4k vs. >4.4M). Among overfitting-based video codecs, C3 is second best in RD (behind HiNeRV and MIMT) but has orders-of-magnitude lower decoding complexity; it widely outperforms FFNeRV. C3 does not yet match the RD of the strongest neural codecs (MLIC+ on images, MIMT/HiNeRV on video), but achieves those RD levels with 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer decoding operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

C3 sits in the lineage of per-instance overfitting neural codecs: COIN [20] first proposed overfitting a neural field to a single image, Cool-chic [43] added a per-image autoregressive entropy model, and Cool-chic v2 [48] pushed RD performance past BPG/HEVC at ~2.3k MACs/pixel. C3 departs from the dominant autoencoder-based neural compression paradigm (BMS, CST, MLIC+, VCT) by retaining the overfitting approach but making targeted improvements to quantization-aware optimization (soft-rounding, Kumaraswamy noise, smaller quantization steps) and architecture (GELU, conditional entropy model, FiLM layers). For video, C3 extends the image-only Cool-chic framework with 3D latent grids, video patches, wider spatial context, and learned custom masking—methodological changes that have no direct precedent in the Cool-chic line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C3 is the first neural codec to match VTM (the H.266 reference implementation) on CLIC2020 while decoding at under 3k MACs/pixel—an order of magnitude below competing neural codecs—and matches VCT on UVG video at less than 0.1% of its decoding complexity. This directly addresses one of the major open problems in neural compression: achieving strong RD performance without prohibitive decoding cost, which is critical for hardware-constrained and streaming applications. Key limitations remain: encoding is very slow (up to 48s per 1000 iterations on the largest images), the autoregressive entropy model is inherently sequential, and the method does not yet match the absolute RD ceiling of the best neural codecs (MLIC+, MIMT). Open questions include whether meta-learning or cross-instance sharing can accelerate encoding, and whether alternative probabilistic models can enable parallel decoding.
