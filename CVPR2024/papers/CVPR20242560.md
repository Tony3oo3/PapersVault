## Problem

Existing graph OOD generalization methods rely on flat environments—either directly provided, randomly split, or inferred into a small fixed number—which fail to capture the hierarchical semantic structure inherent in graph data. In highly heterogeneous datasets like DrugOOD with thousands of distinct training environments (e.g., 6881 scaffolds in IC50-SCA), flat contexts cannot model the interrelationships among environments, leading to performance comparable to or worse than plain ERM. No prior work generates environments in a hierarchical manner for graph invariant learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes graph-structured data (molecular graphs with node and edge features, or general graphs with node attributes) as input and produces a graph-level class label (e.g., IC50/EC50 affinity prediction or sentiment classification) along with inferred hierarchical semantic environment assignments at each level. The input graph is consumed through iterative stochastic edge selection at K hierarchy levels, where each level produces a variant subgraph (environment-relevant) and an invariant subgraph (label-relevant). The final invariant learning stage uses only the coarsest hierarchy's environment labels (e.g., 2 environments) to compute the IRM objective, while the intermediate hierarchies serve as semantic scaffolding.

## Method

The framework has three components: (1) Hierarchical stochastic subgraph generation, where at each hierarchy k a GNN updates node embeddings and a Gumbel-Softmax mechanism stochastically selects edges to partition the graph into variant and invariant subgraphs, with a hierarchical neighbor masking function (Eq. 4) enforcing that edges selected at finer hierarchies are preserved at coarser ones; (2) Hierarchical semantic environment inference, where variant subgraphs at each level are fed to environment-specific classifiers whose softmax outputs yield posterior environment assignments; (3) Robust GIL using the coarsest hierarchy's environments in the standard IRM objective. The training loss combines three terms per hierarchy: an intra-hierarchy environment diversification loss (maximizing mutual information between variant subgraphs and environment labels), an environment-based InfoNCE contrastive loss pulling together same-environment variant subgraph embeddings across hierarchies, and a label-based InfoNCE contrastive loss aligning invariant subgraph embeddings within the same class. The hierarchy progresses from fine-grained (e.g., 6881 environments) to coarse-grained (e.g., 2 environments), allowing the model to capture local environment relationships at lower levels while learning globally diverse, semantically meaningful environments at the top level.

## Results

On the DrugOOD benchmark, the proposed hierarchical semantic environment method achieves the best ROC-AUC across all six datasets (IC50/EC50 × ASSAY/SCA/SIZE). Headline improvements include 70.72% on IC50-SCA (vs. 69.70% for the best baseline CIGA V2), 69.73% on EC50-SCA (vs. 67.98% for GREA), and 80.82% on EC50-ASSAY (vs. 79.24% for GALA). The method also reports up to 1.29% and 2.83% ROC-AUC gains over the best baselines on IC50 and EC50 tasks respectively, with notably low variance (±0.11–0.38) compared to baselines (±0.27–3.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on every DrugOOD dataset where they are reported. Against Euclidean-domain invariant learning methods (ERM, IRM, V-REX, EIIL, IB-IRM), the gains are consistent (1.5–4.5% ROC-AUC), confirming that flat-environment IRM struggles with the high heterogeneity of graph distributions. Among graph-specific baselines, CIGA V2 is the closest competitor on IC50 tasks (e.g., 69.70 vs. 70.72 on IC50-SCA), while GALA leads on EC50-ASSAY (79.24 vs. 80.82) and GREA leads on EC50-SCA (67.98 vs. 69.73). MoleOOD, which infers environments from substructure invariance, underperforms ERM on IC50-SCA (68.02 vs. 68.85), illustrating the failure of flat environment inference with ~6881 fine-grained scaffolds. The method also shows substantially lower variance (±0.11–0.38) than most baselines (±0.40–3.01), suggesting more stable training. On the non-DrugOOD benchmarks (CMNIST-75sp, Graph-SST5, Graph-Twitter), the paper reports comparable or better performance in the appendix, though the primary quantitative evidence is concentrated on DrugOOD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph invariant learning lineage (CIGA, GIL, EIIL) by replacing the flat environment assumption with a hierarchical semantic environment structure. While prior methods either use provided environment labels, randomly split samples, or infer a fixed small number of flat environments, this work generates environments across multiple hierarchical levels using stochastic Gumbel-Softmax subgraph extraction, with intra-hierarchy diversification and inter-hierarchy consistency losses (InfoNCE-based) to regularize the learned environment structure. The core IRM optimization objective is retained, but the environments fed into it are now the high-level semantic environments from the top hierarchy rather than flat or raw labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ERM can outperform invariant learning methods (GREA, MoleOOD) when environments are overly fine-grained (e.g., 6881 scaffolds in IC50-SCA) is a useful diagnostic for practitioners: naive environment granularity can be counterproductive. The hierarchical approach offers a principled way to aggregate fine-grained environments into coarser semantic levels while preserving local structure, which is particularly relevant for molecular property prediction where scaffold-based splits create thousands of near-duplicate environments. Open questions include whether the number of hierarchies and per-hierarchy environment counts can be learned automatically, and whether this framework extends to other graph OOD settings beyond molecular graphs (e.g., social networks, knowledge graphs).
