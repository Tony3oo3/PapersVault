## Problem

Traditional Bayesian inference for structured prediction requires explicit prior and likelihood formulations, making it impractical with deep architectures where distributions are intractable. In the deep learning era, top-down prior information has been largely abandoned in favor of purely data-driven models, even when standalone label data (e.g., large 3D shape collections) is readily available alongside scarce paired data-labels. The paper identifies diffusion models as a new opportunity to seamlessly fuse top-down and bottom-up processes without requiring closed-form distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB image as input and produces a 3D point cloud of 4,096 points as output. A key design constraint is that the reconstruction model is trained on paired image-point cloud data while the prior model is trained on standalone point clouds from a separate, larger dataset (e.g., ShapeNet-R2N2 for Pix3D experiments), explicitly avoiding data leakage between the two. The prior model is frozen and uses off-the-shelf pre-trained weights, so no additional training cost is incurred at inference time.

## Method

BDM couples two diffusion processes—a generative prior model (PVD) and a conditional reconstruction model (PC² or CCD-3DR)—into a joint denoising framework where the prior gradient is fused into the reconstruction gradient at regular intervals (every 32 steps over 1,000 total steps, with 16-step fusion windows). Two fusion strategies are proposed: BDM-M (Merging), a learnable approach that feeds PVD's multi-scale encoder features into PC²'s decoder through zero-initialized convolution layers while freezing the reconstruction encoder, and BDM-B (Blending), a training-free probabilistic point-selection that mixes corresponding points from both point clouds at each denoising step. The key insight is that the multi-step diffusion structure enables flexible, step-wise interaction between the two processes, going beyond the simple gradient summation of standard stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics. Ablations show that late-stage integration is most effective, 50% prior blending ratio is optimal, and 16-step duration balances prior guidance with denoising fidelity.

## Results

On ShapeNet-R2N2 with 100% training data, BDM-B (built on CCD-3DR) achieves CD 56.78 / F1 0.510 on Chair, 56.96 / 0.660 on Airplane, and 66.90 / 0.569 on Car. The advantage is most pronounced under data scarcity: at 10% data, BDM-B reduces Chair CD from 89.79 (CCD-3DR) to 79.26 and lifts F1 from 0.418 to 0.441. On the real-world Pix3D benchmark, BDM-B achieves CD 111.12 / F1 0.466 (Chair), 44.51 / 0.456 (Sofa), and 194.91 / 0.423 (Table), outperforming both PC² and CCD-3DR baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PC², BDM-B consistently improves both CD and F1 across all three ShapeNet categories and all data scales (e.g., Chair 10%: CD 94.67 vs 97.25; Airplane 100%: CD 59.04 vs 65.97). Against the stronger CCD-3DR baseline, BDM-B delivers the largest gains at 10% data—reducing Chair CD by 11.7 points (89.79→79.26) and Airplane CD by 3.95 points (81.29→77.34)—while at 100% data the improvements are more modest (Chair CD 58.47→56.78; Airplane CD 62.77→56.96). One exception: on Car at 100% data, BDM-B has a slightly higher CD (66.90 vs 61.88) but a better F1 (0.569 vs 0.562), suggesting the prior introduces some spatial spread that the F1 threshold captures favorably. On Pix3D, BDM-B wins on all six CD/F1 entries against CCD-3DR (e.g., Chair CD 111.12 vs 111.42, Table CD 194.91 vs 196.28). In a direct comparison with Classifier-Free Guidance on Chair 100% data, BDM-B outperforms CFG (CD 56.78 vs 59.08; F1 0.510 vs 0.495), confirming that the explicit Bayesian fusion is more effective than the implicit guidance mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

BDM builds directly on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) framework and repurposes two existing diffusion architectures—PVD as a shape prior and PC²/CCD-3DR as conditional reconstruction models—into a coupled Bayesian inference loop. Conceptually, it departs from classical MCMC/SGLD Bayesian inference, which requires explicit log-likelihood and log-prior formulations, by replacing those with learned score networks whose gradients are fused step-by-step during denoising. It also distinguishes itself from classifier-free guidance (CFG), which linearly interpolates unconditional and conditional scores, by performing a multi-step, non-linear interaction between the two diffusion processes at chosen intervals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BDM offers a principled, plug-and-play mechanism to exploit abundant standalone 3D shape data (e.g., ShapeNet) to improve single-view reconstruction when paired image-shape data is scarce—a common practical bottleneck. The ablation revealing that 50% prior blending is optimal while 75–100% prior ratio catastrophically degrades performance (CD nearly triples) highlights a non-trivial trade-off between prior guidance and data-driven fidelity that practitioners should account for. Key limitations include the requirement that both the prior and reconstruction processes be diffusion models, and BDM-B's reliance on explicit point-cloud representations, leaving open whether the framework extends to implicit (SDF/occupancy) or mesh-based reconstructions and to tasks beyond 3D shape.
