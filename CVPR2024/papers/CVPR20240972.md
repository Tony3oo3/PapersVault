## Problem

Single-sensor hand mesh reconstruction fails under different challenging conditions: RGB cameras suffer from overexposure and motion blur, while event cameras face foreground scarcity (sparse events when hands are static) and background overflow (excessive events from illumination changes). No prior work had combined event streams and RGB frames in a complementary fashion specifically for 3D HMR, and obtaining high-quality 3D annotations in challenging scenes remains prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240972/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of asynchronously captured event streams (DAVIS346, 346×260) and RGB frames (FLIR, 2660×2300) from potentially different viewpoints. Events are converted into 2-channel time-surface stacked frames by assigning each of the N most recent events a normalized timestamp weight, making them compatible with standard CNN backbones. The network outputs 778 MANO mesh vertices and 21 3D joint coordinates, with the spatial alignment module operating without explicit camera pose or disparity estimation.

## Method

EvImHandNet fuses the two modalities across three dimensions: (1) spatial alignment via deformable convolution with offsets predicted by a ConvLSTM that exploits temporal motion clues to reduce jitter from texture mismatching; (2) a complementary fusion module that learns scene-aware weights to select the most informative modality per spatial location; and (3) temporal attention with relative position encoding over S sequential steps to enforce spatial-temporal consistency. A transformer encoder builds non-local correlations among the fused features, and a transformer decoder with learnable joint and vertex tokens regresses 3D coordinates. To address the train-evaluation gap, EvRGBDegrader applies three targeted augmentations—color jitter for overexposure, optical-flow warping for motion blur, and salt-and-pepper noise on event frames for background overflow—enabling the model to generalize to challenging outdoor scenes and a different event camera (PROPHESEE GEN 4.0) when trained solely on normal indoor data.

## Results

On the EVREALHANDS dataset, EvRGBHand achieves the lowest MPJPE across all challenging scenes: 11.84 mm (Normal), 24.12 mm (Strong Light), and 22.88 mm (Flash), outperforming the best single-sensor baselines by 12–18 mm in strong light and 7–33 mm in normal/flash scenes. In terms of efficiency, EvRGBHand uses 55.92M parameters and 8.15G FLOPs, achieving 6.9 mm lower average MPJPE (16.66 mm) than FastMETRO-Event (141.68M params, 10.79G FLOPs, 23.59 mm MPJPE) while using 60.5% fewer parameters and 24.5% fewer FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvRGBHand outperforms both RGB-based baselines (Mesh Graphormer and FastMETRO-RGB) by 12–18 mm in MPJPE under strong light, where overexposure degrades RGB performance, and matches or slightly exceeds them in normal scenes (11.84 vs. 11.57 mm). Against event-based baselines, EvRGBHand reduces MPJPE by 7–33 mm in normal and flash scenes, where event cameras suffer from foreground scarcity and background overflow. Notably, even the vanilla fusion baseline (EvRGBHand-vanilla, which simply concatenates event and image tokens into a transformer) already beats all single-sensor methods by 2–16 mm, confirming the inherent complementarity of the two modalities. The full EvRGBHand architecture with spatial alignment, complementary fusion, and temporal attention further improves over vanilla by ~2.5–3 mm in challenging scenes. In terms of efficiency, EvRGBHand (55.92M params, 8.15G FLOPs) is substantially lighter than FastMETRO-Event (141.68M, 10.79G) and EvRGBHand-vanilla (277.02M, 17.90G) while achieving the best accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transformer-based mesh reconstruction architecture of FastMETRO [6] (encoder-decoder with learnable joint/vertex tokens) and the time-surface event representation from EventHands [39], but departs from both by introducing a dedicated multi-modal fusion pipeline (spatial alignment via deformable convolution, complementary fusion, and temporal attention) that has no direct predecessor in the HMR literature. It positions itself against the closest prior work, EventCap [53], by replacing loose data-association with tight feature-level fusion, and against single-modality HMR methods (Mesh Graphormer, FastMETRO-RGB, EventHands) by demonstrating that neither modality alone is sufficient under all imaging conditions. The EvRGBDegrader module is a novel data-augmentation strategy that has no direct analogue in prior HMR or event-vision work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that event streams and RGB frames can be fused at the feature level for 3D hand mesh reconstruction, opening a practical path to robust HMR in scenarios where either modality alone degrades (strong light, fast motion, flash). The introduction of EVREALHANDS with 3D annotations across four scene types provides a much-needed benchmark for multi-modal HMR evaluation. Key limitations include the resolution mismatch between the 346×260 event sensor and 2660×2300 RGB cameras, the failure mode when overexposure and motion blur co-occur with challenging poses, and the fact that cross-camera and outdoor generalization are validated only qualitatively. Future work with higher-resolution, lower-noise event sensors and larger annotated challenging-scene datasets would be needed to rigorously quantify the complementary benefits.
