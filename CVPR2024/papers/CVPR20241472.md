## Problem

Current subject-driven image generation methods jointly learn semantic scene and person generation by fine-tuning a single pre-trained diffusion model, creating an irreconcilable training imbalance: sufficient fine-tuning for realistic persons causes catastrophic forgetting of scene priors, while insufficient fine-tuning preserves scenes but yields low-fidelity persons. Moreover, even with adequate fine-tuning, the joint learning itself compromises person generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing the desired scene and one or more reference person images (typically 3–5 per subject) as input. Reference images are encoded by a CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder, and the resulting visual features replace user-specific word embeddings in the text conditioning. The output is a full-resolution person-centric image where the specified person(s) are naturally integrated into the described semantic scene, supporting both single- and multi-subject generation without any test-time fine-tuning.

## Method

Face-diffuser independently fine-tunes two Stable Diffusion v1.5 models: a Text-driven Diffusion Model (TDM) for scene generation and a Subject-augmented Diffusion Model (SDM) for person generation, where SDM's conditioning embeddings are augmented with CLIP-encoded reference image features. The sampling process is divided into three sequential DDIM stages: semantic scene construction by TDM (αT steps), subject-scene fusion via collaboration between TDM and SDM ((β−α)T steps), and subject enhancement by SDM (remaining steps). The key mechanism, Saliency-adaptive Noise Fusion (SNF), computes per-pixel saliency maps from the classifier-free guidance responses of each model (the noise difference between conditioned and unconditioned predictions), applies softmax normalization for cross-model comparability, and uses an argmax mask to spatially blend the two models' predicted noises at every denoising step. Notably, SDM's CFG is modified to ablate only the reference images (not the text), directing its saliency exclusively to person-related regions and thereby preserving fine facial details.

## Results

On the Single-benchmark dataset, Face-Diffuser achieves the highest identity preservation (IP) of 0.708 and prompt consistency (PC) of 0.325 for single-subject generation, surpassing the next-best Subject-Diffusion (IP 0.605, PC 0.228). On the FFHQ-face test set for multi-subject generation, Face-Diffuser reaches IP 0.593 and PC 0.319, outperforming FastComposer (IP 0.461, PC 0.235) by 0.132 and 0.084 respectively. All compared baselines show notably lower PC scores, which the authors attribute to overfitting on text prompts during joint scene-and-person training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Face-Diffuser outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest zero-shot competitor Subject-Diffusion, it gains +0.103 IP and +0.097 PC on single-subject generation, and +0.161 IP and +0.114 PC on multi-subject generation. Against FastComposer (the prior SOTA for multi-subject), the gains are +0.132 IP and +0.084 PC. The fine-tuning-based methods (DreamBooth, Custom-Diffusion) and the tuning-free ELITE trail substantially, with ELITE scoring only 0.228 IP and 0.146 PC on single-subject. The paper also notes that Subject-Diffusion and FastComposer, while competitive on IP, suffer lower PC scores, which the authors link to catastrophic forgetting of semantic scene priors during prolonged joint training. Qualitative comparisons (Figs. 5–7) further show that baselines frequently fail to generate coherent scenes or preserve person identity, whereas Face-Diffuser maintains both simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in subject-driven image generation (DreamBooth, Custom-Diffusion, Subject-Diffusion, FastComposer) where a single diffusion model jointly learns semantic scene and person generation. Instead, it decomposes the problem into two independently fine-tuned Stable Diffusion models—TDM for scenes and SDM for persons—and introduces Saliency-adaptive Noise Fusion (SNF), which uses classifier-free guidance responses as spatial saliency signals to blend the two models' predicted noises during a middle sampling stage. The tuning-free conditioning strategy for SDM (replacing word embeddings with CLIP image features) follows the approach of Subject-Diffusion and FastComposer, but the collaborative two-model sampling pipeline is the core architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode—irreconcilable training imbalance between scene and person generation—that explains why prior joint-learning methods underperform on person-centric tasks, providing a useful diagnostic for the field. The SNF mechanism is a lightweight, training-free collaboration strategy that could potentially generalize to other multi-specialist diffusion pipelines beyond faces. However, evaluation is limited to a single face-centric dataset (FFHQ-face, 70k samples) and one benchmark, leaving open questions about scalability to diverse subject types, complex multi-person interactions, and whether the two-model overhead is justified at scale.
