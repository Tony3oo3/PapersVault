## Problem

Detecting fetal cardiac anatomical structures in ultrasound images across different hospital centers suffers from domain gaps caused by varying equipment and acquisition conditions, while expert annotations at the target hospital are costly or unavailable. Existing unsupervised domain adaptive object detection (UDAOD) methods are designed for natural scenes where inter-object relationships are uncertain, but fetal cardiac structures have fixed topological constraints (e.g., the ventricular septum is always between the left and right ventricles) that these methods fail to exploit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242086/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired fetal ultrasound images from a source domain (with ground-truth bounding boxes for up to 13 anatomical structures) and a target domain (unlabeled), all uniformly resized to 600×1000 pixels. It produces bounding boxes for cardiac structures across two standard views: the four-chamber view (4CC, 9 structures) and the three vessels and trachea view (3VT, 6 structures). The sub-structure matching module is deferred until epoch 30 because early pseudo-labels from the target domain are unreliable, and missing or extra detected classes are completed/removed by referencing source-domain annotations to maintain consistent organ counts.

## Method

M³-UDA combines three matching modules on top of a shared ResNet-101 + FCOS detector. Histogram Matching (HM) applies a non-linear pixel-wise transformation to align brightness and contrast between source and target ultrasound images. Sub-structure Matching (SM) computes angular relationships between organ centroids to build adjacency matrices for each domain, then minimizes the L2 distance between source and target matrices to enforce consistent local topology. Global-structure Matching (GM) samples visual nodes from FPN feature maps, constructs visual graphs via spectral clustering, applies a GNN for representation, uses cross-attention for inter-domain knowledge transfer, and measures graph discrepancy via Wasserstein distance solved with Sinkhorn iteration. The overall loss is a weighted sum of the supervised FCOS detection loss, the SM angular loss, the GM Wasserstein loss, and a node classification cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On the proposed FCS dataset (4CC view), M³-UDA achieves 76.81% mAP for Hospital 1→2 adaptation and 74.55% mAP for Hospital 2→1, outperforming the strongest baselines (CMT at 68.55% and SIGMA++ at 67.47%) by 8.26% and 7.08% mAP respectively. On the 3VT view, M³-UDA reaches 60.39% mAP (Hospital 1→2) and 49.36% mAP (Hospital 2→1). On the public CardiacUDA dataset (site G→R), M³-UDA achieves 94.95% mAP, surpassing SIGMA (92.98%) by 1.97% mAP, though the domain gap is notably smaller (Without DA already reaches 91.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M³-UDA outperforms all seven baselines on the primary 4CC evaluation in both adaptation directions. Against the strongest UDAOD baseline CMT (CVPR'23), M³-UDA gains 8.26% mAP on Hospital 1→2 (76.81 vs. 68.55) and 4.15% on Hospital 2→1 (74.55 vs. 70.40). Against SIGMA++ (TPAMI'23), the previous SOTA for Hospital 2→1, the improvement is 7.08% mAP. The few-shot methods AcroFOD and AsyFOD perform substantially worse on 4CC (25.30% and 48.71% mAP for Hospital 1→2), confirming that even with a small amount of target labels, existing few-shot approaches struggle with the complex multi-structure topology. On 3VT Hospital 2→1, AsyFOD (58.10%) actually surpasses M³-UDA (49.36%) by 8.74%, which the authors note is expected since few-shot methods benefit from target-domain labels that may be unavailable in practice. On CardiacUDA, M³-UDA leads all UDAOD baselines by 1.97% over SIGMA, but the narrow margin (and the fact that Without DA already reaches 91.44%) reflects the simpler four-structure topology and smaller domain gap of that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UDAOD literature (invariant feature learning, pseudo-label self-training, graph-based matching like SIGMA/SIGMA++) from natural scenes with uncertain object relationships to fetal cardiac structure detection, where anatomical topologies are fixed and well-defined. It builds directly on SIGMA's node completion and graph matching framework but departs conceptually by introducing a three-level matching hierarchy (histogram, sub-structure angle, global graph) that exploits the deterministic spatial constraints of cardiac anatomy (e.g., VS always between LV and RV). The histogram matching component repurposes a classical image-processing technique as a domain adaptation regularizer, a use not previously explored in UDAOD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FCS dataset (2,879 images, 13 structures, 2 views, 2 hospitals) is the first multi-center benchmark specifically designed for UDAOD in fetal cardiac imaging, filling a gap where prior UDAOD work targeted only natural scenes or simpler cardiac segmentation. The finding that a simple histogram matching contributes the largest single ablation gain (~12% mAP) suggests that cross-hospital ultrasound domain gaps are dominated by pixel-level intensity shifts rather than structural misalignment, which could redirect future work toward cheaper pre-processing solutions. Limitations include only two hospital centers, two cardiac views, and the observation that few-shot methods still outperform the unsupervised approach on 3VT when even a small amount of target annotation is available, leaving open whether the topological priors generalize to 3D ultrasound or more heterogeneous multi-center settings.
