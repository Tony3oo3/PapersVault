## Problem

Reward-gradient guided denoising for trajectory optimization requires the reward function to be differentiable and fitted to both clean and noised samples, typically necessitating re-training. This limits its use as a general trajectory optimizer, especially for non-differentiable black-box objectives (e.g., those involving discrete heuristics or LLM-generated reward code) and for optimizing novel objectives without additional training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241029/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconditional trajectory diffusion model (no scene conditioning) and a black-box reward function that operates only on clean, final denoised trajectories—eliminating the need for a reward regressor on noisy samples. The reward function in the driving setting is non-differentiable, combining an LQR tracker, a kinematic bicycle model, constant-velocity agent forecasting, and nuPlan scoring metrics. Optionally, natural language instructions are mapped via few-shot LLM prompting to Python generator functions that shape the base reward, enabling temporal dependencies (e.g., sequential lane changes). The output is a 48-dimensional ego-vehicle trajectory: 2D poses (x, y, θ) at 2 Hz over 8 seconds.

## Method

Diffusion-ES performs population-based evolutionary search where the diffusion model serves as both the initial sampler and the mutation operator. At each iteration, trajectories are scored by the black-box reward, elites are resampled via a softmax distribution over rewards (analogous to MPPI), and mutations are applied through a truncated diffusion-denoising process: adding Gaussian noise for t forward steps then denoising for t reverse steps, which projects mutated samples back onto the learned trajectory data manifold. The number of mutation steps t is a time-dependent hyperparameter that linearly decays from 5 to 1 over 20 search iterations, balancing exploration and exploitation. The diffusion model is trained via imitation learning on nuPlan trajectories with T=100 denoising steps and is used unconditionally, fully decoupling trajectory modeling from reward modeling. For instruction following, LLMs generate Python generator functions (retaining internal state across calls) that add auxiliary reward terms or re-weight existing ones, allowing expression of sequential plans without retraining.

## Results

On the nuPlan Val14 closed-loop driving benchmark, Diffusion-ES achieves a driving score of 92, matching the prior state-of-the-art PDM-Closed (92) and substantially outperforming all learning-based baselines: UrbanDriverOL (65), PlanCNN (72), IDM (77), and a conditional diffusion policy (50). On the lane-following task with a differentiable reward, Diffusion-ES achieves lane error 0.61 and speed error 0.79, outperforming reward-gradient guidance (1.22 / 0.96), CEM (2.34 / 2.05), and MPPI (3.29 / 2.74). With minor optimizations (T=10 diffusion steps, M=32 population, K=2 iterations), inference runs at 0.50 s per timestep, matching the 2 Hz simulator frequency with only a 1-point drop in driving score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion-ES ties PDM-Closed (92 vs. 92) on the nuPlan Val14 closed-loop benchmark, but PDM-Closed relies on handcrafted lane-centerline heuristics for proposal generation while Diffusion-ES learns proposals from data. Against all reactive learning-based policies, Diffusion-ES wins by a wide margin: +27 over UrbanDriverOL (65), +20 over PlanCNN (72), +15 over IDM (77), and +42 over the conditional diffusion policy (50). On the lane-following task with a differentiable reward, Diffusion-ES outperforms even reward-gradient guidance (lane error 0.61 vs. 1.22), demonstrating that gradient-free diffusion-guided search can be more effective than backpropagating through noisy intermediate states. On the eight language instruction-following tasks, Diffusion-ES outperforms PDM-Closed, PDM-Closed-Multilane, and conditional Diffusion-ES across all tasks, with the conditional variant performing significantly worse due to fewer in-distribution samples. The paper also shows that a conditional diffusion model (scene-conditioned) is harder to guide than the unconditional one, revealing a trade-off between inference speed and out-of-distribution generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

Diffusion-ES sits at the intersection of two lines of work: diffusion-based planning (Janner et al., 2022) and sampling-based trajectory optimization (CEM, MPPI, PDM-Closed). It departs from reward-gradient guidance by removing the requirement for differentiable rewards fitted to noisy samples, and from conventional evolutionary search by replacing naive Gaussian mutations with truncated diffusion-denoising that keeps proposals on the data manifold. The LLM-prompted reward shaping extends prior language-to-reward work (VoxPoser, Language to Rewards) by using stateful Python generators to express temporally-sequential driving instructions, and is the first to evaluate such shaped rewards in closed-loop driving on a standardized benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a general-purpose, gradient-free trajectory optimizer can match hand-engineered SOTA planners (PDM-Closed) while being far more flexible for complex, non-differentiable, or language-shaped objectives. The finding that unconditional diffusion plus test-time optimization outperforms scene-conditioned diffusion policies highlights an important speed-vs-generalization trade-off that the field has underexplored. Key limitations include the constant-velocity assumption for other agents (no reactive modeling), computational overhead relative to reactive policies, and the small scale of instruction-following evaluation (8 tasks). Open questions include whether search results can be amortized into fast reactive policies and whether memory-prompted reward shaping can handle long-tail scenarios without a human teacher.
