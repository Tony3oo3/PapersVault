## Problem

Video semantic segmentation for driving scenes struggles to establish cross-frame correspondences for distant small objects (subtle motion) and fast-moving targets (rapid position changes), while existing approaches like optical flow fail under high speed and local attention misses fine motion characteristics. The paper identifies that vanishing points (VPs) encode both a static prior (objects near the VP are distant and small) and a dynamic prior (objects move radially away from the VP over time) that remain unexploited for VSS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of driving-scene video frames at 1024×2048 resolution, using 3 previous frames at a sampling interval of k=3. Context features and VP detection operate on bilinearly downsampled (0.5×) inputs, while high-resolution details are extracted from full-resolution frames. VP positions are obtained via a Hough-transform-based Canny edge detector (thresholds 50/150). The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask produced as a weighted blend of a low-resolution context prediction and a high-resolution detail prediction, guided by a learned detail attention map.

## Method

VPSeg is a context-detail framework built on a MiT transformer backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet) with a DAFormer decoder. The MotionVP module establishes explicit cross-frame correspondences by assigning each feature patch a radial direction toward the VP, sampling neighboring patches forward and backward along that direction (with distance scaling by frame interval), and fusing via cross-attention. The DenseVP module applies a scale-adaptive partition in the VP region—overlapping dense patches at stride ⌈s/2⌉ within a 3×3 sparse grid—extracting finer features for distant objects and augmenting the dynamic context through a second cross-attention. The CMA module then integrates the augmented dynamic context with high-resolution local details using a VP proximity map (a VP-centered pseudo-depth map) as a positional prior in cross-attention, producing a detail attention map that adaptively weights context versus detail predictions per class and per spatial location.

## Results

On ACDC, VPSeg (MiT-B3) achieves 77.48% mIoU, 49.42% miIoU, and 41.48% mIA-IoU, outperforming the previous SOTA VSS method MRCFA by +1.85% mIoU and +2.67% mIA-IoU. On Cityscapes, VPSeg reaches 82.46% mIoU and 61.79% miIoU, surpassing CFFM by +1.02% mIoU and +1.70% miIoU. The method adds only 2.2M parameters (4.9%) over the SegFormer baseline while achieving 1124.7 GFLOPs, lower than all compared high-performance VSS methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPSeg (MiT-B3) outperforms all 12 compared baselines on every reported metric across both ACDC and Cityscapes. Against the strongest VSS competitors, VPSeg beats MRCFA by +1.85% mIoU and +2.67% mIA-IoU on ACDC, and CFFM by +1.02% mIoU and +1.70% miIoU on Cityscapes. The gains are most pronounced on the mIA-IoU metric (targeting ambiguous/invalid regions), where VPSeg improves by +3.06% over SegFormer and +2.67% over MRCFA on ACDC, with per-class IA-IoU gains of +8.5% on rider and +3.4% on traffic sign. On the lighter MiT-B1 backbone, VPSeg still surpasses the SegFormer-B1 baseline by +2.61% mIoU on ACDC while using only 14.9M parameters. In terms of efficiency, VPSeg achieves the lowest GFLOPs (1124.7) among all high-performance VSS methods, compared to 1436.4 for MRCFA and 1534.8 for CFFM, though its FPS (3.4) is slightly lower than MRCFA (4.4) due to VP detection overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPSeg departs from the dominant VSS paradigms of feature propagation (Accel, DFF), keyframe scheduling (DVSNet), and learned cross-frame attention (CFFM, MRCFA) by introducing vanishing-point geometry as an explicit, lightweight prior for both motion estimation and feature enhancement. It repurposes the classical Hough-transform VP detection (a traditional computer-vision tool) as a plug-and-play signal within a modern transformer-based context-detail framework built on SegFormer/MiT and DAFormer. The conceptual leap is treating the VP not merely as a calibration artifact but as a structural anchor that simultaneously encodes a dynamic motion direction (objects radiate outward from the VP) and a static scale cue (regions near the VP contain smaller, harder objects), unifying two previously separate concerns in VSS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a simple geometric prior (VP position from Canny + Hough) can outperform a learned monocular depth estimator (MiDaS) as a positional embedding for attention, while being substantially faster, suggesting that hand-crafted structural cues remain competitive in driving-scene understanding. The proposed IA-IoU metric for evaluating performance specifically in ground-truth-ambiguous regions is a useful addition for the adverse-conditions segmentation community. Key limitations include the assumption of a forward-facing camera on a straight road with linear motion, which restricts applicability to curved roads, turns, and non-standard camera rigs; generalizing the VP prior to multi-VP or non-linear motion scenarios remains an open question.
