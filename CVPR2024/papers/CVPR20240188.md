## Problem

Existing text-driven face manipulation methods (e.g., StyleCLIP, DeltaEdit) can only control appearance attributes and fail to precisely manipulate 3D physical attributes like pose and illumination, while 3D-aware face generation methods lack text-driven control. The core difficulty is the large modality gap between text, 2D image, and 3D representation spaces, which causes inaccurate control or undesired attribute changes when naively using text embeddings to infer 3D or latent transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240188/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 2D face image and a free-form text prompt describing desired attribute changes (expression, pose, illumination) as input. It produces a manipulated 2D face image whose 3D face representation θ ∈ R^257 (comprising identity α∈R^80, texture δ∈R^80, expression β∈R^64, illumination γ∈R^27, and pose p∈R^6) is consistent with the text instruction. The 3D representation is rendered via a differentiable 3DMM pipeline (Basel Face Model + Spherical Harmonics + perspective camera), and the final 2D image is decoded from the W+ latent space of a pretrained StyleGAN.

## Method

TcALign employs a two-stage pipeline with two trainable modules. First, a Text-conditional 3D Editor (Γ) operates in 3D space: it projects the CLIP image embedding of a rendered 3D face into an Attribute Embedding Space (AES) spanned by basis attribute embeddings, performs disentangled text manipulation in that subspace, and trains Γ to output a target 3D representation θ_target supervised by CLIP semantic correspondence loss. Second, a Cross-modal Latent Mapping network (Φ) takes the source W+ code w_0 (from e4e) and the 3D difference Δθ = θ_target − θ_0 as joint input to predict a correction vector Δw in W+ space, rather than a global latent code. This residual formulation makes cross-modal alignment tractable because Δw and Δθ represent analogous small transformations. Training uses 3D consistency loss (matching inverse-rendered θ of the generated image to θ_target) and 2D landmark consistency loss to preserve fine facial details, while only Γ and Φ are trained with all other components (StyleGAN, e4e, Deep3DFR, CLIP) frozen.

## Results

On FFHQ, TcALign achieves the best FID of 7.104 and the best identity preservation (IDD 0.107) among all compared methods. It is the only method that simultaneously manipulates all three 3D attributes, achieving TARR:Expression of 0.913, TARR:Illumination of 0.859, and TARR:Pose of 0.714 (lower is better). Compared to the closest text-driven baselines StyleCLIP and DeltaEdit, TcALign improves expression control by 25% and 67% respectively, while also enabling pose and illumination control that those methods cannot perform.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TcALign outperforms all five baselines on every metric it is evaluated on. Against the 3D-aware methods (DiffusionRig, DiscoFaceGAN, GANControl), TcALign achieves substantially lower FID (7.104 vs. 27.6–63.4) and better pose control (TARR:Pose 0.714 vs. 1.57–2.11), while also adding text-driven expression control that none of them support. Against the text-driven methods (StyleCLIP, DeltaEdit), TcALign improves TARR:Expression from 0.728/0.547 to 0.913 and uniquely enables pose and illumination manipulation. In terms of identity preservation, TcALign achieves the lowest IDD (0.107), beating DeltaEdit (0.119) and StyleCLIP (0.124), and dramatically outperforming the 3D-aware baselines (0.224–0.591). The paper also notes that DiffusionRig requires ~20 personalized fine-tuning images per subject, whereas TcALign operates without any per-identity fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends StyleCLIP's text-driven StyleGAN manipulation by incorporating 3D morphable model priors (Basel Face Model, Spherical Harmonics illumination, camera parameters) to enable simultaneous control over expression, pose, and illumination. The Attribute Embedding Space for disentangled text manipulation is adapted from CLIP-PAE, while the 3D editing stage draws on the 3D-aware face generation line (DiscoFaceGAN, DiffusionRig, GANControl) that previously lacked text-driven interfaces. The key architectural departure is the two-stage decomposition: a Text-conditional 3D Editor operates in 3DMM coefficient space, and a cross-modal latent mapping network transfers the resulting 3D difference (Δθ) into a StyleGAN W+ correction vector, rather than predicting a global latent code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to unify text-driven manipulation with 3D physical attribute control (pose, illumination, expression) in a single framework, addressing a gap where prior text-driven methods (StyleCLIP, DeltaEdit) could not control 3D attributes and prior 3D-aware methods (DiffusionRig, DiscoFaceGAN) lacked text interfaces. The correction-vector formulation (predicting Δw from Δθ rather than w_target from θ_target) is a clean design choice that improves identity preservation and multi-attribute precision. Limitations include reliance on 3DMM's fixed topology (limited to face-centric manipulation), dependence on Deep3DFR's reconstruction quality, and evaluation restricted to FFHQ with randomly sampled text attributes; open questions include generalization to non-frontal or occluded faces and whether the 3DMM representation suffices for more complex scene-level lighting.
