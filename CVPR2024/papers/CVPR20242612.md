## Problem

State-of-the-art spatio-temporal action localisation models rely on two-stage pipelines with external person detectors for proposals and complex memory banks for long-range context, making them cumbersome and not truly end-to-end. Proposal-free alternatives have historically lagged behind these proposal-based methods. This paper demonstrates for the first time that a fully end-to-end, purely transformer-based model can outperform two-stage approaches on multiple benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242612/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests a fixed-length video clip (typically T=32 frames at 160p–420p resolution) and directly outputs tubelets: a sequence of S=64 bounding boxes (parameterised by centre, width, height) and action class logits at every frame, with a background class for unused slots. No external person proposals are required as input, and no non-maximal suppression is needed as post-processing. The model can be trained with either sparse keyframe annotations (e.g., 1 fps on AVA) or full per-frame tubelet annotations, yet always predicts coherent tubelets across all frames.

## Method

STAR builds on DETR with a vision encoder (ViViT or CSN) followed by a decoder that processes learned queries into tubelet predictions. The key architectural innovation is factorised queries, decomposed into shared spatial embeddings (repeated across all frames) and per-frame temporal embeddings, which inductively bias each query to track the same actor over time. The decoder uses factorised self-attention (independent spatial attention per frame, then temporal attention per spatial location) and factorised cross-attention (queries attend only to encoder features at the same time index), halving computation while improving accuracy. Training employs a set-based loss with Hungarian matching, offering two variants: per-frame matching for sparse supervision and tubelet matching (enforcing the same spatial query to match the same ground-truth actor across all frames) for full tubelet annotations, which improves temporal consistency of predicted tubes.

## Results

STAR achieves a Frame mAP of 45.1 on AVA-Kinetics and 42.5 on AVA (trained on AVA-K, evaluated on AVA), surpassing the previous best web-scale foundation models VideoMAE-v2 (43.9/42.6) and InternVideo (42.5/41.0) while being fully end-to-end with no external proposals or memory banks. On tubelet-annotated datasets, STAR/L achieves 90.3 fAP and 73.4 vAP50 on UCF101-24, 92.1 fAP and 92.6 vAP50 on JHMDB51-21, and 59.3 fAP and 36.2 vAP50 on MultiSports, setting new state-of-the-art on all three. The model also demonstrates robustness to sparse supervision, retaining within 0.7 vAP points of full tubelet annotations when only 1/24 of frames are labelled.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms all 16 compared baselines on at least one primary metric. Against the strongest non-end-to-end methods, STAR/L* beats VideoMAE-v2 [57] by 1.2 mAP on AVA-K (45.1 vs 43.9) and InternVideo [60] by 2.6 mAP, despite using no external proposals, memory banks, or web-scale private data. Among end-to-end methods, the margin is larger: STAR/L* surpasses TubeR [66] (31.1 mAP) by 11.4 points on AVA, and WOO [9] (28.3) by 14.2 points. On UCF101-24, STAR/L achieves 90.3 fAP, beating HIT [14] (84.8) by 5.5 points and FBIL [8] (86.0) by 4.3 points, though HIT retains a slight edge on vAP50 (74.3 vs 73.4). On JHMDB51-21, STAR/CSN-152 (95.4 vAP50) outperforms TubeR [66] (82.3) by 13.1 points and HIT [14] (88.1) by 7.3 points. On MultiSports, STAR/L (59.3 fAP, 36.2 vAP50) substantially exceeds FBIL [8] (40.8 fAP, 9.9 vAP50) and HIT [14] (33.3 fAP, 8.8 vAP50), reflecting the dataset's difficulty with fast multi-actor motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR is built directly on the DETR architecture for end-to-end object detection, adapting its learned queries, Hungarian matching, and set-based loss to the spatio-temporal setting. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (external person detector + ROI pooling + memory banks) that has held state-of-the-art on benchmarks like AVA, and is most closely related to TubeR, which also applies DETR to tubelet detection. The key architectural departures from TubeR are factorised spatial-temporal queries, factorised attention in the decoder, and a tubelet-level matching objective, all designed to inject temporal inductive biases without external context modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a purely end-to-end transformer model can outperform complex two-stage pipelines relying on external person detectors and offline memory banks, fundamentally simplifying the action localisation pipeline to a single forward pass on a video clip. The ability to predict temporally coherent tubelets even under sparse keyframe supervision (with minimal degradation at 1/24 annotation density) opens the door to leveraging large-scale weakly-annotated video data. The authors identify open-vocabulary action localisation as a natural next step, and the reliance on CLIP pretraining for the largest gains suggests that stronger visual-language alignment will be critical for future progress.
