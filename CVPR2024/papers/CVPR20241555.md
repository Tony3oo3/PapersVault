## Problem

Existing CLIP-based few-shot learning methods (prompt tuning, adapter-based tuning, cache-based tuning) have been proposed without a unified understanding of what makes them effective. The key components governing their performance—logit features, logit predictor, and logit fusion—have not been systematically analyzed, limiting further exploration of CLIP's few-shot potential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241555/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image at inference time and a small set of labeled training samples (1–16 shots per class) during training. It consumes auxiliary features extracted by a frozen MoCo v3 RN50 encoder alongside CLIP's zero-shot logits, and only a lightweight linear probe (a single matrix Ŵ) is optimized. The output is a class label produced by summing CLIP's zero-shot logit with a learned, uncertainty-scaled logit bias.

## Method

AMU-Tuning learns an effective logit bias for zero-shot CLIP through three components: (1) Auxiliary features from a complementary pre-trained model (MoCo v3 RN50) are selected based on empirical metrics of complementarity and superiority relative to CLIP; (2) a feature-initialized linear probe (MTFi LP) is trained with multi-branch training—one branch minimizes cross-entropy on the bias logits alone, the other on the fused prediction—so the probe fully exploits the auxiliary features' discriminative power rather than acting as a mere supplement; (3) an uncertainty-based fusion adaptively scales the logit bias by the inverse kurtosis of CLIP's zero-shot logits, increasing the bias contribution when CLIP is uncertain and decreasing it when CLIP is confident, eliminating the need for a manually tuned fusion hyperparameter.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with RN50 backbone, AMU-Tuning achieves 70.02% top-1 accuracy at 16-shot, outperforming the strongest baseline CaFo (68.79%) by 1.23 points and Tip-Adapter-F (65.51%) by 4.51 points. At 4-shot and 8-shot settings, AMU-Tuning reaches 65.92% and 68.25% respectively, surpassing all compared methods. On OOD benchmarks with ViT-B/16, AMU-Tuning achieves 74.98% on ImageNet-1K, 65.42% on ImageNet-V2, 50.37% on ImageNet-Sketch, 52.05% on ImageNet-A, and 78.09% on ImageNet-R, setting new state-of-the-art across all four OOD splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMU-Tuning clearly outperforms prompt-tuning (CoOp: 62.95%) and adapter-based (CLIP-Adapter: 63.59%, VT-CLIP: 63.92%) methods by 6–7 points at 16-shot on ImageNet-1K. Against the strongest cache-based baseline CaFo (68.79%), AMU-Tuning wins at 4-shot (+0.28), 8-shot (+1.39), and 16-shot (+1.23), but is slightly behind at 1-shot (−1.20) and 2-shot (−0.09), which the authors attribute to CaFo's use of DALL-E-generated extra training samples. Compared to CaFo* (the variant without DALL-E/GPT-3 augmentation), AMU-Tuning leads by 1.02–1.97 points across all shot settings. On OOD benchmarks with ViT-B/16, AMU-Tuning (74.98/65.42/50.37/52.05/78.09) surpasses both CoCoOp (71.02/64.20/47.99/49.71/75.21) and MaPLe (70.72/64.07/49.15/50.90/76.98) on all four OOD splits, with the largest margin on ImageNet-A (+2.34 over MaPLe) and ImageNet-R (+1.11 over MaPLe). Tip-Adapter-F (65.51%) is outperformed by 4.51 points at 16-shot, confirming that the logit-bias formulation with auxiliary features and uncertainty-based fusion yields substantially better few-shot adaptation than a pure cache-based KNN approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the landscape of CLIP-based few-shot learning (prompt tuning à la CoOp, adapter-based methods like CLIP-Adapter, and cache-based methods like Tip-Adapter and CaFo) under a single 'logit bias' formulation, showing that all prior approaches can be viewed as learning different biases added to zero-shot CLIP logits. It departs from prior work by shifting the source of the bias from CLIP's own features to auxiliary features from self-supervised models (MoCo v3, DINO), and by replacing complex cache-based predictors with a simple feature-initialized linear probe trained via multi-branch loss. The uncertainty-based fusion using kurtosis of zero-shot logits replaces the manually tuned trade-off parameter used in Tip-Adapter and CaFo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The logit bias perspective provides a principled decomposition (features, predictor, fusion) that can guide future design of CLIP-based few-shot methods beyond ad-hoc architectural choices. The empirical finding that complementarity to zero-shot CLIP matters more than raw superiority of auxiliary features is a practically useful design principle. Limitations include reliance on a separate pre-trained auxiliary model (MoCo v3 RN50) and the uncertainty metric (kurtosis) being a specific choice that may not generalize to all settings; the paper does not explore whether the framework extends to tasks beyond classification (e.g., detection, segmentation).
