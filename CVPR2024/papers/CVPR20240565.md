## Problem

Existing HOI detectors are trained on closed-set data with narrow vocabularies (e.g., 80 objects, 117 actions), limiting their generalization to open-world scenarios. Language-guided HOI methods further face a bottleneck when encountering entirely new categories with no relevant text descriptions in training data, and struggle with high-ambiguity scenarios where a person simultaneously engages in multiple interactions. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing visual exemplars alongside textual prompts and by scaling training data to cover a vastly broader interaction vocabulary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240565/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the user supplies an RGB image together with freely chosen prompts: textual descriptions (e.g., 'A photo of a woman painting a wall'), visual crops (bounding-box regions defining an object or interaction exemplar), or a hybrid combination (visual prompt for interaction, textual prompt for object). The model outputs human and object bounding boxes, an object class label, and an interaction class label per detected pair, and can simultaneously detect a composition of multiple HOIs for the same person. The prompt set is not fixed at training time, enabling open-vocabulary detection of arbitrary objects and interactions at test time.

## Method

MP-HOI comprises three components: (1) a Representative Feature Encoder that fuses vanilla backbone features with features from a single forward pass (t=0) through a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet, where CLIP image features serve as a text-conditioning proxy via two learned scene-aware adaptors; (2) a cascade of transformer decoders—an instance decoder for pairwise human-object queries followed by an interaction decoder that incorporates global CLIP image features into each interaction query; and (3) a Multi-modal Prompt-based Predictor that encodes user-supplied textual or visual prompts via CLIP and classifies objects/interactions by softmax over query-to-prompt similarity. The key training innovation is reformulating HOI as a cross-modal contrastive learning problem (InfoNCE-style loss) between multi-modal prompts and object/interaction representations, enabling open-set generalization from large-scale data. To support this, the authors build Magic-HOI (186K images unifying six datasets into 2.4K objects, 1.2K actions, 20K interactions) and SynHOI (100K diffusion-generated images) to mitigate the severe long-tail distribution where 79.9% of HOI categories have fewer than 10 instances.

## Results

On HICO-DET, MP-HOI-L (Swin-L) achieves a new SOTA of 44.53 mAP (Full), surpassing the previous best PViC (Swin-L) at 43.35, while MP-HOI-S (ResNet-50) reaches 36.50 mAP, outperforming all ResNet-50 expert models (best: PViC at 33.80) and pre-trained models (best: RLIPv2† at 35.38). On the zero-shot V-COCO benchmark, MP-HOI-S with only 10% fine-tuning data achieves 57.7 AP (Scenario 1), already surpassing several fully-trained expert models, and with full fine-tuning reaches 66.2 AP, beating all expert baselines. On SWiG-HOI and HCVRD, MP-HOI consistently outperforms the GEN-VLKT expert baseline by 1.74 and 4.71 mAP respectively (Swin-L variant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HICO-DET, MP-HOI-S (ResNet-50, 36.50 mAP) outperforms all ResNet-50 expert models including PViC (33.80), GEN-VLKT (33.75), IF (33.51), and DOQ (33.28), as well as the pre-trained RLIPv2† (35.38) which was fine-tuned on HICO-DET with 2200K+ pretraining data. MP-HOI-L (Swin-L, 44.53 mAP) surpasses the strongest expert model PViC (Swin-L, 43.35) by 1.18 mAP and FGAHOI (37.18) by 7.35 mAP. On SWiG-HOI, MP-HOI-S (12.61) and MP-HOI-L (16.21) both beat GEN-VLKT (10.87), with the large variant gaining an additional 3.6 mAP. On HCVRD, MP-HOI-L (11.29) improves over GEN-VLKT (6.58) by 4.71 mAP. On V-COCO zero-shot, MP-HOI-S without fine-tuning (37.5 AP) trails expert models like GEN-VLKT (62.4), but after 10% fine-tuning (57.7) it surpasses IDN (53.3), FCL (52.4), and SCG (54.2), and with full fine-tuning (66.2) it exceeds all expert baselines including GEN-VLKT (62.4) and CDN (61.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cascade decoder architecture of GEN-VLKT and the query-based detection framework of CDN by introducing a multi-modal prompt-based predictor that encodes both textual and visual exemplars via CLIP. A key conceptual departure is the repurposing of Stable Diffusion's UNet internal features (extracted at t=0, i.e., a single forward pass without denoising) as a discriminative feature source for HOI representation, motivated by the observation that diffusion models encode verb-level semantic information beyond noun concepts. The training paradigm shifts from standard classification losses to a unified cross-modal contrastive loss that aligns multi-modal prompts with object/interaction queries, enabling open-set generalization across a 30× larger vocabulary than prior expert models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Magic-HOI (186K images, 2.4K objects, 1.2K actions, 20K interactions) and SynHOI (100K synthetic images) provides the community with the first unified large-scale HOI training resource, directly addressing the data bottleneck that has limited generalization in prior work. The zero-shot transfer to V-COCO (37.5 AP without fine-tuning) and the 6.2 mAP gain on rare classes demonstrate that multi-modal prompting and diffusion-derived features can mitigate long-tail issues without task-specific engineering. Open questions include whether the diffusion feature contribution is truly complementary to CLIP or merely redundant at scale, how visual prompt retrieval scales to truly open-world deployments with thousands of candidate exemplars, and whether the synthetic data pipeline generalizes beyond the HOI domain to other compositional recognition tasks.
