## Problem

Intrinsic image decomposition is inherently ambiguous because lighting and material properties are entangled in the observed appearance, yet prior methods treat it as a deterministic single-solution problem, causing predictions to average over the solution space and lose high-frequency detail. No large-scale real-world decomposition dataset exists, so methods trained on synthetic data suffer from a domain gap when applied to real images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242336/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single indoor RGB image (normalized to mean 0.5, clipped to [0,1]) and produces 10 sampled material explanations, each containing an RGB albedo map plus a BRDF parameter map encoding roughness (R) and metallic (G) channels under the GGX microfacet model. Trained on 256×256 crops, it is evaluated at full resolution (640×480 on InteriorVerse, ~512×384 on IIW). A separate lighting optimization step then fits N point lights with Spherical Gaussian emission profiles and a global SG environment map to the predicted materials.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating appearance decomposition as a conditional generative task: a latent diffusion model (fine-tuned from pre-trained Stable Diffusion V2) samples from the space of valid material explanations rather than predicting a single deterministic output. Conditioning on the input image is achieved two ways—a randomly initialized trainable encoder (same architecture as the frozen VAE encoder, producing 3-channel features concatenated to the 8-channel noisy material latent for 11 total input channels) and a CLIP image embedding fed via cross-attention. Training uses standard noise-prediction L2 loss on the InteriorVerse synthetic dataset (50k+ images, 250 epochs, batch 40, lr 1e-5, AdamW). The key design choice is inverting the typical domain-adaptation direction: instead of training on synthetic data and fine-tuning on a small real dataset, the model starts from a strong real-image prior and adapts to the material task on synthetic data, reducing the domain gap while retaining semantic and perceptual cues from real imagery. Inference uses DDIM sampling (50 steps) to draw multiple material hypotheses, followed by a separate optimization-based lighting fit using OmniData geometry.

## Results

On the synthetic InteriorVerse dataset, the mean of 10 sampled albedos achieves 17.42 dB PSNR, 0.80 SSIM, 0.22 LPIPS, and 25.42 FID, outperforming the strongest baseline Zhu et al. [48] by +1.5 dB PSNR and 45% lower FID (25.42 vs. 46.21). The best-of-10 sample reaches 18.43 dB PSNR, confirming the solution space contains predictions very close to ground truth. On real-world IIW data, the method achieves 22.02 WHDR (comparable to baselines) but dominates in a 27-participant user study with 83.80% Perceptual Quality score versus 6.11% for Zhu et al. [48] and 9.63% for IIW [4], reflecting substantially sharper and more detailed albedo predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest deterministic baseline Zhu et al. [48] (SIGGRAPH Asia 2022), the proposed method wins on every metric: +1.5 dB PSNR (17.42 vs. 15.92), +0.02 SSIM (0.80 vs. 0.78), −0.12 LPIPS (0.22 vs. 0.34), and 45% lower FID (25.42 vs. 46.21) on InteriorVerse. Against Li et al. [27] (CVPR 2020), the gap is even larger: +5.1 dB PSNR and 67% lower FID (25.42 vs. 77.79). On real-world IIW data, the WHDR metric is comparable (22.02 vs. 22.90 for Zhu et al.), but the user-study Perceptual Quality score is dramatically higher (83.80% vs. 6.11%), indicating the baselines' over-smoothed outputs are perceived as inferior despite similar sparse pixel comparisons. The optimization-based IIW [4] baseline (tuned directly on IIW) is outperformed on all synthetic metrics (PSNR 17.42 vs. 9.73, FID 25.42 vs. 62.22) and on real-world WHDR (22.02 vs. 21.00). In the ablation against Pix2Pix [17], the diffusion-based approach from scratch already outperforms the GAN baseline (13.38 vs. 13.69 PSNR is close, but FID 113.31 vs. 84.28 favors Pix2Pix), while the pre-trained Stable Diffusion prior yields the decisive jump to 17.42 PSNR and 25.42 FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of deterministic feed-forward inverse rendering (Li et al. 2020, Zhu et al. 2022) by formulating single-view material estimation as a conditional generative problem, sampling multiple valid albedo/BRDF explanations rather than predicting a single averaged solution. Architecturally, it fine-tunes Stable Diffusion V2's latent diffusion backbone, but targets the material latent space rather than the image space, and conditions on the input image via a learned encoder plus CLIP cross-attention. A key strategic departure from prior work is the direction of domain adaptation: instead of training on synthetic data and fine-tuning on the small real IIW dataset, the authors fine-tune a large-scale real-image prior on synthetic InteriorVerse data, inverting the usual synthetic-to-real transfer pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic formulation directly addresses the fundamental ill-posedness of appearance decomposition, and the 45% FID improvement over the best deterministic baseline demonstrates that the ambiguity is not merely a nuisance but a structural feature that deterministic models handle poorly. However, the method still relies on paired synthetic training data, and the lighting optimization is decoupled from material estimation, leaving open the question of a fully joint probabilistic inverse rendering framework. The authors also note that extending to weakly supervised or real-data-only settings, and enabling text-guided material editing, are natural next steps that could broaden the practical impact of this approach.
