## Problem

Event-based semantic segmentation (ESS) is constrained by the high cost of dense event annotations and the closed-set label mappings inherited from image segmentation, limiting scalability to open-world scenarios. While domain adaptation from images to events has been explored, no prior work has transferred the semantically rich knowledge of vision-language models like CLIP to sparse, asynchronous event streams for open-vocabulary dense prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the framework consumes calibrated RGB frames (3×H×W) alongside event streams converted into regular representations (voxel grids, frame-like reconstructions, or bio-inspired spikes of shape C×H×W), with spatial and temporal alignment via sensor calibration. At inference, only the event branch is retained, taking raw events and arbitrary text prompts (e.g., adjectives like 'driveable', fine-grained labels like 'barrier', or coarse descriptors like 'flat') as input and producing per-pixel open-vocabulary segmentation masks without any additional latency from the image or text encoders.

## Method

OpenESS distills CLIP's vision-language knowledge into an event segmentation network through two synergistic cross-modality objectives. The Frame-to-Event (F2E) contrastive distillation generates superpixels (via SLIC or SAM) on calibrated frames, maps them to corresponding superevents using known event-pixel correspondences, and applies a contrastive loss to align superevent features from the trainable event network with superpixel features from a frozen image backbone. The Text-to-Event (T2E) consistency regularization leverages CLIP's text encoder to form semantically coherent text-frame pairs, then propagates this alignment to event-text pairs via a class-level contrastive objective, mitigating intra-class self-conflicts inherent in heuristic superpixel grouping. At inference, only the event branch is kept, ensuring no extra computational cost, while the densified CLIP image encoder and text encoder provide the open-vocabulary prediction interface.

## Results

In the annotation-free setting (no event labels at all), OpenESS achieves 53.93% mIoU on DDD17-Seg and 43.31% mIoU on DSEC-Semantic, surpassing FC-CLIP (51.12% / 39.42%) and MaskCLIP (31.90% / 21.97%). In the open-vocabulary setting (with full supervision), OpenESS reaches 63.00% mIoU on DDD17-Seg and 57.21% mIoU on DSEC-Semantic, outperforming FC-CLIP (62.01% / 55.67%) and MaskCLIP (61.27% / 55.01%). Notably, the annotation-free OpenESS result on DDD17 (53.93%) is comparable to several fully-supervised methods such as Ev-SegNet (54.81%) and PVT-FPN (53.89%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two open-vocabulary CLIP-based baselines, OpenESS consistently wins: in the annotation-free setting it gains +2.81% mIoU over FC-CLIP and +22.03% over MaskCLIP on DDD17, and +3.89% / +21.34% on DSEC respectively. In the open-vocabulary (fully supervised) setting, OpenESS leads FC-CLIP by +0.99% on DDD17 and +1.54% on DSEC, and leads MaskCLIP by +1.73% and +2.20%. Remarkably, OpenESS's annotation-free mIoU of 53.93% on DDD17 approaches or matches several fully-supervised methods (Ev-SegNet 54.81%, PVT-FPN 53.89%, ESS 53.09%) without using any event labels. In the representation-learning comparison (Table 2), OpenESS with E2VID backbone achieves 63.00% / 57.21% mIoU, outperforming ECDP (59.15% / 59.16% with ResNet-50) and all reconstruction-based (BEiT, MAE) and contrastive (MoCo v3, IBoT, SimCLR) pretraining baselines, while being the only method supporting open-vocabulary predictions. The paper also shows that SAM-generated superpixels outperform SLIC superpixels for the F2E contrastive distillation, and that voxel-grid event representations yield the best overall performance over reconstructions and spikes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenESS extends the open-vocabulary segmentation line (MaskCLIP, FC-CLIP) from RGB images to sparse, asynchronous event streams, which is the first attempt at distilling CLIP's dense prediction capability to event data. It departs from prior ESS methods that rely on domain adaptation (EV-Transfer, ESS) or dense event annotations by introducing two cross-modality objectives—frame-to-event superpixel contrastive distillation and text-to-event semantic consistency regularization—that bridge the large representational gap between events, frames, and text without requiring paired labels. The superpixel-driven distillation draws conceptually from contrastive detection and superpixel-based pretraining, but is the first to apply such a mechanism between event and frame modalities for dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling annotation-free and annotation-efficient ESS, OpenESS addresses the core scalability bottleneck of event-based perception: the cost and difficulty of densely labeling sparse, asynchronous event streams. The annotation-free mIoU of 53.93% on DDD17 approaching fully-supervised baselines (e.g., Ev-SegNet at 54.81%) is practically significant for deployment scenarios where event labels are unavailable. However, the framework still requires calibrated frames during training (only inference is frame-free), and evaluation is limited to two driving datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to diverse indoor or non-driving scenes. Future work could explore removing the frame dependency entirely, scaling to larger event datasets, and integrating with real-time event processing pipelines.
