## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to faithfully render the logical structure of prompts: objects go missing, adjectives leak onto the wrong nouns, and (a newly identified failure mode) possessed objects are depicted as discarded rather than held. Existing attention-guidance methods each target only a subset of these issues based on ad hoc heuristics, lacking a unified theoretical framework that could be systematically extended to new prompt structures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt as input, which is decomposed into predicate-logic propositions (manually or via a syntactic dependency parser) and converted into differentiable loss functions over cross-attention maps. It operates as a training-free guidance layer on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 backbone, applying the guidance term only during the first 25 of 50 denoising steps, and outputs a standard RGB image. No additional annotations (bounding boxes, segmentation masks) are required beyond the prompt itself.

## Method

Predicated Diffusion represents prompt semantics as first-order predicate-logic propositions and treats each pixel intensity in a cross-attention map as a fuzzy truth value in [0,1], using product fuzzy logic to define conjunction, negation, implication, and quantifiers over attention maps (Table 1). Each proposition is mapped to an equation on attention-map intensities, whose negative logarithm yields a differentiable loss; this loss is integrated into the diffusion reverse process as a guidance term g(x_t, t) = −∇_{x_t} L[R], steering the denoising trajectory toward fulfilling the proposition. The framework unifies multiple prompt challenges under one mechanism: existence via existential quantifiers, attribute binding via universal implications (or biimplications for one-to-one correspondence), concurrent existence via logical conjunction (summing losses), and possession via implication (encouraging spatial overlap of attention maps). The paper shows that Attend-and-Excite corresponds to the special case of Gödel fuzzy logic, positioning Predicated Diffusion as a strict generalization that replaces the underlying logic with product fuzzy logic and broadens the scope of expressible propositions.

## Results

Predicated Diffusion achieves the best scores across all 13 metrics in the main comparison (Table 3). For concurrent existence, it reduces missing objects to 18.5% (lenient) / 28.5% (strict) with a fidelity score of 30.3 and CLIP-IQA of 0.775. For one-to-one correspondence, it further reduces missing objects to 10.0% / 16.5% and attribute leakage to 33.0%, while achieving the highest fidelity (44.8) and text-image similarity (0.379). In the possession experiment (Table 4), it cuts possession failure to 29.5% (vs. 52.5% for Stable Diffusion) and attains the highest fidelity (52.0) and CLIP-IQA (0.765).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Predicated Diffusion outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Compared to the Stable Diffusion backbone, it reduces missing objects from 54.7% to 18.5% (concurrent existence) and from 64.8% to 10.0% (one-to-one correspondence), while raising fidelity from 11.0 to 30.3 and from 6.0 to 44.8. Against Attend-and-Excite, the closest competitor on missing objects, Predicated Diffusion further reduces missing objects (18.5% vs. 25.3%) and dramatically cuts attribute leakage (33.0% vs. 64.5%), because Attend-and-Excite lacks a dedicated mechanism for attribute binding. SynGen is the strongest baseline on attribute leakage (40.3%) but still trails Predicated Diffusion (33.0%) and degrades CLIP-IQA (0.750 vs. 0.769 for Predicated Diffusion, and 0.756 for the backbone). Composable Diffusion and Structure Diffusion perform near or below the vanilla Stable Diffusion baseline on most metrics, with Composable Diffusion actually worsening missing objects under the strict criterion (82.3% vs. 66.0%). In the possession experiment, Predicated Diffusion reduces possession failure from 52.5% (Stable Diffusion) to 29.5%, while Attend-and-Excite barely improves it (51.5%) and even lowers fidelity (27.5 vs. 33.5 for the backbone).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a theoretical generalization of Attend-and-Excite, showing that the latter's loss function corresponds to Gödel fuzzy logic, and replaces it with product fuzzy logic to broaden the class of expressible propositions. It also relates to SynGen (which equalizes attention intensities for related word pairs) and Structure Diffusion (which segments prompts into clauses), but departs from both by grounding the guidance in predicate logic rather than heuristic attention-map manipulations. The key conceptual contribution is a unified loss-function framework where each challenge (missing objects, attribute leakage, possession failure) maps to a distinct logical proposition, rather than requiring separate ad-hoc mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified predicate-logic framework offers a principled way to extend prompt-faithfulness guidance to new relationship types (e.g., 2-ary spatial predicates like Above(x,y)) without redesigning the method from scratch, which is a practical advantage over the current patchwork of attention-guidance heuristics. However, the approach requires manual or parser-based proposition extraction, limiting full automation, and the possession experiment uses only 10 prompts, leaving the robustness of that particular result somewhat under-evidenced. Open questions include whether the framework scales to more complex compositional prompts, whether it transfers to newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, DiT-based models), and how to handle predicates that cannot be cleanly mapped to single-word attention maps.
