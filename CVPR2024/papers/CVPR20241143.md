## Problem

LiDAR semantic segmentation is limited by the inherent sparsity of point clouds, and previous multi-frame fusion methods can only aggregate a few temporal scans due to GPU memory constraints, preventing them from exploiting rich long-term temporal information. Additionally, prior approaches largely ignore temporal camera images, which contain complementary semantic and geometric cues that could further densify the input signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241143/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of LiDAR point clouds (window size 16) and temporal camera images (window size 48, sampling step 12) along with ego-vehicle pose matrices that align all frames to the current coordinate. At inference, historical LiDAR predictions and image features from previous timestamps are cached and reused at zero additional cost, so the temporal aggregation introduces no extra latency beyond the single-frame backbone. The output is a per-point semantic label for the current LiDAR frame, with the multi-scan variant additionally distinguishing static vs. moving instances of movable objects.

## Method

TASeg builds on a MinkowskiNet sparse 3D CNN backbone and introduces three components. First, Flexible Step Aggregation (FSA) partitions temporal points into class groups by difficulty and assigns each group a different temporal sampling step (e.g., step 2 for hard classes like bicyclists, step ∞ for easy classes like roads), drastically reducing memory while injecting discriminative density patterns; a Mask Distillation loss then trains a student using pseudo-masks against a teacher using ground-truth masks to recover the performance gap. Second, Temporal Image Aggregation and Fusion (TIAF) projects temporal image features onto temporal LiDAR points, transforms them to the present coordinate via pose matrices, fuses them with 3D sparse convolutions into multi-scale voxel features, and gathers them back onto LiDAR points via trilinear interpolation, effectively expanding the camera FOV and enabling temporal multi-modal fusion. Third, Static-Moving Switch Augmentation (SMSA) is an online data augmentation that shifts temporal point-cloud parts of a movable object to flip its motion state, generating synthetic static or moving samples without additional storage.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI single-scan test set, TASeg achieves 76.5 mIoU, surpassing the previous best UniSeg (75.2 mIoU) by 1.3 mIoU. On the SemanticKITTI multi-scan test set, TASeg reaches 65.7 mIoU, outperforming 2DPASS (62.4 mIoU) by 3.3 mIoU. On the nuScenes test set, TASeg attains 84.6 mIoU, exceeding UniSeg (83.5 mIoU) by 1.1 mIoU. The method ranks 1st on all three tracks, with particularly large gains on difficult classes such as bicyclist (84.6 vs. 80.4 for UniSeg) and traffic sign (73.8 vs. 72.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TASeg outperforms all 21 compared baselines across all three evaluation tracks. On SemanticKITTI single-scan, it leads UniSeg [24] by 1.3 mIoU (76.5 vs. 75.2) and SphereFormer [19] by 1.7 mIoU, with the largest per-class gains on bicyclist (+4.2 over UniSeg) and traffic sign (+5.3). On the multi-scan track, TASeg surpasses 2DPASS [39] by 3.3 mIoU (65.7 vs. 62.4) and SVQNet [4] by 5.2 mIoU, with dramatic improvements on moving classes (e.g., moving truck: 35.2 vs. 16.1 for 2DPASS) enabled by SMSA. On nuScenes, TASeg exceeds UniSeg [24] by 1.1 mIoU (84.6 vs. 83.5) and SphereFormer [19] by 2.7 mIoU. The method also generalizes well: when applied to Cylinder3D [46] and SPVNAS [31] backbones, it yields +3.6 and +4.1 mIoU respectively (Table 10). In terms of efficiency, TASeg without temporal images uses only 37.9M parameters and 79 ms latency—comparable to the MinkowskiNet baseline—while achieving 71.8 mIoU, and the full TASeg (46.7M params, 116 ms) still maintains lower complexity than UniSeg (147.6M params, 145 ms) at higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior multi-frame LiDAR segmentation methods (MSeg3D, SVQNet, MarS3D) that uniformly concatenate or cross-attend over a fixed number of temporal scans, instead proposing class-aware temporal sampling (FSA) that assigns different aggregation steps per class group based on historical predictions. It extends the multi-modal fusion line (2DPASS, UniSeg, MSeg3D) by incorporating temporal images rather than only the present frame, using temporal LiDAR points as geometric mediators to unify historical image features into the current coordinate. The knowledge distillation component is distinct from prior LiDAR distillation work (PVKD, 2DPASS) in that both teacher and student are multi-frame models, differing only in whether they receive ground-truth or pseudo masks for temporal aggregation. The SMSA augmentation is novel for the multi-scan setting, repurposing temporal instance masks to synthesize motion-state transitions without additional data collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TASeg demonstrates that temporal information can be exploited far more efficiently than previously thought—class-aware sampling reduces memory from ~70 GB to ~8.5 GB while simultaneously improving accuracy, challenging the assumption that more temporal frames always require proportionally more resources. The SMSA augmentation addresses a real data-scarcity bottleneck in multi-scan segmentation (moving objects are underrepresented), and its plug-and-play nature makes it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the class-grouping strategy generalizes to unseen classes or new datasets without re-tuning, whether the temporal image fusion degrades gracefully under camera failure (mentioned but not extensively tested), and whether the approach extends to other sensor suites (e.g., radar). The reliance on saved historical predictions at inference, while practical for sequential driving, limits applicability to random-access or offline evaluation settings.
