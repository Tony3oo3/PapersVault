## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction datasets capture static or single-affordance interactions but lack structured representations of complex, long-horizon bimanual manipulation tasks that require fulfilling multiple object affordances in a dependency-constrained sequence. The prior OakInk dataset identified object affordances but provided no human demonstrations of the interaction processes that fulfill them, nor any complex multi-object task decompositions, leaving a gap between affordance knowledge and actionable motion planning.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized 4-view RGB video (848×480, 30 fps: one egocentric + three allocentric) alongside MoCap-derived 3D pose annotations for the human upper body (SMPL-X), hands (MANO), and 75 objects including articulation parameters. The CTC framework consumes a free-text scene description and task goal, producing a Primitive Dependency Graph (a DAG of named Primitive functions with execution order), re-targeted object motion trajectories, and per-Primitive bimanual hand motion sequences that are stitched together via state interpolation into a continuous manipulation trajectory.

## Method

OAKINK2 organizes manipulation into a three-level object-centric hierarchy—Affordance (object-part functionality), Primitive (minimal interaction fulfilling one affordance), and Complex Task (a Primitive Dependency Graph encoding inter-Primitive ordering)—and collects 627 real-world bimanual sequences (363 Primitive, 264 Complex Task) across four scenarios using 12 OptiTrack cameras and 4 RGB cameras, with SMPL-X body fitting via a two-stage MoSH++-aligned procedure. For Complex Task Completion, the paper proposes a three-stage pipeline: (1) GPT-4 acts as a task interpreter that, given scene and goal text, generates executable code instantiating the PDG as ordered function calls, validated by a dependency checker; (2) an Oracle retrieves and re-targets expert object trajectories from the dataset to match the new scene's initial/final poses; (3) MF-MDM, a two-stage diffusion model built on MDM, generates hand motion conditioned on CLIP text embeddings, PointBert object geometry embeddings, and object trajectories via a Sequential Merging layer that aggregates multi-object spatial relationships per frame, followed by a refinement stage (MF-MDM R) that incorporates hand-object distance features to improve interaction fidelity.

## Results

On the OAKINK2 Hand Mesh Reconstruction benchmark, the best single-view method (RLE + HandTailer) achieves a PA-MPJPE of 5.46 mm and RR-MPJPE of 13.08 mm (AUC 0.441), while the best multi-view method (POEM) achieves PA-MPJPE of 6.18 mm, RR-MPJPE of 12.12 mm (AUC 0.581), and world-space MPJPE of 9.17 mm. For the Task-aware Motion Fulfillment (TaMF) task, the proposed MF-MDM model achieves a contact ratio of 0.90, solid intersection volume of 4.17 cm³, PSKL-J of 0.0446/0.0460, and a FID of 1.369, with a perceptual realism score of 3.64±0.85 (vs. 4.66±0.48 for ground truth).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a dataset paper, OAKINK2 provides benchmarks rather than proposing a new HMR method, so the comparisons evaluate existing methods on the new data. In the single-view HMR setting, RLE + HandTailer [32, 39] outperforms METRO [35] by 1.44 mm on PA-MPJPE (5.46 vs. 6.90) and by 4.48 mm on RR-MPJPE (13.08 vs. 17.56), indicating that residual log-likelihood estimation with a dedicated hand refiner is more effective than the transformer-based end-to-end approach. In the multi-view setting, POEM [61] substantially outperforms the simpler KP-based fitting variant from the same paper: POEM reduces PA-MPJPE from 9.20 to 6.18 mm, RR-MPJPE from 15.63 to 12.12 mm, and world-space MPJPE from 19.30 to 9.17 mm, demonstrating the benefit of point-embedded multi-view stereo over direct keypoint fitting. Notably, the multi-view POEM result (RR-MPJPE 12.12 mm, AUC 0.581) surpasses the best single-view result (RR-MPJPE 13.08 mm, AUC 0.441), confirming that additional views provide meaningful accuracy gains on this bimanual manipulation data.

## Relation to Prior Work

OAKINK2 directly extends the OakInk dataset [60], which catalogued object affordances and single-grasp interactions, by introducing human demonstrations that fulfill those affordances and by composing them into long-horizon Complex Tasks structured as Primitive Dependency Graphs. For motion generation, the paper adapts the MDM diffusion backbone [53] with a Sequential Merging layer to condition on multi-object trajectories and text, and for body annotation it follows the two-stage SMPL-X fitting protocol from MoSH++/AMASS [40]. The key conceptual departure from prior hand-object interaction datasets is the object-centric three-level abstraction (Affordance → Primitive Task → Complex Task) and the use of an LLM (GPT-4) to generate executable code that instantiates the PDG, rather than relying on hand-crafted planners or fixed action hierarchies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OAKINK2 is the first large-scale bimanual manipulation dataset that provides structured task decomposition (affordances, primitives, dependency graphs) alongside multi-view video and 3D pose annotations, filling a gap between single-affordance datasets (OakInk, HO3D) and unstructured long-horizon datasets (HOI4D, Ego-Exo4D). The CTC pipeline—LLM-based task decomposition followed by diffusion-based motion fulfillment—offers a practical, modular recipe for text-to-manipulation that can be extended to new objects or embodiments via re-targeting. Limitations include a modest subject count (9), a limited object vocabulary (75 objects, 39 affordances), and qualitative-only evaluation of the full CTC pipeline; open questions include whether the LLM planner generalizes beyond the 38 complex goals, whether TaMF scales to unseen object categories, and whether end-to-end training can replace the staged decomposition.
