## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods based on implicit neural representations (NeRF and its variants) struggle to capture fine geometric details and cannot achieve real-time rendering speeds. While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) delivers both high fidelity and real-time performance for static scenes, its highly customized rasterization pipeline has not been extended to model temporal deformation, leaving a gap between rendering quality/speed and dynamic scene capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242189/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence (single camera) with per-frame time labels and camera poses estimated via COLMAP/SfM, which also yields a sparse point cloud used to initialize the 3D Gaussians. Synthetic experiments run at full 800×800 resolution against a black background; real-world scenes come from NeRF-DS and HyperNeRF at their native resolutions. The learned representation consists of a set of time-independent 3D Gaussians in canonical space (position, quaternion, scale, opacity, spherical-harmonic color) plus a deformation MLP, from which novel-view images, time-interpolated frames, and depth maps can be rendered at over 30 FPS on an RTX 3090 when the Gaussian count stays below 250k.

## Method

The core idea is to decouple static geometry from temporal motion: 3D Gaussians are learned in a time-independent canonical space, while a deformation MLP (8 layers, width 256) takes the detached Gaussian center position and time—both with positional encoding (L=10 for position, L=6 for time in synthetic; L=10 for both in real scenes)—and outputs offsets (δx, δr, δs) that deform each Gaussian at query time. Both components are jointly optimized through the differentiable Gaussian rasterizer, with a 3k-iteration warm-up phase that trains only the Gaussians before the deformation field is activated. An Annealing Smooth Training (AST) mechanism adds linearly decaying Gaussian noise (scaled by β=0.1·Δt, annealed over 20k iterations) to the time input, which mitigates jitter from inaccurate COLMAP poses without extra computational cost. Adaptive density control (cloning and splitting) from 3D-GS is retained to refine Gaussian distribution in dynamic regions, and the deformation network's learning rate decays exponentially from 8e-4 to 1.6e-6.

## Results

On the D-NeRF synthetic dataset (8 scenes, 800×800), the method achieves PSNR ranging from 33.07 (Lego) to 44.62 (Stand Up), with LPIPS as low as 0.0052 (Mutant) and 0.0063 (Stand Up), substantially outperforming all baselines by 5–17 dB in PSNR. On the NeRF-DS real-world dataset (7 scenes), the method achieves mean PSNR 24.11, SSIM 0.8525, and LPIPS 0.1769, surpassing the best baseline (NeRF-DS, PSNR 23.60) by 0.51 dB. The annealing smooth training (AST) mechanism contributes an additional 0.14 dB PSNR and 0.027 LPIPS improvement over the no-AST variant on real-world data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on all 8 D-NeRF synthetic scenes, with particularly large margins over 3D-GS (e.g., 41.54 vs. 29.89 PSNR on Hell Warrior, +11.65 dB) and D-NeRF (41.54 vs. 24.06, +17.48 dB). Among the strongest dynamic-scene baselines, Tensor4D (31.26 PSNR on Hell Warrior) and K-Planes (24.58) are outperformed by 10.28 and 16.96 dB respectively, while TiNeuVox (27.10) and D-NeRF (24.06) trail by 14.44 and 17.48 dB. On the NeRF-DS real-world dataset, the advantage narrows to 0.51 dB over NeRF-DS (24.11 vs. 23.60) and 0.66 dB over HyperNeRF (24.11 vs. 23.45), with the largest gap over 3D-GS at 3.82 dB. The AST ablation shows that without annealing smooth training, the method still beats all baselines (23.97 PSNR) but loses 0.14 dB in PSNR and gains 0.027 in LPIPS compared to the full model, confirming AST's role in temporal smoothness without sacrificing detail. HyperNeRF was excluded from quantitative comparison on its own dataset due to severe pose inaccuracies from COLMAP, though qualitative results in Fig. 1(d) show the method's visual superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) to dynamic scenes by learning Gaussians in a canonical space and applying a deformation MLP to produce time-dependent offsets, directly transplanting the deformation-field paradigm from implicit NeRF-based methods (NeRFies, D-NeRF, HyperNeRF) into the explicit point-based rendering setting. It deliberately departs from grid/plane-based acceleration strategies (Tensor4D, K-Planes, TiNeuVox) on the grounds that dynamic scenes violate the low-rank assumptions those structures exploit. The annealing smooth training (AST) mechanism is a novel contribution that addresses pose inaccuracy in real-world monocular captures without the computational overhead of explicit smoothness losses used in prior dynamic NeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling real-time (>30 FPS) rendering of dynamic scenes with 3D Gaussians, this work removes a key practical barrier that previously confined high-fidelity dynamic reconstruction to slow implicit ray-marching pipelines, opening the door to interactive AR/VR and real-time content production. The point-based representation also offers superior editability and post-production compatibility compared to implicit fields. However, the method's dependence on accurate COLMAP poses, its sensitivity to sparse viewpoint coverage, and its untested performance on complex human motions (e.g., facial expressions) remain open challenges that limit immediate deployment in the most demanding real-world scenarios.
