## Problem

Volumetric microscopy with non-diffracting beams (e.g., Bessel beams) achieves n-fold imaging speedup by axially projecting 3D volumes into 2D images, but this sacrifices depth information within each snapshot. Existing INR-based reconstruction yields globally coherent yet blurry outputs lacking fine detail, while standalone diffusion models fail to produce structurally coherent 3D volumes from such limited projections. The paper addresses how to combine INR's structural coherence with diffusion's detail-enhancement capability to reconstruct high-fidelity 3D volumes from a small number of 2D projections.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of 2D grayscale microscopy projections (downsampled to 128×128 pixels laterally), each representing an axial average of n consecutive slices acquired with a non-diffracting beam, and produces a depth-resolved 3D volume stack of n slices per projection. The 3D coordinates are normalized to [-1,1] and mapped to a 512-dimensional Gaussian embedding space. The step length n (typically 6, extendable to ~16 for sparse features) controls the trade-off between imaging speed and reconstruction difficulty.

## Method

MicroDiffusion is a two-stage framework: first, an INR (MLP with Gaussian positional encoding) is pretrained to map 3D coordinates to intensity values, minimizing MSE between the mean of n synthesized slices and the reference 2D projection; second, the frozen INR output is integrated into a conditional U-Net diffusion model via pixel-wise linear interpolation (X'_t = γ·m_inr + (1−γ)·X_t), replacing the conventional pure-noise starting point with a structurally informed input. Classifier-free guidance conditions the denoiser on both the nearest 2D projection (encoded by an image encoder) and the 3D coordinate (encoded by a positional encoder), with the concatenated feature added at every ResNet block output rather than through cross-attention. During INR inference, a neighbouring-based scheme computes a Gaussian-weighted average over n adjacent slices to mitigate the distribution shift between training (averaged) and inference (single-coordinate) regimes. The diffusion model is trained for 2000 epochs with AdamW (lr=2⁻⁴) after the INR is frozen, using a guidance scale ω to balance conditional and unconditional predictions at sampling time.

## Results

MicroDiffusion achieves the best reconstruction quality across all three microscopy datasets (Dendrite, Vasculature, Neuron) on all three metrics (SSIM, PSNR, DICE). On the Dendrite dataset, it reaches SSIM 0.6742, PSNR 29.74, and DICE 0.7557, improving over the INR baseline by 15.5%, 15.2%, and 64.7% respectively. On Vasculature, it attains SSIM 0.5787, PSNR 22.35, and DICE 0.7158; on Neuron, SSIM 0.4845, PSNR 26.66, and DICE 0.6708. Naive diffusion (without INR guidance or projection conditioning) performs catastrophically, with SSIM values near 0.02–0.03 across all datasets, confirming the necessity of the INR prior and conditional guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MicroDiffusion outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. Compared to the INR baseline (its own prior), the gains are most dramatic on Dendrite (DICE 0.7557 vs. 0.4589, a 64.7% relative improvement) and moderate on Vasculature (DICE 0.7158 vs. 0.7136) and Neuron (DICE 0.6708 vs. 0.6403). Against interpolation-based methods, MicroDiffusion wins on SSIM and DICE across all datasets, though Interpolation-cubic slightly edges it on PSNR for Dendrite (28.85 vs. 29.74 — actually MicroDiffusion still wins). Naive Diffusion (no INR prior, no projection conditioning) collapses to near-zero SSIM (~0.02–0.03) on all datasets, demonstrating that neither the diffusion architecture alone nor simple interpolation-based diffusion variants can recover meaningful 3D structure without the INR global prior and 2D projection conditioning. The Interpolation Diffusion variant (using linear interpolation as prior instead of INR) achieves intermediate results, confirming that the quality of the 3D prior is the dominant factor in reconstruction fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MicroDiffusion sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: Implicit Neural Representations (INR/NeRF) for continuous 3D field modeling and Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) for detail-rich generative reconstruction. It departs from prior INR-diffusion hybrids like IDM (which uses INR as a diffusion decoder) and Magic123 (which relies on pretrained 2D diffusion priors) by instead using a from-scratch INR as a structural prior that is linearly interpolated into the noisy diffusion input at every denoising step. The Gaussian-based positional encoding is borrowed from NeRP, and the conditioning mechanism follows the classifier-free guidance paradigm, but the specific integration of INR output as a pixel-wise structural prior during the reverse diffusion process is the paper's core architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete and practically important trade-off in volumetric optical microscopy: non-diffracting beams (Bessel, Airy) offer n-fold speedup but optically collapse depth, and MicroDiffusion recovers depth-resolved 3D volumes from the resulting 2D projections. Key limitations include evaluation on synthetic projections at 128×128 resolution, a fixed step length of 6 (with sparse data extending to ~16), and the absence of validation on real non-diffracting-beam experimental data. Open questions include how the method handles structured (non-Gaussian) noise inherent to real microscopy, scalability to larger volumes and higher resolutions, and whether the INR-guided interpolation strategy generalizes to other ill-posed 3D reconstruction problems beyond axial projection.
