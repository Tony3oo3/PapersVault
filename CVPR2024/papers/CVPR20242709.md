## Problem

SDS-based image-to-3D methods suffer from collapsed geometry and texture saturation because they uniformly sample diffusion time-steps during optimization, treating coarse-grained structural learning and fine-grained texture refinement as equally important at every iteration. This indiscriminate treatment entangles holistic shape formation with local detail refinement, preventing the student 3D model from progressing naturally from rough structure to precise surface.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single arbitrary reference image and preprocesses it with SAM (segmentation), Dense Prediction Transformer (depth and normal maps), and BLIP2 (text caption) before optimization. It produces a two-stage output: a low-resolution NeRF (Instant-NGP with hash encoding, rendered at 64×64) in stage one, followed by a high-resolution DMTet mesh (rendered at 512×512) in stage two. The full pipeline runs in approximately 20–25 minutes on a single A100 GPU, and supports user-guided multi-instance generation via customized text prompts.

## Method

DTC123 introduces a diffusion time-step curriculum with three coordinated components: (1) an annealed time-step sampling schedule where the midpoint t_mid decreases monotonically and the interval radius Δ narrows over training, allocating more iterations to small time-steps for detail exploration; (2) a progressive student representation that constrains NeRF hash-encoding bands (Eq. 3) in stage one and scales DMTet tetrahedral grids (32→64→128) in stage two, forcing the 3D model to absorb coarse geometry before fine texture; (3) a coarse-to-fine teacher guidance that uses Zero-1-to-3 alone for geometric structure at large time-steps, then combines Zero-1-to-3 (SDF geometry) with Stable Diffusion (color field) at smaller time-steps with dynamic reweighting λ. Additional techniques include LLM-augmented view-specific prompts to sharpen Stable Diffusion conditioning, azimuth-dependent gradient clipping and dropout to mitigate Janus-face artifacts, and Laplacian/normal-map regularization for surface smoothness.

## Results

On NeRF4, DTC123 achieves CLIP-Similarity 0.84, PSNR 25.14, and LPIPS 0.02, outperforming the strongest baseline Magic123 (0.80 / 24.62 / 0.03) on all three metrics. On RealFusion15, DTC123 reaches CLIP-Similarity 0.87, PSNR 21.42, and LPIPS 0.08, again leading Magic123 (0.82 / 19.50 / 0.10). The gains over Magic123 are most pronounced in multi-view consistency (CLIP-Similarity +0.04 to +0.05) and perceptual quality (LPIPS reduced by ~25–33%), while reference-view reconstruction (PSNR) improves by 0.5–1.9 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTC123 outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both NeRF4 and RealFusion15. Against Magic123 (the strongest baseline), DTC123 gains +0.04 CLIP-Similarity on NeRF4 (0.84 vs 0.80) and +0.05 on RF15 (0.87 vs 0.82), indicating substantially better multi-view consistency; the paper attributes Magic123's lower consistency to boundary seams between occluded and non-visible regions. On PSNR, DTC123 leads Magic123 by 0.52 dB on NeRF4 (25.14 vs 24.62) and 1.92 dB on RF15 (21.42 vs 19.50). LPIPS improvements are 0.01 on NeRF4 and 0.02 on RF15, reflecting smoother, less artifact-prone textures. Against RealFusion and NeuralLift, the margins are much larger (e.g., PSNR 25.14 vs 15.37 and 12.55 on NeRF4), confirming that the time-step curriculum provides a clear advantage over earlier SDS variants that lack progressive coarse-to-fine optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTC123 extends the SDS framework (DreamFusion) for image-to-3D generation by introducing a diffusion time-step curriculum that coordinates teacher and student models in a coarse-to-fine manner. While concurrent works (Dreamtime, DreamGaussian, HiFA) employ annealed time-step schedules for training efficiency, DTC123 departs by coupling the annealing with progressive student representation (hash-encoding bands, tetrahedral grid scaling) and dual-teacher guidance (Zero-1-to-3 for geometry, Stable Diffusion for texture), arguing that time-step scheduling alone is insufficient without synchronized student-teacher collaboration. The pipeline builds on Instant-NGP for low-resolution NeRF and DMTet for high-resolution mesh refinement, with LLM-augmented prompts and camera-pose debiasing as auxiliary contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The time-step curriculum is positioned as a plug-and-play training principle that could improve any SDS-based teacher-student pipeline, making it broadly applicable beyond the specific DTC123 architecture. However, the method remains optimization-based (~20-25 min per object on A100), limiting scalability compared to feed-forward approaches like One-2-3-45 or SyncDreamer. Open questions include whether the curriculum generalizes to newer teacher models (e.g., SDXL, video diffusion) and whether the two-stage NeRF→DMTet pipeline can be replaced by a single unified representation. The multi-instance generation capability via user prompts is a practical differentiator but depends on LLM quality for view-specific descriptions.
