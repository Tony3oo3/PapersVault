## Problem

2D and 3D object reconstruction methods struggle under severe occlusion in urban scenes, largely because large-scale ground-truth annotations for amodal (occluded) representations are scarce and difficult to produce. Existing synthetic occlusion approaches either rely on pure CG renderings with a wide domain gap or on 2D-only compositing (e.g., WALT) that generates physically impossible occlusion configurations, limiting training efficiency and realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241629/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The trained model takes a single RGB image from a stationary traffic camera and jointly predicts amodal 2D bounding boxes, instance segmentation masks, 2D keypoint locations, 6-DoF 3D pose (SE(3) rotation and translation), and 3D shape (PCA coefficients for vehicles, SMPL pose/shape parameters for humans). The training data is generated from time-lapse video captured at 60 fps in short bursts from 20 stationary cameras at 4K or HD resolution, yielding approximately 3.3 million car and person instances over 30 days.

## Method

WALT3D is a data-generation pipeline that mines unoccluded objects from time-lapse video via instance segmentation and tracking, reconstructs their 3D pose and shape using off-the-shelf methods (EPnP + shape optimization constrained to a ground plane for vehicles; HMR 2.0 for humans), and then composites non-intersecting 3D objects back into the background image in clip-art style ordered by camera distance to guarantee physically accurate occlusion. The off-the-shelf predictions on the original unoccluded objects serve as pseudo-groundtruth (amodal masks, keypoints, 3D pose, shape) for the composited images. A Swin Transformer + Mask R-CNN backbone is trained end-to-end with multi-task losses covering 2D classification, bounding box, mask, keypoints, and 3D reprojection/shape/pose terms (plus an adversarial SMPL validity loss for humans). The key distinction from WALT's 2D compositing is that 3D-aware placement prevents geometrically impossible overlaps, yielding higher-quality supervision that improves data efficiency by roughly 2.4× (25% of WALT3D data matches 60% of WALT2D data in AP).

## Results

On cross-evaluation with KINS and COCO-Amodal, fine-tuning with WALT3D data yields 28.8 Box AP and 23.5 Mask AP on KINS, and 43.2 Box AP and 38.7 Mask AP on COCO-A, consistently outperforming the WALT2D baseline (28.2/22.4 and 40.4/36.4 respectively). When combined with domain-specific annotations (COCO-A + KINS), WALT3D further boosts performance to 38.8 Box AP / 35.6 Mask AP on KINS and 48.7 Box AP / 45.3 Mask AP on COCO-A. In the data-efficiency study, a model trained on only 25% of WALT3D data matches the AP of one trained on 60% of WALT2D data, and at 15% training data WALT3D achieves +13.9 AP^mask over WALT2D. On MOT17 pedestrian tracking, fine-tuning the detector with WALT3D improves MOTA from 42.2 to 47.2 and DetA from 40.3 to 43.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is WALT2D [Reddy et al., CVPR 2022], which uses 2D-based clip-art compositing to generate amodal training data. WALT3D outperforms WALT2D across all reported metrics: on KINS, WALT3D gains +0.6 Box AP and +1.1 Mask AP (without domain data) and +1.4 Box AP and +2.3 Mask AP (with domain data); on COCO-Amodal, the gains are +2.8 Box AP and +2.3 Mask AP (without domain data) and +0.8 Box AP and +1.8 Mask AP (with domain data). The advantage is most pronounced in the low-data regime, where 25% of WALT3D data matches 60% of WALT2D data, attributed to physically accurate 3D occlusion configurations. The second baseline, 3D-RCNN [Kundu et al., CVPR 2018], is used for vehicle 3D reconstruction comparison; the paper reports that WALT3D-trained models consistently outperform 3D-RCNN on 3D pose estimation, particularly at high occlusion percentages (Fig. 6), though exact per-method 3D numbers are presented in figures rather than a consolidated table. On pedestrian tracking (MOT17), the WALT3D-finetuned detector with SORT improves MOTA by +5.0 and DetA by +2.9 over the COCO-pretrained detector, demonstrating downstream transfer benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct 3D extension of WALT [62], which generated 2D amodal supervision by pasting unoccluded objects from time-lapse video into background images. The key conceptual departure is replacing WALT's 2D paste-based compositing with a geometry-aware 3D compositing pipeline that arranges objects by camera distance, ensuring physically consistent occlusion configurations. For pseudo-groundtruth generation, the paper repurposes off-the-shelf 2D detectors (Mask R-CNN, HMR 2.0) and 3D reconstruction methods (EPnP, differentiable PnP) rather than relying on human annotation, positioning itself between the small human-annotated amodal datasets (KINS, COCO-Amodal) and the domain-gap-prone CG rendering approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automated, label-free pipeline addresses a fundamental bottleneck in occlusion-aware perception: the scarcity of realistic amodal supervision at scale. The data efficiency result (25% of WALT3D data matching 60% of WALT2D data) suggests that geometrically consistent occlusion configurations are a more effective training signal than raw volume, which has implications for how synthetic data should be constructed more broadly. Open questions include generalization beyond stationary traffic cameras, handling rare object categories without parametric shape models, and resolving appearance inconsistencies (lighting, weather) in clip-art composites that the authors acknowledge as a limitation.
