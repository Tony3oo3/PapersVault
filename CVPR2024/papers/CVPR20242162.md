## Problem

Existing neural rendering methods for dynamic humans (e.g., NeuMan, Vid2Avatar) rely on implicit NeRF or SDF representations that are slow to train and render, while the fast 3D Gaussian Splatting framework has not been extended to support animatable humans. The core challenge is that naively applying 3DGS to a human body produces severe artifacts (holes, pop-outs) when articulating individual Gaussians to new poses, because there is no mechanism to coordinate their movements.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 50–100 frames of an in-the-wild monocular video along with camera poses (from COLMAP) and per-frame SMPL pose/shape parameters (from a pretrained regressor). It produces a disentangled representation: ~200K 3D Gaussians for the human (each carrying position, rotation, scale, spherical-harmonic color, opacity, and per-joint LBS weights) and a separate set of static 3D Gaussians for the scene. At test time, novel-pose rendering requires only the LBS transform on the explicit Gaussians—no triplane or MLP evaluation—enabling 60 FPS rendering at HD resolution.

## Method

HUGS represents the human in a canonical Da-pose space using 3D Gaussians initialized at upsampled SMPL mesh vertices, and a feature triplane plus three MLPs (appearance, geometry, deformation) predict each Gaussian's color/opacity, positional offset/rotation/scale, and LBS weights. The key insight is jointly optimizing per-Gaussian LBS weights (regularized toward SMPL's vertex-weighted LBS) so that individual Gaussians move coherently during articulation, avoiding surface-tearing artifacts. During optimization, Gaussians are cloned, split, and pruned (with a per-Gaussian base-scale bookkeeping) to capture details beyond SMPL such as hair and clothing wrinkles. Training uses L1 + SSIM + VGG perceptual losses on both full-frame and human-masked renders, plus an L2 LBS-regularization loss, completing in ~30 minutes on a single GPU. Because the final representation is explicit, test-time animation is a closed-form LBS operation with no neural network evaluation, yielding the 60 FPS rendering speed.

## Results

On the NeuMan dataset, HUGS achieves state-of-the-art PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all six sequences except PSNR on Bike (25.46 vs. NeuMan's 25.55), with average PSNR gains of ~1.2 dB over NeuMan and ~7 dB over Vid2Avatar. On ZJU-MoCap, HUGS achieves SOTA across all six subjects and all three metrics, e.g., PSNR 34.11 on subject 386 vs. 33.53 for the best baseline (InstantNVR). Training takes 30 minutes (96× faster than Vid2Avatar, 336× faster than NeuMan) and rendering runs at 60 FPS (~7600× faster than NeuMan).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUGS outperforms NeuMan [7] (the strongest NeRF-based baseline) by 0.9–2.0 dB PSNR on 5 of 6 NeuMan sequences and by 0.05–0.10 SSIM, while matching NeuMan on Bike PSNR (25.46 vs. 25.55). Against Vid2Avatar [6] (implicit SDF + volume rendering), HUGS gains 6–11 dB PSNR, showing that the 3DGS representation is substantially more effective for this task. General dynamic-scene methods (NeRF-T [54], HyperNeRF [55]) perform poorly (12–22 dB PSNR) as they lack human-specific modeling. On ZJU-MoCap, HUGS surpasses all four baselines (NeuralBody [9], HumanNeRF [8], InstantNVR [56], MonoHuman [14]) on every subject and metric, with the largest margin over NeuralBody (up to +2.3 dB PSNR on subject 387). In speed, HUGS trains 96× faster than Vid2Avatar and 336× faster than NeuMan, and renders at 60 FPS versus ~0.008 FPS for NeuMan and ~0.002 FPS for Vid2Avatar.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUGS transplants the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework—originally designed for static scene photogrammetry—into the animatable human avatar domain, replacing the implicit NeRF/SDF representations used by NeuMan, Vid2Avatar, and HumanNeRF. The human-specific contributions are a forward deformation module that learns per-Gaussian LBS weights (regularized toward SMPL) and a triplane-plus-MLP architecture to predict each Gaussian's color, geometry, and skinning parameters, borrowing the triplane idea from neural occupancy networks. The method departs from prior 3DGS extensions to dynamic scenes (4DGS, Dynamic 3D Gaussians) by introducing pose-controlled articulation via learned LBS rather than per-frame or per-time-step Gaussian parameters, and by explicitly disentangling the human from the scene for cross-scene transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100× training speedup and 60 FPS rendering make animatable human avatars from short monocular videos practically deployable in AR/VR and visual-effects pipelines, where NeRF-based methods were prohibitively slow. The disentangled human-scene representation enables straightforward scene transfer, a capability that was cumbersome in prior NeRF-based approaches. Key open questions remain: SMPL-based LBS cannot model loose or flowing clothing deformation, the 50–100 frame input limits pose-space coverage, and the method does not handle relighting when compositing the human into a new environment—addressing these would require non-linear skinning, generative appearance priors, or explicit illumination modeling.
