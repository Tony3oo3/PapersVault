## Problem

Pre-trained text-to-image models cannot simultaneously preserve a user's identity from a selfie while rendering a specific fine-grained expression in a desired background, because identity and expression are highly entangled and have not been jointly controlled in a unified framework. Existing face manipulation methods either perform swapping or reenactment but not both at once, and expression control is typically limited to 7–8 coarse emotion labels rather than the full emotional space.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes three modalities: a selfie photo (identity), a free-text scene prompt (background), and a text expression label drawn from a 135-word emotion vocabulary. The background prompt is first rendered into an image by a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, and the expression label is used to retrieve a reference face from a 728,946-image, 135-class emotion dataset; these two derived images plus the selfie form the conditioning triplet. The output is a 512×512 RGB face image that preserves the selfie's identity, exhibits the specified fine-grained expression, and is composited into the generated background with matching pose, lighting, and hair.

## Method

DiffSFSR is a latent diffusion model (initialized from SD-1.4) that performs simultaneous face swapping and reenactment by conditioning a denoising UNet on three signals: a masked background image concatenated to the noisy latent, an identity compound embedding (concatenation of ArcFace, FaceNet, and dlib features mapped through a two-layer MLP), and an expression embedding from the DLN network, both injected via cross-attention. Three key designs differentiate it from prior work: (1) the compound identity embedding is deliberately made competitive with the expression encoder to suppress residual identity leakage from the expression path; (2) an improved midpoint sampling procedure corrects a noise-estimation error in DiffSwap's two-step sampling by following DDPM's sequential denoising (z_t → z_0 → z_{t-1} → … → z_{t₁}) rather than jumping directly from z_t to z_{t₁}; (3) the masked background is provided as explicit conditioning during training (not only at inference), forcing the network to focus optimization on facial generation and to infer pose and lighting from background context. Training minimizes a combined loss of diffusion MSE, cosine-similarity identity loss, and MSE expression loss, with PNDM sampling at 50 steps at inference.

## Results

On the SFSR (Swap All) task, DiffSFSR achieves 90.2% ID accuracy, 0.55 expression error, and 6.00 pose error on CelebA-HQ, substantially outperforming the hybrid SimSwap+StyleHEAT baseline (76.9% ID, 2.24 Exp, 7.07 Pose). In the face reenactment (Swap EXP) task, DiffSFSR reaches 99.9% ID and 0.58 Exp on CelebA-HQ, far surpassing StyleHEAT (98.2% ID, 2.43 Exp) and Face2Face (87.9% ID, 3.25 Exp). In face swapping (Swap ID), DiffSFSR achieves the best expression error (0.32) but trails SimSwap (98.8%) on identity accuracy (90.8%), reflecting the trade-off of jointly preserving both identity and expression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In face reenactment (Swap EXP), DiffSFSR decisively outperforms both Face2Face (87.9% ID, 3.25 Exp) and StyleHEAT (98.2% ID, 2.43 Exp) on both identity and expression metrics, achieving 99.9% ID and 0.58 Exp on CelebA-HQ. In face swapping (Swap ID), DiffSFSR achieves the best expression error (0.32 vs. FaceShifter's 0.65 and SimSwap's 0.93) but sacrifices identity accuracy (90.8% vs. SimSwap's 98.8%), as the model jointly optimizes both identity and expression preservation. In the novel SFSR (Swap All) task, DiffSFSR outperforms the hybrid SimSwap+StyleHEAT pipeline by a large margin on all three metrics (90.2% vs. 76.9% ID; 0.55 vs. 2.24 Exp; 6.00 vs. 7.07 Pose). In the user study (Table 2), DiffSFSR scores highest on expression consistency (3.79), realism (3.72), and image quality (3.60), while being statistically comparable to SimSwap and DiffSwap on identity consistency (3.01). Against SDXL, the paper demonstrates qualitatively that the text-to-image model cannot synthesize accurate fine-grained expressions (e.g., 'enjoyment', 'anxiety', 'grief') that DiffSFSR produces faithfully from the 135-class emotion vocabulary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of face swapping and face reenactment, two well-studied manipulation tasks that prior methods (SimSwap, DiffSwap, StyleHEAT, E4S) handle separately or via a switch operator, but never simultaneously. It builds on the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as its generative backbone and on DiffSwap's midpoint sampling, which the authors identify as mathematically suboptimal and correct. The expression conditioning leverages the identity-disentangled DLN encoder, while the identity conditioning introduces a compound embedding from three face recognition models (ArcFace, FaceNet, dlib) to counteract residual identity leakage from the expression encoder — a design motivated by the known entanglement of identity and expression that prior single-embedding approaches could not resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that simultaneous, disentangled control of identity and expression in a single diffusion model is feasible, opening a path toward truly personalized face generation where users specify who they are and how they feel independently. The 135-class fine-grained expression vocabulary is a notable step beyond the 7–8 basic emotions used in prior work, though it still relies on retrieving a reference image from a fixed dataset rather than generating expressions from scratch. Key open questions remain: the identity-expression entanglement is mitigated but not solved (the authors acknowledge a fully identity-agnostic expression encoder is still an open problem), the background is generated by a separate text-to-image model rather than being jointly optimized, and the method has only been validated at 512×512 resolution on relatively clean datasets (CelebA-HQ, FFHQ).
