## Problem

Image-to-lidar distillation methods have improved significantly, yet a large gap persists between distilled and fully supervised 3D features when measured by linear probing (45.0% vs 74.7% mIoU on nuScenes in prior work). Previous efforts focused almost exclusively on designing better distillation losses, leaving unexplored the impact of the 3D backbone architecture, the quality of the 2D teacher, and the diversity of the pretraining dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240509/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method takes a lidar point cloud (N points with 3D Cartesian coordinates and laser return intensity) and a single synchronized RGB image (resized to 224×448, randomly selected from available cameras per batch). Only points visible in the selected camera are loaded, ensuring every point has a matching pixel. The frozen 2D ViT backbone produces pixel features that supervise the trainable 3D backbone via a linear projection head; after distillation, this head is replaced by a batch-norm + linear classifier for downstream semantic segmentation.

## Method

ScaLR replaces the contrastive losses used in prior image-to-lidar distillation with a simple pointwise L2 distance between ℓ2-normalized 3D and 2D features, eliminating false negatives, the need for segment pre-computation, and all loss hyperparameters. No 2D projection head is used, preserving the structure of the pretrained 2D feature space. The paper's central finding is an empirical study showing that scaling the 3D backbone (WaffleIron from 96 to 768-dim features) and the 2D teacher (from DINO ViT-S/8 to DINOv2 ViT-L/14), combined with pretraining on a mixture of four lidar datasets (nuScenes, SemanticKITTI, PandaSet-64, PandaSet-GT), reduces the linear-probing gap to fully supervised features from ~30 points to under 11 points. Multi-dataset pretraining is enabled by the single-camera-per-batch loading strategy, which makes data loading identical regardless of the number of cameras in each dataset. The approach also supports distilling from multiple 2D teachers simultaneously by concatenating their pixel features and using a 2-layer MLP projection head on the 3D side.

## Results

ScaLR with WI-768 pretrained on multiple datasets using DINOv2 ViT-L/14 achieves 67.8% mIoU in linear probing on nuScenes validation, a 22.8-point improvement over the best prior distillation method (Seal, 45.0%) and only ~11 points below the fully supervised WI-768 upper bound (78.7%). On the Robo3D corruption benchmark, ScaLR WI-768 pretrained on multiple datasets achieves a new state-of-the-art mCE of 87.4% and mRR of 83.8%, outperforming all prior 3D backbones including GFNet (mCE 92.6%, mRR 83.3%). In finetuning with only 1% of nuScenes training data, ScaLR WI-768 reaches 50.7% mIoU versus 45.8% for Seal and 40.8% for ST-SLidR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaLR WI-768 dominates all prior image-to-lidar distillation methods by a wide margin: it achieves 67.8% mIoU in linear probing on nuScenes versus 45.0% for Seal [39] (the strongest prior method, which uses a supervised 2D backbone for segment construction), 40.5% for ST-SLidR [41], 38.8% for SLidR [58], and 35.9% for PPKT [38]. The gap is even more pronounced in low-data finetuning: ScaLR reaches 50.7% at 1% data versus 45.8% (Seal) and 40.8% (ST-SLidR). On the Robo3D robustness benchmark, ScaLR WI-768 (multiple datasets) achieves 65.7% average mIoU across eight corruptions, beating GFNet (64.0%), 2DPASS (58.6%), Cylind3D (57.4%), and SPVCNN (57.5%), while also achieving the best mCE (87.4%) and mRR (83.8%). Notably, ScaLR's advantage over prior methods grows with backbone scale and multi-dataset pretraining, suggesting that the three pillars (3D backbone, 2D backbone, and dataset diversity) matter more than the specific distillation loss design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-to-lidar distillation line (PPKT, SLidR, ST-SLidR, Seal) by deliberately shifting focus from designing better distillation losses to the 'three pillars' of backbone scale (2D and 3D) and pretraining data diversity. It departs from prior contrastive-loss-based methods by replacing them with a simple pointwise cosine similarity loss that requires no segments, no hyperparameters, and no 2D projection head. The work builds on the WaffleIron sparse-convolution architecture and DINO/DINOv2 self-supervised ViT features, positioning itself as an empirical study of what actually drives distilled feature quality rather than a new loss design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a trivially simple cosine loss combined with scaled backbones and multi-dataset pretraining closes most of the gap to fully supervised features (67.8% vs 78.7% mIoU on nuScenes) suggests the field may have over-invested in loss complexity at the expense of scale and data diversity. Multi-dataset pretraining yielding equal-or-better performance than single-dataset specialists is practically important for autonomous driving, where data from multiple lidar sensors is readily available. Open questions include whether combining this scalable strategy with more sophisticated distillation losses (or supervised 2D teachers like Seal's) could close the remaining ~11-point gap, and whether the approach transfers to open-vocabulary or detection tasks.
