## Problem

Existing methods that extend CLIP for video understanding add temporal modules to the visual encoder but leave the textual side unchanged, relying solely on coarse category names (e.g., 'clapping' vs. 'slapping') as language supervision. This creates an asymmetry: the visual side models temporal dynamics while the text encoder, which has a documented bias toward spatial nouns over temporal verbs, receives no motion-specific signal, undermining CLIP's core language-supervision principle for temporal feature learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two streams of video input: sparse frames processed by the frozen CLIP ViT for spatial semantics, and dense frames (sampled at 2× the sparse rate) processed by a trainable motion encoder for temporal dynamics. On the text side, it takes pre-generated motion-enhanced descriptions per category (one conceptual description plus k discriminative descriptions contrasting the class with its top-k most similar classes), produced offline by GPT-4. The output is a pair of aligned embeddings — a fused video embedding and a fused text embedding — from which action class labels are predicted via cosine similarity; the motion encoder and text adapter are the only trainable components while the CLIP backbone remains frozen.

## Method

MoTED introduces symmetric temporal disentangling on both the visual and textual sides. On the text side, a Text Motion Adapter (multi-head self-attention) is inserted after the frozen CLIP text encoder to reweight token representations toward motion semantics, followed by adaptive pooling over the k+1 descriptions per category. On the visual side, a parallel Motion Encoder processes dense frames through a 3D convolutional stem and a stack of Temporal Blocks, each comprising Local Motion Extraction (3D convolutions + token merging), Global Motion Extraction (cross-frame self-attention over class tokens from the image encoder), and Feature Integration (cross-attention between local and global features). Training uses two contrastive losses: L_motion aligns the pooled visual motion embedding with the text motion embedding, and L_video aligns cross-attention-fused video and text embeddings, preserving CLIP's original spatial alignment while independently supervising temporal features with the richer LLM-generated descriptions.

## Results

On Kinetics-400 (K400), MoTED with ViT-B/16 achieves 86.2% top-1 accuracy (32 frames, 3 clips), surpassing the closest CLIP-based competitor DiST (85.0%) by 1.2% and ST-Adapter (82.7%) by 3.5%. With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, MoTED reaches 88.8% top-1, outperforming DiST (88.0%) by 0.8%. On the fine-grained SSv2 benchmark, gains are more pronounced: MoTED achieves 71.9% (ViT-B) and 73.8% (ViT-L), beating DiST by 1.0% and 0.7% respectively. In zero-shot evaluation, MoTED leads on all three benchmarks—HMDB51 (58.2%), UCF101 (78.3%), and K600 (69.9%)—with notably lower variance (~1%) than prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoTED outperforms all CLIP-based baselines on K400: it beats DiST (the closest competitor) by 1.2% (ViT-B) and 0.8% (ViT-L), ST-Adapter by 3.5%/1.6%, EVL by 2.0%/1.5%, and X-CLIP by 1.5%. Against non-CLIP video models, MoTED ViT-B surpasses Video Swin (83.1%) by 3.1%, TAdaConvNeXtV2 (83.7%) by 2.5%, and SlowFast (79.8%) by 6.4%. The only methods exceeding MoTED are UniformerV2 (89.3%) and TAdaFormer (89.5%), both of which use additional Kinetics-710 pretraining data beyond CLIP-400M, making the comparison not strictly apples-to-apples. On SSv2, MoTED's advantage over DiST narrows slightly (1.0%/0.7%) but remains consistent, while the gap over EVL widens dramatically (9.5% for ViT-B). In zero-shot transfer, MoTED leads DiST by 2.8% on HMDB51 and 6.0% on UCF101, and Vita-CLIP by 9.6% and 3.3% respectively, with the smallest standard deviation (~1%) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-to-video transfer learning paradigm (X-CLIP, EVL, ST-Adapter, DiST) by identifying and addressing a fundamental asymmetry: prior work adds temporal modules to the visual encoder while leaving the text side to rely solely on coarse category names. Building most directly on DiST's visual-side spatial-temporal disentangling, MoTED introduces a symmetric parallel path on the textual side, where a Text Motion Adapter processes LLM-generated motion-enhanced descriptions (conceptual and discriminative) to produce motion-specific text embeddings. The use of GPT-4 to generate pairwise discriminative descriptions between confusing action categories departs from prior LLM-in-vision work (VFC, LAVILA) that uses LLMs for event descriptions or hard-negative mining, instead targeting the motion concept itself as language supervision for temporal visual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that enriching the language supervision side with LLM-generated motion descriptions is as effective as (and complementary to) adding temporal modules to the visual encoder, challenging the field's implicit assumption that temporal modeling is purely a visual-side problem. The zero-shot generalization gains (especially +3.8% on HMDB51 over DiST) suggest that richer textual motion semantics transfer well to unseen categories. Limitations include dependence on GPT-4 for description generation (cost, reproducibility), the need to compute text-embedding similarities to identify confusing pairs (dataset-specific preprocessing), and evaluation restricted to action recognition benchmarks. Open questions include whether this approach extends to video-text retrieval, dense video understanding, or settings where category names are unavailable at inference time.
