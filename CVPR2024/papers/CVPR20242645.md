## Problem

Electron microscopy scanning of wide field-of-view specimens can take years, and existing super-resolution methods for EM images are limited to 4× magnification, failing to meet the demand for larger factors that would dramatically reduce acquisition time. No generative learning-based framework existed for large-factor (8× or 16×) EMSR, and the repetitive structures and volumetric coherence inherent to EM data had not been exploited through generative priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242645/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a sequence of 2N+1 = 5 consecutive grayscale LR EM images at resolution H×W (N=2) and produces a single super-resolved HR EM image at rH×rW with r ∈ {8, 16}, corresponding to 64× or 256× less data acquired. LR patches are 16×16 for 16× EMSR and 32×32 for 8× EMSR during training. The method is evaluated on both reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS) and downstream neuron segmentation accuracy (VOI, ARAND) using pre-trained Superhuman and MALA networks.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages: (1) a VQGAN is trained to learn a discrete codebook of 1024 latent vectors (d=512) that captures cell-specific structural and textural priors from HR EM images; (2) a latent vector indexer is trained to predict codebook entry probabilities directly from LR images, avoiding the information loss of bicubic interpolation into the VQGAN encoder; (3) a Multi-Scale Prior Fusion (MPF) module fuses decoder features at multiple resolutions using a VGG19-based cosine-similarity mask to preserve LR fidelity, followed by a Pyramid Optical-flow-based Deformable convolution (POD) module for coarse-to-fine axial alignment of adjacent image features and a 3D Spatial-Attention (3DA) module for volumetric feature fusion. The key insight is reframing large-factor EMSR as a generative indexing task—matching degraded LR observations to their nearest HR feature representations in a learned latent space—rather than attempting direct pixel-level upsampling.

## Results

On CREMI C for 16× EMSR, the proposed method achieves the best perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.4790, DISTS 0.2927) and segmentation accuracy (VOI-S 2.96, ARAND-S 0.31; VOI-M 2.26, ARAND-M 0.18) among all compared methods, while EDVR slightly leads on PSNR (23.89 vs. 23.68) and SSIM (0.615 vs. 0.602). For 8× EMSR on CREMI C, the method again achieves the best LPIPS (0.3568), DISTS (0.2291), and all segmentation metrics (VOI-S 2.94, VOI-M 1.50), with PSNR 29.70 and SSIM 0.800 nearly matching EDVR (29.73, 0.802). On the AC4 dataset, similar trends hold: the method dominates on perceptual and segmentation metrics at both 16× and 8× scales, with particularly large VOI-S improvements (e.g., 4.37 vs. 4.82 for EDVR at 16×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against four single-image SR baselines (RCAN, SwinIR, BSRN, Real-ESRGAN) and two video SR baselines (EDVR, BasicVSR), all retrained on the same EM data. Against all single-image methods, the proposed framework achieves substantially better perceptual quality (e.g., LPIPS 0.479 vs. 0.533–0.545 at 16× on CREMI) and segmentation accuracy (VOI-S 2.96 vs. 4.08–5.57), while also matching or exceeding their PSNR/SSIM. Against BasicVSR, the method wins on every metric at both scales and both datasets. The closest competitor is EDVR, which slightly outperforms the proposed method on PSNR (23.89 vs. 23.68 at 16× CREMI) and SSIM, but the proposed method clearly surpasses EDVR on all perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.479 vs. 0.502; DISTS 0.293 vs. 0.318) and all segmentation metrics (VOI-S 2.96 vs. 4.17; ARAND-S 0.31 vs. 0.41 at 16× CREMI), indicating that the generative priors and multi-scale fusion produce structurally more faithful reconstructions that better serve downstream segmentation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VQGAN's encoder-codebook-decoder architecture to learn a discrete latent space of HR EM images, but departs from prior generative restoration methods (e.g., VQFR) by introducing a latent vector indexer that predicts codebook indices directly from LR images rather than interpolating them into the VQGAN encoder. For multi-image feature alignment and fusion, it adapts video super-resolution techniques (SPyNet optical flow, deformable convolutions, spatial attention) to exploit inter-section coherence in volumetric EM data. The key conceptual departure from prior EMSR work is framing large-factor (8×, 16×) reconstruction as a generative indexing problem that leverages the repetitive, predictable structures inherent to EM specimens, rather than treating it as a purely discriminative restoration task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Large-factor EMSR could reduce EM scanning time by 64–256×, making whole-organ and whole-brain connectomics studies far more feasible; the generative prior approach is particularly well-suited to EM because of the repetitive cellular structures that make the latent space compact and learnable. Limitations include reliance on bicubic downsampling for training pairs (not real EM degradation), cell-specific priors that may not transfer across specimen types, and PSNR that trails EDVR slightly. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to new cell types without retraining the codebook, whether real degradation models would further improve performance, and whether the approach extends to the axial (z-direction) SR task.
