## Problem

Training object detection DNNs for automotive radar requires large annotated datasets that are extremely costly to collect, particularly for long-range and adverse-weather scenarios. Generative approaches (GANs) still demand substantial unlabeled real data per radar type and mounting configuration, while existing physical radar simulations require proprietary hardware and signal-processing details that suppliers rarely disclose. No prior work has quantified whether DNNs trained on physically simulated radar images can match or exceed those trained on real data.

## Input / Output

The simulation takes a 3D scene rendered in CARLA, a measured 3D radar point-spread function (PSF) spanning range, azimuth, and Doppler dimensions (obtained from a single narrow-object measurement such as a pole or corner reflector), and a noise variance parameter. It produces a synthetic radar image in range-azimuth format (max over Doppler) with accompanying object bounding-box annotations. The PSF is truncated to retain 99% of its energy, reducing its volume by a factor exceeding 1000 and making the convolution tractable.

## Method

RadSimReal replaces the conventional radar hardware simulation (transmit waveform, antenna array, sampling, beamforming, and signal-processing chain) with a single 3D convolution of scene reflection points with the radar's measured PSF, followed by additive noise. Reflection points are obtained by ray-tracing RF propagation paths from the radar to scene objects in CARLA, with intensities computed from surface material, orientation, and distance. The key insight is that the PSF acts as a black-box encapsulation of all radar-specific hardware and processing, so no proprietary design knowledge is needed—only a one-time measurement. The paper proves mathematical equivalence between this PSF-convolution approach and the full conventional simulation when the complete PSF is used, and shows that truncating to 99% energy makes the difference indistinguishable from noise. Three standard detection architectures (U-Net, RADDet, Probabilistic) are trained on 10,000 synthetic images and evaluated on RADDet, CARRADA, and CRUW, demonstrating performance on par with real-data training for same-dataset splits and superior cross-dataset generalization.

## Results

On the RADDet test set, U-Net trained on RadSimReal achieves AP@0.1/0.3/0.5 of 85.63/82.16/57.64, matching or slightly exceeding the RADDet-trained model (84.76/83.01/55.53). On CRUW, RadSimReal-trained U-Net scores 86.82/77.51/55.47 versus 86.66/78.83/56.54 for CRUW-trained, again comparable. The most striking result is on CARRADA, where RadSimReal-trained U-Net reaches 70.77/62.47/43.96, substantially outperforming the CARRADA-trained model (50.99/49.00/44.65) and the RADDet-trained cross-dataset model (62.40/56.84/30.78). The FID score between RadSimReal synthetic data and RADDet real data (6.54) is comparable to the FID between RADDet train and test splits (6.76), confirming statistical similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between models trained on RadSimReal synthetic data versus models trained on real data (RADDet, CARRADA, or CRUW), all evaluated on real test sets. On same-dataset evaluation (RADDet→RADDet, CRUW→CRUW), RadSimReal-trained models match or marginally exceed real-trained models across all three architectures (U-Net, Probabilistic, RADDet), with differences within ~1–2 AP points. On CARRADA, RadSimReal-trained models substantially outperform both the CARRADA-trained baseline (+19.8 AP@0.1 for U-Net) and the RADDet-trained cross-dataset baseline (+8.4 AP@0.1), likely because CARRADA's small training set (2208 images) limits real-data training. The Probabilistic model shows the largest gain from RadSimReal on RADDet test (52.36 vs 40.68 AP@0.5, +11.7 points). Combining RadSimReal with real RADDet training data (Table 2) yields only marginal improvement (~0.5 AP), confirming the synthetic-to-real domain gap is already negligible.

## Relation to Prior Work

RadSimReal departs from two established lines of work for generating synthetic radar data: (1) generative methods (GANs, VAEs) that require large volumes of unlabeled real radar data per sensor configuration, and (2) conventional physical radar simulations (MaxRay, ViRa, Schußler et al.) that demand full knowledge of proprietary radar hardware parameters and signal processing chains. The key conceptual departure is replacing the entire hardware-level simulation pipeline with a single PSF-based 3D convolution, a standard radar concept repurposed to eliminate the need for radar design expertise. The environmental simulation (ray tracing in CARLA) follows prior physical simulation approaches, but no prior work has evaluated whether physically simulated radar data can close the object detection performance gap to real data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first empirical demonstration that physically simulated radar images can train object detection DNNs to real-data-comparable (and in cross-dataset settings, superior) performance, validating physical simulation as a practical alternative to both real data collection and GAN-based generation. The PSF-based approach removes a significant practical barrier—proprietary radar hardware knowledge—making the tool broadly applicable across radar types. Limitations include validation on a single radar model (TI AWR1843), azimuth-only simulation, and a relatively small number of test datasets; open questions include generalization to elevation, different radar architectures (e.g., 4D radar), and whether the PSF-based equivalence holds under extreme clutter or multi-path conditions.
