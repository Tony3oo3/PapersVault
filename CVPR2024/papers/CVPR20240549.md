## Problem

One-stage weakly supervised semantic segmentation suffers from CAM confirmation bias: the shared backbone features are simultaneously used to generate CAM pseudo-labels and to supervise the segmentation head, so incorrect pseudo-labels reinforce the CAM's own errors in a self-amplifying loop. Existing one-stage methods mitigate this by applying a fixed high threshold to discard unreliable pseudo-labels, but this throws away many correct pixels and leaves semantically ambiguous regions entirely unsupervised, limiting final segmentation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240549/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images cropped to 448×448 with only image-level multi-class labels (no pixel annotations), processed independently by two ViT-B sub-nets. It outputs pixel-level segmentation masks (21 classes on VOC, 80 on COCO) and class activation maps from each sub-net. At inference, multi-scale testing and dense CRF post-processing are applied to the segmentation head output, following standard WSSS practice.

## Method

DuPL employs a co-training dual student architecture where two ViT-B sub-nets (backbone + classifier + segmentation head) cross-supervise each other: each sub-net's CAM pseudo-labels train the other's segmentation head, breaking the self-reinforcing confirmation bias. A symmetrized discrepancy loss with stop-gradient enforces representation-level diversity between the two sub-nets so their CAMs activate different regions. Trustworthy progressive learning then gradually lowers the foreground threshold via cosine descent to include more pixels in supervision, while an adaptive noise filter based on a two-component GMM fitted to per-pixel segmentation losses (via EM) identifies and excludes noisy pseudo-labels. Finally, consistency regularization with strong RandAugment perturbations is applied to the filtered unreliable regions, ensuring every pixel receives some form of supervision rather than being discarded entirely.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, DuPL with ViT-B (ImageNet-1k) achieves 72.2% mIoU on val and 71.6% on test, while DuPL with ImageNet-21k pretrained weights (DuPL†) reaches 73.3% val and 72.8% test. On MS COCO 2014, DuPL† achieves 44.6% mIoU on val. These results surpass the best one-stage competitor ToCo (ViT-B) by +2.4% (val) and +2.2% (COCO val), and are competitive with or exceed multi-stage methods such as OCR (72.7% val) and PPC (72.6% val).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuPL outperforms all one-stage WSSS baselines by a wide margin: +2.4% over ToCo (69.8→72.2 val), +4.9% over TSCD (67.3→72.2), +5.0% over SLRNet (67.2→72.2), +6.2% over AFA (66.0→72.2), and +9.5% over 1Stage (62.7→72.2) on VOC val mIoU. On COCO val, DuPL gains +2.2% over ToCo (41.3→43.5) and +4.6% over TSCD (40.1→43.5). Against multi-stage methods, DuPL† (73.3% val) slightly exceeds OCR (72.7%), PPC (72.6%), and L2G (72.1%), though it trails PPC on VOC test (72.8 vs. 73.6) and ACR on COCO val (44.6 vs. 45.3). The paper also reports that DuPL achieves 90.1% of its fully-supervised upper bound (72.2/80.5), surpassing ToCo's 86.7% ratio, indicating a smaller gap to full supervision. In terms of CAM pseudo-label quality (Table 1), DuPL† reaches 76.0% mIoU on VOC train, exceeding multi-stage pipelines like PPC+PSA (73.3%) and ACR+IRN (72.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CAM-based pseudo-label generation paradigm (Zhou et al., CVPR 2016) and the ViT-B + patch token contrast baseline from ToCo (Ru et al., CVPR 2023), but departs from prior one-stage WSSS methods (1Stage, AFA, ToCo, TSCD) that rely on fixed high thresholds to discard unreliable pseudo-labels. Conceptually, it imports the co-training paradigm from semi-supervised learning to address what the authors identify as an overlooked 'CAM confirmation bias' in one-stage pipelines, where the segmentation head and CAM generator share backbone features and mutually reinforce incorrect activations. The adaptive noise filtering via GMM draws on noisy-label learning (Co-teaching, DivideMix) but is adapted to the online, continuously-updating pseudo-labels unique to one-stage WSSS, and the consistency regularization on discarded regions extends the 'every pixel matters' philosophy beyond prior threshold-based filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic (CAM confirmation bias) and a practical recipe that closes the gap between one-stage and multi-stage WSSS, making the simpler one-stage pipeline competitive without the multi-stage refinement overhead. The dual student requirement doubles backbone compute, and the GMM-based noise filtering plus dynamic threshold schedule introduce several hyperparameters that require careful tuning (as shown in the ablations), which may limit straightforward adoption. Open questions include whether the confirmation bias analysis and dual student remedy generalize to other weak supervision forms (scribbles, bounding boxes) or to non-ViT backbones, and whether the progressive threshold schedule can be replaced by a learned or data-driven criterion.
