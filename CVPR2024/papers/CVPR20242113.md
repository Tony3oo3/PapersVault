## Problem

Existing medical image segmentation models address either multi-scale or multi-frequency features in isolation, overlooking that both dimensions carry complementary information critical for modality-agnostic and domain-generalizable performance. Additionally, multi-task learning with deep supervision introduces information loss during drastic upsampling of low-resolution feature maps, degrading boundary prediction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242113/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 2D medical images resized to 352×352 pixels as input and produces three outputs per decoder stage: a region segmentation mask (core task), a boundary map, and a distance map (sub-tasks). The final segmentation output is an ensemble sum of upsampled sub-task predictions rather than a single parallel prediction, and the architecture supports both binary and multi-label segmentation (e.g., optic disk and optic cup in fundus images).

## Method

MADGNet combines a ResNeSt encoder with a decoder built from MFMSA blocks and E-SDM. The MFMSA block first applies Multi-Frequency Channel Attention (MFCA), which decomposes features into S=3 scale branches, applies 2D DCT with top-K=16 frequency selection to produce frequency-aware channel attention maps via global avg/max/min pooling and two FC layers, then applies Multi-Scale Spatial Attention (MSSA) with learnable foreground/background parameters to extract discriminative boundary cues at each scale before aggregating via upsampling and residual connection. The E-SDM replaces parallel multi-task decoding with a cascading forward stream (each sub-task prediction is conditioned on the previous via sigmoid-gated spatial attention) followed by a backward stream that recursively upsamples and sums all sub-task predictions, so the final core-task output is an ensemble of L+1 upsampled predictions that compensates for information loss from ×16 upsampling.

## Results

On seen clinical settings (Table 1), MADGNet achieves the highest DSC across all six modalities: 90.2% (ISIC2018), 83.7% (COVID19-1), 81.3% (BUSI), 92.0% (DSB2018), 93.9% (CVC-ClinicDB), and 90.7% (Kvasir), improving DSC and mIoU by 1.1% and 1.0% on average over the second-best method M2SNet. On unseen clinical settings (Table 2), MADGNet maintains its lead, e.g., 72.2% DSC on COVID19-2 vs. 68.6% for M2SNet and 88.4% on STU vs. 79.4% for M2SNet. For multi-label fundus segmentation (Table 3), MADGNet achieves 84.9% DSC on OD and 78.5% on OC (REFUGE, seen), and 88.8% and 83.6% on Drishti-GS (unseen), outperforming all baselines. The model uses ~31M parameters with an inference speed of 0.024 s/image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADGNet outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset in both seen and unseen settings. The closest competitor is M2SNet (multi-scale subtraction), which MADGNet surpasses by 1.1% DSC on average across seen datasets and by a wider margin on unseen datasets (e.g., +3.6% DSC on COVID19-2, +9.0% on STU, +10.4% on MonuSeg2018). FRCUNet, the primary multi-frequency baseline, is outperformed by ~2.0% DSC on average; the gap is especially large on Radiology (83.7 vs. 77.3 on COVID19-1) and Dermoscopy (90.2 vs. 88.9 on ISIC2018). Transformer-based methods (TransUNet, HiFormer) struggle most on low-contrast Dermoscopy and noisy Radiology/Ultrasound, trailing MADGNet by 10–15% DSC on those modalities. Classic U-Net variants (UNet, AttUNet, UNet++) are significantly outperformed, particularly on Radiology where UNet scores only 47.7% DSC versus MADGNet's 83.7%. Multi-scale methods (CENet, MSRFNet, DCSAUNet) handle boundaries well but degrade under severe noise, while MADGNet's combined multi-frequency + multi-scale attention maintains consistent performance across all noise levels and lesion sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MADGNet sits at the intersection of two largely separate lines of work in medical image segmentation: multi-scale feature fusion (CENet, MSRFNet, DCSAUNet, M2SNet) and multi-frequency channel attention (FRCUNet, FCANet). The MFMSA block directly adapts FCANet's 2D DCT-based top-K channel attention and nests it within a multi-scale spatial attention pipeline, arguing that prior methods treat scale and frequency as interchangeable or complementary in a trivial way rather than as independent, jointly necessary dimensions. The ESDM departs from standard parallel deep supervision (as in UNet++, M2SNet) by introducing a cascaded forward-backward ensemble that explicitly compensates for information loss during drastic upsampling, a problem the authors identify as underappreciated in the multi-task segmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the most comprehensive cross-modality evaluations in medical image segmentation (15 datasets, 6 modalities, both seen and unseen clinical settings), offering practitioners a strong reference point for modality-agnostic model selection. The ESDM's finding that parallel deep supervision can actively degrade performance relative to an ensemble formulation is a useful practical insight for anyone training multi-task segmentation networks. However, the authors acknowledge that memory efficiency remains a barrier to real clinical deployment, and the modest average gains over the strongest baseline (M2SNet: ~1% DSC) suggest the architectural combination, while effective, may not yet represent a fundamental shift in how segmentation features are extracted.
