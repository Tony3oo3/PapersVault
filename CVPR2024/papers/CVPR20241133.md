## Problem

Existing 3D Scene Graph Generation (3DSGG) methods rely on labor-intensive ground-truth object and predicate annotations and are trained on closed-set classes, making them unable to recognize novel objects or predicates in real-world scenarios. This paper tackles both limitations by proposing an unsupervised, open-vocabulary 3DSGG framework that trains a 3DSG feature extractor without any 3DSG labels and generalizes to unseen classes at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point clouds with class-agnostic instance segmentation, multi-view RGB images with associated camera poses, and free-form text captions as training inputs. Text is not used at sentence level but is decomposed into word-level components by part of speech (Subject, Object, Predicate, Adjective, Adjective-Subject, Adjective-Object, Other). At inference, the model produces object and predicate class labels by matching 3DSG features against CLIP text-encoder embeddings of prompts like 'a point cloud of a {object class}' or 'a point cloud of a {subject} {predicate} a {object}', enabling zero-shot and open-vocabulary recognition without a fixed class set.

## Method

CCL-3DSGG uses a frozen CLIP text encoder and image encoder to extract cross-modality features, which are aligned to a trainable PointNet+GNN 3DSG feature extractor via two contrastive losses. The Text-3DSG (T3D) loss first grammar-parses captions into word-level tokens by part of speech, then aligns object-related words (S, O) to object features and predicate-related words (P, A, AS, AO) to predicate features, with negative samples augmented by exchanging adjectives between different object pairs to preserve sentence structure while altering semantics. The Multi-view Image-3DSG (I3D) loss treats the camera view corresponding to each 3D instance as a positive sample and all other views as negatives, enhancing spatial discrimination of predicates. The total loss is a weighted sum of I3D and T3D losses (both weighted 0.5), and no object or predicate classification losses are used in the unsupervised setting. At test time, open-vocabulary recognition is achieved by computing cosine similarity between the learned 3DSG features and CLIP text embeddings of class-name prompts, optionally augmented with image features when available.

## Results

On the 3DSSG dataset, CCL-3DSGG achieves a mean Recall of 60.1 and Mean Recall of 51.3 across SGCLS and PREDCLS tasks, surpassing the previous SOTA VL-SAT (54.4/47.1) by 5.7 and 4.2 points respectively. In the unsupervised setting (Table 3), the method achieves mR@50 of 19.6 (SGCLS) and 33.2 (PREDCLS), outperforming VL-SAT without classification losses (10.1/13.9) by over 9 points. For open-vocabulary 3DSGG (Table 4), CCL-3DSGG reaches R@50 of 37.1 (SGCLS) and 64.8 (PREDCLS), and for zero-shot 3DSGG it achieves R@50 of 35.5 (SGCLS) and 49.1 (PREDCLS), exceeding VL-SAT by 25.1% on average in the zero-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL-3DSGG outperforms all eight baselines on both SGCLS and PREDCLS in the supervised setting. Against the closest competitor VL-SAT [48] (CVPR'23), the method gains +5.7 in mean Recall (60.1 vs 54.4) and +4.2 in Mean Recall (51.3 vs 47.1), with particularly large improvements on SGCLS R@20 (37.6 vs 32.0, +5.6). Compared to KISGP [61] (NeurIPS'22), the gap widens to +13.7 in mean Recall (60.1 vs 46.4). The earliest baseline SGPN [47] (CVPR'20) trails by a substantial margin of +17.9 in mean Recall. In the unsupervised setting (Table 3), CCL-3DSGG achieves mR@50 of 33.2 on PREDCLS versus VL-SAT's 13.9 and KISGP's 9.2, a 104.4% relative improvement over VL-SAT. For open-vocabulary and zero-shot evaluation (Table 4), the method surpasses VL-SAT by +14.0 R@50 on OV-SGCLS (37.1 vs 23.1) and +5.6 R@50 on zero-shot PREDCLS (49.1 vs 43.5), confirming consistent gains across all evaluation protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's cross-modality contrastive pre-training and VL-SAT's PointNet-based 3DSG feature extractor, but departs from both in critical ways. Where prior 3D-CLIP works (PointCLIP, ULIP) and VL-SAT operate at the sentence level, CCL-3DSGG introduces grammar-based word-level decomposition with part-of-speech tagging and adjective-exchange negative augmentation to achieve finer-grained alignment with 3DSG annotations. It also extends the multi-view image contrastive paradigm by treating the current camera view as a positive and other views as negatives, a design specifically motivated by spatial predicate discrimination. The paper positions itself as the first to combine unsupervised training (no 3DSG labels) with open-vocabulary inference in the 3D scene graph setting, whereas prior OV SGG work (Tao et al., Zhang et al.) was confined to 2D and supervised.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive 3DSG annotations and enabling recognition of unseen object and predicate classes, this work addresses two of the most practical barriers to deploying 3DSGG in real-world robotics and autonomous driving. The ablation showing that word-level grammar parsing is essential (mR@20 drops from 29.4 to 6.8 without it) provides a useful design principle for future cross-modality alignment in structured prediction. However, the paper's impact is tempered by its reliance on indoor 3DSSG/ScanNet scenes, qualitative-only evaluation on ScanNet, and the acknowledged limitation that test-time image fusion is underexploited. Open questions include whether the grammar-parsing strategy generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether quantitative OV benchmarks can be constructed for datasets lacking 3DSG ground truth.
