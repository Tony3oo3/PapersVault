## Problem

Transformer-based portrait matting methods rely on self-attention, which acts as a low-pass filter that captures global semantic structure but neglects high-frequency contour details critical for precise alpha matte boundaries. Existing workarounds like IFormer require manually tuned channel ratios per block, introducing fragility and reducing adaptability. The paper addresses how to make transformers autonomously attend to both low-frequency semantics and high-frequency contours without hand-crafted parameters.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (no trimap or auxiliary background) at resolutions of 224×224, 512×288, or 512×512 and produces a full-resolution alpha matte via bilinear upsampling from a 1/4-scale prediction. Training composites foregrounds from VideoMatte240K-JPEG-SD and AIM with backgrounds from BG10K, and the network is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

EFormer uses a ResNet50 backbone to extract a feature pyramid, then feeds 1/8-resolution features (F_HR) and 1/16-resolution features (F_LR) into a custom transformer decoder. The key mechanism is a Semantic and Contour Detector (SCD) that cascades a cross-attention layer (using F_HR as keys and F_LR as queries/values) followed by a self-attention layer: the cross-attention autonomously allocates attention to discrete contour regions by matching low-resolution semantic queries against high-resolution detail keys, while the subsequent self-attention recovers and reinforces global portrait semantics. Two parallel MLP branches—Contour-Edge Extraction Branch (CEEB) and Semantic Extraction Branch (SEB)—then independently purify the contour and semantic feature streams by merging SCD output with their respective resolution-level embeddings. The purified contour and semantic features are fused and combined with 1/4-resolution backbone features in a segmentation head to produce the final matte, eliminating the need for any manually adjusted channel ratios.

## Results

On VideoMatte240K-JPEG-SD (512×288), EFormer achieves MAD 2.31, MSE 0.66, Grad 0.46, and Conn 0.27, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On the AIM dataset (512×512), EFormer records MAD 7.47, MSE 2.13, Grad 2.83, and Conn 1.90, again leading all compared methods. At the lower 224×224 resolution on VideoMatte240K, EFormer reaches MAD 4.14, MSE 1.43, Grad 0.38, and Conn 0.18, maintaining its advantage over RVM (the strongest baseline) by roughly 32% in MAD and 42% in Grad.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EFormer outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest CNN-based baseline RVM [22], EFormer reduces MAD from 5.99 to 2.31 (−61%) and Grad from 1.10 to 0.46 (−58%) on VideoMatte240K at 512×288, and from 14.84 to 7.47 (−50%) on AIM. Compared to BGMv2 [21], which relies on an auxiliary background image, EFormer achieves lower MAD (2.31 vs. 4.19) and substantially lower Grad (0.46 vs. 1.30) while operating in a trimap-free, single-image setting. MODNet [17] and DeepLabv3 [4] are significantly weaker, with MODNet's MAD of 10.39 being 4.5× higher than EFormer's, and DeepLabv3 trailing by over 6× in MAD. The consistent superiority across both datasets and three resolutions confirms that the cross-attention-based contour detection mechanism provides a clear advantage over purely self-attention or CNN-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

EFormer builds on the DETR-style transformer decoder and the well-documented observation that self-attention in ViT acts as a low-pass filter, neglecting high-frequency contour details. It departs from IFormer's channel-splitting strategy (which requires manually tuned channel ratios per block) by instead using cross-attention between different-resolution feature maps to autonomously guide attention toward contour regions. The cross-resolution attention mechanism is conceptually related to RTFormer's cross-resolution attention for gathering contextual information, but here it is specifically repurposed to isolate and amplify high-frequency boundary features in a portrait matting pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a parameter-free architectural recipe for addressing the known high-frequency deficiency of transformers in dense prediction, which could be adapted beyond portrait matting to other segmentation or matting tasks. However, the evaluation is confined to a single CNN backbone (ResNet50), a single GPU, and a narrow set of trimap-free matting baselines, leaving open whether the cross-resolution attention strategy generalizes to other backbones, larger models, or non-portrait dense prediction. The modest but consistent gains over RVM and BGMv2 suggest the technique is useful but not transformative for the field.
