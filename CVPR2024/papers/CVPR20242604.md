## Problem

Existing real-world 4D human datasets (X-Humans, ActorsHQ, 4DHumanOutfit) provide only raw scans without segmented garment meshes or semantic labels, while synthetic datasets (CLOTH4D, BEDLAM) lack realistic clothing dynamics. This gap limits the ability to develop and evaluate algorithms for clothing simulation, reconstruction, and parsing on data that faithfully represents how real garments deform during motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The parsing pipeline consumes 80k-face textured 3D meshes (with 1k-resolution texture maps) rendered into 24 multi-view images (12 horizontal, 6 upper, 6 lower), combined with the previous frame's multi-view label maps for temporal continuity. It outputs per-vertex semantic labels across categories (skin, hair, shoes, upper, lower, outer clothing), from which segmented garment meshes and registered SMPL/SMPL-X body fits are extracted. The full dataset spans 78k frames across 520 five-second sequences at 30 fps covering 64 outfits.

## Method

The core contribution is a semi-automatic, template-free 4D human parsing pipeline that fuses three complementary label sources via a Graph Cut optimization. First, Graphonomy provides per-pixel semantic votes from each of 24 rendered views; second, RAFT optical flow warps the previous frame's multi-view labels into the current frame for temporal consistency; third, SAM segmentation masks regularize label coherence within locally coherent regions by computing a normalized score function over each mask. These votes are combined in a vertex-wise unary energy (weighted by barycentric coordinates for parser/flow, constant for masks) and an edge-wise binary energy that penalizes adjacent vertices with differing labels, solved as a graph cut over the mesh topology. For the ~3% of frames requiring correction, a human-in-the-loop step introduces manual rectification as an additional high-weight vote function, and the Graph Cut is re-solved, ultimately changing only 1.5% of vertices across 3.2% of frames.

## Results

The semi-automatic 4D parsing pipeline achieves 96.8% vertex-level accuracy without manual intervention, with only 1.5% of vertices requiring rectification across 3.2% of frames. On the parsing ablation (Table 2), the full pipeline (PAR+OPT+SAM) reaches 99.5% accuracy on inner outfits and 98.8% on outer outfits in BEDLAM, substantially outperforming the template-based SMPL+D baseline (84.6%/76.5%) and the multi-view-only SIZER approach (91.0%/71.4%). For the clothing simulation benchmark (Table 3), HOOD* with optimized material parameters achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance across all four garment categories (0.924 cm for Lower, 2.833 cm for Outer), while LBS-based methods (PBNS, NCS) produce significantly higher stretching energy on free-flowing garments (e.g., 1.293 for LBS on Dress vs. 0.009 for HOOD*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 4D human parsing, the proposed template-free pipeline (PAR+OPT+SAM) decisively outperforms both the SMPL+D template-based baseline (ClothCap [36]: 84.6% inner / 76.5% outer on BEDLAM) and the multi-view-only SIZER approach [43] (91.0% / 71.4%), reaching 99.3% / 98.8% respectively. In the clothing simulation benchmark, HOOD* with inverse-optimized material parameters achieves the best Chamfer Distance in all four garment categories, halving the error of the next-best method (e.g., 2.833 cm vs. 4.626 cm for LBS on Outer garments); however, un-tuned HOOD [15] actually performs worst on Outer (5.355 cm CD) due to error propagation, while LBS-based methods (PBNS [4], NCS [5]) remain competitive on tighter-fitting Upper/Lower garments. For single-view clothed human reconstruction (Table 4), SiTH [21] leads on Inner outfits (CD 2.110, IoU 0.755) while PIFuHD [38] is most stable on Outer (CD 2.393), but all seven methods—including SMPL-guided approaches like ICON [47] and ECON [48]—degrade significantly on loose outer garments, confirming the dataset's challenge. In clothing reconstruction (Table 5), SMPLicit [12] is most robust to outer clothing (CD 3.359, IoU 0.618) while BCNet [24] handles inner garments better (CD 2.079 on Upper), yet all three methods trained on synthetic data show a clear domain gap. For video-based reconstruction (Table 6), Vid2Avatar [16] outperforms SelfRecon [25] on both Inner (CD 2.870 vs. 3.180) and Outer (CD 3.014 vs. 4.027), but neither recovers fine surface details of challenging outfits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established but disconnected lines of work: synthetic 4D clothed-human datasets (CLOTH4D, BEDLAM, CLOTH3D) that offer scale and ground-truth labels but lack realism, and real-world 4D human capture datasets (X-Humans, ActorsHQ, 4DHumanOutfit) that provide photorealistic scans but no semantic annotations or segmented garment meshes. For the annotation pipeline, it departs from template-based parsing (ClothCap/SMPL+D) that fails under topology changes, and from multi-view-only voting (SIZER, MGN) that ignores temporal consistency, instead combining Graphonomy, RAFT optical flow, and SAM segmentation within a template-free Graph Cut framework. The contribution is primarily a dataset and benchmark rather than a new algorithmic paradigm, repurposing existing 2D tools in a novel 4D multi-view-temporal fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

4D-DRESS fills a critical gap by providing the first real-world 4D clothing dataset with vertex-level semantic labels, garment meshes, and SMPL(-X) fits, enabling benchmarks that expose the substantial domain gap between synthetic-trained methods and real clothing dynamics. The benchmark results reveal that even state-of-the-art reconstruction and simulation methods degrade significantly on loose garments (dresses, open jackets), with outer-outfit performance dropping sharply compared to inner outfits, suggesting that current approaches have not truly learned clothing physics. Key limitations include the high computational cost of the parsing pipeline, the need for manual 3D editing expertise, and the resulting constraint on dataset scalability. Open questions include whether real-time 4D annotation is feasible, whether learned simulators can handle heterogeneous real-world materials, and whether human body priors are actually beneficial for reconstructing loosely-fitting garments.
