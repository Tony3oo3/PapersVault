## Problem

Existing Siamese and transformer trackers formulate visual object tracking as a one-shot detection problem with a single forward pass, which lacks self-correction and makes them prone to drifting toward distractors with similar appearance. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating tracking as a progressive Noise-to-Target denoising diffusion process that iteratively refines target hypotheses rather than predicting them in one shot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241460/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 template image with a target bounding box and a 256×256 search image (current frame) as input. The target state is internally represented as a group of N=50 point sets (each containing 2 points), which are progressively refined through diffusion steps and finally converted to a bounding box via a Min-Max function. The output includes the predicted bounding box, per-point-set confidence scores, and the full point set representation, with NMS applied across all decoder layers to select the final prediction.

## Method

DiffusionTrack uses a ViT-Base encoder (pretrained from DropTrack) that jointly encodes template and search patches, feeding search features into a decoder composed of 6 stacked diffusion layers. Each diffusion layer performs three operations: RoI pooling to crop instance features, a Simplified Self-Attention layer to model inter-instance relationships, and a dynamic convolution + refinement layer that predicts point-set offsets and confidence scores conditioned on the diffusion step index. During training, Gaussian noise is added to ground-truth point sets at a random diffusion step, and the decoder is trained with focal loss plus IoU/L1 regression losses with intermediate supervision across all layers. At inference, the model starts from random point sets and iteratively denoises them over multiple evaluation steps, using DDIM to generate noisy inputs for subsequent steps, with a renewal strategy that replaces low-confidence estimations with random ones and a voting strategy to aggregate high-confidence predictions. The architecture supports dynamic trade-offs: the number of point sets, inference iterations, and early-exit threshold can all be adjusted to balance speed and accuracy.

## Results

On GOT-10k (the primary benchmark), DiffusionTrack-B256 with 2 iterative steps achieves 75.2% AO, 85.9% SR₀.₅, and 72.0% SR₀.₇₅, surpassing the previous best OSTrack-B256 (71.0% AO) by 4.2 points and SeqTrack-B256 (74.7% AO) by 0.5 points. On LaSOT, DiffusionTrack-L256 attains 72.3% AUC and 79.1% Precision, leading SeqTrack (69.9% AUC) by 2.4 points. On TrackingNet, DiffusionTrack-L256 achieves 85.2% AUC and 89.6% PNorm, and on TNL2K it reaches 56.8% AUC, outperforming all baselines by a notable margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionTrack outperforms all 27 baselines across the four main benchmarks. On GOT-10k, DiffusionTrack-B256(2) leads OSTrack-B256 by 4.2% AO (75.2 vs. 71.0), SeqTrack-B256 by 0.5% (75.2 vs. 74.7), and GRM-256 by 1.8% (75.2 vs. 73.4). On LaSOT, DiffusionTrack-L256(1) achieves 72.3% AUC, beating SeqTrack (69.9%), GRM (69.9%), and ROMTrack (69.3%) by 2.4–3.0 points. On TrackingNet, DiffusionTrack-L256(1) reaches 85.2% AUC, surpassing GRM (84.0%) and OSTrack (83.1%). On TNL2K, DiffusionTrack-L256(1) attains 56.8% AUC, outperforming OSTrack (55.9%) and SeqTrack (54.9%) by 0.9 and 1.9 points respectively. The dynamically optimized trackers (DiMP, ATOM, SiamR-CNN, etc.) trail by 5–15+ points on AUC, confirming that the Noise-to-Target diffusion paradigm provides a substantial advantage over both single-shot detection and template-update strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging trend of applying diffusion models to discriminative perception tasks (DiffusionDet for detection, DDP for segmentation) to visual object tracking, reformulating the problem as a Noise-to-Target denoising process rather than the standard one-shot detection paradigm used by Siamese and transformer trackers. The decoder architecture is directly adapted from DiffusionDet, with key modifications: binary classification instead of multi-class, point-set state instead of bounding boxes (drawing on RepPoints' Min-Max conversion), and an early-exit mechanism for dynamic inference. The use of DDIM for inter-step sampling and the ViT-Base encoder pretrained via DropTrack ground the method in established components, while the point-set diffusion formulation for tracking is the paper's core architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a generative diffusion process can serve as a self-correcting tracker, this work opens a new design axis for VOT: the number of denoising steps and point-set groups become tunable knobs for the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff, something single-pass trackers cannot offer. The ablation showing that removing both ensemble strategies collapses performance from 61.1% to 25.1% AO underscores that the iterative refinement is not a minor add-on but the core mechanism. However, the modest speed gains from early exit (16.7% faster at 0.4% AO cost) and the performance drop in the interleaved encoder-decoder variant suggest that making diffusion-based tracking genuinely faster than single-pass methods remains an open challenge. The paradigm could inspire further work on generative tracking with richer state representations or longer temporal context.
