## Problem

No prior work generates 3D hand-object interaction sequences from a text prompt; existing text-to-motion methods model full-body human motion but omit the object interaction context, severely limiting semantic expressiveness. The core difficulty is data scarcity: existing hand-object motion datasets cover far fewer interaction types and object categories than the real-world distribution, making it hard to learn physically and semantically plausible interactions from limited labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240383/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a canonical 3D object mesh (with its scale, computed as the maximum center-to-vertex distance) as input, and produces a sequence of up to 150 frames of 3D hand-object motion: 99-dimensional vectors per hand (3D translation + 16×6 MANO pose in 6D representation) and a 10-dimensional object state (3D translation + 6D rotation + 1 articulation angle) per frame. It also outputs an N×1 contact probability map on the object surface, where N is the number of FPS-sampled points, which serves as a spatial prior for where hands should contact the object.

## Method

The framework decomposes generation into three stages: (1) a VAE-based contact predictor encodes a normalized FPS point cloud, CLIP text features, and object scale to produce a category-agnostic 3D contact map and object features; (2) a Transformer-based diffusion model denoises the motion by directly predicting x_0 at each step (following MDM), conditioned on text, contact map, object features, and scale, with novel frame-wise and agent-wise positional encodings that distinguish temporal position and agent identity (left hand, right hand, object) simultaneously; (3) a lightweight Transformer refiner, trained with penetration and contact losses, adjusts hand poses in a single feed-forward pass to suppress interpenetration and tighten contact. The diffusion model is further regularized by a distance-map loss (active only when hand-object distance < 2 cm) and a relative-orientation loss to enforce geometric correctness, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on manually annotated text labels from H2O, GRAB, and ARCTIC.

## Results

On all three datasets (H2O, GRAB, ARCTIC), Text2HOI achieves the best scores across every metric. Headline numbers include FID of 0.1744 (H2O), 0.3017 (GRAB), and 0.1329 (ARCTIC); top-3 accuracy of 0.8295, 0.9218, and 0.9205 respectively; and physical realism scores of 0.7574, 0.8839, and 0.8760. The largest margins over baselines appear in physical realism (e.g., 0.8839 vs. 0.5844 for T2M on GRAB) and FID (e.g., 0.1329 vs. 0.3599 for T2M on ARCTIC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text2HOI outperforms all three baselines (T2M, MDM, IMOS) on every metric across all three datasets. The most striking advantage is in physical realism: on GRAB, Text2HOI scores 0.8839 versus 0.5844 (T2M), 0.7382 (MDM), and 0.6418 (IMOS); on ARCTIC, it reaches 0.8760 versus a near-zero 0.0143 for T2M. In terms of FID, Text2HOI achieves the lowest values (0.1744, 0.3017, 0.1329) compared to the best baseline (IMOS at 0.2945, 0.6147, 0.1826). IMOS is the closest competitor on ARCTIC accuracy (0.8190 vs. 0.9205) and FID (0.1826 vs. 0.1329), while MDM is nearly matched on GRAB multimodality (0.5194 vs. 0.5216). T2M consistently underperforms, particularly on GRAB where its accuracy drops to 0.1897, suggesting it struggles most with the hand-object interaction domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: text-to-human-motion generation (T2M, MDM, TEMOS) and hand-object motion synthesis (ManipNet, CAMS). It departs from the former by introducing object interaction as a first-class output, and from the latter by eliminating the need for object trajectory inputs and adding text as the sole semantic conditioning. Architecturally, it inherits the x₀-prediction diffusion formulation from MDM [27], the contact-prediction training objective from Contact2grasp [18], and the distance-map loss from Intergen [19], but combines them in a novel three-stage compositional pipeline (VAE contact map → Transformer diffusion motion → Transformer refiner) that has no direct precedent in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first text-guided 3D hand-object interaction generator, this work establishes a baseline and a publicly released annotated dataset (text labels for H2O, GRAB, ARCTIC) that the field can build upon. The decomposition into category-agnostic contact prediction and text-conditioned motion generation is a practical recipe for overcoming the data-scarcity bottleneck, and the demonstrated generalization to unseen objects suggests the approach could extend to open-vocabulary manipulation. Open questions include scalability to multi-object or multi-agent scenes, real-time inference (1000 diffusion steps), and whether the 150-frame limit constrains modeling of longer, more complex interactions. The requirement for a canonical object mesh as input also limits applicability to scenarios where only a single image or point cloud is available.
