## Problem

Existing masked video modeling (MVM) methods rely almost exclusively on isotropic ViT encoders, which are computationally expensive and lack the hierarchical multi-scale structure needed for dense downstream tasks like segmentation and detection. ConvNets, with their built-in pyramid architecture and low resource consumption, remain largely unexplored in MVM, raising the question of whether they can be adapted to masked video pre-training without the information leakage that dense convolutions introduce in masked regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240777/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of consecutive 224×224 RGB video frames (frame gap = 1), patchified into non-overlapping patches with 75% symmetrically masked at identical spatial positions in both frames. Only visible patches are fed to the encoders via sparse convolution (MinkowskiEngine), and the network outputs reconstructed pixel values for the masked patches along with learned spatio-temporal representations. The sparse convolution layers can be seamlessly converted back to standard dense convolutions for downstream fine-tuning without additional processing.

## Method

VideoMAC replaces dense convolutions in standard ConvNets (ResNet, ConvNeXt-v2) with sparse convolutions to prevent mask dissipation through the hierarchical downsampling stages, preserving the structural integrity of masked regions. Instead of a siamese architecture, it employs an online-target encoder pair where the target encoder's weights are updated via exponential moving average (EMA) of the online encoder, halving computational cost while enabling joint optimization. A novel reconstruction consistency loss (L_c) is introduced that penalizes the discrepancy between the online and target encoders' reconstructed patches, encoding temporal correlation under the assumption that pixel deviation between consecutive frames should be mirrored in their reconstructions. The total loss combines online reconstruction (L_o), target reconstruction (L_t), and the consistency term weighted by γ=1.0, all computed only over masked patches with MSE. A lightweight single-block ConvNeXt decoder (512-dim) reconstructs the masked regions, and the entire pipeline is pre-trained on just 5.58 hours of YouTube-VOS video for 100 epochs.

## Results

On DAVIS17 video object segmentation, VideoMAC (CNXv2-S) achieves 68.4 J&Fm and VideoMAC (RN50) achieves 67.2 J&Fm, surpassing the best ViT-based MVM method SiamMAE (62.0 J&Fm) by 6.4% and 5.2% respectively, while also exceeding the supervised ResNet baseline (66.0 J&Fm). On VIP body part propagation, RN50-based VideoMAC reaches 43.6 mIoU (+6.3% over SiamMAE's 37.3), and on JHMDB human pose tracking, CNXv2-S-based VideoMAC achieves 58.1 PCK@0.1 (+11.1% over SiamMAE's 47.0). Notably, VideoMAC pre-trains on only 5.58 hours of YouTube-VOS data for 100 epochs, a >140× reduction in pre-training time compared to methods using 800 hours of Kinetics-400 for 1600–2000 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMAC outperforms all 13 baselines across the three primary video tasks. Against the strongest MVM competitor SiamMAE [20] (ViT-S/16, 2000 epochs on K400), VideoMAC (RN50) gains +5.2% J&Fm on DAVIS17, +6.3% mIoU on VIP, and +10.2% PCK@0.1 on JHMDB; VideoMAC (CNXv2-S) gains +6.4%, +3.1%, and +11.1% respectively. Both VideoMAC variants also surpass the supervised ResNet-50 baseline [21] on DAVIS17 (67.2/68.4 vs. 66.0 J&Fm) and on VIP mIoU (43.6 vs. 39.5 for RN50), despite using no labels during pre-training. Compared to other MVM methods (MAE-ST, VideoMAE, DropMAE), VideoMAC leads by 5–29% in J&Fm, with the largest margin over VideoMAE (39.3 J&Fm). On the image classification side (Table 5), VideoMAC-pretrained CNXv2-T reaches 82.9% top-1 on ImageNet-1K, matching FCMAE [64] (83.0%) with only 100 epochs vs. 1600, and outperforming the supervised baseline (82.5%) by 0.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMAC extends the masked autoencoder framework of MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) to video by replacing the standard ViT encoder with sparse-convolutional ConvNets, drawing on the sparse/hierarchical masked modeling ideas from SparK (Tian et al., ICLR 2023) and FCMAE/ConvNeXt v2 (Woo et al., CVPR 2023). It departs from the dominant ViT-based MVM lineage (MAE-ST, VideoMAE, DropMAE, SiamMAE) by introducing an online-target encoder pair with EMA updates—borrowed from self-supervised image learning—and a reconstruction consistency loss that models temporal correlation from just two frames rather than multi-frame sequences. The paper positions itself as the first MVM approach explicitly designed for hierarchical ConvNet architectures, filling a gap left by prior work that assumed isotropic ViT backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: VideoMAC pre-trains in 100 epochs on 5.58 hours of video (vs. 1600 epochs on 800 hours for ViT-based MVMs) while outperforming them on dense video tasks, making high-quality video pre-training accessible with far less compute. The finding that hierarchical ConvNets at 7×7 feature resolution surpass isotropic ViTs at 14×14 for segmentation and tracking challenges the prevailing assumption that higher spatial resolution is necessary for dense prediction. A key limitation is the fixed downsampling ratio of standard ConvNets, which constrains patch size; the authors note that future work on adjustable-resolution ConvNets could close this gap. The approach also opens the question of whether other architectural families (e.g., hybrid CNN-Transformer) could further benefit from the reconstruction consistency loss.
