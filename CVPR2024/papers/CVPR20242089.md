## Problem

Current text-to-image generation methods cannot produce isolated objects with accurate alpha mattes containing fine details like hair, fur, and transparencies. This is largely due to the absence of large-scale alpha matting datasets: existing matting datasets contain only 27–726 images because ground-truth alpha is extremely difficult to obtain, while segmentation datasets lack soft boundary information. Without such data, training models that respect precise alpha boundaries remains infeasible.

## Input / Output

The MAGICK dataset provides 150,000 objects, each consisting of an RGB image with pure foreground colors (no background color contamination in edge or transparent pixels), a corresponding alpha matte, and a text caption. For the demonstrated alpha-to-RGB generation task, the model takes an alpha matte and a text prompt as input and produces an RGB image composited on a gray background. The dataset is not restricted to fixed object classes and explicitly includes objects with challenging soft edges and transparencies (glass, water, smoke, fire).

## Method

The pipeline generates keyable images by chaining DeepFloyd (which produces solid, saturated backgrounds but poor foreground detail) with SDEdit using SDXL at strength 0.95 (which adds fine alpha detail while preserving the background), with an intermediate Photoshop Subject Selection step to composite the object onto a brighter, more saturated background. Background color is selected automatically by generating a preview image, computing a saturation-weighted hue histogram, and choosing the least common named color. Alpha is extracted via three complementary methods—a modified per-pixel chroma key algorithm with inpainting-based background estimation, a deep-learning chromakey model, and Photoshop Subject Selection—and the best matte is selected automatically using an MSSSIM-based similarity score across RGBA composites on white and black backgrounds (median score 0.984), with manual selection for the remaining ~40,000 difficult transparency cases.

## Results

In a user study with 52 participants evaluating alpha-to-RGB generation on 27 test alpha masks from the alphamatting.com dataset, the MAGICK-trained ControlNet was preferred 82% of the time over SD1.5 with Canny edge guidance and 77% of the time over SD1.5 with Sketch edge guidance. The dataset itself contains 150,000 objects (110,000 auto-selected, 40,000 manually selected), with a median inter-method similarity score of 0.984, indicating high consistency across the three alpha extraction methods. The model also demonstrated emergent capabilities such as consistent 3D extrusion effects and semantic features (e.g., overcooking on glyph edges) without explicit training for those tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its MAGICK-trained ControlNet (SD1.5 base) against two ControlNet v1.1 configurations using the same SD1.5 backbone: one guided by Canny edges and one guided by Sketch edges, as proposed in the original ControlNet paper [47]. The MAGICK-trained model decisively outperforms both baselines in user preference: 82% vs. 16% over Canny edges and 77% vs. 23% over Sketch edges. The authors note that the off-the-shelf ControlNet v1.1 trained on ADE20K segmentation masks produces results that poorly follow the alpha channel (Fig. 1), making it unsuitable as a direct baseline. The comparison is limited to these two edge-based guidance variants because no existing method directly takes an alpha matte as input and produces an RGB image with adherence to detailed soft edges, hair, or transparencies. The paper does not report standard quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM) against the baselines, relying instead on the subjective user study as the primary evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior synthetic segmentation data generation (DatasetGAN, BigDatasetGAN, Peekaboo) which produce only binary masks lacking fine details like hair, fur, and transparencies, and from existing alpha matting datasets (alphamatting.com, DIM, Semantic Matting) which are limited to 27–726 images due to the tedium of manual alpha extraction. The key methodological contribution is a pipeline that chains DeepFloyd (for solid-color backgrounds), Photoshop Subject Selection (for initial compositing), and SDEdit with SDXL (for fine alpha detail and color-spill removal) to produce keyable images, then extracts alpha via three complementary methods (pixel-based chroma keying, a deep-learning chromakey model, and Photoshop Subject Selection). For the downstream alpha-to-RGB application, the paper builds on ControlNet with Stable Diffusion 1.5, fine-tuning it on the MAGICK dataset rather than on segmentation masks from ADE20K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAGICK's 150,000 objects represent a roughly 200× scale-up over the largest existing alpha matting dataset, directly addressing the data scarcity that has prevented training of generation models with accurate soft boundaries and transparencies. The 82% user preference over Canny-edge-guided ControlNet demonstrates practical utility for alpha-conditioned generation, and the dataset is positioned to improve matting methods, RGBA generation, and compositing pipelines. Key limitations include the synthetic nature of the images (no real-world lighting, textures, or camera artifacts), the 40,000 images requiring manual matte selection (mostly difficult transparencies), and the reliance on proprietary tools (Photoshop Subject Selection) that limit reproducibility. Open questions include whether alpha mattes from generated images transfer to real-world matting and whether the dataset's object distribution adequately covers the long tail of real-world compositing scenarios.
