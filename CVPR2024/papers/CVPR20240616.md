## Problem

Deep functional map methods for unsupervised shape correspondence lack an explicit mechanism to align the extrinsic features produced by the feature extractor, while existing OT-based approaches (e.g., Deep Shells) rely on entropy-regularized OT with O(m²) memory and time complexity, making them computationally prohibitive for mesh-scale correspondence. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing an efficient OT metric (sliced Wasserstein distance) into the learning loop and coupling it with functional map regularizers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangular meshes (X with n_x vertices, Y with n_y vertices) as input and produces a dense point-to-point correspondence via nearest-neighbor search on learned features. Because the feature extractor is DiffusionNet, the pipeline is agnostic to mesh discretization and resolution (e.g., trained on ~10K-face meshes, evaluated on 20K-face meshes for segmentation transfer). In the segmentation transfer setting, the output additionally includes transferred semantic labels (coarse 4-class or fine-grained 17-class) propagated through the computed correspondence.

## Method

The framework is a Siamese network using DiffusionNet as a shared feature extractor, feeding into two parallel branches: (1) a regularized functional map solver that minimizes bijectivity and area-preservation losses on the spectral map C*, and (2) an OT-based feature alignment branch that computes a soft feature similarity matrix Π̂ via softmax of dot products and then minimizes a bidirectional sliced Wasserstein distance (L_biSW) or its energy-based variant (L_biEBSW) between the original and softly-permuted feature distributions. A properness loss (L_proper) bridges the two branches by penalizing deviation of C* from the permutation implied by Π̂. At inference, a single adaptive refinement step applies entropy-regularized OT (Sinkhorn) to the soft correspondence, iteratively updating features through the full loss to sharpen point-to-point matches—keeping the expensive O(m²) Sinkhorn computation to one pass rather than throughout training.

## Results

On near-isometric shape matching (mean geodesic error ×100, lower is better), the method achieves 1.6 on FAUST, 2.2 on SCAPE, and 4.7 on SHREC'19 when trained on FAUST+SCAPE, outperforming the closest competitor ULRSSM (1.7, 3.2, 4.6) and AFMap (1.9, 2.3, 5.8). On non-isometric datasets, it reaches 4.0 on SMAL and 4.2 on DT4D-H, surpassing ULRSSM (4.2, 4.5) and AFMap (5.4, 11.6). For segmentation transfer (mIOU), it achieves 87.8 (coarse) and 60.5 (fine-grained), setting a new state-of-the-art over ULRSSM (84.2, 58.2) and UDMSM (85.3, 45.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised methods (the most relevant comparison group), the proposed method outperforms ULRSSM [12] on SCAPE (2.2 vs. 3.2) and SHREC'19 (4.7 vs. 4.6) when trained on FAUST+SCAPE, while ULRSSM edges ahead on FAUST (1.7 vs. 1.6) and on non-isometric SMAL (4.2 vs. 4.0). AFMap [28] is consistently outperformed across all near-isometric settings (e.g., 5.8 vs. 4.7 on SHREC'19) and non-isometric DT4D-H (11.6 vs. 4.2). Deep Shells [18], the closest OT-based competitor, is dramatically outperformed on SHREC'19 (21.1 vs. 4.7) and SMAL (29.3 vs. 4.0), confirming the advantage of sliced Wasserstein over Sinkhorn-based OT in the training loop. Supervised methods (FMNet, GeomFMaps, TransMatch) overfit to their training domain and degrade severely on cross-dataset evaluation (e.g., TransMatch: 1.7 on FAUST but 13.5 on SCAPE). Axiomatic methods (ZoomOut, BCICP, Smooth Shells) are uniformly weaker, with Smooth Shells [17] being the strongest at 2.5 on FAUST but not evaluated on cross-dataset or non-isometric settings. For segmentation transfer, the method surpasses all baselines in both coarse (87.8 vs. 85.3 UDMSM) and fine-grained (60.5 vs. 58.2 ULRSSM) mIOU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: deep functional maps (FMNet, AFMap, ULRSSM) that learn spectral-based correspondences, and optimal transport for shape matching (Deep Shells, Smooth Shells). The key departure is replacing the quadratic-cost entropy-regularized OT used in Deep Shells with sliced Wasserstein distance, which provides a valid metric at O(m log m) complexity, and coupling it with FM regularizers in a unified unsupervised framework. The adaptive refinement module reuses Sinkhorn-based OT only at inference, decoupling the expensive computation from training. The authors claim to be the first to employ SWD in a shape correspondence setting, though SWD has appeared in point cloud and deformation contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making OT-based shape matching scalable to larger meshes by reducing memory from O(m²) to O(m), while retaining the metric property that Sinkhorn divergence lacks. The consistent (if modest) improvements over ULRSSM across near-isometric, non-isometric, and segmentation-transfer settings suggest the distributional matching perspective adds genuine signal beyond pointwise feature alignment. Open questions include whether the adaptive refinement step generalizes to very large shape collections, how the method behaves with significantly different vertex counts, and whether the SWD-based loss can be further accelerated for real-time applications.
