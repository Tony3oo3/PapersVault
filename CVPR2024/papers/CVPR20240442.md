## Problem

Existing pseudo-label self-training methods for weakly supervised video anomaly detection (WSVAD) generate frame-level labels using only visual modality, neglecting the rich textual category information that humans naturally use to identify anomalous events. Additionally, temporal dependency modeling in these methods is either absent or restricted to a fixed attention range, failing to adapt to the diverse durations of different video events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips (normal and abnormal) with only video-level binary labels and a set of category text labels (e.g., 'fighting', 'accident', 'normal'), processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone producing 512-dimensional frame features. It outputs per-frame anomaly scores for each video, with pseudo-labels generated at frame granularity via a threshold (0.55 for UCF-Crime, 0.35 for XD-Violence) applied to fused text-video match similarities. The two-stage pipeline first generates pseudo-labels through CLIP-based text-video alignment, then trains a frame-level classifier via self-training on those labels.

## Method

TPWNG fine-tunes CLIP's text encoder (unfreezing only the final projection layer) using two ranking losses that enforce correct text-frame matching for normal and abnormal videos respectively, plus a distributional inconsistency loss that penalizes similar similarity distributions between abnormal-event text and normal-event text over the same frames. A learnable text prompt (8 vectors prepended to tokenized category names, inspired by CoOp) and a Normality Visual Prompt (NVP) that aggregates normal-frame features weighted by match similarities are used to enhance the normal-event text embedding, reducing interference from normal frames within abnormal videos. Pseudo-labels are generated by fusing the abnormal-event match similarity with a normality-guided term (weighted by α=0.2) and thresholding. A Temporal Context Self-Adaptive Learning (TCSAL) module, built on a Transformer encoder with a soft mask function whose span parameter z is learned per-input via a sigmoid-gated linear projection, adaptively adjusts the attention range to capture event-specific temporal dependencies. The final classifier is trained with binary cross-entropy on the generated pseudo-labels, regularized by sparsity and temporal smoothing losses.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, TPWNG achieves 87.79% frame-level AUC, surpassing the previous SOTA CLIP-TSA (87.58%) by 0.21%. On XD-Violence, it reaches 83.68% AP, outperforming CLIP-TSA (82.17%) by 1.52%. Compared to other pseudo-label self-training methods, TPWNG improves over MIST by 5.49% AUC on UCF-Crime and over Zhang et al. by 4.94% AP on XD-Violence, demonstrating the benefit of incorporating textual modality into pseudo-label generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPWNG outperforms all 13 compared baselines on both datasets. Against the closest competitor CLIP-TSA [9], which also leverages CLIP but only for visual feature extraction, TPWNG gains 0.21% AUC on UCF-Crime and 1.52% AP on XD-Violence. The most significant margins are over other pseudo-label self-training methods: MIST [6] (82.30% AUC) is surpassed by 5.49% on UCF-Crime, and Zhang et al. [51] (86.22% AUC / 78.74% AP) is outperformed by 1.57% and 4.94% respectively. Among MIL-based one-stage methods, UR-DMU [54] (86.97% / 81.66%) and MGFN [3] (86.67% / 80.11%) are the strongest, yet TPWNG still leads by 0.82% AUC and 2.02% AP. The consistent superiority across both datasets highlights that combining CLIP's text-image alignment with normality-guided pseudo-label generation yields more accurate frame-level supervision than purely visual approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage pseudo-label self-training paradigm for WSVAD by introducing CLIP's text-image alignment capability into the pseudo-label generation stage, departing from prior methods (MIST, Zhang et al.) that rely solely on visual modality. The learnable text prompt mechanism is directly adapted from CoOp, while the TCSAL module implements the adaptive attention span concept from Sukhbaatar et al. for temporal modeling. Unlike prior CLIP-based VAD works (CLIP-TSA, VADCLIP) that use CLIP only for feature extraction or as a classifier, this paper is the first to leverage CLIP's text encoder to generate frame-level pseudo-labels through text-video matching, combined with a novel normality visual prompt to reduce interference from normal frames within anomalous videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that incorporating textual prior knowledge via CLIP can meaningfully improve pseudo-label quality in WSVAD, suggesting a broader principle that multimodal alignment should be exploited wherever fine-grained supervision is scarce. However, the approach is constrained by CLIP's pre-training domain (web-scale image-text pairs), which may limit generalization to highly specialized or rare anomaly categories not well-represented in CLIP's training data. The dataset-specific pseudo-label thresholds (0.55 vs. 0.35) and the reliance on a fixed number of learnable prompt vectors raise open questions about robustness across diverse surveillance scenarios and the scalability of the text prompt mechanism to open-vocabulary anomaly detection.
