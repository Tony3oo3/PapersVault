## Problem

High-quality motion-captured human-scene interaction (HSI) datasets are scarce: existing MoCap datasets are limited to static scenes or single objects, while scalable RGBD-based or synthetic datasets lack realistic 3D contact and dynamic object tracking. This data scarcity, combined with the difficulty of generating long, controllable, physically plausible motions in cluttered 3D environments, hinders progress in HSI modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary 3D voxel occupancy grid (reachable/unreachable cells), a goal location (2D xy for navigation or 3D for joint-specific control like grasping), and frame-wise multi-hot action labels augmented with a continuous progress indicator in [0,1] as conditions. It produces an arbitrary-length SMPL-X parameterized human motion sequence (24 joint locations per frame, converted to pose parameters via MLP) along with dynamic object rotation and translation sequences. The scene is consumed as a local occupancy patch (0–1.8 m vertical extent) centered on the current episode's subgoal, and the action labels are processed per-frame through a Transformer encoder rather than as a single global description.

## Method

The core architecture is an autoregressive conditional DDPM with a Transformer backbone that generates motion in episodes of L_epi frames, each extending from the last k frames of the prior episode with transition-frame noise zeroed via a mask. A Local Scene Perceiver queries the global occupancy grid around the subgoal, encodes the resulting voxel patch via a ViT (patches along xy, z as channels), and feeds the scene embedding into the first Transformer token alongside diffusion-step and action information. A key design choice is the frame-wise action embedding with a progress indicator: a linearly interpolated scalar is appended to each action label so the model tracks how far an action has progressed across multiple episodes, enabling seamless multi-episode actions like drinking. Subgoal conditioning is enforced by masking the diffusion noise on the pelvis xy (for navigation) or specific hand joints (for manipulation), letting the model infer vertical position and fine-grained contact from scene context. After joint-level generation, SMPL-X parameters are fitted via a lightweight MLP followed by optimization, and object trajectories are refined to minimize hand-object distance variance.

## Results

In the static setting (locomotion and scene interaction on TRUMANS), the proposed method achieves the highest Contact score (0.992), lowest max penetration (11.74 cm), and lowest MoCap-discrimination success rate (0.258, near the 0.2 random-guess floor), outperforming Wang et al. (0.581), SceneDiff (0.645), and GMD (0.871). In the dynamic setting (object-level interaction on TRUMANS), the method attains the best FID (0.313 vs. 0.512 for GOAL and 0.711 for IMoS) and reduces scene penetration from 34–37 cm to 11.74 cm. Human studies confirm that only ~25% of participants can distinguish synthesized motions from real MoCap data, approaching the 20% chance level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three static-setting baselines (Wang et al./cVAE, SceneDiff, GMD), the proposed method wins on Contact (0.992 vs. 0.912–0.969), max penetration (11.74 vs. 14.33–21.30 cm), and MoCap-discrimination rate (0.258 vs. 0.581–0.871) on TRUMANS, though SceneDiff edges it slightly on mean penetration (1.691 vs. 1.820). In the dynamic setting, the method substantially outperforms GOAL and IMoS on FID (0.313 vs. 0.512/0.711) and scene penetration (11.74 vs. 34.10/37.48 cm), while matching or exceeding their diversity scores. On the PROX-trained comparison, the method still leads on Contact and max penetration, confirming that TRUMANS as a training dataset yields superior generalization. The ablation removing the action progress indicator causes complete failure (Dis.suc. = 1.000, FID = 2.104), highlighting its critical role. Overall, the method dominates 4 out of 5 metrics in the static setting and 4 out of 4 in the dynamic setting, with the sole near-tie being mean penetration in the static case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied threads: HSI dataset construction (PROX, GRAB, CIRCLE, SAMP) and scene-aware motion generation (cVAE-based, diffusion-based, and autoregressive methods). It builds directly on Shafir et al.'s autoregressive episode-based diffusion framework for long-sequence generation, extending it with a local scene perceiver (ViT over voxel patches) for 3D-aware collision avoidance and a frame-wise action embedding with a progress indicator for multi-episode action control. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the simultaneous achievement of MoCap-level quality and scene-scale diversity: existing datasets trade off between capture fidelity (VICON-based, limited scenes) and scalability (synthetic or RGBD-based, noisy poses), whereas TRUMANS replicates synthetic 3D environments physically for optical MoCap, bridging this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRUMANS (15 hours, 100 scenes, dynamic objects, per-vertex contact, multi-view + ego-view RGBD) is likely to become a standard benchmark for HSI research, and the demonstrated zero-shot generalization to PROX, Replica, ScanNet, and ScanNet++ scenes validates its transferability. The near-random human discrimination rate (~25% vs. 20% chance) suggests synthetic motion quality has reached a practical threshold for downstream applications like robotics simulation and virtual content creation. Open questions include whether the voxel-grid scene representation (a 300× training-speed trade-off over mesh-based approaches) limits fidelity in highly cluttered or outdoor environments, whether the post-hoc object pose optimization generalizes to multi-object or articulated interactions, and whether the method scales to multi-person scenes.
