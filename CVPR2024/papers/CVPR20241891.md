## Problem

Existing visual object trackers focus on improving target representation or prediction robustness but lack a reliable self-recovery mechanism for when targets are lost due to full occlusion, out-of-view, or tracking failure. Prior approaches that attempt recovery rely on fixed similarity thresholds or computationally expensive online adaptation, which fail to generalize across diverse tracking scenarios and appearance changes over time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence with a bounding box annotation in the first frame. The tracker consumes a search region cropped at 4.5× the target area (resized to 384×384) and a template at 2× the target area (192×192), while the detector performs full-frame global search. Output is a per-frame target bounding box, with the system dynamically switching between local tracking and global detection modes based on the predicted target state. Features for the PN tree memory are extracted by a DreamSim-based similarity perception model trained on LaSOT and NIGHTS.

## Method

RTracker introduces a Positive-Negative Tree (PN tree) structured memory that chronologically stores target-relevant (positive) and target-irrelevant (negative) appearance features, with nodes updated via merging or appending and pruned at depth 10. A set of walking rules traverses the tree along positive and negative paths using cosine similarity to classify a new test node as present or absent, establishing a relative distance-based criterion that replaces fixed thresholds. Based on the predicted state, three control flows govern the system: a normal flow using only the MixViT-L tracker, a missing-target flow that activates the MITS detector for global search, and a recovery flow that reinitializes the tracker upon detection. This architecture enables the tracker to self-recover after target loss without requiring online retraining or manual intervention.

## Results

RTracker-L achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across five challenging tracking benchmarks. On VideoCube, it reaches 81.5% NP and 69.6% SUC, surpassing the second-best MixViT-L (78.7% NP) by 2.8%. On LaSOT, it attains 74.7% AUC, outperforming MixViT-L (73.3%) and ARTrack-L (73.1%). On LaSOT_ext, RTracker achieves 54.9% AUC with a ViT-Large backbone, approaching UNINEXT-H's 56.2% (which uses ViT-Huge). On GOT-10k, it scores 77.9% AO, competitive with MITS (80.4%) and ARTrack (78.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTracker outperforms its base tracker MixViT-L on all five benchmarks, with the largest gains on VideoCube (+2.8% NP) and LaSOT_ext (+4.0% AUC), confirming that the PN-tree-guided tracker-detector association adds substantial value over tracking alone. Against the strongest recent baselines, RTracker beats ARTrack-L on VideoCube (+28.3% NP), LaSOT (+1.6% AUC), LaSOT_ext (+2.1% AUC), and TNL2K (+3.4% P), but trails it slightly on GOT-10k AO (77.9 vs. 78.5). Compared to MITS, RTracker wins on VideoCube (+14.8% NP), LaSOT (+2.6% AUC), LaSOT_ext (+4.6% AUC), and TNL2K (+5.2% P), but underperforms on GOT-10k AO (77.9 vs. 80.4). Against UNINEXT-H (which uses a heavier ViT-Huge backbone), RTracker achieves comparable LaSOT_ext AUC (54.9 vs. 56.2) with a lighter ViT-Large, and surpasses it on LaSOT AUC (74.7 vs. 72.2) and TNL2K P (63.7 vs. 62.8). Overall, RTracker sets new best results on VideoCube, LaSOT, LaSOT_ext, and TNL2K, while remaining competitive on GOT-10k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical TLD (Tracking-Learning-Detection) paradigm by replacing its nearest-neighbor classifier with a Positive-Negative Tree memory that uses relative distance-based criteria instead of fixed thresholds for target state determination. It repurposes the SVM-like support vector concept for tracking, storing chronologically ordered positive and negative samples in a tree structure with defined walking rules, while leveraging modern transformer-based trackers (MixViT) and detectors (MITS) as the underlying components. The approach departs from online adaptation methods that rely on periodic template re-initialization with fixed thresholds, offering an adaptive mechanism that generalizes better across diverse tracking scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-recovery from target loss is a practical bottleneck for deploying trackers in real-world long-term applications (surveillance, autonomous navigation), and this paper provides a principled, well-evaluated solution that achieves 80% recovery success on LaSOT versus ~63% for strong baselines. The PN tree mechanism is lightweight and modular, making it potentially applicable to other tracker-detector association frameworks. However, the method incurs a 25% speed penalty and still fails in extreme cases where targets reappear with entirely different appearances alongside similar distractors, pointing to the open question of how to incorporate semantic or identity-level descriptors for robust recovery under drastic appearance change.
