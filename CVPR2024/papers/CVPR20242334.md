## Problem

Existing visual prompting methods for MLLMs are model-specific: prompts trained on one model often cause performance degradation on other models due to cross-model feature corruption, where the learned perturbations overfit to the training model's internal representations. This paper asks whether a single set of shared visual prompts, optimized on only one MLLM, can simultaneously improve the performance of multiple diverse MLLMs on a downstream task without any per-model fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242334/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image and a learnable pixel-level visual prompt δ (a border of width p=30 pixels by default) that is added around the resized image (H−2p × W−2p) to form the prompted input. The same δ is applied identically to all target MLLMs at inference time with no further optimization. Each MLLM then produces an autoregressive text response (e.g., a class label, object count, reasoning answer, or hallucination judgment) conditioned on the prompted image and a task-specific text prompt.

## Method

TVP augments standard visual prompting with two auxiliary losses to improve cross-model transferability. Feature Consistency Alignment (FCA) penalizes the L2 distance between the visual encoder's features of the prompted and non-prompted images, constraining the prompt to preserve task-agnostic pre-trained knowledge rather than corrupting features. Task Semantics Enrichment (TSE) uses CLIP's image-text contrastive objective to encourage the prompted image to be semantically similar to a task-specific text description, explicitly embedding shareable task semantics into the pixel perturbation. The prompt δ is optimized on a single MLLM (or an ensemble of two) by minimizing L = L_LLM + λ₁·L_FCA − λ₂·L_TSE using gradient-normalized updates, then directly transferred to other MLLMs without any further training.

## Results

TVP achieves the highest average improvement (Avg. Δ) across all 6 MLLMs on every evaluated dataset. On CIFAR-10 (top-1 accuracy), TVP trained on InstructBLIP yields +6.69% average improvement over zero-shot, compared to −1.20% for VP and +3.30% for EVP. On SVHN, the gap widens dramatically: TVP achieves +28.55% vs. +12.89% (VP) and +13.23% (EVP). On the counting task (CLEVR), TVP delivers +18.76% average improvement, nearly tripling EVP's +6.14%. For multimodal reasoning (Hatefulmemes, AUC) and hallucination correction (POPE, top-1 accuracy), TVP also leads with +1.69 and +3.19 respectively, while VP causes net degradation (−2.53 and −1.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVP outperforms both baselines (VP [3] and EVP [56]) on all six main datasets and both ensemble settings. Against VP, the advantage is most striking on CIFAR-10 where VP actually degrades average performance (−1.20%) while TVP improves it by +6.69%, and on Hatefulmemes where VP drops AUC by 2.53 points versus TVP's +1.69 gain. Against EVP, TVP consistently leads by a wider margin: +3.39 points on CIFAR-10, +15.32 on SVHN, +12.62 on CLEVR, and +2.29 on POPE. The largest relative gains over EVP occur on counting (CLEVR: +18.76 vs. +6.14, a 3× improvement) and digit recognition (SVHN: +28.55 vs. +13.23, a 2.2× improvement), suggesting that FCA and TSE are particularly effective at preserving task-specific semantics that EVP's gradient normalization alone cannot capture. On the more challenging multimodal reasoning task (Hatefulmemes), absolute improvements are smaller for all methods, but TVP still achieves the only positive average improvement among single-model training settings (+1.69 vs. +0.09 for EVP and −2.53 for VP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual prompting line of work (Bahng et al. [3], EVP [56]) from single-model adaptation to a cross-model transferability setting, where a pixel-space prompt trained on one MLLM must improve several others without further optimization. It draws a direct analogy to transferable adversarial attacks for the transferability framing, and repurposes CLIP as a semantic guidance signal (rather than a target model) via the TSE loss. The FCA loss is conceptually a feature-space regularizer, but its specific motivation—preserving task-agnostic pre-training knowledge across heterogeneous MLLM architectures—departs from prior data-generalization regularizers that focus on base-to-novel or domain shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Prompt as a Service' framing offers a practical pathway for resource-constrained users to adapt multiple MLLMs simultaneously without per-model fine-tuning, which is increasingly relevant as the MLLM ecosystem diversifies. However, the modest gains over EVP (typically 2–5 points) and the notably weaker transfer to VisualGLM (GLM-based) suggest that architectural heterogeneity remains a significant barrier. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger prompt budgets, more diverse model families (e.g., diffusion-based MLLMs), and whether the CLIP-based TSE signal can be replaced by task-specific encoders for domains where CLIP knowledge is limited.
