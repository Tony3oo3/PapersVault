## Problem

Extending vision-language pretraining from 2D chest X-rays to 3D chest CT is hindered by the scarcity of large-scale paired CT-report datasets, making it infeasible to train a versatile multi-disease CT model from scratch. Current per-disease annotation workflows are not scalable across hundreds of pathologies, and no existing method effectively transfers the rich diagnostic knowledge already encoded in pre-trained X-ray expert models to the 3D CT domain.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D chest CT volumes cropped to the lung region and resized to 64×224×320 (HU truncated to [-1150, 350], normalized to [-1,1]), augmented with anatomical segmentation masks (7 lung structures) and voxel spacing tokens in (x,y,z) directions. For zero-shot classification, text prompts of the form 'this is a chest CT with {disease} in lung' are used; for report generation, the model produces free-text radiology reports. Outputs include multi-label pathology predictions across six entities (nodule, opacity, pleural effusion, emphysema, inflammation, calcification) and generated reports.

## Method

BIUD bootstraps a 3D CT image encoder (12-layer ViT/B with 3D patch embedding at 16×32×32, yielding 280 tokens) by distilling knowledge from the CheXzero X-ray expert model. A language-guided retrieval step matches each CT volume to its semantically closest X-ray image via cosine similarity of report embeddings in the expert text encoder's space, enabling pair-wise and semantic-relation knowledge distillation (MSE on both individual embeddings and pairwise cosine similarity matrices). For image-report alignment, a robust contrastive loss (RoCo) corrects false negatives by identifying semantically equivalent reports (e.g., multiple healthy reports) and treating them as additional positives rather than negatives. An entity-focused masking (EFM) strategy selectively masks medical entities and attributes (treating complete phrases like 'solid nodule' as single masking units) to direct the text encoder toward diagnostically critical terms. The text encoder and a cross-attention multi-modal encoder share BERT parameters, supporting both contrastive alignment and image-report matching in a multi-task setup.

## Results

On zero-shot classification over six pathology tasks, BIUD achieves an average F1 of 33.0% on the internal ChestCT-16K test set with a notably high recall of 57.7%, substantially outperforming DCL (F1=25.7%), BLIP (F1=21.6%), and CLIP (F1=16.0%). On the external LIDC nodule detection benchmark, BIUD reaches F1=65.8% (P=83.9%, R=54.1%), surpassing the next-best method DCL (F1=56.0%) by nearly 10 points. For report generation evaluated by disease diagnosis, BIUD attains average F1 of 37.3% on ChestCT-16K and 40.6% on the external ChestCT-EXT dataset, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIUD consistently outperforms all six baselines on the primary zero-shot classification task. Against the strongest competitor DCL [23], BIUD gains 7.3 F1 points on the internal ChestCT-16K set (33.0 vs 25.7) and 9.8 points on the external LIDC nodule detection (65.8 vs 56.0), driven primarily by a much higher recall (57.7% vs 35.6% internally; 54.1% vs 41.4% on LIDC). The gap over BLIP [22] and CLIP [27] is even more pronounced, with BIUD achieving roughly 1.5–2× their F1 scores on the internal set and 4–8× on LIDC. For report generation (Table 2), BIUD leads all methods on both internal (avg F1 37.3) and external ChestCT-EXT (avg F1 40.6) evaluations, with the largest margins over RadFM [36] (F1 4.5/4.7) and the pure language-generation baselines R2Gen [5] and CMN [6]. Notably, BIUD's advantage is most visible in recall, indicating it detects more true-positive pathologies, while precision remains competitive (30.6% internal, 83.9% on LIDC). The fine-tuning experiment (Table 4) further confirms that BIUD pre-training yields a 4.5-point F1 improvement over ImageNet pre-training (39.0 vs 34.5 average), validating the value of language-driven supervision over categorical labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the language-driven medical VLP paradigm (CLIP, CheXzero, BLIP, DCL) from 2D chest X-rays to 3D chest CT, a domain where paired image-report data is scarce. Its core methodological departure is using a pre-trained 2D X-ray expert model (CheXzero) as a knowledge distillation teacher, with a language-guided retrieval mechanism to match semantically similar CT and X-ray images in the absence of paired data. The robust contrastive learning (RoCo) and entity-focused masking (EFM) components address known failure modes of standard contrastive learning in medical settings, where many reports are semantically identical (e.g., healthy patients). The distillation strategy draws on relational KD and SAKD but applies them across imaging modalities rather than within a single modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates a practical pathway to bootstrap 3D CT understanding without the massive annotation effort that task-specific models require, and the reader study showing near-radiologist nodule detection performance (under time pressure) is a compelling early signal for clinical utility. However, the overall F1 scores remain modest (~37–40%), the training data is limited to a single hospital (~12K pairs), and the reader study conditions (30 seconds per case vs. 3–4 minutes in practice) may not reflect real-world radiologist performance. Open questions include whether the 2D-to-3D distillation paradigm generalizes to other body parts and modalities, whether the false-negative correction rules scale to more diverse report vocabularies, and whether the approach can close the remaining gap to full clinical deployment.
