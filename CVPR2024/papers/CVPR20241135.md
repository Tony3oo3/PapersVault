## Problem

Automatic texture design for 3D biped cartoon characters from text remains unsolved due to two compounding challenges: cartoon characters demand textures with semantic harmony, consistent global configuration, and rich high-frequency details that conventional shape-texturing methods (which produce blurry, seam-riddled results) cannot deliver, and high-quality paired text-texture data is scarce because character creation is costly, IP-restricted, and existing datasets lack fine-grained descriptions. This paper presents the first framework to generate high-quality UV-space textures for biped cartoon characters directly from text in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241135/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'A cartoon rabbit wearing blue shirt and white pants') and a topology-consistent 3D biped cartoon mesh from the Rabit parametric model with a fixed UV unwrapping layout, and produces a 1024×1024 RGB UV texture map in approximately 0.03 minutes on a single GPU (6.20 GB). The UV map follows a rigid template structure shared across all characters in the dataset, meaning the generator must learn to place clothing, colors, and details in anatomically correct UV regions rather than producing a free-form 2D image. A post-processing step applies Gaussian boundary filtering and back-view image restoration to eliminate seam artifacts before the texture is applied to the animatable mesh.

## Method

The core architecture fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 1.5 via rank-8 LoRA adapters injected into all U-Net attention projection matrices, keeping the pretrained T2I weights frozen to retain open-domain image knowledge while specializing to UV texture generation. Training data is constructed with a multi-agent captioning pipeline: BLIP2 answers three hard-coded VQA questions on rendered multi-view images, ChatGPT generates follow-up questions to probe color and clothing details, and a final ChatGPT summarization merges multi-view descriptions into a single caption per model. The key technical contribution is an adversarial loss integrated into the diffusion training loop: ControlNet synthesizes realistic multi-view images from rendered depth maps as 'real' samples, while the textured mesh rendered from the denoised latent serves as 'fake' samples, forcing the generator to produce fine-grained cloth wrinkles and surface details that the cartoon dataset alone cannot provide. This adversarial scheme bridges the domain gap between the low-frequency cartoon training data and perceptually realistic texture appearance without requiring additional UV-space data.

## Results

On the 3DBiCar test set of 300 prompts across 15 character species, Make-It-Vivid achieves the highest CLIP score of 29.86, outperforming the next-best methods TEXTure (29.25) and Fantasia3D (29.20). Inference takes only 0.03 minutes per texture with 6.20 GB GPU memory, making it ~465× faster than Fantasia3D (21.55 min) and ~465× faster than LatentPaint (13.95 min). On image quality metrics, the method reduces FID from 42.55 to 35.25 and KID from 6.37 to 5.25 (×10⁻³) compared to the Rabit baseline trained on the same dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Make-It-Vivid wins on CLIP score against all four text-guided baselines: it surpasses TEXTure (29.25) by 0.61, Fantasia3D (29.20) by 0.66, Text2Tex (28.81) by 1.05, and LatentPaint (27.15) by 2.71. On inference speed, it is comparable to the painting-based TEXTure (0.03 vs. 2.38 min) and the unconditional Rabit generator (0.03 vs. 0.01 min), while being roughly 465× faster than the optimization-based LatentPaint, Fantasia3D, and Text2Tex. GPU memory usage (6.20 GB) is lower than all optimization-based baselines (11–20 GB) but higher than Rabit (2.39 GB). On FID and KID (Table 2), the method improves over Rabit by 7.30 FID points and 1.12 KID (×10⁻³), confirming better texture fidelity and diversity. Qualitatively, painting-based methods (TEXTure, Text2Tex) exhibit visible seam artifacts on side and back views, while optimization-based methods (LatentPaint, Fantasia3D) produce multi-view-consistent but lower-detail textures with non-smooth surface artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-guided 3D texturing line of work (LatentPaint, Fantasia3D, TEXTure, Text2Tex) by shifting from implicit representations and multi-view optimization to direct UV-space generation, leveraging a pretrained Stable Diffusion model fine-tuned with LoRA adapters. It builds on the Rabit parametric model and 3DBiCar dataset for topology-consistent biped character representation, and repurposes ControlNet as a depth-conditioned image generator to provide realistic-domain supervision during adversarial training. The key departure from prior UV texture generators (StyleUV, DreamFace) is the focus on full-body biped cartoon characters rather than faces, and the integration of adversarial loss into the diffusion training loop to combat over-smoothing from limited high-frequency training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-forward-pass inference (~0.03 min) versus 14–21 min for optimization-based baselines makes text-guided character texturing practical for interactive pipelines, and the LoRA + adversarial training recipe is a lightweight technique potentially transferable to other diffusion-based generation tasks. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the Rabit topology and 3DBiCar dataset, limiting generalization to other character rigs or non-cartoon domains. The seam-fixing step (Gaussian filtering + image restoration on the back view) is a post-hoc patch that hints at a deeper challenge: diffusion models trained on 2D images inherently lack 3D seam-awareness. Open questions include whether the adversarial-in-diffusion strategy scales to higher-resolution or more geometrically complex UV layouts, and whether the multi-agent captioning pipeline can be automated for truly open-domain character descriptions.
