## Problem

Test-time adaptation (TTA) methods that estimate batch normalization statistics from test batches are vulnerable to data poisoning attacks: even a single malicious sample can arbitrarily shift the mean-based statistics, corrupting predictions on all benign samples in the batch. Existing TTA robustness mechanisms—entropy-based filtering, sharpness-aware minimization, and exponential moving averages—fail to fully eliminate this vulnerability, leaving state-of-the-art methods exposed to both targeted and indiscriminate attacks.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on unlabeled test image batches (e.g., 200 images per batch for CIFAR, 200 for ImageNet) that may contain a fraction of adversarially crafted malicious samples (10–20%). It requires only the pre-trained model parameters and the incoming test batch—no additional training or access to source data. For image classification it outputs a class label per sample; for semantic segmentation it outputs a per-pixel mask. The key constraint is that predictions must remain correct on benign samples despite the presence of malicious ones in the same batch.

## Method

MedBN replaces the mean with the median when estimating the per-channel batch statistics in every BN layer during test-time inference: the centering statistic becomes η_c = med{z_bchw} over spatial and batch dimensions, while the scaling statistic remains the mean of squared deviations from this median, ρ_c² = mean{(z_bchw − η_c)²}. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves that the mean can be driven to infinity by a single malicious sample, whereas the median is bounded as long as fewer than half the batch is malicious. The method is algorithm-agnostic and requires no retraining—it is a drop-in replacement for the standard BN statistics computation inside any existing TTA framework (TENT, EATA, SAR, SoTTA, sEMA, mDIA, etc.). The authors also show that using the median absolute deviation (MAD) for the scaling statistic provides stronger defense but at a substantial clean-performance cost, motivating the choice of mean-of-squared-deviations around the median.

## Results

Under targeted instant attacks (20% malicious samples), MedBN reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) from 83.91% to 19.16% for TeBN on CIFAR-10-C, from 91.78% to 2.80% on CIFAR-100-C, and from 97.78% to 0.36% on ImageNet-C. Under indiscriminate instant attacks, MedBN lowers Error Rate (ER) by up to ~9% on CIFAR-10-C (31.02%→22.34% for TeBN), ~11% on CIFAR-100-C (59.80%→48.55%), and ~12% on ImageNet-C (81.46%→69.74%). Without attacks, MedBN incurs negligible performance degradation (e.g., 14.92%→15.19% ER on CIFAR-10-C, 40.08%→40.77% on CIFAR-100-C).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedBN outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and attack scenario. The most dramatic gains are against TeBN (ASR drops from 83.91%→19.16% on CIFAR-10-C, 97.78%→0.36% on ImageNet-C) and TENT (72.36%→18.36%, 91.47%→0.44%), which rely entirely on mean-based batch statistics. mDIA [54], the prior dedicated defense against TTA poisoning, is also substantially outperformed (33.91%→8.76% on CIFAR-10-C, 32.18%→1.07% on ImageNet-C). Among methods with inherent defensive mechanisms, SoTTA [20] and sEMA [59] already show lower baseline ASRs (21.47% and 18.18% on CIFAR-10-C), but MedBN further reduces them to 7.82% and 8.67% respectively. SAR [40] and ETA [39] (EATA without Fisher regularization) show similar vulnerability levels (~75-77% ASR) and are both reduced to ~18% with MedBN. Under indiscriminate attacks, MedBN consistently achieves the lowest ER across all methods, with the largest absolute improvements on CIFAR-100-C (~11% reduction) and ImageNet-C (~12% reduction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TTA-with-BN-statistics lineage (TeBN, TENT, EATA, SAR, SoTTA, sEMA) and the recently identified data-poisoning vulnerability of TTA methods (Cong et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2023). It departs from prior defenses like mDIA, which interpolate source and test statistics, by identifying the root cause as the use of the mean for BN statistics estimation and replacing it with the median. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) formalizes why mean is arbitrarily manipulable by a single adversarial sample while median remains bounded, providing a principled justification that prior heuristic defenses lacked. The method is algorithm-agnostic, positioning it as a drop-in replacement rather than a new TTA framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art TTA methods with filtering, sharpness-aware minimization, and EMA are still highly vulnerable to a small fraction of malicious samples is a significant security concern for any deployment relying on online adaptation. The practical impact is high because the fix (replacing mean with median in BN) requires no retraining, adds negligible overhead, and integrates into any existing TTA pipeline. Limitations include a small but non-zero performance drop without attacks (~1–2%), the fact that variance is still estimated via mean of squared deviations (MAD was tested but caused larger drops), and the scope being limited to BN layers—open questions remain about extending the approach to LayerNorm, GroupNorm, or non-BN-based TTA methods.
