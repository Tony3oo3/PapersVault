## Problem

Existing MoE-based multi-task dense prediction methods neglect explicit modeling of global relationships among all tasks, since dynamic routing rarely activates the same expert across every task. Additionally, increasing the number of experts to enlarge the task-generic feature space introduces a heavy burden of parameters and FLOPs that scales linearly with expert count, limiting practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240965/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and simultaneously produces pixel-wise predictions for up to five dense tasks (semantic segmentation, human parsing, saliency, surface normals, boundary detection on PASCAL-Context; semantic segmentation, depth, surface normals, boundary on NYUD-v2). A ViT backbone extracts multi-scale features from four selected transformer layers, which are fed into stacked MLoRE decoder modules at each scale; outputs from different scales are concatenated to form the final per-task feature before a lightweight prediction head.

## Method

MLoRE is a decoder-focused module that augments the standard MoE with three parallel paths per task: (1) a task-sharing generic 3×3 convolution that all task features pass through, explicitly modeling global cross-task relationships; (2) task-sharing low-rank expert networks (a 3×3 conv of rank r followed by a 1×1 conv, with r≪C) selected via task-specific top-k routers with position-aware branches; and (3) task-specific low-rank experts for enhanced per-task discrimination. All paths are linear (no activation functions), enabling re-parameterization at inference where the entire module collapses into a single 3×3 convolution per task, so adding more experts increases training-time capacity without increasing inference FLOPs. The low-rank factorization reduces expert parameters by over 60% compared to standard MoE, and the generic path uses gradient stopping to ease multi-task optimization conflicts.

## Results

On PASCAL-Context (ViT-large backbone), MLoRE achieves 81.41 mIoU on semantic segmentation, 70.52 mIoU on human parsing, 84.90 maxF on saliency, 13.51 mErr on surface normals, and 75.42 odsF on boundary detection, while using only 571 GFLOPs and 407M parameters. On NYUD-v2, it reaches 55.96 mIoU (semseg), 0.5076 RMSE (depth), 18.33 mErr (normals), and 78.43 odsF (boundary). The method sets new state-of-the-art on all five PASCAL-Context tasks and all four NYUD-v2 tasks simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLoRE outperforms the closest MoE-based decoder method, TaskExpert (ICCV'23), by +0.77 mIoU on semantic segmentation, +1.10 mIoU on human parsing, and +2.12 odsF on boundary detection, while using 13% fewer FLOPs (571G vs. 622G) and 13M fewer parameters. Against the prompting-based TaskPrompter (ICLR'23), gains are +0.52 mIoU (semseg), +1.63 mIoU (parsing), and +1.92 odsF (boundary). The encoder-focused MoE methods M3ViT and Mod-Squad are substantially outperformed (e.g., 75.64 vs. 72.80/74.10 mIoU semseg in the efficient ViT-small setting). On NYUD-v2, MLoRE beats TaskExpert by +0.61 mIoU (semseg), 0.0081 RMSE (depth), and +0.21 mErr (normals). Older decoder-focused baselines (PAD-Net, MTI-Net, ATRC) are surpassed by 2–4 mIoU on segmentation and 2–3 odsF on boundary, confirming that the combination of explicit global task sharing and low-rank experts yields consistent gains across all evaluated tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoder-focused multi-task learning line initiated by TaskExpert (Ye et al., ICCV 2023), which first applied Mixture-of-Experts to the decoder for dense prediction. MLoRE departs from standard MoE in two key ways: it adds a task-sharing generic convolution path to explicitly model global task relationships (rather than relying on implicit sharing through expert co-activation), and it replaces full-rank expert convolutions with low-rank factorizations inspired by LoRA, enabling re-parameterization at inference following the Diverse Branch Block formulation. The method also draws on the broader MoE-for-MTL literature (M³ViT, Mod-Squad) but positions itself as the first to use linear experts in a decoder MoE for multi-task dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in MoE-based multi-task learning: parameter and FLOP explosion when scaling the number of experts to enlarge feature representation capacity. The low-rank expert design combined with inference-time re-parameterization offers a concrete recipe for deploying larger MoE decoders without proportional compute cost, which is relevant for real-time applications like autonomous driving. An open question is whether the linearity assumption (no activations in experts) generalizes to more complex expert structures or larger task sets beyond the 4–5 tasks evaluated here. The gradient-stopping trick on the generic path is a simple but effective mitigation of gradient conflict, though its interaction with more aggressive multi-task optimization strategies remains unexplored.
