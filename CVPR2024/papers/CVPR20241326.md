## Problem

Two-stage DETR-like detectors suffer from encoding redundancy (self-attention on background queries introduces irrelevant information) and selection redundancy (queries fail to match one-to-one with actual objects), leading to heavy computation and scale bias toward large objects. Existing query filtering methods like Focus DETR apply coarse-grained, scale-dependent filtering that disregards multi-scale token characteristics and favors large-scale objects, making query selection ambiguous in scenarios with weak or small-scale targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at varying resolutions (1024×1024 for CSD, 3620×3700 for ESD, 5120×5120 for MSSD, standard for COCO) and extracts four multi-scale feature maps from a ResNet50 backbone. It outputs standard detection results—bounding boxes with class labels—via one-to-one Hungarian matching, but the key internal difference is that the encoder selectively updates only a filtered subset of queries at each layer and level, reducing FLOPs from 291G (DINO) to 201G while maintaining or improving AP.

## Method

Salience DETR introduces hierarchical salience filtering refinement with three components. First, a scale-independent salience supervision replaces binary foreground/background labels with a continuous confidence computed as the relative distance from each query to the nearest object center (Eq. 2), ensuring small and large objects receive balanced filtering signals. Second, a two-dimensional hierarchical query filtering applies layer-wise ratios {v_t} and level-wise ratios {w_l} so that only the top v_t·w_l queries at each encoder layer and feature level undergo deformable attention encoding, reducing computation proportionally. Third, three query refinement modules address semantic misalignment: absolute background embeddings for unselected queries, a RepVGGPluXBlock-based cross-level token fusion for adjacent feature levels, and NMS-based redundancy removal on two-stage initialization queries to eliminate duplicate selections around similar small objects.

## Results

Salience DETR achieves 49.2% AP on COCO 2017 with only 201G FLOPs (vs. 291G for DINO), outperforming all compared DETR variants. On the task-specific MSSD dataset (massive small/weak objects), it reaches 55.4% AP and 61.9% AP75, a +4.4% AP and +9.4% AP75 improvement over the second-best method (Focus-DETR). On ESD, it achieves 46.5% AP and 41.4% AP75 (the only method exceeding 40% AP75), and on CSD it attains 53.2% AP and 66.5% AR, both state-of-the-art among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Salience DETR outperforms all 19 compared baselines across all four datasets. On COCO 2017, it edges out DINO (49.0→49.2 AP) and Focus-DETR (48.8→49.2 AP) while reducing FLOPs by 31% (201G vs. 291G), and surpasses DN-Def-DETR by +0.6 AP. On the challenging MSSD dataset (87 objects/image, small weak defects), the margin is most dramatic: +4.4 AP over DINO (51.0→55.4) and +6.2 AP over Focus-DETR (49.2→55.4), with APS improving from 20.0 to 28.7. On ESD, Salience DETR leads Focus-DETR by +3.2 AP (43.3→46.5) and DINO by +4.0 AP, and is the only method exceeding 40% AP75. On CSD, gains are more modest (+0.2 AP over DINO and H-Def-DETR at 53.0) but AR improves to 66.5 (vs. 64.0 for DINO). Traditional CNN detectors (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, YOLOX, HTC) are consistently outperformed by 2–11 AP depending on dataset, with the largest gaps on MSSD where DETR-like methods struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

Salience DETR extends the two-stage DETR pipeline established by Deformable DETR and refined by DINO, specifically targeting the query filtering mechanism introduced by Focus DETR. The key departure is replacing Focus DETR's discrete foreground-background supervision with a scale-independent salience confidence that encodes relative distance to object centers, and upgrading from single-level top-k filtering to a hierarchical scheme that applies distinct filtering ratios per encoder layer and per feature level. Three additional query refinement modules (background embedding, cross-level token fusion via RepVGGPluXBlock, and NMS-based redundancy removal) address semantic misalignment introduced by selective encoding, which prior sparse-encoding methods (Sparse DETR, Lite DETR, Focus DETR) did not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that scale bias in query selection is a concrete and measurable problem in DETR-like detectors, particularly for small or low-contrast objects common in industrial defect detection, and that a simple distance-based salience signal can mitigate it without architectural complexity. The 30% FLOPs reduction on COCO while matching or exceeding DINO's AP is practically useful for deployment. However, the redundancy removal relies on NMS (acknowledged as non-end-to-end), the largest gains appear on small self-built datasets (MSSD, ESD, CSD) rather than COCO where the improvement is only +0.2% AP, and the salience supervision's potential for pixel-level tasks (instance segmentation) remains speculative. Open questions include whether the hierarchical filtering ratios can be learned end-to-end and whether the approach generalizes to detection tasks with extreme scale variation beyond industrial settings.
