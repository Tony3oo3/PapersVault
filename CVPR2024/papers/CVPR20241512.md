## Problem

Existing multi-view 3D hand pose estimation methods require expensive multi-view annotations for training and are locked to the exact camera parameters used during training, making them inapplicable when camera layouts change. Single-view estimators avoid these issues but suffer from limited field-of-view and depth ambiguity. This paper asks whether a pre-trained single-view estimator can be adapted to an arbitrary dual-view setting without multi-view labels or known camera parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained single-view DetNet estimator and a set of N unlabeled dual-view RGB image pairs (N=1000) from two arbitrarily placed cameras, with no ground-truth poses or camera intrinsics/extrinsics provided. During adaptation, a rotation matrix R between the two camera coordinate systems is estimated from the initial predictions and refined iteratively. At inference, the adapted estimator processes a dual-view pair and outputs 21×3 3D joint coordinates per view, which are combined (e.g., averaged after rotation alignment) into a final hand pose.

## Method

S2DHand is an unsupervised adaptation framework built on two complementary stereo constraints. First, an attention-based merging (ABM) module enforces cross-view consensus by computing joint-wise attention weights from the peak intensity of each view's 2D heatmap (higher peak → higher confidence), then merging the two predictions in a shared coordinate system weighted by these confidences. Second, a rotation-guided refinement (RGR) module enforces invariance of the inter-view rotation by using BFGS optimization to adjust predictions so that the rotation recovered from them matches the initialized rotation matrix R. A momentum network (temporal moving average, η=0.99) generates the pseudo-labels, while R itself is updated with a slower moving average (η=0.999). The two pseudo-labels are combined with a fixed weight α=0.7, and the estimator is trained with an L2 loss against these pseudo-labels, requiring no ground-truth annotations throughout.

## Results

On the AssemblyHands benchmark, S2DHand reduces overall Mono-M from 56.18 mm to 48.44 mm (Headset 1) and from 58.74 mm to 53.11 mm (Headset 2) under the cross-dataset setting, and from 24.96 mm to 20.16 mm (Headset 1) under the in-dataset setting. The Dual-M metric improves from 50.59 mm to 45.92 mm (Headset 1, cross-dataset) and from 22.67 mm to 19.98 mm (Headset 1, in-dataset). Across all six camera pairs, the average improvement in both Mono-M and Dual-M exceeds 10%, with the maximum improvement exceeding 20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2DHand outperforms all four compared adaptation methods on every metric (Mono-M and Dual-M, both headsets) under the cross-dataset setting. Against the strongest baseline SFDAHPE (also source-free), S2DHand reduces Mono-M by 5.6 mm (Headset 1) and 4.1 mm (Headset 2), and Dual-M by 3.2 mm and 3.0 mm respectively. Against RegDA, the gains are 3.0 mm (Headset 1) and 1.6 mm (Headset 2) on Mono-M. Against DAGEN and ADDA, which are not source-free, S2DHand leads by 6.9 mm and 8.5 mm on Headset 1 Mono-M, respectively. Notably, S2DHand is source-free (requires no source-dataset data during adaptation), matching SFDAHPE in this property while achieving substantially lower error. The fine-tuning upper bound (which uses target labels) still outperforms S2DHand, confirming room for further improvement, but S2DHand closes most of that gap without any supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work: multi-view hand pose estimation (MvHM, UmeTrack) that requires costly multi-view annotations and fixed camera parameters, and single-view domain adaptation methods (SFDAHPE, RegDA, ADDA) that are limited to monocular inference. It repurposes the momentum-network pseudo-labeling paradigm (MoCo-style EMA) and stereo geometry (cross-view consensus, rotation invariance) into an unsupervised adaptation framework that bridges a pre-trained single-view estimator (DetNet) to an arbitrary dual-view setting without any labels or camera calibration. The attention-based merging and rotation-guided refinement modules are novel combinations of joint-wise confidence weighting and BFGS-based geometric optimization, but each component draws on well-known ideas from semi-supervised learning and stereo vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AR/VR headsets (Apple Vision Pro, Meta Quest) increasingly ship with multiple egocentric cameras, the ability to leverage dual-view information without expensive multi-view annotation or rigid camera calibration is practically significant for deployment. The method's limitation to exactly two views and its reliance on a sufficiently accurate pre-trained model for rotation initialization constrain its generality; extending to N-view settings or handling translation explicitly remain open. Additionally, the adaptation requires a fixed set of unlabeled dual-view pairs (N≥1000) per camera layout, so re-adaptation is needed whenever cameras are repositioned, which may be impractical in fully dynamic settings.
