## Problem

Vision Transformers lack the inductive biases (e.g., locality) of CNNs, making them data-hungry and poorly suited for long-tailed datasets where minority classes have few samples. Existing data-efficient ViT training methods (DeiT, DeiT-III) were designed for balanced datasets and perform sub-optimally under class imbalance, while prior long-tailed learning work has been almost entirely restricted to CNN backbones. The paper asks whether ViTs can be trained from scratch on long-tailed data without expensive large-scale pre-training while matching or exceeding SotA CNN-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240138/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist-2018) and produces a single class label per image. During training, the input is split into patch tokens plus two special tokens (CLS and DIST); the CLS token is trained against ground-truth labels while the DIST token is trained against hard-distillation targets from a CNN teacher evaluated on strongly augmented (out-of-distribution) versions of the same image. At inference, the final prediction is the average of the two token logits, requiring no additional expert networks or routing overhead.

## Method

DeiT-LT introduces a three-component distillation scheme for training ViTs from scratch on long-tailed data. First, it distills from a small CNN teacher (ResNet-32/50) using out-of-distribution images created via strong augmentations and Mixup, which induces local, CNN-like attention patterns in early ViT blocks and improves tail-class generalization. Second, it applies Deferred Re-Weighting (DRW) loss to the DIST token's distillation objective, causing the CLS and DIST tokens to diverge into complementary experts: CLS specializes in head classes while DIST specializes in tail classes. Third, it distills from teachers trained with Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), which transfers low-rank, flat-minimum feature geometry to the ViT student across all blocks. The overall loss is a weighted sum of standard cross-entropy on the CLS token and DRW-weighted cross-entropy on the DIST token against the teacher's hard predictions.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, DeiT-LT (PaCo+SAM teacher) achieves 59.1% overall accuracy (66.6% Head, 58.3% Mid, 40.0% Tail), outperforming the best CNN baseline BCL (57.1%) and the ViT baseline (37.5%) by 2.0% and 21.6% respectively. On CIFAR-10 LT (ρ=100), DeiT-LT reaches 87.5% overall accuracy, surpassing the best CNN method GCL (82.7%) by 4.8% and the ViT baseline (62.6%) by 24.9%. On iNaturalist-2018, DeiT-LT achieves 75.1% overall (76.2% Tail), exceeding the best CNN method CBD_ENS (73.6%) and the ViT baseline (54.2%) by 1.5% and 20.9% respectively. Across all datasets, DeiT-LT consistently outperforms its own CNN teacher by 1.6–4.5%, demonstrating that the ViT student generalizes better than the CNN it distills from.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeiT-LT (PaCo+SAM) outperforms all ViT baselines by large margins: 21.6% over ViT (59.1% vs 37.5%) and 10.7% over DeiT-III (59.1% vs 48.4%) on ImageNet-LT, with similar gaps on CIFAR-10 LT (24.9% and 28.4% respectively). Against the strongest CNN baselines, DeiT-LT wins on all four datasets: 2.0% over BCL on ImageNet-LT, 4.8% over GCL on CIFAR-10 LT (ρ=100), and 1.5% over CBD_ENS on iNaturalist-2018. The method also surpasses its own CNN teacher (PaCo+SAM ResNet-50) by 1.6% on ImageNet-LT and 1.7% on iNaturalist-2018, confirming that the ViT student generalizes beyond the teacher. On the Tail sub-metric specifically, DeiT-LT achieves 40.0% on ImageNet-LT (vs 39.3% for the PaCo+SAM teacher and 36.6% for BCL) and 76.2% on iNaturalist-2018 (vs 75.2% for the teacher and 72.9% for ImbSAM), showing the distillation token's specialization on minority classes. The paper explicitly excludes comparisons with methods requiring large-scale pre-training (e.g., RAC [30], VL-LTR [46]), arguing that DeiT-LT's from-scratch training is the key differentiator for domain-specific applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions knowledge distillation for ViT training in the long-tailed setting, deliberately reviving the distillation mechanism that DeiT-III showed to be redundant for balanced ImageNet. It builds on DeiT's CLS/DIST token architecture, LDAM-DRW's re-weighting for class imbalance, and SAM's flat-minima optimization, but departs from all three in key ways: distilling from OOD (strongly augmented) images rather than in-distribution ones, applying DRW specifically to the distillation loss to create a tail-class expert, and using SAM-trained teachers to induce low-rank features. The paper positions itself against the large body of CNN-based long-tailed methods (RIDE, MiSLAS, GCL, etc.) and argues that ViTs, when trained with the right distillation scheme, can match or exceed these without any pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve SotA long-tailed recognition performance with ViTs trained entirely from scratch, removing the dependency on large-scale pre-training that limits domain transfer (medical, satellite, etc.) and introduces dataset biases. The OOD distillation insight—that a teacher's *incorrect* predictions on augmented images are more informative for the student than its correct predictions on clean images—challenges the standard assumption that teacher quality should be maximized. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that tail-class performance remains bounded by the CNN teacher's capacity, leaving open the question of how to surpass the teacher on minority classes without external supervision or synthetic data generation.
