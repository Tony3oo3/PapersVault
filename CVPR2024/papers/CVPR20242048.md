## Problem

Distribution matching-based dataset distillation methods suffer from two key limitations: synthetic datasets exhibit excessively scattered feature distributions within the same class (reducing class discrimination, especially at low IPC), and they match only mean features between real and synthetic datasets, ignoring inter-feature covariance structure. This paper addresses both gaps with two complementary constraints targeting inter-sample and inter-feature relations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large labeled RGB image dataset (SVHN, CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) and produces a synthetic dataset of IPC=1, 10, or 50 images per class optimized via SGD. The class centralization constraint uses a pretrained ResNet18 (trained 30 epochs on the target dataset) to extract features, while the distribution matching loss uses a lightweight 3-block ConvNet, making the two constraints operate in different feature spaces. Synthetic images are initialized randomly and trained with a learning rate of 1 and batch sizes of 256–5000 depending on IPC and dataset.

## Method

The paper introduces two plug-and-play constraints that extend existing distribution matching baselines (DM or IDM). The class centralization constraint extracts features via a pretrained ResNet18 and applies a hinge-style loss that penalizes same-class samples whose distance from the class centroid exceeds a threshold β, encouraging tight intra-class clustering. The covariance matching constraint reshapes per-sample features into (d, hw) tensors to compute local d×d covariance matrices, avoiding the ill-posed full-dimensional covariance estimation when sample count is far below feature dimension; it then minimizes the squared Frobenius distance between real and synthetic local covariance matrices per class. The total objective is L = L_DM (or L_IDM) + λ_CC·L_CC + λ_CM·L_CM, with λ_CC=0.05 and λ_CM=0.01 for IPC≤10, and λ_CC=0.003 for IPC=50. Both constraints are architecture-agnostic and can be attached to any distribution matching baseline without modifying its core optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 at IPC=10, IDM+Ours achieves 59.9% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best (DataDAM at 54.2%) by 5.7% and the DM baseline (48.5%) by 11.4%. At IPC=50, IDM+Ours reaches 69.0% on CIFAR-10 and 51.3% on CIFAR-100, outperforming all prior methods. On TinyImageNet at IPC=10, IDM+Ours achieves 23.3%, exceeding the second-best method by 1.3%. In cross-architecture evaluation on CIFAR-10 (IPC=50), the method maintains accuracies of 69.0%, 67.3%, 67.3%, and 68.3% across ConvNet, AlexNet, VGG11, and ResNet18, with a maximum drop of only 1.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDM+Ours achieves the best accuracy across all tested datasets and IPC settings, outperforming the strongest prior method DataDAM [25] by 5.7% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (59.9% vs. 54.2%), 2.0% on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (69.0% vs. 67.0%), and 1.9% on CIFAR-100 IPC=50 (51.3% vs. 49.4%). Compared to its direct baselines, IDM+Ours improves over IDM [41] by 1.9–2.6% on CIFAR-10 and 1.0–1.5% on CIFAR-100, while DM+Ours improves over DM [39] by up to 6.6% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 and 2.9% on SVHN IPC=10. On TinyImageNet IPC=10, IDM+Ours (23.3%) surpasses DataDAM (18.7%) by 4.6% and IDM (21.9%) by 1.4%. In cross-architecture testing (CIFAR-10, IPC=50), IDM+Ours maintains 67.3–69.0% across ConvNet, AlexNet, VGG11, and ResNet18 with <1.7% variance, whereas KIP [21] drops 10.8% from ConvNet to ResNet18 and DC [40] drops 33% on ResNet18. Classic coreset methods (Herding, K-Center, Forgetting) remain far behind, confirming that learned synthetic datasets substantially outperform selected real samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution matching (DM) and improved distribution matching (IDM) line of work in dataset distillation by identifying two specific weaknesses: dispersed intra-class feature distributions and reliance solely on mean matching. Rather than proposing a new distillation paradigm, it introduces two plug-and-play constraints—class centralization (inter-sample) and covariance matching (inter-feature)—that can be appended to any existing distribution matching objective. The covariance matching constraint draws on the 'small sample problem' in covariance estimation, using local feature descriptors (reshaped spatial tensors) to make the computation tractable when IPC is far smaller than feature dimensionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt these constraints without re-architecting their distillation pipeline, and the cross-architecture consistency (<1.7% drop across four architectures) addresses a practical deployment concern. However, the paper itself notes that at higher compression ratios (IPC ≥ 400), the performance gap between all methods—including random selection—narrows substantially, suggesting current distillation techniques are most impactful only at extreme compression. Open questions include whether these constraints transfer to gradient- or trajectory-matching methods, whether the local covariance formulation generalizes to higher-resolution or non-image modalities, and whether the reliance on a pretrained ResNet18 for class centralization introduces a hidden dependency that limits true from-scratch distillation.
