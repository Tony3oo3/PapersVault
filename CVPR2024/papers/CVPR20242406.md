## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D object detection methods rely on temporal consistency to refine pseudo-labels, but approximately 65% of objects in LiDAR sequences are never fully scanned (incomplete objects), leaving their pseudo-labels with erroneous sizes and positions that cannot be recovered by frame-to-frame alignment. This sparsity-induced label noise misleads iterative self-training and caps detection performance well below fully supervised baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242406/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 11 consecutive LiDAR point cloud frames (5 past, current, 5 future) from a single ego-vehicle trajectory, first removing motion artifacts via Persistence Point Scoring before concatenation. It outputs class-agnostic 3D bounding boxes parameterized as [x, y, z, l, w, h, α, β, τ] (position, dimensions, azimuth, class, tracking ID), where class assignment is determined by predefined commonsense size thresholds rather than learned classifiers. At inference, a single frame is fed to the detection network without downsampling.

## Method

CPD constructs a Commonsense Prototype (CProto) set from high-quality pseudo-labels of complete objects, scored unsupervised via a Completeness and Size Similarity (CSS) metric combining distance, multi-level occupancy, and truncated KL-divergence size similarity. A CProto-constrained Box Regularization (CBR) step then refines all pseudo-labels by replacing their dimensions with the matched CProto box size and re-localizing positions by aligning box boundaries to the densest point clusters. For training, a CProto-constrained Self-Training (CST) module uses a dual-branch architecture: a prototype network receives dense CProto-augmented points while a detection network receives randomly downsampled sparse points, with CSS-weighted detection loss suppressing false-label supervision and geometry contrast losses (cosine feature distance + IoU/location/angle box distance) aligning sparse predictions to dense prototype features.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset validation set, CPD achieves 57.79% and 37.40% 3D AP L1 (IoU 0.5/0.7) for Vehicle, 21.91% and 16.31% for Pedestrian, and 5.83% and 5.06% for Cyclist, substantially outperforming the previous best unsupervised method OYSTER (30.48%/14.66% Vehicle). On KITTI (trained on WOD, tested on KITTI), CPD attains 90.85% and 81.01% 3D AP at IoU 0.5 for easy and moderate car classes, approaching the fully supervised CenterPoint (97.07%/89.23%). On PandaSet, CPD reaches 50.7% BEV AP at IoU 0.3, surpassing OYSTER by 7.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPD outperforms all unsupervised baselines by large margins across all three datasets. On WOD, CPD surpasses OYSTER [41] by 27.31% (Vehicle L1 IoU 0.5: 57.79 vs 30.48) and 22.74% (Vehicle L2 IoU 0.7: 32.13 vs 14.10), and beats MODEST [40] by 39.28% on Vehicle L1 IoU 0.5. On KITTI (cross-dataset, trained on WOD), CPD achieves 72.98% 3D AP at IoU 0.7 for moderate cars, exceeding the weakly supervised COIN [34] (2% labels) by 23.29% and approaching fully supervised CenterPoint [39] (88.55% at IoU 0.7 moderate). On PandaSet, CPD improves over OYSTER by 6.5% BEV AP and 9.3% BEV Recall at IoU 0.7. The only comparison where CPD does not lead is against fully supervised CenterPoint on KITTI easy cars (90.85% vs 97.07% at IoU 0.5), a gap of 6.22% that the authors highlight as evidence of the method's proximity to supervised performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPD extends the pseudo-label generation and iterative self-training paradigm established by MODEST [40] and OYSTER [41] for unsupervised 3D LiDAR detection. Its key departure is the introduction of Commonsense Prototypes (CProto) — high-quality geometric and size templates constructed from complete objects using unsupervised scoring — to refine pseudo-labels of the ~65% incomplete objects that temporal consistency alone cannot recover. The prototype concept is borrowed from few-shot and domain-adaptive 3D detection (Prototypical VoteNet, GPA-3D), but CPD repurposes it in a fully unsupervised setting where no annotations exist to define prototypes, instead relying on commonsense size priors and multi-frame completeness heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPD is the first unsupervised 3D detector to surpass a weakly supervised baseline (2% labels) on KITTI, narrowing the gap to fully supervised CenterPoint to within ~6 AP on easy car detection. This shifts the practical cost-benefit calculus for autonomous driving teams: if pseudo-label quality can be bootstrapped from commonsense geometry, the annotation bottleneck may be largely bypassed for dominant classes. However, performance on minority classes (cyclists: 5.83 AP vs. 57.79 for vehicles) remains weak, and the method's reliance on hand-defined size thresholds and Wikipedia template boxes limits its generality to novel or atypical object categories. Open questions include whether the CProto mechanism transfers across sensor configurations or urban environments with highly variable object sizes.
