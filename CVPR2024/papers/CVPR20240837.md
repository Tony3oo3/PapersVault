## Problem

Data-driven channel pruning methods outperform norm-based criteria but cannot be directly applied to federated learning because local datasets are distributed, confidential, and non-IID. Existing FL pruning approaches either rely on fixed importance scores (channel norms) that ignore local data distributions, or focus on weight-level sparsity rather than structural channel pruning with per-device computational budgets. This paper tackles the gap of performing data-driven, device-specific channel pruning under FL without centralizing data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full CNN (ResNet-56/18/34 or MobileNet-V2), N local non-IID datasets partitioned via Dirichlet distribution, device IDs, and per-device FLOPs budgets (p_s for the server-side sub-network, p_d^n for each device). It outputs a permanently pruned server-side sub-network (serving as a shared weight bank) and N device-wise sub-networks, each represented as a binary architecture vector generated on-the-fly by a hypernetwork from a learned device embedding—no per-device structure is stored. The final pruned model is finetuned for 200 epochs to recover accuracy.

## Method

The core mechanism pairs a learnable embedding layer (mapping device IDs, with ID 0 for the server, to embedding vectors) with a hypernetwork that produces binary channel-keep/prune vectors via Straight-Through Gumbel-Sigmoid. The server-side sub-network (a⁰) and each device-side sub-network (aⁿ) are generated from their respective embeddings, and the effective architecture on device n is the element-wise product a⁰ ⊙ aⁿ, ensuring channels pruned by the server are permanently removed. Training alternates between updating model weights (with frozen HN) and updating the HN/embedding parameters (with frozen weights), using a log-based FLOPs regularization to enforce per-device computational budgets. The full procedure is formulated as a federated bilevel optimization problem, and the paper proves convergence to a stationary point at rate O(K_HN^{-0.5}) with linear speedup in the number of devices and mini-batch size.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-56 at 50% device FLOPs, DWNP achieves 91.88% accuracy (a +0.66% gain over the unpruned 91.22% baseline), outperforming Filter Pruning (90.29%), FedOSP (90.94%), and FedILP (91.14%). On CIFAR-100, DWNP improves over the unpruned model by +1.74% (ResNet-18), +2.17% (ResNet-34), and +1.46% (MobileNet-V2) at 50% device FLOPs. On TinyImageNet, DWNP gains +1.06%/+1.10% Top-1/Top-5 accuracy for ResNet-18 and +0.80%/+0.74% for ResNet-34 at 50% FLOPs, while all baselines either lose accuracy or gain marginally.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DWNP consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-34 at 50% FLOPs, DWNP achieves 71.72% accuracy, beating FedILP (69.49%) by 2.23%, FedOSP (68.76%) by 2.96%, and Filter Pruning (67.83%) by 3.89%. The most striking result is that DWNP is the only method that improves accuracy over the unpruned model in nearly all settings (positive Δ-Acc), while Filter Pruning and FedOSP always lose accuracy. FedILP, which is the closest baseline (DWNP without device-side sub-networks), occasionally matches or slightly exceeds the unpruned model (e.g., +0.44% on ResNet-34) but is still 1.7–2.2% behind DWNP. On TinyImageNet, DWNP's advantage over the best baseline (FedILP) ranges from 0.99% to 1.13% in Top-1 accuracy. The trade-off is that DWNP retains more server-side FLOPs (20% vs. 50% for baselines), meaning the server stores a larger model, but this enables the device-side sub-networks to be more aggressively pruned while preserving accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends differentiable channel pruning with FLOPs constraints (Gao et al., CVPR 2020) into the federated learning setting, where prior pruning methods either rely on fixed criteria like channel norms or require centralized data access. It leverages the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al., 2016) to generate per-device sub-network architectures from learned embeddings, avoiding the need to store separate structures for each device. Unlike prior FL pruning approaches (SCBFwP, FedPrune, LotteryFL, PruneFL) that focus on weight-level sparsity or single global sub-networks, this work introduces device-wise sub-networks with per-device FLOPs budgets and frames the joint optimization as a federated bilevel problem with convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The device-wise formulation addresses a practical gap: heterogeneous edge devices have different resource budgets and data distributions, yet prior FL pruning methods produce a single compressed model for all. The theoretical convergence guarantee for the bilevel optimization is a useful contribution for the FL pruning literature. However, the method requires the server to retain a larger sub-network (20% FLOPs vs. 50% for baselines), increasing server storage, and assumes training and test distributions are similar per device. Experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet with relatively small architectures, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale models or more extreme heterogeneity settings.
