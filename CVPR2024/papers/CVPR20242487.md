## Problem

StyleGAN inversion faces a fundamental distortion-editability trade-off: low-dimensional W/W+ spaces enable clean semantic edits but lose fine image details, while high-dimensional feature spaces F_k reconstruct images faithfully but produce artefacts or fail entirely during editing. Prior methods operating in F_k (Feature-Style, StyleRes) apply ad-hoc transformations to the feature tensor that do not fully resolve editability, and the problem worsens as the feature resolution increases. No existing encoder-based method simultaneously achieves near-perfect reconstruction and robust editing at high feature resolutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image (1024×1024 for faces, 256×256 for cars) and optionally a pre-computed semantic editing direction d in W+ space. It outputs either a high-fidelity reconstruction (inversion mode, Δ=0) or an edited image. Internally, the Inverter predicts both a full W+ code (N×512 vectors) and a high-resolution feature tensor F_k ∈ R^{512×64×64} (k=9), which is substantially larger than the F_5 ∈ R^{512×16×16} used by prior feature-space encoders. The final image is synthesized by the StyleGAN2 generator from F_k' and the edited w_{k+1:N} latents.

## Method

StyleFeatureEditor uses a two-phase training framework. Phase 1 trains an Inverter (ResNet-50 backbone + Feature predictor + Fuser) to jointly predict W+ and a high-resolution F_k, where the Fuser fuses the predicted F_pred with the generator's own k-th layer output G(w_{0:k}) to capture style information. Phase 2 freezes the Inverter and trains a Feature Editor H that transforms F_k into F_k' conditioned on a delta signal Δ. The key insight is that Δ is computed from a pre-trained e4e encoder: it is the difference between the generator's k-th layer features before and after applying the editing direction d to e4e's W+ prediction, encoding which spatial regions need modification. Training pairs for Phase 2 are automatically generated by inverting real images with e4e, applying 13 editing directions, and using the resulting image pairs as supervision, eliminating the need for manually annotated edited images. At inference, the pipeline predicts W+ and F_k, edits W+ by d, computes Δ via e4e, applies the Feature Editor, and synthesizes the final image from F_k' and w'_{k+1:N}.

## Results

On CelebA HQ, StyleFeatureEditor achieves LPIPS 0.019, L2 0.002, FID 3.535, and MS-SSIM 0.922 for inversion quality, outperforming the next-best encoder-based method (Feature-Style: LPIPS 0.067, L2 0.012, FID 10.861) by more than 3× on LPIPS and L2. On editing (measured by FID for smile removal, glasses addition, and age increase), SFE scores 24.388, 73.098, and 41.677 respectively, matching or slightly exceeding StyleRes (24.465, 73.089, 43.698). Inference time is 0.070 s per image on a Tesla V100, comparable to other encoder-based methods and ~1770× faster than the optimization-based PTI (124 s). On Stanford Cars, SFE achieves LPIPS 0.039 and FID 4.035, again substantially ahead of Feature-Style (0.147, 7.180).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFE dominates all 11 baselines on every inversion metric. Against the strongest encoder-based inversion methods (Feature-Style, StyleRes), SFE reduces LPIPS from 0.067/0.076 to 0.019 (≈3.5× improvement) and L2 from 0.012/0.013 to 0.002 (≈6× improvement), while FID drops from 10.861/8.505 to 3.535. Against optimization-based PTI, SFE achieves lower LPIPS (0.019 vs 0.085) and FID (3.535 vs 14.466) while being 1770× faster (0.070 s vs 124 s). On editing quality, SFE is essentially tied with StyleRes: SFE scores 24.388 vs 24.465 on smile removal (slightly better), 73.098 vs 73.089 on glasses addition (tied), and 41.677 vs 43.698 on age increase (slightly better). SFE clearly outperforms all other encoder-based methods (e4e, pSp, StyleTransformer, ReStyle, Padding Inverter, HyperInverter, HyperStyle, HFGI, Feature-Style) on editing FID by 5–30 points. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that removing the Feature Editor H degrades editing FID by ~4–13 points, while removing the Fuser or the inversion loss in Phase 2 primarily hurts reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Feature-Style encoder [44] by increasing the predicted feature tensor from F_5 to F_9 and adding a Fuser module that fuses generator intermediate features with the predicted features. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a separate Feature Editor module trained in a second phase, where synthetic editing pairs are automatically generated using a pre-trained W+-space encoder (e4e [38]) rather than requiring manually annotated edited images. This two-phase framework directly addresses the distortion-editability trade-off identified in prior work: high-dimensional F_k encoders achieve superior reconstruction but lose editability, while W/W+ encoders edit well but reconstruct poorly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× improvement in LPIPS and L2 over StyleRes while maintaining comparable editing FID scores is a substantial practical advance for real-time StyleGAN editing pipelines, and the synthetic-data training strategy for the Feature Editor is a reusable technique for other GAN inversion settings. However, editing quality remains on par with (not better than) StyleRes, and the method is tightly coupled to StyleGAN2's architecture and the e4e encoder for Δ computation, limiting immediate generalizability. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer GAN architectures (e.g., StyleGAN3, diffusion models), whether the e4e dependency can be replaced, and whether the F_9 resolution is the optimal trade-off point for editability.
