## Problem

No existing feed-forward system for 3D human mesh recovery achieves both accurate 3D reconstruction and faithful image-model alignment, particularly in the monocular setting. Optimization-based fitting approaches that refine regression estimates suffer from multiple local minima, sensitivity to initialization, and the need for handcrafted prior terms. Diffusion models, which naturally capture complex data distributions through score matching, had not been explored as a tool for solving inverse problems in 3D human recovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (encoded by a frozen CNN backbone into a context feature vector), an initial SMPL pose estimate from any off-the-shelf regression network (e.g., HMR 2.0 or ProHMR), and task-specific observations such as 2D keypoint detections with confidence scores, multiple uncalibrated views, or adjacent video frames. The output is a refined 144-dimensional SMPL pose vector (24 joints × 6D rotation representation) that better aligns with the observations while remaining consistent with the image-conditional distribution learned by the diffusion model. Shape parameters β are held fixed from the regression estimate in the main experiments.

## Method

ScoreHMR trains a small diffusion model (3 MLP blocks conditioned on timestep and image features) to capture the conditional distribution of SMPL pose parameters given an input image, using pseudo ground-truth annotations from SPIN or EFT. At inference, the regression estimate is mapped to the diffusion latent space via deterministic DDIM inversion, then refined through an iterative loop of guided DDIM sampling where the noise prediction is augmented with a task-specific score term (e.g., keypoint reprojection loss, cross-view consistency loss, or temporal smoothness loss) that approximates the likelihood gradient under a Gaussian noise assumption. This guidance term plays the role of the data term in classical optimization, while the diffusion model serves as a learned parametric prior, eliminating the need for handcrafted energy functions. The same task-agnostic diffusion model is reused across all three applications—single-frame fitting, multi-view refinement, and video motion smoothing—without any per-task retraining, and the loop terminates when the relative change in the guidance loss falls below a threshold.

## Results

In the body model fitting setting, ScoreHMR-b (PARE features + EFT fits) achieves 51.1 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW and 76.6 mm on EMDB 1, outperforming all optimization baselines including SMPLify (60.1/83.5) and ProHMR-fitting (55.1/80.1) when initialized from HMR 2.0. In the human motion refinement setting, ScoreHMR-b reduces PA-MPJPE to 50.5 mm (3DPW) and 75.3 mm (EMDB 1), while cutting acceleration error to 11.1 mm/s² and 11.9 mm/s² respectively — a 21.3% and 40.5% relative improvement over ProHMR-fitting. In multi-view refinement, ScoreHMR-b achieves 44.7 mm MPJPE on Human3.6M and 148.3 mm on Mannequin Challenge, consistently outperforming ProHMR-fitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScoreHMR-b outperforms all six baselines in the primary body model fitting setting. Against SMPLify [2], which actually degrades HMR 2.0 performance (54.3→60.1 on 3DPW), ScoreHMR-b improves it to 51.1 mm — a 9.0 mm absolute gain. ProHMR-fitting [28] fails to improve HMR 2.0 on EMDB 1 (78.7→80.1), while ScoreHMR-b reduces it to 76.6 mm. LGD [47] (55.9) and LFMM [6] (52.2) are optimization-from-scratch baselines that ScoreHMR-b (51.1) surpasses on 3DPW. In the motion refinement setting, ScoreHMR-b dramatically reduces acceleration error compared to VIBE-opt [24] (42.1→11.1 on 3DPW) and ProHMR-fitting (14.1→11.1 on 3DPW, 20.0→11.9 on EMDB 1). The paper notes that ScoreHMR is the only method that improves upon the state-of-the-art HMR 2.0 monocular regression in the single-frame fitting setting, a distinction none of the optimization baselines achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScoreHMR sits at the intersection of regression-based HMR (HMR, PARE, HMR 2.0, ProHMR) and optimization-based model fitting (SMPLify, ProHMR-fitting, LGD), but departs from both by replacing handcrafted energy minimization with score-guided denoising in a diffusion model's latent space. The iterative refinement loop directly adapts DDIM inversion and deterministic sampling (Song et al., 2021) and the Gaussian-noise likelihood score approximation from diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2023), which had previously been applied to image-domain inverse problems (inpainting, deblurring) but not to 3D human recovery. The paper explicitly positions itself as bridging a gap: diffusion models had been used for text-to-motion generation but not as learned priors for solving HMR inverse problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ScoreHMR demonstrates that a single task-agnostic diffusion prior, trained once on generic SMPL pose distributions, can serve as a drop-in refinement step across three distinct inverse-problem settings (single-frame keypoint fitting, multi-view consistency, video temporal smoothing) without any per-task retraining — a practical property that handcrafted optimization pipelines lack. The fact that it is the only tested method that improves upon HMR 2.0 (the current SOTA regression system) in single-frame fitting suggests that learned diffusion priors may capture pose distributions more faithfully than the parametric priors used in traditional optimization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to shape parameter refinement (the paper reports no improvement when including β), whether the Gaussian noise assumption in the guidance term limits accuracy under heavy occlusion, and whether the method generalizes beyond SMPL to other articulated body models.
