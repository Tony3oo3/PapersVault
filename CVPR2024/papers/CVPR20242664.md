## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF methods suffer from two key limitations: reconstruction quality degrades significantly under limited computing budgets, and they lack semantic understanding of the scene. Most approaches adopt a uniform spatio-temporal sampling strategy that does not account for the vastly different motion scales across scene regions, wasting capacity on static areas while under-sampling fast-moving ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242664/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB videos (processed in 100-frame chunks) with calibrated camera poses, and optionally a single user click at any viewpoint and time step for tracking. It outputs photo-realistic novel view renderings of the dynamic scene at real-time rates (~7 FPS on an RTX 4090), as well as object masks at arbitrary novel viewpoints and time steps. Input videos are at high resolutions (up to 2560×1920) but are downsampled by a factor of 2 for the Google Immersive and Neural 3D Video datasets to match prior work protocols.

## Method

Gear-NeRF replaces the unified 4D volume with a series of planar-factorized 4D feature volumes, one per gear level, where shared spatial embeddings (h functions) are paired with gear-specific spatio-temporal embeddings (k functions). A 4D semantic embedding is learned by supervising the NeRF's volume-rendered output against SAM encoder features, providing scene-level semantic structure. Gear assignment is updated iteratively: rendering loss maps identify poorly reconstructed patches, whose centers serve as positive/negative prompts to the SAM decoder to generate upshift masks, and a single gradient step pushes the gear function to increment levels in those regions. Motion-aware sampling then allocates higher temporal resolution (linearly interpolated from 1 to total frames) and denser spatial sampling (each point split into 2^(p−1) sub-points) to higher-gear regions. This stratified, motion-aware allocation of sampling budget, combined with the 4D SAM embedding, simultaneously improves rendering fidelity and enables free-viewpoint object tracking from a single click.

## Results

Gear-NeRF achieves state-of-the-art dynamic novel view synthesis across all three datasets: on Technicolor it reaches 32.21 PSNR / 0.919 SSIM / 0.058 LPIPS, on Google Immersive 28.74 / 0.876 / 0.122, and on Neural 3D Video 31.80 / 0.936 / 0.058. For free-viewpoint object tracking, it attains mIoU of 97.4 (Technicolor), 94.3 (Google Immersive), and 93.4 (Neural 3D Video) at the prompted time step, and additionally reports novel-time-step mIoU of 92.1, 91.5, and 90.6 respectively — a capability no baseline supports. Training time ranges from 148 to 204 minutes per 100-frame chunk, with inference at 6.8–7.9 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gear-NeRF outperforms all four novel-view-synthesis baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across every dataset. On Technicolor it beats the closest competitor HyperReel by 1.17 dB PSNR and 0.032 SSIM; on Google Immersive it surpasses HyperReel by 0.42 dB PSNR; on Neural 3D Video it leads HyperReel by 0.68 dB PSNR and 0.071 LPIPS. MixVoxels is the fastest (14–19 min training, 16–19 FPS) but trails Gear-NeRF by 2–3 dB PSNR on all datasets. ST-NeRF and HexPlane are the slowest (344–527 min) and weakest in quality. For free-viewpoint tracking, Gear-NeRF marginally outperforms SA3D on mIoU (e.g., 97.4 vs. 96.4 on Technicolor) and uniquely supports novel-time-step mask prediction (t-mIoU ≈ 90–92%), a capability SA3D cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gear-NeRF extends the planar-factorized 4D volume representation lineage (HexPlane, HyperReel, K-Planes, Tensor4D) by replacing a single unified 4D feature volume with a series of gear-level volumes, each with its own spatio-temporal resolution. The key conceptual departure is the integration of SAM's semantic encoder/decoder into the 4D NeRF pipeline, which prior dynamic NeRF methods entirely lack. The 'gear' mechanism for motion-stratified sampling is novel: previous approaches apply uniform spatio-temporal sampling regardless of local motion intensity, while Gear-NeRF allocates resolution proportionally to motion scale. The free-viewpoint tracking capability repurposes the learned 4D SAM embedding in a way no prior NeRF-based dynamic scene method has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that semantic understanding (via SAM) can simultaneously improve rendering fidelity and enable object tracking in dynamic NeRFs, suggesting a broader principle that semantic priors are underexploited in 4D reconstruction. The gear-based adaptive sampling offers a practical path toward resource-efficient dynamic scene reconstruction, though the longer training times (~150–200 min per 100-frame chunk) limit real-time applicability. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences or larger scenes, whether the gear mechanism generalizes to multi-object or articulated motion, and whether the tracking capability can be extended beyond single-object prompts to interactive multi-object scenarios.
