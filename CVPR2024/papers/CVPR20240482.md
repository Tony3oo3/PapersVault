## Problem

State-of-the-art query-based transformer RVOS models tend to over-weight action- and relation-related cues in text expressions over appearance-related cues, leading to partial or incorrect mask predictions (e.g., segmenting the wrong person because the model focuses on 'walking' rather than 'white t-shirt'). Existing frameworks lack a mechanism to explicitly balance these competing textual signals during segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of T frames (resized to height ≥360, width ≤576 for LoSh-M; width ≤640 for LoSh-S) and a long text expression describing the target instance. It additionally generates a subject-centric short text expression by stripping predicate and object content (e.g., 'a man in white t-shirt' from 'a man in white t-shirt is walking'). The output is per-frame binary segmentation masks for the referred instance, with the short-text prediction head deactivated at inference time.

## Method

LoSh introduces a long-short text joint prediction framework built on query-based transformer backbones (MTTR or SgMg). A subject-centric short text expression is extracted from the long one, and both are encoded by RoBERTa and fused with shared Video Swin Transformer visual features in a dual-pathway transformer encoder. A unidirectional long-short cross-attention module (short features as key/value, long features as query) replaces even-numbered self-attention layers to inject appearance information into the long-text pathway. A long-short predictions intersection loss penalizes the model when the long-text mask is not contained within the short-text mask, enforcing partial alignment between the two predictions. Additionally, a forward-backward visual consistency loss uses Farneback optical flow to warp visual features from adjacent frames onto each annotated frame, minimizing L2 distance to enforce temporal feature coherence.

## Results

On A2D-Sentences, LoSh-S with Video-Swin-B achieves 81.2% Overall IoU, 73.1% Mean IoU, and 59.9 mAP, outperforming the previous best (SgMg with Video-Swin-B) by +1.3% O-IoU and +1.4 mAP. On Refer-YouTube-VOS, LoSh-S (Video-Swin-B) reaches 67.2 J&F, surpassing SgMg (65.7) by +1.5. On Refer-DAVIS17, LoSh-S (Video-Swin-B) achieves 64.3 J&F, exceeding SgMg (63.3) by +1.0. LoSh-M built on MTTR gains +3.1 mAP and +2.7% O-IoU over the MTTR baseline on A2D-Sentences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoSh consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On A2D-Sentences, LoSh-M improves over MTTR [3] by +3.1 mAP (47.8 vs 44.7) and +2.7% O-IoU (72.9 vs 70.2), while LoSh-S surpasses SgMg [27] by +1.5 mAP (57.6 vs 56.1) with Video-Swin-T and +1.4 mAP (59.9 vs 58.5) with Video-Swin-B. On Refer-YouTube-VOS, LoSh-S (Video-Swin-B) achieves 67.2 J&F, beating SgMg (65.7) by +1.5 and ReferFormer [44] (62.9) by +4.3. On Refer-DAVIS17, LoSh-S (Video-Swin-B) reaches 64.3 J&F, exceeding SgMg (63.3) by +1.0 and ReferFormer (61.1) by +3.2. The older baselines (Gavrilyuk [10], CMPC-V [21], YOFO [18], MLRL [42]) are substantially outperformed, with LoSh-S leading by over 20 mAP on A2D-Sentences and over 15 J&F on Refer-YouTube-VOS compared to the weakest entries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the query-based transformer framework for RVOS established by MTTR [3] and SgMg [27], which themselves adapted DETR/VisTR-style object queries to the referring segmentation setting. Its key departure is the introduction of a subject-centric short text expression alongside the original long expression, creating a dual-branch prediction with a unidirectional long-short cross-attention module and an intersection loss to enforce mask containment. This contrasts with prior linguistic strategies like CMPC-V [21] (word-level entity/relation classification) or actor-action modular scoring [45], which operate at the word or proposal level rather than at the full-expression level. The forward-backward visual consistency loss via Farneback optical flow also distinguishes it from prior flow-augmented methods [45, 48] that use learned flow estimation or flow as an auxiliary input feature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and well-quantified failure mode in query-based RVOS (over 70% of low-IoU predictions mis-attend to action/relation rather than appearance) and provides a targeted, low-overhead fix that is plug-and-play across existing pipelines. The minimal computational cost (5% extra FLOPs, 8% extra training time, unchanged inference time) makes the approach practical for adoption. Open questions include whether the short-expression strategy generalizes to more complex multi-instance scenes or non-English expressions, and whether the unidirectional cross-attention design would benefit from learned gating rather than fixed architecture. The reliance on part-of-speech tagging for short expression generation, while 98.7% accurate, remains a potential fragility point for out-of-domain text.
