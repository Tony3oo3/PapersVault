## Problem

Existing 4D editing pipelines face two critical bottlenecks: dynamic NeRF-based representations require dense ray sampling that is slow and memory-intensive, and 2D diffusion-based editors produce edits that are inconsistent across viewpoints and over time, causing 4D scene optimization to diverge or yield blurry results. No prior method achieves efficient, high-fidelity, and spatiotemporally consistent 4D portrait editing driven by text instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242423/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (2-second, 50-frame segments captured by four sparsely positioned fixed RGB cameras) and a free-form text prompt (e.g., a person's name or a scene description). It outputs an editable 4D GaussianPlanes representation that can be rendered from arbitrary novel viewpoints at any timestep, producing spatiotemporally consistent RGB images. The editing is performed iteratively via a ControlNet-conditioned Stable Diffusion 1.5 editor with OpenPose control signals, and the final 4D model supports real-time rendering via Gaussian splatting rasterization.

## Method

Control4D introduces GaussianPlanes, which structures discrete Gaussian Splatting points via a spatial tri-plane decomposition (color, opacity, rotation as low-rank features on three planes fused by MLPs) and a 4D plane-based decomposition (nine planes from Tensor4D) to regularize per-point position and rotation flow over time. To counteract the inconsistency of frame-by-frame diffusion edits, the method adds latent distribution parameters (mean and variance, also tri-plane decomposed) to each Gaussian point, renders a distribution parameter map, samples latent features, and feeds them alongside rendered RGB into a 2D super-resolution generator. A Wasserstein GAN discriminator is trained on the edited images, and a three-level progressive guidance scheme (global encoder → local encoder conditions, with perceptual and L1 losses added at higher levels) stabilizes training against mode collapse. Training follows a staged strategy—first editing the canonical 3D space with flow frozen, then unfreezing for full 4D optimization—and uses batch-based dataset updates with an attention module (AnimateDiff V2 LoRA) to improve cross-frame editing consistency.

## Results

On the Tensor4D dynamic dataset, Control4D achieves a FID of 18.59 in dynamic scenes and 14.11 in static scenes, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: Tensor4D+GAN at 47.39 and 27.81 respectively). CLIP text-image similarity remains competitive across methods, with Control4D scoring 0.3192 (dynamic) and 0.3323 (static), within ~0.001 of the best-performing baselines. The FID gap is particularly large against the NeRF-based InstructNeRF2NeRF (126.3 FID in static) and the unstructured Tensor4D (155.6 FID in dynamic), demonstrating the combined benefit of GaussianPlanes representation and GAN-based generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Control4D decisively outperforms InstructNeRF2NeRF [18] on FID (14.11 vs. 126.3 in static; the dynamic comparison is not directly reported but the qualitative gap in Fig. 4 is stark), while InstructNeRF2NeRF also scores the lowest CLIP similarity (0.2989), indicating weaker text alignment. Against Tensor4D [57], Control4D reduces FID from 155.6 to 18.59 in dynamic scenes (an 8.4× improvement) and from 118.7 to 14.11 in static scenes, confirming that the GaussianPlanes representation plus GAN generation is far more effective than the raw 4D plane decomposition. Compared to the 2D-only baselines (ControlNet and InstructPix2Pix), Control4D achieves comparable or slightly better CLIP similarity while additionally producing 3D/4D-consistent outputs, whereas the 2D methods cannot report FID since they do not generate multi-view-consistent renderings. The closest competitor is Tensor4D+GAN, which narrows the FID gap (27.81 vs. 14.11 static; 47.39 vs. 18.59 dynamic) but still lags by roughly 2×, and it slightly edges Control4D on CLIP similarity in the static setting (0.3334 vs. 0.3323), suggesting a minor trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and text alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Control4D extends the InstructNeRF2NeRF paradigm of iterative dataset-update editing from 3D NeRFs to 4D dynamic scenes, but replaces the implicit NeRF representation with a structured Gaussian Splatting variant (GaussianPlanes) that borrows tri-plane decomposition from EG3D and 4D plane-based decomposition from Tensor4D. The key departure from prior diffusion-based 3D/4D editing is the introduction of a GAN-based 4D generator that learns a continuous generation space from the inherently inconsistent outputs of a 2D diffusion editor (ControlNet + Stable Diffusion), rather than directly supervising the 4D representation with those inconsistent images. This positions the work at the intersection of efficient explicit 4D representations and generative adversarial distillation of diffusion priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely underexplored task—text-driven 4D portrait editing—and provides a practical pipeline that significantly reduces training time relative to NeRF-based alternatives while achieving strong FID improvements (18.59 vs. 155.6 for Tensor4D in dynamic scenes). However, the method is constrained by its canonical-space-plus-flow formulation, which struggles with rapid non-rigid motion, and by ControlNet's coarse editing granularity, limiting it to identity-level changes rather than fine-grained expression or action edits. Open questions include whether the GAN-based consistency mechanism generalizes beyond portrait domains, whether the iterative optimization can be reduced or replaced with a single-pass approach, and whether the structured Gaussian representation scales to full-body or multi-person 4D scenes.
