## Problem

In one-shot subject-driven generation, a single example image cannot provide all the attributes needed for imaginative customizations (e.g., making a dog run or a candle burn), and existing methods rely on the pre-trained model to fill in missing attributes, which fails for attribute-related prompts. The paper identifies that a subject is not an isolated concept but a specialization of a semantic category, and that existing methods do not explicitly model this inheritance relationship.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241066/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB example image of a subject and a text prompt containing a subject token {S*} (e.g., 'photo of a running {S*}'), and produces generated images of that subject exhibiting both its private identity and category-level public attributes. It operates as a plug-and-play loss term added to existing test-time fine-tuning pipelines (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, ViCo) on Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5, requiring no additional networks or datasets beyond the one example image.

## Method

SuDe models the subject as a derived class of its semantic category by adding a regularization loss that constrains subject-conditioned denoising predictions to semantically belong to the category. The loss is computed by revealing the implicit classifier in the diffusion model via Bayes' theorem, yielding a closed-form expression that compares the subject-conditioned prediction against the category-conditioned and unconditioned predictions (with the latter two gradient-detached). A loss truncation mechanism caps the regularization at a threshold derived from the category's own classification probability, preventing over-optimization that would collapse generation. Theoretically, the combined objective L_sub + w_s·L_sude is shown to model p(x_{t-1} | x_t, c_sub, c_cate), simultaneously conditioning on private attributes (subject identity) and public attributes (category properties), which explains the improved attribute-related generation while preserving subject fidelity.

## Results

On the DreamBench dataset (30 subjects, one-shot), SuDe consistently improves attribute alignment (BLIP-T) across all three baselines and both Stable Diffusion versions. The largest gains appear on ViCo, where BLIP-T improves by +4.2% (SD-v1.4) and +2.6% (SD-v1.5) with default weights, reaching up to +5.3% and +3.9% with flexible w_s. Subject fidelity (DINO-I) remains essentially unchanged (±0.5%), confirming that SuDe does not sacrifice identity preservation. CLIP-T also shows modest improvements (+0.3 to +3.2 points), indicating broader text-alignment benefits beyond attributes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuDe is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to three subject-driven baselines: ViCo, Custom Diffusion, and DreamBooth. On ViCo, SuDe delivers the largest attribute-alignment gains (BLIP-T +4.2% on SD-v1.4, +2.6% on SD-v1.5 with default w_s; up to +5.3% and +3.9% with flexible w_s), while also improving DINO-I fidelity by +3.3 to +3.7 points on SD-v1.4. On Custom Diffusion, gains are more modest (BLIP-T +0.9% to +2.3%), consistent with Custom Diffusion already having stronger attribute handling via its multi-concept design. On DreamBooth, SuDe improves BLIP-T by +1.2% to +3.2% and CLIP-T by +0.5 to +1.5 points, with DINO-I remaining stable (±0.5%). Across all three baselines, CLIP-I fidelity is preserved within ±0.5%, confirming that SuDe does not degrade subject identity. The paper also qualitatively demonstrates that SuDe enables attribute-related generations (e.g., a dog running, a candle burning) that the baselines fail to produce, and that it can be combined with attribute-unrelated prompts for more diverse customizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the test-time fine-tuning paradigm for subject-driven generation (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, ViCo), which learns a subject's identity by optimizing a reconstruction loss on user-provided examples. Its key conceptual departure is an OOP-inspired reframing: the subject is modeled as a 'derived class' of its semantic category (base class), and a new regularization (SuDe) enforces this inheritance by constraining subject-conditioned denoising predictions to remain close to category-conditioned predictions, using the implicit classifier within the diffusion model itself. This contrasts with DreamBooth's class image regularization (L_reg), which merely stabilizes category-conditioned generations without explicitly modeling the subject-to-category affiliation. The theoretical analysis showing that L_sub + L_sude jointly models p(x_{t-1} | x_t, c_sub, c_cate) provides a clean probabilistic justification for the design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in one-shot subject-driven generation where a single example image cannot supply all attributes needed for imaginative prompts (e.g., 'running' for a specific dog), and the plug-and-play SuDe loss offers a low-cost fix that preserves subject fidelity. However, the quantitative gains are modest (1–5% on BLIP-T across baselines and backbones), and the method requires the user to specify the subject's semantic category, which may be ambiguous for novel or composite subjects. Open questions include whether the derived-class modeling extends to multi-subject or cross-category compositions, and whether the implicit-classifier approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion to newer architectures like DiT-based models.
