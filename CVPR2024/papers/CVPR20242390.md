## Problem

Existing MSMO (Multimodal Summarization with Multimodal Output) datasets suffer from insufficient maintenance, data inaccessibility, limited scale, lack of categorization, and absence of verified cross-modal correlations. No publicly available, well-structured dataset simultaneously provides both visual and textual ground-truth summaries with human verification across diverse real-world video categories, leaving a critical gap for benchmarking multimodal summarization methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242390/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video as a sequence of N frames and its transcript as a sequence of M sentences (where M ≠ N in general) as input, and produces per-segment textual summaries and per-segment keyframes as output. The MMSum dataset contains 5,100 untrimmed YouTube videos (1–115 min, avg. 14.5 min) spanning 17 main categories and 170 subcategories, with each video segmented into variable-length segments (avg. ~80 s) that each carry a human-verified keyframe and a short text summary (~22 words). A downstream thumbnail generation task composites the selected keyframe with the generated text using randomly sampled fonts (from 100 Google Fonts) and sizes (175 options).

## Method

The proposed approach consists of two sequential modules. The segmentation module employs a hierarchical BERT architecture with two transformer encoders: a sentence-level encoder (BERT_LARGE) that independently encodes each transcript sentence, and a paragraph-level encoder that receives [CLS] tokens from the first encoder to capture inter-sentence dependencies via cross-attention, producing segment boundaries. The summarization module, following the architecture of Krubiński & Pecina (2023), uses three parallel encoders (frame, video, and text) whose features are fused via multi-head attention; a scoring step selects the most salient keyframe per segment, while a text decoder generates the corresponding summary. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end in a supervised manner on the MMSum dataset, and the paper establishes it as the first publicly available MSMO baseline, outperforming combinations of existing segmentation (LGSS), video summarization (VSUMM), and text summarization (T5, BART, BERT2BERT) components.

## Results

On the MSMO task (Table 5), the proposed method achieves BLEU 33.36, METEOR 30.31, CIDEr 4.06, SPICE 10.28, and BertScore 85.19 for text, and PSNR 36.370, SSIM 0.298, Precision 0.233, Recall 0.275, F1 0.155 for video, outperforming all combination baselines. In the segment-level video summarization setting (Table 3), the method achieves SSIM 0.698 and F1 0.255, compared to VSUMM's SSIM 0.258 and F1 0.171. For segment-level text summarization (Table 4), the method reaches ROUGE-L 13.24 and BertScore 85.19, versus T5's ROUGE-L 5.99 and BertScore 71.95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its end-to-end MSMO method against four pipeline baselines that combine LGSS (segmentation), VSUMM (video summarization), and different text summarizers (T5, BART-large-XSUM, BERT2BERT, BART-large-CNN). The proposed method wins on every metric across both modalities. The largest gains are in BertScore (85.19 vs. 39.20–64.44 for baselines) and PSNR (36.370 vs. 16.234 for all baselines), indicating that the joint segmentation-and-summarization architecture substantially improves both text quality and keyframe selection. Among the baselines, LGSS+VSUMM+T5 is the strongest on BLEU (27.35) and BertScore (62.77), while LGSS+VSUMM+BART-large-CNN leads on METEOR (28.61) and SPICE (9.84). All four baselines share identical video metrics (PSNR 16.234, SSIM 0.198, F1 0.147) because they use the same VSUMM video summarizer, confirming that the video-side improvement in the proposed method comes from its integrated segmentation module rather than a different video encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key architectural components: the hierarchical BERT segmentation approach of Lukasik et al. (EMNLP 2020) for temporal video segmentation, and the multimodal encoder-fusion-decoder architecture of Krubiński & Pecina (Findings 2023) for the summarization module. Conceptually, it extends the MSMO task formulation introduced by Zhu et al. (2018) by addressing the critical data gap: prior MSMO datasets (VMSMO, MM-AVS) suffered from data unavailability, lack of categorization, and limited scale, making systematic benchmarking impossible. The paper positions itself as a resource contribution rather than a methodological departure, deliberately assembling a large-scale, categorized, human-verified dataset to enable fair comparison across video summarization, text summarization, and MSMO tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMSum dataset fills a genuine gap by providing the first publicly available, well-categorized MSMO benchmark (5,100 videos, 170 subcategories), enabling reproducible evaluation in a field where prior datasets were inaccessible or incomplete. The finding that segmentation accuracy is a critical prerequisite for MSMO quality—evidenced by the large performance gap between the integrated pipeline and component-wise combinations—highlights an underappreciated dependency in the task. Open questions remain: the absence of publicly available MSMO baselines limits the benchmark's immediate utility, the thumbnail generation approach is rudimentary (random font/position), and no quantitative evaluation metric for thumbnail quality is proposed. The dataset's YouTube-sourced, untrimmed nature (up to 115 minutes) makes it more representative of real-world video than prior short-clip datasets, but also introduces domain-specific biases.
