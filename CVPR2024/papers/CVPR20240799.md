## Problem

Existing monocular depth estimation models like MiDaS are limited by the data coverage of their labeled training sets, leading to poor generalization on unseen scenes. While large-scale unlabeled images are cheap, diverse, and easy to annotate with a pre-trained MDE model, naively combining them with labeled data in a self-training pipeline fails to improve performance when labeled data is already sufficient. The paper addresses how to effectively exploit ~62M unlabeled images to build a robust zero-shot depth estimation foundation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240799/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a relative (affine-invariant) depth map in disparity space (d=1/t), normalized to [0,1] per image. Three model scales are provided (ViT-S 24.8M, ViT-B 97.5M, ViT-L 335.3M parameters) for different computational budgets. When fine-tuned with metric depth supervision from NYUv2 or KITTI, the same encoder-decoder architecture outputs metric depth maps suitable for downstream 3D tasks.

## Method

The pipeline is two-stage: first, a teacher model (DINOv2-initialized ViT encoder + DPT decoder) is trained on 1.5M labeled images with an affine-invariant loss; second, the teacher pseudo-labels 62M unlabeled images, and a re-initialized student is jointly trained on both sets. The key insight is that simply adding pseudo-labeled images does not help; instead, strong perturbations (color jittering, Gaussian blurring, and CutMix with region-wise affine-invariant loss) are applied to unlabeled images during student training, forcing the model to seek invariant representations. Additionally, a feature alignment loss with a tolerance margin (α=0.85) aligns the student's intermediate features to a frozen DINOv2 encoder, preserving rich semantic priors without the information loss of discrete segmentation labels. This yields an encoder that excels at both depth estimation and semantic segmentation as a multi-task backbone.

## Results

On zero-shot relative depth estimation (Table 2), Depth Anything (ViT-L) outperforms MiDaS v3.1 (ViT-L) across all six unseen datasets, e.g., reducing AbsRel from 0.127→0.076 on KITTI and 0.587→0.458 on Sintel, while improving δ1 from 0.850→0.947 and 0.699→0.760 respectively. After fine-tuning with metric depth, the model sets new SOTAs: δ1 of 0.984 and AbsRel of 0.056 on NYUv2 (vs. VPD's 0.964/0.069), and δ1 of 0.982 and AbsRel of 0.046 on KITTI (vs. NDDepth's 0.978/0.050). The produced encoder also achieves 86.2 mIoU on Cityscapes and 59.4 mIoU on ADE20K for semantic segmentation, surpassing ImageNet-pretrained Swin-L and ConvNeXt-XL encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MiDaS v3.1 (the primary zero-shot baseline), Depth Anything wins on all six evaluation datasets for both AbsRel and δ₁, with the largest gains on Sintel (AbsRel 0.587→0.458) and KITTI (0.127→0.076); notably, even the ViT-S variant (24.8M params, <1/10 the size of MiDaS's ViT-L) beats MiDaS on Sintel, DDAD, and ETH3D. For fine-tuned metric depth on NYUv2, Depth Anything surpasses the previous best VPD (δ₁ 0.984 vs. 0.964, AbsRel 0.056 vs. 0.069) and all other baselines including AdaBins, DPT, P3Depth, SwinV2-L, AiT, and ZoeDepth. On KITTI, it edges out NDDepth (δ₁ 0.982 vs. 0.978, AbsRel 0.046 vs. 0.050) and GEDepth (0.048). In zero-shot metric depth (Table 5), replacing ZoeDepth's MiDaS encoder with Depth Anything's encoder improves AbsRel on all five test sets (e.g., SUN RGB-D 0.520→0.500, DIODE Outdoor 0.814→0.794). For semantic segmentation transfer, the MDE-trained encoder outperforms ImageNet-pretrained Swin-L (84.3) and ConvNeXt-XL (84.6) on Cityscapes (86.2 mIoU) and ViT-Adapter (58.3) on ADE20K (59.4 mIoU), and also beats the original DINOv2 encoder on all downstream tasks (Table 11).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MiDaS framework for zero-shot relative depth estimation by replacing its reliance on diverse labeled datasets with a data engine that scales to 62M unlabeled images, annotated by a teacher model. It departs from prior semi-supervised depth work by showing that naive pseudo-labeling fails when labeled data is already sufficient, and instead introduces strong perturbations (CutMix, color distortions) to create a harder optimization target. For semantic assistance, it replaces the conventional auxiliary segmentation task with a feature-alignment loss against frozen DINOv2 features, motivated by the observation that discrete class-space decoding loses too much information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that cheap, diverse unlabeled images can substantially improve zero-shot depth generalization, offering a practical recipe for building MDE foundation models without expensive sensor data. The negative result—that naive self-training provides no gain when labeled data is sufficient—challenges the common assumption that more pseudo-labeled data always helps and provides a useful design principle for semi-supervised learning in data-rich regimes. The resulting encoder's dual strength in depth estimation and semantic segmentation suggests a path toward unified multi-task visual backbones. Open questions include whether the specific perturbation choices (CutMix, color jitter) are optimal, whether the approach extends to other dense prediction tasks, and how much further performance can improve with even larger unlabeled pools.
