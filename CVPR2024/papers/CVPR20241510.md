## Problem

In Visual Place Recognition, highly confident but incorrect retrievals can cause catastrophic failures in downstream localization pipelines, yet little attention has been given to quantifying the confidence that a retrieved reference is a correct match. Existing uncertainty estimation approaches—retrieval-based distances, data-driven aleatoric uncertainty, and geometric verification—have never been compared on a levelled playing field, and none exploit the spatial locations of reference images in the map, which the authors hypothesize is a critical source of information for detecting perceptual aliasing.

## Input / Output

Given a query image and a pre-built VPR map of 2048-dimensional reference descriptors (from ResNet-50 with GeM pooling) each paired with a 2D or 3D pose, the method produces a scalar uncertainty score s_q for the best-matched reference. The score is designed to be thresholded: queries with s_q > τ are rejected as potentially incorrect matches. Unlike data-driven methods that require retraining the encoder, SUE operates purely at inference time using only the already-computed descriptors and the freely available reference poses, with a runtime of ~1 ms per query.

## Method

The proposed Spatial Uncertainty Estimation (SUE) fits a weighted multivariate Gaussian over the 2D/3D poses of the K nearest-neighbor references, where each pose's weight decays exponentially with its L2 feature-space distance to the query (w_(i) = exp(−λ·d_(i))). The trace of the resulting covariance matrix serves as the uncertainty score: a large spatial spread among top-K matches indicates perceptual aliasing (high uncertainty), while a tight cluster indicates a distinctive location (low uncertainty). The two hyperparameters (K=10, λ=350) are tuned once on Pittsburgh and generalize across six benchmark datasets. The paper further demonstrates that SUE's uncertainty estimates are complementary to geometric verification (DELF-RANSAC), with a linear SVM combining both scores improving binary classification accuracy over either alone, and that SUE is ~3 orders of magnitude faster than GV methods.

## Results

On the AUC-PR metric across six VPR datasets, SUE achieves an average of 0.77, outperforming all efficient uncertainty estimation methods: L2-distance (0.69), PA-Score (0.68), STUN (0.54), and BTL (0.28). Among the computationally expensive geometric verification methods, DELF-RANSAC achieves the highest average AUC-PR of 0.93, followed by SuperPoint-RANSAC (0.87) and SIFT-RANSAC (0.76). SUE's inference time of 1.08 ms is roughly three orders of magnitude faster than DELF-RANSAC (1587 ms), making it practical for real-time deployment while still approaching GV-level uncertainty quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUE clearly dominates the retrieval-based baselines: it outperforms L2-distance (0.77 vs 0.69 avg AUC-PR) and PA-Score (0.77 vs 0.68) on every dataset except where both are near-saturated. Against the data-driven aleatoric uncertainty methods, SUE's advantage is stark — it beats STUN by 0.23 in average AUC-PR (0.77 vs 0.54) and BTL by 0.49 (0.77 vs 0.28), revealing that learned uncertainty from training data does not generalize to the test-time reference map. Among geometric verification methods, SUE slightly edges out SIFT-RANSAC on average (0.77 vs 0.76) but trails DELF-RANSAC (0.93) and SuperPoint-RANSAC (0.87) by 0.16 and 0.10 respectively, with the gap most pronounced on Nordland (0.26 vs 0.84 for DELF) where seasonal appearance changes challenge all methods. The paper further shows that combining SUE with DELF-RANSAC via a linear SVM improves binary classification accuracy over DELF alone (e.g., 89.6% vs 86.6% on Sanfrancisco), confirming that SUE captures complementary information not available from local feature matching alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first systematic, levelled comparison of the three existing categories of VPR uncertainty estimation (retrieval-based, data-driven aleatoric, and geometric verification), which prior evaluations had not jointly assessed. It repurposes the concept of pose-hypothesis distribution—previously studied for pose uncertainty in works like [17, 36]—as a proxy for image-matching uncertainty, yielding the new SUE baseline that uniquely exploits the spatial spread of top-K reference poses. The paper departs from the DUE line of work (BTL, STUN) by arguing that aleatoric uncertainty learned from training data does not capture the test-time reference map's perceptual aliasing structure, a source of information no prior category uses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a trivial L2-distance in feature space outperforms purpose-built learned uncertainty estimators (BTL, STUN) across six datasets challenges the assumption that data-driven uncertainty is inherently superior, and SUE provides a cheap, effective baseline that future VPR uncertainty work must include. The demonstrated complementarity between SUE and geometric verification offers a practical recipe for combining fast and slow confidence signals in real-time localization pipelines. Open questions remain around SUE's sensitivity to uneven reference coverage and scene-depth variation, and whether learned uncertainty can be made to incorporate test-time map structure rather than relying solely on training-time image content.
