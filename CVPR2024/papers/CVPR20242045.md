## Problem

Unsupervised object detection methods like CutLER generate pseudo-labels from a single self-supervised model, limiting mask quality, and restrict self-training to the original training domain. Additionally, prior approaches lack per-instance confidence scores, making it difficult to weight pseudo-labels during detector training. This paper addresses both limitations by ensembling multiple self-supervised ViTs and introducing a cross-domain self-training strategy.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images resized to 480×480 (DINO) or 476×476 (DINOv2) and produces class-agnostic bounding boxes, pixel-level segmentation masks, and a per-instance confidence score in [0,1] derived from the voting consensus ratio. The final detector is a Cascade Mask R-CNN operating in class-agnostic mode, so outputs carry no semantic class labels. During self-training, predictions with confidence below 0.2 are filtered before retraining.

## Method

VoteCut extracts patch-level features from an ensemble of DINO and DINOv2 ViTs with varying patch sizes, computes cosine-similarity affinity matrices, and applies Normalized Cuts to obtain the second-smallest eigenvector per model. 1D K-means clustering over these eigenvectors (with K from 2 to k_max) yields multiple binary mask proposals per image, which are then grouped via greedy IoU-based clustering (threshold 0.6) and refined by pixel-level majority voting (threshold τ^m=0.2) followed by CRF post-processing. Each final mask receives a confidence score equal to its cluster size divided by the maximum cluster size among same-size clusters. CuVLER trains a Cascade Mask R-CNN on these pseudo-labels using a novel instance-level soft-target loss that scales box, mask, and classification losses by the confidence score, combined with a DropLoss-style IoU gating (τ^IoU=0.01). A subsequent self-training stage applies the detector to a target domain, filters low-confidence predictions, and retrains with updated scores, enabling cross-domain adaptation after a single epoch.

## Results

In the in-domain ImageNet evaluation, VoteCut (no CAD) achieves 20.9 AP and 45.0 AR100, substantially outperforming TokenCut (14.4 AP) and MaskCut (10.6 AP). With CAD training, CuVLER reaches 33.2 AP and 59.0 AR100, surpassing CutLER (29.2 AP, 56.7 AR100). In zero-shot evaluation across seven benchmarks, CuVLER improves over the previous SOTA by up to 20% (e.g., +3.8 AP50 on Watercolor, +2.5 AP50 on VOC). In the self-training setup on COCO val2017, CuVLER achieves 20.4 AP50mask and 10.4 APmask, outperforming CutLER (18.9 / 9.7) by up to ~10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TokenCut [34], VoteCut improves AP from 14.4 to 20.9 (+45%) and AR100 from 26.9 to 45.0 (+67%) in the no-CAD in-domain setting. Against MaskCut [33], the improvement is even more pronounced: AP rises from 10.6 to 20.9 (+97%). In the with-CAD setting, CuVLER outperforms CutLER [33] by 4% in AP (33.2 vs 29.2) and 4% in AR100 (59.0 vs 56.7). In the self-training evaluation on COCO 20K, CuVLER achieves 21.6 AP50mask versus LOST [29] at 2.4, MaskDistill [31] at 6.8, and FreeSOLO [32] at 9.7, representing 2–9× gains in segmentation quality. In zero-shot cross-domain evaluation, CuVLER beats the previous SOTA (CutLER) on 6 of 7 benchmarks, with the only exception being a marginal −0.3 AP50 drop on Clipart1K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CutLER pipeline (Wang et al., 2023), which generates pseudo-labels from a single self-supervised model and trains a detector on them, by replacing the single-model MaskCut stage with VoteCut—an ensemble of multiple DINO/DINOv2 models with varying patch sizes whose NCut eigenvectors are combined via IoU-based clustering and pixel voting. It also inherits the NCut eigenvector extraction procedure from TokenCut (Wang et al., 2022) and the DropLoss-inspired IoU gating from CutLER. The key conceptual departures are the introduction of per-mask confidence scores, a soft-target instance-level loss that weights pseudo-labels by their consensus score, and a cross-domain self-training loop that operates in the target domain rather than being restricted to the source domain as in CutLER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that ensembling self-supervised ViTs with heterogeneous patch sizes yields substantially better unsupervised object discovery than any single model, and that a soft-target loss can exploit the full distribution of pseudo-label quality rather than discarding low-confidence proposals. The cross-domain self-training after a single epoch offers a practical path to domain adaptation without extensive annotation. Open questions include the computational cost of running multiple ViTs at inference time for pseudo-label generation, the potential bias introduced by relying on ImageNet as the source domain, and whether the ensemble benefit saturates or continues to grow with additional models.
