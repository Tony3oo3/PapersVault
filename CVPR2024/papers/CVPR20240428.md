## Problem

Prior work on hierarchical semantic segmentation (e.g., HSSN) reports accuracy gains from modeling parent classes alongside leaf classes, but it is unclear whether these gains stem from the semantic hierarchy itself or from other factors. This paper shows that the reported improvements do not generalize across domains and that a flat classifier (modeling only leaf nodes) consistently outperforms hierarchical approaches on unseen test sets. Additionally, flat Euclidean classifiers suffer from a 'parent bias'—non-uniform separation margins between class embeddings—that degrades parent-level accuracy and calibration.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and produces per-pixel segmentation masks at two hierarchical levels: 19 leaf (child) classes and 7 parent (meta) classes, along with per-pixel confidence maps. Parent-level predictions are not independently modeled but are inferred bottom-up by summing the child-class posteriors of each parent's children (Eq. 1), making the parent output a deterministic function of the child output. The hyperbolic variant additionally yields spatially smoother confidence maps compared to its Euclidean counterpart.

## Method

The paper replaces the Euclidean embedding space with the Poincaré ball model for per-pixel classification. Pixel features from a CNN backbone (DeepLabV3+/ResNet-101 or OCRNet/HRNet-W48) are projected onto the Poincaré ball via the exponential map, and classification is performed using gyroplanes (hyperbolic decision boundaries) with hyperbolic multinomial logistic regression. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 2) is that hyperbolic distance is strictly concave in Euclidean distance, which naturally enforces approximately equidistant class embeddings at the ball's periphery, thereby flattening the parent bias without additional regularization. Training uses Riemannian SGD for gyroplane offsets and standard Euclidean SGD for normals, with curvature set to c=1. The flat hyperbolic classifier (Flat-Hyp) matches or exceeds the flat Euclidean baseline in segmentation accuracy and substantially improves parent-level calibration, especially on high-domain-shift datasets.

## Results

On the most challenging cross-domain benchmarks (BDD, Wilddash), the flat hyperbolic model (Flat-Hyp) achieves the best parent-level (Level 1) mIoU of 76.49 and 62.53 respectively, outperforming the flat Euclidean baseline by +0.22/+3.55 and HSSN by +2.21/+3.93. For calibration quality (cwECE) on parent nodes, Flat-Hyp reduces the error by 5.43 points over HSSN on Wilddash and 2.27 on BDD. On child-level (Level 0) segmentation, both flat classifiers substantially outperform HSSN across all five cross-domain datasets in mIoU, mAcc, and aAcc, confirming that hierarchical training does not transfer well beyond the training domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is HSSN [Li et al., CVPR 2022], the state-of-the-art hierarchical semantic segmentation method. On the in-domain Cityscapes test set, all three methods (HSSN, Flat-Euc, Flat-Hyp) achieve comparable parent-level mIoU (~90.7–91.0), confirming that hierarchical training offers no in-domain advantage. However, on all five cross-domain benchmarks, both flat classifiers substantially outperform HSSN on child-level mIoU (e.g., +4.6 on Mapillary, +6.4 on IDD, +1.6 on ACDC, +3.8 on BDD, +2.3 on Wilddash for Flat-Euc). The key differentiator is at the parent level: Flat-Hyp clearly surpasses both Flat-Euc and HSSN on the most challenging datasets (BDD: +0.22 mIoU over Flat-Euc, +2.21 over HSSN; Wilddash: +3.55 over Flat-Euc, +3.93 over HSSN), while Flat-Euc retains a slight edge on less challenging domains (Cityscapes, Mapillary, IDD, ACDC). For calibration (cwECE) on parent nodes, Flat-Hyp is the best-calibrated method on four of six datasets, with the largest gaps on Wilddash (−5.43 vs. HSSN) and BDD (−2.27 vs. HSSN), demonstrating that the hyperbolic representation's uniform separation margins translate into more reliable confidence estimates under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hierarchical semantic segmentation framework of HSSN (Li et al., CVPR 2022) and the hyperbolic neural network formulation of Ganea et al. (NeurIPS 2018), combined with the per-pixel Poincaré ball segmentation of Atigh et al. (CVPR 2022). However, it fundamentally departs from the HSSN line by arguing that the reported benefits of hierarchical training are not attributable to semantic hierarchy at all, and instead attributes the parent-level advantage to the geometric properties of the embedding space. The paper repurposes the Poincaré ball not as a hierarchical embedding tool (as in Poincaré embeddings for taxonomies) but as a mechanism to enforce uniform inter-class separation margins in a flat classification setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that flat classifiers with hyperbolic embeddings outperform dedicated hierarchical training across six cross-domain benchmarks challenges the established motivation for hierarchical segmentation and suggests practitioners should reconsider the added complexity of parent-level classifiers. The first empirical analysis of dense calibration in hyperbolic networks opens a new evaluation axis for this underexplored area. Limitations include evaluation restricted to traffic-scene datasets with a single two-level taxonomy, and the analysis is confined to the Poincaré ball model; extending to other conformal models (e.g., Lorentz) and to deeper or non-tree hierarchies remains open.
