## Problem

Radiance field reconstruction of large-scale dynamic urban environments from multiple vehicle captures remains unsolved: prior methods either handle only static scenes, scale to at most a single short video, or fail to accurately represent and distinguish individual dynamic object instances. The paper addresses the need for a decomposable, scalable representation that spans dozens of sequences with hundreds of fast-moving objects under varying lighting, weather, and seasonal conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242490/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 7-camera surround-view image sequences from dozens of vehicles (global shutter cameras), LiDAR-derived 3D bounding box tracks per dynamic object, ego-vehicle SE(3) poses, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and LiDAR point clouds for scene normalization and ICP alignment. It outputs a multi-level neural scene graph whose latent codes condition two radiance fields, enabling novel view synthesis and depth map generation at arbitrary viewpoints and times. A key structural detail is that object poses are defined relative to the ego-vehicle frame (not the world frame), so refining vehicle poses automatically propagates to all contained object poses through the graph hierarchy.

## Method

The scene is decomposed into a multi-level graph with a root node (world frame), sequence nodes (one per timestamp per vehicle, holding appearance and transient-geometry latent codes via Fourier features), dynamic object nodes (holding per-instance latent codes), and camera nodes. Two radiance fields are used: a static field φ implemented as a multi-scale 3D hash grid with MLP heads conditioned on sequence latents, and a dynamic field ψ implemented as an MLP with positional encoding conditioned on both sequence and object latents, enabling cross-sequence object transfer. A composite ray sampling scheme extends Mip-NeRF 360's proposal network by jointly sampling from the distilled static density and the dynamic field (constrained to bounding-box entry/exit intervals), achieving ~12× speedup over per-node sampling. Training uses photometric, expected-depth, proposal-distillation, and entropy-regularization losses, with hierarchical pose optimization that refines ego-vehicle and object poses through graph edges while keeping multi-camera extrinsics fixed.

## Results

On the proposed Argoverse 2 benchmark (residential + downtown), the method achieves a mean PSNR of 21.15 dB, SSIM of 0.680, and LPIPS of 0.555, outperforming SUDS (20.84 / 0.662 / 0.601) while training 3× faster (17.2 h vs. 54.8 h). On KITTI with 75% training views, the method reaches 28.38 dB PSNR and 0.907 SSIM, surpassing the next-best SUDS (22.77 / 0.797) by over 5 dB. On VKITTI2 at 75%, it achieves 29.73 dB PSNR and 0.912 SSIM, again leading all baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SUDS [58], the closest prior dynamic-scene method, the proposed approach wins on all three metrics (PSNR +0.31, SSIM +0.018, LPIPS −0.046 mean) while training 3.2× faster (17.2 h vs. 54.8 h). On established benchmarks, the margins are much larger: on KITTI (75% views) the method achieves 28.38 dB PSNR vs. SUDS's 22.77 dB and NSG's 21.53 dB, a gap of 5.6 dB over the runner-up. On VKITTI2 (75%), it reaches 29.73 dB PSNR vs. SUDS's 23.87 dB and NSG's 23.41 dB. The Nerfacto-based baselines (static and with time) are consistently the weakest, confirming that simply adding temporal embeddings to a static NeRF is insufficient for multi-sequence dynamic urban scenes. On the image-reconstruction task (Table 3, KITTI), the method also leads PNF [18] (29.36 vs. 27.48 PSNR) and SRN [49] (29.36 vs. 18.83 PSNR), indicating superior fitting of training views as well as generalization to novel views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural scene graph formulation of Ost et al. [35] from single short video clips to a multi-level, multi-sequence graph spanning large geographic areas with hundreds of dynamic objects, drawing on the hierarchical scene graph concept from 3D mapping (Armeni et al. [1], Rosinol et al. [44]). The static radiance field leverages the 3D hash grid of Müller et al. [32] and the proposal sampling of Mip-NeRF 360 [2], while the dynamic object field follows the NeRF-style MLP with positional encoding [31] and the log-scale sinusoidal time basis of SUDS [58]. The key conceptual departure is the composite ray sampling scheme that jointly queries a distilled static density and per-object dynamic densities within a single sampling pass, and the use of per-sequence latent codes to disentangle appearance and transient geometry variation across captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: existing dynamic NeRF methods cannot scale to the multi-sequence, multi-condition captures needed for city-scale digital twins and autonomous driving simulation. The new Argoverse 2-based benchmark (37 captures, 27K+ images, 1300+ dynamic objects) provides a realistic evaluation that prior single-clip benchmarks lacked. Open questions include whether the approach can operate without per-object 3D bounding box tracks (currently a required input), whether it generalizes to real-time rendering for closed-loop simulation, and how it handles more complex dynamics like pedestrians or deformable objects. The hierarchical pose optimization, which exploits multi-camera constraints through the graph structure, is a reusable technique for any multi-sensor radiance field pipeline.
