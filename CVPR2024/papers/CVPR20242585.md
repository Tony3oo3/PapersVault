## Problem

Existing fusion-based few-shot image generation methods rely on similarity measurements or attention mechanisms to fuse features, which perform point-wise matching that is inaccurate under limited data and produces artifacts in texture and structure. The paper asks whether fusing features via distribution-level matching (empirical CDFs) rather than point estimates can yield more consistent and diverse generations from only a handful of samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes k RGB images (k ∈ {2,3,5,7,9}) from a single unseen category at 128×128 (Flowers, Animal Faces) or 64×64 (VGGFaces) and produces new images of the same category. During training, the model is updated episodically over hundreds of k-shot tasks sampled from seen categories; at test time it generates images for unseen categories without further optimization. Generated images are also fed as data augmentation into a ResNet-18 classifier to evaluate downstream classification accuracy.

## Method

F2DGAN is a GAN whose generator receives fused features from two complementary modules. The Feature Distribution Matching Module (FDMM) flattens feature maps at multiple scales, sorts them, and applies a sort-matching algorithm to map one feature vector's empirical CDF onto the category-average CDF, with a stop-gradient trick to preserve gradient flow through the non-parametric matching; an image-matching reconstruction loss (L_ir) enforces channel-wise MSE between the selected input and the category mean. The Variational Feature Learning Module (VFLM) encodes the category-average deep feature into a Gaussian (μ, σ) via a lightweight VAE, samples a variational feature z, and fuses it with the selected feature through the same histogram-matching operation; a feature reconstruction loss (L_fr) combines L2 reconstruction, KL divergence, and an orthogonal projection loss that clusters same-class semantics while separating different classes. The full model is trained end-to-end with episodic adversarial training (hinge loss), an auxiliary classification loss, and the two proposed losses, using a ResNet-style feature extractor and a symmetric generator with skip connections.

## Results

On the 3-shot few-shot image generation task, F2DGAN achieves FID scores of 38.26 (Flowers), 25.24 (Animal Faces), and 4.25 (VGGFaces), outperforming all fusion-based baselines on both FID and LPIPS across all three datasets. In terms of diversity (LPIPS), F2DGAN scores 0.4325, 0.5463, and 0.3521 respectively, surpassing the strongest fusion-based competitor WaveGAN* (0.3868, 0.5076, 0.3255). As a data augmentation technique for low-data classification, F2DGAN-generated images yield the highest top-1 accuracy on all three benchmarks: 84.02% (Flowers), 60.76% (Animal Faces), and 77.92% (VGGFaces), exceeding the next-best method WaveGAN by 4.78, 5.49, and 5.52 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F2DGAN outperforms all five fusion-based baselines (FIGR, DAWSON, MatchingGAN, F2GAN, LoFGAN*, WaveGAN*) on both FID and LPIPS across all three datasets, with the largest FID gap over WaveGAN* being 3.91 on Flowers and 0.71 on VGGFaces. Against transformation-based methods, F2DGAN achieves a lower FID than AGE and HAE* on all datasets but is slightly outperformed by LSO on Animal Faces (25.24 vs. 23.67) and VGGFaces (4.25 vs. 3.98). On diversity (LPIPS), F2DGAN trails HAE* on all three datasets (e.g., 0.4325 vs. 0.4739 on Flowers) and AGE on Animal Faces (0.5463 vs. 0.5575), though it leads LSO and WaveGAN* on diversity. The critical differentiator emerges in downstream classification: F2DGAN-generated images achieve 84.02% / 60.76% / 77.92% top-1 accuracy on Flowers / Animal Faces / VGGFaces, surpassing WaveGAN (79.24 / 55.27 / 72.40) by 4.8–5.5 points and transformation-based methods (AGE, HAE) by 10–14 points, demonstrating that F2DGAN preserves category-specific information far more effectively than transformation-based approaches despite their marginally better FID or LPIPS scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant fusion-based few-shot generation paradigm (LoFGAN, WaveGAN, F2GAN) that relies on attention or similarity-based point-estimate feature matching, instead adopting distribution-level matching via empirical CDFs and the sort-matching algorithm originally developed for exact style transfer (EFDM [49]). It further introduces a variational autoencoder to transform sparse deep features into class-level distributions before matching, addressing the problem that a handful of feature values cannot reliably form histograms. The combination of VAE-based variational feature learning with exact histogram matching for few-shot generation is, to the authors' knowledge, novel in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that treating feature fusion as a distribution-matching problem rather than a point-matching problem yields consistent gains in both generation quality (FID) and downstream classification accuracy (4–5 points over WaveGAN across three datasets), suggesting that statistical character of category features matters more than individual feature alignment in data-scarce regimes. However, the evaluation is limited to three small-scale datasets at 64–128 px resolution, and the method is tightly coupled to a specific GAN architecture with episodic training, leaving open whether the distribution-matching principle transfers to diffusion-based generators or higher-resolution settings. A key open question is whether the VAE's role in smoothing feature distributions could be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives without sacrificing the diversity gains.
