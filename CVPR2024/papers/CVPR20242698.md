## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models excel at generating concrete concepts but fail to produce images that faithfully evoke abstract emotions. Prior image emotion transfer methods adjust only colors and styles over fixed content, achieving merely 29% emotion accuracy, because psychological studies show that visual emotions are primarily triggered by specific semantic contents rather than low-level visual features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single emotion category (one of eight: amusement, awe, contentment, excitement, anger, disgust, fear, sadness) as input and produces a photorealistic RGB image whose content is semantically unambiguous, emotionally faithful to the target category, and diverse across generations. At inference, a random sample is drawn from a per-emotion Gaussian distribution in the learned emotion space, so repeated queries for the same emotion yield different semantic contents (e.g., lakes, oceans, and snow-covered mountains for awe).

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. Stage 1 trains a ResNet-50 emotion encoder with cross-entropy loss on EmoSet to build an emotion space where similar emotions cluster and dissimilar ones separate; each emotion is then represented as a Gaussian distribution for diverse sampling. Stage 2 maps this emotion space into CLIP space via a learned MLP with ReLU non-linearities (while keeping the CLIP text transformer frozen), so that clustered emotion points disperse into diverse semantic regions. The generation is optimized with a combined loss: an attribute loss (symmetric cross-entropy over cosine similarities to object-class and scene-type text embeddings in CLIP space) enforces semantic clarity and diversity, and an emotion-confidence coefficient α_ij—precomputed by averaging a pre-trained emotion classifier's predictions over all images of each attribute—dynamically weights the LDM reconstruction loss against the attribute loss, prioritizing semantic guidance for emotion-specific attributes and pixel-level fidelity for neutral ones.

## Results

On the EmoSet-based EICG task, EmoGen achieves the best scores across all five evaluation metrics: FID 41.60, LPIPS 0.717, Emotion Accuracy (Emo-A) 76.25%, Semantic Clarity (Sem-C) 0.633, and Semantic Diversity (Sem-D) 0.0335. The largest relative gains over baselines appear on Sem-D (up to 88% higher than DreamBooth) and FID (up to 17% lower than Textual Inversion), indicating substantially more diverse and higher-fidelity emotional image generation. A user study with 14 participants further confirms superiority, with 82.14% human-verified emotion accuracy and 88.39% of responses attributing the evoked emotion to image content/semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoGen outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against Stable Diffusion (the general-purpose pipeline), EmoGen improves FID by 2.45 points (41.60 vs. 44.05), raises Emo-A by 5.5 pp (76.25% vs. 70.77%), and nearly doubles Sem-D (0.0335 vs. 0.0199), showing that off-the-shelf diffusion models collapse emotions into narrow, low-diversity semantics. Against Textual Inversion, the closest competitor on Emo-A (74.87%), EmoGen still leads by 1.4 pp while achieving a markedly lower FID (41.60 vs. 50.51) and higher Sem-D (0.0335 vs. 0.0282), indicating that learning a single embedding token for an abstract emotion is insufficient. Against DreamBooth, EmoGen gains the largest margins on Sem-D (0.0335 vs. 0.0178, +88%) and LPIPS (0.717 vs. 0.661), confirming that full-parameter fine-tuning for subject-driven generation is poorly suited to the open-ended, multi-semantic nature of emotions. Qualitatively, baselines tend to produce dense-texture, dim-color images or emotion-agnostic objects (shoes, cars, trees), whereas EmoGen generates diverse, semantically explicit content (lakes, flags, snow-covered mountains) that aligns with human emotional cognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) as its generation backbone and CLIP as the semantic embedding space, but departs from both standard text-to-image generation and prior image emotion transfer work. Where emotion transfer methods (e.g., Weng et al., Sun et al.) modify color and style of a fixed image, and where standard T2I models (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) require concrete textual prompts, EmoGen introduces an intermediate 'emotion space' learned via a ResNet-50 encoder trained with cross-entropy on EmoSet, then maps it to CLIP space through a non-linear MLP. The key conceptual departure is treating emotion as a generative condition rather than a post-hoc modification, and using attribute loss plus emotion confidence to prevent the LDM loss from collapsing diverse emotional semantics into a single point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes EICG as a distinct task with purpose-built evaluation metrics (Emo-A, Sem-C, Sem-D) that go beyond standard FID/LPIPS, and the user study finding that 88.39% of participants attribute their emotional response to image content rather than color or style provides empirical grounding for the content-centric approach. However, the method's limitations are notable: it treats the emotion-content relationship as binary (emotional vs. neutral), ignores color and style as emotion carriers, and the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (76.25% vs. 74.87% Emo-A). Open questions include how to handle multi-valence content (e.g., a rose evoking different emotions by color), whether the Gaussian-sampled emotion representations scale beyond eight categories, and how to integrate low-level visual factors without sacrificing the semantic clarity that drives emotion.
