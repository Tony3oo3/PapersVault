## Problem

Existing neural representation approaches for large-scale LiDAR mapping do not account for dynamic objects, while traditional dynamic-environment mapping methods either require semantic pre-processing, rely on occupancy-based space carving that can erroneously remove static geometry, or depend on labeled training data. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified 4D implicit neural representation that simultaneously reconstructs an accurate static map and segments dynamic objects from a LiDAR sequence, without any semantic labels or pre/post-processing steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N LiDAR point clouds with pre-computed global poses (from SLAM or odometry) as input and is trained offline until convergence. It produces a queryable 4D TSDF F(p,t) at arbitrary spatial-temporal coordinates, from which a static 3D mesh is extracted via marching cubes and per-point dynamic/static segmentation labels are obtained by thresholding the static SDF weight w_p^1. The representation uses a truncated TSDF (τ=0.5 m for driving scenes) and a multi-resolution sparse hash grid with 2 levels and 8-dimensional feature vectors, keeping memory consumption comparable to voxel-based baselines.

## Method

The core architecture encodes spatial geometry in a multi-resolution sparse voxel hash grid (à la Instant-NGP) whose corner features are trilinearly interpolated and decoded by a globally shared 2×64 MLP into K=32 location-dependent weights. These weights multiply K globally shared DCT basis functions φ_k(t) to form the time-varying TSDF F(p,t)=Σ w_p^k φ_k(t); the first basis function is fixed at φ_1(t)=1, so w_p^1 directly encodes the static SDF while the remaining 31 learnable basis functions capture temporal dynamics. Training is unsupervised and uses a piecewise loss: a near-surface loss that constrains only the sign and magnitude bound of the predicted TSDF (since projective distance changes with viewpoint), an Eikonal regularization computed via numerical gradients with a decaying perturbation ε, a free-space L1 loss, and a 'certain free' loss that directly pins the static SDF to τ for points sufficiently far from any measured surface. This design avoids the need for differentiable rendering or nearest-neighbor distance queries, and the shared-basis-function temporal model ensures that static regions remain temporally invariant by construction.

## Results

On the KTH-Dynamic-Benchmark, the method achieves the best associated accuracy (AA) on three of four sequences: 98.97% on KITTI 00, 98.95% on KITTI 05, and 97.53% on Argoverse2, substantially outperforming all baselines. For static mapping quality, the method achieves the best completeness (0.438 cm on ToyCar3, 5.85 cm on Newer College) and Chamfer-L1 distance (0.452 cm and 6.17 cm respectively), with F-scores of 98.35% and 97.50%. On the Newer College dataset, VDB-Fusion incorrectly removes static geometry (trees, ground), inflating its completeness to 7.32 cm, while the proposed method preserves all static structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method clearly outperforms all nine baselines on the KTH-Dynamic-Benchmark's associated accuracy (AA) for the three autonomous driving sequences, with margins of 3.1–43.4 points over the next-best method. VDB-Fusion and Erasor over-segment dynamic objects, dropping static accuracy to 66–77%, while Removert is far too conservative, detecting only 12–42% of dynamic points. SHINE-mapping, the closest neural-representation baseline, retains residual dynamic points on ToyCar3 (accuracy 0.626 cm vs. 0.468 cm) and fails to detect most pedestrians on KITTI 05 (DA = 53.27% vs. 98.36%). NKSR performs worst on accuracy (2.809 cm on ToyCar3) because it cannot eliminate dynamic objects at all. The supervised methods 4DMOS and MapMOS generalize poorly to unseen sequences, achieving DA as low as 4.75% on the Semi-Indoor sequence, whereas the proposed unsupervised method maintains 72.79% DA there without any training labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse hierarchical implicit neural representation for LiDAR mapping (SHINE-mapping, EINRUL) by adding a temporal dimension via globally shared DCT basis functions, a technique borrowed from dynamic NeRF work (DynIBaR, NSFF). It departs from prior dynamic NeRF approaches by using a TSDF rather than a radiance field as the implicit signal, and from traditional dynamic mapping (VDB-Fusion, Octomap, Erasor) by encoding dynamics directly in the map representation rather than filtering them as a pre- or post-processing step. The piecewise near-surface loss and the use of the first (time-independent) basis weight as a direct dynamic-object indicator are novel design choices specific to the TSDF formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified 4D TSDF representation simultaneously solves static map reconstruction and dynamic object segmentation without requiring semantic labels, ground fitting, or post-processing, which is practically valuable for autonomous driving and digital-twin applications. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include dependence on externally estimated poses and the offline-only training paradigm, leaving open the important question of whether the approach can be made incremental or jointly optimized with pose estimation. The sensitivity to per-scan measurement noise (worse accuracy than fusion-based methods on noisy real data) suggests a fundamental trade-off between temporal fidelity and noise robustness that future work must address.
