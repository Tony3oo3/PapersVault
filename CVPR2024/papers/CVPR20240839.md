## Problem

Current 3D human pose and shape (HPS) regression methods face a fundamental trade-off: the better they fit 2D keypoints and pseudo-ground-truth (p-GT), the worse their 3D pose accuracy becomes, due to the mismatch between the approximate scaled-orthographic camera model used in training and the true camera. This paper quantifies this camera/pose bias and asks how to leverage large in-the-wild 2D datasets for generalization without sacrificing 3D accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240839/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and outputs SMPL body pose (θ ∈ R^72), shape (β ∈ R^10), and perspective camera parameters (T), from which a 6890-vertex 3D body mesh is derived. Unusually, the body pose is not regressed as continuous values but as 160 discrete tokens selected from a 2048-entry codebook (each entry 256-dimensional), which are then decoded by a frozen VQ-VAE decoder back into continuous SMPL pose parameters. Global orientation, hand pose, shape, and camera are still predicted directly via linear layers.

## Method

TokenHMR combines two key components on a ViT-H/16 backbone (following HMR2.0). First, Threshold-Adaptive Loss Scaling (TALS) scales down 2D keypoint and p-GT pose losses by a factor of 1% once they fall below empirically determined thresholds (computed from BEDLAM ground-truth projections under the HMR2.0 camera), preventing the network from over-fitting to camera-induced bias while still penalizing gross errors. Second, a VQ-VAE pretrained on AMASS and MOYO motion-capture data discretizes continuous SMPL poses into 160 tokens from a 2048×256 codebook; during HPS training, the transformer outputs per-token logits that are softmaxed and multiplied with the frozen codebook to produce approximate quantized features, which the frozen decoder maps back to continuous pose. This reformulates pose regression as token classification, restricting outputs to the space of valid poses and providing an unbiased prior that improves robustness to occlusion and truncation.

## Results

On the challenging EMDB dataset with the full training set (SD+ITW+BL), TokenHMR achieves MVE 109.4 mm, MPJPE 91.7 mm, and PA-MPJPE 55.6 mm, outperforming HMR2.0 (120.7/99.3/62.8) by 9.4%, 7.6%, and 11.5% respectively. On 3DPW under the same training configuration, TokenHMR reaches MVE 84.6 mm, MPJPE 71.0 mm, and PA-MPJPE 44.3 mm versus HMR2.0's 88.4/77.4/47.4. The tokenizer itself introduces only ~2.5 mm of discretization error (MPJPE 2.2 mm on AMASS), which is negligible relative to the ~90 mm accuracy of SOTA regressors on real data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenHMR consistently outperforms all three baselines across both EMDB and 3DPW. Against HMR2.0 (the primary baseline, same ViT-H/16 backbone), TokenHMR reduces MPJPE by 7.6% on EMDB (91.7 vs. 99.3 mm) and by 8.3% on 3DPW (71.0 vs. 77.4 mm) under the full SD+ITW+BL training setup. Against CLIFF, which uses a full perspective camera model with bounding-box location cues, TokenHMR achieves lower MPJPE on EMDB (91.7 vs. 103.1 mm with SD-only training; 91.7 vs. 69.0 mm on 3DPW where CLIFF is stronger due to its camera model). Against HybrIK, a hybrid analytical-neural IK method, TokenHMR leads by 11.3 mm in MPJPE on EMDB (91.7 vs. 103.0 mm). The ablation rows further show that TALS alone (HMR2.0+TALS) and tokenization alone (HMR2.0+Token) each provide partial improvements, but the full TokenHMR combination yields the largest gains, confirming the two components work synergistically.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenHMR builds directly on the HMR2.0 transformer-based HPS regression framework, replacing its continuous pose regression head with a VQ-VAE-based discrete token classifier. The tokenization strategy draws from VQ-VAE (originally for image synthesis) and T2M-GPT (for motion generation), repurposing these ideas to represent a prior over valid SMPL poses rather than generating sequences. The TALS loss departs from prior work that noted but did not quantify the 2D-3D accuracy trade-off, using BEDLAM's perfect ground truth to establish concrete thresholds below which 2D/p-GT supervision becomes counterproductive. Compared to existing pose priors (GMMs, VAEs like VPoser, normalizing flows), the discrete codebook approach is positioned as less biased toward frequently occurring poses while remaining trivially integrable into standard HPS training pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantification of camera/pose bias—showing that HMR2.0b's output is actually *worse* than ground truth on 2D PCK when projected with the correct camera—provides a concrete, actionable diagnosis for a problem that has plagued the field. TALS is a simple, drop-in loss modification that any existing HPS method can adopt to reduce overfitting to noisy p-GT, making it broadly useful beyond TokenHMR itself. However, the fundamental problem of estimating true camera intrinsics/extrinsics from a single image remains unsolved, and the method still relies on approximate projection; the 7.6% MPJPE improvement, while solid, does not eliminate the underlying bias. Open questions include whether the tokenized representation generalizes to full-body (hands, face) or multi-person settings, and whether the approach can be combined with future camera-estimation methods for further gains.
