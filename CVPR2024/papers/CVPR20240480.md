## Problem

Adversarial training has remained confined to small models (e.g., ResNet-50) and small datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10), lagging far behind the foundation-model era where billion-parameter models are trained on web-scale data. The paper asks whether the scaling principles proven effective in vanilla training—larger models, more diverse data, longer schedules—transfer to adversarial training, and how to make such scaling computationally feasible given the iterative attack-generation overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240480/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at variable resolutions: 112×112 (or 84×84 for ViT-H/14) during the pre-training stage and 224×224 during fine-tuning, with token reduction via resizing, random masking, or block masking in the pre-training phase. For web-scale datasets (LAION-400M, DataComp-1B) where precise class labels are absent, a frozen CLIP text encoder converts natural-language captions into classifier weights via contrastive learning. The output is a 1000-way class prediction on ImageNet-1K, evaluated under AutoAttack at ε∞=4/255, ε₂=2, and ε₁=75.

## Method

AdvXL introduces a two-stage adversarial training pipeline that decouples efficiency from robustness: a long pre-training stage at reduced image resolution (112×112) with a weak attacker (PGD-1, step size 4/255), followed by a short fine-tuning stage at full resolution (224×224) with a stronger attacker (PGD-3). This coarse-to-fine, weak-to-strong schedule yields roughly 5× speedup over one-stage full-resolution PGD-3 training while matching or exceeding final robustness. For web-scale training, a frozen CLIP-A text encoder provides class-agnostic supervision through a contrastive loss, enabling adversarial training on billion-image datasets without curated labels. The framework is then scaled along three axes—model size (ViT-B/16 → ViT-g/14, 87M → 1B parameters), data volume (ImageNet-1K → DataComp-1B, ~1M → ~1.3B samples), and training schedule (up to 5.12B seen samples)—establishing new state-of-the-art l∞, l₂, and l₁ robust accuracy records on ImageNet-1K.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K under AutoAttack (ε∞=4/255, ε₂=2, ε₁=75), AdvXL's ViT-g/14 trained on DataComp-1B achieves 71.0% l∞-robust accuracy, 70.4% l₂-robust accuracy, and 46.7% l₁-robust accuracy, with 83.9% clean accuracy. This represents a new state-of-the-art, surpassing the previous best (Swin-L at 59.6% l∞) by 11.4% in l∞, 14.2% in l₂, and 12.9% in l₁. The smaller ViT-H/14 variant (304M params) also achieves 69.8% l∞-robust accuracy, already exceeding all prior methods by ~10%. The two-stage training (coarse-to-fine, weak-to-strong) delivers over 5× speedup compared to full-resolution, strong-attack training throughout.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdvXL decisively outperforms all prior baselines across every robustness metric. Against the strongest prior l∞-robust model (Swin-L [34], 59.6%), the ViT-g/14 model gains +11.4% in l∞-robust accuracy while using ~12× more compute. Against the best l₂-robust result (ConvNeXt-L+ConvStem 320-eval [52], 56.2%), AdvXL achieves +14.2% improvement in l₂-robust accuracy, and against the best l₁-robust result (33.8%), it gains +12.9%. The smaller ViT-H/14 (304M params) already surpasses all prior methods by ~10% in l∞, demonstrating that the gains come from data and schedule scaling rather than solely from model size. Notably, AdvXL models trained only for l∞-robustness generalize strongly to unseen l₂ and l₁ attacks, a property not observed in prior baselines which show much smaller cross-norm generalization gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training paradigm (PGD-AT from Madry et al.) from its traditional small-scale setting (ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10, or at most ViT-B/ConvNeXT-L on ImageNet-1K) to foundation-model scale (ViT-g/14 on DataComp-1B). It builds on CLIP's contrastive learning framework and the CLIPA training recipe to enable adversarial training on web-crawled image-text pairs without precise class labels. The key methodological departure is a two-stage coarse-to-fine, weak-to-strong training schedule that reduces computational cost by ~5×, making billion-parameter, billion-sample adversarial training feasible for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing new SOTA on all three robustness metrics (l∞, l2, l1) with margins of 11–14%, this work demonstrates that the scaling laws governing vanilla training transfer to adversarial training, opening the door to robust foundation models. The two-stage training recipe is a practical technique likely to be widely adopted for any future large-scale adversarial training effort. Open questions remain: whether the approach extends beyond image classification (e.g., to detection, segmentation, or multimodal tasks), whether the compute requirements (thousands of TPU/GPU-days) can be further reduced, and whether the observed generalizability to unseen attack norms (l2, l1) persists under more diverse or adaptive threat models.
