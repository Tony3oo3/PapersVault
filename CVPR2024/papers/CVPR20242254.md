## Problem

Implicit neural field representations (NeRFs) for articulated subjects achieve high fidelity but suffer from slow rendering, complex backward skinning for animation, and fragility to pose estimation errors. Explicit representations (meshes, point clouds) render fast and deform simply but are limited by fixed topology or constrained point counts, and often require multi-view studio captures. The paper asks whether an explicit approximation of the radiance field can combine the expressiveness of implicit methods with the efficiency and robustness of explicit ones for monocular articulated subject capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242254/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video with per-frame estimated template poses (SMPL for humans, D-SMAL for dogs) and produces a canonical-space 3D Gaussian Mixture Model with N components, each parameterized by a 3D mean, SO(3) rotation, anisotropic scale, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients, together with learnable skinning weights and latent bone transformations. The resulting avatar is explicitly stored per Gaussian (no voxel-grid query at inference) and renders at 150+ FPS at 540×540 on a laptop GPU, with training completing in 30 seconds to 2.5 minutes depending on the dataset.

## Method

GART replaces the template mesh with a 3D Gaussian Mixture Model in canonical space that explicitly approximates the underlying radiance field, giving NeRF-level expressiveness without fixed topology. Animation uses learnable forward skinning: each Gaussian receives a learnable correction Δw added to the template's predefined LBS weights, avoiding the costly backward-skinning root-finding required by implicit methods. To capture loose clothing (e.g., long dresses), the paper introduces latent bones—n_l unobserved rigid transformations parameterized by an MLP or per-frame optimizable table—whose skinning weights are jointly learned per Gaussian, extending the forward skinning equation with an additional summation term. Rendering is performed via differentiable 3D Gaussian Splatting (EWA splatting) with sorted alpha compositing, and training minimizes L1 + SSIM photometric loss plus regularization terms (KNN-neighborhood standard deviation on Gaussian attributes, L2 norms on skinning corrections and scales, and voxel-distilled skinning for spatial smoothness). The optimization follows the densify-and-prune strategy from 3DGS, and the deformation-based training process makes the method robust to noisy template pose estimates.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap, GART achieves the best view synthesis quality with PSNR 32.22, SSIM 0.977, and LPIPS 29.21 after only ~2.5 min of training, surpassing the previous best (Instant-NVR, PSNR 31.01) by over 1 dB while training 2× faster. In the ultra-fast setting (~30 s), GART still reaches PSNR 31.76, outperforming all baselines that require ~10 h of training. On the challenging UBC-Fashion dataset with dynamic loose clothing, GART (PSNR 25.96) outperforms Instant-Avatar (PSNR 19.97) by ~6 dB. At inference, GART renders at 150+ FPS at 540×540 resolution, roughly 10× faster than Instant-Avatar (15 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GART outperforms all nine baselines on the primary ZJU-MoCap benchmark: it beats the closest competitor Instant-NVR [13] by 1.21 dB PSNR and 4.24 LPIPS points while training in half the time, and surpasses the ~10 h NeRF-based methods (HumanNeRF [67], AS [53], Neural Body [52], Anim-NeRF [51]) by 1.5–3.2 dB PSNR. On People-Snapshot [2], GART (30 s) matches or exceeds Instant-Avatar [24] on 2 of 4 sequences (e.g., 30.40 vs 29.65 PSNR on male-3-casual) while rendering 10× faster (150+ FPS vs 15 FPS). On the challenging UBC-Fashion dataset with dynamic long dresses, GART (T-Table variant, PSNR 25.96) dramatically outperforms Instant-Avatar (PSNR 19.97) by ~6 dB, attributed to the latent-bones mechanism capturing clothing motion that backward-skinning NeRFs fail to model. For animal reconstruction on in-the-wild dog videos (National Dog Show, Adobe Stock), GART with D-SMAL templates achieves PSNR 17.86–24.50, establishing a new baseline since no prior monocular neural animal method was directly comparable. The older NeRF-based baselines (NHP [31], PixelNeRF [81]) are substantially outperformed across all metrics, confirming the advantage of explicit Gaussian representations over implicit radiance fields for articulated subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

GART repurposes the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation from static scene rendering to articulated subjects, replacing the fixed-topology mesh in parametric templates (SMPL, SMAL, D-SMAL) with a Gaussian Mixture Model in canonical space. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based human/animal rendering line (HumanNeRF, Neural Body, Instant-Avatar, Instant-NVR) by using explicit Gaussians with forward skinning instead of implicit neural fields requiring costly backward skinning root-finding. The novel latent bones mechanism extends the template skeleton with unobserved degrees of freedom to capture loose clothing deformation, addressing a limitation that both mesh-based and NeRF-based methods struggle with.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup (150+ FPS vs. 15 FPS for Instant-Avatar) and ~200× training speedup (30 s vs. ~10 h for NeRF methods) while matching or exceeding quality make real-time articulated avatar rendering practically viable on consumer hardware. The latent bones approach offers a simple, interpretable solution to the long-standing problem of modeling non-rigid clothing without full cloth simulation. Key limitations remain: the method depends on a category-specific template pose estimator (limiting generalization to species without such priors), and it fits a single video rather than learning category-level knowledge from collections. An open direction is whether GART can be trained across in-the-wild video collections to amortize the per-subject fitting cost.
