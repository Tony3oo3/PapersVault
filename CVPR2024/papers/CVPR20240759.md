## Problem

Existing vision-language models for WSI classification rely on coarse-grained category-level text labels (e.g., disease type), which omit critical fine-grained pathological details such as differentiation grade, vascular invasion, and margin status, thereby limiting model generalizability across diverse downstream tasks. Additionally, the high computational cost of processing tens of thousands of patches per WSI creates a practical barrier to training and deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240759/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a WSI (cropped into 256×256 non-overlapping patches at 20× magnification, yielding ~5,000–7,200 patches per slide) and a corresponding pathology report as input. The raw report is standardized by GPT-4 into a structured fine-grained description covering six diagnostic dimensions (differentiation, stromal/vascular/pleural invasion, adjacent tissue invasion, and margin status), with 'Unknown' as a placeholder for missing information. The model outputs a WSI class label via contrastive alignment, and in zero-shot mode produces histological subtype predictions by matching image features against GPT-4-generated subtype descriptions.

## Method

FiVE introduces a Task-specific Fine-grained Semantics (TFS) module that couples a frozen ResNet image encoder with a LoRA-fine-tuned BioClinicalBERT text encoder through contrastive learning. The TFS module operates in three stages: (1) Fine-grained Guidance construction, where standardized descriptions are randomly split, shuffled, and reassembled into variable-granularity training pairs to provide hierarchical semantic supervision; (2) Diagnosis Prompts, combining manual-designed diagnostic queries with learnable continuous prompt tokens (inspired by LLaMA-Adapter) to form a unified query set; and (3) an Instance Aggregator that applies self-attention across patch embeddings followed by cross-attention with the diagnosis prompts, then concatenates mean-pooled outputs to produce a bag-level feature. A patch sampling strategy (50% ratio, capped at 2,048 instances) further reduces computation by exploiting the redundant spatial distribution of pathological patterns within tissue sections.

## Results

On TCGA Lung Cancer, FiVE achieves 94.62% ACC, 96.33% AUC, and 93.89% F1-score, outperforming the strongest baseline (MHIM-MIL) by 4.69% in ACC. On Camelyon16, FiVE reaches 94.25% ACC, 97.56% AUC, and 93.24% F1-score, surpassing MHIM-MIL by 1.77% in ACC. In few-shot transfer on TCGA Lung Cancer, FiVE attains 91.25% accuracy at 16-shot (a 9.19% gain over the best baseline TOP) and 71.26% at 0-shot, exceeding TOP's 1-shot result. For zero-shot histological subtype classification, FiVE achieves 65.23% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best linear-probe baseline (Attention-pooling) by 6.87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiVE outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets. The closest competitor, MHIM-MIL [28], is surpassed by 4.69% ACC on TCGA Lung Cancer (94.62% vs. 89.93%) and 1.77% ACC on Camelyon16 (94.25% vs. 92.48%). Among the attention-based MIL methods, DTFD-MIL [36] and DSMIL [15] are the next strongest, trailing FiVE by 6.39% and 6.30% ACC respectively on TCGA. CLAM-SB/MB [20] and ABMIL [10] show comparable performance to each other but are consistently 5–7% below FiVE in ACC. TransMIL [26] underperforms all other attention-based baselines, suggesting its transformer aggregation is less effective for WSI-level classification. In the few-shot setting (Table 3), FiVE dominates both TOP [23] and CoOp [40] across all shot counts, with the largest margin of 13.13% at 2-shot over TOP. Notably, FiVE's 0-shot accuracy (71.26%) already exceeds TOP's 1-shot result (71.01%), highlighting the benefit of fine-grained semantic pre-training for data-efficient transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiVE extends the VLM-for-pathology line initiated by Zhang et al. [38] and Qu et al. [23], which used coarse category-level text labels to guide WSI classification, by replacing them with fine-grained pathological descriptions (differentiation grade, invasion patterns, margins) extracted from raw reports via GPT-4. The Task-specific Fine-grained Semantics module fuses ideas from prompt learning in VLMs (CoOp, LLaMA-Adapter's zero-init attention) with MIL bag aggregation (self-attention + cross-attention), a combination not previously explored in the WSI setting. The patch sampling strategy adapts the masking principle from FLIP [17] to the WSI context, exploiting the redundant spatial distribution of pathological patterns across tissue slices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained pathological semantics substantially improve generalization over coarse category labels, with a 9.19% accuracy gain in 16-shot and competitive zero-shot subtype classification (65.23% top-1) on a task that is difficult even for pathologists. However, the approach is validated only on lung cancer (TCGA) and breast cancer (Camelyon16), and the manual-designed standards are organ-specific, raising open questions about transferability to other tissue types and cancer subtypes. The reliance on GPT-4 for report standardization introduces a pipeline dependency and potential error propagation that has not been thoroughly stress-tested. Broader applicability would require demonstrating that the fine-grained prompt framework generalizes beyond the six hand-crafted diagnostic criteria used here.
