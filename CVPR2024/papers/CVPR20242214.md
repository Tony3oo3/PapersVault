## Problem

Despite Transformers dominating NLP and visual perception, diffusion-based visual generative models still rely almost exclusively on CNN-based U-Net backbones. The largest prior diffusion Transformer (DiT-XL) has only ~675M parameters and is limited to class conditioning, leaving open questions about how to adapt Transformer diffusion to free-form text conditioning and whether it can scale competitively with U-Net-based systems like SDXL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242214/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes free-form text prompts as input, encoded by CLIP-L, Flan-T5-XXL, or both (concatenated), and produces 512×512 RGB images (T2I) or 8-frame video clips at 512px short side and 24 FPS (T2V). For video inference, the model additionally accepts a motion conditioning signal controlled by a separate guidance scale λ_M ∈ [1.0, 1.3], distinct from the standard text guidance scale λ_T = 7.5.

## Method

GenTron adapts DiT-XL/2 by replacing one-hot class embeddings with text embeddings, systematically comparing two integration mechanisms (adaLN-Zero vs. cross-attention) and three text encoder choices (CLIP, T5-XXL, or both), finding that cross-attention with combined CLIP+T5XXL embeddings yields the best compositional performance. The model is then scaled from ~930M to ~3.1B parameters (GenTron-G/2) by increasing depth (28→48 blocks), width (1152→1664), and MLP width (4608→6656). For T2V, a lightweight temporal self-attention (TempSelfAttn) layer is inserted between cross-attention and MLP in each block, with zero-initialized output projections for stable fine-tuning from the T2I checkpoint. The key innovation, Motion-Free Guidance (MFG), treats temporal motion as a conditioning signal analogous to text: during training, the temporal attention mask is replaced with an identity matrix with probability p_motion_free (disabling motion and loading repeated image frames as pseudo-video), and at inference a modified classifier-free guidance formula applies separate guidance scales for text and motion, improving per-frame visual quality without sacrificing temporal coherence.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, GenTron-CLIP-T5XXL-G/2 (3.1B params) achieves a mean score of 49.99, surpassing the previous best Transformer-based method PixArt-α (48.15) and U-Net-based SDXL (44.41). In human evaluation against SDXL, GenTron achieves a 51.1% win rate in visual quality (19.8% draw) and a 42.3% win rate in text alignment (42.9% draw) across 3000 responses. On zero-shot FID-30K, GenTron-G/2 scores 14.53 (vs. SDv1.4's 12.94), but the authors note FID misrepresents human preference; GenTron achieves a higher CLIP-Score (0.335 vs. 0.325 for SDv1.4) while using roughly 4× less training data (550M vs. 2B image-text pairs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL (LDM XL, 2.6B U-Net), GenTron-G/2 (3.1B Transformer) wins on T2I-CompBench mean (49.99 vs. 44.41) and CLIP-Score (0.335 vs. 0.329), and also achieves a lower FID-30K (14.53 vs. 17.82); in the 3000-response human study, GenTron is preferred 51.1% of the time for visual quality. Against PixArt-α (the closest Transformer-based competitor), GenTron leads by 1.84 points on T2I-CompBench mean, with the largest gap in color attribute binding (+7.88 points: 76.74 vs. 68.86). Against SDv1.4 (0.9B U-Net), GenTron substantially outperforms on T2I-CompBench (49.99 vs. 31.50) and CLIP-Score (0.335 vs. 0.325) while using ~4× less training data, though it trails on FID-30K (14.53 vs. 12.94). Against Imagen (3.0B, autoregressive), GenTron achieves a higher CLIP-Score (0.335 vs. 0.27) but a notably higher FID-30K (14.53 vs. 7.27), reflecting the known limitations of FID as a quality proxy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenTron directly extends DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2022) from class-conditioned to text-conditioned latent diffusion, replacing one-hot class embeddings with language model embeddings (CLIP, Flan-T5, or both). A key conceptual departure from DiT is the finding that cross-attention, rather than the adaLN mechanism DiT favored for class conditioning, is the superior integration strategy for free-form text — a distinction the paper attributes to the spatially heterogeneous nature of text signals versus the spatially uniform nature of class labels. For video, GenTron departs from the standard recipe of adding 3D convolutions and temporal transformer blocks (as in Stable Video Diffusion) by inserting only a lightweight temporal self-attention layer into each existing Transformer block, and extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) with a novel motion-free guidance term that treats temporal motion as an additional conditioning axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic evidence that a purely Transformer-based diffusion architecture (no U-Net, no 3D convolutions) can match or exceed U-Net-based models like SDXL in both image and video generation, validating the architectural convergence trend seen in NLP and perception. The cross-attention-over-adaLN finding for text conditioning is a practically important design insight that corrects a natural but incorrect extrapolation from DiT's class-conditioning results. However, the video results remain preliminary (8 frames at 4 FPS, ~34M clips), the FID scores do not surpass SDv1.4, and the reliance on internal Meta data limits reproducibility. Open questions include whether the 3B-parameter ceiling can be pushed further, whether the video quality gap versus image quality can be closed with better data, and whether the motion-free guidance generalizes to longer or higher-resolution video.
