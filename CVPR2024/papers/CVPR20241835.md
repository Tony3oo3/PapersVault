## Problem

Existing transform-based privacy-preserving face recognition methods attempt to selectively remove the most visually informative features while retaining identity cues, but their removal is often inadequate, leaving residual texture and structural signals that enable successful recovery attacks. This paper identifies that the fundamental difficulty lies in explicitly minimizing visual features in the protective representation, and proposes instead to invert the problem by maximizing the fidelity of a regeneration so that the subtraction residue is inherently visually uninformative.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB face image X as input and produces a spatial image X_p of the same resolution as the protective representation, unlike many competitors that output high-dimensional frequency channels (e.g., 192×H×W) requiring 5–63× more storage. The transformation is applied client-side using a frozen generative model, and a sample-wise random seed θ determines the channel shuffling, so the same face yields 192! distinct protective images across calls. The service provider then runs a standard FR backbone (IR-50) on X_p to extract identity embeddings for matching.

## Method

MinusFace trains a U-Net autoencoder g to regenerate the face (minimizing L1 loss) and co-trains an IR-50 recognition model f on the residue r = e(X) − e(g(X)) using ArcFace loss, where e is DCT encoding into 192 channels. Performing subtraction in the high-dimensional DCT space avoids the blank-residue problem of spatial subtraction, because the abundant non-principal channels can carry identity features even when the spatial decoding R′ = d(r) remains visually blank. At inference, the 192-channel residue r is randomly channel-shuffled (seed θ) and decoded via IDCT to produce the final spatial image X_p = d(s(r;θ)). The shuffling selectively destroys texture signals (which vary across channels) while preserving structural identity cues (consistent across channels), and the 192! possible shuffles make it infeasible for an attacker to train a consistent recovery model.

## Results

MinusFace achieves 99.78% accuracy on LFW, 96.92% on CFP-FP, 97.57% on AgeDB, 91.90% on CPLFW, 95.90% on CALFW, and 93.37% / 94.70% TPR@FPR(1e-4) on IJB-B / IJB-C. These results are within ≤2% of the unprotected ArcFace baseline (99.77% LFW, 95.60% IJB-C) and on par (±1%) with the strongest frequency-domain SOTAs (DuetFace, PartialFace). On the large-scale IJB-B/C benchmarks, MinusFace substantially outperforms all non-frequency-based PPFR methods, which suffer severe accuracy drops (e.g., PEEP drops to 5.82% on IJB-B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MinusFace is on par (±1%) with the four frequency-domain SOTAs (DuetFace, PartialFace, DCTDP, PPFR-FD) across all seven benchmarks, with the largest gap being ~0.9% on CFP-FP versus DuetFace (96.92 vs. 97.79). It clearly outperforms the steganography-based ProFace (93.77% CFP-FP, 69.39% IJB-B) and the adversarial-noise AdvFace (92.21% CFP-FP, 70.21% IJB-B) by 3–24 points, and dominates the earlier indiscriminate-obfuscation methods (PEEP, InstaHide, Cloak) by 10–90 points on IJB-B/C. The paper's key differentiator is privacy: while frequency-based SOTAs match MinusFace on accuracy, they remain vulnerable to recovery attacks (their protective representations can be inverted by a U-Net trained on 1.3M images), whereas MinusFace's recovered images are highly blurred with the lowest SSIM and PSNR among all compared methods. MinusFace also requires only 1× storage/transmission cost versus 5–63× for frequency-based SOTAs, since its output is a standard spatial image rather than high-dimensional feature channels.

## Relation to Prior Work

MinusFace departs from the dominant transform-based PPFR paradigm, which selectively removes the most visually informative features (frequency-channel pruning, adversarial noise, steganography) while retaining the rest. Instead, it repurposes a concept from image compression: the residual between an original image and its regeneration is naturally visually uninformative, and the paper shows this residual can be co-trained to carry identity features. The high-dimensional DCT mapping and random channel shuffling are borrowed from JPEG compression and standard data augmentation, respectively, but their combination for privacy-preserving biometrics is novel. The method is architecturally simple (U-Net autoencoder + IR-50 + ArcFace loss) and does not build on a single prior PPFR framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that several top-venue PPFR methods (DCTDP, DuetFace, PartialFace, PPFR-FD, AdvFace) are vulnerable to recovery attacks by a sufficiently resourced attacker, undermining their privacy claims—a finding with practical implications for deployed systems. MinusFace's 192! random channel-shuffling space provides a mathematically clean argument against recovery, and its output is a standard spatial image with minimal storage overhead, making it practical for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other biometric modalities (iris, fingerprint), whether adaptive attackers who observe multiple X_p samples from the same identity can exploit correlations, and whether the DCT-based high-dimensional space is truly necessary or whether other invertible transforms would suffice.
