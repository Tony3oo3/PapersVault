## Problem

MLLMs frequently generate hallucinated content that contradicts the visual input, yet existing mitigation strategies (instruction-length constraints, RLHF with manual annotations) do not address the root cause. The paper reveals two representation-level deficiencies: a persistent modality gap between projected visual tokens and textual tokens in the LLM's embedding space, and an entanglement between representations of hallucinating and non-hallucinating texts that prevents the model from distinguishing them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240976/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images (processed by a vision encoder into visual token sequences) paired with ground-truth captions, plus GPT-4-generated hallucinative captions that introduce object-presence errors or attribute distortions (quantity, color, location). Both the visual sequence and each text sequence are individually passed through the frozen LLM with an appended <EOS> token; the final hidden state of that token serves as the global representation for contrastive computation. At inference, the model takes an image and a text instruction and produces a text response, with the HACL-trained interface yielding fewer fabricated details.

## Method

HACL augments the standard cross-modal contrastive loss used in MLLM pretraining by inserting GPT-4-generated hallucinative captions as hard negative samples in the image-to-text direction. Concretely, the visual token sequence (projected through the learnable interface F_α) and the ground-truth text sequence are each fed separately through the LLM, and their last-token hidden states form the anchor and positive pair; the hallucinative caption's last-token representation is added as an explicit negative in the denominator of the image-to-text InfoNCE loss (Eq. 5). A momentum queue of size 16,384 (borrowed from MoCo/ALBEF) supplies additional in-batch negatives. The combined objective—text-generation loss plus the symmetric contrastive loss—is used to optimize only the learnable interface during Stage-1 pretraining, while the vision encoder and LLM remain frozen, avoiding catastrophic forgetting of the language model's representation distribution.

## Results

On MMHal-Bench, LLaVA-HACL achieves an overall score of 2.08 (↑0.53) with a hallucination rate of 0.62 (↓0.15) compared to LLaVA's 1.55/0.76, while MiniGPT-4-HACL improves from 1.39 to 1.80 overall. On the POPE object hallucination benchmark, LLaVA-HACL raises the average F1 from 66.71 to 81.56 (+14.85) and reduces the Yes ratio from 99.90 to 45.19. On multi-modal benchmarks, LLaVA-HACL improves MME from 502.82 to 562.58, and MiniGPT-4-HACL improves MME from 581.67 to 653.94, demonstrating that hallucination mitigation also enhances general visual comprehension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MMHal-Bench, LLaVA-HACL (2.08) outperforms LLaVA-RLHF (2.05) and IDEFICS-80B (2.05), and substantially surpasses Kosmos-2 (1.69) and the base LLaVA (1.55). However, InstructBLIP-13B (2.14) and InstructBLIP-7B (2.10) still achieve slightly higher overall scores, though LLaVA-HACL matches or exceeds them on hallucination rate (0.62 vs. 0.58). On POPE, LLaVA-HACL achieves an average F1 of 81.56, outperforming InstructBLIP (89.27 on Random but 77.32 on Adversarial) and Shikra (86.19/83.16/82.49), while MiniGPT-4-HACL jumps from 43.35 to 83.82 F1. On MME, LLaVA-HACL (562.58) and MiniGPT-4-HACL (653.94) improve over their base models but remain below stronger baselines like Qwen-VL-Chat (1487.58) and LLaMA-Adapter-v2 (1328.40). On VQA benchmarks, LLaVA-HACL consistently outperforms LLaVA (e.g., VQAv2 73.3 vs. 71.3, GQA 42.5 vs. 41.3) and MiniGPT-4-HACL improves over MiniGPT-4 (VQAv2 68.9 vs. 65.2), though both remain below Qwen-VL-Chat and InstructBLIP on several zero-shot VQA tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive learning framework from vision-language pretraining (ALBEF, MoCo) into the MLLM setting, specifically targeting the learnable interface (projection layer) that bridges visual encoders and LLMs. It departs from prior hallucination-mitigation approaches—length-constrained instruction tuning (LRV-Instruction) and RLHF with human annotations (LLaVA-RLHF)—by reframing hallucination as a representation alignment problem solvable at pretraining time. The key conceptual departure is using GPT-4-generated hallucinative captions as hard negative samples in the image-to-text contrastive loss, rather than relying on in-batch negatives or external reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HACL offers a lightweight, annotation-free training-time intervention for MLLM hallucination that requires no RLHF pipeline or human-labeled preference data, making it accessible to groups without large annotation budgets. The representation-space diagnosis (modality gap plus hallucination/non-hallucination entanglement) provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why current interfaces fail. However, the method's dependence on GPT-4 for hallucinative caption generation introduces a cost and quality bottleneck, and the modest gains on already-strong models like LLaVA1.5 (2.08→2.13 on MMHal) suggest diminishing returns as base models improve. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video or multi-image settings and whether the GPT-4-generated hallucinations faithfully capture the distribution of real model hallucinations.
