## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene synthesis methods optimize for visual realism and naturalness but largely ignore physical plausibility (object collisions, floor-plan violations) and agent interactivity (reachability, walkability). This gap is critical for embodied AI, where synthesized scenes must support physics simulation and allow agents to navigate and interact with objects without constraint violations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D floor plan (defining room boundaries and workspace) as a conditioning signal and produces a 3D scene layout of N objects, each specified by a semantic label, 3D size, orientation (cos θ, sin θ), 3D location, and a 32-dim VAE-encoded shape feature. During inference, the shape features are used to retrieve matching meshes from 3D-FUTURE (rigid) or GAPartNet (articulated) asset libraries, with articulated objects placed using expanded bounding boxes that account for joint manipulation extents.

## Method

PHYSCENE is a conditional diffusion model (U-Net with attention blocks) trained with a standard DDPM noise-prediction objective on 3D-FRONT layouts, conditioned on floor-plan embeddings injected at every layer. Its key contribution is three physics-based guidance functions—collision avoidance (negative pairwise 3D IoU of object bounding boxes), room-layout constraint (negative 3D IoU between objects and infinite-thickness wall barriers), and reachability (negative 3D IoU between objects and agent bounding boxes sampled along an A*-planned path between the two largest connected walkable regions)—each converted into a gradient perturbation of the denoising mean via first-order Taylor expansion. The guidance gradients are combined with weighted coefficients and applied at every reverse-denoising step, simultaneously during training (as an additional term in the loss) and inference (as a one-step guided sample). For articulated objects, a VAE encodes 3D-FUTURE shapes into latent features that are matched against GAPartNet assets, enabling cross-dataset retrieval of interactable objects while the expanded bounding boxes feed back into the collision and reachability guidance.

## Results

On floor-plan-conditioned scene synthesis over 3D-FRONT, PHYSCENE achieves the lowest object collision rate (Col_obj) across all three room types: 0.187 (Bedroom), 0.191 (Living Room), and 0.151 (Dining Room), compared to ATISS (0.248/0.316/0.591) and DiffuScene (0.228/0.198/0.160). PHYSCENE also attains the best scene-level collision rate (Col_scene) in every setting (0.36/0.63/0.53) and the highest walkable-area ratio (R_walkable) in Bedroom (0.865) and Living Room (0.815). On perceptual quality, PHYSCENE is competitive with DiffuScene on FID (e.g., 25.52 vs 25.00 in Bedroom) while improving CKL in Bedroom (0.0025 vs 0.0031) and Living Room (0.0015 vs 0.0017).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ATISS (autoregressive transformer), PHYSCENE substantially reduces object collision rates in all room types (e.g., 0.151 vs 0.591 Col_obj in Dining Room) and scene-level collisions (0.53 vs 0.96), while also improving R_walkable in Bedroom and Living Room. However, ATISS achieves lower R_out in Living Room (0.136 vs 0.219) and Dining Room (0.132 vs 0.217), and higher R_reach in those two settings, which the authors attribute to ATISS prioritizing floor-plan adherence over collision avoidance. Against DiffuScene (diffusion-based, no floor-plan condition), PHYSCENE wins on Col_obj, Col_scene, and R_out in Bedroom, and on R_walkable in Bedroom and Living Room, confirming that the physics guidance functions add value on top of a diffusion backbone. DiffuScene retains a slight FID advantage in Living Room (38.69 vs 43.33) and Dining Room (38.31 vs 39.90), and a marginal R_reach edge in Dining Room (0.847 vs 0.789), suggesting a small realism trade-off for the added physical constraints. Overall, PHYSCENE dominates on the physical-plausibility and interactivity metrics that are the paper's core contribution, while remaining competitive on standard perceptual quality scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHYSCENE extends the guided diffusion framework from SceneDiffuser [26] and PhysDiff [61]—which applied physical constraints to motion planning and generation—into the domain of 3D scene layout synthesis. It builds directly on DiffuScene [51] for the diffusion-based scene generation backbone and VAE shape encoding for cross-dataset object retrieval, but departs from both DiffuScene and ATISS [42] by treating physical plausibility (collision, room-boundary adherence, agent reachability) as first-class guidance signals during denoising rather than as post-hoc filtering or autoregressive constraints. The key conceptual move is converting three distinct physical constraints into differentiable guidance functions that tilt the learned scene distribution at each diffusion step, unifying what were previously separate optimization or rejection-sampling problems into a single generative process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete gap: the 3D-FRONT dataset, the de facto standard for indoor scene synthesis, exhibits high rates of physical constraint violations (up to 62.5% of living rooms have object collisions, ~20% of objects are unreachable), meaning scenes generated from it inherit these flaws and are unsuitable for physics-based embodied AI simulation. By demonstrating that explicit physical guidance can reduce collision rates by 30–50% while maintaining competitive visual quality, PHYSCENE provides a practical recipe for producing simulation-ready scenes at scale. However, the method is currently limited to a few room types and excludes small objects, leaving open the critical question of whether this approach scales to the full diversity of household scenes needed for manipulation tasks like pick-and-place. The broader implication is that physical constraints can be embedded into generative models as soft guidance rather than hard post-processing, a pattern likely transferable to other constrained 3D generation tasks.
