## Problem

State-of-the-art generalizable 3D reconstruction methods (MCC, NU-MCC) rely on Transformer-based implicit field learning with dense query-supervision uniformly sampled across a bounded space, treating all positions equally regardless of their relevance to the object. This aimless querying paradigm is computationally intensive and struggles to capture fine shape details because queries are not adapted to the target geometry. The paper asks whether integrating point diffusion can provide shape-adaptive queries that make implicit field learning more effective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB-D image as input, which is decomposed into a 224×224 RGB image I and a partial point cloud P (M=HW points) unprojected from the depth channel. All point clouds are normalized to zero-mean, unit-variance in the CO3D coordinate system. The output is a 3D point cloud of N=50k points, obtained by iteratively denoising and predicting unsigned distance field (UDF) values, with UDF supervision clamped at a maximum of 0.5. Optionally, RGB color can be predicted alongside UDF by extending the implicit value to a 4-dimensional vector.

## Method

IPoD unifies implicit field learning and point diffusion in a single network h_θ that, at each denoising step t, simultaneously predicts the noise ε and the UDF value ν for every point in the current noisy point cloud X_t, conditioned on the input image and partial point cloud. The key insight is that the query points for implicit prediction are the same points being denoised, so they progressively adapt to the object shape rather than being sampled uniformly, allowing the network to focus on valuable regions near the surface. A novel self-conditioning mechanism feeds the predicted UDF values back into the noise-prediction decoder at the next step, creating a cooperative loop where implicit predictions guide denoising and denoising provides better queries for implicit prediction. Training jointly minimizes an L1 loss on UDF and a weighted L2 loss on noise prediction (λ=1.0) over T=1000 diffusion steps. Two backbones are provided: a PVCNN-based version (Ours1) built on PC² and a Transformer-based version (Ours2) built on NU-MCC with anchor prediction, both using a frozen ViT encoder for image features.

## Results

On CO3D-v2 (10 held-out categories), the Transformer-based IPoD (Ours2) achieves the best reconstruction quality with an F-score of 87.2%, Chamfer distance of 0.190, precision of 85.1%, and recall of 90.1%. This represents a 7.8% absolute improvement in F-score and a 28.6% reduction in Chamfer distance over the previous SOTA (NU-MCC: F1 80.9%, CD 0.266). The PVCNN-based variant (Ours1) also outperforms the diffusion baseline PC2-depth by 7.8% in F-score (76.2 vs. 70.7) and 19.2% in Chamfer distance (0.252 vs. 0.312). On MVImgNet, qualitative results confirm zero-shot generalization to 238 unseen categories, with further gains after fine-tuning on 100k cleaned point clouds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPoD (Ours2, Transformer) outperforms all baselines on every metric. Against the strongest prior method NU-MCC [28], it improves F-score by 7.8% (80.9→87.2), reduces Chamfer distance by 28.6% (0.266→0.190), and gains 5.9% in precision (79.2→85.1) and 6.1% in recall (84.0→90.1). Against MCC [61], the gains are even larger: 17.4% F-score improvement (69.8→87.2) and 40% CD reduction (0.316→0.190). The PVCNN-based IPoD (Ours1) beats the diffusion baseline PC2-depth by 7.8% F-score (70.7→76.2) and 19.2% CD (0.312→0.252), while the original PC2 [35] (without depth) is far behind at F1 33.0. The paper also demonstrates that fine-tuning on cleaned MVImgNet point clouds further improves generalization to unseen categories, validating the scalability of the point diffusion approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPoD bridges two previously separate paradigms in single-view 3D reconstruction: Transformer-based implicit field learning (MCC, NU-MCC), which relies on dense uniform query points over a bounded space, and conditional point-cloud diffusion (PC2, PVD), which iteratively denoises a noisy point cloud. The key conceptual move is to treat the implicit field's query points as the noisy sample in a diffusion process, so that denoising simultaneously produces adaptive query positions that concentrate near the object surface. The self-conditioning mechanism, originally proposed for discrete data generation (Chen et al., ICLR 2023), is repurposed here by substituting predicted UDF values for the classical X̃₀ approximation, creating a cooperative loop where implicit predictions guide denoising and denoising guides implicit querying.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cooperative diffusion-implicit framework offers a principled way to address the known weakness of pure implicit methods (wasteful uniform querying) and pure diffusion methods (coarse global shape without fine detail), yielding a 28.6% Chamfer distance improvement over the prior SOTA (NU-MCC) on CO3D-v2. The cleaned 100k-point-cloud MVImgNet dataset is a practical resource for the community. However, the authors acknowledge the method has not been validated on human or scene reconstruction, where severe occlusion and fine-grained geometry pose additional challenges, and the reliance on COLMAP-derived (noisy, incomplete) ground truth limits the ceiling of achievable accuracy.
