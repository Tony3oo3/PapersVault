## Problem

Deep stereo networks deployed in challenging environments (nighttime, rain, low illumination) suffer significant accuracy drops, and while online adaptation can recover performance, it introduces computational overhead that is prohibitive on resource-constrained devices such as UAVs or low-powered vehicles. Prior work treats adaptation as a strictly single-instance task, ignoring that multiple instances of the same model may be operating in different environments and could share adaptation effort. This paper asks whether the adaptation process itself can be formulated as a distributed, federated task so that a passive client benefits from adaptation without incurring any local computational cost.

## Input / Output

The listening client receives a pre-trained stereo backbone and, at each communication round, a set of aggregated weight updates from the server; it applies these updates and produces a disparity map from incoming rectified stereo pairs without running any GPU-intensive adaptation locally. Active clients consume stereo pairs from different domains (to avoid data leakage) and periodically upload either full weight vectors (FedFULL) or a single sampled encoder-decoder block (FedMAD). Input resolutions range from ~375×1242 (KITTI) to 1080×1440 (DSEC), and the output is a single disparity map per stereo pair, evaluated via D1-all and EPE metrics.

## Method

The paper introduces MADNet 2, a revised real-time stereo backbone that replaces the original MADNet's limited-radius correlation volume with RAFT-Stereo's all-pairs correlation volume, building a pyramid of correlation scores at multiple scales (1/64 to 1/4) to extend the search range to the full epipolar line while maintaining a compact, multi-scale decoder design (~60× faster than RAFT-Stereo). On top of this backbone, the FedFULL framework defines a set of active nodes that independently run full or modular adaptation and periodically upload their weights to a central server, which aggregates them via FedAvg-style averaging and broadcasts the result to listening clients. To reduce the substantial data traffic this introduces, FedMAD modifies the client update so that each active node tracks per-block update counts, samples a single block via a softmax-weighted distribution, and transmits only that block to the server, which averages only the received subset. This reduces uplink traffic by roughly 4–5× with negligible accuracy loss, and the listening client retains the original inference speed (e.g., 20 FPS on Jetson AGX Xavier) while gaining accuracy improvements comparable to or exceeding on-device adaptation.

## Results

On KITTI (All domains), MADNet 2 with MAD++ adaptation achieves 1.16% D1-all and 0.84 px EPE, outperforming all SOTA non-adaptive models (RAFT-Stereo: 1.75%, CREStereo: 1.82%, IGEV-Stereo: 2.51%, UniMatch: 2.97%) while running at 8 ms on an RTX 3090 and 70 ms on Jetson AGX Xavier (~15 FPS). On the challenging DSEC nighttime sequences, FedFULL++ achieves 4.99% D1-all (Night#1) at 12 ms inference, matching FULL++ accuracy (4.69%) while maintaining 8.5× lower latency. On DrivingStereo Rainy, FedMAD++ reaches 8.58% D1-all, improving over single-agent FULL++ (10.34%) by ~1.8 points without any local adaptation overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADNet 2 with MAD++ adaptation (1.16% D1-all on KITTI All) outperforms all four SOTA non-adaptive stereo networks—RAFT-Stereo (1.75%), CREStereo (1.82%), IGEV-Stereo (2.51%), and UniMatch (2.97%)—while running 40–400× faster on GPU and remaining real-time on embedded hardware. Against the original MADNet [55], MADNet 2 reduces D1-all from 38.84% to 4.21% without adaptation, and from 2.46% to 1.16% with proxy-label adaptation (MAD++), a >2× improvement. Among real-time baselines (CoEX, HITNet, TemporalStereo), MADNet 2 is the only model achieving >10 FPS on Jetson AGX Xavier (47 ms vs. 177/404/inf ms) while still supporting federated adaptation at the same speed. In challenging domains (DrivingStereo Rainy/Dusky, DSEC Night), federated adaptation (FedFULL++/FedMAD++) consistently matches or exceeds single-agent FULL++/MAD++ accuracy—e.g., 8.58% vs. 10.34% D1-all on Rainy—without incurring any local computational overhead, a benefit unavailable to the non-federated baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: real-time online adaptation for stereo (MAD/MADNet, Tonioni et al. 2019; proxy-label adaptation, Poggi et al. 2021) and federated learning (FedAvg, McMahan et al. 2017). The key conceptual departure is recasting adaptation—which has always been treated as a single-instance, on-device process—as a distributed, federated task where resource-constrained clients offload optimization to more powerful peers. Architecturally, MADNet 2 revises the original MADNet by replacing its limited-radius correlation volume with RAFT-Stereo's all-pairs volume, trading some peak accuracy for a ~60× speedup while preserving the modular block structure needed for federated weight exchange.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: a stereo network on a UAV or low-powered vehicle can now enjoy adaptation-level accuracy improvements without any local compute overhead, simply by receiving aggregated weight updates from peer devices. The finding that federated adaptation can *outperform* single-device adaptation in challenging domains (Dusky, Cloudy) suggests that cross-environment weight averaging provides an implicit regularization benefit. However, the current framework assumes clients process similar image properties, ignores network latency, and offers no reciprocity to active nodes—open questions that limit immediate real-world deployment. The authors envision extension to other online-adaptation tasks (monocular depth, optical flow, segmentation), which could broaden the federated-adaptation paradigm well beyond stereo.
