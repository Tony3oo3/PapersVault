## Problem

FID, the de facto standard metric for evaluating text-to-image generation models, relies on Inception-v3 embeddings and a Fréchet distance that assumes multivariate normality—both of which are empirically violated. The paper demonstrates that FID contradicts human raters, fails to reflect iterative quality improvements in diffusion and autoregressive models, and cannot detect latent-space distortions, making it an unreliable primary quality metric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241440/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CMMD takes two sets of images—a reference set (e.g., COCO 30K) and a generated set sharing the same prompts—and produces a single scalar distance score (lower is better). Images are resized to 336×336 for the CLIP ViT-L/14@336px encoder, and the resulting embeddings are used to compute the squared MMD with a Gaussian RBF kernel (σ=10), scaled by 1000 for readability. Because generated and real images share prompts in text-to-image evaluation, the two sample sizes are equal (m=n).

## Method

CMMD replaces both components of FID: Inception-v3 embeddings are swapped for CLIP ViT-L/14@336px embeddings (trained on 400M image-text pairs, capturing far richer and more diverse visual content), and the Fréchet distance is replaced by the Maximum Mean Discrepancy with a Gaussian RBF kernel. The MMD estimator is unbiased, distribution-free (no normality assumption), and sample-efficient because it avoids estimating a 2048×2048 covariance matrix, requiring only pairwise kernel evaluations that are trivially parallelizable. Empirically, CMMD monotonically tracks quality changes across iterative generation steps, correctly ranks distorted images, agrees with human preferences where FID and KID disagree, and achieves consistent estimates with as few as a few thousand images versus the >20,000 needed for reliable FID.

## Results

In the human evaluation on PartiPrompts (1,633 prompts) against COCO 30K, CMMD correctly identifies the full Muse model (Model-A) as higher quality (CMMD=0.721) over the early-stopped model (Model-B, CMMD=0.951), matching the 92.5% human rater preference for Model-A. In contrast, FID (21.40 vs 18.42), FID∞ (20.16 vs 17.19), and KID (0.0105 vs 0.0080) all incorrectly rank Model-B as better. CMMD also monotonically tracks quality improvements across iterative generation steps (Muse, Stable Diffusion) and distortion levels, while FID shows non-monotonic or inverted behavior. Computationally, MMD distance is ~100× faster than Fréchet distance (71.4 ms vs 7,008 ms for n=30,000, d=2048 on TPUv4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CMMD against three baselines: FID (Heusel et al., 2018), its unbiased variant FID∞ (Chong et al., 2019), and KID (Binkowski et al., 2021). In the human evaluation, all three baselines fail: FID, FID∞, and KID each assign a lower (apparently better) score to the early-stopped Model-B, directly contradicting the 92.5% human preference for Model-A. CMMD is the only metric that correctly ranks Model-A above Model-B. In progressive generation experiments (Muse iterations, Stable Diffusion denoising steps), FID and FID∞ show non-monotonic or inverted trends, while CMMD monotonically improves. Under latent-space distortion, FID incorrectly suggests quality improvement at low distortion levels, whereas CMMD monotonically worsens as expected. On sample efficiency, FID requires >20,000 images for stable estimates while CMMD remains consistent even at much smaller sample sizes, and MMD computation is ~100× faster than Fréchet distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly critiques the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) introduced by Heusel et al. (2018), which has become the de facto standard for text-to-image evaluation, and builds on prior identifications of FID's flaws: Chong & Forsyth (2019) showed FID is a biased estimator and proposed FID∞, while Parmar et al. (2022) demonstrated sensitivity to aliasing in image resizing. The key departure is twofold: replacing Inception-v3 embeddings (trained on 1M ImageNet images, 1000 classes) with CLIP embeddings (trained on 400M image-text pairs), and replacing the Fréchet distance (which assumes multivariate normality) with the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) using a Gaussian RBF kernel (which is distribution-free and unbiased). The resulting CMMD metric thus combines two independently motivated substitutions into a single alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If FID is unreliable as the primary quality metric, it undermines the evaluation framework upon which the entire text-to-image literature is built, potentially leading to incorrect model rankings and premature rejection of good ideas. The paper's human evaluation showing FID actively contradicting 92.5% human preference is a strong but single-experiment result; broader validation across diverse model families and prompt distributions would strengthen the case. Open questions include whether CMMD inherits its own blind spots (e.g., to specific distortion types or aesthetic dimensions), whether the CLIP embedding space introduces its own biases, and whether the field will converge on a new standard or adopt a multi-metric protocol. The practical impact is significant: CMMD's sample efficiency and ~100× speedup over FID computation make it viable for online training monitoring and large-scale model comparisons.
