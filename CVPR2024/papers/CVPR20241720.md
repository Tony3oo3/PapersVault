## Problem

Existing out-of-context (OOC) misinformation detectors assess image-text consistency but fail to provide convincing, human-readable explanations for their judgments, which are essential for debunking misinformation and building public trust. While multimodal large language models (MLLMs) could in principle generate such explanations, general-purpose MLLMs like InstructBLIP perform near-randomly on OOC detection (47.4% accuracy) due to domain mismatch and an inability to access real-time or external knowledge for contextual verification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241720/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

SNIFFER takes a news image and its accompanying caption as the primary input, augmented at inference time with detected visual entities (via Google Entity Detection API) and textual evidence retrieved through reverse image search. The output is a binary OOC judgment (fake/real) paired with a structured natural-language explanation that explicitly names the inconsistent news element, the entity referenced in the text (ent_t), and the entity actually present in the image (ent_v), following a fixed template format.

## Method

SNIFFER builds on InstructBLIP (ViT-G/14 image encoder + Vicuna-13B LLM + Q-Former) and applies two-stage instruction tuning where only the Q-Former parameters are updated. Stage 1 (news-domain alignment) uses ChatGPT-generated captioning instructions from 368K NewsCLIPpings pairs to refine the model's mapping from coarse visual concepts to fine-grained news entities. Stage 2 (task-specific tuning) uses 35,536 GPT-4-generated OOC instructions that explicitly identify the inconsistent element and entities, teaching the model the detection logic. At inference, SNIFFER performs a two-pronged analysis: internal checking (the tuned MLLM identifies image-text inconsistencies) and external checking (the LLM module reasons over retrieved web evidence to assess claim-evidence relevance), then composes both signals into a unified final judgment and explanation via a third LLM call.

## Results

On the NewsCLIPpings Merged/Balance test set (7,264 samples), SNIFFER achieves 88.4% overall accuracy, 86.9% on fake (OOC) samples, and 91.8% on real samples, outperforming all baselines. The strongest prior method, CCN (which also uses retrieved external evidence), reaches 84.7% overall, so SNIFFER gains 3.7 percentage points. In a direct comparison on a 400-sample subset, SNIFFER (86.8%) surpasses GPT-4V (75.5%) by 11.3 percentage points in overall accuracy. Ablation shows the original InstructBLIP scores only 47.4% (below random), and the two-stage instruction tuning alone lifts performance by over 35 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNIFFER outperforms all nine baselines on the primary NewsCLIPpings benchmark. Against the strongest prior method CCN (84.7% All), SNIFFER gains 3.7 points overall and 7.3 points on real samples, despite using only part of the textual evidence. The pre-trained multimodal baselines (CLIP 66.0%, DT-Transformer 77.1%, VisualBERT 58.6%) are all surpassed by 11–30 points, while the from-scratch detectors SAFE (52.8%) and EANN (58.1%) lag even further behind. The Neu-Sym detector (68.2%), which relies on a general-purpose MLLM for sub-question verification, is outperformed by 20.2 points, underscoring the benefit of task-specific instruction tuning. On the 400-sample GPT-4V comparison, SNIFFER (86.8% All, 94.5% Real) beats GPT-4V (75.5% All, 74.0% Real) by 11.3 points overall and 20.5 points on real samples. For cross-dataset generalization, SNIFFER significantly outperforms CMCS on both News400 and TamperedNews across all difficulty levels (varying k for image similarity).

## Relation to Prior Work

SNIFFER extends the InstructBLIP instruction-tuning paradigm (itself building on BLIP-2's Q-Former architecture) into the news domain for the first time, using a two-stage curriculum: news-domain captioning alignment followed by OOC-specific discrimination. It departs from prior OOC detectors (CCN, DT-Transformer, Neu-Sym) that either rely on fixed contrastive/consistency scoring or decompose text into sub-questions for a general MLLM, by instead end-to-end tuning the MLLM to jointly produce a binary judgment and a natural-language explanation. The use of language-only GPT-4 to synthesize instruction data with explicit inconsistency annotations is a notable methodological departure from prior self-instruction or human-annotation pipelines for MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Explainable OOC detection is critical for public trust and effective debunking, yet prior methods offered at best attention-map visualizations without causal reasoning. SNIFFER demonstrates that a 13B task-tuned model can surpass GPT-4V by 11% on this task, suggesting that domain-specific instruction tuning is a viable alternative to scaling up general-purpose MLLMs for narrow but high-stakes applications. Limitations include dependence on external APIs (Google Entity Detection, reverse image search), the fact that only ~60% of test samples have retrievable evidence, and evaluation primarily on a single benchmark (NewsCLIPpings) with synthetic OOC pairs. Open questions include real-time deployment, robustness to more sophisticated OOC variants (e.g., partially correct captions), and whether the GPT-4-generated explanations introduce systematic biases that transfer to the student model.
