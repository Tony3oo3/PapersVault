## Problem

Existing BEV detectors such as BEVFormer lack direct supervision on the BEV feature map itself—its quality is only indirectly shaped through the decoder's detection loss, leaving no mechanism to align BEV elements with ground-truth class, location, and boundary structure. Additionally, the transformer decoder operates as a black box: initialized empty queries recover ground-truth information only at the endpoint via a matching algorithm, with no explicit modeling of how ground-truth instances interact with each other or with the BEV map during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view RGB camera images (360° field-of-view) from the nuScenes dataset and outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels, velocity vectors, and object attributes for 10 categories. During training, ground-truth 3D bounding boxes and class labels are additionally fed into the GT-BEV and GT-QI modules to construct supervision signals, but at inference time no extra inputs are required—the GT modules are entirely training-only, so the inference pipeline is identical to the base BEVFormer.

## Method

CLIP-BEVFormer adds two training-only modules to a standard BEV encoder-decoder (BEVFormer). The GT-BEV module encodes each ground-truth instance's class label and 3D bounding box into a feature vector β via a lightweight encoder (MLP or frozen language model), crops and pools the corresponding region of the BEV feature map to obtain α, then applies a CLIP-style symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss on the α–β similarity matrix to explicitly align BEV elements with their ground-truth class, position, and boundary. The GT-QI module injects the same encoded GT features β directly into the decoder's query pool alongside the standard learned queries; these GT queries pass through the same self-attention, cross-attention, and FFN layers in parallel (preventing information leakage) and are supervised with the standard perception loss without requiring a matching step, since each GT query is already targeted at a specific instance. Together, the two modules provide ground-truth flow guidance to both the BEV encoding and the perception decoding processes, improving detection by 8.5% NDS and 9.2% mAP over BEVFormer-tiny while introducing zero additional parameters or computation at inference.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, CLIP-BEVFormer-tiny with an MLP ground-truth encoder achieves NDS 38.5 and mAP 27.4, improving over the BEVFormer-tiny baseline (NDS 35.5, mAP 25.1) by 8.5% and 9.2% respectively. The base variant with MLP reaches NDS 56.2 and mAP 46.7, surpassing BEVFormer-base (NDS 51.7, mAP 41.6) by 8.7% and 12.3%. The LM-encoder variant of the tiny model attains NDS 38.8 and mAP 27.3, while the base LM variant reaches NDS 55.1 and mAP 44.1. All variants also improve translation, scale, orientation, and velocity errors over their respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-BEVFormer consistently outperforms its direct baselines across all camera-based comparisons. Against BEVFormer-tiny, the MLP variant gains +3.0 NDS and +2.3 mAP, while the LM variant gains +3.3 NDS and +2.2 mAP; both also reduce mATE, mASE, mAOE, and mAVE, with only a marginal mAAE increase. Against BEVFormer-base, the MLP variant achieves the largest absolute gain (+4.5 NDS, +5.1 mAP), and the LM variant still improves by +3.4 NDS and +2.5 mAP. Applied to BEVerse (Swin backbone), CLIP-BEVFormer lifts NDS from 46.6 to 48.3 and mAP from 32.1 to 34.2; applied to BEVFormerV2, it raises NDS from 42.6 to 44.1 and mAP from 35.1 to 37.0. Against non-BEV camera baselines (FCOS3D, PGD, DETR3D), the base MLP variant leads in NDS (56.2 vs. 41.5–42.8) and mAP (46.7 vs. 34.3–36.9). The method trails LiDAR-based methods (CenterPoint-Voxel at NDS 64.8, SSN at 49.8), which is expected given the modality difference, but the base MLP variant (NDS 56.2) narrows the gap to CenterPoint-Voxel to within 8.6 points using cameras only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BEVFormer's spatiotemporal transformer architecture for multi-view BEV feature generation and repurposes CLIP's contrastive learning mechanism—originally designed for image-text alignment—to instead align generated BEV features with ground truth representations. The key conceptual departure from prior BEV detectors is the introduction of explicit ground truth flow supervision during both the BEV encoding phase (via a contrastive loss) and the perception decoding phase (via GT query injection), addressing what the authors identify as a black-box decoding process that only provides endpoint supervision. Notably, the framework demonstrates that a simple MLP can substitute for a full pretrained language model as the ground truth encoder, decoupling the method from the need for large-scale pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the zero-inference-cost design (GT modules are training-only) combined with substantial gains in long-tail detection (up to 46.5% for rare classes) and robustness under simulated sensor failure, both critical for real-world autonomous driving deployment. The finding that a lightweight MLP matches or exceeds a CLIP language model as GT encoder makes the framework broadly deployable without heavy pretrained dependencies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond nuScenes and camera-only settings to LiDAR fusion, tracking, and motion prediction tasks, and whether the contrastive alignment principle transfers to other BEV architectures beyond the BEVFormer family.
