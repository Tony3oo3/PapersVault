## Problem

Existing GNN-based layout generation methods represent each room as a single graph node, which lacks the granularity to capture localized interactions (e.g., only a room's right wall touching an adjacent room) and cannot model higher-order dependencies such as room size constraints that involve all four bounding-box coordinates simultaneously. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a factor graph representation that explicitly encodes both pairwise and higher-order spatial constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241749/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a typed room-adjacency graph (nodes carry room type, approximate position, and normalized size; edges carry one of 10 spatial relation types like left-of, right-above, etc.) and a 2D binary building boundary mask. The boundary mask is not used directly as a CNN embedding but is decomposed into corner points, each of which seeds a higher-order boundary factor. The model outputs per-room bounding-box coordinates (x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max) in a normalized H×W grid, which are then fed to a cascaded refinement network to produce the final single-channel floorplan layout image.

## Method

Each room is decomposed into four variable nodes (x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max) rather than a single node, and the factor graph introduces four types of factor nodes: box factors connecting all four variables of one room (encoding size constraints), relation factors connecting only the relevant coordinate subset between adjacent rooms based on the typed adjacency (e.g., left-of links x_max^s to x_min^o), boundary factors (one per extracted corner point) connecting to every variable in the graph, and a complete factor connecting all variables to capture hidden dependencies. A Factor Graph Neural Network (FP-FGNN) performs L rounds of bipartite message passing where messages are computed by MLPs over concatenated node embeddings and edge features, aggregated with a learnable softmax weighting. Training uses only L1 loss on box coordinates and pixel-wise cross-entropy on the CRN output image, without auxiliary losses like interior or coverage penalties. The two-stage pipeline (box prediction → CRN image synthesis) achieves ~20% improvement in box-level IOU over Graph2Plan while preserving constraint fidelity under partial inputs.

## Results

On the RPLAN dataset, FP-FGNN achieves a box-level IOU-Macro of 0.8685 and IOU-Micro of 0.9165, representing a ~20% absolute improvement over Graph2Plan (0.6796 / 0.7402). At the pixel level, FP-FGNN reaches IOU-Macro 0.5954 and IOU-Micro 0.8019, surpassing Graph2Plan (0.4476 / 0.7575) and HPGM (0.4812 / 0.5179). For constraint preservation, FP-FGNN attains 96.80% relation accuracy and 99.13% location accuracy, both exceeding Graph2Plan (93.58% / 98.05%). In the generative pipeline setting, FP-FGNN achieves a FID of 12.87, substantially lower than Graph2Plan (29.26), RPLAN (21.29), and Constr-LGen (21.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FP-FGNN outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against HPGM [5], the improvement is substantial: box-level IOU-Macro rises from 0.4701 to 0.8685 (+39.8 points) and pixel-level IOU-Micro from 0.5179 to 0.8019 (+28.4 points). Against the primary baseline Graph2Plan [11], FP-FGNN gains ~20 percentage points in box-level IOU-Macro (0.8685 vs. 0.6796) and ~15 points in pixel-level IOU-Macro (0.5954 vs. 0.4476), while also improving relation accuracy from 93.58% to 96.80% and location accuracy from 98.05% to 99.13%. In the generative pipeline evaluation (Table 4), FP-FGNN achieves a FID of 12.87, nearly halving the FID of RPLAN (21.29) and Constr-LGen (21.47), and more than doubling the improvement over Graph2Plan (29.26). Under partial-constraint stress tests (Table 3), FP-FGNN maintains relation accuracy above 94% even when three rooms' information is dropped, whereas Graph2Plan degrades to ~70–78%, highlighting FP-FGNN's robustness to incomplete user specifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage floorplan generation pipeline established by Graph2Plan and HPGM, which use GNNs on room-adjacency graphs to predict bounding boxes before a cascaded refinement network produces the layout image. The key conceptual departure is replacing single-node-per-room GNN representations with a factor graph where each room is decomposed into four variable nodes (x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max) and constraints are encoded as higher-order factor nodes, drawing on the factor graph neural network framework from Dupty & Lee [8] and Zhang et al. [36, 37]. This shift from pairwise GNN message passing to bipartite factor-graph message passing enables modeling of arbitrary-order spatial dependencies (e.g., room size constraints involving all four coordinates simultaneously) that standard GNNs cannot express.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factor graph formulation provides a principled, extensible mechanism for incorporating domain knowledge (boundary corners, typed adjacencies, size constraints) into layout generation, and the demonstrated graceful degradation under partial user constraints makes it well-suited to iterative human-in-the-loop design workflows. However, the evaluation is confined to the RPLAN dataset and 2D residential floorplans, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to 3D layouts, non-residential buildings, or other constrained layout domains. The modest pixel-level IOU-Macro improvement (0.45→0.60) relative to the large box-level gain (0.68→0.87) suggests the CRN refinement stage may be a bottleneck that future work should address. The authors also note the potential to extend the framework to language-guided generation, connecting to the broader trend of multimodal design tools.
