## Problem

Skeleton-based action recognition has benefited from large model architectures and external pre-training, but no prior work has explored using a pre-trained large language model directly as an action recognizer while preserving its rich implicit knowledge of human behaviors. Fine-tuning an LLM on non-linguistic inputs risks degrading its generalizability and erasing pre-learned knowledge, making it unclear whether the LLM's behavioral knowledge can be leveraged without modifying its weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a skeleton sequence (joint positions over V timesteps) as input and outputs a predicted action class name. The skeleton is first compressed into a sequence of W = V/4 discrete tokens, each of dimension 5120 (matching LLaMA-13B's embedding size), forming an 'action sentence.' The LLM receives this token sequence embedded in a natural-language instruction prompt and generates the action label as text, enabling zero-shot evaluation on unseen classes by simply listing them in the prompt.

## Method

LLM-AR performs a two-stage pipeline: (1) an action-based VQ-VAE with a hyperbolic codebook (Poincaré ball model) projects each skeleton sequence into a discrete token sequence, regularized by a human-inductive-biases-guided loss that enforces Zipf's law on token frequency, context-sensitivity via paired-token correlation, and MMD alignment between the codebook and the LLM's first-layer word embeddings; (2) LoRA is applied to LLaMA-13B (r=64, α=16) so the model learns to interpret the action sentences while all pre-trained weights remain frozen. The hyperbolic codebook exploits the tree-like structure of human skeletons, and the linguistic projection ensures the discrete tokens mimic properties of natural language, making them natively processable by the LLM without architectural modification.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D, LLM-AR achieves 95.0% (X-Sub) and 98.4% (X-View) top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous best SkeleTR (94.8% / 97.7%) and DSTformer (93.0% / 97.2%). On NTU RGB+D 120, it reaches 88.7% (X-Sub) and 91.5% (X-Set), surpassing SkeleTR (87.8% / 88.3%) by a notable 3.2 points on X-Set. On Toyota Smarthome and UAV-Human, LLM-AR also achieves the best accuracy (67.0% X-Sub and 46.3% X-Sub respectively), confirming consistent gains across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-AR outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every evaluation protocol on all four datasets. On NTU RGB+D, it edges out the closest competitor SkeleTR [12] by 0.2 points (X-Sub) and 0.7 points (X-View), while leading DSTformer [80] by 2.0 and 1.2 points respectively. On NTU RGB+D 120, the margin over SkeleTR widens to 0.9 (X-Sub) and 3.2 (X-Set), and it surpasses STC-Net [30] by 2.5 points on X-Set. On Toyota Smarthome, LLM-AR beats ML-STGNet [81] by 2.4 points (X-Sub) and UNIK [72] by 3.0 points (X-View2). On UAV-Human, it leads ACFL [61] by 2.1 points. The largest relative gains appear on the harder cross-setup and cross-view protocols, suggesting the LLM's pre-learned knowledge contributes most when domain shift is greatest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard skeleton-based action recognition pipeline (ST-GCN, 2s-AGCN, transformer backbones) by replacing the entire recognition architecture with a frozen LLaMA-13B, and from prior LLM-adaptation works (MotionGPT, Visual Instruction Tuning) that fine-tune LLM weights for non-language tasks. It builds on VQ-VAE for signal-to-token discretization, LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation, and hyperbolic VQ-VAEs for the codebook geometry, but the key conceptual departure is the 'linguistic projection' step: rather than adapting the LLM to the signal, the signal is reshaped to look like language the LLM already understands. The human-inductive-biases-guided regularization (Zipf's law, context-sensitivity, MMD alignment) is a novel loss design not present in prior VQ-VAE or action recognition work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen 13B LLM can serve as a competitive action recognizer, opening a path toward leveraging LLMs' pre-learned world knowledge for perception tasks without the catastrophic knowledge loss associated with full fine-tuning. The unseen-class experiment (62.4% vs 37.7% for all-tuning) is the most compelling evidence that preserving pre-trained knowledge genuinely aids generalization to novel action categories. However, practical adoption is limited by the 13B model size, the separate VQ-VAE training stage, and the lack of real-time inference analysis. Open questions include whether smaller LLMs suffice, whether the linguistic projection generalizes to other non-linguistic modalities (audio, point clouds), and whether the Zipf/context biases are truly necessary or merely a proxy for some other regularization effect.
