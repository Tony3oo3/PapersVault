## Problem

Vision Transformer's Self-Attention lacks explicit spatial priors and incurs quadratic computational complexity when modeling global information, limiting its efficiency and data requirements. Existing remedies (windowing, neighborhood attention) either restrict the receptive field or disrupt distance-based priors, making it difficult to simultaneously achieve global perception, explicit spatial inductive bias, and linear complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242066/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The backbone consumes standard RGB images split into non-overlapping patches (224×224 for classification, shorter side 800 with longer side ≤1333 for detection, 512×512 for segmentation) and produces task-specific outputs: class logits for ImageNet, bounding boxes and instance masks for COCO, or per-pixel class maps for ADE20K. The four-stage hierarchical design outputs multi-scale feature maps at different resolutions, enabling plug-and-play integration with detection heads (RetinaNet, Mask R-CNN, Cascade Mask R-CNN) and segmentation heads (Semantic FPN, UperNet).

## Method

RMT introduces Manhattan Self-Attention (MaSA), which replaces the standard attention score with a 2D bidirectional spatial decay matrix D_nm = γ^{|x_n−x_m|+|y_n−y_m|} that assigns exponentially decreasing attention to tokens farther away in Manhattan distance, thereby embedding an explicit spatial prior directly into the attention weights. To achieve linear complexity without breaking this decay structure, MaSA is decomposed into horizontal and vertical 1D attention passes (each with its own 1D decay matrix), whose composition preserves the full 2D Manhattan decay and yields the same receptive field as the full MaSA. The architecture uses a four-stage hierarchical design where the first three stages employ the decomposed MaSA and the final stage uses the full MaSA, combined with Conditional Positional Encoding (CPE) and a Local Context Enhancement module (depthwise convolution) for local feature refinement. Unlike RetNet, RMT retains Softmax for nonlinearity rather than a gating function, and assigns distinct decay rates γ across attention heads to introduce multi-scale local bias.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, RMT-S achieves 84.1% top-1 accuracy with only 27M params and 4.5 GFLOPs, and RMT-L reaches 85.5% with 95M/18.2 GFLOPs. With token labeling, RMT-S* and RMT-L* further improve to 84.8% and 86.1% respectively. For dense prediction, RMT-B attains 54.5 box AP and 47.2 mask AP on COCO (Cascade Mask R-CNN 3×+MS), and RMT-L achieves 52.8 mIoU on ADE20K (UperNet), setting new state-of-the-art across all evaluated tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RMT consistently outperforms all compared backbones across every model scale on ImageNet-1K. At the small scale, RMT-S (84.1%) surpasses BiFormer-S (83.8%), SMT-S (83.7%), and FAT-B3 (83.6%) by 0.3–0.5%; with token labeling, RMT-S* (84.8%) leads BiFormer-S* (84.3%) and VOLO-D1* (84.2%) by 0.5–0.6%. At the base scale, RMT-B (85.0%) exceeds iFormer-B (84.6%) and STViT-B (84.8%) by 0.2–0.4%, and RMT-B* (85.6%) beats BiFormer-B* (85.4%) by 0.2%. At the large scale, RMT-L (85.5%) outperforms STViT-L (85.3%), FAT-B5 (85.2%), and MaxViT-B (84.9%), while RMT-L* (86.1%) leads UniFormer-L* (85.6%) and WaveViT-L* (85.5%) by 0.5–0.6%. On COCO detection, RMT-B achieves 54.5 box AP with Cascade Mask R-CNN, exceeding CSWin-S (53.7) and DAT-S (52.7) by 0.8 and 1.8 AP respectively. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, RMT-L (52.8 mIoU) surpasses CSWin-B (51.1), InternImage-B (50.8), and Swin-B (48.1) by 1.7, 2.0, and 4.7 mIoU respectively, demonstrating clear advantages in dense prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RMT extends RetNet's one-dimensional, unidirectional temporal decay mechanism (originally designed for causal text modeling) into a two-dimensional, bidirectional spatial decay matrix based on Manhattan distance, thereby transplanting an NLP prior-knowledge technique into the vision domain. This contrasts with prior vision approaches like Swin Transformer's windowing or NAT's neighborhood attention, which introduce spatial priors through structural constraints rather than explicit distance-based decay. The paper also borrows the Local Context Enhancement (DWConv) module from BiFormer, but its core novelty lies in the decomposed MaSA formulation that achieves linear complexity while preserving the spatial decay matrix intact—something that existing sparse attention mechanisms and RetNet's own recurrent/chunk-wise forms cannot do without disrupting the prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RMT addresses two long-standing ViT limitations simultaneously—lack of explicit spatial priors and quadratic attention complexity—within a single unified mechanism, which is conceptually cleaner than combining separate positional encodings with sparse attention. The decomposition form is a practical contribution: it enables linear-complexity global modeling in early stages without sacrificing the spatial prior, making the architecture competitive at both low and high FLOPs budgets. Open questions include whether Manhattan distance is optimal versus other metrics (e.g., Euclidean, learned), whether the fixed per-head decay rates could be learned end-to-end, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D data where the spatial prior structure differs. The paper does not explore recurrent inference, which RetNet supports, leaving a potential efficiency avenue unexplored.
