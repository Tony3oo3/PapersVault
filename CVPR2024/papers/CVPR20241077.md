## Problem

Point annotations in crowd counting are inherently imprecise, placed coarsely within head regions rather than at exact centers, causing density map bias and inaccurate spatial context. Existing remedies either relax pixel-wise losses (introducing background noise) or add segmentation branches supervised by noisy point-derived pseudo masks (lacking precise spatial information). The paper asks how to obtain accurate spatial context to alleviate label variance while simultaneously suppressing background noise within a single systematic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241077/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB crowd scene images (resized to a maximum side of 2048 px and minimum of 416 px, aspect ratio preserved) along with point annotations for training; the NWPU dataset additionally provides head box annotations used to generate higher-quality pseudo masks for the segmenter. It produces a density map (for count estimation via summation), a binary head segmentation mask (for foreground/background distinction), and the final crowd count. When extended to foundation models (e.g., SAM-base), only lightweight adapter-based prompt modules are trained while the backbone remains frozen, reducing trainable parameters to roughly one-quarter of the full CNN variant.

## Method

mPrompt builds a two-branch network sharing an HRNet backbone, with a density-map regressor and a head segmenter coupled by a self-attention gate (Sigmoid(S(x)) ⊗ R(x)). The key mechanism is mutual prompt learning: (1) a *point prompt* refines the segmenter's target mask by unioning a pretrained pseudo mask with the binarized ground-truth density (offline) and the predicted density (online), followed by a spatial K-NN filter to remove background noise; (2) a *context prompt* constrains the predicted density map to fall within the predicted mask region via an intersection-over-union-style loss. Both prompts are optimized end-to-end with a combined loss (MSE density + cross-entropy segmentation + context prompt), interpreted as maximizing mutual information between the regressor and segmenter. The framework extends to foundation models by replacing the segmenter branch with learnable adapter modules that inject context features into a frozen pretrained backbone.

## Results

mPrompt achieves MAE of 52.5 on ShanghaiTechA, 5.8 on ShanghaiTechB, 72.2 on UCF-QNRF, and 50.2 on NWPU(V), with corresponding RMSE values of 88.9, 9.6, 133.1, and 219.0. On the NWPU test set, it reaches MAE 62.1 / RMSE 293.5. The SAM-based variant (mPrompt†*) attains MAE 53.2 on SHA and 58.8 on NWPU(V) while using roughly 1/4 the trainable parameters and 1/5 the FLOPs of the HRNet-based version.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mPrompt achieves the best MAE on three of four benchmarks: ShanghaiTechB (5.8, tied with STEERER), NWPU validation (50.2, beating STEERER's 54.3 by 4.1), and NWPU test (62.1, beating STEERER's 63.7 by 1.6). On ShanghaiTechA, PET leads with MAE 49.3 versus mPrompt's 52.5, while on UCF-QNRF, DDC achieves the lowest MAE of 65.8 compared to mPrompt's 72.2. Against the most recent ICCV'23 baselines (PET and STEERER), mPrompt wins on NWPU(V) and NWPU(T) but trails on SHA and QNRF. The SAM-based variant (mPrompt†*) narrows the gap to within ~1 MAE on SHA and ~4.5 MAE on NWPU(V) while reducing trainable parameters to roughly 25% and FLOPs to 20% of the HRNet version, demonstrating that the mutual prompt mechanism transfers effectively to foundation-model backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the density map regression paradigm for crowd counting (Lempitsky & Zisserman, 2010) and the segmentation-auxiliary-branch line of work (Modolo et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2019) by introducing a mutual prompt learning framework where a regressor and a segmenter iteratively refine each other's supervision signals. Unlike prior segmentation-based methods that rely on noisy pseudo-masks derived directly from point annotations, mPrompt uses point prompt learning (union of pseudo-mask with binarized density maps, plus K-NN filtering) to produce cleaner target masks, and context prompt learning to constrain the density map within predicted head regions. The paper also extends prompt learning from its NLP origins into the backward gradient propagation domain and adapts the framework to foundation models (SAM) via adapter-based learnable prompt modules, a direction not explored in prior crowd counting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual prompt learning framework offers a principled way to handle label variance in crowd counting without requiring cleaner annotations, and the demonstrated robustness to up to 50% box-annotation noise (Fig. 8) is practically valuable for real-world deployments where annotation quality is imperfect. The extension to SAM with only ~1/4 the parameters and ~1/5 the FLOPs of the HRNet variant suggests a path toward parameter-efficient crowd counting on foundation models. However, the SOTA claims are selective (PET still leads on SHA MAE), and the authors themselves note the framework's generalizability to crowd localization, object detection, and visual tracking remains unexplored, leaving open whether the mutual prompt mechanism transfers beyond the density-map regression setting.
