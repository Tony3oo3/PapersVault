## Problem

3D occupancy prediction for autonomous driving typically requires dense 3D annotations (LiDAR point clouds or voxel labels) that are extremely laborious to produce, limiting scalability. Existing self-supervised approaches (SceneRF, BTS) handle only monocular cameras and decode occupancy directly from 2D image features, ignoring 3D feature interactions and failing to generalize to surround-view scenarios. The paper asks whether meaningful 3D occupancy can be learned purely from video sequences while supporting both monocular and multi-camera settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242234/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes surround-camera RGB images (6 cameras on nuScenes) or a single monocular image, processed as video sequences where temporal frames provide self-supervision signals. It outputs a voxelized 3D occupancy grid (H×W×D) where occupancy is determined by zero-thresholding a learned SDF field, plus rendered depth maps and optional semantic labels. For semantic prediction, pseudo-labels come from an off-the-shelf open-vocabulary segmentor (OpenSeeD) rather than 3D annotations, and the SDF field serves as the intermediate geometric representation from which all outputs are derived.

## Method

SelfOcc lifts 2D image features (ResNet50 + FPN) into a 3D representation (BEV or TPV) via deformable cross-attention, then decodes this representation through a lightweight MLP into a signed distance field (SDF) rather than a density field, exploiting the SDF's explicit physical meaning and gradient properties for easier regularization. The key training innovation is an MVS-embedded depth loss that replaces the standard reprojection loss: instead of warping a single predicted depth through bilinear interpolation (which limits the receptive field to four adjacent pixels), it computes a weighted photometric loss over all M uniformly-spaced depth proposals along each ray, effectively enlarging the optimization receptive field across the entire epipolar line. Volume rendering follows the NeuS SDF-to-opacity formulation to synthesize color and depth views from temporal frames. The total loss combines this depth loss with RGB photometric loss, a Hessian smoothness term, a sparsity penalty for invisible regions, and an Eikonal constraint, all optimized without any 3D ground truth.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, SelfOcc(TPV) achieves an IoU of 45.01 and mIoU of 9.30 using only video sequences as supervision, outperforming the LiDAR-supervised TPVFormer (IoU 17.20) and the 3D-supervised FB-OCC (IoU 42.06) in geometric accuracy. On SemanticKITTI, SelfOcc(TPV) reaches an IoU of 21.97, surpassing the previous best self-supervised method SceneRF (13.84) by 58.7%. For novel depth synthesis on SemanticKITTI, SelfOcc(TPV) achieves Abs Rel 0.1562 and δ1 78.92%, outperforming SceneRF (0.1681, 75.07%). On nuScenes surround-view depth estimation, SelfOcc(TPV) attains Abs Rel 0.215 and RMSE 6.706, setting a new state-of-the-art among self-supervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfOcc(TPV) outperforms the closest self-supervised baseline SceneRF by 58.7% in IoU on SemanticKITTI (21.97 vs. 13.84) and also surpasses it on all novel depth synthesis metrics (e.g., Abs Rel 0.1562 vs. 0.1681 on SemanticKITTI; 0.4003 vs. 0.7631 on nuScenes). On Occ3D-nuScenes, SelfOcc(TPV) achieves a higher IoU than all 3D-supervised methods including FB-OCC (45.01 vs. 42.06) and BEVFormer (45.01 vs. 26.88), and dramatically exceeds the LiDAR-supervised TPVFormer (45.01 vs. 17.20), though it trails FB-OCC in mIoU (9.30 vs. 14.30) due to weaker foreground semantic prediction. For surround-view depth estimation on nuScenes, SelfOcc(TPV) beats SurroundDepth (Abs Rel 0.215 vs. 0.280) and MonoDepth2 (0.215 vs. 0.287), and slightly edges out SimpleOcc (0.215 vs. 0.224). On KITTI-2015 monocular depth estimation, SelfOcc(TPV) is competitive with DevNet (Abs Rel 0.103 vs. 0.100) and BTS (0.103 vs. 0.102) but does not clearly surpass them. The key differentiator is that SelfOcc requires no 3D labels at all, whereas all 3D-supervised baselines (FB-OCC, BEVFormer, TPVFormer) depend on laborious voxel annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfOcc sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) vision-based 3D occupancy prediction (MonoScene, TPVFormer, BEVFormer), which typically requires dense 3D supervision; (2) self-supervised NeRF-based scene reconstruction (SceneRF, BTS), which handles only monocular views and uses density fields; and (3) neural implicit surface reconstruction (NeuS), which provides the SDF-to-opacity rendering formulation. The key departure is conditioning NeRF-style volume rendering on explicit 3D representations (BEV/TPV) rather than per-scene MLPs or 2D image features, and replacing the standard photometric reprojection loss with an MVS-embedded strategy that distributes depth optimization across multiple proposals along each ray. This combination enables the first self-supervised surround-view occupancy prediction while retaining the geometric regularity of SDF fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SelfOcc eliminates the need for laborious 3D occupancy annotation, enabling training on the vast pool of unlabeled driving video. The MVS-embedded depth loss is a transferable technique that could benefit other NeRF-based reconstruction pipelines. However, several limitations remain: semantic prediction depends on an off-the-shelf open-vocabulary segmentor (OpenSeeD) whose misalignment caps foreground-class performance, the absolute IoU on SemanticKITTI (21.97) is still far below supervised methods, and the method requires ground-truth camera poses. Open questions include whether the approach can handle dynamic scenes, whether the SDF formulation can be made more amenable to novel view synthesis (where it underperforms density fields), and whether removing the 2D segmentation dependency is feasible.
