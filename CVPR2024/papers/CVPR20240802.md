## Problem

Existing 3D human generation methods cannot simultaneously guarantee global structural accuracy, fine-grained local geometry details, high resolution, and computational efficiency. Two-stage approaches that fit 3D from 2D images lack a 3D backbone for view consistency, while native 3D generation methods struggle with high-fidelity details and suffer from limited generalization and high inference costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240802/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 24 3D joint positions (SMPL skeleton), a DensePose IUV map, and optionally a text prompt (routed through Text2Human for image conditioning), all combined with Gaussian noise in a 128×128×8 latent space. The output is a 3D human mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from an occupancy field reconstructed from a 512×512×32 Fourier Occupancy Field, where depth is encoded in the phase of trigonometric basis functions. The compact spherical embedding represents each joint as a 10 cm-radius sphere whose FOF is computed and concatenated channel-wise into a 512×512×(8K) conditioning tensor.

## Method

Joint2Human is a latent diffusion model (U-Net denoiser, T=1000 steps) trained on FOF representations compressed by a VAE from 512×512×32 to 128×128×8. The key pose-guidance mechanism is a compact spherical embedding of 3D joints: for each of K=24 joints, a sphere is extended in 3D space and its FOF is computed, then all K sphere FOFs are concatenated channel-wise to form a conditioning signal that encodes both semantic joint identity and depth information without relying on SMPL parametric priors. A high-frequency enhancer (a subsidiary decoder with skip connections from the main decoder) recovers discarded high-frequency FOF terms to restore fine geometric details. A multi-view recarving strategy rotates the initial mesh 90° along yaw, re-encodes it through the full diffusion pipeline (T'=200 steps), and blends the resulting occupancy fields with the original via weighted averaging to eliminate directional artifacts while preserving multi-view consistency.

## Results

On the CustomHumans test set, Joint2Human (fine) achieves FID_normal of 23.89 and FID_shade of 41.20, outperforming the strongest baseline Chupa (fine) which scores 29.90 and 45.49 respectively. In a user study with 123 participants, Joint2Human is preferred over Chupa and AG3D on all three criteria: global structure (49.10% vs. 32.20% vs. 18.70%), local detail (52.03% vs. 29.92% vs. 18.05%), and overall impression (51.71% vs. 29.27% vs. 19.02%). The method also completes generation in under one minute per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline is Chupa [22], the current SOTA for 3D human geometry generation. Joint2Human (fine) beats Chupa (fine) by 6.01 points on FID_normal (23.89 vs. 29.90) and by 4.29 points on FID_shade (41.20 vs. 45.49), indicating both sharper geometry and more realistic shading. AG3D [9] is compared qualitatively only, since it relies on 2D training data and its code is unavailable; in the user study, Joint2Human is preferred over AG3D on all metrics (e.g., 52.03% vs. 18.05% for local detail). In the capability comparison (Table 1), Joint2Human is the only method that simultaneously checks all five boxes: global structure/view consistency, local detail, high resolution, loose clothing handling, and computational efficiency. GNARF [1] and Get3Dhuman [46] are listed in Table 1 but not quantitatively compared; GNARF lacks local detail and high resolution, while Get3Dhuman lacks global structure and computational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Fourier Occupancy Field (FOF) representation for 3D human geometry and the latent diffusion framework (VAE + U-Net denoiser) from Rombach et al., repurposing a 2D diffusion model to operate directly on 3D FOF features rather than on 2D images. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (generate 2D views, then reconstruct 3D via NeRF or differentiable rendering, as in Chupa and EVA3D) and from native 3D methods that rely on tri-planes or SDFs (AG3D, GNARF), instead treating FOF as a 2D-image-compatible 3D representation amenable to diffusion. A key conceptual departure is replacing SMPL/SMPL-X parametric pose conditioning—which the authors argue causes overfitting and limits diversity—with a compact spherical embedding of 3D joints, and adding a high-frequency enhancer plus multi-view recarving to recover details lost in the low-frequency FOF encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent trade-off in 3D human generation: methods that ensure global view consistency (native 3D approaches) tend to lack fine-grained local detail, while image-based methods capture detail but suffer from multi-view inconsistency. By achieving both simultaneously with sub-minute inference, Joint2Human offers a practical pipeline for VR/AR, gaming, and film applications. However, the method still struggles with extreme poses (e.g., stooping, headstands), and the training cost (8×A100 for 8 days on the diffusion model) remains substantial. Open questions include whether the multi-view recarving strategy generalizes beyond the single 90° rotation used here, and whether the FOF-based approach can scale to higher resolutions or more diverse clothing without further architectural changes.
