## Problem

Existing large vision models excel in transfer learning but cannot handle diverse tasks (captioning, detection, grounding, segmentation) with simple text instructions, because they lack a unified representation spanning both spatial hierarchy (image-level to pixel-level) and semantic granularity (coarse captions to fine-grained descriptions). This is compounded by the scarcity of large-scale, comprehensive visual annotations and the absence of a single architecture that integrates all these dimensions without task-specific heads.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form text prompt as task instruction, and always produces a text-form response. For spatial tasks, the output encodes regions as sequences of quantized location tokens (1,000 bins per coordinate) in the tokenizer vocabulary, supporting three formats: 4-coordinate bounding boxes for detection, 8-coordinate quad boxes for text detection, and variable-length polygon vertex sequences for segmentation. This eliminates task-specific prediction heads entirely.

## Method

Florence-2 uses a DaViT vision encoder to produce visual token embeddings, which are concatenated with prompt text embeddings and processed by a standard encoder-decoder transformer under a single cross-entropy language modeling loss. The key data contribution is FLD-5B (5.4B annotations on 126M images), generated by an iterative data engine that first annotates with multiple specialist models (DINO, Grounding DINO, SAM, OCR APIs) for consensus labeling, then filters and enhances with GPT-4/GPT-4V, and finally refines iteratively by re-annotating with the trained model itself. All annotation types—text, region-text pairs, and text-phrase-region triplets—are standardized into the same seq2seq format, enabling unified multi-task training without architectural modifications per task.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation, Florence-2-L (0.77B params) achieves 135.6 CIDEr on COCO Caption (vs. 84.3 for 80B Flamingo), 84.4 R@1 on Flickr30k grounding (vs. 78.7 for 1.6B Kosmos-2), and 35.8 mIoU on RefCOCO RES — a capability unsupported by prior foundation models. After fine-tuning on public data, Florence-2-L reaches 93.4/95.3/92.0 Accuracy on RefCOCO val/test-A/test-B, 80.5 mIoU on RefCOCO RES, 85.2 R@1 on Flickr30k, and 43.4 mAP on COCO Det val2017, outperforming specialist models with comparable or far larger parameter counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Florence-2-L (0.77B) outperforms the specialist model UNINEXT (0.74B) on RefCOCO val Accuracy (93.4 vs. 92.6) and RefCOCO+ val (88.3 vs. 85.2), while also adding COCO detection (43.4 mAP) and RefCOCO RES (80.5 mIoU) capabilities that UNINEXT does not report. Against PolyFormer, Florence-2-L leads on RefCOCO val (93.4 vs. 90.4) and RefCOCO RES (80.5 vs. 76.9 mIoU), despite PolyFormer using a purpose-built regression head for polygon coordinates. The 13B-parameter Ferret model is surpassed by Florence-2-L on RefCOCO val (93.4 vs. 89.5) and RefCOCO+ val (88.3 vs. 82.8). In zero-shot settings, Florence-2-L achieves 135.6 CIDEr on COCO Caption versus 84.3 for the 80B Flamingo model and 84.4 R@1 on Flickr30k versus 78.7 for the 1.6B Kosmos-2 model. Among generalist models, Florence-2-L dominates UniTAB across all RefCOCO/+/g splits (e.g., 93.4 vs. 88.6 on RefCOCO val) and is the only generalist to report RefCOCO RES (80.5 mIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

Florence-2 is the direct successor to Florence-1, extending the same sequence-to-sequence vision foundation model paradigm but with a substantially more comprehensive data engine and multitask training objective. Architecturally, it follows the unified seq2seq formulation established by Pix2Seq, Unified-IO, and Kosmos-2, where region outputs are encoded as quantized location tokens in the vocabulary. The key conceptual departure is in data: rather than combining existing sparse human-annotated datasets or web-crawled noisy text (as in CLIP, GrIT, or M3W), the Florence data engine autonomously generates 5.4B multi-granularity annotations via iterative specialist-model annotation and refinement, covering spatial hierarchy (image-level through pixel-level) and semantic granularity (brief through more-detailed text) simultaneously within a single training corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FLD-5B dataset and its iterative annotation pipeline represent a practical recipe for scaling comprehensive visual annotations beyond what human labeling can achieve, potentially reusable for other vision foundation model efforts. The finding that a 0.77B-parameter model matches or exceeds 80B Flamingo on zero-shot captioning and outperforms 1.6B Kosmos-2 on grounding suggests that annotation breadth and granularity may matter more than raw parameter count for unified vision understanding. Open questions include whether the data engine's quality ceiling is bounded by the specialist models it relies on, whether the approach extends to video or 3D, and whether the compact model's advantage persists as task complexity grows beyond the evaluated benchmarks.
