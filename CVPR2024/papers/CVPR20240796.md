## Problem

The two dominant datasets for online mapping, nuScenes and Argoverse 2, suffer from severe geographical overlap between training, validation, and test sets, with over 80% of nuScenes and 40% of Argoverse 2 evaluation samples within 5 m of a training sample. This data leakage causes state-of-the-art methods to be evaluated on memorized locations rather than true generalization, inflating reported performance and distorting conclusions about algorithmic design choices. The paper identifies this problem and proposes geographically disjoint splits to enable fair evaluation of online mapping methods.

## Input / Output

The evaluated methods consume multi-view RGB images from six surround cameras and optionally a LiDAR point cloud, lifting perspective-view features to a bird's-eye-view (BEV) representation before prediction. Vector-based methods output polylines with class labels (lane dividers, road boundaries, pedestrian crossings) within a 60 m evaluation range, scored via Chamfer-distance-thresholded mAP; segmentation-based methods output rasterized BEV grids with per-cell class assignments, scored via IoU. The key distinction from standard perception tasks is that the ground truth is a static HD map queried at the ego-vehicle pose, not dynamic object annotations.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a data splitting methodology combined with a systematic re-evaluation of six state-of-the-art online mapping methods. They construct two types of geographically disjoint splits: Near Extrapolation (70/15/15 train/val/test partitioned by manually defined geographic regions within the same cities, preserving zone-class, weather, and time-of-day diversity) and Far Extrapolation (city-wise splits with multiple folds enabling cross-validation across different training/evaluation city combinations). Re-training on these splits reveals performance drops of up to 46.5 mAP on nuScenes and 10 mAP on Argoverse 2, with the ranking among methods largely preserved. The paper further re-validates prior ablation studies on the clean splits, finding that the relative superiority of GKT over BEVFormer lifting and the benefit of depth supervision as an auxiliary task are less pronounced or reversed compared to conclusions drawn on the leaked original splits.

## Results

On the Near Xtrapolation split, MapTRv2 (camera-only) on nuScenes drops from 72.7 mAP (Original) to 26.2 mAP, a loss of 46.5 mAP. On Argoverse 2, the same method drops from 65.3 to 53.1 mAP, a more modest 12.2 mAP decrease. Under Far Xtrapolation (city-wise cross-validation), nuScenes methods collapse to ~21 mAP (MapTRv2 CV = 21.4) while Argoverse 2 methods retain ~45–47 mAP (StreamMapNet CV = 46.6, MapTRv2 CV = 45.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Eighte online-mapping baselines are re-ealuated: VectorMapNet, MapTR, MapTRv2, StreamMapNet (vector-based) and HDMapNet, GKT, CVT, IPM (segmentation-based). The ranking among methods is preserved across splits—MapTRv2 and StreamMapNet remain the top performers on both nuScenes and Argoverse 2—but the absolute performance gap between the best and worst methods narrows dramatically on nuScenes (from a 19.6 mAP spread to a 10.4 mAP spread). On Far Xtrapolation, StreamMapNet slightly edgesceeds MapTRv2 on Argoverse 2 (46.6 vs 45.5 CV mAP), reversing the near-split ordering. Segmentation methods (Table 5) show the same pattern: IPM leads on both datasets, but its advantage over GKT and CVT shrinks from ~20 IoU (Original) to ~10 IoU (Near). Re-validation of lifting-method ablations (Table 8) reveals that the previously reported superiority of GKT over BEVFormer is not reproduced on the geographically clean split, with differences under 2 mAP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new online mapping architecture but instead re-examines the evaluation infrastructure underpinning the entire field. It draws on the GeoML literature on spatial cross-validation and block partitioning (Roberts et al. 2017, Sun et al. 2021) and applies those principles to the autonomous driving datasets nuScenes and Argoverse 2, which were originally designed for object detection and motion forecasting with temporal splits. Prior online mapping work (MapTR, MapTRv2, VectorMapNet, StreamMapNet, HDMapNet) has been evaluated on the original temporally-split data, and a few isolated attempts at geographic partitioning exist (PON's coarse neighborhood split, UniFusion's city-wise split), but none provide balanced, multi-fold, geographically disjoint splits with both near and far extrapolation settings. The paper thus carves new ground by repurposing spatial data partitioning concepts from GeoML to expose and fix a systematic evaluation flaw in the online mapping community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that over 80% of nuScenes validation/test samples lie within 5 m of a training sample means that all previously reported SOTA numbers for online mapping are substantially inflated, and the field's understanding of which design choices matter (lifting method, auxiliary supervision) may be incorrect. The provided geographically disjoint splits (Near and Far Extrapolation) offer a concrete, adoptable fix that the community can use as a drop-in replacement, and the Far Extrapolation cross-validation protocol points toward a more realistic long-term evaluation target. Open questions remain about how to handle intra-set spatial correlation (which the authors acknowledge but leave for future work), whether the current dataset sizes are sufficient for meaningful training under proper splits, and how to extend these principles to other driving datasets (Waymo, Argoverse 1).
