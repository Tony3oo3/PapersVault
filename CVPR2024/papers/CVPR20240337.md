## Problem

Existing HSI restoration methods face a fundamental trade-off: model-based approaches rely on subjective handcraft priors that cannot fully capture complex image characteristics, while deep learning methods require large paired datasets and generalize poorly to unseen data. Recent diffusion-based restoration methods either cannot handle the variable and high spectral dimension of HSIs (e.g., DDRM) or require time-consuming per-dataset self-supervised training (e.g., DDS2M), leaving a gap for a generalizable, training-free framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded HSI tensor (H×W×B) with a known linear degradation operator (identity for denoising, blur+downsample for super-resolution, random mask for inpainting) and a pre-trained 3-channel diffusion model trained on RGB remote sensing images. It outputs a restored clean HSI of the same spatial and spectral dimensions. Crucially, the diffusion model never operates on the full B-band HSI; instead it samples a reduced K-band image (K≪B, typically K=3) whose band indices are determined by RRQR, making the spectral dimension compatible with the pre-trained model's 3-channel input.

## Method

HIR-Diff decomposes the clean HSI as X = A ×₃ E, where A is a K-band reduced image and E is a B×K coefficient matrix. The coefficient matrix E is estimated noise-robustly via rank-K SVD of the degraded image combined with RRQR factorization to select band indices that maximize |det(V_s)|, ensuring numerical stability and information diversity. The reduced image A is then recovered by a pre-trained diffusion model using DDIM sampling with a novel guidance function that combines a data-fidelity term (‖H(A×₃E) − Y‖²_F) and a total-variation regularization (‖A×₃E‖_TV), injected as a gradient correction on the predicted noise at each reverse step. A new exponential noise schedule (ᾱ_t = e^{−kt/T}, normalized) is proposed to match the conditional sampling dynamics—rapid initial convergence followed by slow detail refinement—enabling 5× acceleration (20 steps vs. 100) with minimal PSNR loss. The entire pipeline is unsupervised: no per-dataset training is required, and the pre-trained model is used as-is.

## Results

On HSI denoising (σ=50), HIR-Diff achieves 40.77/36.01/41.95 dB PSNR on WDC Mall/Houston/Salinas, outperforming the best baseline NGMeet (41.07/35.77/42.23) on WDC and Houston while remaining competitive on Salinas. For noisy super-resolution (×4), it reaches 34.68/30.68/37.53 dB PSNR, surpassing the best supervised method MCNet (33.55/29.60/35.13) by 1.1–2.4 dB. For inpainting (mask rate 0.8), it achieves 36.82/31.19/39.40 dB PSNR, beating the closest competitor DDS2M (36.18/30.05/38.23) while running ~100× faster (17–20 s vs. 1354–4920 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model-based methods, HIR-Diff outperforms BM4D and ETPTV by 5–10 dB across all settings, while NGMeet remains the strongest model-based competitor (e.g., 42.23 vs. 41.95 dB on Salinas σ=50), showing that handcrafted low-rank priors can be competitive on well-structured data. Against supervised DL methods (T3SC, SST, SERT for denoising; SFCSR, SSPSR, MCNet, RFSR, PDENet for super-resolution), HIR-Diff consistently wins by 1–4 dB PSNR, demonstrating superior generalization since it requires no task-specific training. Against the closest unsupervised diffusion baseline DDS2M, HIR-Diff achieves comparable or slightly better PSNR (e.g., 40.77 vs. 39.13 on WDC σ=50; 39.40 vs. 38.23 on Salinas inpainting 0.8) while being 50–300× faster (17 s vs. 3132 s on WDC denoising). For super-resolution, HIR-Diff surpasses the best supervised method MCNet by 1.1 dB (×2, WDC) to 2.4 dB (×4, Salinas) without any training on the target dataset. For inpainting at high mask rates (0.9), HIR-Diff leads all baselines by 2.7–5.3 dB over the next-best method (DDS2M on Salinas: 37.61 vs. 36.80 dB), and is ~85× faster than DDS2M (16 s vs. 1354 s on Salinas). DIP2d and DIP3d, the earlier unsupervised deep prior methods, are consistently outperformed by 5–10 dB and are 10–1000× slower, confirming the advantage of leveraging a pre-trained diffusion prior over per-image optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work applying pre-trained diffusion models to inverse problems (DDRM, DDS2M) to the hyperspectral domain, where the variable and large number of spectral bands makes direct application infeasible. It is directly inspired by the unsupervised pansharpening framework of Rui et al. [38], which similarly decomposes the target into a low-rank product and uses a pre-trained diffusion model for one factor. The key departure is the use of SVD combined with RRQR factorization to robustly estimate the coefficient matrix from the degraded observation alone, and the design of a TV-regularized guidance function plus an exponential noise schedule tailored to the conditional sampling process, building on DDIM [43] as the base sampler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a single, training-free (per-task) pipeline that handles denoising, super-resolution, and inpainting uniformly, which is practically valuable given the scarcity of clean HSI training pairs. The 5× sampling acceleration via the exponential noise schedule is a useful and well-motivated contribution for real-time or resource-constrained applications. Limitations include the reliance on a pre-trained RGB (3-channel) diffusion model, which constrains the reduced image to K=3 bands and may limit expressiveness for highly diverse spectral content; generalization to other degradation types (e.g., mixed noise, motion blur) or to very high band counts remains untested. An open question is whether the exponential schedule and TV guidance transfer to other conditional diffusion settings beyond HSI restoration.
