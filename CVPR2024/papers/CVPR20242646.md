## Problem

Decentralized deep learning methods achieve competitive accuracy but require communication at every minibatch, creating prohibitive overhead for large pre-trained models in real-world deployments. Existing approaches also degrade significantly under non-IID data distributions and sparse topologies. The paper asks whether advanced model merging techniques—previously used only for combining independently trained models—can replace frequent weight averaging in decentralized learning to reduce communication rounds while preserving convergence guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local subset of RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64) and a pre-trained CNN (VGG16, ResNet-20, or ResNet-50) initialized with 100 epochs of local training. The network topology (ring or fully connected) and merging frequency n are additional inputs. The output is a set of consensus model parameters shared across agents, which then classify images into their respective class labels. Unusually, the merging step consumes intermediate activations from both models to compute layer-wise permutation matrices before any parameter averaging occurs.

## Method

DIMAT alternates between local first-order optimization (SGD, MSGD, or Adam) and a periodic model-merging communication step with neighboring agents, where the merging frequency n is tunable to control communication cost. The key mechanism replaces vanilla weight averaging with activation matching: for each layer, a linear assignment problem is solved to find a permutation matrix that aligns hidden units between two models by maximizing cross-correlation of their activations, after which the permuted weights are averaged using the mixing matrix. This permutation-aware merging enlarges the spectral gap of the effective mixing matrix (ρ′ ≤ ρ), yielding a tighter error bound and faster initial convergence while maintaining the optimal O(√(1/NK) + N/((1−ρ′)K) + N/((1−√ρ′)²K)) sublinear rate for nonconvex objectives. The permutation matrices are time-varying, recomputed at each merging step to adapt to the evolving feature representations of the agents.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-20 and 5 agents, DIMAT achieves 73.99% test accuracy under IID fully-connected topology, matching Weight Averaging (73.97%) while dramatically outperforming SGP (41.39%) and CDSGD (39.69%). Under non-IID ring topology, DIMAT reaches 20.45% accuracy versus only 8.00% for WA and 11.95% for SGP, demonstrating a 12.45-point advantage over the next-best method. With VGG16 on the same dataset, DIMAT attains 68.08% (IID FC) and 52.99% (non-IID FC), consistently exceeding SGP and CDSGD by 30+ percentage points across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIMAT decisively outperforms both SGP and CDSGD across all four evaluation settings (IID/non-IID × FC/Ring), with margins of 20–50 percentage points in test accuracy on CIFAR-100. Against Weight Averaging (WA), DIMAT is essentially tied under IID fully-connected topology (73.99% vs. 73.97% for ResNet-20; 68.08% vs. 66.70% for VGG16) but pulls ahead substantially under ring topology, where the sparser connectivity amplifies the benefit of activation-matching-based merging (69.07% vs. 58.49% for ResNet-20 IID Ring). In the most challenging non-IID ring setting, DIMAT achieves 20.45% versus WA's 8.00% (ResNet-20) and 25.28% versus 25.15% (VGG16), confirming that model merging provides a meaningful advantage when data heterogeneity and sparse communication compound. Weight Matching (WM), the activation-free variant of the same merging idea, performs poorly (38.13% IID FC, 9.37% non-IID FC for VGG16), validating that activation-based permutation alignment is critical. Cross-Gradient Aggregation (CGA) was included as a baseline but did not converge in the explored setting and was therefore omitted from the final tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIMAT sits at the intersection of two research threads: decentralized deep learning (CDSGD, SGP, CGA) that relies on gossip-based weight averaging for consensus, and model merging techniques (Git Re-Basin / activation matching) that align networks via permutation symmetries without retraining. The paper's core departure is replacing the vanilla averaging step in the communication protocol with activation-matching-based merging, which it shows enlarges the spectral gap of the effective mixing matrix (ρ′ ≤ ρ) and thus tightens the convergence bound. Conceptually, this repurposes a technique previously studied only for post-hoc multi-task model combination into an iterative training-time communication operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIMAT offers a practical path toward reducing the communication and computation overhead that currently limits decentralized learning of large pre-trained models in real-world deployments, while retaining provable convergence guarantees. However, several open questions remain: the scalability of activation matching as the number of agents grows, the significant accuracy drop under non-IID data with sparse (ring) topologies, and whether combining merging with quantization can further reduce communication bits. The paper also notes that CGA did not converge in their experimental setup, leaving the comparison with the strongest non-IID baseline incomplete.
