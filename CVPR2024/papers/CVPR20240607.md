## Problem

Existing real-world anime super-resolution methods directly transplant photorealistic SR techniques without accounting for anime-specific production characteristics. The paper identifies two under-addressed challenges: hand-drawn lines that become distorted and faint under video compression, and unwanted color artifacts introduced by GAN training with photorealistic-domain perceptual loss. Additionally, prior work relies on video datasets and networks despite anime production's heavy reuse of identical hand-drawn frames, making temporal modeling unnecessary.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution anime image (extracted as an I-Frame from video sources, rescaled to 720P original production resolution) and produces a 4× upscaled high-resolution anime image. The degradation model operates on the full HR image rather than cropped patches, and the network is a compact 1.03M-parameter GRL variant. Training uses 256×256 HR patches with a batch size of 32 on a single GPU, requiring only 13.3% of the training samples used by AnimeSR.

## Method

APISR combines four key components: (1) an API dataset curated by extracting I-Frames via ffmpeg and selecting the top-10 frames per video using IC9600 image complexity assessment, yielding 3,740 high-complexity images; (2) a prediction-oriented compression module that applies video codecs (H.264, H.265, WebP, AVIF) in single-frame mode to synthesize realistic video compression artifacts without sequential frames, paired with a shuffled resize module that randomly places resize operations in the degradation pipeline; (3) a hand-drawn line enhancement pipeline that applies three rounds of unsharp masking to GT, extracts edges via XDoG with outlier filtering and passive dilation, and merges the sharpened line map back into a pseudo-GT for supervised training; (4) a balanced twin perceptual loss that combines ResNet50 features (pre-trained on the Danbooru anime dataset) with VGG19 features (ImageNet) using a balanced layer-scaling distribution to eliminate color artifacts while preserving photorealistic structural guidance. The GAN is trained in two stages: 300K iterations with L1 loss, then 300K iterations with the twin perceptual loss and adversarial loss.

## Results

On the AVC-RealLQ benchmark (4× scaling), APISR achieves the best performance across all no-reference metrics: NIQE 6.719, MANIQA 0.514, and CLIPIQA 0.711, while using the smallest model size (1.03M parameters). This represents a substantial margin over the closest anime-specific baselines AnimeSR (NIQE 8.109, MANIQA 0.462, CLIPIQA 0.539) and VQD-SR (NIQE 8.202, MANIQA 0.464, CLIPIQA 0.567). Notably, APISR achieves these results with only 13.3% of AnimeSR's and 25% of VQD-SR's training sample complexity, and requires zero training on the degradation model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APISR outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the two anime-specific methods, the gains are most striking: over AnimeSR, APISR improves NIQE by 1.39 points (8.109→6.719), MANIQA by 0.052 (0.462→0.514), and CLIPIQA by 0.172 (0.539→0.711); over VQD-SR, the improvements are NIQE 1.48, MANIQA 0.050, and CLIPIQA 0.144. The photorealistic baselines (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN, RealBasicVSR) are substantially weaker on anime content, with MANIQA scores 0.05–0.15 lower than APISR, confirming that domain-specific design matters. APISR also uses the fewest parameters (1.03M vs. 1.47–16.70M for others) and requires no separate degradation-model training, while achieving results with only 13.3% of AnimeSR's training data. The paper attributes the CLIPIQA gap primarily to the balanced twin perceptual loss eliminating unwanted color artifacts that plague AnimeSR and VQD-SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the real-world anime SR line initiated by AnimeSR (NeurIPS 2022) and VQD-SR (ICCV 2023), but departs from their reliance on video-based datasets and networks by arguing that anime production's frame-reuse practice makes image-based methods sufficient. The degradation model builds on Real-ESRGAN's high-order degradation and VCISR's video compression synthesis, but reimagines the latter as a single-frame prediction-oriented compression module. The balanced twin perceptual loss is novel in combining a photorealistic VGG-19 feature extractor with a ResNet-50 pre-trained on the Danbooru anime dataset, addressing a domain-mismatch issue that prior GAN-based anime SR methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that SOTA anime SR can be achieved with only 13.3% of AnimeSR's training samples and a 1.03M-parameter network (vs. 1.5M) makes anime SR substantially more accessible and efficient. The I-Frame + ICA curation pipeline and prediction-oriented compression module are reusable techniques that could transfer to other stylized-content restoration tasks. However, evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (AVC-RealLQ, 46 clips), and the 720P rescaling assumption may not generalize to all anime productions. Open questions include whether the twin perceptual loss strategy extends to other non-photorealistic domains (e.g., illustration, watercolor) and whether the explicit degradation model's zero-training requirement holds under more diverse real-world degradation distributions.
