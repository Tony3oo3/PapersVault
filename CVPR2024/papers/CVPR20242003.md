## Problem

Existing neural scene reconstruction methods relying on RGB captures fail in poorly-lit or texture-deficient outdoor scenes, while LiDAR-based approaches are constrained by low angular sampling rates that limit geometric detail. Although gated imaging provides robust depth cues independent of ambient illumination, no prior work has integrated the gated image formation model into a differentiable neural rendering pipeline for full 3D scene reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized video sequences from a stereo gated camera (1280×720, 10-bit, 120 Hz) split into three active slices with different gating delays (covering close, mid, and far range segments) plus one passive slice, along with jointly optimized camera poses. It produces a neural field encoding volumetric density, surface normals, scene reflectance, and ambient radiance, from which novel depth maps, novel views, and voxelized 3D occupancy grids can be extracted. The gated slices operate at 808 nm wavelength with 240–370 ns laser pulses, and the method handles scenes up to 160 m range.

## Method

Gated Fields integrates a physics-based gated image formation model—including range intensity profiles, angular-dependent attenuation, and shadow estimation via illuminator-to-point transmittance—directly into a differentiable volume rendering equation. The scene is represented by multi-resolution hash-encoded neural fields that predict density, normals, reflectance, and ambient light, conditioned on a shared spatial embedding, while the illuminator profile (Gaussian beam) and gating parameters (pulse duration, exposure, delay) are learned as explicit physical parameters. A proposal network enables efficient ray sampling, and the full pipeline is trained end-to-end with photometric reconstruction loss, stereo depth regularization, shadow supervision, normal-density consistency, and spatial reflectance smoothness. This joint optimization of scene properties and acquisition parameters eliminates the need for pre-calibrated range intensity profiles and enables accurate reconstruction in both day and night conditions.

## Results

On the Gated Fields Dataset (night sequences), Gated Fields achieves 7.92 m RMSE, 0.12 m MAE, and 97.90% σ3 accuracy for depth synthesis, outperforming the next-best neural field method (LiDAR-NeRF) by 21.87% in MAE and 30.35% in RMSE. For day sequences, it reaches 6.15 m RMSE and 0.09 m MAE. In 3D occupancy reconstruction, Gated Fields achieves 22.25% IoU (vs. 20.03% for LiDAR-NeRF), and in novel view synthesis it attains 32.28 dB PSNR, surpassing K-Planes (27.42 dB day / 19.35 dB night) and Mip-NeRF360 (17.16 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gated Fields outperforms all 15 baselines on depth synthesis in both day and night settings. Against the strongest gated baselines, it beats GatedStereo [90] by 0.08 m MAE (night) and matches it on day (0.09 m), while surpassing Gated2Gated [89] by 0.16 m MAE (night). Among RGB-based feed-forward methods, the best performer CREStereo [50] achieves 0.13 m MAE at night, still 0.01 m worse than Gated Fields; monocular RGB methods (DPT, DepthFormer, AdaBins, SimIPU) all lag by 0.17–0.42 m MAE. RGB+LiDAR neural field methods (StreetSurf, SUDS, NLSPN) improve over pure RGB but remain 0.03–0.06 m MAE behind Gated Fields at night, and their performance degrades significantly in low-light conditions. LiDAR-NeRF [84] is the closest competitor (0.12 m MAE night, 0.10 m day), but Gated Fields still wins on RMSE (7.92 vs 10.21 m night) and all σ-threshold metrics. In 3D occupancy reconstruction, Gated Fields achieves 22.25% IoU vs. 20.03% for LiDAR-NeRF and 8.96% for SUDS, with a substantial recall advantage (66.51% vs. 34.44%). For novel view synthesis, Gated Fields reaches 32.28 dB PSNR, outperforming K-Planes (27.42 dB day, 19.35 dB night) and Mip-NeRF360 (17.16 dB), which struggle to model the gated imaging formation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gated imaging model from Gated2depth [35]—which was designed for feed-forward depth estimation—into a differentiable volume rendering framework inherited from NeRF [62] and Mip-NeRF 360 [7], using multi-resolution hash encoding [64] for spatial representation. The key departure is that prior gated imaging work (Gated2Gated, Gated Stereo) treated gated captures as a depth estimation problem, while prior neural field work (NeRF, StreetSurf, LiDAR-NeRF) operated on RGB or LiDAR inputs; Gated Fields is the first to jointly learn scene geometry, active illumination, and material properties by embedding the full gated image formation physics (range intensity profiles, shadow transmittance, laser cone model) directly into the rendering integral. This positions the work at the intersection of active sensing and neural rendering, rather than as an incremental improvement to either line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving and robotics: RGB-based reconstruction degrades severely at night or in adverse weather, while LiDAR's low angular resolution (128 lines) limits geometric detail. By showing that a single gated stereo camera can outperform both RGB and LiDAR neural field methods by 21.87% MAE in depth and 11% IoU in 3D reconstruction, the paper validates gated imaging as a viable primary sensing modality for large-scale scene reconstruction. Limitations include the requirement for specialized VCSEL-based gated hardware, restriction to static scenes, and per-scene optimization (no feed-forward generalization). Open questions the authors raise include adaptive gating with dynamic feedback and extension to dynamic scenes.
