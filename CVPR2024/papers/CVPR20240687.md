## Problem

Downstream video synthesis tasks (controllable generation, editing, inpainting, outpainting) require either costly training of video foundation models or per-input fine-tuning, while existing training-free image-to-video adaptation methods produce flickering videos due to weak temporal modeling. Moreover, prior approaches are designed for a single task and fail to generalize across different video synthesis objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240687/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts task-specific inputs—text prompts, control conditions (depth maps, canny edges, human pose sequences), source video frames, or binary masks—and produces 8-frame videos at 512×512 resolution with 50 diffusion timesteps and classifier-free guidance scale 7.5. No per-video optimization (e.g., Null-Text Inversion) is required; the pipeline runs in approximately 60 seconds per video on a single GPU, leveraging parallel frame-wise computation in the image model stage.

## Method

BIVDiff is a three-stage sequential pipeline that decouples task-specific generation from temporal coherence: (1) a task-specific image diffusion model (ControlNet, Instruct Pix2Pix, Prompt2Prompt, or Stable Diffusion Inpainting) generates frames independently; (2) Mixed Inversion performs DDIM Inversion with both the image model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) and the video model (VidRD), then takes a weighted sum α·Z_img^T + (1−α)·Z_video^T to bridge the latent distribution gap between the two models; (3) the mixed inverted latents are fed into a text-to-video diffusion model for temporal smoothing. The key design choice is sequential bridging (IDM first, then VDM) rather than alternating or fusing denoising steps, which ablations show preserves both task fidelity and temporal coherence. The mixing ratio α is task-dependent (1.0 for ControlNet, 0.25 for Prompt2Prompt, 0.1 for inpainting), trading off temporal smoothing strength against the VDM's open generation capability.

## Results

On the DAVIS/LOVEU-TGVE benchmark, BIVDiff achieves the best frame consistency in both tasks: 92.67 for controllable video generation (depth control) and 93.50 for video editing, outperforming all baselines. In user study (25 raters, 1–5 scale), BIVDiff obtains the highest average scores of 3.11 (controllable generation) and 2.65 (video editing), with the largest gap in perceived quality (3.38 vs. 2.74 for Text2Video-Zero in generation). Inference time is competitive at 61 s and 64 s per video, far faster than Tune-A-Video (11 min 26 s) and FateZero (221 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In controllable video generation, BIVDiff edges out ControlVideo on frame consistency (92.67 vs. 92.63) and substantially surpasses it in user-study average (3.11 vs. 2.76), while also beating Text2Video-Zero on both frame consistency (92.67 vs. 91.69) and user-study average (3.11 vs. 2.96). In video editing, BIVDiff achieves the highest frame consistency (93.50) by a clear margin over Text2Video-Zero (91.57), FateZero (90.75), and Tune-A-Video (90.46), and leads in user-study average (2.65 vs. 2.38, 2.32, 2.29 respectively). The only metric where a baseline wins is textual alignment in video editing, where Tune-A-Video scores 28.33 vs. BIVDiff's 26.16, though Tune-A-Video requires per-video one-shot tuning and takes over 11 minutes per video. BIVDiff's inference time (61–64 s) is comparable to Text2Video-Zero and ControlVideo, and roughly 10× faster than Tune-A-Video and 3.5× faster than FateZero.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIVDiff builds directly on DDIM Inversion (Song et al., ICLR 2021) as its bridging mechanism, extending it into a novel 'Mixed Inversion' that blends image- and video-model inverted latents to resolve distribution gaps. Conceptually, it departs from prior training-free video synthesis methods (ControlVideo, FateZero) that modify attention operations within a single image model, and from per-input fine-tuning approaches (Tune-A-Video, VideoP2P) that require costly optimization. Instead, BIVDiff treats the image and video diffusion models as fully decoupled black boxes connected only through a latent-space interface, enabling any task-specific IDM (ControlNet, Instruct Pix2Pix, Prompt2Prompt, SD Inpainting) to be paired with any general VDM (VidRD, ZeroScope) without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's plug-and-play design means that as new image diffusion models emerge for specific tasks, they can be immediately adapted to video synthesis without retraining, lowering the barrier to video generation for downstream applications. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 frames at 512×512 resolution, and the mixing ratio α must be manually tuned per task, raising questions about scalability to longer, higher-resolution videos and to newer foundation video models with different latent structures. An open question is whether the distribution-gap problem addressed by Mixed Inversion is fundamental to the image-video latent space mismatch or merely an artifact of the specific model pairs tested (SD 1.5 + VidRD).
