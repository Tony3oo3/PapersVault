## Problem

Existing 3D self-supervised pre-training methods for autonomous driving (contrastive and MAE-based) were adapted from 2D image paradigms and struggle with the inherent sparsity and irregularity of point clouds, while also being modality-specific. No prior work has leveraged 3D differentiable neural rendering as a unified pre-training objective that simultaneously encodes continuous geometric structure and 2D appearance, nor has a single pre-training framework been shown to transfer effectively to both 2D image backbones and 3D point-cloud backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241436/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a masked LiDAR point cloud (voxel size 0.075×0.075×0.2 m, 80% block-wise mask) or masked multi-view images at 1600×900 (30% block-wise mask with sparse convolution on visible regions). Both modalities are converted into a shared 180×180×5 volumetric grid; the rendering decoder then samples 512 rays per view with 96 points per ray to produce rendered RGB and depth maps. The practical output is pre-trained encoder/FPN/view-transformation weights that are transferred to downstream detection or segmentation heads.

## Method

UniPAD masks the input, encodes visible regions with a modality-specific backbone (VoxelNet for points, ConvNeXt for images) plus an FPN, and lifts features into a unified 3D volumetric representation—via direct voxelization for points or Lift-Splat-Shoot for images. A NeuS-style SDF decoder (6-layer MLP) predicts a signed distance field from trilinearly-interpolated voxel features, and a 4-layer RGB MLP conditioned on surface normals and geometry features predicts color; both are integrated along rays with occlusion-aware transmittance weights to render RGB and depth. A depth-aware ray sampling strategy projects LiDAR onto the image plane and restricts sampling to pixels with depth below a threshold, cutting memory while focusing supervision on scene-relevant regions. Training minimizes L1 losses on rendered color and depth, and the resulting pre-trained weights (encoder, FPN, and view transformation) are transferred to downstream 3D detection or segmentation tasks.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, UniPAD achieves 73.2 NDS and 69.9 mAP for multi-modal (LiDAR+Camera) 3D object detection, 70.6 NDS for LiDAR-only detection, and 47.4 NDS for camera-only detection, improving the UVTR baselines by 3.0, 2.9, and 2.4 NDS respectively. For 3D semantic segmentation, UniPAD reaches 79.4 mIoU on the validation set and 81.1 mIoU on the test set, surpassing the SpUNet baseline by 6.1 mIoU. In pre-training comparisons, UniPAD outperforms all competing self-supervised strategies, yielding +7.7 NDS over the best image-based pre-training (3D Detector) and +9.1 NDS over the best point-based pre-training (Contrast-based) on their respective modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For multi-modal detection, UniPAD-M (73.2 NDS) surpasses all prior single-model methods including SparseFusion (72.8), CMT-M (72.9), DeepInteraction (72.6), and ObjectFusion (72.3), while achieving a 3.0 NDS gain over its own UVTR-M baseline. For LiDAR-only detection, UniPAD-L (70.6 NDS) exceeds TransFusion-L (70.1) and VoxelNeXt (68.7), and for camera-only detection, UniPAD-C (47.4 NDS) outperforms BEVFormerV2 (46.7) and CMT-C (46.0). In the pre-training ablation (Tables 3–4), UniPAD dominates all competing self-supervised strategies: it beats the 3D Detector pre-training by 1.2 NDS (32.9 vs 31.7) on camera modality and the Contrast-based method by 6.6 NDS (55.8 vs 49.2) on LiDAR modality. For segmentation, UniPAD (79.4 mIoU val) edges out WaffleIron (79.1) and SphereFormer (78.4), and its test-set score of 81.1 is competitive with SphereFormer's 81.9. The method consistently improves across seven different backbones (ConvNeXt-S/B/L, VoxelNet, SpUNet) and three view transformations (BEVDet, BEVDepth, BEVFormer), with gains ranging from 5.2 to 9.1 NDS, demonstrating broad generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPAD departs from the dominant 2D-inspired pre-training paradigms (contrastive learning and masked autoencoding) by repurposing SDF-based neural rendering (NeuS) as a self-supervised pretext task for autonomous driving. It inherits the masking strategy from MAE and the 2D-to-3D view transformation from Lift-Splat-Shoot, but unifies both LiDAR point clouds and multi-view images under a single volumetric rendering decoder, a combination not previously explored. The color conditioning on surface normals follows UniSurf, while the overall framing—rendering as a representation-learning objective rather than a novel-view-synthesis tool—distinguishes it from prior NeRF-for-driving works like MARS and NeRF-LiDAR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a rendering-based pretext task consistently outperforms contrastive and MAE-based pre-training across seven backbones, two modalities, and two perception tasks, UniPAD offers a practical and modality-agnostic recipe for improving 3D perception in autonomous driving. The memory-efficient depth-aware ray sampling strategy is a useful engineering contribution that makes volumetric rendering tractable for large-scale driving scenes. Key limitations include the memory cost of explicit voxelization at high resolution and the reliance on LiDAR depth for the sampling strategy, which restricts applicability to purely camera-only settings. Future work could explore infusing semantic supervision (e.g., from SAM) into the rendering target to further enrich learned representations.
