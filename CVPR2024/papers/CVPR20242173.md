## Problem

Conventional HDR pipelines fuse multiple LDR exposures in image space using algorithms optimized for human visual perception (smooth tonemapping, noise reduction), which discards information critical for downstream computer vision tasks. This image-space fusion is designed independently of the detection objective, leading to lost detail and low contrast in challenging automotive scenarios such as tunnel entrances, backlights, and mixed indoor/outdoor lighting where luminance ranges can reach 180 dB.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242173/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n (typically 3) 12-bit LDR raw images captured at different exposure shifts (e.g., 2^{-5}, 2^0, 2^5) from an HDR sensor, processes each through a differentiable ISP, and produces bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores for six automotive object classes (Bike, Bus & Truck, Car & Van, Person, Traffic Light, Traffic Sign). Unlike conventional pipelines that produce a single fused HDR image before detection, this approach never reconstructs a merged image; instead it operates on separate feature streams from each exposure throughout the network. The system runs at 57 FPS on an Nvidia Jetson AGX Orin with 111 GFLOPS.

## Method

The core contribution is a local cross-attention fusion module that replaces image-space exposure fusion with feature-domain fusion inside the detector. At each spatial location (r,c) of the feature map, the module computes attention weights across the n exposure feature maps using learned query vectors Q and projected keys (via a learned W^K matrix), with a key modification: the softmax is normalized over both the query and exposure axes (2D softmax) rather than only the exposure axis as in standard attention. The fused feature map is a weighted sum of the per-exposure features, and the entire pipeline—exposure control, differentiable ISP, shared-weight feature extraction (ResNet-28), fusion, and Faster-RCNN heads—is trained end-to-end with standard detection losses. This adds only 16,896 parameters (d=128, q=4) and achieves 44.2 mAP, outperforming the best image-space HDR baseline (Raw HDR) by 2.7% mAP and Deep HDR by 2.3% mAP.

## Results

The proposed Local Cross-Attention Fusion achieves 44.2 mAP on the automotive HDR test set (1996 annotated frames across sunny, cloud/rain, backlight, tunnel, dusk, and night conditions), outperforming the strongest image-space HDR baseline (Hanji et al. PPNE, 43.1 mAP) by 1.1% and the two primary HDR baselines Raw HDR (41.5 mAP) and Deep HDR (41.9 mAP) by 2.7% and 2.3% respectively. The method runs at 57 FPS (111 GFLOPS) on an Nvidia Jetson AGX Orin with 3 exposures, adding only 16,896 parameters over the single-exposure pipeline. Gains are consistent across all six object classes, with the largest improvements on Traffic Sign (+3.5% over PPNE) and Traffic Light (+0.5% over PPNE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Local Cross-Attention Fusion (44.2 mAP) outperforms all five external baselines. Against the two primary HDR image-space fusion methods, it gains +2.7% mAP over Raw HDR (41.5) and +2.3% over Deep HDR (41.9), with the largest per-class gains on Traffic Sign (+3.5% over Deep HDR) and Traffic Light (+1.0% over Deep HDR). It also surpasses the pre-ISP noise-aware merging method of Hanji et al. (PPNE, 43.1 mAP) by 1.1% and the classical Debevec-Malik fusion (41.7 mAP) by 2.5%. Compared to the closest LDR baseline (Onzon et al. neural auto-exposure, 41.1 mAP), the feature-domain fusion adds 3.1% mAP, confirming that multi-exposure feature fusion captures information lost in single-exposure capture. The method also beats the gradient-based LDR auto-exposure of Shim et al. (14.6 mAP) by a wide margin of 29.6% mAP, though that baseline uses a fundamentally different (and weaker) exposure strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the conventional HDR pipeline (multi-exposure capture → image-space fusion → ISP → detection) by moving the fusion step into the feature domain, where a novel local cross-attention module combines per-exposure feature maps before the detection heads. It builds directly on the authors' prior neural exposure control work [31] for auto-exposure and adapts the query-key-value attention mechanism from [37] with a key modification: a 2D softmax normalization across both query and exposure axes, tailored to the fusion objective. The approach is positioned against both classical HDR fusion (Debevec & Malik, noise-optimal merging) and learned image-space HDR (Deep HDR), arguing that image-space fusion optimized for human perception discards information critical for downstream detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For autonomous driving and outdoor robotics, the 2.7% mAP gain over the best conventional HDR baseline in challenging tunnel/backlight scenarios translates to fewer missed detections in safety-critical situations, and the 57 FPS runtime on Jetson AGX Orin demonstrates practical deployability. The end-to-end, task-driven formulation opens a path toward co-optimizing sensor readout, ISP, and perception jointly, which the authors flag as future work alongside extension to segmentation and optical flow. Limitations include evaluation on a single automotive dataset and sensor, a single detector architecture (Faster-RCNN), and the relatively small gap between the proposed method and simple max-pooling fusion (1.0% mAP), suggesting the attention mechanism's advantage may be narrower than the headline number implies.
