## Problem

Existing camera-based 3D perception tasks—object detection, BEV semantic segmentation, depth estimation, and vector map construction—each address only a narrow facet of scene understanding, preventing a unified end-to-end solution. Moreover, lifting 2D BEV features to 3D loses critical geometry (height, shape), while naively predicting dense 3D occupancy incurs prohibitive memory costs. The paper tackles both gaps by introducing camera-based 3D panoptic segmentation as a new paradigm that jointly models detection and segmentation within a single occupancy representation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images from 6 surround cameras (360° horizontal FOV) at the current frame plus k history frames, each cropped to 640×1600 (or 320×800 with R50). It outputs a dense 3D panoptic voxel volume at 200×200×16 resolution (0.256 m × 0.256 m × 0.125 m per voxel) with per-voxel semantic labels and instance IDs for thing classes, alongside 3D bounding boxes. The same architecture can be reconfigured for dense occupancy prediction at 0.4 m isotropic voxel size on the Occ3D benchmark.

## Method

PanoOcc replaces 2D BEV queries with a 3D grid of learnable voxel queries (50×50×16) that explicitly encode height, then aggregates multi-view and multi-frame image features via voxel self-attention, deformable cross-attention, and 3D voxel alignment for temporal fusion. A coarse-to-fine decoder upsamples the coarse voxel features to 200×200×16 through 3D deconvolutions, avoiding expensive high-resolution 3D convolutions while preserving accuracy. An optional occupancy sparsify module learns a binary occupancy mask to prune empty voxels (retaining ~5% of the grid) and switches subsequent layers to sparse convolutions, cutting memory from 15 G to 9 G. Detection and segmentation are trained jointly end-to-end: a voxel-selection head propagates foreground information from the semantic branch to the detection branch, and a post-hoc Refine module uses high-confidence 3D boxes to assign instance IDs, producing the final panoptic output.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set for 3D semantic segmentation, PanoOcc achieves 71.6 mIoU with R101-DCN and 74.5 mIoU with InternImage-XL, setting new state-of-the-art for camera-based methods and approaching LiDAR-based performance (RPVNet: 77.6 mIoU). For 3D occupancy prediction on Occ3D-nuScenes, PanoOcc reaches 42.13 mIoU (with camera visibility mask), surpassing the previous best BEVFormer* (39.24 mIoU) by 2.9 points. For 3D panoptic segmentation, PanoOcc achieves PQ 62.1 and PQ† 66.2, matching LiDAR-based EfficientLPS (PQ 62.0) while using only camera input, though detection mAP (48.4) remains below LiDAR methods (63.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against camera-based semantic segmentation baselines, PanoOcc (R101-DCN) outperforms OccFormer by 1.2 mIoU (71.6 vs. 70.4), TPVFormer by 2.7 mIoU (71.6 vs. 68.9), and BEVFormer by 15.4 mIoU (71.6 vs. 56.2), with the largest gains on small/irregular objects like traffic cone (+3.2) and motorcycle (+5.5). With the larger InternImage-XL backbone, PanoOcc reaches 74.5 mIoU, narrowing the gap to the best LiDAR method RPVNet (77.6) to just 3.1 points. On Occ3D-nuScenes occupancy prediction, PanoOcc* (42.13 mIoU) beats BEVFormer* (39.24) by 2.9 mIoU and CTF-Occ (28.53) by 13.6 mIoU, with particularly strong gains on dynamic classes (car: 49.44 vs. 47.57; pedestrian: 33.26 vs. 28.85). For panoptic segmentation, PanoOcc (PQ 62.1) matches LiDAR-based EfficientLPS (PQ 62.0) but trails the strongest LiDAR method LidarMulitiNet (PQ 81.8) primarily due to weaker detection (mAP 48.4 vs. 63.8), indicating the segmentation quality is competitive while detection remains the bottleneck. The efficiency comparison (Table 6) shows PanoOcc uses 24G training memory vs. TPVFormer's 33.5G while achieving higher mIoU (71.6 vs. 68.9) and faster inference (203ms vs. 268ms), despite having an additional detection branch.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoOcc extends BEVFormer's query-based feature transformation paradigm from 2D BEV queries to 3D voxel queries, adding voxel self-attention, voxel cross-attention, and 3D temporal alignment in place of 2D BEV alignment. It departs from TPVFormer's tri-plane decomposition by demonstrating that a full 3D voxel representation, combined with coarse-to-fine upsampling, is both more accurate and more memory-efficient than splitting into three 2D planes. The occupancy sparsify module borrows sparse convolution techniques from LiDAR-based perception (SECOND, Super Sparse 3D) and applies them to camera-derived voxel features, while the joint detection-segmentation training framework unifies tasks that prior camera-based methods (BEVFormer, TPVFormer, OccFormer) handle independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PanoOcc establishes camera-based 3D panoptic segmentation as a viable end-to-end task, showing that a single unified occupancy representation can simultaneously support dense semantic segmentation, instance-level object discrimination, and 3D detection—something previously only achievable with separate pipelines or LiDAR input. The remaining gap to LiDAR-based panoptic methods (48.4 vs. 63.8 mAP) highlights that camera-only detection remains the bottleneck, and the slight mIoU drop from occupancy sparsify (0.654→0.639) suggests the sparsity threshold is not yet optimally tuned. Open questions include whether the unified representation generalizes to more complex urban scenes, whether the coarse-to-fine scheme scales to higher resolutions needed for long-range perception, and whether the joint learning benefit persists with larger backbones or more diverse datasets.
