## Problem

Existing video object state change (OSC) approaches assume a closed vocabulary, where each state transition is tied to only 1–2 objects seen during training, preventing generalization to novel objects. This paper introduces an open-world formulation that requires temporally localizing the three OSC states (initial, transitioning, end) for objects never encountered during training, while also handling passive and off-camera transformations that prior work overlooks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240515/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video sampled at 1 fps, encoded by a frozen InternVideo backbone, and augments each frame's global feature with a localized object-centric feature from an off-the-shelf detector's bounding box. The OSC category (e.g., 'chicken + shredding') is assumed known at inference time, and the model outputs a per-frame classification into four classes: initial, transitioning, end, or background. No text input is required at test time, making the model purely video-based despite relying on text and VLMs during training.

## Method

VIDOSC combines three architectural techniques for object-agnostic state prediction: (1) a shared state vocabulary that collapses the label space from 3×(object×transition) pairs down to 3 states per transition, forcing the model to learn state representations invariant to object identity; (2) a 3-layer transformer encoder with sinusoidal positional embeddings followed by a 1-layer MLP decoder, enabling each frame's prediction to attend to the full temporal context; and (3) object-centric input features formed by concatenating global and detector-localized features to emphasize the changing object. Training is supervised entirely by pseudo-labels generated via a VLM (CLIP or VideoCLIP) that scores each frame against three textual state descriptions, with a causal-ordering constraint and ambiguity threshold to filter unreliable labels. OSC training examples are automatically mined from HowTo100M ASR transcriptions using LLAMA2, eliminating the need for manual annotation at scale.

## Results

On the closed-world ChangeIt benchmark, VIDOSC achieves 0.57 State Prec.@1 and 0.84 Action Prec.@1, surpassing the best prior method MultiTaskChange (0.49 / 0.80) by 8% and 4% respectively. On the open-world ChangeIt split, VIDOSC reaches 0.48 State Prec.@1 on novel OSCs, nearly doubling the 0.25 of the next-best baseline (LookForTheChange). On the new HowToChange dataset, VIDOSC attains 60.7% Prec.@1 on known and 58.2% on novel OSCs, outperforming the strongest VLM baseline VideoCLIP (48.3% / 44.8%) by over 12 points, while maintaining only a 2.5% known-to-novel gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIDOSC outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the self-supervised baselines, VIDOSC gains 8% State Prec.@1 over MultiTaskChange [51] on closed-world ChangeIt (0.57 vs 0.49) and a dramatic 26-point gain on novel OSCs in the open-world split (0.48 vs 0.22), where MultiTaskChange suffers a 19% known-to-novel drop versus VIDOSC's 8%. Against the zero-shot VLM baselines, VIDOSC surpasses VideoCLIP [61] by 12.4 points on HowToChange Prec.@1 for known OSCs (60.7% vs 48.3%) and by 13.4 points for novel OSCs (58.2% vs 44.8%), demonstrating that learned temporal modeling and object-agnostic state prediction substantially exceed what frozen vision-language features can achieve. CLIP [44] and InternVideo [59] trail even further, with CLIP achieving only 47.5% Prec.@1 on HowToChange known OSCs. Notably, VIDOSC narrows the known-to-novel performance gap to just 2.5% on HowToChange (60.7% vs 58.2%), compared to 4.1% for MultiTaskChange and 3.5% for VideoCLIP, validating the shared state vocabulary design for open-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the closed-world video OSC line (LookForTheChange, MultiTaskChange, Alayrac et al.) in three fundamental ways: it introduces an open-world formulation where the model must generalize state localization to objects never seen in training, it replaces frame-wise modeling with a transformer encoder-decoder that captures temporal progression, and it adopts an object-centric perspective that handles passive transformations (melting, drying) without requiring a visible human actor. The training pipeline repurposes LLMs (LLAMA2) for OSC mining from ASR transcriptions and VLMs (CLIP, VideoCLIP) for pseudo-label generation, borrowing the web-scale text-supervision paradigm from image classification but applying it to temporal video state localization. The HowToChange dataset extends the HowTo100M collection with fine-grained temporal OSC annotations, filling a gap left by ChangeIt's limited vocabulary (44 OSCs, 1–2 objects per transition).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HowToChange is the first benchmark that meaningfully tests open-world generalization for video OSCs, with 9.3× more OSC categories and 20.5 objects per state transition on average, making it a critical resource for the field. The VLM pseudo-labeling pipeline offers a practical recipe for scaling OSC training without exhaustive manual annotation, a technique transferable to other weakly-supervised video tasks. However, the model still assumes the OSC category is known at test time (e.g., from a recognition system), and the 2.5% known-novel gap, while small, indicates that full open-world generalization remains unsolved. The authors note future directions including concurrent OSC processes and spatial understanding, suggesting the current formulation is a first step toward comprehensive video state reasoning.
