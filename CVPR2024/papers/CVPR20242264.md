## Problem

Existing VLM pruning methods are task-specific, requiring the dense model to be re-pruned from scratch for every new downstream task, which is time-consuming and assumes the full parameter set remains stored. This paper formalizes Task-Agnostic Vision-Language Pruning (TA-VLP): finding a single pruned subnetwork that retains transferability to multiple unknown vision-language tasks without any task-specific priors or gradient feedback during pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242264/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained VLM (BLIP_BASE or XVLM_CLIP) and a small calibration set of image-text pairs (~5% of the standard 4M pretraining corpus, sampled from CC3M and VisualGenome with VQA pairs excluded to avoid data leakage). It produces a single binary parameter mask of fixed sparsity (63%, 75%, or 90%) that, when applied to the dense weights, yields a sparse VLM ready for fine-tuning on any downstream task. No gradients are computed at any point; only forward passes over the calibration data are required.

## Method

MULTIFLOW models each linear layer as a directed bipartite graph where nodes are activations and edges are parameters, then scores each parameter by combining its magnitude with the saliency of the input and output nodes it connects (input saliency = average emitted signal, output saliency = average received signal, both estimated via activation norms over calibration data). The key differentiator is a multimodal-aware distribution step: rather than applying a global top-k threshold, the method computes per-layer, per-modality pruning budgets from the emergent magnitude distribution of pretrained weights, then selects the top-k parameters within each layer-modality group using the flow-based scores. This prevents layer collapse and modality bias that arise from naive global ranking. The entire procedure is gradient-free, relying solely on forward passes, and the same saliency scores can be reused across different sparsity targets without recomputation.

## Results

On Image-Text Retrieval (MSCOCO), MULTIFLOW achieves the best performance across all metrics and sparsity levels for both XVLM_CLIP and BLIP_BASE. At 75% sparsity with XVLM, MULTIFLOW reaches TR@1 = 56.94% and IR@1 = 73.87%, outperforming the second-best CHITA++ (54.32% / 70.33%) by +2.62% and +3.54% respectively. At 63% sparsity, the gap is smaller (+0.76% TR@1, +0.90% IR@1 over CHITA++). On VQA and Image Captioning, MULTIFLOW is the best performer in 3 of 4 instances, with the gap widening at higher sparsity (e.g., +1.06% BLEU@4 and +4.47 CIDEr over the second-best at 75% for XVLM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MULTIFLOW consistently outperforms all five baselines on ITR for both models at both sparsity levels. Against CHITA++ (the strongest second-order baseline), MULTIFLOW leads by +0.76%/+0.90% (TR@1/IR@1) at 63% and +2.62%/+3.54% at 75% on XVLM, with the gap widening as sparsity increases. OMP (magnitude-only) is the second-best for BLIP at 63% but falls behind MULTIFLOW by ~1% on TR@1; at 75% MULTIFLOW clearly dominates. SNIP and ITERSNIP (gradient-based PaI methods) perform well on XVLM but catastrophically fail on BLIP (near-random scores), highlighting architecture sensitivity. LAMP (layer-adaptive magnitude) also collapses for BLIP at 75% sparsity (TR@1 ≈ 1.5%), while CHITA (non-iterative Hessian-based) similarly fails for BLIP at 75%. The paper notes there is no fixed second-best method across settings, reinforcing that task-agnostic pruning requires a dedicated approach rather than repurposing task-specific or unimodal methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing VLM pruning methods (UPop, EfficientVLM) that are inherently task-specific, and instead formalizes Task-Agnostic Vision-Language Pruning (TA-VLP) as a new problem: prune once, transfer to multiple unknown tasks. Methodologically, it extends the pruning-at-initialization (PaI) lineage (SNIP, its iterative variant) by replacing gradient-based saliency with a gradient-free information-flow score computed over a bipartite graph of activations and parameters, adapting the activation-norm technique from LLM pruning (Wanda) to the multimodal setting. The key conceptual departure is the multimodal distribution prior: rather than applying a uniform top-k mask, MULTIFLOW uses the emergent per-modality weight-magnitude distribution from pretraining to allocate layer-wise sparsity, addressing depth and modality biases that cause model collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic benchmark of eight pruning algorithms under the TA-VLP setting, revealing that no single competitor consistently ranks second across models, tasks, and sparsity levels — evidence that ad-hoc task-agnostic methods are needed. A notable open question is the large remaining gap to dense performance (e.g., ~40% drop in retrieval recall at 90% sparsity), suggesting that preserving transferable multimodal representations under extreme compression is still unsolved. The finding that architecture design (explicit modality disentanglement in XVLM vs. interleaved encoder-decoder in BLIP) dramatically affects prunability opens a direction for designing inherently prunable VLMs. The 41× speed advantage over CHITA++ while matching or exceeding its accuracy makes the gradient-free approach practically attractive for large-scale VLM deployment.
