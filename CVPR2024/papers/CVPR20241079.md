## Problem

Object removal in Multiplane Images (MPI) for novel view synthesis remains unexplored, and naive inpainting strategies (pre-inpaint, post-inpaint, layer-inpaint) produce inconsistent results due to multiple inpainting passes across views or depth layers. Additionally, MPI's high-dimensional structure makes it inaccessible for real-time user interaction on social media platforms, where users need to directly annotate unwanted objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241079/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a calibrated stereo image pair (512×512 for RealEstate10K, 640×360 for UCSD) to generate a 32-layer MPI, then encodes it into a single RGB embedding image where the user draws one free-form mask over the unwanted object. The output is a modified MPI with the object removed, from which novel views can be rendered without per-scene training. The entire pipeline requires only a single inpainting pass on the 2D embedding image rather than repeated inpainting across multiple views or depth layers.

## Method

CORE-MPI encodes MPI (color + alpha across 32 depth layers) into a standard RGB embedding image via a U-Net-based steganography network, enabling single-mask object removal in a user-friendly 2D space. A dual-network inpainting architecture (both using LaMa) handles the removal: one branch restores the reference image for color fidelity, while the other inpaints the embedding image to preserve hidden geometric information, with a learned fusion module blending the two outputs. Training introduces a pseudo-reference loss that uses a pretrained inpainting network's output as a soft ground truth to stabilize color recovery on large masks, and a disparity consistency loss that directly supervises geometric fidelity by comparing disparity maps before and after removal. A GAN discriminator conditioned on the disparity map further enforces geometric coherence in the inpainted region. The pipeline is trained in two stages (embedding+restoration, then inpainting+restoration) and requires no per-scene training, achieving sub-second inference.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, CORE-MPI achieves the best PSNR (29.79 dB), SSIM (0.943), LPIPS (0.0628), and FID (59.62) among all methods, outperforming the strongest baseline Layer-inpaint (28.70 dB, 0.936, 0.0710, 65.87) by roughly 1.1 dB in PSNR and 6.3 in FID. On UCSD, CORE-MPI leads with PSNR 38.37 dB, SSIM 0.983, LPIPS 0.0220, and FID 43.83, surpassing Pre-inpaint (36.84 dB, 0.976, 0.0290, 46.16). In the cross-method comparison against SPIn-NeRF on 10 scenes, CORE-MPI achieves a mean LPIPS of 0.1300 vs. 0.2551 and FID of 110.74 vs. 184.90, winning on every individual scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four MPI-specific baselines (Pre-inpaint, Post-inpaint, Layer-inpaint, and the embedding ablations), CORE-MPI achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID on both RealEstate10K and UCSD. The closest competitor is Layer-inpaint, which edges out CORE-MPI on disparity L1/L2 errors on RealEstate10K (0.0410/0.0560 vs. 0.0459/0.0648), but CORE-MPI still leads on all rendering quality metrics by a clear margin. On UCSD, Pre-inpaint has a marginally lower disparity L1 (0.0141 vs. 0.0149), yet CORE-MPI dominates on every other metric. In the direct comparison with SPIn-NeRF (the only NeRF-based object-removal baseline), CORE-MPI wins on all 10 scenes in both LPIPS and FID, with an average LPIPS improvement of 0.125 (0.1300 vs. 0.2551) and FID improvement of 74.16 (110.74 vs. 184.90), while also requiring no per-scene training and running in under one second per inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three previously separate threads: MPI-based novel view synthesis (StereoMag, LLFF), steganographic embedding of 3D data into 2D images (Wu et al.'s JPEG-embedded novel views), and GAN-based image inpainting (LaMa). It departs from the dominant NeRF-based object removal line (SPIn-NeRF, OR-NeRF) by operating entirely in MPI space, which eliminates the need for scene-specific training. The key conceptual move is repurposing steganography not merely for transmission but as an interaction surface: the embedding image becomes the canvas on which a user draws a single mask, and the inpainting network must simultaneously restore visible color and hidden geometric (disparity) information — a problem no prior inpainting work was designed to address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first systematic study of object removal in MPI, the paper establishes baselines (pre-inpaint, post-inpaint, layer-inpaint) and shows that all naive strategies fail due to cross-view or cross-layer inconsistencies, validating the need for the embedding-image formulation. The practical implication for social media is significant: a user can interact with a standard RGB image in real time, and the system reconstructs a clean MPI without per-scene training. However, the authors acknowledge that the inpainting network does not explicitly model disparity internally; the geometric fidelity relies on an external consistency loss and a dual-branch fusion, leaving open the question of whether a natively geometry-aware inpainting model could outperform this dual-network strategy.
