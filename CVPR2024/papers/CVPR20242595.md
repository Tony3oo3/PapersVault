## Problem

LiDAR-Camera (LC) fusion is the top-performing sensor configuration for 3D object detection, but LiDAR's high cost limits its deployment in consumer vehicles. Camera-Radar (CR) fusion is more accessible yet underperforms LC by a significant margin, and existing knowledge distillation methods lack a fusion-to-fusion distillation path that accounts for the large domain gap between LiDAR and radar measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242595/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the student model consumes 6 camera images at 256×704 resolution and a sparse radar point cloud (with per-point velocity measurements from the Doppler effect), producing 3D bounding boxes with class labels over a 108m×108m×8m detection region in a 180×180 BEV grid. During training, the frozen LC teacher additionally ingests a dense LiDAR point cloud, but this modality is entirely absent at test time. The radar branch uses PointPillars encoding while the camera branch uses a Swin-T backbone with perspective-to-BEV view transformation.

## Method

CRKD unifies both the LC teacher and CR student in a shared BEV feature space (built on BEVFusion with a gated adaptive fusion network) and designs four distillation losses tailored to the cross-modality gap. Cross-Stage Radar Distillation (CSRD) supervises the sparse radar features against the teacher's scene-level objectness heatmap through a learned calibration module, rather than direct feature imitation. Mask-Scaling Feature Distillation (MSFD) performs foreground-aware feature alignment where the mask is expanded based on object range and velocity to compensate for BEV view-transformation misalignment. Relation Distillation (RelD) enforces scene-level geometric consistency by matching cosine-similarity affinity matrices at multiple scales. Response Distillation (RespD) applies class-specific weighted soft-label supervision, assigning higher weights to dynamic classes to exploit radar's direct velocity measurements. The student is trained for 20 epochs with the teacher frozen, combining all four KD losses with the standard detection loss.

## Results

On the nuScenes val split, CRKD (SwinT backbone, 256×704 resolution) achieves 46.7 mAP and 57.3 NDS, improving over the BEVFusion-CR baseline (43.2 mAP, 54.1 NDS) by +3.5 mAP and +3.2 NDS. On the test split, CRKD reaches 48.7 mAP and 58.7 NDS, the best NDS among all compared methods and second-best mAP (behind only RCM-Fusion at 49.3 mAP with a larger R101 backbone and 900×1600 resolution). Per-class AP analysis shows the largest gains on dynamic classes (e.g., +6.3 on Truck, +4.8 on Motorcycle), confirming that CRKD effectively leverages radar's velocity measurements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRKD (SwinT, 256×704) ties with X3KD(L2CR) SwinT on the val split (both 46.7 mAP, 57.3 NDS) but substantially outperforms it on the test split (48.7 vs 44.1 mAP; 58.7 vs 55.3 NDS), where X3KD only reports the R50 variant. Against the direct BEVFusion-CR baseline (same architecture, no KD), CRKD gains +3.5 mAP and +3.2 NDS on val. On the test split, CRKD achieves the best NDS (58.7) among all methods, surpassing RCM-Fusion (58.0) despite using a smaller backbone (SwinT vs R101) and lower resolution (256×704 vs 900×1600); RCM-Fusion leads on mAP by 0.6 points. Among camera-only distillation methods (UVTR, BEVDistill, UniDistill, X3KD-LC2C, DistillBEV, BEVSimDet), CRKD leads on both mAP and NDS on the val split, with the closest competitor being X3KD(LC2C) at 39.0 mAP / 50.5 NDS. The per-class breakdown shows CRKD's advantage is most pronounced on dynamic classes (Truck +6.3, Motorcycle +4.8, Bicycle +4.4 over baseline), validating the class-weighted RespD design that prioritizes radar's velocity advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRKD extends the cross-modality knowledge distillation line of work (UniDistill, DistillBEV, X3KD) that previously supported single-modality-to-single-modality or fusion-to-single-modality paths (L2C, C2L, LC2C) by introducing the first fusion-to-fusion KD path from a LiDAR-Camera teacher to a Camera-Radar student. The architecture is built directly on BEVFusion, with the addition of a gated adaptive fusion network inspired by prior work on modality weighting. The four distillation losses draw on established ideas—relation distillation from MonoDistill, response distillation from CMKD—but are specifically redesigned to handle the large domain gap between LiDAR and radar point clouds (sparsity, noise, object-level vs. geometry-level semantics) and the range/velocity-induced misalignment in BEV view transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRKD addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: LiDAR cost prevents LC fusion from reaching consumer vehicles, while CR sensors are already ubiquitous. By using LiDAR only during training, CRKD offers a path to near-LC performance with a low-cost, weather-robust sensor suite at inference. The BEV-space KD framework and the four loss designs are modular and could transfer to other sensor-pair KD configurations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond nuScenes, whether it extends to other perception tasks (the authors mention occupancy mapping), and whether the performance gap to LC can be closed further as CR architectures mature.
