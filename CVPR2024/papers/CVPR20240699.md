## Problem

Existing methods for reconstructing clothed humans from monocular videos either bake albedo, material, and lighting into a single entangled neural representation, preventing relighting and material editing, or they approximate secondary shading effects (shadows, indirect illumination) via learned MLPs that are over-smoothed and fail to generalize to novel poses. The paper addresses this by explicitly modeling secondary shading through Monte-Carlo ray tracing combined with volumetric scattering, enabling physically based inverse rendering that generalizes to arbitrary poses and lighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video of a clothed human (with estimated SMPL pose parameters) and produces a fully disentangled avatar: an SDF-based geometry, spatially varying albedo, roughness and metallic material maps, and an environment lighting map. Geometry reconstruction completes in under 30 minutes, and full training (including PBR loss) takes 4 hours on a single RTX 3090. The resulting avatar can be animated to novel poses and relit under arbitrary environment maps without retraining.

## Method

The approach represents the clothed human as an articulated NeRF using iNGP hash grids with SDF for geometry and a separate hash grid for radiance/material, with Fast-SNARF providing inverse LBS for canonical-space warping. The key insight is modeling rendering as volumetric scattering rather than surface-based rendering: the phase function and volumetric albedo are jointly parameterized by a simplified Disney BRDF (albedo, roughness, metallic), which naturally handles edges and boundaries where surface normals are ill-defined. Secondary rays are traced in canonical space using a hybrid ray-marching scheme that first performs a parallel zero-crossing search over 64 uniform samples to locate the SDF surface, then accumulates importance weights only beyond that point, avoiding the non-convergence issues of sphere tracing under articulation. Training uses L1 losses on both the radiance field and PBR rendering paths, with eikonal and curvature regularization on the SDF, and 512 uniformly sampled secondary rays per primary ray during training (1024 with light importance sampling for relighting).

## Results

On the RANA dataset, IntrinsicAvatar achieves an albedo PSNR of 22.83, SSIM of 0.8816, and LPIPS of 0.1617, substantially outperforming R4D (18.24 / 0.7780 / 0.2414) and R4D* (18.23 / 0.8254 / 0.2043). The normal estimation error drops to 9.96°, representing a 77% reduction over R4D (42.69°) and a 64% reduction over R4D* (27.38°). For relighting on novel poses, the method achieves PSNR 18.18, SSIM 0.8722, and LPIPS 0.1279, while R4D* fails to produce reasonable results due to its inability to generalize to unseen poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only quantitative baseline is Relighting4D (R4D) [15], the sole publicly available method for physically based inverse rendering of clothed humans under unknown illumination without pre-training on ground truth materials. IntrinsicAvatar wins decisively on every metric: it reduces normal estimation error by 77% (42.69° → 9.96°) over R4D and by 64% (27.38° → 9.96°) over the improved R4D* variant that adds a mask loss. Albedo quality improves by roughly 4.6 dB in PSNR over both baselines. The most striking gap appears in novel-pose relighting: R4D* relies on learned visibility MLPs that are over-smoothed and cannot generalize to unseen poses, producing poor relighting results, whereas IntrinsicAvatar's explicit Monte-Carlo ray tracing in canonical space generalizes naturally to arbitrary poses without additional training. The paper also notes that RANA [28] was excluded as a quantitative baseline because it pre-trains on ground truth albedo, which is unavailable in the monocular setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of articulated neural radiance fields (building on Fast-SNARF, SMPL-based LBS, and iNGP/VolSDF for fast geometry) and physically based inverse rendering (building on NeRFactor, TensoIR, and InvRender for static scenes). It departs from prior human inverse rendering methods (Relighting4D, RANA, [70]) that approximate secondary shading via learned MLPs or simplified spherical models, instead employing explicit Monte-Carlo ray tracing in canonical space. The key conceptual departure is modeling the human body as a volumetric scattering medium rather than a surface, which naturally handles edges and boundaries where surface-based rendering gives ambiguous predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to combine explicit ray tracing with body articulation for physically based inverse rendering of dynamic clothed humans from monocular video, enabling relighting and re-posing without retraining or additional pose-specific visibility networks. The practical impact is significant for AR/VR applications requiring editable, relightable human avatars from casual video. Limitations include dependence on SMPL alignment (which introduces artifacts on the RANA dataset), single-subject scope, and 4-hour per-subject training time. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex clothing (e.g., flowing fabrics), multi-person scenes, and real-time rendering.
