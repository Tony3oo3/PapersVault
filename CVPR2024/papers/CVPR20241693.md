## Problem

Model stealing from black-box MLaaS models is constrained by the high cost and limited availability of real samples, while data-free methods require tens of millions of queries for high-dimensional inputs and suffer from GAN model collapse. Existing approaches treat each real sample as a single query point, ignoring the rich decision-boundary information encoded in the sample gradient that could be extracted at low additional query cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241693/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of real RGB images (10k–20k, e.g., 224×224) and queries a black-box victim model (ResNet-34) with superpixel-level perturbed versions of each image, requiring only ~132k–271k total queries versus ~1M+ for data-free baselines. It produces a trained proxy ResNet-34 model whose predictions and per-superpixel gradient responses mimic the victim, along with purified superpixel gradient maps that serve as the distillation target during proxy training.

## Method

SPSG comprises two modules. The SuperPixel Gradient Query (SPGQ) module segments each image into superpixels (via QuickShift, SLIC, or Felzenszwalb) and applies forward-difference perturbations at the superpixel level rather than pixel level, reducing per-image queries from ~10⁶ to ~10² while keeping pairwise image distances within the Gaussian range that evades Prada detection. The Sample Gradient Purification (SGP) module then filters each channel's gradients by retaining only values exceeding β·max (for positives) or below β·min (for negatives, β=0.5), zeroing the rest, and normalizing survivors to [0,1] to suppress variance from redundant neurons and cross-model numerical discrepancies. Training uses a composite loss: L_prob (cross-entropy matching of proxy vs. victim outputs) plus L_grad, where L_grad combines per-superpixel output matching on perturbed images (L_grad,1) with cosine similarity between the purified victim superpixel gradients and the proxy model's pixel gradients averaged over each superpixel region (L_grad,2).

## Results

On CUB-200-2011 with 20k real samples (probability query), SPSG achieves 67.48% agreement and 61.21% test accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (DS) by 10.44 and 4.82 percentage points respectively, while using only 271k queries versus ~1000k for data-free methods. On Indoor Scenes with 20k samples, SPSG reaches 70.11% agreement and 70.27% accuracy, outperforming DFMS (68.14%/67.18%) by ~2 points. In the hard-label setting on CUB-200 with 20k samples, SPSG achieves 34.66% agreement and 34.12% accuracy, beating the best baseline (Black-Box Dissector) by 3.07 and 3.66 points. Adversarial transferability (ASR) on CUB-200 also favors SPSG, reaching 39.68% under PGD (probability) versus 39.51% for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPSG outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and sample budget tested. Among data-free MS methods, SPSG leads by 5–12 points in agreement and 4–8 points in accuracy on CUB-200 and Indoor Scenes, while requiring roughly 25% of the query volume (e.g., 271k vs. 997k–1489k queries at 20k samples). Among data-driven MS methods (Knockoff, ActiveThief, Black-Box Dissector, InverseNet), SPSG achieves 4–12 points higher agreement and 3–7 points higher accuracy on Caltech256 and Diabetic Retinopathy, with the gap widening as real samples increase. The closest competitor is DS (dual student) on CUB-200 probability, which reaches 56.39% accuracy at 20k samples versus SPSG's 61.21%; the paper notes that DS would need at least 20k additional real samples to match SPSG's 10k-sample performance. In the hard-label setting, SPSG maintains its lead over all baselines, with the largest margin over Knockoff (34.12% vs. 25.92% accuracy at 20k on CUB-200). For adversarial transferability, SPSG's proxy model yields the highest ASR under FGSM, BIM, and PGD in both hard-label and probability settings, confirming that the stolen model captures decision-boundary structure more faithfully than any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model stealing literature by repurposing the sample gradient — a quantity well-studied in adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD) and interpretability (Grad-CAM, saliency maps) — as a training signal for proxy models. Prior data-free MS methods (DFME, DFMS, EDFBA) and data-driven MS methods (Knockoff, ActiveThief, InverseNet) rely solely on query outputs (probabilities or hard labels) to train the proxy; SPSG departs by extracting superpixel-level gradient information from each real sample via finite-difference queries, then purifying those gradients to reduce variance. The superpixel segmentation step borrows directly from image-processing tools (QuickShift, SLIC, Felzenszwalb) and the gradient purification (threshold filtering + normalization) is a new mechanism not present in prior MS or interpretability work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPSG demonstrates that a single real sample can yield roughly 100× more model information than its label alone, cutting query budgets to ~25% of the most efficient data-free baselines while simultaneously evading the Prada distribution-shift defense. The ablation confirming that gradient purification is essential (not merely a tuning detail) and the failure of grid-based segmentation (vs. content-aware superpixels) highlight that the spatial structure of the gradient signal matters. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-ResNet architectures, to defenses beyond Prada (e.g., input smoothing, query rate limiting), and whether the superpixel gradient signal degrades for very fine-grained or high-resolution tasks where superpixel granularity becomes a bottleneck.
