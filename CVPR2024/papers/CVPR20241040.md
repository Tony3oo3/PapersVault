## Problem

Existing Large Multimodal Models either generate ungrounded text responses or are limited to single-object grounding, user-specified region inputs, or lack dense pixel-wise object grounding in conversational settings. No prior model can produce natural language responses seamlessly interleaved with corresponding segmentation masks, and no standard benchmark exists for evaluating visually grounded conversation generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GLaMM accepts an RGB image, a free-form text instruction, and optionally one or more user-specified bounding boxes as region prompts. The output is a natural language response where specific phrases are wrapped in <p>...</p> tokens followed by <SEG> tokens that decode into binary segmentation masks, producing densely grounded captions at the pixel level. The model operates at three granularities—scene-level (whole image), region-level (user-specified RoI), and pixel-level (generated masks)—within a single unified architecture.

## Method

GLaMM integrates five components trained end-to-end: a ViT-H/14 CLIP global image encoder, a region encoder that builds a hierarchical feature pyramid from four CLIP layers followed by RoIAlign to produce 14×14 RoI features, a Vicuna-based LLM, a SAM-based grounding image encoder, and a SAM decoder-like pixel decoder. A vision-to-language (V-L) projection maps both global and region image features into the LLM's token space, while a language-to-prompt (L-P) projection transforms the last-layer embeddings of <SEG> tokens into the pixel decoder's feature space to produce binary masks. Special vocabulary tokens <bbox> and <SEG> are inserted into prompts to trigger region-level interpretation and pixel-level grounding, respectively. The model is pretrained on the newly introduced GranD dataset (11M images, 7.5M unique concepts, 810M regions with segmentation masks) created via a four-level automated annotation pipeline, then fine-tuned on GranD_f (214K samples) for the Grounded Conversation Generation task.

## Results

On referring expression segmentation, GLaMM achieves 79.5/83.2/76.9 mIoU on refCOCO val/testA/testB, 72.6/78.7/64.6 on refCOCO+, and 74.2/74.9 on refCOCOg val/test, outperforming the previous best (LISA-7B) by 4.6/4.1/4.6 points on refCOCO. On the novel GCG task, GLaMM scores 47.2 CIDEr, 30.8 AP50, 66.3 mIoU, and 41.8 Recall on the validation set, surpassing LISA* (33.9 CIDEr, 25.2 AP50, 62.0 mIoU, 36.3 Recall). For region-level captioning, GLaMM (fine-tuned) reaches 106.0 CIDEr on refCOCOg and 180.5 CIDEr on Visual Genome, and for zero-shot image captioning it achieves 95.3 CIDEr on Flickr30k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLaMM decisively outperforms all referring-expression segmentation baselines: it beats LISA-7B (the strongest prior method) by 4.6 mIoU on refCOCO val and 6.3 on refCOCOg val, and surpasses GRES, LAVT, and CRIS by 5–9 points across splits. On the GCG task, GLaMM dominates LISA* (adapted for GCG) on every metric (CIDEr 47.2 vs 33.9, AP50 30.8 vs 25.2, mIoU 66.3 vs 62.0, Recall 41.8 vs 36.3) and Kosmos-2 (CIDEr 47.2 vs 27.6, Recall 41.8 vs 28.3), though BuboGPT edges it slightly on METEOR (17.2 vs 16.2) while GLaMM leads on all grounding metrics. For region-level captioning, GLaMM (fine-tuned) surpasses GPT4RoI by 2.3 METEOR and 35.3 CIDEr on Visual Genome, and GRIT by 34.4 CIDEr on refCOCOg. On zero-shot image captioning, GLaMM leads all LMM baselines on Flickr30k (95.3 CIDEr vs InstructBLIP 82.8, Shikra-13B 73.9, Kosmos-2 66.7) but trails CoCa (120.6) and BLIP-2 (121.6) on NoCap CIDEr, indicating a trade-off between grounded and holistic captioning quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLaMM sits at the intersection of two research threads: region-aware LMMs (Kosmos-2, Shikra, GPT4RoI) that interpret user-specified bounding boxes but cannot produce pixel-level outputs, and segmentation-oriented LMMs (LISA) that generate masks but are limited to single-object grounding without conversational context. It builds directly on SAM for its pixel decoder and on CLIP ViT-H/14 for global and region-level encoding, while using a Vicuna-based LLM as the reasoning backbone. The key departure is the end-to-end integration of all three granularities (scene, region, pixel) within a single model that accepts both textual and visual prompts, rather than relying on external grounding modules or restricting to one interaction level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of GCG as a unified task and the GranD dataset (7.5M concepts, 810M regions, 33M grounded captions) provide the field with both a new evaluation protocol and a large-scale training resource that were previously absent. The automated annotation pipeline is particularly impactful for scalability, though its reliance on multiple SoTA detectors and GPT-4 for verification raises open questions about annotation fidelity at this scale. A key limitation is that the GCG benchmark is self-defined, making cross-paper comparisons difficult until the community adopts it. Future work will need to validate whether the dense automated annotations introduce systematic biases that affect downstream generalization.
