## Problem

Document Layout Analysis (DLA) models are trained and evaluated almost exclusively on clean, digitally-generated documents, yet real-world scanned or photographed documents suffer from a wide range of degradations (rotation, blur, noise, illumination shifts, etc.) that cause severe performance drops. No systematic benchmark, perturbation taxonomy, or robustness-specific metric existed to quantify how well DLA models resist these real-world corruptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242576/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB document image as input and outputs bounding boxes with class labels for layout elements (e.g., text, title, list, table, figure; up to 74 classes on M6Doc). For benchmarking, the same clean images are corrupted with 12 perturbation types at 3 severity levels, and performance is reported as mAP per perturbation, a perturbation average (P-Avg), and the proposed Mean Robustness Degradation (mRD) score, which normalizes model degradation by the measured perturbation effect (mPE) to decouple image damage from model robustness.

## Method

RoDLA builds on the DINO detection framework with an InternImage backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet22K) and introduces two robustness-enhancing modules in the Encoder: (1) a Channel-wise Attention (CA) block that performs self-attention along the channel dimension to aggregate strongly correlated feature channels and simplify computation, and (2) spatial-wise Average Pooling Layers (APL) with dynamically predicted dilation (via a two-layer 3×3 convolutional Dilation Predictor) that constrains long-range token perception and reduces overemphasis on perturbed or irrelevant tokens. The synergy between channel-wise feature aggregation and spatial-wise attention regularization enables the model to extract perturbation-insensitive representations. Training is performed exclusively on clean data using standard supervised detection loss; robustness is achieved purely through architectural design rather than data augmentation or adversarial training. Ablations show that placing APL in the Encoder (rather than the Decoder) and combining it with CA in the Encoder yields the best trade-off between clean accuracy and robustness.

## Results

On PubLayNet-P, RoDLA achieves 96.0% mAP on clean data, 70.0% P-Avg across 12 perturbations, and 116.0 mRD (lower is better), outperforming the strongest same-pretraining baseline DINO (95.4% mAP, 69.0% P-Avg, 120.7 mRD). On DocLayNet-P, RoDLA reaches 80.5% mAP, 65.6% P-Avg, and 135.7 mRD, surpassing the previous best (SwinDocSegmenter: 76.9% mAP, 58.5% P-Avg, 282.7 mRD) by +3.6% mAP and +7.1% P-Avg. On M6Doc-P, RoDLA attains 70.0% mAP, 61.7% P-Avg, and 147.6 mRD, improving P-Avg by +12.1% over the next best method (DiT: 60.6% P-Avg, 164.8 mRD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among methods sharing the same ImageNet pre-training, RoDLA (InternImage backbone) achieves the best clean mAP (96.0%) and the lowest mRD (116.0) on PubLayNet-P, edging out DINO (95.4% mAP, 120.7 mRD) by +0.6% mAP and 4.7 mRD. Co-DINO, despite competitive clean accuracy (94.2–94.3%), suffers severe robustness degradation (mRD 230.3–254.1), indicating its collaborative assignment strategy is highly sensitive to perturbations. SwinDocSegmenter drops dramatically under blur (93.7% → 37.3% mAP on P9), while RoDLA maintains 91.3% under the same condition. DiT and LayoutLMv3 with Cascade R-CNN benefit from extra IIT-CDIP pre-training and show competitive P-Avg (76.7% and 73.6%), but the paper notes this is not a fair comparison due to the additional document-specific pre-training data. On DocLayNet-P and M6Doc-P, RoDLA clearly dominates all baselines: +3.6% mAP and +7.1% P-Avg over SwinDocSegmenter on DocLayNet-P, and +12.1% P-Avg with 91.6 lower mRD over DiT on M6Doc-P.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoDLA is architecturally inspired by DINO's denoising anchor box framework, augmented with a Channel-wise Attention module adapted from Zhou et al.'s analysis of ViT robustness and a Token-aware Average Pooling mechanism from Guo et al.'s work on robustifying token attention. The paper departs from prior DLA research, which has focused almost exclusively on clean digital documents, by introducing a systematic perturbation taxonomy (12 types, 3 severity levels) grounded in real-world scanning and photography artifacts. It also departs from prior robustness benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-C) that measure degradation relative to a single pre-selected baseline, instead proposing the mPE metric that combines image quality assessment with multi-model degradation to decouple perturbation severity from model-specific robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive robustness benchmark for DLA, covering ~450K images across three datasets and 12 perturbation types, providing the field with a standardized way to evaluate how well layout analysis models handle real-world document degradation. The mPE/mRD metrics offer a more principled evaluation framework that avoids the baseline-dependency problem of prior robustness benchmarks. Key limitations include restriction to vision-only single-modal DLA (no multi-modal robustness evaluation) and the absence of human-in-the-loop validation, leaving open whether the synthetic perturbations adequately capture the full distribution of real-world document degradation. The finding that multi-modal LayoutLMv3 does not outperform unimodal DiT in robustness raises questions about whether textual modality provides genuine robustness benefits under perturbation.
