## Problem

Rendering photorealistic 4D human performance in real time while maintaining a compact, GPU-friendly representation remains unsolved. Implicit NeRF-based methods produce blurry high-frequency details and suffer from slow training, while existing dynamic 3DGS variants either fix appearance attributes (sacrificing view-dependent quality) or rely on implicit deformation fields that break down under large motions. The paper tackles how to explicitly disentangle motion from appearance in a Gaussian representation to achieve both high fidelity and compression efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240068/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB video captured by 81 pre-calibrated Z-CAM cinema cameras at 3840×2160 and 30 fps, along with OpenPose estimates for hand/face importance sampling. It produces a per-frame set of ~200,000 4D Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, spherical-harmonic coefficients, opacity, scaling) that are then compressed via residual compensation, quantization, and RANS entropy encoding to under 2 MB per frame, enabling real-time playback on VR/AR headsets.

## Method

HiFi4G bridges 3D Gaussian Splatting with Embedded Deformation (ED) non-rigid tracking through a dual-graph mechanism: a coarse ED graph built from NeuS2 mesh sequences and DynamicFusion-style keyframe tracking provides motion priors, while a fine-grained Gaussian graph (KNN k=16) constrains inter-Gaussian coherence. A 4D optimization scheme balances these priors against photometric loss using a temporal regularizer on appearance attributes (SH, opacity, scaling) and a smooth regularizer on motion attributes (position, rotation) that enforces locally as-rigid-as-possible deformations, with an adaptive displacement-based weight that penalizes flickering in near-static regions. For compression, the method applies motion-compensated residual coding (subtracting warped keyframe Gaussians), per-attribute quantization (9-bit appearance / 11-bit motion for non-keyframes), and RANS entropy coding, achieving ~25× compression with negligible quality loss.

## Results

On the authors' 81-camera dataset, HiFi4G (after compression) achieves PSNR 35.79, SSIM 0.986, LPIPS 0.0214, and VMAF 84.31, outperforming all baselines on every metric while requiring only 1.65 MB per frame. On ActorsHQ, the compressed version reaches PSNR 34.70, SSIM 0.967, LPIPS 0.1013, and VMAF 87.54 at 2.14 MB per frame. The compression scheme achieves approximately 25× reduction (from ~43–51 MB to <2.2 MB per frame) with minimal quality loss (e.g., PSNR drops only 0.42 dB on their dataset).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiFi4G (after compression) dominates all four baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, VMAF) on both datasets. Against the closest competitor HumanRF [22], HiFi4G gains +4.6 dB PSNR and +3.4 VMAF on their dataset while using 8.5× less storage (1.65 vs. 11.38 MB). Against Dynamic 3D Gaussians [40], the advantage is most dramatic: +5.5 dB PSNR and +32 VMAF on their dataset, with Dynamic 3D Gaussians suffering from fixed appearance attributes that sacrifice view-dependent detail. NeuS2 [69] produces the highest PSNR among baselines (32.95) but at 14.6× the storage of HiFi4G and with noticeably blurry high-frequency details. Instant-NSR [81] is the weakest baseline, showing severe geometric artifacts and the lowest VMAF (68.31), confirming its heavy reliance on tracked mesh geometry. Notably, even on VMAF—a perceptual metric that rewards temporal smoothness—HiFi4G's explicit Gaussian approach outperforms HumanRF's MLP-based rendering, which benefits from inherent smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiFi4G bridges two previously separate paradigms: the explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (3DGS) and traditional non-rigid tracking via Embedded Deformation (ED). It departs from implicit deformation-field approaches (NeRF-based dynamic variants, 4DGS) that sacrifice GPU-friendliness and explicitness, and from per-frame 3DGS that ignores temporal consistency. The dual-graph mechanism (coarse ED graph + fine-grained Gaussian graph) is the key architectural contribution, drawing on Fusion4D's key-volume strategy and DynamicFusion's non-rigid registration to provide motion priors that guide Gaussian optimization. The compression pipeline (residual compensation + RANS entropy coding) extends standard point-cloud compression ideas to the 4D Gaussian parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~25× compression to under 2 MB per frame is a practically significant win for deploying high-fidelity human performance in VR/AR headsets and other resource-constrained platforms, where existing methods require 10–50 MB per frame. The paper's explicit representation also enables seamless integration into standard GPU rasterization pipelines, unlike implicit NeRF-based methods. Key limitations remain: heavy reliance on accurate segmentation (fragile under human-object interaction), per-frame mesh reconstruction as a bottleneck, and dependence on fast GPU sorting that limits mobile/web deployment. An open direction the authors highlight is a more synergistic coupling between tracking and rendering to eliminate the per-frame reconstruction step.
