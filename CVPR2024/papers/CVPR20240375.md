## Problem

Zero-shot video object segmentation models trained on large-scale datasets fail to generalize to unseen videos, and existing test-time training (TTT) methods (TENT, BN, TTT-Rot, TTT-MAE) yield unstable or negligible improvements on ZSVOS because typical test data lacks the severe domain shifts those methods are designed to correct. The paper identifies that task-specific 3D cues—specifically, the fact that primary moving objects are close to the camera and thus have small depth—can serve as a reliable self-supervised signal for TTT even in the absence of domain shift.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB video frames (resized to 512×512) and pre-computed optical flow maps (from RAFT) as input, and produces a binary segmentation mask for the primary moving object along with a per-pixel depth map. During TTT, each frame is consumed twice under different photometric distortions (random horizontal flip, resize, crop, brightness/contrast/saturation/hue jitter), and the depth decoder must output consistent depth maps for both augmented views. Depth supervision during pre-training comes from off-the-shelf monocular depth predictors (MonoDepth2, LiteMono, or ZoeDepth), which are free but assumed noisy.

## Method

DATTT is a two-stage framework built on a two-stream ZSVOS architecture (MiT-b1 or Swin-Tiny image encoder + flow encoder) augmented with a depth decoder and a depth-aware modulation layer that lets depth features modulate mask-decoder features at each scale via MLP-generated parameters. In the training stage, the model is jointly optimized with binary cross-entropy for segmentation and scale-invariant log loss for depth (λ=0.1). During TTT, the depth decoder is frozen and only the image encoder is fine-tuned by minimizing the depth consistency loss between two augmented views of the same frame. Two video-specific TTT strategies are introduced: momentum-based weight initialization (TTT-MWI), which initializes each frame's encoder from the previous frame's adapted weights rather than the pre-trained weights, and loop-through-video (TTT-LTV), which iterates through the entire video multiple times so that both past and future frame information accumulates globally. The modulation layer is critical: without it, the depth branch provides only marginal gains, but with it, TTT yields consistent improvements of 3–12 J across datasets.

## Results

On DAVIS-16, DATTT with Swin-Tiny achieves J=85.8 and F=88.5, while with MiT-b1 it reaches J=86.0 and F=87.9, matching or exceeding all prior ZSVOS methods. On Long-Videos, the method achieves J=77.3 / F=79.9 (Swin-T) and J=75.6 / F=77.1 (MiT-b1), substantially outperforming the next best method (HFAN: J=74.9, F=76.1). On FBMS, Swin-T achieves J=78.8, edging out HCPN (78.3). In the TTT comparison (Table 4), DATTT yields the largest and most consistent gains over all prior TTT strategies, with improvements of up to +12.4 in J on Long-Videos and +12.0 on MCL (Swin-T backbone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TTT baselines (Table 4), DATTT consistently outperforms TENT [48] and BN [36], which show negligible or even negative gains (e.g., TENT drops −0.5 J on DAVIS-16 with MiT-b1), and substantially surpasses TTT-Rot [39] and TTT-MAE [11], which degrade on several datasets (TTT-MAE drops to J=55.7 on MCL vs. 61.9 for DATTT). Against SOTA ZSVOS methods (Table 5), DATTT (Swin-T) achieves the best FBMS score (J=78.8 vs. HCPN's 78.3) and the best Long-Videos scores (J=77.3, F=79.9 vs. HFAN's 74.9/76.1), while on DAVIS-16 it is within 0.2 J of HFAN (85.8 vs. 86.2) but leads on F (88.5 vs. 87.1). The MiT-b1 variant matches HFAN on DAVIS-16 J (86.0 vs. 86.2) and clearly wins on Long-Videos. Overall, DATTT is competitive with or superior to all six ZSVOS baselines across the three datasets, demonstrating that test-time training with depth consistency provides a practical path to SOTA-level segmentation without task-specific architectural complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage test-time training (TTT) paradigm established by TTT-Rot (Sun et al., ICML 2020) and TTT-MAE (Gandelsman et al., NeurIPS 2022) from image recognition to zero-shot video object segmentation, replacing rotation or reconstruction objectives with a depth-consistency signal. It departs from prior video TTT work (Azimi et al., WACV 2022; Volpi et al., CVPR 2022) by introducing video-specific adaptation strategies—momentum-based weight initialization and loop-through-video training—that exploit temporal redundancy rather than treating frames independently. The depth modulation layer is a novel architectural component that enables cross-task feature interaction between the segmentation and depth decoders, going beyond the standard shared-encoder-plus-auxiliary-head design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a geometrically grounded self-supervised signal (depth consistency) is substantially more effective for TTT in ZSVOS than generic objectives like entropy minimization, BN adaptation, rotation prediction, or masked reconstruction, which all fail to yield consistent gains. This suggests that task-aligned auxiliary signals matter more than the specific TTT mechanism itself. Limitations include the dependence on pre-computed (and noisy) monocular depth maps, the additional inference cost of multi-epoch TTT per video, and the fact that the method still trails dedicated SOTA ZSVOS architectures. Open questions include whether depth-based TTT generalizes to other video understanding tasks and whether the approach can be made fully self-contained without external depth predictors.
