## Problem

Existing sketch-conditional diffusion models (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, SGDM) produce deformed, non-photorealistic outputs when given amateur freehand sketches because their spatial-conditioning approach directly translates sketch contours into the output image domain. These methods also require careful per-sample tuning of conditioning weights and depend on precise textual prompts, undermining the promise that 'what you sketch is what you get' for non-artist users.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single freehand sketch as input (no textual prompt required at inference) and produces a photorealistic RGB image. The sketch is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder into a 257×1024 patch-wise embedding, which the sketch adapter compresses into a 77×768 textual-equivalent embedding that conditions the frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 UNet via cross-attention. The system is trained on the Sketchy dataset (12,500 images, 125 categories) and generalises to TU-Berlin, QuickDraw, synthetic edgemaps, and different stroke styles without retraining.

## Method

The core idea is to eliminate spatial sketch-conditioning entirely by converting the input sketch into an equivalent fine-grained textual embedding via a sketch adapter (1D convolutions + vanilla attention + FC layers) that bridges the 257×1024 CLIP sketch embedding to the 77×768 text embedding space, then conditioning the frozen SD UNet through standard cross-attention. Three complementary mechanisms ensure fidelity: (i) a discriminative SBIR loss that uses Tweedie's formula to estimate a clean image from the noisy latent at each step and enforces cosine similarity between the sketch and generated image in a pre-trained FG-SBIR embedding space; (ii) a super-concept preservation loss that distils text-to-image knowledge from synthetic captions during training (not used at inference); and (iii) an abstraction-aware time-step sampling function S_ω(t) = (1/T)(1 − ω cos(πt/T)) where ω is derived from a CLIP-based sketch abstraction classifier (trained with MagFace loss), skewing the denoising schedule toward later timesteps for more abstract sketches to preserve high-level semantics.

## Results

On the Sketchy dataset, the proposed method achieves the best FID-C (16.20), FGM (0.81), and MOS (4.52±0.1) among all compared methods, while also attaining the lowest FID-I (25.07). The FGM improvement over the strongest prior SoTA (ControlNet, 0.62) is 23.45%, and the MOS advantage over the best competitor (ControlNet, 3.68±0.2) is 1.36±0.2 points. Notably, the method operates without any textual prompt at inference, whereas all diffusion-based competitors require one.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against ControlNet (the strongest prior SoTA), it improves FGM by 23.45% (0.81 vs. 0.62), FID-C by 23.7% (16.20 vs. 21.22), and MOS by 1.36 points (4.52 vs. 3.68), while also achieving a lower FID-I (25.07 vs. 26.68). T2I-Adapter and PITI trail further behind, with PITI showing a particularly poor FID-I of 84.71 due to pronounced edge-adherence. SGDM (qualitative comparison only) produces plausible colour schemes but substantial shape deformations, while GAN-based Pix2Pix and CycleGAN exhibit visible artefacts attributable to their weaker generators. The two text-based baselines (B-Classification and B-Captioning) confirm that simply converting sketches to text prompts is insufficient for fine-grained fidelity, with FGM scores of only 0.36 and 0.47 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and a pre-trained fine-grained sketch-based image retrieval model (Sain et al., CVPR 2023), but departs fundamentally from the spatial-conditioning paradigm used by ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, and SGDM. Rather than injecting sketch features directly into UNet encoder/skip-connection layers (which the authors diagnose as the root cause of edge-bleeding and shape deformities), it converts sketches into text-equivalent embeddings via a learned adapter and conditions generation through cross-attention. The abstraction-aware time-step sampling and discriminative SBIR loss are novel training mechanisms that have no direct precedent in prior sketch-conditional diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing controllable diffusion methods produce deformed outputs for the very amateur sketches that non-artist users would naturally produce, undermining the 'what you sketch is what you get' promise. By eliminating spatial conditioning and the need for textual prompts at inference, it lowers the barrier to sketch-driven generation. However, the method still relies on class-structured paired data (Sketchy) and struggles with categorical ambiguity between shape-similar objects (e.g., apple vs. pear), leaving open the question of whether the approach extends to truly open-vocabulary or multi-object scenes without class labels.
