## Problem

State-of-the-art video-language alignment models, despite large-scale pretraining, are not robust to semantically plausible contrastive changes in captions such as replacing entities, flipping event order, or altering actions. This fragility undermines their reliability for real-world alignment evaluation, yet prior work has not provided a comprehensive dataset covering the full spectrum of such misalignments with corresponding explanations to train models against them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241602/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video sampled at 1 frame per second (encoded by CLIP) and a text caption (encoded by LLaMA-7B) as a multimodal instruction. It produces two outputs in a generative instruction-response format: a binary entailment label ('Yes'/'No') indicating whether the video entails the caption, and an open-ended natural language explanation (NLE) describing the specific discrepancy when the caption does not entail the video. The entailment score is derived from the log-probability ratio of 'Yes' vs. 'No' tokens, enabling ROC-AUC computation.

## Method

The core contribution is the VideoCon dataset, constructed by first filtering temporally-challenging video-caption pairs (where alignment cannot be inferred from a single frame, using VNLI max-frame scores), then prompting PaLM-2 to generate contrast captions across seven misalignment types (object, action, attribute, counting, spatial relation, hallucination, event order flip) along with natural language explanations. Generated pairs are quality-filtered via NLI models to remove non-contradictory captions and poorly-grounded explanations. The mPLUG-Owl-Video model (CLIP visual encoder + LLaMA-7B text decoder) is then fine-tuned in a multitask setting on both the entailment classification and NLE generation tasks using maximum likelihood loss. The key insight is that deliberately constructed contrastive negatives spanning diverse misalignment categories are far more effective than random negatives (Owl-Rand) or simply scaling pretraining data, yielding a 12-point AUC gain on human-generated contrast captions and SOTA zero-shot performance on temporal retrieval and video QA benchmarks.

## Results

On the primary video-language entailment task, Owl-Con achieves 84.6 ROC-AUC on the VideoCon (LLM) test set, surpassing the strongest baseline End-to-End VNLI (67.0) by 17.6 points and the base model Owl-Base (57.2) by 27.4 points. On the out-of-distribution human-generated contrast captions, Owl-Con reaches 78.3 AUC, a 12-point improvement over Owl-Base (66.8). On downstream zero-shot tasks, Owl-Con sets new SOTA with 15.2 mAP on SSv2-Temporal, 11.4 mAP on SSv2-Events, and 41.1% accuracy on ATP-Hard video question answering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Owl-Con outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated metric. Against the strongest baseline End-to-End VNLI [60], Owl-Con gains 17.6 AUC points on VideoCon (LLM) test, 0.6 mAP on SSv2-Temporal (15.2 vs 14.6), 1.0 mAP on SSv2-Events (11.4 vs 10.4), and 2.1% accuracy on ATP-Hard (41.1 vs 39.0). Compared to the base model Owl-Base [61], the gains are larger: 27.4 AUC on the LLM test, 4.3 mAP on SSv2-Temporal, 4.6 mAP on SSv2-Events, and 4.0% on ATP-Hard. The contrastive training ablation Owl-Rand (random negatives) improves over Owl-Base by only 2.5 AUC, confirming that the specific contrast captions in VideoCon are critical rather than merely adding more negative examples. On SSv2-Events, Owl-Con surpasses the previous SOTA TACT [5] by 3.6 mAP (11.4 vs 7.8), and on ATP-Hard it exceeds VFC [37] by 9.7% accuracy (41.1 vs 31.4). VideoCLIP [55] and ImageBind [14] perform near or below random on the LLM test set (53.2 and 57.1 AUC), highlighting their severe fragility to semantically plausible caption perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on mPLUG-Owl-Video (Owl-Base) as a generative video-language backbone and fine-tunes it with a novel LLM-generated contrast caption dataset. Conceptually, it extends the Visual Entailment (VE) task from images to videos and connects to prior work identifying robustness failures in video-text models under semantically plausible caption perturbations (Park et al., Momeni et al.). The key departure from prior approaches is the use of a targeted, LLM-driven data generation pipeline (PaLM-2) to systematically produce contrast captions across seven misalignment types, rather than relying on scaling pretraining data or simple temporal-order losses as in TACT. The NLE generation task is adapted from image-text alignment work (Sammani et al.) and extended to the video domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully constructed contrastive training set is far more effective than random negatives or scaling pretraining data for improving video-language alignment robustness, with a 12-point AUC gain on human-generated contrast captions and SOTA results on temporally-challenging downstream tasks. A notable open question is whether the approach generalizes to multi-misalignment contrast captions and web-scale datasets with lower-quality captions, which the authors acknowledge as future work. The finding that an atemporal VNLI model outperforms dedicated video-language models on alignment benchmarks raises important questions about whether current video encoders genuinely exploit temporal structure, suggesting the field needs stronger temporal benchmarks and training signals.
