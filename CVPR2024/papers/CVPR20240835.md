## Problem

NeRFs produce blurry reconstructions when trained on motion-blurred images, and existing event-based deblur NeRF methods either fail to recover accurate color content (E-NeRF) or constrain the NeRF to predefined camera poses during exposure (E2NeRF), limiting quality under challenging motion. The paper addresses how to fuse events and frames in a way that explicitly models blur formation while remaining adaptable to real sensor non-idealities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240835/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes timestamped blurry color frames (100 ms exposure) and a synchronized event stream from a Color-DAVIS346 sensor (346×260, RGBG Bayer pattern) along with camera poses (motor encoder at 100 Hz for real data, Blender poses for synthetic). Events are microsecond-resolution polarity-tagged brightness changes; for color event cameras, the luma conversion is removed and the loss is applied per-channel. The output is a sharp radiance field rendered via volumetric integration, enabling novel view synthesis at arbitrary poses.

## Method

Ev-DeblurNeRF builds on a DP-NeRF-style backbone that learns an SE(3) motion field G_Φ to warp rays across M discrete sub-exposure positions, then renders blurry colors via weighted averaging. Three event-based supervision signals are introduced: (1) a single-integral loss L_ev that matches rendered log-brightness differences between consecutive event pairs to observed polarities, (2) an Event Double Integral (EDI) loss L_EDI that uses the analytic relationship between events and the blurred frame to produce a model-based sharp color prior, and (3) a learnable event camera response function (eCRF), an MLP that modulates rendered colors by polarity to compensate for real sensor non-idealities. TensorRF explicit feature volumes are incorporated into the coarse and fine MLPs to accelerate convergence by ~10×. The total loss combines the blurry-image photometric loss, the event supervision, and the EDI prior with fixed weights (λ_b=1, λ_e=0.1, λ_EDI=1), trained for 30k iterations with Adam.

## Results

On the synthetic Ev-DeblurBlender dataset, Ev-DeblurNeRF achieves an average PSNR of 31.02 dB, LPIPS of 0.08, and SSIM of 0.91, outperforming the best image-only baseline DP-NeRF (27.68 dB) by +3.34 dB and the best event-based baseline E2NeRF (26.00 dB) by +5.02 dB. On the real-world Ev-DeblurCDAVIS dataset, the method reaches 32.96 dB PSNR and 0.91 SSIM, surpassing E2NeRF (30.48 dB) by +2.48 dB and the best image-only baseline DP-NeRF+TensoRF (26.83 dB) by +6.13 dB. Training takes approximately 3.5 hours on an A100 GPU, making it 6.9× faster than E2NeRF (~24 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ev-DeblurNeRF outperforms all 10 baselines on both datasets. Against image-only NeRF methods, it leads DP-NeRF [18] by +3.34 dB (synthetic) and +6.13 dB (real), PDRF [6] by +4.73 dB, and Deblur-NeRF [24] by +7.15 dB on synthetic data. Among event-based deblur NeRF methods, it surpasses E2NeRF [30] by +5.02 dB (synthetic) and +2.48 dB (real), and E-NeRF [16] by +8.02 dB on synthetic data, with the gap attributed to E-NeRF's lack of explicit blur modeling and E2NeRF's restriction of event supervision to exposure time only. The deblur-then-NeRF pipelines (MPRNet+NeRF, PVDNet+NeRF, EFNet+NeRF) all underperform, with EFNet*+NeRF being the closest at 28.99 dB but still 2.03 dB below, due to scene-inconsistent deblurring that introduces floaters. On real data, the model-based EDI+NeRF baseline (29.82 dB) is outperformed by +3.14 dB, confirming that the learnable eCRF and continuous event supervision provide meaningful gains over a purely model-based prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ev-DeblurNeRF builds directly on the DP-NeRF/Deblur-NeRF lineage for modeling blur formation via SE(3) motion estimation over exposure, and on PDRF's TensorRF explicit features for fast convergence. It departs from E-NeRF by explicitly modeling the blur formation process and adding the Event Double Integral (EDI) as a model-based prior, and from E2NeRF by applying event supervision continuously (not only during exposure) and introducing a learnable event camera response function (eCRF) to absorb real-sensor non-idealities. The key conceptual contribution is the unified treatment of model-based (EDI) and learning-based (eCRF) event supervision within a single NeRF optimization, allowing the network to follow the physics-based solution when accurate and diverge when the sensor deviates from the ideal event model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap: NeRFs deployed in robotics and mobile platforms routinely encounter motion blur, and prior event-assisted approaches either lost color fidelity (E-NeRF) or underutilized event data (E2NeRF). The +6.13 dB gain over image-only baselines and the 6.9× training speedup over E2NeRF make event-assisted NeRF deblurring more accessible for real-time or resource-constrained settings. Open questions include generalization to stereo event-frame rigs where pixel alignment is imperfect, handling of low-frequency or inaccurate pose estimates for event supervision, and whether the learnable CRF can be distilled into a fixed calibration for deployment without end-to-end training.
