## Problem

Existing unsupervised saliency methods can only produce region-level saliency maps and cannot differentiate individual salient instances within a scene. Instance-level saliency requires fine-grained discrimination not only between salient and non-salient regions but also between different salient regions of varying prominence, a capability that no prior unsupervised method has demonstrated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242474/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 1024×1024 resolution and extracts key features from the last multi-head attention block of a frozen DINO ViT-S backbone. It produces per-instance salient segmentation masks via a Mask-RCNN detection head, guided by a scene-level saliency map that suppresses background. Training uses only unlabeled images (no pixel masks, bounding boxes, or class labels), with pseudo-labels iteratively derived from the DINO features themselves via Normalized Cut.

## Method

SCoCo exploits the observation that DINO transformer features exhibit intra-region saliency coherence and inter-region saliency contrast of varying degrees. A Global Background Adaptation (GBA) module uses a bifurcation-and-combination architecture with learnable smooth-Swish saliency threshold functions and a scene-level contrastive loss to adaptively separate salient regions from diverse backgrounds. A Locality-aware Similarity (LAS) module computes channel-wise cosine similarities between each pixel and its surrounding neighborhood, aggregates them via cascaded 3×3 convolutions, and feeds the resulting coherence features into an instance-level contrastive loss that uses triplet sampling (positive/negative pseudo-instances from Normalized Cut) to learn cross-instance contrast while preserving intra-instance coherence. The two modules' outputs are combined in a Mask-RCNN head, and the entire pipeline is trained in two stages: first with DINO-derived pseudo-labels, then with SCoCo-refined pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the SIS dataset, SCoCo achieves 71.4% mAP₅₀ and 55.7% mAP₇₀, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline FOUND (67.1%/53.8%) by 4.3/1.9 points and the best weakly-supervised method WSID-Net (68.4%/47.1%) by 3.0/8.6 points. On ILSO-1K, SCoCo reaches 70.7% mAP₅₀ and 58.3% mAP₇₀, surpassing all weakly-supervised methods (best: WSID-Net at 68.3%/51.7%) and all unsupervised baselines (best: FOUND at 66.2%/51.3%). On ILSO-2K, SCoCo scores 64.8% mAP₅₀ and 50.4% mAP₇₀, again leading all unsupervised and weakly-supervised approaches. While fully-supervised methods (e.g., OQTR at 88.1%/81.7% on SIS) still lead, SCoCo narrows the gap substantially compared to prior unsupervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoCo outperforms all six unsupervised baselines across all three datasets. The closest unsupervised competitor is FOUND [57] (66.2%/51.3% on ILSO-1K, 67.1%/53.8% on SIS), which SCoCo surpasses by 4.5/7.0 and 4.3/1.9 mAP₅₀/mAP₇₀ points respectively; CutLER [70] with Cls init (68.6%/54.8% on ILSO-1K) is also clearly beaten. SCoCo also surpasses all six weakly-supervised methods, with the strongest being WSID-Net [62] (68.3%/51.7% on ILSO-1K, 68.4%/47.1% on SIS), which SCoCo exceeds by 2.4/6.6 and 3.0/8.6 points. Against the seven fully-supervised methods, SCoCo trails the best (OQTR [46] at 89.2%/81.0% on ILSO-1K, 88.1%/81.7% on SIS) by roughly 18–26 mAP₅₀ points, but the gap is notably smaller than what any weakly-supervised or unsupervised baseline achieves. The paper also shows that simply retraining unsupervised object detectors (FreeSOLO, CutLER, VitDet) with pseudo salient-instance labels does not close the gap, confirming that the GBA and LAS modules are essential for instance-level discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes frozen self-supervised transformer features (DINO) for a new detection objective: rather than using them for generic object segmentation (as in FreeSOLO, CutLER) or region-level saliency (as in FOUND, SelfMask), SCoCo exploits the intra-region coherence and inter-region contrast structure within DINO features to separate individual salient instances. The GBA module with learnable saliency threshold functions departs from prior USOD methods that rely on fixed or content-agnostic convolutional operations, while the LAS module introduces a locality-aware cosine-similarity computation over spatial neighborhoods that is distinct from the self-attention mechanisms used in prior SID detectors. The training signal comes from two novel contrastive losses (scene-level and instance-level) rather than the BCE or pseudo-label distillation strategies common in unsupervised SOD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first method to detect salient instances without any annotated labels, SCoCo opens a path toward fine-grained scene understanding in settings where pixel-level or even image-level annotations are impractical (e.g., medical imaging, open-world scenes). The key limitation is that the method operates on high-level, low-resolution ViT features, causing it to miss small salient instances; the authors identify multi-granularity feature modeling as a natural next step. The performance gap to fully-supervised methods (roughly 15–18 mAP points) remains substantial, suggesting that the coherence/contrast signal in self-supervised features, while informative, does not yet fully replace explicit supervision for precise instance boundaries.
