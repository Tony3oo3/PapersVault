## Problem

Transferring knowledge from the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to a compact panoramic semantic segmentation model is non-trivial because SAM provides only instance masks without semantic labels, and the large capacity gap between SAM and a lightweight student hinders effective knowledge transfer. Additionally, equirectangular projection (ERP) panoramic images suffer from severe distortion and large field-of-view effects that degrade both SAM's and any 2D-trained model's performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled panoramic ERP images at 400×2048 resolution and produces a 21-class semantic segmentation map. During training, the input is consumed via a horizontal overlapping sliding window (400×512 patches, stride 256) to mitigate ERP distortion; SAM is kept frozen and used solely to generate instance masks and boundary maps for each patch. The student is a compact SegFormer variant (B0–B5, 3.7M–25M parameters) that processes the full ERP image directly at inference.

## Method

GoodSAM introduces a three-model framework (frozen SAM, a teacher assistant TA, and a compact student) with two key modules. The Distortion-Aware Rectification (DAR) module generates reliable ensemble logits by: (1) imposing prediction-level consistency on overlapping window regions, (2) refining boundary pixels using SAM's boundary maps via a multi-source voting strategy, and (3) adaptively fusing SAM instance masks with TA semantic logits through a Cross-Task Complementary Fusion (CTCF) block that applies size-dependent area-proportion thresholds and Shannon entropy to assign the highest-confidence semantic label per instance. The Multi-level Knowledge Adaptation (MKA) module then transfers knowledge at two scales—whole-image (CE loss from TA's concatenated patch predictions) and patch-level (CE loss from ensemble logits weighted by a confidence map from CTCF)—plus boundary supervision, enabling the compact student to learn distortion-aware and boundary-aware segmentation without any labeled panoramic data.

## Results

On the DensePASS test set, GoodSAM-S (25.4M params) achieves 60.56% mIoU, outperforming the previous SOTA DATR-S (56.81%) by +3.75% mIoU, DPPASS-S (56.28%) by +4.28%, and Trans4PASS-S (55.22%) by +5.34%. The ultra-compact GoodSAM-M (3.7M params) reaches 55.93% mIoU, surpassing the much larger DATR-T (14.72M, 54.60%) and Trans4PASS-T (13.95M, 53.18%). On WildPASS, GoodSAM-S (Segformer-B2) achieves 65.18% mIoU, beating DPPASS-S (63.53%) by +1.65% and Trans4PASS-S (62.91%) by +2.27%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoodSAM-S (25.4M) decisively outperforms all same-parameter UDA baselines on DensePASS: +3.75% mIoU over DATR-S (56.81%), +4.28% over DPPASS-S (56.28%), and +5.34% over Trans4PASS-S (55.22%). The standout result is GoodSAM-M (3.7M params) achieving 55.93% mIoU, which surpasses the 4× larger DATR-T (14.72M, 54.60%) and Trans4PASS-T (13.95M, 53.18%), demonstrating that SAM-guided knowledge transfer compensates for extreme parameter reduction. On WildPASS, GoodSAM-T (Segformer-B1) beats DPPASS-T by +2.04% mIoU (62.42 vs 60.38) and GoodSAM-S (Segformer-B2) beats DPPASS-S by +1.65% (65.18 vs 63.53), confirming generalization across datasets. Per-class gains are most pronounced on structurally complex categories: GoodSAM-S improves 'building' by +5.42%, 'fence' by +5.5%, and 'car' by +5.93% over DATR-S. Older supervised baselines (ERFNet, Omni-sup, P2PDA) are substantially outperformed, with GoodSAM-S exceeding the best supervised method Omni-sup by +17.54% mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of panoramic semantic segmentation UDA methods (pseudo labeling, adversarial training, prototypical adaptation) that all require labeled 2D source-domain data, by instead leveraging the frozen Segment Anything Model as a zero-shot instance segmentation teacher. The key conceptual departure is introducing a 'teacher assistant' (a pre-trained SegFormer) to supply semantic labels that SAM cannot provide, and bridging the large capacity gap between SAM and a compact student through multi-level knowledge adaptation. The Cross-Task Complementary Fusion block, which adaptively merges SAM's instance masks with TA's semantic logits using size-dependent thresholds and Shannon entropy, is a novel fusion mechanism distinct from prior instance-to-semantic label assignment strategies (SSA, SEPL).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to transfer SAM's instance segmentation knowledge into a compact panoramic semantic segmentation model without any labeled data, achieving SOTA mIoU with as few as 3.7M parameters—substantially lighter than competing 25M+ parameter UDA methods. However, the approach still depends on a pre-trained TA model (SegFormer) for semantic grounding, so it is not fully label-free in the strictest sense, and SAM remains frozen and unadapted to the panoramic domain. Open questions include whether fine-tuning SAM for ERP distortion could further close the domain gap, and whether the sliding-window training overhead (multiple forward passes per image) limits practical deployment. The paper's future work on distilling SAM's zero-shot capability into the student suggests a path toward even more self-contained models.
