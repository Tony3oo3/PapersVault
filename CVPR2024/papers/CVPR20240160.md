## Problem

Existing Open Set Domain Generalization (OSDG) methods rely on complex architectures with marginal gains, while recent VLM-based approaches require expensive fine-tuning of large vision encoders, making them impractical. Additionally, all prior DG/OSDG benchmarks assume identical and fixed label sets across source domains, which does not reflect real-world data where source domains contain disparate, random subsets of classes—leading to unreliable performance conclusions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and outputs either a known class label or an 'unknown' rejection label for open-set inference. During training, the lightweight student model (e.g., ResNet18, EfficientNet-B0, MobileNetV3) is supervised by CLIP's image-text similarity scores rather than raw labels, and CLIP's pretrained text embeddings are used to regularize the student's classifier weights—meaning the text modality is consumed only at training time and is absent at inference.

## Method

SCI-PD distills knowledge from a frozen CLIP model into any lightweight vision backbone via three perturbation mechanisms. Score Perturbation (SP) detects when CLIP's predicted class disagrees with the ground truth, then injects a scaled one-hot GT vector into CLIP's similarity distribution before τ-Softmax re-normalization, balancing CLIP's semantic signal against label correctness. Instance Perturbation (IP) weights each sample's classification loss by the reciprocal of CLIP's maximum similarity raised to a power α, up-weighting uncertain instances that carry richer domain-invariant semantics. Class Perturbation (CP) aligns the student classifier's weight matrix with the pairwise similarity matrix of CLIP's text embeddings via a symmetric cross-entropy loss, preserving the semantic geometry of the text encoder in the student's decision boundary. The final training objective combines the perturbed classification loss and the class perturbation loss with a trade-off hyperparameter β, and the entire pipeline avoids fine-tuning CLIP, requiring only standard supervised training of the lightweight student.

## Results

On OfficeHome, SCI-PD achieves 61.73% average accuracy and 57.33% H-score, outperforming the best closed-set DG method XDED (57.82% Acc, 52.96% H-score) by 3.91% and 4.37% respectively, and the best OSDG method DAML (59.04% Acc, 54.46% H-score) by 2.69% and 2.87%. On PACS, SCI-PD reaches 70.16% Acc and 61.21% H-score, surpassing CIRL (67.35% Acc, 46.36% H-score) by 2.81% and 14.85%. On DomainNet, SCI-PD attains 34.16% Acc and 36.50% H-score, exceeding CLIPBase by 2.73% and 2.14%. The proposed H²-CV metric confirms superior robustness: SCI-PD achieves 5.20% on OfficeHome vs. 9.67% for XDED and 11.94% for RISE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCI-PD outperforms all 12 compared baselines on OfficeHome across all three metrics. Against the strongest closed-set DG method XDED, SCI-PD gains 3.91% accuracy and 4.37% H-score while reducing H²-CV from 9.67% to 5.20%, indicating substantially better robustness across hybridness levels. Compared to the best OSDG method DAML, SCI-PD improves by 2.69% accuracy and 2.87% H-score. Against the VLM-based CLIPBase (which fine-tunes the full ViT-B/16), SCI-PD achieves 3.62% and 3.41% gains on accuracy and H-score respectively, while using a lightweight ResNet-18 student. The CLIP-based RISE method, despite being a recent VLM-DG approach, underperforms SCI-PD by 7.43% accuracy and 5.92% H-score, with particularly severe degradation at H=0. On PACS, SCI-PD's advantage over CIRL is most dramatic in H-score (61.21% vs. 46.36%), and on DomainNet it surpasses all conventional DG methods even under the most data-scarce setting (H=1/10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Open Set Domain Generalization (OSDG), which extends closed-set DG to handle unseen target classes (DAML, EDir-CORAL, MEDIC); (2) Vision-Language Models for DG, where prior work (RISE, MIRO, CLIP-Base) fine-tunes or re-trains CLIP's vision encoder for the target task; and (3) knowledge distillation, which transfers teacher knowledge to lightweight students. The key departure is rejecting the fine-tuning paradigm: instead of adapting a large VLM, SCI-PD distills CLIP's semantic knowledge into any lightweight backbone via three perturbation signals (score, instance, class). Additionally, the paper introduces the HDG benchmark, which generalizes prior OSDG benchmarks by allowing source domains to have disparate label sets rather than requiring identical fixed splits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HDG benchmark and H²-CV metric expose a practical blind spot: many SOTA DG/OSDG methods that look strong under identical-label-set evaluation degrade sharply when source domains have overlapping but non-identical class coverage, a situation that naturally arises in real-world data collection. This reframes the evaluation question from 'does the method work on this split?' to 'is the method robust across splits?', which is more aligned with deployment reality. Limitations include the continued dependence on a large CLIP teacher (so the distillation saves inference cost but not the upfront cost of obtaining the teacher), moderate gains over CLIPBase (~3–4%), and the HDG splits being artificially constructed rather than drawn from naturally heterogeneous sources. Open questions include whether the perturbation design transfers to other VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2-based) and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal DG settings.
