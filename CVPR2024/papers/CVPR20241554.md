## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for object detection only quantize the backbone and neck while leaving the detection head in full precision, missing significant compression gains. When the head is quantized, performance degrades severely because regression tasks (bounding box prediction) are more sensitive to quantization noise, exhibit non-uniform weight distributions, and are poorly calibrated by classification-oriented metrics like local reconstruction error or diagonal Hessian approximation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241554/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and produces bounding boxes with class labels, operating on one-stage (RetinaNet, YOLOF) and two-stage (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN) detectors with ResNet-50/101 backbones. Full quantization is applied to all intermediate layers at W-bit weights and A-bit activations (e.g., W4A4), with the first layer held at 8-bit and the final prediction layer kept in full precision. Calibration uses a small set of COCO images, and the quantized model is deployed with integer arithmetic where the LLAQ quantizer replaces weight multiplication with a bit-shift operation.

## Method

Reg-PTQ introduces two regression-specialized techniques. First, Filtered Global Loss Integration Calibration (FGIC) combines a local Hessian-guided reconstruction loss with a global prediction loss (cross-entropy for classification plus Lp loss for regression), and applies a two-step filtering mechanism that discards bounding boxes below a confidence threshold θ_C and those with IoU below θ_I between quantized and full-precision outputs, ensuring clean gradients for fine-tuning scaling factors. Second, the Learnable Logarithmic Affine Quantizer (LLAQ) applies a learnable transformation ψ(x) = k*·log(x) + a* (with separate parameters for positive and negative sides of the distribution center) to project the quasi-Laplace/Gaussian weight distribution of regression layers into a near-uniform space before uniform quantization, with the quantized values stored as exponents for efficient bit-shift inference. The theoretical analysis shows that distance-based Lp loss functions in regression inherently produce non-uniform weight distributions, explaining why uniform quantizers and classification-oriented calibration metrics fail for detection heads.

## Results

On COCO, Reg-PTQ achieves near-lossless mAP under W4A8 quantization (e.g., 37.4% on RetinaNet ResNet-50 vs. 37.4% full-precision) and only a 0.7% drop under W4A4 (36.7% on RetinaNet ResNet-50). Under the more aggressive W3A3 setting, Reg-PTQ reaches 28.1% mAP on RetinaNet ResNet-50 and 29.1% on Faster R-CNN ResNet-101, outperforming the best baseline by 1.6–5.0 mAP. The fully quantized detectors achieve approximately 7.6× and 5.4× reduction in FLOPs and storage respectively under INT4, with up to 14.2× FLOPs reduction under W2A4 on Faster R-CNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reg-PTQ consistently outperforms all six baselines (AdaRound, AdaQuant, BRECQ, PD-Quant, QDrop, SubSetQ) across every bit-width and detector architecture. The advantage is most pronounced at low bit-widths: under W2A4, Reg-PTQ leads the best baseline (QDrop) by 4.0 mAP on RetinaNet ResNet-50 (23.9 vs. 19.9) and by 4.0–5.0 mAP on two-stage detectors under W3A3 (e.g., 28.1 vs. 23.6 on Faster R-CNN ResNet-50). Several baselines suffer catastrophic failure at low bits—AdaRound drops to 0.6 mAP on Faster R-CNN ResNet-50 under W4A4, and PD-Quant collapses to 1.7 mAP under W2A4—while Reg-PTQ maintains stable performance. Under W4A8, Reg-PTQ achieves near-lossless results (within 0.3–0.7 mAP of full-precision), marginally ahead of QDrop and SubSetQ. The gains are particularly large for two-stage detectors (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN), where the regression heads are more complex and existing PTQ methods degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTQ lineage established by BRECQ (block-wise Hessian-guided calibration) and AdaRound (adaptive rounding for weight tuning) by identifying that their core assumptions break down for regression heads in object detectors. While prior detection-quantization work (Q-DETR, Q-YOLO, DetPTQ) addressed specific architectures or left the detection head unquantized, Reg-PTQ positions itself as the first universal framework for full quantization across diverse detector families. Conceptually, it departs from the classification-centric calibration paradigm by introducing a global prediction loss with bounding-box filtering (FGIC) and a non-uniform quantizer (LLAQ) motivated by the theoretical link between distance-based regression losses and Laplace/Gaussian weight distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: full INT4 quantization of detectors including heads yields ~7.6× computation and ~5.4× storage reduction with <1% mAP loss, making edge deployment of modern detectors substantially more feasible. The theoretical insight that distance-based objectives inherently produce non-uniform weight distributions (unlike classification) provides a principled explanation for why uniform quantizers underperform on regression heads, which should inform future quantization design beyond detection. Open questions include whether the logarithmic-affine transformation generalizes to transformer-based detectors (only briefly mentioned in supplementary), whether the two-step filtering thresholds require per-architecture tuning, and whether the approach extends to other regression-heavy tasks like keypoint estimation or depth prediction.
