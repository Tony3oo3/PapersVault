## Problem

Existing VLM evaluation benchmarks overwhelmingly target the third-person perspective, and the few that address first-person views cover only narrow task subsets (e.g., spatial/temporal/causal in EgoTaskQA, or object/action/person in EgoVQA). The broader capability of VLMs to 'think' from a first-person perspective—encompassing object recognition, activity understanding, spatial reasoning, forecasting, and planning—remains largely unexplored, despite being critical for embodied AI and robotics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240982/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item pairs a single egocentric image frame (extracted from Ego4D videos, sampled every few dozen frames to ensure diversity) with a manually crafted first-person text question. VLMs produce free-form open-ended text answers, which are then scored by a GPT-4 judge on a 0 / 0.5 / 1 scale (wrong / partially correct / correct). The 700-question benchmark spans 12 fine-grained dimensions across 6 capability categories, with at least 50 distinct clips per dimension drawn from 595 source videos to guarantee scene diversity.

## Method

EgoThink is constructed by first defining six core first-person capabilities (object, activity, localization, reasoning, forecasting, planning) and decomposing them into twelve fine-grained dimensions (e.g., existence, attribute, affordance, location, spatial relationship, counting, comparison, situated reasoning, navigation, assistance). Frames are extracted from the Ego4D egocentric video dataset, filtered for clarity and egocentric characteristics, and manually annotated by six annotators (each responsible for two dimensions) with three additional reviewers ensuring correctness. For evaluation, GPT-4 serves as an automatic judge for the open-ended answers, achieving a Pearson correlation of 0.68 with human raters. The paper then conducts zero-shot evaluation of 21 VLMs (ranging from ~7B to ~13B parameters, plus GPT-4V) and performs ablation analyses on LLM size, instruction tuning, and visual grounding information to identify which architectural components most influence first-person reasoning performance.

## Results

On the EgoThink benchmark (700 open-ended QA pairs across 12 dimensions), GPT-4V achieves the highest average single-answer grading score of 65.5, evaluated by GPT-4 as automatic judge. Among open-source models, LLaVA-1.5-13B (55.3) and LLaVA-13B-Llama2 (55.1) lead the ~13B group, while ShareGPT4V-7B (51.9) is the best ~7B model. Counting is the most challenging dimension, where even GPT-4V scores only 42.0, and forecasting is similarly difficult (GPT-4V: 55.0). Notably, BLIP-2-11B surpasses GPT-4V on both localization sub-dimensions (Location: 90.0 vs. 86.0; Spatial: 66.0 vs. 62.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V leads on 10 of 12 dimensions (attribute 82.0, activity 59.5, counting 42.0, situated reasoning 83.0, forecasting 55.0, navigation 64.0, assistance 84.0, etc.) but is outperformed by BLIP-2-11B on both localization sub-dimensions (Location 90.0 vs. 86.0; Spatial 66.0 vs. 62.0) and by InstructBLIP-11B on existence (74.0 vs. 62.0). Among ~7B open-source models, ShareGPT4V-7B (51.9 avg) and LLaVA-7B (49.6) are the strongest, with ShareGPT4V-7B also leading on existence (67.0) and attribute (75.0) within its size class. Among ~13B models, LLaVA-1.5-13B (55.3) and LLaVA-13B-Llama2 (55.1) are the top performers, with LLaVA-1.5-13B winning on comparison (56.0) and LLaVA-13B-Llama2 winning on navigation (49.0) and assistance (71.0). InstructBLIP-11B (51.5) is competitive on existence and forecasting but weak on navigation (16.0) and assistance (17.0). The ~7B to ~13B scaling gap is most pronounced for LLaVA (49.6 → 55.1) and LLaVA-1.5 (39.0 → 55.3), while PandaGPT actually degrades from 46.2 to 43.1, suggesting that simply scaling the LLM without better alignment does not guarantee improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM evaluation landscape by addressing a clear gap identified in Table 1: most comprehensive benchmarks (MME, MMBench, LVLM-eHub, etc.) evaluate third-person capabilities, while the few first-person benchmarks (EgoTaskQA, EgoVQA) cover only narrow subsets of capabilities. Conceptually, it builds on the LLM-as-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., MT-Bench) for scoring open-ended VLM outputs, and draws its visual data from the Ego4D egocentric video dataset. The six-capability, twelve-dimension taxonomy is inspired by the natural questions humans ask when interacting with the world ('What is around me?', 'What am I doing?', etc.), positioning first-person thinking as a distinct and underexplored axis of VLM evaluation beyond standard object/activity/spatial tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoThink provides the first comprehensive, multi-dimensional benchmark for first-person perspective reasoning in VLMs, a capability directly relevant to embodied AI and robotics where agents must reason from their own viewpoint. The finding that even GPT-4V averages only ~65.5 and struggles most on counting (42.0) and forecasting (55.0) highlights substantial headroom, while the observation that trainable parameter count (not total parameters) drives performance offers a practical design signal. Key limitations include the small scale (700 images), image-only modality (no video), and reliance on GPT-4 as evaluator (0.68 Pearson correlation with humans). Open questions include whether video input would substantially change the difficulty landscape, whether the evaluation protocol generalizes to longer/more complex answers, and how these capabilities transfer to real robotic control loops.
