## Problem

Super-resolution models trained on synthetic degradation data (e.g., BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN) produce over-smoothed outputs on real-world images because the gap between simulated and authentic degradation remains unaddressed. Existing remedies—unsupervised GAN-based methods, single-image self-supervised approaches, and supervised fine-tuning on limited real pairs—each suffer from instability, reliance on repetitive patterns, or poor cross-sensor generalization. The paper asks whether supervised pre-training and self-supervised learning on test images can be merged to close this domain gap without requiring paired HR/LR data in the target domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241398/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single real-world LR image (128×128 patches for 4× SR) and produces a 512×512 super-resolved HR image. No paired HR reference is needed at test time; the only prerequisite is a pre-trained LR reconstruction network (trained on 6,000 in-house real paired images). The framework is architecture-agnostic and has been applied to CNN (Real-ESRGAN+, BSRGAN), Transformer (SwinIR-GAN), VQGAN (FeMaSR), and diffusion (StableSR) backbones, with fine-tuning performed collectively on the entire test set or per-image.

## Method

LWay operates in two stages. First, an LR reconstruction branch is pre-trained: a Degradation Encoder extracts a 512-dimensional embedding from an LR image, and a Reconstructor uses that embedding together with an HR image to synthesize the corresponding LR image, optimized with L1 + LPIPS loss. Second, for an unseen test LR image, a pre-trained SR model produces an initial SR output; this output is passed through the frozen Reconstructor conditioned on the test image's degradation embedding, and a self-supervised loss (L1 + LPIPS) between the reconstructed LR and the original LR image drives fine-tuning of a subset of SR parameters. A Discrete Wavelet Transform isolates the high-frequency component of the LR image, which is normalized into a [0,1] weight map and applied element-wise to the loss, concentrating optimization on high-frequency detail recovery while protecting low-frequency content from artifacts.

## Results

On RealSR Nikon, LWay applied to Real-ESRGAN+ yields a +1.77 dB PSNR gain and −0.0531 LPIPS reduction; on the same sensor, FeMaSR gains +2.46 dB PSNR and −0.071 LPIPS. Across all five model families (CNN, Transformer, VQ, Diffusion) and all five sensor datasets, every metric improves consistently, with the largest LPIPS drops reaching −0.0796 (FeMaSR on Canon). In the real-world film restoration user study (24 raters, 0–10 scale), LWay-augmented StableSR surpasses the second-best method by more than 2 points, while DASR, DiffBIR, and DARSR score below the original LR images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LWay is an additive training framework, so for the five primary baselines (Real-ESRGAN+, BSRGAN, SwinIR-GAN, FeMaSR, StableSR) it consistently improves every metric; the largest gains appear on VQ-based FeMaSR (+2.44–2.46 dB PSNR) and CNN-based Real-ESRGAN+ (+1.67–1.77 dB PSNR), while BSRGAN shows the smallest PSNR gains (+0.04–1.07 dB) but still improves LPIPS by up to −0.0552. In the real-world film-restoration comparison (Section 4.3), LWay-augmented StableSR outperforms ZSSR, DASR, LDM, DiffBIR, DARSR, and CAL_GAN in both visual quality and the 24-rater user study, where it leads the second-best method by >2 points on a 0–10 scale. Notably, DASR, DiffBIR, and DARSR received user scores *below* the original LR input, indicating they degrade rather than restore real-world images. The paper also shows that supervised fine-tuning on one real sensor's paired data transfers poorly to another sensor (Table 5, Figure 7), whereas LWay's self-supervised per-test-set fine-tuning avoids this domain gap entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves a new training paradigm (LWay) that sits between the three established approaches to real-world SR: GAN-based unsupervised learning (CycleGAN, KernelGAN, CinCGAN), supervised learning on synthetic paired data (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN), and single-image self-supervised learning (ZSSR, Deep Image Prior). Its core conceptual departure is exploiting the asymmetry that HR→LR degradation is easier and more robust than LR→HR reconstruction: a lightweight LR reconstruction network is pre-trained to extract degradation embeddings, then frozen as a self-supervised 'degradation simulator' for fine-tuning any off-the-shelf SR model on unseen test images. The DWT-based high-frequency weighting further distinguishes it from prior self-supervised SR methods that operate uniformly across all frequency bands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of LWay—requiring no architectural changes and no paired target-domain data—makes it immediately applicable to the full spectrum of modern SR backbones (CNN, Transformer, VQGAN, diffusion), which is practically significant for real-world deployment. A notable finding is that supervised fine-tuning on real paired data from one sensor transfers poorly to another (Table 5, Figure 7), underscoring that the synthetic-to-real gap is not merely a data-quantity issue but a distributional one that per-image self-supervision addresses more effectively. Open questions include whether the 6K-image dependency for the LR reconstruction network can be further reduced, how the approach extends to video or multi-frame SR, and whether the self-supervised fine-tuning signal remains informative for extremely diverse or adversarial degradations.
