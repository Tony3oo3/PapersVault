## Problem

Existing physics-based motion generation methods either rely on imitation learning (constrained to training distributions) or large-scale pretrained models (producing physically implausible motions), and none can learn open-vocabulary physical interaction skills from arbitrary text without manually engineered, task-specific rewards. This paper addresses the gap of enabling a humanoid agent to acquire novel, physically plausible motions—including interactions with dynamic objects—from any natural language description.

## Input / Output

The method takes an open-vocabulary text instruction (e.g., 'kick the white ball', 'raise arm, open the door') of arbitrary length and complexity, along with a physics simulation environment in IsaacGym that may contain dynamic objects. It produces a temporally coherent 3D humanoid motion sequence at 120 Hz simulation frequency, where the high-level policy selects atomic actions at 6 Hz and the low-level controller executes them at 30 Hz. The agent is rendered from a fixed camera at (3 m, 0 m, 1 m) with dynamic orientation tracking to the pelvis, and the rendered image is encoded by CLIP-ViT-B/32 for reward computation.

## Method

AnySkill is a hierarchical framework with two levels: a shared low-level controller trained via GAIL (following CALM) on 93 motion patterns to distill a 64-dimensional latent space of physically plausible atomic actions, and a per-instruction high-level PPO policy (two-layer MLP) that composes these atomic actions by outputting latent codes. The key insight is using CLIP image-text cosine similarity between the agent's rendered image and the instruction as the high-level reward, which eliminates manual reward engineering and naturally encodes the surrounding environment to enable object interactions without architectural changes. A latent-representation alignment term (Gaussian kernel on distance to a sampled latent) is added to keep the high-level policy within the learned action manifold. An early termination strategy resets the environment with 80% probability after eight consecutive CLIP-similarity drops or deterministically if head height falls below 15 cm, preventing convergence to suboptimal local minima. A state-to-CLIP-feature projection MLP, fine-tuned on 104 million accumulated states, replaces real-time rendering for a 10.4× training speedup.

## Results

On 5 open-vocabulary text descriptions evaluated by 24 MTurk workers (0–10 scale), AnySkill (full) achieves a Success score of 6.16, Naturalness of 6.23, Smoothness of 6.51, Physical plausibility of 6.93, and CLIP similarity of 24.18, outperforming both AvatarCLIP (Success 4.29, CLIP_S 21.11) and MotionCLIP (Success 3.16, CLIP_S 21.16) across all five metrics. In the reward-function comparison (Table 3), AnySkill's composite CLIP + latent-alignment reward also surpasses VLM-RMs (CLIP_S 19.46), CLIP-S (19.78), VideoCLIP/AvgPool (20.25), and ASE velocity reward (18.39). For interaction motions with dynamic objects, AnySkill maintains a CLIP_S of 24.49 while preserving a Success score of 5.42, demonstrating that the image-based reward generalizes to object interaction without algorithmic changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnySkill outperforms both open-vocabulary motion generation baselines—AvatarCLIP [11] and MotionCLIP [43]—on every metric in Table 1, with the largest gaps in Success (+1.87 over AvatarCLIP, +3.00 over MotionCLIP) and CLIP similarity (+3.07 and +3.02 respectively), indicating substantially better text-motion alignment. In the reward-function ablation (Table 3), AnySkill's composite reward beats VLM-RMs [37] by +3.01 in Success and +4.72 in CLIP_S, and beats CLIP-S [69] by +2.36 in Success and +4.40 in CLIP_S. Against VideoCLIP/AvgPool [56], AnySkill wins on Success (+1.07), Naturalness (+0.27), Physics (+0.23), and CLIP_S (+3.93), though AvgPool edges out AnySkill by 0.04 on Smoothness (6.55 vs 6.51). The ASE velocity-reward variant [32] is the weakest baseline, trailing AnySkill by +3.43 in Success and +5.79 in CLIP_S, confirming that simple velocity penalties are insufficient for open-vocabulary skill learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnySkill builds directly on CALM's GAIL-based latent action space for the low-level controller, but departs from prior open-vocabulary motion generation (MotionCLIP, AvatarCLIP) by grounding generation in a physics simulator rather than learned motion priors, and from physics-based character control (DeepMimic, AMP, ASE) by replacing handcrafted task-specific rewards with a CLIP image-text similarity signal. The key conceptual departure is the hierarchical decomposition: a shared, instruction-agnostic low-level policy ensures physical plausibility, while a per-instruction high-level policy handles open-vocabulary composition, sidestepping the need for manual reward engineering that plagues prior interactive character control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnySkill demonstrates that a frozen CLIP model can serve as a sufficiently general reward for learning physically plausible interactions with arbitrary objects, eliminating the per-task reward design bottleneck that has limited physics-based character control. However, the approach requires training a new high-level policy for each instruction (~2.2 hours), limiting true zero-shot deployment, and struggles with prolonged or temporally complex actions due to the frame-level nature of CLIP similarity. Open questions include whether the per-instruction training can be amortized into a single generalized high-level policy, whether temporal CLIP variants or video-language models can address the duration limitation, and whether the method scales to multi-object or multi-agent interactions.
