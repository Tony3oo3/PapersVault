## Problem

Detecting procedural errors (omission, addition, modification, slip, correction) in egocentric task videos remains largely unaddressed: existing procedural video research focuses on segmentation, recognition, and planning using only error-free videos, and prior error datasets contain only action-ordering errors or rely on real-time human correction. This paper tackles the dual gap of both a diverse egocentric error dataset and a detection framework that must identify errors never seen during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240958/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes egocentric RGB video (1280×720 at 15 fps) from which I3D frame features are pre-extracted, along with per-frame object/hand bounding boxes and contact states (touching or not) from a Faster R-CNN detector. At inference it produces per-frame step labels, a binary error/normal flag per frame, an error type per segment, and segment boundaries. Notably, training uses only normal (error-free) videos with full framewise step supervision, and the error threshold per step is calibrated on a validation set of normal videos only.

## Method

EgoPED fuses two feature streams: holistic framewise features from a pluggable temporal action segmentation backbone (ActionFormer, MSTCN++, or DiffACT) and relational features from a 3-layer GCN operating on a per-frame interaction graph whose nodes are object classes and edges connect active (hand-contacted) objects. A contrastive step prototype learning (CSPL) module then learns k K-means prototypes per step via an InfoNCE loss that pulls frame features toward their step's nearest prototype while pushing them away from features of other steps, capturing the natural variation of correct execution across videos. At inference, a frame is flagged as erroneous if its maximum cosine similarity to the prototypes of its predicted step falls below a per-step threshold (mean + γ·std from validation similarities), and omission errors are detected by computing the edit distance between the predicted step sequence and the closest training transcript to identify missing steps. The framework is trained in two stages—first the TAS loss alone, then end-to-end with the CSPL loss—and requires no error examples or task graph at training time.

## Results

On the EgoPER dataset, EgoPED (with ActionFormer backbone) achieves 57.0% segment-wise Error Detection Accuracy (EDA) and 62.0% framewise AUC averaged across all five cooking tasks, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline S3R (43.0% EDA, 54.9% AUC) and reconstruction-based methods HF²-VAD (27.1% EDA, 59.9% AUC). On HoloAssist, EgoPED improves EDA by ~17% over all baselines (68.0–71.0% vs. 51.2–51.6% for S3R). On the ATA dataset under weak supervision, EgoPED with DiffACT backbone achieves 61.7% F1_error, surpassing the prior best ATA method (59.2%) and CDFL (0.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoPED outperforms all four video anomaly detection baselines on EgoPER by a wide margin: it gains +14.0 EDA over S3R (57.0 vs. 43.0) and +29.9 EDA over HF²-VAD (57.0 vs. 27.1), while also achieving the highest AUC (62.0 vs. 59.9 for HF²-VAD and 54.9 for S3R). The reconstruction-based methods (HF²-VAD and HF²-VAD+SSPCAB) produce highly fluctuating framewise predictions that hurt segment-level EDA despite competitive AUC scores, highlighting their unsuitability for procedural error detection. On HoloAssist, EgoPED (AF) achieves 68.0% EDA (verb) and 71.0% EDA (noun), beating S3R by ~17% and HF²-VAD by ~44–48% in EDA, though S3R slightly edges out EgoPED on AUC for the verb setting (48.6 vs. 47.3). On ATA under weak supervision, EgoPED (DiffACT) surpasses the prior state-of-the-art ATA method [24] in F1_error (61.7 vs. 59.2) and Edit score (74.0 vs. 68.8), while CDFL [34] and TASL [44] achieve 0.0% F1_error, indicating they cannot detect errors at all. The paper notes that EgoPED's advantage is robust across different action segmentation backbones (AF, MSTCN++, DiffACT), with AF yielding the best overall results due to its boundary detection module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of procedural task understanding and anomaly detection, but departs from both. Prior procedural task work (action segmentation, recognition, planning) has been studied almost exclusively on error-free videos, while video anomaly detection methods target goal-agnostic, semantically identifiable deviations (e.g., a person falling) rather than procedure-dependent errors requiring long-range temporal reasoning. The few existing procedural error datasets (Assembly101, ATA, HoloAssist) cover only narrow error types (ordering or slip), and the paper introduces a broader taxonomy (omission, addition, modification, slip, correction) with a dedicated egocentric dataset. Methodologically, EgoPED repurposes off-the-shelf temporal action segmentation backbones and augments them with a contrastive prototype learning module and a GCN-based relational feature branch built from active object detection, rather than proposing a new segmentation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EgoPER dataset (28 hours, 5 cooking tasks, multimodal, with object/active-object bounding boxes and dense error annotations) fills a notable gap in the procedural video literature and provides a benchmark for a task that was previously under-addressed. The framework's modularity—working with any TAS backbone and requiring only error-free training videos—makes it practically relevant for wearable task assistants that cannot assume access to error examples. Key limitations include the restriction to the cooking domain, the offline setting, and the underutilization of available modalities (depth, audio, gaze). Open questions include scaling to more diverse task domains, online/real-time detection, and automatically selecting the number of prototypes per step.
