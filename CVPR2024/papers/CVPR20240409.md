## Problem

Image manipulation localization models suffer from severe overfitting because existing manually forged datasets are tiny (at most ~5K images) and synthetic data exhibits a large domain gap from real-world manipulations. The paper addresses this by proposing a paradigm to automatically annotate web-scale manually forged images using constrained IML models, and by constructing a 123K-image dataset (MIML) that dramatically outperforms prior benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240409/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the IML task, a single RGB image resized to 512×512 is fed to APSC-Net, producing a pixel-level binary manipulation mask. For the constrained auto-annotation task (CAAA), an authentic-forged image pair is first classified as SPG or SDG; SPG pairs are processed by concatenating the authentic image, forged image, and their pixel-wise difference map along the channel dimension into a segmentation network, while SDG pairs undergo cross-level correlation matching between multi-scale feature maps. The MIML dataset spans resolutions from 45×120 to 13846×9200 pixels, far exceeding prior datasets.

## Method

The CAAA paradigm splits constrained IML into two branches: for SPG (shared background), a Difference-Aware Semantic Segmentation model denoises the raw difference map using semantic cues from both images; for SDG (shared foreground), a Semantic Aligned Correlation Matching model performs top-down feature fusion followed by cross-level correlation (matching level-i features of one image against all levels of the other) to handle semantic misalignment caused by rescaling. A self-supervised classifier (trained on synthetically generated SPG/SDG pairs) routes each input pair to the correct branch. A Quality Evaluation Score (QES) metric, based on the ratio of high-confidence pixels to total predicted area, automatically filters unreliable pseudo-labels without ground truth. The resulting MIML dataset (123,150 images from imgur.com) is used to train APSC-Net, which combines an Adaptive Perception Module (adaptive weighted fusion of multi-level features) and a Self-Calibration Module (segmentation-based and classification-based recalibration of the initial mask prediction) on a ConvNeXt-Base backbone.

## Results

On the four standard IML benchmarks, APSC-Net trained with MIML achieves IoU of 0.810 (CASIAv1), 0.525 (NIST16), 0.498 (Coverage), and 0.962 (Columbia), with corresponding F1-scores of 0.848, 0.590, 0.568, and 0.973. The most dramatic improvement is on NIST16, where APSC-Net (w/MIML) reaches IoU 0.525 versus 0.279 for the previous best (TruFor), a 88% relative gain. The MIML dataset alone boosts PSCC-Net's IoU on IMD20 from 0.125 to 0.470 (+277%) and CAT-Netv2 from 0.157 to 0.547 (+248%), confirming the dataset's broad utility across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APSC-Net (w/MIML) outperforms all ten baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest prior IML method TruFor [6], it gains +0.180 IoU on CASIAv1 (0.810 vs 0.630), +0.246 on NIST16 (0.525 vs 0.279), +0.052 on Coverage (0.498 vs 0.446), and +0.214 on Columbia (0.962 vs 0.748). CAT-Netv2 [12] is the closest competitor on CASIAv1 (0.684) and Columbia (0.760) but is surpassed by 0.126 and 0.202 IoU respectively; on NIST16 and Coverage it trails by 0.287 and 0.260 IoU. Among CIML baselines, DMVN [32] and DMAC [19] are far behind on IMD20: on SPG, APSC-Net's DASS reaches IoU 0.834 (VAN backbone) versus 0.578 (DMVN) and 0.573 (DMAC); on SDG, SACM reaches 0.702 versus 0.317 (DMVN) and 0.518 (DMAC). Notably, DMVN and DMAC trained jointly on both SPG and SDG data perform worse than their single-group counterparts, validating the paper's category-aware paradigm. The remaining IML baselines (ManTra-Net, RRU-Net, MVSS-Net, PSCC-Net, IF-OSN, EVP) are all surpassed by at least 0.1 IoU on every benchmark, with the largest gaps on NIST16 where several methods score below 0.10 IoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two established lines: (1) image manipulation localization (IML), where prior work has explored noise-domain features (Zhou et al., Dong et al.), semantic suppression (Sun et al., Zhou et al.), and various CNN architectures (ManTraNet, PSCC-Net, CAT-Net, TruFor); and (2) constrained IML (CIML), where prior methods (DMVN, DMAC) uniformly apply a single correlation-matching model to all image pairs. The key conceptual departure is the CAAA paradigm, which argues that Shared Probe Group (SPG) and Shared Donor Group (SDG) pairs have fundamentally different structure (shared background vs. shared foreground) and should be handled by separate models—difference-aware semantic segmentation for SPG and cross-level correlation matching for SDG—rather than a single unified correlator. The paper further repurposes the trained CIML models as pseudo-labelers for web-scale IML data, a pipeline not previously explored in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIML dataset (123,150 manually forged images) is roughly 60× larger than the prior largest IML dataset (IMD20) and is publicly released, directly addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck that has limited generalization in IML models. The CAAA + QES auto-annotation pipeline offers a practical, scalable recipe for expanding IML training data from web sources without manual labeling, and the QES metric provides a ground-truth-free quality filter that correlates well with IoU. Limitations include dependence on the quality of the underlying CIML annotator, a single source (imgur.com) that may introduce stylistic bias, and the QES threshold (0.5) being somewhat heuristic. Open questions include whether the auto-annotation approach transfers to manipulation types underrepresented on imgur (e.g., GAN-generated forgeries) and whether the dataset's diversity is sufficient for all real-world forensic scenarios.
