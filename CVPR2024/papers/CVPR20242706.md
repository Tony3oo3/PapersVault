## Problem

Existing debiasing methods that operate without bias labels primarily emphasize bias-conflicting (BC) samples during training but fail to provide explicit spatial guidance on where in the image the model should focus to learn intrinsic features. Without knowing which spatial regions correspond to class-defining attributes versus spurious bias attributes, the model cannot be directly steered toward the correct features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242706/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes two RGB images per iteration: the original input x and an auxiliary bias-negative sample x^BN from the same class, both processed through a shared CNN backbone to produce intermediate spatial feature maps of shape h×w×c. At inference, only the debiased model f_d is used with a single RGB image, producing a class label via global average pooling and a linear classifier. The IE weight is a spatial map of shape h×w that modulates the intermediate features before classification.

## Method

The method constructs bias-contrastive pairs (x, x^BN) where x^BN is a BC sample dynamically selected from a candidate pool using a bias-negative (BN) score that tracks the biased model's classification loss over training via exponential moving averages, excluding samples whose loss decreases (indicating bias attributes). An Intrinsic Feature Enhancement (IE) weight is computed by combining a common feature score (dot-product similarity between spatially corresponding features of z and z^BN) and a relative-exploitation score (comparing Grad-CAM explanation maps to identify features under-exploited in x relative to x^BN). The IE weight is element-wise multiplied with the intermediate features to produce spatial guidance g(z) that amplifies under-exploited intrinsic regions. Training uses a composite objective: a reweighted CE loss on x, a guidance loss (L1 distance between GAP(z) and GAP(g(z)) plus CE on the guided features), and a BN loss (CE on x^BN) weighted by the BN score to emphasize BC samples.

## Results

The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art classification accuracy across all three datasets and all bias severities. On Waterbirds (1% bias), it reaches 65.22% accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline LfF+BE (62.58%) by 2.64 points. On BFFHQ (1% bias), it achieves 77.56%, outperforming LfF+BE (75.08%) by 2.48 points. On BAR (1% bias), it reaches 75.14%, exceeding DisEnt+BE (73.29%) by 1.85 points. Gains are consistent even under extreme bias severity (0.5%), where the method leads by 2.42 points on Waterbirds and 4.32 points on BFFHQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and bias severity setting. Against the strongest no-bias-label baselines, LfF+BE and DisEnt+BE [12], the method gains 2.64 points on Waterbirds (1%), 2.48 points on BFFHQ (1%), and 1.85 points on BAR (1%). It also surpasses methods that use explicit bias labels (LNL [8], EnD [21]) or assume known bias types (HEX [23], ReBias [2]) by 2–5 points on Waterbirds and BFFHQ. The margin is largest on BFFHQ at 0.5% bias severity, where the method leads LfF+BE by 4.32 points (71.68 vs. 67.36), indicating that explicit spatial guidance for intrinsic features is particularly beneficial under extreme bias. Notably, DisEnt+BE [12] degrades severely on Waterbirds (51.65–54.21%), while the proposed method maintains consistent gains, suggesting the IE weight provides more stable guidance than feature-space augmentation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the debiasing-without-bias-information line of work (LfF, BiasEnsemble, DisEnt) that identifies and upweights bias-conflicting (BC) samples during training. Its key departure is providing explicit spatial guidance for intrinsic features rather than merely reweighting samples: it constructs bias-contrastive pairs (BA + BC samples) and uses a common-feature score combined with a Grad-CAM-based relative-exploitation score to compute an intrinsic-feature-enhancement (IE) weight that highlights under-exploited intrinsic regions. The bias-negative (BN) score, which tracks a biased model's classification loss via EMA to dynamically filter BA samples from the auxiliary dataset, builds on the multi-biased-model filtering of BiasEnsemble and the misclassification-based BC identification of JTT. The bias-contrastive pair design draws inspiration from SelectMix and X²-model, but repurposes the pair for spatial feature identification rather than feature interpolation or intra-class compactness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that telling a model *where* to look (spatial guidance) complements telling it *which samples* to prioritize (reweighting), yielding consistent SOTA gains across three datasets and four bias severities without requiring bias labels. However, the method introduces non-trivial overhead: an auxiliary forward pass, Grad-CAM computation, EMA tracking of per-sample losses, and dynamic dataset filtering at every iteration, which may limit scalability to very large datasets or high-resolution images. Open questions include whether the Grad-CAM-based exploitation signal remains reliable for deeper or transformer-based backbones, whether the approach transfers to video or multi-modal debiasing, and whether the spatial guidance can be made differentiable to avoid the current non-differentiable argmax in the common-feature matching.
