## Problem

Existing quantization-based federated learning methods are limited to fixed-precision quantization, assigning the same bit-width to all layers regardless of their importance. Mixed-precision quantization (MPQ), which allocates varying bit-widths per layer, has never been explored in FL settings, and existing MPQ techniques require training full-precision models or perform computationally expensive searches that are infeasible on resource-constrained edge devices.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a server-delivered global model converted to a fixed-point binary representation matching its individual average bit-width budget v_n (ranging from 2 to 8 bits across the heterogeneous fleet), along with its local private dataset. The output is a mixed-precision quantized global model with a layer-wise bit-width vector b ∈ ℤ^L, where the server produces a customized fixed-point model G_n for each client n by applying a greedy pruning-growing procedure to satisfy that client's specific budget constraint. Model parameters are stored as ternary binary matrices B ∈ {0,1}^{B×C×K} with per-layer scaling factors, enabling integer-arithmetic-only computation on devices.

## Method

FedMPQ represents model parameters in B-bit fixed-point binary form and trains them using a straight-through estimator with a WAGE-inspired integer-only update rule (power-of-two rounding and Bernoulli sampling for sub-integer gradients). A group Lasso regularizer is added to the local training objective, weighted by each layer's parameter count, to promote sparsity in the binary representation; layers whose most-significant bits fall below a sparsity threshold ε are then pruned, effectively reducing their precision. At the server, collected local models (quantized at potentially different per-layer bit-widths) are de-quantized to full precision and aggregated via weighted averaging, after which a greedy pruning-growing algorithm (Alg. 2) adjusts the global bit-width vector to match each client's budget—pruning layers with the most parameters first when over budget, and growing layers with the fewest parameters first when under budget, with tie-breaking based on prior-round sparsity signals.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (α=0.5, N=10 clients), FedMPQ achieves 67.1% top-1 test accuracy, nearly matching the FPQ8 baseline (68.4%) while outperforming the fixed-precision AQFL baseline by 9.1%. On CIFAR-100, FedMPQ reaches 41.1% accuracy, within 0.2% of FPQ8 (41.3%) and 8.2% above AQFL (32.9%). On Tiny-ImageNet, FedMPQ achieves 27.0% versus AQFL's 23.5%. Across all three datasets and heterogeneity levels (α ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1}), FedMPQ consistently outperforms AQFL while approaching FPQ8 performance, despite training under heterogeneous per-client bit-width budgets (2–8 bits).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMPQ is compared against five baselines: FP32 FedAvg (full-precision), FedPAQ and UVeQFed (communication-efficient but full-precision local training), FPQ8 (fixed 8-bit quantization-aware training), and AQFL (fixed-precision adaptive quantization-aware training). Against the most relevant baseline AQFL, FedMPQ wins decisively: +9.1% on CIFAR-10, +8.2% on CIFAR-100, and +3.5% on Tiny-ImageNet at α=0.5, with the gap widening under higher heterogeneity (α=0.1). Against FPQ8, FedMPQ nearly matches on CIFAR-10 (67.1% vs 68.4%) and CIFAR-100 (41.1% vs 41.3%) at α=0.5, despite operating under heterogeneous per-client budgets as low as 2 bits. Against the full-precision baselines (FP32, FedPAQ, UVeQFed), FedMPQ trails by 10–11% on CIFAR-10, which is expected given the severe bit-width constraints; however, FedPAQ and UVeQFed require clients to train full-precision models, which is infeasible on the resource-constrained devices FedMPQ targets. The scalability experiments (Table 2) confirm that FedMPQ's advantage over AQFL persists as the number of clients grows from 10 to 40, though the gap to FPQ8 widens with system size.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedMPQ extends the quantization-aware federated learning line initiated by AQFL, which first deployed fixed-precision quantization-aware training in FL to handle device heterogeneity. It draws on the binary-representation and bit-pruning framework of BSQ and the integer-arithmetic gradient update strategy of WAGE, but departs from all existing MPQ methods (search-based, optimization-based, metric-based) by eliminating the need for a full-precision reference model on the client side. The server-side pruning-growing aggregation mechanism is a new contribution not present in prior FL quantization work, which typically either discards precision differences or requires uniform bit-widths across clients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedMPQ addresses a practically important gap: real-world FL deployments involve devices with genuinely heterogeneous memory and compute budgets, and fixed-precision quantization wastes the bit-width budget of layers that don't need it. The finding that server-side pruning-growing (Algorithm 2) contributes the largest accuracy gain in the ablation suggests that how mixed-precision models are aggregated and redistributed matters as much as how they are locally trained. However, the evaluation is limited to small models (ResNet-20/44) and small datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet) with at most 40 clients, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures, ImageNet-scale data, or thousands of clients. Convergence guarantees for the mixed-precision aggregation with heterogeneous bit-widths are also not provided.
