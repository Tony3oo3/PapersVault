## Problem

Existing Joint Registration and Segmentation (JRS) methods with shared encoders treat registration and segmentation as independent tasks, relying on naive joint or alternating optimization that fails to model the coupling dependencies between task-specific decoders, resulting in unstable training. Additionally, prior approaches directly warp the labeled template for data augmentation, introducing an appearance gap between deformed labels and unlabeled images that degrades segmentation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240085/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single labeled brain MRI template (128×128×128, 1 mm isotropic) with its 13-structure FreeSurfer segmentation mask, plus a large pool of unlabeled volumes from mixed-style datasets (ABIDE, ADNI, PPMI). It outputs a per-voxel segmentation mask and a bidirectional deformation field (ψ and ψ⁻¹) mapping between the template and each unlabeled volume. A key design choice is the two-phase protocol: the shared encoder is pretrained on multi-style data and then frozen, so only the two task-specific decoders are optimized on the target dataset, enabling rapid adaptation to new datasets like OASIS.

## Method

Bi-JROS formulates the interaction between the registration decoder (upper/leader level) and segmentation decoder (lower/follower level) as a Stackelberg bi-level optimization problem, where the segmentation decoder's optimal solution constrains the registration decoder's feasible region. A Gradient Response (GR) algorithm solves this nested problem by computing the coupled gradient ∇_w Φ_r via the implicit function theorem and a Gauss-Newton outer-product approximation, reducing second-order Hessian inversions to products of first-order derivatives. The Appearance Conformity Constraint (ACC) generates pseudo-labeled pairs by applying the forward deformation ψ to the label and a weighted fusion of the reverse deformation (ψ⁻¹∘ψ) to the unlabeled image, avoiding direct warping of the labeled template and thereby closing the style gap. The frozen shared encoder, pretrained on heterogeneous multi-style data, provides task-agnostic features that accelerate convergence when transferring to new datasets.

## Results

On the mixed brain MRI dataset (ABIDE + ADNI + PPMI), Bi-JROS achieves a segmentation Dice of 82.8±2.2%, a registration Dice of 80.8±2.0%, and a registration NCC of 0.387±0.007, setting new state-of-the-art across all three metrics. On the unseen OASIS dataset, Bi-JROS reaches 81.4±0.6% segmentation Dice and 0.362±0.004 NCC in 7.8 hours of fine-tuning, outperforming BRBS (80.3% Dice, 0.342 NCC in 9.6 hours) and demonstrating faster adaptation to new data. The method also achieves the best segmentation performance on 10 out of 13 anatomical structures in per-structure evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-JROS outperforms all 14 baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest JRS competitor BRBS [18], it gains +1.0% segmentation Dice (82.8 vs 81.8), +0.8% registration Dice (80.8 vs 80.0), and a substantial +0.063 NCC (0.387 vs 0.324). Compared to UReSNet [11], the gains are +1.6% Seg Dice and +1.7% Reg Dice. Over earlier JRS methods like DataAug [42] and DeepAtlas [41], improvements reach +4.4% and +4.7% Seg Dice respectively. Against standalone registration methods (TransMorph, VoxelMorph) and standalone segmentation methods (CPS, U-Net), Bi-JROS simultaneously delivers both tasks, with registration Dice exceeding TransMorph by +3.4% and segmentation Dice exceeding CPS by +7.7%. On the OASIS rapid-adaptation test, Bi-JROS also beats BRBS by +1.1% Seg Dice and +0.020 NCC while training 1.8 hours faster (7.8 h vs 9.6 h).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Joint Registration and Segmentation (JRS) paradigm with shared encoders, building directly on U-ResNet [11] and Zhao et al. [43], which first demonstrated that a single encoder can serve both registration and segmentation. The key departure is the introduction of a bi-level (Stackelberg game) optimization formulation that explicitly models the coupling between registration and segmentation decoders, replacing the naive alternating or joint training strategies used in prior JRS methods. The Gradient Response algorithm draws on the Gaussian-Newton approximation from BRBS [18] but applies it within the nested bi-level structure, and the Appearance Conformity Constraint repurposes bidirectional deformation (inspired by CycleMorph [22] and LT-Net [38]) to generate pseudo-labels that avoid the style mismatch problem in prior one-shot segmentation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frozen shared encoder trained on multi-style data offers a practical pathway to rapid domain adaptation (7.8h vs 9.6h for BRBS on OASIS), which is valuable in clinical settings where new scanner protocols or patient populations emerge frequently. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (BRBS) are modest (~1% Dice), and the evaluation is confined to brain MRI with 13 anatomical structures, leaving open whether the bi-level formulation generalizes to other organs, modalities, or segmentation granularities. A key open question is whether the added complexity of the nested optimization and Hessian-based gradient computation is justified over simpler alternatives like carefully scheduled alternating training, particularly as model scale increases.
