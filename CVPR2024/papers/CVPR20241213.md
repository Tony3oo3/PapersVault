## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud semantic segmentation networks assume a closed-set, static world where all object categories are known at training time, making them unable to identify unknown objects or incrementally incorporate new classes. While 2D open-set methods (uncertainty estimation, generative reconstruction) exist, they degrade in 3D scenes due to irregular geometry and unbalanced semantic distributions, and the combined open-world problem (open-set identification plus incremental learning) has been barely explored for point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241213/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a raw 3D point cloud P_in ∈ R^{N×(3+c)} with N points, each carrying xyz coordinates and c-channel features, and produces two outputs: a per-point semantic probability vector O_S ∈ R^{N×C} over C known classes and a per-point uncertainty scalar O_U ∈ R^{N×1}. At inference, a threshold λ on O_U determines whether each point is assigned argmax(O_S) (known) or a dedicated unknown label (max K^t + 1). During the IL stage, the model head is extended by n novel classes and the output is supervised by a distilled ground truth combining teacher soft targets with one-hot novel labels.

## Method

PDF builds on a transformer encoder-decoder backbone (PointTransformer or StratifiedTransformer) and adds three components. First, a lightweight U-decoder with skip connections from the backbone decoder estimates per-point uncertainties of the probability output, replacing hand-crafted scores like MSP or MaxLogit. Second, a pseudo-labeling scheme generates supervision for the U-decoder: the HUA algorithm iteratively grows a seed set of low-MSP points by merging neighbors with high combined distance-and-uncertainty similarity, then the GBD algorithm embeds the resulting point set in a graph, computes its MST, fits edge weights with a two-component GMM, and cuts edges above μ₁−εσ₁ to isolate unknown objects from background contamination. Third, an incremental knowledge distillation strategy uses the frozen open-set model as a teacher, producing soft probability targets (Eq. 12) that are masked with one-hot novel-class labels to form a distilled ground truth, and the student is trained with KL-divergence to absorb new classes without catastrophic forgetting of known ones.

## Results

On S3DIS with PointTransformer, PDF achieves 73.1 AUPR and 96.2 AUROC for unknown class identification, substantially outperforming the previous best (APF: 31.6 AUPR, 90.0 AUROC) while maintaining competitive closed-set mIoU (68.6 vs. 69.3). On ScanNetv2 with StratifiedTransformer, PDF reaches 67.8 AUPR and 90.9 AUROC, again leading all baselines. For incremental learning on S3DIS, PDF attains 64.3 mIoU_novel and 70.3 mIoU_old, surpassing REAL (61.3 / 70.3) and approaching the upper bound (72 / 69.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDF dramatically outperforms all baselines on the unknown-class identification metrics (AUPR and AUROC). On S3DIS/PointTransformer, AUPR jumps from 31.6 (APF) to 73.1 and AUROC from 90.0 to 96.2, a gap of over 40 points in AUPR. On ScanNetv2/StratifiedTransformer, PDF leads REAL by 9.7 AUPR (67.8 vs. 58.1) and 0.6 AUROC (90.9 vs. 90.3). The closed-set mIoU is slightly lower than MSP/MaxLogit (which leave the network unmodified) by 0.3–1.2 points, but remains within 0.7 of the best 3D-specific baselines (REAL, APF). For incremental learning on S3DIS, PDF achieves 64.3 mIoU_novel, beating REAL (61.3) by 3.0 points and LwF (23.9) by a wide margin, while matching REAL's mIoU_old of 70.3. Direct finetuning without distillation collapses to 0.0 mIoU_old, confirming catastrophic forgetting that PDF's knowledge distillation strategy effectively prevents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the maximum softmax probability (MSP) uncertainty estimation from Hendrycks et al. (ICLR 2017) and the knowledge distillation framework of Hinton et al. (2015), repurposing them for 3D point cloud open-world segmentation. It departs from prior 3D open-world methods—REAL (ECCV 2022), which relies on redundancy classifiers and synthetic unknown objects, and APF (CVPR 2023), which uses adversarial prototypes—by instead leveraging the backbone's probability output directly through a lightweight U-decoder for uncertainty, a heuristic pseudo-labeling pipeline (HUA + GBD) for unknown-class supervision, and a distillation-based incremental learning strategy. The framework is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated on both PointTransformer and StratifiedTransformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world 3D perception agents must handle objects outside their training distribution and continuously learn new categories without catastrophic forgetting. The GBD algorithm's use of minimum spanning trees and Gaussian mixture models for 3D boundary detection is a creative adaptation of 2D edge-detection ideas to irregular point clouds. However, the authors acknowledge that performance degrades on outdoor scenes with sparse or incomplete objects, and evaluation is limited to two indoor datasets (S3DIS, ScanNetv2), leaving open whether the pseudo-labeling scheme generalizes to large-scale outdoor LiDAR or multi-modal settings. The modest mIoU trade-off (68.6 vs. 69.8 on S3DIS) for large AUPR gains also raises the question of whether the uncertainty branch can be made less costly to closed-set accuracy.
