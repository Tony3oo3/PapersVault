## Problem

Reconstructing a fully textured 3D human mesh from a single image is ill-posed: appearance in unobserved regions (e.g., the back) is missing, and 3D geometry is ambiguous in 2D. Prior data-driven methods produce oversmoothed textures and geometry in unseen regions, while diffusion-based optimization approaches require hours and struggle with clothing details. The paper addresses this by decomposing the task into a generative hallucination subproblem and a reconstruction subproblem, enabling high-quality full-body textured meshes in under two minutes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242284/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single front-view RGB image and estimated SMPL-X body parameters (pose and shape) as input. It produces two outputs: a hallucinated back-view image (intermediate) and a full-body textured 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes at 512³ resolution from a learned SDF and RGB field. The entire pipeline runs in under two minutes on a single GPU, compared to 10 minutes (Zero-1-to-3) or 6 hours (Magic123) for optimization-based alternatives.

## Method

SiTH is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (back-view hallucination) fine-tunes a pretrained Stable Diffusion U-Net with ControlNet-style trainable plugins, conditioning on the front-view image (via CLIP and VAE features), a back-view UV map, and a silhouette mask to generate a perceptually consistent back-view image; only cross-attention layers and ControlNet parameters are trained while the backbone stays frozen. Stage 2 (mesh reconstruction) uses a data-driven SDF+RGB predictor with pixel-aligned feature querying from both front and back images, where a learned normal predictor feeds the geometry branch and a raw image encoder feeds the color branch. A skinned SMPL-X body mesh serves as a 3D anchor, providing a local positional embedding (signed distance, offset vector, UV coordinates, and visibility label) that resolves depth ambiguity. Both stages are trained jointly on the same 500-scan THuman2.0 dataset, and inference requires no per-sample optimization.

## Results

On the CAPE benchmark, SiTH achieves the best Chamfer distance (1.899/2.261 cm), normal consistency (0.816), f-Score (37.763), and LPIPS (1.977×10⁻²) among all compared methods. On the higher-quality CustomHumans benchmark, SiTH again leads with CD 1.871/2.045 cm, NC 0.826, f-Score 37.029, and LPIPS 3.929/6.803×10⁻² (front/back). Notably, SiTH is trained on only 500 public THuman2.0 scans yet matches or exceeds methods trained on larger commercial datasets (e.g., ICON, ECON), and completes full textured mesh generation in under 2 minutes versus 10 minutes (Zero-1-to-3) or 6 hours (Magic-123) for optimization-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiTH outperforms all seven quantitative baselines (PIFu, PIFuHD, PaMIR, 2K2K, FOF, ICON, ECON) on every reported metric across both CAPE and CustomHumans. The closest competitor is PaMIR on CAPE (CD 2.190 vs 1.899 cm; f-Score 36.7 vs 37.8) and PIFuHD on CustomHumans f-Score (39.1 vs 37.0), though SiTH still leads on CD, NC, and LPIPS there. Methods trained on commercial datasets (ICON, ECON) underperform SiTH despite larger training data, while SiTH uses only 500 public THuman2.0 scans. In user studies, SiTH ranks first (avg. 1.05–1.55) versus PaMIR (2.15–2.36), PIFu (3.07–3.45), and PHOHRUM (3.14–3.45) for both geometry and texture quality. Against optimization-based diffusion methods, SiTH produces more accurate geometry and clothing details in under 2 minutes, compared to 10 minutes for Zero-1-to-3 and 6 hours for Magic-123; Chupa, which relies on text conditioning rather than image conditioning, fails to generate correct clothing styles and is preferred by only 36% of users versus 64% for SiTH. For back-view hallucination specifically, SiTH outperforms Pix2PixHD (overfits on 500 subjects), DreamPose (fails on unseen images), and Zero-1-to-3 (inconsistent body poses) in both perceptual quality and pose consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiTH decomposes single-view textured human reconstruction into two subproblems—generative back-view hallucination and mesh reconstruction—drawing on two distinct lineages. The hallucination module adapts pretrained Stable Diffusion with ControlNet-style conditioning (UV map, silhouette mask, CLIP image features) to produce spatially aligned back-view images, departing from optimization-based diffusion approaches (DreamFusion, Zero-1-to-3, Chupa) by operating as a feed-forward model. The reconstruction module follows the PIFu/PIFuHD pixel-aligned implicit function framework augmented with SMPL-X skinned body priors (as in PaMIR, ICON), but now leverages both front and hallucinated back views to resolve 3D ambiguity. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating the diffusion model as a learned view-synthesis component within a deterministic reconstruction pipeline rather than as an optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SiTH addresses the long-standing gap in single-view human reconstruction where unobserved regions (back-view clothing, wrinkles, textures) were either oversmoothed or absent. By training on only 500 public THuman2.0 scans yet matching methods trained on proprietary commercial data, it lowers the barrier to high-quality textured human generation. The introduction of a CustomHumans-based benchmark with higher-quality ground truth also improves evaluation rigor in the field. Open questions remain: the hallucinated back view is inherently stochastic and cannot replicate exact geometry, SMPL-X pose estimation errors propagate through the pipeline, and whether the two-stage decomposition generalizes beyond front/back view pairs to arbitrary multi-view settings is unexplored.
