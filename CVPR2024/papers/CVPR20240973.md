## Problem

In the standard Downsampling-Enhancement-Upsampling pipeline for UHD image enhancement, resampling operators and the inner enhancement model are treated as fully disentangled components, causing the resampling step to discard information that the model would have favored at full resolution. This leads to a measurable performance drop that worsens with increasing downsampling scale, yet prior work has devoted almost no attention to the resampling operators themselves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240973/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a UHD low-light RGB image (e.g., 3840×2160) and produces an enhanced image at the same resolution, operating through a frozen pre-trained enhancer (CNN-I, CNN-H, or Restormer) at an intermediate downscaled resolution. A key design constraint is that LMAR is compatible with any standard interpolation resampler (nearest, bilinear, bicubic, Lanczos2, Lanczos3) and is trained on only one resizer type before being applied to all others, adding roughly 0.3 MB of trainable parameters on top of the frozen backbone.

## Method

LMAR correlates the resizer and enhancer through two stages without modifying the frozen enhancement model. First, Compensatory Kernel Estimation (CKE) uses an MLP (implicit neural representation) to predict per-pixel 3×3×3 convolution kernels from three inputs: a relative coordinate grid, a pixel-cell scale ratio, and a back-projection residual between the UHD image and its upsampled-downsampled version. Second, Steganographic Resampling (SR) applies these kernels to the UHD image, downscales the resulting compensatory embedding, and concatenates it with the normally downsampled image through a channel-reduction convolution to form a new learnable input. Training optimizes a bottleneck feature consistency loss (SmoothL1 plus L2-normalized attention loss) that aligns the enhancer's intermediate features on the compensated downscaled input with those on the full-resolution input, while an adversarial discriminator enforces distributional similarity between the compensated and naive downscaled images. The two objectives are optimized alternately, and the entire process is trained with random scale factors sampled uniformly from [1, 4].

## Results

On UHD-LOL4K with CNN-I backbone, LMAR applied to Bicubic resampling yields up to +0.96 dB PSNR gain at ×4 downsampling (26.36 vs. 25.40) and +0.84 dB at ×5 (24.88 vs. 24.04), while maintaining near-identical performance at full UHD resolution (31.89 vs. 31.92 dB). The largest single gain reported is +1.25 dB PSNR for Lanczos3 at the ×1.8 scale (30.13 vs. 28.88). On 4KIL with Restormer, gains are more modest but consistent, reaching +0.17 dB at ×5 (25.89 vs. 25.72). The added overhead is minimal: only ~0.3 MB extra parameters and <1 ms additional inference time per resampling operation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing enhancement methods; instead, it benchmarks each resampling operator (Nearest, Bilinear, Bicubic, Lanczos2, Lanczos3) with and without LMAR, using the same pre-trained enhancer. LMAR consistently improves every resampler at every downsampling scale, with gains growing as the downsampling factor increases: from ~+0.2 dB at ×1.5 to +0.8–1.0 dB at ×4–×5 on UHD-LOL4K. At full UHD resolution (2160×3840), LMAR introduces negligible change (≤0.03 dB), confirming it does not degrade the original pipeline. A key practical finding is that LMAR trained on a single resampler (e.g., Bicubic) transfers to all other resamplers without retraining, yielding gains across the board. On the 4KIL dataset with the Restormer transformer backbone, gains are smaller (≤0.2 dB) but still uniformly positive, demonstrating cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard Downsampling-Enhancement-Upsampling paradigm for UHD image processing, where resampling operators and enhancement models are treated as independent, disentangled components. It builds on LIFF's scale-related implicit representations (relative coordinate grids and pixel cells) and the back-projection residual from super-resolution literature, repurposing both as inputs to a lightweight MLP that predicts per-pixel compensatory kernels. Unlike prior learnable resampling work (TAD, learned downscalers) that replaces interpolation operators with heavy neural networks, LMAR retains standard interpolation resamplers and instead injects model-aware compensatory information into the downscaled image, establishing a correlation between the resizer and a frozen pre-trained enhancer without retraining it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: UHD content (4K/8K) is increasingly common, and the downsampling-enhancement-upsampling pipeline is the standard workaround for computational constraints, yet the resampling step has been largely neglected. LMAR offers a drop-in improvement (up to ~1.2 dB PSNR at aggressive downsampling) that is compatible with any interpolation resampler and requires no enhancer retraining, making it broadly applicable. Limitations include evaluation only on low-light enhancement tasks, a modest ~300 KB parameter overhead, and the need for a pre-trained enhancer with accessible bottleneck features. Open questions include whether the model-aware resampling principle generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring, super-resolution) and whether the compensatory information can be distilled into a single forward pass without the iterative adversarial training.
