## Problem

Existing learning-based trajectory prediction models overfit to spurious correlations in training data, making them brittle under even small distribution shifts or perturbations not present during training. No prior approach in autonomous driving has effectively separated causal (invariant) from spurious (variant) factors in scene representations to achieve robust, generalizable prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242665/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5 seconds of past 2D agent trajectories, vector HD map polylines, and agent dynamics within a 100-meter radius, encoding them into a Dynamic Heterogeneous Information Network (DyHIN) with node types {lane, agent} and edge types {left, right, successor, predecessor, lane-agent}. Output is a set of K multimodal future trajectory hypotheses (6s on AGV2, 8s on WOMD) produced by a regression-head plus classification-head combination, where at inference only the disentangled causal representation is used for prediction.

## Method

CaDeT encodes driving scenes as a DyHIN processed by a Dynamic Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (DyHGT) augmented with a disentangled attention block that computes complementary softmax masks (α_C and α_S) to split node representations into causal and spurious subsets. A novel uncertainty-driven causal intervention mechanism then creates multiple intervened distributions by sampling perturbed feature statistics (mean and standard deviation) from non-parametric Gaussian estimates over mini-batch embeddings in the latent space, maintaining realism without altering the graph structure. The training objective combines a standard task loss computed on the causal representation with an invariance loss (L_do) that minimizes the variance of a holistic prediction loss across the intervened distributions, effectively cutting the backdoor path P_S ← P_C → y via do-calculus. At inference, only the causal representation is fed to the prediction head, discarding spurious patterns entirely.

## Results

On the AGV2 leaderboard, CaDeT achieves b-minFDE6 of 1.86, minADE6 of 0.67, minFDE6 of 1.24, and MR6 of 0.15, placing it second only to the QCNet ensemble (†) on k=6 metrics while surpassing all single-model baselines. On k=1 metrics, CaDeT records minADE1=1.74, minFDE1=4.33, and MR1=0.58. In robustness evaluations, CaDeT achieves a perturbation resistance score (PRS) of 87.6% under targeted adversarial perturbations at full training data, compared to 78.5% for Forecast-MAE, and maintains 84.6% PRS even when trained on only 50% of data. On the unseen-domain generalization test, CaDeT reduces minADE6 from 0.897 (Forecast-MAE) to 0.638, a 27% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaDeT outperforms all single-model baselines on the AGV2 k=6 metrics: it beats QCNet (1.91→1.86 b-minFDE6, 0.65→0.67 minADE6, 1.29→1.24 minFDE6), MTR (1.98→1.86 b-minFDE6), and Forecast-MAE (2.02→1.86 b-minFDE6, 0.70→0.67 minADE6, 1.39→1.24 minFDE6). Against the QCNet ensemble (†), CaDeT trails by small margins (e.g., 1.86 vs 1.78 b-minFDE6, 0.67 vs 0.62 minADE6) but surpasses the Forecast-MAE ensemble on all four k=6 metrics by up to 7%. In adversarial robustness (Table 2), CaDeT achieves PRS of 87.6% vs 78.5% (Forecast-MAE) at full data and 84.6% vs 65.96% at 50% data under targeted perturbations. On unseen-domain generalization (Table 3), CaDeT reduces minADE6 from 0.897 to 0.638 (27% improvement over Forecast-MAE). In the CausalAgents causal-perturbation benchmark on WOMD (Table 4), CaDeT achieves 92% PRS versus MTR's 80%, a 12-point gap, while also attaining the best test-set minADE6 (0.545) and minFDE6 (1.136) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Dynamic Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (DyHGT) as its scene-encoding backbone, extending it with a disentangled attention block that splits node representations into causal and spurious components. Conceptually, it departs from prior causal approaches in trajectory prediction (dual-encoder architectures, counterfactual feature injection, backdoor adjustment via cross-attention) by proposing a more general uncertainty-driven intervention in the latent space, borrowing the non-parametric feature-statistics uncertainty estimation from out-of-distribution generalization work. It also brings disentangled representation learning—a technique well-established in vision, NLP, and video analysis but largely unexplored in trajectory prediction—into the driving domain, framing spurious correlations as a causal backdoor problem rather than a domain-shift problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical autonomous driving, the demonstrated robustness gains (up to 27% improvement on unseen cities, 12% higher PRS under causal agent removal) address a well-recognized failure mode of current prediction models that overfit to spurious correlations. The finding that the model retains most of its robustness even at 50% training data suggests a path toward more data-efficient deployment. Open questions include whether the causal invariance assumption (that a sufficient causal subset always exists) holds in all driving scenarios, the computational overhead of the 1000-sample intervention set at inference, and whether the approach extends to longer prediction horizons or more complex multi-agent interactions beyond the current 6–8 second windows.
