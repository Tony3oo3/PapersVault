## Problem

Existing certified defenses against adversarial attacks are designed for unimodal models and achieve suboptimal robustness guarantees when naively extended to multi-modal settings, because the space of perturbed multi-modal inputs under per-modality l0-bounded attacks cannot be formulated as a single l0 ball. This paper addresses the absence of any certified defense specifically designed for multi-modal models, where an attacker can simultaneously perturb all modalities (e.g., both RGB and depth images, or both visual and audio streams).

## Input / Output

The method accepts a multi-modal input M = (m_1, ..., m_T) where each modality is a sequence of basic elements (pixels for images, frames for audio/video, 3D points, etc.). For road segmentation, the input is a 375×1242 RGB image (3 channels) paired with a same-resolution single-channel depth image, producing a per-pixel freespace/non-freespace mask. For emotion recognition, the input is 108 image frames plus 79,380 audio frames, producing a single class label. Certification is performed per basic element for segmentation (yielding Certified Pixel Accuracy, F-score, IoU) or per sample for classification (yielding Certified Accuracy).

## Method

MMCert independently sub-samples k_i basic elements from each of the T modalities (without replacement), then builds an ensemble classifier by aggregating predictions over N randomly sub-sampled multi-modal inputs via majority vote. The key distinction from randomized ablation is that each modality has its own sub-sampling budget k_i, enabling adaptive control of the retained fraction k_i/n_i per modality rather than a single global fraction k/n. The certification theorem (Theorem 1) derives a combinatorial lower bound on the overlap between pre-attack and post-attack sub-sampled spaces using binomial coefficients, and applies the Neyman-Pearson Lemma to certify that the ensemble prediction is unchanged under any attack modifying at most r_i elements in modality i. For segmentation, the Holm-Bonferroni procedure is adapted to certify the maximum number of per-element predictions while bounding the overall false-certification probability at α. Training uses randomized ablation data augmentation (keeping 0–5% of elements per modality) to align the training distribution with the sub-sampled test distribution.

## Results

On the RAVDESS emotion recognition dataset, when the attacker can modify 8 frames in both visual and audio modalities (r₁=r₂=8), MMCert guarantees correct predictions for over 40% of test samples, while randomized ablation drops to 0% certified accuracy. On the KITTI Road segmentation dataset, MMCert consistently achieves higher Certified Pixel Accuracy, Certified F-score, and Certified IoU than randomized ablation across all tested perturbation budgets (r₂ = 1×, 2×, 3×, 4× r₁). For different attack types on RAVDESS at r₁=r₂=10, modification attack reduces certified accuracy to 0%, while addition attack maintains >40% and deletion attack maintains >60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is randomized ablation [Levine & Feizi, 2019], the state-of-the-art certified defense for l₀ attacks on unimodal inputs, adapted to the multi-modal setting by pooling all modalities' elements into a single list before sub-sampling. MMCert outperforms randomized ablation on both the RAVDESS (emotion recognition) and KITTI Road (road segmentation) datasets across all tested perturbation budgets. The gap is most dramatic at moderate attack strengths: at r₁=r₂=8 on RAVDESS, MMCert retains >40% certified accuracy while randomized ablation falls to 0%. The paper attributes MMCert's advantage to two factors: (1) adaptive per-modality sub-sampling ratios (k₁/n₁, k₂/n₂) versus randomized ablation's uniform k/n ratio, and (2) a smaller, more stable sub-sampled input space since k₁ and k₂ are fixed per modality rather than fluctuating around a shared total k. When the attacker has asymmetric capability (r₂ > r₁), allocating a larger k₁/k₂ ratio further widens MMCert's margin over randomized ablation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMCert extends the randomized smoothing/ablation certification framework (Cohen et al. 2019; Levine & Feizi 2019) from unimodal to multi-modal settings by replacing the single global l0 ball with independent per-modality sub-sampling. The key departure from prior work is that when an attacker perturbs all modalities simultaneously with per-modality l0 bounds, the perturbed input space is a product of combinatorial sets rather than a single l0 ball, making direct extension of unimodal certificates suboptimal. For segmentation, the paper adapts the Holm-Bonferroni-based certification from Fischer et al. (2021) to the multi-modal case, and the overall proof structure follows the Neyman-Pearson-based argument from Jia et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first certified defense for multi-modal models, which are increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and medical imaging where adversarial robustness guarantees are essential. The paper demonstrates that naively extending the state-of-the-art unimodal certified defense (randomized ablation) to multi-modal inputs yields 0% certified accuracy in some settings, highlighting a non-trivial gap that practitioners would miss without this analysis. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets, consideration of only l0-like attacks (modification/addition/deletion of discrete elements), and a single baseline comparison; extending to continuous perturbation norms (l2, linf) and more diverse multi-modal architectures remains open.
