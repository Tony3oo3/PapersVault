## Problem

Motion segmentation from point trajectories requires jointly estimating cluster assignments and subspace models, creating a chicken-and-egg problem that is especially severe for high-dimensional motion models. Existing subspace clustering methods (SSC, LRR, GPCA) either scale poorly or require global optimization over all trajectories simultaneously. The paper exploits the observation that in high-dimensional trajectory space, sufficiently long tracks uniquely identify their generating motion, enabling a decoupled approach where clustering and motion estimation are solved independently.

## Input / Output

The method takes individual point trajectories of varying length (15–100 frames of 2D x,y coordinates) as input, optionally with a binary visibility mask indicating occluded frames. It produces 128-dimensional L2-normalized feature embeddings for hierarchical clustering, a time-indexed subspace basis matrix B ∈ R^{2F×k} (with k=4 for rigid motions) for motion parameter recovery, and completed trajectories via iterative subspace projection. Unlike global methods, trajectory completion operates per-track, requiring only a sufficiently long visible sub-trajectory to bootstrap the subspace estimate.

## Method

The architecture is a PointNet-style encoder using 1D temporal convolutions (x,y as two input channels) followed by global maxpooling over time, a small MLP, and L2 normalization to yield a fixed-size 128-dim embedding; notably, no cross-point pooling is applied, relying on the disjoint-subspace assumption that a single trajectory suffices to identify its motion. A coordinate-MLP decoder g_φ maps the embedding plus a continuous time index to rows of the subspace basis, parameterized by 64 damped cosine basis functions with trainable frequencies, amplitudes, and phases. Training is two-stage: first the encoder is pre-trained with a supervised InfoNCE contrastive loss using ground-truth cluster labels, then the decoder is added and the full network is trained jointly with a residual loss (projecting trajectories onto the predicted subspace) and a feature-difference loss (requiring the embedding of the reconstructed trajectory to match the original), with input dropout simulating random occlusions. This design makes the embedding simultaneously discriminative for clustering and geometrically informative for motion recovery, achieving sub-10ms inference per sequence.

## Results

On Hopkins155 (fully visible trajectories), the proposed method achieves 0.62% mean classification error with a 0.0% median, outperforming all baselines including SSC (2.45%), RSIM (1.01%), and ALC_sp (3.37%), while running in just 9 ms per sequence. On the occluded Hopkins12 dataset, the method records 5.12% mean error, which is competitive but higher than RSIM (0.68%) and ALC_sp (1.28%). On KT3DMoSeg (up to 54% corruption, 10–20 frame sequences), the method achieves 5.12% mean error, again trailing RSIM (0.70%) and ALC_sp (1.07%) but offering substantially faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the fully visible Hopkins155 benchmark, the proposed method (0.62% mean error) clearly outperforms all subspace-clustering baselines: SSC (2.45%), RSIM (1.01%), ALC_sp (3.37%), LRR (5.41%), GPCA (10.34%), and sequential RANSAC (9.76%), while also being 100× faster than SSC (9 ms vs. 920 ms) and orders of magnitude faster than MSL (19 h) and ALC_sp (6 min). On the occluded Hopkins12 dataset, the method (5.12% mean) trails RSIM (0.68%) and ALC_sp (1.28%), which exploit global matrix-completion strategies, though the paper notes its per-trajectory completion is inherently limited when individual sub-trajectories are too short. On KT3DMoSeg, the same pattern holds: RSIM (0.70%) and ALC_sp (1.07%) achieve lower error than the proposed method (5.12%), but the latter still surpasses RANSAC, GPCA, LSA, and LRR. In the cross-assumption comparison (Table 2), spectral methods (KerAdd 0.36%, Subset 0.31%) slightly edge out the proposed method (0.62%) on Hopkins155, while SYNCH (1.19%) and MODE (1.37%) are clearly worse; on Hopkins12 the proposed method (5.12%) is outperformed by all pairwise-relation methods. The key differentiator is inference speed: the proposed method processes a full sequence in under 10 ms, compared to seconds or minutes for most classical baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the traditional subspace clustering paradigm (RANSAC, GPCA, SSC, LRR) that jointly estimates subspaces and cluster assignments, by decoupling the two: a PointNet-style encoder with 1D temporal convolutions learns a trajectory-to-embedding mapping, and a coordinate-MLP (analogous to implicit neural representations like NeRF/SIREN) decodes the embedding into a subspace basis using a damped cosine trajectory basis. The training objective combines InfoNCE contrastive loss for discriminative clustering with a geometric residual loss that forces the embedding to encode actual motion parameters, effectively repurposing metric learning for geometric model identification. The approach also draws on the observation that high-dimensional trajectories (long time sequences) uniquely identify their generating motion, making the disjoint-subspace assumption practically valid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of clustering from subspace estimation eliminates the 'chicken and egg' problem that plagues traditional multi-model fitting, yielding a 10–1000× speedup (7–10 ms vs. seconds to hours) while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy on fully visible trajectories. Key limitations include the reliance on linear subspaces (affine camera assumption), the per-trajectory (non-global) nature of trajectory completion which struggles with >90% individual occlusion, and the fixed rank-4 subspace assumption. Open questions include extending to pinhole cameras, non-linear motion models, and incorporating global trajectory information for severely occluded sequences.
