## Problem

Existing all-in-one adverse weather removal methods, such as ViWS-Net, are trained on a fixed set of weather types and severely degrade when encountering unseen weather conditions at test time, with no mechanism for online adaptation. Moreover, diffusion models have not been applied to video-level adverse weather removal, and the potential synergy between the iterative nature of diffusion reverse processes and test-time adaptation has remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded video clip of 5 frames at 256×256 resolution as input and produces the corresponding restored clip. During test-time adaptation, it additionally consumes the previous clip and its restored result, from which 5 tubelets of 128×128 are randomly cropped to serve as the self-calibration proxy task. Clips are processed sequentially in an overlapping fashion, with the overlapped regions ensembled between consecutive clips to fuse pre- and post-adaptation information.

## Method

Diff-TTA is the first diffusion-based framework for all-in-one video adverse weather removal, built on a NAFNet backbone with 3D convolutions in the first and last layers. Instead of i.i.d. Gaussian noise, it introduces an ARMA-based temporal noise model (p=1, q=1, φ=0.6, τ=0.3) that generates frame-correlated noise sequences via sequential pre- and post-order passes, enabling the denoising network to learn temporal dependencies without heavy temporal encoding parameters. The key innovation is Diffusion Tubelet Self-Calibration (Diff-TSC), a proxy task embedded into each denoising step of the DDIM reverse process: tubelets from the previous clip's degraded/restored pair are used to compute the noise-estimation loss, and the network weights (excluding the last layer, following SHOT) are updated with a small learning rate at every timestep. This design exploits the natural iteration of diffusion sampling to perform adaptation without incurring the multiplicative inference cost that iterative TTA imposes on standard CNNs or Transformers, making Diff-TTA 90× faster than WeatherDiffusion.

## Results

On seen weather conditions (Rain, Haze, Snow), Diff-TTA achieves an average of 29.63 PSNR and 0.9453 SSIM, outperforming the second-best all-in-one method ViWS-Net (29.17 PSNR, 0.9394 SSIM) by 0.46 dB and 0.0059 respectively. On unseen weather conditions, Diff-TTA achieves 22.57 PSNR / 0.7281 SSIM on VRDS (rain streak + raindrop) and 22.35 PSNR / 0.7719 SSIM on RVSD (snow + fog), surpassing ViWS-Net by 1.00 dB and 2.52 dB respectively. The method is also 90× more efficient than WeatherDiffusion (6.01 s vs. 542.76 s per 5-frame clip at 256×256).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-weather baselines, DDMSNET leads on snow (32.43 PSNR) and RDD-Net on rain (30.34 PSNR), but both collapse on other weather types (e.g., RDD-Net drops to 18.36 PSNR on haze), yielding lower averages than Diff-TTA. Among multi-weather image methods, WGWS-Net (26.31 avg PSNR) and TKL (26.72) are the strongest but still trail Diff-TTA by 3+ dB on average; WeatherDiffusion is the weakest multi-weather baseline (24.12 avg PSNR) and is 90× slower. The closest competitor, ViWS-Net (the only prior video-level all-in-one method), is outperformed by Diff-TTA on rain (+0.91 dB), snow (+0.37 dB), and average (+0.46 dB), though ViWS-Net edges ahead on haze (24.51 vs. 24.56 PSNR is actually a Diff-TTA win by 0.05 dB). On unseen weather, the gap widens substantially: Diff-TTA beats ViWS-Net by 1.00 dB on VRDS and 2.52 dB on RVSD, while all image-based multi-weather methods (All-in-one, TransWeather, TKL, WeatherDiffusion) fall 1–4 dB behind, confirming that test-time adaptation is critical for out-of-distribution generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: all-in-one video adverse weather removal (ViWS-Net, WeatherDiffusion), diffusion-based restoration (DDIM, DiffIR), and test-time adaptation (TENT, SHOT). It departs from prior all-in-one weather removal by replacing the adversarial weather-type discrimination of ViWS-Net with a weather-agnostic diffusion denoising process, and from prior diffusion restoration work by extending the forward process to a temporal ARMA-based noise model rather than adding temporal parameters to the network. The key conceptual departure is integrating test-time adaptation directly into the iterative DDIM reverse process, using a tubelet self-calibration proxy task at each denoising step, rather than treating adaptation as a separate pre- or post-processing stage as in TENT or SHOT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: existing all-in-one weather removal models degrade sharply on unseen weather combinations (e.g., rain streak + raindrop, snow + fog), and the 90× efficiency over WeatherDiffusion makes diffusion-based video restoration viable for real-time applications like autonomous driving. The diffusion-TTA synergy—exploiting the shared iterative structure of denoising and adaptation to avoid multiplicative inference cost—is a clean design principle that could transfer to other iterative generative restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer video sequences beyond 5-frame clips, whether the ARMA temporal noise model generalizes to more complex inter-frame dynamics, and whether the tubelet-based proxy task remains effective under severe distribution shifts beyond the tested weather combinations.
