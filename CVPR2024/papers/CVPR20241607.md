## Problem

Context-aware emotion recognition (CAER) models suffer from context bias: they learn spurious correlations between background semantics and emotion labels (e.g., seaside scenes always mapped to positive emotions), causing severe mispredictions when contexts appear with atypical emotions. Existing debiasing approaches rely on predefined interventions that fail to properly decouple the beneficial context prior from the harmful context bias, yielding sub-optimal solutions.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a standard RGB image containing a person and their surrounding scene. The subject is localized via bounding boxes (annotated in EMOTIC, detected by Faster R-CNN in CAER-S), and the context branch receives the image with the subject region zeroed out. The output is a multi-label emotion score vector (26 categories on EMOTIC, 7 on CAER-S) computed as the difference between a factual fused score and a counterfactual fused score, yielding a debiased Total Indirect Effect (TIE) prediction.

## Method

CLEF formulates a causal graph over input X, subject features S, context features C, ensemble representations E, and predictions Y, identifying the context bias as the adverse direct effect along X→C→Y and the useful context prior as the indirect effect along X→C/S→E→Y. A non-invasive context branch (ResNet-152 pre-trained on Places365) receives subject-masked images to explicitly capture the direct context effect, while the vanilla CAER model provides the factual ensemble prediction. During training, both the factual outcome Y_{c,e}(X) and a counterfactual outcome Y_{c,e*}(X) (where ensemble representations are blocked via a trainable uniform-distribution parameter) are supervised with cross-entropy loss, regularized by KL divergence to keep the counterfactual estimate stable. At inference, the debiased prediction is the Total Indirect Effect: TIE = φ(Y_c, Y_e) − φ(Y_c, Y_e*), which subtracts the pure context bias from the total causal effect. The framework is model-agnostic and can be attached to any existing CAER architecture without modifying its internal structure.

## Results

On the EMOTIC dataset (26-class mAP), CLEF consistently improves all five integrated CAER methods by 2.77–4.02 percentage points, with EmotiCon+CLEF achieving the best overall mAP of 38.05%, surpassing the previous SOTA MCA (37.73%). On the CAER-S dataset (7-class accuracy), EmotiCon+CLEF reaches 90.62%, edging out VRD (90.49%) and substantially outperforming RRLA (84.82%) and MCA (79.57%). The largest per-method gain is GNN-CNN+CLEF (+4.02% mAP on EMOTIC), attributed to its fine-grained context exploration being more susceptible to spurious correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEF is model-agnostic and is applied to five structurally diverse CAER backbones (EMOT-Net, CAER-Net, GNN-CNN, CD-Net, EmotiCon), each showing consistent mAP gains of 2.77–4.02% on EMOTIC and 1.97–3.08% accuracy on CAER-S. The strongest CLEF variant, EmotiCon+CLEF (38.05% mAP), outperforms all standalone SOTA methods including MCA (37.73%), SIB-Net (35.41%), VRD (35.16%), RRLA (32.41%), TEKG (31.36%), and HLCR (30.02%) on EMOTIC. On CAER-S, EmotiCon+CLEF (90.62%) narrowly beats VRD (90.49%) and substantially surpasses RRLA (84.82%), GRERN (81.31%), and MCA (79.57%). Against the prior debiasing method CCIM [58], CLEF achieves higher mAP on both datasets (e.g., 31.67 vs. 30.88 for EMOT-Net on EMOTIC; 77.03 vs. 75.82 on CAER-S), attributed to CCIM's reliance on predefined context confounders that fail to capture pure context bias. The GNN-CNN baseline benefits most from CLEF (+4.02%), consistent with its graph-based context exploration being more prone to spurious context–emotion shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the context-aware emotion recognition (CAER) literature, where methods like EMOT-Net, CAER-Net, GNN-CNN, CD-Net, and EmotiCon extract ensemble representations from subject and context features. It departs from the prior debiasing effort CCIM [58], which relied on predefined context confounder dictionaries and memory-query operations to approximate interventions, by instead adopting a counterfactual inference framework (inspired by counterfactual VQA [34]) that decomposes the total causal effect into a natural direct effect (the harmful bias) and a total indirect effect (the valuable context prior). The causal graph formulation and NDE/TIE decomposition follow Pearl's structural causal model, but the specific instantiation—using a non-invasive masked context branch to estimate the direct effect and a uniform-distribution parameter to represent the no-treatment condition—is novel to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLEF provides a principled, model-agnostic mechanism to separate beneficial context priors from harmful context biases in CAER, which is a persistent and under-addressed failure mode in unconstrained emotion recognition. The consistent 2–4% gains across five architecturally diverse baselines on two datasets demonstrate broad applicability without requiring modifications to the underlying CAER model. Limitations include reliance on accurate subject bounding boxes for the masking operation, the pragmatic (rather than theoretically grounded) uniform-distribution assumption for the no-treatment condition, and evaluation restricted to static images; extending the framework to video-based or multi-agent dynamic contexts remains open. The causal graph, while stated to be general, has only been validated on a single task formulation (multi-label classification), leaving its transferability to regression or ordinal emotion settings untested.
