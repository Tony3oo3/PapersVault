## Problem

Conventional logit-based knowledge distillation assumes a shared temperature between teacher and student in the softmax, which implicitly forces the student to match the teacher's logit magnitude and variance. This is both theoretically unjustified (temperatures derive from independent Lagrangian multipliers) and practically harmful, as lightweight students struggle to produce logits of comparable range to cumbersome teachers, and the shared-temperature setting can even yield misleading KL-divergence evaluations of student quality.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) fed to both a pre-trained teacher and a trainable student CNN, producing K-dimensional logit vectors (K=100 or 1000 classes). The Z-score standardization is applied to these logit vectors before softmax, not to the images; a single base temperature τ is shared between teacher and student, while the per-sample standard deviation is computed adaptively from each logit vector. The final output is a class label, with the distillation loss computed on the standardized probability distributions.

## Method

The paper derives the softmax temperature from Lagrangian multipliers in an entropy-maximization formulation, proving that teacher and student temperatures are theoretically independent and can vary per sample. To exploit this, it proposes a plug-and-play Z-score logit standardization pre-process: each logit vector is centered by its mean and scaled by its standard deviation divided by a base temperature τ, i.e., Z(x;τ) = (x − x̄)/σ(x)/τ, applied to both teacher and student logits before softmax and KL divergence. This maps arbitrary logit ranges into a bounded interval [−√(K−1)/τ, √(K−1)/τ] while preserving logit rank (monotonicity), thereby decoupling the student's logit magnitude and variance from the teacher's. The training objective becomes λ_CE·CE(y, softmax(z)) + λ_KD·τ²·L(softmax(Z(v;τ)), softmax(Z(z;τ))), and the pre-process can be dropped into existing logit-based KD methods (KD, CTKD, DKD, MLKD) without architectural changes.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the proposed Z-score logit standardization consistently improves all four logit-based KD methods, with gains ranging from 0.10% to 3.14% top-1 accuracy depending on the teacher/student pair. The largest gain is +3.14% for vanilla KD (73.97%→77.11%) with WRN-40-2→ResNet8×4, where the capacity gap is most pronounced. On ImageNet, KD+Ours improves top-1 accuracy by +0.39% (ResNet34/ResNet18) and +1.68% (ResNet50/MN-V1), while KD+CTKD+Ours reaches 72.92% top-1 on ResNet50/MN-V1, surpassing all feature-based baselines. After applying the pre-process, vanilla KD achieves performance comparable to SOTA feature-based methods like SimKD and CAT-KD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Z-score standardization is a plug-and-play pre-process that consistently improves all four logit-based baselines (KD, CTKD, DKD, MLKD) across every teacher/student configuration tested. The gains are most substantial when the capacity gap between teacher and student is large: vanilla KD improves by +3.14% (WRN-40-2→ResNet8×4) and +1.67% (ResNet50→MN-V2), while DKD and MLKD see smaller but consistent gains of +0.10% to +1.19%. After applying the pre-process, vanilla KD (75.56% on ResNet32×4→SHN-V2) surpasses feature-based baselines FitNet (73.54%), AT (72.73%), and RKD (73.21%), and approaches CRD (75.65%) and OFD (76.82%). On ImageNet, KD+CTKD+Ours (72.92% top-1, ResNet50/MN-V1) outperforms all feature-based methods including ReviewKD (72.56%), SimKD (72.25%), and CAT-KD (72.24%). The method does not directly compete with feature-based methods as a standalone approach but rather augments existing logit-based pipelines, making it orthogonal to feature-based baselines like FitNet, AT, RKD, CRD, OFD, ReviewKD, SimKD, and CAT-KD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Hinton et al.'s original knowledge distillation framework, which introduced the temperature-scaled softmax and KL divergence loss, and on CTKD, which predicted sample-wise temperatures via adversarial learning but still assumed a shared temperature between teacher and student. The key departure is a theoretical derivation from the entropy-maximization principle showing that the temperature arises as a Lagrangian multiplier with no constraint tying teacher and student values together, thereby exposing the conventional shared-temperature practice as an unjustified assumption that implicitly enforces logit magnitude matching. The proposed Z-score standardization is positioned as a plug-and-play pre-process that can be layered onto any existing logit-based KD method (KD, CTKD, DKD, MLKD) without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification that shared temperature in KD implicitly forces a student to match the teacher's logit range and variance — an unnecessary constraint given the capacity gap — challenges a foundational assumption in the KD literature dating to 2015. The Z-score pre-process is simple, parameter-light (one base temperature τ), and consistently improves multiple logit-based methods across CIFAR-100 and ImageNet, making it an attractive drop-in enhancement for practitioners. Open questions include whether the theoretical framework extends naturally to feature-based or relation-based distillation, whether the base temperature τ can be made fully adaptive (e.g., learned per-sample), and whether the benefits persist in non-classification settings such as detection or segmentation beyond the supplementary experiments.
