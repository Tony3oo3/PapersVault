## Problem

Forward-based 3D generative models require a shape representation that is simultaneously fast to preprocess per-shape, parameter-efficient, and structurally compatible with modern neural architectures. Existing representations—volumetric grids, tri-planes, point clouds, meshes, and neural fields—each violate at least one of these three design principles, limiting the quality and scalability of 3D shape generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240683/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise matrix X₀ ∈ R^{1024×355} (each row encoding a 3D center, a scalar scale, and a 7×7×7 local SDF grid) plus a conditioning vector (class label or text embedding) and produces a generated M-SDF matrix X₁ of the same shape. The output is permutation-equivariant—row order carries no meaning—and a 3D mesh is recovered by extracting the zero level set of the resulting SDF via Marching Cubes at arbitrary resolution (64³ to 512³).

## Method

Mosaic-SDF (M-SDF) approximates a shape's Signed Distance Function as a weighted sum of n=1024 local 7×7×7 volumetric grids, each parameterized by a 3D center pᵢ, a uniform scale sᵢ, and grid values Vᵢ. A ReLU-based partition-of-unity weighting ensures each grid contributes only in its local neighborhood, concentrating parameters near the surface. Preprocessing per shape involves farthest-point sampling for centers, SDF evaluation at grid nodes, and brief fine-tuning with an L1 + gradient-matching loss, completing in under 2 minutes on a single A100. For generation, the paper trains a 328M-parameter permutation-equivariant Transformer (24 layers, 16 heads, no positional encoding) via Flow Matching with Conditional Optimal Transport paths and Classifier-Free Guidance, treating each M-SDF row as a token. This yields a simple matrix-form representation that is both parameter-efficient (355K params vs. cubic scaling of grids) and directly compatible with set-based transformer architectures.

## Results

On ShapeNetCore-V2 (3D Warehouse), M-SDF achieves the best FPD and KPD across all five evaluated classes (airplane, car, chair, sofa, table), with FPD ranging from 0.44 (airplane) to 0.63 (sofa) and KPD from 0.19 (chair) to 0.62 (sofa). M-SDF is competitive on coverage (COV-CD: 45.71–52.97%) and distributional similarity (MMD-CD: 2.87–15.47), though it trails Neural Wavelet slightly on 1-NNA-CD for airplane (62.62% vs. 68.32%) and S2VS for car (70.00% vs. 84.57%). For surface extraction, M-SDF scales to 512³ resolution in 21.48 s, while S2VS runs out of memory at 256³ and 3DILG requires 159.56 s at 512³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-SDF consistently outperforms all three baselines on FPD and KPD across every class, with particularly large margins over 3DILG (e.g., FPD 0.44 vs. 1.83 on airplane; 0.46 vs. 2.84 on car). Against Neural Wavelet (NW), M-SDF achieves substantially lower FPD (0.44 vs. 0.81 on airplane) and KPD (0.50 vs. 1.26), though NW edges out M-SDF on 1-NNA-CD for airplane (68.32% vs. 62.62%) and table (56.41% vs. 51.31%), suggesting NW captures slightly more diverse nearest-neighbor structure. Against S2VS, M-SDF wins decisively on FPD and KPD (e.g., 0.47 vs. 0.83 FPD on table) and on MMD-CD for car (2.87 vs. 4.13), but S2VS achieves higher 1-NNA-CD on car (84.57% vs. 70.00%) and sofa (62.66% vs. 61.71%), indicating S2VS generates shapes closer to individual training samples in those categories. On surface extraction efficiency, M-SDF scales to 512³ in 21.48 s while S2VS hits OOM at 256³ and 3DILG requires 159.56 s at 512³, giving M-SDF a clear practical advantage for high-resolution mesh extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and its permutation-equivariant variant (Klein et al., 2023) as the generative backbone, but its core contribution is a new 3D shape representation—Mosaic-SDF—that departs from all prior representation families (volumetric grids, triplanes, point clouds, neural fields, meshes) by using a set of small local grids centered near the shape boundary. The key conceptual departure is that instead of encoding the SDF with a global neural network (INR, triplane MLP) or a full-resolution volume, M-SDF uses independently-fitted local patches with a partition-of-unity blending, yielding a simple matrix compatible with standard transformer architectures without positional encoding. This positions the work at the intersection of representation design and generative modeling, arguing that the representation choice is the bottleneck for forward-based 3D generation rather than the generative algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M-SDF offers a practical, drop-in representation for 3D generative models that is fast to preprocess (under 2 minutes per shape on one A100), parameter-efficient (355K params for 1024 local grids), and architecturally simple (a matrix fed to a transformer), making it a strong candidate for scaling 3D generation to larger datasets. The paper's limitations are clearly stated: it encodes only geometry (no texture, color, or lighting), uses a simple linear projection of local grids into the transformer (no convolutions or autoencoders), and achieves only permutation equivariance rather than full orientation equivariance. The text-to-3D results on 600K shapes are qualitative only, leaving open whether the representation's advantages persist at scale with stronger text conditioning. An open question is whether incorporating local coordinate frames or convolutional sub-networks per grid could further improve fidelity without sacrificing the representation's simplicity.
