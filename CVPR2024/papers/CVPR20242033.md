## Problem

Text-to-image person ReID models are constrained by small, manually annotated datasets, resulting in poor cross-dataset generalization. This paper studies the transferable setting where a model trained on a large-scale MLLM-generated dataset must be directly deployed to unseen benchmarks without fine-tuning, but faces two challenges: MLLMs produce descriptions with repetitive sentence structures and contain factual errors that mislead training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242033/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a free-form text description of a person (tokenized to a maximum of 77 tokens including [SOS]/[EOS]) and retrieves ranked pedestrian images from a gallery. During training, each image (resized to 384×128 pixels) is paired with four MLLM-generated captions (static and dynamic instructions from Qwen-VL and Shikra), and both the full description T^full and a noise-masked variant T^nam are fed independently into the text encoder, with only T^nam contributing to the loss.

## Method

The framework builds on CLIP-ViT/B-16 with a 12-layer transformer text encoder, trained via the Similarity Distribution Matching (SDM) loss on 1M images from the LUPerson-MLLM dataset. Two key techniques address MLLM description quality: (1) Template-based Diversity Enhancement (TDE) uses multi-turn ChatGPT dialogue to generate 46 sentence-pattern templates, which are randomly injected into the MLLM captioning prompt to produce structurally varied descriptions; (2) Noise-Aware Masking (NAM) computes cosine similarity between each text token embedding and all image patch token embeddings at encoder layer 10, identifies tokens whose maximum similarity is low (indicating mismatch), and masks them with elevated probability (centered around p=0.15) in the next training epoch. Unlike MLM, NAM does not predict masked tokens and instead simply removes noisy words from the loss computation, allowing the model to focus on reliable image-text alignments.

## Results

In the direct transfer setting (no fine-tuning on target data), the model trained on 1.0M LUPerson-MLLM images achieves Rank-1 of 57.61% on CUHK-PEDES, 38.36% on ICFG-PEDES, and 51.50% on RSTPReid, substantially outperforming prior pre-training datasets (MALS: 19.36/7.93/22.85; LUPerson-T: 21.88/11.46/22.40). In the traditional fine-tuning setting with the IRRA backbone, the method reaches Rank-1 of 76.82% (mAP 69.55) on CUHK-PEDES, 67.05% (mAP 41.51) on ICFG-PEDES, and 68.50% (mAP 53.02) on RSTPReid. With the APTM backbone, it further improves to 78.13% (mAP 68.75) on CUHK-PEDES and 69.95% (mAP 54.17) on RSTPReid, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method (Ours 1.0M + IRRA) outperforms the strongest CLIP-backbone baseline IRRA [24] by 3.44% Rank-1 on CUHK-PEDES (76.82 vs 73.38), 3.59% on ICFG-PEDES (67.05 vs 63.46), and 8.30% on RSTPReid (68.50 vs 60.20), with mAP gains of 3.45, 3.45, and 5.85 points respectively. Against the two prior pre-training datasets, MALS [64] and LUPerson-T [42], the proposed LUPerson-MLLM dataset yields 2.77% and 2.45% Rank-1 improvements on CUHK-PEDES, and 6.60% and 6.30% on RSTPReid, confirming that TDE and NAM produce more transferable representations. In the ALBEF-backbone setting, Ours+APTM surpasses RaSa [4] by 1.62% Rank-1 on CUHK-PEDES (78.13 vs 76.51) and 3.05% on RSTPReid (69.95 vs 66.90), while also beating APTM [64] by 1.60% on CUHK-PEDES and 2.45% on RSTPReid. In the direct transfer setting (Table 3), the gap is even more dramatic: Ours (1.0M) achieves 57.61% Rank-1 on CUHK-PEDES versus 19.36% for MALS and 21.88% for LUPerson-T, a margin exceeding 35 points, demonstrating that the diversity and noise-aware strategies are critical for zero-shot generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP architecture and the SDM loss from IRRA [24] for cross-modal alignment, but departs from prior text-to-image ReID pre-training work (MALS [64], LUPerson-T [42]) by using MLLMs (Qwen-VL, Shikra) to directly caption real pedestrian images rather than synthesizing images via diffusion or inserting CLIP-predicted attributes into fixed templates. The 'transferable' framing is borrowed from CLIP's zero-shot transfer paradigm, and the two proposed techniques—Template-based Diversity Enhancement (TDE) and Noise-Aware Masking (NAM)—are novel contributions that address specific failure modes of MLLM-generated text not considered in prior pre-training datasets. The multi-turn ChatGPT dialogue for template generation is a practical but lightweight approach to instruction design that complements rather than replaces the MLLM captioning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LUPerson-MLLM dataset (1M images × 4 captions) is a substantial open resource that could benefit the broader text-to-image ReID and cross-modal retrieval community. The finding that 0.1M MLLM-captioned images with TDE+NAM outperform 1.5M MALS images in direct transfer suggests caption quality (diversity and noise control) matters more than raw data volume, which has implications for how future large-scale training data is constructed. Open questions include whether TDE scales beyond 46 templates, whether NAM's layer-10 similarity heuristic generalizes to other encoder architectures, and whether the approach extends to other MLLM-generated data scenarios beyond pedestrian ReID. The authors acknowledge that NAM may occasionally fail to mask noisy tokens, leaving a residual quality ceiling.
