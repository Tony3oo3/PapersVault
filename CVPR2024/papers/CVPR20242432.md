## Problem

Zero-shot 6D object pose estimation requires detecting and localizing all instances of a novel object—completely unseen during training—in cluttered RGB-D scenes. Existing approaches either rely on object-specific training data, restrict to known categories, or use time-intensive render-and-compare refiners, leaving a gap in generalizable, efficient pipelines that handle both segmentation and pose estimation for truly novel objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242432/cropped_page0_idx22.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a depth map of a cluttered scene, along with a CAD model of the target novel object (used to render 42 templates at evaluation). It outputs instance segmentation masks for every detected instance of that object and a 6D pose (rotation matrix and translation vector) per instance. The ISM requires no re-training of SAM or DINOv2; the PEM is trained on 2 million synthetic images from ShapeNet-Objects and Google-Scanned-Objects and operates on point sets of 196 (coarse) and 2048 (fine) points sampled from the depth crop and rendered templates.

## Method

SAM-6D decomposes the task into an Instance Segmentation Model (ISM) and a Pose Estimation Model (PEM). ISM prompts SAM with evenly sampled 2D grid points to generate all class-agnostic proposals, then retains valid ones via a three-term object matching score combining DINOv2 class-embedding similarity (semantics), patch-embedding similarity to the best-matched template (appearance), and IoU between the proposal bounding box and the 2D projection of the object under a rough pose (geometry). PEM formulates pose estimation as a partial-to-partial point matching problem, introducing learnable background tokens that absorb non-overlapped points and yield a soft assignment matrix without iterative optimal transport. A two-stage pipeline follows: Coarse Point Matching uses 196 sparse points processed by stacked Geometric Transformers to derive an initial pose, which is then injected as positional encodings into Fine Point Matching, where 2048 dense points are processed by Sparse-to-Dense Point Transformers (SDPTs) that apply Geometric Transformers on sparse subsets and distribute enhanced features back to the dense set via Linear Cross-attention, ultimately producing the refined pose through weighted SVD.

## Results

On the seven core BOP datasets, SAM-6D with SAM-based segmentation achieves a mean Average Recall (AR) of 70.4 for 6D pose estimation, outperforming the strongest baseline MegaPose† (62.8) by 7.6 points. For instance segmentation, SAM-6D with SAM and all three matching scores (semantic, appearance, geometry) achieves 48.1 mean mAP, surpassing CNOS with SAM (40.4) by 7.7 points. The ablation on YCB-V shows the two-stage point matching (coarse + fine) reaches 84.5 AR versus 77.6 for coarse-only and 40.2 for fine-only, and the background token design achieves 84.5 AR in 1.36 s versus 81.4 AR in 4.31 s for optimal transport.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM-6D outperforms all four baselines across all seven BOP datasets in the zero-shot pose estimation setting. Against MegaPose† (the strongest baseline at 62.8 mean AR), SAM-6D with SAM segmentation gains +7.6 points, with the largest margins on TUD-L (+5.3), IC-BIN (+12.1), and ITODD (+13.4). Against ZeroPose† (57.0 mean AR), the improvement is +13.4 points, and against GigaPose† (57.9), it is +12.5 points. For instance segmentation, SAM-6D with all three matching scores (48.1 mean mAP) beats CNOS with SAM (40.4) by 7.7 points and ZeroPose with SAM (36.5) by 11.6 points, with the geometric matching score contributing the largest single-term gain (+1.7 to +3.7 over semantic-only). Notably, SAM-6D's own ISM masks further boost pose estimation by ~1–5 AR points over using CNOS masks, validating the tight integration of the two sub-networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM-6D builds directly on the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for zero-shot instance segmentation, extending CNOS's semantic-only proposal filtering with additional appearance (DINOv2 patch embeddings) and geometry (IoU-based) matching terms. For pose estimation, it departs from render-and-compare approaches (MegaPose) and single-stage point matching (ZeroPose, GigaPose) by introducing a two-stage coarse-to-fine point registration pipeline that uses Geometric Transformers for sparse correspondence and a novel Sparse-to-Dense Point Transformer (combining Geometric and Linear Transformers) for dense correspondence. The background token design for handling partial-to-partial point matching is a conceptual departure from optimal transport-based assignment, offering a simpler and faster alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot 6D pose estimation is critical for robotic manipulation and AR where annotated training data for every object is infeasible, and SAM-6D demonstrates that a well-engineered pipeline combining foundation models (SAM, DINOv2) with a two-stage point matching strategy can outperform more complex render-and-compare methods without time-intensive refiners. The background token mechanism provides a practical, differentiable alternative to optimal transport for partial overlap scenarios, which is common in real-world cluttered scenes. Limitations include the requirement for RGB-D input and a CAD model of the target object, and the SAM-based variant incurs ~4.4s per-image runtime. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to RGB-only settings, whether the PEM can be made fully zero-shot (currently trained on 2M synthetic images), and how performance scales to extremely cluttered or multi-instance scenes.
