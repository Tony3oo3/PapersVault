## Problem

Current MLLMs trained with in-context learning (ICL) learn to answer in a specific format rather than understanding causal relationships between image-label pairs, and they fail entirely when both the images and labels are novel and unseen. This paper addresses the gap of enabling MLLMs to acquire and retain genuinely new concepts from a single conversation by establishing explicit causal links between demonstration and query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241475/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a support set of image-label pairs (2–16 shots) that are causally linked to the query, along with a query image and a set of candidate labels, and outputs the predicted class label as a text response. Unlike standard ICL where demonstrations come from unrelated tasks, here the support set directly defines the mapping the model must apply to the query. Evaluation uses the ISEKAI dataset (AI-generated images with fabricated labels from Midjourney/Stable Diffusion) and ImageNet-100, with accuracy measured across 2- to 16-shot settings.

## Method

The paper introduces Link-Context Learning (LCL), a causal-relevant few-shot setting where the support set and query set share a direct causal relationship, and fine-tunes Shikra (a 7B MLLM) to exploit it. Training uses ImageNet-1k split into ImageNet-900 (for constructing demonstrations) and ImageNet-100 (held out for evaluation), with CLIP-based class similarity to build ordered neighbor sets for hard-negative mining. The model is trained to maximize the likelihood of the correct label conditioned on the query, cluster prototypes from the support set, and previously generated labels, while keeping the visual encoder frozen. Four training strategies are explored—[2-way], [2-way-random], [2-way-weight], and [mix]—differing in shot sampling schedules and whether original Shikra tasks are co-trained, with the [mix] strategy preserving zero-shot performance while achieving 83% accuracy at 6-shot on ImageNet-100.

## Results

On the novel ISEKAI-10 benchmark (entirely unseen generated images), the best LCL strategy ([mix]) achieves 73% accuracy at 6-shot, substantially outperforming OpenFlamingo (50%) and Otter (14%), while vanilla Shikra scores 0% across all shot settings. On ISEKAI-pair, the [2-way-random] strategy reaches 49% at 16-shot versus OpenFlamingo's 40% and Otter's 1%. On ImageNet-100 (real-world classes held out from training), the [mix] strategy peaks at 83% at 6-shot and the [2-way] strategy reaches 79% at 16-shot, surpassing OpenFlamingo's 78% and Otter's 26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: OpenFlamingo, Otter, vanilla Shikra, Flamingo (9B/80B), and BLIP-2. On the primary ISEKAI-10 benchmark, the proposed [mix] strategy outperforms OpenFlamingo by 23 percentage points (73% vs. 50% at 6-shot) and Otter by 59 points (73% vs. 14%), while vanilla Shikra fails entirely (0%). On ISEKAI-pair, the [2-way-random] strategy leads OpenFlamingo by 9 points at 16-shot (49% vs. 40%) and Otter by 48 points. On ImageNet-100, the [mix] strategy achieves 83% at 6-shot, edging out OpenFlamingo's peak of 78% (at 12-shot) and far exceeding Otter's 26%. On zero-shot VQAv2, the 7B model (75.06 dev / 75.25 std) shows a modest ~2-point drop relative to the 13B Shikra baseline (77.3/77.5), confirming that the LCL fine-tuning does not significantly degrade general zero-shot capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Shikra as its baseline MLLM and positions itself in deliberate contrast to multimodal in-context learning (as pursued by Flamingo, OpenFlamingo, and Otter), arguing that ICL's causally-irrelevant demonstrations teach format-following rather than genuine concept acquisition. The key conceptual departure is the 'link-context learning' setting, where the support set and query set share a causal mapping (e.g., the same renamed class), requiring the model to extract and apply a specific input-label correspondence rather than generalize across heterogeneous tasks. The training strategy borrows from contrastive learning (hard-negative mining via CLIP-computed class similarity) and instruction tuning, but applies them specifically to enforce causal linkage between demonstrations and queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ISEKAI dataset (entirely fabricated image-concept pairs generated by Stable Diffusion and Midjourney) fills a genuine gap: prior few-shot evaluation on MLLMs is confounded by the models' pretraining exposure to real-world concepts, making it impossible to isolate true in-conversation learning. The false-rate ablation (accuracy dropping to 0% when label mappings are corrupted, versus OpenFlamingo retaining 38% accuracy under the same corruption) provides a clean diagnostic for whether a model has actually learned the causal mapping or is merely pattern-matching. However, the validation is limited to image classification on a single 7B architecture, and the ISEKAI dataset is small (40 categories), leaving open whether LCL generalizes to more complex multimodal reasoning tasks or scales to larger models.
