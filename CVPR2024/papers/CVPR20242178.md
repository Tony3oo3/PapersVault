## Problem

Traditional SfM pipelines depend on detecting repeatable keypoints as a first step, which breaks down in texture-poor scenes where keypoint repeatability is poor. While recent detector-free matchers (e.g., LoFTR) overcome this detection bottleneck, their pair-dependent matching produces inconsistent feature locations across views, yielding fragmentary tracks that are incompatible with standard SfM systems. This paper addresses both the detection bottleneck and the multi-view inconsistency problem simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a set of unordered RGB images and recovers global camera poses in SE(3), per-image intrinsic parameters, and a 3D scene point cloud. Internally, a detector-free matcher (LoFTR, MatchFormer, or AspanTransformer) produces semi-dense correspondences between all image pairs, which are then quantized to a coarse grid (cell size r=4 or 8) before feeding into incremental SfM. The refinement stage operates on local image patches of size 15×15 pixels centered at each keypoint, with up to 16 views processed per multi-view matching call.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) a coarse SfM phase that quantizes detector-free matches to a grid to enforce multi-view consistency, then runs COLMAP incremental mapping to obtain initial poses and structure; (2) an iterative refinement loop (run twice) that alternates between feature track refinement and geometry refinement. The feature track refinement module is a transformer-based multi-view matcher: it selects a reference view by medium keypoint scale, extracts 15×15 patches via an S2DNet CNN backbone, flattens and concatenates query-view features, then applies n=2 rounds of self- and cross-attention (using linear attention for efficiency) to produce multi-view-contextualized features. A 7×7 grid of candidate reference locations is sampled, each correlated against query-view features to produce heatmaps; the candidate track minimizing total variance is selected as the refined track. The geometry refinement phase performs standard geometric bundle adjustment on the refined tracks followed by track topology adjustment (adding previously unregistered points, merging tracks, filtering outliers), repeated five times per iteration. The only learned component is the multi-view feature transformer, trained on MegaDepth with L2 loss on keypoint locations.

## Results

On the ETH3D dataset, the proposed framework with LoFTR achieves AUC@1°=59.12, AUC@3°=75.59, and AUC@5°=79.53 for multi-view pose estimation, outperforming the strongest detector-based baseline SP+SG+PixSfM (50.82/68.52/72.86) by 8.3°/7.1°/6.7° respectively. On the IMC dataset with large viewpoint and illumination changes, Ours(LoFTR) reaches AUC@3°=46.94 and AUC@5°=59.14, surpassing SP+SG+PixSfM (46.30/58.43). On the newly introduced Texture-Poor SfM dataset, the detector-free framework achieves AUC@3°=26.07 and AUC@10°=45.43, dramatically outperforming the best detector-based method SP+SG+PixSfM (14.00/24.55) by over 12° and 21° respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed detector-free SfM framework consistently outperforms all detector-based baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest detector-based baseline SP+SG+PixSfM, Ours(LoFTR) gains +8.3° on ETH3D AUC@1° (59.12 vs. 50.82), +0.64° on IMC AUC@3° (46.94 vs. 46.30), and a massive +12.07° on Texture-Poor AUC@3° (26.07 vs. 14.00), demonstrating the critical advantage of eliminating keypoint detection in low-texture scenes. Compared to the detector-free baseline LoFTR+PixSfM (which uses the same LoFTR coarse matches), the iterative refinement pipeline yields +4.77° on ETH3D AUC@1° (59.12 vs. 54.35) and +5.41° on Texture-Poor AUC@3° (26.07 vs. 20.66), validating the multi-view matching and geometry refinement stages. On the IMC dataset, Ours(AspanTrans.) achieves the best AUC@3° (47.58) and AUC@10° (73.29), slightly edging out Ours(LoFTR), while on Texture-Poor, Ours(MatchFormer) leads with AUC@5°=37.57 and AUC@10°=48.55, showing the framework's compatibility with different detector-free matchers. The 3D triangulation results (Table 2) further confirm the advantage: Ours(LoFTR) achieves 80.24% accuracy at 1cm and 29.54% completeness at 5cm on ETH3D, surpassing SP+SG+PixSfM (79.01%/11.28%) and LoFTR+PixSfM (74.42%/27.31%) on both metrics simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the recent success of detector-free semi-dense matchers (LoFTR, MatchFormer, AspanTransformer) and the standard incremental SfM pipeline (COLMAP), but departs from both in a key way: it addresses the multi-view inconsistency problem that prevents detector-free matchers from being plugged into existing SfM systems. The coarse-to-fine strategy—quantizing matches for an initial model, then iteratively refining via a transformer-based multi-view matching module and geometric bundle adjustment—differs from prior refinement approaches like PixSfM (which performs feature-metric BA requiring large memory) and PatchFlow (which estimates pairwise dense flow). The multi-view feature transformer, trained on MegaDepth, is the only learned component beyond the off-the-shelf matcher, positioning the work as a systems-level integration rather than a new matching architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework opens up SfM reconstruction for texture-poor scenes (deep sea, lunar surface, low-textured objects) that were previously intractable for detector-based pipelines, and the new 17-scene texture-poor dataset with ARKit ground-truth poses provides a much-needed benchmark for this regime. A practical limitation is speed: the semi-dense matching phase makes the overall pipeline substantially slower than sparse-feature systems (4.2 hours for 2000 images), which constrains very large-scale applications. The memory efficiency advantage over PixSfM (0.37 GB vs. 161.7 GB at 500 images) is significant and suggests the separated track/geometry refinement design is the right architectural choice for scaling detector-free SfM. Open questions include whether the iterative refinement can be made more parallelizable and whether the approach extends to video-rate or streaming SfM settings.
