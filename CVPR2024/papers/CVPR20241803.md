## Problem

Photometric stereo achieves high-fidelity reconstruction from sparse viewpoints but is impractical outside the lab: it requires a dark room, calibrated or directional lighting, and multi-stage pipelines prone to error accumulation. Neural sparse-view methods, in turn, depend on dense sampling or large-scale pre-training, limiting generalizability. The multi-view uncalibrated point-light PS setting—where light positions are unknown, ambient light is present, and the pipeline is end-to-end—remains entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

For each of a small number of viewpoints (as few as two with wide baselines), the method takes N+1 RGB images: one ambient-only image and N images each additionally lit by a single uncalibrated point-light at an unknown position, plus binary object masks. The ambient image is subtracted from the point-light images to isolate the point-light contribution. The output is a continuous SDF surface, per-point specular BRDF parameters (roughness and specular albedo) from an MLP, optimized point-light positions, and a per-pixel diffuse albedo that is solved analytically rather than represented as a 3D field.

## Method

The framework builds on VolSDF volume rendering, representing geometry as an MLP-parameterized SDF converted to a Laplace density, and models appearance with a Disney BRDF under a physically realistic lighting model that combines static ambient light with uncalibrated point-light sources. The key technical contribution is an 'optimal diffuse albedo' strategy: instead of learning the diffuse albedo with a 3D MLP, it is excluded from the optimization and solved per-pixel as a linear least-absolute-deviation problem via a few iterations of iterative reweighted least squares, making the L1 rendering loss differentiable with respect to the remaining parameters. Training is fully end-to-end, jointly optimizing SDF parameters, point-light positions, and specular BRDF parameters under a composite loss of L1 rendering error (with a truncation heuristic that uses only the τ brightest views per pixel to suppress cast-shadow artifacts), an Eikonal regularizer, and a silhouette BCE mask loss. Because the point-light model provides absolute depth cues from a single viewpoint, the method handles wide camera baselines that defeat directional-light or normal-map-based approaches.

## Results

On four synthetic objects (dog1, dog2, girl1, girl2) reconstructed from 5–6 viewpoints, the proposed method achieves RMSE×100 of 0.3–0.4 and MAE of 4.6–10.2, dramatically outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, SVolSDF [56], reaches RMSE×100 of 0.9–2.2 and MAE of 17.2–23.9, while the photometric stereo baselines [10]D, [10]P, and PS-NeRF [60] range from RMSE×100 of 1.5–13.9 and MAE of 18.8–47.3. In the extreme 2-viewpoint wide-baseline setting, the method with optimal diffuse albedo produces clean reconstructions whereas PS-NeRF [60] and the MLP-albedo ablation both exhibit severe shape distortions and artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all three baselines on both RMSE and MAE across every synthetic object. Against SVolSDF [56] (the strongest baseline), the gap is roughly 3–7× in RMSE (e.g., 0.4 vs. 2.1 on dog1) and 2–3× in MAE (e.g., 4.6 vs. 22.0 on dog1). The calibrated multi-view PS method of Cheng et al. [10] performs worst in both its directional ([10]D) and point-light ([10]P) variants, with RMSE up to 13.9 and MAE up to 47.3, indicating that even with known light positions the multi-stage pipeline accumulates significant error. PS-NeRF [60], the closest prior PS-NeRF approach, achieves moderate RMSE (1.5–2.1) but still lags the proposed method by 4–5×, and qualitatively produces global shape distortions and texture-induced artifacts that the proposed method avoids. In the 2-viewpoint wide-baseline setting (Fig. 5), PS-NeRF [60] and the MLP-albedo ablation both fail with severe distortions, while the full method with optimal diffuse albedo recovers accurate geometry, highlighting the critical role of the point-light model for absolute depth estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VolSDF's decoupled geometry-appearance volume rendering framework and extends the optimal diffuse albedo strategy from Guo et al.'s single-view L2 formulation to a multi-view L1 setting solved via iterative reweighted least squares. It departs from prior multi-view PS approaches (PS-NeRF, calibrated PS methods) by eliminating the dark-room assumption, replacing directional lights with uncalibrated near point-lights, and unifying the pipeline into a single end-to-end optimization rather than multi-stage estimation. The ambient-light handling follows a simple subtraction trick from Zhang et al., justified by the near-field geometry that makes point-light contributions well-exposed relative to ambient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dark-room, calibration, and dense-viewpoint requirements, this work makes high-fidelity photometric stereo practically deployable outside controlled labs, potentially enabling accurate 3D scanning with consumer hardware (smartphone camera + LED) from as few as two wide-baseline viewpoints. The optimal diffuse albedo via IRLS is a clean, reusable technique that proves particularly critical in extreme sparsity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger or more complex scenes, how it handles non-achromatic or multiple simultaneous light sources, and whether the explicit cast-shadow and indirect-lighting approximations remain sufficient for highly concave geometries.
