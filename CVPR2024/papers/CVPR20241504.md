## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models produce smooth but uncontrollable animations, while traditional 3D pipelines offer precise control over geometry, motion, and camera paths but require laborious manual texturing and rendering. No existing method bridges this gap by taking a low-fidelity, untextured animated 3D mesh and directly generating a high-quality, temporally consistent stylized video using a 2D diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241504/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes depth maps and UV coordinate maps (encoding 2D texture coordinates plus object IDs in RGB channels) rendered from an animated 3D mesh, along with a free-form text prompt, and produces a sequence of 512×512 stylized animation frames. Unlike video-editing baselines that require a high-fidelity source video, the input here is an untextured proxy rendering, making DDIM inversion inapplicable. The pipeline is entirely zero-shot, requiring no training or fine-tuning of the underlying diffusion model.

## Method

The core idea is to repurpose a frozen StableDiffusion v1.5 model with a depth-conditioned ControlNet as a multi-frame renderer by injecting ground-truth 4D correspondences at multiple stages. First, noise is initialized in the canonical UV space as a Gaussian texture and projected to each frame, replacing DDIM inversion which fails on untextured inputs. Second, at each diffusion step, a random subset of keyframes undergoes extended self-attention; their pre-attention features are concatenated with the current frame's features to form augmented keys and values (pre-attention injection), while their post-attention features are projected into UV space, blended via sequential filling plus averaging to avoid over-smoothing, and projected back to guide the current frame's attention output (post-attention injection). The final attention output is a weighted combination of the inflated-attention result and the UV-warped keyframe features. An AdaIN normalization applied only to background pixels suppresses color flickering across frames.

## Results

On the proposed 4D-guided animation task, the method achieves a frame consistency score of 0.9845 and prompt fidelity of 0.3227 (CLIP-based metrics). Among T2I-based approaches, it attains the best frame consistency, outperforming Pix2Video (0.9630) and TokenFlow (0.9822), while also achieving the highest prompt fidelity among methods with strong temporal consistency. Only the proprietary T2V model Gen1 (0.9907) surpasses it on frame consistency, but Gen1 is limited to generating a few seconds of animation. The method is evaluated at 512×512 resolution using StableDiffusion v1.5 with a depth-conditioned ControlNet and 50 denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper adapts two video-editing methods—Pix2Video and TokenFlow—to its untextured-mesh setting as baselines, since no prior work directly addresses 4D-guided generation from low-fidelity renderings. On frame consistency, the proposed method (0.9845) outperforms both Pix2Video (0.9630) and TokenFlow (0.9822), with the gap over Pix2Video being particularly large (+0.0215) due to Pix2Video's lack of a correspondence injection mechanism. On prompt fidelity, the method (0.3227) substantially exceeds both baselines—Pix2Video (0.2983) and TokenFlow (0.2737)—because TokenFlow's token propagation introduces over-smoothing that degrades texture quality, while Pix2Video reuses pre-attention features from all previous frames, diluting prompt adherence. The only method that beats the proposed approach on frame consistency is Gen1 (0.9907), a proprietary T2V model trained on video data, but Gen1 is constrained to very short clips and lacks the fine-grained structural control offered by the 3D-guided pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a pre-trained 2D text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as a multi-frame renderer by injecting ground-truth 4D correspondences from animated 3D meshes into its self-attention layers. It builds directly on the pre-attention feature propagation of Pix2Video and the extended-attention/token-propagation mechanism of TokenFlow, but departs from both by replacing their reliance on DDIM inversion and learned correspondences with UV-space canonical representations derived from explicit 3D geometry. The depth-conditioned ControlNet provides structural guidance, while the novel UV-space noise initialization and combined pre-/post-attention feature blending in UV space address the temporal-consistency challenges that arise when no high-fidelity source video is available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical bridge between traditional 3D authoring tools and generative AI, letting artists prototype geometry and motion in 3D and outsource appearance synthesis to a diffusion model—bypassing laborious texturing, lighting, and rendering steps. It enables arbitrarily long, temporally consistent animations at a level of structural control that current T2V models (limited to a few seconds) cannot match. Limitations include the need for a rigged 3D scene as input, 512×512 resolution, and evaluation on relatively simple scenes (single objects, short character animations). Open questions include scalability to complex multi-object scenes with occlusion, whether the UV-space blending generalizes to non-rigid deformations beyond rigid-body and skeletal animation, and how the method interacts with higher-resolution or video-native diffusion backbones.
