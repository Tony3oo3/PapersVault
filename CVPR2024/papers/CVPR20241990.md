## Problem

Sparse matching pipelines are degraded by two distinct error sources: outlier mismatches with large errors and inlier matches with small but systematic positional inaccuracies. Existing post-processing methods either only reject outliers (leaving inlier errors uncorrected) or only refine matches locally (ignoring global geometric context), and no prior work jointly addresses both problems in a unified, matcher-agnostic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241990/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an image pair and a pre-computed set of correspondences (each a 4D coordinate pair (x1,y1,x2,y2)) as input, and for each match extracts a 41×41 pixel patch from each image centered on the corresponding point. It outputs a per-match confidence score in [0,1] (via sigmoid) and a 2D calibration offset (dx, dy); matches below a threshold θ_f are discarded, and the remaining matches have their second-image point shifted by the predicted offset to yield the final calibrated correspondence set.

## Method

FC-GNN is a self-attention GNN whose processing units are matches rather than keypoints. Each match is embedded by summing a relative positional encoding (4D offsets to its k=8 nearest neighbors in matching space, projected via MLP) with a patch embedding (flattened 41×41 pixel pair projected via MLP), both to 256 dimensions. To avoid O(n²) full attention, each match attends only to its k nearest neighbors in the matching space, and 9 cascaded residual attention layers progressively expand the receptive field from local to near-global. Two MLP decoder heads produce the confidence score and the 2D calibration offset. Training is supervised on MegaDepth with synthetic noise: outlier noise replaces correct matches with random point pairs, and inlier noise adds Gaussian-distributed sub-pixel offsets; the loss combines a log-loss classification term for filtering and an L2 regression term on offsets applied only to inliers.

## Results

On HPatches homography estimation, FC-GNN achieves the best overall AUC across all four matcher configurations, with the largest gains on SP+SuperGlue (67.0/77.2/86.9% at 3/5/10 px vs. 54.0/68.6/82.3% for the unrefined origin). On outdoor pose estimation (MegaDepth), FC-GNN boosts SP+SuperGlue from 49.13% to 54.67% AUC@5°, making it comparable to the dense matcher ASpanFormer (55.36%). On indoor pose estimation (ScanNet), FC-GNN improves SP+SuperGlue from 15.31% to 18.46% AUC@5° in texture-poor scenes. Runtime is 6–13 ms per image pair, roughly 10× faster than Patch2Pix (102–129 ms) while delivering superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FC-GNN consistently outperforms both OANet and Patch2Pix across all three evaluation tasks and all four matcher configurations. Against OANet (filtering-only), FC-GNN gains are especially large on SP+SuperGlue, where OANet severely over-filters (dropping matches from 0.61k to 0.14k) and degrades overall AUC from 54.0% to 28.5% at 3 px, while FC-GNN improves it to 67.0%. Against Patch2Pix (filtering + local refinement), FC-GNN wins on every configuration; for example, on SP+MNN outdoor pose estimation, FC-GNN reaches 45.58% AUC@5° versus Patch2Pix's 39.29%, and on SP+SG indoor pose estimation, FC-GNN achieves 18.46% versus Patch2Pix's 15.33% AUC@5°. Compared to the dense matcher ASpanFormer, FC-GNN applied to SP+SuperGlue surpasses it on outdoor pose estimation (54.67% vs. 55.36% AUC@5° is close, but FC-GNN runs in 6.4 ms vs. 163.6 ms) and on homography estimation (67.0% vs. 64.3% overall AUC@3 px), while FC-GNN also modestly improves ASpanFormer itself (64.3% → 66.1% overall AUC@3 px). The key differentiator is that FC-GNN combines global contextual attention with local patch information and explicit calibration offsets, whereas OANet only filters and Patch2Pix only refines locally without context.

## Relation to Prior Work

FC-GNN builds directly on the attention-based GNN matching architectures of SuperGlue, Seeded GNN, and ClusterGNN, but departs in two key ways: it uses matches (rather than keypoints) as processing units with self-attention only, and it introduces a joint filtering-and-calibrating objective that unifies outlier rejection with sub-pixel offset correction. This contrasts with prior methods that either filter (OANet) or refine (Patch2Pix) in isolation, and with dense-feature-based refiners that require expensive ResNet feature extraction. The k-nearest-neighbor attention mechanism, inspired by the local coherence of geometric correspondences, replaces full-attention to reduce complexity from O(n²) to O(kn).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FC-GNN positions itself as a lightweight, plug-and-play optimization module that can be appended to any existing matching pipeline (SIFT, SuperPoint, SuperGlue, ASpanFormer) at 6–13 ms cost, making it a practical candidate for real-time applications. The finding that calibration offsets significantly improve accuracy even on already well-filtered matches (e.g., SP+SG) suggests that sub-pixel refinement is an underexploited dimension in matching pipelines. Limitations include its two-view scope (explicitly not designed for multi-view SfM track optimization), reliance on synthetic noise training from MegaDepth, and the fact that gains over dense matchers like ASpanFormer are modest. Open questions include whether the calibration formulation extends to 3D correspondences or multi-view settings without track-disconnection issues.
