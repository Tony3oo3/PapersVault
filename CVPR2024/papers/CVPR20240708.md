## Problem

Existing 3D-aware one-shot head reenactment methods rely on linear face models (e.g., FLAME/3DMM) to disentangle identity from expression, which limits expressiveness, causes identity leakage from the driver, and produces unnatural or uncanny expressions. No prior method achieves fully volumetric, model-free disentanglement of appearance and expression while maintaining real-time rendering and multi-view consistency suitable for holographic displays.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240708/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source portrait image (lifted into tri-planes once) and a driver image or video frame (frontalized per frame), with the driver's head pose estimated separately by an off-the-shelf 3D tracker. Output is a set of view-consistent rendered face images at up to 45 viewing angles for holographic displays, produced at 128×128 base resolution and upscaled via a GFPGAN/StyleGAN2-based super-resolution module. Each view renders at ~32 fps on a single RTX 4090 using TensorRT and half-precision inference.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) a fine-tuned Lp3D transformer-based encoder lifts both source and driver into a shared canonical tri-plane representation, frontalizing them to decouple pose from expression; (2) separate CNN encoders extract coarse appearance features from the frontalized source and expression features from the frontalized driver, which are concatenated with a tri-plane encoder output and processed by transformer blocks to produce an expression tri-plane residual added to the source tri-planes; (3) the resulting tri-planes are rendered via NeRF volumetric ray marching with the driver's pose, followed by neural upsampling. The key distinction from prior work is that expression is learned entirely from RGB images in volumetric space without any parametric face model, and a GAN-based cross-identity regularization loss (conditioned on source identity) prevents the expression module from leaking driver identity. Training is self-supervised on CelebV-HQ video pairs, with Lp3D fine-tuned using a mixed real+synthetic reconstruction loss to generalize to in-the-wild portraits.

## Results

On the HDTF dataset, VOODOO 3D achieves the best self-reenactment scores across all metrics: PSNR 22.83, SSIM 0.768, LPIPS 0.168, NAKD 0.012, and ECMD 0.426, outperforming the strongest baseline MegaPortraits (PSNR 21.10, LPIPS 0.291). For cross-reenactment, it attains FID 36.4 and CSIM 0.754, substantially beating MegaPortraits (FID 61.7) and ROME (FID 172). On CelebA-HQ, the method achieves FID 23.6 for cross-reenactment, nearly halving the FID of the next-best method MegaPortraits (29.2) while maintaining competitive identity similarity (CSIM 0.608 vs. 0.647).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOODOO 3D outperforms all five quantitative baselines on HDTF across nearly every metric. Against the strongest 2D baseline MegaPortraits [27], it improves PSNR by 1.73 dB, reduces LPIPS by 42% (0.168 vs. 0.291), and cuts cross-reenactment FID by 41% (36.4 vs. 61.7). Against the 3D mesh-based ROME [43], gains are even larger: FID drops from 172 to 36.4 in cross-reenactment and PSNR rises from 18.46 to 22.83. The tri-plane-based OTAvatar [59] is the closest 3D competitor but still trails by a wide margin (FID 72.4 vs. 36.4; ECMD 0.901 vs. 0.754 in cross-reenactment). On CelebA-HQ, HeadNeRF [36] is the weakest baseline (FID 113, CSIM 0.346), while MegaPortraits [27] leads in CSIM (0.647 vs. 0.608) but loses on FID (29.2 vs. 23.6) and ECMD (0.77 vs. 0.79). The qualitative comparison with LPR [54] in Fig. 7 shows VOODOO 3D achieving superior identity consistency, particularly for non-frontal poses and extreme expressions where LPR's reliance on FLAME blendshapes degrades.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lp3D [81] for real-time 3D lifting into tri-plane representations and on EG3D [20] as the underlying tri-plane generative architecture. It departs from the dominant 3D-aware reenactment lineage (LPR [54], ROME [43], HeadNeRF [36], OTAvatar [59]) by replacing linear face models (FLAME/3DMM) for expression disentanglement with a fully volumetric, self-supervised approach that extracts expression features directly from frontalized RGB images. Conceptually, it extends the 2D insight from MegaPortraits [27]—that avoiding low-dimensional linear expression spaces improves fidelity—into the 3D volumetric setting, while adding the critical 3D lifting and frontalization step that makes volumetric expression transfer feasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is practically significant for holographic 3D teleconferencing (up to 45 parallel views at ~32 fps), a setting where view consistency is non-negotiable and linear face models produce visibly uncanny results. The ablation revealing that without frontalization the model entirely ignores the driver's expression highlights a previously underappreciated coupling between pose and expression in volumetric feature spaces. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond faces to full-body reenactment, whether the self-supervised training on CelebV-HQ is sufficient for truly diverse populations, and whether the fine-tuning recipe (mixed real/synthetic losses) transfers to other 3D lifting backbones. Limitations with extreme side views (>90°), stylized portraits, and accessories remain.
