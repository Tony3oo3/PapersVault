## Problem

Pre-training point cloud backbones remains challenging due to the unordered and non-uniform density nature of point clouds. Existing contrastive methods struggle with negative sample selection, while generative methods (Point-MAE, Point-M2AE) only reconstruct masked patches and cannot capture global density distribution, and 2D-projection methods (TAP, Ponder) inevitably lose geometric information. No prior work has leveraged the hierarchical denoising structure of diffusion models to extract multi-level geometric priors from point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud of 1,024 points (sampled from 3D shapes), partitions it into 64 patches of 32 points each via FPS+KNN, and masks 80% of patches. The transformer encoder processes only visible patches to produce latent features, which are aggregated into a 768-dim condition vector. The CPDM then recovers the original point cloud point-to-point from a Gaussian-noised version, and the resulting pre-trained encoder backbone is transferred to downstream tasks (classification, segmentation, detection) where it outputs class labels, per-point semantic masks, or 3D bounding boxes.

## Method

PointDif formulates pre-training as conditional point-to-point denoising: a Condition Aggregation Network (CANet) pools visible and masked patch features into a global condition vector, which guides a Conditional Point Diffusion Model (CPDM) composed of six Point Condition Networks (PCNets) to predict the added noise at each diffusion step. Each PCNet uses a reset-gate mechanism where the condition adaptively modulates which geometric features are retained, enabling point-by-point guidance that preserves density information. A recurrent uniform sampling strategy divides the 2,000 diffusion steps into 4 intervals and computes the MSE loss at one sampled timestep per interval per sample, then averages, ensuring the encoder learns both low-level texture (early steps) and high-level semantic structure (late steps) under balanced supervision. The encoder, CANet, and CPDM are jointly trained end-to-end, and the framework is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated on Transformer, MinkowskiNet, and BiResNet architectures.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN, PointDif achieves 91.91% (OBJ-ONLY), 93.29% (OBJ-BG), and 87.61% (PB-T50-RS) overall accuracy, exceeding the strongest prior pre-training method TAP by 2.4%, 2.9%, and 1.9% respectively. On S3DIS Area 5 semantic segmentation, PointDif attains 70.0% mIoU and 77.1% mAcc, outperforming Point-BERT (68.9%), MaskPoint (68.6%), and Point-MAE (68.4%). For 3D object detection on ScanNet, PointDif reaches 43.7% AP50 with 3DETR, surpassing Point-MAE (42.8%) and TAP (41.4%). On the outdoor SemanticKITTI benchmark, pre-training MinkowskiNet with PointDif yields 71.3% mIoU, a 1.1% gain over training from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointDif consistently outperforms all prior pre-training methods across every evaluated task. On ScanObjectNN classification, it beats TAP (the closest competitor) by 2.4–2.9% and Point-MAE by 3.3–3.6% on the three subsets. On S3DIS segmentation, PointDif achieves 70.0% mIoU, improving over Point-BERT (68.9%), MaskPoint (68.6%), and Point-MAE (68.4%) by roughly 1.4–1.6%. For ScanNet detection, PointDif reaches 43.7% AP50, surpassing Point-MAE (42.8%) by 0.9% and TAP (41.4%) by 2.3%, while also outperforming same-domain pre-trained methods MaskPoint (42.1%) and Point-BERT (38.3%) despite being pre-trained on the different-domain ShapeNet dataset. On SemanticKITTI outdoor segmentation, PointDif pre-training adds 1.1% mIoU over MinkowskiNet trained from scratch (71.3% vs. 70.2%), and on CAGroup3D detection it improves AP25 by 0.9% and AP50 by 0.5% over the train-from-scratch variant. The framework's flexibility is demonstrated by consistent gains when applied to diverse backbones (Transformer, MinkowskiNet, BiResNet), confirming that the diffusion-based pre-training is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointDif extends the masked autoencoder pre-training pipeline (Point-BERT, Point-MAE) by replacing the patch-reconstruction objective with a conditional diffusion denoising process, drawing directly on the DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020) and the point-cloud diffusion architecture of Luo & Hu (2021). It departs from prior generative pre-training methods (TAP, Ponder) that project 3D to 2D, and from contrastive methods (PointContrast, DepthContrast) that struggle with negative sample selection, by framing pre-training as iterative point-to-point recovery from noisy point clouds. The recurrent uniform sampling strategy is a novel training-time contribution not present in standard DDPM or prior point cloud pre-training work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PointDif provides the first demonstration that diffusion-based denoising can serve as an effective pre-training objective for point cloud backbones, offering a principled alternative to masked reconstruction that naturally captures global density distribution through point-to-point guidance. The framework's backbone-agnostic design (validated on Transformer, MinkowskiNet, and BiResNet) makes it a practical tool for the community. However, improvements over strong baselines like TAP and Point-MAE are modest (1–3%), pre-training relies on the synthetic ShapeNet dataset, and the paper does not explore whether the approach scales to larger pre-training corpora or benefits from more recent diffusion formulations (score-based, consistency models).
