## Problem

CLIP pretraining offers strong distributional robustness, yet directly fine-tuning CLIP-initialized segmentation networks degrades out-of-distribution performance far below ImageNet-pretrained baselines. The paper asks how to leverage CLIP's language-visual alignment for domain generalized semantic segmentation without sacrificing its inherent robustness to distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240331/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB driving scene images cropped to 768×768 during training and produces pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks over 19 shared classes. The text modality is used exclusively during an offline style-mining phase (via CLIP's text encoder to guide prompt-driven style optimization) and is not required at inference time. The backbone is a ResNet-50 or ResNet-101 initialized with CLIP weights, with the attention pooling layer removed and a randomly initialized DeepLabv3+ decoder head appended.

## Method

FAMix combines three ingredients: (i) minimal fine-tuning, where only the last few layers of the CLIP-initialized backbone are updated while the majority remains frozen, preserving pretrained distributional robustness; (ii) language-driven local style mining, where low-level CLIP features are divided into patches, each patch's dominant class is identified from ground truth, and PIN optimizes style statistics (mean, std) to match prompts formed by concatenating a random style descriptor with the class name, populating per-class style banks; (iii) patch-wise style mixing during training, where each patch's original statistics are convexly combined with a randomly sampled augmented style from the corresponding class bank using a Beta(0.1, 0.1) mixing coefficient, then applied via AdaIN. The key distinction from MixStyle is that mixing occurs between source statistics and language-augmented proxy-domain statistics (rather than source-source), and is performed locally per patch in a class-aware manner, enabling exploration of intermediate domains without any additional training images.

## Results

On the primary single-source DGSS benchmark (trained on GTAV, evaluated on 8 unseen domains), FAMix with ResNet-50 achieves a mean mIoU of 38.88%, outperforming the strongest baseline TLDR* (36.81%) by +2.07 mIoU and SHADE (33.32%) by +5.56 mIoU. With ResNet-101, FAMix reaches 41.11% mean mIoU. In the multi-source setting (trained on GTAV+Synthia), FAMix achieves 48.84% mean mIoU, surpassing TLDR* (46.40%) by +2.44 mIoU. FAMix also leads in the Cityscapes-as-source setting with 47.65% mean mIoU vs. 46.17% for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAMix outperforms all 11 compared baselines on the primary GTAV→8-domain benchmark. Against the strongest non-extra-data baseline SHADE [58], FAMix gains +5.56 mIoU (RN50) and +5.98 mIoU (RN101). Against TLDR* [24], which uses additional texture images, FAMix still leads by +2.07 mIoU (RN50) without any extra data. Against WildNet* [29], which leverages ImageNet for content/style extension, FAMix achieves +5.04 mIoU (RN50). In the multi-source setting (Table 4), FAMix beats TLDR* by +2.44 mIoU and SPC-Net [19] by +2.92 mIoU. In the Cityscapes-as-source setting (Table 3), FAMix leads by +1.48 mIoU over WildNet* and +2.08 mIoU over SiamDoGe [51]. Notably, FAMix does not require any external images for augmentation, unlike TLDR* and WildNet*, making it strictly more data-efficient while achieving superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAMix sits at the intersection of domain generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS) and CLIP-based representation learning. It rethinks MixStyle [62] by replacing the mixing of source-domain statistics with a mixing between source statistics and language-augmented statistics mined via PIN [10], and it adopts the minimal fine-tuning philosophy from the CLIP robustness preservation literature [26, 50, 15] but adapts it to the pixel-level segmentation setting. The key departure from prior DGSS work is the use of CLIP pretraining (rather than ImageNet) as the backbone initialization, combined with a language-driven local style augmentation that requires no additional images beyond the source domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that CLIP pretraining can be effectively harnessed for DGSS, provided the fine-tuning is carefully constrained; the finding that naive CLIP fine-tuning actually degrades OOD performance below ImageNet pretraining (14.69 vs. 25.90 mean mIoU) is a useful caution for practitioners. The language-driven augmentation offers a principled, image-free source of style randomization that outperforms noise-based alternatives across all SNR levels, suggesting that semantic structure in the perturbation matters. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond driving-scene segmentation to other domains, whether the one-time offline style mining step remains effective when the target distribution is far from any prompt-describable style, and whether the reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder limits scalability to open-vocabulary or very large class sets.
