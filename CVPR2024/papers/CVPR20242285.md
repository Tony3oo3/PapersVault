## Problem

Facial attribute classification models trained on biased data exhibit unfair accuracy gaps across subpopulations because spurious attributes (e.g., gender) correlate with the target attribute (e.g., smiling). Most existing fairness-aware methods require explicit labels of these spurious attributes, which are often unavailable due to annotation cost, subjectivity, or the difficulty of identifying which attributes are spurious. This paper asks how to train a fair FAC model on biased data using only target attribute labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB facial images as input and outputs a binary attribute prediction (e.g., Smiling vs. Non-smiling). During training, only the target attribute label is available; no spurious attribute annotations are used. A pre-trained StyleGAN provides the latent space for manipulation, and a reference model (CLIP) is used solely during a grid-search step to validate that the target attribute remains unchanged after editing. The final classifier is a linear probe on a frozen contrastive encoder.

## Method

DiGA is a two-stage framework. In the bias detection stage, a GAN is trained on the data, and two linear classifiers are learned in latent space with different regularization strengths, yielding two biased semantic directions that encode both target and spurious attribute information. These directions are combined with coefficients found via grid search (validated by CLIP) to cancel the target attribute semantics, leaving a direction that encodes only spurious attributes. In the augmentation stage, each training image's latent code is edited along this spurious direction with a degree sampled uniformly from a range, producing augmented views that vary in spurious attributes while preserving the target. A BYOL-style contrastive encoder with momentum is trained to minimize distance between these augmented views, learning representations invariant to spurious attributes. A linear classifier is then trained on the frozen encoder for final prediction. The uniform-distribution sampling of editing degrees (rather than a single fixed point) provides richer supervision, improving both accuracy and fairness over single-point augmentation baselines.

## Results

On CelebA (T=Smiling, S=Male), DiGA achieves 88.4% accuracy, 81.9% worst-group accuracy, and 7.4 EO, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On UTKFace (T=Gender, S=Ethnicity), DiGA reaches 92.7% accuracy, 89.0% worst-group accuracy, and 6.8 EO. On the non-facial Dogs and Cats dataset (T=Species, S=Color), DiGA attains 88.4% accuracy, 81.1% worst-group accuracy, and 7.8 EO. The largest relative gains appear in EO (fairness), where DiGA reduces the gap by 3–7× compared to baselines (e.g., 7.4 vs. 25.3 for ERM on CelebA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiGA outperforms all six baselines on every metric (accuracy, worst-group accuracy, and EO) across all tested settings. Against ERM (ResNet baseline), DiGA improves worst-group accuracy by 11.8 points (81.9 vs. 70.1) and reduces EO by 71% (7.4 vs. 25.3) on CelebA T=s/S=m, while also matching or exceeding ERM's overall accuracy. Among regularization-based methods, CVaR DRO and EIIL improve fairness over ERM but still trail DiGA by 15+ points in EO; EIIL is the closest fairness competitor (19.7 EO) yet sacrifices 0.5% accuracy. Among reweighting-based methods, LfF achieves the best worst-group accuracy among baselines (77.5) but still lags DiGA by 4.4 points, while JTT and MAPLE show moderate fairness gains with notable accuracy drops (e.g., MAPLE drops to 86.7% on UTKFace vs. DiGA's 92.7%). The gap widens under higher data bias: at β=90%, DiGA's EO advantage over the best baseline (LfF at 17.0) reaches 9.6 points, and under incomplete labels (10% ratio), DiGA maintains 8.4 EO while baselines degrade to 23–28.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generation-based fairness line (e.g., FairFaceGAN, latent-space de-biasing) by removing the requirement for spurious attribute labels, which prior methods like IRM, resampling, and fair contrastive learning all depend on. It builds on InterfaceGAN-style latent space manipulation for attribute editing and BYOL-style momentum contrastive learning, but introduces a key conceptual departure: using two biased semantic directions (trained with different regularization strengths) to algebraically isolate spurious attribute semantics, with a theoretical existence guarantee (Theorem 1). The distributionally-sampled augmentation degree (uniform over a range rather than a single flip) is a deliberate extension over prior single-point generative augmentation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical gap addressed—fair FAC without spurious attribute annotations—is significant because such labels are often unavailable due to subjectivity, cost, or ambiguity. The interpretability benefit (explicitly visualizing which attributes are spurious in image space) is a useful diagnostic that prior black-box debiasing methods lack. Limitations include dependence on GAN reconstruction quality, a grid-search procedure for combination coefficients, and evaluation primarily on facial datasets; the Dogs-and-Cats experiment suggests but does not fully establish generality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or continuous spurious attributes and whether the reference-model assumption (CLIP or similar) can be eliminated entirely.
