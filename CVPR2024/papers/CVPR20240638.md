## Problem

Existing WSSS methods that incorporate contextual knowledge to improve CAM completeness suffer from a knowledge bias between global in-class features and instance-specific features, causing prototypes to erroneously activate similar or frequently co-occurring categories (e.g., cat vs. dog). This bias is exacerbated by large intra-class variation, where regions of the same class exhibit very different appearances even within a single image, making reliable label propagation from image-level to pixel-level difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240638/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images with only image-level category labels (no pixel annotations) and produces class activation maps (CAMs) that serve as localization seeds. These CAMs are refined via CRF into pseudo-labels that train a separate segmentation network (e.g., DeepLabV2) to yield final pixel-level semantic masks. CPAL is architecture-agnostic and plugs into existing WSSS pipelines (IRN, AMN, MCTformer, CLIP-ES) as an additional supervisory branch without modifying the backbone or CAM generation architecture.

## Method

CPAL models each instance's category features as an anchor prototype via masked average pooling over CAM-guided regions, then retrieves context prototypes from a FIFO support bank that is periodically clustered via online k-means into per-class prototype groups. A pairwise positiveness score (dot product) softly measures relevance between the anchor and each candidate context prototype, and the top-K most relevant neighbors are selected to generate a Prototype-Aware CAM (PACAM) by aggregating cosine similarities between spatial features and the selected prototypes. A feature distribution alignment step computes a per-class shift term δ that pushes sparse mini-batch instance features toward the dense cluster centers in the bank, reducing intra-class distributional bias. Training is unified through a combined loss: standard BCE classification loss plus an L1 self-supervised consistency loss between the original CAM and the PACAM, which implicitly encourages the model to learn more discriminative and complete object representations.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 test, AMN+CPAL (ResNet-101) achieves 72.9% mIoU, and CLIP-ES+CPAL reaches 74.7% mIoU, both setting state-of-the-art among image-label-only WSSS methods. On the more challenging MS COCO 2014 val, MCTformer+CPAL with ResNet-38 backbone achieves 46.5% mIoU, outperforming all ResNet-38 baselines (next best: ACR at 45.3%). As a plug-in, CPAL improves IRN by +7.3% mIoU on VOC test (64.8%→72.1%) and MCTformer by +5.6% on pseudo-mask quality (69.1%→74.7% on VOC train).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPAL is evaluated as a plug-in on four existing WSSS methods (IRN, AMN, MCTformer, CLIP-ES), consistently improving all of them. On VOC 2012 test, AMN+CPAL (72.9%) surpasses AMN (69.6%) by +3.3%, LPCAM+AMN (70.4%) by +2.5%, and ACR (71.9%) by +1.0%. On COCO 2014 val with ResNet-38, MCTformer+CPAL (46.5%) outperforms all ResNet-38 baselines including ACR (45.3%) and USAGE (42.7%) by +1.2% and +3.8% respectively. CLIP-ES+CPAL achieves the overall best result at 74.7% VOC test and 46.8% COCO val, improving over CLIP-ES alone by +0.8% and +1.4%. The largest single gain is IRN+CPAL on VOC test (+7.3% over IRN's 64.8%), demonstrating CPAL's effectiveness even on older architectures. On VOC train pseudo-mask quality, MCTformer+CPAL reaches 74.7%, exceeding SFC (73.7%) and Mat-Label (72.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based learning paradigm (originally from few-shot learning) into WSSS by combining instance prototypes (as in SIPE) with context prototypes drawn from a support bank (as in RCA's memory bank). It departs from prior context-mining methods that treat all context prototypes uniformly by introducing a positiveness-weighted selection mechanism that adaptively adjusts each context prototype's contribution to the current instance. The feature distribution alignment module, which shifts sparse instance features toward dense cluster centers in the bank, is a novel regularization not present in prior prototype-based WSSS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of CPAL (improving IRN, AMN, MCTformer, and CLIP-ES without architectural changes) makes it broadly applicable to existing WSSS pipelines, and the self-supervised consistency loss between CAM and PACAM provides a simple yet powerful training signal. However, the method still depends on CAM quality as its foundation, and the support bank's k-means clustering may not scale gracefully to very large or long-tailed datasets. Open questions include whether the positiveness mechanism generalizes to other weak supervision signals (scribbles, bounding boxes) and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D segmentation settings.
