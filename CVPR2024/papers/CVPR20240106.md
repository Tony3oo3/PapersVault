## Problem

Generative replay has become a prominent strategy for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in class incremental learning, but its application to class incremental object detection (CIOD) has been largely unexplored due to the complexity of multi-label scenes. This paper is the first to apply a diffusion-based generative model (Stable Diffusion) to CIOD, addressing the challenge of generating realistic, spatially controlled synthetic images containing previously learned object classes without storing any real old-task data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For synthetic image generation, the method consumes text prompts describing object counts and scene style, grounding inputs (class labels and normalized bounding box coordinates extracted from old annotations), and CLIP image embeddings from corresponding old real images, producing 512×512 RGB images via Stable Diffusion 1.4. For detection, the Deformable DETR backbone (ResNet-50, 300 object queries) takes standard COCO-resolution RGB images and outputs per-query class logits and 4-coordinate normalized bounding boxes. The pseudo-labeling mechanism additionally processes new-task images through the old model to recover old-class object annotations that would otherwise be treated as background.

## Method

SDDGR extends Stable Diffusion with GLIGEN's gated self-attention mechanism to accept grounding inputs (class labels encoded via CLIP text embeddings and bounding boxes encoded via Fourier embeddings, fused through an MLP), enabling spatially and semantically controlled generation of multi-object scenes. An iterative class-wise refiner uses the previous task's trained detector to score generated images, progressively lowering the acceptance threshold from 0.8 to 0.05 per cycle until each class reaches a target count N, with a fallback class-specific generation strategy for under-represented classes. Rather than training directly on synthetic images, the method applies L2 knowledge distillation across all six decoder layers, enforcing the new model's classification and regression outputs to match the old model's predictions on synthetic data. Pseudo-labeling of old-class objects in new-task images (using a confidence threshold of 0.3 on the old model's query logits) prevents their misclassification as background during incremental training.

## Results

On COCO 2017 in the two-phase setting, SDDGR achieves 43.0% AP (40+40) and 40.9% AP (70+10), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art CL-DETR by +1.0% and +0.5% AP respectively, with larger gains of +2.0% and +1.5% in AP.5. In the multi-phase setting (40+10+10+10+10), SDDGR reaches 36.8% AP at the final phase, outperforming CL-DETR by a substantial 8.7% AP margin. Notably, SDDGR achieves these results without storing any real data from previous tasks, whereas CL-DETR relies on a 10% replay buffer of real images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDDGR outperforms all five baselines across every metric in both two-phase scenarios. Against the closest competitor CL-DETR (which uses Deformable DETR + 10% real-data replay buffer), SDDGR gains +1.0% AP and +2.0% AP.5 in the 40+40 setting, and +0.5% AP and +1.5% AP.5 in the 70+10 setting, while using no real data from prior tasks. The gap widens dramatically in multi-phase settings: SDDGR achieves 36.8% AP at the 5th phase of 40+10+10+10+10 versus CL-DETR's 28.1% (+8.7%), and 41.1% AP at the 3rd phase of 40+20+20 versus CL-DETR's 35.3% (+5.8%). Among the GFLv1-based baselines, ERD [11] is the strongest (36.9% AP in 40+40), but SDDGR still exceeds it by 6.1% AP. The CNN-based methods (LWF, RILOD, SID) show substantially lower performance, with LWF dropping to only 7.1% AP in the 70+10 scenario, highlighting the advantage of the Transformer-based D-DETR backbone combined with diffusion-based generative replay.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: generative replay for continual learning and controllable diffusion-based image generation. For CIOD, prior methods (CL-DETR, OW-DETR, ERD) rely on partial experience replay with stored real data or label-level distillation, while generative replay in classification (DGR, MRGAN, DDGR) has used GANs or simpler diffusion models. SDDGR departs from both by leveraging a large pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) augmented with GLIGEN's grounding mechanism to generate multi-object scenes with class and bounding-box control, then feeding those synthetic images through L2 knowledge distillation rather than direct training. The pseudo-labeling strategy for old objects in new-task images extends the attention-driven pseudo-labeling idea from OW-DETR to a broader role in both training and synthetic-image refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDDGR demonstrates that a pre-trained diffusion model can replace stored real data as the replay mechanism in CIOD, achieving state-of-the-art results without any memory of previous-task images—a practical advantage for privacy-sensitive or storage-constrained deployments. The particularly large multi-phase gains (up to 8.7% AP over CL-DETR in 40+10+10+10+10) suggest that synthetic-data distillation may scale better than real-data replay as the number of incremental phases grows. However, evaluation is confined to COCO 2017 with a single detector architecture (Deformable DETR), and the generation pipeline (iterative refinement, class-specific fallback) is computationally expensive, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other datasets, larger class counts, or real-time incremental settings.
