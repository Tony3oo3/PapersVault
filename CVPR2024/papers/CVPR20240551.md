## Problem

Existing dynamic human rendering methods condition on static poses and reconstruct each frame independently, ignoring temporal motion dynamics. This causes them to generate identical appearances for the same pose under different velocities or trajectories, failing to capture the secondary motion of clothing (e.g., T-shirt wrinkles that differ between fast and slow arm swings). Additionally, modeling full 4D motion volumes demands an impractical amount of training observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse multi-view video sequences with pre-estimated SMPL body meshes as input, from which it extracts a 4D motion representation comprising the static 3D pose, per-vertex surface velocity (temporal derivative), and a weighted motion trajectory aggregated over a sliding window of w past timesteps. Given a target camera viewpoint, it outputs high-resolution novel-view images of the clothed human with time-varying appearances, rendered in two stages: a low-resolution volumetric feature map followed by a geometry-aware super-resolution pass.

## Method

SurMo projects the extracted 4D motion (pose, velocity, trajectory) onto the UV surface manifold of a clothless SMPL template, then lifts it via a motion encoder into a compact surface-based triplane defined in a u-v-h coordinate system, where h is a signed distance to the body surface that parameterizes temporal clothing offsets. During training, a physical motion decoder enforces learning of spatial and temporal dynamics by predicting the surface normal (spatial derivative) and surface velocity (temporal derivative) at the next timestep t+1 from the triplane features at time t. For rendering, a conditional NeRF-based volumetric surface-conditioned renderer queries only points near the body surface (using nearest-face barycentric coordinates and signed distance) to produce low-resolution features, which are then upscaled by a geometry-aware super-resolution network. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with adversarial, pixel, perceptual, face-identity, velocity/normal, and volume-rendering losses.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap (6 sequences, averaged over all test views and poses), SurMo achieves LPIPS 0.075, FID 67.7, SSIM 0.833, and PSNR 24.82, outperforming the closest competitor HumanNeRF (LPIPS 0.106, FID 88.4, SSIM 0.792, PSNR 23.62) by a clear margin on all four metrics. The gains over Neural Body (LPIPS 0.164, FID 125.1) and Instant-NVR (LPIPS 0.202, FID 145.0) are even larger, with FID reduced by roughly 45–53% relative to those baselines. Qualitative evaluations on MPII-RDDC and AIST++ further demonstrate that SurMo uniquely synthesizes time-varying clothing wrinkles, motion-dependent shadows, and high-fidelity fast-motion sequences where baselines produce static or blurry results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurMo outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest competitor HumanNeRF [61], SurMo reduces LPIPS from 0.106 to 0.075 (−29%) and FID from 88.4 to 67.7 (−23%), while improving SSIM by 0.041 and PSNR by 1.2 dB. The gap over Neural Body [43] is even wider (FID 67.7 vs. 125.1; PSNR 24.8 vs. 21.4), and Instant-NVR [9] trails most significantly (FID 145.0, LPIPS 0.202). Qualitatively, HumanNeRF produces nearly identical T-shirt wrinkles for similar poses with different motion directions, whereas SurMo renders distinct time-varying wrinkles; on MPII-RDDC, SurMo captures motion-dependent self-occlusion shadows that HumanNeRF misses entirely. On AIST++ fast dance sequences, Neural Body yields blurry results due to its sparse 3D point-cloud pose representation, while SurMo's denser surface-based triplane preserves high-frequency clothing detail. Comparisons with ARAH [58], DVA [47], and HVTR++ [18] are deferred to the supplementary material, as those methods require driving-view inputs for telepresence and are not directly comparable under the standard novel-view-synthesis protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

SurMo departs from the dominant pose-guided paradigm (Neural Body, HumanNeRF, Instant-NVR) that treats dynamic human rendering as a static-pose-to-image mapping with a passive time embedding, by explicitly encoding temporal dynamics (velocity, motion trajectory) as first-class inputs. It repurposes EG3D's triplane representation, replacing the volumetric 3D coordinate system with a surface-based UV-plus-height system that inherits SMPL body topology priors, and introduces a physical motion decoder that predicts next-frame surface normals and velocities as an auxiliary training signal. The volumetric surface-conditioned renderer and geometry-aware super-resolution module follow the hybrid rendering strategy established by EG3D and prior 3D-GAN work, but are adapted to articulated human geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical demonstration that SOTA dynamic human rendering methods produce pose-dependent rather than genuinely time-varying appearances, a gap that limits their utility for applications requiring physically plausible secondary motion (e.g., AR/VR, film). The surface-based triplane is a practical and efficient representation choice for humans specifically, since non-rigid garment deformations concentrate near the body surface, and the physical motion decoding offers a lightweight training signal that avoids expensive 4D volume supervision. Open questions include generalization beyond single-subject settings, handling of explicit lighting changes, real-time inference, and whether the SMPL-based surface projection generalizes to highly deformed or non-standard garments.
