## Problem

Constructing equivariant neural networks for the affine group remains a long-standing challenge because existing group-convolution approaches require discretizing or sampling the group, which becomes impractical for high-dimensional continuous groups and leads to exponential memory growth with depth. The paper addresses this by building affine equivariant networks from differential invariants of symmetric PDEs, eliminating the need for any group discretization or sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240470/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes 2D grayscale images (28×28 for scale tasks, 56×56 for rotation-scale, 40×40 for affine) as input, replicated along the channel dimension to match the required number of channels. Spatial derivatives are computed via Gaussian derivative kernels applied as convolutions. The final output is a class label obtained by applying global spatial pooling to the equivariant feature map (yielding invariant features) followed by two fully connected layers; intermediate layers produce equivariant feature maps that transform under the group action.

## Method

The core mechanism models feature extraction as the iterative solution of a symmetric PDE whose right-hand side is a learnable function (MLP, implemented as 1×1 convolutions) of differential invariants of the target group. To handle the affine group's fractional differential invariants that cause division-by-zero, the paper introduces SupNorm Normalized Differential Invariants (SNDIs): polynomial relative differential invariants are normalized by their global SupNorm, yielding true invariants that avoid singularities while preserving more information than classical quotients. The resulting InvarPDEs-Net stacks multiple PDE iterations (each an equivariant operator with a skip connection), and InvarLayer extracts a single iteration as a drop-in equivariant layer with freely specifiable input/output channel counts. Because the number of invariants is bounded by the derivative order rather than the group dimension, model size stays constant regardless of group complexity, and the framework extends directly to continuous subgroups (scale, rotation-scale) by swapping in the corresponding invariants.

## Results

On the affNIST out-of-distribution benchmark (trained on MNIST, tested on affine-perturbed images), InvarLayer achieves 98.45% ± 0.15 accuracy with 365K parameters, surpassing affConv (95.08% ± 0.31, 373K params) by 3.37% and RU CapsNet (97.69%, >580K params) by 0.76%. InvarPDEs-Net also outperforms affConv at 95.72% ± 0.12 with fewer parameters (340K). On scale-equivariance tasks, InvarPDEs-Net achieves 98.30% on Scale-MNIST and 89.62% on Scale-Fashion, and on rotation-scale tasks it reaches 95.80% on RS-MNIST and 79.48% on RS-Fashion, all with ~500K parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary affine-equivariance benchmark (affNIST), InvarLayer decisively outperforms affConv by 3.37% (98.45% vs. 95.08%) while using fewer parameters (365K vs. 373K), and also beats RU CapsNet (97.69%) by 0.76% despite using far fewer parameters. InvarPDEs-Net also edges out affConv (95.72% vs. 95.08%) with 340K parameters. On scale-equivariance tasks, InvarPDEs-Net achieves the best results on both Scale-MNIST (98.30% vs. 97.92% for SESN) and Scale-Fashion (89.62% vs. 87.48% for SE-CNN), while InvarLayer is competitive on Scale-MNIST (97.75%) and also leads on Scale-Fashion (89.50%). On rotation-scale tasks, InvarPDEs-Net clearly surpasses RST-CNN on both RS-MNIST (95.80% vs. 93.19%) and RS-Fashion (79.48% vs. 78.64%), whereas InvarLayer ties RST-CNN on RS-MNIST (93.15% vs. 93.19%) but trails on RS-Fashion (74.51% vs. 78.64%), though the authors note that minor hyperparameter tuning lifts InvarLayer to 93.40% and 76.08% respectively. Overall, InvarPDEs-Net consistently wins or ties across all three group-equivariance settings, while InvarLayer's advantage is most pronounced in the affine setting where its drop-in ResNet-32 integration proves decisive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learnable PDE framework of Liu et al. [33, 34], which constructed shift- and rotation-equivariant networks via symmetric PDEs built from differential invariants of the Euclidean group, to the much larger affine group. It departs from the dominant group-convolution and sampling-based approaches to affine equivariance (affConv [37], Finzi et al. [16], Mironenco & Forré [39]) by avoiding any discretization or sampling of the group entirely, instead relying on a precomputed set of differential invariants. The key technical departure is the introduction of SupNorm normalized differential invariants (SNDIs), which replace classical fractional differential invariants to eliminate division-by-zero failures while provably retaining more information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that affine equivariance can be achieved in a deep network without parameterizing or sampling the group, directly overcoming the exponential memory growth that limits affConv to shallow architectures. The SNDI construction is a reusable theoretical tool that may benefit other invariant-based methods beyond this specific architecture. Open questions include extending the framework to more general Lie groups (where differential invariants exist but may be harder to compute), to non-planar manifolds (spheres, 3D volumes), and to larger-scale benchmarks beyond MNIST/Fashion-MNIST variants. The InvarLayer's variable performance across settings (e.g., 74.51% on RS-Fashion vs. 79.48% for InvarPDEs-Net) suggests the layer design still has room for refinement.
