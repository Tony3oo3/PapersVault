## Problem

Structured light depth sensing requires visible patterns on the target surface, which is undesirable for applications like xR, advertising, and non-intrusive inspection. Infrared structured light avoids visual interference but cannot recover surface reflectance or texture in the visual spectrum. The paper asks whether shape and reflectance can be captured simultaneously and completely invisibly to the naked eye.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes polarimetric images (from a quad-Bayer RGB-polarimetric or monochrome polarimetric camera) of an object illuminated by a structured polarization pattern projected through a 1024×768 liquid crystal SLM, where each pixel encodes a different AoLP rather than intensity. The known projected AoLP pattern serves as the code for correspondence. Outputs include a triangulated depth map, a directly estimated per-pixel surface normal map (not derived from depth differentiation), FMBRDF parameters (refractive index, specular albedo, roughness, microfacet shape, correlation), and relit renderings under novel illumination.

## Method

SPIDeRS replaces intensity modulation in structured light with per-pixel AoLP modulation, realized by a TN liquid crystal SLM that rotates the polarization orientation of incident polarized light via voltage-dependent birefringence, making the projection invisible. The key decoding insight is that specular reflection preserves the projected AoLP (modeled by a diagonal Mueller matrix) while diffuse reflection and ambient light corrupt it; the paper synthesizes an unpolarized-light reference image by averaging two captures at perpendicular AoLPs and subtracts it to isolate the specular component, yielding a robust AoLP extraction formula. For BRDF and normal recovery (in the absence of ambient light), the method performs joint optimization of FMBRDF parameters and per-pixel surface normals against captured Stokes vectors, using PCA of triangulated 3D points for initialization. The entire pipeline is physics-based with no learned components, using phase-shifting with Gray codes for sub-pixel encoding accuracy.

## Results

SPIDeRS achieves depth reconstruction accuracy comparable to classic intensity-based structured light across objects of various materials and colors, both with and without ambient light (Figure 6 reports mean and median depth errors in mm per object). For surface normals, SPIDeRS directly estimates per-pixel normals from polarimetric reflection, yielding more detailed geometry than normals derived by differentiating (PCA) the reconstructed depth map (Figure 8 reports mean and median angular errors in degrees). The method also recovers FMBRDF parameters (refractive index, specular albedo, roughness, microfacet shape, and correlation) enabling plausible relighting under novel directional and colored illuminations (Figure 9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SPIDeRS against two structured-light baselines: classic intensity-modulated structured light (same phase-shifting + Gray-code encoding, amplitude modulation via a polarizing filter) and polarization-coded (PC) structured light [Huang et al., 2017]. Against classic structured light, SPIDeRS achieves comparable depth accuracy while being completely invisible and simultaneously capturing surface texture — a capability the intensity method lacks. Against PC structured light [11], SPIDeRS is substantially more robust: PC structured light suffers decoding errors from DoLP reduction and AoLP alteration by diffuse and ambient light, and by construction cannot capture texture (it requires a polarizing filter in front of the camera). For surface normals, SPIDeRS outperforms the PCA-from-depth baseline by directly exploiting polarimetric specular reflection, producing finer geometric detail without the noise amplification inherent in depth differentiation. The paper does not report a single unified numerical table; quantitative depth errors (mean/median in mm) and normal angular errors (mean/median in degrees) are annotated per-object in Figures 6 and 8 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the structured light lineage by replacing intensity or color modulation with per-pixel angle-of-linear-polarization (AoLP) modulation, making the projected pattern invisible to the naked eye and to standard cameras. It builds directly on Baek et al.'s simplified polarimetric specular and diffuse reflection Mueller matrices for decoding the reflected pattern, and on the authors' own FMBRDF model for joint BRDF and surface normal estimation. Unlike prior polarization-coded structured light (Huang et al.), which encodes binary patterns via color channels and thus remains visible, SPIDeRS uses a continuous AoLP encoding via a liquid-crystal SLM, enabling sub-pixel accuracy and simultaneous texture capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPIDeRS demonstrates that a single set of invisible structured light patterns can yield depth, surface normals, and reflectance simultaneously, opening a path toward fully stealth 3D+material capture for xR, robotics, and nondestructive inspection. Key limitations include the requirement for specialized hardware (SLM + polarimetric camera), the co-axial constraint on specular reflection (viewing/lighting angle discrepancy < 20°), and the restriction to dielectric surfaces for BRDF recovery. Open questions include extending to RGB polarization projection, dynamic scenes, and whether the approach scales to larger or more complex environments without the current parallax trade-off.
