## Problem

PU learning still lags significantly behind supervised classification because unreliable binary labels provide insufficient semantic supervision for learning discriminative representations, which in turn undermines downstream label disambiguation. The paper identifies that real-world PU data typically contain finer-grained latent sub-categories beneath the coarse binary split, and that exploiting this structure is key to closing the performance gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240999/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of explicitly positive-labeled images (1000 per dataset) and a much larger unlabeled pool, along with a 500-sample golden support set drawn from validation for meta-evaluation. Inputs are standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/STL-10, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) or brain MRI scans for the Alzheimer task. The output is a binary classifier producing a probability score in [0,1] per sample, with the unlabeled set's pseudo-labels iteratively refined during training.

## Method

LaGAM combines a hierarchical contrastive learning module with a meta-disambiguation objective. The contrastive module constructs a Latent Group-Aware (LGA) positive set by intersecting k-means cluster membership with same-predicted-label agreement (dichotomized cut-off) and unioning kNN neighbors, then optimizes an InfoNCE loss jointly with instance-discrimination to pull same-group samples together while separating across the binary boundary. For label disambiguation, instead of Self-PU's importance reweighting, LaGAM directly distills pseudo-labels of unlabeled samples via a bi-level meta-optimization: the inner loop trains the classifier on pseudo-labeled data, and the outer loop minimizes the evaluation loss on the support set with respect to the pseudo-labels themselves. Label updates use projected (binary) gradients combined with an Exponential Moving Average to ensure stability, and the classifier backbone is frozen during meta-steps to avoid interference with representation learning.

## Results

With a CNN backbone, LaGAM achieves 89.6% and 90.6% accuracy on CIFAR-10-1 and CIFAR-10-2, 92.6% and 85.9% on CIFAR-100-1 and CIFAR-100-2, and 87.5% and 81.9% on STL-10-1 and STL-10-2, outperforming the best baselines by up to 13.0% on CIFAR-100-2. With a ResNet-18 backbone, LaGAM reaches 96.2% on CIFAR-10-1 and 92.1% on CIFAR-100-1, approaching the supervised upper bound (98.7% and 94.2% respectively). On the real-world Alzheimer dataset, LaGAM achieves 77.8% accuracy, 84.5% F1, and 83.2% AUC, surpassing the best baseline Dist-PU by 6.1%, 10.8%, and 6.1% respectively. The paper reports an average 6.8% accuracy improvement over the current state-of-the-art across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaGAM outperforms all six baselines on 5 out of 6 CNN-backbone setups, with the sole exception being STL-10-2 where P3MIX-C (84.1%) edges out LaGAM (81.9%). The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100-2 (85.9% vs. Dist-PU's 72.9%, a 13.0% gap) and STL-10-1 (87.5% vs. P3MIX-C's 80.7%, a 6.8% gap), where the finer-grained latent categories and naturally unlabeled training data challenge existing methods. With a ResNet-18 backbone, LaGAM's advantage widens dramatically: it surpasses Dist-PU by 7.3–17.5% and P3MIX-C by 17.6–36.7% across all six setups, while P3MIX-C degrades severely (e.g., 76.1% on CIFAR-10-1 vs. 88.7% with CNN), suggesting its mixup-based strategy is incompatible with deeper architectures. On the Alzheimer dataset, LaGAM leads Dist-PU by 6.1% in accuracy (77.8% vs. 71.7%), 10.8% in F1 (84.5% vs. 73.7%), and 6.1% in AUC (83.2% vs. 77.1%), confirming practical utility in a real-world medical diagnosis setting. Notably, LaGAM does not require the SCAR assumption or class-prior knowledge that uPU, nnPU, and Self-PU depend on, making it more broadly applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaGAM departs from the two dominant PU learning paradigms—cost-sensitive reweighting (uPU, nnPU, Self-PU) and reliable sample selection (PAN, VPU, P3MIX, Dist-PU)—by reframing the bottleneck as a representation learning problem rather than a loss-design or sample-filtering problem. It builds on Self-PU's meta-learning framework but replaces importance reweighting with direct pseudo-label distillation, and adapts contrastive learning (MoCo/SimCLR-style instance discrimination) to the PU setting through a hierarchical positive-set construction that incorporates k-means clustering, a dichotomized binary-label cutoff, and kNN neighbor smoothing. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that binary PU labels are semantically impoverished and that latent finer-grained categories within the positive and unlabeled pools can be exploited via contrastive objectives to produce more discriminative embeddings before any label disambiguation occurs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The persistent gap between PU and fully supervised classifiers has limited practical adoption of PU learning in domains like medical diagnosis where negative labels are unavailable; LaGAM's 6.8% average improvement and near-supervised performance on CIFAR-10/100 and STL-10 suggest that investing in representation quality (via contrastive structure discovery) is more impactful than further refining risk estimators or selection heuristics. Limitations include reliance on k-means clustering quality in the embedding space, the added computational cost of second-order meta-gradients, and evaluation primarily on small-to-medium image datasets (CIFAR, STL-10) plus one real-world medical dataset. Open questions include whether the latent-group assumption generalizes to tabular or text PU data, how the method scales to ImageNet-sized datasets, and whether the warm-up-then-meta schedule can be made adaptive rather than fixed at 20 epochs.
