## Problem

Iterative stereo matching methods like RAFT-Stereo and IGEV-Stereo rely on recurrent units with fixed receptive fields, which forces the network to focus on a single frequency band during each update. This causes simultaneous loss of high-frequency details (edges, thin objects) and low-frequency structure (smooth, textureless regions), leading to blurred boundaries and false matches. The paper asks whether hidden disparity information at multiple frequencies can be adaptively selected and fused per-region during iterative refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240951/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rectified stereo image pair (left and right RGB images), downsamples features to 1/4 resolution, and constructs a 4-level all-pairs correlation pyramid. The output is a full-resolution disparity map obtained by upsampling 1/4-resolution predictions via convex combination. Training uses 320×720 crops on Scene Flow (200k steps, 22 iterations) followed by dataset-specific fine-tuning (e.g., 384×512 or 384×768 crops for ETH3D/Middlebury), with 32 iterations at inference for ablations and 16 for KITTI.

## Method

The core contribution is the Selective Recurrent Unit (SRU), which replaces the single GRU in iterative stereo networks with parallel GRU branches using different convolutional kernel sizes (1×1 and 3×3), each capturing a distinct receptive field and frequency band. A Contextual Spatial Attention (CSA) module—comprising a Channel Attention Enhancement and a Spatial Attention Extractor derived from CBAM—generates spatial attention maps from multi-level context features that act as per-pixel fusion weights: small-kernel (high-frequency) outputs receive high weights in edge regions while large-kernel (low-frequency) outputs dominate in textureless areas. The fused hidden state is fed into the next iteration, and at 1/4 resolution the SRU additionally ingests disparity and local cost volume to produce disparity residuals. The module is plug-and-play: it is validated by swapping GRUs in RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, and DLNR (where LSTMs replace GRUs inside SRU), consistently improving EPE with only ~4% parameter overhead. Training uses a weighted L1 loss across iterations (γ=0.9), AdamW with one-cycle LR (2e-4), and gradient clipping to [-1,1].

## Results

On Scene Flow, Selective-IGEV achieves a new state-of-the-art EPE of 0.44 px (vs. 0.47 for IGEV-Stereo and 0.53 for RAFT-Stereo). On KITTI 2012, Selective-IGEV attains the best 2-noc (1.59) and 2-all (2.05) among all published methods; on KITTI 2015 it leads all D1 metrics (D1-all: 1.55). On ETH3D, Selective-IGEV achieves Bad 0.5 of 3.06 and AvgErr of 0.12, and on Middlebury it reaches Bad 2.0 of 2.51 and AvgErr of 0.91, both ranking 1st among all published methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Selective-IGEV outperforms its direct baseline IGEV-Stereo [34] across all benchmarks: Scene Flow EPE 0.44 vs. 0.47, ETH3D Bad 0.5 3.06 vs. 3.52, and Middlebury Bad 2.0 2.51 vs. 4.83. Selective-RAFT similarly surpasses RAFT-Stereo [17] by 14.6% on KITTI 2012 2-noc (1.64 vs. 1.92) and by 25.2% on Scene Flow edge-region EPE (2.40 vs. 3.21). Against the strongest iterative competitor CREStereo [16], Selective-IGEV wins on ETH3D Bad 0.5 (3.06 vs. 3.58) and Middlebury Bad 2.0 (2.51 vs. 3.71), while CREStereo retains a slight edge on ETH3D Bad 1.0 (0.98 vs. 1.23). On Middlebury, Selective-IGEV dominates all prior methods including DLNR [44] (Bad 2.0: 2.51 vs. 3.20) and EAI-Stereo [43] (Bad 2.0: 2.51 vs. 3.68). On Scene Flow, Selective-IGEV's EPE of 0.44 surpasses LaC+GANet [18] (0.72) by 38.9% and ACVNet [32] (0.48) by 8.3%, establishing a clear margin over both aggregation-based and iterative baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative stereo matching paradigm established by RAFT-Stereo and IGEV-Stereo, where recurrent units (GRUs) iteratively refine disparity predictions from local cost volumes. The key conceptual departure is from the fixed single-kernel receptive field of standard GRUs: the proposed Selective Recurrent Unit (SRU) replaces each GRU with a pair of GRUs at different kernel sizes, whose outputs are fused via a Contextual Spatial Attention (CSA) module derived from CBAM. The frequency-domain motivation draws on octave convolution and frequency-domain learning literature, but is applied here specifically to the recurrent update mechanism rather than to feature extraction or cost aggregation. The module is validated as a drop-in replacement across three distinct iterative architectures (RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, DLNR), positioning it as a general-purpose upgrade rather than a new end-to-end architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SRU+CSA (only ~4% parameter increase) makes it practically attractive for practitioners working with any iterative stereo matching backbone, and the SOTA results on KITTI, ETH3D, and Middlebury confirm its effectiveness. The observation that CSA generates qualitatively different attention patterns in different networks (noise-filtering bias in Selective-RAFT vs. high-frequency preservation in Selective-IGEV) suggests the module is genuinely adaptive rather than a fixed inductive bias. Open questions remain: the receptive fields are still bounded by predefined kernel sizes, adding branches increases memory and compute, and the paper acknowledges that combining convolutions with self-attention for complementary receptive fields is an unexplored direction. Whether the adaptive frequency-fusion principle transfers to other iterative estimation tasks (optical flow, depth) is left untested.
