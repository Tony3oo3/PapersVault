## Problem

Pose-free novel view synthesis from stereo pairs requires jointly estimating camera pose, correspondences, and rendering, but existing methods either treat these as separate pipelines (causing misalignment) or assemble them in multi-task frameworks that fail to exploit their intrinsic complementarity. This is especially problematic under extreme viewpoint changes and minimal image overlap, where prior pose-free NeRF methods (DBARF, FlowCAM) degrade significantly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 256×256 unposed RGB images with known camera intrinsics, plus a desired rendering viewpoint P_{1←t}, and produces a synthesized novel view, a relative camera pose (6D rotation + 3D translation), a dense bidirectional flow map with confidence scores, and per-pixel depth. Unlike standard generalized NeRF tasks, no camera pose between the input pair is provided at inference; the evaluation protocol partitions test scenes by overlap ratio (Small <0.5, Medium 0.5–0.75, Large >0.75) to stress-test wide-baseline performance.

## Method

CoPoNeRF builds a shared multi-level representation (ResNet-34, 3 scales) and constructs full 4D cost volumes via pairwise cosine similarity at each level, then refines them with a self-attention aggregation block that jointly processes features and cost volumes. The refined cost volume serves triple duty: argmax yields dense flow (with cyclic-consistency occlusion masks), a modified essential-matrix module (replacing the attention map with the cost volume) regresses relative pose, and softmax over the volume produces a matching distribution that inter-conditions feature maps for the epipolar-sampling renderer. Training uses four losses—L1 photometric, SSIM-based matching, geodesic+L2 pose, and a novel triplet consistency loss coupling flow, pose, and depth—under a teacher-forcing strategy where ground-truth poses are injected into the renderer during training to stabilize gradient flow to the pose head.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (average across overlap splits), CoPoNeRF achieves 19.54 PSNR, 0.638 SSIM, and 0.398 LPIPS for pose-free novel view synthesis, outperforming DBARF (14.79 PSNR) and FlowCAM (18.24 PSNR) by substantial margins. On ACID, it reaches 22.44 PSNR and 0.649 SSIM, again surpassing both pose-free baselines. For relative pose estimation, CoPoNeRF achieves 3.61° mean rotation error and 12.77° mean translation error on RealEstate10K, compared to 11.14°/93.30° for DBARF and 7.43°/50.66° for FlowCAM. Notably, with ground-truth poses, CoPoNeRF's learned representation yields 22.78 PSNR on RealEstate10K, exceeding the state-of-the-art Du et al. (21.83 PSNR) under the same GT-pose setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPoNeRF decisively outperforms both pose-free NeRF baselines: on RealEstate10K it gains +4.75 PSNR over DBARF (19.54 vs 14.79) and +1.30 PSNR over FlowCAM (19.54 vs 18.24), with even larger SSIM improvements (0.638 vs 0.570 and 0.455). For pose estimation, CoPoNeRF reduces mean rotation error to 3.61° on RealEstate10K, halving DBARF's 11.14° and FlowCAM's 7.43°, and cutting translation error from 93.30° (DBARF) and 50.66° (FlowCAM) down to 12.77°. Against end-to-end pose estimators Rockwell et al. (12.59° rot) and RelPose (8.29° rot), CoPoNeRF is substantially more accurate, though PDC-Net+ (2.19° rot) remains superior because it is trained with ground-truth depth-derived correspondences. In the GT-pose rendering comparison, CoPoNeRF's shared representation (22.78 PSNR) surpasses Du et al.'s state-of-the-art (21.83 PSNR), demonstrating that joint multi-task learning yields a stronger feature backbone than single-task training. The gap to Du et al. under pose-free conditions (19.54 vs 21.83 PSNR) highlights the residual cost of pose uncertainty, yet CoPoNeRF still exceeds PixelNeRF with GT poses (14.44 PSNR) by over 5 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPoNeRF builds on the essential-matrix-as-inductive-bias formulation from Rockwell et al. [46] and the epipolar-line feature sampling strategy from Du et al. [20], but departs fundamentally from prior pose-free NeRF methods (DBARF, FlowCAM) by replacing their staged or modular pipelines with a single shared 4D cost-volume representation that simultaneously drives correspondence, pose, and rendering. Where DBARF used local cost volumes and FlowCAM relied on an external RAFT flow network, this work constructs full pairwise similarity volumes across multi-level features and uses them as a matching distribution to inter-condition features, unifying all three tasks under one architecture trained end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to handle pose-free novel view synthesis under extreme viewpoint changes and minimal overlap, a regime where prior pose-free NeRFs degrade severely. The finding that the jointly learned representation actually surpasses the SOTA generalized NeRF (Du et al.) when given ground-truth poses suggests multi-task geometric learning produces richer scene encodings than single-task optimization. Key limitations include the requirement for known camera intrinsics and the fact that PDC-Net+ (trained with GT depth correspondences) still outperforms on pose estimation, pointing to depth as a natural next ingredient. Open questions include whether the unified formulation extends beyond stereo pairs to sparse multi-view settings and whether the teacher-forcing training strategy generalizes to other pose-estimation architectures.
