## Problem

Existing full-body motion capture requires either lab-bound optical systems, 6+ expert-level IMU sensors in specialized suits, or VR/AR headsets limited to indoor settings, making 3D motion data acquisition prohibitively expensive and inaccessible to the general public. This paper tackles the challenge of capturing high-quality 3D full-body motion from only two smartwatch IMUs and a head-mounted camera, while operating robustly in expansive indoor and outdoor environments with non-flat terrain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an egocentric RGB video (processed via DROID-SLAM into camera poses and a 3D point cloud) and IMU signals (orientation in angle-axis and acceleration) from two smartwatches on the wrists. It outputs a 3D full-body motion sequence as root translation plus local joint rotations for all J joints in a metric world coordinate, along with per-frame foot contact probabilities. A key design choice is that all inputs are normalized to a sliding temporal window anchored at the current head pose, with absolute head height (relative to a tracked floor level) and the head up-vector appended to preserve global cues that window normalization would otherwise erase.

## Method

The core pipeline has two stages: a multi-stage Transformer regressor (F_est) and a manifold-based motion optimizer (F_opt). F_est is split into F^end (predicts hand/foot end-effector positions as intermediate representations) and F^body (regresses full joint rotations and foot contacts), both using Transformer encoders over sliding windows of head pose, IMU signals, head height, and up-vector. A floor-level tracking algorithm updates the reference ground plane by projecting the most recent predicted foot-contact position onto the SLAM point cloud, enabling correct head-height computation on stairs, hills, and multi-floor buildings. F_opt then refines the initial motion in the latent space of a convolutional autoencoder (motion manifold), minimizing a loss that enforces 2D egocentric keypoint reprojection or 3D third-person pose cues while regularizing toward the initial estimate and penalizing foot slip, thereby resolving lower-body ambiguities without sacrificing temporal naturalness.

## Results

On the TotalCapture dataset, the proposed method (using only 2 smartwatch IMUs + head-mounted camera) achieves r.MPJPE of 3.77 cm, MPJPE of 4.27 cm, and Root PE of 3.25 cm, dramatically outperforming 6-IMU baselines PIP (r.MPJPE 4.40, MPJPE 34.69, Root PE 34.15) and TIP (r.MPJPE 4.88, MPJPE 41.79, Root PE 41.18). The root drift reduction is 87.7% vs PIP and 89.8% vs TIP in MPJPE, and 90.5%/92.1% in Root PE respectively. On the HPS dataset (large-scale scenes), the method achieves MPJPE(r.) of 8.65 cm versus 31.47 cm for AGRoL, a 3.6× improvement, demonstrating robustness in expansive outdoor settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PIP [55] (6 IMUs + physics-based optimization), the proposed method wins on r.MPJPE (3.77 vs 4.40 cm), MPJPE (4.27 vs 34.69 cm), and Root PE (3.25 vs 34.15 cm), while MPJVE is essentially tied (19.56 vs 19.51 cm/s); PIP has lower jitter (0.95 vs 5.23). Against TIP [23] (6 IMUs + Transformer), the proposed method dominates on every metric: r.MPJPE 3.77 vs 4.88, MPJPE 4.27 vs 41.79, Root PE 3.25 vs 41.18, and Jitter 5.23 vs 16.52. Against AGRoL [12] (3× 6DoF VR sensors), the comparison is mixed on AMASS (AGRoL slightly better r.MPJPE at 4.58 vs 5.20, but ours better on MPJVE 17.00 vs 19.31 and Jitter 1.64 vs 2.60), but the proposed method dramatically outperforms on HPS (MPJPE 8.65 vs 31.47 cm), where AGRoL's VR-optimized assumptions break down in large-scale scenes. Overall, the key finding is that integrating head 6DoF poses from a monocular SLAM pipeline into the estimation network eliminates the root drift that plagues IMU-only methods, achieving near-zero drift (3.25 cm Root PE) with only 2 wrist sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-sensor motion capture line (PIP, TIP, TransPose) by replacing six expert-level IMU sensors with two consumer smartwatches, and departs from VR-based upper-body tracking (AGRoL, QuestSim) by not relying on 6DoF position data from specialized hardware. The multi-stage Transformer architecture follows AvatarPoser [22] and TransPose [56], but the key architectural departure is integrating SLAM-derived head 6DoF poses directly into the estimation pipeline rather than using them only for post-hoc root drift correction as in EgoLocate. The motion optimization stage repurposes the convolutional autoencoder motion manifold from Holden et al. [18] to incorporate egocentric and third-person visual cues, a use not previously explored in the IMU capture literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the hardware requirement to two smartwatches and a head-mounted camera, this work makes 3D full-body motion capture accessible to the general public and enables large-scale, long-term, multi-person capture in unconstrained outdoor environments—scenarios previously infeasible without lab-grade equipment. The floor-level tracking algorithm addresses a practical gap (non-flat terrain) that prior sparse-IMU methods ignore. Open questions include robustness to SLAM catastrophic failures, explicit handling of body shape variation (currently trained on mean shape), and whether the multi-person visual cue sharing scales beyond small groups in cluttered scenes.
