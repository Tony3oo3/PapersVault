## Problem

Diffusion-based novel view synthesis methods (e.g., Zero-1-to-3 XL) produce high-quality individual images but suffer from two critical inconsistencies: the rendered content drifts from the input object, and the generated view does not faithfully match the requested camera pose. Existing fixes require costly retraining of 2D models or introduce explicit 3D representations that degrade visual quality due to pose errors. The paper asks whether a pre-trained video diffusion model can serve as a training-free consistency prior without any new training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of an object and a target camera pose (azimuth/elevation) as input, and produces a single novel-view image at that pose. Internally, it constructs a smooth Slerp camera trajectory of F frames from the initial view to the target, denoising all F latent frames jointly. The video diffusion model (ZeroScope) operates with a null text prompt, relying entirely on the view-conditioned model (Zero-1-to-3 XL) for content guidance. Evaluation uses 100 GSO shapes rendered at 25 views (15° elevation, azimuth −45° to 45°) at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

The core idea is to reformulate single-image novel view synthesis as generating a short video of a camera scanning around the object, so that a pre-trained video diffusion model's temporal consistency prior can be exploited without any training. A smooth camera trajectory of F poses is created via Slerp from the input view to the target view, and all F frame latents are initialized from Gaussian noise. At each denoising step, the noise estimate for frame f is a weighted sum: λ_view · ε_view(z^f, x⁰, v^f) + λ_video · ε_video(z^{1:F})^f, combining the view-conditioned denoiser (Zero-1-to-3 XL) with the video denoiser (ZeroScope). The weighting follows a linearly decaying schedule where λ_view = 1 is constant and λ_video decays from 1.0 to 0.5 over the first 50 steps, balancing global structure (early steps) against detail preservation (late steps). This mechanism is analogous to classifier-free guidance but applied across two architecturally different models, each enforcing a different aspect of the desired output (content fidelity vs. temporal/camera consistency).

## Results

On 100 shapes from the Google Scanned Objects dataset (25 views each), ViVid-1-to-3 achieves PSNR 24.05, SSIM 0.917, LPIPS 0.099, FOR₈ 0.178, and FOR₁₆ 0.060, outperforming Zero-1-to-3 XL (PSNR 23.47, SSIM 0.909, LPIPS 0.101, FOR₈ 0.203, FOR₁₆ 0.068) on all five metrics. The proposed optical flow outlier ratio (FOR) metric reveals that the improvement is most pronounced at larger viewing angles, where pose consistency is hardest. Make-It-3D, which builds an explicit 3D representation, scores substantially worse (PSNR 17.13, FOR₈ 0.876), indicating that 3D distillation introduces significant rendering degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViVid-1-to-3 outperforms Zero-1-to-3 XL (its own view-conditioned backbone) on every metric, with the largest relative gain on the proposed FOR₈ metric (0.178 vs. 0.203, a 12% reduction in flow outliers), confirming that the video diffusion prior primarily improves pose and content consistency rather than raw pixel fidelity. Against Make-It-3D, the 3D-distillation baseline, ViVid-1-to-3 wins by a wide margin on all metrics (e.g., PSNR 24.05 vs. 17.13; FOR₈ 0.178 vs. 0.876), demonstrating that explicit 3D representations introduce blurriness and misalignment that degrade 2D rendering quality. The remaining baselines—SyncDreamer, Magic123, One-2-3-45, and DreamGaussian—are compared only qualitatively in Figure 5, where ViVid-1-to-3 is shown to preserve object-specific details (e.g., tail direction, horn shape) more faithfully than all of them. The paper notes that 3D-representation methods (Magic123, One-2-3-45, DreamGaussian, Make-It-3D) tend to produce blurrier outputs due to pose inconsistencies inherited from the 2D diffusion stage, while 2D-only methods (Zero-1-to-3 XL, SyncDreamer) lack the temporal smoothness that the video diffusion prior provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the view-conditioned diffusion model Zero-1-to-3 XL for single-image novel view synthesis and the video diffusion model ZeroScope (itself built on Stable Diffusion), combining them in a training-free denoising pipeline. It departs from two dominant lines of prior work: (1) 2D view-conditioned diffusion methods (SyncDreamer, Consistent123, Zero123++) that require costly retraining or fine-tuning to improve consistency, and (2) 3D-representation-based methods (Magic123, DreamGaussian, Make-It-3D) that distill diffusion priors into NeRF or Gaussian Splatting but inherit pose errors from the 2D stage, leading to blurry renderings. The key conceptual departure is reframing novel view synthesis as a short video generation problem—a camera scanning around the object—so that the temporal coherence prior of a video diffusion model can be exploited without any new training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free combination of two off-the-shelf diffusion models offers a low-cost path to more consistent novel views, and the proposed optical-flow-based metric (FOR_k) addresses a real gap in evaluation where standard PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS are unreliable due to the ill-posed nature of single-view synthesis. However, the method still lacks an explicit 3D representation and can produce multi-view inconsistencies, limiting its use in downstream 3D reconstruction pipelines. A natural open question is whether the improved 2D consistency can be transferred to 3D distillation stages (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) to reduce the blurriness that currently plagues those approaches, which the authors identify as a promising future direction.
