## Problem

Existing synthetic robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-C, ImageNet-9, Stylized-ImageNet) are restricted to narrow variation types—low-level corruptions, simple cut-and-paste backgrounds, or texture-only alterations—and suffer from poor visual fidelity. This paper asks whether diffusion models can serve as a data source for generating more diverse, higher-quality synthetic test images that expose common failure modes across a broad range of vision models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241034/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 113 object categories (overlapping ImageNet and ObjectNet) and 547 nuisance attributes (468 backgrounds, 47 textures, 32 materials from the Broden dataset) as structured text prompts (e.g., 'A backpack in the wheat field') fed to Stable Diffusion 2.1. It produces a curated hard test set of 4,835 synthetic RGB images (3,764 background, 498 texture, 573 material variants) that are validated as single-class, high-fidelity, and functionally recognizable by human annotators, serving as a unified benchmark for both zero-shot classification and VQA evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a hard-sample mining strategy called 'shared perception failure': images are generated exhaustively for all category–nuisance pairs, then only those causing simultaneous misclassification by four surrogate models (CLIP ViT-L/14, CLIP ViT-L/14-336px, CLIP ResNet-50, and vision ResNet-50) are retained. The key insight is that these shared failures are *transferable*—they reliably degrade the accuracy of previously unseen target models (CLIP ViT-B/16, LLaVa, MiniGPT-4), with diminishing returns beyond four surrogates. A human-in-the-loop quality control step via Amazon Mechanical Turk (679 workers, 91.09% agreement) filters out generation artifacts, ensuring each image is valid, single-class, and high-quality. The resulting benchmark reduces accuracy by up to 60% for standard vision models and 17–30% for foundation VQA models, outperforming prior synthetic benchmarks in both difficulty and image fidelity.

## Results

ImageNet-D causes a top-1 accuracy drop of up to 60% across 25 evaluated models. For vision classifiers, ResNet101 drops from 67.66% (ImageNet) to 12.64% (ImageNet-D total, −55.02%), ViT-L/16 drops from 68.67% to 9.27% (−59.40%), and CLIP ViT-B/16 drops from 67.95% to 21.9% (−46.05%). For large vision-language models, LLaVa drops from 79.32% to 49.65% (−29.67%) and MiniGPT-4 drops from 88.77% to 71.96% (−16.81%). ImageNet-D consistently yields the lowest accuracy among all robustness benchmarks tested (ObjectNet, ImageNet-9, Stylized-ImageNet) for every model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImageNet-D is the hardest benchmark across all 14 models in Table 3, outperforming (i.e., yielding lower accuracy than) ObjectNet, ImageNet-9, and Stylized-ImageNet for every vision classifier and CLIP variant. For example, ResNet101 scores 12.64% on ImageNet-D versus 32.34% on ObjectNet and 81.85% on ImageNet-9; ViT-B/16 scores 12.00% versus 34.79% (ObjectNet) and 82.49% (ImageNet-9). For VQA models, ImageNet-D is comparable to or harder than Stylized-ImageNet (e.g., LLaVa: 49.65% vs. 61.94%) but easier than ObjectNet (76.02%), reflecting the different evaluation protocol. On the augmentation front (Table 4), methods that reduce ImageNet-C mCE (AugMix: 81.74→74.70, DeepAugment: 81.74→70.31) fail to improve ImageNet-D accuracy, which actually drops slightly (e.g., AugMix: 10.22→8.75%), demonstrating that ImageNet-D captures a distinct robustness dimension not addressed by corruption-focused augmentation. The paper also shows that pretraining on ImageNet-22K (ConvNeXt-Pretrained) improves ImageNet-D robustness more than architecture changes (ViT→Swin/ConvNeXt), and that training on diffusion-generated 'Synthetic-easy' images boosts ImageNet-D accuracy by 19.26% over baseline ResNet18.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as a generative engine, repurposing it from a training-data augmentation tool into a test-set construction pipeline. It departs from prior synthetic robustness benchmarks—ImageNet-C (low-level corruptions), ImageNet-9 (cut-and-paste backgrounds), and Stylized-ImageNet (AdaIN texture transfer)—by generating high-fidelity images with controllable high-level factors (background, texture, material) via language prompts, and by introducing a 'shared failure mining' strategy that selects only images causing simultaneous errors across multiple surrogate models. The approach also connects to the broader line of work on using diffusion-generated images for perception tasks (e.g., StableRep, DREAM-OOD), but uniquely applies them to adversarial test-set curation rather than training augmentation or outlier detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ImageNet-D exposes a robustness gap that prior benchmarks miss: data augmentation methods (AugMix, DeepAugment, ANT) that substantially reduce ImageNet-C corruption error actually fail to improve—or even worsen—ImageNet-D accuracy, suggesting these benchmarks measure fundamentally different robustness axes. The finding that LLaVa-NeXT, which outperforms LLaVa-1.5 on standard multimodal benchmarks, scores lower on ImageNet-D highlights that current evaluation suites may not capture all failure modes. Open questions include whether the benchmark scales meaningfully as diffusion models improve, whether the 113-category scope is sufficient for broad generalization claims, and whether the shared-failure mining strategy can be automated for future model releases without re-running the full pipeline.
