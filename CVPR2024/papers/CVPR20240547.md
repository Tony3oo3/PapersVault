## Problem

Existing 3D human motion reconstruction methods from monocular videos either regress local pose directly (failing under occlusions and lacking global coherence) or rely on test-time optimization with learned priors (slow, prone to local minima, and requiring manual tuning). Diffusion models have shown promise for motion synthesis from clean inputs but have not been adapted to the reconstruction setting where the model must denoise and infill noisy, partially occluded observations while remaining faithful to image evidence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy, temporally inconsistent SMPL-X motion sequence (144-frame clips) with per-joint visibility masks for both root and body joints, obtained from off-the-shelf per-frame regressors on RGB or RGB-D video. It outputs a complete, smooth motion in consistent global coordinates, decomposed into root trajectory features (2D position, angular rotation, height, global translation, and 6D global orientation) and local body features (21 joint positions relative to pelvis, joint rotations in 6D, 10-dim body shape, and 4 binary foot-contact labels). Velocities are included in the representation for local features but excluded from trajectory to avoid global drift.

## Method

RoHM decomposes reconstruction into two diffusion denoisers: TrajNet (U-Net) for global root trajectory and PoseNet (transformer) for local body pose, each conditioned on the corresponding noisy input and occlusion mask. A TrajControl module, inspired by ControlNet, fine-tunes TrajNet with an additional control signal from denoised local pose via zero-initialized 1D convolutions, preserving the ability to operate on trajectory-only input when clean pose is unavailable. At inference, an iterative scheme (K=2 iterations) alternates between TrajNet and PoseNet, with each model receiving the other's previous-iteration output as conditioning, progressively closing the gap between global and local estimates. Test-time score guidance further refines PoseNet sampling by penalizing foot skating (using predicted contact labels) and enforcing 2D joint reprojection consistency with OpenPose detections. Training on AMASS uses a curriculum that gradually increases Gaussian noise levels and joint occlusion rates, with auxiliary losses on 3D joint position, velocity, and foot skating.

## Results

On AMASS (Occ-L., noise level 3), RoHM achieves 34.8 mm GMPJPE (all joints) and 57.4 mm GMPJPE (occluded joints), reducing occluded-joint error by ≥48% compared to HuMoR (167.9 mm) and by 20% compared to MDM++ (71.9 mm). Foot skating is reduced to 0.078 (vs. 0.230 for HuMoR, a 66% improvement) and foot-ground penetration to 0.69 mm (vs. 2.59 mm for HuMoR). On PROX (RGB-D), RoHM achieves 0.038 skating ratio and 3.36 mm ground penetration, substantially outperforming HuMoR (0.117 skating, 54.76 mm penetration). On EgoBody, RoHM reduces GMPJPE to 314.7 mm and skating to 0.010, compared to HuMoR's 340.3 mm and 0.147 skating.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoHM outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against HuMoR [67], the primary optimization-based competitor, RoHM achieves ≥48% lower GMPJPE on occluded joints and ≥44% lower foot skating on AMASS, while also reducing ground penetration from 54.76 mm to 3.36 mm on PROX (RGB-D) and from 17.44 mm to 0.96 mm on EgoBody. Against the adapted diffusion baseline MDM++ [82], RoHM wins on all accuracy and plausibility metrics (e.g., 34.8 vs. 49.2 mm GMPJPE-all, 0.078 vs. 0.102 skating at noise level 3), though MDM++ is marginally better on ground penetration (0.67 vs. 0.69 mm). Against CLIFF [49] (per-frame regressor), RoHM reduces skating from 0.707 to 0.116 and ground penetration from 61.80 mm to 9.73 mm on PROX RGB. Against LEMO [100] (RGB-D only), RoHM cuts skating from 0.176 to 0.038 and penetration from 34.22 mm to 3.36 mm. Against PhaseMP [72] (RGB only), RoHM achieves lower skating (0.116 vs. 0.180) and dramatically lower ground penetration (9.73 vs. 46.96 mm) on PROX RGB. Notably, RoHM is 30× faster at inference than HuMoR while starting from a more challenging initialization (per-frame regressors vs. VPoser-t optimization).

## Relation to Prior Work

RoHM sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: optimization-based motion reconstruction (HuMoR, PhaseMP, LEMO) that combine learned motion priors with iterative minimization, and diffusion-based motion synthesis (MDM, PriorMDM, BodyDiffusion) that generate motion from text, music, or sparse keypoints. The key departure is repurposing diffusion's iterative denoising process as a data-driven alternative to test-time optimization for *reconstruction* (matching image evidence under noise and occlusion) rather than synthesis, and decomposing the problem into separate global-trajectory and local-pose diffusion models with a ControlNet-inspired conditioning module (TrajControl) to capture cross-space correlations. This contrasts with prior diffusion motion models that either model global and local motion jointly or require noise-free input, and with optimization methods that are slow, prone to local minima, and require manual tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoHM offers a practical 30× speedup over optimization-based SOTA (HuMoR) while achieving substantially better accuracy on occluded joints and physical plausibility metrics, making robust monocular motion reconstruction more accessible for AR/VR and robotics pipelines. The unified framework handles denoising, spatial infilling, and temporal in-betweening without retraining, and the score-guided sampling provides a clean mechanism for incorporating physics and image constraints at test time. Open questions include real-time inference (currently not achievable), incorporation of 3D scene geometry for human-scene interaction, and extension to full-body modeling including hands and facial expressions. The reliance on per-frame regressors for initialization also means upstream errors can propagate, though the diffusion denoising partially mitigates this.
