## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image generation methods (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) require per-concept test-time finetuning through heavy online optimization, making them computationally expensive, storage-demanding, and poorly scalable. Additionally, these finetuning-based approaches often struggle to simultaneously preserve fine-grained identity details and maintain strong language-image alignment when generating diverse poses, viewpoints, and backgrounds.

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of N reference images (N ≥ 1) of a target concept and a text prompt containing a placeholder token (e.g., 'sks') inserted before the class noun (e.g., 'A photo of a sks woman playing guitar'). During training, each single image is preprocessed by cropping the object region, masking the background, and applying random augmentations (rotation up to 45°, horizontal flip, random crop) before resizing to 224×224; at inference, multiple images are background-masked without augmentation. The output is a single generated image at Stable Diffusion's native resolution that preserves the input subject's identity while following the prompt's scene, style, and pose instructions, produced in a single forward pass (~6 seconds) without any per-concept optimization.

## Method

InstantBooth extends Stable Diffusion V1-4 with two image encoders and adapter layers, trained exclusively on text-image pairs without concept-specific paired data. A Concept Encoder (frozen CLIP image encoder + learnable linear projection) maps each input image to a global 768-d embedding that replaces the placeholder token in the CLIP text embedding, while a Patch Encoder extracts 257 local patch tokens per image (concatenated across N images) to capture fine identity details. Gated self-attention adapter layers are inserted between cross-attention and self-attention in every U-Net transformer block, conditioning on the patch embeddings via a learnable scalar gate γ and a fixed weight β. During training, β=1 maximizes identity capture; at inference, β is reduced to 0.3 for balanced sampling, and a concept token renormalization factor α=0.4 rescales the global embedding to prevent it from dominating cross-attention and causing language forgetting. Only the linear projection heads and adapter parameters are updated (learning rates 1e-4 and 1e-6 respectively), keeping the CLIP encoders and U-Net frozen, yielding a 100× speedup over finetuning-based baselines.

## Results

On the PPR10K person category, InstantBooth achieves the best alignment score (0.3140) and face similarity (0.2456) among all compared methods, surpassing DreamBooth (0.3088 / 0.2408) and Textual Inversion (0.2556 / 0.1130). Its reconstruction score (0.7329) is lower than DreamBooth (0.8335) and Textual Inversion (0.7832), but the authors attribute this to their model generating diverse poses and backgrounds rather than copying the input. Critically, InstantBooth requires only ~6 seconds per generation, making it ~100× faster than Textual Inversion (~1500 s) and ~100× faster than DreamBooth (~600 s). In a user study, InstantBooth also leads on all three perceptual metrics (Quality 3.53, Alignment 3.58, Identity 3.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstantBooth outperforms DreamBooth on both alignment (0.3140 vs. 0.3088) and face similarity (0.2456 vs. 0.2408) while being ~100× faster (6 s vs. ~600 s). Against Textual Inversion, the gap is larger: InstantBooth leads by 0.058 in alignment and 0.133 in face similarity, with a ~250× speed advantage. ELITE, the only other zero-shot method, is clearly weaker on all three metrics (alignment 0.2329, face sim 0.1873, recon 0.7666), though it shares the same ~6 s inference time. The sole metric where InstantBooth trails is reconstruction (0.7329 vs. 0.8335 for DreamBooth), which the authors explain reflects their model's ability to vary pose and background rather than a failure in identity preservation—face similarity, which is pose-invariant, confirms InstantBooth's superior identity retention. In the user study, InstantBooth edges out DreamBooth on quality (3.53 vs. 3.50) and alignment (3.58 vs. 3.50) while matching it on identity (3.55 vs. 3.55), and decisively beats Textual Inversion across all three perceptual dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a middle ground between two existing paradigms for personalized text-to-image generation: test-time finetuning methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) that optimize a concept token per subject, and encoder-based zero-shot methods (ELITE, SuTI) that map images to embeddings but struggle with identity or require paired training data. Architecturally, it repurposes GLIGEN's adapter-layer idea to inject patch-level visual features into a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net, while borrowing DreamBooth's placeholder-token mechanism to slot a global CLIP image embedding into the text prompt. The key conceptual departure is that both the global concept embedding and the patch-level adapter conditioning are learned entirely on generic text-image pairs, eliminating any per-concept optimization at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× reduction in per-concept generation time (6 s vs. ~600 s for DreamBooth) makes personalized image generation feasible in interactive or high-throughput settings where per-subject finetuning is prohibitive. The finding that a simple concept-token renormalization (scaling by α) is sufficient to counteract 'language forgetting' caused by a dominant concept embedding is a practical diagnostic tool applicable to any method that injects visual tokens into cross-attention. A notable limitation is that the current adapter structure supports only a single concept per generation, and the model is trained and evaluated on just two categories (person, cat), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to arbitrary object classes or multi-subject scenes without additional training data.
