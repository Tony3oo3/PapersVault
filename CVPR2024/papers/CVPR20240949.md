## Problem

Extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes requires modeling complex point motions from sparse spatiotemporal input, yet existing approaches either store per-timestamp Gaussians (incurring O(tN) memory) or rely on implicit NeRF pipelines with non-real-time rendering. The paper tackles how to achieve real-time dynamic scene rendering at high resolution while maintaining compact storage and high reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes multi-view RGB video frames with known camera poses (view matrix M = [R,T]) and timestamps, initialized with SfM points from the first frame (Neu3D) or 200 randomly selected frames (HyperNeRF). At inference, given a novel camera pose and timestamp, it outputs a rendered image (tested at 800×800 for synthetic and 1352×1014 for real scenes) along with the full set of deformed 3D Gaussians, which enables downstream tasks like 3D tracking and scene composition.

## Method

4D-GS maintains a single canonical set of 3D Gaussians and predicts their per-timestamp deformation via a compact Gaussian deformation field network F. The spatial-temporal structure encoder uses a multi-resolution HexPlane (6 planes at 2 resolutions, inspired by K-Planes) to encode both spatial neighborhood and temporal context of each Gaussian, followed by a tiny MLP that merges the queried voxel features. A multi-head decoder with separate MLPs for position (ΔX), rotation (Δr), and scaling (Δs) then produces the deformed Gaussians G′ = G + ΔG, which are rendered via standard differentiable splatting. Training proceeds in two stages: a 3000-iteration static 3D-GS warm-up to establish stable geometry, followed by joint optimization of Gaussians and the deformation network with L1 + total-variation loss. This design reduces memory from O(tN) to O(N+F) and connects adjacent Gaussians through shared voxel features, yielding more coherent motion than per-Gaussian tracking.

## Results

On the synthetic D-NeRF benchmark (800×800), 4D-GS achieves the best PSNR of 34.05 dB, SSIM of 0.98, and LPIPS of 0.02, while rendering at 82 FPS with only 18 MB storage and 8 minutes training time. On HyperNeRF (960×540), it reaches 25.2 dB PSNR and 0.845 MS-SSIM at 34 FPS, outperforming V4D (24.8 dB, 0.29 FPS) by a wide margin in both quality and speed. On the high-resolution Neu3D dataset (1352×1014), 4D-GS achieves 31.15 dB PSNR and 0.049 LPIPS at 30 FPS, trading a small PSNR gap to Im4D (32.58 dB) for a 6× speed advantage and substantially lower LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the synthetic D-NeRF benchmark, 4D-GS outperforms all baselines on PSNR (34.05 vs. 33.72 for V4D, 32.68 for FFDNeRF) and matches the best SSIM/LPIPS, while rendering at 82 FPS—over 39× faster than V4D (2.08 FPS) and 33× faster than TiNeuVox-B (1.5 FPS)—with 21× less storage than V4D (18 MB vs. 377 MB). 3D-GS achieves 170 FPS but fails on dynamic scenes (23.19 dB PSNR), confirming that static splatting cannot model motion. On HyperNeRF, 4D-GS leads in PSNR (25.2 vs. 24.8 V4D, 24.3 TiNeuVox-B) and MS-SSIM (0.845 vs. 0.842 FFDNeRF) while training in 30 minutes versus 5.5 hours for V4D. On Neu3D, 4D-GS trails Im4D (32.58 dB) and MSTH (32.37 dB) on PSNR by 1.4–1.5 dB, but achieves the best LPIPS (0.049 vs. 0.056 MSTH, 0.208 Im4D) and renders at 30 FPS compared to ~5 FPS for Im4D and 2 FPS for MSTH, making it the fastest high-quality option for free-view rendering in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: the explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting representation with differentiable splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for real-time static rendering, and the decomposed multi-resolution neural voxel encoding (K-Planes, HexPlane) for compact dynamic scene representation. Rather than storing per-frame Gaussians as in Dynamic3DGS (O(tN) memory) or adding a temporal Gaussian dimension as in 4D-GS by Yang et al., it maintains a single canonical set of 3D Gaussians and predicts their per-timestamp deformation via a lightweight spatial-temporal encoder and multi-head MLP decoder. The key architectural departure from prior dynamic NeRF methods is replacing volume rendering with differentiable splatting of deformed Gaussians, which preserves the real-time rendering advantage of 3D-GS while adding temporal dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 82 FPS at 800×800 for dynamic scenes with only 18 MB storage closes a significant practical gap: prior methods offered either high quality (NeRF-based, <3 FPS) or real-time speed (static 3D-GS) but not both for dynamic content. The compact O(N+F) memory model makes long-sequence and multi-scene deployment feasible. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: large motions, missing background points, and imprecise camera poses degrade optimization; the method cannot separate static and dynamic Gaussian components under monocular settings; and scaling to urban-scale scenes requires a more compact deformation field. The modest quality margin over the best NeRF baselines (34.05 vs 33.72 PSNR on synthetic) suggests the representation is not yet quality-leading, but the speed-storage-quality tradeoff is a meaningful step toward practical 4D content creation.
