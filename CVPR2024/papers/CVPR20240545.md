## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D scene generation methods rely on explicit mesh representations and iterative warping-inpainting pipelines, which accumulate geometric and appearance errors over long sequences and fail on large-scale outdoor scenes due to depth estimation noise stretching mesh geometry. These methods also lack an efficient rectification mechanism to correct errors as they propagate, limiting them primarily to indoor settings with constrained camera paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240545/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing the target scene and a user-defined 6-DOF camera trajectory (a sequence of N poses) as input. It produces a tri-planar feature-based NeRF that represents the full 3D scene, from which novel view image sequences can be rendered at arbitrary viewpoints along or beyond the given trajectory. Unlike video-generation baselines that produce a fixed pixel sequence, the output is a persistent 3D representation supporting interactive navigation and re-rendering from any camera pose.

## Method

The core contribution is a progressive generation pipeline built around a tri-planar feature-based NeRF as the unified 3D representation, replacing the mesh used by prior methods. Scene initialization generates an initial image via Stable Diffusion and a depth map via Zoedepth, then constructs a multi-view supporting database using differentiable spatial transformers to prevent overfitting. For extrapolated content, volume rendering produces a coarse view and a 2D feature map (computed via quadrature over the geometric feature decoder), which are jointly fed into a scene-adapted Stable Diffusion model fine-tuned with a T2I-Adapter-style feature adapter that injects the 3D features as conditioning. Refined views are appended to the supporting database, and the NeRF is updated incrementally using sparse ray sampling based on information gain to avoid catastrophic forgetting. A global-local consistency regularization term performs test-time optimization by constraining the diffusion model's output to agree with views produced by local monocular-depth warping, further suppressing residual 3D inconsistencies.

## Results

On the primary text-to-scene benchmark (Table 1), 3D-SceneDreamer achieves the best 3D consistency across all metrics: Depth Error (DE) of 0.13, Camera Error (CE) of 0.176, and SfM rate of 0.89, substantially outperforming the closest mesh-based competitor SceneScape (DE=0.18, CE=0.394, SfM=0.76). In visual quality, it also leads with CLIP Score 29.97, BRISQUE 23.64, NIQE 4.66, and Inception Score 2.62. Against text-to-video methods (Table 2), it achieves the lowest flow-warping error (FE=0.028 vs. 0.032 for GEN-2) and highest IS (2.69 vs. 2.38). Against text-to-panorama methods (Table 3), it attains the best BRISQUE (24.15 vs. 31.54 for MVDiffusion) and NIQE (4.96 vs. 5.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two closest text-to-scene baselines (SceneScape and Text2Room, both mesh-based), 3D-SceneDreamer wins on every reported metric: it reduces CE by 55% (0.176 vs. 0.394) and DE by 28% (0.13 vs. 0.18) over SceneScape, while also improving CLIP Score by ~1.1 points and IS by 0.39. The gap widens dramatically against older warping-inpainting methods (Inf-Zero, 3DP, PixelSynth), where CE is 4–7× lower. Against text-to-video methods (GEN-2, VideoFusion), the method achieves the lowest flow-warping error (0.028 vs. 0.032) and highest Inception Score (2.69 vs. 2.38), confirming that the explicit NeRF representation yields superior temporal and multi-view consistency. Against text-to-panorama methods (MVDiffusion, Text2Light), it leads on all four visual-quality metrics, with a particularly large BRISQUE margin (24.15 vs. 31.54 for MVDiffusion), despite not being specifically designed for panoramic generation. The paper does not report any metric where a baseline outperforms the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-3D scene generation line initiated by Text2Room and SceneScape, which rely on explicit mesh representations and warping-inpainting pipelines. It departs from these by replacing the mesh with a tri-planar feature-based NeRF (building on 3D-GAN's tri-planar architecture and NeRF's volume rendering) as a unified 3D representation, and introduces a generative refinement stage that conditions a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model on rendered 3D features (via a T2I-Adapter-style feature adapter) to correct coarse novel views before they enter the supporting database. Unlike Text2NeRF, which is limited to camera-centric scenes, the progressive incremental training strategy here supports arbitrary 6-DOF camera trajectories over both indoor and outdoor environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical unified pipeline for text-driven 3D scene generation that handles the previously problematic outdoor setting, where mesh-based methods fail due to depth estimation noise causing geometry stretching. The generative refinement mechanism addresses a fundamental limitation of iterative warping-inpainting pipelines—cumulative error—by explicitly injecting global 3D information into the diffusion process. Open questions include scalability to very large or dynamic scenes, the sensitivity to monocular depth estimation quality (still a dependency), and whether the incremental training strategy (inspired by IMAP) degrades over very long trajectories. The requirement for 4 A100 GPUs also limits immediate accessibility.
