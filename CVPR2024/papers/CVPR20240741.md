## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, adapters, prompt tuning) reduce trainable parameters but still require loading the full pre-trained backbone into memory, making them impractical under resource-constrained conditions. No established approach existed for constructing a genuinely smaller student model from a pre-trained Transformer that could be directly fine-tuned without the original large model present.

## Input / Output

The compressed model accepts the same inputs as the original backbone: RGB images at 224×224 for vision-only tasks (classification, segmentation, detection) or image-text pairs for vision-language tasks (retrieval, VQA, entailment, grounding). The architecture is preserved layer-for-layer, so outputs are structurally identical to the original model (class logits, per-pixel masks, box coordinates, answer tokens, similarity scores). The key difference is that every fully-connected weight matrix W ∈ R^{d_in × d_out} is replaced by a product of two smaller matrices U ∈ R^{d_in × d_lr} and V ∈ R^{d_out × d_lr}, reducing both parameter count and FLOPs by the compression ratio κ (typically 2× or 3×).

## Method

PELA introduces an intermediate pre-training stage in which SVD-based low-rank approximation decomposes every weight matrix in a pre-trained ViT into two smaller factors, then trains only the low-rank model while the original is frozen. Two modules guide this training: (1) a Feature Distillation loss that aligns per-layer token features between the low-rank student and the frozen teacher via L2 distance, and (2) a Regularized Weight Perturbation loss that bounds the l∞-norm reconstruction error between the recovered matrix UV^T and the original W within a radius ε. The total objective is L_base + α·L_fd + β·L_rwp, optimized on the same pre-training data (e.g., ImageNet). After this stage, only the compact low-rank model is retained for downstream fine-tuning, eliminating the need to store or load the original large backbone—unlike LoRA or adapters, which still require the full pre-trained weights at inference time.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, PELA achieves 81.0% top-1 accuracy with DeiT-Base (vs. 81.8% original, 0.8% drop), 82.5% with Swin-Base (vs. 83.5%, 1.0% drop), and 83.9% with DeiT-III-Large (vs. 84.9%, 1.0% drop), while reducing parameters by 1/2 to 2/3. On semantic segmentation (ADE20K), PELA achieves 43.2 mIoU (DeiT-Base), 47.2 mIoU (Swin-Base), and 45.6 mIoU (DeiT-III-Large), with only 0.5–1.4 mIoU degradation. On the vision-language ALBEF model, PELA uses only 1/3 of the original parameters (173M vs. 419M) while maintaining within ~1 point on retrieval, SNLI-VE, VQA, and visual grounding tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PELA consistently outperforms all efficient-learning baselines by a large margin: on ImageNet, it beats TinyBERT by 3.0–4.7 points and MaskAlign by 2.8–4.4 points across DeiT, Swin, and DeiT-III-Large, while using comparable or fewer parameters. Against LoRA, PELA wins on both accuracy and model size—for example, on ADE20K segmentation with DeiT-Base, PELA achieves 43.2 mIoU at 78.9M params versus LoRA's 40.6 mIoU at 124.8M params; on Swin-Base object detection, PELA reaches 49.0 AP at 103.0M params versus LoRA's 46.1 AP at 149.0M params. ToMe reduces FLOPs but retains full parameter count and suffers larger accuracy drops (76.4% vs. 81.0% on DeiT). On the ALBEF vision-language model, PELA (173M params) matches or exceeds LoRA (431M params) on most tasks, with the sole exception being retrieval where LoRA edges ahead by ~0.5–1.0 R@1. The key distinction is that PELA eliminates the need to store the original large model entirely, whereas LoRA, adapters, and prompt tuning all require the full pretrained weights at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

PELA carves a distinct niche between two established compression paradigms: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters, prompt tuning) which keeps the full pretrained backbone and trains only small additions, and knowledge distillation (TinyBERT, MaskAlign) which requires designing a separate student architecture. The paper repurposes SVD-based low-rank approximation not as a post-hoc compression step but as the core mechanism during an intermediate pre-training stage, entirely replacing original weight matrices with low-rank factorizations. The two proposed modules—layer-wise feature distillation and l-infinity weight perturbation regularization—borrow from feature-based KD and adversarial robustness literature respectively, but are specifically designed to stabilize the low-rank model's training. This positions PELA as fundamentally different from LoRA, which the paper explicitly contrasts: PELA eliminates the need to store and reload the full pretrained weights at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained deployment: reducing model size by 1/3 to 2/3 while maintaining within ~0.6 points of the original across diverse vision and vision-language tasks is a compelling efficiency-accuracy trade-off. The finding that the compressed model's freed memory enables larger batch sizes that can actually outperform the original baseline (PELA+ on Swin-Base) highlights a secondary benefit often overlooked in compression literature. Open questions include whether per-layer dynamic compression ratios (left as future work) could further optimize the trade-off, and whether the approach extends to non-Transformer architectures or to the increasingly dominant diffusion model paradigm. The requirement for an intermediate pre-training stage, while more efficient than full pre-training, still adds a non-trivial training cost that may limit adoption in fast-iteration settings.
