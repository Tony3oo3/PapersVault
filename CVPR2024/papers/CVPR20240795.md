## Problem

Pretrained vision-language models implicitly learn to associate image regions with words, yet distilling this capability into pixel-level open-vocabulary semantic segmentation remains an open challenge. Naive cross-attention from VLMs produces over-segmented masks, while GradCAM-style sharpening yields under-segmented masks focused only on discriminative parts. Existing solutions require extensive finetuning on image-text pairs or pixel-level validation annotations, limiting practicality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240795/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (divided into P×P patches) and a text prompt formatted as 'A picture of [class 1] [class 2] ... [class K]' containing all K target class names. It produces a continuous-valued salience map per class, which after thresholding, Gaussian blur, and Dense CRF yields a binary segmentation mask per class. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no pixel-level annotations at any stage, including hyperparameter tuning, which uses only class names and a CLIP-based contrastive reward.

## Method

PnP-OVSS is a four-step training-free pipeline: (1) extract text-to-image cross-attention maps from a specific layer and head of a pretrained VLM (e.g., BLIP or BridgeTower); (2) sharpen each class's attention map via GradCAM using gradients from the image-text matching (ITM) loss; (3) apply Salience DropOut, which iteratively zeros out the top 50% of patches by aggregate (class-agnostic) salience for four rounds, forcing the model to attend to less discriminative but still relevant object parts, then sums salience maps across all iterations; (4) refine boundaries with Gaussian blur followed by Dense CRF. The key insight is that Salience DropOut bridges the over-segmentation of raw cross-attention and the under-segmentation of GradCAM by progressively removing the most salient regions. Hyperparameters (cross-attention layer, head, threshold, blur variance) are tuned via random search using a CLIP-based reward that contrasts masked image regions against a black image, eliminating the need for dense validation annotations.

## Results

PnP-OVSS + BLIP_Flickr at 768 resolution achieves 54.1 mIoU on Pascal VOC-20, 42.8 on Pascal Context-59, 31.8 on COCO Object-80, 32.5 on COCO Stuff-171, and 23.5 on ADE20K-150, all without any finetuning or dense-label hyperparameter tuning. Compared to the closest no-finetuning baselines (MaskClip, Reco), it gains +29.4% mIoU on Pascal VOC, +13.2% on Pascal Context, +14.0% on COCO Object, and +11.4% on ADE20K. Against methods that do require finetuning but not image-text pairs (Group 2), it still leads by +13.7% on Pascal VOC and +12.8% on ADE20K. The method also outperforms 6 of 10 image-text-finetuned baselines on Pascal VOC and 8 of 9 on Pascal Context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PnP-OVSS decisively outperforms all no-finetuning baselines (Group 3): it beats MaskClip [89] by +29.4 mIoU on Pascal VOC (54.1 vs 22.1 at 224 res) and Reco [58] by +15.6 on Pascal Context (42.8 vs 27.2). Against finetuned-but-no-image-text baselines (Group 2), PnP-OVSS leads ZeroSeg [8] by +13.9 on Pascal VOC (54.1 vs 40.8) and MaskClip w/ ST [89] by +11.7 on Pascal Context (42.8 vs 31.1). Among image-text-finetuned methods (Group 1), PnP-OVSS + BLIP_Flickr surpasses TCL+PAMR [6,2] on Pascal Context (42.8 vs 30.4) and COCO Stuff (32.5 vs 22.4), and edges out CLIPpy [51] on Pascal VOC (54.1 vs 52.2). However, PACL [46] remains the strongest baseline, achieving 72.3 on Pascal VOC and 50.1 on Pascal Context—substantially above PnP-OVSS's 54.1 and 42.8—thanks to its 224×4 overlapping-patch strategy and extensive finetuning on 30M image-text pairs. On COCO Object, PnP-OVSS (31.8) narrowly beats TCL+PAMR (31.6) and CLIPpy (32.0 is slightly higher), while on ADE20K it leads all Group 1 methods except PACL (23.5 vs 31.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant OVSS paradigm of pooling VLM tokens into single vectors (as in ViL-Seg, SegClip, GroupViT) by instead extracting segmentation directly from text-to-image cross-attention layers, preserving finer-grained spatial correspondence. It builds on the GradCAM-style sharpening of attention maps using ITM-loss gradients introduced in Plug-and-Play VQA [62], but repurposes it for dense segmentation rather than captioning. The key conceptual departure is Salience DropOut, an iterative procedure that addresses the over/under-segmentation trade-off inherent in cross-attention and GradCAM, combined with a CLIP-based contrastive reward that eliminates the need for pixel-level annotations even during hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PnP-OVSS demonstrates that high-quality open-vocabulary segmentation can be extracted from off-the-shelf VLMs with zero additional training and zero dense annotations, a practical advantage for practitioners who lack segmentation labels or compute budgets for fine-tuning. The plug-and-play design (validated on both BLIP and BridgeTower) suggests the approach generalizes across VLM architectures with cross-attention and ITM losses. However, the method remains sensitive to the choice of cross-attention layer and head (20+ mIoU swing), and still trails the strongest fine-tuned baseline (PACL) by a meaningful margin on several datasets, leaving open whether the gap can be closed without some form of adaptation.
