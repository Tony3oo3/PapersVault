## Problem

Building effective motion-language models is hindered by the scarcity of 3D human motion data compared to the vast image-text corpora used to train vision-language models. Existing approaches train motion encoders from scratch on each dataset and cannot handle varying skeleton structures, resulting in poor performance on small-scale datasets and no cross-dataset generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240202/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D motion sequences (skeleton joint positions in xyz coordinates, up to 224 frames) and free-form text descriptions as input. Motion sequences are converted into 16×16 'motion patches' by partitioning joints into five body parts, interpolating each part to 16 sample points, and stacking positions across 16 consecutive frames via a sliding window, yielding 70 patches per sequence. The model outputs 256-dimensional motion and text embeddings in a shared space, from which cosine similarity scores drive retrieval, zero-shot classification, and cross-skeleton transfer.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified 'motion patch' representation that maps skeleton joints of arbitrary structure into 16×16 patches analogous to image patches: joints are grouped into torso, left arm, right arm, left leg, and right leg, ordered by distance from the torso, linearly interpolated to 16 points per part, z-score normalized, and stacked across 16 frames. These patches are fed into a ViT-B/16 encoder initialized with ImageNet-21k pre-trained weights, enabling transfer learning from the image domain to the motion domain. The text encoder is DistilBERT, and the two encoders are trained jointly with a CLIP-style symmetric cross-contrastive loss (temperature 0.07) over B×B similarity matrices. This design decouples the motion encoder from skeleton-specific dimensions, allowing the same model to process SMPL (22-joint) and KIT (21-joint) skeletons without architectural changes.

## Results

On HumanML3D (All protocol), the proposed method achieves R@1=10.80 and R@10=38.02 for text-to-motion retrieval, and R@1=11.25 and R@10=37.40 for motion-to-text retrieval, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art TMR (R@1=8.92, R@10=33.37) by ~2.1 points on R@1. On KIT-ML, the method reaches R@1=14.02 and MedR=10.50 for text-to-motion retrieval, surpassing TMR (R@1=10.05, MedR=14.00). In zero-shot motion classification on BABEL-60, the method achieves 41.33% Top-1 accuracy, matching the supervised 2s-AGCN baseline (41.14%) without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on the primary text-to-motion retrieval task. Against TMR [38] (the prior state-of-the-art), the method gains +1.88 R@1 and +4.65 R@10 on HumanML3D, and +3.97 R@1 and +5.34 R@10 on KIT-ML, with MedR dropping from 25.00 to 19.00. TEMOS [37] and T2M [15] are significantly weaker (R@1 of 2.12 and 1.80 respectively on HumanML3D), as they were not designed for retrieval. On zero-shot BABEL-60 classification, the method (41.33% Top-1) slightly exceeds the supervised 2s-AGCN [46] (41.14%) but falls short on Top-5 (68.97% vs. 73.18%), while clearly surpassing MotionCLIP [50] (40.90% Top-1) and TMR [38] (30.13% Top-1). For cross-skeleton transfer, fine-tuning the HumanML3D model on KIT-ML yields R@1=15.28, exceeding even the model trained solely on KIT-ML (14.02) and TMR (10.05). On multi-person interaction retrieval (InterHuman), the method achieves R@1=9.51 vs. TMR's 5.38, a 77% relative improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style contrastive learning framework to the motion domain by repurposing ViT (pre-trained on ImageNet-21k) as a motion encoder, departing from prior motion-language models (TMR, MotionCLIP) that either train motion encoders from scratch or reduce motion to a single rendered frame. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'motion patches'—a skeleton-agnostic representation that maps 3D joint coordinates into a format structurally identical to image patches, thereby enabling direct transfer of pre-trained ViT weights. This positions the work at the intersection of transfer learning from the image domain and the motion-language modeling literature, filling the gap between the data-rich image-text space and the data-scarce motion-text space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The motion patch representation addresses two persistent bottlenecks in motion-language research: limited data scale and heterogeneous skeleton structures across datasets. The demonstrated cross-skeleton transfer (where fine-tuning a HumanML3D-trained model on KIT-ML outperforms training directly on KIT-ML) suggests a practical pathway to building unified motion-language models from diverse motion capture sources. However, the paper acknowledges that generalization may still be constrained by the fundamental gap between motion-text and image-text data scales, and the method has not yet been applied to text-to-motion generation, which remains the more impactful downstream application.
