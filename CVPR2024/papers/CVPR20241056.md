## Problem

Neural Radiance Fields for Free-Viewpoint Video (FVV) require substantial storage for 4D scene data and often suffer from slow rendering or limited capacity for complex motions and long sequences. Existing compact representations trade off quality on dynamic scenes, while real-time methods like ReRF still need 843–1220 KB/frame, making hour-long FVV impractical for AR/VR devices. The paper targets the joint challenge of achieving order-of-magnitude storage reduction (10–100 KB/frame) while preserving real-time rendering speed and competitive visual quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view video sequences with known camera parameters and produces a compressed FVV representation consisting of four per-frame image sequences (density grid, xy-plane, xz-plane, yz-plane) encoded with HEVC/H.265, plus 16-bit quantized MLP decoder weights. Each frame's representation is a 3D density grid and three 2D feature planes (10 channels each), serialized into single-channel 2D images after 12-bit quantization and empty-space culling. The compressed output supports real-time novel view synthesis at 0.1–0.24 s/frame on a V100 GPU, with a one-hour video fitting in 1–10 GB.

## Method

TeTriRF represents each frame with a hybrid of a 3D density grid and a tri-plane (three orthogonal 2D feature planes), combining the fast point-sampling and empty-space masking of explicit grids with the compactness of planar factorization for appearance features. A grouped multi-frame training scheme jointly optimizes N=20 consecutive frames using intra-group L1 regularization (minimizing frame-to-frame density and feature differences to reduce codec bitrate) and inter-group regularization (initializing each group from the previous group's last frame with a cross-group L1 loss and gradient blocking). A two-pass progressive scaling strategy upscales grid and plane resolutions mid-training, then downscales to eliminate floaters while reusing the same MLP decoder, avoiding the need for a separate coarse stage. Rendering uses a deferred shading model that integrates appearance features along rays and decodes radiance through a shallow MLP with positional encoding of viewing direction. The final compression pipeline normalizes values, quantizes to 12-bit integers, culls empty space via a density threshold, flattens multi-channel planes into 2D single-channel images preserving spatial continuity, and encodes the resulting sequences with HEVC at adjustable CRF.

## Results

On the NHR dataset, TeTriRF (high quality, CRF=20) achieves the best PSNR of 32.57 dB and SSIM of 0.978, outperforming all baselines while using only 85.33 KB/frame — over 14× smaller than ReRF (1220 KB) and 65× smaller than TiNeuVox (5580 KB). In low-quality mode (CRF=33), TeTriRF achieves competitive PSNR (30.42 dB) at just 11.76 KB/frame, a two-order-of-magnitude reduction in storage. Rendering time is 0.1 s/frame on NHR and 0.24 s/frame on DyNeRF, making it 22× faster than TiNeuVox (18.14 s) and 2× faster than ReRF (0.21 s). Training time is 0.55 min/frame, roughly 38× faster than ReRF (21.2 min/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeTriRF (high) achieves the highest PSNR on both NHR (32.57 vs. 31.91 for HumanRF) and ReRF (30.18 vs. 30.33 for ReRF) datasets, while using 14–65× less storage than all baselines. On the DyNeRF dataset, TeTriRF (high) matches NeRFPlayer's PSNR (30.43 vs. 30.29) but renders 15× faster (0.24 s vs. 3.5 s) and is 39× smaller (62.5 KB vs. 2427 KB); however, KPlanes still leads on PSNR (31.38) and LPIPS (0.212 vs. 0.248) on DyNeRF. In low-quality mode, TeTriRF matches or exceeds KPlanes and TiNeuVox on PSNR (30.42 vs. 30.18/30.45 on NHR) at a fraction of the storage (11.76 KB vs. 2986/5580 KB). Training time is the most dramatic advantage: TeTriRF trains in 0.55 min/frame versus 21.2 min for ReRF and 2.4 min for TiNeuVox, a 38× and 4.4× speedup respectively. Rendering speed is competitive with ReRF (0.1 s vs. 0.21 s on NHR) and far ahead of KPlanes (2.2 s), HumanRF (3.95 s), and TiNeuVox (18.14 s). The only clear loss is on DyNeRF quality metrics where KPlanes retains a ~1 dB PSNR and ~0.03 LPIPS advantage, though at 8.7× the storage and 48× the rendering time.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeTriRF extends the planar factorization lineage of K-Planes by unfolding a tri-plane representation along the temporal dimension rather than introducing a separate spatio-temporal plane, and augments it with an explicit 3D density grid borrowed from DVGO-style voxel methods. The deferred shading rendering pipeline is inherited from NeRF baking and MERF, while the grouped multi-frame training with intra/inter-group L1 regularization is a novel contribution designed specifically to produce temporally consistent, low-entropy features amenable to standard video codec compression. The key departure from prior FVV methods (ReRF, NeRFPlayer, MixVoxels) is the hybrid tri-plane + voxel-grid representation combined with an off-the-shelf HEVC compression pipeline, rather than custom compression schemes or tensor decompositions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 10–100 KB per frame with competitive PSNR makes hour-long free-viewpoint video fit within the memory budgets of consumer AR/VR devices, a practical threshold that prior methods (1–5 MB/frame) could not reach. The reliance on standard video codecs (H.265) for compression is notable because it unlocks hardware-accelerated decoding on end-user devices, a significant systems advantage over custom decompression networks. Open questions include generalization to moving-camera sequences (the paper only tests stationary cameras), scalability to even longer sequences beyond the 3000-frame Kpop test, and whether the temporal-consistency regularization can be relaxed for scenes with truly discontinuous motion without degrading compression.
