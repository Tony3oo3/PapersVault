## Problem

Existing occluded human mesh recovery methods rely on conventional backbones and 2D prior knowledge (key-points, segmentation masks) that degrade under severe occlusion, as they over-depend on 2D alignment while ignoring rich structural information in natural images. Although diffusion models encode powerful knowledge of object structure and spatial relationships, prior diffusion-based pose estimation methods use slow iterative denoising and fail to repurpose the pre-trained denoising U-Net's perception capabilities for regression tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 cropped RGB image and pre-detected 2D key-points (with confidence scores from OpenPose) as input. The image is encoded to a 4×32×32 latent code by a frozen VQGAN encoder, and the 2D key-points are converted into Gaussian heatmaps and also projected through an MLP into cross-attention prompt tokens. The output is a full SMPL parameterization (72-D pose, 10-D shape, 3-D camera), from which a 6890-vertex mesh and 3D joint coordinates are derived via differentiable forward kinematics and perspective projection.

## Method

DPMesh repurposes the pre-trained denoising U-Net from Stable Diffusion V1-5 as a single-step feature extraction backbone, bypassing the iterative denoising loop entirely. Two complementary conditions guide the U-Net: (1) 2D heatmaps concatenated channel-wise with the latent image and processed through a ControlNet trainable-copy branch with zero-convolution injection into the U-Net's decoding layers, and (2) 2D joint coordinates elevated by a two-layer MLP to replace text embeddings in all cross-attention blocks. Multi-scale features from three decoding layers are concatenated with their corresponding cross-attention maps to capture both implicit structural priors and explicit occlusion-aware spatial information. A cascade Transformer decoder regresses SMPL parameters, using a VQVAE codebook for pose (predicting discrete indices) and linear layers for shape and camera. A Noisy Key-point Reasoning (NKR) distillation strategy—where a teacher trained on ground-truth heatmaps supervises a student trained on noisy heatmaps via SimCLR and MSE losses—further hardens the framework against 2D detection errors. LoRA (rank 64) fine-tunes only the cross-attention linear layers to preserve the generative prior while adapting to the regression task.

## Results

On the occlusion-specific benchmarks, DPMesh achieves MPJPE of 70.9 mm on 3DPW-OC, 82.2 mm on 3DPW-PC, 97.1 mm on 3DOH, and 79.9 mm on 3DPW-Crowd, outperforming all prior methods by 2.8–12.6 mm over the strongest baseline (JOTR). On the standard 3DPW test split, DPMesh reaches 73.6 mm MPJPE and 47.4 mm PA-MPJPE, surpassing JOTR (76.4 / 48.7) and 3DCrowdNet (81.7 / 51.2). On CMU-Panoptic, DPMesh attains a mean MPJPE of 110.4 mm, improving over JOTR (114.7 mm) and 3DCrowdNet (127.6 mm). All results are reported without fine-tuning on the 3DPW training split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPMesh outperforms all 13 baselines across every reported benchmark. The closest competitor is JOTR [28], which DPMesh beats by 4.8 mm MPJPE on 3DPW-OC (70.9 vs. 75.7), 4.3 mm on 3DPW-PC (82.2 vs. 86.5), 1.6 mm on 3DOH (97.1 vs. 98.7), and 2.5 mm on 3DPW-Crowd (79.9 vs. 82.4). On the standard 3DPW test split, DPMesh improves over JOTR by 2.8 mm MPJPE (73.6 vs. 76.4) and over 3DCrowdNet [7] by 8.1 mm (73.6 vs. 81.7). Earlier methods such as SPIN [25], PyMAF [55], and OCHMR [22] lag substantially, with MPJPE gaps of 15–41 mm on occlusion subsets, confirming that conventional backbones struggle under severe occlusion. On CMU-Panoptic, DPMesh achieves 110.4 mm mean MPJPE, improving over JOTR (114.7) and 3DCrowdNet (127.6), while older multi-person methods (Zanfir et al. [52,53], Jiang et al. [19], REMIPS [11]) remain 20–43 mm worse. Notably, DPMesh achieves these gains without any fine-tuning on the 3DPW training set, indicating strong generalization from the diffusion prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPMesh repurposes the pre-trained denoising U-Net from Stable Diffusion (a text-to-image generative model) as a single-step feature extraction backbone, departing from both conventional perception backbones (ResNet, ViT, Swin) and prior diffusion-based pose estimators that rely on iterative multi-step denoising. The condition injection mechanism directly adapts ControlNet's trainable-copy-and-zero-convolution architecture, but replaces the original element-wise addition with channel concatenation to preserve latent-space integrity. The noisy key-point reasoning module draws on SimCLR-style contrastive distillation to build robustness against 2D detector errors, extending the self-supervised distillation paradigm into the mesh recovery pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structural and spatial knowledge acquired during large-scale text-to-image generative pre-training transfers effectively to a perception regression task, offering a practical alternative to perception-pretrained backbones without requiring task-specific pre-training. The single-step inference design makes the approach computationally tractable compared to iterative diffusion-based pose estimators, though the full U-Net backbone remains heavier than a ResNet or ViT. Open questions include whether the generative prior advantage persists at larger model scales, whether the approach extends to other 3D reconstruction tasks (e.g., object or scene-level), and how the method behaves when the 2D key-point detector fails catastrophically rather than producing mild noise.
