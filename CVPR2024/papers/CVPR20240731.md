## Problem

Assessing how individual training samples influence a pretrained model's behavior typically requires leave-one-out retraining, which is prohibitively expensive at scale. Existing influence-function approximations are unreliable in non-convex settings, while exact retraining methods lack flexibility for diverse evaluation objectives beyond a single influence metric. The paper seeks an efficient, general-purpose framework that can compute training-data attributions for arbitrary model-behavior queries without full retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset D (clustered into K groups via k-means), a pretrained target model M with its full training trajectory {θ_0, …, θ_τ}, and a set of test samples for evaluation. It produces a compact synset S of K×ipc synthetic images (ipc=1 in experiments, so |S| ≪ |D|) that encodes per-cluster data influence, and an attribution matrix W ∈ ℝ^{K×|y_t|} mapping binary perturbation vectors to changes in per-class confidence scores. The synset is designed to be directly releasable for privacy, as reverse-gradient-matched images lack recognizable object patterns.

## Method

DDM operates in two stages. During offline training, the training data is partitioned into K clusters, and a small synset S is optimized via reverse gradient matching: the gradient of the synset at the end of the training trajectory (θ_{τ−t}) is matched to the negative gradient of the corresponding real-data cluster at the beginning (θ_t), i.e., ∇L(θ_{τ−t}, S_κ) = −∇L(θ_t, D_κ). This reversal means fine-tuning the pretrained model with S_κ approximates unlearning cluster D_κ, and the shorter effective trajectory reduces accumulated matching error compared to standard forward gradient matching. During online evaluation, the synset is perturbed by removing selected clusters, the target model is fine-tuned on the perturbed synset for a few steps, and predictions on test samples are collected. The attribution matrix W is then recovered by solving a weighted least-squares problem over |P_t| ≥ K random perturbation patterns, with a hierarchical (class-wise then cluster-wise) distillation strategy to accelerate the search for the most influential data.

## Results

On the influence function task (Dist_1 metric, higher is better), DDM achieves 10.8 on MNIST, 6.8 on CIFAR-10, and 5.3 on CIFAR-100, outperforming all baselines across all three datasets. For model diagnostics (detecting useless training data on MNIST), DDM retains 85.4% accuracy after removing the 20% most-influential clusters, compared to 82.9% for Scaleup and 81.5% for Koh et al. In the unlearning accuracy comparison (Table 4), DDM's synset fine-tuning recovers the full target-network accuracy (e.g., 95.7% on MNIST, 85.0% on CIFAR-10, 56.1% on CIFAR-100), whereas standard gradient matching (DDM-Match) drops to 40.2% on CIFAR-10 and 7.8% on TinyImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDM outperforms all three influence-function baselines on every dataset. On MNIST, DDM (10.8) edges out Scaleup (10.4), Koh et al. (10.0), and FASTIF (9.8) by 0.4–1.0 points. On CIFAR-10, DDM (6.8) leads Scaleup and FASTIF (both 6.5) by 0.3 and Koh et al. (5.6) by 1.2. The gap is most pronounced on CIFAR-100, where DDM (5.3) surpasses Scaleup (3.9) by 1.4, Koh et al. (2.6) by 2.7, and FASTIF (2.4) by 2.9, indicating that the advantage of explicit retraining-based attribution grows with dataset complexity. In the model-diagnostics task (Table 3), DDM also consistently achieves the highest retained accuracy after removing 10–50% of training data, e.g., 85.4% vs. 82.9% (Scaleup) at 20% removal on MNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the dataset distillation paradigm—specifically the gradient matching objective of Zhao et al. (ICLR 2021)—by inverting it into a 'reverse gradient matching' scheme that distills a synset capable of unlearning specific data clusters from a pretrained model, rather than reproducing the full training trajectory. It positions itself at the intersection of influence functions (Koh et al., FASTIF, Scaleup), machine unlearning (Bourtoule et al.), and datamodels (Ilyas et al., ModelPred), but departs from all of these by operating at the parameter level via a compact synthetic set rather than through Hessian-based approximations or thousands of retrained models. The key conceptual shift is that the synset is optimized to match the *reverse* of the training gradient trajectory, so fine-tuning from the final model with the synset approximates the model that would have been trained without the target data cluster.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, one-time-offline-cost solution for training data attribution that avoids the prohibitive retraining cost of leave-one-out evaluation, and the reverse gradient matching yields a side benefit of stronger privacy (synthetic images lack recognizable object patterns). However, the evaluation is limited to image classification on relatively small datasets (MNIST through TinyImageNet) and operates at the cluster level rather than individual samples, leaving open whether the approach scales to NLP, reinforcement learning, or very large datasets. The cluster-level granularity also means the attribution matrix cannot pinpoint a single responsible training point, which limits its utility for fine-grained data curation or GDPR-style individual sample removal.
