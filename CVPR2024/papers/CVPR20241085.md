## Problem

Customized diffusion models (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, SVDiff, Textual Inversion) can be exploited by malicious actors to generate deceptive images from a few publicly posted photos, yet existing adversarial defenses like Anti-DreamBooth and Mist suffer from poor generalization across different T2I model variants and high computational cost. The paper asks whether a more targeted and efficient attack can be designed by exploiting the specific architectural vulnerability of cross-attention layers in latent diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241085/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAAT takes a small set of clean face images (4 per subject at 512×512 resolution) and a text prompt (default: 'a photo of a person') as input, and produces perturbed adversarial images x+δ where the perturbation is bounded by ‖δ‖ ≤ η (default η=0.1, making noise visually imperceptible). The perturbed images are then fed into a customized LDM fine-tuning pipeline, which subsequently generates degraded images that fail to reproduce the target subject's identity. The method requires no prior class images, unlike Anti-DreamBooth which needs 200 reference images.

## Method

CAAT's key insight is that cross-attention layers in LDMs contain the fewest parameters yet undergo the most parameter change during fine-tuning, making them a high-leverage target for adversarial disruption. Unlike standard PGD attacks that search for perturbations on a frozen model, CAAT simultaneously optimizes the W_K and W_V matrices of cross-attention layers (mimicking Custom Diffusion's training) while performing gradient-ascent perturbation search on the input image, creating a co-adaptive loop where the model becomes more robust and the perturbation must adapt accordingly. This dual optimization disrupts the text-to-image mapping learned during subsequent fine-tuning, causing the model to recognize the subject's presence but fail to bind it to the correct identity. The entire attack trains for 250 steps with learning rate 1×10⁻⁵ and step size α=5×10⁻³, completing in approximately 2.5 minutes on an RTX 3090—roughly twice as fast as Anti-DreamBooth and Mist.

## Results

CAAT achieves 12 best and 3 second-best scores out of 16 metrics across four customized diffusion models (Custom Diffusion, DreamBooth, SVDiff, Textual Inversion). On DreamBooth, CAAT reduces Face Similarity to 0.32 and increases FID to 371 (vs. 0.40/307 for Anti-DreamBooth and 0.32/275 for Mist). On Textual Inversion, CAAT drives ImageReward to -1.30 and FID to 396, substantially outperforming both baselines. Training time is approximately 2.5 minutes on an RTX 3090, roughly twice as fast as Anti-DreamBooth (~5.5 min) and Mist (~5 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAAT outperforms both Anti-DreamBooth and Mist on the majority of metrics across all four target models. Against Anti-DreamBooth, CAAT achieves substantially lower Face Similarity on DreamBooth (0.32 vs. 0.40) and Textual Inversion (0.14 vs. 0.15), and much higher FID on DreamBooth (371 vs. 307) and Textual Inversion (396 vs. 378). Against Mist, CAAT dominates on DreamBooth (FID 371 vs. 275, IR -0.14 vs. 0.13) and Textual Inversion (IR -1.30 vs. -0.85, FID 396 vs. 348), while Mist achieves slightly lower FS on SVDiff (0.21 vs. 0.34). CAAT is also approximately twice as fast to train (~2.5 min vs. ~5–5.5 min) and does not require the 200 class images that Anti-DreamBooth needs by default. The only metrics where CAAT does not achieve the best score are SVDiff FS (Mist: 0.21 vs. CAAT: 0.34) and Custom Diffusion FS (Mist: 0.39 vs. CAAT: 0.42), though CAAT still leads on the corresponding FID and IR for those models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAAT builds on the PGD adversarial attack framework (Madry et al.) but departs from prior image-cloaking methods like Anti-DreamBooth and Mist, which add perturbations to images while keeping model parameters frozen. The key conceptual departure is simultaneously updating the cross-attention layer parameters (W_K, W_V) during the perturbation search, inspired by Custom Diffusion's observation that these layers undergo the most parameter change during fine-tuning despite having the fewest parameters. This positions CAAT as a targeted, parameter-aware variant of PGD rather than a purely input-space attack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAAT offers a practical, lightweight tool for users to protect their portrait rights before publishing images, with roughly 2× speedup over existing methods and no requirement for additional class images. Its demonstrated generalization across four distinct customized LDM variants (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, SVDiff, Textual Inversion) and multiple Stable Diffusion versions strengthens its applicability. However, the evaluation is limited to face images from a single dataset (CelebA-HQ subset of 10 subjects), and robustness to real-world distortions (compression, deformation) is only briefly addressed in supplementary material. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-face subjects, whether effective defenses exist against cross-attention-targeted perturbations, and how the attack interacts with emerging safety filters in diffusion models.
