## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods for 3D human pose estimation rely on a single pose augmentor with adversarial training, which cannot simultaneously handle target domains that closely resemble the source and those that deviate significantly from it. The discriminator's enforcement of source-synthesized similarity further constrains exploration of out-of-source distributions, and the pose estimator's optimization never encounters simulated domain shifts, limiting its adaptability at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D pose keypoints (J×2, 16-keypoint model with Hip as origin) from the source domain as input and produces 3D joint coordinates (J×3) in camera coordinates as output. During training, the framework additionally generates weak- and strong-augmented 3D poses, projects them back to 2D via known camera parameters, and feeds all three pose types (source, weak, strong) through the estimator in a meta-optimization loop. The 2D-to-3D lifting backbone (e.g., VPose, SemGCN, PoseFormer, MixSTE) remains structurally unchanged.

## Method

The framework employs two pose augmentors with differential strategies: the weak augmentor enhances similarity across both proximate pairs (OR-BA, BA-BL, BL-RT) and one-state-gap pairs (OR-BL, BA-RT) using a Laplacian-weighted similarity loss, while the strong augmentor enhances similarity only for proximate pairs and actively enlarges dissimilarity for one-state-gap pairs. Differential discrimination uses two WGAN-GP games—source vs. weak-augmented (updating the weak augmentor) and weak-augmented vs. strong-augmented (updating the strong augmentor)—so the strong augmentor is freed from direct source constraints. A two-step meta-optimization then simulates domain shifts: the first step meta-trains on source and meta-tests on weak-augmented poses, while the second step meta-trains on weak-augmented and meta-tests on strong-augmented poses, aligning optimization directions across the three pose types to learn domain-invariant representations.

## Results

On the 3DHP cross-dataset benchmark (Table 1), the dual-augmentor framework achieves 92.9% PCK, 60.7% AUC, and 63.1 mm MPJPE, outperforming the strongest baseline CEE-Net by 3.0% PCK, 2.5% AUC, and 6.6 mm MPJPE. On 3DPW (Table 2), it reaches 73.2 mm PA-MPJPE and 106.6 mm MPJPE, surpassing DH-AUG by 6.1 mm and 6.2 mm respectively. On the full Human3.6M cross-scenario task (Table 3), it achieves 44.4 mm MPJPE and 34.6 mm PA-MPJPE, a 2.9 mm and 2.2 mm improvement over CEE-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across all three evaluation settings. Against the closest competitor CEE-Net [16] (AAAI'23), gains are 3.0% PCK / 2.5% AUC / 6.6 mm MPJPE on 3DHP, 3.6 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW, and 2.9 mm MPJPE / 2.2 mm PA-MPJPE on Human3.6M cross-scenario. Against DH-AUG [11] (ECCV'22), the improvement is 3.4% PCK / 2.8% AUC / 8.1 mm MPJPE on 3DHP and 6.1 mm PA-MPJPE / 6.2 mm MPJPE on 3DPW. PoseAug [37] (TPAMI'23) and PoseGU [7] (CVIU'23) trail by 4.3–6.6 mm MPJPE on 3DHP; PoseGU does not report MPJPE on 3DPW. Non-DG baselines (VPose, EvoSkeleton, RepNet) are 30–40 mm worse in MPJPE on 3DHP, confirming that the DG augmentation strategy is essential. The ablation in Table 8 further shows that either single augmentor alone (WA: 74.5 mm, SA: 71.0 mm MPJPE) underperforms the dual-augmentor combination (63.1 mm) by 8–11 mm, validating the necessity of both weak and strong augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial pose augmentation paradigm established by PoseAug (Zhao et al., TPAMI 2023) and DH-AUG (Huang et al., ECCV 2022) by replacing the single generator-discriminator pair with a dual-augmentor architecture (weak and strong), each governed by differential generation and discrimination strategies. The discrimination structure borrows from WGAN-GP (Gulrajani et al., NeurIPS 2017), while the meta-optimization component draws on the MAML-style domain-shift simulation introduced by Li et al. (AAAI 2018) for domain generalization. The key departure is the use of weak-augmented poses as an intermediary in the discrimination chain, decoupling the strong augmentor from direct source-domain constraints, and the introduction of Laplacian-weighted similarity to capture kinematic structure during augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-augmentor design offers a principled solution to the fundamental uncertainty in DG for 3D HPE—namely, that the target domain's distance from the source is unknown—by simultaneously covering both near-source and far-source target regimes. The consistent improvements across four different 2D predictors and four lifting backbones (Table 5) suggest the framework is backbone-agnostic and practically deployable. However, the main quantitative results rely on ground-truth 2D keypoints and single-frame backbones, leaving open whether the gains persist in fully end-to-end video-based pipelines with noisy 2D inputs. The relatively modest ablation contribution of meta-optimization (2.4 mm on 3DHP) also raises the question of whether simpler multi-domain training schedules could achieve similar benefits.
