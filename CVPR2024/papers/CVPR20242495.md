## Problem

When only a few input views are available, vanilla NeRF overfits to the given views, producing severe artifacts and 2D-plane geometry instead of accurate 3D structure. Prior approaches addressed this through learned scene priors or additional regularization terms, but none questioned the underlying network architecture itself. This paper asks whether the vanilla MLP structure is sufficient for few-shot view synthesis and proposes an orthogonal, structure-level solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242495/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–9 posed RGB images per scene (object-centric 360° scenes on Blender, forward-facing real-world scenes on LLFF and Shiny) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and performs per-scene optimization. It outputs rendered novel views at the same resolution as inputs along with estimated depth maps. Unlike prior-based methods that require a large pre-training dataset, this is a plug-and-play per-scene approach that can be dropped into existing NeRF pipelines without changing inputs or outputs.

## Method

The core contribution is the multi-input MLP (mi-MLP), which feeds the positional-encoded 3D location and viewing direction into every layer of the network rather than only the first, creating shorter input-to-output paths. This causes deeper layers to receive larger gradients while shallower layers receive smaller ones during early training, effectively restricting model capacity to prevent overfitting while preserving full capacity for later detail recovery. The network is split into a Density Branch (lower-frequency positional encoding) and a Color Branch (higher-frequency encoding with cross-branch interaction), reflecting the assumption that geometry is smoother than appearance. Two additional regularizations are introduced: a background loss that samples rays from pixels outside the image boundary to suppress background artifacts in object-centric scenes, and a sampling annealing schedule that linearly increases the number of points sampled along each ray during training to first capture coarse geometry before refining details.

## Results

On the Blender dataset (8 input views), the proposed mi-MLP achieves a PSNR of 24.70, SSIM of 0.885, and LPIPS of 0.087, dramatically outperforming the vanilla NeRF baseline (PSNR 14.73) by nearly 10 dB. On LLFF (3 input views), the method reaches PSNR 19.75 / SSIM 0.614 / LPIPS 0.300, and on the challenging Shiny dataset (3 views with reflections and refraction), it achieves PSNR 18.25 / SSIM 0.475 / LPIPS 0.416. The method attains state-of-the-art results across all three datasets and all view-count settings, with the geometric average metric consistently the best.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is FreeNeRF [50], which the proposed method beats by 0.44 dB PSNR on Blender 8-view (24.70 vs. 24.26) and by 0.12 dB on LLFF 3-view (19.75 vs. 19.63), while also achieving lower LPIPS (0.087 vs. 0.098 on Blender). On the Shiny dataset with complex view-dependent effects, the advantage over FreeNeRF widens to 1.05 dB PSNR (18.25 vs. 17.20), as regularization-based methods like RegNeRF [23] and DietNeRF [12] actually perform worse than vanilla NeRF due to mismatched physical priors. Prior-based methods (MVSNeRF [4], PixelNeRF [51], SRF [7]) suffer from domain gaps and achieve substantially lower scores (e.g., MVSNeRF PSNR 16.45 vs. 18.25 on Shiny). The vanilla NeRF baseline is beaten by a massive margin of ~10 dB PSNR on Blender, confirming that the network-structure change alone (mi-MLP) is the dominant contributor to the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in few-shot NeRF, where prior work (DietNeRF, RegNeRF, InfoNeRF, FreeNeRF, MixNeRF) addresses overfitting through learned priors or additional regularization terms while retaining the vanilla MLP architecture. The authors challenge the assumption that the NeRF MLP structure is sufficient, proposing instead a multi-input MLP (mi-MLP) that feeds positional-encoded inputs into every layer, creating shorter input-to-output paths that naturally suppress early overfitting. The separate modeling of density and colors with different positional-encoding frequencies builds on RegNeRF's observation that geometry is piecewise-smoother than appearance, and the background regularization and sampling annealing are practical extensions of ideas from FreeNeRF and RegNeRF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mi-MLP is a plug-and-play architectural change that is orthogonal to existing regularization strategies, as demonstrated by consistent improvements when combined with FreeNeRF, InfoNeRF, and DietNeRF. This opens a new axis of improvement for NeRF-based pipelines beyond adding losses or priors, with potential applicability to 3D generation and other NeRF-adjacent tasks. Limitations include evaluation on only three standard datasets (Blender, LLFF, Shiny), per-scene optimization (no generalization across scenes), and a theoretical gradient-ratio analysis that relies on loose assumptions about initialization. An open question is whether the per-layer input incorporation generalizes to other implicit-representation architectures (e.g., SIREN, hash-grid encodings) or to dynamic and generative NeRF variants.
