## Problem

Existing event-based VFI methods follow the conventional motion-estimation-and-warping paradigm, making them highly dependent on keyframe quality and susceptible to severe occlusion, missing moving objects, and non-linear motion in real-world scenarios. This paper asks whether directly providing a reliable structural reference for the intermediate frame—reconstructed from event data—can bypass the need for explicit motion estimation and occlusion detection entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242248/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB keyframes (I_0, I_1) and the inter-frame event stream, which is split at the target interpolation timestamp τ into two segments and discretized into 3D voxel grids (B=8 temporal bins) of shape B×H×W. The output is a single interpolated frame I_τ at the specified timestamp. Training uses 256×256 crops from Vimeo90k-Septuplet with synthetic events (ESIM), while evaluation spans resolutions up to 1280×720 (GoPro) and real event-RGB pairs (HS-ERGB, DAVIS240).

## Method

The framework inverts the conventional role of events and keyframes: events are used to reconstruct a structural reference R for the intermediate frame via an event-aware reconstruction strategy (customized erosion/dilation on an event-presence mask to suppress noise in low-confidence regions), while keyframes serve as auxiliary information aligned to this reference. The E-PCD module extends the PCD architecture with bidirectional deformable-convolution alignment, using event voxel grids to explicitly guide which keyframe regions are moving versus static at each pyramid level, and fusing aligned features with the reference at every scale. A Transformer-based decoder then refines the coarse synthesis. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a hybrid LPIPS + L1 loss at each stage, supervised by a pseudo ground truth (masked grayscale GT) for the reconstruction stage to prevent the network from relying on inductive biases from full images.

## Results

On synthetic datasets, the proposed method achieves 39.17 dB PSNR / 0.977 SSIM on Vimeo90k-Triplet, 36.95/0.975 on GoPro (1-frame skip), and 33.04/0.914 on SNU-FILM Hard, outperforming all baselines. On the most challenging SNU-FILM Extreme subset, it reaches 31.46 dB PSNR, surpassing the next-best method (TimeLens) by 2.82 dB. On real-world datasets, it achieves 35.89/0.959 on HQF (1-frame) and 33.56/0.921 on HS-ERGB Far (7-frame), exceeding CBMNet-L by 1.12 dB and 3.09 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against the strongest E-VFI competitor CBMNet-L [21], it gains 1.48 dB on Vimeo90k-Triplet, 0.88 dB on GoPro (1f), and a striking 3.45 dB on SNU-FILM Hard, indicating superior handling of extreme motion. TimeLens [40] and A2OF [45], the other leading E-VFI methods, are surpassed by 2.82 dB on SNU-FILM Extreme, where occlusion and large motion are most severe. Pure motion-based VFI methods (BMBC [30], RIFE [15], UPR-Net [17]) lag by 3–14 dB on GoPro, confirming the advantage of event-guided synthesis over warping. The synthesis-based VFI method VFIT-B [38] and the synthesis-based E-VFI method SuperFast [11] [11] trail by 2–7 dB, suggesting that without an explicit structural reference, synthesis alone struggles with occluded or absent content. On real-world HS-ERGB Far (7-frame skip), the method leads CBMNet-L by 3.09 dB PSNR and 0.051 SSIM, the largest real-data margin, underscoring robustness to complex real-world motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant motion-based E-VFI paradigm (TimeLens, CBMNet, A2OF, TimeReplayer) that estimates optical flow and warps keyframes, instead drawing on the synthesis-based VFI line (FLAVR, VFIT) while inverting the role of events and keyframes: events provide the structural reference for the interpolated frame, and keyframes serve as auxiliary information for completion. The E-PCD module is a direct adaptation of the PCD deformable convolution architecture from EDVR, extended with explicit event-based guidance and multi-level reference fusion. The event-aware reconstruction strategy, using customized erosion/dilation on a binary event mask, is a novel contribution not seen in prior event-based video reconstruction work, which typically relies on U-Net architectures without confidence-aware masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating explicit motion estimation, this work addresses a persistent failure mode in VFI—severe occlusion and missing moving objects—that has resisted improvement through increasingly accurate flow estimation. The conceptual reframing (events as structural reference rather than motion cue) opens a path for future E-VFI methods that may not require bidirectional flow at all. However, the approach is inherently limited to event-camera settings, and its reliance on a U-Net-based reconstruction stage means performance in extremely sparse-event scenes (e.g., static backgrounds with minimal motion) remains a concern, as acknowledged by the authors' own ablation on water/sky regions. Whether the event-based reference paradigm can be extended to longer temporal gaps or combined with learned generative priors for texture synthesis remains an open question.
