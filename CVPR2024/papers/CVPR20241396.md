## Problem

Existing image outpainting methods treat unobserved areas as purely unknown and extend scenes based solely on semantic consistency, ignoring the rich geometric and semantic cues embedded in shadows cast by those unobserved areas. This oversight leads to generated content that misaligns with the global shadowing direction of the scene. The paper is the first to explicitly exploit shadow information from unobserved regions to guide outpainting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241396/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 partial RGB image with one or more unobserved regions and produces a fully outpainted image along with predicted scene layouts (object categories and bounding boxes) for the unobserved instances. It is designed as a plug-in module that augments an existing outpainting pipeline (SGT), so the input is the same partial image the base model receives, and the output is the base model's outpainted image refined by shadow-aware constraints. The intermediate refined instance-level shadow masks and purified shadow feature maps are also produced as byproducts of Stage 1.

## Method

The approach is a three-stage end-to-end pipeline. Stage 1 extracts instance-level shadow regions by combining pretrained shadow detection, instance shadow detection, and shadow removal, then iteratively merges disjoined shadows (via a connectivity encoder that scores pairwise region similarity using illumination features) and splits overlapped shadows (via Overlapping BiLayers) until convergence. Stage 2 concatenates the purified shadow representations of unobserved instances with GNN node features (GTwE) for scene layout expansion and with layout-to-image encoder features (LayoutDiffusion with RePaint-style conditioning) for image generation. Stage 3 introduces two Diffusion-GAN-based discriminators—a local shadow-instance alignment discriminator and a global semantic-shadow consistency discriminator—that enforce visual plausibility of the generated shadows relative to both the new instances and the entire scene, with the full pipeline trained jointly under a weighted sum of the base model loss and the two adversarial losses.

## Results

On Visual Genome, SGT+ (SGT with the shadow plug-in) reduces object rAVG from 10.51 to 8.93 and improves Hit@1/3 from 22.9/45.7 to 27.9/50.4, while on COCO-stuff it reduces object rAVG from 10.54 to 8.83 and improves Hit@1/3 from 26.4/52.3 to 28.4/55.3. For layout prediction, mIoU improves from 22.7 to 28.4 on VG and from 32.6 to 38.1 on COCO-stuff. For image quality, FID decreases from 20.12 to 19.47 on VG and from 19.12 to 18.38 on COCO-stuff, confirming that shadow cues yield both more accurate semantics and more visually coherent outpainting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares exclusively against SGT [69] (Scene Graph Transformer for semantics-guided image outpainting), which serves as both the base pipeline and the sole baseline. SGT+ wins on every reported metric across both datasets: object rAVG drops by ~1.6 on VG and ~1.7 on COCO-stuff, object Hit@1 improves by ~5 points on both, and relationship Hit@1/3 gains 2–3 points. For layout quality, mIoU improves by 5.7 points on VG (22.7→28.4) and 5.5 points on COCO-stuff (32.6→38.1). FID also improves modestly (20.12→19.47 on VG, 19.12→18.38 on COCO-stuff). Because no other outpainting method is included as a baseline, the comparison is limited to demonstrating the plug-in's incremental gain over SGT rather than positioning against the broader outpainting literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: shape-from-shadow (which recovers object geometry from shadows in simple, single-object scenes with known categories) and scene-graph-based image outpainting (which extends images using semantic co-occurrence learned via GNNs). It departs from shape-from-shadow by handling complex multi-instance scenes with unknown semantics, disjoined/overlapped shadows, and semantic contamination from cast planes, and departs from prior outpainting methods (SGT, Boundless, etc.) by introducing shadow representations as an explicit conditioning signal for both scene layout expansion and layout-to-image generation. The pipeline is built as a plug-in on SGT [69], reusing Overlapping BiLayers [25] for shadow decoupling, RePaint-style conditioning [38] for diffusion-based generation, and Diffusion-GAN [65] discriminators for alignment enforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that shadows carry actionable geometric and semantic cues for outpainting that prior methods discard, and the plug-in design makes the approach composable with other outpainting pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to a single base method (SGT) on two datasets, and the reliance on multiple pretrained shadow detection/removal models introduces a fragile dependency chain that may not generalize to scenes with atypical illumination. Open questions include whether the shadow-extraction pipeline scales to dynamic scenes (moving light sources, multiple light sources), whether the approach transfers to 3D or video outpainting, and whether the moderate FID improvements (~3%) justify the added complexity in practice.
