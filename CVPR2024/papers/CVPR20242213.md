## Problem

Traditional CAD-based 3D modeling demands substantial technical expertise, creating a barrier for novice users in the metaverse era. While AR/VR devices enable intuitive 3D sketching, existing sketch-to-3D methods either require precise line drawings (penalizing novice users) or are limited to a single shape category, and the available 3D sketch-shape training data is extremely scarce (~1,000 samples in one category).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242213/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes rudimentary 3D VR sketches drawn by novice users in AR/VR headsets, represented as point clouds, and generates complete high-quality 3D meshes. The 3D shape is internally represented as a signed distance function (SDF) volume produced in a coarse-to-fine manner: first a low-resolution occupancy volume, then a high-resolution sparse SDF volume via an octree-based network, from which the mesh is extracted via Marching Cube. The system supports seven object categories (car, sofa, airplane, bench, display, watercraft, table) with a single unified model.

## Method

Deep3DVRSketch formulates sketch-to-3D as a conditional generation problem using a two-stage coarse-to-fine diffusion model (low-res occupancy → high-res sparse SDF with octree convolutions), trained in three decoupled stages: (1) pre-training the diffusion generator on large 3D shape datasets with CLIP-embedded rendered image conditioning, (2) training a sketch encoder—initialized from the pre-trained Uni3D point encoder—to map 3D sketch point clouds into the CLIP latent space while freezing the generator, and (3) joint fine-tuning of both encoder and generator for improved sketch-shape alignment. An adaptive curriculum learning strategy sequences training samples from easy to hard based on per-point SDF prediction difficulty scores, mitigating the data scarcity challenge. Classifier-free guidance is employed to maintain sample diversity under conditional inputs.

## Results

On the Chair category (single-category setting), Deep3DVRSketch achieves FID 8.77 and CD 0.0220, outperforming the closest 3D-sketch baseline Luo et al. (FID 11.53, CD 0.0305) and dramatically surpassing 2D-sketch methods (Deep3DSketch: FID 108.79, CD 0.1362). On the Car category, the gap widens further: FID 7.92 and CD 0.0047 vs. Luo et al.'s FID 20.78 and CD 0.0303. In the multi-category setting across all seven KO3D+ categories, Deep3DVRSketch achieves FID 15.54 and CD 0.0304, while Luo et al. degrades to FID 30.53 and CD 0.0455. In a user study, participants rated the generated models 4.06/5 for fidelity and 4.03/5 for quality (vs. 2.02 and 1.98 for Luo et al.), and completed modeling tasks over 10× faster (89.4 s vs. 869.2 s with CAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deep3DVRSketch consistently outperforms all four baselines on both FID and CD across every evaluation setting. Against the closest 3D-sketch competitor, Luo et al. [40] (normalizing-flow-based, single-category), the paper achieves roughly 24% lower FID and 28% lower CD on Chair, and a much larger margin on Car (FID 7.92 vs. 20.78; CD 0.0047 vs. 0.0303). Against 2D-sketch methods, the advantage is overwhelming: LAS-Diffusion [68] achieves comparable FID (11.22) but worse CD (0.0501), while Deep3DSketch [9] and Deep3DSketch+ [8] produce severely degraded results (FID > 100, CD > 0.12), confirming that 2D projections lose critical spatial information. In the multi-category setting, Luo et al. suffers mode collapse and degrades substantially (FID 30.53), whereas Deep3DVRSketch maintains FID 15.54 with a single unified model. The user study further confirms the practical advantage: fidelity and quality MOS scores are roughly double those of Luo et al., and end-to-end modeling time is reduced by over 10× compared to conventional CAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the coarse-to-fine SDF diffusion framework of LAS-Diffusion [68] for high-resolution 3D shape generation, and repurposes the Uni3D pre-trained point encoder [70] (aligned to CLIP latent space) as the 3D sketch encoder. It departs from the closest prior work, Luo et al. [40], which uses deterministic normalizing-flow regression to minimize sketch-model distance—a formulation that penalizes novice users' imprecise drawings—by reframing the problem as conditional diffusion generation, decoupling output quality from input precision. The adaptive curriculum learning strategy [30] is applied here for the first time in a conditional 3D generation setting to address data scarcity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of KO3D+ (4,200 sketch-shape pairs across 7 categories) provides the first substantial benchmark for 3D VR sketch-to-shape research, and the demonstrated 10× speedup over CAD positions this as a practical tool for metaverse-era content creation. Key limitations include reliance on sketches drawn over existing ShapeNet models (not fully free-form), coverage of only 7 categories, and the need for a pre-trained CLIP-aligned encoder that constrains the latent space. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly unconstrained freehand 3D sketches, scales to open-vocabulary categories, and maintains quality at higher resolutions or with more complex topologies.
