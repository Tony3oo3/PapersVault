## Problem

Non-autoregressive Transformers (NATs) offer fast parallel image generation but produce noticeably lower quality than diffusion models. The authors argue this gap is not inherent to NATs but stems from suboptimal, heuristic-driven choices in their training and generation strategies (e.g., re-masking schedules, sampling temperatures, mask-ratio distributions). The paper asks whether these strategies can be automatically optimized rather than hand-designed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation (ImageNet-256/512), the input is a single class label; for text-to-image (MS-COCO, CC3M), it is a free-form text prompt. Generation proceeds in the latent VQ-token space of a pre-trained VQGAN (codebook size 1024) over T steps (4–48), after which the final token sequence is decoded to a full-resolution RGB image (256×256 or 512×512). The model architecture is a U-ViT-style Transformer with 46M (small) or 194M (large) parameters.

## Method

AutoNAT formulates the design of all NAT scheduling functions—re-masking ratio r(t), sampling temperature τ₁(t), re-masking temperature τ₂(t), guidance scale s(t), and the training mask-ratio distribution p(r)—as a single hyperparameter optimization problem targeting FID. Because evaluating p(r) requires a full model retraining while evaluating the generation variables only requires inference, the authors propose an alternating optimization algorithm: the generation strategy is updated via finite-difference gradient descent (cheap), and the training strategy is updated via greedy line search over Beta-distribution parameters (expensive). This alternation avoids the bottleneck of slow training evaluations blocking fast generation-strategy updates. The optimized strategies are found once on a small model (ImageNet-256, T=4) and then transferred to larger models and other datasets with only a lightweight re-optimization of the generation strategy.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, AutoNAT-S (46M params) achieves FID 4.30 in just 4 steps (0.2 TFLOPs) and FID 3.52 in 8 steps (0.3 TFLOPs), while AutoNAT-L (194M params) reaches FID 2.68 in 8 steps (0.9 TFLOPs), surpassing DiT-XL (675M, 416 steps, 4.1 TFLOPs, FID 3.13). On ImageNet-512, AutoNAT-L attains FID 3.74 with 1.2 TFLOPs, outperforming DiT-XL (18.0 TFLOPs, FID 3.84) with roughly 15× less compute. On MS-COCO, AutoNAT-S (45M, 8 steps, 0.3 TFLOPs) achieves FID 5.36, beating U-ViT (58M, 50 steps, 2.2 TFLOPs, FID 5.48) with 7× less computation. On CC3M, applying AutoNAT to the pre-trained Muse model improves FID from 7.67 to 6.90 at the same 8-step budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoNAT-S (46M) outperforms MaskGIT (227M, FID 6.18) and Token-Critic (422M, FID 4.69) on ImageNet-256 with far fewer parameters and steps, achieving FID 3.52 in 8 steps at 0.3 TFLOPs versus 0.6 and 1.9 TFLOPs respectively. AutoNAT-L (194M) surpasses the strongest diffusion baselines: it beats DiT-XL (675M, FID 3.13 at 4.1 TFLOPs) with FID 2.68 at 0.9 TFLOPs, and U-ViT-H (501M, FID 2.77 at 4.6 TFLOPs) with FID 2.68 at 0.9 TFLOPs — roughly 5× less compute. On ImageNet-512, AutoNAT-L (FID 3.74, 1.2 TFLOPs) edges out DiT-XL (FID 3.84, 18.0 TFLOPs) with 15× lower cost. On MS-COCO, AutoNAT-S (FID 5.36, 0.3 TFLOPs) beats U-ViT (FID 5.48, 2.2 TFLOPs) and Frido (FID 8.97) with 7× less compute. On CC3M, AutoNAT applied to Muse improves FID from 7.67 to 6.90 at the same 8-step budget, and outperforms Draft-and-Revise (FID 9.65) and LDM-4 (FID 17.01) substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MaskGIT framework for non-autoregressive image generation (parallel masked-token decoding with re-masking) and its extensions (Muse, Token-Critic, MAGE), which all rely on hand-crafted heuristic scheduling functions for re-masking ratio, sampling temperature, and guidance scale. The key departure is reframing the design of these scheduling functions and the training mask-ratio distribution as a unified hyperparameter optimization problem, solved via an alternating algorithm that separates the expensive training-strategy search from the cheaper generation-strategy search. Architecturally, it adopts the U-ViT backbone and VQGAN tokenizer, keeping the model family unchanged while optimizing only the surrounding strategy configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the performance gap between NATs and diffusion models is largely attributable to suboptimal heuristic strategies rather than an inherent architectural limitation, closing much of the FID gap while retaining a ~5× inference speedup. The counterintuitive finding that a fixed mask ratio outperforms the carefully designed heuristic p(r) challenges a widely adopted design principle in the NAT literature. Open questions include whether the optimized strategies transfer to substantially larger models or new tokenizer designs (e.g., MAGVIT-v2), and whether the alternating optimization framework generalizes to other iterative generative paradigms beyond NATs.
