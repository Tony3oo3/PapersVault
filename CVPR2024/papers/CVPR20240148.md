## Problem

Visual foundation models trained on 2D image data exhibit strong semantic generalization, but it remains unclear whether their internal representations actually encode the 3D geometry of scenes or merely capture 2D statistical patterns. Prior benchmarks focus on semantic and localization tasks, leaving 3D awareness—specifically surface geometry encoding and multiview consistency—largely unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240148/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For single-view tasks, a single RGB image is passed through a frozen backbone to extract dense feature maps, which are then decoded by a lightweight DPT-style multiscale probe into per-pixel depth or surface normal predictions. For multiview tasks, pairs of RGB images of the same object or scene are encoded independently, and correspondence is computed directly by matching dense feature vectors across views without any additional training. Evaluation spans both scene-level (NYUv2, Paired ScanNet) and object-level (NAVI, SPair-71k) settings, with object depth normalized to [0,1] to handle scale ambiguity.

## Method

The paper is an empirical probing study rather than a new architecture: it freezes the weights of ten visual foundation models spanning classification, self-supervised, vision-language, generative, and segmentation pretraining, then evaluates what their representations encode. For depth and surface normals, a trainable DPT-style multiscale decoder is trained for 10 epochs on frozen features using AdaBins binned losses and an uncertainty-aware angular loss, respectively. For correspondence, no probe is trained; instead, dense feature maps from two views are matched directly via nearest-neighbor search in feature space, following keypoint-free correspondence pipelines. The key design choice is probing frozen features rather than fine-tuning, isolating what the pretrained representations actually contain from the transferability of their weights. The study further analyzes cross-task performance correlations and the effect of training objective, revealing that self-supervised models (DINOv2) best encode surface geometry while all models fail at large-viewpoint multiview consistency.

## Results

This is an empirical probing study rather than a method paper, so headline numbers are relative rankings rather than absolute scores (full quantitative tables are in Appendix B). For single-view 3D (depth and surface normals), DINOv2 achieves the best performance, with StableDiffusion a close second, while CLIP and MAE produce only coarse prior-based estimates (e.g., 'floor pixels are close'). Cross-task correlation for single-view tasks exceeds 0.82, confirming a shared underlying 3D surface representation. For multiview consistency, all models degrade with larger viewpoint changes, but StableDiffusion and SAM suffer the sharpest drops, whereas DINOv2 and DeiT maintain more stable performance. Correlation between single-view and multiview tasks drops substantially, indicating that encoding surface geometry does not imply 3D view consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the ten evaluated models, discriminative self-supervised models (DINOv2, DINO, iBOT) consistently outperform all other supervision types on both depth and surface normal estimation, with DINOv2 (trained on LVD-142M) clearly ahead of DINO and iBOT (trained on ImageNet-1k). StableDiffusion (text-conditioned generation) is the strongest non-SSL model, approaching DINOv2 on single-view 3D but collapsing on multiview correspondence at large viewpoint changes. CLIP and SigLIP (vision-language models) perform the worst on 3D tasks despite their strong semantic generalization, suggesting language supervision does not encourage geometric encoding. Surprisingly, MiDaS (depth-supervised) and SAM (segmentation-supervised) perform only on par with or worse than classification-trained DeiT III on 3D probing, indicating that dense 2D supervision does not translate to 3D awareness. For multiview consistency, DINOv2 and DeiT III show the most stable performance across viewpoint bins, while StableDiffusion and SAM go from top performers at small viewpoint changes to the worst at large changes, revealing a semantic rather than geometric correspondence mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on probing and benchmarking visual foundation models (Goldblum et al., Walmer et al.) from semantic and localization tasks to 3D geometric understanding. It builds on prior analyses of 3D knowledge in generative models (Bhattad et al., Chen et al., Zhan et al.) but departs by using dense trainable probes (DPT decoder) rather than binary classification or generative-specific techniques, enabling evaluation across a much broader set of model families including SSL, VLM, classification, and segmentation backbones. The multiview consistency axis draws on correspondence estimation literature (Amir et al., Tang et al., Zhang et al.) but reframes it as a test of 3D geometric consistency rather than semantic matching, explicitly distinguishing the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark reveals a critical gap: current visual foundation models encode single-view surface properties (depth, normals) but lack true multiview 3D consistency, with performance collapsing at large viewpoint changes. The finding that semantic correspondence performance is driven by semantic priors rather than geometric understanding challenges a common assumption in the field and suggests that models are learning view-dependent representations rather than 3D-aware ones. Limitations include comparing models trained at different scales and with different data, using minimal probing that may underestimate capacity, and covering only two aspects of 3D understanding. Open questions include whether 3D supervision during pretraining would close the multiview gap, how video generation models fare, and whether the view-consistent-but-not-3D-consistent pattern reflects a fundamental limitation of 2D training data.
