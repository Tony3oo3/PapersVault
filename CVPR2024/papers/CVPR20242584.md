## Problem

Existing semi-supervised domain adaptation (SSDA) methods assume source and target label spaces match exactly (closed-set), while universal domain adaptation (UniDA) assumes all target data is unlabeled and lumps target-private classes into a single 'unknown' category. This paper introduces Universal SSDA (UniSSDA), a practical setting combining partially labeled target data with potentially mismatched label spaces, and identifies a critical failure mode—common-class bias—where models overfit to classes shared across domains at the expense of target-private classes, causing up to 40% misclassification of target-private samples as common classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes source domain images with full labels, a small set of k-shot labeled target samples (k=3 in main experiments), and the remaining unlabeled target images, all at 224×224 resolution. Unlike UniDA, the output provides fine-grained class predictions for target-private classes rather than a single 'unknown' label. Domain-specific classifier masks constrain predictions to only the classes present in each domain, and the supervised auxiliary classifier is discarded after training, leaving only the main h∘f model for inference.

## Method

The core contribution is Prior-Guided Pseudo-Label Refinement (PGPR), which exploits the observation that a supervised classifier trained only on labeled data is less susceptible to common-class bias than a semi-supervised classifier trained with naive pseudo-labels. A second linear classification head h̃ is added on top of the shared feature extractor f, trained exclusively on labeled source and target samples with gradients blocked in f. For each unlabeled target sample, pseudo-labels are refined in two steps: (1) group reweighted refinement, which rescales the semi-supervised classifier's per-class probabilities so that the total probability mass assigned to each class group (source-private, target-private, common) matches the supervised classifier's estimate; and (2) classifier aggregated refinement, which averages the reweighted probabilities with the supervised classifier's output to produce the final pseudo-label. Training combines cross-entropy on labeled data (with AdaMatch-style random logit interpolation for covariate-shift robustness) and a confidence-thresholded pseudo-label loss on unlabeled data with weak/strong augmentation consistency regularization. The strategy is plug-and-play, requiring only the addition of the supervised head and the two refinement steps, and is demonstrated to be effective across CNN backbones (ResNet-34, AlexNet, ResNet-18, Swin-T) and frozen foundation model encoders (DINOv2, CLIP ViT-L/14).

## Results

On Office-Home (ResNet-34), the proposed method achieves 70.7% overall target accuracy across all five UniSSDA settings, outperforming the best SSDA baseline AdaMatch (67.6%) by 3.1% and the supervised baseline S+T (66.7%) by 4.0%. On DomainNet-126, the proposed method reaches 68.6% overall, a 5.7% gain over the next-best method PAC (62.9%). With frozen foundation model encoders on DomainNet-345 and VisDA, the proposed method attains 74.3% and 83.2% overall (DINOv2) and 76.7% and 88.0% overall (CLIP), consistently surpassing UniDA baselines DANCE and UniOT by 1.1–5.4% and 2.7–5.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines across every UniSSDA setting and dataset. Among SSDA methods, AdaMatch is the closest competitor, but the proposed method still leads by 3.1% (Office-Home) and 5.7% (DomainNet-126) overall; CDAC and PAC degrade sharply in open-partial settings (e.g., CDAC drops to 43.7% on DomainNet-126 open-partial vs. 62.5% for the proposed method). Among UniDA methods, UniOT suffers the largest gap, losing 7.2% to the proposed method on DomainNet-345 open-partial (64.3% vs. 71.5% with DINOv2), while DANCE is more competitive but still trails by 1.1–2.7% overall. In the partially-labeled target scenarios (Table 5), the proposed method beats long-tailed SSL (DRw), additional SSDA (ProML), and UniDA (OVANet) baselines by 1.0–7.6% depending on the setting. The PGPR refinement is also shown to be plug-and-play: adding it to CDAC, PAC, or AdaMatch yields up to 7.8% improvement in open-partial settings, confirming that the bias mitigation is orthogonal to the underlying adaptation algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem setting (UniSSDA) at the intersection of Semi-Supervised Domain Adaptation (SSDA) and Universal Domain Adaptation (UniDA), two lines of work that have developed largely in parallel. SSDA methods (AdaMatch, CDAC, PAC) assume closed-set label spaces, while UniDA methods (DANCE, UniOT, OVANet) assume fully unlabeled targets; UniSSDA relaxes both constraints simultaneously. Methodologically, the proposed Prior-Guided Pseudo-Label Refinement (PGPR) builds on AdaMatch's training pipeline (logit interpolation, weak/strong augmentation, consistency regularization) and adds a lightweight supervised classifier head as a prior source for reweighting pseudo-labels, drawing conceptually on the dual-classifier idea from DST but adapted for frozen foundation model encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniSSDA is a practically motivated setting—real deployments often have some target annotations and label space mismatch—and establishing it as a benchmark with a clear failure mode (common-class bias) gives the community a concrete target for future work. The PGPR strategy's plug-and-play nature (demonstrated in Table 7 where it improves CDAC, PAC, and AdaMatch by up to 7.8%) makes it immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the supervised prior head remains effective with very few target labels (k=1), and whether the common-class bias diagnosis generalizes to other forms of distribution shift beyond label space mismatch.
