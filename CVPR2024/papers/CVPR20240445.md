## Problem

Video motion magnification must amplify imperceptible motions without amplifying photography noise, but prior spatial decomposition strategies—representation learning with color-induced disentanglement and multi-domain phase-fluctuation modeling—suffer from incomplete texture-shape separation, complex predefined phase algorithms, and high computational overhead. No prior method exploits the natural frequency spectrum property that low-frequency components carry stable spatial structure (suitable for motion modeling) while high-frequency components carry energetic detail (suitable for appearance preservation) as a principled decomposition axis.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of RGB video frames—a reference frame I(x,0) and a query frame I(x,t), each of size H×W×3—and produces a single magnified frame I_m(x,t) of the same spatial resolution, with a user-specified magnification factor α (e.g., α=20 for static breathing, α=10 for dynamic motion in real-world evaluation; α up to 100 in synthetic tests). Internally, the architecture processes features at three progressively downsampled resolutions (H/2, H/4, H/8) with correspondingly increasing channel widths (C, 2C, 4C), and the final output is reconstructed to full resolution via inter-level PixelShuffle operations.

## Method

FD4MM introduces a Multi-level Isomorphic Architecture that recursively applies an Adaptive Frequency Decoupling Encoder (AFDE) at three scales (shallow, middle, deep), using dilated convolution (rate 2) to extract low-frequency structure and residual subtraction to isolate high-frequency detail at each level, yielding a stable deep-band motion field δ(x,t)=ΔL_d(x,t) and three bands of high-frequency details. Sparse High-pass and Low-pass Filters, built on a Transformer backbone, replace Softmax with ReLU to compute sparse attention matrices—applying a MaxPool high-pass operator to query projections for detail enhancement and an AvgPool low-pass operator for motion structure preservation—thereby suppressing noise-induced degradation before amplification. A Point-wise Nonlinear Magnifier (1×1 convolution with GELU) amplifies the filtered motion field by factor α, and a Sparse Frequency Mixer progressively recouples magnified low-frequency with high-frequency details at each level using low-frequency-guided sparse attention, avoiding ringing artifacts. Training combines a Charbonnier magnification loss, a LoG-based edge loss, and a novel contrastive regularization that pulls magnified frames toward ground-truth VGG-19 features while pushing them away from un-magnified query features in perceptual metric space.

## Results

On the Real-world Test Dataset (12 video scenes, α=20 static / α=10 dynamic), FD4MM achieves an average MANIQA score of 0.6780, outperforming the strongest baseline MDLMM (0.6551) by 2.3 points. On the Synthetic Test Dataset, FD4MM consistently attains the best SSIM and LPIPS across all magnification factors (α ∈ {5,10,20,50,100}) and noise levels (σ ∈ {0.01,0.05,0.1,0.2}). In terms of efficiency, FD4MM requires only 24.9G FLOPs and 82.9 ms inference time at 720×720, reducing FLOPs by 1.63× and speeding up inference by 1.68× compared to MDLMM (65.4G FLOPs, 222.1 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FD4MM outperforms all eight baselines on the average MANIQA score across 12 real-world video scenes. The closest competitor, MDLMM [44] (CVPR'23), achieves 0.6551 vs. FD4MM's 0.6780 — a 2.3-point gap — and FD4MM also leads MDLMM on 11 of 12 individual scenes (losing only on the Bottle scene by 0.0002). Among traditional hand-crafted filters, Anisotropy [46] (0.6535) is the strongest, but FD4MM surpasses it by 2.5 points, with particularly large gains on dynamic scenes like Cattoy (0.6946 vs. 0.6482) and Gunshot (0.6345 vs. 0.6199). In efficiency, FD4MM (24.9G FLOPs, 82.9 ms) is 2.6× faster than MDLMM (65.4G, 222.1 ms), 6.8× faster than LNVMM (562.0G, 198.5 ms), and 3.2× faster than LBVMM (268.6G, 105.7 ms), while using only 1.47M parameters. On the Synthetic Dataset, FD4MM achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS at every tested magnification factor and noise level, confirming consistent superiority over all learning-based baselines [34, 43, 44] and hand-crafted methods [45, 46, 69].

## Relation to Prior Work

FD4MM positions itself as a third paradigm for Video Motion Magnification, departing from both Representation Learning (Oh et al. 2018, Dorkenwald et al. 2020, Singh et al. 2023) which relies on color-translation-induced texture-shape disentanglement, and Multi-domain Learning (Singh et al. 2023, MDLMM) which models motion via phase fluctuations requiring complex Fourier pre-processing. Instead, it repurposes the Fourier spectrum's energy distribution as a principled basis for spatial decomposition: stable low-frequency components model the motion field while high-frequency components preserve spatial details, implemented through a multi-level isomorphic encoder with dilated convolutions. The sparse attention mechanism (ReLU replacing Softmax) draws on recent findings in efficient Transformers (Wortsman et al. 2023, Li et al. 2023) but applies it specifically to noise-sensitive motion and detail filtering, and the contrastive regularization adapts self-supervised contrastive learning to a pixel-level magnification task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-decoupling paradigm offers a cleaner alternative to prior spatial decomposition strategies by avoiding incomplete disentanglement and complex phase mapping, and the 1.63× FLOPs reduction with 1.68× faster inference makes VMM more practical for real-time applications like robotic sonography and remote health monitoring. The sparse attention design and the finding that a 3-level (not deeper) isomorphic architecture is optimal provide useful architectural guidance. Open questions include whether the frequency-decoupling principle generalizes to other subtle-signal enhancement tasks (e.g., audio, sensor data), whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer video sequences, and whether the contrastive regularization can be made more computationally efficient for large-batch training.
