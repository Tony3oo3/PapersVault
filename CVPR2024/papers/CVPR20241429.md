## Problem

Unsupervised keypoint learning methods rely on handcrafted losses (spatial locality, equivariance, reconstruction) and class-specific datasets, making them brittle on unaligned or in-the-wild data and heavily dependent on knowing object locations a priori. The paper asks whether the emergent semantic knowledge in large pretrained text-to-image diffusion models can be repurposed to discover keypoints without any supervision or dataset-specific architecture design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241429/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of class-specific RGB images (e.g., 202K CelebA faces, 11.8K CUB birds, or 30-image CUB subsets) and optimizes a set of text embedding tokens against them; at inference, a single RGB image is passed through the frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 U-Net. The output is a set of K 2D keypoint locations extracted as argmax positions of cross-attention maps from U-Net layers 7–10 (native 16×16 or 32×32, bicubic-upsampled to 128×128), with the optimized text tokens themselves serving as reusable, transferable keypoint descriptors.

## Method

The core mechanism is to optimize text embedding tokens so that the cross-attention maps between those tokens and image features in a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net become tightly localized single-mode Gaussians. Two losses drive optimization: a localization loss that penalizes the L2 distance between each token's attention map and a Gaussian centered at its argmax, and an equivariance loss enforcing invariance under small affine transforms (±15° rotation, ±0.25W translation, 100–120% scale). The softmax in the cross-attention computation naturally enforces mutual exclusivity among tokens, preventing degenerate solutions where all tokens attend to the same region. To stabilize training, losses are applied only to the top-κ most spatially localized tokens (ranked by KL divergence to their Gaussian target), and final keypoints are selected via furthest point sampling over the training set. At test time, attention maps are ensembled across multiple augmented views of the input image, and the query tensor is bicubic-upsampled to 128×128 for finer localization.

## Results

On the challenging unaligned CUB-200-2011 dataset (K=4), the method achieves a normalized ℓ₂ error of 5.4, nearly halving the error of the best prior unsupervised method (Autolink† at 11.3). On the most difficult human pose benchmark, Tai-Chi-HD (K=10), it reaches a cumulative ℓ₂ of 234.89 (relative ℓ₂ = 7.02), outperforming even video-supervised baselines (best supervised: 316.10 / 9.45). On DeepFashion (K=16), it achieves 70% PCK, surpassing the previous best of 66% (Autolink†). On unaligned Human 3.6M (K=16), it obtains a relative ℓ₂ of 5.77, improving over Autolink (7.59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines on unaligned settings: on CUB-all (K=4) it achieves 5.4 vs. 11.3 (Autolink†), 12.1 (GANSeg), and 14.7 (LatentKeypointGAN); on Tai-Chi-HD it reaches 234.89 cumulative ℓ₂ vs. 316.10 (Autolink†) and 337.50 (Autolink), even beating video-supervised methods like Zhang et al. [68] (343.67) and Siarohin et al. [51] (389.78). On DeepFashion it improves PCK from 66% (Autolink†) to 70%, and on unaligned Human 3.6M it reduces relative ℓ₂ from 7.59 (Autolink) to 5.77. On CelebA wild (K=8) it achieves 4.35 vs. 5.24 (Autolink†) and 5.63 (LatentKeypointGAN-tuned). The one setting where it trails is heavily pre-processed/aligned data: on CUB-aligned (K=10) it scores 5.06 vs. 3.51 (Autolink†), and on standard Human 3.6M it reports 4.45 vs. 2.76 (Autolink†), reflecting that alignment priors benefit existing methods in those controlled settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on extracting spatial information from pretrained diffusion model cross-attention maps, building directly on Hedlin et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who optimized attention for a user-specified query point to find correspondences between two images. The key departure is shifting from pairwise image matching to dataset-level keypoint discovery: rather than requiring a user-provided location, the method optimizes text embeddings over a class-specific image set so that cross-attention maps become localized Gaussians, with an equivariance loss preventing degenerate fixed-position solutions. This contrasts with prior unsupervised keypoint methods (Thewlis, Zhang, LatentKeypointGAN, Autolink) that rely on autoencoders, GANs, or handcrafted spatial losses trained from scratch on each dataset, and instead repurposes the emergent semantic knowledge already encoded in Stable Diffusion's cross-attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that large pretrained diffusion models encode rich, transferable spatial-semantic structure that can be extracted for low-level geometry tasks without any architectural modification or retraining, opening a path to keypoint discovery for object categories that lack large annotated datasets. The cross-domain generalization results (bird tokens responding to human body parts, face tokens transferring across species) suggest these attention-based keypoints carry genuine semantic meaning rather than dataset-specific artifacts. However, the method still requires a small set of class-specific training images (30–100), underperforms on heavily aligned data where prior methods exploit strong positional priors, and the reliance on a specific diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) raises questions about robustness across architectures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D keypoints, whether zero-shot keypoint discovery is possible without any training images, and whether the technique generalizes to other frozen generative models.
