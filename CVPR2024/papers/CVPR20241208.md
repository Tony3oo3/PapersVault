## Problem

Existing adversarial example methods for protecting image copyright against diffusion models produce chaotic textures that are difficult to interpret as copyright violations, do not embed traceable ownership information, and require time-consuming per-image optimization against the target model. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether adversarial examples can be generated fast, carry visible personal watermarks for copyright tracing, and transfer across multiple generative models and generation scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241208/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original RGB image and a binary watermark image (black background with white text such as an artist name) as inputs, concatenated along the channel dimension and fed into a conditional generator. The output is a single adversarial example with subtle pixel-level perturbation (bounded by a user-specified threshold, default 10/255) that, when passed to a diffusion model, forces the generated image to display the visible watermark. Training a generator for one personal watermark requires only 5–10 samples in 2–3 minutes, and inference produces one adversarial example in approximately 0.2 seconds.

## Method

The core architecture is a conditional GAN with a generator G (encoder-decoder producing a perturbation conditioned on the watermark), a discriminator D, and a frozen target latent diffusion model θ. Three losses are jointly optimized: (1) an adversarial loss that minimizes the L2 distance between the VAE latent representations ε(x′) and ε(m), operating in LDM latent space to ensure transferability across generation scenarios (image-to-image, textual inversion, DreamBooth, LoRA, Custom Diffusion) and models; (2) a standard GAN loss that compels the adversarial example to remain visually indistinguishable from the original; and (3) a weighted perturbation loss (a soft-hinge variant) that assigns larger weights to watermark-region pixels, suppressing perturbation magnitude where the watermark would otherwise become visible in the adversarial example itself. The key design choice is formulating the attack in the LDM latent space rather than pixel space, which decouples the generator from any specific diffusion model and enables black-box transferability to unseen models such as Dreamshaper 8, NovelAI, and Runway AI.

## Results

Under text-guided image-to-image generation on WikiArt, the proposed method achieves NCC of 0.31 (vs. 0 for AdvDM and 0.09 for Mist) and FID of 245.5 (vs. 228.2 and 205.8), indicating stronger watermark visibility and greater disruption of the diffusion model's ability to imitate the original. The adversarial examples maintain high image quality (PSNR 30.1, SSIM 0.80) while being generated in 0.2 s per image, compared to 32 s (AdvDM) and 35 s (Mist). Under textual inversion on ImageNet, the method reaches NCC 0.40 and FID 412.9, again outperforming both baselines on watermark visibility and generation disruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two adversarial-example-based copyright protection methods: AdvDM [28] and Mist (fuse mode) [27]. On WikiArt image-to-image generation, the proposed method dominates both baselines on every metric: it achieves NCC 0.31 versus 0 (AdvDM) and 0.09 (Mist), meaning the generated images carry a clearly visible watermark where neither baseline does. FID is also highest for the proposed method (245.5 vs. 228.2 and 205.8), confirming stronger disruption of the diffusion model's imitation. On adversarial-example quality, the proposed method achieves the best PSNR (30.1 vs. 29.1 and 29.0) and lowest MSE (0.0037), while generating examples 160× faster than AdvDM (0.2 s vs. 32 s) and 175× faster than Mist (0.2 s vs. 35 s). The same pattern holds under textual inversion on ImageNet, where the proposed method reaches NCC 0.40 and FID 412.9 compared to AdvDM (NCC 0, FID 327.3) and Mist (NCC 0.15, FID 318.4), with slightly better PSNR (27.1 vs. 26.3 and 26.7). In summary, the proposed method wins on all headline metrics—watermark visibility, generation disruption, perturbation subtlety, and inference speed—against both baselines across both evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial-example-based copyright protection line (AdvDM, Mist, Anti-DreamBooth) by repurposing the perturbation to carry a visible personal watermark rather than merely producing chaotic textures. It departs from the iterative-optimization paradigm of those methods by training a conditional GAN generator (inspired by AdvGAN) that produces adversarial examples in 0.2 s, and it borrows the LDM latent-space representation to design a transferable adversarial loss. The weighted perturbation loss is a targeted modification of the soft-hinge loss used in prior adversarial attacks, adding region-specific weighting to keep watermark-region perturbations invisible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical speedup (0.2 s vs. 32–35 s per image) and the low sample requirement (5–10 images, 2–3 min training) make watermark-embedded adversarial protection feasible for individual artists, which prior iterative methods made impractical. However, defenses (JPEG, RS, TVM) measurably degrade watermark visibility (NCC drops from 0.31 to 0.16–0.21), and black-box transferability, while present, yields weaker watermarks than the white-box setting. Open questions include robustness against adaptive defenses, extension to video or 3D generation, and whether the visible-watermark signal survives more aggressive post-processing pipelines used in commercial tools.
