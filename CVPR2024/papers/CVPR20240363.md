## Problem

Existing audio-visual deepfake detection methods either rely on supervised contrastive learning that only captures dissonance within the training corpus, or use uni-modal cues that ignore audio-visual correspondences entirely. This limits generalization to unseen deepfake generators, as the model may overlook subtle audio-visual correspondences that are inherently difficult for generative models to replicate faithfully.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240363/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes video frames sampled at 5 fps (with facial regions cropped and background removed via FaceX-Zoo) and audio waveforms at 16 kHz converted to log-mel spectrograms. These are tokenized into 16×16 2D patches (audio) and 2×16×16 3D spatio-temporal patches (visual), then segmented into 8 equal temporal slices that preserve cross-modal temporal alignment. At inference, the video is split into overlapping blocks (step size T/8) and the final real/fake decision is made by averaging output logits across all blocks.

## Method

AVFF is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 performs self-supervised representation learning on real talking-face videos (LRS3) using a dual contrastive + autoencoding objective, where the key novelty is a complementary masking strategy: 50% of temporal slices are masked in each modality such that masked audio slices correspond to visible visual slices and vice versa. Instead of MAE-style shared learnable mask tokens, the masked slices are replaced with cross-modal predictions generated by A2V and V2A networks (single-layer MLP + transformer block) that convert visible tokens from one modality into the other's embedding space, explicitly enforcing audio-visual correspondence. Stage 2 trains a supervised classifier on FakeAVCeleb by concatenating the unimodal encoder outputs with the cross-modal network outputs for each modality, reducing them via uni-modal patch reduction networks, and feeding the combined embedding into a classifier head with cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On the FakeAVCeleb dataset (70/30 train-test split), AVFF achieves 98.6% accuracy and 99.1% AUC, outperforming the best audio-visual baseline AVoiD-DF (83.7% ACC, 89.2% AUC) by 14.9% in accuracy and 9.9% in AUC. Against the strongest visual-only baseline RealForensics (89.9% ACC, 94.6% AUC), AVFF gains 8.7% in accuracy and 4.5% in AUC. For cross-manipulation generalization, AVFF maintains AUC > 92% and AP > 93% across all five manipulation categories, while on cross-dataset evaluation (KoDF) it reaches 95.5% AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVFF substantially outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary FakeAVCeleb intra-dataset evaluation, with the largest margin over the audio-visual state-of-the-art AVoiD-DF (+14.9% ACC, +9.9% AUC) and the visual-only RealForensics (+8.7% ACC, +4.5% AUC). Among visual-only baselines, RealForensics is the strongest (89.9% ACC) but discards audio at inference, limiting it to visual deepfakes; all other visual methods (Xception, LipForensics, FTCN, CViT) fall well below 90% ACC. Among audio-visual baselines, AVoiD-DF leads (83.7% ACC) but is still 14.9% behind AVFF; MDS, VFD, AVFakeNet, and Emotions Don't Lie all score below 84% ACC. For cross-manipulation generalization (Table 2), AVFF achieves the best or near-best AP/AUC in every category (e.g., 99.9–100% AUC on FVFA-FS and FVFA-GAN), whereas baselines like AV-DFD drop to 55.4% AUC on FVFA-GAN and Xception to 68.5%. On cross-dataset generalization to KoDF (Table 3), AVFF (95.5% AUC) slightly trails RealForensics (93.6% AUC, 95.7% AP) on AP but leads on AUC, and clearly outperforms all other baselines (next best: AVAD at 86.9% AUC).

## Relation to Prior Work

AVFF builds directly on the dual contrastive-autoencoding paradigm of CAV-MAE and the masked-token reconstruction framework of MAE/MARLIN, but departs from them in two key ways: (1) it replaces the shared learnable mask tokens with cross-modal predictions (A2V/V2A networks) that explicitly force each modality to reconstruct the other's masked content, and (2) it introduces a complementary masking strategy where masked slices in one modality are precisely the visible slices in the other, creating a strict temporal correspondence constraint. In the deepfake detection literature, prior audio-visual methods (AVoiD-DF, AV-DFD, MDS) rely on supervised contrastive alignment or single-stage classification on labeled deepfake data; AVFF instead pre-trives representations exclusively on real videos via self-supervision, treating deepfake detection as a downstream task that exploits the learned correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.9% accuracy gap over prior audio-visual SOTA on FakeAVCeleb, combined with consistently high cross-manipulation generalization (AUC > 92% across all unseen manipulation categories), suggests that explicitly modeling audio-visual correspondences through self-supervised pre-training is a substantially more effective detection strategy than supervised contrastive alignment on deepfake data. The t-SNE analysis showing clear real/fake separation without any deepfake exposure during Stage 1 is a strong conceptual validation. However, the method is limited to talking-head videos with both modalities present, and struggles with asynchronous audio, multiple speakers, or occluded faces. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-humanoid or diffusion-based deepfakes, and whether the complementary masking strategy can be extended to other multi-modal tasks beyond forensics.
