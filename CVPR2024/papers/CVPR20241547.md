## Problem

Selfies are inherently limited: they capture only the upper body, distort facial geometry due to close-range perspective, and make it difficult to compose the subject within a scene. Existing human reposing and virtual try-on methods assume third-person-view input and cannot handle the multi-view, close-up nature of selfies. This paper introduces the 'total selfie' — a full-body image as if someone else photographed you from a few feet away — and provides the first method to generate one from arm's-length selfies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four close-up selfies (face, upper body, lower body, shoes), a background image, and a reference photo from the user's collection (auto-selected by clothing-type similarity via human parsing) as input. The reference photo is not used directly; instead, a Canny Edge contour is extracted from its semantic segmentation to serve as the pose control signal, and a scaled bounding box defines the inpainting mask. The output is a single full-body RGB image composited into the background with the target pose, preserving the user's identity, expression, and clothing.

## Method

The core is a diffusion-based inpainting model adapted from Paint-By-Example, where the text-conditioning pathway is replaced with concatenated CLIP image embeddings of the four selfies, forcing the network to learn clothing and identity from compressed vectors rather than spatial warping. The model is trained self-supervised on a synthetic paired dataset where full-body images are generated by Stable Diffusion and selfies are simulated via homography warping of OpenPose keypoints. At test time, a per-capture fine-tuning step (200 augmented images placing real selfies into the masked background) bridges the synthetic-to-real domain gap, preserving clothing texture and generating pose-specific details like wrinkles. A modified ControlNet (Canny Edge variant) provides pose guidance by replacing text embeddings with the same four-selfie conditioning vector. Post-hoc appearance refinement uses DreamBooth-trained models with SDEdit to recover face and shoe details that the VAE decoder cannot resolve at full-body scale.

## Results

Evaluated on 17 captures from 12 people with real ground-truth full-body photos, Total Selfie achieves the best scores on all four metrics: LPIPS 0.187, SSIM 0.708, PSNR 17.01 dB, and FID 139.4 (all evaluated on the foreground region). The closest baseline, DreamBooth, scores 0.224 LPIPS and 159.2 FID, while the weakest baseline, DisCo, reaches only 0.287 LPIPS and 245.7 FID. Ablation variants (removing fine-tuning, appearance refinement, or face undistortion) show consistent but smaller degradations, confirming each component's contribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Total Selfie outperforms all four baselines on every metric. DisCo performs worst (LPIPS 0.287, FID 245.7) because it relies on OpenPose skeleton guidance and assumes third-person-view input, making it poorly suited for selfie inputs. Paint-By-Example (LPIPS 0.252, FID 184.6) is also ineffective since it was designed for single-image exemplar inpainting rather than full-body generation, and can only condition on one reference at a time. LaDI-VTON (LPIPS 0.263, FID 172.8) produces visible clothing artifacts, likely because its training data does not include selfie-style garment references. DreamBooth is the strongest baseline (LPIPS 0.224, FID 159.2) but still generates inaccurate outfits because the pretrained text-to-image model lacks strong priors for linking separate selfie crops into a coherent full-body image. Total Selfie's per-capture fine-tuning and appearance refinement close this gap, yielding a 16.6% FID improvement over DreamBooth and a 43.2% FID improvement over DisCo.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the exemplar-based inpainting framework of Paint-By-Example [60] to a multi-image conditioning setting, extending its single-reference CLIP embedding to concatenate embeddings from four body-part selfies. For pose control, it repurposes ControlNet [65] by replacing text embeddings with the multi-image condition, and for fine-grained detail recovery it borrows DreamBooth [46] and SDEdit [35] as post-processing steps. The key conceptual departure from prior reposing [53], virtual try-on [36], and inpainting work is that all of those assume a third-person-view or full-body input, whereas this paper's input consists solely of close-range selfies with limited field of view and perspective distortion, requiring a synthetic training pipeline (homography-based selfie simulation) and per-capture fine-tuning to bridge the domain gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a practically relevant task—generating a full-body, third-person-view photo from only phone selfies—that has no direct prior solution, and demonstrates a workable pipeline for it. However, the method relies on per-capture fine-tuning (200 augmented images) and multiple post-processing stages, raising questions about computational cost and generalizability. Open limitations include inability to generate hard shadows under strong sunlight, imprecise body-shape and scale inference from close-up inputs, and sensitivity to clothing mismatch between the reference pose photo and the input selfies. Future work on inferring 3D body shape directly from selfies and automatically selecting optimal target poses would strengthen the approach.
