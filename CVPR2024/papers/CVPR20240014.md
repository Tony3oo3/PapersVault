## Problem

Existing self-supervised facial landmark methods rely on instance-level SSL (MOCO, BYOL) that neglects the dense prediction nature of landmark tasks, aggregate features into memory-intensive hypercolumns, and naively supervise correspondences among all spatial feature pairs. This paper asks whether a region-level SSL backbone (MAE) and selective refinement of only salient correspondences can yield superior landmark representations without labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240014/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB face images resized to 136×136 with a center 96×96 crop, processed by a ViT/DeiT backbone with patch size 8. For landmark detection, it outputs 2D coordinates via a lightweight regressor trained on concatenated first- and second-stage features; for landmark matching, it produces per-patch feature representations and a cosine-similarity map between a reference and test image. At inference, the cover-and-stride technique spatially expands the feature map to match the resolution expected by prior work without quadratically increasing computation.

## Method

SCE-MAE is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 pretrains a DeiT backbone with MAE (masked image modeling), which naturally forms more locally distinct features for sparse landmark regions than instance-level contrastive SSL. Stage 2 introduces the Correspondence Approximation and Refinement Block (CARB): patch tokens are split into attentive (landmark) and inattentive (non-landmark) groups via CLS-token similarity, inattentive tokens are compressed into K_c=4 cluster centers using density peak clustering, and a lightweight projector is trained with a novel Locality-Constrained Repellence (LCR) Loss that penalizes erroneous strong correspondences weighted by spatial proximity (locality) and correspondence type (repellence coefficients for att-att, att-inatt, inatt-inatt pairs). By operating only on attentive tokens and inattentive cluster-center proxies rather than all feature pairs, the method achieves selective correspondence without expensive hypercolumns, directly honing only the correspondences critical for landmark disambiguation.

## Results

On landmark detection (measured as % inter-ocular distance error), SCE-MAE with DeiT-B achieves 2.07% on MAFL, 5.23% on AFLW-M, 5.33% on AFLW-R (corrected), and 3.95% on 300W, outperforming the best prior method (LEAD) by 15.2%, 13.6%, 6.6%, and 18.9% respectively. On landmark matching (Mean Pixel Error on MAFL), SCE-MAE DeiT-B achieves 0.27 (same identity) and 1.61 (different identity), surpassing LEAD (0.48 / 2.50) by 44% and 36% respectively. Notably, even the smallest backbone (DeiT-T, 5.4M params) outperforms all prior methods while using a fraction of the parameters and avoiding memory-intensive hypercolumns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCE-MAE outperforms all three baselines (DVE, CL, LEAD) on every dataset and both tasks. On landmark detection, the largest gains are on 300W (18.9% over LEAD, 18.4% over CL) and MAFL (15.2% over LEAD), while gains on AFLW-R are more modest (~6–7%). On landmark matching, improvements are more dramatic: SCE-MAE DeiT-B reduces Mean Pixel Error from 2.50 to 1.61 (different identity, 36% over LEAD) and from 0.48 to 0.27 (same identity, 44% over LEAD). A key architectural distinction is that CL and LEAD rely on 3840-dim hypercolumns (memory-intensive), whereas SCE-MAE operates on vanilla 256–1024-dim feature maps without hypercolumns, yet still achieves superior accuracy. Under limited annotation (1–100 samples on AFLW-M), SCE-MAE also leads by 8.6% on average and up to 20.1% over LEAD, with notably lower standard deviation across repeated runs, indicating greater robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm for self-supervised landmark estimation (CL, LEAD) in two key ways: it replaces instance-level SSL backbones (MOCO, BYOL) with a region-level Masked Autoencoder, arguing that MIM's reconstruction objective more naturally produces locally distinct features for dense prediction tasks. In the second stage, it abandons the memory-intensive hypercolumn constructions used by CL and LEAD in favor of operating directly on vanilla MAE patch tokens, and introduces the CARB block that uses density-peak clustering to approximate non-landmark regions and a Locality-Constrained Repellence Loss to refine only salient correspondences rather than all pairwise feature relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a region-level MIM backbone alone (without any second-stage refinement) already outperforms the full CL and LEAD pipelines (Table 4) suggests that the choice of pretraining paradigm may matter more than the correspondence-learning objective for dense landmark tasks, a point that could reshape how future self-supervised dense-prediction methods are designed. The selective correspondence idea—systematically discarding unimportant token pairs before optimization—offers a general principle for reducing redundant computation in correspondence-based methods. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes beyond the single-category (face) setting, whether the density-peak clustering step is truly necessary or whether simpler thresholding suffices, and whether the cover-and-stride inference trick can be replaced by native multi-scale architectures.
