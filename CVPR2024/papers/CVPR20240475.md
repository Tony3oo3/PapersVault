## Problem

3D visual grounding models require dense, manually annotated text descriptions for every object, a process that is prohibitively expensive (e.g., ScanRefer took ~4,984 man-hours). While language-free training paradigms have been explored in 2D via pseudo-text generation or CLIP-based feature substitution, direct transfer to 3D is hindered by the absence of a 3D visual-language pre-trained model comparable to CLIP and by the greater complexity of spatial relations among 3D objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240475/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes a point cloud (N points with 3D coordinates plus F-dimensional auxiliary features) and corresponding multi-view RGB images from ScanNet, with no text at all. At inference, the input shifts to a point cloud plus a natural-language sentence query, and the model outputs a single 3D bounding box (center c and size s) for the referred object. The multi-view images are processed through a pre-trained 2D mask generator to obtain class-agnostic segmentation masks, which are then projected into 3D space using camera intrinsics and extrinsics to recover spatial relations.

## Method

The core idea is to use CLIP's shared image-text embedding space as a bridge: multi-view images are encoded by the CLIP visual encoder to produce pseudo-language features that substitute for real text during training. The Pseudo-Language Feature Generation (PFG) module extracts local features (cropped mask regions through CLIP) and global features (full image with mask-weighted intermediate CLIP layers), blends them, and adds random noise to better mimic linguistic variability. To capture the spatial relations that textual descriptions typically encode, the Neighboring Relation-aware Modeling (NRM) module projects 2D masks into 3D, identifies k nearest neighbors, and derives 2D relational features via cross-attention, supervised by a proxy object-category classification loss. The Cross-modality Relation Consistency (CRC) module computes analogous 3D proposal relation features and enforces alignment between 2D and 3D relation embeddings through a contrastive consistency loss. The full pipeline is built on a PointNet++ backbone with 3DVG-Transformer's voting-and-detection module for proposal generation, and at inference the same CLIP text encoder processes the query sentence (split into local noun and global sentence features) to ground the target object.

## Results

On ScanRefer, 3DLFVG achieves 30.53% Acc@0.25 and 23.61% Acc@0.5 overall, outperforming the strongest language-free baselines (Pseudo-Q: 26.90/16.96; Zero-shot-RIS: 26.67/20.02) by 3.6 and 3.6 points respectively. On Nr3D, the method reaches 18.28% Acc@0.25 and 13.32% Acc@0.5 overall, surpassing Zero-shot-RIS (16.99/11.89) and Pseudo-Q (14.23/8.07). On Sr3D, 3DLFVG attains 19.25% Acc@0.25 and 14.99% Acc@0.5, again leading all language-free baselines. Notably, the gap is largest on the 'Multiple' subset of ScanRefer (22.03 vs. 19.49 for Pseudo-Q), reflecting the benefit of the relation injection modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DLFVG outperforms all language-free baselines across all three datasets. On ScanRefer, it beats Pseudo-Q by 3.63 points (Acc@0.25) and 6.65 points (Acc@0.5) overall, and surpasses Zero-shot-RIS by 3.86 and 3.59 points respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on the 'Multiple' subset (22.03 vs. 19.49 for Pseudo-Q; 22.03 vs. 18.34 for Zero-shot-RIS at Acc@0.25), confirming the effectiveness of the NRM and CRC relation modules. On Nr3D, 3DLFVG leads Zero-shot-RIS by 1.29 (Acc@0.25) and 1.43 (Acc@0.5) overall, and Pseudo-Q by 4.05 and 5.25 points. On Sr3D, the margins are 1.82 (Acc@0.25) and 1.52 (Acc@0.5) over Zero-shot-RIS, and 7.51 and 7.77 over Pseudo-Q. The method still trails fully-supervised 3DVG-Transformer (49.36/36.43) by a substantial margin, but narrows the gap considerably compared to prior language-free approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the language-free training paradigm—previously explored in 2D visual grounding via explicit pseudo-text generation (Pseudo-Q) or implicit CLIP feature substitution (Zero-shot RIS)—to the 3D point cloud domain. It builds on 3DVG-Transformer for proposal generation and on CLIP's image-text alignment space as the core mechanism for producing pseudo-language features from multi-view images. The key departure from prior 2D language-free work is the introduction of a relation injection strategy (NRM + CRC) that explicitly models 2D-3D spatial relational consistency, addressing the fact that 3D descriptions encode far richer positional and relational information than their 2D counterparts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: ScanRefer required ~4,984 man-hours of annotation, and eliminating text supervision during training could dramatically lower the barrier to building 3D grounding systems. However, the performance gap to fully-supervised methods remains substantial (30.53 vs. 49.36 Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer), suggesting that CLIP's 2D-pretrained alignment is an imperfect proxy for true 3D-language understanding. Open questions include whether 3D-specific pre-trained vision-language models (which are currently absent) could close this gap, whether the relation injection strategy generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether the approach can be combined with emerging 3D foundation models.
