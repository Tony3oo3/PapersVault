## Problem

DCNv3, the core sparse operator in InternImage, suffers from two critical limitations: its CUDA kernel is memory-bound with redundant accesses that make it slower than optimized dense attention, and its softmax-normalized aggregation weights (bounded to 0–1) constrain expressive power and slow convergence. This is counter-intuitive given DCNv3's ConvNet inductive bias, which should yield faster convergence than global attention.

## Input / Output

DCNv4 operates on feature maps of shape (H, W, C) with a 3×3 sparse sampling window, producing output of the same shape. In practice it is deployed as a drop-in replacement inside hierarchical backbones (224×224 or 384×384 RGB images for classification; 800×1280 or 1024×1024 for detection/segmentation) and inside U-Net for latent diffusion (image latent + class label → generated 256×256 image). The operator's key structural detail is that channels within the same group (C/G channels) share identical sampling offsets and aggregation weights per spatial location, which the optimized kernel exploits for parallelism.

## Method

DCNv4 makes two core changes to DCNv3. First, it removes the softmax normalization on spatial aggregation weights, converting them from bounded 0–1 modulation scalars to unbounded dynamic weights analogous to convolution kernels; this is justified by the observation that each output location has its own dedicated aggregation window (unlike attention where all queries share the same window), so the degeneration problem that motivates softmax in attention does not apply. Second, it rewrites the CUDA kernel to eliminate redundant memory access: one thread processes D′ channels within the same group (sharing offset/weight reads and bilinear interpolation coefficients), vectorized 128-bit load/store instructions merge multiple scalar memory operations, and half-precision (FP16/BF16) halves the bytes transferred. Micro-design changes further merge the offset and weight linear layers into a single projection and remove the LN-GELU sub-network, yielding >3× forward speedup over DCNv3 and faster convergence than DCNv3, convolution, and attention.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, FlashInternImage (InternImage with DCNv4) achieves 83.6% (Tiny), 84.4% (Small), 84.9% (Base), and 88.1% (Large) top-1 accuracy while delivering 50–87% higher throughput than its InternImage counterpart. At the operator level, DCNv4 runs in 0.606 ms (FP32) vs. 1.45 ms for DCNv3 on a 56×56×128 feature map, a >3× speedup, and outperforms cuDNN DWConv(7×7) (0.981 ms) and FlashAttention-2 (2.46 ms). On COCO detection, FlashInternImage-B reaches 50.1 box AP / 44.5 mask AP (1× schedule) and 50.6 / 45.4 (3×+MS), surpassing InternImage-B (48.8 / 44.0 and 50.3 / 44.8). For image generation, replacing U-Net convolutions with DCNv4 improves FID from 2.94 to 2.44 on ImageNet 256×256 with 34% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InternImage (DCNv3), FlashInternImage wins on both speed and accuracy: it is 50–87% faster in throughput while matching or slightly exceeding top-1 accuracy at every scale (e.g., 84.4% vs. 84.2% at Small, 88.1% vs. 87.7% at Large). Against ConvNeXt, FlashInternImage-S (84.4%) outperforms ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) by 0.6% while being faster, and FlashInternImage-L (88.1%) beats ConvNeXt-XL (87.8%) with 30–130% higher throughput. Against Swin Transformer, FlashInternImage dominates at every scale in both accuracy and speed (e.g., 84.9% vs. 83.5% at Base with 2.5× throughput). At the operator level, DCNv4 surpasses cuDNN DWConv(7×7), FlashAttention-2, window attention, and PyTorch attention across all tested resolutions, confirming its advantage as a sparse operator. In the generative setting, U-Net+DCNv4 achieves a lower FID (2.44 vs. 2.94) with 34% fewer parameters than the standard U-Net baseline, and in the universal-operator test, DCNv4 plugged into ConvNeXt-B and ViT-B matches or slightly improves accuracy while boosting throughput by 20–33%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCNv4 is a direct successor to DCNv3, the core operator of InternImage, which itself extends the deformable convolution lineage (DCNv1/v2) by combining sparse sampling with input-dependent attention weights. The key conceptual departure is removing softmax normalization from spatial aggregation weights, drawing an analogy to standard convolution's unbounded weight range and arguing that softmax is only necessary when multiple queries share the same key-value set (as in dense attention) but not when each location has a dedicated aggregation window. The speed optimization is inspired by FlashAttention's insight that memory access, not FLOPs, is the practical bottleneck, and applies vectorized load/store and half-precision techniques to the DCN CUDA kernel. The paper also positions DCNv4 as a universal operator that can replace both depthwise convolution (in ConvNeXt) and dense self-attention (in ViT), bridging the ConvNet-Transformer divide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3×+ forward speedup makes deformable convolution practically competitive with heavily optimized operators like cuDNN depthwise convolution, removing the primary barrier to adopting DCN-based backbones in production. The finding that softmax actively harms convergence for dedicated-window operators (demonstrated via the ConvNeXt ablation in Table 1) is a conceptual insight that may generalize to other sparse/dynamic operators beyond DCN. The generative model application (U-Net in latent diffusion) opens an underexplored direction, though the paper acknowledges the architecture and hyperparameters are likely suboptimal for DCNv4 in this setting. Open questions include whether the speed advantage transfers to non-NVIDIA hardware or mobile accelerators, and whether the softmax-free formulation interacts well with other normalization strategies at scale.
