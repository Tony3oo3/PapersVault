## Problem

Existing adversarial defense methods (e.g., PGD-AT, TRADES, MART) are trained against specific attack types and exhibit severe accuracy collapse when evaluated against unfamiliar attack modalities such as spatially-constrained (STA, FWA) or optimization-based (CW, DDN) attacks, revealing poor robust generalization. Additionally, most defenses sacrifice clean-example accuracy to gain adversarial robustness. The paper asks whether the well-known 'robust overfitting' phenomenon of FGSM-AT networks—near-perfect accuracy on FGSM adversarial examples but near-zero on all others—can be exploited at test time to defend against arbitrary unknown attacks.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images at the native resolution of each benchmark (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 300×200 for Caltech-101) and outputs a single class label. Inference is a two-pass procedure: the image is first classified to obtain a pre-predicted label, then FGSM purification modifies pixels within an ℓ∞ ball of radius ξ=8/255, and the purified image is re-classified for the final label. No additional network or auxiliary model is required at test time.

## Method

TPAP has two phases. In training, a standard CNN (ResNet-18, VGG-16, or WideResNet-34) is trained with single-step FGSM adversarial training (ε=8/255) until it reaches a 'robust overfitting' state: >90% accuracy on FGSM adversarial examples in training, <10% on other attack types, while retaining high clean accuracy on the test set. At test time, the frozen network first performs a pre-classification pass to obtain a predicted label y_pred(x); this label then guides an untargeted FGSM purification step that shifts each pixel by γ = β·sign(∂L_CE(f_θ(x), y_pred(x))/∂x) with β=ξ, producing a purified image x_pur = x + γ. The purified image is fed back into the same network for the final prediction. The key insight is that the FGSM-RO-DNN's vulnerability to non-FGSM attacks means adversarial examples are misclassified, and the purification step—moving pixels away from the misclassified label—effectively cancels the adversarial perturbation, pulling the example back within the correct decision boundary where the network's FGSM robustness ensures correct classification.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, TPAP achieves 86.25% clean accuracy (highest among all methods) and 79.06% PGD-20 adversarial accuracy, substantially outperforming PGD-AT (48.91%), TRADES (54.97%), and MART (55.39%). Against spatial-constrained attacks, TPAP reaches 31.4% STA and 52.83% FWA accuracy, compared to near-zero (0.35% and 3.19%) for PGD-AT. The TPAP+MART combination further boosts CW2 to 92.38%, DDN2 to 90.05%, and STA to 85.7% on CIFAR-10. On SVHN, TPAP achieves 90.73% PGD-20 and 80.45% STA accuracy with VGG-16, far exceeding PGD-AT (53.64% and 0.51%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPAP outperforms PGD-AT [25], TRADES [56], and MART [43] on clean accuracy (86.25% vs. 84.54%, 83.22%, 82.14%) and on PGD-20/PGD-100 adversarial accuracy (79.06%/80.50% vs. 48.91%/47.70%, 54.97%/54.07%, 55.39%/54.80%) on CIFAR-10. Against spatial-constrained attacks (STA, FWA), TPAP achieves 31.4% and 52.83% respectively, while all three AT baselines drop below 7%, confirming that PGD-based training provides essentially no defense against geometric attacks. TPAP loses to TRADES and MART on CW2 (61.37% vs. 71.62%/74.30%) and DDN2 (64.50% vs. 24.24%/25.83%), but the TPAP+MART combination recovers this gap, reaching 92.38% CW2 and 90.05% DDN2. Against SOAP [34], TPAP improves clean accuracy by 2.18% and PGD-20 by 27.64%. The TPAP+MART variant also achieves the best STA accuracy (85.70%) across all methods, demonstrating that combining FGSM robust overfitting with MART's misclassification-aware weighting yields complementary strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 'catastrophic overfitting' phenomenon observed in FGSM adversarial training (Wong et al., 2020; Andriushchenko et al., 2020) — previously treated as a failure mode — as a deliberate design prior for test-time defense. It builds directly on FGSM (Goodfellow et al., 2014) for both training and purification, but departs from the adversarial training lineage (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART) by not attempting to make the network robust to all attacks simultaneously. Instead, it positions itself alongside adversarial purification methods (SOAP, diffusion-based denoisers) while avoiding their need for auxiliary networks or long training, using only a single FGSM step at inference to 'counter changes with changelessness.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap: most adversarial training methods generalize poorly to spatially-constrained or geometric attacks (STA, FWA), where PGD-AT networks collapse to near-zero accuracy. TPAP's substantial improvements on these attack types (e.g., 31.4% vs 0.35% on STA for ResNet-18/CIFAR-10) and its 4× reduction in training time make it practically relevant. However, the method's inability to defend against its own training attack (FGSM) and its failure under strong perturbations (ε=0.3) limit its deployment scope. Open questions include whether the robust-overfitting property is truly unique to FGSM or extends to other single-step methods, and whether multi-step purification could mitigate the FGSM weakness without sacrificing the overfitting prior.
