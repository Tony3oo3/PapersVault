## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: diffusion models support editing but are slow (operating on raw motion sequences), autoregressive models achieve high fidelity but are sequential and non-editable, and latent-space alignment methods are fast but produce low-fidelity motion. No prior approach simultaneously achieves real-time speed, high generation quality, and native motion editability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language text prompt (processed via CLIP into sentence-level and word-level embeddings) and, for editing tasks, a partial motion sequence with [MASK] tokens placed at the regions to be filled. Output is a full 3D human motion sequence at 20 FPS (HumanML3D) or 12.5 FPS (KIT-ML), up to 10 seconds in duration, decoded from discrete motion tokens back to continuous joint positions. For long-sequence generation, multiple text prompts are chained with single-step transition generation between them.

## Method

MMM uses a two-stage pipeline: (1) a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer with a large 8192-entry codebook (32-dim factorized codes) that quantizes 3D motion into discrete tokens while preserving fine-grained representations, and (2) a conditional masked motion transformer that learns bidirectional attention over motion tokens and CLIP text embeddings (sentence prepended, words via cross-attention). During training, a random subset of motion tokens is replaced with [MASK] and the transformer predicts all masked tokens concurrently given the unmasked context and text. Inference employs iterative parallel decoding with a cosine scheduling function (MaskGIT-style), starting from an all-mask canvas and progressively unmasking the highest-confidence tokens each iteration, achieving high fidelity in just 10 iterations. Editability is inherent: placing [MASK] tokens at desired spatial or temporal locations causes the model to fill gaps while maintaining smooth transitions with surrounding conditioned motion, requiring no additional training.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MMM achieves the best FID of 0.089 (and 0.080 in a second configuration), outperforming the next-best autoregressive model T2M-GPT (FID 0.116) and AttT2M (FID 0.112), and substantially beating diffusion-based methods like MLD (FID 0.473) and MDM (FID 0.544). MMM also leads on R-Precision Top-1 (0.515), Top-2 (0.708), Top-3 (0.804), and MM-Dist (2.926). On KIT-ML, MMM achieves the best FID of 0.429 and MM-Dist of 2.977. Inference speed is 0.081 AITS (average inference time per second of motion), making MMM two orders of magnitude faster than motion-space diffusion models (MDM: 28.11 s per motion) and at least 2× faster than autoregressive models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMM outperforms all 11 baselines on FID and MM-Dist on HumanML3D, with the largest margins over motion-space diffusion models: MDM (FID 0.544) and MotionDiffuse (FID 0.630) are 5–6× worse, while latent-space diffusion MLD (FID 0.473) and Fg-T2M (FID 0.243) still trail by 2.7× and 2.7× respectively. Among autoregressive models, MMM improves FID from 0.116 (T2M-GPT) and 0.112 (AttT2M) to 0.089, and gains ~2 points on R-Precision Top-1 (0.515 vs. 0.491/0.499). On KIT-ML, MMM achieves the best FID (0.429) and MM-Dist (2.977), again leading all baselines. In editing tasks (Table 3), MMM surpasses MDM on both motion in-betweening (FID 0.071 vs. 2.371 with text; 0.114 vs. 3.417 without) and upper body editing (FID 0.103 vs. 4.827 with text; 0.134 vs. 7.436 without). The only metric where MMM is not best is MModality (1.226), where M2DM (3.587) and MDM (2.799) generate more diverse samples per prompt, suggesting MMM's outputs are more text-consistent but less varied.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMM departs from the three dominant paradigms in text-to-motion generation—latent-space alignment (T2M, TEMOS), motion-space diffusion (MDM, MotionDiffuse, MLD), and autoregressive decoding (T2M-GPT, AttT2M)—by adopting a masked generative framework inspired by BERT and MaskGIT. The motion tokenizer builds on VQ-VAE with codebook factorization (from VQ-GAN), while the conditional masked transformer replaces causal attention with bidirectional self-attention over discrete motion tokens and CLIP text embeddings. The key conceptual departure is that parallel, iterative decoding of multiple tokens per step (with cosine scheduling) simultaneously achieves the fidelity of autoregressive models and the editability of diffusion models, without the sequential bottleneck or the multi-step denoising overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMM resolves a practical trilemma in text-to-motion generation: prior methods forced a trade-off among generation speed, motion fidelity, and editability, whereas MMM claims to satisfy all three in a single architecture. The two-orders-of-magnitude speedup over motion-space diffusion (0.081 s vs. ~28 s on RTX A5000) makes real-time and long-sequence generation feasible, and the innate editability (in-betweening, body-part modification, long-sequence stitching) requires no additional training or separate inpainting pipeline. Open questions include whether the 10-second maximum sequence length of the training data limits generalization to truly long or complex multi-action sequences, whether the discrete tokenization preserves sufficient temporal smoothness for high-FPS applications, and how the approach scales to multi-person or object-interaction motion.
