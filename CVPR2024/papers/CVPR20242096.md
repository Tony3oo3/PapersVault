## Problem

Delta Denoising Score (DDS) provides a cleaner editing gradient than Score Distillation Sampling by using a reference branch, but it neglects structural consistency between the source and edited images, causing pose, layout, and spatial details to be lost. No prior work has applied contrastive structural regularization (e.g., CUT loss) to pretrained latent diffusion models in a training-free manner, leaving a gap in balancing content transformation with structure preservation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a source image paired with a source text prompt and a target text prompt, all processed through a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.4 encoder into latent space. The output is a decoded edited image (or, in the NeRF extension, a fine-tuned 3D radiance field) that reflects the target semantics while retaining the source's spatial structure. No additional networks or training are required; the entire pipeline is zero-shot and training-free.

## Method

CDS augments the standard DDS loss with a patchNCE contrastive loss computed on intermediate self-attention features of the LDM's denoising U-Net, eliminating the need for an auxiliary encoder as in the original CUT. During each denoising step, patches are randomly sampled from the target branch's self-attention feature map; the patch at the same spatial location in the reference branch's feature map serves as the positive, while all other patches serve as negatives. The cross-entropy contrastive objective pulls corresponding patches together and pushes non-corresponding ones apart in the feature space. The key insight is that self-attention features encode disentangled spatial similarity information that naturally aligns with CUT's patch-correspondence requirement, whereas using the raw score output or cross-attention features either over-constrains semantic changes or fails to preserve structure. The total loss is the sum of the DDS denoising loss and the weighted contrastive loss, optimized via gradient descent on the target latent.

## Results

On the cat→dog task, CDS achieves 97.5% CLIP accuracy with a DINO-ViT structure distance of 0.020 and LPIPS of 0.079, outperforming DDS (0.023 / 0.080) on both structure metrics while maintaining comparable text alignment. On the harder cat→cow task, CDS achieves 97.9% CLIP accuracy with structure distance 0.033 and LPIPS 0.112, again beating DDS (0.040 / 0.116) on structure preservation. In the user study (20 participants, 5-point scale), CDS scores highest on all three criteria: Text match 4.43, Structure 4.65, and Overall Quality 4.20, compared to DDS's 4.06 / 4.05 / 3.64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDS consistently outperforms all five baselines on structure preservation metrics (DINO-ViT distance and LPIPS) across both cat→dog and cat→cow tasks. Against the closest baseline DDS, CDS reduces structure distance by ~13% (0.020 vs 0.023 on cat→dog; 0.033 vs 0.040 on cat→cow) and LPIPS by ~1–4%, while CLIP accuracy remains within 0.4–1.7 percentage points. Sampling-based methods (SDEdit+word swap, DiffuseIT) suffer large structure degradation (Dist 0.052–0.175), and inversion-based methods (DDPMinv+P2P, DDPMinv+PnP) show both lower CLIP accuracy (86–95%) and worse structure scores. In the user study, CDS leads all baselines on every criterion, with the largest gap over DDS on Structure (4.65 vs 4.05) and Quality (4.20 vs 3.64), confirming that the CUT loss improves perceptual realism alongside structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Delta Denoising Score (DDS) framework—which itself builds on Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) for image editing—by incorporating the Contrastive Unpaired Translation (CUT) loss to enforce structural consistency. The key departure from prior work is the application of CUT loss to latent diffusion models (LDM) using intermediate self-attention features, eliminating the need for an auxiliary encoder that pixel-domain CUT applications (e.g., [13, 28]) required. The authors position this as the first zero-shot, training-free integration of CUT loss into a pretrained LDM within the DDS framework, distinguishing it from supervised attention-based editing methods like Prompt-to-Prompt and Plug-and-Play diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDS offers a practical, training-free recipe for text-guided image editing that achieves a better balance between content transformation and structural preservation than DDS alone, with the added benefit of extending naturally to 3D NeRF editing. The finding that self-attention features (rather than score outputs or cross-attention features) are the appropriate space for contrastive regularization is a useful practical insight for the LDM editing community. Limitations include sensitivity to random patch selection and failure on unconventional poses, and the approach remains tied to the DDS optimization loop, which constrains its applicability to settings where iterative latent optimization is feasible.
