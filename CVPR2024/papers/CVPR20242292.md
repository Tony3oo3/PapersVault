## Problem

Prior fine-grained sketch-based image retrieval (FG-SBIR) methods addressed individual sub-factors of sketch abstraction (style, partiality, drawing order) in isolation, but no framework modeled abstraction as a whole. The paper identifies that retrieval granularity should be negatively correlated with sketch abstraction level—a rough contour sketch cannot reasonably retrieve a specific product—yet the standard triplet loss treats all sketches equally regardless of their abstraction level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242292/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a freehand sketch query (256×256, potentially at 30%, 60%, or 100% completion) and a gallery of RGB photos, producing a ranked photo list. Unusually, the internal representation is a 9×512 feature matrix embedding rather than a standard 512-dim vector, where the abstraction identification head dynamically zeros out lower rows for more abstract sketches, effectively reducing the retrieval granularity. The pre-trained StyleGAN generator is used only during training for reconstruction regularisation and is discarded at inference.

## Method

The architecture uses a shared VGG-16 backbone followed by separate sketch and photo feature matrix encoders (9 stride-2 conv blocks each) that produce 9×512 matrix embeddings aligned to the coarse-to-fine disentanglement of a pre-trained StyleGAN2 latent space. An abstraction identification head (Gumbel-Softmax over 3 classes) predicts whether to use only the coarse group (rows 1–3), coarse+medium (rows 1–6), or all rows (1–9) for distance computation, with ground truth derived from 30%/60%/100% vector sketch rendering. Training combines three losses: a StyleGAN reconstruction loss that distils disentangled latent structure into the matrix embedding, a differentiable Acc.@q surrogate loss where q varies with abstraction level (q=10, 5, 1 for coarse/medium/fine) to make retrieval tolerance adaptive, and a cross-entropy abstraction identification loss. This replaces the standard triplet loss, which the authors show via an entropy pilot study is ill-suited to abstraction-varying sketches.

## Results

On the standard FG-SBIR benchmark, the proposed method achieves Acc.@1 of 72.1% and Acc.@5 of 80.9% on QMUL-ChairV2, and 45.3% and 77.3% on QMUL-ShoeV2, outperforming the strongest prior method SketchPVT (71.2/80.1 and 44.1/70.8) on all four metrics. The human study MOS score is 4.4±0.5 (ChairV2) and 4.1±0.5 (ShoeV2), substantially exceeding the next-best StyleMeUp (3.7±0.2 and 3.3±0.3). On the forensic sketch-photo matching task (IIIT-D), the method reaches 91.39% Acc.@1 and 97.38% VR@FAR=1%, surpassing the prior best RGM (88.94% and 97.87%) in accuracy while using only one sketch-photo pair per subject.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor SketchPVT [59], it gains +0.9 Acc.@1 on ChairV2 and +1.2 on ShoeV2, but the most striking margin is in human-rated MOS: +1.1 (ChairV2) and +1.2 (ShoeV2), indicating substantially better perceptual match quality. The classic Triplet-SN [83] and HOLEF-SN [64] baselines are far behind, with the proposed method exceeding them by +24.7 and +21.4 Acc.@1 on ChairV2 respectively. Generative-translation baselines (B-pSp, B-pix2pix, B-CycleGAN) all underperform even Triplet-SN, confirming that naive sketch-to-photo conversion is insufficient for fine-grained retrieval. Among the recent FG-SBIR methods, Partial-OT [18], CrossHier [55], StyleMeUp [56], and On-the-fly [8] are all surpassed by 8–21 Acc.@1 points. On the forensic task (IIIT-D), the method beats RGM [17] by +2.45 Acc.@1 and CDL [73] by +6.04 Acc.@1, while using only one sketch-photo pair per subject versus the much larger CUFSF dataset used by those methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard FG-SBIR framework established by Yu et al. (Sketch Me That Shoe, CVPR 2016) by replacing the conventional d-dimensional feature vector with a 9×d feature matrix embedding, and by repurposing the disentangled coarse-to-fine latent hierarchy of a pre-trained StyleGAN (Karras et al., CVPR 2020) as a structural regularizer rather than a generative model. Prior SBIR works addressed individual sub-factors of sketch abstraction (style invariance, partiality, early retrieval) in isolation, whereas this paper models abstraction holistically through a dynamic row-selection mechanism and a parametric surrogate loss that adapts retrieval strictness to the input's abstraction level. The differentiable Acc.@q loss departs from the de facto triplet loss by directly approximating the test-time ranking metric, drawing on the surrogate-loss literature (Smooth-AP, SoDeep) but introducing a q-parameter that encodes abstraction-aware tolerance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that StyleGAN's latent disentanglement can serve as an inductive bias for cross-modal feature learning without requiring the GAN at inference, a repurposing that could inform other sketch-to-photo or sparse-to-dense retrieval settings. The forensic sketch-photo matching extension (91.39% Acc.@1 on IIIT-D with only one sketch-photo pair per subject, outperforming methods trained on the much larger CUFSF dataset) suggests the approach is robust to data scarcity, though this is validated on a single dataset. Open questions include whether the three-level abstraction discretization is sufficient for continuous abstraction gradations, whether the per-category StyleGAN pre-training requirement limits scalability to open-vocabulary or scene-level retrieval, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively small QMUL datasets used for evaluation.
