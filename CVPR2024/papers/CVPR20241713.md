## Problem

Existing deep learning image registration methods either use non-iterative pyramid architectures that underutilize the trained network's alignment capacity, or employ cascade-based strategies that incur substantial computational overhead by repeatedly encoding and decoding entire networks. The paper identifies that a trained registration network, once fixed, can be iteratively reapplied to progressively refine the deformation field, but naively cascading the full network ignores the multi-scale structure of pyramid architectures and leads to accuracy degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241713/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D medical volumes (fixed and moving images) normalized to [0,1], specifically 128×128×96 CT scans at 2.5 mm isotropic resolution (FLARE) or 160×192×160 MRI scans at 1 mm isotropic resolution (Mindboggle). It produces a global deformation field φ that warps the moving image to align with the fixed image. Uniquely, the number of inference passes per flow estimator is not fixed but adaptively determined per image pair by the IterStop mechanism, so the effective computational cost varies with the difficulty of each registration.

## Method

IIRP-Net builds on RP-Net, a streamlined 4-level pyramid consisting of a weight-sharing 3D convolutional feature extractor (channels 8→16→32→32 with average pooling) and four residual flow estimators, each containing two ResBlocks that expand the receptive field for capturing long-range correlations. The key innovation is an iterative inference strategy applied during inference only: at each pyramid scale, the residual flow estimator is repeatedly applied to the warped feature maps (internal iteration at the feature-map level, preserving the coarse-to-fine structure), rather than cascading the entire network externally. An IterStop mechanism adaptively halts each estimator's iterations when the NCC improvement between successive deformation fields drops below a threshold δ=0.005, mimicking the stopping criterion of optimization-based registration. Training remains fully unsupervised with a local NCC similarity loss (window w=9) plus a diffusion smoothness regularizer, and the iterative inference strategy introduces zero additional training cost, only a modest increase in inference time.

## Results

IIRP-Net achieves state-of-the-art Dice scores of 73.3% on FLARE (3D abdomen CT) and 65.8% on Mindboggle (brain MRI), outperforming the best prior method SDH-Net by 1.1% and 2.2% respectively. The method also attains the lowest MSE (3.591×10⁻³ on FLARE, 1.436×10⁻³ on Mindboggle) and HD95 (23.07 mm on FLARE, 4.84 mm on Mindboggle) among all compared methods. Notably, IIRP-Net uses only 410.1K parameters—roughly 43× fewer than SDH-Net (17,862K)—while training in 31.9% of SDH-Net's time and inferring in 19.4% of its time on FLARE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against U-Net-based methods, IIRP-Net achieves Dice improvements of 32.5% over VoxelMorph and 22.7% over TransMorph on FLARE, and 10.8% and 4.6% respectively on Mindboggle. Among pyramid-based methods, IIRP-Net surpasses the best (Image2Grid) by 5.4% Dice on FLARE and 3.8% on Mindboggle, while also achieving the lowest MSE and HD95 in this category. Against cascade-based methods, IIRP-Net outperforms SDH-Net (the strongest baseline) by 1.1% Dice on FLARE and 2.2% on Mindboggle, while using only 410K parameters versus SDH-Net's 17,862K and training/infering at roughly one-third and one-fifth of the time. The iterative inference strategy also improves diffeomorphic quality: on Mindboggle, IIRP-Net reduces non-positive Jacobian voxels to <0.08% versus <0.4% for RP-Net and <0.2% for SDH-Net. RCN, which cascades three VoxelMorphs, is outperformed by IIRP-Net on all metrics on both datasets despite using more parameters (958K vs 410K).

## Relation to Prior Work

IIRP-Net synthesizes two established lines of DLIR research—pyramid-based methods (DualPRNet++, NICE-Net, PIViT, Image2Grid) and cascade-based methods (RCN, SDH-Net)—into a single streamlined framework. Rather than cascading entire networks as in RCN/SDH-Net, it repurposes the trained pyramid's individual residual flow estimators as iteratively applicable functions at the feature-map level, an 'internal' iteration that preserves the coarse-to-fine structure. The IterStop mechanism explicitly borrows the stopping-criterion concept from traditional optimization-based registration, framing the fixed network as a driving force analogous to gradient descent. The residual flow estimators with ResBlocks deepen the otherwise minimal pyramid architecture to expand receptive field, drawing on standard residual learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that iterative inference adds zero training cost and only modest inference overhead (0.06s vs 0.02s on FLARE), making it a drop-in enhancement for existing pyramid registration networks. The finding that naive external iteration of pyramid networks actually degrades accuracy (Figure 1) while internal per-scale iteration improves it provides a useful design principle for future pyramid-based methods. A stated limitation is that IterStop relies on intensity-based similarity (NCC), which may misalign with anatomical structure in weak-texture regions; developing structure-aware stopping criteria is an open direction. The very low parameter count (410K) achieving SOTA suggests that architectural simplicity plus smart inference-time strategies can rival much larger cascade networks.
