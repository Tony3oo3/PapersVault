## Problem

Multi-task partially supervised learning (MTPSL) trains dense prediction models where each sample is labeled for only a subset of tasks, inevitably producing noisy and low-quality prediction maps. While prior work (e.g., XTC) improves label efficiency via cross-task consistency, it does not directly address the residual noise in predictions. This paper asks whether a generative denoising framework can be repurposed to rectify noisy multi-task dense predictions under partial supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized per dataset: 288×384 for NYUD, 128×256 for Cityscapes) and produces up to six dense prediction maps (semantic segmentation, depth, surface normals, saliency, human parsing, boundary) at the same spatial resolution. During training, each sample carries ground-truth labels for only one or a random subset of tasks; the diffusion process operates on the model's own initial predictions or feature maps rather than on the input image, using 2 diffusion steps by default. The denoised outputs are supervised only on the labeled tasks, while unlabeled tasks are refined implicitly through cross-task conditioning.

## Method

DiffusionMTL is a two-stage pipeline: a shared-encoder / task-specific-decoder backbone first produces noisy initial predictions, and a Multi-Task Denoising Diffusion Network (MTDNet) then refines them via a DDPM-style forward-noise / reverse-denoise loop. Two orthogonal variants are provided—Prediction Diffusion (denoising in the output space) and Feature Diffusion (denoising in a high-dimensional latent feature space)—both using 4 cross-attention transformer blocks as the denoiser. The key architectural insight is a Multi-Task Conditioning mechanism: initial maps from all T tasks are projected, channel-stacked, and flattened into a condition vector F_cond that serves as the query in cross-attention, while the noisy target-task map (plus a sinusoidal step embedding) provides key and value, enabling the denoiser to exploit complementary information across tasks. Training is end-to-end with task-specific losses on labeled tasks only; unlabeled task decoders are implicitly regularized through the shared conditioning signal, and the entire system is optimized with just 2 diffusion steps and ~133M parameters (ResNet-18 backbone).

## Results

On PASCAL under the one-label setting, DiffusionMTL (Prediction) achieves 59.43 mIoU on semantic segmentation and +3.23% MTL Perf Δm, outperforming the re-implemented XTC* (55.08 mIoU, +0.37% Δm) by a large margin. On Cityscapes, DiffusionMTL (Feature) reaches 75.67 mIoU on semantic segmentation with only −2.13% MTL Perf Δm, compared to XTC*'s −14.74%. On NYUD under the random-label setting, DiffusionMTL (Feature) achieves −0.77% MTL Perf Δm versus XTC*'s −3.44%. The model uses only 133M parameters (ResNet-18 backbone), substantially fewer than XTC's 173M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against XTC [6] (and its re-implementation XTC*), the previous state-of-the-art for multi-task partially supervised learning. DiffusionMTL wins on all three benchmarks under both one-label and random-label settings. On PASCAL one-label, DiffusionMTL (Prediction) improves MTL Perf Δm from +0.37% (XTC*) to +3.23%, and DiffusionMTL (Feature) further pushes it to +3.65%, while also using 40M fewer parameters (133M vs. 173M). On Cityscapes, the improvement is most dramatic: MTL Perf Δm goes from −14.74% (XTC*) to −2.13% (DiffusionMTL Feature), a gain of over 12 percentage points. On NYUD, both DiffusionMTL variants consistently outperform XTC* under both settings, with the random-label Feature Diffusion variant reducing the negative MTL Perf Δm from −3.44% to −0.77%. The MTL Baseline (same backbone, stronger decoder, no cross-task mechanism) is also clearly surpassed, confirming that the denoising diffusion framework provides substantial gains beyond simply using a stronger decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework [7] for its forward diffusion and reverse denoising processes, but repurposes a generative model paradigm (typically used for image synthesis) toward a deterministic multi-task dense prediction task. It addresses the Multi-Task Partially Supervised Learning (MTPSL) problem formalized by Li et al. [6] (XTC), which focuses on cross-task consistency to improve label efficiency. The key departure is that rather than improving the optimization process to reduce noise, DiffusionMTL explicitly models and removes noise from prediction maps via a dedicated denoising diffusion network, introducing a Multi-Task Conditioning mechanism that uses cross-attention to share information across tasks during denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that generative denoising models can effectively rectify noisy multi-task predictions under partial annotation, offering a complementary strategy to prior cross-task consistency approaches. The consistent gains across three benchmarks and two labeling settings, achieved with fewer parameters than XTC, suggest the denoising paradigm is a viable and efficient addition to the MTPSL toolkit. Limitations include the use of only two diffusion steps, evaluation on relatively small-scale datasets (NYUD has 795 training images), and the added inference cost of iterative denoising. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets with more tasks, whether more diffusion steps yield diminishing returns, and whether the framework extends to other partially-supervised settings beyond dense prediction.
