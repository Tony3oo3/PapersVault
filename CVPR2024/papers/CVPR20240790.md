## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models can be conditioned on text, layout, edges, and segmentation maps, but lack the ability to control interactions between objects (e.g., a person feeding a cat). This interaction-level control is critical for generating coherent scenes with interacting characters, yet remains an open problem because the relationship between subject, action, and object is intricate and not captured by simple layout or text conditioning alone.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text caption plus one or more HOI triplets, each specifying a subject label, action label, object label, and their bounding boxes. The action bounding box is not provided directly but is computed via a 'between' operation on the subject and object boxes, yielding the spatial focus area of the interaction. The output is a 512×512 RGB image generated by a latent diffusion model, with the interaction conditions injected as additional conditioning alongside the text caption.

## Method

InteractDiffusion is a pluggable Interaction Module with three components that extends a pre-trained GLIGEN/Stable Diffusion model. (1) InToken converts each HOI triplet into three tokens (subject, action, object) by fusing CLIP text embeddings with Fourier-encoded bounding boxes through separate MLPs. (2) InBedding adds a learnable instance embedding to group tokens belonging to the same interaction and a role embedding to distinguish subject/action/object roles, encoding the intricate multi-instance interaction structure. (3) InFormer inserts a gated Interaction Self-Attention layer between the existing visual self-attention and text cross-attention in each UNet transformer block, performing self-attention over the concatenation of visual and interaction tokens with a token-slicing operation to recover only visual outputs. Only the new module's parameters are trained (original weights frozen), and a scheduled-sampling hyperparameter ω allows the interaction control to be progressively disabled in later diffusion steps to balance interaction fidelity against rendering quality.

## Results

On HICO-DET, InteractDiffusion achieves FID 18.69 and KID 0.00676, matching or slightly exceeding the best baseline (GLIGEN*: FID 18.82, KID 0.00694) in generation quality. For interaction controllability measured by FGAHOI Swin-Tiny mAP, InteractDiffusion scores 29.53 (Default/Full) and 23.02 (Default/Rare), substantially outperforming GLIGEN (21.73/15.35) and GLIGEN* (25.23/17.45). Under the Swin-Large detector, the gap widens: InteractDiffusion reaches 31.56 (Default/Full) vs. 26.45 for GLIGEN*, while the real-image upper bound is 37.18. The method narrows the gap to real images from ~11 mAP points (GLIGEN*) to ~7.6 mAP points under Swin-Tiny.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractDiffusion outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against StableDiffusion (text-only conditioning), the improvement is dramatic: HOI detection mAP jumps from 0.63 to 29.53 (Swin-Tiny Default/Full), confirming that text captions alone cannot control interaction placement. Against GLIGEN (layout-conditioned), InteractDiffusion gains +7.8 mAP (29.53 vs. 21.73) on Default/Full and +7.7 on Default/Rare, showing that bounding-box layout alone is insufficient for interaction coherence. The closest comparison is GLIGEN* (GLIGEN fine-tuned on HICO-DET), where InteractDiffusion still leads by +4.3 mAP on Default/Full (29.53 vs. 25.23) and +5.6 on Default/Rare (23.02 vs. 17.45), while matching generation quality (FID 18.69 vs. 18.82). Under the stronger Swin-Large detector, the advantage over GLIGEN* widens to +5.1 mAP (31.56 vs. 26.45), suggesting the interaction control benefit is not an artifact of a weak detector. Notably, InteractDiffusion's Swin-Tiny scores (29.53/23.02) nearly match the real-image upper bound (29.94/22.24), indicating near-indistinguishable interaction rendering under the smaller detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the controllable text-to-image diffusion paradigm established by Stable Diffusion and GLIGEN by introducing a new conditioning modality: human-object interaction (HOI) triplets with bounding boxes. Architecturally, it follows GLIGEN's design of inserting a gated self-attention layer into the UNet transformer blocks, but departs by tokenizing interaction-specific information (subject, action, object) into distinct tokens and encoding their relational structure via instance and role embeddings. Unlike prior HOI synthesis work (InteractGAN, layout-proposal methods) that required pose templates or reference images, InteractDiffusion operates end-to-end with simple bounding boxes and triplet labels as conditions, making it a pluggable module for existing T2I models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to introduce interaction-level control into T2I diffusion models, opening applications in e-commerce, gaming, and interactive storytelling where object relationships matter beyond mere placement. A notable limitation is that while generated images are nearly indistinguishable from real ones under a Swin-Tiny HOI detector, the gap widens substantially with a Swin-Large detector, indicating that finer interaction details (e.g., hand-object contact geometry) remain under-rendered. Open questions include generalization beyond the HICO-DET triplet vocabulary, handling multi-person or multi-interaction scenes at scale, and whether the pluggable module transfers to newer diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models).
