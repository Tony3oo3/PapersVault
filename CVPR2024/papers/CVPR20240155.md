## Problem

Existing audio-visual segmentation (AVS) datasets suffer from 'commonsense' biases that assume specific objects are always the sound source, and current audio-visual contrastive learning methods rely on unsupervised instance-based pairing that cannot effectively mine informative samples to constrain cross-modal alignment. These issues lead to models that favour presumed sound sources regardless of actual audio content and generalise poorly beyond biased training distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240155/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (H×W×3) and an audio file encoded as a Mel Spectrogram (T×F Mel filter banks) and produces a pixel-level multi-class segmentation mask (H×W×C) including background. In the VPO-MSMI setting, stereo audio is synthesised by modulating left/right channel volumes based on the object's horizontal position in the image to provide spatial disambiguation cues. Experiments are conducted at both 224×224 resolution (for fair comparison with prior AVSBench work) and original image resolution.

## Method

The CAVP method modifies the standard cross-attention fusion by using visual features as queries (rather than audio) and replacing softmax with sigmoid to highlight multiple regions of varying sizes in the attention map. The key innovation is a supervised contrastive learning loss that mines informative contrastive pairs by randomly re-pairing audio and visual features within a batch, exploiting the VPO dataset construction where images and audio are matched by semantic class. This enables the creation of positive sets (same class in both modalities), hard negatives (one modality matches, the other does not), and easy negatives (neither matches), which are then optimised via a supervised InfoNCE objective. The overall training combines a per-pixel cross-entropy segmentation loss with the contrastive pairing loss, and the VPO benchmark itself is built by matching COCO semantic segmentation masks with VGGSound audio files across three difficulty settings (single-source, multi-source, multi-source multi-instance).

## Results

On AVSBench-Object (SS) with ResNet50 backbone, CAVP achieves 85.77% mIoU and 92.86% Fβ, surpassing the second-best methods (BAVS at 77.96% mIoU and DiffusionAVS at 86.90% Fβ) by +7.81% and +5.96% respectively. On AVSBench-Object (MS), CAVP reaches 62.39% mIoU and 73.62% Fβ, improving over the second-best AQFormer by +6.69% and +6.72%. The largest gains appear on AVSBench-Semantics, where CAVP achieves 44.70% mIoU and 57.76% Fβ, outperforming the second-best BAVS by +19.77% mIoU and +28.13% Fβ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAVP outperforms all 10 baselines across every benchmark and metric. Among Transformer-based methods, BAVS [25] is the closest competitor on AVSBench-Object (SS) mIoU (77.96 vs. 85.77), while AQFormer [19] leads on Object (MS) mIoU (55.70 vs. 62.39). Among per-pixel classification methods, DiffusionAVS [29] achieves the highest Fβ on Object (SS) (86.90 vs. 92.86), and TPAVI [54] is the strongest per-pixel baseline on Object (MS) mIoU (47.88 vs. 62.39). The gap is most dramatic on AVSBench-Semantics, where CAVP's 44.70% mIoU nearly doubles the second-best BAVS (24.68%) and more than doubles TPAVI (20.18%), suggesting that the supervised contrastive loss with informative sample mining is particularly effective for multi-class semantic segmentation. On the original-resolution AVSBench-Semantics (Table 2), CAVP also leads AVSegFormer [13] and TPAVI [54] by +4.13 to +10.11% mIoU depending on the split. On the newly proposed VPO benchmarks (Table 3), CAVP consistently outperforms both AVSegFormer and TPAVI by 4.76–6.14% mIoU and 3.39–6.20% FDR across all three settings (SS, MS, MSMI).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TPAVI architecture (Zhou et al., ECCV 2022) by reversing the cross-attention query direction—using visual features as query rather than audio—and removing the element-wise multiplication that suppresses the audio representation. For the contrastive learning component, it builds on supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020) but departs from prior AV-CL methods that treat each audio-visual pair as an independent contrastive class, instead mining informative positive/negative samples from arbitrary audio-visual pairings within a batch. The VPO benchmark is conceptually similar to the parallel AVS-Synthetic work (Liu et al., 2023) but addresses its limitations by supporting multi-source and multi-instance settings, multi-class labels, and spatial audio simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VPO benchmark provides a cost-effective, scalable alternative to expensive manual AVS annotation, and the validation experiment (statistically equivalent TPAVI performance on VPO-constructed vs. original AVSBench data, p > 0.6) gives practitioners confidence in this approach for building future datasets. The finding that reversing the cross-attention query direction yields a consistent ~4.5% mIoU gain challenges the standard audio-as-query convention in the field. Open questions include whether the approach generalises to video (temporal information is absent), whether the class imbalance from filtering trivial cases limits real-world applicability, and whether depth-based spatial audio modelling would further improve multi-instance localisation.
