## Problem

Existing box-supervised instance segmentation methods rely on the bounding box tightness prior, which is violated when objects are occluded or separated into multiple parts, leading to poor mask predictions in those regions. Although extreme points (topmost, leftmost, bottommost, rightmost) are freely obtained during standard bounding box annotation and lie on the true object boundary, they have been largely neglected as a supervision signal for instance segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240102/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images annotated with four extreme points per object (topmost, leftmost, bottommost, rightmost), which carry the same annotation cost as a bounding box. In the first stage, each object is cropped around its extreme points and resized to 512×512 for the pseudo label generator, which outputs a binary foreground mask. In the second stage, full-resolution images are fed to a standard instance segmentation model (e.g., SOLOv2, Mask2Former) that produces per-instance class labels and pixel-level masks.

## Method

EXITS is a two-stage framework. In stage 1, a ViT-based pseudo label generator is trained on single-object crops using a point retrieval algorithm: extreme points are shifted inward by a margin δ to form initial foreground seeds, while points outside the bounding box serve as background seeds. Labels are then propagated to unlabeled points inside the box via random walk on a fully connected graph whose transition probability matrix is derived from the multi-head self-attention of a warm-up trained similarity extractor (ViT), normalized to doubly stochastic form via Sinkhorn-Knopp. Pseudo foreground/background points are assigned by thresholding the difference between foreground and background propagation scores, and the generator is trained with a sparse point dice loss plus a CRF loss using an EMA teacher. In stage 2, the frozen generator produces pseudo masks that supervise any standard instance segmentation model in a fully supervised manner. The fully connected graph structure is critical: it enables label propagation across spatially distant regions, allowing the method to correctly segment objects split by occlusion where the bounding box tightness prior fails.

## Results

On COCO val2017, EXITS achieves Mask AP of 36.1 (ResNet-50 + SOLOv2), 39.8 (ResNet-101-DCN + SOLOv2), 40.5 (ResNeXt-101-DCN + SOLOv2), and 44.2 (Swin-Small + Mask2Former), with retention rates of 95.4–96.3% relative to fully supervised counterparts. On PASCAL VOC val2012, EXITS reaches 40.4 AP (ResNet-50) and 41.4 AP (ResNet-101), outperforming the previous best box-supervised method by 1.8 AP. On LVIS v1.0, EXITS achieves 24.9 Mask AP with 96.5% retention using ResNeXt-101-64 + Mask R-CNN. On the Separated COCO subset, EXITS generates pseudo masks with 66.6 mIoU, a 7.3-point improvement over MAL (59.3 mIoU), demonstrating superior handling of occluded, multi-part objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXITS consistently outperforms all box-supervised baselines across every backbone and instance segmentation model tested on COCO. Against the strongest prior box-supervised method MAL [35], EXITS gains +1.1 AP (ResNet-50), +1.6 AP (ResNet-101-DCN), +1.6 AP (ResNeXt-101-DCN), and +0.9 AP (Swin-Small + Mask2Former), while also improving retention rates by 2–4 percentage points. On PASCAL VOC, EXITS (40.4 AP, ResNet-50) surpasses BoxTeacher [13] (38.6 AP) by 1.8 AP and SIM [38] (36.7 AP) by 3.7 AP. On LVIS v1.0, EXITS beats MAL by 0.4 AP and 1.5–1.6% retention in both ResNeXt-101-32 and ResNeXt-101-64 settings. The most striking advantage appears on the Separated COCO subset, where EXITS's pseudo masks achieve 66.6 mIoU versus MAL's 59.3 mIoU (+7.3 points), confirming that the fully-connected-graph label propagation effectively handles occluded, multi-part objects where the bounding box tightness prior fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

EXITS builds directly on the two-stage pseudo-label pipeline introduced by MAL [35], adopting its ViT-based pseudo label generator and CRF refinement, but replaces the bounding box tightness prior with a label propagation mechanism over extreme points. It departs from the broader family of box-supervised methods (BBTP, BoxInst, DiscoBox, BoxTeacher, SIM, BoxLevelSet) that all rely on the assumption that every line crossing the box must intersect the object — an assumption violated by occlusion. The use of extreme points as supervision draws from the annotation-efficiency literature (Papadopoulos et al. [48], ExtremeNet [67]) and medical imaging work [15, 50], but repurposes them for general-scene weakly supervised instance segmentation via random-walk propagation on a fully connected graph constructed from MHSA attention, rather than path-connected scribble generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical insight that extreme points are a free byproduct of modern bounding box annotation workflows (no additional labeling cost) makes this a drop-in improvement for existing annotation pipelines. The 7.3%p mIoU gain on Separated COCO and the consistent AP improvements across COCO, VOC, and LVIS demonstrate that the box tightness prior is a genuine bottleneck for occluded objects, and that semantic label propagation can alleviate it. The decoupled design (pseudo label generator + arbitrary instance segmentation model) enhances practicality. However, the authors acknowledge that differentiating occluded objects of the same class remains unresolved, and the method still requires a warm-up phase to train the similarity extractor, adding a small but non-trivial training overhead.
