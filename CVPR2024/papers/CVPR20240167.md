## Problem

Learned keypoint descriptors lack robustness to in-plane camera rotation, and existing remedies are suboptimal: training for rotation invariance sacrifices distinctiveness on upright images, while test-time augmentation (running the descriptor for multiple rotations) incurs prohibitive runtime. The paper asks whether one can learn a lightweight linear transform in description space that encodes image rotation, enabling rotation-equivariant matching with a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240167/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images of the same scene with arbitrary relative in-plane rotation. It detects N keypoints per image (5,000–20,000 depending on benchmark), produces D=256-dimensional unit-norm descriptors per keypoint, and outputs mutual-nearest-neighbor correspondences via dual-softmax matching. The key structural property is that the D-dimensional descriptor space admits a D×D steerer matrix (for C4) or a Lie-algebra generator (for SO(2)) that rotates descriptions without re-running the network, so a single descriptor pass suffices for all rotation hypotheses.

## Method

The core contribution is the steerer: a group representation ρ on the descriptor space that makes the keypoint descriptor rotation-equivariant, i.e., f(ρ_in(g)v) = ρ(g)f(v). Using representation theory of C4 and SO(2), the paper characterizes all admissible steerers as block-diagonal matrices with specific eigenvalue structures (identity, frequency-1, permutation, or spread). Three training settings are explored: (A) optimizing a D×D steerer for a frozen descriptor, (B) jointly optimizing descriptor and steerer, and (C) fixing the steerer's eigenvalue structure and training only the descriptor. A theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) shows that any descriptor preserving inner products under rotation automatically admits an orthogonal steerer, explaining why existing descriptors are approximately steerable. For matching, the paper uses max-matches or max-similarity over steered descriptions (discretizing SO(2) into C8), or a Procrustes-based matcher for frequency-1 steerers that also recovers per-correspondence relative rotation.

## Results

On Roto-360, the best model (DeDoDe + SO2-Spread-B) achieves 95%/97%/98% correct matches at 3px/5px/10px thresholds, outperforming the previous SOTA RELF (92%/93%/94%) and SIFT (78%/78%/79%). On AIMS, the SO2-Freq1-B model with Procrustes matching achieves 64%/59%/60% average precision on North Up / All Others / All splits, surpassing SE2-LoFTR (58%/51%/52%). On upright MegaDepth-1500, the C4-Perm-G model matches the original DeDoDe-G (52/69/81 AUC@5°/10°/20°) while also achieving 53/69/82 on the rotated MegaDepth-C4 split, demonstrating that equivariance does not sacrifice upright performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIFT (78/78/79) and ORB (79/85/87) on Roto-360, the proposed SO2-Spread-B with DeDoDe (95/97/98) achieves a 17–20 point improvement, confirming that learned equivariant descriptors substantially outperform handcrafted rotation-invariant features. Against the previous state-of-the-art RELF [31] (92/93/94 with SuperPoint), the method gains 3–4 points at 3px and 4 points at 10px, while also running approximately 3× faster. On AIMS, the SO2-Freq1-B Procrustes model (64/59/60 AP) outperforms SE2-LoFTR [9] (58/51/52) by 6 points on North Up and 8 points on All Others, with the largest gap on heavily rotated images. On upright MegaDepth-1500, the C4-Perm-G model (52/69/81) ties the original DeDoDe-G (52/69/81), showing no degradation from equivariance training, while on the rotated MegaDepth-C4 split it improves from 13/17/21 to 53/69/82. The C4-B model (Setting B, joint optimization) slightly underperforms the fixed-steerer C4-Perm-B on upright images (51/67/79 vs 50/67/79), suggesting that the eigenvalue structure of the steerer matters more than the specific training procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DeDoDe descriptor architecture [19] and adapts the classical concept of steerable filters [21] to the deep learning keypoint description setting. It departs from two dominant strategies for rotation robustness: (1) hard-coding equivariance into network layers via equivariant ConvNets, and (2) data augmentation that enforces invariance at the cost of distinctiveness on upright images. Instead, the paper introduces a post-hoc linear 'steerer' in description space, grounded in the representation theory of C4 and SO(2), which can be optimized for a fixed descriptor, jointly with a descriptor, or used to constrain a descriptor's training. This positions the work as a lightweight, architecture-agnostic alternative to both equivariant CNNs and test-time augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The steerer framework offers a practical path to rotation-invariant matching without the 8× runtime cost of test-time augmentation or the architectural constraints of equivariant CNNs, and it achieves SOTA on AIMS and Roto-360 while remaining competitive on upright MegaDepth. A key open question is the initialization sensitivity of joint steerer-descriptor optimization: the eigenvalue structure of the learned steerer strongly affects performance, yet no principled method exists to select it a priori. The finding that invariant steerers (all eigenvalues = 1) underperform spread-eigenvalue steerers on upright images challenges the common assumption that rotation invariance is always beneficial, suggesting that the *type* of equivariance matters as much as its presence.
