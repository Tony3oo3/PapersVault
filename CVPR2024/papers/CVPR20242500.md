## Problem

Autonomous driving perception models face a long-tailed distribution of road objects, and when novel categories emerge, the deployed detector cannot recognize them. The iterative process of identifying missing categories, curating relevant data, labeling, and retraining currently demands substantial human effort, with industry solutions remaining proprietary. This paper asks whether VLMs and LLMs can automate the entire data engine loop—issue identification, data curation, auto-labeling, and verification—to enable continuous self-improvement at reduced cost.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a large unlabeled image pool collected by on-car cameras (from Mapillary, nuImages, Cityscapes, BDD100k, Waymo, and KITTI) along with a text prompt naming the novel category identified by the Issue Finder. It produces pseudo-labels (bounding boxes with class assignments) for both novel and known categories, an updated Fast-RCNN detector, and a verification pass/fail signal. A key detail is that the detector operates at real-time inference speed (unlike OVOD methods at ~3 FPS), and the pseudo-labeling pipeline specifically enlarges box crops before CLIP classification to compensate for the small-object, wide-aspect-ratio characteristics of AV imagery.

## Method

AIDE is a four-stage iterative pipeline: (1) Issue Finder uses Otter dense captioning to detect objects mentioned in captions but absent from the detector's label space, exploiting the finding that captioning models are more reliable zero-shot identifiers than OVOD detectors; (2) Data Feeder uses BLIP-2 text-guided retrieval (with a template like 'An image containing {}') to pull relevant images from the pool, outperforming CLIP image-similarity search; (3) Model Updater performs two-stage pseudo-labeling—OWL-v2 generates box proposals (retaining only boxes, discarding its unreliable labels) and CLIP zero-shot classification on enlarged crops assigns class labels—then trains the detector with pseudo-labels of both novel and known categories to mitigate catastrophic forgetting; (4) Verification prompts ChatGPT to generate diverse scene descriptions, retrieves matching images via BLIP-2, and has humans confirm or correct predictions, feeding corrections back into the next training iteration.

## Results

On the combined Mapillary and nuImages benchmark, AIDE (with Data Feeder) achieves 12.0% AP on novel categories and 26.6% AP on known categories, outperforming the SOTA OVOD method OWL-v2 (9.7% / 17.9% AP) by 2.3% and 8.7% AP respectively, while incurring only $0.6 in training cost and $0 in labeling cost. After a single round of LLM-guided Verification, AIDE further improves to 14.2% AP on novel categories at a total cost of $1.59, still well below the $2.1 cost of semi-supervised learning. Catastrophic forgetting on known categories is limited to −3.3% AP, compared to −28.7% for the semi-supervised baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIDE outperforms the strongest OVOD baseline OWL-v2 [11] by 2.3% AP on novel categories (12.0% vs 9.7%) and by 8.7% AP on known categories (26.6% vs 17.9%), demonstrating that the two-stage pseudo-labeling (OWL-v2 boxes + CLIP filtering) and continual training with known-category pseudo-labels effectively mine open-vocabulary knowledge while preserving prior performance. Against the earlier OVOD method OwL-ViT [4], AIDE achieves a 6× improvement on novel categories (12.0% vs 2.0% AP). Compared to the semi-supervised baseline UTeacher-v1 [5], AIDE wins on both novel (12.0% vs 6.3%) and known (26.6% vs 1.2%) categories, and suffers dramatically less catastrophic forgetting (−3.3% vs −28.7%), because AIDE explicitly includes known-category pseudo-labels during continual training. The fully-supervised upper bound (24.1% novel, 29.9% known) remains higher, but requires $1005.2 in labeling cost versus AIDE's $0, making AIDE a far more cost-effective solution for iterative AV deployment. In the Model Updater ablation (Table 5), OWL-v2 box generation outperforms both SAM (3.9% avg AP) and VL-PLM/RPN (6.3% avg AP), confirming that open-vocabulary detectors provide better recall for novel AV categories than generic region proposal networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIDE composes existing VLM/LLM components (Otter for dense captioning, BLIP-2 for text-guided retrieval, OWL-v2 for zero-shot box generation, CLIP for label filtering, ChatGPT for scenario generation) into a systematic automated data engine pipeline for AV perception. It departs from OVOD methods by adding continual training with pseudo-labels to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, and from semi-supervised/active learning by removing the assumption that labeled and unlabeled data share the same label space. The paper positions itself as the first systematic academic treatment of AV data engines with VLM integration, contrasting with industry solutions (Tesla, Cruise) that remain trade secrets and rely heavily on human intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that VLMs and LLMs can substantially reduce the human labeling bottleneck in AV perception ($0.6 vs. $1005.2 for fully supervised on one novel category) while maintaining or improving detection accuracy, lowering the entry barrier for AV development. However, absolute AP on novel categories remains modest (~12%), the benchmark covers only 5 categories across 2 datasets, and the authors acknowledge VLM/LLM hallucination as a limitation requiring human oversight for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include scalability to dozens of novel categories simultaneously, handling of more semantically ambiguous or rare objects, and whether the iterative loop converges reliably in long-running operational settings.
