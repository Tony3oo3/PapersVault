## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, adapters, prompt-tuning) reduce the number of trainable parameters but still require backpropagating gradients through the entire pretrained backbone, so training-time and memory costs remain high. This limits their practicality for adapting very large foundation models (billions of parameters) on modest hardware. The paper asks whether an adaptation method can be simultaneously efficient in parameters, training time, and memory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224) or video clips (32 frames at 224×224, tubelet 14×14×2, yielding 4096 tokens) and outputs a class label via a linear head on the side network's final output. The frozen backbone (ViT or ViViT) produces intermediate activations at each of its L layers, which are fed into the parallel side network; for video, the token axis is further decomposed into separate spatial and temporal dimensions that the adaptor processes independently before summing.

## Method

LoSA (Low-Rank Side Adaptation) trains a lightweight parallel network on top of a fully frozen backbone, eliminating all backpropagation through the backbone. Each adaptor layer g applies a low-rank MLP (down-project to rank r ≪ d, GeLU, up-project back to d, scaled by α) and alternates between channel-mixing and token-mixing in the style of MLP-Mixer, so that even layers mix across tokens and odd layers mix across channels. For video, token-mixing is factored into separate spatial and temporal projections that are summed. The side network is initialized as an identity (its input y₀ is the backbone's final output b_L, and residual connections preserve the backbone representation at initialization), and each adaptor layer adds only 2rd+1 parameters. Because no gradients flow through the backbone, the method avoids caching backbone activations for the backward pass, yielding large reductions in both training FLOPs and peak memory compared to LoRA, adapters, or prompt-tuning.

## Results

On the VTAB benchmark (ViT-Base backbone), LoSA with r=16 achieves 78.4% average accuracy with only 0.19M trainable parameters, outperforming the prior best HST (78.1% with 0.78M params, 4× more) and all other PEFT methods. On Kinetics-400 video classification, LoSA scales to a frozen ViViT-e backbone (4B params) achieving 86.2% top-1 accuracy, surpassing full finetuning of ViViT-H (84.9%) and ST-Adapter on ViViT-g (83.9%), while using only 8.03M trainable parameters and 12.54 GB memory on a single V100. On iNaturalist 2018 with ViT-g, LoSA is 2.35× faster to train than LoRA while achieving 0.4 points higher accuracy, and uses less training memory than all baselines except linear probing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VTAB, LoSA (r=16) achieves the best average accuracy (78.4%) with 0.19M parameters, beating HST (78.1%, 0.78M params) by 0.3 points while using 4× fewer parameters, and outperforming Convpass (76.6%), RS (76.3%), and FacT-TK (75.6%) by 1.8–2.8 points. At the extreme low-parameter end, LoSA r=4 (0.05M params) still achieves 77.8%, surpassing FacT-TK (75.6% at 0.06M) and BitFit (65.2% at 0.10M) by 2.2 and 12.6 points respectively. On Kinetics-400 video classification, LoSA on ViViT-e (86.2%) outperforms full finetuning of ViViT-H (84.9%) by 1.3 points and ST-Adapter on ViViT-g (83.9%) by 2.3 points, while being the only method that scales to the 4B-parameter backbone on a single V100. On iNaturalist 2018 (ViT-g), LoSA is 2.35× faster to train than LoRA with 0.4 points higher accuracy, and uses less training memory than all baselines except linear probing (which it outperforms by 8 accuracy points). Prompt-tuning is notably the worst in training speed and inference FLOPs due to added tokens, despite being parameter-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoSA builds on two distinct lines of work: the low-rank factorization of weight matrices introduced by LoRA, and the parallel-network-on-frozen-backbone paradigm from LST in NLP. However, it departs fundamentally from the dominant PEFT literature (adapters, prompt-tuning, LoRA) by eliminating backpropagation through the backbone entirely, rather than merely reducing the number of trainable parameters within it. The architectural choice of a low-rank mixer (alternating channel and token projections inspired by MLP-Mixer) replaces the full transformer used by LST, enabling the side network to operate at the backbone's hidden dimension without additional down-projection overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central argument—that parameter efficiency does not imply training-time or memory efficiency because backprop through the frozen backbone dominates compute—reframes how the community should evaluate adaptation methods and is likely to influence future benchmarking practices. The practical demonstration of adapting a 4B-parameter ViViT-e on a single V100 without model parallelism is a significant scalability milestone for practitioners with limited hardware. Limitations include the focus on classification tasks (the authors note future work on more complex vision tasks), inference cost remaining dominated by the backbone, and the method not addressing scenarios where the backbone itself needs task-specific representation changes beyond what a parallel refiner can provide.
