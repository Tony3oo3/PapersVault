## Problem

The state-of-the-art video mirror detector VMD-Net relies on dual correspondences between mirror interior and exterior, but these correspondences can occur entirely inside or outside mirrors, making the approach unreliable. Additionally, the VMD-D dataset is dominated by small mirrors with limited spatial and content variation, poorly reflecting real-world scenarios. The paper identifies that motion between the inside and outside of a mirror is systematically inconsistent (e.g., motion inside is smaller due to increased depth perception) and that this cue has never been exploited for video mirror detection.

## Input / Output

The network consumes three temporally adjacent RGB frames (I_{N-2}, I_{N-1}, I_N) resized to 224×224 pixels. Optical flow is computed between frames N-2 and N-1 using a frozen RAFT model, then linearly extrapolated to estimate flow for frame N. The model outputs per-pixel binary mirror segmentation masks for both frames N-1 and N, corresponding mirror boundary edge maps, and motion-based mirror location predictions, all at the same spatial resolution.

## Method

MG-VMD extracts multi-scale features from all three frames via a shared ResNext-101 + DeepLabV3 backbone (initialized from VMD-Net weights) and introduces two novel modules that exploit motion inconsistency. The Motion Attention Module (MAM) embeds the optical flow field with positional information and processes it through six sequential transformer blocks with self-attention to predict mirror locations, capturing long-range temporal and spatial dependencies. The Motion-Guided Edge Detection Module (MEDM) fuses low- and high-level image features with the optical flow via a cross-attention mechanism, where the flow field acts as a query to weight edge feature contributions, producing motion-guided boundary maps. A Fusion Refinement Module (inspired by PMD-Net) combines MAM predictions, MEDM edge maps, and backbone features through convolutional layers into a binary classification head. Training uses a composite loss with weighted BCE terms for mirror, motion-mirror, and edge predictions on both frames, plus a temporal coherence loss enforcing consistency between consecutive frame predictions.

## Results

On the proposed MMD dataset, MG-VMD achieves the best performance across all four metrics: 87.25% Accuracy, 0.1275 MAE, 0.8694 Fβ (β=0.3), and 0.7251 IoU. The closest competitor, VMD-Net (previous SOTA for video mirror detection), scores 85.44% Accuracy, 0.1456 MAE, 0.8123 Fβ, and 0.7226 IoU. The method also outperforms strong video salient object detection baselines such as FS-Net (85.33% Accuracy, 0.7078 IoU) and F3Net (83.89% Accuracy, 0.6706 IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MG-VMD outperforms all eight baselines on every metric. Against the most relevant prior video mirror detection method, VMD-Net [12], MG-VMD gains +1.81% Accuracy, −0.018 MAE, +5.71% Fβ, and +0.25% IoU, with the Fβ gap being the most substantial. Among image-based mirror detectors, PMD-Net [11] achieves a competitive Fβ of 0.8472 but suffers a very low IoU of 0.4241, indicating poor spatial precision; MG-VMD surpasses it by +13.16% Accuracy and +30.10% IoU. MirrorNet [30] trails MG-VMD by 3.73% Accuracy and 5.89% IoU. Among video salient object detection methods, FS-Net [8] is the strongest competitor (85.33% Accuracy, 0.7078 IoU), still 1.92% behind MG-VMD in Accuracy. F3Net [9] is competitive on Fβ (0.8473 vs. 0.8694) but lags on IoU by 5.45%. The weakest baselines are MGA [10] (56.60% Accuracy) and ALGRF [23] (72.38% Accuracy), which are significantly outperformed, confirming that general-purpose saliency methods are poorly suited to the mirror detection task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video mirror detection task established by VMD-Net (Lin et al., CVPR 2023), which modeled dual correspondences between mirror interiors and exteriors. The authors argue that dual correspondences are unreliable since they can occur entirely inside or outside mirrors, and instead propose modeling motion inconsistency cues derived from optical flow fields. Architecturally, the method builds on PMD-Net's (Lin et al., CVPR 2020) fusion refinement strategy and repurposes a frozen optical flow estimator (RAFT-inspired) as a feature source rather than a direct detector, combining it with transformer-based self-attention (MAM) and cross-attention (MEDM) modules atop a shared ResNext-101/DeepLabV3 backbone. The conceptual motivation draws from neuroscience studies (Tamura et al.) showing humans exploit dynamic perceptual cues to distinguish mirrors, a cue previously unexploited in computational mirror detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMD dataset addresses a real gap: VMD-D's bias toward small, centrally-located mirrors limits generalization to real-world scenarios, and MMD's inclusion of large/full mirrors, low-contrast scenes, and varied spatial distributions makes it a more practical benchmark. The motion inconsistency framing offers a principled alternative to correspondence-based approaches that are known to fail in certain configurations. However, the paper acknowledges a significant open problem: windows exhibit similar motion inconsistency to mirrors, causing over-prediction, and no solution is offered. The modest performance gains over VMD-Net (e.g., IoU 0.725 vs. 0.723) suggest the motion cue helps but is not a silver bullet, and the method's reliance on a frozen optical flow estimator may limit robustness in highly dynamic or occluded scenes.
