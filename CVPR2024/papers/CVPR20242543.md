## Problem

Real-time monocular full-body motion capture that is accurate in world space with physically plausible foot-ground contact remains unsolved, particularly under hand-held moving cameras. Existing learning-based methods either rely on expensive SLAM preprocessing for camera estimation, use camera-centric formulations that create ambiguous regression targets (the same motion appears as different trajectories under different cameras), or require iterative optimization too slow for real-time use.

## Input / Output

The pipeline is split into two stages: an off-the-shelf 2D skeleton detector (PoseDet + RTMPose at 60 fps) extracts 2D joint sequences from monocular video frames, which are then fed as a sliding window into the proxy-to-motion network. The network outputs SMPL body shape and pose parameters, local translations in a human-centric coordinate frame, relative camera extrinsics, hand joint angles, and per-joint contact indicators. Local per-frame motions are accumulated along the sliding window to reconstruct global world-space trajectories, and a 3-iteration neural descent module refines the initial predictions.

## Method

The key insight is to learn motions from a human-centric perspective rather than a camera-centric one: the network predicts local translations and poses in a human coordinate system together with relative camera extrinsics, so the same underlying motion yields consistent outputs regardless of camera trajectory. Training data is synthesized by projecting AMASS 3D motions (augmented with InterHand gestures) through random virtual camera trajectories to produce 2D skeleton sequences with exact world-space ground truth, enabling a consistency loss across viewpoints. A cross-attention mechanism fuses body and hand temporal features for better wrist compatibility. For physical plausibility, a contact-aware neural descent module iteratively refines predictions by computing 2D reprojection misalignment and foot-ground contact status (velocity and height), then uses a cross-attention architecture between state tensors (SMPL params, camera pose, temporal context) and deviation tensors (misalignment, contact) to predict parameter updates—avoiding explicit gradient back-propagation and enabling 73 fps refinement on a single GPU.

## Results

On the EgoBody dataset, ProxyCap achieves W-MPJPE of 385.5 mm, WA-MPJPE of 131.3 mm, PA-MPJPE of 73.5 mm, and ACCEL of 49.6, substantially outperforming the non-SLAM baseline GLAMR (416.1 / 239.0 / 114.3 / 173.5) across all four metrics. On the RICH dataset, ProxyCap records W-MPJPE 629.8 mm, WA-MPJPE 343.6 mm, PA-MPJPE 56.0 mm, and ACCEL 25.3, again beating GLAMR (653.7 / 365.1 / 79.9 / 107.7) on every metric. SLAM-based methods (SLAHMR, PACE) report lower W-MPJPE and WA-MPJPE but require expensive offline SLAM preprocessing and run at 0.07–5.4 fps, whereas ProxyCap operates at 30 fps real-time on an RTX 4060 laptop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GLAMR (the only other non-SLAM baseline), ProxyCap wins on all four metrics on both EgoBody and RICH: on EgoBody it reduces W-MPJPE by 30.6 mm (385.5 vs 416.1), WA-MPJPE by 107.7 mm (131.3 vs 239.0), PA-MPJPE by 40.8 mm (73.5 vs 114.3), and ACCEL by 123.9 (49.6 vs 173.5); on RICH the gaps are 23.9 mm (W-MPJPE), 21.5 mm (WA-MPJPE), 23.9 mm (PA-MPJPE), and 82.4 (ACCEL). Against the SLAM-based methods SLAHMR and PACE, ProxyCap reports higher W-MPJPE and WA-MPJPE (e.g., 385.5 vs 141.1 on EgoBody for SLAHMR), but these methods require offline SLAM reconstruction of the scene, depend on texture-rich backgrounds, and run at 0.07–5.4 fps, making them unsuitable for real-time or in-the-wild hand-held capture. The paper positions ProxyCap as the first real-time (30 fps) full-body capture system that does not rely on SLAM, achieving competitive accuracy with an order-of-magnitude speed advantage over all baselines (60 fps initial estimation + 73 fps neural descent vs. 1.9–2.2 fps + 0.07–5.4 fps for competitors).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the temporal convolution encoder architecture from Pavllo et al. [39] and the broader line of learning-based monocular motion capture (PyMAF-X, FrankMocap), but departs from the camera-centric formulation used by GLAMR, SLAHMR, and PACE by reframing motion recovery in a human-centric coordinate system. It also extends the learned-optimization paradigm (HUND, Song et al., LVD) by introducing a contact-aware neural descent module that jointly considers foot-ground contact and 2D misalignment, rather than relying on explicit gradient back-propagation. The proxy-to-motion data generation strategy (2D skeletons + AMASS motions with random virtual cameras) is a novel middle ground between expensive synthetic image rendering and the limited diversity of real-world annotated datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProxyCap is the first system to achieve real-time (30 FPS) monocular full-body capture with physically plausible foot-ground contact in world space without requiring SLAM or texture-rich backgrounds, making it applicable to hand-held camera scenarios that previously necessitated expensive post-processing pipelines. The human-centric motion formulation offers a clean conceptual alternative to camera-centric regression that may generalize to other dynamic-camera capture tasks. However, the method assumes planar ground and cannot handle motions like stair climbing or boardskating, and the W-MPJPE accuracy still lags behind SLAM-based methods (SLAHMR, PACE) on EgoBody and RICH, suggesting a trade-off between speed and absolute accuracy that remains an open question.
