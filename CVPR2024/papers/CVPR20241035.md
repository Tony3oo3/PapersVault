## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), the dominant paradigm for text-to-3D generation, is fundamentally vulnerable to geometry collapse and poor fine-grained textures. The paper reveals that SDS's optimization process is equivalent to sampling along a stochastic differential equation (SDE) trajectory, where the inherent randomness produces unpredictable guidance signals that do not consistently point toward the desired target at each iteration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 3D object or large-scale 360° scene through a two-stage pipeline: a coarse low-resolution NeRF (rendered at 64×64) followed by a high-resolution textured DMTet mesh (rendered at 512×512). A key design choice is that a single fixed Gaussian noise vector ε* is sampled once at the start and reused across all training iterations, ensuring all ODE trajectories converge to the same target image rather than diverging as in standard SDS.

## Method

Consistent3D replaces the stochastic SDS loss with a Consistency Distillation Sampling (CDS) loss that distills the deterministic ODE sampling prior into the 3D model. At each iteration, the rendered image is perturbed with the fixed noise to form x_{t1}, then one Euler step of the ODE solver produces a less-noisy adjacent sample x̂_{t2}; the CDS loss minimizes the distance between the diffusion model's prediction at x_{t1} and the stop-gradient prediction at x̂_{t2}, effectively pulling the 3D model along the deterministic ODE flow. The time-step schedule uses a monotonically decreasing deterministic anchor t2 (following a square-root schedule over training iterations) paired with a randomly sampled t1 in a small interval [t2+δ, t2+Δ], which provides self-calibration against cumulative error. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that upon convergence, the rendered image error is O(Δ), where Δ is the maximum time-step gap, confirming high-fidelity generation. The framework is built on Instant NGP for the coarse stage and DMTet for the refinement stage, guided by Stable Diffusion v2.1 as the 2D score estimator.

## Results

On the DreamFusion gallery (40 text prompts, 120 rendered viewpoints per object), Consistent3D achieves a CLIP R-Precision of 0.348, outperforming DreamFusion (0.310), Magic3D (0.311), and ProlificDreamer (0.336). The improvement over the strongest baseline (ProlificDreamer, which uses Variational Score Distillation) is +0.012, while the gap over the original SDS-based methods (DreamFusion, Magic3D) is approximately +0.037–0.038. Qualitative comparisons in Figure 5 further show that Consistent3D produces more photorealistic textures and more robust geometry than all three baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Consistent3D outperforms all three baselines on CLIP R-Precision. Against DreamFusion (SDS, 0.310) and Magic3D (SDS, 0.311), the gains are substantial (+0.038 and +0.037 respectively), reflecting the advantage of deterministic ODE-based guidance over the stochastic SDE trajectory that SDS implicitly follows. Against ProlificDreamer (VSD, 0.336), which already addresses some of SDS's variance issues through variational score distillation, Consistent3D still leads by +0.012, suggesting that the consistency distillation approach more faithfully captures the deterministic sampling prior. Qualitatively, Figure 5 shows Consistent3D producing sharper fine-grained textures and fewer geometry artifacts (e.g., floaters, Janus faces) compared to all baselines, consistent with the ablation finding that fixed noise and the monotonic time-step schedule reduce cumulative optimization error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion as a discrete SDE trajectory sampling process, identifying the inherent stochasticity as the root cause of geometry collapse and texture degradation in text-to-3D generation. It then draws on the SDE-ODE duality from score-based generative modeling (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and the consistency distillation framework (Song et al., 2023) to propose a Consistency Distillation Sampling (CDS) loss that replaces stochastic SDE guidance with deterministic ODE trajectory sampling. The method departs from prior SDS-based approaches (DreamFusion, Magic3D) and VSD-based approaches (ProlificDreamer) by reframing 3D generation as a deterministic sampling problem with a monotonically decreasing time schedule, rather than a conventional training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical diagnosis of why SDS is fragile and offers a principled fix grounded in the ODE deterministic prior, which could influence how future text-to-3D methods design their optimization objectives. The Theorem 1 error bound (O(Δ)) gives a concrete convergence guarantee that prior SDS-based methods lacked. However, the approach still depends on a pre-trained 2D diffusion model for score estimation, uses a single Euler step for the ODE solver, and the fixed-noise assumption may limit diversity; whether the deterministic prior generalizes to multi-object scenes, video-to-3D, or other 3D representations (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) remains open.
