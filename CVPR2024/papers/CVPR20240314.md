## Problem

Classifier-free guidance in diffusion models doubles the per-step inference cost by requiring two U-Net forward passes (conditioned and unconditioned), and standard distillation to reduce this cost requires training a full-sized student model that must be retrained for every fine-tuned domain variant. The paper asks whether a lightweight external module can absorb the guidance computation while leaving the base model frozen, so that a single trained module transfers across fine-tuned models without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240314/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, a scalar guidance value g (sampled uniformly from [2,9] during training), and random noise in the VAE latent space (512×512 Stable Diffusion v1.5). The guidance value is broadcast into a spatial tensor of shape (C,H,W) for the full guide model, or a 1D array of length C for the tiny guide model, and is combined with timestep and text embeddings before injection. The output is a single-pass noise prediction that replaces the two-pass CFG computation, yielding a generated image with roughly half the FLOPs of the teacher.

## Method

The core mechanism is an external guide network G, inspired by ControlNet, that takes the guidance value, timestep embedding, and text embedding as inputs and injects its output feature maps into the decoder layers of the frozen U-Net, effectively encoding the 'guidance strength' that trades off sample quality and diversity. Two architectures are studied: a full guide model (a U-Net mirroring ControlNet, ~42% of base parameters) and a tiny guide model (zero-convolution layers only, ~1% of base parameters) that removes the encoder blocks since the guidance scalar carries little spatial information. Training minimizes L2 loss between the guide-augmented single-pass prediction and the teacher's CFG output (1+g)ε(z_t,c) − gε(z_t,∅), with the base U-Net held fixed throughout. After CFG distillation, progressive distillation (halving sampling steps iteratively) further reduces inference to 8–16 steps. Because the base model weights are never modified, the trained guide module can be plugged directly into DreamBooth-fine-tuned U-Nets for different domains (watercolor, realistic, 3D cartoon) with zero additional training.

## Results

On the COCO 30k evaluation set, the full guide model at 16 steps achieves FID 20.74 and CLIP 29.91 at guidance g=8, matching the two-pass teacher (FID 21, CLIP 26.60) while halving FLOPs. At 50 steps, the full guide model reaches FID 18.19 and CLIP 30.05, slightly outperforming the teacher on both metrics. The tiny guide model (only 8.27M parameters, ~1% of the base) achieves FID 19.74 and CLIP 30.23 at 50 steps, but degrades to FID 36.22 at 8 steps due to its reduced capacity. In the plug-and-play generalizability test (Table 3), the tiny guide model plugged into DreamBooth fine-tuned models achieves FID within 2–5 points of full CFG across three domains (Realistic, 3D Cartoon, Water Color) without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DDIM 8×2-step baseline [28], the proposed method is dramatically superior: at g=8, the full guide model at 50 steps achieves FID 18.19 versus 116.60 for DDIM, a gap of nearly 100 FID points, while also using fewer FLOPs. Against the standard two-pass Stable Diffusion v1.5 teacher (PLMS 50×2-step) [17], the full guide model at 50 steps slightly outperforms the teacher (FID 18.19 vs 21.00, CLIP 30.05 vs 26.60) at roughly half the FLOPs, and the 16-step variant (FID 20.74) is essentially on par with the teacher. Against full classifier-free guidance [4] in the plug-and-play generalizability setting (Table 3), the tiny guide model is slightly worse in FID (e.g., 19.88 vs 14.71 on Realistic, 22.23 vs 18.00 on 3D Cartoon) but the gap is modest and CLIP scores are within 1 point, all without any domain-specific retraining. The tiny guide model's main weakness is at very low step counts (8 steps), where its reduced capacity causes significant quality degradation (FID 36.22 at g=8) compared to the full guide model (FID 29.53), though fixing the guidance value partially mitigates this (FID 23.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the ControlNet paradigm—an external lightweight network that injects feature maps into a frozen diffusion U-Net—from conditional image control to the task of distilling classifier-free guidance into a single forward pass. It builds directly on progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho) for reducing sampling steps and on the CFG formulation (Ho & Salimans) as the target to be distilled, but departs from standard distillation by keeping the base model entirely frozen and training only a 1% parameter guide module. The key conceptual shift is treating guidance as an external control signal (analogous to ControlNet's edge maps or skeletons) rather than something that must be absorbed into the student's weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play property—where a single trained guide model transfers across DreamBooth-fine-tuned domains without retraining—addresses a real practical pain point: standard distillation must be redone for every domain-specific fine-tune. Halving inference FLOPs while maintaining comparable FID is a meaningful efficiency gain for deployment. However, the evidence is limited to one base model (SD v1.5) and three fine-tuned domains, and the tiny guide model degrades noticeably at very low step counts (8 steps), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models (SDXL, Imagen) or to other guidance mechanisms. The requirement to run the U-Net and guide module in parallel (rather than a simple batch-of-2) also introduces implementation overhead that partially offsets the FLOP savings in practice.
