## Problem

Existing 3D human modelling methods based on NeRF or multi-resolution hash encoding require hours of training and still render at only a few FPS, making them impractical for real-world applications. While Gaussian Splatting enables real-time rendering for static and dynamic scenes, adapting it to articulated humans from monocular videos raises two unresolved challenges: how to properly integrate articulated human structure (pose, LBS weights) into the Gaussian framework, and how to optimize the Gaussian set fast enough to converge in minutes rather than hours.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video (100 frames sampled every 5), calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, per-frame human region masks, and pre-estimated SMPL shape (β) and pose (θ) parameters. It produces an articulated 3D Gaussian Splatting model of approximately 13k Gaussians (3.5 MB storage) defined in a canonical space, which can be posed to arbitrary new poses via LBS and rendered at up to 189 FPS at 1024p resolution on an RTX 3090. Training converges in 1–2 minutes, and the learned LBS weights and rectified poses are cached at inference for zero additional MLP cost.

## Method

GauHuman encodes 3D Gaussians in a canonical space and transforms them to a posed space by rotating and translating each Gaussian's position and covariance with an LBS matrix, exploiting the Gaussian property that affine transforms of Gaussians remain Gaussians. Rather than learning LBS weights from scratch, an MLP predicts small offsets added to the nearest SMPL vertex's LBS weights (via a softmax over log-weights), and a separate pose-refinement MLP predicts relative joint-angle corrections to the input SMPL pose. For fast optimization, 3D Gaussians are initialized from SMPL vertex points (encoding human structure), split and clone operations are gated by a KL-divergence distance measure between nearby Gaussians (reducing time complexity from O(U²) to O(U) via k-NN), a novel merge operation combines redundant close Gaussians, and a SMPL-prior-based prune removes Gaussians far from the body. Rendering uses a tile-based differentiable rasterizer with a composite loss of color, mask, SSIM, and LPIPS terms.

## Results

On ZJU_MoCap, GauHuman achieves the best PSNR (31.34 dB) and LPIPS* (30.51) among all compared methods, while training in just 1 minute and rendering at 189 FPS. On MonoCap, it reaches 33.45 dB PSNR and 13.35 LPIPS* with 2-minute training and 154 FPS. These results are achieved with only ~13k 3D Gaussians (3.5 MB storage), compared to 100k+ Gaussians or millions of sampled points in NeRF-based baselines. The closest competitor in quality is AS (32.48 dB PSNR on MonoCap, 13.18 LPIPS*), but AS requires 10 hours of training and renders at only 0.29 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauHuman outperforms all nine baselines on PSNR for both datasets: 31.34 dB on ZJU_MoCap (vs. 31.01 for InstantNVR, the nearest competitor) and 33.45 dB on MonoCap (vs. 32.68 for HumanNeRF). On LPIPS*, GauHuman is best on ZJU_MoCap (30.51 vs. 33.38 for HumanNeRF) and nearly tied on MonoCap (13.35 vs. 13.18 for AS). However, GauHuman does not achieve the best SSIM on either dataset — AS leads with 0.975 (ZJU) and 0.988 (Mono) versus GauHuman's 0.965 and 0.985. The most striking advantage is speed: GauHuman trains in 1–2 minutes and renders at 154–189 FPS, compared to 10 hours training and <1.5 FPS for NeRF-based methods (NB, AN, AS, HumanNeRF), 1.5 hours and 10–16 FPS for DVA, and 3–10 minutes and 0.75–4 FPS for the hash-encoding methods (InstantNVR, InstantAvatar). Generalizable methods (PixelNeRF, NHP) are both slower to fine-tune (1h after 10h pre-training) and substantially worse in quality (e.g., NHP achieves only 28.25 dB PSNR on ZJU_MoCap). DVA is the only other method with real-time-capable rendering (16.5 FPS) but still requires 90× more training time and produces visible artifacts on garments and faces.

## Relation to Prior Work

GauHuman sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), which achieved real-time rendering for static scenes, and articulated human NeRF methods (HumanNeRF, Neural Body, AN, AS), which modelled posed humans in a canonical space via LBS but required hours of training and sub-1 FPS rendering. The paper transplants the canonical-space + LBS paradigm from HumanNeRF into the Gaussian Splatting framework, replacing the implicit radiance field with explicit 3D Gaussians whose positions and covariances are transformed by the LBS matrix. It further departs from the original 3D Gaussian Splatting adaptive control by introducing KL-divergence-guided split/clone, a novel merge operation, and SMPL-prior-based pruning, all motivated by the structured nature of human geometry rather than the unstructured point clouds of static scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 189 FPS rendering speed (vs. <1 FPS for NeRF-based methods) and 1–2 minute training (vs. ~10 hours) make real-time, interactive 3D human avatars from a single monocular video practically feasible for AR/VR, telepresence, and game applications that were previously out of reach. The paper also demonstrates that only ~13k Gaussians (3.5 MB) are sufficient for photorealistic results, suggesting the representation is highly compact. Key open questions remain: extracting editable 3D meshes from the Gaussian representation, and handling loose or complex clothing that violates the LBS assumption—both acknowledged as limitations. The KL-divergence-guided adaptive control and human-prior initialization are general ideas that could transfer to other articulated or structured 3D Gaussian Splatting tasks beyond humans.
