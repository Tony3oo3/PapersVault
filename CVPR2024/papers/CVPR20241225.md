## Problem

Existing geometry problem solvers produce solutions that are either redundant and vague (symbolic methods applying unnecessary theorems) or opaque black boxes (neural generative models), making them unsuitable for educational settings where explainable reasoning is essential. Additionally, the small scale and incomplete annotations of available GPS datasets (e.g., Geometry3K lacks solution steps, GeoQA lacks theorem annotations) prevent neural components from adequately learning geometric theorem knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a geometric diagram (parsed by PGDPNet into primitives: lines, angles, arcs, and their relations) and a textual problem description (parsed by rule-based regex into primitives, values, and the target query) as input, unifying both into a single formal-language proposition set. The output is a numerical answer accompanied by an ordered sequence of reasoning steps, where each step explicitly records the child target group, the applied theorem rule, and the parent target it serves, ensuring every step is justified by a specific need rather than being a generic forward deduction.

## Method

E-GPS introduces a Top-Down Problem Solver (TD-PS) that reasons backward from the problem target via tree search, using two mechanisms: target decomposition (splitting a parent target into child targets based on a theorem's premise conditions) and condition processing (recursively checking whether child targets are solvable from known propositions). This 'focus on what is needed' approach guarantees that every theorem application in the final solution is necessary, eliminating the redundancy inherent in forward-reasoning methods. To guide the search efficiently, a transformer-based theorem predictor (seq2seq, 6 layers, 12 heads) is trained to predict relevant theorem indices given the proposition set and target. To address the data scarcity for this predictor, a Bottom-Up Problem Generator (BU-PG) forward-applies theorems on original problems to automatically construct 17,607 new geometry problems with explicit multi-step solutions (aug-Geo3K), which are then used to train the enhanced predictor for both one-step and multi-step theorem prediction.

## Results

On Geometry3K with KB¹, E-GPS achieves 64.7% accuracy with 3.49–4.19 average steps, outperforming Inter-GPS (57.5%, 7.10 steps) by 7.2% while halving the number of theorem applications. With ground-truth parsing, E-GPS(GT) reaches 79.8% accuracy at 3.35–3.99 steps versus Inter-GPS(GT) at 78.3%. Under the larger KB², E-GPS*(GT) attains 89.8% accuracy with only 1.57–2.18 steps, marginally surpassing GeoDRL*(GT) (89.4%, 2.34 steps). On theorem prediction (aug-Geo3K), E-GPS achieves rSum of 435.0 versus Inter-GPS's 189.2, with 88.9% R@5 on one-step and 80.3% R@5 on multi-step prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three neural-based baselines (FiLM 31.7%, FiLM-BERT 32.8%, FiLM-BART 33.0%), E-GPS (64.7%) wins by over 30 percentage points, confirming the advantage of symbolic reasoning over pure feature-based approaches for geometry. Compared to Inter-GPS (57.5%, 7.10 steps with GT), E-GPS improves accuracy by 7.2% while reducing average steps from 7.10 to 3.35–3.99, demonstrating that top-down target decomposition eliminates redundant theorem applications. Against GeoDRL (68.4%, 2.34 steps with KB²), E-GPS* (67.9%) is marginally lower by 0.5% in accuracy but achieves fewer steps (1.63–2.28 vs. 2.34); with GT parsing, E-GPS*(GT) edges out GeoDRL*(GT) by 0.4% (89.8% vs. 89.4%) while using fewer steps (1.57–2.18 vs. 2.34). On theorem prediction, E-GPS's BU-PG-augmented predictor dramatically outperforms Inter-GPS's predictor (rSum 435.0 vs. 189.2), with 41.0% and 29.4% absolute gains at R@3 and R@5 on one-step prediction respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symbolic-based geometry problem solving lineage (Inter-GPS, GeoDRL) by inverting the reasoning direction: instead of forward-chaining from known conditions toward the target, TD-PS performs top-down tree search starting from the target and decomposing it via theorem preconditions. It reuses established components—PGDPNet for diagram parsing, rule-based text parsing, and a transformer seq2seq model for theorem prediction—but introduces two genuinely new mechanisms: the target-decomposition/condition-processing solver and the BU-PG data augmentation pipeline that converts forward theorem applications into well-annotated training problems. The work departs from neural-based solvers (NGS, GeoQA) by rejecting end-to-end generation in favor of explicit, rule-grounded reasoning steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BU-PG augmentation strategy (17,607 constructed problems from 3,002 originals) offers a practical, generalizable recipe for alleviating the chronic data scarcity in symbolic reasoning benchmarks, and the 41% R@3 gain in theorem prediction demonstrates its value. However, the approach remains tightly coupled to a fixed theorem knowledge base and a specific parsing pipeline; accuracy gains over strong baselines are marginal (≤1.5%), and the solver cannot guarantee shortest solutions. Open questions include scaling to larger theorem bases, handling ambiguous or incomplete diagram parsing, and whether the top-down decomposition generalizes beyond planar geometry to 3D or non-Euclidean settings.
