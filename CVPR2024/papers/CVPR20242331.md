## Problem

Existing 3D human-object interaction methods predict isolated elements (contact, affordance, spatial relation) from the perspective of either the human or the object, overlooking the inherent correlations between interaction counterparts. This leads to two forms of uncertainty: intention uncertainty from the diversity of HOIs, and region uncertainty from limited views and mutual occlusions that hide interaction regions in images. The paper addresses this by jointly anticipating all three elements while exploiting the intention and geometric affinities shared between humans and objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image with manually segmented human and object masks, a pre-inferred SMPL-H mesh (6890 vertices parameterized by pose θ∈R^{52×3} and shape β∈R^{10}), and an object point cloud as input. It produces three outputs: per-vertex contact probabilities on the 6890 SMPL-H vertices, per-point affordance probabilities on the object point cloud, and a 3D object center position. The spatial relation is represented via a proxy sphere with a category-specific predefined radius, and the human mesh must be recovered separately (e.g., via UBody + DWPose) before feeding into LEMON.

## Method

LEMON first extracts image features (HRNet) and geometric features (DGCNN or PointNet++) for the human mesh and object point cloud. A multi-branch attention mechanism uses the image feature as shared key/value and human/object features as queries to extract intention tokens, with a cosine similarity loss enforcing semantic consistency between the two tokens. Normal curvatures of both geometries are encoded to high dimension and cross-attended to capture geometric correlations; these curvature-guided features are then fused with the intention-conditioned geometric features via cross-transformer layers to produce contact and affordance representations. The contact feature is injected as a constraint into a cross-attention module that predicts the object center position, with a combined loss of focal+dice (contact/affordance), cross-entropy + cosine similarity (semantic consistency), and L2 distance on both absolute and pelvis-relative coordinates (spatial relation).

## Results

On the 3DIR benchmark, LEMON (DGCNN backbone) achieves 0.78 F1 and 7.55 cm geometric error for dense human contact, 88.51 AUC and 41.34 aIOU for object affordance, and 0.010 MSE for spatial relation prediction. These represent improvements of 59.1% F1, 77.0% geometric error reduction, 28.0% SIM, and 80.3% MSE reduction over the respective strongest baselines (DECO, IAG, and PopUp). The PointNet++ backbone variant (Ours P.) also outperforms all baselines, confirming the framework's robustness to backbone choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEMON outperforms all six baselines across every metric on the 3DIR benchmark. For human contact, LEMON (DGCNN) achieves 0.78 F1, surpassing DECO (0.69 F1, +13.0%) and BSTRO (0.55 F1, +41.8%), while reducing geometric error from 15.25 cm (DECO) to 7.55 cm. For object affordance, LEMON reaches 88.51 AUC and 41.34 aIOU, beating IAG (86.63 AUC, 38.57 aIOU) and 3D-AffordanceNet (85.49 AUC, 35.42 aIOU). For spatial relation, LEMON achieves 0.010 MSE, a 76.2% reduction over PopUp (0.027 MSE) and an 76.2% reduction over DJ-RN (0.042 MSE). The consistent superiority across all three interaction elements supports the paper's central claim that jointly modeling intention and geometric correlations between interaction counterparts yields better results than estimating each element in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEMON departs from the prevailing paradigm of predicting isolated interaction elements (contact, affordance, spatial relation) from a single counterpart's perspective. Prior contact methods (HULC, BSTRO, DECO) map image semantics to human vertices without leveraging object geometry; affordance methods (3D-AffordanceNet, IAG) reason from object structure or 2D interactions; spatial relation methods (DJ-RN, CHORUS, Object Pop-Up) project 2D relations or use synthetic views. LEMON's conceptual shift is to treat the human and object as coupled counterparts whose intention affinity (object affordance revealing human intent) and geometry affinity (matching curvatures) jointly constrain all three elements, using multi-branch attention for intention extraction and curvature-guided cross-attention for geometric correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified joint-prediction framework and the 3DIR dataset (5k images with dense contact, affordance, and spatial relation annotations) provide a practical testbed for 3D HOI understanding that was previously fragmented across separate benchmarks. A key limitation is the dependency on pre-inferred SMPL-H meshes, which restricts deployment to settings where human pose is already available; the authors note integrating HMR and multi-modal signals (text, audio) as future directions. The finding that removing the affordance branch degrades spatial relation prediction (MSE 0.010→0.019) suggests the three elements are more interdependent than prior isolated-prediction methods assumed, pointing toward tighter coupling in future architectures.
