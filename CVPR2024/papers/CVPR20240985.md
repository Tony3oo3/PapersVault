## Problem

Despite the rapid proliferation of diverse 3DMM-driven volumetric head avatar representations (NeRFBlendShape, INSTA, Next3D), no practical method exists to perform high-level 3D avatar editing that is simultaneously adaptable across representations, user-friendly via single-image 2D editing, faithful across expressions and viewpoints, and flexible enough to support both global style transfer and local geometry changes. Existing approaches either cause identity shifts, fail to preserve multi-view consistency, or are locked to a specific avatar framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240985/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single 2D edited image (produced by any off-the-shelf tool such as Photoshop, DragGAN, or InstructPix2Pix) together with the original volumetric head avatar and its 3DMM expression parameters. It outputs a decoupled 3D expression-aware modification field—comprising a geometry deformation (point displacement) and a texture modification (color + blending weight)—that is applied at render time to produce consistent novel views of the edited avatar under arbitrary camera poses and facial expressions, without modifying the underlying avatar representation.

## Method

The core architecture is an expression-aware modification generator with separate geometry and texture branches, each producing a tri-plane feature map conditioned on 3DMM mesh vertices (via FLAME) and a 1024-dim latent code, yielding a deformation field and a modification color field that are decoupled from the original avatar's implicit field. Training uses a novel expression-dependent distillation scheme: geometry pairs are synthesized by perturbing 3DMM shape parameters in Next3D, while texture pairs are created by applying 2D editing tools (segmentation-based painting, makeup transfer) to rendered faces and lifting them to 3D via PTI inversion, with the teacher's latent code concatenated to the modification code as implicit guidance. At inference, an auto-decoding optimization in W-space searches for the modification latent code that best matches the user-edited image, using L2 + LPIPS losses with a segmentation-based reweighting strategy that upweights fine-grained regions (eyes, lips, eyebrows) to prevent underfitting of detailed texture edits.

## Results

In the user study (30 participants, 12 editing groups), GeneAvatar achieves 43.33% overall preference for geometry editing and 79.44% for texture editing, substantially outperforming all baselines. For identity preservation, GeneAvatar scores 42.22% (geometry) and 76.11% (texture) in the user study, and 0.8845 / 0.9147 cosine similarity (ArcFace) for cross-view identity consistency, versus the next-best baselines at 0.8704 / 0.8476. Temporal consistency is also strongest for GeneAvatar at 41.67% (geometry) and 80.00% (texture), indicating stable editing across expression and viewpoint changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeneAvatar outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric for both geometry and texture editing. Roop (face-swapping) is weakest on texture editing (5.56% overall user preference, 0.7320 identity similarity) because it cannot transfer non-face-like textures, and also underperforms on geometry (29.44% overall). PVP (personalized StyleGAN inversion) preserves identity better than Roop (0.8704 vs. 0.8373 geometry identity similarity) but produces smaller-magnitude edits and suffers from expression/pose shifts, yielding only 23.33% overall geometry and 12.78% overall texture preference. Next3D (3DMM-based 3D GAN fine-tuned twice) achieves the lowest user-study scores across the board (3.89% geometry, 2.22% texture overall) due to significant identity shift in untouched regions, though its identity similarity scores (0.8547/0.8500) are closer to GeneAvatar than the other baselines. GeneAvatar's advantage is most dramatic in texture editing, where it leads the next-best method (PVP) by over 66 percentage points in overall user preference and by 0.067 in ArcFace identity similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Next3D 3DMM-based 3D GAN architecture (tri-plane feature maps conditioned on FLAME mesh vertices) and StyleGAN's W-space latent optimization, repurposing them as a *modification* generator rather than a full avatar generator. It departs from prior 3D avatar editing approaches (3D GAN fine-tuning, PVP's personalized StyleGAN inversion, face-swapping) by formulating editing as a decoupled, expression-aware modification field that is representation-agnostic, and from prior 2D editing by lifting single-image edits into a consistent 3D field via auto-decoding. The knowledge-distillation training scheme—drawing paired geometry edits from Next3D's latent space and texture edits from 2D makeup/segmentation tools with PTI inversion—repurposes large-scale generative models as synthetic data sources, a strategy borrowed from the broader 'learn from pre-trained generators' literature (InstructNeRF2NeRF, DreamEditor).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generic, representation-agnostic formulation is practically significant: a single editing pipeline works across NeRFBlendShape, INSTA, and Next3D avatars without retraining, lowering the barrier for downstream VR/AR applications. The expression-aware modification field addresses a real gap—prior methods either lose multi-view/expression consistency or cause identity drift—making the edited avatar usable in animation pipelines. Key limitations remain: the method cannot add new objects (hats, accessories) or modify hairstyle, and the evaluation is limited to 19 avatars and 12 editing groups, leaving open whether the distillation scheme and modification-field formulation generalize to body avatars, extreme expressions, or larger-scale deployment. The reliance on a 3DMM-based GAN as the distillation teacher also constrains the diversity of learnable modifications to what that teacher can produce.
