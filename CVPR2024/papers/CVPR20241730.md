## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs rely on modality-specific encoders and projection modules that differ in architecture, making it difficult to unify them into a single scalable framework. Pretrained encoders with reliable performance are restricted to common modalities (image, audio, video), limiting MLLMs from expanding to rarer modalities like point clouds, depth maps, IMU, and fMRI. The paper asks whether a single universal encoder and projection module can effectively align all modalities to language within one model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241730/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the eight modalities is first converted to a token sequence by a lightweight 1D or 2D convolution layer (e.g., audio becomes a 2D spectrogram, point clouds are sampled with 2D geometric prior), then processed by the shared encoder. Learnable modality tokens (30×1024 per modality) are concatenated with the input tokens and passed through the UPM; only the projected modality tokens are extracted as a fixed-length summary fed to the LLM, regardless of the original input length. The LLM (LLaMA2-7B) generates free-form text responses including captions, short answers, multi-turn conversation replies, and reasoning chains, with modality tokens always positioned at the start of the input sequence.

## Method

OneLLM replaces modality-specific encoders with a single frozen CLIP-ViT as a universal cross-modal encoder, leveraging the finding that pretrained transformers transfer well across modalities. The key architectural component is the Universal Projection Module (UPM), which consists of K=3 parallel projection experts (each 8 transformer blocks, 88M parameters) combined via a soft mixture-of-experts router that computes input-dependent routing weights, allowing the module to dynamically allocate capacity across modalities. Training follows a progressive alignment pipeline: first an image projection module is trained on LAION-400M to establish the image-to-language bridge, then multiple copies of this pretrained module are mixed into the UPM, and additional modalities are aligned in stages ordered by data magnitude (video/audio/point cloud, then depth/IMU/fMRI) with balanced sampling to prevent catastrophic forgetting. A curated 2M-item multimodal instruction dataset spanning all eight modalities is then used for full fine-tuning of the LLM to unlock instruction-following and reasoning capabilities.

## Results

On the 12 image-text benchmarks (Table 1), OneLLM-7B achieves 59.5 on GQA, 71.6 on VQAv2, 58.9 on OKVQA, 45.9 on Vizwiz, 115.9 CIDEr on NoCaps, and 1392.0 on MME. On video QA, OneLLM-7B reaches 57.3% on NextQA and 56.5% on MSVD, outperforming ChatBridge-13B and AnyMAL-13B. On audio tasks, OneLLM-7B achieves 29.1 CIDEr on Clotho Caption and 57.9% on Clotho AQA. On point cloud captioning (Objaverse), OneLLM-7B scores 42.2 BLEU-1 and 45.3 ROUGE-L, substantially exceeding PointLLM-13B (9.7 BLEU-1, 12.8 ROUGE-L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneLLM-7B substantially outperforms other MLLMs: it beats ChatBridge-13B by 17.7 points on GQA (59.5 vs 41.8) and 13.7 on OKVQA (58.9 vs 45.2), and surpasses AnyMAL-70B on VQAv2 (71.6 vs 64.2), OKVQA (58.9 vs 42.6), and Vizwiz (45.9 vs 33.8) despite using a 7B LLM versus 70B. On video QA, OneLLM-7B leads ChatBridge-13B on MSVD (56.5 vs 45.3) and AnyMAL-13B on NextQA (57.3 vs 47.9) and How2QA (65.7 vs 59.6). On audio, OneLLM-7B exceeds ChatBridge-13B on Clotho Caption CIDEr (29.1 vs 26.2) and matches fully-finetuned Pengi on Clotho AQA (57.9 vs 64.5, zero-shot vs fine-tuned). However, OneLLM-7B trails vision-specialized LLMs: LLaVA-v1.5 leads on GQA (62.0 vs 59.5), VQAv2 (78.5 vs 71.6), TVQA (58.2 vs 34.0), and MME (1510.7 vs 1392.0), while Qwen-VL dominates on TVQA (61.5 vs 34.0) and SQA (68.2 vs 63.4). The paper frames this as evidence that the gap between MLLMs and vision LLMs has narrowed, with OneLLM achieving competitive vision performance while simultaneously handling seven additional modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneLLM departs from the dominant MLLM paradigm (X-LLM, ChatBridge, AnyMAL) that assigns each modality its own pretrained encoder and projection module, instead leveraging the insight from Lu et al. (2021) and MetaTransformer (2023) that a single frozen pretrained transformer can serve as a universal cross-modal computation engine. The architecture combines this frozen CLIP-ViT encoder with a Universal Projection Module (UPM) that applies the soft mixture-of-experts routing mechanism from Puigcerver et al. (2023) over multiple image-pretrained projection experts, and uses learnable modality tokens in the spirit of BLIP-2's Q-Former to produce fixed-length representations. The progressive alignment pipeline (image → video/audio/point cloud → depth/IMU/fMRI) is a practical departure from joint multimodal pretraining, motivated by data-scale imbalance across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OneLLM demonstrates that a single unified encoder-projection-LLM stack can handle eight heterogeneous modalities (including rarely-studied ones like IMU and fMRI), offering a scalable template where adding a new modality requires only a lightweight tokenizer and additional projection experts rather than an entirely new encoder. The curated 2M-item multimodal instruction dataset is a practical resource for the community. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the absence of large-scale high-quality training data for non-image modalities and the lack of fine-grained understanding for high-resolution images or long video/audio sequences, leaving open whether the frozen-encoder approach can be replaced by a truly modality-agnostic learned encoder as data scales.
