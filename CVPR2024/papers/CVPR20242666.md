## Problem

Dynamic scene novel view synthesis has struggled to simultaneously achieve photorealistic quality, real-time high-resolution rendering, and compact model storage. Grid-based 4D representations (e.g., K-Planes, HexPlane) cannot fully adapt to scene structure dynamics and degrade at high resolution, while per-frame 3D Gaussian approaches incur prohibitive model size and training overhead for video-length sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (18–46 synchronized cameras) with known camera poses and initializes from SfM sparse point clouds across all timestamps. It produces a compact Spacetime Gaussian model that renders 6-DoF video at arbitrary novel viewpoints and timestamps, supporting resolutions up to 8K at 60+ FPS on a single RTX 4090 GPU. Training is conducted at half resolution (1352×1014) for the Neural 3D Video Dataset and full resolution (2048×1088) for Technicolor, with a 50-frame sequence taking 40–60 minutes on an A6000.

## Method

The core representation is the Spacetime Gaussian (STG), which extends each 3D Gaussian with three temporal components: a 1D Gaussian radial basis function for temporal opacity (modeling emerging/vanishing content), a cubic polynomial for position trajectory, and a linear polynomial for quaternion rotation. Instead of storing 48 spherical harmonic coefficients per Gaussian, the method stores a compact 9-dimensional feature vector (3 base RGB + 3 view-direction + 3 time) that is rasterized via differentiable splatting and decoded by a shallow 2-layer MLP into the final pixel color, reducing per-Gaussian parameters while preserving view- and time-dependent appearance. A guided sampling strategy additionally places new Gaussians along rays at high-error pixels, constrained by a coarse depth map from the splatting pass, to improve convergence in sparsely covered distant regions. Optimization uses standard differentiable splatting with L1 + D-SSIM loss and aggressive density control to keep model size compact (~200 MB for 300 frames).

## Results

On the Neural 3D Video Dataset (6 scenes, 300 frames), the method achieves 32.05 PSNR, 0.044 LPIPS, and 140 FPS at 1352×1014 resolution with a 200 MB model size, outperforming all baselines on quality and speed simultaneously. On the Google Immersive Dataset, it reaches 29.2 PSNR and 99 FPS (vs. 4 FPS for HyperReel), and on the Technicolor Dataset it achieves 33.6 PSNR at 86.7 FPS. The lite-version model renders 8K 6-DoF video at 66 FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. Against the closest quality competitor MixVoxels-X (31.73 PSNR, 0.064 LPIPS), it gains +0.32 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 31%, while rendering 30× faster (140 vs. 4.6 FPS) at half the model size (200 vs. 500 MB). Compared to HexPlane (31.71 PSNR, 0.075 LPIPS), the improvement is +0.34 dB PSNR and 41% lower LPIPS, with HexPlane not reporting FPS. Against NeRFPlayer (30.69 PSNR, 0.111 LPIPS, 0.05 FPS, 5130 MB), the method leads by +1.36 dB PSNR, 60% lower LPIPS, 2800× faster rendering, and 25× smaller model. On the Google Immersive Dataset, it surpasses HyperReel by +0.4 dB PSNR, 58% lower LPIPS (0.081 vs. 0.193), and 25× faster rendering (99 vs. 4 FPS) at the same per-frame size (1.2 MB). The speed advantage is most dramatic: 140 FPS vs. 37.7 (MixVoxels-L), 10.9 (StreamRF), 4.6 (MixVoxels-X), 2 (HyperReel), 0.3 (K-Planes), and 0.05 (NeRFPlayer), making it the only method achieving real-time high-resolution rendering among all compared approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from static to dynamic scenes by introducing Spacetime Gaussians with temporal opacity and polynomial motion/rotation, departing from the grid- or plane-factorized representations (K-Planes, HexPlane, MixVoxels) that dominate prior dynamic view synthesis. It also replaces the standard spherical harmonic color encoding with a compact 9-dimensional feature vector processed by a tiny MLP, a design choice that trades per-primitive parameters for a shared neural decoder. Among concurrent 4D Gaussian approaches, the paper distinguishes itself by using polynomial (rather than linear or per-frame discrete) motion models and by adding guided sampling to address sparse coverage in distant regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 8K 6-DoF rendering at 60+ FPS with a ~200 MB model for 300 frames is a practical milestone that makes dynamic free-viewpoint video viable for VR/AR and broadcast applications where prior methods were too slow or too large. The feature-splatting design (9-dim features + 2-layer MLP instead of 48-dim SH) offers a reusable pattern for balancing per-primitive storage with view/time-dependent appearance in any splatting-based renderer. Key open questions remain: the method cannot train on-the-fly (limiting streaming use), is restricted to multi-view inputs (no monocular generalization), and the polynomial motion model may struggle with highly non-smooth or topologically changing dynamics.
