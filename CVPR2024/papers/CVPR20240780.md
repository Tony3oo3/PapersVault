## Problem

Existing neural implicit representations based on SDF or UDF suffer from interpolation errors when converting to explicit surface points via marching cubes, because grid edge points are estimated by linearly interpolating between corner values. This degradation is particularly severe for non-watertight shapes, complex multi-layer structures, and small point clouds where large grids amplify the error. The paper asks whether a distance field formulation can enable direct, interpolation-free surface point estimation while still handling arbitrary topologies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud sampled from the shape surface (normalized to the [-1,1]^3 cube) and produces grid edge points (GEP) at a configurable resolution (default 257^3 grid corners for fair comparison with marching cubes), optionally followed by a watertight mesh via masked screened Poisson reconstruction. During training, 2M spatial points are sampled per shape, and rays are discretized on 257×257 orthogonal planes with 256 points sampled per ray every 10 epochs. Unlike SDF/UDF methods that require a single scalar per point, UODFs output three distance values plus a binary mask per point, one for each orthogonal direction.

## Method

UODFs redefine the distance field so that each spatial point stores the closest surface distance along each of three orthogonal directions (LR, FB, UD) rather than the global minimum over all directions. Each UODF component is a collection of 1D distance fields along parallel rays, with the property that the 1D derivative magnitude is always 1 and discontinuities can occur between adjacent rays. Three separate 10×256 MLP networks (one per direction) regress the UODF values, taking as input a 45-dimensional vector combining 3D coordinates with a 42-dimensional position-encoded 2D ray coordinate; companion 3×256 MLPs predict a binary mask for undefined (non-intersected) rays. Training uses a composite loss of value regression, derivative magnitude constraint (|UODF'|=1), and a predicted-surface-point consistency term. At inference, surface points along each ray are estimated directly from equally-spaced sample points by segmenting on gradient sign and averaging predictions within each segment, then points from the three orthogonal directions are fused by retaining only those supported by at least three adjacent edges—eliminating the interpolation step entirely.

## Results

On the MGN10 non-watertight dataset, UODFs achieves a CD-GEP of 0.227×10⁻⁵, outperforming the closest UDF baseline GIFS (4.95×10⁻⁵) by over 20×, and CD-Mesh of 1.93×10⁻⁵ with NC-Mesh of 99.6%. On the Thingi32 watertight dataset, UODFs achieves CD-GEP of 0.378×10⁻⁵, beating the SOTA SDF method NGLOD5 (0.432×10⁻⁵) and CD-Mesh of 2.69×10⁻⁵ vs. NGLOD5's 2.83×10⁻⁵. In the multi-resolution experiment (Fig. 9), UODFs consistently outperforms MeshUDF at all grid resolutions from 32³ to 256³, even when MeshUDF is given ground-truth UDF values as input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UODFs decisively outperforms all six baselines across both watertight and non-watertight settings. On MGN10 (non-watertight), UODFs achieves CD-GEP of 0.227×10⁻⁵, beating GIFS (4.95×10⁻⁵) by ~22×, HSDF (13.0×10⁻⁵) by ~57×, and NDF (128×10⁻⁵) by ~564×, while also achieving the highest NC-Mesh (99.6% vs. GIFS's 92.0%). On Thingi32 (watertight), UODFs (0.378×10⁻⁵ CD-GEP) narrowly beats NGLOD5 (0.432×10⁻⁵) and substantially outperforms NGLOD3 (0.684×10⁻⁵) and SIREN (149×10⁻⁵). In the multi-resolution comparison (Fig. 9), UODFs surpasses MeshUDF at every tested resolution (32³–256³) even though MeshUDF receives ground-truth UDF values, demonstrating that the interpolation-free estimation strategy eliminates the error source that plagues marching-cubes-based methods. The paper also notes that UODFs' non-watertight reconstruction accuracy (Table 2) is on par with its watertight accuracy (Table 1), whereas UDF-based methods degrade significantly on open shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by redefining the distance field for neural implicit representation: instead of taking the minimum distance over all directions (SDF/UDF), UODFs measure distance along a single orthogonal direction, yielding three separate 1D fields per shape. Conceptually, it draws a loose parallel to the 2001 directed distance fields (DDFs) [19] but repurposes the directional-distance idea for neural fitting and interpolation-free grid-edge-point estimation, a problem DDFs never addressed. It departs from the UDF-based line (NDF, HSDF, GIFS, MeshUDF) that still relies on extended marching cubes and thus inherits interpolation error, and from SDF-based methods (SIREN, NGLOD) that are restricted to watertight topology. The paper also challenges the working assumption that MLPs can only fit continuous fields, demonstrating that the discontinuous UODF between adjacent rays is well-learnable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UODFs offer a unified, interpolation-free reconstruction pipeline that handles watertight, non-watertight, multi-layer, and assembling shapes without topology-specific sign assignment, which is practically valuable for downstream tasks (classification, segmentation) that consume surface point clouds. The multi-resolution experiment showing UODFs outperforming MeshUDF even when the latter receives ground-truth UDF values underscores that the interpolation-free formulation is fundamentally superior, not just a fitting improvement. Key open questions include whether the three directional fields can be collapsed into a single network (currently a parameter overhead), whether the approach extends to real-time rendering, and how it scales to very high-resolution or large-scene reconstruction.
