## Problem

YOLO detectors dominate real-time object detection but rely on NMS post-processing, which adds latency, introduces sensitive hyperparameters (confidence and IoU thresholds), and destabilizes both speed and accuracy. DETRs eliminate NMS through end-to-end bipartite matching but are too computationally expensive for real-time use, so their NMS-free advantage is negated by slow inference. The paper asks whether a DETR can be redesigned to outperform the best YOLOs in both speed and accuracy while remaining truly end-to-end.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241367/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RT-DETR takes a 640x640 RGB image and produces a fixed set of bounding boxes with class labels in a single forward pass, requiring no NMS or any other post-processing. The backbone's last three feature stages (S3, S4, S5) are fed into the encoder, and the decoder outputs one-to-one object predictions via bipartite matching. A distinctive practical feature is flexible speed tuning: removing trailing decoder layers at inference time trades a small AP drop for significant latency reduction without retraining.

## Method

RT-DETR replaces the vanilla multi-scale Transformer encoder with an efficient hybrid encoder that decouples intra-scale interaction from cross-scale fusion. Intra-scale interaction (AIFI) applies a single-scale Transformer only to the highest-level feature S5, while cross-scale fusion (CCFF) uses a CNN-based PANet-style structure with RepConv blocks to merge S3, S4, and the processed S5, cutting encoder latency by roughly 24% while improving AP. For query initialization, the paper proposes uncertainty-minimal query selection, which defines feature uncertainty as the L2 discrepancy between predicted localization and classification distributions and integrates this uncertainty into the classification loss, so that the top-K encoder features selected as initial object queries carry jointly high classification and localization confidence rather than merely high classification scores. The decoder is a standard DETR-style Transformer with auxiliary prediction heads, and the full system is trained end-to-end with bipartite matching on COCO (optionally pre-trained on Objects365).

## Results

RT-DETR-R50 achieves 53.1% AP on COCO val2017 at 108 FPS on T4 GPU (TensorRT FP16), while RT-DETR-R101 reaches 54.3% AP at 74 FPS. After pre-training on Objects365, RT-DETR-R50/R101 further improves to 55.3%/56.2% AP. The model uses 42M/76M parameters and 136/259 GFLOPs respectively, with a 640×640 input size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-DETR-R50 outperforms all L-scale YOLO variants in both AP and FPS: it beats YOLOv5-L by 4.1% AP and 100% FPS, YOLOv7-L by 1.9% AP and 96.4% FPS, YOLOv8-L by 0.2% AP and 52.1% FPS, YOLOv6-L by 0.3% AP and 9.1% FPS, and PP-YOLOE-L by 1.7% AP and 14.9% FPS. RT-DETR-R101 similarly dominates X-scale YOLOs, improving over YOLOv5-X by 3.6% AP and 72.1% FPS, YOLOv7-X by 1.4% AP and 64.4% FPS, YOLOv8-X by 0.4% AP and 48.0% FPS, and PP-YOLOE-X by 2.0% AP and 23.3% FPS. Against the strongest end-to-end DETR with the same backbone, DINO-Deformable-DETR-R50 (50.9% AP, 5 FPS), RT-DETR-R50 gains 2.2% AP and is approximately 21× faster. The paper also notes a limitation: RT-DETR-R50 trails YOLOv8-L by 0.5% AP on small objects (AP_S), and RT-DETR-R101 trails YOLOv7-X by 0.9% AP on small objects, acknowledging that small-object detection remains a weakness relative to the best YOLO variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

RT-DETR sits at the intersection of two dominant real-time detection paradigms: the CNN-based YOLO family (fast but NMS-dependent) and the Transformer-based DETR family (NMS-free but computationally prohibitive for real-time use). It builds directly on DETR's encoder-decoder architecture with bipartite matching, Deformable-DETR's multi-scale deformable attention, and DINO's mixed query selection scheme, but fundamentally restructures the encoder into a hybrid CNN-Transformer design (AIFI + CCFF) and replaces confidence-based query selection with an uncertainty-minimal criterion. The paper also departs from prior evaluation practice by establishing an end-to-end speed benchmark that includes NMS post-processing time, revealing that YOLOs' commonly reported speeds overstate their true inference latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RT-DETR demonstrates that the NMS-free end-to-end paradigm can be made practical for real-time deployment, offering a concrete alternative to YOLOs that eliminates hyperparameter sensitivity (confidence and IoU thresholds) and supports flexible speed tuning via decoder layer count without retraining. The acknowledged limitation on small-object detection (0.5–0.9% AP below the best YOLOs) remains an open problem, and the authors suggest distillation from large DETR models as a promising direction. More broadly, the end-to-end speed benchmark and the quantitative NMS analysis shift how the community should report and compare real-time detector performance, making previously hidden costs visible.
