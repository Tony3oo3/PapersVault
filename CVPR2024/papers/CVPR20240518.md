## Problem

Language-guided 3D shape editing lacks a large-scale dataset with exact ground-truth edited shapes for each individual edit, making it difficult to train and benchmark editing models in a compositional (multi-step) setting. Existing datasets like ShapeTalk rely on manual annotation, conflate multiple factors of variation in single edit pairs, and do not support the study of recursive edit chains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240518/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D shape represented as a point cloud (encoded by a frozen PC-AE or 3D2VS autoencoder) and a sequence of natural language edit instructions (encoded by frozen BERT), and produces an edited 3D point cloud after recursively applying each instruction. The dataset provides, for every consecutive shape pair in a chain, a precise edit vector in GeoCode's parameter space, a set of natural language instructions, and the exact ground-truth target shape, enabling per-step supervision. Chains average 14 shapes in length, with 158K unique shapes across 26K chains.

## Method

The dataset is synthesized by reconstructing 3DCOMPAT++ CAD shapes into GeoCode's parameter space via a DGCNN encoder, filtering out the top 8% by ResNet-50 rendering similarity, then interpolating between shape pairs in parameter space to generate chains of fine-grained edits with dependency-aware ordering. Text instructions are generated rule-based from parameter changes and augmented with random adjectives, direction inversions, and T5 paraphrasing. For the neural editor, a latent mapper (MLP for PC-AE, transformer-based latent diffusion for 3D2VS) is trained with a simple L2 loss between predicted and ground-truth autoencoder latents, while all autoencoder and text-encoder parameters remain frozen. The PC-AE variant predicts an edit vector added to the input latent (optionally decoupled into direction and magnitude), while the 3D2VS variant uses cross-attention with BERT embeddings to denoise the target latent. Multi-step evaluation metrics (average and final edit error) are adapted from trajectory prediction literature to assess recursive editing quality.

## Results

On the ShapeWalk chained editing benchmark (averaged over chain lengths 10, 15, 20), the best Chamfer Distance results come from the decoupled OURS_{512×4} variant, achieving F_CD = 2.670×10⁻⁴ and A_CD = 2.524×10⁻⁴. For L₂ latent distance, the decoupled LATEFUSION_{1024} variant leads with F_L2 = 1.405 and A_L2 = 1.245. Decoupling the edit direction and magnitude consistently improves over coupled variants across both metrics. In the auxiliary edit-recognition task, the classifier reaches 96.9% average accuracy on the realistic set but drops to 58.7% on subtle parameters like legs roundness/indentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against any external baseline methods. The authors explicitly note that ChangeIt3D/ShapeTalk [3] is not directly comparable because its neural-listener distillation method is not applicable when ground-truth edited shapes are available for direct supervision. All quantitative comparisons in Table 1 are internal ablations among variants of the proposed latent mapper (LATEFUSION vs. OURS architecture, coupled vs. decoupled edit prediction, and different bottleneck dimensions). Among these, the decoupled OURS_{512×4} MLP achieves the best Chamfer Distance (F_CD = 2.670×10⁻⁴), while the decoupled LATEFUSION_{1024} achieves the best L₂ latent distance (F_L2 = 1.405). The DIRECTGEN variant (predicting the full edited latent without regressing an edit vector) performs worst across all metrics, confirming that explicit edit-vector regression is beneficial. The absence of external baselines limits the ability to assess ShapeWalk's competitive standing relative to prior language-guided editing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GeoCode's interpretable shape program as the parameter space in which all edits are defined and interpolated, and on PC-AE/3D2VS shape autoencoders as the latent space in which neural editors operate. Its closest conceptual predecessor is ShapeTalk (ChangeIt3D), which also pairs 3D shapes with language edit instructions, but ShapeTalk relies on manual annotation of shape pairs with multiple confounded factors of variation and provides no mechanism for edit chains. ShapeWalk departs from this by synthesizing fine-grained, single-factor edits through parameter-space interpolation, generating ground-truth intermediate shapes, and organizing edits into chains to enable compositional editing. The multi-step evaluation metrics are borrowed from the trajectory prediction literature (Social LSTM, etc.), repurposed to measure cumulative drift in recursive shape editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShapeWalk is the first large-scale dataset providing ground-truth intermediate shapes for multi-step language-guided 3D editing, filling a gap that makes compositional editing studyable and benchmarkable without manual annotation. Its zero-annotation, scalable pipeline (reconstruct → interpolate → generate instructions) could in principle be extended to any shape program and domain, though the current instantiation is limited to GeoCode's chair-centric parameter space. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex or open-ended shape programs, whether the PC-AE/3D2VS autoencoder bottleneck fundamentally limits fine-grained edit fidelity, and whether the modest quantitative gains over simple MLP baselines will hold as shape representations improve.
