## Problem

Recent generalizable novel view synthesis methods learn rendering operators that act over individual camera rays, effectively treating target images as collections of independent pixels. This pointwise formulation produces grainy artifacts and limits geometric coherence, because information cannot be shared across neighboring rays during rendering. The paper asks whether a global rendering operator acting over all camera rays jointly can be learned efficiently and whether the right representation to enable such global rendering exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241801/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V posed RGB input images (typically 3–16 views) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus the target camera parameters, and produces a single novel-view RGB image at the target resolution. Input images are projected via homographies onto D depth planes parallel to the target image plane, forming a 5D plane sweep volume (D×V×3×H×W) that serves as the sole geometric input to the network. Rendering occurs in a low-resolution latent space at H/4 × W/4 before a 4× upsampling stage produces the final output, and the model is trained in a generalizable (scene-agnostic) fashion on a corpus of scenes.

## Method

ConvGLR is a four-stage convolutional architecture that renders novel views globally from a plane sweep volume. First, the 5D PSV is treated as a 4D tensor and depths are grouped by a factor G to reduce computational load. Second, a multi-view matching block aggregates information across views in a depth-independent manner while downsampling spatial resolution 4× via strided 2D convolutions and resblocks. Third, global latent rendering progressively collapses the depth dimension to 1 by iteratively pairing depths and processing them with 2D resblocks, emulating 3D convolutions without expensive transposes. Finally, a 4× upsampling stage (bilinear interpolation + resblocks) produces the output image. The key insight is that the PSV structure implicitly encodes epipolar geometry: operations along depth correspond to operations along epipolar lines, operations along the view dimension mix corresponding epipolar lines, and operations along height/width mix neighboring camera rays—enabling global rendering with standard 2D convolutions. Two additional conditioning channels (normalized spatial coordinates and angular dot products between input view directions and the target normal) are concatenated to the PSV to provide spatial adaptivity and view-dependence.

## Results

On the Generalizable DTU benchmark (10 input views), ConvGLR achieves 31.65 PSNR / 0.952 SSIM / 0.080 LPIPS, outperforming the previous best GPNR (28.50 PSNR) by over 3 dB. On Sparse DTU with 9 input views (generalizable, unknown scene), ConvGLR reaches 26.98 full-image PSNR versus 22.23 for PixelNeRF and 20.32 for MVSNeRF. On the ILSH public challenge, ConvGLR scores 30.17 masked PSNR, beating the challenge winner OpenSpaceAI (27.02) by more than 3 dB and the organizing team's MPFER-H (28.90) by 1.27 dB, while rendering in 0.71 s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConvGLR outperforms every baseline across all five experimental setups. Among generalizable methods, it beats GPNR by 3.15 dB PSNR on Generalizable DTU, MVSNeRF by 6.66 dB on Sparse DTU (9-view), and IBRNet by 5.61 dB on Generalizable DTU; on Generalizable RFF it surpasses GPNR by 1.22 dB PSNR. Among scene-specific NeRF variants, ConvGLR (generalizable, no per-scene training) exceeds the best sparse-view methods—FlipNeRF (25.12) and MixNeRF (25.03) on Sparse DTU 9-view—by roughly 2 dB, and outperforms RegNeRF (24.93) by 2.05 dB. On the Spaces dataset, ConvGLR leads MPFER by 0.86 dB PSNR (12-view dense) and 0.96 dB (4-view large), and DeepView by 1.82 dB (12-view dense). On the ILSH challenge, ConvGLR surpasses the winner OpenSpaceAI by 3.15 dB masked PSNR and the organizing team's MPFER-H by 1.27 dB, while also being the fastest method (0.71 s vs. 1.50 s for MPFER-H and 76–806 s for others). No baseline is competitive with ConvGLR on any metric in any setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the plane sweep volume (PSV) representation from multi-view stereo (Collins 1996) and its application to view synthesis via multiplane images (Szeliski 1998, DeepView 2019), but departs fundamentally from both the fixed volumetric rendering used by NeRF-family methods and the pointwise learned rendering of recent light field networks (GPNR, LFNR). The key conceptual move is recognizing that the PSV's 5D tensor structure naturally decomposes epipolar geometry into three orthogonal mixing operations (along depth, view, and spatial dimensions), enabling a single convolutional architecture to render all camera rays jointly in a low-resolution latent space rather than processing rays independently. This positions ConvGLR as a bridge between the layered-representation tradition (which keeps depths separated and alpha-blends) and the learned-rendering tradition (which collapses depth), by learning the depth integration globally with convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 2–3 dB margins over strong baselines across five experimental setups (sparse and generalizable, multiple datasets) demonstrate that global rendering is a meaningfully better paradigm than pointwise ray processing for sparse-view synthesis, and the 0.71-second inference time makes it practical. The PSV-as-structured-tensor insight is reusable: any future method that needs to mix information across epipolar lines, between views, and between adjacent rays can exploit the same dimensional decomposition. Open questions include scene-adaptive depth plane sampling (currently uniform in depth or disparity), whether the approach scales to wider baselines or dynamic scenes, and whether the 40M-parameter model can be made more efficient without sacrificing the global rendering quality that the ablations show is critical.
