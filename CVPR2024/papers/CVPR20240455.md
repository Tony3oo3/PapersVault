## Problem

Most online continual learning research focuses almost exclusively on model stability (alleviating catastrophic forgetting), while the model's plasticity—its ability to acquire new knowledge from a one-epoch data stream—is largely overlooked. This paper establishes a quantitative lower bound AA ≳ LA × (1 − RF) showing that both plasticity and stability are necessary for good final accuracy, and demonstrates that a significant plasticity gap persists between online and offline CL even with memory replay and multiple updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images arriving in a one-epoch stream (seen exactly once) together with samples drawn from a fixed-size memory buffer (500–10,000 entries depending on dataset). Each incoming sample is additionally augmented into a chain of three progressively harder variants via geometric distortion and RandAugment, so the network processes four views per sample per step. Output is a class label; at inference the two peer models can either predict independently or be ensembled for roughly 1% additional accuracy.

## Method

CCL-DC introduces two peer continual learners of identical architecture (ResNet-18) that train simultaneously in a peer-teaching manner. The CCL component adds a KL-divergence distillation term between the two peers' logits (Eq. 9), increasing optimization parallelism and flexibility. The Distillation Chain (DC) component generates a chain of samples with increasing difficulty (geometric distortion → RandAugment × 2) and distills the teacher's predictions on harder samples into the student's predictions on easier samples (Eq. 10), acting as a learned entropy regularizer that suppresses overconfidence. The total loss is the sum of the baseline method's loss, the CCL peer-distillation loss, and the DC chain-distillation loss (Eq. 11), making the strategy a plug-in that can be layered onto any existing online CL baseline without modifying its core mechanism.

## Results

CCL-DC is applied as a plug-in on top of six online CL baselines and consistently improves Average Accuracy (AA) by large margins across all four datasets. On CIFAR-100 (M=2k), ER improves from 31.89% to 44.45% (+12.6 pp), and GSA from 35.08% to 44.74% (+9.7 pp). On Tiny-ImageNet (M=5k), ER jumps from 19.16% to 29.39% (+10.2 pp), and OnPro from 21.13% to 32.00% (+10.9 pp). The relative gain is remarkably stable (~50% for ER) regardless of memory buffer size, demonstrating scalability. On ImageNet-100 (M=5k), the best result is OnPro+CCL-DC at 47.93%, up from 38.75%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL-DC is not a standalone method but a plug-in strategy applied on top of each baseline, so the comparison is always 'baseline vs. baseline + CCL-DC.' Every one of the six baselines (ER, DER++, ER-ACE, OCM, GSA, OnPro) is improved across all four datasets and all memory sizes, with no case of degradation. The absolute gain is largest for weaker baselines: ER on CIFAR-100 (M=2k) gains +12.6 pp, and OCM on ImageNet-100 gains +19.5 pp (23.67→43.19). Even the strongest baselines benefit: GSA on CIFAR-100 (M=5k) improves from 41.41% to 55.39% (+14.0 pp), and OnPro on ImageNet-100 from 38.75% to 47.93% (+9.2 pp). The relative improvement is remarkably consistent (~50% for ER across all memory sizes on Tiny-ImageNet), indicating the method scales with available resources. Among the CCL-DC-enhanced methods, OnPro+CCL-DC achieves the best ImageNet-100 score (47.93%), while GSA+CCL-DC leads on CIFAR-100 (M=5k) at 55.39%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: replay-based online continual learning (ER, DER++, ER-ACE, OCM, GSA, OnPro) and collaborative/peer-teaching learning (Codistillation, DML). It departs from the dominant online CL paradigm that treats catastrophic forgetting (stability) as the sole challenge, arguing instead that plasticity—the ability to acquire new knowledge from a single-pass data stream—is an equally critical and previously underexplored bottleneck. The Distillation Chain component repurposes data-augmentation-based difficulty ordering (from curriculum learning) as an entropy regularizer, a use not seen in prior CL or collaborative learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantitative bound AA ≳ LA × (1−RF) gives the field a clear diagnostic: improving either plasticity or stability should raise final accuracy, and the consistent ~50% relative gain across six baselines, four datasets, and multiple memory sizes demonstrates that the plasticity gap is a real, exploitable bottleneck. The plug-and-play nature of CCL-DC means practitioners can adopt it without redesigning their baseline. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond CIL to TIL/DIL, whether it scales to larger backbones or non-image modalities, and why ER-ACE on Tiny-ImageNet shows degraded stability when CCL-DC is added—suggesting the method's interaction with aggressive representation-anchoring losses is not yet fully understood.
