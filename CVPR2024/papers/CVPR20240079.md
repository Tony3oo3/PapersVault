## Problem

Existing RL-based reward finetuning methods for diffusion models (DDPO, DPOK) rely on policy gradients that are unstable at scale, failing to produce reasonable outputs when trained on more than a few hundred prompts. No method had achieved stable black-box reward maximization for diffusion models on large-scale prompt datasets (100K+ prompts), limiting generalization to complex, unseen prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240079/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated image via a finetuned Stable Diffusion v1.4 UNet (50 DDPM denoising steps, classifier-free guidance scale 5.0). During training, the model consumes full denoising trajectories (sequences of noisy-to-clean latent images) and receives scalar reward scores from generic off-the-shelf black-box reward models (HPSv2, PickScore, LAION aesthetic) that only require clean images as input, making the approach compatible with any non-differentiable reward function.

## Method

PRDP converts the KL-regularized RLHF objective into an equivalent supervised regression objective called Reward Difference Prediction (RDP), which tasks the diffusion model with predicting the reward difference between two generated images from their denoising trajectories. The authors prove that the model achieving zero RDP loss is exactly the maximizer of the original RL objective, eliminating the need for high-variance policy gradients. For stable optimization, they introduce proximal updates that clip the per-step log probability ratio between the current and a snapshot policy (analogous to PPO clipping), preventing excessively large parameter shifts. The algorithm uses online optimization, sampling denoising trajectories from the current policy each epoch rather than a fixed offline dataset, which the authors show yields substantially better reward scores than offline training with the same number of reward queries.

## Results

On large-scale training (100K+ prompts from HPDv2), PRDP achieves 0.3175 HPSv2 and 0.2424 PickScore on the HPDv2 training set, substantially outperforming both the unfinetuned Stable Diffusion v1.4 (0.2685 / 0.2092) and DDPO (0.2464 / 0.2032). Critically, DDPO degrades below the unfinetuned baseline on most unseen prompt categories, with its HPSv2 score on HPDv2 Photo dropping to 0.2093 (vs. 0.2750 for SD v1.4), while PRDP maintains consistent gains across all six evaluation sets. On the small-scale setup (45 prompts), PRDP matches or slightly exceeds DDPO (0.3471 vs. 0.3398 HPSv2; 0.2700 vs. 0.2664 PickScore), confirming equivalent reward maximization in the regime where policy gradients are stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRDP is compared against two baselines: DDPO (a PPO-based RL finetuning method) and the unfinetuned Stable Diffusion v1.4. At small scale (45 prompts), PRDP slightly outperforms DDPO on both reward models (HPSv2: 0.3471 vs. 0.3398; PickScore: 0.2700 vs. 0.2664), confirming parity with established policy gradient methods. At large scale (100K+ prompts), the comparison becomes stark: DDPO fails catastrophically, producing reward scores *below* the unfinetuned SD v1.4 on most evaluation sets (e.g., HPSv2 Photo: 0.2093 vs. 0.2750; PickScore Photo: 0.1780 vs. 0.2172), while PRDP consistently improves over SD v1.4 by 0.04–0.05 in HPSv2 and 0.02–0.03 in PickScore across all six unseen prompt categories. The paper attributes DDPO's failure to the high variance of policy gradient estimates that becomes unmanageable beyond ~400 prompts, a limitation that PRDP's supervised regression formulation avoids entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRDP draws a direct conceptual lineage from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which reformulated the RLHF objective for language models as a supervised classification loss, and from PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), whose clipping mechanism inspires the proximal updates here. However, PRDP departs from both the language-model DPO formulation (which uses a binary preference classifier) and the prior diffusion-model RL methods (DDPO, DPOK) by introducing a supervised *regression* objective—predicting reward differences from denoising trajectories—that provably shares the same optimal solution as the KL-regularized RL objective. This positions PRDP as the diffusion-model analogue of DPO, but with a regression rather than classification loss, and with an explicit proximal constraint to ensure training stability at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRDP is the first method to demonstrate stable black-box reward finetuning of diffusion models on 100K+ prompts, a regime where the prior best method (DDPO) collapses to generating noise. The clean theoretical equivalence between the RDP regression loss and the RL objective provides a principled justification for the approach, and the KL regularization's role in preventing reward hacking (e.g., with the LAION aesthetic score) addresses a practical concern for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D diffusion models, whether it remains competitive when differentiable rewards allow direct backpropagation (as in DRaFT), and whether the full-trajectory sampling requirement limits efficiency at even larger scales or with newer architectures like SDXL or FLUX.
