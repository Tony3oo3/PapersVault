## Problem

Identifying and segmenting first-person camera wearers in a third-person view is challenging because the wearer's own appearance is invisible in their egocentric view, the two viewpoints may have minimal or no overlapping field of view, and multiple wearers can coexist in the same scene. Prior methods rely on ground-truth pre-masks and optical flow from previous frames, use body-worn cameras as the 'third-person' view rather than true scene-overview cameras, and—critically—modern ViT backbones can achieve near-perfect accuracy on existing datasets by overfitting to the third-person input alone, without genuinely leveraging the egocentric view.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a synchronized first-person (egocentric) RGB frame, a third-person (exocentric) RGB frame, and a pool of candidate segmentation masks generated by a pre-trained Mask R-CNN (score threshold 0.9) from the third-person view. The candidate mask is concatenated with the third-person image to form a 4-channel input, while the first-person image remains 3-channel. For each candidate, the model outputs a scalar confidence score in [0, 1] via a Sigmoid head; the candidate with the highest score is selected as the predicted camera wearer, enabling multi-wearer disambiguation within a single third-person frame.

## Method

PersonEnvironmentNet (PEN) fuses two complementary branches. The personal branch extracts all visible individuals from the egocentric view via Mask R-CNN, crops the candidate from the exocentric view, encodes them with a shared ViT, and applies multi-head attention to model which individuals the candidate can plausibly see. The environmental branch uses GlueStick (an attention-based GNN matching points and line segments) to produce line-intensity masks in both views, which encode geometric cues about the camera wearer's pose and visible region; these are concatenated with the filtered candidate and passed through a ViT. To prevent the model from memorizing candidate appearances, 'ghost samples'—randomly generated masks labeled 0.5—are injected during environmental-branch training. A fusing network concatenates the intermediate features of both branches (before their respective MLP heads), applies a multi-head attention layer, and produces the final score. Training proceeds in four stages: frozen-backbone attention-head warmup (3 epochs), full personal-branch training (30 epochs), environmental-branch training (30 epochs), and frozen-branch fusion fine-tuning (30 epochs), all with AdamW and pre-trained TorchVision ViT weights.

## Results

On the proposed TF2023 dataset, PEN (self-attention fusion) achieves an IoU of 0.44, accuracy of 52.9%, and EgoRate of 0.97 (IoU) / 0.96 (Acc), substantially outperforming the best two-stream baseline (ViT + contrastive loss) which reaches only IoU 0.33, Acc 39.9%, and EgoRate 0.31/0.32. On the prior IUShareView dataset, PEN attains 99.7% accuracy with an EgoRate of 0.77, matching the two-stream baseline's 99.3% accuracy while the latter's EgoRate is 0.0, indicating the baseline entirely overfits to the third-person input. The personal branch alone (IoU 0.40, Acc 48.9%) and environmental branch alone (IoU 0.39, Acc 47.2%) each already surpass all baselines, and their fusion yields the final gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline is the Third-first model from Xu et al. (ECCV 2018), which was originally designed to require a ground-truth pre-mask from the previous frame; the authors adapted it by feeding the current-frame candidate mask instead. On TF2023, PEN outperforms Third-first by a wide margin: IoU 0.44 vs. 0.26, accuracy 52.9% vs. 31.1%, and EgoRate 0.97 vs. 0.52. The internal two-stream baselines (ViT and Swin backbones with contrastive or BCE loss) are also clearly surpassed by PEN on all three metrics, with the best two-stream variant (ViT + contrastive) reaching only IoU 0.33 and EgoRate 0.31. On IUShareView, PEN achieves 99.7% accuracy (vs. 99.3% for the two-stream ViT baseline) while maintaining an EgoRate of 0.77, whereas the two-stream baseline's EgoRate is 0.0, confirming that the baseline's near-perfect accuracy is entirely due to overfitting on the third-person input rather than genuine cross-view understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the camera-wearer identification task introduced by Fan et al. [14] and Xu et al. [52] by removing the dependency on pre-frame masks and optical flow, supporting multiple simultaneous camera wearers in a single third-person view, and adopting a more realistic elevated third-person camera angle. The environmental branch is built directly on GlueStick [38], repurposing its matched-line output as a geometric cue for cross-view alignment rather than for standard image registration. The personal branch combines Mask R-CNN person detection with ViT feature extraction and multi-head attention, a standard recipe applied here to the novel cross-view matching setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EgoRate metric and the discovery that a strong ViT baseline achieves 99.3% accuracy on IUShareView while completely ignoring the first-person input (EgoRate = 0) expose a critical evaluation blind spot in cross-view matching research: high accuracy on a small, biased dataset can mask the absence of genuine cross-view understanding. TF2023, at roughly two orders of magnitude larger than IUShareView with more complex multi-actor scenes, provides a necessary stress test. Open questions include whether the modest absolute performance (52.9% Acc) reflects a fundamental difficulty in the task or simply the limits of the current architecture, and whether EgoRate generalizes as a diagnostic beyond this specific task formulation.
