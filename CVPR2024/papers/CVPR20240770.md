## Problem

Molecular machine learning depends heavily on large labeled datasets, but obtaining labels for molecules is expensive and labor-intensive. Existing pseudo-labeling approaches (self-training, single graph kernels) rely on a single domain of knowledge, making them over-confident, sensitive to kernel choice, and unable to capture the full structural diversity of molecular data. No prior framework systematically integrates multiple heterogeneous molecular structural knowledge sources into a unified weak-supervision pipeline with explicit conflict resolution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of unlabeled molecular graphs (represented as atom-bond graphs with node/edge features) plus a small manually-labeled subset selected via clustering over pairwise similarity matrices. Multi-label classification is decomposed into independent binary subtasks (property present/absent), and the output is a per-molecule probability vector over these binary tasks, which is then used to train a GNN-based molecular property classifier. The labeled subset is intentionally minimal—only prominent cluster centers are annotated—making the pipeline nearly label-free in practice.

## Method

MDP constructs multiple molecular labeling functions (LFs) from three complementary perspectives: graph-kernel similarity (graphlets, shortest paths, subtrees), molecular-fingerprint Tanimoto similarity (MACCS, Morgan), and topological-feature distance. Each LF produces a pairwise similarity matrix; a clustering step selects representative molecules for manual annotation, and weak supervision signals are generated by weighting labeled examples by their normalized similarity to each unlabeled molecule. A label synchronizer—an MLP with a learnable confidence vector Ω—then aggregates the z LFs' signals, dynamically estimating inter-LF correlations (negative weights for conflicting LFs, positive for concordant ones) to emit calibrated probabilistic pseudo-labels. A GNN classifier is trained jointly on ground-truth labels (supervised loss) and the probabilistic pseudo-labels (unsupervised loss), while the synchronizer is optimized via an adaptive loss on the labeled subset, yielding a total three-term binary cross-entropy objective.

## Results

MDP achieves the best AUC on 7 out of 8 MoleculeNet binary classification benchmarks under weak supervision, with headline scores of 0.632 (BACE), 0.618 (BBBP), 0.653 (Tox21), and 0.665 (HIV). The only dataset where MDP does not lead is ToxCast (0.602 vs. 0.616 for context_pred), attributed to the large number of binary tasks and label sparsity. Across all eight datasets, MDP delivers an average AUC improvement of 9.5% over the strongest baseline, with the largest gains on ClinTox (+0.070 over drop_node) and Tox21 (+0.084 over edge_pred).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDP outperforms all four vanilla GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GIN) by a wide margin on every dataset, with the largest gap on ClinTox (0.615 vs. 0.455 for GCN, a +0.160 AUC gain). Against semi-supervised methods (pseudo-labeling, self-training), MDP leads on all eight datasets, confirming that integrating multiple labeling functions with a learned synchronizer is more effective than relying on a single confidence-thresholding strategy. Among self-supervised pre-training methods (infomax, context_pred, attr_mask, edge_pred), MDP surpasses the best of these (edge_pred or context_pred) on 7 of 8 datasets; the sole exception is ToxCast, where context_pred (0.616) edges out MDP (0.602) due to the extreme label sparsity across 1,287 binary tasks. For contrastive learning baselines (GraphLoG, drop_node, perturb_edge, subgraph), MDP achieves the largest absolute gains on HIV (+0.090 over drop_node) and ClinTox (+0.070 over drop_node), while the margin is smallest on ToxCast where the contrastive methods benefit from the high-dimensional task structure. Overall, the 9.5% average AUC improvement over the strongest per-dataset baseline validates the benefit of systematic multi-source weak supervision over any single paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDP adapts the data programming paradigm (Snorkel, MeTaL) from text and image domains to molecular graphs, where heuristic labeling rules are infeasible due to structural complexity. Rather than hand-crafted rules, it constructs labeling functions from similarity matrices derived from graph kernels, molecular fingerprints (MACCS, Morgan), and topological features, then uses a lightweight MLP-based label synchronizer to resolve inter-LF conflicts. This departs from prior molecular pseudo-labeling approaches (self-training, graph kernel semi-supervision) that rely on a single similarity source, and from self-supervised GNN pre-training methods that introduce a pretext-task gap with the downstream objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for molecular property prediction when labeled data is scarce, requiring only a small manually-labeled subset selected via clustering rather than full annotation. However, the label synchronizer's simplicity (single-layer MLP) limits its effectiveness on high-dimensional multi-task settings like ToxCast, and the method has not been validated on 3D molecular representations or regression tasks. An open question is whether the similarity-based LF design can be extended to capture physicochemical or quantum-mechanical properties beyond structural descriptors, and whether the approach generalizes to other graph-structured domains beyond molecules.
