## Problem

Existing dense SLAM methods either rely on handcrafted representations (points, surfels, SDFs) that require rich geometric features and cannot synthesize novel views, or on implicit neural radiance fields that are computationally inefficient, hard to edit, and suffer from catastrophic forgetting. This paper asks how an explicit volumetric representation—3D Gaussians—can be leveraged to simultaneously track camera pose and build a high-fidelity, editable scene map in real time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241810/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an online stream of RGB-D frames (e.g., 876×584 or 1200×980) with known camera intrinsics, processing each new frame sequentially. It outputs per-frame 6-DOF camera poses and an incrementally growing 3D Gaussian map where each Gaussian is parameterized by only 8 values (RGB color, 3D center, isotropic radius, opacity). The map can be differentiably rendered into RGB, depth, and silhouette images at up to 400 FPS, enabling both tracking and novel-view synthesis from arbitrary camera positions.

## Method

SplaTAM represents the scene as a set of isotropic, view-independent 3D Gaussians and renders RGB, depth, and silhouette images via differentiable alpha-compositing (splatting). Each online frame triggers a three-step pipeline: (1) camera tracking minimizes a silhouette-masked L1 loss on rendered depth and color with respect to pose, initialized by forward velocity propagation; (2) Gaussian densification adds new Gaussians at pixels where the rendered silhouette is low or ground-truth depth is in front of predicted depth beyond a threshold; (3) map update optimizes all Gaussian parameters against a selected subset of overlapping keyframes using RGB+depth+SSIM losses. The rendered silhouette is the key mechanism that captures the map's epistemic uncertainty, restricting tracking losses to well-mapped regions and enabling structured, capacity-increasing map expansion without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On ScanNet++, SplaTAM achieves an ATE RMSE of 1.2 cm, dramatically outperforming Point-SLAM (343.8 cm) and ORB-SLAM3 (158.2 cm), which both fail to track due to large inter-frame displacements. On Replica, SplaTAM reduces trajectory error to 0.36 cm (vs. 0.52 cm for Point-SLAM), and on TUM-RGBD it achieves 5.48 cm (vs. 8.92 cm for Point-SLAM). For novel-view synthesis on ScanNet++, SplaTAM achieves 24.41 dB PSNR and 2.07 cm depth L1 error, while Point-SLAM fails entirely (11.91 dB PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplaTAM decisively outperforms Point-SLAM [30], the prior SOTA dense radiance-field SLAM method, on all four datasets: by ~287× on ScanNet++ (1.2 vs 343.8 cm), 31% on Replica (0.36 vs 0.52 cm), 39% on TUM-RGBD (5.48 vs 8.92 cm), and marginally on Orig-ScanNet (11.88 vs 12.19 cm). On novel-view rendering (ScanNet++), SplaTAM achieves 24.41 dB PSNR versus Point-SLAM's 11.91 dB, as Point-SLAM's tracking failure cascades into rendering failure. Against feature-based methods, SplaTAM wins on ScanNet++ (1.2 vs 158.2 cm for ORB-SLAM3) and Replica (0.36 vs 0.38 cm for DROID-SLAM), but loses on TUM-RGBD where ORB-SLAM2 achieves 1.98 cm due to the dataset's extreme motion blur and sparse depth. Among other dense baselines, SplaTAM beats NICE-SLAM [54] (5.48 vs 15.87 cm on TUM-RGBD; 0.36 vs 1.06 cm on Replica), Vox-Fusion [46] (5.48 vs 11.31 cm on TUM-RGBD), and ESLAM [12] (0.36 vs 0.63 cm on Replica), while Kintinuous [42] (4.84 cm) and ElasticFusion [43] (6.91 cm) are competitive on TUM-RGBD.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplaTAM adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) — originally designed for offline novel-view synthesis with known poses — into an online SLAM framework by simplifying the representation (isotropic Gaussians, view-independent color) and jointly optimizing camera poses and map parameters via differentiable rendering. It departs from the dominant implicit neural radiance field SLAM line (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, Point-SLAM) by replacing neural networks with an explicit volumetric primitive set, gaining fast rasterization, direct gradient flow, and trivially editable map capacity. It also moves beyond traditional dense SLAM representations (surfels, SDFs, point clouds) by enabling dense per-pixel photometric optimization in real time rather than relying on sparse geometric features or pixel sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a simple explicit 3D Gaussian map can simultaneously achieve sub-cm tracking, 400 FPS rendering, and high-fidelity novel-view synthesis, SplaTAM challenges the field's recent pivot toward implicit neural representations for SLAM and offers a more practical, editable, and scalable alternative. The introduction of a ScanNet++-based benchmark with held-out novel views addresses a critical gap: prior SLAM benchmarks could not evaluate rendering quality on unseen viewpoints. Open questions include handling motion blur and aggressive rotation (acknowledged limitations), removing the dependency on known intrinsics and dense depth, and scaling to large-scale scenes via sparse data structures like OpenVDB.
