## Problem

Existing spatial gene expression prediction methods from WSIs either focus solely on individual spot images, neglecting surrounding tissue context, or process all patches simultaneously with scalability constraints. Additionally, prior work suffers from inadequate validation—using same-patient replicates in both training and testing, limited single-dataset evaluation, and inconsistent normalization—leading to overestimated performance. TRIPLEX addresses these gaps by integrating multi-resolution biological context and establishing a rigorous cross-validation benchmark.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three image inputs at different resolutions: a 224×224 target spot crop, a 1120×1120 neighbor view (a single contiguous image centered on the spot, rather than a grid of adjacent spot crops, to handle non-uniform ST spot spacing), and the global view comprising all spot images in the WSI. For the global and neighbor views, ResNet18 features are pre-extracted to limit computational cost, while the target spot encoder is fully trained. Output is a 250-dimensional gene expression vector per spot, with values normalized by spot-wise sum, log-transformed, and smoothed with adjacent neighbors.

## Method

TRIPLEX employs three independent encoders: a global encoder (frozen ResNet18 features fed into transformer blocks with an Atypical Position Encoding Generator that reshapes tokens into a spatial grid with zero-filling for irregular WSI layouts, then applies convolutions to recover positional context), a target encoder (trained ResNet18 producing 49×512 tokens), and a neighbor encoder (frozen ResNet18 producing 25×512 tokens followed by trained self-attention blocks with relative position encoding). Fusion is achieved via cross-attention where the global token serves as query and target/neighbor tokens serve as key-value pairs; the two cross-attention outputs are summed to form the final prediction token. A knowledge-distillation-inspired fusion loss trains individual token predictors to match both ground-truth labels and the fusion token's soft predictions, with a hyperparameter α balancing the two objectives, thereby forcing each resolution-specific branch to internalize gene-expression-relevant information.

## Results

On cross-validation across three ST datasets, TRIPLEX achieves the best MSE (0.228 on BC1, 0.202 on BC2, 0.268 on SCC) and highest PCC(M) (0.314, 0.206, 0.374) and PCC(H) (0.497, 0.352, 0.490) among all compared methods. On external 10X Visium validation, TRIPLEX attains an average PCC(H) of 0.293 and the lowest MSE (0.351, 0.282, 0.285) across three independent Visium samples, outperforming the next-best model (BLEEP) by 0.03–0.10 in PCC(H). The GNAS cancer marker gene prediction reaches PCC 0.583 (BC1) and 0.554 (BC2), substantially exceeding all baselines (best prior: 0.411 ST-Net on BC1, 0.371 ST-Net on BC2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIPLEX outperforms all five external baselines on every metric (MSE, PCC(M), PCC(H)) across all three internal ST datasets. Against the strongest prior method EGN [25], TRIPLEX improves PCC(M) by 0.117 (BC1), 0.095 (BC2), and 0.093 (SCC), and PCC(H) by 0.169, 0.149, and 0.102 respectively. ST-Net [7] and BLEEP [24] (local-only methods) are consistently outperformed, with BLEEP showing the weakest PCC(M) on BC1 (0.151 vs. TRIPLEX 0.314). HisToGene [18] (global ViT-based) underperforms TRIPLEX on all datasets, particularly on SCC where its PCC(M) drops to 0.133. Hist2ST [26] (transformer + GNN) shows severe instability on SCC (MSE 1.291, PCC(M) 0.004), while TRIPLEX maintains stable performance (MSE 0.268, PCC(M) 0.374). On external 10X Visium validation, TRIPLEX also beats all baselines, with the largest gap over EGN on Visium-1 (PCC(H) 0.241 vs. 0.024) and a consistent 0.03–0.10 PCC(H) advantage over BLEEP, the next-best external model.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIPLEX extends the spatial gene expression prediction line initiated by ST-Net (single-spot Densenet regression) and advanced by HisToGene and Hist2ST (ViT/GNN-based global context modeling), but departs from their sequential or single-resolution processing by simultaneously encoding three distinct scales—target spot, local neighbor view, and global WSI view—via separate encoders and cross-attention fusion. It also draws on MIL feature-extraction strategies (frozen ResNet18 for pre-extracted patch features) to handle the computational burden of thousands of spots, and adapts PEG into APEG to accommodate the irregular spatial layout of ST spots. The fusion loss, inspired by knowledge distillation, is a novel training objective not present in prior spatial transcriptomics predictors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a more rigorous benchmark (leave-one-patient-out cross-validation, no same-patient leakage, uniform normalization) that exposes overestimation in prior evaluations, and demonstrates that the local neighbor view is the single most informative resolution for gene expression prediction—a finding that shifts emphasis away from purely global or single-spot models. Limitations include evaluation on only 250 pre-selected genes, three tissue types (breast and skin cancer), and relatively small ST datasets; generalization to full 20,000-gene panels, other organ types, and clinical deployment remain open. The external Visium validation is encouraging but limited to three samples, leaving questions about robustness across broader patient populations and staining protocols.
