## Problem

Equivariant Descriptor Fields (EDFs) achieve data-efficient, generalizable 6-DoF manipulation learning from few demonstrations, but their energy-based training requires over 10 hours. Conversely, SE(3)-diffusion-based methods train quickly but demand many demonstrations and fail to generalize to unseen configurations. The paper asks whether diffusion-based generative modeling can inherit the bi-equivariance and locality of EDFs while achieving dramatically faster training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241825/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two point clouds: a scene point cloud observed in the scene frame and a grasp point cloud observed in the end-effector frame, with no pre-training, object segmentation, or category labels required. It outputs a 6-DoF end-effector pose (an SE(3) element) sampled via Langevin dynamics using the learned bi-equivariant score function. The scene and grasp clouds are processed asymmetrically—the scene encodes the target geometry while the grasp cloud provides the local contact context that anchors the diffusion frame.

## Method

Diffusion-EDFs defines a bi-equivariant diffusion process on SE(3) where the Brownian kernel (left-invariant but not right-invariant) is made bi-equivariant by conjugating the noise with a learned diffusion frame whose origin is selected near contact-rich sub-geometries of the scene. The score model decomposes into translational and rotational parts, each computed as a weighted sum over query points on the grasp cloud of an equivariant tensor product between two EDFs (one encoding the scene, one encoding the grasp), using Clebsch-Gordan coefficients to map to type-1 vectors. The EDFs are implemented with a U-Net-like multiscale Equiformer GNN that maintains wide receptive fields via point pooling while preserving local high-frequency details. Training uses a standard MSE score-matching loss against the explicit Brownian kernel gradient, requiring no integration over the full diffusion kernel, and converges in 20–45 minutes on a single GPU from 5–10 human demonstrations—roughly 15× faster than the original EDFs.

## Results

On the simulation pick-and-place benchmark (mug and bottle tasks), Diffusion-EDFs achieves 95% total success rate on the default (in-distribution) setting and maintains 79–91% total success rate across all four out-of-distribution scenarios (unseen instances, poses, clutters, and all combined), without any pre-training or object segmentation. In the most adversarial setting (unseen instances + poses + clutters), Diffusion-EDFs scores 79% (mug) and 89% (bottle) total success, while R-NDFs drops to 53% (mug) and 84% (bottle) even with pre-trained weights, and SE(3)-Diffusion Fields collapses to 3% (mug) and 0% (bottle). Training requires only 5–10 human demonstrations and 20–45 minutes on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion-EDFs consistently outperforms both baselines across all four out-of-distribution scenarios. Against R-NDFs (the state-of-the-art SE(3)-equivariant method), Diffusion-EDFs achieves 14–45 percentage-point higher total success rates on the mug task (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.81 on default; 0.91 vs. 0.00 on unseen clutters) and 12–15 points higher on the bottle task, while requiring no pre-trained weights or object segmentation that R-NDFs depends on. Against SE(3)-Diffusion Fields (the state-of-the-art diffusion-based method), the gap is even larger: Diffusion-EDFs scores 0.95 vs. 0.75 (mug pick) on the default setting and 0.91 vs. 0.06 (mug pick) on unseen clutters, with SE(3)-Diffusion Fields dropping to near-zero (0.01–0.11) when rotational augmentation is removed. Notably, R-NDFs achieves 0.00 success rate without object segmentation in every scenario, and SE(3)-Diffusion Fields is similarly crippled without segmentation, whereas Diffusion-EDFs operates end-to-end on raw point clouds. On real hardware, Diffusion-EDFs successfully completes three tasks (mug-on-hanger, bowls-on-dishes, bottles-on-shelf) from only 4–10 demonstrations, including sequential and multimodal tasks that segmentation-based baselines cannot handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: SE(3)-equivariant descriptor fields (EDFs, Ryu et al. ICLR 2023), which achieve data-efficient manipulation learning through bi-equivariant energy-based models, and SE(3)-diffusion fields (Urain et al. ICRA 2023), which apply denoising diffusion on the SE(3) manifold for grasp pose generation. The key departure is replacing the slow energy-based training of EDFs with a bi-equivariant diffusion score-matching objective, while preserving the locality and symmetry properties that give EDFs their generalizability. A notable theoretical contribution is showing that standard Brownian diffusion on SE(3) is only left-invariant (not bi-invariant), and proposing a diffusion frame selection mechanism to recover full bi-equivariance of the diffusion kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× training speedup over EDFs (from 10+ hours to under an hour) makes equivariant manipulation learning practically accessible, and the consistent ~80–99% success rates across all four OOD scenarios—without pre-training or object segmentation—demonstrate that symmetry-based inductive biases can substitute for the segmentation pipelines that baselines require. Two limitations remain open: the method cannot perform control-level or trajectory-level inference (only pose-level), and it requires a separate grasp observation step, preventing fully closed-loop operation. Future work on geometric impedance control integration and single-observation segmentation could address these gaps.
