## Problem

In Visual Question Answering, textual descriptions struggle to distinguish among multiple entities of the same category in a scene, and existing VQA datasets lack a mechanism for explicit localization in such multi-entity scenarios. This paper tackles the gap by introducing a method that enables pre-trained vision-language models to precisely localize a specific entity among visually similar ones without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241775/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene image, a natural-language question, and a bounding box indicating the subject's location as input. It produces a binary pixel-level mask localizing the target entity among multiple same-category instances. The subject is rendered as a white bounding box overlaid on the image, and the final output is the mask corresponding to the highest-frequency answer across multiple color-permutation queries, enabling evaluation via S-measure, F-max, and MAE at the pixel level.

## Method

Mask4Align is a two-stage, training-free pipeline built on pre-trained VLMs (CLIP or ALBEF) as VQA modules. In the first stage, a tagging tool (RAM) extracts all entity tags from the scene as candidate answers, and the VQA module identifies the target entity tag given the question and subject bounding box. In the second stage, SAM segments all instances of that tag, the scene is converted to grayscale, and up to six distinct colors are overlaid on the masks to create disambiguating visual cues; aligned candidate answers (e.g., 'the RED apple') are then fed back into the VQA module. When the number of entities exceeds the six available colors, a Recursive-Grouping-VQA (RG-VQA) procedure applies K-means spatial clustering to group masks, recursively narrows the candidate set, and selects the entity with the highest answer frequency across multiple color-order permutations. The key insight is that color masks preserve image coherence while injecting a semantic channel that pre-trained VLMs can exploit, outperforming alternatives like bounding-box highlighting or entity occlusion.

## Results

On the GazeFollow test set, ALBEF+Mask4Align achieves 0.745 AUC, 0.263 L2 Distance, and 32.4° angular error, outperforming the ALBEF baseline (0.688 AUC, 0.300 Dist, 39.2° Ang) by 0.057 AUC and 6.8° angular error. On the dedicated ME-VQA multi-entity dataset, ALBEF+Mask4Align reaches 0.821 S-measure, 0.789 F-max, and 0.071 MAE, substantially exceeding the ALBEF+Random baseline (0.514 Sα, 0.572 Fmax, 0.342 MAE). The CLIP-based variant also shows consistent gains: 0.677 AUC on GazeFollow (vs. 0.648 baseline) and 0.656 Sα on ME-VQA (vs. 0.408 random baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Mask4Align against two VQA module baselines: CLIP (zero-shot VQA via prompt templates) and ALBEF (fine-tuned VQA with candidate answer input). On GazeFollow, ALBEF+Mask4Align outperforms the ALBEF baseline by 0.057 AUC (0.745 vs. 0.688), 0.037 L2 Distance (0.263 vs. 0.300), and 6.8° angular error (32.4° vs. 39.2°). CLIP+Mask4Align similarly beats the CLIP baseline by 0.029 AUC (0.677 vs. 0.648) and 2.8° angular error (39.5° vs. 42.3°). On the dedicated ME-VQA dataset, the gains are substantially larger: ALBEF+Mask4Align exceeds ALBEF+Random by 0.307 S-measure (0.821 vs. 0.514) and 0.217 F-max (0.789 vs. 0.572), while CLIP+Mask4Align surpasses CLIP+Random by 0.248 S-measure (0.656 vs. 0.408). The method consistently wins across all metrics and both VQA backbones, with the largest relative improvements observed on the multi-entity-specific ME-VQA benchmark where textual descriptions are most ambiguous.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes existing off-the-shelf components—CLIP/ALBEF as VQA modules, SAM for segmentation, and RAM for entity tagging—into a novel two-stage inference pipeline that requires no additional training. Conceptually, it draws inspiration from CPT's use of color overlays for visual-language alignment, but inverts the application: rather than using color during training to teach a model, it injects color at inference time as a disambiguation signal for entities that natural language cannot distinguish. The paper positions itself as the first to formally define multi-entity localization as a VQA sub-problem, departing from standard VQA where answers are free-text descriptions that inherently struggle with visually similar entities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a genuine gap in VQA: scenes with multiple same-category entities where textual descriptions are ambiguous. Its training-free nature makes it immediately applicable to any VQA model that accepts candidate answers, lowering the barrier to adoption. However, the ME-VQA evaluation dataset is extremely small (75 images), limiting confidence in generalizability, and the fixed six-color palette cannot adapt to model-specific color biases (as shown by the 'red fruit' vs. 'orange fruit' failure). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very dense scenes, whether color selection can be learned per-model, and whether the method transfers to VLMs that do not support candidate-answer input (e.g., the current best VQA models).
