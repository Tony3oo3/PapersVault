## Problem

Standard Transformer-based image restoration methods use dense attention over all tokens, which introduces noisy interactions from irrelevant regions and propagates redundant features that degrade reconstruction quality. Existing remedies—Top-K token selection and superpixel-space attention—are either sensitive to task-specific hyperparameters or still aggregate over all available tokens, leaving redundancy unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240212/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single degraded RGB image of arbitrary resolution (H×W×3) and outputs a restored RGB image of the same size, computed as the sum of the input and a predicted residual. Internally, the image is processed in non-overlapping 8×8 windows for attention computation within a U-shaped encoder-decoder with a bottleneck stage. Training uses the Charbonnier loss (ε=10⁻³) with AdamW and cosine LR decay, and evaluation is performed on 128×128 patches for deraining (Y-channel PSNR/SSIM) and full images for haze and raindrop tasks.

## Method

AST introduces two complementary modules within a U-shaped Transformer backbone. The Adaptive Sparse Self-Attention (ASSA) block runs a sparse branch (squared ReLU activation on QKᵀ/√d + B) that zeros out negative query-key scores to filter irrelevant interactions, alongside a dense branch (standard softmax) that preserves sufficient information flow; learnable scalar weights (initialized to 1) are softmax-normalized to adaptively fuse the two branches, letting the network shift emphasis toward sparsity in deeper layers. The Feature Refinement Feed-forward Network (FRFN) replaces the standard FFN with an enhance-and-ease scheme: a partial convolution (PConv) selectively enhances informative channels, while a gated depthwise-convolution path suppresses redundant channels via element-wise multiplication. The encoder stages contain only FRFN blocks (no attention, to avoid low-pass filtering in early layers), while decoder and bottleneck stages combine ASSA and FRFN, with skip connections fusing encoder and decoder features.

## Results

On the SPAD benchmark for rain streak removal, AST-B achieves 49.51 dB PSNR and 0.9942 SSIM, surpassing the previous best Transformer-based method DRSformer (48.53 dB) by 0.98 dB and the best CNN-based method Fu et al. (45.03 dB) by 4.48 dB. On AGAN-Data for raindrop removal, AST-B reaches 32.32 dB PSNR, outperforming the prior best AWRCP (31.93 dB) by 0.39 dB. On Dense-Haze for real haze removal, AST-B attains 17.12 dB PSNR, exceeding the best Transformer baseline MB-TaylorFormer-B (16.66 dB) by 0.46 dB. With geometric self-ensemble (AST-B+), gains are further extended to 49.72 dB (SPAD), 32.45 dB (AGAN-Data), and 17.27 dB (Dense-Haze).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AST-B outperforms all 16 baselines on SPAD, with the largest margin over CNN-based methods (4.48 dB over Fu et al.) and a clear 0.98 dB advantage over the closest Transformer competitor DRSformer, which uses Top-K channel selection. On AGAN-Data, AST-B beats the previous best AWRCP by 0.39 dB and the diffusion-based WeatherDiff_128 by 2.66 dB, while matching or exceeding MAXIM-2S and IDT on SSIM. On Dense-Haze, AST-B surpasses all 15 baselines, gaining 1.32 dB over the best CNN method AECR-Net and 0.46 dB over the best Transformer method MB-TaylorFormer-B. Among the Transformer-based baselines, AST-B consistently outperforms Uformer, Restormer, IDT, and DRSformer across all three tasks, confirming that the adaptive sparse attention and feature refinement design provides a systematic advantage over standard dense or single-branch sparse attention. The ablation in Table 4 further shows ASSA gains 0.96 dB over Swin SA, 0.76 dB over Top-k SA, and 0.49 dB over Condensed SA at comparable FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

AST builds on the U-shaped encoder-decoder Transformer architecture of Uformer [82] and incorporates squared ReLU-based sparse self-attention from the NLP literature [102], but departs from prior efficient-attention designs (SwinIR, Restormer, DRSformer, CODE) by rejecting the either/or choice between dense and sparse attention. Rather than relying on task-sensitive Top-K selection or superpixel-space projection, AST introduces an adaptive two-branch mechanism that learns to weight a ReLU² sparse branch against a softmax dense branch, complemented by a novel FRFN that uses partial convolution and gating to perform channel-wise feature refinement. This positions the work as a principled middle ground between the information-loss risk of pure sparsity and the noisy-interaction risk of pure density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive sparse-dense attention design offers a task-agnostic alternative to parameter-sensitive selection strategies (e.g., Top-K), which is practically valuable for practitioners deploying restoration models across multiple degradation types without re-tuning hyperparameters. However, the paper acknowledges that AST remains a task-specific model and struggles with heavy real-world degradation, leaving open the question of whether a single unified architecture can handle diverse degradations simultaneously. Future directions noted include injecting domain-specific priors (dark channel, Retinex) and developing a general-purpose low-quality image restoration model.
