## Problem

Image-based motion planning requires generated motions to be both kinematically feasible and task-successful, but prior retrieval-based diffusion methods (e.g., R2-Diff) follow a roundabout path—forward-diffusing the retrieved motion toward a Gaussian prior before reverse-diffusing back to the target—making refinement inefficient and error-prone. The paper asks whether one can shorten this path by diffusing directly between the retrieved and target motions while still preserving motion feasibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB-D image of the robot and its environment as input. It outputs a sequence of L=100 end-effector poses in task space, where each pose comprises a 3D position (image coordinates plus depth), a 6D rotation vector, and a continuous grasp state in [0,1]. Inverse kinematics is applied post-hoc to convert the task-space trajectory into joint angles for execution in the Coppelia simulator.

## Method

READ retrieves a candidate motion from an image-motion database via learned image-feature similarity, then refines it through asymmetric diffusion: the forward process performs mean-reverting SDE interpolation (IR-SDE) in a frozen VAE latent space between the retrieved and target motions, preserving kinematic semantics, while the reverse process performs a one-step cold-diffusion refinement in the original high-dimensional task space. The reverse network is a transformer encoder that fuses spatially-aligned temporal embedding (STE) image features, pose encodings, and a continuous time embedding, with skip connections to the input poses. Because cold diffusion decouples the forward and reverse processes, the two can operate in different spaces without consistency constraints. Training is supervised with MSE loss, using k-nearest-neighbor motions (k=3) as augmented retrieval targets to improve robustness.

## Results

On 16 RLBench manipulation tasks, READ achieves 70.2% average success rate (Avg16) and 88.8% on the 12-task subset (Avg12), outperforming the strongest retrieval-based baseline R2-Diff (62.9% / 81.0%) by 7.3 and 7.8 percentage points respectively. READ also surpasses the best no-retrieval method RVT (65.9% / 74.3%) by 4.3 and 14.5 points. Ablations confirm that asymmetric diffusion (forward in latent space, reverse in original space) is critical: READ-O (original-space only) drops to 60.1% Avg16, and READ-L (latent-space only) drops to 38.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

READ outperforms all seven baselines on both Avg16 and Avg12. Against the closest retrieval-based competitor R2-Diff [36], READ gains +7.3 points on Avg16 (70.2 vs. 62.9) and +7.8 on Avg12 (88.8 vs. 81.0), with the largest per-task margins on BB (+30), PK (+34), and PU (+26). Among no-retrieval methods, RVT [16] is the strongest (65.9% Avg16) but still trails READ by 4.3 points; DDPM [19] and VPSDE [47] lag further at 54.4% and 50.2% respectively. DMO-EBM [35] (56.6% Avg16) and VINN [37] (18.7%) are substantially weaker, with VINN failing because its retrieval model is trained only on object appearance rather than motion-relevant features. VPSDE+CG [18] (14.6% Avg16) performs worst among retrieval methods, as classifier-free guidance over-constrains the predicted motion toward the retrieved trajectory, introducing small errors that cause task failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

READ extends the retrieval-based diffusion line of work (SDEdit, R2-Diff, condition-based methods like RDM and ReMoDiffuse) by replacing the roundabout forward-reverse path through a Gaussian prior with a direct interpolation between the retrieved and target motions in a latent space, drawing on IR-SDE for the forward SDE formulation. Its key conceptual departure is asymmetric diffusion—performing the forward process in a low-dimensional latent space (preserving kinematic feasibility) and the reverse process in the original task space (achieving high-resolution refinement)—enabled by cold diffusion's decoupling of forward and reverse designs. The reverse process also leverages Spatially-Aligned Temporal Embedding (STE) from prior work, which is only applicable in the original space, further motivating the asymmetric split.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

READ demonstrates that the choice of diffusion space for forward vs. reverse processes is a critical design axis in retrieval-based motion planning, with the ablation showing a ~30-point gap when both processes are confined to the latent space. The practical finding that a single reverse step (N=1) achieves 68.8% success versus 70.2% for N=100 suggests the method could be deployed with minimal iterative cost. However, the evaluation is limited to simulated RLBench tasks in CoppeliaSim with a fixed 1000-pair retrieval database, and the reliance on inverse kinematics and a pre-built image-motion database constrains real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the asymmetric design generalizes to multi-step or long-horizon tasks, whether the retrieval database can be learned online, and whether the approach transfers to joint-space planning without IK.
