## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods rely on sample-wise iterative optimization whose computational cost scales with the number of images per class (IPC) and image resolution, making large-IPC distillation prohibitively expensive (e.g., 90+ hours for 100-IPC on ImageWoof). This paper asks whether generative diffusion models can produce effective surrogate datasets far more efficiently, and identifies that the key missing properties in raw diffusion-generated data are representativeness (coverage of the full distribution) and diversity (mutual dissimilarity among generated samples).

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale labeled image dataset (ImageNet subsets at 256×256 resolution) and a pre-trained DiT latent diffusion model as input. It produces a small surrogate dataset of synthetic images with class labels, where the number of images per class is set by the IPC parameter. Notably, GPU memory and compute remain constant across all IPC settings since the diffusion model is fine-tuned once and images are generated from random noise via 50 denoising steps, decoupling generation cost from dataset size.

## Method

The method fine-tunes a pre-trained DiT model via PEFT (Difffit) with two additional minimax objectives on top of the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss. The representativeness term L_r = max_θ min_m σ(ẑ_θ, z_m) pulls each generated latent embedding toward the farthest real sample in a sliding memory bank, encouraging coverage of under-represented regions of the distribution. The diversity term L_d = min_θ max_d σ(ẑ_θ, z_d) pushes each generated embedding away from the most similar previously generated sample in a second memory bank, preventing mode collapse. The paper provides a theoretical justification by casting the joint objective as a tri-level stochastic control problem over the diffusion SDE, arguing that the set of controls satisfying all three criteria (generative fidelity, representativeness, diversity) is sufficiently large so no trade-off is incurred. This minimax formulation, rather than naive mean-matching, is what preserves diversity while enhancing representativeness, yielding state-of-the-art distillation performance in under one hour on a single GPU.

## Results

On the challenging ImageWoof subset, the proposed method achieves 65.7% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18 at 100-IPC, surpassing the best prior dataset distillation method IDC-1 (58.3%) by 7.4% and random selection (61.5%) by 4.2%. The method requires less than 1/20th the distillation time of previous approaches (under 1 hour vs. 90+ hours for IDC-1 at 100-IPC) while maintaining consistent GPU consumption across all IPC settings. On ImageNet-1K, the method reaches 58.6% at 50-IPC, outperforming RDED (56.5%) and the DiT baseline (52.9%). The MMD between surrogate and original feature distributions is also lowest (4.0–4.3) compared to all baselines (4.8–6.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every IPC setting and test architecture. Against the strongest prior DD method IDC-1, the gap widens with IPC: at 10-IPC the margin is modest (39.2 vs. 39.1 on ResNetAP-10), but at 100-IPC the method leads by 8.4% (64.5 vs. 56.1), confirming that pixel-level optimization degrades as the parameter space grows. Against the generative baseline DiT, the minimax criteria yield gains of 4.9–10.4% depending on IPC, with the largest improvement at 50-IPC (56.3 vs. 49.3 on ResNetAP-10). The feature-matching method DM is outperformed at all IPCs (e.g., 65.7 vs. 60.2 at 100-IPC ResNet-18), and the coreset method Herding, which is competitive at large IPCs, is still surpassed by 2.8–6.1%. On ImageNet-1K (Table 4), the method beats RDED by 2.1–2.3% and DiT by 4.7–5.7%, validating scalability beyond 10-class subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant sample-wise iterative optimization paradigm in dataset distillation (IDC-1, DM, GLaD) by replacing per-image pixel/embedding optimization with a single generative diffusion model fine-tuned via PEFT (Difffit on DiT). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of minimax criteria—pulling generated samples toward the farthest real sample in a memory bank and pushing them away from the most similar generated sample—which reframes representativeness and diversity as explicit training objectives within the diffusion loop rather than emergent properties of distribution matching. The theoretical analysis borrows from stochastic control of diffusion processes (Tzen & Raginsky) to frame the combined generative-accuracy, representativeness, and diversity objectives as a trilevel optimization, though the authors acknowledge solving only a discretized approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: distilling a 100-IPC surrogate for a 10-class ImageNet subset drops from over 90 hours (IDC-1) to under 1 hour with constant GPU memory regardless of IPC, making dataset distillation accessible to individual researchers and enabling personalized data condensation. The finding that pixel-level methods degrade below random selection at high IPC on fine-grained data (ImageWoof) is a useful cautionary result for the field. Open questions include whether the minimax criteria transfer to non-classification tasks (segmentation, detection), whether the approach generalizes beyond ImageNet-style natural images, and whether the theoretical trilevel formulation can yield tighter guarantees than the current plausibility argument.
