## Problem

Dense monocular reconstruction from images or video is hampered by visual ambiguities (textureless or specular surfaces) that cause pixel-level multi-view geometry to fail or produce inconsistent results. Existing approaches either impose weak hand-crafted local priors (e.g., smoothness) or use rigid per-image depth predictions that are difficult to integrate into multi-view optimisation. The paper asks whether purely local but strong neural priors, applied at a coarser-than-pixel granularity, are sufficient to resolve these ambiguities while retaining the flexibility of multi-view optimisation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB images (downsampled to 120×160 for VO) and, for depth completion, sparse metric depth points from an external sensor. Internally, each image is split into ~100–300 overlapping segments by SAM, and per-pixel surface normals are predicted by a CNN; the optimisable state is a single log-depth scale per segment rather than a per-pixel depth. Output geometry is represented as a dense 3D point cloud (not a depth image) because overlapping primitives can yield conflicting depths in their intersection; camera poses are SE(3) matrices and trajectories are produced for the VO task.

## Method

The core contribution is the SuperPrimitive representation: each image is segmented into minimal regions (via SAM with smallest-mask selection) and each region is assigned an unscaled depth map obtained by integrating predicted surface normals through a PDE solved with batched conjugate gradients (~100 ms for all segments). The only per-primitive optimisable parameter is a single depth scale, reducing the optimisation dimensionality from O(pixels) to O(segments). The back-end performs multi-view photometric alignment by warping depth-scaled primitives into other views and minimising per-segment L1 photometric residuals with Adam, jointly estimating relative poses and depth scales without requiring pre-established correspondences or explicit occlusion handling. For monocular VO, a keyframe-based sliding window (size 5) alternates tracking (adapted Lucas-Kanade on the latest keyframe) and mapping (joint photometric cost over temporally neighbouring and supplementary views), requiring no special initialisation heuristics because the two-view SfM formulation bootstraps the system directly.

## Results

On the TUM RGBD Freiburg 1 split (8 sequences), the proposed monocular VO system achieves a mean ATE of 0.153 m, outperforming all compared methods including DepthCov (0.167), TartanVO (0.188), DeepV2D (0.215), and DeepFactors (0.241). On the VOID depth completion benchmark (150-point setting), the method achieves iMAE 47.32 and iRMSE 83.40 in a fully zero-shot manner, beating the zero-shot VI-Depth (DPT-Hybrid+NYUv2) baseline on three of four metrics. For few-view SfM on ScanNet, depth estimation quality saturates after just 2 supplementary views and consistently outperforms DeepV2D across all view counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TUM VO, the proposed method achieves the best mean ATE (0.153 m), beating DepthCov (0.167) by 8.4%, TartanVO (0.188) by 18.6%, DeepV2D (0.215) by 28.8%, and DeepFactors (0.241) by 36.5%; it is best or second-best on 5 of 8 individual sequences. On VOID depth completion (zero-shot group), the method outperforms VI-Depth with DPT-Hybrid+NYUv2 on iMAE (47.32 vs 55.90) and iRMSE (83.40 vs 85.20), and also beats all other zero-shot baselines (VOICED, NLSPN, ScaffNet, KBNet, MonDi) on every reported metric. The only method that beats Ours on VOID is the trained VI-Depth (DPT-Hybrid) variant (iMAE 46.62, iRMSE 74.67), which was fine-tuned on the VOID training set. On few-view SfM (ScanNet), the method consistently outperforms DeepV2D in iMAE and iRMSE at every supplementary-view count, while requiring no external pose initialisation or global depth prior. Notably, the method is purely monocular and uses no global bundle adjustment, yet still surpasses systems that incorporate learned global priors or RGB-D inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the pixel-level dense reconstruction paradigm (DTAM, DSO) and from global monocular depth prediction approaches (CodeSLAM, DPT-based methods) by introducing a new intermediate representation: SuperPrimitives, which are SAM-segmented image regions augmented with surface-normal-integrated geometry up to an unknown scale. Conceptually, it sits between superpixel-based methods and parametric primitive approaches (planes, quadrics, object-level SLAM), but replaces hand-crafted geometric assumptions with learned local priors from off-the-shelf neural networks. The multi-view back-end adapts Lucas-Kanade-style photometric tracking to operate per-primitive rather than per-pixel, reducing the optimisation dimensionality from per-pixel depth to per-primitive depth scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that strong local single-view priors (segmentation + normals) combined with multi-view photometric consistency can replace both global depth prediction networks and elaborate hand-crafted heuristics, enabling zero-shot performance on depth completion and competitive monocular VO without task-specific training. Open questions include the computational cost of SAM (querying 300+ points per frame), the 2–3 FPS tracking speed limiting real-time deployment, the absence of explicit occlusion handling or primitive correspondence, and whether the approach generalises to outdoor or highly dynamic scenes. The framework also raises the question of whether semantic primitive association (which the authors note SAM can partially provide) could further improve robustness once these models become cheaper.
