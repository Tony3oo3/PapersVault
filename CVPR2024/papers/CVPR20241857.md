## Problem

Snapshot-based dataset pruning methods produce coresets that fluctuate across training epochs and fail to generalize to different pruning rates. Existing remedies that incorporate training dynamics (e.g., Forgetting, AUM) rely on averaging over the full training trajectory, which inadvertently overlooks well-generalized samples whose contributions are stable rather than extreme. The paper identifies the need to simultaneously capture extensive training dynamics and highlight samples with high temporal variability in their contribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full training dataset of RGB images with class labels and a pre-specified neural network architecture. During a single training pass, it records per-sample predicted probability vectors (not pixel-wise gradients) at each epoch, constrained to a K-epoch sliding window with EMA to limit memory. The output is a coreset of M samples paired with per-sample importance scores, which are then used to weight the loss during subsequent coreset training (importance-aware training).

## Method

TDDS employs a temporal dual-depth scoring strategy. In the inner level, each sample's per-epoch contribution is estimated by projecting its gradient onto the accumulated gradient direction, which is approximated via a KL-based loss difference between adjacent epochs—avoiding expensive pixel-wise gradient computation while retaining non-target class information. In the outer level, the variance of these projected contributions over a K-epoch window is computed to identify well-generalized samples, replacing the averaging operation used by prior methods. An exponential moving average across windows aggregates scores over the full training trajectory without storing all intermediate predictions. Finally, the selected coreset is trained with sample-wise importance weighting, transferring the relative contribution structure from full-dataset training to the reduced set.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 under Strategy-E, TDDS achieves 54.51% top-1 accuracy at 90% pruning rate (only 10% data retained), surpassing the best baseline (Moderate, 41.82%) by 12.69% and Random (46.68%) by 7.83%. On CIFAR-10 at the same 90% pruning rate, TDDS reaches 85.46% accuracy versus 83.77% for Random. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-34, TDDS achieves 53.91% top-1 at 90% pruning, outperforming Forgetting (52.14%) and Moderate (52.45%). With Swin-T on ImageNet-1K, TDDS attains 78.33% at 50% pruning, exceeding Dyn-Unc (77.64%) by 0.69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDDS consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every pruning rate on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The gap is most dramatic at aggressive pruning: on CIFAR-100 at 90% pruning, TDDS (54.51%) exceeds Moderate (41.82%) by 12.69%, Forgetting (25.82%) by 28.69%, Dyn-Unc (15.20%) by 39.31%, and EL2N (9.10%) by 45.41%. Moderate is the closest competitor at lower pruning rates (e.g., 80% on CIFAR-10: 87.75% vs. TDDS 91.30%) but degrades sharply at 90% pruning, while snapshot-based methods like EL2N and AUM collapse to near-chance accuracy. Under Strategy-P (extended training), TDDS also leads CCS [51] by 0.54% on CIFAR-100 at 70% pruning (69.46% vs. 68.92%). On ImageNet-1K, TDDS maintains its advantage over Forgetting and Moderate at 90% pruning (53.91% vs. 52.14% and 52.45%), and with Swin-T it surpasses Dyn-Unc by 0.69% at 50% pruning (78.33% vs. 77.64%), confirming cross-architecture generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDDS sits at the intersection of three research threads: snapshot-based pruning (EL2N, Moderate, Entropy), training-dynamics-based selection (Forgetting, AUM), and variance-based importance estimation (VoG, Dyn-Unc). It departs from the averaging-based dynamics incorporation of Forgetting and AUM by replacing the mean with temporal variance as the selection criterion, and from Dyn-Unc by substituting pixel-wise gradient computation with a KL-based loss-difference proxy. The inner-level gradient projection onto the accumulated direction follows Du et al.'s sharpness-aware minimization framework, while the outer-level variance maximization is motivated by optimization-space matching between coreset and full dataset, drawing on Fisher's gradient-variance matching for OOD generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.69% accuracy gap over the best baseline at 90% pruning on CIFAR-100 is substantial and consistent across CIFAR-10, ImageNet-1K, and multiple architectures (ResNet-18/34, Swin-T), suggesting the dual-depth design addresses a real weakness in prior methods rather than a dataset-specific artifact. The EMA-based implementation and KL-loss proxy make the method practical for large-scale settings like ImageNet without pixel-wise backpropagation. Open questions include whether the KL-based loss difference generalizes beyond classification (e.g., regression, detection), whether the importance-aware coreset training (Eq. 13) transfers to other selection criteria, and whether the method's advantage persists under non-i.i.d. or noisy-label conditions.
