## Problem

Adversarially trained models exhibit significant disparities in robust accuracy across classes (the 'robust fairness' issue), where average robustness improves but worst-class robustness lags far behind. Existing remedies rely on heuristic reweighting, require extra validation sets, or are locked to specific training methods like TRADES. The paper identifies the root cause as an unknown group (class) distribution shift induced by adversarial perturbations during training, and asks whether principled optimization can replace ad-hoc reweighting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100) and outputs class labels, operating within a fixed perturbation budget (ε=8/255 on CIFAR-10). A key operational detail is that FAAL is a plug-in intermediate maximization step: it requires only the per-instance cross-entropy loss and per-class CW margin loss computed on the current mini-batch's adversarial examples, with no access to a validation set or historical epoch statistics. It can be applied as a 2-epoch fine-tuning on an already adversarially trained model or integrated into training from scratch after a warm-up period.

## Method

FAAL extends the standard min-max adversarial training into a min-max-max framework by inserting an intermediate maximization that learns Class-wise Distributionally Adversarial Weights (CDAW) via Distributional Robust Optimization. Specifically, it formulates the worst-case class distribution as a KL-divergence ball of radius τ around the uniform distribution over classes, and solves the resulting conic convex optimization per mini-batch to obtain the adversarial weight vector w*^cda. The per-class losses used for this optimization are CW margin losses (max_j≠y z_j − z_y) rather than cross-entropy, which better captures inter-class discrepancy. The weighted loss then drives the outer minimization to update model parameters, effectively training the model to resist unknown group distribution shifts. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows that the FAAL loss is an upper bound on the true test loss with probability ≥ 1 − e^{−τn+O(n)}, and the method is fully decoupled from the choice of inner adversary (PGD, TRADES, MART, AWP).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WRN-34-10, FAAL_AT fine-tunes a PGD-AT model in just 2 epochs to achieve 50.81% average AutoAttack accuracy (35.70% worst-class), outperforming FRL-RWRM which requires 80 epochs (49.60% / 31.70% at τ=0.07). When combined with AWP, FAAL_AT-AWP on TRADES-AWP reaches 53.93% average AA accuracy (37.00% worst-class) versus 46.53% (28.60%) for FRL. Training from scratch on CIFAR-10 with Preact-ResNet18, FAAL_TRADES achieves 33.60% worst-class AA accuracy, surpassing WAT_TRADES (30.70%) and CFA_TRADES (26.50%) while maintaining competitive average robustness (48.48% vs. 46.16% and 50.10%). On CIFAR-100, FAAL_TRADES attains 3.00% worst-class AA accuracy, matching WAT_TRADES and exceeding CFA_TRADES (2.00%) and TRADES (1.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FRL-RWRM (the only prior fine-tuning method for robust fairness), FAAL_AT wins on worst-class AutoAttack accuracy across nearly all base models: +4.0% on PGD-AT (35.70% vs. 31.70%), +0.7% on TRADES (35.30% vs. 34.60%), +3.7% on MART (34.00% vs. 30.30%), and +8.4% on TRADES-AWP (37.00% vs. 28.60%), while requiring only 2 epochs versus FRL's 80. FAAL also preserves or improves clean accuracy (e.g., 86.23% vs. 85.12% on PGD-AT), whereas FRL typically sacrifices 1–3% clean accuracy. In the train-from-scratch setting (Table 2), FAAL_TRADES achieves the best worst-class AA accuracy (33.60%) among all methods, beating WAT_TRADES by 2.9% and CFA_TRADES by 7.1%, though CFA_TRADES retains a slight edge in average AA accuracy (50.10% vs. 48.48%). On CIFAR-100 (Table 3), FAAL_TRADES matches WAT_TRADES on worst-class AA (3.00%) while achieving higher average AA (23.57% vs. 22.89%) and higher clean accuracy (55.87% vs. 53.99%), but CFA_TRADES still leads in average AA (24.56%). FAAL's key practical advantage is that it is adversary-agnostic (pluggable into any min-max AT), requires no validation set, and adds negligible per-epoch overhead (2.69 min vs. 2.63 min for TRADES on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard min-max adversarial training framework (Madry et al.) into a min-max-max formulation by inserting an intermediate maximization over class-wise distributionally adversarial weights, solved via KL-Divergence DRO. It borrows the group DRO uncertainty set formulation from Sagawa et al. and Hu et al., but departs from prior robust fairness methods (FRL, WAT, CFA) by replacing heuristic or validation-set-dependent reweighting with a per-batch conic convex optimization that requires no extra validation data and is agnostic to the underlying AT method. The key conceptual departure is reframing the robust fairness problem as an overfitting issue caused by unknown group distribution shift induced by adversarial perturbations, rather than as a purely empirical reweighting challenge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 2-epoch fine-tuning result: transforming a biased robust model into a fair one at 40× lower computational cost than FRL's 80-epoch fine-tuning, without sacrificing average clean or robust accuracy. The plug-and-play design (compatible with PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, AWP) makes it broadly applicable. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with small CNNs (WRN-34-10, PRN-18, RN-18), and the τ hyperparameter requires manual tuning per dataset. Open questions include whether the DRO-based reweighting scales to larger datasets (ImageNet), deeper architectures, or non-class-based groupings, and whether the theoretical guarantee in Theorem 1 translates to tighter practical bounds.
