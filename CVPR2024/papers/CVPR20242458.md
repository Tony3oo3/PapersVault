## Problem

Existing counting tasks—whether class-specific or class-agnostic—treat all objects of the same class indifferently, ignoring fine-grained attributes that distinguish them. Real-world applications (traffic monitoring, retail analytics, warehouse inventory) require counting objects conditioned on specific attributes within a class, such as 'red apples in the left bowl' versus 'green apples elsewhere.' This paper introduces Referring Expression Counting (REC) to close this gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes a query RGB image and a natural-language referring expression (composed of a class noun plus one or more attributes) as input. The referring expression is internally decomposed into a subject part (object + local attributes like color, material, gender) and a context part (relational attributes like location, relative size). The model outputs a fixed set of 900 point predictions (DETR-style); positive detections are selected by thresholding class logits against the CLS token (threshold 0.25) and individual text tokens (threshold 0.35), and the final count is the sum of positive predictions with their 2D point coordinates.

## Method

GroundingREC adapts the GroundingDino open-set detector by replacing bounding-box regression with point regression (L1 loss) and using the CLS token as the global semantic representation of the referring expression rather than individual text tokens. Two novel modules are added: (1) a global-local feature fusion module that splits image tokens into lower-layer (local) and higher-layer (global) groups, performs cross-attention where local tokens attend to the subject text and global tokens attend to the context text, then lets local tokens query global tokens to inject relational context; and (2) a contrastive learning module that, for multiple referring expressions describing different attributes of the same class, pushes the matched image embedding toward the positive attribute text embedding and away from negative attribute embeddings. Training freezes the BERT text encoder and Swin-T image backbone, optimizing only the fusion modules and GroundingDino's cross-modality encoder/decoder with AdamW (lr=1e-5), using a weighted sum of L1 localization, cross-entropy classification, and contrastive losses.

## Results

On the REC-8K benchmark, GroundingREC achieves MAE 6.80 and RMSE 18.13 on the validation set, and MAE 6.50 and RMSE 19.79 on the test set, substantially outperforming the next-best method CounTX (MAE 11.84/25.62) and the finetuned GroundingDino baseline (MAE 8.88/21.95). In terms of localization, GroundingREC reaches F1 0.69 on the test set versus 0.66 for finetuned GroundingDino and 0.35 for unfinetuned GroundingDino. On the FSC-147 class-agnostic counting benchmark in a zero-shot setting, GroundingREC achieves MAE 10.12 on the test set, surpassing the previous best zero-shot method (finetuned GroundingDino, MAE 10.82) and matching the top few-shot method CACViT (MAE 9.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroundingREC outperforms all eight baselines on the REC-8K benchmark. Against the text-based counting methods, it reduces MAE by 5.04 over CounTX (6.50 vs. 11.84 test), 6.50 over ZSC-Swin-T (6.50 vs. 13.00), and 10.77 over TFOC (6.50 vs. 17.27). Against the open-set detection baseline GroundingDino, GroundingREC improves MAE from 8.88 to 6.50 (−26.6%) and F1 from 0.66 to 0.69 after finetuning, and from 11.71 to 6.50 (−44.4%) and F1 from 0.35 to 0.69 without finetuning. On FSC-147 in the zero-shot setting, GroundingREC (MAE 10.12 test) beats finetuned GroundingDino (10.82) and CounTX (15.88), and is within 1.0 MAE of the best few-shot method CACViT (9.13), while all other zero-shot baselines (ZSC 22.09, TFOC-text 24.79, unfinetuned GroundingDino 54.40) are substantially worse. The paper notes that ZSC and CounTX cannot report localization metrics because they output density maps rather than point predictions, limiting their fine-grained attribute discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GroundingDino, an open-set object detection model, adapting its DETR-style architecture from bounding-box regression to point-based counting and repurposing its text-image cross-attention for attribute-aware object localization. Conceptually, it departs from the class-agnostic counting (CAC) line (ZSC, CounTX, TFOC) which treats all objects of a given class identically, and from referring expression detection/segmentation which localizes but does not count. The key positioning is that REC occupies an unexplored intersection: counting objects that match a fine-grained referring expression specifying attributes *within* a single class, rather than counting all instances of a class or detecting a single referred object.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The REC task and REC-8K benchmark open a practically motivated research direction—fine-grained, attribute-conditioned counting for applications like traffic flow analysis, retail demographics, and inventory management—that neither class-specific nor class-agnostic counting addresses. The paper honestly identifies persistent failure modes (negation, complex spatial relations, text-in-image, ambiguous expressions) that point to unresolved challenges in text reasoning and image-text alignment. An open question is whether the global-local fusion and contrastive learning modules generalize beyond the moderate-scale REC-8K dataset, and whether the zero-shot CAC competitiveness (matching few-shot methods) reflects a broader property of strong open-set detectors rather than the proposed modules specifically.
