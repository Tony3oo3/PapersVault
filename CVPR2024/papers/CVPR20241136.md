## Problem

State-of-the-art deep learning point cloud filtering methods (e.g., ScoreDenoise, IterativePFN) move noisy points along stochastic trajectories, which are error-prone, sensitive to discretization step size, and often require regularization or post-processing to maintain good point distributions. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating filtering as an optimal transport problem that moves points along straight paths, reducing discretization errors and eliminating the need for regularization while preserving the original point distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241136/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy point cloud patch (k-nearest neighbors around a reference point, typically n points) and outputs filtered positions for all patch points simultaneously. Training uses Gaussian noise up to σ_H = 2% of the bounding sphere radius, with intermediate states created as linear interpolations between high-noise and clean variants. Inference starts from an unknown noise level (modeled as an intermediate state) and iteratively applies the Euler ODE solver over N steps, with the DistanceModule scaling the trajectory to ensure convergence at the surface. The network is lightweight at ~530K parameters (17% of IterativePFN's 3.2M).

## Method

StraightPCF models noisy patches as intermediate states X_t = (1−t)X_0 + tX_1 between a high-noise variant X_0 and the clean surface X_1, then learns a constant flow velocity via a DGCNN-based VelocityModule trained to predict δ(X_1, X_0) = X_1 − X_0 at each intermediate state. To improve trajectory straightness, K=2 VelocityModules are coupled and jointly fine-tuned with a loss that enforces constant velocity across segments while pulling intermediate positions toward the linear interpolation. A separate DistanceModule estimates a scalar d_φ (via Sigmoid of a max-pooled feature) representing the relative distance from the current state to the clean surface, which scales the velocity to prevent overshooting. Inference applies a modified Euler solver where each step is scaled by d_φ, yielding T = K·N total filtering steps that converge near the clean surface without any regularization term.

## Results

On the PUNet dataset at 10K resolution and 3% noise, StraightPCF achieves CD 3.287 and P2M 1.126, outperforming the best baseline IterativePFN (CD 4.241, P2M 1.376) by 22.5% and 18.2% respectively. On the unseen PCNet dataset at 10K/3% noise, StraightPCF reduces CD error by 17.1% and P2M error by 24.1% over IterativePFN. At 50K resolution on PCNet (3% noise), StraightPCF achieves CD 1.816 and P2M 0.445, a 13.2% and 9.4% improvement over the next best method. On real-world Kinect data, StraightPCF obtains the lowest CD (1.296×10⁻⁴) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StraightPCF achieves the best CD (Chamfer Distance) across nearly all settings on both PUNet and PCNet, with the most dramatic gains at high noise (3%): 22.5% CD improvement over IterativePFN on PUNet 10K and 17.1% on PCNet 10K. On P2M (Point-to-Mesh), StraightPCF dominates on PCNet across all noise levels (e.g., 0.445 vs. 0.716 for IterativePFN at 50K/3%), but on PUNet 50K, DeepPSR achieves slightly lower P2M at 1% (0.076 vs. 0.111) and 2% (0.296 vs. 0.266) noise, suggesting StraightPCF's straight-flow approach trades a small amount of surface proximity for superior point distribution. Against PCN and PointFilter, StraightPCF wins decisively on both metrics across all settings, with PCN being the weakest baseline by a large margin. On real-world Kinect data, StraightPCF achieves the lowest CD (1.296) but its P2M (0.664) is marginally higher than PointFilter (0.644) and ScoreDenoise (0.652), which the authors attribute to the sparse nature of Kinect scans. Notably, IterativePFN generalizes poorly beyond its training noise scale (σ=2%), while StraightPCF maintains strong performance at 3% noise on unseen PCNet shapes, demonstrating better generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes point cloud filtering as an optimal transport problem between high-noise and clean patch variants, drawing directly on the Reflow mechanism (Liu et al., 2023) and its extension to point cloud generation (Wu et al., 2023), which demonstrated that straight paths between distributions reduce discretization error. It departs from the dominant stochastic-trajectory paradigm used by ScoreDenoise (score-based) and IterativePFN (displacement-based), both of which model filtering as a reverse Markov process requiring many small steps. Architecturally, it retains the DGCNN graph-convolution backbone shared with IterativePFN but replaces its stacked IterationModules with a coupled VelocityModule stack plus a DistanceModule, yielding a network roughly 83% smaller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The straight-flow formulation eliminates the need for regularization (graph Laplacian, post-processing) that prior methods require to avoid clustering artifacts, and the 17% parameter count makes the method practical for resource-constrained settings. A key open question is whether the optimal-transport framing generalizes beyond Gaussian noise to structured sensor artifacts (e.g., missing data, non-uniform density), and the authors note degraded performance on very sparse inputs. The approach also raises the broader question of whether other iterative 3D refinement tasks (normal estimation, shape completion) could benefit from straight-path formulations instead of stochastic ones.
