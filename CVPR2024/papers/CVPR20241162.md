## Problem

Existing occupancy forecasting methods can only predict discrete future snapshots at fixed frequencies matching sensor sampling rates, and multi-sensor fusion pipelines require strictly synchronized LiDAR and camera inputs. This limits real-time responsiveness and makes the system brittle to the asynchronous data arrival that is common in practice. The paper asks whether a single model can continuously stream occupancy predictions at arbitrary future timestamps while fusing multi-modal data that arrives at irregular, non-aligned intervals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous LiDAR point cloud streams (sampled every 0.2 s, voxelized to 200×200×13 over a 100 m × 100 m range at 0.5 m resolution) and surround camera image streams (sampled every 0.5 s, resized to 224×480 or 320×800), each independently encoded into BEV feature maps. Rather than requiring frame-aligned inputs, the model ingests timestamped BEV features in chronological order regardless of modality. The output is a future BEV occupancy flow (video panoptic instance segmentation) at any user-specified set of future timestamps; the prediction granularity is decoupled from the training label frequency and controlled solely by the ODE integration step, enabling arbitrarily dense or sparse output.

## Method

StreamingFlow's core component, SpatialGRU-ODE, adapts the GRU-ODE-Bayes continuous-time framework to spatial BEV feature maps of shape [B, C, H, W]. The GRU gates (reset, update, candidate) are reinterpreted as CNN blocks that compute the derivative of the BEV state (Eq. 4), and an ODE solver (Euler or Midpoint) integrates this derivative forward in time to propagate the BEV state between observations. For asynchronous fusion, incoming BEV features from LiDAR and camera are treated as modality-agnostic measurements: the state propagates via ODE until a new observation arrives, at which point a dual-pathway SpatialGRU update module fuses the predicted state with the new measurement and computes a KL-divergence auxiliary loss between their implicit distributions. This predict-then-update loop eliminates the need for frame synchronization, and because the learned derivative is continuous, the same trained model can predict at any future timestamp or horizon without retraining, achieving zero-shot generalization to unseen longer prediction windows.

## Results

On nuScenes, StreamingFlow-base achieves 53.9 IoU and 52.7 PQ for future instance occupancy flow prediction, surpassing the best vision-only method (BEVerse with Swin-small, 40.8 IoU / 36.1 PQ) by +13.1 IoU and the best LiDAR-only method (BE-STI, 40.1 IoU / 41.0 PQ) by +13.8 IoU. On Lyft L5, StreamingFlow-tiny reaches 56.9 IoU and 55.9 PQ, outperforming the reproduced BEVFusion-tiny (54.6 IoU / 55.7 PQ) by +2.3 IoU. The method also demonstrates zero-shot generalization to unseen future horizons up to 8s, with only −0.9 IoU and −1.8 PQ degradation compared to a model explicitly trained with 8s ground-truth labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamingFlow consistently outperforms all single-modality baselines by large margins: it beats the strongest vision-only method BEVerse (Swin-small) by +7.0 IoU / +10.0 PQ, the best LiDAR-only method BE-STI by +7.7 IoU / +5.1 PQ, and FIERY by +10.8 IoU / +15.9 PQ. Against the closest fusion-based competitor FusionAD, StreamingFlow-base (using a smaller image resolution of 800×320 vs. 1600×900) still leads by +2.4 IoU and +1.6 PQ. On Lyft L5, StreamingFlow-tiny surpasses the reproduced BEVFusion-tiny by +2.3 IoU and +0.2 PQ, and dominates ST-P3 by +20.6 IoU / +23.5 PQ. Notably, the tiny variant (PillarNet + Effi-B4) already exceeds all vision-only and LiDAR-only methods, indicating that the asynchronous ODE-based fusion contributes more than simply scaling up backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamingFlow builds directly on GRU-ODE-Bayes [5], which models continuous dynamics from sporadic observations, and adapts it to the BEV feature space for occupancy prediction. It departs from the dominant BEV fusion paradigm (BEVFusion, FusionAD) that requires strict multi-modal synchronization and fixed-interval prediction, replacing it with an asynchronous trigger-based update mechanism. In the occupancy forecasting lineage (FIERY, StretchBEV, BE-STI, MotionNet), all prior methods produce discrete snapshots at predetermined future timestamps; StreamingFlow reframes the problem as continuous ODE propagation, decoupling prediction granularity from training label frequency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The streaming formulation enables real-time occupancy updates as new sensor data arrives, which is practically important for latency-sensitive autonomous driving stacks. The zero-shot generalization to unseen 8-second horizons (only −0.9 IoU vs. fully supervised) suggests the ODE dynamics capture genuine scene motion structure rather than memorizing label intervals. However, the ablation reveals that on nuScenes, conventional synchronous fusion still outperforms SpatialGRU-ODE by ~2.4 IoU, indicating the async advantage is dataset- and scenario-dependent. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer prediction windows beyond 8 s, whether real-time inference on embedded hardware is feasible given the iterative ODE solver, and whether the framework extends to other BEV tasks (detection, planning) beyond occupancy.
