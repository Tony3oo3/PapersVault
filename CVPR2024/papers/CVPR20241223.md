## Problem

Existing traffic scene datasets (KITTI, Cityscapes, BDD100K) are collected from driving platforms and are ill-suited for traffic monitoring scenes captured by high-hanging cameras (4.5–6 m), which observe far more crowded intersections with much greater instance-size variance. Models trained on driving datasets suffer significant performance drops on monitoring scenes due to a large domain gap, yet no dedicated dataset with pixel-level and instance-level annotations existed to study or improve traffic monitoring scene parsing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241223/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB traffic monitoring images at native resolution 2942×1989 (downsampled to 769×769 for CNN backbones or 1024×1024 for transformer backbones during training) and produces a 21-class pixel-level semantic segmentation map plus per-instance masks for six traffic-participant categories (person, rider, car, truck, bus, motorcycle, bicycle). The scenes are substantially denser than driving datasets—averaging 42 traffic participants per image (10.7 humans, 31.3 vehicles) versus 5–19 in prior driving benchmarks—and exhibit a wide scale range from small distant vehicles to large foreground objects.

## Method

The proposed Detail Refining Decoder (DRD) is built on the SegNeXt MSCAN-Base encoder and consists of two stages. The first stage follows a DeepLabv3+ design, fusing ASPP features with ×8-downsampled encoder features to produce a fine-level high-resolution feature map. The second stage is a Region Refining Module that introduces N learnable region tokens; it computes multi-head cross-attention between the region tokens (as queries) and the flattened high-resolution features (as keys/values) to obtain region embeddings, passes them through an FFN, then generates per-region attention maps by projecting the refined tokens and features into query/key spaces. These N attention maps are applied via broadcast multiplication to the high-resolution feature tensor, selectively refining pixel affinities within each semantic region before a final convolution produces the segmentation map. This design avoids the prohibitive cost of full self-attention on high-resolution features while still capturing long-range spatial dependencies specific to each semantic region.

## Results

On the TSP6K validation set, the proposed Detail Refining Decoder (DRD) with MSCAN-Base backbone achieves 75.8% mIoU and 58.4% iIoU, outperforming the strongest prior method SegNeXt MSCAN-Large (74.8% mIoU, 57.7% iIoU) by 1.0% mIoU and 0.7% iIoU, while using fewer parameters (46.1M vs. 48.9M) and lower FLOPs (90.1 vs. 258.6 GFLOPs). On the test set, DRD reaches 75.9% mIoU and 58.0% iIoU. For instance segmentation, QueryInst achieves the best APseg of 31.5% on validation, and for UDA, HRDA attains the highest mIoU of 54.1% when adapting from Cityscapes to TSP6K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRD outperforms all 18 prior scene parsing methods on both mIoU and iIoU across validation and test sets. Compared to the closest competitor SegNeXt MSCAN-Large (74.8% mIoU), DRD gains 1.0% mIoU and 0.7% iIoU on validation while using 6% fewer parameters and 65% fewer FLOPs. Against Swin-UperNet (74.9% mIoU, 121.3M params, 1184.6 GFLOPs), DRD achieves 0.9% higher mIoU with only 38% of the parameters and 7.6% of the compute. Among CNN-based methods, DeepLabv3+ (73.1% mIoU) and DANet (72.3% mIoU) are the strongest, but both trail DRD by over 2.5% mIoU. Notably, SETR with ViT-Large backbone performs worst (70.5% mIoU, 44.9% iIoU), confirming that pure transformer backbones struggle with the small-object-heavy monitoring scenes. For instance segmentation (Table 4), QueryInst leads with 31.5% APseg, while Mask-RCNN achieves 23.5% APseg; for UDA (Table 5), HRDA dominates with 54.1% mIoU (Cityscapes→TSP6K), far exceeding DAFormer (39.5%) and the baseline (26.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the traffic scene parsing line of work (KITTI, Cityscapes, BDD100K) by shifting the observation platform from in-vehicle driving cameras to elevated traffic monitoring cameras (4.5–6 m), a domain the authors argue has been largely neglected. Methodologically, the proposed Detail Refining Decoder (DRD) builds directly on SegNeXt's MSCAN encoder and DeepLabv3+'s ASPP-based encoder-decoder structure, while the region refining module borrows the learnable region-token idea from Mask2Former to compute cross-attention between N region tokens and high-resolution patch features. The paper also positions TSP6K as a complement to SA-1B's monitoring traffic images, which are class-agnostic, by providing class-known pixel and instance annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSP6K is the first large-scale traffic monitoring dataset with both semantic and instance annotations, filling a clear gap for applications like traffic flow analysis that require elevated-camera understanding. The empirical results reveal a substantial domain gap: UDA methods that achieve ~66% mIoU on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes drop to ~45% on SYNTHIA→TSP6K, and instance segmentation APs remain below 32%, indicating that existing methods are far from solving the crowded, multi-scale monitoring setting. Limitations include geographic restriction to China (no left-hand-driving scenes), RGB-only modality, and a relatively modest 6,000-image scale, leaving open whether the findings generalize and whether larger multi-modal collections would further close the driving-to-monitoring gap.
