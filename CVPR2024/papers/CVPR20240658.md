## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion-based text-to-video models achieve high frame resolution but largely overlook intricate temporal dynamics, leading to action occurrence disorders, crude motions, and unsmooth transitions. The root cause is the modality heterogeneity between succinct abstract text and the specific, redundant frames required to render actions. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly modeling temporal dynamics through structured scene representations guided by LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240658/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt as input (ranging from simple action labels in UCF-101 to multi-action descriptions averaging 50.4 words in ActivityNet) and produces a 256×256 video of 16 keyframes sampled from a 2-second clip, interpolated to higher frame rates, with a 32×32×4 latent space. The text is first processed by ChatGPT via in-context learning to extract ordered actions and imagine enriched scenes before the diffusion backbone generates the video. The DSG-encoded features are injected into the 3D-UNet decoder at multiple levels via cross-attention, alongside CLIP-encoded raw text features.

## Method

The core contribution is the Dynamic Scene Manager (Dysen) module, a three-step pipeline that leverages ChatGPT (GPT-3.5/GPT-4) with in-context learning to (1) extract key actions with proper chronological ordering, (2) convert them into a sequential Dynamic Scene Graph (DSG) skeleton, and (3) enrich each scene graph frame with additional triplets and attributes using a sliding window context mechanism for temporal coherence. The enriched DSG is encoded by a novel Recurrent Graph Transformer (RGTrm, 12 layers, 8 heads) that models temporal dynamics through recurrent query updates across frames. The resulting fine-grained spatio-temporal features are fused into the backbone latent video diffusion model's 3D-UNet decoder via two sequential cross-attention blocks (first with DSG features, then with CLIP text features). Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training the autoencoder on WebVid, pre-training the text-conditioned diffusion model with DSG annotations, and finally updating the full Dysen-VDM system with the dynamic scene managing module.

## Results

On UCF-101 (fine-tuning), Dysen-VDM achieves 95.23 IS and 255.42 FVD, setting a new state-of-the-art over the previous best (Latent-Shift: 92.72 IS, 360.04 FVD). In the zero-shot setting, it scores 35.57 IS / 325.42 FVD on UCF-101 and 12.64 FID / 0.3204 CLIPSIM on MSR-VTT, outperforming all baselines. On the action-complex ActivityNet benchmark, human evaluation shows Dysen-VDM leading with 86.6 action faithfulness, 92.4 scene richness, and 87.3 movement fluency (out of 100), surpassing CogVideo by 19.1, 17.4, and 5.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dysen-VDM wins across all evaluated settings. On UCF-101 fine-tuning, it improves IS by 2.51 over Latent-Shift (92.72) and 4.49 over Latent-VDM (90.74), while reducing FVD by 104.62 over Latent-Shift (360.04) and 102.92 over Latent-VDM (358.34). In the zero-shot UCF-101 setting, it beats MakeVideo (33.00 IS, 367.23 FVD) and AlignLatent (33.45 IS, 550.61 FVD) on both metrics. On MSR-VTT zero-shot, it achieves the best FID (12.64 vs. 13.17 for MakeVideo) and CLIPSIM (0.3204 vs. 0.3049 for MakeVideo, 0.3005 for VideoFactory). On the action-complex ActivityNet human evaluation, Dysen-VDM dominates all three baselines (CogVideo, VDM, Latent-VDM) by large margins, with the largest gap in scene richness (92.4 vs. 75.0 for CogVideo) and movement fluency (87.3 vs. 81.5 for CogVideo, 46.8 for VDM). Notably, despite not using large-scale pre-training, Dysen-VDM outperforms CogVideo (which relies on extensive pre-training) on all ActivityNet human-evaluation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Video Diffusion Model (VDM) and Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) as its backbone T2V synthesizer, and on Dynamic Scene Graph (DSG) representations as the structured semantic encoding of video content. It departs from prior diffusion-based T2V work, which has largely focused on improving frame resolution and spatial fidelity, by explicitly modeling temporal dynamics through an LLM-driven planning pipeline. The key conceptual departure is treating ChatGPT as a 'dynamic scene manager' that performs action planning, event-to-DSG conversion, and scene imagination via in-context learning, then encoding the resulting DSG with a novel recurrent graph Transformer (RGTrm) integrated into the 3D-UNet decoder via cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies temporal dynamics modeling as the under-addressed 'crux' of T2V synthesis and provides a concrete, modular solution that significantly improves motion faithfulness and scene richness, especially for multi-action prompts. The LLM dependency (ChatGPT API) raises practical concerns about cost, reproducibility, and latency that limit immediate deployment, and the DSG representation may not capture all forms of continuous motion. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos (beyond 2-second clips), whether the DSG bottleneck constrains expressiveness for highly complex scenes, and whether the gains persist when the LLM is replaced by a smaller or open-source model.
