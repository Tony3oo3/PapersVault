key_contribution: Proposes MULDE, a video anomaly detector that modifies denoising score matching to train a scalar neural network whose gradient approximates the negative log-density gradient (recovering the log-density itself up to a constant), models this across multiple noise scales with a cross-scale regularization term, and combines multi-scale estimates via a Gaussian mixture model at test time, achieving state-of-the-art micro AUC-ROC on five benchmarks in both object-centric and frame-centric settings.
limitations: The method requires that anomalous and normal video features be distinguishable in the chosen feature space, with no theoretical guarantee this holds for arbitrary feature extractors. Additionally, while the noise scale at test time is eliminated via the GMM, the upper bound of the training noise range must be set manually and should be sufficiently large to cover test-time anomalies, for which no automatic selection procedure exists.

surprise_factor: The conceptual point that the log-density itself (not its gradient) is the appropriate anomaly indicator is clean and well-illustrated (Fig. 2 shows gradient norms are low at both modes and inter-mode minima), but the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (1–4 AUC points) and the overall contribution is a well-executed reformulation of known score-matching machinery rather than a surprising empirical result. The fact that a 2-layer MLP with 4096 units outperforms diffusion models and GAN-based VAD methods is notable but not deeply unexpected given the task's structure.

## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods rely on heuristic assumptions—such as reconstruction error or self-supervised task failure—that lack a principled statistical link between data abnormality and detection performance. The paper addresses this by asking whether a neural network can directly approximate the probability density of normal video features, enabling anomaly detection through simple likelihood thresholding without heuristic justifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241895/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted, component-wise standardized video feature vectors (e.g., 512-dim CLIP deep features, FlowNet velocity histograms, and 17-keypoint pose vectors for object-centric mode; or 1152-dim Hiera-L features from 16-frame sequences for frame-centric mode) and produces a single scalar anomaly score per frame or object. At inference, the shallow network outputs a 16-dimensional vector of log-density estimates at evenly spaced noise scales, which a fitted Gaussian mixture model collapses into the final score. The entire anomaly-indicator forward pass takes under one millisecond, making feature extraction the dominant computational cost.

## Method

MULDE modifies denoising score matching by training the gradient of a scalar-valued network f_θ rather than the network itself, so that ∇f_θ approximates the negative log-density gradient and f_θ approximates the negative log-density up to a constant—guaranteeing a conservative (curl-free) vector field. To avoid committing to a single noise scale, the network is conditioned on σ sampled log-uniformly over [1e-3, 1.0], with a σ² weighting factor emphasizing smaller scales. A regularization term β·f_θ(x,σ)² penalizes log-densities of noise-free inputs to align the unknown additive constants C_σ across scales. At test time, the 16-scale log-density vector is scored by a GMM fitted to normal data via EM. The architecture is a 2-layer MLP (4096 units, GELU activations—ReLU is excluded because the loss requires twice-differentiability) trained with Adam (β₁=0.5, β₂=0.9, batch 2048).

## Results

In the object-centric setting, MULDE achieves frame-level AUC-ROC of 99.7% (micro) on Ped2, 94.3% on Avenue, and 86.7% on ShanghaiTech, outperforming all 13 baselines on the micro score. In the frame-centric setting, MULDE reaches 81.3% (micro) on ShanghaiTech, 78.5% on UCF-Crime, and 72.8% on UBnormal, advancing the state of the art by 3.8 percentage points on UCF-Crime (the largest public VAD benchmark) and by over 2pp on ShanghaiTech and UBnormal. Inference requires less than 1 ms per feature vector, making the anomaly indicator negligible compared to the 33–130 ms feature extraction step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MULDE outperforms all 18 baselines on the micro AUC-ROC in both object-centric and frame-centric settings. The closest competitor, AccI-VAD [32] (which also models the density of normal features but with a GMM/kNN instead of a neural network), is beaten by 0.8–1.0 pp on micro score across all three object-centric datasets. MSMA [27], which uses the norm of the log-density gradient rather than the log-density itself, is outperformed by 1.2–2.6 pp on micro score, confirming that the log-density is a superior anomaly indicator to its gradient. Reconstruction-based methods (CAE-SVM, VEC, SSMTL, HF2) and adversarial methods (BA-AED, CT-D2GAN) trail MULDE by 2–8 pp on micro score. On the frame-centric UCF-Crime benchmark, MULDE improves the previous best (FPDM [42] at 74.7%) by 3.8 pp, and on UBnormal it surpasses the object-centric SOTA (STG-NF [13]) by 1.1 pp in the frame-centric setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MULDE builds directly on Vincent's denoising score matching [38] and Song & Ermon's multiscale extension [35], but departs from both by training the *gradient* of a scalar log-density network rather than the network itself, thereby recovering the log-density (up to a constant) instead of only its gradient. This distinguishes it from MSMA [27], which uses the norm of the multiscale score as an anomaly indicator, and from the dominant VAD paradigm of reconstruction-error or auxiliary-task methods (auto-encoders, jigsaw puzzles, future-frame prediction) that rely on the heuristic assumption that anomalies increase task error. The paper also contrasts with distribution-modeling approaches like GMMs (AccI-VAD [32]) and normalizing flows [13], arguing that a neural energy-based model offers both the expressive power of deep networks and the principled likelihood-based anomaly criterion that those methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding anomaly detection in an explicit statistical model of normal-data density, MULDE replaces the heuristic 'reconstruction error is higher for anomalies' assumption with a mathematically well-defined criterion, making failure modes interpretable (e.g., a missed anomaly means the log-density approximation is insufficient). The method is feature-agnostic, requires only a shallow MLP and a GMM at inference (sub-millisecond per feature), and achieves state-of-the-art micro AUC-ROC across five benchmarks in both object-centric and frame-centric settings. Open questions include whether non-Gaussian noise (e.g., emulating random motion) could better capture certain anomaly types, how to automatically select the upper noise-scale bound, and whether the approach extends to weakly-supervised or fully supervised VAD settings.
