## Problem

Existing semi-supervised breast ultrasound segmentation methods perform augmentation and contrastive learning at the image or instance level, overlooking that different regions within a single image have vastly different learning difficulty. Under strong augmentations like CutMix, the hardest regions (ambiguous boundaries, low-contrast lesions) are randomly cropped out and thus inadequately trained, causing the model to underperform precisely on the cases that matter most clinically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240110/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 500×500 breast ultrasound images, partitioned into a small labeled set (1/2, 1/4, or 1/8 of the training split) and a large unlabeled set, and produces a pixel-level binary segmentation mask (lesion vs. background). Internally, each image is divided into a grid of 25×25 patches for hardness scoring, and the teacher network's probability maps drive both the augmentation shielding and the contrastive sampling. The final prediction is taken from the teacher network, and evaluation uses Dice and IoU on the test split.

## Method

PH-Net builds on a mean-teacher (EMA) framework and introduces two patch-level mechanisms. First, Adaptive Patch Augmentation (APA) computes a normalized average-entropy hardness score for each 25×25 patch in the teacher's probability map, then shields the top-k hardest patches (with k growing linearly as β·t/T·N²) so they are excluded from random CutMix cropping, ensuring hard regions receive consistent supervision. Second, Hard-Patch Contrastive Learning (HPC) adds a 256-dim projector to the student branch, rigorously samples features only from the hardest k patches where two independent teacher pseudo-labels agree, maintains a memory bank of class-specific anchor features, and applies a cosine-similarity contrastive loss (τ=0.5) to tighten intra-class compactness and widen inter-class separation within those hard patches. The total training objective combines standard cross-entropy on labeled data, confidence-filtered consistency loss on augmented unlabeled data (threshold γ=0.9), and the contrastive loss, all weighted equally.

## Results

On the UDIAT dataset with ResNet-50 backbone, PH-Net achieves 87.76% Dice (1/2), 86.23% Dice (1/4), and 84.99% Dice (1/8), outperforming the fully supervised baseline (SupOnly) by +3.14%, +2.78%, and +3.89% respectively. On the BUSI dataset with U-Net backbone, PH-Net reaches 77.05%, 76.26%, and 72.79% Dice under the same partition protocols. All improvements are statistically significant (P < 0.05) via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and the method also reports lower standard deviations than most competitors, indicating more stable performance across runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PH-Net outperforms all eight baselines across every partition protocol on both datasets. On UDIAT with ResNet-50, the largest margin is against the fully supervised SupOnly baseline (+3.89% Dice at 1/8), while the closest competitor AugSeg [44] is beaten by +0.98% Dice under the most challenging 1/8 setting (84.99% vs. 84.01%). On BUSI with U-Net, PH-Net surpasses the dedicated BUS method RA-UGMT [11] by +0.48%, +0.47%, and +0.38% Dice under 1/2, 1/4, and 1/8 respectively, and edges out AugSeg [44] by +0.12% to +0.94% depending on the protocol. The method also exhibits notably lower standard deviations (e.g., 0.33% at UDIAT 1/8 vs. 1.20% for iMAS), suggesting more consistent training. Among the image-augmentation-based methods (iMAS, AugSeg, BCP), PH-Net consistently leads, confirming that patch-level hardness-aware augmentation is more effective than instance-level or image-level strategies for BUS segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PH-Net extends the mean-teacher consistency regularization framework (Tarvainen & Valpola) and standard CutMix augmentation (French et al.) by introducing a patch-level hardness metric that adaptively shields high-entropy regions from strong augmentation. It departs from prior image-level adaptive methods (iMAS, AugSeg) that treat all pixels uniformly, and from pixel-level contrastive approaches (U²PL) that are inapplicable to few-class medical segmentation, by operating at an intermediate patch granularity. The hard-patch contrastive learning module draws on the memory-bank contrastive learning paradigm but restricts sampling to the hardest patches identified by the same entropy-based hardness score, creating a unified hardness-driven pipeline for both augmentation and contrastive training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in semi-supervised BUS segmentation—insufficient training on ambiguous, low-contrast lesion boundaries—by ensuring hard regions are preserved during augmentation and receive targeted contrastive supervision. However, the method still fails on lesions with extremely ambiguous contours or minimal background distinction, and its evaluation is limited to two 2D BUS datasets with small sample sizes (163 and 780 images). Open questions include whether the patch-wise hardness concept generalizes to other noisy medical imaging modalities (CT, MRI) or to 3D volumetric segmentation, and whether the fixed patch size (25×25) can be made adaptive to lesion scale.
