## Problem

Mainstream SR models (CNNs, window-attention, and KNN-graph methods) treat all pixels with equal aggregation degree, regardless of content. This degree-equivalence rigidity is mismatched with the unbalanced nature of SR, where only a small proportion of high-frequency, detail-rich pixels demand heavy reconstruction while the majority of flat regions need minimal restoration. The paper asks whether graph flexibility can be fully exploited to break this rigidity and improve SR performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241755/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single low-resolution RGB image and outputs a high-resolution reconstruction at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. Training uses 64×64 patches from DIV2K+Flickr2K with random flip/rotation augmentation; spatial upscaling is performed by a pixel-shuffle upsampler at the output stage. Lightweight variants (IPG-Tiny, IPG-S) reduce depth and channel width for computation-constrained deployment while retaining the same architectural design.

## Method

IPG constructs degree-flexible pixel-level graphs at both local and global scales within a MetaFormer backbone of Multiscale Graph-aggregation Blocks (MGB). A detail-rich indicator D_F (the L1 difference between a bilinearly downsampled-then-upsampled feature map and the original) assigns proportionally higher node degrees to high-frequency pixels, breaking the fixed-degree constraint of KNN graphs. Pixels rather than patches serve as graph nodes to avoid misalignment during aggregation, and node connections are searched within a local neighborhood for detail reconstruction and within a strided global sampling space for distant feature transfer. Aggregation uses edge-conditioned message passing with cosine similarity as the correlation metric, augmented by relative position encoding to preserve spatial information. Local and global graphs are distributed alternately across Graph Aggregation Layers (GAL) within each MGB block, with Channel Attention and ConvFFN modules completing the MetaFormer-style token-mixing and channel-mixing pipeline.

## Results

On the most challenging SR×4 task, IPG achieves 28.13 dB PSNR on Urban100, exceeding the previous SOTA (HAT at 27.97 dB) by 0.16 dB. Across all five benchmarks (Set5, Set14, BSDS100, Urban100, Manga109) and all three upscaling factors (×2, ×3, ×4), IPG consistently outperforms every baseline. For example, on Set5×4 IPG reaches 33.15 dB PSNR vs. 33.08 (CAT-A) and 33.04 (HAT); on Manga109×4 it scores 32.53 dB vs. 32.48 (HAT). The lightweight variant IPG-Tiny also leads all lightweight baselines, achieving 26.78 dB on Urban100×4 vs. 26.67 dB (SRFormer-light) with comparable FLOPs (~61 G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPG outperforms all 16 baselines across every dataset and upscaling factor. The closest competitor is HAT [5], which IPG surpasses by 0.16 dB on Urban100×4 (28.13 vs. 27.97), 0.13 dB on Urban100×3 (30.36 vs. 30.23), and 0.03 dB on Urban100×2 (34.48 vs. 34.45); on Set5×4 the gap is 0.11 dB (33.15 vs. 33.04). CAT-A [6] and ART [47] trail IPG by roughly 0.2–0.3 dB on Urban100×4. Among graph-based methods, IGNN [52] (the only prior graph SR baseline) lags IPG by a substantial 1.29 dB on Urban100×4, highlighting the benefit of degree-flexible pixel-node graphs over fixed-degree patch-node KNN graphs. In the lightweight setting (Table 4), IPG-Tiny beats SRFormer-light [53] by 0.11 dB on Urban100×4 (26.78 vs. 26.67) and SwinIR-NG [7] by 0.17 dB, while using fewer FLOPs (61.3G vs. 62.8G and 63.0G respectively). CNN-based lightweight baselines (CARN, IMDN, LAPAR-A, LatticeNet, ESRT) are all outperformed by 0.4–0.7 dB on Urban100×4.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPG builds on the MetaFormer architecture (Han et al., 2022) as its structural backbone and adopts edge-conditioned graph aggregation (Simonovsky & Komodakis, 2017) as its core mixing operation. It departs from prior graph-based SR methods like IGNN (Zhou et al., 2020) in three key ways: replacing fixed-degree KNN graphs with a detail-aware degree-flexible scheme, using pixel nodes instead of patch nodes to avoid misalignment, and combining local and global graph sampling rather than choosing one scale. Conceptually, the paper unifies the rigidity of convolution, window attention, and KNN graphs under a single 'degree-equivalence' critique, positioning IPG as the first SR model to break this constraint through content-adaptive node degrees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The degree-flexible graph construction offers a principled way to allocate reconstruction effort where it is needed most, addressing the long-tailed nature of SR that prior work (Gou et al., 2023) tackled only through loss design. However, the practical impact is tempered by modest gains (~0.1 dB over SOTA on Urban100×4) and the acknowledged lack of hardware acceleration for graph operations, making IPG slower than transformer baselines. Open questions include whether degree flexibility can be made more efficient (e.g., via learned sparse connectivity), whether the detail-rich indicator generalizes to other restoration tasks, and whether the pixel-node approach can scale to higher resolutions without prohibitive search costs.
