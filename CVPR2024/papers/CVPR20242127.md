## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D shape decomposition methods are designed and evaluated on synthetic or highly curated object datasets, and none generalizes to the complexity and diversity of real-world aerial LiDAR scans. Moreover, even unsupervised deep learning approaches tend to learn abstract feature representations that are difficult to interpret, limiting their practical utility for tasks like environmental monitoring and mapping where human-readable decompositions are essential.

## Input / Output

The method takes large aerial LiDAR point clouds (up to 98M points over 7.7 km²) with per-point laser reflectance intensity as input, processing them as square patches subsampled to 10⁵ points and voxelized into 64×64×64 grids. The output is a set of 64 slots, each producing a deformed instance of one of 6 learned free-form 3D prototypes with an associated activation probability; the Chamfer distance is computed in 4D (3D spatial coordinates normalized to [0,1]³ plus intensity in [0,0.1]). From this decomposition, the model yields a point-cloud reconstruction, per-slot instance segmentation, and—after minimal manual annotation of the prototypes—full-scene semantic segmentation.

## Method

The Learnable Earth Parser is a probabilistic analysis-by-synthesis model in which S=64 slots (analogous to Slot Attention) each select one of K=6 learnable free-form 3D prototypes and apply a constrained affine deformation (anisotropic scaling, y-axis tilt of ±π/10, z-axis rotation, and translation) to reconstruct an input patch. Slot activation and prototype choice are modeled as (multi-)Bernoulli random variables predicted by per-slot MLPs, enabling a differentiable reconstruction loss built from an asymmetric Chamfer distance with separate accuracy and coverage terms. The encoder uses 6 3D sparse convolutions followed by 6 strided convolutions, and training employs a multi-stage curriculum that progressively unfreezes translation/rotation, intensity, scale, point positions, and anisotropic scaling. Regularization losses enforce sparse and uniform slot/prototype usage to prevent degenerate solutions, and a greedy post-processing step prunes prototypes whose removal increases reconstruction loss by less than 5%.

## Results

On the Earth Parser Dataset (7 aerial LiDAR scenes, 98M points), the Learnable Earth Parser achieves the best symmetric Chamfer distance in 6 out of 7 scenes (e.g., 0.29 on Urban, 0.40 on Greenhouses, 0.44 on Power Plant) and the best class-averaged mIoU across all 7 scenes (ranging from 52.2 on Power Plant to 96.9 on Crop Fields). On ShapeNet-Part planes, it reaches 68.6 mIoU vs. 34.5 for AtlasNet v2 and 1.34 Chamfer distance vs. 2.91 for SuperQuadrics. On DALES, it achieves 0.29 Chamfer and 82.7 mIoU, outperforming all reconstruction-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: k-means (intensity+elevation clustering), SuperQuadrics (Paschalidou et al., CVPR 2019), DTI-Sprites (Monnier et al., ICCV 2021), and AtlasNet v2 (Deprelle et al., NeurIPS 2019). The proposed method wins on both reconstruction and semantic segmentation against all baselines that support both tasks: it beats AtlasNet v2 by 10–54 mIoU points across scenes (e.g., 96.9 vs. 43.1 on Crop Fields, 83.2 vs. 48.8 on Urban) and by 2–3× on Chamfer distance in most scenes. Against DTI-Sprites, the advantage is even larger on reconstruction (e.g., 0.29 vs. 2.99 Chamfer on Urban) and on segmentation (e.g., 83.2 vs. 47.3 mIoU on Urban). The k-means baseline, while not performing reconstruction, is a surprisingly strong semantic segmentation competitor, beating the proposed method on Crop Fields (93.8 vs. 96.9 is actually won by Ours) but approaching it on Forest (71.5 vs. 83.7) and Windturbines (87.6 vs. 93.4). SuperQuadrics provides only reconstruction (no semantic segmentation) and is outperformed on Chamfer distance in 6 of 7 scenes, with a particularly large gap on Windturbines (13.5 vs. 6.65). On ShapeNet-Part, the method doubles AtlasNet v2's mIoU (68.6 vs. 34.5) and halves SuperQuadrics' Chamfer distance (1.34 vs. 2.91).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic reconstruction framework of Paschalidou et al. (Superquadrics Revisited, CVPR 2019) from a single parametric shape family to multiple free-form learnable prototypes, drawing the prototype concept directly from Loiseau et al. (3DV 2021). The slot-based decomposition and curriculum learning strategy are adapted from Monnier et al. (DTI-Sprites, ICCV 2021), which itself was an image-domain analogue. The key conceptual departure from prior unsupervised 3D decomposition work (AtlasNet, Tulsiani et al.) is the shift from curated synthetic single-object datasets to large-scale, uncurated real-world aerial LiDAR scenes containing diverse, multi-layered structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of deep unsupervised 3D shape analysis at the scale of real-world aerial LiDAR (7.7 km², 98M points), and the introduced Earth Parser Dataset fills a gap in publicly available diverse-scene benchmarks. The interpretability of learned prototypes—each typically corresponding to a single object type—makes the approach practically useful for environmental monitoring and mapping without annotation. However, the method is trained per-scene and relies on the intensity channel, limiting cross-scene generalization; the surprisingly strong k-means baseline for semantic segmentation also raises questions about how much of the deep model's value lies in reconstruction fidelity versus label propagation. Open questions include whether prototypes can be learned across scenes and whether the approach extends to LiDAR without intensity.
