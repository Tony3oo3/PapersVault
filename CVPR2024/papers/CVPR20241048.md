## Problem

Deepfake detectors trained on specific forgery methods (e.g., FaceSwap, DeepFakes) overfit to method-specific pixel-level artifacts and degrade sharply when tested on unseen forgery types. Existing augmentation strategies operate in RGB space and rely on reproducing particular blending or warping artifacts, limiting their robustness to post-processing and their extensibility to entire-face synthesis without such artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB face images (128×128 crops from FF++_c23 for training) and outputs a binary real/fake probability via a single student encoder with an FC head. During training, images are routed to per-forgery-type teacher encoders (EfficientNet-B4) or a frozen ArcFace encoder for real faces, and all augmentation is performed on the resulting latent feature maps (B×C×h×w) rather than on pixels. At inference, only the lightweight student encoder is deployed.

## Method

LSDA employs a teacher-student distillation architecture where dedicated teacher encoders learn domain-specific forgery features, and a single student encoder is distilled to produce a generalizable binary classifier. The key mechanism is latent-space data augmentation applied to teacher features: within-domain augmentation uses centrifugal transformation (pushing samples away from the batch centroid toward hard examples), affine rotation of element positions, and additive Gaussian noise; cross-domain augmentation applies Mixup interpolation between latent vectors of different forgery types. A learnable convolutional fusion layer concatenates original and augmented features, and the student is trained end-to-end with a combined objective of binary cross-entropy, multi-class domain classification (to keep teacher encoders domain-specific), and MSE-based distillation loss aligning student features to the augmented teacher representations. The real-face branch leverages a pre-trained and frozen ArcFace model to supply comprehensive identity-agnostic real features, avoiding the need to learn real representations from scratch.

## Results

On the DeepfakeBench cross-dataset protocol (trained on FF++_c23, evaluated frame-level AUC), LSDA achieves an average AUC of 0.826, surpassing the best prior method SPSL (0.767) by ~6 percentage points. Per-dataset gains range from +1.7% on DFDC to +6.5% on CDF-v2. On the video-level AUC comparison against recent SOTA (Table 2), LSDA reaches 0.911 on CDF-v2 and 0.770 on DFDC, outperforming the next-best SBI (0.906 / 0.724) and PCL+I2G (0.900 / 0.744).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSDA outperforms all 22 baselines in Table 1 across every test dataset, with the largest average margin over the strongest frequency-domain method SPSL (0.826 vs. 0.767, +5.9 pp) and the spatial method UCF (0.826 vs. 0.763, +6.3 pp). On the video-level AUC benchmark (Table 2), LSDA beats the previous best SBI by 0.5 pp on CDF-v2 (0.911 vs. 0.906) and by a substantial 4.6 pp on DFDC (0.770 vs. 0.724), also surpassing PCL+I2G (0.900/0.744) and RealForensics (0.857/0.759). In the extensibility evaluation on GAN- and Diffusion-generated faces (Table 3), LSDA consistently exceeds SBI by 2.3–10.6 pp (e.g., 0.854 vs. 0.744 on DDPM), confirming that latent-space augmentation generalizes beyond blending-artifact-based forgeries. The smallest relative gain appears on DFDC (+1.7 pp frame-level), likely because DFDC's diverse generation pipeline already spans a wider forgery space, whereas the largest gains on CDF-v2 (+6.5 pp) and DFD (+6.4 pp) reflect the benefit of bridging narrow domain gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSDA synthesizes ideas from three distinct threads in the deepfake detection literature: (1) RGB-space data augmentation methods (FWA, Face X-ray, I2G, SBI) that synthesize pseudo-fake images via pixel-level blending to teach detectors about manipulation artifacts; (2) knowledge distillation, repurposed here as a teacher-student framework where per-forgery-type teacher encoders transfer augmented latent knowledge to a single student encoder; and (3) face recognition priors (ArcFace), borrowed from RealForensics [20], to anchor the 'real' representation. The key departure is moving augmentation from the pixel/RGB domain into the encoder's latent feature space, where centrifugal, affine, additive, and cross-domain Mixup operations enlarge the forgery manifold without relying on specific pixel-level artifacts like blending boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent-space formulation offers a practical robustness advantage: because detection does not depend on fragile pixel-level artifacts, the detector degrades less under unseen post-processing (compression, blurring) and extends naturally to entire-face synthesis (GAN, diffusion) where blending artifacts are absent. This addresses a real deployment concern that RGB-based augmentation methods struggle with. Open questions include whether the per-forgery-type teacher architecture scales gracefully as new forgery methods emerge, whether the approach transfers to video-level temporal detection, and whether the distillation overhead is justified in low-resource settings. The method remains primarily an image-level detector, and its video-level results (Table 2) are competitive but not dramatically ahead of temporal methods.
