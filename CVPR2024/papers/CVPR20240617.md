## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: DreamBooth-style fine-tuning achieves high identity fidelity but requires 10–30 minutes of per-ID optimization, while tuning-free single-image embedding methods run in seconds but sacrifice both ID fidelity and facial diversity. No prior method simultaneously delivers efficient single-pass inference, strong identity preservation from multiple reference images, and flexible text controllability for human portrait generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary number N (typically 1–4) of ID images at 1024×1024 resolution, each masked to isolate the target person before encoding, along with a free-form text prompt. The output is a single 1024×1024 photorealistic human photo generated in ~10 seconds via 50 DDIM steps on SDXL. Unusually, the number of input ID images is variable at inference time, and images from different identities can be mixed in the same stack to produce a blended new identity.

## Method

PhotoMaker encodes each input ID image with a fine-tuned CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder, fuses each resulting embedding with the class-word feature vector from the text embedding via two MLP layers, and concatenates all fused embeddings along the sequence dimension to form a variable-length stacked ID embedding. This stacked embedding replaces the class-word token in the text embedding, so the diffusion model's existing cross-attention layers adaptively integrate ID information without any additional network modules. Training fine-tunes part of the CLIP image encoder, projection layers, and LoRA residuals on attention matrices, driven by an ID-oriented dataset built through an automated pipeline (VGGFace2 name list → web scraping → RetinaFace detection → ArcFace verification → Mask2Former segmentation → BLIP-2 captioning with class-word marking). The key architectural insight is that stacking preserves per-image identity detail while providing a unified multi-view representation, enabling both superior ID fidelity over single-image methods and the novel capability of identity mixing by simply substituting images from different people into the stack.

## Results

On the universal recontextualization benchmark (25 IDs × 40 prompts), PhotoMaker achieves the best ID fidelity scores: 73.6% CLIP-I and 51.5% DINO, surpassing all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., +7.4% DINO over IPAdapter). It also attains the best generation quality with an FID of 370.3, while maintaining a fast inference speed of 10 seconds per image—approximately 130× faster than DreamBooth (1284 s) and 240× faster than Textual Inversion (2400 s). PhotoMaker trails IPAdapter on Face Similarity (61.8% vs. 67.1%) and DreamBooth on CLIP-T (26.1% vs. 29.8%), but leads on the combined balance of ID preservation, diversity, and speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhotoMaker decisively outperforms all four baselines on the two primary ID-fidelity metrics: CLIP-I (73.6% vs. 62.8–71.2%) and DINO (51.5% vs. 39.3–46.2%), with the largest gap over DreamBooth (+11.7% DINO) and FastComposer (+11.3% DINO). Against IPAdapter, the closest tuning-free competitor, PhotoMaker gains +5.3% on DINO and +2.4% on CLIP-I while sacrificing 5.3% on Face Similarity (61.8% vs. 67.1%). DreamBooth retains the edge on text-prompt fidelity (CLIP-T 29.8% vs. 26.1%) and Textual Inversion leads on face diversity (59.3% vs. 57.7%), but both are 128–240× slower in personalization time. In terms of generation quality (FID), PhotoMaker (370.3) is competitive with all methods and slightly better than DreamBooth (374.5) and FastComposer (375.1), though Textual Inversion achieves the lowest FID (363.5). Overall, PhotoMaker offers the best trade-off between ID fidelity, generation quality, and inference speed among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhotoMaker sits at the intersection of two lines of work: test-time fine-tuning methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) that achieve high ID fidelity at the cost of 10–30 minutes of per-identity optimization, and tuning-free embedding-based methods (IPAdapter, FastComposer, ELITE) that encode a single reference image into the semantic space of the diffusion model. Its key conceptual departure is the stacked ID embedding: rather than projecting one image into a fixed-size token, it concatenates CLIP embeddings from multiple ID images and splices them into the text embedding in place of the class word, letting the model's existing cross-attention layers handle ID–text fusion without any additional network modules. The method also introduces a purpose-built ID-oriented data construction pipeline (crawling, ArcFace verification, Mask2Former segmentation, BLIP-2 captioning) that addresses a gap in existing human datasets, which either lack ID annotations or focus solely on face crops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving DreamBooth-level ID fidelity in ~10 seconds (≈130× speedup) with no per-identity fine-tuning, PhotoMaker makes personalized human photo generation practical for interactive and large-scale deployment. The emergent identity-mixing capability—blending characteristics of two or more distinct IDs into a novel face by simply feeding images of different people into the stacked embedding—has no direct analogue in prior tuning-free or fine-tuning methods and opens applications in creative content generation and character design. Limitations include reliance on a celebrity-centric training pipeline with empirically tuned thresholds, evaluation on only 25 held-out IDs, and the fact that the method is demonstrated primarily on SDXL; whether the stacked-embedding trick transfers cleanly to other backbones or to non-celebrity, in-the-wild identities remains an open question.
