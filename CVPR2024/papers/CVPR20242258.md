## Problem

Existing Retrieval and Deformation (R&D) methods for 3D shape generation from object scans rely on single global features for retrieval (losing local geometry and failing under occlusion) and dense point matching for deformation (vulnerable to noise and outliers). These limitations make them infeasible for the common real-world scenario of noisy, partially observed object scans.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242258/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full or partial object point cloud (2048 points normalized to a unit cube, canonicalized via an external pose estimator) and produces a deformed 3D mesh and point cloud that tightly matches the target. It operates on a pre-constructed database of 1419 CAD models (PartNet) and handles occlusion ratios up to 75% by weighting keypoint contributions according to local point density. Inference runs in ~30 ms per instance on a single GPU, compared to 0.7 s (Uy et al.) or ~45 s (ShapeFlow) for baselines.

## Method

KP-RED uses category-consistent sparse keypoints (detected unsupervised via KeypointDeformer-style training) as a unified intermediate representation for both retrieval and deformation. For retrieval, PointNet extracts point-wise features that are pooled within each keypoint's support ball region; a geometric self-attention module then captures region-to-region relations to produce local retrieval tokens, which are concatenated in a fixed semantic order to form a global deformation-aware embedding compared via L1 distance. Training employs an auxiliary dual reconstruction task that forces each token to encode both the local geometry of its region and cues about how that region deforms. For deformation, the same self-attention predicts per-keypoint influence vectors restricted to local cage vertices, and the deformed cage is computed as a weighted sum of keypoint displacements, then interpolated onto the source shape via mean value coordinates. For partial inputs, a confidence-based dynamic weighting scheme uses local point density to down-weight unreliable keypoints in both retrieval scoring and keypoint supervision, with a teacher-student keypoint predictor trained to match full-shape keypoint predictions.

## Results

On PartNet (full shapes), KP-RED achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.089, outperforming ShapeFlow (0.340) by 73.8% and Uy et al. (0.637) by 85.7%. On the real-world Scan2CAD dataset (partial shapes), KP-RED achieves an average Unilateral Chamfer Distance of 0.060, exceeding ShapeFlow (0.265) by 77.3%, U-RED (0.207) by 71.0%, and Uy et al. (0.210) by 71.4%. Inference time is approximately 30 ms per instance, compared to 0.7 s for Uy et al. and ~45 s for ShapeFlow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KP-RED consistently outperforms all three baselines across every category and both datasets. On PartNet full shapes, KP-RED (avg. CD 0.089) beats the strongest baseline ShapeFlow (0.340) by a factor of ~3.8×, and Uy et al. (0.637) by ~7×; U-RED (0.551) is intermediate but still far behind. On Scan2CAD partial shapes, KP-RED (avg. UCD 0.060) surpasses ShapeFlow (0.265) by 77.3%, U-RED (0.207) by 71.0%, and Uy et al. (0.210) by 71.4%. Notably, U-RED performs poorly on chairs (0.834 CD on PartNet) while excelling on tables (0.326), indicating category-dependent fragility, whereas KP-RED maintains consistent performance across all three categories. In terms of speed, KP-RED runs in ~30 ms per instance versus 0.7 s for Uy et al. and ~45 s for ShapeFlow (which requires online optimization), making it the only method that is both the most accurate and the fastest.

## Relation to Prior Work

KP-RED extends the keypoint-driven neural-cage deformation framework from KeypointDeformer [19] and Neural Cages [48] by replacing their global-feature-based influence vector prediction with a local-global self-attention mechanism that preserves fine-grained geometric details. It departs from the dominant dense point-matching paradigm in joint retrieval-and-deformation (R&D) methods (Uy et al. [41], U-RED [11], ShapeFlow [21]) by using sparse, semantically consistent keypoints as the intermediate representation for both retrieval embedding and deformation control. The retrieval module introduces a novel deformation-aware local-global feature embedding trained with an auxiliary region-level reconstruction task, which is distinct from prior global-pooling or flow-based retrieval strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: KP-RED achieves real-time inference (~30 ms) compared to ~45 s for ShapeFlow, and requires no part annotations unlike Uy et al. and U-RED, making it more deployable in real-world pipelines. The robustness to partial inputs (only 30.6% error increase from 25% to 75% occlusion, versus ~60–70% for baselines) addresses a key limitation of prior R&D methods in real scanning scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three evaluated categories (chair, table, cabinet), how it scales to larger databases, and whether the unsupervised keypoint discovery remains reliable for highly asymmetric or novel object categories.
