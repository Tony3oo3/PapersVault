## Problem

Existing generative models for 3D indoor scene synthesis suffer from fundamental trade-offs: GANs lack diversity due to mode collapse, VAEs sacrifice fidelity for coverage, and autoregressive models accumulate sequential prediction errors while struggling to capture holistic inter-object relationships. No prior method jointly models the full scene configuration—object semantics, spatial placements, and surface geometries—within a single generative framework that can produce physically plausible, diverse, and symmetric arrangements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

A scene is represented as a fixed-size unordered set of N objects (padded with 'empty' objects), where each object is a D-dimensional vector concatenating 3D location, 3D size, 2D orientation (cos/sin), C-dimensional class label, and F-dimensional shape code from a pretrained FoldingNet auto-encoder. The diffusion operates on the resulting N×D tensor. For conditional tasks, a partial object set or BERT text embeddings (48×768) are injected as conditioning. The output is denoised object attributes that drive shape retrieval from the 3D-FUTURE CAD library to produce final surface geometries placed at the predicted locations, sizes, and orientations.

## Method

DiffuScene applies a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) directly on unordered object-set tensors, avoiding the sequential error accumulation of autoregressive approaches by jointly denoising all object attributes simultaneously. The denoiser is a UNet-1D with skip connections interleaved with self-attention blocks to aggregate inter-object spatial relationships, and employs separate prediction heads for bounding-box parameters, class labels, and geometry codes to prevent attribute bias. Training combines the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss with an IoU regularization term that penalizes pairwise bounding-box intersections at each denoising step. A key design choice is the joint diffusion of shape latent codes alongside layout attributes, which enables the model to discover symmetric object pairs (e.g., matching nightstands flanking a bed) through shape-aware retrieval. For conditional applications, known objects are held fixed during reverse diffusion (scene completion), only positions/orientations are denoised given fixed sizes and classes (re-arrangement), or BERT text embeddings are injected via cross-attention layers (text-conditioned synthesis).

## Results

On 3D-FRONT unconditional scene synthesis, DiffuScene achieves the best FID (17.21 bedroom, 32.60 dining, 36.18 living), KID (0.70, 0.72, 0.88), SCA (52.15%, 55.50%, 57.81% — closer to 50% is better), and CKL (0.35, 0.22, 0.21) across all three room types, outperforming ATISS, Sync2Gen, and DepthGAN. In scene completion and re-arrangement (Table 4), DiffuScene also leads on FID, KID, and PIoU while producing more symmetric pairs (e.g., 3.69 vs. 1.42 for ATISS in living rooms). A user study on text-conditioned synthesis shows 62% of 45 users prefer DiffuScene over ATISS in realism and 55% in text matching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuScene outperforms all four baselines across every metric and room type in unconditional synthesis. Against the closest competitor ATISS [44], DiffuScene reduces FID by 1.4–7.7 points, KID by 1.0–4.3 points, and CKL by roughly 50–67%, while also achieving SCA values much closer to the ideal 50% (e.g., 52.15% vs. 61.71% in bedrooms). Sync2Gen [75] and DepthGAN [76] are substantially weaker, with FID values 14–64 points higher and SCA scores far from 50%, indicating poor scene diversity and plausibility. In scene completion and re-arrangement (Table 4), DiffuScene beats both ATISS and LEGO-Net [70] on FID, KID, and PIoU (fewer object intersections), while generating significantly more symmetric pairs (e.g., 3.69 vs. 1.42 for ATISS in living rooms). In the text-conditioned user study, DiffuScene is preferred over ATISS by 62% of users for realism and 55% for text matching, confirming qualitative advantages beyond aggregate metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffuScene applies the DDPM denoising diffusion framework to 3D indoor scene synthesis, departing from the dominant GAN, VAE, and autoregressive paradigms used by prior methods like DepthGAN, Sync2Gen, and ATISS. The key conceptual departure is representing scenes as unordered object sets and jointly diffusing all attributes (location, size, orientation, semantics, and shape codes) simultaneously, rather than predicting objects sequentially as autoregressive models do. The shape latent feature diffusion for geometry retrieval is a novel addition absent from prior scene synthesis work, which typically only predicted bounding boxes. The denoiser uses a UNet-1D with attention blocks rather than the transformer architecture employed by concurrent work LEGO-Net, and an IoU loss term explicitly penalizes object intersections during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first diffusion models purpose-built for 3D indoor scene synthesis, demonstrating that the unordered-set diffusion formulation can capture holistic scene composition patterns including symmetries and spatial relationships that sequential models struggle with. The unified framework enabling scene completion, re-arrangement, and text-conditioned synthesis from a single trained model is practically useful for interior design and data augmentation pipelines. However, significant limitations remain: the method is restricted to single rooms of a fixed type, shape retrieval is constrained within semantic classes (limiting style diversity), textures come from retrieved CAD models rather than being generated, and the approach requires 3D-labeled training data. Open questions include whether the formulation scales to multi-room or outdoor scenes, whether texture diffusion can be integrated, and whether 2D-only supervision suffices for learning scene priors.
