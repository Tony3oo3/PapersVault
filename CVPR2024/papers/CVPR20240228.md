## Problem

Cross-modal matching datasets sourced from the Internet inevitably contain mismatched image-text pairs, and the hinge-based triplet loss widely used for noisy correspondence division is not robust to such noise because its fixed margin has varying sensitivity across different noisy pairs. This causes unreliable clean/noisy separation and degraded retrieval performance, particularly under moderate-to-severe noise ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240228/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (preprocessed via FasterRCNN to extract top-36 regions) and text captions, projecting them into a joint embedding space via a fully-connected image encoder and a Bi-GRU text encoder with graph-reasoning-based similarity. Beyond the final retrieval output (ranked cross-modal matches), the method also produces per-pair clean/noisy set assignments and soft correspondence labels in [0,1] that modulate the training margin, effectively converting hard binary correspondence labels into continuous confidence scores.

## Method

The core contribution is the Equivariant Similarity Consistency (ESC) regularization, which enforces that for any two matched pairs, the cross-instance similarity from image A to text B equals the cross-instance similarity from image B to text A (s_aj = s_ja), derived from the principle that semantic variation in one modality should be proportional to that in the other. For data division, the highest-similarity pair in each mini-batch serves as an anchor point, and ESC is computed between anchors and all other pairs; this ESC loss is combined with a hinge-based triplet loss and fit with a two-component Beta Mixture Model to assign clean/noisy posterior probabilities. Training then proceeds in a co-teaching scheme with two independently initialized models: clean pairs are optimized with a soft-margin triplet loss plus an additional ESC regularization term (using pseudo-negative pairs from the clean set), while noisy pairs are trained with the soft-margin triplet loss alone, where the margin is scaled by each pair's soft correspondence label.

## Results

On the real-world noisy CC152K dataset, ESC achieves R@1 of 42.8 (image→text) and 44.8 (text→image), with a total rSum of 375.9, outperforming the best baseline CRCL (41.8 / 41.6, rSum 373.7) by 1.0 and 3.2 points on R@1 respectively. Under synthetic 40% noise on MS-COCO, ESC reaches rSum 523.9, surpassing CRCL (518.1) by 5.8 points. On Flickr30K with 40% noise, ESC achieves rSum 489.6 versus CRCL's 487.6, with the gap widening to 3.5 points at 60% noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESC outperforms all nine baselines on the real-world CC152K benchmark, with the largest margin over CRCL (+3.2 on T→I R@1) and BiCro (+2.7 on T→I R@1). Among noisy-correspondence-specific methods (NCR, DECL, BiCro, MSCN, CRCL), ESC consistently leads on rSum across all three datasets and all noise ratios tested. General matching methods (SCAN, VSRN, IMRAM, SAF, SGR) degrade catastrophically under synthetic noise — e.g., VSRN drops from rSum 295.4 to 55.0 on Flickr30K at 40% noise — confirming that standard triplet-based training is highly sensitive to mismatched pairs. On MS-COCO with 40% noise, ESC's rSum advantage over the best baseline (CRCL) reaches 5.8 points, the largest gap observed across all settings. The advantage of ESC over CRCL grows with noise severity on Flickr30K (0.8 → 2.0 → 3.5 points at 20/40/60% noise), suggesting the ESC regularization becomes more critical as the triplet loss's margin sensitivity worsens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noisy correspondence learning framework established by NCR (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2021), which introduced the clean/noisy data division via small-loss criterion and soft-margin triplet loss for cross-modal matching. The key departure is identifying that the hinge-based triplet loss (originating from VSE++) is inherently non-robust to noise correspondence, and proposing the Equivariant Similarity Consistency (ESC) regularization—derived from the equivariant similarity principle in Wang et al. (ICCV 2023)—as a complementary constraint for both data division and clean-sample training. The training pipeline reuses the co-teaching strategy from Han et al. (NeurIPS 2018) and the soft correspondence label computation from BiCro (Yang et al., CVPR 2023), positioning ESC as a plug-and-play regularization rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ESC regularization offers a simple, interpretable constraint (cross-instance similarity equality) that practitioners can easily add to existing noisy correspondence pipelines, and it consistently improves R@1 by 1–3% over strong baselines across synthetic and real-world noise. However, the gains are modest, the method is validated only on image-text retrieval with a specific backbone (FasterRCNN + Bi-GRU + graph reasoning), and the authors explicitly avoid the 80%+ noise regime where prior methods reportedly still function. Open questions include whether the equivariant proportionality assumption holds for other modality pairs (audio-visual, point cloud-text) and whether ESC's benefit persists when the underlying similarity function changes.
