## Problem

Point cloud registration remains challenging when overlap between two clouds is extremely low, as geometric-only features lack sufficient discriminability. While color information has been exploited in local registration (e.g., color ICP variants), it has not been effectively integrated into global registration pipelines with multi-stage geometric-color fusion, leaving a gap in robust registration under low-overlap, low-geometric-distinctiveness scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240868/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two color point clouds, each consisting of 3D coordinates (R³) and per-point RGB values in [0,1]³, and outputs a rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³). Color is converted to HSV space for processing; the dense input points are downsampled to 4 hierarchical levels, with the original dense points excluded from the primary registration to avoid clustering artifacts and color noise. The final transformation is estimated via fine registration on up-sampled points within the identified overlap region.

## Method

ColorPCR employs a two-stage geometric-color fusion pipeline. First, the Hierarchical Color Enhanced Feature Extraction (CEFE) module performs 4-level KPConv-based downsampling, injecting color into the first three levels via a color-enhanced convolution (G1) that concatenates HSV values (scaled by σ_c) with geometric features before applying kernel-point convolution; the fourth (superpoint) level omits color for stability. Second, the GeoColor Superpoint Matching Module encodes transformation-invariant global context using two positional embeddings: a Pair-wise Hue-Distance embedding (combining hue difference and Euclidean distance via sinusoidal encoding) and a Triplet-wise Angular embedding, both fed into a GeoTransformer for color-guided superpoint feature extraction. A key design choice is using only the hue channel (not full HSV) in the positional embedding to mitigate noise from lighting and camera angle variations, while full HSV is used in the feature extraction stage. The method is prior-free and can operate with either RANSAC or the RANSAC-free LGR estimator.

## Results

On Color3DMatch, ColorPCR achieves a peak Registration Recall (RR) of 97.0% (at 1000 RANSAC samples) and Feature Matching Recall (FMR) of 99.5% across all sample counts. On the more challenging Color3DLoMatch (overlap 0.1–0.3), ColorPCR reaches 88.9% RR and 97.0% FMR, substantially outperforming the next-best method PEAL (81.7% RR, 92.9% FMR). Inlier Ratio peaks at 87.8% on C3DM and 68.0% on C3DLM. The method is also RANSAC-free capable, achieving 97.3% RR on C3DM and 87.1% on C3DLM with the LGR estimator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColorPCR outperforms all 11 baselines on both datasets across every metric (FMR, IR, RR). On Color3DLoMatch (low overlap), the advantage is most pronounced: ColorPCR achieves 88.9% RR, beating PEAL (81.7%) by 7.2 points, GeoTransformer+MAC (78.9%) by 10 points, and GeoTransformer (75.0%) by 13.9 points. On Color3DMatch (higher overlap), ColorPCR (97.0% RR) still leads PEAL (94.6%) by 2.4 points and GeoTransformer+MAC (95.7%) by 1.3 points. The gap widens dramatically as overlap decreases: at 10–15% overlap, ColorPCR improves RR by ~40% over Predator, ~30% over CoFiNet, and ~25% over GeoTransformer. Among RANSAC-free methods, ColorPCR with LGR achieves 97.3% RR on C3DM and 87.1% on C3DLM, surpassing GeoTransformer (91.5%/74.0%) and CoFiNet (87.6%/64.8%). The CEFE backbone also transfers effectively: replacing the backbone in Predator, CoFiNet, and GeoTransformer with CEFE yields 4.9–7.1% RR gains on C3DM and 10.4–13.9% on C3DLM, confirming its generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

ColorPCR extends the correspondence-based registration pipeline established by Predator, CoFiNet, and GeoTransformer by injecting color information at two stages: a color-enhanced KPConv backbone (CEFE) for hierarchical feature extraction, and a hue-based positional embedding within a GeoTransformer-style superpoint matching module. It departs from prior color-aware registration work (4DICP, color-ICP variants) that operates in local registration by applying color to the global, deep-learning-based registration setting, and from PEAL which uses color only indirectly through 2D image overlap detection. The FPN-style multi-level downsampling and the sinusoidal positional embedding follow established patterns from the 2D matching and point cloud literature, but the specific multi-stage geometric-color fusion strategy is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Color3DMatch and Color3DLoMatch fills a notable gap in the field, as no publicly available color point cloud registration benchmarks existed prior to this work. The demonstrated ~40% registration recall improvement over Predator at 10–15% overlap highlights that color can be a decisive signal when geometric distinctiveness is minimal, suggesting a broader principle: multi-modal fusion becomes critical precisely in the hardest regimes. Limitations include reliance on color availability (many real-world LiDAR or depth-only settings lack it), the synthetic colorization of 3DMatch scenes, and untested generalization to outdoor or dynamically lit environments. Open questions include whether the hue-only embedding strategy transfers to scenes with minimal color variation and whether the approach extends to non-rigid or partial-overlap registration.
