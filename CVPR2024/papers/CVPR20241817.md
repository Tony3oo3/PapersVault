## Problem

3D point cloud analysis in autonomous systems requires invariance to arbitrary rotations and reflections, but rotation-sensitive methods rely heavily on data augmentation and increased model capacity, while existing equivariant methods (e.g., spherical harmonic-based filters) have limited expressiveness. Steerable 3D spherical neurons, which are provably O(3)-equivariant, had never been integrated into an end-to-end trainable framework, leaving their practical utility unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241817/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud of N points (typically 1024 for classification, 2048 for segmentation), centered at the origin and normalized to a unit sphere, and produces an O(3)-invariant, permutation-equivariant descriptor of shape N×C×C′. This descriptor can be globally pooled for classification (yielding a class label) or used per-point for part segmentation. The descriptor is invariant under the full orthogonal group O(3), meaning it is unchanged under both rotations and reflections of the input.

## Method

TetraSphere introduces a TetraTransform (TT) layer that embeds 3D points into conformal 5D space and applies K steerable 3D spherical neurons, each producing a 4D output in a learned tetra-basis, yielding an O(3)-equivariant 4D feature map. An equivariant max-pooling over the K tetra-bases selects the most active basis, and the resulting 4D vectors are propagated through a VN-DGCNN backbone (vector-neuron graph CNN) that performs equivariant edge convolutions and pooling. O(3)-invariance is then obtained by computing point-wise inner products between equivariant features at two different depths, following the vector-neuron framework. The key insight is that the learned 4D tetra-basis projections are more expressive than raw 3D coordinates, and the entire pipeline adds fewer than 0.0002% additional parameters over the VN-DGCNN baseline while achieving state-of-the-art results on ScanObjectNN, ModelNet40, and ShapeNet.

## Results

On the hardest real-world subset of ScanObjectNN (PB_T50_RS, ~15K point clouds with 50% occlusion, random rotation, and scaling), TetraSphere (K=4) achieves 79.2% classification accuracy under z/z and z/SO(3) settings and 79.0% under SO(3)/SO(3), setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the previous best (3D-GFE, 73.5%) by over 5 points. On the easier OBJ_BG subset, TetraSphere (K=2) reaches 87.3% accuracy, outperforming the prior best (Yu et al., 86.6%). On synthetic data, TetraSphere (K=8) attains 90.5% on ModelNet40 classification and 82.3% mIoU on ShapeNet part segmentation, the best among equivariant methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TetraSphere outperforms all eight compared baselines on the challenging PB_T50_RS subset. Against the equivariant baseline VN-DGCNN [10] (77.9–78.5%), TetraSphere gains 1.3–1.7 points, confirming the benefit of the 4D TetraTransform. The previous real-data SOTA, 3D-GFE [8] (73.5%), is surpassed by a margin of 5.7 points. Rotation-robust methods Li et al. [23] (74.6–74.9%) and PaRINet [6] (71.6–72.2%) trail by 4–7 points, while the registration-based Yu et al. [48] (77.2–77.4%) is outperformed by 1.8–2.0 points. Rotation-sensitive methods (PointCNN [25], DGCNN [41]) collapse under arbitrary test rotations (14.9–16.1% on z/SO(3)), highlighting the necessity of equivariance. On synthetic ModelNet40, TetraSphere (90.5%) edges out TFN [31] (89.7%) and VN-DGCNN (89.5%), while on ShapeNet part segmentation it achieves 82.3% mIoU vs. 81.4% for VN-DGCNN and 78.1% for TFN.

## Relation to Prior Work

TetraSphere sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: the SO(3)-equivariant Vector Neuron framework (VN-DGCNN) of Deng et al., which propagates equivariant 3D vector features through graph convolutions, and the steerable 3D spherical neurons of Melnyk et al., which are SO(3)-equivariant filter banks derived from conformal geometry. The paper's conceptual move is to embed the steerable neurons' 4D output (spanned by a tetra-basis) directly into a 4D extension of vector neurons, thereby unifying the two equivariant mechanisms into a single end-to-end architecture. This departs from the dominant rotation-invariant paradigm of hand-crafted pair-point features (PPF-based methods) and from augmentation-heavy rotation-sensitive approaches, positioning itself as a learned, geometrically-grounded equivariant alternative that also achieves O(3) (reflection) invariance as a byproduct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that steerable 3D spherical neurons—previously only analyzed in isolation—can serve as a useful, near-zero-cost front-end in a deep equivariant network, setting new SOTA on the challenging perturbed ScanObjectNN benchmark. However, the finding that the trained model effectively collapses to a single tetra-basis (K=1) raises open questions about whether the multi-basis aggregation mechanism is truly necessary or whether a simpler single-neuron lift would suffice. Broader applicability beyond classification and part segmentation (e.g., registration, detection, or continuous 3D tasks) remains unexplored, and the modest 1–3% gains over VN-DGCNN on synthetic data suggest the 4D representation's advantage is most pronounced under real-world noise and occlusion rather than in clean settings.
