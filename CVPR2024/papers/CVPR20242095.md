## Problem

Existing vision-centric 3D scene understanding methods either predict discrete voxel grids (occupancy prediction) that cannot represent continuous obstacle surfaces, or rely on LiDAR point cloud inputs for SDF-based reconstruction with sparse or inaccurate supervision. No prior method achieves continuous, camera-only SDF-based 3D perception with dense and geometrically accurate surface constraints in driving scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242095/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6 surround-view RGB images (256×704 to 640×1600 depending on model size) and produces a continuous signed distance field φ(x) and semantic field S(x) over a 200×200×16 voxel grid spanning [-40m, -40m, -1m, 40m, 40m, 5.4m]. From these implicit fields, the system can extract a 3D semantic mesh via marching cubes, discrete occupancy voxel grids for standard evaluation, or a temporally accumulated semantic scene reconstruction. LiDAR points and occupancy GT are used only as supervision signals during training, not as input at inference.

## Method

SurroundSDF uses a CNN backbone + LSS + BEV encoder to produce voxel features, then a query-based approach with trilinear interpolation and sinusoidal positional encoding feeds 3-layer MLP heads (with sine activations) to predict SDF and semantic values at arbitrary 3D points. The key training innovation is the Sandwich Eikonal formulation: LiDAR points constrain SDF=0 on the surface, occupied grids constrain the minimum SDF to be negative (interior), and free grids constrain SDF to be positive (exterior), effectively 'sandwiching' the surface with dense constraints on both sides. Multi-frame point cloud fusion densifies the surface supervision. A joint supervision loss combines the SDF-derived free probability (via softmax and inv-sigmoid) with semantic logits through a Dice loss, resolving the observed inconsistency where optimal geometric and semantic thresholds diverge.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes validation set, SurroundSDF achieves 42.37 mIoU with a ResNet50 backbone (Small version), 46.0 mIoU with ResNet101 (Medium), and 53.01 mIoU with ConvNext-B (Large), setting new state-of-the-art results for camera-based 3D semantic occupancy prediction. For 3D scene reconstruction, the method achieves an absolute relative error (Abs Rel) of 0.174, outperforming all compared depth estimation and occupancy-based methods, with a δ<1.25 score of 0.747.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurroundSDF outperforms all 10 compared baselines across both occupancy prediction and scene reconstruction tasks. With the same ResNet50 backbone, it surpasses FB-OCC (40.69) by 1.68 mIoU and even beats FB-OCC with TTA (42.06) by 0.31 mIoU, while leading SurroundOcc (36.32) by a substantial 6.05 mIoU. At the ResNet101 scale, it exceeds BEVFormer and VoxFormer (both 40.6) by 5.4 mIoU. With ConvNext-B, it edges out UniOCC (51.5) by 1.51 mIoU and FB-OCC with InternImage-H and TTA (52.79) by 0.22 mIoU. For 3D scene reconstruction (Abs Rel), SurroundSDF achieves 0.174, beating SurroundDepth (0.224), R3D3 (0.259), SurroundOcc (0.274), and FB-OCC (0.342), demonstrating that the SDF-based continuous representation yields significantly more accurate geometry than discrete voxel occupancy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit SDF-based scene reconstruction line (SIREN, LODE) from point-cloud-driven settings to a fully camera-only surround-view perception framework. It departs from SIREN's sparse LiDAR-only surface supervision and LODE's occupancy-grid-as-surface approximation by introducing the Sandwich Eikonal formulation, which simultaneously constrains SDF values on the surface (via LiDAR points) and on both sides of the surface (via occupancy GT), yielding denser and more accurate geometric supervision. The 3D encoder follows BEVStereo's LSS-based architecture, positioning the contribution primarily in the SDF supervision paradigm and the unification of occupancy prediction with continuous scene reconstruction under a single implicit representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SurroundSDF demonstrates that SDF-based implicit representations can match or exceed discrete voxel-grid occupancy methods on semantic prediction while simultaneously producing continuous, accurate 3D geometry—a capability voxel methods fundamentally lack. The robustness experiments (graceful degradation under coarser OCC GT and sparser LiDAR) suggest the Sandwich Eikonal formulation is a practical solution to the SDF supervision bottleneck that has limited prior implicit methods in driving. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger scenes or higher resolutions, whether real-time inference is feasible for deployment, and whether the LiDAR dependency during training can be further reduced. The method is evaluated only on nuScenes, leaving generalization to other driving datasets untested.
