## Problem

Generating photorealistic 3D urban scenes directly from satellite imagery remains challenging due to the large view gap between bird's-eye and street-level perspectives and the expansive scene scale. Prior approaches either produce 2D images/videos with poor inter-frame consistency, operate at the object level, or rely on per-scene optimization, none of which enable efficient rendering from truly arbitrary views while maintaining geometric fidelity to the satellite-derived structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242422/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed 3D point cloud geometry (approximately 1 million points at ~400/m² density, resampled via Poisson disk sampling for uniform spatial distribution) and a set of camera poses as input. It produces a 3D scene representation consisting of per-point features (32 channels) anchored to the geometry, a 2D background panorama at infinite distance, and rendered RGB-D frames from any query viewpoint. The geometry is treated as a given constraint; the model generates only the appearance (color and density) on top of it, with a confidence mask zeroing out points corresponding to dynamic objects like cars and pedestrians.

## Method

Sat2Scene introduces a two-stage pipeline: a 3D sparse diffusion model (time-conditional UNet with Minkowski sparse convolutions, T=1000 steps) denoises per-point RGB colors directly in 3D space, operating exclusively on occupied voxels to avoid the memory explosion of dense grids for city-scale scenes. A separate feed-forward 3D encoder then extracts per-point features from the colorized point cloud, which are aggregated at render time via inverse-distance-weighted KNN (K=12, R=3m) around each ray sample. A small network predicts color and density from the aggregated feature and view direction, and standard volume rendering composites the foreground with a 2D panoramic sky background. The key architectural insight is that sparse 3D convolutions make diffusion tractable on large outdoor point clouds, while the point-anchored feature representation with KNN aggregation enables arbitrary-view rendering without per-scene optimization, achieving both photorealism and strong inter-frame consistency.

## Results

On the HoliCity dataset (624 scenes, ~5k images), Sat2Scene achieves the best FVD (20.30) and KVD (1.90×10⁻²) among all methods, indicating superior overall video quality. It also leads on all temporal consistency metrics: PSNR 31.54 dB, SSIM 0.956, and LPIPS 0.237, substantially outperforming Sat2Vid (PSNR 25.25, SSIM 0.741) and MVDiffusion (PSNR 17.56, SSIM 0.593). In the user study (32 participants, 30 questions), 81.46% of votes favored Sat2Scene over the baselines (15.62% for MVDiffusion, 2.92% for Sat2Vid). The only metrics where Sat2Scene trails are single-frame FID (71.98 vs. 50.78) and KID (5.91 vs. 4.14), where MVDiffusion benefits from its large pre-trained LDM backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sat2Scene outperforms all three baselines on video-level quality (FVD 20.30 vs. 22.79 for MVDiffusion and 37.06 for Sat2Vid) and on every temporal consistency metric (PSNR 31.54 vs. 17.56/25.25; SSIM 0.956 vs. 0.593/0.741; LPIPS 0.237 vs. 0.259/0.252), confirming that 3D-space generation yields far more consistent multi-frame sequences than 2D diffusion or GAN-based approaches. The user study strongly corroborates this: 81.46% of participants preferred Sat2Scene videos, compared to only 15.62% for MVDiffusion and 2.92% for Sat2Vid. The sole exception is single-frame photorealism, where MVDiffusion achieves lower FID (50.78 vs. 71.98) and KID (4.14 vs. 5.91), attributed to its billion-parameter pre-trained LDM backbone memorizing richer texture patterns. InfiniCity, which requires per-scene optimization and is based on GANcraft, reports only FID (108.47) and KID (8.40), both substantially worse than Sat2Scene, and is not directly comparable on video metrics. On the OmniCity generalization test, Sat2Scene produces consistent bird-view and street-view videos from satellite-inferred geometry, while MVDiffusion fails to generalize, generating street-view-biased images even for bird-view trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the satellite-to-ground synthesis line (S2G, Sat2Vid, InfiniCity) from 2D image/video generation to full 3D scene representation generation, and simultaneously adapts 3D diffusion models (DiffRF, Point-UV Diffusion, NF-LDM) from dense voxel grids to sparse point-based representations. The key conceptual departure is using 3D sparse convolutions in the diffusion denoiser to operate directly on occupied geometry (building facades, road surfaces) rather than full volumetric grids, making city-scale scenes tractable. For rendering, it follows the Point-NeRF / NSVF lineage of point-anchored features with KNN aggregation, but replaces per-scene optimization with a feed-forward encoder, distinguishing it from InfiniCity's GANcraft-based per-scene training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that diffusion models can operate effectively in a 3D sparse setting for large-scale outdoor scene generation, overcoming the memory bottleneck that has limited prior 3D diffusion work to objects or small scenes. The strong inter-frame consistency (PSNR 31.54, SSIM 0.956) and 81% user preference over baselines validate that 3D-space generation inherently solves the temporal coherence problem that plagues 2D multi-view diffusion approaches. Limitations include inferior single-frame photorealism compared to LDM-based MVDiffusion (FID 72 vs 51), a ~7-day training pipeline, and reliance on ~1M-point geometry per scene. Open questions include scaling to truly city-wide extents, handling dynamic objects, and whether the sparse diffusion formulation can be accelerated with fewer denoising steps.
