## Problem

State-of-the-art large-scale dynamic NeRF methods like SUDS require over 64 hours of training and ~13 seconds of inference per frame due to high-resolution rendering and random ray sampling across videos. While low-resolution rendering with CNN upsampling (as in UniSim) can reduce both times, it degrades reconstruction accuracy. The paper addresses how to simultaneously accelerate training and inference while improving rendering quality for large-scale dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240462/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (1242×375), 2D optical flow, and LiDAR depth from driving sequences of up to 90 timesteps (9 seconds) with two camera perspectives. During training, rays are sampled in 16×16 patches (16 patches per batch, 4096 rays total) rather than individually, and the radiance fields output a low-resolution feature map (414×125) that is 3× upsampled by the reference decoder to produce the final full-resolution rendered RGB image. The reference decoder additionally ingests a high-resolution reference patch or image retrieved from known views via optical-flow-guided structural similarity search.

## Method

PaReNeRF builds on SUDS's three-branch radiance field (static, dynamic, far-field) but introduces three modifications: (1) patch-based ray sampling replaces random per-ray sampling, reducing data-loading overhead by a factor of h×w; (2) the radiance fields output a low-resolution feature map (414×125) instead of full-resolution RGB, which is upsampled 3× by a lightweight CARN-based reference decoder using PixelShuffle; (3) a prior information search module uses 2D optical flow to identify candidate regions in forward/backward views, then slides patches within those regions to find the one with maximum structural similarity to the current patch, which is fed into the reference decoder as a high-resolution reference. At inference, the most structurally similar known-view image serves as the reference. The reference decoder fuses the CARN-encoded LR feature map with the reference-image features through cascaded residual modules before decoding to RGB, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with SUDS's combined losses computed in a patch-wise fashion.

## Results

On the KITTI image reconstruction benchmark, PaReNeRF achieves a PSNR of 32.64 and SSIM of 0.933, outperforming the SOTA baseline SUDS (PSNR 28.31, SSIM 0.870) by 13% in PSNR. Training time is reduced from 64 hours (SUDS) to 26 hours (a 60% reduction), and inference time drops from 12.86 s to 1.61 s (an 87% reduction). On VKITTI2, PaReNeRF achieves PSNR 30.89 vs. SUDS* 30.85 with inference time of 1.35 s vs. 11.89 s. In novel view synthesis (Table 3), PaReNeRF consistently leads across all train/test splits (75%, 50%, 25%) on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaReNeRF decisively outperforms SUDS, the primary SOTA baseline, on every reported metric: PSNR improves from 28.31 to 32.64 (+4.33 dB), SSIM from 0.870 to 0.933, training time drops from 64 h to 26 h (−60%), and inference time falls from 12.86 s to 1.61 s (−87%). Against PNF (PSNR 27.48, SSIM 0.870), PaReNeRF gains +5.16 dB PSNR and +0.063 SSIM. Compared to NSG (PSNR 26.66, SSIM 0.806), the improvement is +5.98 dB PSNR and +0.127 SSIM, with training time reduced from 51 h to 26 h. Against the original NeRF (PSNR 23.34) and NeRF+Time (PSNR 24.18), PaReNeRF leads by over 8 dB in PSNR. In novel view synthesis with only 25% of training views, PaReNeRF still achieves PSNR 21.05 on KITTI vs. 20.76 for SUDS, and avoids the ghosting artifacts that degrade SUDS renderings at lower view fractions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaReNeRF builds directly on SUDS (CVPR 2023), adopting its three-branch (static/dynamic/far-field) hash-table radiance field architecture and loss formulation, while replacing SUDS's random ray sampling with patch-based sampling and its direct high-resolution rendering with a low-resolution feature map decoded by a CARN-based reference decoder. The low-resolution rendering plus CNN upsampling strategy follows UniSim (CVPR 2023), and the reference-based quality recovery draws on the broader line of NeRF-with-reference work (NeRF-SR, pixelNeRF, MVSNeRF), but adapts it to dynamic scenes via an optical-flow-guided, structural-similarity-based search that prior reference-based NeRF methods (designed for static, object-centric scenes) do not address. The paper thus positions itself at the intersection of efficient NeRF rendering and reference-based super-resolution, targeting the specific bottleneck of large-scale dynamic scene reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% training speedup and 87% inference speedup while simultaneously improving PSNR by ~13% over SUDS is practically significant for autonomous driving simulation, where current methods require over two days to train a nine-second clip. However, the evaluation is limited to short (≤9 s) KITTI and VKITTI2 sequences, and the authors acknowledge that truly real-time training and rendering remain open. The reference decoder's reliance on structural similarity matching and optical flow for prior search may not generalize cleanly to scenes with large viewpoint changes or occlusions, and the added decoder introduces architectural complexity that could complicate further scaling. A key open question is whether the patch-sampling + reference-decoder paradigm can be extended to longer, multi-scene sequences without degrading the reference search quality.
