## Problem

Existing learning-based single-view 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental efficiency-quality tradeoff: implicit representations (NeRF, triplanes) are slow to render or require hundreds of GPUs for training, while explicit voxel methods scale poorly with resolution. No prior method achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction quality while being trainable on a single GPU and operating at real-time inference speeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240302/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (or N images with relative camera poses) at 128×128 resolution and produces a 'Splatter Image' — a 2D H×W×(12+k_c) tensor where each pixel encodes one 3D Gaussian's opacity, depth, 3D offset, scale, rotation quaternion, and spherical-harmonic colour coefficients. This pseudo-image is then rendered via differentiable Gaussian Splatting to generate novel views at 588 FPS. The 3D representation is stored in image format rather than a point cloud or grid, which is the key structural choice enabling the use of standard 2D image-to-image networks.

## Method

The core design maps an input image to one 3D Gaussian per pixel using a U-Net (SongUNet) with only 2D convolutions, reducing 3D reconstruction to image-to-image translation. Each Gaussian's 3D position is parameterised as depth along the camera ray plus a learned 3D offset, allowing the network to place Gaussians off-ray to reconstruct unseen (occluded) parts of the object for full 360° coverage. Training minimises L2 and LPIPS losses between rendered target views and ground truth, made feasible by the fast Gaussian Splatting renderer that produces full images at every iteration rather than sparse pixel samples. For multi-view input, the same network is applied per view with FiLM conditioning on relative camera poses and lightweight cross-view attention at the lowest U-Net resolution; individual Gaussian mixtures are then warped to a common frame and unioned. View-dependent appearance is modelled with first-order spherical harmonics, whose rotation under viewpoint change is handled analytically via a simple 3×3 matrix transform.

## Results

On ShapeNet-SRN, Splatter Image achieves SOTA single-view reconstruction with PSNR 24.00 / SSIM 0.92 / LPIPS 0.078 on Cars and PSNR 24.43 / SSIM 0.93 / LPIPS 0.067 on Chairs, outperforming all deterministic baselines. On multi-class ShapeNet it reaches PSNR 29.38 / SSIM 0.95 / LPIPS 0.047, and on Google Scanned Objects it surpasses OpenLRM (PSNR 21.06 vs 18.06, LPIPS 0.111 vs 0.129) while training on only 2 GPUs for 3.5 days versus hundreds of GPUs for LRM. At test time the method is >1000× faster than PixelNeRF and VisionNeRF (0.45 s vs 463–608 s for 250 novel views), and at training time it is 246× faster than implicit NeRF-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Splatter Image outperforms all seven ShapeNet-SRN baselines on LPIPS for both Cars and Chairs, and on PSNR for Cars (24.00 vs. best prior 23.95 NeRFDiff); on Chairs it matches VisionNeRF's PSNR (24.43 vs 24.48) while winning on LPIPS (0.067 vs 0.070). On multi-class ShapeNet (Table 2) it beats VisionNeRF (PSNR 29.38 vs 28.76, LPIPS 0.047 vs 0.065) and all other baselines across all three metrics. On CO3D it surpasses PixelNeRF on every metric for both Hydrants (LPIPS 0.150 vs 0.203) and Teddybears (LPIPS 0.231 vs 0.290). On Google Scanned Objects it clearly outperforms OpenLRM (PSNR 21.06 vs 18.06, SSIM 0.88 vs 0.84, LPIPS 0.111 vs 0.129) despite using 50× fewer GPUs for training. In the two-view setting (Table 5) it edges out PixelNeRF (PSNR 26.01 vs 25.66) and also beats absolute-pose methods SRN, CodeNeRF, and FE-NVS. In speed (Table 7) it is the fastest method by a wide margin: 0.45 s test time vs 463 s (PixelNeRF) and 608 s (VisionNeRF), and 0.033 s forward pass vs 7.4–9.7 s for NeRF-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Splatter Image ports 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from its original per-scene optimization setting into a feed-forward, learning-based monocular reconstruction framework, which no prior work had done. Architecturally, it repurposes the SongUNet (Song et al., 2021) as a standard image-to-image predictor, storing one 3D Gaussian per pixel in a 2D 'Splatter Image' — a design that departs from the implicit (NeRF, triplane) and explicit (voxel grid) representations dominating prior single-view reconstructors like PixelNeRF, VisionNeRF, and LRM. The multi-view extension via cross-view attention and FiLM pose conditioning follows patterns from MVDream and similar multi-view diffusion works, but applied here to a deterministic Gaussian-based reconstructor rather than a generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gap — single-GPU training versus 128 A100 GPUs for LRM, and 38 FPS inference versus seconds for NeRF-based methods — fundamentally lowers the barrier to experimenting with and deploying single-view 3D reconstruction, and enables image-level perceptual losses (LPIPS) during training that were previously infeasible. The finding that a 2D image can encode full 360° 3D geometry by allocating background pixels to unseen object parts is a useful inductive bias that may generalize to other reconstruction settings. Open questions include whether the one-Gaussian-per-pixel constraint limits expressiveness for highly complex or non-rigid objects, how the method scales to scene-level (non-object-centric) reconstruction, and whether the deterministic formulation can be combined with the generative approaches it positions as complementary.
