## Problem

Existing prompt-based LLM approaches for Diagram Question Answering (DQA) treat the LLM as a black-box answerer with no interaction with the diagram parsing process, causing inference to rely heavily on the model's prior knowledge while marginalizing visual features. Additionally, DQA datasets are domain-specific and small (e.g., ~3,000 diagrams in AI2D), making it impractical to fine-tune large models per domain. The paper asks how to leverage LLMs' rich background knowledge to actively guide diagram parsing tools rather than merely prompting for a final answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a diagram image, a natural language question, an optional text context, and a candidate answer set (4–6 options for multiple-choice, 2 for true/false) and outputs the selected answer. The LLM (GPT-3.5) and all diagram parsing tools (ResNet-101, FastSAM, CLIP) operate with frozen parameters; only the T5-based Answer Inference module is trained (20 epochs, lr=5e-5, single A800 GPU). The LLM is queried in a multi-turn conversation format across three stages, with each turn producing structured outputs (background knowledge text, semantic labels, relationship triples) that are consumed by the downstream inference module.

## Method

CoG-DQA introduces a Chain-of-Guiding Learning Module with three progressive stages—global knowledge supplement, visual objects extraction, and visual relationships generation—where each stage pairs a frozen DPT (ResNet for global features, FastSAM+CLIP for object masks, embedding similarity for relationships) with an LLM guiding component that uses multi-turn prompts to supply domain knowledge, semantic labels, and relationship semantics. An Interaction component at each stage aligns LLM outputs with DPT features (e.g., CLIP-based mask-to-label matching, cosine-similarity gating for edge construction), producing a visual graph G_v. The Answer Inference module encodes both a visual graph and a textual graph (built via OpenIE triplet extraction and CoreNLP coreference resolution) with a Graph Attention Network, fuses them via single-head attention and a gated mechanism, and feeds the result into a T5 Transformer decoder for answer prediction. The key design insight is that the LLM acts as a knowledge-guiding chain that enriches each parsing step with domain-specific semantics, rather than serving as a standalone question-answering oracle.

## Results

CoG-DQA achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on all four DQA datasets: 78.85% on SQA-I, 54.60% on TQA-DMC, 68.28% on CSDQA, and 79.14% on AI2D. The most significant improvement is on CSDQA, where CoG-DQA surpasses the previous SOTA (MM-CoT-large) by 11.00 percentage points (68.28% vs. 57.28%), while the smallest gain is on TQA-DMC (+1.27%). Under the chain-of-thought setting on SQA-I, CoG-DQA reaches 96.13% accuracy with ground-truth lecture/explanation, exceeding both GPT-3.5 (94.13%) and human performance (87.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoG-DQA outperforms all 13 baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest prompt-based baseline MM-CoT-large [49], CoG-DQA gains +5.32 pp on SQA-I (78.85 vs. 73.53), +1.27 pp on TQA-DMC (54.60 vs. 52.51), +11.00 pp on CSDQA (68.28 vs. 57.28), and +3.38 pp on AI2D (79.14 vs. 75.76). The gap is most pronounced on CSDQA, a specialized computer-science domain where prior methods (GPT-4 2-shot at 48.03%, LLaVa-1.5-13B at 48.65%) struggle significantly, highlighting CoG-DQA's advantage in domain-specific reasoning. Among finetune-based models, ISAAQ [11] is the strongest on SQA-I (66.53%) and AI2D (67.93%), but CoG-DQA exceeds it by 12.32 and 11.21 pp respectively. QA-based models (MCAN, BAN, VisualBERT, RAFR) consistently underperform, with the best (VisualBERT) reaching only 62.17% on SQA-I and 42.86% on CSDQA, confirming that without external knowledge integration, standard VQA architectures are insufficient for diagram reasoning. Notably, even GPT-4 2-shot [33] (71.49% on SQA-I, 48.03% on CSDQA) is outperformed, suggesting that direct LLM prompting without structured diagram parsing remains limited for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM prompting and diagram parsing, departing from two established lines of work. Prior DQA methods either pre-train language models on external knowledge bases (ISAAQ, WSTQ, MoCA) or use LLMs in a one-shot prompt-to-answer fashion (ScienceQA, MM-CoT, GoT), with minimal interaction between the LLM and the visual parsing pipeline. CoG-DQA introduces a structured multi-turn 'chain-of-guiding' where the LLM actively directs frozen diagram parsing tools (segmentation, object detection, relationship extraction) at three granularities, and a dual-graph (textual + visual) GAT inference module that was not present in prior DQA architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in nature of the CoG module—improving Unified-QA, ISAAQ, and MM-CoT without retraining—makes it a practical component for existing DQA pipelines, and the 11% gain on CSDQA highlights its value in small, domain-specific settings where annotated data is scarce. However, the framework's reliance on a proprietary LLM (GPT-3.5) for the guiding chain, the frozen DPTs, and the multi-stage pipeline complexity raise questions about deployment cost and scalability to more visually complex or open-ended diagrams. Open questions include whether the guiding chain generalizes to non-multiple-choice formats, whether open-source LLMs can substitute effectively, and whether the dual-graph inference module's gains persist as diagram complexity grows beyond the tested datasets.
