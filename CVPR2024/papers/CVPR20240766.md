## Problem

Viewgraphs from unordered image collections contain highly redundant edges that inflate reconstruction time, and repeated scene structures introduce false edges that cause ghosting and superimposed reconstructions. These two problems have been addressed independently in the literature under opposing assumptions—sparsification assumes most edges are true, while disambiguation assumes many are false—leaving no unified scheme that handles both simultaneously without intermediate partial reconstructions or multiple hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a viewgraph with geometrically verified edges (obtained from COLMAP or any SfM front-end), the epipolar inlier count for each edge, and a single minimum edge score parameter m. It outputs a sparsified viewgraph with redundant and false edges removed, ready as a pre-processing step for any downstream incremental or global SfM pipeline. No intermediate 3D reconstruction, GPU, or learned model is required; the entire procedure runs on CPU in less than 1% of the reconstruction time.

## Method

Each edge is scored by examining every camera triplet it participates in: within a triplet, an edge's score is the ratio of its epipolar inlier count to the maximum inlier count among the three edges, and the final edge score is the average over all triplets. To handle joints (triplets sharing only a node), a triplet graph is constructed and only edges in its largest connected component are retained, ensuring scores reflect global connectivity. Edge selection is formulated as a combinatorial optimization that maximizes the average quality of retained edges while penalizing the average quality of removed edges via a regularization parameter λ. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that the optimal solution reduces to a simple thresholding rule, with the threshold computed adaptively from the graph's maximum node degree and node count, yielding a single-hyperparameter algorithm that simultaneously sparsifies generic viewgraphs and removes false edges in ambiguous scenes.

## Results

On generic datasets, the proposed method reduces COLMAP reconstruction time by 50–80% (e.g., Notre Dame: 2054→758 min; Piccadilly: 1969→1000 min; Trafalgar: 6875→3698 min) while maintaining or improving mean reprojection error and recovering most cameras and 3D points. On ambiguous datasets, the method successfully disambiguates all large- and medium-scale scenes (recovering more cameras and 3D points than Doppelgangers on most), and also sparsifies the graph to further cut reconstruction time (e.g., Notre Dame: 1160→1059 min vs. Doppelgangers; Seville: 87→24 min). The method itself executes in <1% of the total reconstruction time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is Doppelgangers [3], a learned disambiguation method using a neural network. The proposed method wins on disambiguation coverage: it successfully disambiguates Church on Spilled Blood, Books, Cereal, and Oats, where Doppelgangers fails entirely (no probability threshold works). On large-scale ambiguous datasets (Notre Dame, Seville, Ellis Island, Berliner Dom), the proposed method recovers more cameras and 3D points than Doppelgangers while achieving equal or lower reprojection error (e.g., Notre Dame: 5420 vs. 5481 cameras; Seville: 475 vs. 447 cameras). The proposed method also consistently reduces reconstruction time due to its sparsification side-effect (e.g., Seville: 24 min vs. 87 min; Alexander Nevsky: 13 min vs. 31 min). On small-scale sequential datasets [49], the proposed method sometimes over-splits (marked √*), recovering fewer cameras than Doppelgangers, though it still achieves lower reprojection error. On Temple of Heaven, Doppelgangers is slightly faster (32 vs. 54 min) and recovers marginally fewer 3D points, representing the one case where the baseline has a time advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—viewgraph sparsification (Snavely et al., Havlena et al., Shen et al., Shah et al.) and disambiguation of repeated structures (Zach et al., Roberts et al., Heinly et al., Cai et al.)—under a single triplet-based scoring mechanism. Prior sparsification methods typically rely on intermediate partial reconstructions, multiple hyperparameters, or pipeline-specific designs, while disambiguation methods often assume specific probability distributions on missing correspondences or exploit loop-consistency checks. The key departure is formulating edge selection as a combinatorial optimization whose solution is provably equivalent to a simple adaptive threshold, requiring only one hyperparameter and no intermediate reconstruction, and positioning the method as a pipeline-agnostic preprocessing step for any SfM system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale SfM practitioners: a 50–80% reduction in reconstruction time with maintained or improved accuracy, plus removal of ghost artifacts and superimposed reconstructions, all from a single lightweight preprocessing step that requires no GPU and no pipeline modification. Limitations include over-splitting on small sequential datasets with low edge redundancy, failure to disambiguate certain scenes (e.g., Cup), and dependence on COLMAP's initial viewgraph quality. Open questions include how the method interacts with learned feature matchers (SuperPoint, LightGlue) that change inlier statistics, whether triplet enumeration scales to extremely large graphs (tens of thousands of cameras), and whether the adaptive threshold can be further simplified or made dataset-agnostic.
