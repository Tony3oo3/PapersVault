## Problem

Classical 2-frame optical flow methods cannot fully exploit temporal coherence across a video sequence, while existing multi-frame approaches (e.g., VideoFlow, TransFlow) incur heavy computational overhead, require access to unseen future frames in offline mode, and fail to run in real-time. This makes them unsuitable for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving where flow must be estimated online with only past observations available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241196/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MemFlow consumes a current frame pair (I_t, I_{t+1}) at 1/8 internal resolution along with a persistent memory buffer of size l_max (set to 1 in practice) that stores key-value pairs of historical context and motion features. It outputs a per-pixel 2D displacement vector field f_{t→t+1}. In prediction mode (MemFlow-P), only I_t and the memory buffer are available, and the method outputs a predicted future flow without needing I_{t+1}. The resolution-adaptive re-scaling enables zero-shot generalization from 368×768 training resolution to 1920×1080 (1080p Spring) without retraining.

## Method

MemFlow builds on SKFlow's GRU-based architecture with 4D correlation volumes but introduces a memory module with two operations: a read-out step where an attention mechanism queries the memory buffer using the current context feature to retrieve an aggregated motion feature, and an update step where the final context and motion features are prepended to the buffer's key and value tensors after each frame. The GRU iteratively refines the flow (15 iterations by default) using context, motion, and aggregated motion features, with a learnable scalar α (initialized to 0) controlling the contribution of the memory read-out. A key technical contribution is resolution-adaptive re-scaling of the attention logits using log_{n_avg}(L + H·W)/√D_k, which prevents similarity-score dilution at longer sequences and enables cross-resolution generalization. For future prediction, the correlation volume is replaced by a small convolutional network that regresses flow from context, aggregated motion, and past flow features, requiring no retraining for the downstream task.

## Results

On the zero-shot generalization benchmark (trained on FlyingChairs + FlyingThings3D), MemFlow-T achieves EPE of 0.85 on Sintel Clean and 2.06 on Sintel Final, with Fl-all of 12.8% on KITTI-15. The lighter MemFlow variant achieves 0.93 / 2.08 EPE on Sintel and 13.7% Fl-all on KITTI, representing a 23.8% and 15.4% error reduction over its 2-frame baseline SKFlow (1.22 / 2.46 EPE, 15.5% Fl-all). On the 1080p Spring dataset after finetuning, MemFlow reaches 0.471 EPE and 1.42% Fl-all, outperforming CroCo-Flow (0.498 EPE, 1.51% Fl-all) despite CroCo-Flow being pretrained on 5.3M additional real-world image pairs. MemFlow runs at 5.6 fps on A100 for 1024×436 video, with a 5-iteration variant reaching 14.5 fps while retaining near-SOTA generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemFlow(-T) outperforms all 2-frame baselines (RAFT, GMA, GMFlow, GMFlowNet, SKFlow, MatchFlow, FlowFormer++, EMD-L) on Sintel Final EPE and KITTI Fl-all, with the largest margins over RAFT (0.63 px on Sintel Final, 3.7% Fl-all on KITTI) and GMFlow (0.40 px on Sintel Final, 9.7% Fl-all on KITTI). Among multi-frame competitors, MemFlow decisively beats VideoFlow-MOF on all four metrics (e.g., 2.08 vs 2.56 Sintel Final EPE) and VideoFlow-BOF on Sintel Final and KITTI, while matching TransFlow on Sintel Clean (both 0.93) and beating it on Sintel Final (2.08 vs 2.33) and KITTI Fl-all (13.7% vs 14.4%). MemFlow-T achieves the best Sintel Clean EPE (0.85) and KITTI Fl-epe (3.38) in the table, surpassing even EMD-L (0.88) and FlowFormer++ (0.90) on Sintel Clean. On the Spring 1080p benchmark, MemFlow after finetuning beats CroCo-Flow by 0.027 EPE and 0.09% Fl-all, and VideoFlow cannot run at all due to out-of-memory on a single A100 80 GB GPU. For flow prediction (Table 4), MemFlow-P achieves 7.56 EPE on FlyingThings3D, substantially outperforming OFNet (13.76) and the warped-oracle upper bound (14.76), and for video prediction it matches VPVFI (SSIM 0.825 vs 0.827) without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

MemFlow builds directly on the GRU-based iterative refinement architecture of SKFlow and the 4D correlation volume formulation of RAFT, inheriting their loss functions and feature extraction pipeline. Its key conceptual departure is replacing explicit multi-frame processing (as in VideoFlow or TransFlow, which require 5-frame offline windows) with a compact memory buffer that accumulates motion states over time, enabling online real-time operation. The resolution-adaptive re-scaling mechanism adapts the log-scaling fix for attention dilution proposed by Chiang and Cholak, repurposing it for cross-resolution generalization in optical flow. The memory read-out design draws inspiration from memory-augmented video segmentation methods (XMem, MemVit) but applies it to the distinct problem of temporal motion aggregation for flow estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MemFlow addresses a practical gap: multi-frame optical flow methods that capture temporal coherence have been too computationally expensive or required future frames for real-time deployment in safety-critical settings like autonomous driving. The finding that a memory buffer of length 1 (effectively a 3-frame equivalent) outperforms explicit 5-frame processing suggests that compact temporal state accumulation is sufficient, opening the door to efficient long-sequence flow estimation. The resolution-adaptive re-scaling enabling 1080p inference from lower-resolution training is a clean, low-cost solution to a common deployment challenge. Open questions include whether longer-range motion history (beyond one frame) can be effectively modeled without the computational blowup of explicit multi-frame attention, and whether the memory approach generalizes to other dense prediction tasks.
