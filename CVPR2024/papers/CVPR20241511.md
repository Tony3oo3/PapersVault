## Problem

State-of-the-art Referring Image Segmentation methods suffer from a persistent language-image modality gap at the pixel and word level. They rely on sentence-level language features for alignment and lack explicit training supervision for fine-grained visual grounding, resulting in weak object-level correspondence between visual and language features. This causes models to fail on complex expressions requiring reasoning over inter-object relationships or containing rare and ambiguous clauses.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form natural language referring expression as input, processed by a Swin-B image encoder and BERT-base language encoder respectively. It outputs a binary pixel-level segmentation mask identifying the referred object. During training, the ground-truth segmentation mask is additionally consumed by a Mask Encoder (2-layer MLP on center coordinates) to support the Mask Grounding auxiliary task, and the language input is randomly corrupted with mask tokens for the same task.

## Method

MagNet introduces three synergistic components. First, Mask Grounding is an auxiliary task that randomly masks textual tokens and requires the model to predict them using a BERT-like predictor that jointly attends to language features, image features, and mask center-coordinate embeddings—forcing the model to learn which words correspond to which visual objects. Second, a Cross-modal Alignment Module (CAM) performs bidirectional cross-attention between language and image features at four encoder stages, with pyramid pooling at multiple scales to resolve granularity mismatches between text and image representations. Third, a Cross-modal Alignment Loss (CAL) combines pixel-to-pixel and pixel-to-text contrastive terms to explicitly align segmentation features with both positive/negative pixel sets and pooled language features. The total loss is a weighted sum of BCE, Dice, CAL, and grounding cross-entropy losses.

## Results

On the single-dataset setting, MagNet achieves 75.24 oIoU on RefCOCO val, 66.16 on RefCOCO+ val, and 65.36 on G-Ref val(U), setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks simultaneously. On RefCOCO val, MagNet also leads in mIoU (77.43), P@0.5 (87.79), P@0.7 (80.01), and P@0.9 (38.77), surpassing the previous best (SADLR) by 0.91, 0.89, 1.25, and 1.41 points respectively. Under the multiple-dataset setting, MagNet further improves to 76.55 oIoU on RefCOCO val and 67.79 on G-Ref val(U), outperforming LISA-7B and PolyFormer by up to 2.48 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagNet is the first method to consistently outperform all prior SOTA methods across every evaluation subset of RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and G-Ref simultaneously. Against the closest competitor ReLA [45], MagNet gains +1.42 oIoU on RefCOCO val, +0.12 on RefCOCO+ val, and +0.36 on G-Ref val(U). Against MCRES [62], the margins are larger: +0.32 on RefCOCO val but +1.84 on RefCOCO+ val and +1.85 on G-Ref val(U), indicating MCRES overfits to RefCOCO. Against LAVT [64], the gains are most substantial, reaching +4.02 on RefCOCO+ val and +4.12 on G-Ref val(U). On the harder G-Ref benchmark, MagNet surpasses SADLR [65] by +1.76 on val(U) and +2.47 on test(U), and outperforms PVD [10] by +2.23 on val(U). In the multiple-dataset setting, MagNet (76.55 RefCOCO val, 67.79 G-Ref val(U)) also beats LISA-7B [38] and PolyFormer [46], which rely on large language models or additional datasets, by up to 2.48 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagNet builds directly on LAVT's cross-attention architecture for language-image fusion, replacing its one-way alignment with a bidirectional Cross-modal Alignment Module (CAM) that injects multi-scale pyramid-pooled global context. The core novelty, Mask Grounding, repurposes the masked language modeling paradigm from BERT but departs from both standard MLM and MaskedVLM by incorporating segmentation mask coordinates as an additional conditioning signal, forcing the model to learn explicit word-to-object correspondence rather than relying on implicit alignment through mask prediction. The cross-modal alignment loss extends prior contrastive losses (CoupAlign, CRIS) by jointly optimizing pixel-to-pixel and pixel-to-text consistency within a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that Mask Grounding is a plug-and-play auxiliary task (yielding 0.89–1.52 oIoU gains when added to LAVT, ReLA, and CRIS without architectural changes) makes it a broadly applicable technique for the RIS community, not just a component of one specific architecture. The paper's diagnosis that prior SOTA methods lack fine-grained visual grounding in language features — rather than merely needing better alignment modules — reframes where the bottleneck lies in RIS. Open questions include whether Mask Grounding transfers to other multi-modal dense prediction tasks (the authors suggest this as future work), whether the BERT vocabulary constraint limits open-vocabulary generalization, and whether the technique remains effective at larger model scales or with different language encoders.
