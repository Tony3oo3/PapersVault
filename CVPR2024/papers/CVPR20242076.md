## Problem

Existing NeRF inpainting methods rely on explicit 2D RGB and depth inpainting (e.g., LaMa) applied independently per view, which produces view-inconsistent imagery and poor geometry completion due to misalignment between inpainted depth and RGB. The adaptation of diffusion priors—already proven effective for 3D generation via Score Distillation Sampling—to the NeRF inpainting problem remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242076/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a posed RGB image sequence (60 training views at 1008×567 for Real-S, or 192×256 for Real-L), per-frame binary masks specifying the region to remove, a text prompt describing the missing content, and optionally ground-truth depth maps. It outputs a fully trained NeRF that renders inpainted novel views from arbitrary camera poses. All latent inputs to the diffusion model are resized to 256×256, and training runs for 10,000 iterations on 4 V100 GPUs.

## Method

MVIP-NeRF replaces explicit 2D inpainting supervision with two SDS-based losses computed in the latent space of Stable Diffusion: an appearance loss that backpropagates through rendered RGB (only masked pixels) and a geometry loss that backpropagates through rendered normal maps, enabling joint appearance-geometry completion without explicit depth inpainting. Normal maps are generated via a differentiable plane-fitting procedure (K=9 nearest neighbors) to reduce noise from raw density gradients. A multi-view score function shares the noise estimator and timestep t across N=5 simultaneously rendered views, so each gradient step accounts for cross-view interactions and prevents detail blurring under large viewpoint changes. The final training objective combines unmasked RGB/depth reconstruction losses with the multi-view appearance SDS and single-view geometry SDS, using annealed timestep scheduling and classifier-free guidance (CFG 7.5–25 for appearance, 2.5–7.5 for geometry).

## Results

On the Real-S dataset (limited view variation), MVIP-NeRF achieves the best PSNR (17.667 dB), LPIPS (0.507), and Depth L2 (1.499) among all methods, outperforming Remove-NeRF+LaMa and SPIn-NeRF+LaMa. On the Real-L dataset (large view variation), the method leads in PSNR (25.690 dB), LPIPS (0.181), and Depth L2 (0.021), with the depth error being roughly 1.8× lower than Remove-NeRF and 4.3× lower than SPIn-NeRF. The FID metric is competitive but not uniformly best: on Real-S it is slightly higher than both baselines, while on Real-L it is better than SPIn-NeRF (100.45 vs 103.57) but worse than Remove-NeRF (100.45 vs 88.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two NeRF inpainting baselines that both rely on explicit 2D inpainting via LaMa: Remove-NeRF [35] (which uses uncertainty-based view selection) and SPIn-NeRF [17] (which uses perceptual loss for multi-view consistency). MVIP-NeRF outperforms both baselines on PSNR, LPIPS, and Depth L2 across both datasets. On Real-S, the LPIPS improvement over Remove-NeRF is substantial (0.507 vs 0.665), and the Depth L2 error is dramatically lower (1.499 vs 8.748), reflecting the benefit of diffusion-prior geometry over explicit depth inpainting. On Real-L with large view variation, the method achieves the best LPIPS (0.181) and Depth L2 (0.021), while Remove-NeRF's view-selection mechanism gives it a lower FID (88.25 vs 100.45), suggesting it better preserves global texture statistics at the cost of geometric accuracy. The FID gap on Real-S (255.51 vs 239.99 for SPIn-NeRF) indicates that the diffusion prior, while producing more perceptually realistic local details, does not always yield the best global feature distribution match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in NeRF inpainting (Remove-NeRF, SPIn-NeRF, InpaintNeRF360, NeRF-In), which relies on explicit 2D inpainting (e.g., LaMa) to generate per-view RGB and depth supervision and then resolves multi-view inconsistencies post hoc. Instead, it repurposes the Score Distillation Sampling framework from DreamFusion—originally designed for text-to-3D generation—as an implicit inpainting signal, eliminating the need for any explicit 2D inpainter. The NeRF rendering pipeline is built directly on SPIn-NeRF, and the multi-view score distillation extends the single-view SDS gradient to jointly optimize across N sampled views sharing the same noise level, a formulation not present in prior SDS-based 3D generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing explicit 2D inpainting supervision with diffusion-prior-driven SDS, the method addresses a fundamental weakness of prior approaches: view-inconsistent and geometrically misaligned inpainting results. The co-optimization of RGB and normal maps via separate SDS losses is a practical recipe for jointly recovering appearance and geometry without depth inpainting, which prior methods showed to be unreliable. However, the approach inherits SDS limitations—high computational cost from iterative denoising, sensitivity to CFG hyperparameters, and inability to remove shadows—leaving open questions about scalability to unbounded scenes and real-time editing.
