## Problem

Traditional two-view relative pose estimation (e.g., the five-point algorithm) yields scaleless translation, creating a fundamental ambiguity in global SfM that is especially severe for collinear camera paths. Existing self-supervised depth/pose networks prioritize depth accuracy over pose accuracy and assume small viewing-angle differences between adjacent frames, failing under rapid camera rotation. Research on panoramic-camera 3D reconstruction remains relatively limited despite the modality's omnidirectional FoV advantages.

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of equirectangular panoramic images (resized to 320×640 during training) and produces a dense depth map at three scales (H×W, H/2×W/2, H/4×W/4) from the depth-net, plus a 6-DoF relative pose (rotation matrix via Procrustes mapping of a 9-dim vector, and a 3-dim scaled translation vector) from the pose-net. The full SfM pipeline then aggregates pairwise relative poses into global camera poses via rotation averaging and the proposed L2IRLS translation averaging, which directly optimizes per-pair translation scales constrained within ±50% of the network's initial estimate.

## Method

PanoPose pairs a lightweight ResNet-18 depth-net (Monodepth2-style) with a Croco transformer-based pose-net that regresses relative pose via MLP estimators. Two key innovations address pose accuracy: (1) a rotation-only pre-training stage that randomly rotates panoramic images around a y-dominant axis and supervises the pose-net with the known rotation, enabling robust estimation under large viewing-angle differences; and (2) a fusion block of three transformer layers (self-attention + cross-attention with RoPE) that injects multi-scale depth features into the translation estimator to improve scale accuracy. Training is fully self-supervised via photometric reprojection loss (L1 + SSIM), an edge-aware depth smoothness loss, and a pose consistency loss that penalizes asymmetry between forward and inverse relative pose predictions. The global SfM pipeline combines Chatterjee–Govindu rotation averaging with a novel L2IRLS translation averaging that treats per-pair scale as an explicit optimization variable with IRLS reweighting.

## Results

On relative pose estimation (Table 1), PanoPose achieves the best median RSE across all five datasets (e.g., 0.0115 on PanoSUNCG, 0.0101 on Mapillary Metropolis, 0.0519 on Campus) and the best mean RRE on 360VO-Seq1 (0.1520°) and PanoSUNCG (0.1559°), outperforming the best self-supervised baseline by 87% in mean RRE and 81% in mean RTAE on those datasets. On global pose estimation (Table 2), PanoPose combined with the proposed L2IRLS translation averaging achieves the lowest ATE across all tested sequences (e.g., 1.99 m on 360VO-Seq3, 0.98 m on 360VO-Seq6, 2.61 m on Building), reducing mean ATE by 29% and median ATE by 27% compared to the five-point algorithm with the same averaging method. The traditional five-point algorithm retains an advantage in rotation accuracy (RRE) and translation direction (RTAE) on most datasets due to precise feature matching, but cannot estimate scale, yielding RSE values of 0.5–1.9 versus PanoPose's 0.01–0.26.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four self-supervised learning baselines (SfMLearner, MonoDepth2, NonLocal-DPT, BiFuse++), PanoPose consistently wins on median RSE across all five datasets and on mean/median RRE on 360VO-Seq1 and PanoSUNCG, where it reduces mean RRE by 87% and mean RTAE by 81% over the best self-supervised competitor. BiFuse++ is the weakest self-supervised baseline, showing extreme RTAE values (33.6° mean on Mapillary Metropolis, 66.9 mean RSE on Campus), while NonLocal-DPT and MonoDepth2 are closer but still trail PanoPose on scale accuracy. The traditional five-point algorithm (with RootSIFT features) dominates on RRE and RTAE in most settings (e.g., 0.029° mean RRE on 360VO-Seq1 vs. PanoPose's 0.152°), but it cannot estimate translation scale, yielding RSE values of 0.5–1.9 versus PanoPose's 0.01–0.26; on PanoSUNCG (textureless indoor scenes) and Building (real-world outdoor), PanoPose surpasses five-point on both RRE and RTAE. For global pose (Table 2), PanoPose + L2IRLS achieves the best ATE in every tested configuration (e.g., 0.98 m on 360VO-Seq6 vs. 1.64 m for five-point + BATA), while five-point retains a slight edge on ARE due to its more accurate pairwise rotations. The proposed L2IRLS translation averaging is most effective when paired with PanoPose's scale estimates; when paired with five-point (unit-scale translations), it actually degrades ATE to the worst value, confirming that scale-aware initialization is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoPose extends the self-supervised depth-and-pose estimation framework of SfMLearner [42] and the lightweight depth-net design of Monodepth2 [10] to the panoramic imaging domain, replacing the CNN pose-net with the Croco transformer backbone [37] pre-trained via cross-view completion. It departs from the closest prior work, BiFuse++ [35], by operating in native equirectangular projection rather than cubic projection and by introducing a depth-to-pose fusion block that injects multi-scale depth features into the translation estimator. The proposed L2IRLS translation averaging also differs from existing methods (BATA, LUD) by directly optimizing the relative translation scale as a free variable, which is only feasible because the network outputs scaled translations rather than unit vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By learning scaled relative pose end-to-end, PanoPose addresses the scale ambiguity that has long plagued global SfM, and the panoramic camera model provides natural robustness to large viewpoint changes that pinhole-based methods struggle with. The rotation-only pre-training strategy (60% RRE reduction) and the depth fusion block (up to 73% RSE reduction) are practical techniques that could transfer to other self-supervised pose estimation settings. However, the reliance on the computationally heavy Croco transformer backbone limits real-time applicability, and the authors acknowledge that cross-dataset generalization remains weak—a key barrier for learning-based SfM to become broadly deployable. Whether the panoramic-specific pre-training can be adapted to pinhole or fisheye cameras, and whether lighter backbones can match the accuracy, remain open questions.
