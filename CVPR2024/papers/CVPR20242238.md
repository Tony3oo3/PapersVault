## Problem

Existing federated unsupervised learning (FUSL) methods with non-IID data suffer from two coupled issues: representation collapse in local models that entangles and degrades global and other local models, and inconsistent representation spaces across clients due to the absence of supervision signals. Prior approaches either rely on large batch sizes and Frobenius-norm regularization that deactivates neurons, or aggregate models with fixed sample-ratio weights that fail to reconcile conflicting local optima.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes unlabeled RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100) partitioned across K clients with non-IID class distributions parameterized by a Dirichlet concentration α. Each client applies two random augmentations to produce paired views, which are encoded by a shared ResNet-18 backbone into 512-dimensional l2-normalized feature vectors. The output is a global self-supervised encoder producing unified representations, evaluated via KNN accuracy, linear probing, and fine-tuning with 1%/10% labeled data; the method is agnostic to the backbone self-supervised architecture (BYOL, SimCLR, or SimSiam).

## Method

FedU² introduces two orthogonal modules. The Flexible Uniform Regularizer (FUR) at each client minimizes an unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) divergence between local data representations and samples drawn from a spherical Gaussian distribution, using soft marginal constraints to flexibly disperse representations without strict one-to-one coupling—this prevents representation collapse while preserving class separability and avoiding privacy leakage. The Efficient Unified Aggregator (EUA) at the server formulates model aggregation as a multi-objective optimization that maximizes the worst-case model deviation change rate across clients, solved exactly via ADMM on the dual formulation to obtain balanced aggregation weights. This ensures all clients' models update in a consistent parameter space, yielding unified representations for the same class across different clients. The overall training alternates local self-supervised optimization (with FUR) and server-side EUA aggregation over T communication rounds, with convergence guarantees under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 Cross-Silo (K=10, α=0.1), FedU²-BYOL achieves 85.58% linear probing accuracy, 78.64% FT-1%, and 86.24% FT-10%, outperforming the strongest baseline FedBYOL (83.29/74.04/81.40) by 2.3/4.6/4.8 points. On the more challenging CIFAR-100 Cross-Device (K=100, α=0.1) setting, FedU²-BYOL reaches 38.09% LP accuracy versus 28.27% for FedBYOL and 17.91% for Orchestra. Across all four dataset-setting combinations, FedU² consistently achieves the highest scores among all compared FUSL methods, with the largest gains observed under severe non-IID conditions (α=0.1) and cross-device settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedU²-BYOL outperforms all BYOL-based baselines across every setting: it beats FedBYOL by 2.3–9.8 LP points, FedU-BYOL by 3.3–10.0 points, FedEMA-BYOL by 3.4–9.7 points, and Orchestra by 29.0–8.4 points (largest gap on CIFAR-100 Cross-Device where Orchestra collapses to 17.91% LP). In the SimCLR group, FedU²-SimCLR surpasses L-DAWA-SimCLR on CIFAR-10 Cross-Silo (82.37 vs 75.48 LP) and CIFAR-100 Cross-Silo (56.56 vs 51.11 LP), while on CIFAR-100 Cross-Device the gap narrows to 0.28 LP points (41.56 vs 41.28), indicating L-DAWA's layer-wise divergence scaling is competitive under extreme heterogeneity. FedU²-SimCLR also dominates FedX-SimCLR by 4.1–4.9 LP points across all settings. In the SimSiam group, FedU²-Simsiam improves over FedDecorr-Simsiam by 25.3 LP points on CIFAR-10 Cross-Device (68.50 vs 43.18), confirming that Frobenius-norm decorrelation is unsuitable for FUSL with limited non-IID data. Overall, FedU² achieves the best score in 11 out of 12 evaluation columns for BYOL and 11 out of 12 for SimCLR, with the sole near-tie being CIFAR-100 Cross-Device LP where L-DAWA is within 0.3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends federated unsupervised learning (FUSL) by addressing two underexplored challenges: representation collapse entanglement across clients and inconsistent representation spaces due to non-IID data. For collapse mitigation, it departs from FedDecorr's Frobenius-norm decorrelation (which requires large batches and deactivates neurons) by introducing unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) to match local representations to a shared spherical Gaussian prior, borrowing the transport framework from Benamou and Séjourné. For aggregation, it moves beyond FedAvg's sample-ratio weighting and L-DAWA's layer-wise angular scaling by formulating server-side aggregation as a multi-objective optimization over model deviation change rates, solved via ADMM, drawing on the multi-objective FL formulation of Hu et al. The framework is explicitly agnostic to the self-supervised backbone (BYOL, SimCLR, SimSiam), positioning it as a plug-in regularizer and aggregator rather than a new self-supervised objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap in FUSL: existing methods either ignore representation collapse in non-IID clients or handle it in ways that leak privacy or require large batches. The UOT-based uniform regularizer is a clean solution that avoids both pitfalls, and the multi-objective aggregator provides a principled alternative to heuristic weighting. However, validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale data, deeper architectures, or real-world federated deployments with heterogeneous hardware. A key open question is whether the UOT computation cost (solving a quadratic program per batch) remains tractable at larger embedding dimensions or batch sizes beyond 128.
