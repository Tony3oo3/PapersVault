## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods suffer from two inherent limitations: single-pass vector quantization introduces irreducible approximation errors that cap reconstruction fidelity, and unidirectional autoregressive decoding constrains the model to local context while accumulating errors over the sequence. Discrete diffusion alternatives address bidirectionality but require hundreds of iterative denoising steps, making them computationally expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240046/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text description as input, encoded via a pretrained CLIP text encoder into a fixed-length feature vector. The output is a 3D human pose sequence of variable length N with D-dimensional per-frame pose features (following the T2M representation), decoded from a hierarchical stack of V+1 discrete token sequences (each of length n = N/4 after 4× downsampling) through the RVQ-VAE decoder. Generation completes in approximately 15 forward passes (10 for the base layer plus 5 for residual layers) regardless of motion length.

## Method

MoMask combines three components: (1) a Residual VQ-VAE that hierarchically quantizes motion latents into V+1 ordered token layers via iterative residual quantization, with a quantization dropout strategy that randomly disables trailing layers during training to force each layer to be independently informative; (2) a Masked Transformer (M-Transformer) based on BERT that predicts randomly masked base-layer tokens conditioned on text, using a cosine-scheduled mask ratio (borrowed from MaskGIT) so that during inference it iteratively un-masks tokens over L=10 steps, retaining only high-confidence predictions each round; and (3) a Residual Transformer (R-Transformer) that progressively predicts each subsequent residual layer's tokens given all preceding layers, sharing embedding parameters across adjacent layers. Both transformers employ classifier-free guidance (10% unconditional training) to sharpen text-motion alignment at inference.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MoMask achieves a FID of 0.045 (vs. 0.141 for T2M-GPT and 0.103 for ReMoDiffuse), R-Precision Top-1 of 0.521, and multimodal distance of 2.958, setting new state-of-the-art on all three primary metrics. On KIT-ML, MoMask reaches FID 0.204, R-Precision Top-1 0.433, and MM-Dist 2.779, trailing only ReMoDiffuse (FID 0.155) on FID while leading on R-Precision and MM-Dist. Even the base-layer-only variant (MoMask base) already outperforms most baselines (FID 0.082 on HumanML3D), and the full model with residual layers further narrows the gap to near-perfect reconstruction (reconstruction FID 0.019). Inference requires only ~10 iterations regardless of motion length, positioning MoMask favorably on the quality–efficiency trade-off compared to diffusion and autoregressive baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMask decisively outperforms all autoregressive VQ baselines (TM2T, T2M-GPT, MotionGPT) on both datasets, reducing FID from 1.501/0.141 (TM2T/T2M-GPT) to 0.045 on HumanML3D and from 3.599/0.514 to 0.204 on KIT-ML. Against diffusion-based methods (MDM, MLD, MotionDiffuse), MoMask achieves substantially lower FID (0.045 vs. 0.473–0.630 on HumanML3D) while also improving R-Precision and MM-Dist, indicating better semantic alignment. The closest competitor is ReMoDiffuse, which achieves a slightly lower FID on KIT-ML (0.155 vs. 0.204) but relies on retrieval from a large external database; MoMask leads ReMoDiffuse on R-Precision (0.433 vs. 0.427) and MM-Dist (2.779 vs. 2.814) on KIT-ML, and clearly on HumanML3D (FID 0.045 vs. 0.103). The base-layer-only variant already surpasses T2M-GPT and MLD on FID, confirming that the residual quantization hierarchy provides meaningful additional fidelity. In a user study, MoMask is preferred over MDM, MLD, and T2M-GPT in the majority of side-by-side comparisons, and 68% of inpainting results are preferred over MDM.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoMask combines two previously separate research threads: generative masked modeling (MaskGIT, Muse, MagVIT) and hierarchical residual vector quantization (SoundStream, AudioLM), applying both to the text-to-motion generation problem. It departs from prior discrete motion generators (TM2T, T2M-GPT, MotionGPT) that rely on single-pass VQ and unidirectional autoregressive decoding, replacing them with multi-layer RVQ tokens and a bidirectional masked transformer that fills tokens in ~15 iterations. The residual transformer for progressive layer-by-layer token prediction is a new architectural component not present in prior motion or image generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoMask addresses two fundamental limitations of prior discrete motion generation—quantization error and unidirectional decoding—yielding a 3× FID improvement over T2M-GPT on HumanML3D while requiring only 15 inference iterations versus hundreds for discrete diffusion. The seamless applicability to temporal inpainting without fine-tuning suggests the masked-modeling formulation is more flexible than autoregressive alternatives. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer or multi-person motions, whether the modest multimodality scores (lower than MDM) reflect a genuine trade-off with fidelity, and whether the 5-layer RVQ sweet spot generalizes across datasets and motion complexity.
