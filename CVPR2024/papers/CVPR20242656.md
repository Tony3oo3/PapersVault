## Problem

DETR-based semi-supervised object detection suffers from three interrelated issues: one-to-one assignment produces inaccurate pseudo-labels while one-to-many assignment generates overlapping predictions, the lack of multi-scale feature fusion degrades detection of small and occluded objects, and there is no effective mechanism to filter low-quality pseudo-labels produced during one-to-many matching. These limitations collectively reduce training efficiency and detection accuracy in challenging scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242656/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in a teacher-student semi-supervised setup: the teacher network receives weakly augmented unlabeled images to generate pseudo-labels, while the student network receives strongly augmented unlabeled images alongside labeled data. The backbone (ResNet-50) extracts multi-scale features at two resolutions (W₁ and W₂), and 900 learnable object queries are fed to the DETR decoder. Output is a set of bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores, produced end-to-end without NMS in the final stage.

## Method

Sparse Semi-DETR extends Semi-DETR with two modules. The Query Refinement Module fuses high-resolution features (F_t1) and low-resolution features (F_t2) from weakly augmented images via an attention mechanism: F_t2 is channel-reduced from 256 to 16, attentional weights are computed and normalized, and the resulting representation is combined with F_t1 through cosine similarity and concatenation to produce refined queries that are merged with original queries as decoder inputs. The Reliable Pseudo-Label Filtering Module employs m=6 groups of augmented ground truths for one-to-many assignment, selects top-k=4 predictions per pseudo-box, and uses remaining predictions to filter duplicates, with a confidence threshold σ=0.4. Training follows a stage-wise schedule: the first half of iterations uses one-to-many matching (with the filtering module active), and the second half switches to one-to-one matching for NMS-free inference. A DN-DETR-style attention mask prevents information leakage between refined and original query streams.

## Results

On COCO-Partial with 10% labeled data, Sparse Semi-DETR achieves 44.3 mAP, surpassing Semi-DETR (43.5) by 0.8 mAP and Omni-DETR (41.3) by 3.0 mAP. On COCO-Full (100% labeled + unlabeled), it reaches 51.3 mAP, a 2.1 mAP gain over the supervised DINO baseline (49.2) and 0.9 mAP over Semi-DETR (50.4). On Pascal VOC, it achieves 65.51 AP₅₀:₉₅ and 86.30 AP₅₀, outperforming the supervised DINO baseline by 5.91 and 5.1 points respectively. For small objects specifically (AP_S), Sparse Semi-DETR gains 1.7 mAP over Semi-DETR at 10% labels (26.9 vs 25.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sparse Semi-DETR consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across all three evaluation settings. Against the closest DETR-based competitor Semi-DETR, it gains 0.4/0.7/0.8 mAP at 1%/5%/10% labels on COCO-Partial and 0.9 mAP on COCO-Full (51.3 vs 50.4). The margin over Omni-DETR is larger at 3.0–3.3 mAP on COCO-Partial, reflecting the advantage of the one-to-many assignment with the proposed filtering over Omni-DETR's one-to-one strategy. Against one-stage methods, the gap is substantial: +8.5 mAP over Dense Teacher and +8.5 mAP over PseCo at 1% labels, narrowing to +7.2/+8.2 mAP at 10%. On Pascal VOC, the improvement over Semi-DETR is modest (+0.31 AP₅₀:₉₅, +0.20 AP₅₀), while the gain over the supervised DINO baseline is +5.91 AP₅₀:₉₅. The most notable advantage is on small objects (AP_S), where Sparse Semi-DETR leads Semi-DETR by 1.2–1.7 mAP, validating the Query Refinement Module's design goal.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sparse Semi-DETR extends the DETR-based semi-supervised object detection lineage (DETR → Omni-DETR → Semi-DETR) by adding two targeted modules to Semi-DETR's architecture: a Query Refinement Module that fuses high- and low-resolution features via an attention mechanism, and a Reliable Pseudo-Label Filtering Module inspired by Hybrid-DETR's hybrid matching. It also inherits DN-DETR's attention mask for information-leakage protection. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior DETR-based SSOD work by explicitly addressing the tension between one-to-many assignment (which improves recall but causes overlapping predictions) and one-to-one assignment (which is NMS-free but produces inaccurate pseudo-labels), proposing query-level refinement as a middle path rather than relying solely on assignment-strategy switching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and partially resolves a concrete weakness in DETR-based SSOD—poor small-object detection due to the absence of multi-scale feature integration—that has persisted since the field's inception. However, the gains are modest (0.8 mAP over Semi-DETR on COCO 10%), and the authors acknowledge that full one-to-many training still requires NMS to reach peak performance, leaving the end-to-end NMS-free promise partially unfulfilled. Open questions include whether the query refinement generalizes beyond the DINO+ResNet-50 backbone, whether the filtering parameters (m=6, k=4, σ=0.4) transfer across datasets, and whether the approach can be extended to other DETR variants (e.g., DAB-DETR, Co-DETR) without re-derivation.
