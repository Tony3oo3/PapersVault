## Problem

Improving zero-shot reasoning in generative vision-language models typically requires second-stage instruction tuning with human-labeled or LLM-generated task-specific data, incurring high annotation costs. This paper asks whether VLMs' zero-shot image-to-text generation can be enhanced using only unlabeled image-text pairs and lightweight NLP tools, without any downstream task annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240465/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224) and a text prompt (e.g., a VQA question or captioning instruction) and autoregressively generates text. During training, the input is an image paired with a correction instruction prompt and a perturbed caption; the expected output is the corrected caption with mismatched concepts restored. Training data is constructed from ~1M unlabeled COCO and Visual Genome image-text pairs, requiring no human labels or external LLM generation.

## Method

The paper introduces Image-Conditioned Caption Correction (ICCC), a second-stage tuning task where the VLM must identify and fix concept mismatches between an image and a perturbed caption. A Concept Extractor uses spaCy's universal dependency parser to decompose captions into five concept types (entity phrase, predicate phrase, attribute phrase, noun word, verb word), and a Correction Task Constructor perturbs captions via two operations: *replace* (substituting a concept with another of the same type from a global concept base) and *swap* (exchanging positions of two same-type concepts), selected by a Bernoulli parameter p_s. Training fine-tunes only the Q-Former and projection layer (freezing the ViT-G/14 encoder and LLM) with a standard language modeling objective, mixing ICCC samples with original image-text pairs at ratio p_c to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The key insight is that enforcing correction of structured linguistic mismatches grounded in visual evidence strengthens the model's ability to align visual concepts with language at multiple granularities, improving zero-shot generalization across VQA and captioning without any task-specific annotation.

## Results

On BLIP-2 OPT-2.7B, ICCC yields a +5.4 point gain on GQA (33.5→38.9), +2.9 on OK-VQA (26.6→29.5), and +6.2 on NoCaps CIDEr (105.7→111.9). On BLIP-2 OPT-6.7B, the largest improvement is +6.2 on VQAv2 (52.6→58.8) and +3.0 on VSR (48.5→51.5). On InstructBLIP (Vicuna-7B), ICCC improves GQA by +1.4 (48.4→49.8), VSR by +2.0 (61.1→63.1), and NoCaps SPICE by +1.5 (14.2→15.7), all without any human-labeled or LLM-generated instruction data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external competing methods; instead, the baseline is each model's own pre-trained checkpoint without ICCC. ICCC consistently outperforms the base model across all three BLIP-2 LLM backbones (OPT-2.7B, OPT-6.7B, FlanT5-XL) and InstructBLIP, with the largest gains on smaller models (e.g., +5.4 on GQA for OPT-2.7B) and on captioning CIDEr scores (+5.5 to +6.2). The only exception is a marginal −0.5 drop on VSR for BLIP-2 OPT-2.7B, while all other metrics improve. Ablation (Table 3) further shows that ICCC outperforms standard second-stage caption fine-tuning (COCO+VG only) by +5.4 on GQA and +3.2 on NoCaps CIDEr, and also surpasses a VQAv2-instruction-tuning baseline on GQA (38.9 vs. 41.3) and OK-VQA (29.5 vs. 38.0), confirming that the correction task is more effective than conventional instruction tuning for zero-shot generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the second-stage instruction tuning paradigm established by InstructBLIP and LLaVA, which rely on human-labeled or LLM-generated task-specific data, by replacing that data with automatically constructed caption-correction samples derived from unlabeled image-text pairs. Conceptually, it draws on the language-structure-based data augmentation line (NegCLIP, SVLC) that uses dependency parsers to create hard negatives for contrastive image-text matching, but repurposes this idea for generative image-text generation (ITG) rather than similarity learning. The base architectures (BLIP-2's Q-Former, InstructBLIP's instruction-aware visual feature extractor) remain unchanged; the contribution is entirely in the training task and data construction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is in reducing the annotation bottleneck for second-stage VLM tuning: a lightweight spaCy parser on ~1M unlabeled captions suffices, with no LLM calls or human labels, yet yields consistent zero-shot gains across VQA and captioning benchmarks. The ablation showing that random word masking with correction instructions fails to improve performance (while structured dependency-based perturbation succeeds) provides evidence that linguistic structure, not merely the correction framing, drives the benefit. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets and models (the authors acknowledge compute limitations), whether it generalizes beyond Q-Former-style architectures, and whether the gains compound with other instruction-tuning strategies.
