## Problem

Existing implicit neural representation methods for sparse-view CBCT reconstruction (e.g., DIF-Net) query only local pixel-aligned features from projections and treat all views equally, which fails on complicated anatomies like the chest where low-contrast organs are hard to distinguish locally and different views provide complementary information about specific structures (e.g., the patella is visible in the right-left view but overlaps the femur in the anterior-posterior view).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N (6–10) sparse-view 2D CBCT projections of 256×256 pixels, simulated via digitally reconstructed radiographs (DRRs) with viewing angles uniformly sampled over a 180° half-rotation, along with known scanning parameters (viewing angles, source-to-origin distance). It outputs a 3D CT volume at 256³ resolution where each voxel's attenuation coefficient is estimated by evaluating the implicit function at the voxel centroid. During training, the CT volume is resampled to isotropic spacing (1.6 mm for chest, 0.8 mm for knee) and cropped/padded to 256³; the loss is computed over 10,000 randomly sampled 3D points per iteration.

## Method

C²RV extends DIF-Net's implicit attenuation-field formulation with two key components. First, multi-scale 3D volumetric representations (MS-3DV) back-project encoder feature maps at three scales (resolutions 16, 8, 4) from all views into 3D voxelized spaces, then apply 3D residual convolutions to enable cross-regional feature learning that captures global anatomical context. Second, a scale-view cross-attention (SVC-Att) module adaptively aggregates features: a self-attention step performs cross-view attention on the N pixel-aligned features queried from projections, followed by a cross-attention step where the multi-scale voxel-aligned features serve as the reference and the self-attended multi-view features serve as the source. Three SVC-Att modules (8-head multi-head attention) are stacked and followed by a linear layer to predict the attenuation coefficient. The network is trained end-to-end with MSE loss using SGD (momentum 0.98, lr 0.01, 400 epochs, batch size 4) on a shared U-Net encoder-decoder (C=128 channels, encoder output at W/16 × H/16).

## Results

On the LUNA16 chest CT dataset, C²RV achieves 29.23 dB PSNR / 92.78 SSIM×10⁻² with 6 views, 29.95/93.49 with 8 views, and 30.70/94.03 with 10 views at 256³ resolution. On the knee CBCT dataset, it reaches 29.73/93.64 (6-view), 30.68/94.42 (8-view), and 31.55/95.01 (10-view). These represent improvements of 3.6–3.5 dB PSNR and 7.9–8.4 SSIM×10⁻² over the strongest prior method (DIF-Net) on chest CT, and 2.2–2.6 dB / 3.0–4.5 SSIM×10⁻² on knee CT. Notably, C²RV with only 6 views outperforms all other methods even with 10 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²RV outperforms all nine baselines across every view count and both datasets. The closest competitor is DIF-Net [18], the direct predecessor: C²RV gains 3.68 dB PSNR and 8.38 SSIM×10⁻² on 6-view chest CT (29.23 vs. 25.55) and 2.61 dB / 4.52 SSIM×10⁻² on 6-view knee CT (29.73 vs. 27.12). Among self-supervised methods, NeRP [31] is the strongest (23.55/74.46 on chest 6-view), still trailing C²RV by 5.68 dB. Among data-driven denoising methods, FreeSeed [20] leads (25.59/77.36 on chest 6-view), a gap of 3.64 dB. PixelNeRF [43], adapted from novel-view synthesis, achieves 24.66/78.68 on chest 6-view, 4.57 dB below C²RV. The traditional iterative methods FDK [4] and SART [1] are far behind (15.34 and 19.70 dB respectively on chest 6-view), confirming that learning-based approaches are necessary for extreme sparse-view settings. A key qualitative finding is that DIF-Net degrades substantially on the more complex chest anatomy while C²RV maintains consistent gains, attributed to its cross-regional (MS-3DV) and cross-view (SVC-Att) feature learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DIF-Net [18], the authors' own MICCAI 2023 work that first formulated sparse-view CBCT reconstruction as a data-driven implicit neural representation problem. It departs from DIF-Net by addressing two identified limitations: (1) only local pixel-aligned features are queried per point, making it hard to distinguish low-contrast organs, and (2) all views are aggregated equally despite varying informativeness per anatomy. The proposed C²RV introduces multi-scale 3D volumetric representations (MS-3DV) for cross-regional learning and scale-view cross-attention (SVC-Att) for adaptive multi-scale/multi-view aggregation, positioning itself between self-supervised NeRF-based methods (NAF, NeRP, which need 20–50 views) and 2D slice-wise denoising approaches (FBPConvNet, FreeSeed, which lack 3D consistency).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for interventional radiology: C²RV reconstructs CT from as few as 6 views with quality exceeding what other methods achieve from 10 views, directly reducing patient radiation exposure. The downstream lung segmentation validation (Dice 96.72/96.93) demonstrates that the reconstructed volumes are clinically usable, not just visually plausible. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is dataset-specificity—the model fails on unseen anatomies (e.g., chest-trained model on head CT), leaving few-shot or zero-shot cross-anatomy adaptation as an important open problem. The cross-regional 3D volumetric feature idea and the scale-view attention mechanism could potentially transfer to other sparse 3D reconstruction tasks beyond CBCT.
