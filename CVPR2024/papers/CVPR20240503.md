## Problem

Conventional visible-infrared fusion (VIF) systems rely on frame-based cameras with limited dynamic range (~60 dB) and frame rates, producing over/underexposed or motion-blurred inputs in extreme lighting and high-dynamic scenes, which degrades fusion quality. This paper introduces an event camera as a visible sensor to overcome these hardware limitations, and addresses the open question of how to effectively harmonize asynchronous event signals with infrared frames for high-quality fusion.

## Input / Output

The system takes a continuous event stream (represented as a 10-channel voxel grid) over a time window and a set of M motion-blurred infrared frames as input. For each infrared frame, the corresponding event segment within its exposure window is divided into K temporal sub-segments for the reconstruction network, while the full segment feeds the deblurring network. The output is a sharp, fused visible-infrared image per infrared frame, combining blur-free visible textures with deblurred thermal information.

## Method

EVIF employs a multi-task collaborative framework with three networks (E2VID for event-to-visible reconstruction, EFNet for event-guided infrared deblurring, and a fusion network) trained progressively in three stages. A cross-task event enhancement module uses bidirectional ConvGRUs to extract spatial-temporal texture features from the reconstruction task, then merges them into the deblurring task's event features via a Cross-Task Channel Attention (CTCA) mechanism that computes queries from deblurring features and keys/values from reconstruction features to preserve motion cues while compensating appearance loss. The fusion stage applies a bi-level min-max mutual information optimization: transformer blocks act as learnable information filters, MI between filtered modality features is minimized (via KL-divergence-based estimation) to reduce redundancy, while MI between the fused feature and original modality features is maximized (via InfoNCE contrastive loss) to prevent information loss. This min-max scheme balances feature distinctiveness and completeness in the decoded feature space.

## Results

On the KAIST synthetic dataset, EVIF achieves the best scores on three of six standard VIF metrics: Entropy (EN=7.326), Mutual Information (MI=1.978), and SSIM (1.285). The information-theory-based metrics (EN and MI) are highlighted as evidence that the bi-level mutual information optimization mechanism effectively preserves complementary information from both modalities. On the deblurring sub-task, EVIF with cross-task event enhancement reaches 33.94 PSNR, surpassing the frame-based NAFNet baseline (31.23 PSNR) and the EVIF variant without enhancement (33.32 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVIF wins on three of six metrics (EN, MI, SSIM) against all nine frame-based VIF baselines, with the MI advantage (1.978 vs. CDDFuse's 1.939) confirming the effectiveness of the bi-level mutual information optimization. However, EVIF trails on CE (1.986 vs. DIDFuse's 1.462 and U2Fusion's 1.678) and AG (2.120 vs. GFCE's 3.645 and HMSD_GF's 3.069 with NAFNet deblurring), indicating that gradient-rich fusion methods still produce sharper edges. On QCB, LRRNet with NAFNet deblurring (0.382) narrowly edges out EVIF (0.377), while EVIF leads all other baselines. The deblurring ablation (Table 2) shows that event-based deblurring with cross-task enhancement (33.94 PSNR) substantially outperforms frame-based NAFNet (31.23 PSNR), validating the core premise that event cameras provide superior motion cues for infrared restoration. Qualitative results on real captured data further demonstrate that EVIF maintains clarity under extreme lighting and high-speed motion where all frame-based baselines degrade severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established VIF task by replacing the conventional frame-based visible camera with an event camera, positioning itself as the first work to leverage events for visible-infrared fusion under extreme lighting and high-dynamic motion. It builds directly on E2VID for event-to-visible texture reconstruction and EFNet for event-guided deblurring, but departs from treating these as independent pipelines by introducing a multi-task collaborative framework with cross-task channel attention and bi-level min-max mutual information optimization. The fusion mechanism draws on transformer-based feature processing and InfoNCE-style contrastive objectives, but the specific bi-level MI min-max formulation for balancing modality distinctiveness against information completeness is a new design choice not seen in prior VIF or multi-task learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical gap: conventional VIF systems degrade under over/under-exposure and motion blur, which are common in real-world driving and surveillance scenarios. By demonstrating that event cameras can mitigate both issues simultaneously, the work opens a path toward more robust multimodal perception systems. However, the evidence is somewhat limited—synthetic events from ESIM on a single driving dataset (KAIST), qualitative-only real-data comparisons, and a modest PSNR gain from the cross-task enhancement (33.32→33.94)—leaving open questions about generalization to non-driving scenes, the cost-benefit of adding an event camera to existing VIF hardware, and whether the multi-task collaboration framework transfers to other event-based fusion tasks.
