## Problem

Most text-driven motion generation methods ignore the 3D scene context, yet scene geometry and physical constraints are essential for realistic human-scene interactions. The specific challenge is that text descriptions in this setting contain spatial references (e.g., 'sit on the chair near the desk') that require the model to both locate the correct target object among many instances and generate motions coherent with that object's geometry — a combination of spatial reasoning and conditional generation that prior work like HUMANISE handles only implicitly through auxiliary regression losses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene point cloud (N points with 3D position and surface normal, S ∈ R^{N×6}) and a text instruction following the Sr3D template specifying an action (walk, sit, stand up, lie), a target object, and one or more anchor objects linked by spatial relations (proximity, between, support). The output is a full SMPL-X motion sequence comprising global translation, 6D global orientation, and 6D joint pose parameters for all joints; body shape β is a fixed condition, and the starting position and pose are also generated by the model rather than specified.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage decomposition: (1) language grounding via ChatGPT, where a spatial scene graph is built from 3D object detections and fed to ChatGPT through a two-prompt strategy (first identifying relevant object categories, then selecting the specific target instance from simplified edge sentences), and (2) object-centric diffusion-based motion generation using volumetric sensors (a 4×4×4 m³ environment sensor, a bounding-box-aligned target sensor, and per-frame trajectory sensors) that replace raw point clouds with compact, scale-normalized geometry. The motion generation itself is hierarchical: a first diffusion model (transformer decoder) produces the character trajectory conditioned on text (CLIP-encoded), environment sensor, and target sensor; a second diffusion model then completes local poses and global orientation conditioned on the trajectory sensors. Both models are pre-trained on the full AMASS dataset (with zeroed text features) and fine-tuned on HUMANISE, and the entire pipeline generalizes to unseen PROX scenes without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the HUMANISE test set (3.1k sequences, 100 scenes), the proposed method achieves a goal distance of 0.384 m, substantially outperforming HUMANISE (0.995 m), GMD* (1.229 m), and MDM* (2.048 m). It also attains the best action-recognition accuracy (97.1%), the lowest FID (0.12), and the highest human-perceptual scene score (3.54/5) and quality score (3.50/5). On diversity (4.78 vs. real 4.82) and multimodality (2.31 vs. real 2.28), the method is competitive with GMD* but slightly behind, while HUMANISE lags considerably on both (4.25 and 2.66).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms HUMANISE [83] on every metric: goal distance is 2.6× lower (0.384 vs. 0.995 m), accuracy is 7.4 points higher (97.1% vs. 89.7%), and FID is 9× lower (0.12 vs. 1.11), confirming that explicit LLM-based grounding plus object-centric representation is far more effective than HUMANISE's implicit auxiliary-task grounding with a full-scene point transformer. Against GMD* [38] and its HUMANISE-center-guided variant GMD^HC, the proposed method wins on goal distance (0.384 vs. 1.229/1.130 m), scene score (3.54 vs. 2.50/2.49), accuracy (97.1% vs. 96.6%/96.4%), FID (0.12 vs. 0.24/0.23), and quality score (3.50 vs. 2.64/2.76), while GMD* edges ahead on diversity (4.91 vs. 4.78) and multimodality (2.28 vs. 2.31) — both within the range of the real data. MDM* [75] is the weakest baseline, trailing the proposed method by over 5× in goal distance and 1.3× in FID. For the object-localization sub-task (Table 3), the ChatGPT-based two-stage prompting achieves 75.6% IoU accuracy and 0.61 m center distance under predicted detection, surpassing BUTD-DETR [34] (62.5% / 1.33 m) and HUMANISE's regression (– / 1.48 m); Mistral-7B is a close open-source alternative (72.4% / 0.74 m), while LLaMA-2-7B underperforms (40.3% / 1.55 m).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of text-driven motion generation (MDM, GMD, TEMOS) and scene-aware motion synthesis (NSM, hierarchical HGI), but departs most directly from HUMANISE, the only prior work combining both. Where HUMANISE uses a monolithic cVAE with a point-transformer scene encoder and implicit object-center regression, this paper decomposes the problem into an explicit LLM-based 3D grounding stage (converting scene graphs to text and querying ChatGPT) followed by an object-centric diffusion-based generation stage using volumetric sensors. The volumetric sensor representation inherits from NSM and ManipNet, while the hierarchical trajectory-then-motion structure follows the Pi et al. (ICCV 2023) framework, and the text encoding uses CLIP as in MDM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical two-stage pipeline (LLM grounding + object-centric diffusion) offers a modular, interpretable alternative to end-to-end scene-motion models, and the demonstrated zero-shot generalization to the PROX dataset suggests the approach can transfer across scene distributions without retraining. However, the method is constrained by short motion durations (60% are 1–3 s), template-based text descriptions, and static scenes only; extending to dynamic objects, longer sequences, and free-form language remains open. The reliance on an external LLM at inference also introduces latency and fragility (detector misses, prompt sensitivity), which the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
