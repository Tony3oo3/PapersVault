## Problem

Adversarial training has been almost exclusively evaluated on balanced datasets, yet real-world data typically follows long-tailed distributions, leaving its practical efficacy unverified. The only prior work addressing this gap, RoBal, relies on a complex multi-component design (cosine classifier, balanced softmax loss, class-aware margin, TRADES regularization) that incurs substantial training time and GPU memory. Additionally, it remains unclear whether data augmentation—which on balanced data only mitigates robust overfitting without improving robustness—behaves differently under long-tailed distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10-LT or CIFAR-100-LT with an imbalance ratio (IR) of 10–100, where head classes contain up to 50× more samples than tail classes. During training, adversarial examples are generated via PGD-10 (10 steps, step size 2/255, ε=8/255) on the augmented inputs. The model outputs a 10- or 100-way class label, and robustness is evaluated under FGSM, PGD-20, CW, LSA, and AutoAttack at the same ε=8/255 perturbation budget.

## Method

The paper proposes AT-BSL, which pairs standard adversarial training (AT) with Balanced Softmax Loss (BSL) that adds a class-count-dependent bias b_i = τ_b·log(n_i) to the logits, eliminating the need for RoBal's cosine classifier, class-aware margin, and TRADES regularization while matching its robustness at roughly half the training time and memory. The key empirical finding is that adding data augmentation (MixUp, CutMix, RandAugment, AutoAugment, TrivialAugment, AugMix, or Cutout) to AT-BSL not only suppresses robust overfitting but also yields a substantial robustness gain (up to +6.66% under AutoAttack on CIFAR-10-LT with WideResNet-34-10), a behavior absent on balanced data. Ablation studies attribute this gain to increased training-data diversity: using a single augmentation type fails to improve robustness, while progressively adding more augmentation types to the pool monotonically increases robustness, confirming that the diversity of augmented examples—rather than any specific transformation—drives the improvement.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (IR=50) with WideResNet-34-10, AT-BSL-AuA achieves 75.17% clean accuracy and 37.15% robustness under AutoAttack (AA), a +6.66% improvement over the prior SOTA RoBal (30.49% AA). With ResNet-18, AT-BSL-RA reaches 70.86% clean accuracy and 34.24% AA robustness, surpassing RoBal (29.54% AA) by +4.70%. At the final checkpoint (after 100 epochs), AT-BSL-AuA retains 35.11% AA robustness versus RoBal's 25.71%, demonstrating that data augmentation effectively mitigates robust overfitting under long-tailed distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 8 baselines: 6 balanced-distribution adversarial training methods (AT, TRADES, MART, AWP, GAIRAT, LAS-AT) and 2 long-tailed-specific methods (RoBal, REAT). AT-BSL-AuA decisively outperforms all baselines on every attack metric. Against the closest long-tailed competitor RoBal, the improvement is +6.66% on AA (37.15 vs 30.49) and +4.42% on PGD (40.84 vs 36.42) with WideResNet-34-10. Against the concurrent REAT, the gap is +3.95% on AA (37.15 vs 33.20). Even without augmentation, AT-BSL alone surpasses RoBal on AA (32.80 vs 30.49) while using roughly half the training time and memory (21s vs 39s per epoch, 946 vs 1722 MiB GPU). The balanced-distribution baselines (AT, TRADES, MART, AWP, GAIRAT, LAS-AT) all fall 3–12% below the long-tailed methods on AA, confirming that class-imbalance handling (via BSL) is essential for practical robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adversarial training framework of Madry et al. (AT) and the Balanced Softmax Loss from Ren et al., combining them into a streamlined AT-BSL method that replaces the more complex RoBal (Wu et al., CVPR 2021) — the only prior published work on adversarial training under long-tailed distributions. It departs from the balanced-data adversarial training literature (Rebuffi et al., Rice et al., Wu et al.), where data augmentation was shown to mitigate robust overfitting but not improve robustness, by demonstrating the opposite under long-tailed settings. The paper also identifies a critical discrepancy between RoBal's published learning rate schedule and its code implementation, which masked the robust overfitting phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that data augmentation improves adversarial robustness under long-tailed distributions — in direct contrast to balanced-data results — opens a new line of inquiry into how class imbalance interacts with regularization and generalization. The practical simplification from RoBal to AT-BSL (removing cosine classifier, class-aware margin, and TRADES regularization) significantly reduces training overhead without sacrificing performance, making long-tailed adversarial training more accessible. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CIFAR-10-LT and CIFAR-100-LT with CNN architectures; whether the augmentation-robustness link extends to larger datasets (ImageNet-LT), transformer backbones, or other modalities remains open. The mechanistic explanation (increased training diversity) is supported by ablation but not yet formalized theoretically.
