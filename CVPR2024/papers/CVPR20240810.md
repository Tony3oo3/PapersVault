## Problem

Laplace-Beltrami operator eigenfunctions, the standard basis for functional maps, are robust to isometric deformations but fail to capture high-frequency extrinsic details such as creases and bending. The recently proposed elastic thin-shell hessian basis aligns these details well but lacks isometric invariance and performs poorly in learned pipelines when used alone. No prior work provides a principled framework for combining non-orthogonal extrinsic basis functions with intrinsic LBO eigenfunctions within the functional map optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D meshes and per-vertex point descriptors (DiffusionNet features by default) as input. It produces a block-diagonal hybrid functional map matrix C = [[C¹¹, 0], [0, C²²]], where C¹¹ maps between LBO eigenfunction spaces and C²² maps between elastic eigenfunction spaces. Dense point-to-point correspondences are then extracted via nearest-neighbor search in the combined hybrid basis or through ZoomOut-style map refinement. The total spectral resolution k is split between the two bases (e.g., k_LB=140, k_Elas=60 for ULRSSM), with the highest-frequency LBO modes replaced by the lowest-eigenvalue elastic modes.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical generalization of the functional map least-squares problem from the Frobenius norm to the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, which properly accounts for the anisotropic mass-matrix metric on non-orthogonal basis spaces (Lemma 4.1 for the data term, Proposition 4.2 for the commutativity regularizer). This yields a closed-form k²×k² linear system solvable via Kronecker products. On top of this, the paper constructs a hybrid basis by concatenating LBO eigenfunctions (low-frequency, isometric-invariant) with elastic thin-shell hessian eigenfunctions (high-frequency, crease-sensitive), and enforces a block-diagonal structure on the functional map (C¹² = C²¹ = 0), decoupling the optimization into two independent sub-problems. For learned pipelines, a linear annealing schedule L(C) = L_LB(C) + μ·L_Elas(C) is introduced, favoring coarse isometric alignment early in training and crease-level detail alignment later, which outperforms fine-tuning from LBO-pretrained descriptors. The framework is demonstrated as a drop-in replacement in three pipelines: GeomFMaps (supervised), ULRSSM (unsupervised), and Smooth Shells (axiomatic).

## Results

The proposed hybrid functional maps achieve up to 45% reduction in mean geodesic error on TOPKIDS (9.2 → 5.0 with Hybrid ULRSSM) and up to 15% improvement on non-isometric settings (SMAL: 3.9 → 3.3). On SHREC'19, Hybrid GeomFMaps reduces geodesic error from 7.9 to 5.6 (×100). Across all six benchmarks (FAUST, SCAPE, SHREC'19, SMAL, DT4D-H, TOPKIDS), the hybrid variants consistently outperform their LBO-only counterparts in axiomatic, supervised, and unsupervised settings, with the largest gains in non-isometric and topologically noisy conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct LBO-based baselines it extends, the hybrid variants win on nearly all datasets: Hybrid ULRSSM beats ULRSSM [12] on all six benchmarks (e.g., TOPKIDS 5.0 vs 9.2, SMAL 3.3 vs 3.9, SHREC'19 3.6 vs 4.6); Hybrid GeomFMaps beats GeomFMaps [15] on all five reported datasets (SHREC'19 5.6 vs 7.9, SMAL 7.6 vs 8.4); Hybrid Smooth Shells beats Smooth Shells [18] on SMAL (28.4 vs 30.0), DT4D-H inter (5.7 vs 6.4), and TOPKIDS (7.5 vs 10.8), while tying on FAUST and SCAPE. Against other unsupervised methods, Hybrid ULRSSM outperforms AttentiveFMaps [26] (TOPKIDS 5.0 vs 23.4), SSCDFM [51] (SMAL 3.3 vs 5.4), DUO-FMNet [17] (DT4D-H inter 3.5 vs 15.8), and Deep Shells [19] (SHREC'19 3.6 vs 21.1) by large margins. In the axiomatic category, Hybrid Smooth Shells substantially outperforms ZoomOut [35] (TOPKIDS 7.5 vs 33.7) and DiscreteOp [43] (TOPKIDS 7.5 vs 35.5). In the supervised category, Hybrid GeomFMaps dominates FMNet [28] across all shared datasets (e.g., SCAPE 2.8 vs 33.0, SMAL 7.6 vs 42.0). The paper does not report any setting where a hybrid variant is outperformed by its corresponding LBO-only baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional maps framework (Ovsjanikov et al. 2012) by generalizing the descriptor-based linear system to non-orthogonal basis sets via a Hilbert-Schmidt norm formulation, building directly on Hartwig et al.'s (2023) elastic thin-shell hessian basis and their earlier non-orthogonal ZoomOut adaptation. The key conceptual departure is the hybrid block-diagonal functional map that jointly spans LBO and elastic eigenfunction spaces, treating them as complementary rather than competing bases. The approach is positioned as a drop-in replacement for the LBO basis in existing pipelines (GeomFMaps, ULRSSM, Smooth Shells), requiring only reformulation of data and regularization terms in the weighted HS-norm rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework for incorporating non-orthogonal basis functions into learned functional map pipelines is foundational and could enable future work with other extrinsic or learned bases beyond the elastic thin-shell hessian. The 45% geodesic error reduction on TOPKIDS (topological noise) and 15% on non-isometric settings demonstrate practical value, and the drop-in nature means existing practitioners can adopt the hybrid basis without retraining from scratch. Limitations include the computational cost of the expanded k²×k² system (tractable only for ~60 elastic basis functions) and the observation that the elastic basis underperforms when used alone in learned settings, suggesting the hybrid formulation is essential rather than optional. Open questions include extending the framework to partial shapes and noisy point clouds, where non-orthogonal bases could be particularly beneficial.
