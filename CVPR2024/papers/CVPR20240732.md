## Problem

Generating high-resolution images with diffusion models incurs prohibitive latency because computation scales super-linearly with spatial resolution, and existing multi-GPU strategies (tensor parallelism, data parallelism) fail to accelerate single-image generation. Tensor parallelism is impractical due to the large activation sizes in diffusion models, while naive patch splitting across GPUs produces visible seams from the absence of inter-patch interaction. The paper asks whether multiple GPUs can be exploited for single-image diffusion inference without sacrificing quality or incurring prohibitive communication costs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an off-the-shelf pre-trained diffusion model (primarily SDXL) as input, requiring no retraining or fine-tuning. The latent representation is spatially partitioned into N patches (one per GPU), and the model generates a single high-resolution image up to 3840×3840 pixels. The method is compatible with standard few-step samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver) and classifier-free guidance, where the guidance batch can be further split across devices for additional speedup.

## Method

DistriFusion introduces displaced patch parallelism, which splits the image into N patches assigned to N GPUs and exploits the high similarity between adjacent denoising steps to reuse stale activations from the previous timestep as global context for the current step. Only the first step uses synchronous communication; subsequent steps scatter each device's fresh patch into the cached full-shape activation from the prior step, then apply sparse layer operations (convolution, linear, cross-attention) exclusively on the fresh 1/N region. An asynchronous AllGather broadcasts each device's activation to all others in the background, hiding communication within computation. A corrected asynchronous GroupNorm approximates global normalization statistics by adding a local correction term to stale statistics, avoiding synchronous aggregation. For few-step samplers where adjacent-step similarity degrades, a short warm-up phase of standard synchronous patch parallelism is prepended to restore quality.

## Results

On COCO Captions with SDXL (50-step DDIM, 1024×1024), DistriFusion on 8 A100s achieves PSNR 30.5 dB, LPIPS 0.211 (w/Orig.), and FID 24.4 (w/Orig.), closely matching the original single-GPU model (LPIPS 0.797, FID 24.0 w/G.T.) while reducing per-device MACs from 338T to ~42T. Latency drops from 5.02s (1 GPU) to 1.77s (8 GPUs), a 2.8× speedup. At higher resolution (3840×3840), the speedup scales to 6.1× on 8 A100s (22.9s vs 140s), and combining with batch splitting for classifier-free guidance yields a total 6.6× end-to-end speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Naïve Patch (independent per-patch generation with no inter-patch interaction), DistriFusion substantially outperforms on all quality metrics: at 8 devices, PSNR improves from 27.8 to 30.5 dB, LPIPS (w/Orig.) drops from 0.857 to 0.211, and FID (w/Orig.) falls from 252 to 24.4, while total MACs remain comparable (338T vs 324T). Against ParaDiGMS (Picard-iteration parallel denoising), DistriFusion achieves better quality (LPIPS 0.211 vs 0.320; FID 24.4 vs 25.1) at roughly half the total computation (338T vs 657T MACs), since ParaDiGMS wastes FLOPs on speculative future-step guesses. On latency at 8 devices, both methods achieve a 2.8× speedup over the single-GPU baseline, but DistriFusion does so without the quality degradation or step-count dependency that limits ParaDiGMS (which drops to only 1.3× at 25 steps). The communication analysis (Table 2) further shows DistriFusion requires 60% less communication than synchronous tensor parallelism and cuts the synchronous patch-parallelism overhead by another 50–60% via asynchronous AllGather pipelined with computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DistriFusion departs from the tensor parallelism paradigm dominant in LLM serving, which is impractical for diffusion models due to their large spatial activations. It draws on sparse computation techniques from SIGE (cross-attention reformulation for region-selective computation) and patch-based inference from MCUNetV2, but repurposes them for a fundamentally different goal: multi-GPU single-image latency reduction rather than memory savings. The key conceptual departure is from ParaDiGMS, which parallelizes denoising steps via speculative Picard iterations; DistriFusion instead exploits the temporal similarity between adjacent diffusion steps to reuse stale activations, introducing 'displaced patch parallelism' as a new parallelism axis distinct from data, tensor, or pipeline parallelism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes multi-GPU single-image generation practical for the first time in diffusion models, achieving up to 6.1× speedup on 8 A100s for 3840×3840 images while preserving quality — a significant efficiency win for interactive and high-resolution applications. The training-free, model-agnostic nature means it can be immediately applied to any U-Net-based diffusion model without retraining. Open questions include whether the stale-activation approximation degrades further at very few steps (e.g., 4-step distilled models), how it interacts with emerging DiT architectures that may have different spatial computation patterns, and whether the NCCL-based asynchronous communication bottleneck can be eliminated with remote-memory-access hardware. The paper also hints at combining with batch splitting for classifier-free guidance, suggesting further speedups are possible.
