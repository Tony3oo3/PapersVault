## Problem

Existing learning-based SfM methods, particularly DPESFM, rely on linear permutation-equivariant layers that limit expressiveness and require scene-specific fine-tuning to achieve good accuracy on novel sequences. This paper asks whether a more expressive architecture—specifically graph attention networks—can learn SfM-specific geometric primitives well enough to generalize to unseen scenes at inference time without any fine-tuning, while remaining fast enough to be practical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241549/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a sparse set of normalized 2D keypoint tracks matched across m views and n scene points, represented by a binary observability matrix O ∈ {0,1}^{m×n} and the corresponding 2D measurements. It outputs camera poses (3D center plus unit-norm quaternion for Euclidean reconstruction, or 12 camera matrix elements for projective) and 3D coordinates for all scene points. The sparsity pattern is scene-dependent and fixed throughout the network, and the architecture handles variable numbers of views and points without scene-specific adaptation.

## Method

The architecture maintains four feature types—projection, view, scene point, and global—and processes them through L=12 layers of GATv2-based graph cross-attention, replacing the linear equivariant maps used in DPESFM. Each layer follows a fixed update pattern: projection features are aggregated into view and scene point features via bipartite graph cross-attention (edges defined by the observability matrix), these are further aggregated into a single global feature, and the global information is distributed back to projection features via concatenation and a feed-forward network. The key insight is that the dynamic attention weights of GATv2 allow the model to learn more complex, data-dependent relations between views and points than fixed linear equivariant maps, improving generalization. Training uses the Adam optimizer with non-squared reprojection error loss (substituting negative depth for points behind the camera), gradient normalization, random rotational camera perturbations as data augmentation, and artificial outlier injection as a regularizing strategy.

## Results

On the Olsson dataset (10 novel test scenes), with data augmentation and bundle adjustment, the proposed GASfM achieves an average reprojection error of 0.85 px, average rotation error of 0.144°, and average translation error of 0.07 m, closely matching COLMAP (0.66 px, 0.074°, 0.03 m) while running 5–10× faster. Without data augmentation, GASfM still outperforms DPESFM on reprojection error (1.92 vs. 3.52 px) and rotation error (1.391° vs. 2.596°) after BA. Inference alone (without BA) takes 0.06–0.63 s per scene, compared to 16–583 s for COLMAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary learning-based baseline is DPESFM (Moran et al., ICCV 2021), which uses linear permutation-equivariant layers. GASfM consistently outperforms DPESFM across all metrics: with data augmentation + BA, GASfM achieves 0.85 px average reprojection error vs. DPESFM's 4.86 px (a ~5.7× improvement), 0.144° rotation error vs. 9.406°, and 0.07 m translation error vs. 2.38 m. Against the conventional COLMAP pipeline, GASfM + BA is competitive on reprojection error (0.85 vs. 0.66 px) and translation error (0.07 vs. 0.03 m), and actually achieves lower rotation error than COLMAP on 8 out of 10 scenes, while running 5–10× faster (inference alone is 0.06–0.63 s vs. COLMAP's 16–583 s). DPESFM struggles particularly with data augmentation, where its reprojection error degrades to 436.51 px on inference alone, suggesting its linear equivariant layers are less robust to the geometric perturbations introduced by augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DPESFM (Moran et al., ICCV 2021), which formulated learned SfM using linear permutation-equivariant layers, by replacing those linear maps with GATv2 graph cross-attention layers that operate over a bipartite graph of projection, view, scene-point, and global features. The authors argue that the nonlinear attention mechanism better captures the implicit geometric primitives of SfM (epipolar constraints, triangulation geometry) than linear equivariant maps, while preserving the same permutation-equivariance property. The work also positions itself against traditional pipelines (COLMAP, incremental SfM) and other learned approaches (DeepSfM, PoseDiffusion), emphasizing that unlike DPESFM, the proposed model generalizes to unseen scenes without scene-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a learned SfM model can match COLMAP's reconstruction quality (reprojection error 0.85 vs 0.66 px, translation error 0.07 vs 0.03 m) at 5–10× lower runtime, without requiring per-scene fine-tuning, making learned SfM practically viable for fast initialization pipelines. The finding that artificial outlier injection during training acts as a regularizer—reducing reprojection error by ~40% over the no-outlier baseline—is a useful training strategy that may transfer to other geometric learning tasks. However, generalization remains limited (trained on a single small dataset of ~13 scenes), the model still depends on bundle adjustment for final accuracy, and scaling to larger, more diverse datasets is an open challenge that the authors acknowledge.
