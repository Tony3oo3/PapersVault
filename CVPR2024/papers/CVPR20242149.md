## Problem

Classical face morphing relies on discretized warping and pixel-level blending, while generative approaches (StyleGAN, diffAE) blend faces without aligning features over time, producing intermediate frames with temporal incoherence and artifacts from unrelated training-set identities. No prior method provides a single differentiable representation that simultaneously encodes the direct and inverse time-dependent warpings needed for feature alignment, enabling seamless blending in multiple domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242149/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 256×256 RGB face images (each represented as a coord-based sinusoidal MLP) and a set of landmark correspondences detected via DLib. It produces a continuous time-dependent morphing I: R²×[0,1]→C (a video interpolating the two faces) along with the underlying neural warping field T: R²×[-1,1]→R², which encodes both the forward deformation T_t and its inverse T_{-t} in a single network. The morphing can be instantiated via linear blending, Poisson seamless cloning in the gradient domain, or generative blending through diffAE latent-space interpolation.

## Method

The core architecture is a sinusoidal MLP (SIREN) with one hidden layer of 128 neurons that parameterizes a time-dependent warping T: R²×[-1,1]→R², trained to satisfy three constraints: identity at t=0, the inverse property T_t∘T_{-t}=Id for all t, and landmark alignment T(p_j,t)=T(q_j,1−t). A thin-plate energy term (Frobenius norm of the Hessian integrated over space-time) regularizes the deformation, exploiting the closed-form derivatives of the sinusoidal activation to avoid discretization. For blending, the warped images I_i(x,t)=I_i(T(x,i−t)) are combined either by point-wise linear interpolation, by training a second neural network to match blended Jacobians in a Poisson boundary-value problem (seamless cloning/mixing), or by embedding the warped images in diffAE's latent space, interpolating the resulting code curves, and decoding back to image space. The key design choice is encoding both direct and inverse transformations in one network, so that warping image I_0 forward in time and I_1 backward in time requires only evaluating T at positive and negative time arguments, eliminating the need for explicit map inversion at inference.

## Results

On the FRLL dataset (1220 morphings at t=0.5), the proposed neural warping with seamless-mix blending achieves the best FID of 22.604, substantially outperforming StyleGAN3 (35.653) and diffAE (41.356). The linear-blending variant achieves the best balanced perceptual similarity with LPIPS(I,I₁) of 0.164, beating both StyleGAN3 (0.173) and diffAE (0.186). The seamless-clone and seamless-mix variants achieve the lowest LPIPS(I₀,I) scores (0.093 and 0.081 respectively), reflecting their design of transferring target features onto the source image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StyleGAN3 (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2021), the proposed method wins on all three metrics: the linear-blending variant improves FID from 35.653 to 31.950 and LPIPS from 0.173/0.174 to 0.164/0.158, while the seamless-mix variant achieves a much larger FID gap (22.604 vs. 35.653). Against diffAE (Preechakul et al., CVPR 2022), the linear variant again wins on FID (31.950 vs. 41.356) and both LPIPS scores, and the seamless-mix variant extends the FID advantage to 22.604 vs. 41.356. The paper also compares qualitatively against MorDiff (Damer et al., 2023) for pose morphing (Fig. 11), where the proposed generative blending (neural warping + diffAE) produces smoother temporal transitions than both pure diffAE and MorDiff, though no quantitative scores are reported for this comparison. Notably, the seamless-clone/mix variants show asymmetric LPIPS (much closer to I₀ than I₁), which is expected since they transfer warped features of I₁ onto the I₀ background; the linear variant is the fairest head-to-head comparison on balanced perceptual similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes SIREN (sinusoidal MLPs) from implicit image representation to parameterize the warping field itself, encoding both direct and inverse time-dependent deformations in a single compact network. It adapts the classical thin-plate spline energy and Poisson image editing variational problem into differentiable loss terms, eliminating the discretization required by mesh-based morphing (Beier & Neely, Liao et al.) and the non-invertible latent-space interpolation of generative methods (StyleGAN3, diffAE). The key conceptual departure is treating warping and blending as disentangled, independently optimizable stages, where the warping is a smooth time-dependent diffeomorphism learned end-to-end with energy-based regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentangled warping-then-blending framework offers a practical path to temporally coherent face morphing that generative latent-space interpolation cannot provide, with direct implications for biometric security (face-morphing attack detection) and digital content creation. Limitations include the requirement that the learned transformation remain invertible (collapsing regions break the inverse), the 2D-only formulation, and reliance on DLib landmark detection. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D surface morphing, whether the thin-plate energy can be replaced by learned regularizers, and whether the method scales to multi-face or video morphing scenarios.
