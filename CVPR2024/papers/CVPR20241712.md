## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods for face anti-spoofing directly align diverse source-domain samples into a compact Euclidean feature space, neglecting the inherent hierarchical structure in FAS data (e.g., 2D vs. 3D attacks, lighting, background). This flat alignment can lose discriminative information and hinder generalization to unseen domains and attacks, while also providing poor interpretability of the learned feature space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a detected RGB face crop resized to 256×256 and outputs a binary live/spoof label. Rather than a standard sigmoid head on the raw feature, the final prediction is produced by a Prototype-oriented Classifier that computes hyperbolic distances from the input feature to all 2K leaf prototypes and aggregates them through a learnable weight vector W ∈ ℝ^K before applying a sigmoid, making the decision explicitly conditioned on the prototype-guided distribution.

## Method

HPDR embeds features in a Poincaré ball (curvature c=0.01) and introduces two sets of learnable prototypes: 2K leaf prototypes (K per class, carrying label information) and K′ non-leaf prototypes (label-free, modeling higher-level hierarchy). Hyperbolic Prototype Learning (HPL) optimizes three levels of relations: (1) Prototype-Instance contrastive loss pulls each feature toward its nearest same-class leaf prototype and away from others, with balanced assignment enforced per mini-batch; (2) Instance-Instance consistency constrains augmented and original features to be close in hyperbolic distance and to have matching distance vectors to all leaf prototypes; (3) Prototype-Prototype loss constructs a hierarchical tree over leaf prototypes by sampling lowest-common-ancestors from non-leaf prototypes via Gumbel noise and enforcing a Dasgupta-cost-inspired triplet margin, plus regularization pushing leaf prototypes away from the origin and from each other. A DepthNet backbone extracts features that are mapped into the Poincaré ball via the exponential map at the origin, and the full loss (prediction + L_PI + L_II + L_PP) is optimized with Adam (lr=0.001) using iterative prototype re-assignment each step.

## Results

On the four Leave-One-Out (LOO) domain generalization tasks, HPDR achieves the best HTER across all settings: 11.30% (O&M&I→C), 11.26% (O&C&M→I), 4.58% (O&C&I→M), and 9.93% (I&C&M→O), with corresponding AUC values of 94.42%, 92.49%, 96.02%, and 95.26%. On the cross-attack evaluation (HQ-WMCA, 8 attack types), HPDR achieves a mean ACER of 14.6%±8.5, outperforming DCN (19.9%±18.3) and CDCN (23.3%±17.7). In the limited-source-domain setting (2 source domains), HPDR also leads with HTER of 22.22% (M&I→C) and 21.07% (M&I→O).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPDR achieves the lowest HTER on all four LOO tasks, beating the strongest DG baseline AMEL [64] by 0.58% (O&M&I→C), 7.34% (O&C&M→I), 5.65% (O&C&I→M), and 1.38% (I&C&M→O). On AUC, HPDR leads on O&M&I→C (94.42% vs. AMEL's 94.39%) and I&C&M→O (95.26% vs. 93.96%), but trails SSAN-M [53] on O&C&M→I (92.49% vs. 94.58%) and EBDG [12] on O&C&I→M (96.02% vs. 97.17%). Among prototype-learning baselines, HPDR outperforms CPL [55] and PCL [58] by 5.36–7.86% HTER across tasks, and among hyperbolic baselines it beats HBL [18] by 2.03–8.74% HTER. On the cross-attack HQ-WMCA benchmark, HPDR's mean ACER of 14.6%±8.5 substantially improves over DCN [24] (19.9%±18.3) and CDCN [59] (23.3%±17.7), with the largest gains on Glasses (25.6% vs. 48.6%) and Makeup (15.7% vs. 30.7%). In the limited-source-domain setting (only 2 source domains), HPDR still leads all baselines, e.g., 22.22% HTER vs. AMEL's 24.52% on M&I→C.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: domain generalization for face anti-spoofing (which typically aligns features in Euclidean space via adversarial or contrastive objectives), hyperbolic embedding methods (building on the Poincaré ball framework of Khrulkov et al., CVPR 2020), and prototype-based representation learning. The key departure is from prior DG-FAS methods that perform direct feature alignment in a flat space, which the authors argue neglects the natural hierarchical structure of attack types and domain attributes. By introducing learnable leaf and non-leaf prototypes in hyperbolic space and optimizing multi-level relations (prototype-instance, instance-instance, prototype-prototype), HPDR reframes domain alignment as hierarchical distribution refinement rather than uniform compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled argument that FAS data possesses an inherent tree-like hierarchy (2D vs. 3D attacks, subcategories, domain attributes) that Euclidean alignment inherently distorts, and hyperbolic geometry provides a low-distortion embedding for such structures. The cross-attack results on HQ-WMCA show notably lower variance (8.5 vs. 18.3 std) compared to prior methods, suggesting the hierarchical structure yields more consistent generalization across diverse unseen attacks. Open questions include whether the learned hierarchy is truly interpretable and aligned with human-attack taxonomies, whether the approach scales to more complex or open-world attack spaces, and whether the fixed curvature (c=0.01) and prototype count (K=20) generalize beyond the four standard FAS benchmarks.
