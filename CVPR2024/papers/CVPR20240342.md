## Problem

Existing text-supervised semantic segmentation methods suffer from an alignment-level discrepancy: image-text alignment matches whole images to whole texts, while region-text alignment matches a single region to a text that may contain multiple semantic concepts. Neither approach enforces the one-to-one correspondence between a semantically homogeneous image region and the specific word segment that describes it, leading to suboptimal segmentation quality and train-test mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240342/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes 224×224 RGB images paired with free-form text captions from CC3M and CC12M, with no pixel-level annotations. At inference, the image segmenter takes an RGB image and a set of class names (from MMSegmentation defaults) to produce a pixel-wise segmentation mask, refined via pixel-adaptive mask refinement (PAMR). The model is evaluated zero-shot on six benchmarks spanning 19–171 classes, including both foreground-only and full-annotation settings.

## Method

CoDe jointly decomposes each image-text pair into image regions and word segments via a noun selector, an image segmenter (TCL-based), and a text segmenter (CLIP text encoder with two learnable multi-head attention layers). A region-word highlighting module fills masked areas with learnable prompts (P^v for images, P^t for texts) to prevent domain shift from zero tokens when feeding masked inputs to CLIP. Region-word alignment is then enforced through a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss between CLIP embeddings of the highlighted regions and highlighted word segments, with a learnable temperature. The full training objective combines this contrastive loss with knowledge-guided noun embedding loss (KgCoOp), image segmentation loss, and text segmentation loss, optimized over 50k iterations on CC3M+CC12M.

## Results

CoDe achieves the best mIoU on all six zero-shot semantic segmentation benchmarks, with an average mIoU of 31.8 across PASCAL VOC (57.7), PASCAL Context (30.5), COCO-Object (32.3), COCO-Stuff (23.9), Cityscapes (28.9), and ADE20K (17.7). This represents a 5.65% average improvement over the closest competitor TCL (30.1 avg mIoU). The method is trained solely on CC3M + CC12M image-text pairs without any pixel-level annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDe outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset where they are compared. The closest competitor is TCL (CVPR 2023), which also uses CC3M+CC12M and region-text alignment; CoDe improves its average mIoU from 30.1 to 31.8, with notable gains on Cityscapes (28.9 vs 24.0) and COCO-Stuff (23.9 vs 22.4). SimSeg (CVPR 2023) is competitive on PASCAL VOC (57.4 vs 57.7) but lags significantly on PASCAL Context (26.2 vs 30.5) and COCO-Object (29.7 vs 32.3), and does not report results on the three 'without background' benchmarks. Among methods evaluated on all six datasets, GroupViT (20.2 avg) and CoCu (24.6 avg) are substantially outperformed, with CoDe gaining over 11 mIoU points over GroupViT and 7.2 over CoCu. Methods using additional training data (e.g., YFCC14M in GroupViT and CoCu, RedCaps12M in PGSeg) still fall short of CoDe, which trains only on CC3M+CC12M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-supervised semantic segmentation line initiated by TCL [5], which it directly builds upon for the image segmenter and its training loss. The key conceptual departure is from region-text alignment (TCL) to region-word alignment via joint image-text co-decomposition, motivated by the observation that a single text caption contains multiple semantic concepts while segmentation requires homogeneous regions. The prompt learning mechanism for masked feature extraction draws from the broader prompt tuning literature (CoOp, VPT) and repurposes it for the specific challenge of domain shift introduced by zeroing out masked pixels or tokens before feeding them to CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The region-word alignment formulation provides a cleaner train-test correspondence that addresses a genuine structural mismatch in prior methods, and the prompt-based highlighting is a practical technique likely to be reusable in other masked-feature-extraction settings. However, the method remains tightly coupled to CLIP as a frozen backbone, relies on NLTK part-of-speech tagging for noun selection (which is brittle for non-English or informal text), and the text segmenter is a relatively lightweight two-layer attention module. Open questions include whether the co-decomposition idea generalizes to structured or multi-sentence descriptions, whether the approach benefits from stronger vision-language backbones, and whether the modest gains (~1.7 mIoU over TCL) persist at larger training scales.
