## Problem

LiDAR novel view synthesis remains largely unexplored compared to camera-based NVS, and existing LiDAR NVS methods are restricted to static scenes, struggle with the sparsity and large-scale extent of driving point clouds, and lack realistic modeling of intensity and ray-drop characteristics. This paper addresses the combined challenge of dynamic reconstruction under large vehicle motion, efficient large-scale scene representation, and physically plausible LiDAR point cloud generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241335/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of LiDAR point cloud frames (each with K points of 3D coordinates and 1D reflection intensity), corresponding SE(3) sensor poses, and timestamps, with the entire scene scaled into a unit cube and 768 samples drawn per laser ray. Given a novel sensor pose and arbitrary timestamp, it synthesizes a full LiDAR point cloud with per-point depth, intensity, and ray-drop probability via differentiable volume rendering along angularly-spaced laser beams within the sensor's FOV.

## Method

LiDAR4D introduces a 4D hybrid planar-grid representation that combines low-resolution multi-planar features (K-Planes-style decomposition into xy, xz, yz, xt, yt, zt planes) for smooth geometry with high-resolution multi-level hash grid features (Instant-NGP-style) for high-frequency intensity, concatenated in a coarse-to-fine manner to handle scenes spanning hundreds of meters. A flow MLP predicts inter-frame displacement from encoded spatio-temporal coordinates, and multi-frame dynamic features are aggregated to achieve temporal consistency; chamfer distance between flow-warped and adjacent ground-truth point clouds provides an explicit geometric supervision signal. Separate MLP heads predict density, intensity, and ray-drop probability from the aggregated features and position-encoded viewing directions, which are then alpha-composited along each ray. A lightweight U-Net, randomly initialized and optimized at runtime, performs global ray-drop mask refinement using the full rendered probability, depth, and intensity maps as input, preserving cross-region patterns that point-wise MLP prediction cannot capture.

## Results

On KITTI-360, LiDAR4D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.1089 and F-score of 0.9272 for point cloud synthesis, outperforming the best prior method LiDAR-NeRF (CD 0.1438, F-score 0.9091) by 24.3% in CD error. For depth reconstruction, LiDAR4D reaches PSNR 27.48 dB and LPIPS 0.1051, substantially ahead of all baselines. On NuScenes, the method maintains its lead with a 24.2% CD reduction over LiDAR-NeRF (0.2443 vs 0.3225), confirming generalizability across different LiDAR configurations (64-beam vs 32-beam).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiDAR4D outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric on both KITTI-360 and NuScenes. Against the primary implicit static baseline LiDAR-NeRF [39], LiDAR4D reduces CD error by 24.3% (0.1089 vs 0.1438) on KITTI-360 and 24.2% (0.2443 vs 0.3225) on NuScenes, while also improving depth LPIPS from 0.2797 to 0.1051 and intensity SSIM from 0.3831 to 0.5304. Compared to explicit mesh-based methods (LiDARsim, NKSR) and the point-cloud-based PCGen, LiDAR4D achieves 20–30× lower CD error, demonstrating the advantage of implicit neural representations over explicit surface reconstruction for large-scale scenes. Among dynamic NeRF baselines (D-NeRF, TiNeuVox-B, K-Planes), LiDAR4D consistently leads, with the largest gains in perceptual depth quality (LPIPS 0.1051 vs 0.30–0.35) and intensity fidelity (PSNR 18.56 vs 17.0–17.4), attributed to the hybrid planar-grid representation and global ray-drop refinement. Notably, LiDAR4D is the only method that handles dynamic scenes using LiDAR-only input without requiring RGB images or ground-truth 3D object labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiDAR4D extends the LiDAR NVS line initiated by LiDAR-NeRF and NFL (which handle static scenes only) by introducing dynamic space-time reconstruction, drawing on the spatio-temporal neural field paradigm of D-NeRF, K-Planes, and TiNeuVox. Its hybrid multi-planar + hash-grid representation combines K-Planes' parameter-efficient planar decomposition with Instant-NGP's high-resolution hash encoding, specifically to address the scale and sparsity of driving scenes. The scene-flow prior and chamfer-distance geometric constraint adapt the Neural Scene Flow Prior idea to LiDAR, while the U-Net-based ray-drop refinement departs from the point-wise MLP approach in LiDAR-NeRF by enforcing global spatial consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first LiDAR-only dynamic NVS framework, LiDAR4D opens the door to realistic sensor data generation for autonomous driving without RGB supervision or ground-truth 3D labels, which is practically valuable for data augmentation and closed-loop simulation. However, the authors acknowledge that long-distance vehicle motion, occlusion, and foreground-background separation remain unsolved, and evaluation is constrained to the ego-car trajectory, preventing true decoupling of spatial and temporal novelty. The gap between dynamic and static reconstruction quality suggests the problem is still far from fully addressed.
