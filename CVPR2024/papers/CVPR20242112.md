## Problem

Current multimodal large language models perform vision-language alignment at image-level or bounding-box level, which introduces irrelevant background features and semantic deviation for fine-grained region understanding. The lack of mask-based instruction data and the computational burden of ViT-based encoders at high resolution further hinder pixel-level visual understanding in MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Osprey takes an RGB image (512×512), one or more binary pixel-level mask regions, and a text instruction containing special <region> tokens as input. Each <region> token is replaced at inference with a visual mask token (from mask-pooling over multi-scale CNN features) and a spatial token (from the binary mask resized to 224×224, flattened, and projected). The model outputs free-form text responses including object categories, detailed region descriptions, attribute answers, and reasoning, with the option for short-form single-word/phrase outputs when prompted.

## Method

Osprey replaces the standard ViT-based CLIP encoder with a ConvNeXt-Large convolutional CLIP backbone, which generalizes efficiently to 512×512 input resolution and produces multi-scale feature maps suitable for region-level pooling. A Mask-Aware Visual Extractor applies mask-pooling over four CNN feature levels for each region, projects each level through a linear layer, sums them, and passes through an MLP to yield a visual mask token; a separate spatial token is derived from the flattened binary mask to preserve geometric position. These tokens are interleaved with text embeddings (via a <region>→<mask><position> substitution) and fed into a Vicuna LLM. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) image-text alignment on CC3M with only the MLP connector trainable, (2) mask-text alignment on object and part datasets to train the Mask-Aware Visual Extractor, and (3) end-to-end fine-tuning on the newly curated Osprey-724K dataset (724K mask-text instruction pairs spanning object-level, part-level, and negative/short-form robustness samples) with the vision encoder frozen.

## Results

On open-vocabulary segmentation, Osprey achieves 50.64% PQ, 29.17% AP, and 49.78% mIoU on Cityscapes, surpassing the best box-level baseline GPT4RoI by +15.94% PQ and the best mask-level baseline Ferret by +15.07% PQ. On ADE20K-150, Osprey obtains 41.89% PQ and 41.24% AP. For referring object classification, Osprey reaches 65.24% SS on LVIS and a striking 73.06% SS / 52.72% S-IoU on part-level PACO, outperforming Ferret by +14.38% SS and +26.76% S-IoU on PACO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Osprey decisively outperforms all box-level baselines (Kosmos-2, Shikra, GPT4RoI) across every task, with the largest gap on open-vocabulary segmentation (+15.94% PQ over GPT4RoI on Cityscapes) and detailed description (+27.57% over GPT4RoI on RefCOCOs). Against the strongest mask-level competitor Ferret, Osprey wins on Cityscapes segmentation (+15.07% PQ), LVIS classification (+1.46% SS), and PACO part classification (+14.38% SS, +26.76% S-IoU), but slightly trails on ADE20K mIoU (29.63 vs. 31.77) and POPE Random hallucination (89.47 vs. 90.24). On the POPE Adversarial setting, Osprey achieves 85.33%, beating both Shikra (83.10%) and Ferret (82.36%), demonstrating the benefit of its negative sample mining strategy. On Ferret-Bench, Osprey-7B* leads in both Referring Description (72.2% vs. 68.7%) and Referring Reasoning (67.8% vs. 67.3%) over Ferret. The pure CLIP baselines (ConvNeXt-L and Surgery-ViT-L) are substantially weaker, confirming that the LLM-based instruction tuning and mask-aware extraction are critical for fine-grained region understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

Osprey extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm established by LLaVA/LLaVA-1.5 (vision encoder + connector + LLM) by replacing the standard ViT-based CLIP encoder with a convolutional ConvNeXt-Large CLIP backbone and introducing a Mask-Aware Visual Extractor that performs multi-level mask-pooling (borrowed from MaskCLIP) combined with spatial position tokens. It departs from prior region-level MLLMs (Kosmos-2, Shikra, GPT4RoI, Ferret) that use sparse bounding boxes as referring inputs, instead operating on fine-grained pixel-level masks to achieve tighter region-text alignment. The three-stage training recipe (image-text alignment, mask-text alignment, end-to-end fine-tuning) follows the LLaVA-1.5 multi-stage paradigm but adds a dedicated mask-feature alignment stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Osprey bridges a practical gap between SAM (which produces pixel-accurate masks but no semantics) and MLLMs (which provide rich language but only at image or box granularity), enabling a seamless pipeline where SAM segments and Osprey describes. The Osprey-724K dataset is a significant resource for the community, being the first large-scale mask-text instruction dataset. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4-generated annotations (potential quality ceiling), the 512×512 resolution trade-off between accuracy and inference speed, and the fact that mask input is still required at inference (though SAM can supply them). Open questions include whether this approach extends to video, multi-region compositional reasoning, and whether the GPT-4 data pipeline can be replaced with human-verified annotations for higher fidelity.
