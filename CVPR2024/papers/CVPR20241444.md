## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for diffusion models treat all network components uniformly during reconstruction and calibration, overlooking that temporal features (generated from time-step t through embedding layers) are independent of the sampling data and form a finite set. This causes temporal feature disturbance—where quantized temporal features no longer correctly encode their corresponding time-step—leading to denoising trajectory deviation and severe quality degradation, particularly under 4-bit weight quantization.

## Input / Output

The framework quantizes the UNet denoising network within diffusion models (DDPM, LDM, Stable Diffusion v1-4), taking Gaussian noise plus optional class labels or text prompts as input and producing synthetic images at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 512×512 (Stable Diffusion). The key structural detail is that the Temporal Information Block—comprising the time embed and per-block embedding layers—receives only the scalar time-step t as input, making its activations a finite set of size T (e.g., 50 or 1000) that is entirely decoupled from the noisy latent x_t flowing through the rest of the network.

## Method

TFMQ-DM introduces a Temporal Information Block that consolidates all time embed and embedding layer modules into a unified, x_t-independent unit. For weight quantization, Temporal Information Aware Reconstruction (TIAR) replaces the standard block-level reconstruction loss with a direct temporal feature loss (Eq. 10), so that embedding layers are optimized solely to preserve their own output rather than being dragged by x_t-dependent gradients during backpropagation. For activation quantization, Finite Set Calibration (FSC) assigns T separate (step-size, zero-point) pairs—one per time-step—to activations within the Temporal Information Block, exploiting the fact that the activation range varies substantially across time-steps. The base weight quantization uses BRECQ or AdaRound with 20k reconstruction iterations, while FSC uses Min-max estimation per time-step, achieving a 2.0× speedup in calibration time over prior methods with negligible inference overhead.

## Results

On LSUN-Bedrooms 256×256 with LDM-4 under w4a8 quantization, TFMQ-DM achieves FID 3.68 and sFID 7.65, reducing FID by 2.26 and sFID by 7.51 over the previous SOTA (PTQD). On the challenging CelebA-HQ 256×256 dataset, TFMQ-DM achieves FID 8.76 (vs. 15.47 for PTQD and 15.55 for Q-Diffusion), a 6.71 FID reduction over SOTA, approaching the full-precision FID of 8.74. On ImageNet 256×256 (class-conditional, LDM-8), TFMQ-DM achieves FID 10.29 and IS 221.82 under w4a8, outperforming PTQD (FID 10.40, IS 214.73) and Q-Diffusion (FID 10.68, IS 212.51). For text-guided generation with Stable Diffusion on MS-COCO, TFMQ-DM achieves FID 13.36 under w4a8, a 1.13 reduction over Q-Diffusion (14.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFMQ-DM consistently outperforms all three prior diffusion-model PTQ baselines across every dataset and quantization setting. Against PTQ4DM [41], the gains are most dramatic under w4a8: FID drops from 20.72 to 3.68 on LSUN-Bedrooms and from 17.08 to 8.76 on CelebA-HQ, indicating PTQ4DM's block-level reconstruction is particularly harmful at 4-bit. Against Q-Diffusion [23], TFMQ-DM reduces FID by 2.72 (LSUN-Bedrooms), 6.79 (CelebA-HQ), and 1.48 (FFHQ) under w4a8, with the largest gap on face-centric datasets where temporal feature disturbance is most damaging. Against PTQD [10], the most recent SOTA, TFMQ-DM still achieves FID reductions of 2.26 (LSUN-Bedrooms), 6.71 (CelebA-HQ), and 1.45 (FFHQ) under w4a8, and a 5.28 sFID reduction on ImageNet. Against the BRECQ [24] baseline (used as the ablation starting point), TFMQ-DM reduces FID from 9.36 to 3.68 and sFID from 22.73 to 7.65 on LSUN-Bedrooms w4a8, confirming that both TIAR and FSC contribute substantially. Notably, TFMQ-DM is the first method to achieve near full-precision FID under 4-bit weight quantization (e.g., 3.68 vs. 2.98 on LSUN-Bedrooms, 8.76 vs. 8.74 on CelebA-HQ), a gap of less than 0.7 FID across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTQ-for-diffusion-models line initiated by PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, and PTQD, all of which apply block-wise reconstruction (inheriting from BRECQ/AdaRound) at the Residual Bottleneck Block granularity. The key departure is the identification of a Temporal Information Block—modules that encode the time-step t independently of the input data—and the argument that prior methods' coarser reconstruction target and data-dependent calibration inadvertently disturb these temporal features. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the standard block-reconstruction paradigm by redefining the block boundary to align with the temporal/data independence structure unique to diffusion UNets, rather than the architectural residual blocks used by all prior diffusion PTQ work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving near-full-precision FID at 4-bit weight quantization for the first time makes low-bit deployment of diffusion models practically viable, and the 2× calibration speedup reduces the already-substantial PTQ overhead. The temporal feature disturbance diagnosis provides a reusable conceptual lens: any future quantization or compression method for diffusion models must account for the data-independence of time-encoding modules. Open questions include extending the framework to QAT for sub-4-bit regimes, handling the analogous 'textual feature' modules in Stable Diffusion, and whether the Temporal Information Block abstraction generalizes to non-UNet diffusion backbones (e.g., DiT/transformer-based models).
