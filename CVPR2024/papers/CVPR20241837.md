## Problem

Existing gait recognition methods rely on convolutional networks whose fixed parameters and non-linear functions limit adaptability to real-world distractors such as occlusions, viewpoint changes, and carrying conditions. These methods extract shared information across views and attire but discard task-relevant knowledge specific to particular conditions, and they suffer from length generalization issues when trained on short frame subsets but tested on full-length sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a gait silhouette sequence X ∈ R^{T×H×W} resized to 64×64 pixels, where T is the number of frames. During training, 20–40 frames are randomly sampled per sequence, while testing uses the full sequence. The output is a part-based gait feature embedding F ∈ R^{P×C} (P horizontal slices, C feature dimension) used for identity retrieval via rank-k accuracy on four benchmarks.

## Method

VPNet combines three components: (1) a ResNet backbone augmented with a Forward cycle Temporal Shift (FTS) module that cyclically shifts one quarter of channel features along the temporal axis before 3D convolution, enabling global receptive fields without extra parameters; (2) a trainable part-based prompt pool of key-value pairs where, for each body-part slice, the temporally-averaged feature is matched against pool keys via cosine similarity to select top-k value templates that are concatenated to the gait sequence along the temporal dimension; (3) a dynamic transformer head with a context window mask that restricts attention to a global branch (each frame attending to prompt templates) and a local branch (each frame attending to neighbors within a fixed window length l=16), thereby addressing length generalization. Training uses a combined loss of triplet loss, cross-entropy loss, and a cosine embedding loss that supervises key selection, with a randomized restart strategy to prevent prompt codebook collapse.

## Results

On the in-the-wild Gait3D dataset, VPNet-L achieves 75.4% rank-1 and 87.1% rank-5 accuracy, surpassing the previous best silhouette-based method DyGait by 9.1% in rank-1. On GREW, VPNet-L reaches 80.0% rank-1 and 89.4% rank-5, outperforming GaitGCI by 11.5%. On the in-the-lab benchmarks, VPNet-M attains 92.4% mean rank-1 on OUMVLP (matching HSTL) and VPNet-T achieves 94.9% mean rank-1 on CASIA-B, both setting new state-of-the-art results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPNet-L dominates all 13 baselines across the four datasets. On Gait3D, it outperforms the strongest prior silhouette method DyGait (66.3%) by 9.1% in rank-1 and the multimodal SMPLGait (46.3%) by 29.1%. On GREW, VPNet-L (80.0%) beats GaitGCI (68.5%) by 11.5% and GaitGraph (1.3%) by 79.7%. On OUMVLP, VPNet-M (92.4% mean) ties HSTL (92.4%) and marginally exceeds GaitGCI (92.1%) and DANet (90.7%). On CASIA-B, VPNet-T (94.9% mean) edges out DANet (94.6%), HSTL (94.3%), and DyGait (94.1%), with the largest gap in the cross-dressing CL condition (90.0% vs. 89.9% for DANet). The ablation in Table 7 confirms that the prompt pool and dynamic attention contribute most under in-the-wild conditions (Gait3D drops from 75.4% to 72.8% without prompts), while their impact is smaller on the controlled CASIA-B benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPNet extends the appearance-based gait recognition lineage (GaitSet, GaitGL, DANet) by replacing the standard pooling head with a dynamic transformer that incorporates prompt learning—a technique previously established in NLP and continual learning (DualPrompt, Learning to Prompt) but not previously applied to gait. The backbone builds on the Temporal Shift Module (TSM) by introducing a forward-cyclic variant (FTS) specifically motivated by the asymmetry of forward vs. backward walking, and combines it with 3D convolution for local spatio-temporal aggregation. The key conceptual departure is the part-based prompt pool with key-value pairs that dynamically selects task-relevant templates (encoding viewpoint, attire, occlusion) and concatenates them with gait features, bridging the gap between fixed-parameter CNNs and adaptive sequence modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that prompt learning can effectively encode task-relevant invariance knowledge for gait recognition, achieving SOTA across both in-the-wild (Gait3D, GREW) and in-the-lab (OUMVLP, CASIA-B) benchmarks with particularly large margins on real-world data. The dynamic context window mask provides a practical solution to the length generalization problem that plagues gait methods trained on short subsequences but tested on full sequences. A notable limitation acknowledged by the authors is the absence of pre-training, unlike NLP prompt learning which typically fine-tunes large pretrained models; the authors identify large-scale gait pre-training with prompt engineering as a key future direction. The work also signals a field-level shift: with in-the-lab performance near saturation, the authors argue gait research should increasingly focus on outdoor, in-the-wild scenarios.
