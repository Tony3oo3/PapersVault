## Problem

Existing high-quality face capture methods require expensive studio apparatus (Light Stage) or inconvenient home-capture conditions such as direct sunlight (SunStage) or polarization filters in a darkroom (PolFace). Moreover, all prior democratized approaches only model facial skin, ignoring the highly specular eyes, hair, and mouth interior. This paper addresses the gap of capturing the complete face with all these heterogeneous regions from a single, easily-obtained smartphone flashlight video in a dim room.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single ~25-second co-located smartphone flashlight video (captured with an iPhone X, resized to 960×720) recorded in a dim room where the flashlight is the dominant light source, along with per-frame camera intrinsics from off-the-shelf calibration. The flashlight is assumed to share the camera position. The output is a triangle mesh with UV maps for diffuse albedo, specular albedo, roughness, and normals, plus two sphere meshes for the eyeballs with predefined specular properties, all exportable to standard CG software like Blender for relighting in novel environments.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid face representation that splits the face into an eyeball region (two sphere meshes with fixed specular albedo and roughness, learned diffuse albedo) and an other-region (neural SDF field for geometry plus a neural field for Disney BRDF parameters). A novel mesh-aware volume rendering technique converts the eyeball meshes into an analytic SDF and uses a differentiable select operator to merge it with the neural SDF, enabling seamless joint optimization. Lighting is modeled compactly as a point light (flashlight) plus 2nd-order spherical harmonics (ambient), requiring only a single flashlight-on sequence. Training is regularized by two novel losses: a composition loss that uses face-parsing masks to enforce each region represents only its own area, and a reflectance regularization loss that fits AlbedoMM per-frame to supervise the volume-rendered specular albedo, improving diffuse-specular disentanglement under the single-light-source constraint.

## Results

On the hold-out validation images from the co-located flashlight sequence (averaged over 5 subjects), the proposed method achieves PSNR 26.12, SSIM 0.8808, and LPIPS 0.1642, substantially outperforming the enhanced NextFace++ baseline (PSNR 17.62, SSIM 0.7339, LPIPS 0.2727). The combined lighting model (point light + 2nd-order SH ambient) yields a PSNR gain of 0.08 dB (night w/ curtain), 0.05 dB (noon w/ curtain), and 0.61 dB (noon w/o curtain) over a point-light-only baseline. Training completes in ~70 minutes on a single RTX 3090, and the capture itself takes approximately 25 seconds with a smartphone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NextFace++ (a model-based 3DMM + AlbedoMM pipeline enhanced with the paper's camera parameters and combined lighting model), the proposed method wins decisively on all three metrics: PSNR improves by 8.5 dB (26.12 vs 17.62), SSIM by 0.147, and LPIPS drops by 0.109, reflecting both better geometry fitting (hybrid SDF + mesh vs. BFM-constrained) and more plausible specular reconstruction around the eyes. Against the Light Stage-based solution [18] (polarized spherical gradient illumination, high-def DSLR), the paper acknowledges a clear quality gap: the Light Stage produces cleaner diffuse albedo (thanks to polarization-based diffuse/specular separation) and higher-resolution pore-level detail, while the smartphone-based method is limited by camera quality and subject motion over the 25-second capture. Comparisons to NeRO (multi-view unconstrained environment) and WildLight (same single-flashlight input) are deferred to the supplementary material; the paper positions NeRO as a somewhat easier-to-use but less controlled setup, and WildLight as requiring two sequences (flashlight on/off) rather than one. PolFace is excluded from comparison due to being closed-source.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) studio-based face capture (Light Stage, single-shot systems) which achieves high quality but requires expensive apparatus; (2) recent democratization efforts like SunStage (sunlight) and PolFace (polarized smartphone in darkroom) that reduce cost but still impose inconvenient constraints; and (3) neural-field inverse rendering (WildLight, IRON, VolSDF) that provides the representational backbone. The key departure is a hybrid representation that integrates predefined eyeball sphere meshes directly into the VolSDF volume rendering pipeline (extending EyeNeRF's mesh-guided approach) and a compact combined lighting model (point light + 2nd-order SH) that eliminates the need for a second flashlight-off sequence, unlike WildLight. AlbedoMM is repurposed as a per-frame specular albedo regularizer rather than a full reflectance prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes high-quality relightable face capture genuinely accessible to non-experts at home with only a smartphone and a dim room, removing the need for polarization filters, darkrooms, or outdoor sunlight. The hybrid mesh+neural-SDF representation is a practical pattern for handling regions with very different reflectance properties (highly specular eyes vs. rough skin) within a single differentiable pipeline, and the exported assets are directly usable in standard CG software. Open questions include automating eyeball placement (currently manual), reducing the 25-second capture time to avoid motion blur, and closing the remaining quality gap with Light Stage (pore-level detail, cleaner diffuse-specular separation). The approach's reliance on a co-located flashlight assumption and a single dominant light source limits generalization to more complex lighting.
