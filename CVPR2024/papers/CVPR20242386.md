## Problem

Prior 3D wireframe reconstruction methods (e.g., Line3D++, LiMAP) rely on explicit cross-view line segment matching, which is fragile under view-dependent occlusions and endpoint truncation, and requires redundant short 2D line segments rather than the parsimonious wireframe graphs produced by modern parsers. No matching-free formulation existed for reconstructing structured 3D wireframes (line segments + junctions) directly from multi-view 2D wireframe observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes n posed RGB images with known intrinsics/extrinsics and their corresponding 2D wireframe graphs (undirected graphs from HAWPv3, where vertices are 2D junctions and edges are long line segments) as input. It outputs a 3D wireframe graph G=(V,E) with N learned 3D junctions and M 3D line segments, plus a set of global 3D junctions that can serve as initialization for 3D Gaussian Splatting with ~20× fewer points than SfM.

## Method

NEAT builds on VolSDF with two jointly optimized neural components: a Neural Attraction Field (4-layer coordinate MLP) that volume-renders 3D line segments by predicting endpoint displacements at each ray point using view direction, SDF normals, and SDF features, and a Global 3D Junction Perceiver that learns N 3D junctions from randomly initialized latent queries. The key insight is line-to-point attraction: every 3D line endpoint must coincide with a scene junction, enabling self-supervised training without cross-view matching. Training uses DBScan clustering on the noisy rendered line cloud endpoints as pseudo-labels, followed by Hungarian bipartite matching between perceived junctions and clustered labels, with a combined loss of image rendering, Eikonal, line projection, and junction matching terms. After optimization, a distillation step indexes line segments to junctions, applies least-squares refinement of junction positions, and performs visibility-based filtering to produce the final sparse wireframe.

## Results

On the DTU dataset (12 scenes), NEAT achieves the best junction accuracy (ACC-J = 0.7718) and completeness (COMP-L = 6.1064), while producing 624 3D line segments and 503 junctions—far more than LiMAP (231 lines) or Line3D++@HAWP (249 lines). On BlendedMVS (4 scenes), NEAT again leads in junction accuracy (ACC-J = 0.1949 vs. 0.3712 for LiMAP and 0.3743 for Line3D++@HAWP) and completeness (COMP-L = 6.4621). However, both baselines achieve lower line-segment accuracy (ACC-L): LiMAP reaches 0.8547 on DTU and 0.3169 on BlendedMVS, compared to NEAT's 0.8002 and 0.1802 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEAT is compared against two matching-based baselines: LiMAP [17] (CVPR 2023) and Line3D++@HAWP [12] (CVIU 2017), both of which rely on cross-view line-segment matching for 3D reconstruction. NEAT decisively outperforms both on junction accuracy (ACC-J) across all scenes: on DTU it achieves 0.7718 vs. 1.0944 (LiMAP) and 0.9019 (Line3D++@HAWP), and on BlendedMVS 0.1949 vs. 0.3712 and 0.3743. NEAT also wins on completeness (COMP-L) on both datasets (6.1064 vs. 7.7756/8.5086 on DTU; 6.4621 vs. 6.9415/6.8760 on BlendedMVS) and produces substantially more 3D line segments (624 vs. 231/249 on DTU), indicating a more complete wireframe. However, both baselines achieve lower (better) line-segment accuracy (ACC-L): LiMAP leads with 0.8547 on DTU and 0.3169 on BlendedMVS, while NEAT reports 0.8002 and 0.1802. The trade-off is that NEAT's matching-free formulation yields more junctions and lines at the cost of slightly noisier individual line-segment placement, whereas the matching-based methods produce fewer but more precisely placed segments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant matching-based paradigm for 3D wireframe/line reconstruction (Line3D++, LiMAP, ELSR), which triangulates 3D line segments from cross-view 2D line correspondences. Instead, it repurposes the volume rendering machinery of VolSDF [49]—originally designed for dense SDF surfaces—to render sparse 3D line segments via a coordinate MLP that outputs endpoint displacements, drawing the 'attraction field' concept from 2D wireframe parsing (HAWPv3 [46]). The key architectural departure is the Global 3D Junction Perceiver, a query-based module that distills a sparse set of 3D junctions from the noisy rendered line cloud via DBScan clustering and Hungarian bipartite matching, eliminating the need for any explicit cross-view line matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEAT demonstrates that structured 3D wireframe reconstruction is achievable without any cross-view correspondence search, which removes a major failure mode (occlusion-induced endpoint truncation) that plagues matching-based methods. The distilled 3D junctions also serve as a compact initialization for 3D Gaussian Splatting, reportedly outperforming COLMAP SfM points with ~20× fewer points, hinting at broader utility for structured scene representations. However, scalability is limited by the predefined junction count (e.g., 1024), inside-out scenes remain problematic due to the VolSDF rendering formulation, and the entire pipeline is sensitive to 2D wireframe detection quality. Open questions include automatic determination of junction count, extension to large-scale scenes, and whether the junction-perceiving mechanism generalizes beyond wireframe tasks to other structured 3D representations.
