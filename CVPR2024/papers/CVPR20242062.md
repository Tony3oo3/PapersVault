## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based MRI super-resolution methods require many denoising iterations (inefficient) and tend to introduce artifacts or structural distortions that misalign with the true high-resolution anatomy. This paper rethinks how DMs should be applied to MRI SR by operating in a compact latent space and coupling the generative prior with a large-window Transformer decoder to preserve anatomical fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution complex MRI image of the target contrast (e.g., T2-FLAIR, T2) and a high-resolution reference image of a different contrast (e.g., T1) with the same anatomy, both represented as [H×W×2] real-imaginary channel pairs, and produces a 4× upscaled HR image of the target contrast. The reference image is fused via a Cross-Attention Transformer Layer (CATL) to supply high-frequency structural cues that the LR target lacks.

## Method

DiffMSR operates the diffusion model in a highly compact latent space (dimension 4Ĉ) rather than pixel space, so only 4 denoising iterations are needed to generate a prior-knowledge vector Z that guides reconstruction. A Prior Extraction (PE) module compresses the HR target into Z, and a Condition Extraction (CE) module compresses the LR target into a conditioning vector C for the denoising network. The decoder is a Prior-Guide Large Window Transformer (PLWformer) whose PL-MSA module uses permuted self-attention (token reduction factor k=2 on K/V) to expand the window to 16×16 without increasing FLOPs, while Z modulates both PL-MSA and PG-FFN as dynamic parameters. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 jointly optimizes PE and PLWformer with L1 image loss plus frequency-domain data-consistency loss; Stage 2 trains the DM (with CE and PLWformer jointly) to generate Ẑ from Gaussian noise conditioned on C, adding a latent-space L1 diffusion loss.

## Results

DiffMSR achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all four datasets at 4× upscaling: 33.78 dB / 0.8765 on FastMRI, 34.12 dB / 0.9362 on Clinical Brain, 33.85 dB / 0.9274 on Clinical Tumor, and 35.47 dB / 0.9607 on Clinical Pelvic. The method requires only 4 diffusion iterations and runs at 39 ms per image with 58.6 G FLOPs and 6.8 M parameters, compared to 1455 ms, 461 G FLOPs, and 86.1 M parameters for the strongest baseline (DisC-Diff). This represents a ~37× speedup and ~12.7× parameter reduction over the closest diffusion-based competitor while improving PSNR by 1.2–1.5 dB on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMSR outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and both metrics (PSNR and SSIM). Against the strongest baseline DisC-Diff [27] (a diffusion model designed specifically for multi-contrast SR), DiffMSR gains +1.54 dB PSNR on FastMRI, +1.27 dB on Clinical Brain, +1.21 dB on Clinical Tumor, and +1.21 dB on Clinical Pelvic, while using 12.7× fewer parameters (6.8 M vs 86.1 M) and running 37× faster (39 ms vs 1455 ms). Against the best Transformer-based method MC-VarNet [15], the gains are +2.13 dB (FastMRI) to +1.96 dB (Pelvic) in PSNR. CNN-based methods (MCSR, MINet, MASA) trail by 3–5 dB in PSNR, reflecting their limited ability to capture long-range dependencies and high-frequency details. The paper also notes that DisC-Diff, while achieving high metric scores, introduces structural distortions and hallucinated details not present in the original HR images, whereas DiffMSR preserves anatomical structure while restoring high-frequency information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: latent diffusion models (LDM, Rombach et al. 2022), efficient diffusion for image restoration (DiffIR, Xia et al. 2023), and permuted self-attention for super-resolution (SRFormer, Zhou et al. 2023). The key conceptual departure from prior DM-based MRI SR methods (DisC-Diff, MC-DDPM, Score-MRI) is moving the diffusion process into a highly compact 4C-dimensional latent space rather than pixel or full-feature space, exploiting the observation that SR reconstruction only adds details to an existing image rather than generating one from scratch. The PLWformer decoder borrows the permuted K/V attention mechanism from SRFormer to expand the receptive field to 16×16 windows without proportional FLOPs increase, while the two-stage training protocol follows DiffIR's encoder-then-diffusion paradigm. The Cross-Attention Transformer Layer for fusing multi-contrast inputs is inherited from WavTrans (Li et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for clinical deployment: a 37× speedup over the previous best DM-based method (39 ms vs. 1455 ms) while simultaneously improving PSNR by 1–1.5 dB across four datasets makes diffusion-based MRI SR viable in time-sensitive clinical workflows. The conceptual framing—that SR reconstruction fundamentally requires fewer diffusion steps than image synthesis because the LR image already provides the structural prior—offers a generalizable principle for other restoration tasks. Limitations include evaluation only at 4× upsampling, relatively small clinical datasets (637 brain slices, 1600 pelvic slices), and the method's dependence on a paired reference contrast image being available, which may not always hold in practice. Open questions include whether the 4-iteration sufficiency generalizes to 3D volumetric MRI or to higher upsampling factors where more structural detail must be hallucinated.
