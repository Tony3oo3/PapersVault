## Problem

Deep image inverse tone mapping (ITM) methods can recover lost textures in over-exposed regions but, when applied frame-by-frame to video, produce temporally inconsistent results (flicker) and ignore potentially useful information in the temporal domain. The paper identifies that during video filming, smooth exposure changes mean regions over-exposed in one frame are often normally exposed in distant frames—these 'temporal clues' are underexploited by existing video ITM approaches that only look at short adjacent-frame windows.

## Input / Output

The method takes an LDR video sequence and produces a linear-luminance HDR video sequence, focusing specifically on reconstructing over-exposed regions via a soft over-exposed mask that blends the inverse-camera-curve-mapped LDR with the network prediction. Training pairs are synthesized at 512×512 resolution by simulating exposure changes on SICE HDR images (with random perspective transforms to mimic camera motion) and REDS LDR videos, while evaluation uses 512×512 frames from four real-world HDR video benchmarks (HDM-HDRv, LiU-HDRv, MPI-HDRv) and synthetic REDS-val sequences.

## Method

The core contribution is a Global Sampling and Local Propagation strategy: key frames are uniformly sampled from the sequence, and reference frames are sampled globally with large stride to capture temporal clues from distant frames where over-exposed regions are normally exposed. The Incremental Clue Aggregation Module (ICAM), built on a 5-level U-Net, aligns each reference frame to the key frame using a flow-correction convolution (refining GMA optical flow to handle large luminance differences), flow-guided deformable convolution for local alignment, and Swin Transformer blocks for global feature aggregation, fusing references incrementally. The Feature and Clue Propagation Module (FCPM) then propagates reconstructed key-frame features to neighboring frames via a BiRNN-like bidirectional propagation with corrected optical-flow warping, ensuring temporal consistency while reducing computational cost. Training uses L1 pixel loss, VGG-16 perceptual loss, and a 3D patchGAN adversarial loss (least-squares GAN) for the FCPM stage.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the best HDR-VDP-3 (higher is better) and HDR-VQM (lower is better) scores across all four test datasets. On REDS-val, it reaches 7.891 HDR-VDP-3 and 0.398 HDR-VQM, outperforming the strongest baseline BasicVSR++ (7.254 / 0.443) by +0.64 and −0.045 respectively. On the real-world MPI-HDRv dataset, the margin is largest: 7.970 HDR-VDP-3 vs. 7.225 for Single HDRI, and 0.037 vs. 0.049 HDR-VQM. The method also runs 2.4× faster than processing every frame with ICAM alone (521 ms vs. 1236 ms per 512×512 frame on a V100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on both HDR-VDP-3 and HDR-VQM across all four datasets. The closest competitor is BasicVSR++ [3] on REDS-val and LiU-HDRv, where the proposed method gains +0.64 and +0.33 in HDR-VDP-3 respectively; on HDM-HDRv and MPI-HDRv, Single HDRI [22] is the nearest baseline, with the proposed method leading by +0.41 and +0.75 in HDR-VDP-3. Among single-frame image ITM methods (HDRCNN, Diff HDRI, Single HDRI, FMNet), the largest gaps appear on MPI-HDRv, where the proposed method's 7.970 HDR-VDP-3 exceeds the best image method (Single HDRI, 7.225) by over 10%. The video-specific baselines Deep VITM [37] and BasicVSR++ [3] are consistently outperformed, confirming that the Global Sampling and Local Propagation strategy with explicit temporal clue exploitation yields substantially better reconstruction than 3D-convolution or generic video-restoration propagation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video super-resolution propagation paradigm from BasicVSR/BasicVSR++ to the inverse tone mapping domain, adapting their bidirectional feature propagation and alignment blocks to handle the unique challenge of brightness changes between frames. It departs from prior video ITM work (Deep VITM, which applies 3D convolution to short adjacent-frame windows) by introducing a Global Sampling and Local Propagation strategy that explicitly exploits long-range temporal clues arising from exposure changes during filming. The flow correction convolution is a novel adaptation of optical flow alignment to the ITM setting, where standard brightness-constancy assumptions break down due to over-exposure, and the combination block borrows its multi-scale feature fusion design from ExpandNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video ITM remains substantially underexplored compared to image ITM, and this paper provides the first systematic exploitation of temporal exposure changes as a source of recoverable information, filling a clear gap. The dataset synthesis method (combining LDR video datasets with HDR image datasets via simulated exposure) is a practical contribution that lowers the barrier to training video ITM models. Open questions include whether the fixed-stride global sampling generalizes to videos with irregular or rapid exposure changes, whether the approach handles real-world camera auto-exposure more robustly than the synthetic training distribution, and whether the computational cost (still ~521ms/frame) is acceptable for real-time applications.
