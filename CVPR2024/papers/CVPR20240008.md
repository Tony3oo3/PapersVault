## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D editing methods either perform only global edits or produce coarse localizations that lack the fine-grained detail needed for precise local stylization. Moreover, prior local editing approaches operate on voxels or NeRFs rather than meshes and do not natively produce texture maps compatible with standard graphics pipelines. Standard Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) also only leverages the first low-resolution stage of cascaded diffusion models, missing the high-resolution supervision available in super-resolution stages.

## Input / Output

The method takes a UV-mapped 3D mesh (vertices and faces) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired local edit. It produces two outputs: a high-resolution RGB texture map and a binary localization map, both extracted from neural fields (MLPs parameterized on 3D surface coordinates). Because the MLPs are defined in 3D space via inverted UV mapping, the resulting texture maps are inherently smooth across UV seams and can be queried at sub-texel resolution for super-resolution, making them directly usable in standard rendering pipelines.

## Method

3D Paintbrush represents both the localization and texture as MLPs over 3D surface coordinates (obtained by inverting the UV mapping), avoiding seam discontinuities while enabling super-resolution queries. Three visual losses are optimized simultaneously: a local texture loss on the localization-masked texture, a localization loss that enforces sharp boundaries via a yellow-highlight rendering, and a background loss that captures superfluous shape elements outside the edit region. The key technical contribution is Cascaded Score Distillation (CSD), which extends SDS to all stages of a cascaded diffusion model (e.g., DeepFloyd IF) by computing per-stage noise-prediction gradients on multi-resolution renderings of the mesh and combining them with user-defined weights λ^i. This allows the base stage to provide global semantic placement while super-resolution stages contribute fine-grained detail, with the weights offering intuitive control over the granularity of the supervision.

## Results

In a perceptual study with 39 users evaluating 9 meshes, 3D Paintbrush achieves a localization score of 4.80/5, substantially outperforming SATR (1.89) and 3D Highlighter (2.03). For local editing quality, the full method scores 4.88/5, compared to Latent Paint (2.14) and Vox-E (2.15); even the ablated variant using standard SDS alone (4.06) exceeds all baselines. The evaluation uses meshes sourced from TurboSquid, 3D ShapeNets, and Thingi10k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D Paintbrush decisively outperforms all four baselines on both evaluation axes. On localization, it scores 4.80 versus 1.89 for SATR and 2.03 for 3D Highlighter — a margin of roughly 2.8–2.9 points, indicating far sharper and more semantically accurate edit regions. On local editing quality, it achieves 4.88 compared to 2.14 (Latent Paint) and 2.15 (Vox-E), again a gap of about 2.7 points. Notably, even the ablated version using only standard SDS (4.06) still surpasses every baseline, suggesting that the mesh-native representation and simultaneous localization-texture optimization contribute substantially beyond the CSD loss alone. The paper also reports CLIP R-Precision and additional qualitative comparisons in the supplemental material, further supporting these perceptual findings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion to the full cascaded structure of modern text-to-image diffusion models, introducing Cascaded Score Distillation (CSD) that simultaneously distills scores across all resolution stages rather than only the base stage. For the localization component, it builds on the visual-loss formulation from 3D Highlighter but couples it tightly with a concurrent texture optimization, departing from prior local-editing methods (Vox-E, DreamEditor) that rely on attention maps without explicit visual supervision. The method is also the first to perform text-driven local editing natively on meshes with UV-mapped texture outputs, whereas prior local-editing work operates on voxels or NeRFs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSD is a general technique that can be applied to any optimization supervised by a cascaded diffusion model (images, video, alternative 3D representations), making it broadly useful beyond this specific texturing task. The simultaneous localization-and-texture formulation enables clean compositing of multiple local edits without fringe artifacts, which is practically important for production 3D pipelines. Key limitations include the 4-hour optimization time on a single GPU, susceptibility to the Janus effect, and semantic leakage where strongly associated objects (e.g., Egyptian necklace with a pharaoh headdress) get pulled into the edit region. Open questions include extending the approach to geometry deformation, normal maps, and reducing optimization cost.
