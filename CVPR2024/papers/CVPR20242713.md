## Problem

Conditional diffusion models for super-resolution suffer from a training-sampling discrepancy: during training the denoiser sees noisy images built from ground-truth HR references, but during sampling it must operate on its own imperfect predictions, causing error accumulation across the multi-step chain. This gap limits both training efficiency and final generation quality, yet prior remedies either modify the sampling procedure or require architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242713/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image as a fixed conditioning signal throughout the denoising chain and outputs a high-resolution RGB image at the target scale (4×, 8×, or 16×16→128×128). Crucially, DREAM modifies only the training objective—no changes to the network architecture, the forward/reverse diffusion schedule, or the sampling algorithm are required—so the same standard DDPM sampling loop (Algorithm 2) is used at inference time.

## Method

DREAM introduces two coupled mechanisms into the standard conditional DDPM training loop with just three additional lines of code. First, diffusion rectification performs an extra forward pass with a frozen copy of the denoiser to produce a self-estimated HR image, then reconstructs the noisy input from this estimate so the trainable network learns to correct both the injected Gaussian noise and the model's own estimation error (Δε). Second, estimation adaptation blends the self-estimate with the ground-truth HR image via a schedule λ_t = (√(1−ᾱ_t))^p that shifts weight toward ground truth at low noise levels (preserving perceptual detail) and toward the self-estimate at high noise levels (aligning training with sampling), thereby balancing the perception–distortion tradeoff. The gradient is stopped on the rectification term, and reusing the same Gaussian noise for both the original and rectified noisy images further simplifies the computation.

## Results

On DIV2K 4× SR, DREAM-enhanced SR3 achieves PSNR 28.10 dB and SSIM 0.79, outperforming the standard SR3 (27.02 dB, 0.76) by over 1 dB, while DREAM-enhanced ResShift reaches PSNR 28.44 dB and SSIM 0.79 versus ResShift's 25.30 dB and 0.68. On CelebA-HQ 8× face SR, DREAM boosts SR3's PSNR from 23.85 to 24.63 dB and reduces FID from 61.98 to 56.01. DREAM also accelerates training convergence by 2–3× (reaching convergence at ~100k–150k iterations vs. 400k for standard SR3) and requires 10–20× fewer sampling steps for comparable quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREAM consistently outperforms its diffusion-based baselines: SR3+DREAM gains +1.08 dB PSNR over SR3 on DIV2K and +0.78 dB on CelebA-HQ (24.63 vs. 23.85), while ResShift+DREAM gains a dramatic +3.14 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.211 to 0.124. Against GAN-based methods (ESRGAN, RankSRGAN) and flow-based methods (SRFlow, HCFlow, HCFlow++), DREAM-enhanced diffusion models achieve substantially higher PSNR (28.10–28.44 vs. 26.22–27.09) while matching or exceeding their perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.121–0.124 vs. 0.110–0.128). The only method that beats DREAM on PSNR is the regression-based RRDB (29.44 dB), but RRDB's LPIPS (0.253) is far worse, confirming the perception-distortion tradeoff. On CelebA-HQ, DREAM-enhanced SR3 also outperforms IDM in PSNR (24.63 vs. 24.50) and FID (56.01 vs. 53.22 for IDM+DREAM), and achieves the best consistency score (2.12) among all methods in Table 2. The paper notes that HCFlow++ achieves a slightly better LPIPS (0.110) but attributes this to its GAN loss, which is orthogonal to DREAM's training framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

DREAM builds directly on the conditional DDPM framework of SR3 (Saharia et al., 2022) and the foundational DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020), modifying only the training objective while leaving the network architecture and sampling procedure unchanged. The diffusion rectification component adapts step-unrolling techniques previously explored in depth estimation (Saxena et al., 2023; Ji et al., 2023) and text generation (Savinov et al., 2022) to conditional low-level vision tasks, while the estimation adaptation component introduces an adaptive schedule that blends ground-truth and self-estimated signals. Prior work on training-sampling discrepancy (Ning et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023) addressed this gap in unconditional diffusion models via reweighting or sampling adjustments; DREAM is the first to tailor a self-prediction-based rectification specifically to conditional SR and to pair it with an adaptive ground-truth injection that explicitly manages the perception-distortion tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a three-line code modification that accelerates training convergence by 2–3× and reduces required sampling steps by 10–20× without any architectural or inference-time changes makes DREAM immediately adoptable across existing diffusion-based SR pipelines. The finding that naive self-alignment (DRM, p=0) degrades perceptual quality despite improving PSNR provides a useful diagnostic for the perception-distortion tradeoff in diffusion training, and the adaptive schedule offers a principled way to navigate it. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other conditional diffusion tasks (inpainting, style transfer, video SR), whether the heuristic λ_t schedule can be learned end-to-end, and whether the extra forward pass during training becomes a bottleneck at scale. The method has been validated only on SR with pixel-space and latent-space diffusion baselines, so its transferability to other generative settings remains untested.
