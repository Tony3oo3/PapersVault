## Problem

Existing defenses against adversarial patch attacks on object detectors rely on attack-specific training data, prior knowledge of patch entropy distributions, or model modifications, making them ineffective against unseen patch types such as naturalistic patches. No prior method can localize and remove diverse adversarial patches (noise, printable, naturalistic) of arbitrary shape, size, location, and quantity without any prior attack knowledge or additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241731/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (digital or physically captured) that may contain one or more adversarial patches of arbitrary shape, size, and location, and produces a defended image where detected patch regions are filled with black pixels, along with a binary patch localization mask. It operates as a training-free preprocessing step that can be plugged in front of any pre-trained object detector (Faster R-CNN, YOLOv2/v3/v5/v8) without modifying the detector architecture or requiring adversarial training data.

## Method

PAD exploits two inherent, appearance-agnostic properties of adversarial patches: semantic independence (patches carry no semantic correlation with surrounding regions) and spatial heterogeneity (patches exhibit different image quality due to independent generation). Semantic Independence Localization computes mutual information between a sliding window and its four neighbors to produce a heat map highlighting regions with abnormally low semantic correlation. Spatial Heterogeneity Localization recompresses the image at multiple JPEG quality factors and measures per-pixel squared differences to identify regions whose compression behavior deviates from the rest of the image. A Fusion Block normalizes and weight-sums both heat maps, applies adaptive percentile-based thresholding and OPEN-CLOSE-OPEN morphological operations, then matches the resulting localization map against zero-shot SAM segmentations using an Intersection-over-Area criterion to obtain precise patch boundaries. The final patch regions are removed via simple black-pixel inpainting, requiring no training, no attack-specific data, and no assumptions on patch count or proportion.

## Results

On the INRIA Person dataset, PAD achieves the highest mAP@0.5 across all tested detectors (Faster R-CNN, YOLOv3, YOLOv5s) and all 11 adversarial patch types. On Faster R-CNN, PAD recovers mAP to 96.36/96.26% on DPatch (localized noise), 84.55–88.95% on printable patches, and 68.40–89.21% on naturalistic patches, consistently outperforming all baselines. For the most challenging naturalistic patches (P1–P6), PAD improves mAP by more than 10% (absolute) over the best competing method on average. Clean-image performance is preserved with negligible degradation (≤0.45% mAP drop).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAD outperforms all four baselines across every patch type and detector. LGS [33] (denoising-based) matches PAD on localized noise patches (95.96 vs 96.36 mAP on DPatch-75) but degrades sharply on naturalistic patches, e.g., 61.34 vs 68.40 on P1 and 58.66 vs 83.23 on P6. SAC [28] (external segmenter) is competitive on noise patches (96.23 on DPatch-75) but its segmenter, trained only on noise-like data, nearly fails on unseen naturalistic patches (48.00 on P6 vs PAD's 83.23). Jedi [42] (entropy-based) shows unstable results due to sensitivity to high-entropy background regions, scoring 66.12 on P6 versus PAD's 83.23, and requires manual tuning of entropy thresholds per patch type. ObjectSeeker [50] (certifiably robust) performs worst, sometimes below undefended (e.g., 38.53 on P6 vs 47.66 undefended), because its partition-based guarantee breaks down with multiple patches of varying sizes. On the physical APRICOT dataset, PAD reduces ASR to 2.27% without any training, matching SAC (which was trained on APRICOT masks) within 0.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAD departs from the three dominant lines of adversarial patch defense—denoising-based (LGS), trained-segmenter-based (SAC, APM), and entropy-based (Jedi)—by requiring no attack-specific training data, no prior entropy distribution knowledge, and no model modification. Conceptually, it repurposes two well-studied signals from unrelated fields: mutual information from information theory (to quantify semantic independence between adjacent image regions) and JPEG recompression residuals from image forensics (to detect spatial quality heterogeneity). The use of SAM as a zero-shot segmentation backbone for boundary refinement, rather than a trained patch segmenter, further distinguishes it from prior localization approaches that depend on adversarial training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, detector-agnostic design makes PAD immediately deployable on any pre-trained object detector without retraining, addressing a key practical barrier of adversarial training and segmenter-based defenses. A notable limitation is the reliance on the assumption that patches are both semantically independent and quality-heterogeneous relative to their surroundings; patches generated with matching compression and semantically coherent content could evade detection. The simple black-pixel inpainting, while effective in experiments, may degrade performance in scenes where the patch occludes critical object regions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video-based detection, 3D perception, or patches that are deliberately designed to mimic local image statistics.
