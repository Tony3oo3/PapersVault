## Problem

Query-based temporal action detection (TAD) detectors, inspired by DETR's set-prediction paradigm, have failed to achieve full end-to-end modeling because they still rely on hand-crafted components like sliding windows and NMS. The paper identifies the root cause as the conventional normalized coordinate expression (mapping to [0,1]), which introduces matching instability and heightened sensitivity to localization errors as video duration increases, causing performance to degrade with extended temporal coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240994/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed video sequences processed by a pre-trained backbone (I3D, SlowFast, or R(2+1)D) into temporal feature sequences of shape T₀×C, where each timestep corresponds to a snippet of consecutive frames. The output is a variable-length set of action instances, each containing a class label and start/end timestamps expressed in actual video timeline values (seconds) rather than normalized coordinates. The number of predicted queries adapts to video length via sector-based selection, so longer videos naturally produce more proposals without a fixed query budget.

## Method

TE-TAD reformulates the coordinate expression from normalized [0,1] values to actual timeline values, using center offsets scaled by reference width and an exp-based width update (d̂ = exp(ln(d_prev) + Δd)) for scale-invariant refinement across decoder layers. A two-stage design generates initial proposals from multi-scale encoder features (produced by stride-2 convolutions), with reference centers and widths aligned to the real video timeline via frame-rate and stride parameters. An adaptive query selection (AQS) mechanism divides the video into equal-length sectors and performs top-k selection within each sector, dynamically scaling the total query count with video duration. The decoder preserves full self-attention across all queries to maintain the one-to-one bipartite matching paradigm, eliminating the need for NMS and sliding windows entirely. Training uses standard bipartite matching loss with focal classification loss and DIoU + log-ratio regression loss, with auxiliary losses at every decoder layer.

## Results

On THUMOS14, TE-TAD achieves 66.4 mAP@AVG without NMS, a +9.7 point improvement over the best prior query-based method (TadTR at 56.7), and reaches 67.9 with NMS, approaching the anchor-free TriDet (69.3). On ActivityNet v1.3, TE-TAD attains 37.0 mAP@AVG (37.1 with NMS), slightly surpassing TadTR (36.8) and competitive with anchor-free MENet (37.7). On EpicKitchens, TE-TAD achieves 24.1 (verb) and 22.6 (noun) mAP@AVG without NMS, remaining competitive with TriDet (25.4/23.8) despite eliminating NMS entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TE-TAD decisively outperforms all prior query-based detectors on THUMOS14: it surpasses TadTR (56.7) and Self-DETR (56.7) by +9.7 mAP@AVG without NMS, and ReAct (55.0) by +11.4. Against anchor-free methods, TE-TAD without NMS (66.4) is within 2.9 points of TriDet (69.3) and 0.4 points of ActionFormer (66.8), while with NMS (67.9) it narrows the gap to TriDet to just 1.4 points. On ActivityNet v1.3, TE-TAD (37.0) edges out TadTR (36.8) by a narrow margin, consistent with the dataset's less diverse video lengths reducing the advantage of time-aligned coordinates. On EpicKitchens, TE-TAD without NMS (24.1 verb / 22.6 noun) trails TriDet (25.4 / 23.8) by 1.3 and 1.2 points respectively, but remains competitive with ActionFormer (23.5 / 21.9) and ASL (24.6 / 22.6). Notably, TE-TAD achieves these results without any NMS or sliding window, whereas all anchor-based and anchor-free baselines require NMS, and prior query-based methods (TadTR, Self-DETR) still rely on partial NMS via cross-window fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TadTR (Liu et al., IEEE TIP 2022), adopting its encoder-decoder architecture with temporal deformable attention, and on Deformable DETR (Zhu et al., ICLR 2020) for the two-stage proposal generation paradigm. The key conceptual departure is from the universally adopted normalized coordinate expression (sigmoid-based [0,1] coordinates inherited from DETR) used in all prior query-based TAD detectors (RTD-Net, ReAct, TadTR). The paper identifies this normalization as the root cause of performance degradation under extended temporal coverage, and replaces it with actual timeline values, while also introducing an adaptive query selection mechanism that departs from the fixed top-k selection standard in object detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic (normalized coordinates cause matching instability and sensitivity that worsen with video length) and a clean remedy, enabling the first truly end-to-end query-based TAD without NMS or sliding windows. The length-invariant property is practically important for real-world video understanding where durations vary widely. Limitations include modest gains on ActivityNet (where video lengths are less diverse) and competitive-but-not-SOTA results on EpicKitchens, suggesting the approach's advantage is most pronounced in length-diverse settings. Open questions include whether the time-aligned coordinate reformulation transfers to other variable-length sequence detection tasks and whether the adaptive query selection can be further optimized for extremely long videos.
