## Problem

Adversarial training consistently degrades standard generalization accuracy, yet existing explanations attribute this trade-off to isolated factors (training bias, data size, local Lipschitzness) without a unified framework. This paper asks whether the degradation can be interpreted through a single principled lens—model complexity—and whether such a lens can yield a practical regularization that mitigates the trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method operates on normalized RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN) and outputs class labels over K classes. Unusually, the proposed regularization acts exclusively on softmax-transformed logits rather than raw weights or gradients, making it a black-box procedure that requires no prior knowledge of model parameters. The regularization is applied only during early and late training epochs (controlled by breakpoints E₁ and E₂), leaving the middle training phase unmodified to minimize computational overhead.

## Method

The paper first establishes non-trivial upper and lower bounds on the Cross-Entropy Rademacher complexity of a ReLU-activated MLP using the Fisher-Rao norm, revealing that a logit-based variable Γ_ce (the ratio of correctly- to misclassified-sample logit margins) governs the rate of change of these bounds. Empirically, Γ_ce is shown to be sensitive to model width and the adversarial trade-off factor λ, and to correlate positively with the CE generalization gap in early epochs but negatively in late epochs. Exploiting this epoch-dependent relationship, the authors propose LOAT, which combines two regularization terms: (1) Standard Logit-Oriented Regularization (SLORE), which penalizes the l2-disparity between softmax logits of correctly and misclassified samples in opposite directions depending on the training phase, and (2) Adaptive Adversarial Logit Pairing (ALP), which synchronizes clean and adversarial logit distributions for the relevant sample subset. The combined variant (LORE) is plugged as an additive term into any existing adversarial training objective (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, DM-AT) with negligible per-epoch overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, LOAT-enhanced methods consistently improve both clean and adversarial accuracy: MART+LORE boosts clean test accuracy from 80.61% to 81.53% and PGD-20 robustness from 51.65% to 52.15%; TRADES+SLORE improves FGSM accuracy by 4.30% (57.89%→62.19%); PGD-AT+SLORE raises PGD-7 accuracy from 49.86% to 51.46%. On WideResNet-34-10, PGD-AT+LORE achieves the largest clean accuracy gain (+0.82%, 84.47%→85.29%), while MART+SLORE improves PGD-7 robustness from 71.55% to 72.87%. In the EDM-augmented setting (Table 2), gains are even larger: TRADES+LORE on WRN-28-10(Swish) improves clean accuracy by 1.80% (88.87%→90.67%) and MART+LORE by 1.35% (88.83%→90.18%). Computational overhead is minimal, with per-epoch time increasing by only 1–10 seconds depending on architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRADES (Zhang et al., ICML 2019), LOAT consistently improves clean accuracy across all three architectures (e.g., +0.28% for ResNet-18, +0.69% for ResNet-50, +0.65% for WRN-34-10 with SLORE) and substantially boosts FGSM robustness on ResNet-18 (+4.3–4.6%), though PGD-20 accuracy sometimes drops slightly. Against MART (Wang et al., ICLR 2020), LORE delivers the strongest gains: +0.92% clean accuracy on ResNet-18, +0.80% on ResNet-50, and +0.81% on WRN-34-10, while also improving PGD-7 robustness by 0.72% on ResNet-18 and 1.32% on WRN-34-10. Against PGD-AT (Madry et al., ICLR 2018), SLORE provides the most reliable improvements, raising PGD-7 accuracy by 1.60% on ResNet-18 and 1.70% on ResNet-50, with clean accuracy gains of 0.22–0.82% depending on architecture. Against TRADES(LSE) (Pang et al., ICML 2022), LOAT matches or slightly exceeds clean accuracy while improving FGSM robustness by ~4.6% on ResNet-18. Against S2O (Jin et al., 2022), LOAT integration (Table 5) yields consistent small gains in both clean and adversarial accuracy across TRADES, MART, and PGD-AT, confirming architecture- and algorithm-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Fisher-Rao norm framework (Liang et al., AISTATS 2019) as a geometrically invariant complexity measure, applying it to derive Rademacher complexity bounds for Cross-Entropy loss in ReLU-activated MLPs—a setting where prior norm-based capacity analyses (spectral norm, path-SGD) have been too architecture-specific. It incorporates Adversarial Logit Pairing (Kannan et al., 2018) as a component of its regularization and echoes the softmax-transformed surrogate approach from TRADES (LSE) (Pang et al., ICML 2022). The key departure is the introduction of the logit-based variable Γ_ce, which links model complexity to classification performance on clean samples, and the epoch-dependent regularization strategy that activates only in the initial and final phases of training, distinguishing it from weight-landscape regularizers (S2O, AWP) that operate throughout training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical lens (Fisher-Rao norm → Rademacher complexity bounds) for understanding the robustness-accuracy trade-off, and translates this into a practical, black-box regularization (LOAT) that integrates with existing adversarial training algorithms at negligible computational cost. However, the theoretical analysis is confined to MLPs, leaving open whether the Fisher-Rao norm bounds and the Γ_ce correlation structure hold for the CNN and Transformer architectures used in practice. The empirical gains, while consistent across architectures and algorithms, are modest (typically 0.5–1.5% in clean accuracy, up to ~2.3% in DM-AT settings), and the epoch-dependent sign flip in the Γ_ce–generalization-gap correlation, while conceptually interesting, has not been validated on larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet.
