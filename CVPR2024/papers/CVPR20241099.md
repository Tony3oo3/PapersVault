## Problem

Single-view clothed human reconstruction remains challenging for loose-fitting garments, and the Fourier Occupancy Field (FOF) representation, while compact, loses high-frequency geometric detail when estimated with a standard 2D CNN. Additionally, existing back-view prediction methods ignore texture/style consistency between the predicted back and the reference front view, leading to implausible clothing appearance on unseen sides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241099/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image of a clothed human and produces a textured 3D mesh. Internally it generates a predicted back-view image (via an HR-Net) and a Fourier occupancy field C ∈ R^{H×W×31} (N=15 basis functions per pixel column), which is converted to a binary occupancy volume and extracted as a mesh via marching cubes. Texture is assigned by rasterizing both the reference and predicted back-view images onto the mesh surface.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) a Siamese HR-Net predicts the back-view image, trained with L1 loss plus a style-consistency loss (Gram matrix of VGG19 first-layer features) between the predicted back and the reference front to enforce clothing color/pattern coherence; (2) a conditional diffusion model generates the FOF in the Haar wavelet domain—DWT decomposes the 31-channel FOF into four quarter-resolution sub-bands, a U-Net denoiser (conditioned on multi-scale image features and timestep) predicts the clean wavelet coefficients z̃₀ rather than noise, and DDIM sampling is used at inference; (3) a refinement module takes the IWT-reconstructed initial FOF and sharpens it using high-resolution image features. The wavelet domain reduces spatial dimensions by 4×, cutting compute while explicitly separating low- and high-frequency geometry for more faithful detail recovery.

## Results

On geometry reconstruction, Diffusion-FOF achieves the best Chamfer distance across all three datasets: 2.131 cm on THuman2.0, 0.934 cm on 2K2K, and 0.906 cm on CLOTH4D, outperforming the strongest baseline GTA by 0.167 cm, 0.196 cm, and 0.196 cm respectively. On the 2K2K texture evaluation, the method reaches PSNR 26.362 / SSIM 0.929 / LPIPS 0.142 for back-view prediction (vs. SiCloPe's 17.579 / 0.844 / 0.281) and PSNR 25.252 / SSIM 0.925 / LPIPS 0.157 for rendered texture (vs. GTA's 18.352 / 0.842 / 0.236). Normal error is also lowest on all three datasets (2.031, 1.502, 1.517 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion-FOF outperforms all nine baselines on every geometric metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is GTA [45], which Diffusion-FOF beats by 0.167 cm Chamfer on THuman2.0 (2.131 vs. 2.298), 0.196 cm on 2K2K (0.934 vs. 1.130), and 0.196 cm on CLOTH4D (0.906 vs. 1.102). ECON [39] is second-closest on CLOTH4D Chamfer (1.132) but trails by 0.226 cm. On texture, the method substantially surpasses SiCloPe [24] in back-view prediction (PSNR 26.36 vs. 17.58, a ~9 dB gain) and both PIFu [27] and GTA [45] in rendered texture (PSNR 25.25 vs. 14.33 and 18.35 respectively). The original FOF [9] baseline, which uses a plain 2D CNN without diffusion or wavelet decomposition, is outperformed by 0.343 cm Chamfer on THuman2.0 and 0.198 cm on 2K2K, confirming the benefit of the wavelet-domain diffusion generation. D-IF [42] achieves competitive P2S on 2K2K (0.912) but lags significantly on Normal error (2.125 vs. 1.502), indicating less accurate surface orientation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Fourier Occupancy Field (FOF) representation of Feng et al., which encodes 3D geometry as a 2D vector field via Fourier series, but departs from its use of a plain 2D CNN by replacing the estimator with a conditional diffusion model operating in the Haar wavelet domain. It also extends the back-view estimation line of work (SiCloPe) by introducing a VGG-based style consistency loss and a Siamese training strategy to enforce texture coherence between front and back views. The diffusion model component draws on DDPM/DDIM formulations previously applied to depth estimation and 3D shape generation, but is the first to generate FOF coefficients conditioned on multi-scale image features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Single-view clothed human reconstruction is a practically important task for AR/VR and digital avatar creation, and this paper demonstrates that combining generative modeling (diffusion) with a compact geometric representation (FOF) in the wavelet domain can close the gap to multi-view methods while remaining single-image. The style consistency constraint for back-view prediction is a simple but effective idea that could transfer to other front-back estimation tasks. However, the authors note that severe self-occlusion still degrades geometric accuracy, and the iterative diffusion sampling (even with DDIM) may limit real-time applicability compared to the original FOF's single-pass CNN inference.
