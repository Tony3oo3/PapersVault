## Problem

SAM's zero-shot performance degrades significantly on medical images due to its natural-image-only training, and existing prompt-free SAM adaptations (e.g., SAMed, AutoSAM) yield inferior results compared to prompted methods because they lack the medical knowledge that prompts provide. The paper addresses how to efficiently integrate medical priors into SAM's decoding pipeline without requiring prompts, extensive training data, or full model fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240298/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D medical image slice (CT or MRI) at 512×512 for few-shot training or 224×224 for fully-supervised training, with no external prompts—only a learned default embedding is fed to the prompt encoder. The output is a full-resolution multi-organ segmentation mask (H×W), produced by ensembling the probability maps from both hierarchical decoding stages. The first stage operates at H/4×W/4 resolution while the second stage upsamples to full resolution via a U-shaped pixel decoder with skip connections.

## Method

H-SAM introduces a two-stage hierarchical mask decoder on top of a frozen SAM image encoder with LoRA adapters. In Stage 1, SAM's original lightweight decoder generates a prior probabilistic mask; in Stage 2, this prior guides two novel mechanisms: (1) a Class-Balanced Mask-Guided Self-Attention (CMAttn) block that perturbs the prior mask with Gaussian noise whose variance is inversely proportional to category frequency, then uses self-attention to recalibrate image embeddings for balanced category representation; and (2) a Learnable Mask Cross-Attention that replaces Mask2Former's binary mask-attention with an element-wise product of the untransformed probabilistic mask and the softmax attention map, enabling gradient flow and spatially varying foreground importance. A hierarchical pixel decoder with U-shaped skip connections from Stage 1 to Stage 2 further refines the output from H/4×W/4 to full resolution. Training uses deep supervision with combined CE and Dice losses at both stages, with a stage-weight λ_w that decays exponentially from 0.8.

## Results

On the Synapse multi-organ CT dataset, H-SAM achieves 80.35% Mean Dice with only 10% of 2D slices (1 volume), outperforming the best prompt-free SAM variant SAMed (75.57%) by 4.78%. Under fully-supervised training, H-SAM reaches 86.49% Mean Dice and 8.18 HD, surpassing recent medical segmentation networks DAE-Former (82.43%) and MERIT (84.90%). On the LA dataset, H-SAM achieves 89.22% Dice using only 4 labeled scans and no unlabeled data, exceeding the best semi-supervised method BCP (88.02%) which uses 76 additional unlabeled scans. On PROMISE12, H-SAM reaches 87.27% Dice with just 3 labeled cases, outperforming the semi-supervised MLB-Seg (78.27%) by ~9% despite using no unlabeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompt-free SAM variants, H-SAM consistently wins: on Synapse 10%, it beats SAMed by 4.78% Dice (80.35 vs 75.57), SAM Adapter by 22.07% (80.35 vs 58.28), and AutoSAM by 24.66% (80.35 vs 55.69); under fully-supervised training the gap over SAMed narrows to 4.61% (86.49 vs 81.88). Against non-SAM medical segmentation networks, H-SAM surpasses DAE-Former (86.49 vs 82.43) and MERIT (86.49 vs 84.90) on fully-supervised Synapse, and also outperforms nnU-Net on both LA (89.22 vs 64.02) and PROMISE12 (87.27 vs 84.22). Against semi-supervised methods that leverage extensive unlabeled data, H-SAM wins on LA (89.22 vs BCP 88.02, SS-Net 86.33, MC-Net 83.59) using only 4 labeled scans and zero unlabeled scans, and on PROMISE12 (87.27 vs MLB-Seg 78.27, SS-Net 73.19, MC-Net 72.66) using only 3 labeled cases versus 37 unlabeled cases for the competitors. The efficiency analysis (Table 6) further shows H-SAM (112.3M params, 4 transformer layers) outperforms SAMed with 6 layers (116.2M params, 78.05% Dice) and SAM Adapter (131.5M params, 72.80% Dice), confirming superior performance per parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-SAM extends the prompt-free SAM adaptation line initiated by SAMed (LoRA on the image encoder with the original decoder) and AutoSAM (frozen encoder with added prediction heads) by fundamentally rethinking the decoder rather than the encoder. It repurposes the mask-attention mechanism from Mask2Former into a learnable, gradient-friendly variant and introduces a two-stage hierarchical decoding procedure where the first stage's probabilistic mask acts as a spatial prior for the second stage. The hierarchical pixel decoder borrows U-Net skip connections to recover fine-grained detail lost at SAM's native H/4 resolution, a design choice absent from prior SAM medical adaptations that largely leave the pixel decoder untouched.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that a purely supervised few-shot prompt-free SAM adaptation (3–4 labeled cases) can match or exceed semi-supervised methods trained on 37–76 additional unlabeled scans, suggesting that strong pretraining plus targeted decoder adaptation may reduce the need for the unlabeled data pipelines that semi-supervised medical segmentation typically requires. Limitations include evaluation on only 2D slices across three datasets, a single LoRA rank, and the semi-supervised comparison being somewhat asymmetric (those methods are designed to exploit unlabeled data, so outperforming them without it is expected to some degree). Open questions include whether the hierarchical decoding strategy transfers to 3D volumetric segmentation, whether it generalizes beyond abdominal CT and cardiac/prostate MRI, and whether the class-balanced noise augmentation remains effective as the number of target classes grows.
