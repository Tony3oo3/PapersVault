## Problem

Existing GAN inversion methods struggle to align generated faces with multiple simultaneous conditions (text prompts and visual inputs like semantic masks or scribble maps) because they lack a principled way to fuse heterogeneous modalities into a GAN's latent space. Meanwhile, diffusion models handle multi-modal conditioning flexibly but rely on autoencoders trained on unstructured web data, producing unrealistic facial images and offering no 3D-aware generation capability. The paper bridges this gap by using diffusion model features as an encoder that drives GAN inversion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing facial attributes (e.g., 'black hair, sideburns') paired with a visual condition — either a 19-class semantic mask from CelebAMask-HQ or a scribble map extracted via PiDiNet — and produces 256×256 photo-realistic 2D face images, 3D-aware multi-view face images from arbitrary camera angles (via EG3D), or stylized face images. The visual condition is consumed through ControlNet's conditional branches while the text is injected via cross-attention layers in the denoising U-Net, and the final output is rendered entirely by a frozen pre-trained GAN (StyleGAN or EG3D).

## Method

The approach uses ControlNet's denoising U-Net (middle and decoder blocks) as a frozen diffusion-based encoder that, at each denoising step t, extracts semantic feature maps h_t, multi-scale intermediate features f_t, and cross-attention maps a_t. A trainable mapping network M (structured like pSp's map2style) converts h_t into a 512-dim latent code w_t^m, while a novel Attention-based Style Modulation Network (AbSMNet) T uses f_t and a_t to produce scale (w_t^γ) and shift (w_t^β) codes that modulate w_t^m element-wise to form the final latent code w_t fed to the frozen GAN. The cross-attention maps are used to pixel-wise refine intermediate features, amplifying text- and structure-relevant regions before feeding them into separate local and global scale/shift sub-networks whose outputs are combined via learnable weights. A key training insight is the multi-denoising-step strategy: early steps (t=T) emphasize text-relevant features while later steps (t=0) preserve structural details, so the model is trained across all steps to capture both. Training is two-stage — M is first trained against the GT image, then T is trained against the diffusion model's own output I_0^d for attribute control while an ArcFace identity loss preserves the subject's identity.

## Results

On CelebAMask-HQ with text + semantic mask inputs, the proposed method (2D GAN) achieves FID 46.68, LPIPS 0.30, ID 0.76, ACC 83.41%, and mIoU 43.82%, while the 3D GAN variant achieves FID 44.91, LPIPS 0.28, ID 0.78, ACC 83.05%, and mIoU 43.74%. For text + scribble map inputs, the 2D variant reaches FID 55.60 and ID 0.72, substantially outperforming ControlNet (FID 93.26, ID 0.21). The method achieves the best LPIPS, ID, ACC, and mIoU scores across all compared baselines in both 2D and 3D domains, though IDE-3D retains a lower FID (39.05) in the 3D setting due to its use of an additional RGB image input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TediGAN (GAN, 2D), the proposed method improves FID from 54.83 to 46.68, ID from 0.63 to 0.76, and mIoU from 40.01 to 43.82, while matching SSIM (0.63 vs 0.62). Against the diffusion-based baselines UaC and ControlNet, the method achieves substantially higher ID scores (0.76 vs 0.32 and 0.30) and better LPIPS (0.30 vs 0.38/0.41), though UaC and ControlNet marginally edge out on FID (45.87/46.41 vs 46.68). Against Collaborative diffusion, the method dominates on all metrics, with FID 46.68 vs 48.23 and ID 0.76 vs 0.31. In the 3D domain, the method outperforms IDE-3D on LPIPS (0.28 vs 0.40), SSIM (0.64 vs 0.41), ID (0.78 vs 0.54), ACC (83.05% vs 47.07%), and mIoU (43.74% vs 10.98%), though IDE-3D achieves a lower FID (39.05 vs 44.91) because it additionally uses an RGB image as input. For scribble-map-based generation, the method dramatically outperforms ControlNet, reducing FID from 93.26 to 55.60 and improving ID from 0.21 to 0.72.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a pre-trained diffusion model (ControlNet) as a feature-extraction encoder whose outputs are mapped into the latent space of a pre-trained GAN (StyleGAN or EG3D), inverting the typical role where diffusion models serve as generators. It builds directly on learning-based GAN inversion (pSp's map2style architecture) and multi-modal face generation methods (TediGAN, UaC, Collaborative), but departs from them by leveraging the cross-attention and intermediate features of a denoising U-Net across multiple denoising steps rather than relying on CLIP-based optimization or direct diffusion generation. The multi-denoising-step training strategy, which exploits the observation that early steps encode text-relevant semantics while later steps preserve structural details, is a novel training protocol not present in prior GAN inversion or diffusion-based face generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling multi-modal feature extraction (DM) from high-fidelity image synthesis (GAN), this approach offers a practical path to controllable face generation that inherits the flexibility of diffusion conditioning and the photorealism of GANs, applicable to both 2D and 3D domains without additional data or loss terms. The notable limitation is that the method cannot transfer extremely distinct artistic styles into the photorealistic GAN domain, and 3D style transfer remains an open problem requiring better mapping of pose-related diffusion features into GAN latent space. For practitioners, the modular design (frozen DM encoder + trainable mapping/modulation networks + frozen GAN) makes it relatively easy to adapt to new GAN backbones or conditioning modalities.
