## Problem

Applying dropout to the video modality in AVSR improves robustness to missing frames but simultaneously degrades performance on complete multimodal input, and AVSR systems still underperform unimodal ASR when video is entirely absent. The paper identifies the root cause as dropout-induced modality bias—an excessive shift of the latent decision distribution from a multimodal to a unimodal (audio-dominated) distribution—and formalizes this through the Modality Bias Hypothesis (MBH).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes far-field audio and far-field video from real-life home TV recordings with high speech overlap (42% training, 49% test) and naturally blurred or obstructed frames. Missing video frames are padded with zeros (no interpolation or repetition), and the model must handle test-time dropout rates from 0% to 100% across segment, utterance, and interval dropout patterns. Output is a speech transcription evaluated by CER (utterance-level, MISP2021) or concatenated minimum-permutation CER (session-level, MISP2022).

## Method

The proposed MDA-KD framework trains a teacher model on complete audio-visual pairs and a student model on video-dropped pairs, then constrains the student's intermediate representation distribution (sampled from cross-modal encoder layers) to approximate the teacher's via a temperature-scaled KL divergence loss, preventing the latent decisive features from collapsing into an audio-only unimodal distribution. For severely or entirely missing video, a Modality-Specific Adapter (MS-Adapter) based on LoRA is inserted into the audio branch's self-attention layers, enabling a dynamic switch from the synergistic decision pattern P(y|z^u) to the audio-specific pattern P(y|z^a) while cutting off the video branch computation to reduce inference cost. The overall system uses a dual-branch Conformer architecture (N=3, M=9, K=6) with a dual cross-attention fusion module, trained with a combined CTC + attention loss plus the KD loss, and evaluated with ROVER rescoring across multiple model configurations.

## Results

On MISP2021 (utterance-level AVSR with oracle diarization), the proposed system achieves 21.53% CER, reducing the previous SOTA (USTC, 24.58%) by 3.05 points absolute. On MISP2022 (session-level AVSR), it achieves 28.06% cpCER with ROVER rescoring, outperforming the next-best system (NPU, 29.13%) by 1.07 points. In the dropout robustness comparison (Table 3), the method achieves 21.11% CER with complete video and 24.94% CER with fully missing video, outperforming all three baseline dropout strategies across every test dropout rate from 0.00 to 1.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine compared baselines across both datasets. Against the three dropout-based robustness methods (Table 3), it achieves 21.11% CER at 0% missing video (vs. 22.00–22.54% for Cascade Utt [19], AV Dropout Utt [21], and Dropout Utt [20]) and 24.94% at 100% missing video (vs. 25.46–28.15%), winning by 0.5–3.2 points at every dropout rate. On MISP2021, it surpasses USTC [18] (24.58%), NIO [43] (25.07%), and SJTU [42] (34.02%) by 3.05, 3.54, and 12.49 CER points respectively, while using only 1000 hours of audio and no extra video data. On MISP2022, it beats NPU [46] (29.13%), NIO [44] (29.58%), and XMU [45] (31.88%) by 1.07, 1.52, and 3.82 cpCER points. Notably, the method also surpasses the unimodal ASR baseline (A0, 25.13% CER) when video is entirely missing (24.94%), a feat none of the dropout baselines achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Modality Venn Diagram framework from multimodal knowledge distillation [28], extending it into a Modality Bias Venn Diagram (MBVD) that decomposes task-relevant features into modality-specific and modality-general components. It departs from prior dropout-based robustness approaches (Chang et al. [19], Shi et al. [21], Makino et al. [20]) by diagnosing *why* dropout degrades complete-input performance—identifying a distributional shift from multimodal to unimodal in the latent space—rather than treating dropout as a plug-and-play fix. The MDA-KD framework repurposes knowledge distillation in a non-standard way: the teacher and student share the same architecture, and the distillation signal is a hidden-representation distribution constraint rather than a logit-level loss, while the MS-Adapter directly extends LoRA [39] from foundation-model fine-tuning to modality-specific decision switching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of dropout-induced modality bias as the root cause of the robustness–performance tradeoff provides a unifying explanation that generalizes beyond AVSR to any modality-biased multimodal system (sentiment analysis, speech enhancement, etc.), and the MBH offers a systematic vocabulary for reasoning about this tradeoff. Practically, the MDA-KD + MS-Adapter pipeline achieves SOTA on MISP2021/2022 while closing the gap to pure ASR on fully missing video, a combination no prior dropout method achieved. Limitations include validation only on Mandarin AVSR and two datasets; the MBH remains a descriptive hypothesis rather than a formal theorem, and whether the distributional-shift diagnosis holds for modality-balanced systems (e.g., emotion recognition) is left as future work.
