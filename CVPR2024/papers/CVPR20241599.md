## Problem

Large CLIP models rely on heavy transformer encoders with significant memory and latency overhead, making them impractical for on-device mobile deployment. Smaller architectures that fit mobile constraints suffer from subpar accuracy, and standard contrastive learning at small scale produces poor results that fail to provide useful signal for rapid architecture exploration. The paper addresses both gaps: designing efficient hybrid architectures and introducing a training method that dramatically improves learning efficiency without adding train-time compute cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241599/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (224×224 for ViT-B/16-based variants, smaller resolutions for the S0/S1/S2 efficient variants) and a text prompt as input, producing a shared embedding space where image and text embeddings are compared via cosine similarity. For zero-shot classification, the image embedding is matched against text embeddings of candidate class descriptions; for retrieval, similarity scores rank candidate images or captions. All latency measurements are taken on iPhone 12 Pro Max via Core ML with batch size 1, targeting sub-5 ms total (image + text) inference for the smallest variants.

## Method

The core training contribution is multi-modal reinforced training: the DataComp dataset is reinforced once with (i) synthetic captions generated by CoCa, (ii) strong image augmentations, and (iii) precomputed embeddings from an ensemble of two strong ViT-L/14 CLIP teachers, yielding DataCompDR. At training time, the loss combines standard CLIP contrastive loss with a knowledge distillation term (KL divergence between teacher and student image-text affinity matrices), weighted by λ, and is computed over both real-caption and synthetic-caption batches. Because all teacher knowledge is stored in the dataset, training incurs zero additional compute overhead compared to vanilla CLIP training, yet achieves 10×–1000× learning efficiency gains. On the architecture side, MobileCLIP uses hybrid CNN-transformer encoders: the image encoder (MCi) is based on FastViT with structural reparameterization and a reduced MLP expansion ratio (3.0 vs. 4.0) to increase depth at fixed parameter count, while the text encoder (MCt) introduces Text-RepMixer blocks that decouple train-time and inference-time computation via reparameterizable skip connections, combining 1-D convolutions with a small number of self-attention layers for efficiency.

## Results

MobileCLIP-B (ViT-B/16:Base) trained on DataCompDR-1B achieves 76.8% zero-shot ImageNet-val accuracy, 65.6% IN-shift, and 65.2% average performance across 38 DataComp benchmarks, setting a new state-of-the-art for ViT-B/16-based CLIP models. MobileCLIP-S2 (MCi2:Base) achieves 74.4% IN-val and 63.7% average on 38 datasets while being 2.3× faster and more accurate than the previous best ViT-B/16 CLIP. The fastest variant, MobileCLIP-S0 (MCi0:MCt), is approximately 5× faster and 3× smaller than OpenAI ViT-B/16 CLIP while matching its average accuracy (58.1% vs 58.1%). On the ARO compositional benchmark, MobileCLIP-B achieves 54.6% VG Relation and 68.4% VG Attributes, substantially outperforming all other ViT-B/16 models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileCLIP-B outperforms the previous best ViT-B/16 model, SigLIP-B/16 [77], by 0.8% on IN-val (76.8 vs 76.0) and 2.9% on average 38-dataset performance (65.2 vs 62.3), while also being 26.3% smaller in parameters. Against DataComp-B/16 [18], MobileCLIP-B gains 3.3% IN-val (76.8 vs 73.5) and 3.7% average performance, and against DFN-B/16 [16] it gains 0.6% IN-val and 4.3% average. MobileCLIP-S2 surpasses DataComp-B/32-256 [18] (trained 2.6× longer) by 1.6% IN-val and 2.8% average while being 1.5× smaller and 1.4× faster. At the small-model scale, MobileCLIP-S0 dramatically outperforms TinyCLIP-40M/32 [68] by 8.0% IN-val (67.8 vs 59.8) and 6.9% average, and beats OpenAI-RN50 [47] by 8.0% IN-val and 10.0% average. On the ARO compositional benchmark, MobileCLIP-B achieves 19.5% higher VG Relation accuracy and 12.4% higher VG Attributes accuracy than SigLIP, with 69.7% and 50.3% higher recall@1 on Flickr30k-Order and COCO-Order respectively, indicating that DataCompDR reinforcement substantially improves compositional understanding beyond what standard contrastive training achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unimodal dataset reinforcement strategy of Faghri et al. [14] to the multimodal CLIP setting, combining synthetic captions from a pretrained captioning model (CoCa) with offline ensemble-teacher embeddings stored directly in the dataset. Architecturally, it builds on FastViT [62] and MobileOne [61] for the image encoder and introduces a hybrid convolutional-attention text encoder (Text-RepMixer) inspired by RepMixer, departing from the purely transformer-based text encoders used in prior efficient CLIP work like TinyCLIP [68]. The key conceptual departure from prior efficient-CLIP training (SLIP, CLIPA, TinyCLIP) is that the training-time compute cost is identical to standard CLIP training—no online teacher evaluation or caption generation—because all additional knowledge is precomputed and stored, making rapid architecture exploration feasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10×–1000× learning efficiency gains (well-controlled in Fig. 6) mean that a single 8×A100 node can produce a competitive ViT-B/16 CLIP in roughly one day, dramatically lowering the barrier to efficient model design and iteration. The reinforced-dataset paradigm is a one-time cost amortized over many experiments, making it particularly attractive for teams exploring architecture search under tight compute budgets. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other modalities (audio, video), how storage overhead (140 TB for DataCompDR-1B) scales with dataset size, and whether the ensemble-teacher requirement (two strong pretrained CLIP models) limits applicability in settings where no strong teacher exists. The improved ARO benchmark performance also suggests that reinforced training may mitigate the compositional-understanding degradation typically seen with noisy web-scale data.
