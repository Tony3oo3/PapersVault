## Problem

Vision-language models are pretrained on static image-text pairs and thus fail to align with the dynamics of an embodied visual world, while LLMs that excel in text-based environments cannot perceive visual signals. Training an embodied agent directly in a noisy visual world is further hampered by sparse rewards, noisy observations, and VLM hallucination. The paper addresses how to efficiently train a VLM agent in a visual world by distilling the decision-making skills of an LLM expert operating in a parallel text world.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each interaction step, EMMA receives a free-form or template textual task instruction (e.g., 'clean an apple and put it in the fridge') and a pixel observation rendered by the Ai2Thor simulator, and produces a single high-level textual action (e.g., 'go to cabinet 1'). The pixel observation is also converted into a PDDL-based textual equivalent (Observed Objects, Relations, Inventory, Locations) to serve as the state input for the LLM expert in the parallel TextWorld. The VLM architecture keeps the ViT, Q-Former, and LLM decoder frozen, updating only a linear projection layer that maps 32 visual tokens into the LLM's embedding space.

## Method

EMMA is trained via a novel DAgger-DPO algorithm that performs cross-modality interactive imitation learning between a VLM student in the visual world and an LLM expert in a parallel text world. The LLM expert is a 'retrospective' system composed of an actor (text-davinci-003 prompted to generate actions) and a critic (same LLM prompted to reflect on the VLM's historical trajectories), with a FIFO long-term memory storing the critic's feedback to improve future actor outputs. The DAgger loop rolls out the VLM in the visual environment, converts each pixel observation to its textual equivalent, queries the LLM actor for an expert action, and aggregates (state, student-action, expert-action) tuples. The DPO loss replaces standard cross-entropy, maximizing the log-odds ratio of the expert action over the student action relative to a behavior-cloned reference policy, which prevents premature convergence and maintains exploration. The VLM is initialized via behavior cloning on 170K rule-based expert demonstrations to provide the reference policy and stabilize early training.

## Results

On the ALFWorld benchmark (134 OOD tasks, template instructions), EMMA achieves an average success rate of 0.82 with 19.5 average interaction steps in the visual environment, substantially outperforming all VLM baselines (best: InstructBLIP at 0.22) by 20%–70% in success rate. EMMA's performance is comparable to the strongest LLM agent (Reflexion, 0.91) operating in the easier textual environment, while requiring only pixel observations. In the free-form instruction setting, EMMA retains 0.68 average success rate, far exceeding all other VLM agents (≤0.02) and approaching Reflexion's 0.78 in the textual setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMMA (0.82 avg success rate) decisively outperforms all four VLM baselines in the visual environment: InstructBLIP (0.22), MiniGPT-4 (0.16), LLaMA-Adapter (0.13), and BLIP-2 (0.04), with the largest gap on Clean (0.94 vs. 0.26) and Look (0.88 vs. 0.17) tasks. Against the two vision-only models (ResNet-18 at 0.06, MCNN-FPN at 0.05), EMMA's advantage is overwhelming, confirming the critical role of language priors in VLMs. In the textual environment, Reflexion achieves 0.91 and DEPS/AutoGen reach 0.76–0.77; EMMA's 0.82 in the harder visual setting is competitive with these LLM agents that benefit from perfectly semantic state descriptions. Notably, EMMA also requires fewer interaction steps (19.5) than all VLM baselines (26–30 steps), indicating more efficient task completion. Under noisy observations (Fig. 5), EMMA degrades more gracefully than Reflexion, and on free-form instructions EMMA (0.68) retains strong performance while all other VLMs collapse to near-zero (≤0.02).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established threads: the InstructBLIP VLM architecture (frozen ViT + Q-Former + LLM with a trainable projection layer), the DAgger interactive imitation learning framework, and DPO as the optimization objective. The key conceptual departure is the 'parallel world' setup: an LLM expert operates in a text-based TextWorld while the VLM student operates in a visual Ai2Thor environment, with pixel observations converted to PDDL-style textual descriptions to bridge the modality gap. This differs from EUREKA, which uses an LLM to generate reward functions for RL, and from standard DAgger, which assumes the expert and student share the same observation modality. The addition of a retrospective critic with long-term memory to improve expert actions over trials extends the Reflexion-style self-correction idea into the imitation learning loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that cross-modality imitation from a text-world LLM expert can substantially close the gap between VLM agents and LLM agents in embodied tasks, with EMMA achieving 0.82 average success rate versus 0.22 for the best prior VLM baseline (InstructBLIP) on ALFWorld. The generalization to free-form, open-vocabulary instructions (0.68 vs. near-zero for other VLMs) suggests the approach transfers LLM-level linguistic flexibility to visual agents. However, the method relies on simulator-provided metadata for the visual-to-text conversion, limiting direct applicability to real-world settings where such structured observations are unavailable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex 3D environments, whether the text-davinci-003 dependency can be replaced with open-source LLMs, and whether the DPO regularization is necessary or whether simpler preference-based objectives suffice.
