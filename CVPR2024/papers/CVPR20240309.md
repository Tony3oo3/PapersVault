## Problem

SAM produces class-agnostic, high-confidence local masks that cannot reliably distinguish the desired object category from its parts (e.g., segmenting 'clothes' instead of 'person'), making direct use of SAM's top-scored proposals inadequate for category-specific instance segmentation. Furthermore, standard MIL-based proposal selection inherits two failure modes: the 'group' issue, where adjacent same-category objects are merged into one mask, and the 'local' issue, where the classifier favors the most discriminative sub-region over the full object extent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image together with one annotated point per object (each carrying a 2D coordinate and a class index), which are fed into SAM as point prompts to generate M category-agnostic mask proposals per object. These proposals are converted to bounding boxes via minimum bounding rectangles and combined with depth features from a ResNet backbone. The final output is a set of pixel-level instance segmentation masks with per-instance class labels, produced by a SOLOv2 head trained end-to-end on the pseudo-labels generated during training.

## Method

SAPNet comprises a pseudo-label generation branch and a SOLOv2 segmentation branch trained jointly end-to-end. The generation branch uses a Proposal Selection Module (PSM) that applies MIL with a novel Point Distance Guidance (PDG) penalty—computed from Euclidean distances between same-category annotated points—to suppress group predictions, followed by a Proposals Refinement Module (PRM) that refines boxes using positive/negative bags constructed by a PNPG stage and a Box Mining Strategy (BMS) that adaptively merges the selected box with larger enclosing proposals to resolve the local issue. The PRM replaces standard cross-entropy with a Focal Loss on positive bags and introduces a dedicated negative loss weighted by the predicted bag score. The segmentation branch is supervised by both the refined PRM masks and filtered SAM masks via a Multi-mask Proposals Supervision (MPS) scheme, with the total loss combining Dice loss, Focal classification loss, and the PSM/PRM MIL losses.

## Results

On COCO-17 val, SAPNet with ResNet-50 and 1x schedule achieves 31.2 mAP (51.8 mAP50, 32.3 mAP75), surpassing the previous point-annotated SOTA (AttnShift, 21.2 mAP) by 10.0 mAP and the box-annotated BoxInst (33.2 mAP) is only 2.0 mAP higher. With ResNet-101 and 3x schedule, SAPNet reaches 34.6 mAP, matching Mask R-CNN (34.6 mAP) under fully-supervised mask annotation. On VOC2012, SAPNet (ResNet-101) achieves 64.8 AP50 and 58.7 AP75, outperforming all point- and box-annotated methods and reaching 92.3% of Mask R-CNN's fully-supervised performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAPNet decisively outperforms all prior point-annotated instance segmentation methods: it surpasses BESTIE† (17.7 mAP) by 13.5 mAP and AttnShift (21.2 mAP) by 10.0 mAP on COCO, and beats AttnShift† (57.1 AP50) by 7.7 AP on VOC2012. Against box-annotated methods, SAPNet (ResNet-101, 3x) exceeds BoxInst (33.2 mAP) by 1.4 mAP and DiscoBox (32.0 mAP) by 2.6 mAP on COCO, and surpasses BoxInst (61.4 AP50) by 3.4 AP50 on VOC2012. The two internal baselines (SAM-top1 at 24.6 mAP and MIL-SOLOv2 at 26.8 mAP) confirm that the full SAPNet pipeline contributes 4.4–6.6 mAP over naive SAM or single-MIL selection. Under the strongest setting (ResNet-101, 3x), SAPNet matches Mask R-CNN (34.6 mAP) and SOLOv2 (34.8 mAP) under full mask supervision, effectively closing the gap between point-annotated and fully-supervised instance segmentation. On VOC2012, SAPNet achieves 92.3% of Mask R-CNN's AP50 (64.8 vs 68.8) while requiring only single-point annotations per instance.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAPNet sits at the intersection of weakly-supervised instance segmentation and foundation-model prompting. It inherits the MIL-based proposal selection paradigm from WSDDN and P2BNet, and adopts SOLOv2 as its segmentation head, but departs from prior point-supervised methods (WISE-Net, BESTIE, AttnShift) by repurposing SAM as a zero-shot proposal generator rather than learning mask prediction from scratch. The key conceptual departure is treating SAM's class-agnostic scoring as a source of semantic ambiguity to be resolved through a dedicated selection module (PSM with PDG) and refinement module (PRM with BMS), rather than simply using SAM's top-scored mask as a pseudo-label. This positions the work as a semantic-aware wrapper around SAM for category-specific segmentation, distinct from prior SAM-based efforts that focused on improving SAM's raw annotation quality or speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in showing that a single point annotation per object, combined with SAM's proposal generation, can close most of the gap to fully-supervised instance segmentation (31.2 AP vs. 34.6 AP for Mask R-CNN on COCO), offering a compelling label-efficiency trade-off for practitioners. The modular design (SAM proposals → MIL selection → refinement → SOLOv2 supervision) provides a reusable recipe that could be adapted to other foundation models or annotation modalities. However, the approach still requires per-object point annotations with class labels, and the multi-stage selection pipeline (PSM → PNPG → PRM → BMS) adds considerable architectural complexity and hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., T_min1, T_min2 thresholds). Open questions include whether the MIL-based selection generalizes to open-vocabulary or novel-category settings, and whether the two-stage refinement can be collapsed into a single differentiable pass.
