## Problem

Category-level 6D pose estimation must handle large intra-class shape variation, yet existing methods relying on mean-shape priors fail when objects within a category differ structurally. While DINOv2 provides SE(3)-consistent semantic features, no prior work has explored how to fuse these semantic priors with object-specific geometric features in a way that is itself SE(3)-consistent, thereby simplifying the downstream rotation estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241179/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB-D image, segments the target object with Mask R-CNN, back-projects the depth to a 3D point cloud (2048 points sampled for RGB/radial features, 300 points for geometric features), and crops the RGB image to a 210×210 bounding box for DINOv2 feature extraction. It outputs the full 9DoF pose: a 3×3 rotation matrix (SO(3)), a 3D translation vector, and a 3D metric size vector, with translation and size estimated by a separate PointNet++ head and rotation by a spherical-representation network.

## Method

SecondPose fuses two SE(3)-consistent feature streams: a semantic stream using DINOv2's 11th-layer token features (which encode category-level semantic correspondences) and a geometric stream computing hierarchical Point Pair Features (pairwise distance, two normal-to-vector angles, and normal-normal angle) across panels of increasing radius to capture local-to-global structure. Both streams, along with RGB values, are projected onto a spherical feature map, processed by separate ResNet extractors, concatenated, and fused by a shared ResNet to produce the SECOND representation. The key insight is that this fused representation is approximately rotation-invariant, so the pose estimator only needs to learn the mapping from the SE(3)-consistent representation to the canonical space rather than from raw point clouds, making rotation-equivariance far easier to learn. Rotation is regressed as a 9D matrix with L1 loss on a VI-Net-style spherical representation, while translation and size use a lightweight PointNet++ head with L1 loss, trained jointly on a 25%/75% mix of real and synthetic NOCS data.

## Results

On NOCS-REAL275, SecondPose achieves 56.2% mAP at 5°/2cm, 63.6% at 5°/5cm, 74.7% at 10°/2cm, and 86.0% at 10°/5cm, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art VI-Net (50.0%, 57.6%, 70.8%, 82.1%) by 6.2%, 6.0%, 3.9%, and 3.9% respectively. On the more challenging HouseCat6D dataset, SecondPose reaches 83.7/66.1 IoU25/IoU50, outperforming VI-Net (80.7/56.4) by 9.7% on IoU50. The method also outperforms the best mean-shape-prior method (DPDN, 46.0% at 5°/2cm) by 10.2% absolute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SecondPose outperforms all 18 compared baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. Against the strongest prior-free competitor VI-Net [27], it gains 6.2% on 5°/2cm and 3.9% on 10°/2cm on REAL275, and 9.7% on IoU50 on HouseCat6D. Compared to the best mean-shape-prior method DPDN [25] (46.0% at 5°/2cm), SecondPose improves by 10.2% absolute, and against SPD+CATRE [29] (45.8%) by 10.4%. On HouseCat6D, SecondPose also surpasses FS-Net [5] (74.9/48.0) and GPV-Pose [8] (74.9/50.7) by 8.8% and 8.8% on IoU25 respectively, with particularly large gains on categories like Box (54.5 vs. 44.8 IoU25) and Teapot (81.0 vs. 52.6 IoU50). The only category where SecondPose does not lead is Tube (75.6 vs. 94.9 IoU25 for VI-Net), suggesting residual difficulty with highly symmetric objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

SecondPose sits at the intersection of two previously separate threads in category-level pose estimation: mean-shape-prior methods (SPD, SGPA, SAR-Net) that struggle with intra-class variation, and prior-free methods (VI-Net, IST-Net, HS-Pose) that rely solely on learned geometric representations. The paper's key departure is the first direct fusion of DINOv2's patch-wise semantic features with object-specific hierarchical geometric features (derived from classical Point Pair Features) into a unified SE(3)-consistent representation, rather than treating semantic and geometric cues as independent inputs. Architecturally, it builds on VI-Net's spherical feature map and PointNet++ heads for translation/size, while the geometric stream generalizes single-panel PPFs into a multi-scale hierarchical panel construction inspired by CNN receptive fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.4% improvement over VI-Net on REAL275 and the 9.7% IoU50 gain on the photometrically challenging HouseCat6D demonstrate that semantic priors from foundation models can meaningfully complement geometric features for pose estimation, opening a path toward leveraging increasingly powerful vision transformers in 3D perception pipelines. A key limitation is the hard dependency on DINOv2: when the foundation model fails to provide meaningful semantic information (e.g., for out-of-distribution objects or extreme viewpoints), the method cannot compensate. Open questions include whether the SE(3)-consistency argument generalizes to other foundation models (SAM, CLIP), whether the approach extends to fully novel categories without any training data, and whether the 9 FPS inference speed is sufficient for real-time robotic manipulation.
