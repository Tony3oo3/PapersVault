## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture synthesis methods produce beat-aligned motions but fail to generate semantically coherent gestures tied to specific words, and offer no mechanism to control which conditioning modalities (audio vs. text) drive the output. Furthermore, most available datasets contain only monologues, leaving multi-party conversational gesture synthesis largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241298/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-modal conditioning set C = {audio signal, text tokens, speaker identity token, and optionally co-participant text/audio and a binary speech-activity mask for dyadic settings}, plus inference-time controls: per-modality guidance weights w_c and a user-selected set of words for excitation. It outputs a 3D joint-position sequence x ∈ R^{N×J×3} covering both body and hand articulations, with the chunked latent design enabling autoregressive perpetual rollout beyond the training window length.

## Method

ConvoFusion operates in a decoupled, chunked latent space: separate VAE encoders handle body and hand motions (addressing their different scales), and motion is split into M short chunks each encoded independently but decoded jointly by a single transformer decoder to enforce temporal consistency. A latent diffusion model with a transformer denoiser conditions on all modalities via separate cross-attention heads. The key controllability mechanism is a modified classifier-free guidance: during training, individual modalities are randomly replaced with null-embeddings (10% drop probability) rather than dropping the entire conditioning, so at inference the user can scale each modality's contribution independently via Eq. (5). A second, fine-grained control—Word-Excitation Guidance (WEG)—computes a gradient of a max-attention objective over user-selected text tokens and applies it to the latent at each denoising step, amplifying gesticulation at those words. The paper also introduces the DND GROUP GESTURE dataset (5 participants, 6 hours, markerless mocap) to support dyadic and group-level evaluation.

## Results

On the BEAT monadic test set, ConvoFusion achieves a FID of 271, Beat-Alignment of 0.82, and SRGR of 0.365, outperforming MLD (FID 475, BeatAlign 0.76, SRGR 0.214) on all three metrics while remaining competitive with CaMN (FID 142, BeatAlign 0.74, SRGR 0.443). In the dyadic setting on DND GROUP GESTURE, ConvoFusion matches ground-truth Beat-Alignment (0.90) and produces non-static motions (L1 Div 1.19 vs. MLD's 0.31), whereas DiffGesture exhibits extreme jitter (Diversity 2176, L1 Div 1308). A user study confirms that ConvoFusion is preferred over both CaMN and MLD in both monadic and dyadic settings, and that Word-Excitation Guidance (WEG) significantly improves perceived semantic alignment at target words.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four baselines, DiffGesture and Multi-Context produce extremely jittery motions (FID ≥ 10³), rendering them non-competitive despite high Beat-Alignment scores that are artifacts of the jitter. Against CaMN, ConvoFusion wins on Beat-Alignment (0.82 vs. 0.74) but trails on FID (271 vs. 142) and SRGR (0.365 vs. 0.443), indicating CaMN still captures slightly better semantic recall at the cost of lower beat alignment. Against MLD, ConvoFusion clearly wins on FID (271 vs. 475), Beat-Alignment (0.82 vs. 0.76), and SRGR (0.365 vs. 0.214), though MLD achieves higher Diversity (16.98 vs. 9.82). In the dyadic setting, ConvoFusion matches ground-truth Beat-Alignment (0.90) and produces substantially more dynamic motions than MLD (L1 Div 1.19 vs. 0.31), while DiffuGesture remains unusable due to extreme jitter. The user study further confirms ConvoFusion's perceptual advantage over both CaMN and MLD in monadic and dyadic settings, with WEG providing a measurable boost in perceived semantic coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConvoFusion builds directly on the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al.) and the motion-specific latent diffusion of MLD (Chen et al., CVPR 2023), but departs from MLD's single-vector latent by introducing chunked, time-aware latents that enable perpetual rollout and finer temporal alignment with speech. The classifier-free guidance mechanism (Ho & Salimans) is repurposed from a single null-embedding dropout to per-modality random dropouts, enabling independent control over audio, text, and speaker-identity contributions at inference. The word-excitation guidance draws on attention-based semantic guidance from text-to-image diffusion (Attend-and-Excite, Diffusion Self-Guidance) and adapts it to the gesture domain. The DND GROUP GESTURE dataset extends the multi-party gesture data landscape beyond the two-person Talking with Hands and the single-speaker BEAT datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DND GROUP GESTURE dataset is the first to provide full-body, markerless mocap of five interacting speakers with multi-channel audio and gesture annotations, filling a critical gap for multi-party conversational gesture research. The per-modality guidance and word-excitation mechanisms offer a practical control framework that could be adopted by other conditional diffusion systems beyond gestures. However, the dataset's scale (5 participants, 6 hours) limits generalizability claims, and the dyadic evaluation is restricted to the authors' own data. Open questions include whether the time-aware chunked latent scales to longer sequences without drift, how the approach performs with more than two speakers simultaneously, and whether the modest semantic-recall gain from WEG (0.34→0.40) translates to perceptually meaningful improvements in longer utterances.
