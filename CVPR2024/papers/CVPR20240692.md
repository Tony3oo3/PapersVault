## Problem

Existing depth completion methods are designed and validated for relatively dense inputs (64-line LiDAR, 500 sampled points), but their performance on very sparse regimes (4-line LiDAR, 32 points) remains unverified. With sparser inputs, the distance between image pixels and their nearest depth point increases sixfold, and the primarily convolutional, locally-biased processing in prior methods fails to let each depth point adaptively influence large or global image regions. The paper also highlights that models must handle variable sparsity levels and non-uniform point distributions (e.g., SIFT keypoints, SLAM-tracked points) for real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image and a set of sparse 2D-projected depth points (with their depth values) as input, and outputs a dense depth map at the same spatial resolution. Notably, the encoder can operate on RGB alone without a depth map input; depth information is fused exclusively at the decoder stage via the ASC module. The framework handles a wide range of input sparsities (1–64 LiDAR scan lines on KITTI, 2–500 points on NYUv2) and diverse point distributions (random, SIFT, SLAM-tracked), and can also function as a pure monocular depth estimator when zero depth points are provided.

## Method

The core contribution is the Affinity-Based Shift Correction (ASC) module, applied iteratively at each decoder stage. It first predicts an initial depth map from decoder features, then encodes each input depth point into a feature vector via a single transformer layer (concatenating sampled image features with 2D positional embeddings and the depth value). Cross-attention between image pixel features and point features yields a soft affinity matrix that determines how much each depth point should influence each pixel. The shift correction then adjusts each pixel's depth by the affinity-weighted average of depth errors at the input points, effectively propagating scale and offset corrections from known points to semantically related regions. A learned correction confidence map (conditioned on distance-to-nearest-point) blends the initial and corrected predictions, and the first (coarsest) decoder stage uses feature-only fusion to avoid scale ambiguity. The module is plug-and-play: it can be attached to any encoder-decoder depth completion or monocular estimation model, enabling a single network to perform both tasks.

## Results

On KITTI with variable scan-line training (1–64 lines), the proposed method (NLSPN Base) achieves RMSE 1829.0 mm / MAE 633.9 mm at 4 lines, outperforming NLSPN (1963.3 / 690.2) by 6.8% in RMSE. On NYUv2 with variable point training, the Effb5 variant reaches REL 0.0977 / RMSE 0.3994 m at 2 points, a 26% REL improvement over NLSPN (0.1321 / 0.4973). Cross-dataset transfer to nuScenes (8-line, trained on 1–64 KITTI lines) yields RMSE 6538 mm with the RGB-input variant, a 15% improvement over NLSPN (7693 mm). The method also enables joint monocular estimation and completion, with BEiT-L + ASC achieving REL 0.088 at 0 points and 0.016 at 500 points on NYUv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ASC module consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every sparsity level and dataset. On KITTI (variable scan lines), it beats NLSPN by 6.8% RMSE at 4 lines (1829.0 vs 1963.3 mm) and by 4.2% at 64 lines (884.8 vs 923.2 mm), with the gap widening for sparser inputs. On NYUv2 (variable points), the Effb5 variant achieves 26% lower REL at 2 points (0.0977 vs NLSPN's 0.1321) and 10% lower REL at 8 points (0.0687 vs 0.0844), while the NLSPN-base variant still improves over NLSPN at all sparsities. Against SparseFormer (re-implemented), the method wins on both KITTI and NYUv2 at every sparsity level, with the largest margin at 2 points on NYUv2 (REL 0.1272 vs 0.1583). On the SIFT-keypoint benchmark (Table 7), the method matches the re-implemented SparseSPN (REL 0.028 vs 0.026) without requiring a ViT-B surface-normal network. For cross-dataset transfer to nuScenes, the RGB-input variant trained on variable KITTI lines (1–64) reduces RMSE by 15% at 8 lines compared to NLSPN trained on the same variable setting (6538 vs 7693 mm), and substantially outperforms the 64-line-only NLSPN (14099 mm). The method also improves over monocular-only baselines (BTS, AdaBins) when depth points are available, while maintaining comparable 0-point performance, confirming the ASC module's plug-and-play applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NLSPN's non-local spatial propagation architecture as its primary base, and extends the ASC module to monocular depth estimation backbones (BTS, AdaBins) to unify estimation and completion. It departs from the dominant paradigm of convolutional depth-point processing (CSPN, NLSPN, MultiStack) by introducing a cross-attention-based affinity mechanism that lets each depth point adaptively influence global image regions, addressing the failure of local propagation under extreme sparsity. Compared to SparseFormer, which computes a simple weighted sum over input depths per pixel, the proposed shift-correction formulation uses depth *errors* of related regions to iteratively refine predictions, avoiding the boundary-blurring artifact of naive depth averaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a practical gap: most depth completion methods are unverified on the very sparse inputs (4-line LiDAR, 32 points) that affordable sensors and SfM pipelines actually produce, and the 6× increase in pixel-to-point distance makes this a non-trivial regime. The finding that RGB-only encoder input (without depth) outperforms RGBD input for handling unseen sparsity levels and distributions is a useful design insight for deployment flexibility. Open questions include whether the transformer-based affinity computation scales efficiently to real-time multi-sensor fusion, and whether the variable-sparsity training strategy generalizes beyond the tested datasets to truly novel sensor geometries (e.g., solid-state LiDAR with non-uniform angular resolution).
