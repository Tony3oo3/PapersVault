## Problem

Existing model IP protection methods (NTL, CUTI) require concurrent access to both source and target training data to restrict a model's generalization to unauthorized domains, making them impractical under data privacy policies. This paper targets the more practical and challenging settings where only a well-trained source model is available (source-free) or no data at all (data-free), and asks whether one can selectively remove a model's generalization capability to specific unauthorized domains without retraining from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241343/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen, well-trained source model (VGG11/13/19, ResNet, Swin-T, or Xception) and, depending on the variant, either labeled source+target data (SA-MAP), unlabeled target images only (SF-MAP), or no data whatsoever (DF-MAP). The output is a binary pruning mask of the same shape as the model weights, which when applied to the frozen checkpoint yields a sub-network that retains source-domain accuracy while degrading target-domain accuracy. The mask is the sole learned artifact; the underlying weights remain fixed throughout training.

## Method

MAP is grounded in the Inverse Transfer Parameter Hypothesis: a well-trained model contains a sub-network that preserves source-domain accuracy while losing target-domain generalization, and the pruned parameters are precisely those responsible for unauthorized-domain transfer. The framework freezes the source model and learns a binary mask by optimizing an objective that minimizes KL divergence on (synthesized) source samples while maximizing KL divergence on target samples, with a min-clamp (α=1.0, λ=0.1) to prevent over-degradation of domain-invariant knowledge. In the source-free variant (SF-MAP), a replay-based generation module—comprising a fresh generator (trained with predictive entropy, activation loss, and JS-divergence consistency) and a memory generator (trained with L1 reconstruction loss)—synthesizes pseudo-source samples to replace unavailable source data. The data-free variant (DF-MAP) further introduces a diversity generator with learnable mean and variance shifts that produces neighborhood domains in multiple style directions, constrained by mutual information minimization for style diversity and class-conditional MMD for semantic consistency, enabling IP protection with zero access to any real data.

## Results

In the source-free setting (the paper's primary contribution), SF-MAP achieves a mean Source Drop of only 8.8% and ST-D of 0.24, compared to NTL (68.9% source drop, ST-D 1.01) and CUTI (87.4% source drop, ST-D 1.08). In the source-available setting, SA-MAP attains the best ST-D of −0.004 with a negative source drop (−0.3%), slightly outperforming CUTI (ST-D 0.004) and NTL (ST-D 0.013). In the data-free setting, DF-MAP achieves ST-D < 1.0 across all four digit transfer pairs (mean ST-D 0.27), confirming that the generalization region is successfully narrowed without any access to real data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: NTL [49], which amplifies MMD between source and target domains, and CUTI-Domain [50], which constructs a middle domain as a barrier. In the source-available setting, SA-MAP marginally outperforms both baselines on the proposed ST-D metric (−0.004 vs. CUTI's 0.004 and NTL's 0.013), while also achieving a negative source drop (−0.3%), indicating pruning actually improves source accuracy slightly. In the source-free setting, the advantage is dramatic: SF-MAP reduces source degradation from 68.9% (NTL) and 87.4% (CUTI) to just 8.8%, while achieving a lower ST-D of 0.24 versus 1.01 and 1.08, meaning the target domain is still effectively degraded relative to the preserved source performance. In the data-free setting, no direct baselines exist (the authors note no prior work addresses this scenario), but DF-MAP maintains ST-D below 1.0 on all four digit transfer pairs, confirming the method's viability without any real data access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model IP protection line initiated by NTL (which amplifies MMD between source and target domains) and CUTI-Domain (which constructs barrier middle domains), both of which require concurrent access to source and target data. The key departure is reframing IP protection as a pruning problem grounded in an 'Inverse Transfer Parameter Hypothesis' (an extension of the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis), where a learnable binary mask selectively removes target-domain-specific parameters while preserving source-domain knowledge. For the source-free and data-free variants, the method borrows from data-free knowledge distillation (fresh/memory generators for pseudo-source synthesis) and domain generalization (diversity generation via learnable mean/variance shifts) to operate without any real data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-free and data-free formulations address a practical gap: in real deployments, the model owner often cannot share training data due to privacy regulations, yet still needs to restrict unauthorized domain usage. The ST-D metric is a useful contribution that forces methods to balance source preservation against target degradation rather than optimizing one at the expense of the other. However, experiments are limited to small-scale benchmarks (digit datasets, CIFAR-10, STL-10, VisDA-2017) with VGG-family backbones primarily, and the data-free results are noticeably weaker (mean ST-D 0.27 vs. 0.24 for source-free, with one domain reaching 0.91). Open questions include scalability to large-scale datasets and modern architectures, robustness of the binary mask to fine-tuning attacks, and whether the pruning-based approach generalizes beyond classification to tasks like segmentation or generation.
