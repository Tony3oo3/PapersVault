## Problem

Conditional diffusion models trained on large web-scraped datasets (e.g., LAION-5B) rely on aggressive filtering to remove misaligned image-condition pairs, discarding up to 90% of available data. This filtering is both hard to calibrate and wasteful, as discarded images still belong to the target distribution and could improve generation quality. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can learn to selectively trust or ignore its conditioning based on an explicit measure of alignment quality, without throwing away any samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard condition (text prompt, class label, or semantic segmentation map) augmented with a coherence score c ∈ [0,1] that quantifies how well the condition matches the image. For text, the score is the CLIP score binned into 8 discrete levels; for class labels, it is the entropy of a classifier's resampled label distribution; for semantic maps, it is a pixel-level confidence map from a segmentation network. At inference, the user can freely set c to modulate how strongly the condition steers generation, effectively interpolating between unconditional sampling (c=0) and fully conditional sampling (c=1).

## Method

CAD conditions the denoising network ε_θ on both the original condition y and a coherence score c, modifying the standard diffusion loss to L = E[‖ε − ε_θ(X_t, y, c, t)‖]. The coherence score is injected as an additional token (sinusoidal embedding + MLP for class labels, discrete bin embedding for text) or as a concatenated pixel-level map (for semantic segmentation via ControlNet), allowing the model to learn how much weight to give the condition at each coherence level. A key architectural consequence is that the model naturally interpolates between unconditional and conditional behavior: low-coherence samples drive unconditional denoising while high-coherence samples drive conditional denoising, eliminating the need for random conditioning dropout during training. The paper further proposes Coherence-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance (CA-CFG), which replaces the standard CFG formula ε̂ = ε(x,y) + ω(ε(x,y) − ε(x,∅)) with ε̂ = ε(x,y,1) + ω(ε(x,y,1) − ε(x,y,0)), using the gap between maximum and minimum coherence predictions as the guidance direction instead of a dropped-out unconditional branch.

## Results

On the primary text-to-image task (COCO-10K, zero-shot), CAD achieves a FID of 69.4, substantially outperforming the Filtered baseline (85.8) and the unconditioned Baseline (91.9), while also improving Precision (0.373 vs 0.281), Recall (0.078 vs 0.061), Density (0.265 vs 0.175), and Coverage (0.315 vs 0.233). For class-conditional generation on CIFAR-10 with label noise β=0.5, CAD reduces FID to 4.75 (vs 5.66 Baseline, 9.48 Filtered) and reaches 90.6% accuracy (vs 50.7% Baseline). On ADE20K semantic-map conditioning, CAD lowers FID from 33.67 to 30.88 and improves mIoU from 22.6 to 23.7 over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CAD against three baselines: (1) Baseline — standard conditional diffusion training without any coherence information; (2) Filtered — discarding the 3 lowest-coherence bins of training samples (analogous to CLIP-score thresholding used in LAION-5B); and (3) Weighted — weighting the diffusion loss by the normalized coherence score. On text-to-image (COCO-10K), CAD wins on FID by 16.4 points over Filtered (69.4 vs 85.8) and 22.5 points over Baseline (91.9), while also dominating on all manifold metrics (Precision, Recall, Density, Coverage). The Weighted baseline is consistently the worst performer, suggesting that simply re-weighting the loss is insufficient. On CIFAR-10 class-conditional generation (β=0.5), CAD achieves FID 4.75 and 90.6% accuracy, beating Baseline (FID 5.66, 50.7% Acc) and Filtered (FID 9.48, 63.4% Acc); notably, filtering dramatically degrades quality here. On ADE20K semantic conditioning, CAD improves FID (30.88 vs 33.67) and mIoU (23.7 vs 22.6) over the baseline, with the largest gains in Recall (0.824 vs 0.757) and Coverage (0.934 vs 0.904). A user study (36 annotators) confirms that CAD images are preferred 95% of the time for quality and 89% for prompt adherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RIN architecture for efficient diffusion training and the Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) framework, modifying both to incorporate a per-sample coherence score as an additional conditioning signal. Conceptually, it extends the noisy-label learning literature from classification (where confidence scores have been used to weight or filter training examples) to conditional image synthesis, where the coherence score is embedded as a continuous conditioning variable rather than a binary filter. The key departure from prior practice is rejecting the dominant data-cleaning strategy (e.g., LAION-5B discarding 90% of pairs via CLIP-score thresholding) in favor of retaining all samples and letting the model learn to discount low-coherence conditions, while also replacing random conditioning dropout in CFG with a coherence-based guidance gap (CA-CFG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: retaining the vast majority of web-scraped training data (rather than discarding 90%) while simultaneously improving generation quality and prompt adherence addresses a core bottleneck in scaling text-to-image models. The CA-CFG modification is a clean, general-purpose refinement that removes the need for random dropout during training. The main open question is the reliability of coherence score extraction — the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality of the score (CLIP score, classifier confidence, segmentation confidence), and the authors acknowledge that unreliable scores can introduce biases. Future work on more robust, task-agnostic coherence estimation would strengthen the approach's generality.
