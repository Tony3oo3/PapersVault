## Problem

Existing unsupervised VOS methods fuse appearance and motion cues via simple summation or concatenation, making them fragile when one modality is unreliable, and their temporal aggregation strategies either rely on a single anchor frame or require expensive iterative refinement over all frames, failing to efficiently capture global video context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240485/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes RGB video frames and pre-computed optical flow maps (generated by RAFT and converted from 2-channel to 3-channel RGB format) at 352×352 or 512×512 resolution, processing 4-frame snippets during training. It outputs binary segmentation masks at the same spatial resolution as the input, with no post-processing required. Optical flow is computed offline and cached, so inference only involves the segmentation network itself.

## Method

The paper proposes two prototype-based attention modules within a VGG-16 two-stream encoder-decoder with ASPP. Inter-Modality Attention (IMA) generates prototypes via channel-wise softmax on encoder features, computes self-correlation maps (cosine similarity between prototypes and features), and then performs cross-attention between appearance and motion prototypes to mutually transfer value features, replacing naive summation with adaptive, prototype-level information exchange. Inter-Frame Attention (IFA) uniformly samples N reference frames from the video, generates prototypes for each, and uses query-key attention to propagate global temporal context to the query frame, followed by a spatial correlation step to align the transferred context with the query's spatial layout. Both modules are applied at the 4th and 5th encoding blocks (IMA) and 5th block (IFA, in parallel with IMA). Training is two-stage: the appearance branch is pre-trained on DUTS, then both branches are jointly fine-tuned on DAVIS 2016 and YouTube-VOS 2018 with cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On the DAVIS 2016 validation set, DPA achieves a G_M score of 86.9% at 352×352 resolution and 87.6% at 512×512, outperforming all prior methods including HFAN (87.0% at 512×512) and D²Conv3D (86.0%). On the FBMS test set, DPA reaches J_M of 81.2% (352×352) and 83.4% (512×512), surpassing the previous best OAST (81.9%) at the higher resolution. On the more challenging YouTube-Objects dataset, DPA achieves a mean J score of 73.7%, exceeding TMO (71.5%) and AMC-Net (71.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPA outperforms all 21 compared baselines on the DAVIS 2016 validation set. At 352×352, DPA (G_M=86.9) surpasses the closest competitors HFAN (87.0 at 512×512), D²Conv3D (86.0), TMO (86.1), and PMN (85.9); at 512×512, DPA extends its lead to G_M=87.6, beating HFAN by 0.6 points. On FBMS, DPA at 512×512 (J_M=83.4) exceeds OAST (81.9) and TMO (79.9) by 1.5 and 3.5 points respectively, demonstrating robustness to multi-object scenarios. On YouTube-Objects, DPA achieves a mean J of 73.7%, outperforming TMO (71.5%), AMC-Net (71.1%), and RTNet (71.0%) by 2.2–2.6 points, with particularly large gains on Cat (+7.6 over TMO) and Dog (+5.0 over TMO). Notably, DPA achieves these results without post-processing, while several baselines (PDB, AGS, COSNet, AD-Net, MATNet, DFNet, RTNet, AMC-Net) rely on post-processing steps. The inference speed of 24.2 fps at 352×352 is competitive with fast methods like PMN (41.3 fps) and TMO (43.2 fps) while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines in unsupervised VOS: two-stream appearance-motion fusion (MATNet, FSNet, HFAN, AMC-Net, PMN, TMO) and temporal context aggregation (COSNet, AGNN, ADNet, F2Net, IMP). It departs from prior modality fusion by replacing naive summation or concatenation with a prototype-based cross-attention mechanism (IMA) that adaptively allocates features between modalities, and from prior temporal methods by replacing iterative refinement or single-anchor-frame propagation with a uniform-sampling memory bank and attention readout (IFA). The prototype framework, inspired by OCR's object-contextual representations, is repurposed here as a feature-refinement preprocessing step for both attention modules rather than as a segmentation head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual prototype attention design offers a principled, computationally efficient alternative to iterative temporal refinement, achieving SOTA on DAVIS 2016, FBMS, and YouTube-Objects with only ~4M additional parameters over the baseline. However, the method still depends on a pre-trained optical flow estimator (RAFT) and a VGG-16 backbone, and the margin over the strongest competitor (HFAN) is modest (~0.6% GM at 512×512), leaving open whether the prototype attention mechanism generalizes beyond unsupervised VOS or benefits from more modern backbones. The uniform frame-sampling strategy in IFA also raises questions about whether adaptive or content-aware sampling could further improve temporal context capture.
