## Problem

Existing face retouching methods are designed for single images and produce unstable, flickering results when applied to video sequences, while generic video enhancement methods like ProPainter lack the precision to localize and remove specific facial imperfections. There is no dedicated approach that jointly models the temporal motion of imperfections and leverages cross-frame context to achieve stable, high-fidelity face retouching in videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target frame and up to 6 reference frames (all resized to 512×512) from a raw face video, along with their encoder features. It produces a retouched target frame where imperfection regions are replaced with normal skin, plus a refined per-frame imperfection map (values normalized to [0,1]) that localizes the defects. Training uses paired raw-retouched sequences constructed from FFHQR image pairs via random cropping, flipping, and translation to emulate video data.

## Method

VRetouchEr adopts an encoder–transformer–decoder architecture with two key modules. The Flow-based Imperfection Refinement (FIR) module estimates per-pixel displacement of imperfections between consecutive frames using a flow network trained against SpyNet-derived ground truth, then warps the previous frame's imperfection map into the current frame and fuses it with the current frame's detection via adaptive convolution, yielding a more stable and precise imperfection map. The Multi-frame Masked Attention (MMA) mechanism injects these maps into each transformer block: target features masked by the imperfection map serve as queries, while reference-frame features masked by the complement (normal skin) serve as keys and values, so cross-attention selectively replaces defect regions with clean skin from multiple frames while preserving unchanged regions. Training jointly optimizes flow estimation, imperfection localization (L1 against ground-truth difference maps), content fidelity (L1 + VGG-19 perceptual loss), and a sequence-level adversarial loss, all from scratch.

## Results

On FFHQR-Seq, VRetouchEr achieves PSNR 39.75 dB, SSIM 0.9813, LPIPS 0.0169, and VFID 6.375, outperforming the second-best method BPFRe (PSNR 38.69, VFID 7.604) by 1.06 dB in PSNR and 1.23 in VFID. On the in-the-wild MRFV dataset, VRetouchEr reaches PSNR 37.63 dB and VFID 10.368, a substantial 44.8% relative improvement in VFID over BPFRe (18.772). In a user study with 50 participants, VRetouchEr received 88.45% Rank-1 votes, far exceeding BPFRe (11.15%) and ProPainter (0.25%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRetouchEr outperforms all eight baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, VFID) across both FFHQR-Seq and MRFV. The closest competitor is BPFRe [45] (Xie et al., CVPR 2023), a single-image face retouching method; VRetouchEr surpasses it by 1.06 dB PSNR and 1.23 VFID on FFHQR-Seq, and by 1.31 dB PSNR and 8.4 VFID on MRFV, where the gap widens due to BPFRe's instability on video sequences. ProPainter [51] (Zhou et al., CVPR 2023), a video inpainting method, ranks second on MRFV but still trails VRetouchEr by 1.36 dB PSNR and 9.98 VFID, reflecting its lack of imperfection-specific localization. Image restoration methods (MPRNet [48], RestoreFormer [42], GPEN [47]) cluster in the middle, achieving 35.7–38.5 dB PSNR on FFHQR-Seq but degrading more sharply on MRFV (35.7–35.8 dB). Pix2PixHD [41] (Wang et al., CVPR 2018) and BIPNet [11] (Dudhane et al., CVPR 2022) perform weakest, with Pix2PixHD scoring only 33.51 dB PSNR on MRFV. The user study confirms these quantitative findings: VRetouchEr dominates with 88.45% first-place votes, while BPFRe and ProPainter split the remaining top ranks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VRetouchEr carves out a new niche at the intersection of single-image face retouching (AutoRetouch, BPFRe, ABPN) and video inpainting/restoration (ProPainter, BIPNet), which neither handles adequately for the specific problem of tracking and removing facial imperfections across frames. It repurposes optical flow estimation (SpyNet) not for general motion compensation but specifically to model the displacement of imperfections, a targeted use that departs from ProPainter's general-purpose flow. The multi-frame masked attention mechanism extends the standard cross-attention paradigm of vision transformers by using imperfection maps as soft masks to selectively attend to normal-skin regions in reference frames, a design choice that distinguishes it from generic video transformer approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Face video retouching is practically critical for live-streaming, virtual conferencing, and professional video production, yet prior methods either ignore temporal context (single-image GANs) or lack imperfection-specific localization (general video inpainting). A key limitation is that training relies on emulated video sequences (FFHQR-Seq) constructed from static image pairs, and the real-world evaluation set (MRFV) is modest at 200 videos, leaving open questions about generalization to longer sequences, diverse skin tones, and real-time deployment. The finding that imperfection-specific flow (rather than general optical flow) substantially stabilizes detection across frames is a useful insight that could inform other temporal editing tasks.
