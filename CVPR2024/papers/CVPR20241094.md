## Problem

NeRFs trained on low-resolution multi-view images cannot represent high-frequency scene details, and naively upscaling individual views with 2D super-resolution models produces cross-view inconsistencies that blur the resulting 3D reconstruction. Prior super-resolution NeRF methods either require high-resolution reference images of the target scene or enforce view-consistency only in the low-resolution domain, leaving a gap for HR-consistent, view-consistent detail generation from LR inputs alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241094/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes low-resolution multi-view RGB images (400×400 on NeRF-Synthetic, 504×378 on LLFF) and a text prompt as input, and produces a super-resolution NeRF capable of rendering at 4× the input resolution (1600×1600 and 2016×1512 respectively). No high-resolution reference images or large multi-view HR datasets are required; the 2D super-resolution prior is entirely borrowed from a pretrained Stable Diffusion ×4 latent upscaler. Evaluation uses 100 test poses in a circular arrangement, scored with NIQE (no-reference quality) and warped LPIPS (cross-view consistency) rather than comparison against HR ground truth.

## Method

DiSR-NeRF combines two key components: Iterative 3D Synchronization (I3DS) and Renoised Score Distillation (RSD). I3DS alternates between an upscaling stage (rendering LR NeRF images at 4× resolution and independently upscaling each with a diffusion model) and a synchronization stage (retraining the NeRF on the upscaled images using standard NeRF optimization), exploiting NeRF's inherent multi-view consistency to progressively discard inconsistent details and converge on coherent HR features. RSD is a novel score-distillation objective that replaces both pure ancestral sampling (which generates structurally inconsistent details) and standard SDS (which over-smooths) by optimizing zero-initialized learnable latent residuals toward the predicted denoised latents at intermediate timesteps along a linearly decreasing schedule, effectively transforming ancestral sampling into a coarse-to-fine optimization that yields sharper, LR-consistent details. The entire pipeline uses the pretrained SD×4 latent diffusion upscaler as the 2D SR backbone, with the UNet providing noise predictions and the VAE handling image-to-latent encoding and decoding.

## Results

On NeRF-Synthetic, DiSR-NeRF achieves the best NIQE of 5.386 and warped LPIPS of 0.144, outperforming the closest competitor IN2N (NIQE 5.847, LPIPS 0.173) and NeRF-SR (NIQE 6.012, LPIPS 0.158). On the real-world LLFF dataset, DiSR-NeRF similarly leads with NIQE 5.544 and LPIPS 0.141, beating IN2N (NIQE 6.473, LPIPS 0.157) and NeRF-SR (NIQE 7.038, LPIPS 0.156). The method achieves the largest margins over DreamFusion (NIQE gap of ~3.2–3.3) and the raw NGP baseline (NIQE gap of ~3.6–4.4), confirming that both I3DS and RSD contribute substantially to quality and view consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiSR-NeRF outperforms all six baselines on both NIQE and warped LPIPS across both datasets. The closest competitor is IN2N (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF adapted with SD×4 ancestral sampling), which DiSR-NeRF beats by 0.46 NIQE and 0.029 LPIPS on NeRF-Synthetic, and by 0.93 NIQE and 0.016 LPIPS on LLFF — the gap being larger on real-world data where cross-view inconsistency is more pronounced. NeRF-SR (without its HR refinement module) is second-closest, trailing DiSR-NeRF by 0.63 NIQE and 0.014 LPIPS on synthetic, and 1.49 NIQE and 0.015 LPIPS on LLFF. DreamFusion (SDS applied directly to NeRF renders) performs poorly, with NIQE 8.624/8.857, confirming that direct SDS supervision over small patches causes severe blurring and failure to converge. The raw NGP baseline (no SR) and the independent SD×4 upscaling baseline both show substantially worse quality, validating that both the 3D synchronization (I3DS) and the RSD objective are necessary for high-quality, view-consistent super-resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiSR-NeRF sits at the intersection of NeRF-based 3D reconstruction and diffusion-model-based 2D super-resolution. It builds directly on the NeRF formulation (Mildenhall et al.) for the 3D representation and on latent diffusion upscalers (Stable Diffusion ×4, from Rombach et al.) as the source of SR priors, while adapting the Score Distillation Sampling objective from DreamFusion (Poole et al.) into a new Renoised Score Distillation (RSD) loss. The key departure from the closest prior work, Super-NeRF (Han et al.), is twofold: (1) replacing the GAN-based ESRGAN latent search with a diffusion upscaler's score function for sharper details, and (2) introducing the Iterative 3D Synchronization (I3DS) loop that explicitly enforces multi-view consistency in the HR domain rather than only in the LR domain. The paper also contrasts with NeRF-SR and RefSR-NeRF, which require HR reference images, by showing that a pretrained 2D diffusion upscaler can substitute for such references entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DiSR-NeRF removes the need for high-resolution multi-view captures or large HR datasets, making NeRF super-resolution feasible for devices with inherently low-resolution sensors (drones, CCTV). The I3DS alternating-training pattern and the RSD objective are modular ideas that could transfer to other 3D generation or editing tasks where a 2D generative prior must be reconciled with multi-view geometry. Limitations include evaluation on only 8+8 scenes at a fixed 4× upscaling factor, reliance on the Instant-NGP backbone, and the absence of a quantitative comparison with Super-NeRF (code unavailable). Open questions include whether I3DS scales to higher upscaling factors, larger scenes, or dynamic content, and whether RSD generalizes beyond the Stable Diffusion ×4 upscaler to other diffusion architectures.
