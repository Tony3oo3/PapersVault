## Problem

Existing query-based temporal action detection methods, inspired by DETR, identify each action by its center point and predict duration via offsets. This instance-level paradigm overlooks the fact that actions in videos are fundamentally defined by their start and end boundaries, leading to sub-optimal boundary localization. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a dual-level framework that explicitly models both instance-level semantics and boundary-level details for multi-label TAD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240530/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted two-stream I3D features (pre-trained on Kinetics) at a stride of 4, 8, or 16 frames depending on the dataset, producing a feature tensor X ∈ R^{T×D}. For MultiTHUMOS and TSU, feature sequences are cropped into fixed-length windows (256 and 96 frames respectively) with a 0.75 stride ratio during training and 0.25 during inference; for Charades, the full video feature is input directly with zero-padding. The output is a sparse set of action instances, each comprising a start timestamp, end timestamp, and category score, produced without any NMS post-processing.

## Method

DualDETR employs two groups of decoder queries—boundary-level (start and end) and instance-level—each decomposed into position and content vectors, decoded through a two-branch structure where the encoder feature map is partitioned along channels (D/4 for start, D/4 for end, D/2 for instance) so each branch attends to level-specific semantics via deformable cross-attention. A joint query initialization strategy aligns the dual-level queries by matching them one-to-one with encoder proposals and initializing their position vectors from proposal boundaries and content vectors from proposal features. After each decoder layer, a mutual refinement module exchanges position information between the two levels (averaging boundary-derived center/duration with instance-derived center/duration), enabling complementary refinement. Training uses bipartite matching with focal classification loss, temporal IoU loss, and L1 regression loss, following the standard DETR-style end-to-end optimization.

## Results

On MultiTHUMOS, DualDETR achieves 32.6% DetmAP and 45.5% SegmAP, outperforming the previous best query-based method PointTAD by +9.1% DetmAP (32.6 vs. 23.5) and +5.7% SegmAP (45.5 vs. 39.8). On Charades, DualDETR reaches 15.3% DetmAP (+3.2% over PointTAD) and 23.2% SegmAP. On the highly-overlapped TSU dataset, DualDETR achieves 20.8% DetmAP and 34.8% SegmAP with 5.5 GFLOPs, surpassing MS-TCT by +10.2% DetmAP while using fewer FLOPs (5.5 vs. 6.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualDETR decisively outperforms all query-based multi-label TAD baselines on DetmAP: +9.1% over PointTAD (23.5→32.6) on MultiTHUMOS and +3.2% over PointTAD (12.1→15.3) on Charades, confirming the benefit of dual-level boundary modeling. On SegmAP, DualDETR leads on MultiTHUMOS (45.5 vs. CTRN's 44.0 and MS-TCT's 43.1) but trails the best segmentation-oriented methods on Charades (23.2 vs. MS-TCT's 25.4 and CTRN's 25.3), indicating that frame-wise classification remains slightly favored by dense-prediction architectures on that dataset. On TSU, where actions heavily overlap, DualDETR achieves 20.8% DetmAP vs. MS-TCT's 10.6% and 34.8% SegmAP vs. MS-TCT's 33.7%, while using fewer FLOPs (5.5 vs. 6.6). Against traditional TAD methods on MultiTHUMOS (Table 3), DualDETR surpasses TriDet (30.7), ActionFormer (29.6), and TadTR (27.4) by 1.9–15.2% in average DetmAP, demonstrating that methods designed for single-label scenarios degrade substantially under multi-label overlap. The convergence study (Fig. 4) further shows DualDETR converges faster than PointTAD and comparably to the dense-prediction ActionFormer, attributed to the joint query initialization providing strong positional and semantic priors from early decoder layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualDETR extends the query-based TAD lineage initiated by DETR and adapted to video by methods like TadTR and PointTAD, which detect actions at the instance level (center point + duration). The key departure is the introduction of a parallel boundary-level query group alongside the instance-level group, motivated by the observation that actions—unlike objects—are primarily defined by their start/end boundaries rather than centroids. The two-branch decoding with channel-partitioned encoder features and the joint query initialization strategy (drawing on DAB-DETR's position/content vector formulation and Deformable DETR's attention mechanism) are novel architectural choices that go beyond simply adding boundary features to a single decoder, as attempted by RTDNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial det-mAP gains (+9.1% on MultiTHUMOS) demonstrate that the instance-level-only paradigm used by prior query-based TAD methods leaves significant boundary localization performance on the table, particularly in multi-label scenarios with overlapping actions. The two-branch design with explicit feature separation offers a principled way to handle the granularity mismatch between holistic action semantics and fine-grained boundary cues. Open questions include whether the channel-dimension partitioning is the optimal way to separate boundary and instance semantics, whether the approach scales to even longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the dual-level idea transfers to other temporal detection or segmentation tasks beyond TAD.
