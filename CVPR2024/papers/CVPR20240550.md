## Problem

Existing learning-with-noisy-labels (LNL) methods rely heavily on the model's own predictions for relabeling and sample selection, creating a self-guided loop where inevitable prediction errors compound across iterations. This over-reliance on uncertain predictions degrades generalization and produces complex, poorly separated feature manifolds, even when consistency regularization or semi-supervised losses are added. The paper asks whether incorporating explicit distributional structure from the feature space can break this dependency on the model's own (potentially wrong) predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and Red Mini-ImageNet, 64×64 for Animal-10N, 256×256 for Mini-WebVision) paired with noisy class labels. Beyond the standard class prediction output, the method computes structural soft labels for every training sample: a C-dimensional probability vector derived from reverse k-NN arrow counts in the normalized feature space. These structural labels are applied to the full training set (not just a selected subset), which is the key distinction from prior relabeling approaches that only operate on high-confidence samples.

## Method

The core mechanism is a reverse k-NN distribution estimator: each sample emits k arrows to its k nearest neighbors in the L2-normalized feature space, and the number of arrows received from samples predicted as class c approximates P(x|c). Applying Bayes' rule with class priors yields a structural soft label l_st for every sample, which is robust to uniform noise and smooths non-uniform noise when k_st is sufficiently large. Training builds on SSR's relabeling (threshold-based) and k-NN sample selection, then adds a structural loss: cross-entropy between the model's output on strongly augmented + mixup-ed versions of all samples and their structural labels. The total loss combines this structural loss with a standard cross-entropy on selected samples and a feature consistency loss (cosine similarity between weakly and strongly augmented features), encouraging the model to preserve the smoothness/cluster assumption across the entire dataset rather than only on a confidently selected subset.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with instance-dependent noise (IDN), LSL achieves 97.13% test accuracy at 20% noise and 95.81% at 50% noise, outperforming the strongest baseline InstanceGM (96.68% and 95.90%) across all noise levels. On CIFAR-100 IDN, LSL reaches 80.94% at 20% noise and 77.95% at 50% noise, surpassing InstanceGM (79.69% and 77.19%) and SSR (78.84% and 72.83%). On real-world noisy datasets, LSL improves over SSR by 1.1% on CIFAR10N-W (94.57% vs 93.50%), 3.4% on CIFAR100N-F (74.46% vs 71.99%), and 0.6% on Animal-10N (89.1% vs 88.5%). On large-scale Mini-WebVision, LSL achieves 81.40% Top-1 accuracy, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSL outperforms all 23 baselines on CIFAR-10 IDN across all five noise rates, with the largest margin over SSR at 50% noise (95.81% vs 94.06%, +1.75%) and over DivideMix at 50% noise (95.81% vs 93.04%, +2.77%). On CIFAR-100 IDN, the advantage over SSR grows substantially at higher noise: +5.12% at 50% noise (77.95% vs 72.83%), while the gap over InstanceGM is a more modest +0.76%. On real-world noisy labels, LSL beats SSR by 1.1% on CIFAR10N-W and 3.4% on CIFAR100N-F, and by 0.6% on Animal-10N (89.1% vs 88.5%). On Red Mini-Imagenet (CNWL), LSL is second-best behind InstanceGM (54.68% vs 58.38% at 20% noise), indicating the method is less effective on this particular dataset. On large-scale Mini-WebVision and ImageNet ILSVRC12, LSL achieves the best Top-1 accuracy (81.40% and 77.00% respectively), surpassing SSR by 0.48% and 1.24%, confirming strong generalization to large-scale settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SSR (Feng et al., 2021) as its base framework for confidence-based relabeling and k-NN sample selection, and repurposes the Reverse k-NN algorithm (Tao et al., 2004) from database search into a distribution-estimation tool for generating soft structural labels. The key conceptual departure from prior LNL methods (DivideMix, SSR, InstanceGM) is that instead of relying exclusively on the model's own predictions to guide relabeling and selection—a self-referential loop that accumulates errors—the paper injects an external distributional signal computed over the feature manifold. This draws on the smoothness and cluster assumptions from semi-supervised learning, framing noisy-label robustness as a problem of preserving structural invariants rather than merely filtering clean samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural label approach offers a principled way to break the self-guided prediction loop that limits generalization in existing LNL methods, and the simplicity of the reverse k-NN computation makes it easy to plug into existing pipelines. However, the method still depends on the model's predicted labels to group samples before computing reverse k-NN, so it inherits some of the same vulnerability it aims to mitigate. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-instance-dependent noise patterns, how the k_st hyperparameter should be set for very large-scale datasets where pairwise distance computation becomes expensive, and whether the structural label signal remains informative when the model's feature space is poorly calibrated early in training.
