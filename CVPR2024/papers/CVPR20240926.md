## Problem

Existing zero-shot temporal action localization (ZS-TAL) methods require fine-tuning on large annotated training sets, which is impractical when labeled data is unavailable and inherently degrades out-of-distribution generalization. The paper demonstrates that state-of-the-art ZS-TAL models (EffPrompt, STALE) suffer a >15% mAP drop when evaluated cross-domain, exposing a critical gap: no prior method can localize and recognize unseen actions in arbitrary videos without any training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single untrimmed video (RGB frames extracted at original frame rate, resized to 224×224) and a predefined set of action class names (prompted as 'a video of action {CLS}') as input, and produces M action proposals each consisting of a class label and start/end time displacement. Adaptation is performed per-sample: only the vision and language projection layers of CoCa (ViT-L/14) are updated for up to 50 steps (THUMOS14) or 25 steps (ActivityNet-v1.3) with early stopping, after which all optimized parameters are discarded and re-initialized before processing the next video.

## Method

T3AL performs test-time adaptation of a frozen CoCa VLM in three stages. First, video-level pseudo-labelling averages all frame visual embeddings and selects the class with maximum cosine similarity to its text embedding. Second, self-supervised prediction refinement partitions frames into positive (high-scoring) and negative (low-scoring) sets relative to the pseudo-label, then adapts the projection layers using a BYOL-inspired Representation loss (pulling positive frame embeddings together, exploiting temporal adjacency as free augmentation) and a Separation loss (pushing positive scores toward 1 and negative scores toward 0). Third, text-guided region suppression uses CoCa's captioning decoder to generate frame-level captions within each proposal, computes pairwise cosine similarities among region-level caption embeddings, and suppresses proposals whose textual representation is insufficiently consistent with the others. The key distinction from prior ZS-TAL work is that no labeled training data is used at any stage; the model deduces the localization task entirely from the unlabelled test video itself.

## Results

On THUMOS14 (50%-50% split), T3AL achieves 10.4% average mAP (IoU 0.3–0.7), substantially outperforming the strongest zero-shot VLM baseline CoCa (3.5%) by +6.9% absolute mAP. On ActivityNet-v1.3 (50%-50%), T3AL reaches 14.3% average mAP (IoU 0.5/0.75/0.95) versus 1.1% for CoCa, a +13.2% absolute gain. The video-level pseudo-labelling step alone (T3AL_{T=0}) already yields 4.8% on THUMOS14 and 13.3% on ActivityNet-v1.3, showing that the test-time adaptation step adds a further +1.0% and +1.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T3AL dramatically outperforms all naive VLM baselines: on THUMOS14 (50%-50%), it achieves 10.4% avg mAP versus 3.5% for CoCa, 3.1% for CLIP_16, and 3.0% for CLIP_32, representing a 3× improvement over the best zero-shot VLM. On ActivityNet-v1.3 (50%-50%), the gap widens further: T3AL scores 14.3% avg mAP versus 1.1% for CoCa and 0.3% for CLIP_16. However, T3AL still trails the training-based ZS-TAL methods—EffPrompt (21.9%) and STALE (22.2%) on THUMOS14, and EffPrompt (19.6%) and STALE (20.5%) on ActivityNet-v1.3—by roughly 11–12% absolute mAP. The two-stage CLIP_16 w/Detector baseline (15.7% on THUMOS14) also exceeds T3AL, though it relies on a separately trained proposal detector. The critical distinction is that T3AL requires no annotated training data at all, whereas all competing methods are fine-tuned on labeled sets; the paper's oracle experiments (Fig. 4) show that with perfect pseudo-labels, region counts, and positive/negative selection, T3AL reaches 22.6% avg mAP on THUMOS14, matching in-domain training-based SOTA without any training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in ZS-TAL (EffPrompt, STALE, UnLoc) that requires fine-tuning a VLM on labeled training data, instead repurposing test-time adaptation (TTA) techniques originally developed for image classification (TENT, MEMO, TPT) to the video domain. It builds on CoCa as the VLM backbone and adapts the BYOL self-supervised loss to operate over temporal frame sequences, using the video's natural temporal dimension as a free source of augmented views. The text-guided suppression step is a novel use of CoCa's captioning decoder to filter spurious proposals, a capability not exploited by prior ZS-TAL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that existing ZS-TAL methods suffer a >15% mAP drop in cross-domain settings, raising practical concerns about their robustness in real-world deployment where data distributions shift. The oracle experiments suggesting that test-time adaptation could match trained SOTA (22.6% mAP) without any labeled data is a provocative finding that challenges the assumption that supervised training is necessary for action localization. Key limitations remain: the method assumes a single dominant action per video, relies on heuristically selected positive/negative frames that may include uninformative content (black screens, titles), and the actual (non-oracle) performance still trails trained methods by roughly 12 mAP points on THUMOS14.
