## Problem

Existing selective prediction methods for vision-language models require access to model internals (features, gradients), retraining, or a held-out validation set, none of which are available for commercial black-box VLMs accessed via API. This paper asks whether a model's reliability on a visual question can be assessed using only the question, image, and the model's own responses, without any privileged access to its architecture or parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240253/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (480×480) and a natural-language visual question as input, and produces a consistency score in [0,1] representing the fraction of k generated rephrasings on which the black-box model gives the same answer. This score is thresholded to yield an abstain/answer decision. The black-box model is queried k+1 times per input (once for the original question, k times for rephrasings), and the VQG model is queried k times to generate the rephrasings, making the total API cost linear in the number of rephrasings.

## Method

The core mechanism is cyclic rephrasing generation as approximate neighborhood sampling: a small VQG model (~200M params, initialized from BLIP and fine-tuned on VQAv2 with a standard language modeling loss) is trained to approximate p(Q|A,V), the inverse of the VQA task. Given a black-box model's predicted answer a₀ for input (v,q), the VQG model generates k rephrasings q′ by sampling from p(Q|a₀,v) using nucleus sampling (top-p=0.9). Each rephrasing is fed back to the black-box model, and the consistency score is the fraction of rephrasings yielding the same answer. The key insight is that surface-form variations of a question (rephrasings) are mapped close together in feature space, making them functional substitutes for ε-neighborhood samples without requiring feature access. The approach is model-agnostic, requires no retraining of the black-box model, no validation set, and works even when the VQG model is an order of magnitude smaller than the black-box model (200M vs 11B parameters).

## Results

On OK-VQA at a 20% risk level, filtering predictions by neighborhood consistency (n≥5) increases coverage from 0.25 to 0.44 for BLIP and from 0.21 to 0.47 for ALBEF, meaning the model can answer nearly twice as many questions while maintaining the same error rate. On AdVQA at the same 20% risk level, coverage improves from 0.01 to 0.01 (BLIP) and 0.0 to 0.04 (ELBEF), showing smaller but consistent gains. Across all three datasets (VQAv2, OK-VQA, AdVQA), higher consistency levels (n≥3 to n≥5) consistently identify lower-risk slices of the test set compared to the unfiltered baseline (n≥0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external named baselines; instead, it evaluates the proposed consistency-based selective prediction against the unfiltered baseline (n≥0, i.e., no consistency filtering). On OK-VQA, the n≥5 consistency filter outperforms the n≥0 baseline by 0.19 coverage (BLIP: 0.44 vs 0.25) and 0.26 coverage (EBEF: 0.47 vs 0.21) at a fixed 20% risk level, representing a 76% and 124% relative increase in the number of questions the model can safely answer. On AdVQA, the improvement is more modest (EBEF: 0.04 vs 0.0 at 20% risk) but still directionally consistent. The paper also demonstrates that consistency defines a different ordering over instances than raw model confidence, showing that confidence alone fails to separate correct from incorrect answers on out-of-distribution and adversarial inputs while consistency does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the neighborhood-consistency principle from Attribution-Based Confidence (Jha et al., 2019), which perturbs inputs in feature space using gradient guidance, to a black-box setting where feature access is unavailable. The key conceptual move is treating rephrasings of a visual question as approximate samples from the ε-neighborhood of the original question in feature space, leveraging the linguistic insight that surface-form variations of the same deep structure should map to nearby points. Prior selective VQA work (Whitehead et al., 2022; Dancette et al., 2023) requires model internals or held-out validation sets, while this paper introduces a small VQG model as a proxy to generate rephrasings without any access to the black-box model's parameters, gradients, or training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As frontier vision-language models become increasingly available only as API endpoints, practitioners need practical ways to assess answer reliability without model internals; this paper provides a simple, model-agnostic procedure that requires only a small auxiliary VQG model and a handful of additional API calls per query. The finding that consistency and confidence define fundamentally different orderings over questions is practically useful for deployment. However, the study is limited to models ≤13B parameters and a <700M VQG model, leaving open whether the approach transfers to truly large, architecturally different black-box models (e.g., GPT-4V) or to non-VQA multimodal tasks. The reliance on a VQG model trained on VQAv2 also raises questions about domain generality.
