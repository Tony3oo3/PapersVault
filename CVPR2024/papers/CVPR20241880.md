## Problem

Existing point-based image editing methods fail to simultaneously satisfy user-friendly interaction, high editing precision, and broad generalization. DragGAN is limited by StyleGAN's restricted generative capacity, while DragDiffusion requires time-consuming LoRA fine-tuning and hand-drawn masks, making it impractical for interactive use. No prior approach achieves precise dragging on diffusion models without per-image adaptation or manual mask annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241880/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a set of handle points with corresponding target points, and a text prompt as input, and produces a single edited image where the content at each handle point has been moved to its target position. Notably, the modifiable region mask is generated automatically from the latent's gradient field rather than requiring user annotation. The pipeline operates entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion 1.5 (VAE-encoded), with optimization performed on the latent at the 35th DDIM inversion step and final image decoded via the VAE.

## Method

EasyDrag is a zero-shot, fine-tuning-free framework built on Stable Diffusion that combines three key components. First, stable motion supervision replaces DragGAN's drifting feature tracking by always computing cosine-similarity losses against features extracted from the original image using the 2nd and 3rd upper U-Net layers, ensuring the optimization target remains fixed. Second, an auto mask generation mechanism uses normalized gradients of the optimized latent z_t (threshold g=0.4) to dynamically determine which regions are modifiable, updating the mask each time a handle point advances toward its target. Third, reference guidance injects mutual self-attention from the DDIM inversion trajectory into the denoising process, blending the reference prediction with the normal prediction at scale w to preserve identity consistency without requiring LoRA fine-tuning. The overall pipeline is: DDIM inversion → iterative latent optimization with stable motion supervision and auto mask → denoising with reference guidance.

## Results

On the DragBench dataset (205 images), EasyDrag without mask achieves the best Image Fidelity (IF = 0.910) and a competitive Mean Distance (MD = 32.55), while with mask it achieves the best MD (29.07) with IF = 0.889. Inference time is 30.6 s (no mask) or 27.3 s (with mask) on a single A100 GPU, roughly 2.3× faster than DragDiffusion (69.4 s). The method also outperforms SDE-Drag on both metrics (IF 0.910 vs 0.871; MD 32.55 vs 45.44) and DragGAN by a wide margin (IF 0.910 vs 0.695; MD 32.55 vs 57.3).

**Table 1: Quantitative evaluation on DragBench. IF (↑) measures image fidelity; MD (↓) measures mean distance of handle points from targets.**

| Method | IF (↑) | MD (↓) | Time (s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| DragGAN | 0.695 | 57.30 | 59.1 |
| DragDiffusion | 0.882 | 36.71 | 69.4 |

| **Ours w/ mask** | 0.889 | **29.07** | **27.3** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

EasyDrag outperforms all four baselines on the primary DragBench evaluation. Against DragDiffusion (the closest competitor), EasyDrag improves IF from 0.882 to 0.910 and MD from 36.71 to 32.55 while cutting inference time from 69.4 s to 30.6 s, largely because it eliminates the LoRA fine-tuning step. Against SDE-Drag, EasyDrag leads on both IF (0.910 vs 0.871) and MD (32.55 vs 45.44). Against DragGAN, the gap is substantial on both metrics (IF 0.910 vs 0.695; MD 32.55 vs 57.3), reflecting the limited generative capacity of StyleGAN. In the motion-supervision ablation (Table 2), EasyDrag's stable motion supervision achieves MD 29.07 versus DragonDiffusion's 52.21, though DragonDiffusion edges it slightly on IF (0.899 vs 0.889), confirming that the supervision strategy is the key differentiator for dragging precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits directly in the lineage of DragGAN (point-based editing on StyleGAN) and DragDiffusion (extending that framework to diffusion models via LoRA fine-tuning), but deliberately departs from both by eliminating LoRA fine-tuning and manual mask inputs. It borrows feature-correspondence ideas from DIFT for its stable motion supervision, draws on DragonDiffusion's energy-based formulation for the similarity loss, and adapts mutual self-attention (à la MasaCtrl) into a reference guidance mechanism for identity preservation. The key conceptual departure is learning motion features from the *original* image rather than from the progressively changing latent, which the authors argue fixes the misleading supervision inherited from DragGAN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing LoRA fine-tuning and manual masks, EasyDrag makes point-based diffusion editing substantially more accessible and faster (~30 s vs ~69 s per edit), which is practically important for interactive applications. The finding that auto-generated masks outperform user-drawn ones (IF 0.910 vs 0.889) challenges the assumption that precise user-specified regions are necessary for controlled editing. However, the method still struggles with very small editing regions and long-distance drags, and the evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (DragBench) and SD-1.5 variants, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer, more powerful diffusion backbones or to 3D/video editing.
