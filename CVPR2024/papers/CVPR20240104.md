## Problem

Diffusion models require many forward passes of a large denoising network to generate a single image, making inference slow and expensive. Existing speedup techniques—improved solvers and distillation—treat the U-Net as a black box and only reduce the number of steps, leaving the internal redundancy of the network untapped. This paper asks whether individual layer blocks within the denoising network can be identified as redundant across timesteps and skipped.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a photorealistic RGB image at 512×512 (LDM-512) or 768×768 (EMU-768) resolution, using classifier-free guidance with strength 5.0. The input is consumed through the standard diffusion pipeline (iterative denoising from Gaussian noise conditioned on text), and the output is a single image. The key operational detail is that the method requires no architectural modification to the base model; it only adds a small number of per-channel scale-shift parameters per cached block, optimized in a lightweight fine-tuning pass (~15k iterations on 8 A100s).

## Method

Block Caching exploits the observation that SpatialTransformer block outputs in a diffusion U-Net change smoothly and often negligibly between consecutive timesteps. An automatic cache schedule is derived by measuring the relative L1 change (L1_rel) of each block across timesteps and retaining a cached output until the accumulated change exceeds a threshold δ (default 0.5), at which point the block is recomputed. To prevent artifacts from feature misalignment, a lightweight scale-shift adjustment is added: per-channel scale and shift vectors (predicted from the timestep embedding via a linear layer) are applied at each cached block. These parameters are optimized with a distillation-inspired procedure where the cached model (student) is unrolled through the full denoising trajectory and matched against the uncached model (teacher) evaluated at the student's intermediate states, with all original weights frozen. Only SpatialTransformer blocks are cached, not ResBlocks, because ResBlocks change less smoothly and are critical for local detail generation.

## Results

On LDM-512 with the DPM solver, block caching with 20 steps plus scale-shift adjustment achieves FID 15.95 at 3.59 img/s (1.65× speedup), outperforming both the 20-step baseline (FID 17.15) and the latency-matched 14-step baseline (FID 18.67). With 50 steps, the full method reaches FID 15.18 at 1.59 img/s (1.82× speedup), beating the 30-step baseline (FID 17.21). On EMU-768, human evaluation across 1320 votes shows the caching method is preferred over the latency-matched baseline in every configuration, with win rates of 27.8–34.7% and loss rates of 17.9–28.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the naive step-reduction baseline: running the same LDM-512 or EMU-768 model with fewer denoising steps to match the latency of the caching configuration. On LDM-512, block caching with scale-shift at 20 steps (DPM) achieves FID 15.95, beating the 14-step baseline (FID 18.67) by 2.72 points while running at comparable speed (3.59 vs. 3.10 img/s). At 50 steps, the full method (FID 15.18) outperforms the 30-step baseline (FID 17.21) by 2.03 points. On EMU-768, human evaluation confirms the advantage: across all four solver/step configurations, the caching method wins more votes than it loses (e.g., 34.7% win vs. 28.4% lose for DPM 20-step), with the gap widening at 50 steps (27.8% win vs. 17.9% lose). The method also surpasses the same-step-count baseline without caching (e.g., FID 15.95 vs. 17.15 for DPM 20 steps), indicating that caching itself introduces a beneficial regularization effect on the denoising trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of speeding up diffusion models by treating the denoising U-Net as a black box. Prior work has focused on reducing the number of solver steps (DDIM, DPM-Solver) or distilling the network into a smaller/fewer-step student (progressive distillation, consistency models), all of which operate on the network's input/output interface. Here, the authors instead analyze the internal per-layer behavior across timesteps and exploit temporal redundancy in intermediate feature maps, introducing block caching as a new axis of acceleration orthogonal to solver and distillation approaches. The scale-shift adjustment mechanism is loosely inspired by distillation (student-teacher matching) but is far lighter, optimizing only per-channel scale and shift parameters while freezing all original weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 1.5×–1.8× inference speedup that is model-agnostic (LDM, EMU) and solver-agnostic (DDIM, DPM) is practically significant for deploying diffusion models in latency-sensitive applications. The finding that caching can *improve* FID over the same-step baseline (15.95 vs. 17.15) is a useful counterintuitive result suggesting that the denoising trajectory benefits from a mild momentum effect. Open questions include whether the technique transfers to diffusion transformer (DiT) architectures, video diffusion, or models with different block structures, and whether the per-model caching schedule computation (requiring forward passes over many prompts) can be made cheaper or learned end-to-end.
