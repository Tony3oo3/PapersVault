## Problem

Current multimodal systems rely on task-specific architectures and large supervised training sets, making them difficult to scale to new tasks. Humans, by contrast, solve new multimodal tasks in context with only a few demonstrations or simple instructions. This paper asks whether scaling up a generative multimodal model with a unified autoregressive objective can yield strong task-agnostic in-context learning across diverse understanding and generation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240434/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of text tokens and visual embeddings (64 per image, derived from 8×8 or 16×16 mean-pooled patches at 448×448 resolution), video frame sequences, and for generation, subject images paired with location images (bounding boxes drawn on black canvases). Outputs include free-form text responses, 1024×1024 generated images decoded from regressed visual embeddings via a diffusion-based decoder, and video clips from a 3D temporal U-Net decoder. The unified autoregressive formulation means the same next-token prediction objective governs both text generation and visual embedding regression.

## Method

Emu2 (37B parameters) connects a pretrained EVA-02-CLIP-E-plus visual encoder to a LLaMA-33B transformer via mean pooling each image into 8×8 patches followed by a linear projection, and decodes regressed visual embeddings through an SDXL-based visual decoder. The key architectural simplification over Emu is replacing the C-Former bridge with this direct mean-pool + projection connection. Pretraining proceeds in two stages: first on 162M image-text and 7M video-text pairs with captioning-only loss, then with joint text classification and image regression loss on interleaved, grounded, and language-only data. The visual decoder is trained independently as an image autoencoder (detokenizer) without requiring autoregressive inference of the language model at each step, enabling off-the-shelf training. Instruction tuning yields Emu2-Chat (conversational VQA) and Emu2-Gen (controllable generation accepting text, locations, and subject images as conditions), both using the same unified generative objective.

## Results

Emu2-Chat achieves state-of-the-art results across visual question answering and LMM benchmarks: 84.9 on VQAv2, 64.8 on OKVQA, 65.1 on GQA, 54.9 on VizWiz, and 66.6 on TextVQA. On comprehensive LMM benchmarks, it scores 48.5 on MM-Vet, 703.8 on TouchStone, 62.8 on SEED-Bench, and 34.1 on MMMU, surpassing all prior models. In the few-shot setting, the 37B Emu2 base model outperforms Flamingo-80B and IDEFICS-80B on VQAv2 (68.8 vs. 66.8/65.4 at 16-shot), VizWiz (57.0 vs. 48.4/48.3), and TextVQA (50.3 vs. 37.6/36.3) with a substantially smaller parameter count. For controllable generation, Emu2-Gen achieves 0.686 CLIP-I and 0.297 CLIP-T on MS-COCO text-to-image, and 0.766 DINO / 0.850 CLIP-I on DreamBench subject-driven generation, both setting new records among zero-shot methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Emu2-Chat outperforms LLaVA-1.5-13B by 4.9 points on VQAv2 (84.9 vs. 80.0), 1.8 on GQA (65.1 vs. 63.3), 1.3 on VizWiz (54.9 vs. 53.6), 5.3 on TextVQA (66.6 vs. 61.3), and 13.1 on MM-Vet (48.5 vs. 35.4). Against CogVLM, Emu2-Chat leads on VQAv2 (84.9 vs. 83.4), OKVQA (64.8 vs. 58.9), and TouchStone (703.8 vs. 662.6), but trails on TextVQA (66.6 vs. 68.1) and MMMU (34.1 vs. 30.1). Compared to Qwen-VL-13B-Chat, Emu2-Chat gains 6.7 on VQAv2, 8.2 on OKVQA, 7.6 on GQA, 16.0 on VizWiz, and 5.4 on TextVQA. In the few-shot setting (Table 1), the 37B Emu2 base model surpasses both Flamingo-80B and IDEFICS-80B on VQAv2 (68.8 vs. 66.8/65.4 at 16-shot), VizWiz (57.0 vs. 48.4/48.3), and TextVQA (50.3 vs. 37.6/36.3), though it lags on Hateful Memes (66.0 vs. 70.0 for Flamingo-80B). For subject-driven generation, Emu2-Gen achieves 0.766 DINO and 0.850 CLIP-I in zero-shot, surpassing Kosmos-G (0.694/0.847) and SuTI (0.741/0.819), while matching or exceeding fine-tuning methods like DreamBooth (0.668/0.803) without per-subject training.

## Relation to Prior Work

Emu2 is a direct architectural simplification and scale-up of Emu (14B), replacing the C-Former cross-attention module with mean pooling over 8×8 patches plus a linear projection, while retaining the unified predict-the-next-multimodal-element autoregressive objective. It inherits the generative pretraining paradigm from Emu but departs from the Flamingo/IDEFICS/Kosmos line of work by treating visual tokens as continuous embeddings regressed in the same autoregressive stream as text tokens, rather than using discrete visual tokens or cross-attention adapters. The instruction-tuning stage (Emu2-Chat) follows the LLaVA/InstructBLIP recipe of role-token formatting and cross-entropy on answer tokens, while the generation stage (Emu2-Gen) extends the Kosmos-G subject-driven formulation with a unified text+location+image conditioning interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Emu2 demonstrates that a single 37B autoregressive model can serve as both a strong few-shot multimodal understanding system and a controllable image/video generator, reducing the need for task-specific architectures. The practical insight that the visual decoder can be trained independently as a 'detokenizer' without the language model lowers the barrier to swapping in better decoders. Open questions include whether the pronounced zero-shot weakness (e.g., 33.5 on VQAv2 vs. 67.0 at 4-shot) reflects a fundamental limitation of the generative pretraining objective or can be closed with more diverse pretraining data, and how the model's in-context learning generalizes to truly novel task formats beyond those seen during pretraining. The paper also raises important societal concerns about misuse of a unified understanding-and-generation model.
