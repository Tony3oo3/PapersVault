## Problem

Existing two-stage motion prediction refinement methods either employ all context information at high computational cost or apply fixed refinement configurations (retrieval radius, context encoding, iteration count) uniformly across all scenarios. This is sub-optimal because different driving scenarios have varying prediction quality and refinement potential: some trajectories benefit from iterative refinement while already-accurate ones can be degraded by over-refinement, and no prior work adapts refinement configurations or iteration count per scenario.

## Input / Output

SmartRefine is a plug-in refinement module that requires only a generic interface from any backbone model: the predicted multi-modal trajectories and their trajectory feature embeddings. It additionally consumes the vectorized HD map and surrounding agents' states to retrieve local context around adaptively selected anchors. The output consists of refined multi-modal trajectories (Laplace-distributed waypoints), per-trajectory probabilities, and a scalar quality score in [0,1] that drives the adaptive stopping criterion at inference.

## Method

SmartRefine adaptively refines trajectories through four coupled mechanisms: (1) adaptive anchor/context selection, where the trajectory is divided into N segments and context is retrieved around each segment endpoint with a radius R = β(1/2)^(i-1)·v that decays exponentially with iteration i and scales with agent velocity v; (2) anchor-centric context encoding, which transforms retrieved context features into the coordinate frame centered and aligned with each anchor rather than the agent's current position; (3) recurrent multi-segment refinement, where cross-attention fuses anchor-centric context embeddings with trajectory embeddings one segment at a time to predict per-segment offsets; and (4) adaptive iteration termination, where a quality score q_i = (d_max − d_i)/(d_max − d_min) computed via a GRU+MLP decoder over trajectory embeddings determines whether further refinement is needed, stopping when the score stops improving or a maximum iteration cap is reached. The entire module is decoupled from the backbone, adding only ~130–408M FLOPs, and is trained with a combined loss of cross-entropy classification, Laplace NLL regression, and L1 quality-score supervision.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 validation set, SmartRefine consistently reduces minFDE across all five backbone models: ProphNet (1.004→0.967), mmTransformer (1.081→1.023), HiVT (0.969→0.911), DenseTNT (1.624→1.553), and QCNet-no-ref (1.304→1.258). On the Argoverse 2 test set, the QCNet+SmartRefine combination achieves minFDE 1.23, outperforming all published ensemble-free works on the Argoverse 2 leaderboard (single agent track) at submission time. The refinement module adds only 130M FLOPs on Argoverse and 408M on Argoverse 2, negligible compared to backbone FLOPs ranging from 1.2G to 55.8G, with latency increases of roughly 10–25 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmartRefine is evaluated as a plug-in refinement module on five state-of-the-art backbones (HiVT, ProphNet, mmTransformer, DenseTNT, QCNet) and improves every one of them on both Argoverse and Argoverse 2. The largest relative minFDE reductions on the Argoverse 2 test set are 7.5% for mmTransformer (1.34→1.24), 6.9% for ProphNet (1.30→1.21), and 4.2% for DenseTNT (1.66→1.59). On the Argoverse 2 validation set, QCNet+SmartRefine achieves minFDE 1.240 with only 8.0M parameters and 56.2G FLOPs, outperforming the original QCNet (1.253, 7.7M params, 55.8G FLOPs) while adding just 0.4G FLOPs. The paper reports that QCNet+SmartRefine outperforms all published ensemble-free entries on the Argoverse 2 leaderboard (single agent track) at submission time. Notably, the refinement module adds only ~130M FLOPs on Argoverse and ~408M on Argoverse 2, which is 1–3% of the backbone FLOPs, confirming the method's efficiency claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

SmartRefine extends the two-stage trajectory refinement paradigm established by DCMS, QCNet, R-Pred, and MTR, which all generate coarse trajectories and then refine them using scene context. However, prior refinement methods either employ all context without selection (DCMS, QCNet) or use fixed manual rules for context retrieval and a fixed number of iterations (R-Pred, MTR). SmartRefine departs by making three aspects adaptive per scenario: anchor/context selection with velocity-dependent retrieval radius, anchor-centric (rather than agent-centric) context encoding, and the number of refinement iterations governed by a learned quality score. Crucially, it is decoupled from the prediction backbone via a generic interface, unlike prior refinement modules that are tightly coupled to specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can add SmartRefine to any existing motion prediction backbone with minimal engineering effort and modest compute overhead (130M–408M Flops vs. 1.2G–55.8G backbone), making it practically attractive for real-time autonomous driving where latency is critical. The empirical finding that initially high-quality trajectories degrade under further refinement (Section 4.4) provides a concrete mechanistic justification for adaptive iteration counts rather than fixed multi-iteration schemes. Open questions include whether the quality score generalizes to multi-agent joint prediction (acknowledged as future work), whether the adaptive radius and anchor-count choices transfer across datasets with different map densities, and whether the GRU-based quality score decoder could be replaced by lighter alternatives for edge deployment.
