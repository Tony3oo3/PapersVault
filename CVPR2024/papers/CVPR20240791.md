## Problem

Generalizing novel view synthesis to real-world, in-the-wild scenes from a single image remains unsolved: existing methods rely on synthetic data, object-centred aligned datasets, category-specific training, or simplified 3-DOF camera models that cannot handle diverse backgrounds, scale ambiguities, and casually captured scenes. NViST tackles this by training a feed-forward transformer on a large-scale real-world multiview dataset (MVImgNet) with a full 6-DOF relative camera pose model, eliminating the need for canonicalization of training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240791/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (downsampled by ×12 to 160×90, preserving aspect ratio) and two scalar camera parameters—normalized focal length f and camera distance z—as conditioning. It outputs a novel view image rendered via differentiable volume rendering from a predicted radiance field encoded as a vector-matrix (VM) representation (three 48×48 matrices and three 48-dim vectors), queried at 48 points per ray. The camera pose is expressed purely relatively (input camera at identity rotation, target as R_{i→j}), so no canonical reference frame is required.

## Method

NViST is a feed-forward encoder-decoder transformer: a fine-tuned MAE (ViT-B) encoder produces 576 feature tokens from 5×5 patches, and a decoder with 816 learnable output tokens maps them to a VM radiance field via cross-attention (output→feature) and self-attention (output→output) blocks. Camera parameters are injected through adaptive layer normalization (regressing shift, scale, and gate per block) rather than concatenation, which the ablation shows is superior. The decoder learns 2D-to-3D correspondence implicitly through cross-attention rather than projecting spatially aligned features onto rays, and self-attention enables reasoning about occlusions. Training is end-to-end with L2 photometric, LPIPS perceptual, and distortion-based regularisation losses; encoder weights are updated during training to produce smoother, segment-focused features. The VM representation (from TensoRF) outperforms triplanes in the ablation, and the full 6-DOF relative pose model distinguishes NViST from prior 3-DOF object-centred approaches like Zero-1-to-3.

## Results

On MVImgNet (the primary in-the-wild benchmark), NViST achieves PSNR 20.83, SSIM 0.57, and LPIPS 0.29, outperforming PixelNeRF (PSNR 17.02) and VisionNeRF (PSNR 19.82) by substantial margins on all three metrics. On the synthetic ShapeNet-SRN benchmark, NViST achieves the best PSNR on both Cars (23.91) and Chairs (24.50) categories, though it trails diffusion-based baselines on LPIPS (e.g., 0.122 vs. 0.078 for SSDNeRF on Cars). The model also generalizes zero-shot to out-of-distribution phone-captured scenes and an unseen category (toy-cars) without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary MVImgNet benchmark, NViST decisively outperforms both PixelNeRF and VisionNeRF across all metrics, with a +3.81 dB PSNR gain over PixelNeRF and +1.01 dB over VisionNeRF, while reducing LPIPS from 0.54/0.47 to 0.29. On ShapeNet-SRN, NViST achieves the highest PSNR on both Cars (23.91) and Chairs (24.50), beating PixelNeRF, VisionNeRF, VD, and SSDNeRF, but it lags behind SSDNeRF and VisionNeRF on LPIPS (0.122 vs. 0.078 on Cars; 0.090 vs. 0.067 on Chairs), suggesting the diffusion-based methods produce sharper textures on aligned synthetic objects. The paper notes that NViST uses only relative pose on ShapeNet-SRN (not exploiting the canonical alignment), so the PSNR gap to absolute-pose methods is expected to narrow. Qualitatively, NViST clearly surpasses Zero-1-to-3 on phone-captured inputs, where Zero-1-to-3 fails due to its 3-DoF centered-object assumption and lack of background modeling. No quantitative comparison is possible with LRM or GenVS as their code is not publicly available.

## Relation to Prior Work

NViST sits at the intersection of several research threads: it adopts the pretrained MAE encoder (He et al., 2022) as a self-supervised feature extractor, the vector-matrix (VM) radiance field representation from TensoRF (Chen et al., 2022), and NeRF's volume rendering and positional encoding (Mildenhall et al., 2020). Its key departure from prior single-image NVS methods like PixelNeRF and VisionNeRF is the use of cross-attention to learn 2D-to-3D token correspondence rather than projecting spatially aligned CNN features onto rays, and the use of adaptive layer normalization to condition on camera parameters. Unlike diffusion-based approaches (Zero-1-to-3, DreamFusion) that assume centered objects with 3-DOF cameras and no background, NViST is a feed-forward transformer trained on real-world multi-view data (MVImgNet) that handles full scenes with a 6-DOF relative-pose camera model, removing the need for canonicalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NViST demonstrates that a feed-forward transformer trained on large-scale real-world multi-view data can perform in-the-wild novel view synthesis without diffusion priors, GAN losses, or score distillation, while generalizing to unseen categories and out-of-distribution phone captures. This is practically significant because it removes the canonicalization and object-centering assumptions that have limited prior methods to controlled settings. Limitations include loss of sharpness from ×12 downsampling and the absence of adversarial or diffusion-based detail enhancement. Open questions include whether a probabilistic formulation or multi-view input could further improve fidelity, and whether the approach scales to the full MVImgNet dataset (only a subset was used due to compute constraints).
