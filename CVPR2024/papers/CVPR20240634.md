## Problem

Caption-based VLMs such as OpenFlamingo and BLIP-2, trained on image-caption pairs, lack the ability to localise objects because their training data contains no explicit spatial grounding. Existing approaches to add localisation require large supervised detection datasets and substantial compute to train unified expert VLMs. This paper asks whether localisation can be unlocked in a fully frozen caption-based VLM using no supervised detection data at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240634/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (224×224 for OpenFlamingo, 364×364 for the BLIP-2 variant) and a text prompt of the form 'In the image is a <obj> located at', and produces bounding box coordinates as a sequence of text tokens in the format [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] via standard next-token prediction. No special detection head or region proposal network is introduced; the output is generated by the VLM's native language model. The low native resolution makes localising small objects (32×32 to 96×96 pixels) particularly challenging.

## Method

PIN is a lightweight learnable spatial prompt (~1.2M parameters, 0.04% of the 3B VLM) inserted directly after the frozen vision encoder. It is initialized from 64-dimensional sinusoidal positional embeddings, refined by a two-layer MLP with SwiGLU activations, and projected to match the vision encoder's 1024-dimensional output, then added element-wise to the vision features (x_v* = x_v + π). Training uses a simple next-token prediction (cross-entropy) objective on a synthetic dataset where objects rendered by Stable Diffusion from LVIS categories are pasted onto BG20-k backgrounds at known positions, providing self-generated supervision without any manually annotated detection data. The entire VLM remains frozen, and the PIN can be trivially deactivated to restore the original caption-based behaviour, distinguishing it from fine-tuning approaches like LoRA that risk overfitting to synthetic artifacts.

## Results

On Pascal VOC (≤3 objects), PIN achieves 0.45 mIoU with OpenFlamingo and 0.44 mIoU with BLIP-2, outperforming all PEFT baselines. On COCO, PIN reaches 0.35 mIoU (OpenFlamingo) and 0.34 mIoU (BLIP-2), and on LVIS it attains 0.26 mIoU for both VLMs. On RefCOCO Test-A, PIN achieves 26.4 P@0.3 in a fully zero-shot setting, compared to only 3.7 for the in-context learning Flamingo baseline. The PIN module contains only ~1.2M parameters (~0.04% of the 3B VLM), requiring no additional output heads or supervised detection data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIN outperforms CoOp [70] by a wide margin (0.45 vs. 0.28 mIoU on PVOC with OpenFlamingo), which the authors attribute to CoOp's lack of spatial positional information in its learnable tokens. Against Visual Prompt Tuning [22], PIN achieves 0.45 vs. 0.34 mIoU (VPT on fusion network) and 0.45 vs. 0.42 mIoU (VPT on vision encoder) on PVOC, with the gap being especially pronounced for medium-sized objects (mIoU_M: 0.27 vs. 0.16 and 0.21). Compared to LoRA [20] fine-tuning the ViT encoder, PIN edges out the LoRA baseline (0.45 vs. 0.44 mIoU on PVOC) while using approximately 5× fewer parameters, and avoids the risk of overfitting to synthetic data artifacts. On BLIP-2, PIN similarly dominates both VPT variants (0.44 vs. 0.33/0.32 mIoU on PVOC), confirming that the approach generalises across different VLM architectures. The paper also notes that PIN can be trivially deactivated to restore the VLM's original captioning abilities, a flexibility not available when fine-tuning the vision encoder with LoRA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning paradigm from encoder-only models (CLIP-based CoOp, VPT) to generative VLMs with a decoder, introducing a spatially-structured positional prompt (PIN) that is added to the frozen vision encoder output. It departs from expert-based VLMs (GLIP, Kosmos-2, MiniGPT-V2) that require large annotated detection datasets and substantial compute, instead relying on a 1.2M-parameter module trained on synthetic data generated via Stable Diffusion following X-Paste's copy-paste methodology. The approach is conceptually distinct from LoRA or VPT baselines in that it injects explicit sinusoidal positional structure rather than learning arbitrary tokens, and it operates entirely outside the VLM's parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that object localisation can be unlocked in frozen caption-based VLMs with negligible parameter overhead and no supervised detection data, offering a practical path to adding spatial grounding to existing VLM deployments. A notable limitation is the 224×224 input resolution inherited from the base VLMs, which constrains precision on small objects, and the 1B-parameter LLM in OpenFlamingo limits handling of complex referring expressions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger VLMs (e.g., 70B+), whether the synthetic-data bottleneck can be further reduced, and whether the PIN concept generalises to other spatial tasks like keypoint estimation or segmentation.
