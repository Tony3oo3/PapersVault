## Problem

Existing MLLMs struggle with fine-grained image understanding because (1) referential comprehension (RC) instruction tuning datasets cover too narrow a range of tasks—typically only visual grounding, grounding caption, and pointQA—limiting generalization to new RC tasks, and (2) the visual encoder is frozen during instruction tuning, preventing it from acquiring region-level perception. The paper addresses both bottlenecks by constructing diverse RC tasks from existing annotations and adapting the visual encoder in a parameter-efficient manner.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images resized to 224×224, text instructions, and optionally bounding box coordinates formatted as normalized [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] tuples with 3 decimal places in [0,1] range, treated as plain text tokens. Outputs are free-form text responses that may embed bounding box coordinates, object class names, spatial relation labels (e.g., top-left, bottom-right), relation predicates, or integer counts, all in the same dialogue format. This text-based coordinate representation allows boxes to appear in both the prompt and the generated response without requiring a separate regression head.

## Method

Pink follows the LLaVA architecture (ViT-L/14 CLIP encoder → linear projection → Vicuna-7B LLM) but introduces two key innovations. First, a dataset construction pipeline converts Visual Genome relation triplets and object annotations into four new RC tasks (visual relation reasoning, coarse visual spatial reasoning, object counting, object detection), and a self-consistent bootstrapping method extends Object365 bounding boxes into referring-expression-bounding-box pairs by generating descriptions via grounding caption and filtering them with a visual grounding IOU threshold (λ=0.5). Second, instead of freezing the visual encoder, Pink inserts lightweight Adapters (hidden dim 8) before each self-attention layer in both the visual encoder and LLM, training only ~6.7M parameters total across two stages: projection-layer alignment on 595K CC3M pairs, then instruction tuning on ~396K diverse RC samples. This design lets the visual encoder adapt to fine-grained perception without the semantic collapse that full fine-tuning causes on small-scale instruction data.

## Results

On fine-tuned RC tasks, Pink-G (ViT-G encoder) achieves 91.5% on RefCOCO_val, 93.4% on RefCOCO_testA, and 88.0% on RefCOCO_testB, outperforming Qwen-VL (89.4/92.3/85.3) by 2.1–2.7 points despite using a lower input resolution (224 vs. 448) and far fewer training samples. On conventional multi-modal tasks, Pink (only 6.7M trainable parameters) achieves 47.8% on IconQA, 66.3% on VSR, 59.5% on OK-VQA, and 52.6% on GQA, surpassing Shikra-7B (24.3/63.3/53.5/47.4) across all four benchmarks. On MMBench, Pink attains 74.1% overall accuracy, the highest among all compared models, with particularly strong gains in relation reasoning (73.2% vs. 62.0% for mPlug-Owl) and fine-grained cross-instance perception (67.2% vs. 58.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Shikra-7B [4], Pink wins on every RC benchmark: +1.3 on RefCOCO_val, +3.8 on RefCOCO_testB, +1.6 on RefCOCOg_val, +0.8 on Visual-7W, and +1.4 on LookTwice, while also substantially outperforming Shikra on conventional tasks (e.g., 47.8% vs. 24.3% on IconQA, 59.5% vs. 53.5% on OK-VQA). Against Qwen-VL [1], Pink-G (same ViT-G encoder) wins by 2.1–2.7 points on all RefCOCO splits, 2.9–3.0 points on RefCOCO+ splits, and 1.2–2.3 points on RefCOCOg, despite using 224×224 input vs. Qwen-VL's 448×448 and 519K vs. 50M instruction-tuning samples. Against Kosmos-2 [26], Pink (zero-shot, without Object365) already surpasses Kosmos-2 on RefCOCO_val (54.1 vs. 52.3) and RefCOCO_testA (61.2 vs. 57.4); with Object365 bootstrapping (Pink*), the gap widens to 77.0 vs. 52.3 on RefCOCO_val. Against OFA-L [39], Pink-G leads by 11.5 points on RefCOCO_val and 20.2 points on RefCOCO+_testB. On MMBench, Pink (74.1% overall) outperforms mPlug-Owl [43] (68.5%), Qwen-VL (61.8%), LLaVA-1.5 [18] (59.5%), and Kosmos-2 (58.2%), with the largest margins in relation reasoning (+11.2 over mPlug-Owl) and fine-grained cross-instance perception (+8.8). The only comparison where Pink does not lead is GQA under the fine-tuning setting, where LLaVA-1.5 [18] achieves 62.0% vs. Pink's 52.6% (though Pink+ with additional GQA training data reaches 64.5%). InstructBLIP [6] is only compared on a subset of conventional tasks (IconQA 43.1, VSR 54.3, GQA 49.2), where Pink leads by 4.7, 12.0, and 3.4 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pink builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT encoder + linear projection + Vicuna-7B LLM) and the broader visual instruction tuning paradigm, but departs from prior MLLMs in two key ways. First, rather than freezing the visual encoder during instruction tuning (as in LLaVA, Shikra, InstructBLIP) or relying on GPT4-generated RC data (as in ChatSpot, Shikra), Pink introduces lightweight Adapters into both the visual encoder and LLM and constructs diverse RC instruction data by repurposing annotations from Visual Genome and Object365 via a self-consistent bootstrapping procedure. Second, it extends the RC task space beyond the visual grounding and grounding caption tasks used by Kosmos-2 and Shikra to include visual relation reasoning, coarse spatial reasoning, object counting, and object detection, arguing that this diversity is necessary for generalization to unseen RC tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained image perception in MLLMs can be substantially improved with only 6.7M trainable parameters and ~400K instruction tuning samples, challenging the assumption that large-scale data (e.g., Qwen-VL's 50M samples) or full model tuning is necessary. The self-consistent bootstrapping method offers a practical, low-cost alternative to GPT4-based data generation for constructing RC training pairs, which is valuable for academic reproducibility. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher input resolutions (Pink uses 224×224 vs. 448 for Qwen-VL), whether the Adapter-based visual encoder tuning generalizes to other encoder architectures or modalities, and whether the gains persist as model scale increases beyond 7B LLMs.
