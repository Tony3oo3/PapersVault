## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection and pose estimation suffers from high uncertainty due to the absence of 3D perception cues. Existing methods mitigate this by leveraging additional modalities (stereo, LiDAR) or extra training data, which increases annotation and acquisition costs. This paper asks whether diffusion models can serve as effective representation learners for 3D perception from a single image without any auxiliary modalities or training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240665/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB image (256×256 at training) and first localizes objects with a fixed 2D detector (e.g., CenterNet, YOLOv7). The denoising network operates on a latent vector encoding 3D box dimensions and orientation angles, conditioned on ResNet-34 image features and the diffusion timestep. The final 3D bounding box is produced by combining the diffusion-estimated dimension matrix D and rotation matrix R with a translation vector T solved separately as the vector yielding the closest projection to the 2D detection window.

## Method

MonoDiff formulates 3D detection as a reverse diffusion process where a high-variance distribution is progressively denoised into a low-variance estimate of box parameters. The key architectural departure is replacing the standard N(0,I) initialization with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) fitted via EM on ground-truth box parameters, since bounding box dimensions exhibit heterogeneous variance across axes and object classes. The forward process is modified so that noise is sampled from the selected GMM component's covariance rather than an isotropic Gaussian, and the denoising network f(φ_t, ψ, t) predicts φ_{t−1} in a single step. A 2D reprojection loss projects the estimated 3D box onto the image plane and penalizes mismatch with the 2D detection via bounding-box and IoU losses, providing geometric supervision without extra annotations. An SNR-based dynamic weighting factor λ_t scales the reprojection loss at each timestep to account for increasing uncertainty in early reverse steps, and DDIM sampling is used to reduce inference to a small number of steps.

## Results

On the KITTI test set, MonoDiff achieves the best AOS and AP3D across all three object categories without additional modalities: Car (AOS 91.54, AP3D 23.12), Pedestrian (AOS 46.00, AP3D 9.91), and Cyclist (AOS 52.42, AP3D 5.55). On the Waymo validation set, MonoDiff attains 3D mAP 32.28 and 3D mAPH 31.49 overall, surpassing the previous SOTA MonoNeRD (31.18/30.70) which relies on stereo images during training. The method runs at 11.7 FPS on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU, comparable to other state-of-the-art monocular 3D detectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoDiff outperforms all nine baselines on KITTI across every object category and difficulty level. The most notable margin is against MonoNeRD (ICCV'23), the prior SOTA that uses stereo images during training: MonoDiff improves Car AP3D from 18.50 to 23.12 (+4.62) and Pedestrian AOS from 22.44 to 46.00 (+23.56) without any additional modality. Against LPCG (ECCV'22), which uses LiDAR point cloud guidance, MonoDiff gains +3.54 AP3D on Car and +1.78 on Pedestrian. On Waymo, MonoDiff leads MonoNeRD on overall 3D mAP (32.28 vs 31.18) and 0–30 m range (63.94 vs 61.11), but narrowly trails on the 30–50 m mAPH (25.47 vs 25.71). Against DID-M3D (ECCV'22), MonoDiff achieves +2.62 overall 3D mAP on Waymo and +4.97 AP3D on KITTI Car. The consistent wins over methods relying on pseudo-LiDAR, stereo, or cross-modality distillation (CMKD) confirm that the diffusion-based representation learning is effective without auxiliary data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoDiff extends the diffusion-model-for-perception line initiated by DiffusionDet (2D detection) and DiffRef3D (LiDAR-based 3D detection) to the monocular 3D setting, repurposing the forward/reverse diffusion process as a structured-output generator for bounding box parameters. It departs from prior monocular 3D detectors (MonoNeRD, LPCG, DID-M3D) that rely on auxiliary modalities—stereo images, pseudo-LiDAR, or LiDAR supervision—by treating the diffusion process itself as the representation learner, conditioned only on monocular image features. The key methodological departure from standard DDPMs is the GMM-based forward process, motivated by the anisotropic variance across 3D box dimensions, and the 2D reprojection loss with SNR-based weighting to bridge the 3D/2D correspondence at each denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MonoDiff demonstrates that a purely monocular pipeline can match or exceed methods that consume stereo or LiDAR data during training, reducing annotation and sensor costs for autonomous driving perception. The GMM initialization and SNR-weighted reprojection loss are practical, transferable ideas for any diffusion-based structured-output task where parameter scales are heterogeneous. Open questions include whether the fixed 2D detector bottleneck limits end-to-end optimization, whether the approach generalizes to datasets with more diverse object geometries beyond KITTI/Waymo, and whether the 100-step reverse process (reduced via DDIM) can be further compressed without quality loss.
