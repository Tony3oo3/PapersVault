## Problem

Existing datasets for human behavior understanding capture only one level of the multi-level hierarchy—individual attributes, pairwise interactions, or group-level context—leaving a gap in comprehensively modeling the dynamics and context of human social behavior in crowded scenes. Furthermore, no prior benchmark has systematically evaluated whether state-of-the-art multi-modal large language models can actually perceive and reason about human social interactions across these levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241534/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset extends JRDB's 64 minutes of egocentric robot data (stereo RGB 360° cameras, dual LiDAR, audio, GPS) with three-level annotations: individual demographic labels (gender, 5-tier age, 3-tier race), 20-category multi-label frame-level interaction annotations between each pair within a social group, and structured text descriptions per group following a fixed template encoding body-position-content engagement, salient scene content, venue, and purpose. For evaluation, models receive either cropped bounding-box regions (guided) or full video frames (holistic) and are prompted to extract named entities, which are scored as single- or multi-label classification with F1.

## Method

JRDB-Social introduces a three-level annotation framework where individual attributes, intra-group multi-label interactions (categorized into shared physical activities, joint engagement with external entities, and interpersonal exchanges), and group-level contextual text descriptions are all aligned to existing JRDB 2D/3D bounding boxes and social group annotations. Each label carries a difficulty rating (Easy/Medium/Hard) reflecting occlusion, distance, and lighting challenges, and annotations are cross-validated against JRDB-Act atomic actions for consistency. The evaluation framework tests seven multi-modal LLMs (Video-LLaMA, VALLEY, Otter, MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP) under two paradigms—Guided Perception (ground-truth bounding boxes direct attention) and Holistic (full video, no cropping)—with the holistic setting further split into a Counting approach (quantify occurrences) and a Binary approach (yes/no detection). A Five Ensemble Strategy aggregates five inference iterations per model to improve reliability, and for group-level analysis, frame-level cropping at 512×512 with a 2.5 scaling factor was selected over fixed-mask alternatives based on F1 performance.

## Results

In the Guided Perception experiment (Table 1), Video-LLaMA (LLaMA-2 13B) achieves the best overall F1 of 0.3025, followed closely by Video-LLaMA (7B) at 0.2874 and MiniGPT-4 (7B) at 0.2816. Performance degrades sharply at higher levels: the best model scores 0.7139 on Gender but drops to 0.1252 on Salient Scene Content (SSC), the hardest category. In the Holistic Counting experiment (Table 2), overall F1 drops to 0.1965 (Video-LLaMA 13B), while the simplified Holistic Binary experiment (Table 3) recovers to 0.4848 (InstructBLIP 7B), indicating that counting fine-grained details without localization cues is the most challenging setting for current VLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video-LLaMA (both 13B and 7B) leads in the Guided Perception setting with overall F1 of 0.3025 and 0.2874 respectively, outperforming MiniGPT-4 (0.2816), InstructBLIP (0.2169/0.2135), Valley (0.1603/0.1400), and OTTER (0.1105). In the Holistic Counting experiment, Video-LLaMA (13B) again leads at 0.1965, with MiniGPT-4 close behind at 0.1684; Valley and OTTER are excluded due to near-zero performance (≤0.0217). In the Holistic Binary experiment, InstructBLIP (7B) edges out all others at 0.4848, narrowly beating InstructBLIP (13B) at 0.4846 and Video-LLaMA (13B) at 0.4622, while OTTER (0.4542) and MiniGPT-4 (0.4180) trail. Across all three experiments, every model shows a consistent performance drop from individual-level attributes (Gender F1 up to 0.98) to intra-group interactions (F1 ~0.28–0.39) to social group context (SSC F1 ≤0.24), revealing that current VLMs struggle most with higher-level social reasoning rather than basic attribute recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the JRDB egocentric robot dataset (Martin-Martin et al., 2021) and its JRDB-Act extension (Ehsanpour et al., 2022) by adding a three-level annotation hierarchy—individual demographic attributes, frame-level multi-label intra-group interactions, and social group contextual descriptions—that no prior dataset provides simultaneously. Unlike existing datasets that isolate one level (atomic actions, pairwise interactions, or group captioning), JRDB-Social unifies all three within a single robot-centric, crowded-scene setting. The empirical evaluation departs from standard video understanding benchmarks by probing multi-modal LLMs (Video-LLaMA, VALLEY, Otter, MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP) specifically on their capacity to reason about social dynamics rather than generic scene content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a genuine gap: prior social-behavior datasets lack the robot's egocentric perspective in crowded real-world environments, and none combine demographic, interaction, and contextual annotations at frame-level granularity. The evaluation reveals a stark performance cliff—VLMs achieve F1 above 0.7 on individual gender prediction but drop below 0.1 on salient scene content and body-position-context tasks—highlighting that current models fundamentally lack the compositional reasoning needed for social group understanding. Open questions include whether scaling training data with finer-grained social annotations or designing architectures with explicit group-relation modeling can close this gap, and whether the 64-minute, 54-sequence scale is sufficient to draw generalizable conclusions about VLM social competence.
