## Problem

Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), the standard metric for evaluating video generation models, is known to occasionally contradict human judgment, but the extent and source of its bias toward per-frame spatial quality over temporal consistency have not been systematically quantified. This paper asks: how sensitive is FVD to temporal quality, how much can FVD be reduced without improving motion, and which component of the pipeline (feature extractor, training objective, dataset) is responsible for the content bias?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241704/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes pairs of real and generated video clips (resized to 224×224) and a pretrained video feature extractor (I3D or VideoMAE-v2 variants) that produces logit-layer feature vectors. For the distortion experiments, the same video set is corrupted with spatial-only or spatiotemporal variants of elastic transformation and motion blur, and the output is a temporal sensitivity ratio (relative FVD increase from adding temporal inconsistency, normalized by FID-verified frame quality). For the resampling experiments, a large candidate pool of 16,384 generated videos (including frozen versions with no motion) is weighted to minimize a weighted Fréchet distance, yielding a 2,048-video subset and a reduced FVD* score.

## Method

The paper performs three complementary empirical analyses. First, it quantifies FVD's temporal sensitivity by applying matched-pair distortions (spatial-only vs. spatiotemporal) that produce equivalent frame-quality degradation (verified by FID) but differ in temporal consistency, then measuring the relative FVD increase. Second, it probes the temporal perceptual null space by optimizing per-video weights to minimize a weighted FVD over an 8× larger candidate set, demonstrating that FVD can drop 40–70% even when all videos are frozen (no motion), while the same procedure with VideoMAE-v2 features yields only 5–13% reduction. Third, it ablates the source of the bias by comparing I3D (supervised, Kinetics-400), TimeSFormer (supervised, Kinetics-400), VideoMAE-v1 (self-supervised, smaller ViT), and VideoMAE-v2 (self-supervised, larger ViT), concluding that the self-supervised masked-autoencoder objective—not architecture or dataset—is the primary factor that restores temporal sensitivity.

## Results

FVD computed with I3D features shows only a 3.6%–35.7% increase when temporal consistency is destroyed (spatiotemporal vs. spatial distortion), while FVD with VideoMAE-v2-PT features increases roughly 5× more under the same elastic-transformation corruption. In the perceptual-null-space experiment, resampling 16,384 frozen (motionless) videos reduces I3D FVD by 31–55% without any temporal improvement, whereas VideoMAE-v2-SSv2 FVD drops by only 5–13%. In two real-world long-video case studies, I3D FVD incorrectly favors models with collapsed or periodic motion artifacts, while VideoMAE-v2 FVD aligns with human judgment (e.g., +284% FVD penalty for LSTM-collapsed StyleGAN-V at 128 frames vs. −18% with I3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three feature extractors for FVD: I3D (Carreira & Zisserman, CVPR 2017), VideoMAE-v1 (Tong et al., NeurIPS 2022), and TimeSFormer (Bertasius et al., ICML 2021), all trained on Kinetics-400 except VideoMAE-v2 which is pre-trained on diverse unlabeled data. I3D exhibits the strongest content bias, with FVD increasing only 3.6% on FaceForensics and 14–18% on other datasets when temporal consistency is destroyed. TimeSFormer, despite using a ViT architecture, shows a similar content bias to I3D because it is trained with a supervised classification objective on the same Kinetics-400 data. VideoMAE-v1, which shares the self-supervised MAE objective with v2 but uses a smaller ViT, already shows substantially greater temporal sensitivity than both I3D and TimeSFormer, indicating that the self-supervised reconstruction objective—not the architecture or model scale—is the primary factor mitigating the content bias. VideoMAE-v2-SSv2 (fine-tuned on the motion-centric SSv2 dataset) achieves the highest temporal sensitivity, detecting even mild motion-blur-induced temporal degradation that I3D completely misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FID analysis methodology of Kynkäänniemi et al. (2022) — specifically the perceptual null space probing via weighted resampling — from the image domain to video generation evaluation. It builds directly on the FVD metric (Unterthiner et al., 2018) and its standard I3D feature extractor, while drawing on prior evidence that Kinetics-trained classifiers are biased toward object appearance over motion. Unlike Skorokhodov et al., who studied low-level preprocessing artifacts in FVD, this work isolates the training paradigm of the feature extractor (supervised vs. self-supervised) as the root cause of the content bias, using VideoMAE-v2 and TimeSFormer as controlled comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FVD is the de facto standard for evaluating video generation models, and this paper demonstrates that it can be gamed by up to 70% through selective resampling without any temporal quality improvement, and can even rank motion-collapsed videos above realistic ones. The practical implication is that model comparisons and hyperparameter choices guided by I3D-based FVD may be unreliable, particularly for long-video generation and out-of-domain content. Open questions remain: the quadratic attention cost of VideoMAE-v2 limits evaluation of very long videos, resolution and aspect-ratio constraints persist, and whether other widely-used video metrics (e.g., VBench, EvalCrafter) share similar temporal blind spots is unexplored.
