## Problem

Interactive point-based image editing (drag-based editing) was previously limited to GAN-based methods like DRAGGAN, whose generality is constrained by the narrow capacity of pretrained GANs. While large-scale diffusion models offer far broader domain coverage, existing diffusion-based editing methods rely on text-prompt manipulation and cannot achieve the precise pixel-level spatial control required for drag operations. This paper bridges that gap by being the first to enable interactive point-based editing with diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 512×512 via Stable Diffusion 1.5's VAE), one or more handle-target point pairs specifying where content should be dragged, and an optional binary mask delineating the editable region (unmasked areas are regularized to remain unchanged). It produces a single edited RGB image. The approach works on both real photographs and images generated by various SD-1.5 fine-tunes (Counterfeit-V2.5, Majicmix Realistic, Interior Design Supermix), and handles both small-magnitude and large-magnitude drag instructions.

## Method

DragDiffusion is a three-stage pipeline built on Stable Diffusion 1.5. First, identity-preserving fine-tuning injects LoRA (rank 16) into all attention projection matrices and trains for only 80 steps to encode the input image's identity into the UNet. Second, the core latent optimization performs DDIM inversion to obtain the latent at a single time step (t=35 by default), then iteratively minimizes a motion supervision loss that matches UNet feature maps (from the 3rd upsampling block) at shifted handle locations to their original positions via stop-gradient, combined with a mask-preservation term on the denoised latent; after each gradient step, handle points are re-localized by nearest-neighbor search in feature space. The key insight is that UNet features at one specific diffusion step already contain sufficient semantic and geometric information for spatial control, eliminating the need to optimize latents across multiple time steps as in prior diffusion editing methods. Third, reference-latent-control steers the final DDIM denoising by replacing key and value vectors in the self-attention modules of the optimized latent with those computed from the original latent, preserving identity coherence in the output.

## Results

On the DRAGBENCH dataset, DRAGDIFFUSION is evaluated using two metrics: Image Fidelity (IF, higher is better, computed as 1 − mean LPIPS) and Mean Distance (MD, lower is better, computed via DIFT point correspondence). The full DRAGDIFFUSION method (t=35 inversion steps, 80 LoRA fine-tuning steps, UNet block-3 features) achieves the best joint IF/MD trade-off, significantly outperforming the DRAGGAN baseline on both metrics. Ablations confirm that removing identity-preserving fine-tuning causes a catastrophic increase in MD, while removing reference-latent-control primarily degrades IF. The optimal inversion step is t=35 (within the effective range t∈[30,40]), and 80 LoRA fine-tuning steps provide the best balance of effectiveness and efficiency (~25 s on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DRAGGAN [Pan et al., 2023], a GAN-based interactive point-based editing method. DRAGDIFFUSION significantly outperforms DRAGGAN on both Image Fidelity and Mean Distance on DRAGBENCH (Fig. 8), with the advantage being particularly pronounced on diverse real-world images where GAN capacity is limited. DRAGGAN struggles with out-of-domain content and cannot perform content-filling edits, whereas DRAGDIFFUSION handles multiple object categories, diverse styles, and even diffusion-generated images across SD-1.5 variants (Counterfeit-V2.5, Majicmix Realistic, Interior Design Supermix). The paper also ablates two internal components: removing identity-preserving fine-tuning (LoRA) causes a catastrophic MD increase (poor spatial accuracy), while removing reference-latent-control primarily reduces IF (identity drift), confirming both are necessary for the full method's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DRAGGAN (Pan et al., 2023), a GAN-based interactive point-based editing framework, to the diffusion model paradigm, replacing the limited-capacity StyleGAN backbone with large-scale pretrained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion). It departs from prior diffusion-based editing methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, Imagic, etc.) that rely on text-embedding guidance by instead optimizing a single diffusion latent step for pixel-level spatial control, motivated by PCA observations that UNet features at one specific timestep encode sufficient geometric and semantic structure. The identity-preserving fine-tuning leverages LoRA (as in DreamBooth/Mix-of-Show), while the reference-latent-control mechanism adapts the self-attention steering idea from MasaCtrl to the denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By porting drag-based editing to diffusion models, this work substantially broadens the applicability of interactive point-based editing beyond the narrow domains covered by individual GAN models, and the introduction of DRAGBENCH provides the first systematic evaluation protocol for this task. The practical finding that only 80 LoRA fine-tuning steps (~25 s on A100) suffice for identity preservation—far fewer than the ~1000 steps needed for subject-driven generation—makes the pipeline accessible for interactive use. Open questions include the sensitivity to the inversion-step choice (t ∈ [30,40]), robustness to large-magnitude drags, and whether the single-step optimization can be made more principled or generalized to video and 3D editing.
