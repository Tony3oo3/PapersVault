## Problem

Traditional frame-based photometric stereo requires capturing multiple high-dynamic-range images under sequentially lit light sources, making it slow and bandwidth-intensive, which precludes real-time applications. Event cameras offer high temporal resolution, high dynamic range, and low data rates, but their output is logarithmic radiance *changes* rather than absolute radiance values, making it unclear how to reformulate the photometric stereo problem to exploit this modality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (16-bit Prophesee EVT 3.0 format) from a single event camera (Prophesee EVK4 HD, IMX636 sensor) triggered by a continuously rotating LED light source (up to 1800 rpm), together with the lighting direction vector L(t) at each event timestamp. Each pair of consecutive events at a pixel is converted into a 3D 'null space vector' orthogonal to the surface normal. The output is a per-pixel surface normal map; EventPS-OP produces normals at 30+ fps in an asynchronous pipeline, while the deep-learning variants (EventPS-FCN, EventPS-CNN) use synchronous pipelines for higher quality at lower throughput.

## Method

The key insight is that under the Lambertian model, the albedo cancels from the event-triggering equation, so each pair of consecutive events yields a null space vector z_k = L(t_{k+1}) − exp(p_{k+1}C)·L(t_k) that is orthogonal to the surface normal; at least two linearly independent such vectors uniquely determine the normal. EventPS-OP stacks all null space vectors into a 3×(K−1) matrix and recovers the normal via SVD (smallest eigenvector of Z Zᵀ), with a frequency-domain filter to reject shadow-boundary and specular events. For non-Lambertian robustness, EventPS-FCN adapts PS-FCN by replacing frame images with per-time-bin summed null-space-vector images plus a light-direction channel and two temporal convolution layers, while EventPS-CNN adapts CNN-PS by replacing the per-pixel grayscale observation map with a 3-channel (x,y,z) null-space-vector map. Both deep-learning variants are trained on synthetic event streams (rendered via ray tracing and converted with ESIM) from Blobby and Sculpture objects, with EventPS-FCN fine-tuned from the original PS-FCN checkpoint.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT-Ev dataset, EventPS-FCN achieves the best average MAE of 16.26°, followed by EventPS-CNN (16.45°) and EventPS-OP (19.73°). Critically, these results are obtained while using only 25.86% (OP), 39.82% (FCN), and 26.61% (CNN) of the data rate required by their FramePS counterparts to reach the same MAE. On the real dataset, EventPS-FCN achieves the lowest average MAE of 14.7°, and the optimization-based EventPS-OP runs at over 1000 fps, enabling real-time surface normal estimation at 30+ fps in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three EventPS variants against their direct FramePS counterparts: EventPS-OP vs. TH28 (least-squares with thresholding [45]), EventPS-CNN vs. CNN-PS [13], and EventPS-FCN vs. PS-FCN [2]. All three EventPS methods match or approach the MAE of their FramePS counterparts while requiring substantially less data: EventPS-OP uses only 25.86% of TH28's bandwidth, EventPS-CNN uses 26.61% of CNN-PS's bandwidth, and EventPS-FCN uses 39.82% of PS-FCN's bandwidth. In terms of absolute MAE, EventPS-FCN (16.26°) and EventPS-CNN (16.45°) outperform EventPS-OP (19.73°), with the deep-learning variants benefiting from learned priors for non-Lambertian surfaces. The data-rate advantage is most pronounced for EventPS-OP and EventPS-CNN, which achieve comparable accuracy to FramePS with roughly 3× less data. On the real dataset, EventPS-FCN achieves the best average MAE (14.7°), confirming that the learned approach generalizes well to real-world event signals with noise and non-ideal reflectance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends photometric stereo from frame-based absolute radiance measurements to event-camera radiance changes, introducing the 'null space vector' formulation where consecutive event pairs yield vectors orthogonal to the surface normal. It adapts two established deep-learning PS architectures (PS-FCN and CNN-PS) to the event modality by replacing image inputs with null space vector maps, while also offering a lightweight SVD-based optimization variant. The work sits at the intersection of event-camera 3D reconstruction (structured light, EMVS) and photometric stereo, but is the first to exploit the event camera's inherent albedo invariance and low-bandwidth properties for normal estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EventPS demonstrates that real-time (30+ fps) surface normal estimation is achievable with only ~31% of the bandwidth required by frame-based PS, opening the door to time-sensitive applications like dynamic 3D reconstruction and high-speed inspection. The albedo invariance property—where events naturally cancel surface reflectance—is a clean theoretical insight that simplifies a long-standing PS challenge. Key limitations include the mechanical constraint of a rotating light source (circle pattern leaves blind areas for high-elevation normals), degradation of event quality at high rotation speeds, and the current reliance on a controlled darkroom setup, leaving open questions about ambient-light robustness and non-mechanical illumination alternatives.
