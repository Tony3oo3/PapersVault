## Problem

Existing point-to-point contrastive learning methods for semi-supervised 3D semantic segmentation are sensitive to confusing points, which can be sampled as undesired pairs and lead to insufficient modeling of point-wise representations. The paper asks how to find efficacious supervisory signals for contrastive learning that are robust to such outliers while still exploiting the unlabeled data effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240811/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds in two branches: a labeled branch receives a small set of densely-annotated frames, and an unlabeled branch receives a large set of unlabeled frames processed through two different augmentations. At inference, only the shared backbone and classifier are used to produce per-point semantic labels. The memory bank stores 500 features per class (split evenly between global and local parts), and the dual-space hardness sampling operates on the geometric coordinates and projected features of each unlabeled point.

## Method

DDSemi replaces point-to-point contrast with a density-guided point-to-anchor contrast: a category-wise memory bank in each unlabeled stream stores high-quality features (updated via a hybrid progressive + FIFO scheme), and per-class anchors are estimated as density-weighted means of the top-K highest-density features. Two contrastive losses are computed — an inter-contrast loss that pulls features from one augmented view toward anchors estimated from the other view (enforcing cross-view semantic consistency), and an intra-contrast loss that tightens the feature distribution within a single view. A dual-space hardness sampling strategy then reweights each point's intra-contrast loss by combining its geometric density (normalized neighbor count within radius R) and its feature-space density (mean cosine similarity to k-NN in the class memory bank), so that points in sparse regions of either space receive larger weights and are more strongly pulled toward their class anchor.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI with 10% labeled data, DDSemi achieves 65.1% mIoU, surpassing the previous best LiM3D (62.2%) by 2.9 points. On nuScenes at the same 10% ratio, DDSemi reaches 70.2% mIoU, outperforming LaserMix Voxel (69.9%) and CPS (66.3%). On the indoor S3DIS dataset with 10% labels, DDSemi attains 66.8% mIoU, a substantial 9.1-point improvement over the prior best GPC (57.7%). The method consistently leads across all labeled ratios (1%–50% outdoor, 5%–40% indoor) on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDSemi outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and labeled ratio. Against the strongest outdoor competitor LiM3D [24], DDSemi gains +0.9 to +3.4 mIoU on SemanticKITTI (e.g., 65.1 vs. 62.2 at 10%). Versus GPC [18], the prior best on SemanticKITTI and S3DIS, DDSemi improves by +3.1 mIoU on SemanticKITTI (10%) and a striking +9.1 mIoU on S3DIS (10%), where GPC scores only 57.7%. On nuScenes, DDSemi edges out LaserMix Voxel [20] by +0.3 mIoU at 10% and pulls ahead by +3.3 mIoU at 50% (76.5 vs. 73.2). The 2D-extended baselines (MeanTeacher, CBST, CPS) trail DDSemi by 4–10 mIoU on outdoor scenes and 8–12 mIoU on S3DIS, confirming that domain-specific 3D semi-supervised methods are necessary. SSS-Net [11], evaluated only on S3DIS, is surpassed by DDSemi by +15.7 mIoU at 10% (66.8 vs. 51.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the point-to-point contrastive learning strategy used in GPC [18] by introducing a point-to-anchor contrast mechanism, where per-class anchors are estimated from a density-guided memory bank rather than pairing individual points. The memory bank concept is adapted from Alonso et al. [1], who applied class-wise memory banks for pixel-level contrastive learning in 2D segmentation, here extended to 3D point clouds with a progressive-plus-FIFO update scheme. The density-guided anchor estimation draws on the density-peaks clustering insight of Rodriguez and Laio [31], while the dual-space hardness sampling is a novel reweighting strategy that jointly considers geometric sparsity (local neighbor count) and feature-space sparsity (k-NN density in the memory bank), a combination not present in prior semi-supervised 3D segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The point-to-anchor formulation offers a practical and well-motivated fix for the well-known sensitivity of point-to-point contrast to confusing/outlier points, and the dual-space hardness sampling provides a lightweight mechanism to focus learning on under-represented regions without architectural changes. However, the method still depends on pseudo-label confidence thresholds (γ_e, γ_a) that require tuning, and the memory bank introduces additional storage and k-NN computation overhead that may limit scalability to very large outdoor scenes. An open question is whether the density-guided anchor estimation generalizes to open-set or long-tail segmentation settings where class distributions are highly imbalanced, and whether the geometric-density component transfers to non-LiDAR point clouds with very different sampling densities.
