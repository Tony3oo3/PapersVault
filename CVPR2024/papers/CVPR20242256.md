## Problem

Existing Group Activity Recognition methods predominantly model Human Relations (HRs) via attention or graph networks while largely neglecting Human-Object Interactions (HOIs), or treat objects merely as auxiliary scene data rather than central interaction elements. This paper identifies and empirically validates (via Granger Causality Tests) a bidirectional causality between HRs and HOIs, showing that each can predict the other, and proposes a framework that exploits this mutual predictive relationship to achieve more comprehensive group activity understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242256/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10-frame video clips with three feature streams: person features (aggregated HRNet keypoints per individual, D=256), object features (coordinates and motion speed from GIRN annotations), and kinematic features (joint coordinates grouped by subgroups). It produces a group activity class label (8 classes on VOLLEYBALL, 5 on COLLECTIVE ACTIVITY) alongside individual action labels (9 actions), with the individual predictions obtained by averaging node-level predictions from both the interaction and relation graphs.

## Method

Bi-Causal comprises two parallel modules: a Relation Module (dual-path Transformer with S-T and T-S encoders) modeling HRs, and an Interaction Module (IM) that constructs T per-frame graphs over person and object nodes, computes edges via dot products, and applies self-attention across the temporal sequence of edges to capture dynamic HOIs. A Causality Communication Channel enables bidirectional information exchange: empty tokens are inserted into both graphs, extracted as exchange tokens, and used to reconstruct the opposite graph (relation token guides interaction reconstruction and vice versa). Mutual consistency is enforced via symmetric KL divergence between the interaction and relation classification outputs. Training uses a multi-task objective combining relation loss, interaction loss, fusion loss, individual action loss, and the KL consistency term, with a final Transformer encoder fusing both representations for the group activity prediction.

## Results

On the VOLLEYBALL dataset, Bi-Causal achieves 96.1% group activity accuracy using only keypoint modality (HRNet backbone), surpassing the previous keypoint-only SOTA COMPOSER (94.6%) by 1.5% and the multi-modal GroupFormer (95.7%) by 0.4%. On the COLLECTIVE ACTIVITY dataset, it reaches 94.7%, outperforming keypoint-only COMPOSER (94.1%) by 0.6%. On the RE-ANNOTATED VOLLEYBALL dataset, Bi-Causal attains 96.8%, exceeding COMPOSER (96.2%) by 0.6% and RGB-based DECOMPL (95.2%) by 1.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-Causal outperforms all keypoint-only baselines on VOLLEYBALL: it beats COMPOSER [53] by 1.5% (96.1 vs. 94.6), GIRN [24] by 3.9% (96.1 vs. 92.2), and AT [12] by 3.8% (96.1 vs. 92.3). Against RGB-only methods, it surpasses ACCG [43] by 0.6% (96.1 vs. 95.5) and Tamura et al. [34] by 0.1% (96.1 vs. 96.0) on VD, but trails Tamura et al. on CAD (94.7 vs. 96.5) and GroupFormer multi-modal on CAD (94.7 vs. 96.3), as the COLLECTIVE ACTIVITY dataset lacks fixed interactive objects. On the RE-ANNOTATED VOLLEYBALL dataset, Bi-Causal leads all methods at 96.8%, exceeding COMPOSER by 0.6% and RGB-based DECOMPL by 1.6%. The paper also validates on the NBA dataset (weakly supervised setting) in supplementary materials. Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced in scenarios with substantial human-object interaction (volleyball), while the gap narrows on simpler collective activity scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAR literature that has increasingly relied on Transformer-based and GNN-based architectures to model human relations (GroupFormer, Dual-AI, Actor-Transformers, COMPOSER) by introducing human-object interactions as a first-class, causally coupled component rather than auxiliary scene data. It draws on the causal-reasoning line in CV (Xie et al.'s actor-centric causality graph, Yuan et al.'s global causal context) but departs from those works by applying Granger Causality to validate a bidirectional HR↔HOI relationship and then architecting a Causality Communication Channel to exploit it. The Interaction Module repurposes GCN-style graph evolution with temporal self-attention over per-frame edge sets, a design that goes beyond the joint-level person-object modules in GIRN or the coordinate-based ball tracklets in POGARS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing bidirectional causality between HRs and HOIs and validating it with Granger tests, the paper offers a principled justification for jointly modeling these two information streams rather than simply concatenating features, which could inform future GAR and multi-agent interaction architectures. However, the evaluation is concentrated on volleyball (a single fixed interactive object) and the COLLECTIVE ACTIVITY dataset requires a heuristic salient-object detection workaround, leaving open how well the causal framework generalizes to scenes with multiple, diverse, or absent objects. The authors themselves note the need to improve bi-causality representation and expand to other GAR scenarios, and the Granger validation, while supportive, is limited to one dataset and a narrow range of sequence windows.
