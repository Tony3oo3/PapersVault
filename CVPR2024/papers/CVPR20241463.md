## Problem

Existing 3D diffusion models for scene completion either project LiDAR data to range images (losing occluded regions) or operate on fixed-resolution voxel grids (losing fine-grained detail). Standard DDPM formulations assume data near N(0,I), but scene-scale LiDAR point clouds span a much larger spatial range with a non-Gaussian distribution, making direct application of shape-completion diffusion infeasible without normalizing the data and destroying structural detail.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single sparse 3D LiDAR scan (50 m radius, 18,000 points via farthest point sampling) and produces a completed dense point cloud of 180,000 points. During inference, the input scan is concatenated K=10 times to form the initial noisy point cloud, and a refinement network further upsamples the output by predicting κ=6 offset vectors per point. The generation is conditioned on the input scan via classifier-free guidance, so the output retains the structural information of the observed scan while filling in unseen regions.

## Method

The core reformulation treats the diffusion process as a local point-wise offset problem: instead of the standard x^t = √(α̅_t)·x^0 + √(1−α̅_t)·ε, the authors set x^0 = 0 and add the scaled noise directly to each ground-truth point (p_m^t = p_m + √(1−α̅_t)·ε), preserving the original scene scale without normalization. The noise predictor is a MinkUNet using sparse convolutions, conditioned on the input scan through per-point nearest-neighbor embeddings and a sinusoidal timestep encoding, with classifier-free guidance (s=6.0) to balance conditional and unconditional predictions. A novel noise regularization loss (L_mean + L_std, weighted by r=5.0) constrains the predicted noise distribution to approximate N(0,I), stabilizing the denoising trajectory. Inference uses DPM-Solver to reduce 1,000 denoising steps to 50, followed by a refinement network that upsamples the completed scene by predicting multiple offset vectors per point.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI validation set, the proposed method achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.434 m (0.375 m with refinement) and the best Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) of 0.416 (refined), outperforming all baselines on both metrics. In the occupancy evaluation (Table 3), the refined method achieves the highest IoU at the finest 0.1 m voxel resolution (24.75%), surpassing MID (16.98%) and LODE (6.06%), though SDF-based methods retain an advantage at coarser 0.5 m resolution. Generalization to KITTI-360 and a self-collected Ouster OS-1 dataset confirms competitive performance without fine-tuning, with the refined method achieving the best JSD on both external datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four scene completion baselines: LMSCNet, LODE, MID (Make It Dense), and PVD (Point-Voxel Diffusion). The diffusion baseline PVD performs worst on all metrics (CD 1.256 m, JSD 0.498), confirming that shape-level 3D diffusion methods cannot be directly transferred to scene-scale data. Against the SDF-based methods LODE and LMSCNet, the proposed method wins decisively on CD (0.434 vs. 1.029 and 0.641 m) and on JSD after refinement (0.416 vs. 0.451 and 0.431), with the advantage attributed to operating directly on points rather than a fixed voxel grid. Against MID, the non-refined method wins on CD (0.434 vs. 0.503 m) but trails slightly on JSD (0.444 vs. 0.470); the refined version wins on both (0.375 m CD, 0.416 JSD). In the fine-grained occupancy evaluation at 0.1 m voxel resolution, the refined method achieves 24.75% IoU, outperforming MID (16.98%) and LODE (6.06%), while at coarser 0.5 m resolution SDF-based methods (MID 45.02%, LODE 43.56%) slightly edge out the proposed method (42.49%), reflecting their inherent voxel-grid advantage at coarse scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) to the scene-scale LiDAR domain, departing from prior 3D diffusion methods (e.g., PVD, Lyu et al.) that operate on normalized single-object shapes, and from LiDAR-specific diffusion approaches (Nakashima, Zyrianov) that project point clouds into range images. The key conceptual departure is reformulating the forward noising process as a local point-wise offset rather than a global mixture with Gaussian noise, which avoids the need to normalize scene-scale data into a unit range. The refinement upsampling stage is directly adopted from Lyu et al. (2022), and the noise regularization loss is a novel addition not present in prior DDPM formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local point-wise diffusion formulation provides a practical recipe for scaling DDPMs to scene-scale 3D data without voxelization or image projection, which could benefit other large-scale point cloud generation tasks. A notable limitation is that the method cannot generate unconditional scenes—it always requires an input LiDAR scan as conditioning—restricting its utility for synthetic data augmentation. Open questions include whether the formulation extends to semantic completion (predicting per-point labels), whether the noise regularization generalizes to other diffusion architectures, and whether the approach scales to even larger scenes or higher point counts without the computational overhead of sparse convolutions.
