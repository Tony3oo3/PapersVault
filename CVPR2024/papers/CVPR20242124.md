## Problem

Conventional logit-based knowledge distillation relies solely on the global logit output obtained via global average pooling, which fuses semantic information from multiple regions and classes into a single vector. This coupling transfers ambiguous knowledge to the student, particularly harming fine-grained classification where different classes share similar global appearance. Existing logit distillation methods (KD, DKD, NKD) all operate on this mixed global representation without exploiting the spatial structure of the logit output map.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for CUB200 and ImageNet) and produces class labels. Internally, SDD operates on the spatial logit output maps (K×h×w) produced by projecting the penultimate feature maps through the classifier's weight matrix, rather than on the single K-dimensional global logit vector. Multi-scale average pooling is applied to these spatial maps at scales m ∈ {1, 2, 4} to extract region-specific logit vectors, and the final output remains a standard K-class softmax prediction.

## Method

SDD replaces the single global average pooling in conventional KD with multi-scale average pooling over the logit output map, producing local logit vectors at multiple spatial granularities (scales m ∈ M). Each local logit vector is then classified as either consistent (its argmax matches the global prediction) or complementary (its argmax differs), and the distillation loss is computed separately for each group with complementary terms weighted by a factor β (default 2.0) to emphasize ambiguous samples. The total SDD loss sums the distillation losses across all scales and both groups, combined with the standard cross-entropy loss. Crucially, SDD is a single-branch method: all multi-scale logit outputs are computed by the same classifier applied to the same feature map, introducing no extra parameters or structural complexity. The approach is plug-and-play, compatible with any logit-based distillation loss (KD, DKD, NKD), and degenerates to conventional KD when only the global scale m=w is used.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with heterogeneous teacher-student pairs (Table 2), SD-KD improves over KD by 1.08–2.32% top-1 accuracy across six pairs, while SD-DKD and SD-NKD gain 0.51–1.01% and 0.61–1.03% respectively. On ImageNet (ResNet-50 → MobileNet-V1), SD-KD achieves 72.24% top-1 accuracy, a +1.74% gain over KD (70.50%). On the fine-grained CUB200 benchmark, gains are substantially larger: SD-DKD improves over DKD by up to 6.41% (VGG13→MobileNetV2), and SD-KD over KD by up to 5.82%, demonstrating SDD's particular strength when classes share similar global semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDD consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every teacher-student pair and dataset. Against the logit-based baselines, SD-KD beats KD by 1.08–2.32% on CIFAR-100 and 0.84–1.74% on ImageNet; SD-DKD beats DKD by 0.51–1.01% (CIFAR-100) and 0.32–1.03% (ImageNet); SD-NKD beats NKD by 0.61–1.03% (CIFAR-100) and 0.37–0.54% (ImageNet). Against feature-based baselines, SD-DKD surpasses ReviewKD (77.78%) by 0.27% on the R32x4→ShuffleNetV2 pair and outperforms CRD (75.65%) by 2.40% on the same pair. On the fine-grained CUB200 task, the advantage over feature-based methods is even more pronounced: SD-DKD (64.86%) exceeds ReviewKD (58.66%) by 6.20% on VGG13→MobileNetV2, and SD-KD (59.80%) exceeds CRD (56.45%) by 3.35% on the same pair. Notably, SDD introduces no extra parameters or FLOPs compared to the base logit distillation methods, as it reuses the same classifier for multi-scale logit computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDD extends classic logit-based knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.) by replacing the single global average pooling with multi-scale spatial pooling over the logit output map, thereby decoupling the fused multi-class semantic signal into region-specific logit outputs. It is conceptually complementary to DKD (Zhao et al.), which decouples knowledge at the class level (target vs. non-target), and NKD (Yang et al.), which normalizes non-target logits; SDD operates at the spatial scale before logit computation and can be combined with both. The paper positions itself against feature-based distillation methods (FitNets, CRD, ReviewKD) by arguing that the perceived superiority of feature distillation over logit distillation stems from the ambiguity introduced by global logit coupling, not from an inherent limitation of the logit interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a zero-parameter, zero-extra-compute modification to the standard KD loss (multi-scale pooling + consistent/complementary weighting) yields consistent 1–2% gains across diverse teacher-student pairs and up to 6.4% on fine-grained CUB200, making it an easy drop-in improvement for anyone already using logit distillation. The consistent/complementary decomposition offers a principled way to regularize against ambiguous samples, which is particularly relevant for fine-grained and long-tail settings. Open questions include whether the spatial-scale decoupling transfers to transformer-based backbones (which lack the same spatial logit map structure), whether the scale set M can be learned or selected automatically, and whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks such as detection or segmentation.
