## Problem

Existing decoupled text-to-3D pipelines (e.g., Fantasia3D) rely on 2D RGB diffusion models to optimize rendered surface normals, which is suboptimal due to the distribution gap between natural images and normal maps, causing unstable optimization and poor geometric fidelity. Additionally, generated textures often retain shadows and specular highlights because of the inherent ambiguity in separating material reflectance from illumination. The paper addresses both issues by learning a dedicated Normal-Depth diffusion prior and introducing an albedo diffusion model for appearance disentanglement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240211/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 3D geometry (represented as DMTet or NeRF) along with spatially-varying PBR material parameters (diffuse color, roughness, metallic, and tangent-space normal variation) compiled into a 2D texture map importable into graphics engines. The Normal-Depth diffusion model operates on 512×512 images encoded to 64×64 latent space with 4 channels (3 for normals, 1 for depth), and the albedo diffusion model conditions on the rendered depth map at each viewpoint to ensure geometric alignment of the generated albedo.

## Method

The core contribution is a Normal-Depth diffusion model built on the Stable Diffusion architecture with the VAE extended from 3 to 4 channels to jointly encode normals and depth. It is pre-trained on LAION-2B using pseudo-labels from Normal-Bae and Midas, then fine-tuned on 270K Objaverse objects with a four-view diffusion strategy (camera poses encoded via MLP) to capture object-level geometry. For geometry generation, SDS is applied to the rendered normal+depth composite on a DMTet representation, optionally combined with SDS on rendered normals from Stable Diffusion for finer surface details. For appearance modeling, a depth-conditioned albedo diffusion model (fine-tuned SD 2.1 on Objaverse) imposes SDS on the albedo component, regularizing it to be free of shadows and specular highlights, while the full PBR rendering loss is applied to the final RGB output.

## Results

On the geometry evaluation (CLIP score on uniformly-shaded renders), Ours (NeRF) achieves the highest score of 26.06, followed by Ours (Sphere) at 25.88, outperforming the next-best MVDream (24.80) by over 1 point. On the appearance evaluation (CLIP score on textured renders), Ours (Sphere) scores 31.71 and Ours (NeRF) scores 31.36, both trailing only ProlificDreamer (31.80) by a narrow margin. In a user study with 119–192 participants, Ours (NeRF) received 75% and 70% votes for best textured model and best geometry respectively, while Ours (Sphere) received over 59% and 58%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RichDreamer clearly outperforms all baselines on geometry quality: Ours (NeRF) leads by 1.26 points over MVDream (26.06 vs. 24.80) and by 2.68 points over ProlificDreamer (26.06 vs. 23.38) on the Geometry CLIP score. On appearance quality, Ours (Sphere) (31.71) and Ours (NeRF) (31.36) are competitive with ProlificDreamer (31.80), trailing by only 0.09 and 0.44 points respectively, while substantially outperforming MVDream (28.73), Magic3D-IF (27.82), and Fantasia3D (26.41). The gap over the DMTet-based baseline Fantasia3D is particularly large on geometry (25.88 vs. 17.54, a +8.34 improvement), highlighting the benefit of the dedicated Normal-Depth diffusion prior over using a generic RGB diffusion model for normal optimization. The paper attributes ProlificDreamer's slight appearance edge to its additional LoRA fine-tuning during optimization, while noting that visual inspection shows RichDreamer produces more accurate and detailed textured models. In the user study, both variants of RichDreamer dominate, with Ours (NeRF) winning 75% of textured-model votes and 70% of geometry votes against all other methods combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled text-to-3D generation paradigm established by Fantasia3D, which first optimizes geometry via SDS on rendered normals and then models appearance. The key departure is replacing the suboptimal use of an RGB diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) to supervise normal maps with a purpose-built Normal-Depth diffusion model, trained on LAION-2B using pseudo-labels from monocular estimators (Normal-Bae, Midas) and fine-tuned on Objaverse with MVDream's four-view strategy. The SDS optimization framework from DreamFusion remains the core mechanism, and the PBR material model follows prior work, but the addition of a depth-conditioned albedo diffusion model for appearance regularization is a new component not present in prior decoupled pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is a recipe for learning generalizable 3D geometric priors without large-scale 3D datasets: use monocular depth/normal estimators as pseudo-labels on billions of web images, then fine-tune on synthetic 3D data. This addresses a fundamental bottleneck in text-to-3D (limited 3D training data) and the ablation showing that Objaverse-only fine-tuning severely degrades generalization validates the approach. Limitations include the ~1.5-hour per-object optimization cost, the albedo model only regularizing the diffuse component (specular remains unaddressed), and reliance on CLIP scores as a proxy for geometric quality. Open questions include whether the pseudo-label quality from current monocular estimators is the ceiling, and whether this two-stage (real pre-train + synthetic fine-tune) recipe transfers to other 3D generation tasks beyond single-object text-to-3D.
