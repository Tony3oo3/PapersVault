## Problem

Scaling laws relating model size, data volume, computation, and performance have been well established in NLP and vision, but their applicability to Scene Text Recognition (STR) remains unexplored. This gap leaves practitioners without principled guidance on how to allocate parameters, data, and compute when training STR models, and obscures whether the same power-law relationships observed in language modeling transfer to the text recognition setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240042/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models consume cropped text images from natural scenes (handling variable illumination, occlusion, distortion, and perspective) and produce a recognized text string standardized to a 36-character lowercase alphanumeric set. Text is tokenized via BPE (Fairseq) or SentencePiece into word pieces for the decoder. Evaluation uses word accuracy across six benchmarks (IIIT5k, SVT, IC13, IC15, SVTP, CUTE80), with polygonal-annotated samples converted via minimum bounding box and perspective transformation rather than direct clipping.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical study by scaling two Transformer-based STR architectures—TrOCR (43M–1B params) and PARSeq (22M–682M params)—across model sizes, data volumes (0.75M–75M+ samples), and compute budgets (100–1000 GPU-hours), fitting smooth power-law equations to the resulting accuracy curves. To support large-scale training, the authors construct REBU-Syn, a 24M-sample dataset combining 6M real images from 14+ public sources with 18M synthetic samples (MJSynth, SynthText, Curved SyntheText, SyntheAdd), plus an optional 60M generated MJST+ corpus. Key empirical findings include: (1) large models achieve lower error rates than small models at equal data volume and converge in fewer epochs; (2) an optimal real-to-synthetic data ratio of 1:3 maximizes accuracy, with diminishing returns beyond 1:4; (3) STR-task pre-training outperforms ImageNet-21k pre-training, suggesting visual pre-training without textual alignment is counterproductive. Applying these principles to CLIP4STR-L with REBU-Syn yields a new SOTA of 97.42% average word accuracy across the six benchmarks.

## Results

The proposed CLIP4STR-L model trained on REBU-Syn achieves a new state-of-the-art top-1 average word accuracy of 97.42% across 6 common test benchmarks (IC13, IIIT5k, SVT, CUTE80, IC15, SVTP), surpassing the previous best CLIP4STR-L (97.06%) by 0.36%. The smaller CLIP4STR-B variant also sets a new record at 97.25%, improving over the original CLIP4STR-B (96.89%) by 0.36%. The scaling law analysis confirms smooth power-law relationships between model parameters (43M–1B), data volume (0.75M–75M), and computation (100–1000 GPU hours) and recognition accuracy for both TrOCR and PARSeq architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP4STR-L* (97.42%) outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the 6-benchmark average, with the largest margins over MJ+ST-trained methods: +4.19% over TrOCR-Large (93.23%), +3.64% over MaskOCR ViT-L (93.78%), and +2.27% over ABINet with MJ+ST (92.66%). Against the strongest real-data baselines, CLIP4STR-L* leads MAERec-B (96.15%) by 1.27%, ABINet-Real (96.01%) by 1.41%, and PARSeqA#-Real (95.57%) by 1.85%. The improvement over the original CLIP4STR-L (97.06%) of +0.36% is attributed to the REBU-Syn dataset (3:1 synthetic-to-real ratio) and task-related pre-training rather than ImageNet pre-training. Notably, the paper shows that ImageNet-21k pre-training actually hurts STR performance (96.74% vs. 97.03% for scratch-trained ViT-L), while STR-task pre-training followed by real-data fine-tuning yields the best results. On individual benchmarks, CLIP4STR-L* achieves the highest scores on IC13 (99.42%), CUTE80 (99.65%), and SVTP (98.13%), though it is slightly behind CLIP4STR-L on IIIT5k (99.13% vs. 99.43%) and SVT (98.61% vs. 98.15%—actually higher), confirming consistent gains across both regular and irregular text categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the scaling-law methodology well established in NLP (Kaplan et al., 2020) and Vision Transformers (Zhai et al., 2022) into the Scene Text Recognition domain, where prior work has focused almost exclusively on architectural improvements at fixed model and data scales. It systematically scales three Transformer-based STR backbones—TrOCR, PARSeq, and CLIP4STR—across parameter counts from ~22M to 1B and data volumes from 0.75M to 75M samples, while also introducing the REBU-Syn dataset (6M real + 18M synthetic) as a controlled training substrate. Conceptually, the paper positions STR as a domain where the power-law relationships observed in language modeling and image classification have not yet been validated, and it fills that gap with a comprehensive empirical sweep.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaways—optimal 1:3 real-to-synthetic data ratio, larger models converging in fewer epochs, and task-related pretraining outperforming ImageNet pretraining—give STR practitioners concrete, low-cost levers for improving accuracy without architectural redesign. The REBU-Syn dataset and the publicly released models lower the barrier for reproducing large-scale STR training. Open questions remain: whether the observed power laws persist on harder recognition targets (handwriting, historical scripts), how to reduce the prohibitive cost of training 1B-parameter models, and whether the 1:3 ratio generalizes beyond English-centric data. The finding that ImageNet-21k pretraining degrades STR performance, while intriguing, is supported by only a handful of configurations and warrants further investigation.
