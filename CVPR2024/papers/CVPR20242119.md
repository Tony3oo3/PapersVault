## Problem

Deploying motion deblurring on resource-constrained mobile devices is challenging because small-scale kernel-free models suffer significant performance drops under large motion, while larger models or prior-based methods incur prohibitive computational costs (100–1300+ GMACs). The paper identifies that image residual errors (blur-sharp pixel differences) are structured by motion type and spatial frequency, suggesting they can be discretized into a small number of categories rather than regressed as continuous values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242119/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB blur image as input and produces a deblurred RGB image as the final output. Internally, it also generates a blur segmentation map — a per-pixel class index (R=16 for RealBlur, R=8 for other datasets) represented as a one-hot vector — which categorizes each pixel's blur type. During training, images are randomly cropped to 256×256; evaluation is performed on full-resolution images (up to ~1920×1200 on RSBlur), and on-chip execution time is measured at 2000×2000 resolution for commercial deployment comparison.

## Method

The core contribution is decomposing the deblurring regression into two stages: (1) a blur pixel discretizer that classifies each pixel into one of R blur classes, and (2) a discrete-to-continuous (D2C) converter that regresses the continuous residual error conditioned on the class map. In stage 1, a NAFNet-based discretizer and a U-Net-based kernel estimator are jointly trained in logarithmic Fourier space, where deconvolution reduces to subtraction; the discretizer also predicts a latent sharp image to constrain the ill-posed kernel estimation. Basis kernels produce R deconvolved class images, and pixel-wise sampling guided by the segmentation map yields a deconvolved image. In stage 2, a frozen discretizer's segmentation map (binarized to argmax) is fed alongside the blur image into a NAFNet-based D2C converter that predicts the continuous residual error added to the blur image. This decomposition reduces total cost to ~14 GMACs (SegDeblur-S) while matching or exceeding 64 GMACs baselines, because the classification task is inherently cheaper than direct regression and the segmentation map provides GT-like structural guidance.

## Results

On RealBlur-J, SegDeblur-S (14.44 GMACs) achieves 32.53 dB PSNR, matching NAFNet-64 (63.64 GMACs) at 32.50 dB while using only 23% of the compute. SegDeblur-L* (62.68 GMACs) reaches 33.51 dB PSNR and 0.938 SSIM, outperforming all baselines including UFPNet (33.35 dB at 243 GMACs) and FFTFormer (32.62 dB at 131 GMACs). On RSBlur, SegDeblur-L* achieves 34.63 dB PSNR, surpassing Uformer-B (33.98 dB) and NAFNet-64 (33.97 dB). On ReLoBlur, SegDeblur-S (14.44 GMACs) matches LBAG+ (154.44 GMACs) at 34.86 vs 34.85 dB PSNR with only 10% of the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper wins across all 16 baselines on every dataset tested. On RealBlur-J, SegDeblur-L* (62.68 GMACs) outperforms UFPNet (33.35 dB, 243 GMACs) by 0.16 dB at 4× less compute, and beats FFTFormer (32.62 dB, 131 GMACs) by 0.89 dB at half the cost. The efficiency gain is most dramatic on ReLoBlur, where SegDeblur-S (14.44 GMACs) matches LBAG+ (154.44 GMACs) at 34.86 vs 34.85 dB PSNR with only 9.3% of the FLOPs. On RSBlur, SegDeblur-L* (34.63 dB) surpasses Uformer-B (33.98 dB, 89.50 GMACs) and NAFNet-64 (33.97 dB, 63.64 GMACs) by ~0.65 dB. On GoPro, SegFFTformer (34.38 dB, 135.81 GMACs) improves over the FFTFormer baseline (34.14 dB, 131.45 GMACs) with only 4 additional GMACs for the blur segmentation map. The small model SegDeblur-S also beats NAFNet-32 (31.99 dB, 16.25 GMACs) by 0.54 dB on RealBlur-J despite using fewer GMACs (14.44), demonstrating that the blur segmentation map compensates for reduced network capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant kernel-free paradigm (NAFNet, MIMO-UNet, FFTFormer) that treats deblurring as a direct blur-to-sharp regression, and from prior-based methods (UFPNet, MSDI-Net) that generate expensive self-generated priors. Its core conceptual move is to decompose the regression into a low-cost pixel-level blur classification (producing a 'blur segmentation map') followed by a discrete-to-continuous conversion, leveraging the logarithmic Fourier transform [7] to make basis kernel estimation tractable. The architecture builds directly on NAFNet as the backbone for both the discretizer and the D2C converter, with a U-Net serving as the kernel estimator, positioning the work as an efficiency-oriented re-framing of existing components rather than a new network family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× computational reduction (14 GMACs vs. 64+ GMACs) while matching or exceeding larger models on large-motion scenarios is practically significant for on-device deployment, as validated against Samsung EnhanceX and Google Unblur. The blur segmentation map as a 'GT-like' discrete prior offers a reusable inductive bias that could transfer to other degradation types (the authors note video and defocus deblurring as extensions). Open questions include whether the fixed number of classes (R=8–16) generalizes to arbitrarily complex multi-object motion, whether the logarithmic Fourier formulation introduces artifacts at very high frequencies, and whether the approach's advantage persists when the D2C converter is scaled to transformer backbones beyond the modest FFTFormer experiment.
