## Problem

Existing LVLMs rely solely on instruction fine-tuning for alignment, leaving them prone to unhelpful, hallucinated, or harmful responses, and their multi-turn dialogue data exhibits weak inter-turn dependencies that limit the ability to refine responses from feedback. No prior work simultaneously addresses all three alignment criteria (helpfulness, honesty, harmlessness) and multi-turn interaction ability for LVLMs.

## Input / Output

At training time, the model consumes an image, a text question, a score verbalizer (one of 'bad', 'mediocre', 'good', 'excellent'), a 5–7 word critique NLF, a refinement NLF with concrete improvement suggestions, and all preceding interaction turns. At inference, DRESS generates a single text response conditioned on the '<excellent> [Nice response.]' prefix; in multi-turn mode it additionally receives prior responses and refinement NLF to produce a refined response. The feedback dataset contains 63K samples across conversation, reasoning, and adversarial data types, with up to 4 interaction turns per sample.

## Method

DRESS generalizes conditional reinforcement learning to handle non-differentiable natural language feedback by training the model to generate the j-th turn response conditioned on the numerical score verbalizer, critique NLF, refinement NLF, and all preceding turns, minimizing cross-entropy loss over the response tokens. The key insight is a two-type NLF decomposition: critique NLF (concise strength/weakness summaries) drives alignment with the 3H criteria, while refinement NLF (concrete suggestions toward ground truth) teaches the meta-skill of incorporating feedback in multi-turn interactions. Feedback is generated by GPT-4 using COCO dense annotations as ground truth, with a chain-of-thought rationale preceding the score to improve scoring precision. A regularization term (image captioning loss from pretraining, weighted α=1) preserves visual knowledge acquired during the SFT stage. The architecture pairs EVA-CLIP-Giant (1.3B) with Vicuna-13B via a linear projector and LoRA adapters, trained in two stages: SFT on 193K samples followed by RLAIF on 33K feedback samples.

## Results

On the LLaVA Eval helpfulness benchmark, DRESS achieves an average score of 74.70 (Conversation 77.67, Description 62.17, Reasoning 84.27), outperforming the best baseline InstructBLIP (72.64) by 2.06 points. On LLaVA Bench, DRESS scores 26.45 in overall helpfulness versus 24.17 for miniGPT4, with notable gains in Relevance (37.18 vs 35.17) and Level of Detail (21.87 vs 20.17). For harmlessness on VLSafe, DRESS achieves 88.56 safety and 91.98 persuasiveness, substantially exceeding the next-best model miniGPT4 (75.05 / 74.14). On honesty (COCO captioning), DRESS reduces CHAIR_i to 2.34 and CHAIR_s to 3.30 under Instruction-1, the lowest among all instruction-tuned LVLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRESS outperforms all six baselines on the primary helpfulness metric: it leads LLaVA Eval average by 2.06 points over InstructBLIP (74.70 vs 72.64) and LLaVA Bench helpfulness by 2.28 points over miniGPT4 (26.45 vs 24.17). On harmlessness (VLSafe), DRESS's safety score of 88.56 exceeds miniGPT4 (75.05) by 13.5 points and LLaVA (38.20) by 50.4 points, while its persuasiveness of 91.98 is the highest by a wide margin. On honesty (COCO CHAIR metrics), DRESS achieves the lowest hallucination rate among instruction-tuned models (CHAIR_i 2.34, CHAIR_s 3.30 under Instruction-1), though BLIP-2 (which lacks instruction tuning) is slightly better on Instruction-2 CHAIR_s (3.50 vs 9.84). On fundamental VQA capability, DRESS (63.2 average) trails InstructBLIP (66.0) by 2.8 points, confirming that the RLAIF stage does not degrade but also does not improve raw visual understanding. The ablation in Table 6 further shows that removing the RLAIF stage drops the LLaVA Eval average from 74.70 to 70.05 (−6.24%), and removing either critique or refinement NLF individually costs 2.8–1.2 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRESS extends the RLAIF paradigm established for text-only LLMs (e.g., RLHF, language-feedback training) to the vision-language domain, building on the standard LVLM architecture of a frozen image encoder (EVA-CLIP) connected to an LLM (Vicuna) via a projector. It departs from prior LVLMs (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-HF) that rely solely on supervised instruction fine-tuning for alignment by introducing a dedicated RLAIF stage that exploits LLM-generated natural language feedback. The key methodological departure is the categorization of NLF into critique (for alignment) and refinement (for multi-turn interaction), combined with a generalized conditional reinforcement learning objective that handles the non-differentiable nature of textual feedback via language modeling loss conditioned on the feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRESS is the first work to jointly address all three alignment criteria (helpfulness, honesty, harmlessness) and multi-turn interaction ability in LVLMs, and it releases the VLSafe dataset (4.7K adversarial samples) and 63K NLF-annotated samples that fill a gap in the LVLM alignment literature. A notable finding is that instruction fine-tuning can actually increase hallucination rates relative to pretraining-only models, suggesting that SFT alone is insufficient for honesty. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4 for both feedback generation and evaluation (introducing potential circularity), the harmlessness evaluation being bounded by GPT-4's own safety understanding, and the multi-turn interaction evaluation using a simulated setting rather than a standard benchmark. Open questions include whether the NLF-conditioned training generalizes to more complex, open-ended multi-turn scenarios and whether the approach scales to larger model families.
