## Problem

Adapters have been consistently reported to underperform other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, VPT, NOAH) on vision tasks, yet no systematic study has examined how adapter position, inner structure, initialization, and data normalization choices affect their performance with ViTs. This paper identifies multiple implementation pitfalls in prior adapter work and shows that, when configured correctly, the simple adapter concept can surpass more complex adaptation mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 (VTAB) or randomly cropped to 224×224 with horizontal flipping (FGVC) and produces a class label via a linear classifier on the [CLS] token. The backbone is a frozen ViT-B/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21k; only the adapter parameters (~0.07–0.35M depending on rank) and the classifier are trained, while the full backbone (85.8M params) remains frozen. Input data must use ImageNet normalization (μ=(0.485,0.456,0.406), σ=(0.229,0.224,0.225)) to match the pre-training distribution.

## Method

Adapter+ places a bottleneck adapter (down-projection to rank r, GELU, up-projection to hidden dimension d) in the Post-Adapter position—after the FFN and its skip connection in each transformer layer—so the adapter can post-hoc modify features already transformed by the frozen module. The key architectural addition is a learned, channel-wise scaling vector s∈R^d applied to the adapter output, which outperforms both layer-wise scalar scaling and layer normalization. The adapter uses Houlsby initialization (zero-centered Gaussian, σ=0.01, truncated at 2σ) with biases in both linear layers, and no internal layer normalization. Training uses AdamW (lr=1e-3, weight decay=1e-4) with stochastic depth (linearly increasing 0→0.1 for frozen layers, 0.1 for adapters) over 100 epochs with cosine schedule and 10-epoch warmup, requiring no per-task hyperparameter optimization.

## Results

Adapter+ achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 77.6% on the VTAB benchmark (19 tasks) with only 0.20M trainable parameters (rank r=8), without any per-task hyperparameter optimization. On the FGVC fine-grained classification benchmark (5 datasets), Adapter+ reaches 90.7% average accuracy with 0.34M parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (89.0%) by 1.7 pp while using 250× fewer parameters. The method demonstrates the best parameter-accuracy trade-off among all evaluated approaches, outperforming methods with 2–3× more parameters such as VPT-Deep (0.60M, 76.4%) and SPT-Adapter (0.43M, 76.4%) on VTAB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adapter+ (r=8, 0.20M params) outperforms all competing parameter-efficient methods on VTAB: it beats FacT-TK≤32 (76.5%, 0.10M) by 1.1 pp, Consolidator (76.5%, 0.30M) by 1.1 pp, SPT-Adapter (76.0%, 0.43M) by 1.6 pp, VPT-Deep (76.4%, 0.60M) by 1.2 pp, LoRA (75.6%, 0.29M) by 2.0 pp, and SSF (74.6%, 0.24M) by 3.0 pp. On FGVC, Adapter+ (90.7%, 0.34M) ties SSF (90.7%, 0.39M) on average accuracy but uses fewer parameters, and outperforms SPT-LoRA (90.1%, 0.52M) by 0.6 pp, VPT-Deep (89.7%, 0.85M) by 1.0 pp, and full fine-tuning (89.0%, 86.0M) by 1.7 pp. Notably, Adapter+ also surpasses full fine-tuning on VTAB (77.6% vs. 74.2%) despite using 429× fewer parameters, and shows the most robust performance across VTAB subgroups (Natural: 83.6%, Specialized: 85.9%, Structured: 63.1%) with the smallest variance between groups. The re-evaluations of prior methods (correcting data normalization and removing test-set-based early stopping) generally reduce their reported numbers, widening Adapter+'s lead over most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the bottleneck adapter concept introduced by Houlsby et al. for NLP transformers and the FFN-only (Post) placement proposed by Pfeiffer et al., extending both to vision transformers for the first time in a systematic study. It departs from prior adapter work by replacing the layer-wise scalar scaling (from He et al.'s unified PEFT framework) with a learned channel-wise scaling vector, and by showing that the internal layer normalization recommended by Pfeiffer et al. is actually detrimental for ViTs. The paper positions Adapter+ as a correction to the suboptimal adapter configurations that have been used as baselines in recent PEFT-for-vision literature, arguing that implementation choices (position, initialization, normalization, data preprocessing) explain much of the apparent gap between adapters and more complex methods like LoRA or VPT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple, well-configured adapter with channel-wise scaling achieves state-of-the-art VTAB accuracy (77.6%) without per-task hyperparameter tuning, offering a robust and low-maintenance alternative to more complex PEFT methods. This is particularly valuable for practitioners who need to adapt a frozen ViT across many diverse tasks without extensive per-task tuning. Limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (ViT-B/16) and a single pretraining corpus (ImageNet-21k), leaving open whether the findings generalize to larger ViTs, different pretraining regimes (e.g., DINOv2), or non-classification tasks. An open question is whether the channel-wise scaling mechanism interacts differently with self-supervised or contrastive pretraining, where feature geometry may differ from supervised ImageNet pretraining.
