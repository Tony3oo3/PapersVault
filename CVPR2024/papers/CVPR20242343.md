## Problem

In adversarial transfer learning, prior work has focused almost exclusively on full finetuning and has overlooked how initialization—both the pretrained backbone and the linear classification head—affects downstream adversarial robustness. This paper reveals that Parameter-Efficient Finetuning (PEFT) methods fundamentally fail to achieve adversarial robustness when initialized from a standard (non-robust) pretrained model, and that the linear head initialization strategy is a critical but previously unexplored factor determining how much robustness is preserved from pretraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100; higher-resolution images for Caltech-256, CUB-200, and Stanford Dogs) and outputs a class label. Resizing and normalization are integrated directly into the model architecture because the attacker is assumed to have no access to pre-processed images. Robustness is evaluated under PGD-10 and AutoAttack with ε=8/255 for low-resolution datasets and ε=4/255 for high-resolution datasets, matching the pretraining attack budget.

## Method

The core contribution is Robust Linear Initialization (RoLI): the linear classification head of a robust pretrained model is initialized with weights obtained from adversarial linear probing (rather than random weights), and then adversarial finetuning (full finetuning or PEFT methods such as LoRA, Adapter, or Bias) is performed on top. The key insight is that adversarial linear probing uniquely preserves the robust features inherited from the pretrained backbone, so using its learned head weights as initialization maximally transfers that robustness into subsequent finetuning stages. The paper further demonstrates that a robust pretrained model is a prerequisite for PEFT methods to succeed, that larger non-robust models (e.g., CLIP) cannot substitute for robust pretraining, and that transferred robustness of adversarial linear probing strongly correlates (Pearson r=0.896) with transferred accuracy of standard linear probing, explaining when linear probing outperforms other methods.

## Results

Across five image classification datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Caltech-256, CUB-200, Stanford Dogs), RoLI-Full-FT achieves the best overall performance with 84.01% average clean accuracy and 46.25% average PGD-10 robustness (ε=8/255 for low-res, ε=4/255 for high-res). On average across four finetuning methods (Full-FT, Adapter, LoRA, Bias), RoLI improves clean accuracy by 3.88% and robust accuracy by 2.44% over random linear initialization (RanLI). Under AutoAttack, RoLI-Full-FT achieves 57.0% robustness on Stanford Dogs, surpassing all other methods by a notable margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoLI-Full-FT (Swin Transformer) substantially outperforms both prior SOTA baselines: TWINS-AT (ResNet-50) achieves 74.38% avg clean / 36.41% avg PGD, while RoLI-Full-FT reaches 84.01% / 46.25%, a gain of +9.63% clean and +9.84% robust accuracy. AutoLoRA (ResNet-50) scores 69.62% / 39.32%, trailing RoLI-Full-FT by +14.39% clean and +6.93% robust accuracy. Notably, RoLI-Full-FT also beats the best RanLI PEFT method (RanLI-Bias at 79.07% / 43.74%) by +4.94% clean and +2.51% robust accuracy, confirming that the initialization strategy matters beyond just the finetuning method. The largest single-dataset gains from RoLI over RanLI appear on Stanford Dogs (+20.78% PGD for Full-FT) and Caltech-256 (+6.29% PGD for Full-FT), where adversarial linear probing initialization is most beneficial. A caveat is that TWINS-AT and AutoLoRA use ResNet-50 while RoLI uses Swin Transformer, so the backbone difference partially contributes to the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial transfer learning framework of Shafahi et al. (2019), which established random linear initialization (RanLI) as the standard for adapting robust pretrained models to downstream tasks. It departs from prior adversarial transfer learning methods (TWINS, AutoLoRA) that exclusively applied full finetuning, by systematically studying six finetuning techniques including PEFT methods (LoRA, Adapter, VPT, Bias) in the adversarial setting. The key conceptual shift is identifying initialization—both the backbone's robustness and the linear head's weights—as the dominant factor, rather than the finetuning method itself, which prior work treated as the primary lever for robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that PEFT methods cannot achieve meaningful adversarial robustness without a robust pretrained model, even with adversarial training during finetuning, has direct practical implications: practitioners cannot simply apply parameter-efficient methods to standard pretrained models and expect adversarial guarantees. RoLI offers a simple, broadly applicable initialization strategy that improves SOTA across five datasets, though it introduces a two-step training cost. Open questions include whether these insights generalize beyond image classification (e.g., NLP, audio), whether the robustness-accuracy trade-off of RoLI persists at larger model scales, and whether the initialization step can be made more computationally efficient than full adversarial linear probing.
