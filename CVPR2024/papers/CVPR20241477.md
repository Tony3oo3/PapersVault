## Problem

Personalized text-to-image generation requires preserving the appearance of a custom subject while generating novel variations. Finetuning-based methods (e.g., DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion) achieve this but demand expensive per-subject optimization and multiple reference images, while finetuning-free encoder-based methods (e.g., BLIPDiffusion, ELITE) suffer from information loss in the encoding step, leading to poor appearance preservation especially for uncommon or unique subjects. No prior approach is simultaneously finetuning-free, encoder-free, and capable of high-fidelity subject preservation.

## Input / Output

JeDi accepts any number of reference images (1–4) and corresponding text prompts, treating the reference images as known elements in a joint-image set and generating the remaining images as new personalized samples. The model operates in the latent space of StableDiffusion V1.4, and during sampling the known reference images are replaced at each denoising step with their forward-diffusion noised versions (the replacement trick), so the output is a set of images sharing the same subject but differing in pose, background, and context per the text prompts.

## Method

The core architectural change is a coupled self-attention layer: within the diffusion U-Net, the self-attention features at each spatial location attend to every location across all images in the same-subject set (not just within a single image), implemented by two reshaping operations around a standard self-attention block. This is applied at multiple U-Net resolutions to enforce both high-level semantic and low-level attribute consistency across the generated image set. Training uses a synthetic S³ dataset (1.6M same-subject image sets) created by prompting SDXL with ChatGPT-generated captions to produce photo collages, then augmenting diversity via background inpainting and InstructPix2Pix stylization. Personalization at test time is cast as inpainting: reference images are fed as known inputs with a binary mask, and the model denoises only the missing images. An image-guidance term (analogous to classifier-free guidance but over reference images) is added during sampling to further improve fidelity to the input subject.

## Results

On the DreamBooth test set (30 subjects, 25 prompts each), JeDi with 3 reference images achieves the best subject preservation scores: MDINO 0.8037 and MCLIP-I 0.9011, substantially outperforming finetuning-based baselines (DreamBooth: MDINO 0.7115; CustomDiffusion: MDINO 0.7109). With only 1 reference image, JeDi still surpasses all finetuning-free baselines on MDINO (0.7510 vs. 0.6692 for BLIPDiffusion and 0.6805 for ELITE) and MCLIP-I (0.8764 vs. 0.8620/0.8615). JeDi(3 inputs) also leads on DINO (0.6791) and CLIP-I (0.8139) among all methods, while CLIP-T text alignment remains competitive (0.2932).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against finetuning-free baselines, JeDi(1 input) wins decisively on subject preservation: MDINO improves by 8.2 points over BLIPDiffusion (0.7510 vs. 0.6692) and 7.1 points over ELITE (0.7510 vs. 0.6805), while also leading on MCLIP-I and DINO. Against finetuning-based baselines, JeDi(3 inputs) achieves a large MDINO margin of ~9 points over both DreamBooth (0.8037 vs. 0.7115) and CustomDiffusion (0.8037 vs. 0.7109), and a ~3.3-point MCLIP-I advantage over DreamBooth (0.9011 vs. 0.8683). The only metric where JeDi(3 inputs) trails is CLIP-T text alignment (0.2932 vs. 0.3015 for CustomDiffusion), suggesting a slight trade-off between subject fidelity and prompt adherence at higher reference counts. Overall, JeDi demonstrates that a finetuning-free approach can match or exceed finetuning-based methods when given the same number of reference images, while requiring no per-subject optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

JeDi builds on the latent diffusion U-Net architecture (Stable Diffusion) and the inpainting formulation from Repaint, but departs fundamentally from both finetuning-based personalization (DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion) and encoder-based finetuning-free methods (BLIPDiffusion, ELITE) by eliminating both per-subject optimization and the information-losing encoding step. The core architectural contribution—coupled self-attention that lets spatial features co-attend across multiple images in a set—is a minimal modification (two reshaping operations) to the standard U-Net self-attention, yet it enables the model to learn a joint distribution over same-subject images. The classifier-free guidance framework is extended from text-only to joint text-and-image guidance, and the training data pipeline repurposes LLMs (ChatGPT) and single-image diffusion (SDXL) to synthesize multi-image same-subject collages at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing both finetuning and image encoders, JeDi addresses the two main practical barriers to personalized generation: computational cost at test time and information loss in the encoding bottleneck. The coupled self-attention mechanism is simple enough to be adopted in other multi-image diffusion settings, and the S3 synthetic data pipeline offers a reusable recipe for generating same-subject training sets. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully beyond 4 reference images, how it handles multi-concept or compositional personalization, and whether the technique transfers to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) that lack the U-Net's multi-resolution attention hierarchy. The paper is limited to a single base model (SD V1.4) and a single evaluation benchmark, leaving generalizability somewhat untested.
