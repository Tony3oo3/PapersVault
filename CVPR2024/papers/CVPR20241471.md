## Problem

Patch2Self (P2S), the de-facto standard for self-supervised dMRI denoising, requires solving a least-squares regression over all n voxels from d−1 held-in volumes, making it computationally prohibitive as acquisition resolution and dimensionality grow. Beyond efficiency, P2S operates as a black-box with no mechanism to identify which spatial regions disproportionately influence the denoising outcome, limiting its interpretability for medical imaging applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4D dMRI volume of shape l×w×h×d (e.g., 116×116×72×65 for PPMI, 81×106×76×160 for Stanford HARDI) and produces a denoised 4D volume of identical shape. Each 3D volume is flattened into a column of the Casorati matrix A∈R^{n×d}; the denoiser is trained on a sketched sub-matrix C∈R^{s×d} with s≈d≪n, but the final denoised volume is predicted by applying the learned linear map Φ_J to the full A_{-j}, so the output retains full spatial resolution regardless of sketch size.

## Method

P2S2 replaces the full Casorati matrix regression of P2S with a sketch-and-solve procedure: a random sketching matrix S∈R^{s×n} (s≈d≪n) is applied to A to form a coreset C=SA, on which the J-invariant self-supervised linear regression is solved. Among three sketching strategies—CountSketch, SRFT, and leverage score sampling—randomized leverage score sampling yields the best denoising accuracy and additionally provides interpretability by highlighting voxels with high influence on the solution. A Rank-Revealing QR (RRQR) decomposition of C is used to rank rows by independence, remove redundant rows, and iteratively calibrate the optimal sketch size s via a self-supervised loss elbow criterion. Theoretically, the paper proves that with k=O(ρ log ρ/ε²) leverage-score-sampled rows (where ρ is the rank of A_{-j}), the MSE of the sketched solution is within a (1+ε) factor of the exact P2S solution, and that ρ can be recovered directly from the sketched matrix via RRQR.

## Results

On simulated data (256×256×4×63), P2S2 trained on only 20K samples (7% of data) matches P2S across all SNR levels: at SNR 20, P2S2 achieves MSE 1.15 vs. P2S's 1.07, and R² 0.83 vs. 0.84. On the HCP 7T dataset (61M voxels), P2S2 uses just 50K samples (0.083%) via leverage score sampling and produces comparable DKI (radial and mean kurtosis) estimates to P2S. Empirical speedup scales with data dimensionality: on Stanford HARDI (81×106×76×160), the speedup over P2S is substantially larger than on PPMI (116×116×72×65) or simulated data, with the QR calibration step adding <1 s overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Patch2Self (P2S) [Fadnavis et al., NeurIPS 2020], the current de-facto standard for dMRI denoising. P2S2 matches P2S almost exactly on denoising quality: at SNR 20, MSE differs by only 0.08 (1.15 vs. 1.07) and R² by 0.01 (0.83 vs. 0.84); at SNR 25 the gap shrinks to 0.01 in MSE. The one notable exception is at SNR 5, where P2S2's R² drops to 0.10 versus P2S's 0.17, though its MSE is actually slightly lower (2.82 vs. 2.98). On downstream tasks (DKI kurtosis estimation on HCP 7T and CSD-based tractography on Stanford HARDI), P2S2 trained on 0.083% and 8.3% of the data respectively produces visually and quantitatively comparable results to P2S. Among sketching strategies, randomized leverage score sampling outperforms CountSketch and SRFT, which in turn outperform uniform sampling and deterministic top-leverage-score selection at every sketch size tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

P2S2 extends Patch2Self (P2S), which itself recasts 4D dMRI denoising as a J-invariant self-supervised linear regression on a Casorati matrix, by replacing the full over-determined system with a sketched coreset via randomized matrix multiplication. The sketching toolkit—CountSketch, SRFT/SRHT, and leverage score sampling—is drawn directly from the randomized numerical linear algebra literature (Drineas, Mahoney, Woodruff, and others), and the RRQR-based calibration procedure adapts rank-revealing factorizations to the self-supervised setting. The interpretability contribution via statistical leverage scores departs from the prior treatment of P2S as a black-box denoiser, connecting it to classical regression diagnostics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dMRI acquisitions grow in resolution and field strength (7T, multi-shell, multi-dimensional encoding), P2S's O(nd²) per-volume cost becomes a bottleneck for large-scale cohort analyses; P2S2's demonstrated 60%+ training-set redundancy and empirical speedups address this directly. The leverage-score interpretability is particularly valuable for medical imaging, where clinicians need to understand which anatomical regions drive denoising decisions and where structured artifacts (e.g., band-like noise in 7T data) originate. Open questions include whether the sketch-and-solve approach extends to non-linear or deep denoising backbones, how the optimal sketch size behaves across vastly different acquisition protocols, and whether the interpretability signal from leverage scores can be formalized into a diagnostic tool for artifact detection.
