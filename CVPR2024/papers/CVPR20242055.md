## Problem

Existing generative unlearning methods erase high-level concepts (styles, objects) or individual features (beard, hat) but cannot remove a specific person's identity from a pre-trained GAN. This is critical because GAN inversion can reconstruct any identity—even one absent from the training set—and subsequent editing tools enable further misuse. The paper introduces generative identity unlearning as a new task: erasing an entire identity from a 3D GAN using only a single image, while preserving overall generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB source image and a pre-trained EG3D model (mapping network, StyleGAN2-style generator, and neural renderer). The source image is embedded into the latent space via the GOAE inversion network, and only the generator backbone G is updated (Map and R remain frozen). The output is a modified generator G_u that, when rendered at 128×128 with the source latent code, produces a face with a distinct identity, while maintaining generation fidelity for all other latent codes.

## Method

GUIDE consists of two components. First, UFO (Un-Identifying Face On latent space) determines the target latent w_t by extrapolating from the average latent w̄ in the direction opposite to the source latent w_u at a fixed distance d=30, ensuring the target represents a clearly distinct identity even when w_u is close to w̄. Second, Latent Target Unlearning (LTU) optimizes G_u with three losses: a local unlearning loss (L2 on tri-plane features plus perceptual and ArcFace identity loss on rendered images) that shifts the source identity toward the target; an adjacency-aware unlearning loss that applies the same local loss to N_a=2 neighboring latent codes around both source and target to cover the full identity neighborhood; and a global preservation loss (perceptual loss on N_g=2 random latent codes distant from source/target) that constrains distribution shift. The generator is fine-tuned with Adam at lr=1e-4, and the total objective is L_total = L_local + λ_adj·L_adj + λ_global·L_global.

## Results

In the single-image unlearning setting (Table 1), GUIDE achieves the lowest identity similarity (ID) across all three scenarios: 0.14 (Random), 0.06 (InD/FFHQ), and 0.03 (OOD/CelebAHQ), compared to the baseline's 0.19, 0.16, and 0.12 respectively. GUIDE also preserves generation quality best, with FID_pre of 10.80, 8.00, and 7.88 and ΔFID_real of 6.64, 3.05, and 3.34, outperforming the baseline (FID_pre: 11.73, 9.00, 9.52; ΔFID_real: 7.46, 4.15, 4.75). In the multi-image OOD test (Table 2), GUIDE reduces ID_others from 0.28 (baseline) to 0.17, confirming thorough identity erasure across unseen images of the same person.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Since generative identity unlearning is introduced as a new task, the paper constructs a simple baseline rather than comparing to external methods. The baseline uses the average latent code (w̄) as the unlearning target and optimizes only the local unlearning loss (L2 + perceptual + identity loss). GUIDE outperforms this baseline on every metric across all three scenarios (Random, InD, OOD). The key advantage comes from two components: (1) the UFO extrapolated target latent (w_t) dramatically reduces ID similarity, especially in the Random scenario where the source latent is close to w̄ (ID drops from 0.19 to 0.12), and (2) the global preservation loss (L_global) recovers the FID_pre and ΔFID_real degradation caused by the extrapolation and adjacency losses, bringing FID_pre from 13.02 back to 7.88 in the OOD setting while maintaining the low ID of 0.03. In the multi-image OOD test, the adjacency-aware loss (L_adj) is critical for erasing identity from unseen images of the same person, reducing ID_others from 0.28 to 0.14, with GUIDE's final value of 0.17 reflecting the trade-off with preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on EG3D (a 3D GAN with tri-plane representation) and GOAE (a GAN inversion network) as its backbone, but its conceptual lineage is in the machine unlearning literature. It departs from prior generative unlearning work (ESD, Kumari et al., Forget-Me-Not) that targets high-level concepts or artistic styles, and from GAN feature-unlearning methods (Moon et al., Tiwary et al.) that erase attributes like 'beard' or 'hat.' GUIDE instead formulates unlearning of a specific *identity*—a person's face—from the generator using only a single image, even when that identity was never in the training set, which no prior work addresses. The UFO target-latent construction (extrapolating past the mean latent) and the three-part loss decomposition (local, adjacency-aware, global preservation) are the paper's methodological contributions on top of this new task framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored privacy risk: GAN inversion can reconstruct and edit a person's identity from a pre-trained generator even if that person was never in the training data, and no existing unlearning method handles this. The single-image, no-full-dataset requirement makes the approach practically deployable. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 3D face architecture (EG3D on FFHQ), the baseline is self-constructed and simple, and the method has not been validated on 2D GANs or diffusion models. Open questions include whether the latent-space extrapolation strategy generalizes to other generative architectures, how unlearning interacts with subsequent inversion queries, and whether multiple identities can be unlearned sequentially without compounding distribution shift.
