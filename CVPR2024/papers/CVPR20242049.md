## Problem

While scaling laws for LLMs are well understood, the scaling properties of diffusion-based text-to-image models remain largely unexplored. It is unclear which architectural dimensions (channel width vs. transformer depth) are most effective to scale, how training data should be scaled, and how model size, compute, and dataset size jointly govern text-image alignment performance. The high training cost and heterogeneous training settings across existing models make fair, controlled comparison of denoising backbones extremely difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All models take a text prompt (up to 77 tokens) encoded by OpenCLIP-H (1024-dim) as conditioning and generate 256×256 RGB images via a latent diffusion process in SDXL's VAE latent space (128×128). Inference uses DDIM with 50 steps and classifier-free guidance. Evaluation is tracked over training via TIFA (VQA-based faithfulness on 4K prompts), ImageReward (human preference proxy on MSCOCO-10K), CLIP score, FID, and HPSv2, with early-stage metrics shown to predict final performance.

## Method

The paper conducts large-scale controlled ablations at 256×256 resolution (batch size 2048, up to 600K steps) across three axes: (1) denoising backbone design, comparing SD2, SDXL, and DeepFloyd UNets then ablating 15 SDXL variants (channels 128–384, transformer depth [0,2,2]–[0,4,14]) and 4 PixArt-α Transformer variants (hidden dim 1024–1536, depth 28–56); (2) training data scaling, using curated LensArt (250M) and SSTK (350M) datasets with and without synthetic caption augmentation (50% random flip between original and top-5 synthetic captions); (3) joint scaling, fitting power-law functions S=0.47C^0.02, S=0.77N^0.11, S=0.64D^0.03 for TIFA score as a function of compute, parameters, and image-noun pairs. Key findings: SDXL's design (cross-attention only at 2×/4× downsampling, more transformer blocks at lower resolution) is superior; increasing transformer depth is more parameter-efficient than widening channels; a TD4_4 variant achieves comparable performance at 45% fewer parameters and 28% less inference compute; and data quality/diversity (measured by unique image-noun pairs) matters more than raw dataset size.

## Results

SDXL's UNet (2.4B) achieves 0.82 TIFA within 150K training steps, converging 6× faster than SD2 UNet (0.9B) and 3× faster than the naively scaled SD2-C512 (2.2B), while reaching a higher final TIFA of 0.84 at 600K steps. The paper identifies an efficient variant (TD4_4, 1.32B params) that matches SDXL's performance with 45% fewer parameters and 28% less inference compute. On the data side, combining LensArt + SSTK (610M images with synthetic captions) accelerates SDXL convergence by 2.5× and improves ImageReward from 0.345 to 0.524 over the base LensArt dataset. Scaling transformer depth in the UNet is shown to be more parameter-efficient for text-image alignment than increasing channel width.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD2 UNet (0.9B), SDXL's UNet achieves 6× faster TIFA convergence (0.82 at 150K vs. ~300K+ steps) and a higher ceiling (0.84 vs. ~0.80 TIFA), despite 2× slower per-iteration FLOPs, meaning it reaches the same score at 2× reduced total training cost. The naively scaled SD2-C512 (2.2B) improves over base SD2 but converges slower in FLOPs, confirming that simply widening channels is less efficient than SDXL's architectural redesign (removing the 4th downsampling level, concentrating cross-attention at 2× and 4×, and adding transformer depth at lower resolutions). DeepFloyd IF-XL (2.0B, 512 channels) is outperformed by SDXL in both final TIFA and convergence speed, largely because IF-XL allocates only 12% of compute to attention versus SDXL's 64%. Against PixArt-α (0.61B–1.08B Transformer), the UNet variants consistently achieve higher TIFA and ImageReward at the same training steps (e.g., SD2 UNet reaches 0.80 TIFA and 0.2 ImageReward at 250K steps while the 0.9B PixArt-α variant reaches only 0.78 and 0.1), indicating that Transformers lack the inductive bias of UNets for text-image alignment when trained from scratch. The paper's key finding is that the TD4_4 variant (1.32B) matches SDXL's TIFA with 45% fewer parameters and 28% less inference compute, and converges 1.7× faster in wall-clock time, making it the most efficient architecture identified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM scaling-law literature (Kaplan et al., Hoffmann et al.) to diffusion-based text-to-image models, where prior work has largely studied model scaling in isolation (DiT for class-conditional generation, SDXL's architectural improvements) without controlled cross-architecture comparison. It builds directly on the UNet design space of SD2/SDXL/DeepFloyd and the Transformer backbone of PixArt-α/DiT, but departs from prior T2I papers by training all variants under identical dataset, text encoder, and training settings—something the authors note has been infeasible due to differing training configurations and prohibitive cost. The synthetic-caption data augmentation strategy is positioned as concurrent with DALL-E 3's caption enhancement, while the power-law fitting follows the Cherti et al. CLIP scaling-law methodology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, controlled evidence that UNet architecture design (cross-attention placement, transformer depth) dominates raw parameter count for text-image alignment, and that a 45%-smaller UNet variant can match SDXL performance—offering a concrete, actionable path for practitioners to reduce training and inference cost. The fitted power-law scaling functions (S = 0.47C^0.02, S = 0.77N^0.11, S = 0.64D^0.03) give predictive tools for resource allocation, though the small exponents suggest strong diminishing returns. Key limitations include evaluation at 256×256 resolution only, a single text encoder (OpenCLIP-H), and the finding that Transformer backbones still underperform well-designed UNets in controlled settings, leaving open whether architectural innovations (e.g., MM-DiT in SD3) can close this gap at scale.
