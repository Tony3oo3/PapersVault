## Problem

Vision-centric autonomous driving perception systems degrade significantly in low-light conditions, yet collecting paired day-night driving data is impractical and risks harming daytime performance. Existing low-light enhancement methods either require paired supervision or optimize purely for human visual quality without considering whether the enhanced images actually improve downstream 3D detection accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241849/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image (1600×900 from nuScenes, resized to 512×512 for processing), a depth map (from LiDAR point-cloud projection during training, or from a frozen monocular depth estimation network at inference), and a text prompt (generated by a large image captioning model) as multi-modal conditions. It produces a pixel-level enhanced bright image in the same spatial resolution. During training, synthetic night images are generated from daytime data via a dynamic sRGB→RAW→noise→ISP degradation pipeline, eliminating the need for real paired data.

## Method

LightDiff builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 backbone with a ControlNet-style trainable UNet encoder copy that injects fused multi-modal conditions. The key architectural novelty is a Multi-Condition Adapter that computes softmax attention weights between the latent feature matrices of the degraded image and depth map, producing a weighted combination that adaptively balances the contribution of each modality per spatial location. Training uses a dynamic degradation process to synthesize day-night pairs from 24,745 daytime nuScenes images, followed by RL fine-tuning where a reward model scores predicted clean latents on LiDAR depth consistency (MSE) and distribution alignment (MMD) with real daytime features, applied only at low noise timesteps. At inference, a Recurrent Lighting Inference (ReLI) loop iteratively refines the text prompt and depth map using the model's own output as new input, requiring only two iterations to stabilize.

## Results

On the nuScenes nighttime validation set, LightDiff achieves 0.176 AP with BEVDepth and 0.170 AP with BEVStereo for 3D car detection, improving over the raw nighttime baseline (0.134 / 0.124 AP) by +4.2% and +4.6% respectively. For no-reference image quality, LightDiff attains the best scores on MUSIQ (51.674), MANIQA (0.086), NIMA (4.594), ILNIQE (20.250), and NIQE (3.516) among all compared methods. The method is trained entirely on synthetically degraded daytime images (no real night–day pairs) and requires no retraining of the downstream 3D detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightDiff outperforms all 12 baselines on 3D car detection AP for both BEVDepth (0.176 vs. next-best EnlightenGAN* at 0.138) and BEVStereo (0.170 vs. next-best EnlightenGAN* at 0.128), a margin of roughly 27–34% relative improvement over the closest competitor. Several enhancement methods (SCI, Zero-DCE++, URetinex-Net, ExposureDiffusion, ShadowDiffusion) actually degrade 3D detection AP below the raw nighttime baseline, indicating that generic low-light enhancement can introduce artifacts harmful to downstream perception. On image quality metrics, LightDiff leads on MUSIQ (51.674 vs. ShadowDiffusion's 40.695), ILNIQE (20.250 vs. 57.984), and NIQE (3.516 vs. 5.607), but ShadowDiffusion edges ahead on TReS (67.086 vs. 58.622) and HyperIQA (0.446 vs. 0.407), suggesting a trade-off between perceptual realism and task-oriented enhancement. Among retrained unsupervised baselines trained on the same nuScenes daytime set, LightDiff still leads by a wide margin (0.176 vs. 0.138 AP for BEVDepth), confirming that the multi-condition adapter and RL-based reward modeling provide gains beyond simply matching the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework and the ControlNet architecture for conditional image generation, extending them to the domain of low-light enhancement for autonomous driving. It departs from prior diffusion-based enhancement methods (ShadowDiffusion, ExposureDiffusion) that require paired day-night training data by instead synthesizing degraded images through a physically-motivated sRGB→RAW→ISP degradation pipeline. The multi-condition adapter, which dynamically weights depth, RGB, and text modalities, represents a novel architectural contribution beyond ControlNet's single-condition injection, while the RL-based reward modeling using LiDAR depth consistency and MMD distribution alignment is a new loss formulation not seen in prior enhancement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete safety gap in vision-centric autonomous driving by showing that enhanced nighttime images can improve 3D detection AP by 4–5% without retraining the detector, which is practically significant for deployment. A notable finding is that several popular enhancement methods (SCI, Zero-DCE++) actually *degrade* 3D detection performance despite improving visual quality, highlighting that perceptual brightness does not guarantee better downstream perception. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), only the front camera being enhanced, and only the 'Car' category being reported; generalization to other object classes, sensor configurations, and real-world deployment remains open. The authors also acknowledge that synthesizing realistic dark images with vehicle lights is difficult, constraining the training data pipeline.
