## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models systematically produce images with median brightness because their noise schedules retain non-zero SNR at the terminal timestep during training, while inference starts from pure Gaussian noise. This train-inference distribution mismatch causes the model to hallucinate residual low-frequency data signals that are absent at test time. Existing fixes—offset noise and zero-terminal-SNR rescaling with v-prediction—either break the i.i.d. noise assumption or require full-network fine-tuning that invalidates the large ecosystem of community fine-tunes (LoRA, ControlNet, etc.).

## Input / Output

The OMS module consumes a standard Gaussian noise tensor in the VAE latent space (e.g., 4×64×64 for SD1.5/SD2.1) together with a text prompt encoded by OpenCLIP ViT-H, and outputs a single modified latent that replaces the initial noise before the pre-trained model's standard DDIM/DDPM sampling loop begins. The OMS prompt can be set independently of the main diffusion prompt, enabling separate control of low-frequency attributes (brightness, color) without altering the semantic content prompt. The module is trained once per latent domain and is then shared across all base models operating in that same space.

## Method

OMS trains a compact auxiliary UNet ψ to perform v-prediction on pure Gaussian noise x_T^S ~ N(0,I), with the regression target v_T^S = −x_0 and an L2 loss, effectively learning to predict the L2-mean data direction from noise. The predicted x̃_0 is then combined with x_T^S through either the SDE reverse step (Eq. 17) or a DDIM ODE step (Eq. 20) to reconstruct x̃_T^T, a latent whose marginal distribution matches the training-time terminal distribution (smaller Gaussian radius r^T vs. r^S). This reconstructed latent is fed as the starting point into the frozen pre-trained model, which runs its usual multi-step sampling unchanged. The module is trained for only 2,000 steps on LAION-2B, requires as few as 3.7 M parameters, and supports classifier-free guidance with an independent CFG weight ω_ψ. Because the OMS prompt C_ψ is decoupled from the diffusion prompt C_θ, users can steer brightness and color by simply changing the OMS prompt (e.g., "dark" vs. "bright") while keeping the semantic prompt fixed.

## Results

On MS-COCO zero-shot generation (10k captions, DDIM 50 steps, guidance 7.5), OMS dramatically reduces the Wasserstein distance between generated and ground-truth image mean distributions: SD1.5 drops from 22.47 to 7.84, SD2.1 from 21.63 to 7.70, and SDXL from 11.08 to 7.25. ImageReward improves across all models (e.g., SD1.5: 0.1991→0.2289; SDXL: 0.8246→0.8730), and Precision, Density, and Coverage all increase. FID slightly worsens (e.g., SD1.5: 12.52→14.74), which the authors attribute to a known negative correlation between FID and visual aesthetics in COCO zero-shot settings, and Recall decreases modestly (e.g., SD1.5: 0.55→0.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OMS-enhanced models against their unmodified (RAW) counterparts for SD1.5/SD2.1 (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and SDXL (Podell et al., 2023). The headline win is on Wasserstein distance: OMS reduces it by ~65% for SD1.5 (22.47→7.84) and SD2.1 (21.63→7.70), and by ~35% for SDXL (11.08→7.25), indicating a much broader and more accurate coverage of the brightness distribution. ImageReward improves consistently (SD1.5: +0.030, SDXL: +0.048), and Precision, Density, and Coverage all increase. However, FID worsens for all three models (e.g., SD1.5: 12.52→14.74), which the authors explicitly flag as expected due to FID's known negative correlation with visual aesthetics in zero-shot COCO evaluation. Recall drops modestly across the board (e.g., SD1.5: 0.55→0.46), suggesting OMS trades some recall for improved precision and distributional coverage. Overall, OMS wins on the metrics most relevant to its stated goal (distribution breadth and low-frequency fidelity) while sacrificing a small amount of recall and FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the v-prediction parameterisation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), but departs from two prior remedies for the terminal-SNR brightness bias: offset noise (Guttenberg & CrossLabs), which is an ad-hoc perturbation that breaks the iid structure of the noise, and zero-terminal-SNR rescaling (Lin et al., 2024), which mandates switching to v-prediction and full-network fine-tuning. OMS instead introduces a small auxiliary v-prediction network that maps standard Gaussian noise to the data-adulterated terminal latent the pre-trained model expects, leaving all original parameters and schedules untouched. The theoretical observation that the terminal-timestep prediction under SNR=0 reduces to the L2 mean of the data (schedule-independent) is the key conceptual bridge that makes this single-step correction principled rather than heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the open-source diffusion ecosystem: a single 3.7M-parameter module can be shared across SD1.5, SD2.1, LCM, and community LoRA models without any retraining of the backbone, preserving compatibility with thousands of existing fine-tuned checkpoints. The finding that such a tiny network suffices suggests the low-frequency correction at the terminal timestep is a very simple function, opening the door to even lighter-weight or analytically derived corrections. Limitations include that OMS only addresses low-frequency (brightness, global color) artifacts and does not improve high-frequency detail or structural coherence; FID actually degrades slightly, which the authors attribute to the known FID–aesthetics trade-off on zero-shot COCO. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion, non-VP schedules, or modalities beyond RGB images, and whether the schedule-independence of the terminal prediction holds under all practical noise schedules.
