## Problem

Existing PEFT-based continual learning methods either reuse previously learned parameters or randomly expand new ones, creating shared parameters between new and old tasks that cause interference. This interference prevents the model from achieving a good trade-off between stability (retaining old-task performance) and plasticity (adapting to new tasks), a gap that prior methods have not addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240956/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and outputs class labels, operating in the class-incremental, exemplar-free setting where task identities are unavailable at inference and no historical data can be retrieved. The pre-trained ViT-B/16 backbone processes each image, and a classifier head with a local cross-entropy loss (restricted to the current task's classes) produces the prediction. A key intermediate computation is the input matrix H_t of the current task, whose SVD determines the dimensionality reduction matrix B_t before any training begins.

## Method

InfLoRA expands a LoRA-like branch (matrices A_t and B_t) per task, proving that fine-tuning A_t is equivalent to fine-tuning the pre-trained weight W within the subspace spanned by the rows of B_t (Proposition 1). Unlike standard LoRA's random Gaussian initialization of B, InfLoRA designs B_t via SVD of the current task's input matrix projected onto the orthogonal complement of old-task gradient space (maintained by DualGPM), ensuring the update subspace is simultaneously aligned with the new task's gradient direction and orthogonal to all old-task gradients. During training, only A_t is updated while W, all prior branches, and B_t remain frozen; after each task, the branch is absorbed into W so the total expanded parameter count stays constant at (d_I + d_O)r. The method uses a local cross-entropy loss and is inserted into the key and value projections of all Transformer blocks.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (10 tasks), InfLoRA achieves 75.65% final accuracy (ACC10) and 80.82% averaged accuracy (ACC̄10), outperforming the strongest baseline LAE (71.70% / 76.71%) by over 4 points on both metrics. On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), InfLoRA reaches 91.70% ACC̄10, and on DomainNet (5 tasks) it achieves 79.57% ACC̄5, both leading all compared methods. The method also expands only (d_I + d_O)·r parameters per layer regardless of the number of tasks, matching the parameter budget of L2P and DualPrompt while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfLoRA outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and task count evaluated. On ImageNet-R (10 tasks), it leads LAE by 3.95 points in ACC10 and 4.11 points in ACC̄10, and surpasses C-LoRA by 3.76 points in ACC10. On CIFAR-100, InfLoRA's ACC̄10 of 91.70% edges out CODA-P (91.08%) and DualPrompt (90.06%), while on DomainNet it achieves 79.57% ACC̄5 versus CODA-P's 78.72% and DualPrompt's 77.71%. The gap widens with more tasks: at 20 tasks on ImageNet-R, InfLoRA leads the next-best method (LAE) by 4.03 points in ACC̄20. Notably, InfLoRA uses a comparable number of expanded parameters to L2P, DualPrompt, and LAE, yet consistently delivers 3–5 points higher accuracy, indicating that the interference-free subspace design is the key differentiator rather than parameter budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfLoRA extends the LoRA parameterization for continual learning by explicitly designing the low-rank subspace (via the dimensionality reduction matrix B_t) to be orthogonal to old-task gradients while lying within the new-task gradient space. It builds on DualGPM for maintaining orthonormal bases of the old-task gradient space and on the observation that ViT fine-tuning gradients lie in a low-dimensional subspace spanned by inputs. Unlike prior PEFT-based continual learning methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P, LAE, C-LoRA) that either reuse or randomly expand parameters without considering interference, InfLoRA provides a theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) that tuning A_t is equivalent to subspace-constrained fine-tuning of the full weight, and then leverages this to eliminate cross-task interference by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a principled, interference-free mechanism for PEFT in continual learning that addresses the stability-plasticity tradeoff at the subspace level rather than through regularization or memory buffers. The ablation confirming that both the new-task gradient alignment (N_t) and old-task orthogonality (M_t^perp) are necessary—neither alone suffices—validates the design. Limitations include reliance on DualGPM's approximation quality (which degrades over very long task sequences), the shrinking available subspace as tasks accumulate, and evaluation restricted to ViT-B/16 on three datasets. Open questions include whether the subspace design generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether the SVD-based projection can be made adaptive per-layer, and how the method behaves under extreme task counts where M_t^perp becomes very low-dimensional.
