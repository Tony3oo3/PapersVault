## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for vision-language models, such as TPT and DiffTPT, require computationally intensive prompt optimization via backpropagation on each test sample, taking over 12 and 34 hours respectively on ImageNet. This computational burden is fundamentally misaligned with the real-time demands of test-time adaptation in practical deployment scenarios, and no prior work has systematically addressed the efficiency gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240941/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled test images one at a time (batch size 1) with no access to any training data, using hand-crafted prompts with CLIP's text encoder to generate class embeddings. It produces a final class prediction by summing CLIP's zero-shot logits with positive and negative cache predictions, where the caches are updated incrementally as each new test sample arrives. The entire pipeline requires only matrix multiplications and entropy computations—no gradient computation or parameter updates.

## Method

TDA maintains two lightweight dynamic key-value caches: a positive cache that stores high-confidence pseudo labels (one-hot vectors) paired with their CLIP image features, and a negative cache that stores negative pseudo labels (vectors with −1 entries for classes whose probability exceeds a threshold p_l) from uncertain predictions. The positive cache operates as a per-class priority queue that progressively replaces higher-entropy entries with lower-entropy ones up to a fixed shot capacity k, while the negative cache admits only samples whose prediction entropy falls within [τ_l, τ_h] to avoid both high-noise and over-confident samples. At inference, the test feature is used as a query against both caches via Tip-Adapter's adaptation function A(z) = α·exp(−β(1−z)), and the final prediction is the sum of CLIP's zero-shot logits, the positive cache prediction, and the negative cache prediction. The negative cache specifically targets the absence of certain classes rather than the presence of one, making the system more robust to noisy pseudo labels without any backpropagation.

## Results

On the OOD benchmark, TDA achieves 63.89% OOD average accuracy with CLIP-ViT-B/16, outperforming TPT (60.81%) by 3.08% and DiffTPT (60.52%) by 3.37%. With CLIP-ResNet-50, TDA reaches 46.63% OOD average, surpassing TPT (43.89%) by 2.74% and DiffTPT (45.69%) by 0.94%. On the cross-domain benchmark, TDA (ViT-B/16) attains 67.53% average accuracy across 10 datasets, exceeding TPT (65.10%) and DiffTPT (65.47%). Critically, TDA completes ImageNet testing (50K images) in 16 minutes versus 12h 50min for TPT and 34h 45min for DiffTPT, while also improving accuracy over the CLIP baseline by +1.54%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDA outperforms all six baselines on both the OOD and cross-domain benchmarks. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline, TDA (ViT-B/16) gains +4.47% OOD average accuracy (63.89% vs 59.42%) and +2.94% on the cross-domain average (67.53% vs 64.59%). Among train-time adaptation methods, TDA surpasses CoOp (+4.61% OOD avg), CoCoOp (+3.98%), and Tip-Adapter (+3.62%) with ViT-B/16, though Tip-Adapter is only evaluated on the OOD benchmark since it cannot handle new classes. Against the two test-time adaptation baselines, TDA beats TPT by 3.08% OOD average and 2.43% cross-domain average (ViT-B/16), and beats DiffTPT by 3.37% OOD average and 2.06% cross-domain average. The efficiency gap is dramatic: TDA requires only 16 minutes to test 50K ImageNet images, compared to 12h 50min for TPT and 34h 45min for DiffTPT (excluding DiffTPT's additional diffusion-based image generation time), while simultaneously achieving higher accuracy than both.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDA directly extends Tip-Adapter's static key-value cache architecture by making the cache dynamic: instead of relying on pre-labeled few-shot samples, it progressively collects pseudo labels from an unlabeled test stream, enabling test-time adaptation without access to source data. It departs from the dominant test-time prompt tuning paradigm (TPT, DiffTPT) that requires iterative backpropagation to optimize a learnable prompt per sample, replacing it with a non-parametric, training-free cache update. The negative pseudo labeling mechanism draws on negative learning literature (NLNL, joint negative-positive learning) but applies it in a novel way—selecting negative labels only from uncertain predictions rather than all noisy labels—to complement the positive cache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gain (reducing test-time from ~13 hours to 16 minutes on ImageNet) makes test-time adaptation of vision-language models practically deployable in real-world streaming scenarios where per-sample optimization is infeasible. The combination of positive and negative caches provides a principled way to handle pseudo label noise without additional training, which is a common failure mode in self-training-based adaptation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large class spaces (beyond 1000), whether it extends to multi-modal or video tasks, and how performance degrades under severe distribution shifts where even high-confidence pseudo labels become unreliable.
