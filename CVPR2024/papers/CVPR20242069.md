## Problem

Existing talking-face generation methods require a source face image alongside audio, raising privacy concerns and preventing users from freely crafting diverse virtual portraits that match their own voice. Two fundamental challenges remain unsolved: (1) disentangling the entangled identity, content, and emotion cues from a single audio signal without relying on unreliable pseudo-pair cross-reconstruction, and (2) producing visually diverse yet temporally coherent videos from a single model without introducing separate offline components.

## Input / Output

The only required input is a single audio clip (16 kHz, converted to 80-band mel-spectrograms and MFCC sequences of length L=32); optional text prompts can specify audio-unrelated attributes such as beard, hairstyle, or pupil color. The output is a 512×512 talking-face video where the first frame is generated in a 'diverse mode' (free appearance) and all subsequent frames in a 'coherent mode' that preserves identity, background, and temporal continuity. No source face image is needed, though reference images may be supplied for geometry and semantic guidance.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, Progressive Audio Disentanglement (PAD) uses 3DMM coefficients as a structured prior to sequentially decouple identity (facial shape α and semantic embeddings via a Transformer encoder with InfoNCE contrastive loss), content (lip-specific expression coefficients supervised by a lip-reading expert), and emotion (global expression via an additional encoder-decoder), each stage conditioning on the previous stage's output. Second, Controllable Coherent Frame (CCF) generation keeps the LDM backbone frozen and injects conditions through three sequentially trained adapters: a Textual Inversion Adapter that maps identity/emotion vectors into CLIP token embeddings for cross-attention, a Spatial Conditional Adapter that fuses 3D mesh, identity, and adjacent-frame features (with the mouth region masked to prevent shortcut copying), and a Mask-guided Blending Adapter that composites the background in the VAE decoder using MODNet masks at 512 resolution. Autoregressive inference alternates between diverse mode (frame 1) and coherent mode (frames 2–H, using the previous frame as spatial reference), enabling long, temporally consistent videos from a single model.

## Results

On the talking-face generation benchmark (Table 2), the full model (Ours-E) achieves the best EACC (0.090), LMD (2.67), FID (28.37), PSNR (29.66), and SSIM (0.67) among all compared methods, with only Wav2Lip slightly ahead on Sync (5.93 vs. 5.91). In the audio-to-face comparison (Table 1), the method dramatically outperforms CMP, reducing FID from 228.05 to 25.78 and improving emotion cosine similarity from 0.23 to 0.76, while also gaining substantial margins on shape (0.75 vs. 0.60) and age (0.58 vs. 0.41) similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four talking-face baselines, Ours-E wins on five of six metrics: it achieves the lowest EACC (0.090 vs. 0.097 for EAMM, the next best), lowest LMD (2.67 vs. 2.71 for PC-AVS), lowest FID (28.37 vs. 31.62 for SadTalker), highest PSNR (29.66 vs. 29.55 for SadTalker), and highest SSIM (0.67 vs. 0.64 for SadTalker). Wav2Lip retains a narrow edge on Sync (5.93 vs. 5.91), which the authors attribute to its explicit synchronization-scoring supervision during training. In the audio-to-face setting, the method dominates CMP across every metric, with the most striking gap in FID (25.78 vs. 228.05) and emotion similarity (0.76 vs. 0.23), confirming that progressive disentanglement plus frozen LDMs far surpasses the single-stage geometry-only approach. Notably, the method operates in a fundamentally different paradigm—generating diverse faces from audio alone—whereas all talking-face baselines require a source face image as input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: audio-driven talking face generation (Wav2Lip, SadTalker, DiffTalk), audio-to-face reconstruction (CMP, Speech2Face), and controllable diffusion-based image synthesis (LDMs, T2I-Adapter, DreamBooth). It departs from talking face methods by eliminating the source face requirement, and from audio-to-face methods by producing temporally coherent video rather than static images. The key architectural departure is freezing the LDM backbone and injecting hierarchical conditions through three lightweight adapters (TIA, SCA, MBA) rather than training the full UNet, combined with a progressive 3DMM-based audio disentanglement (identity → content → emotion) that replaces the cross-reconstruction strategy used in prior disentanglement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical privacy concern (users don't want to provide real face images) and demonstrates that a single audio input suffices to generate diverse, audio-synchronized talking face videos, opening a path toward voice-only virtual avatar creation. The frozen-LDM-plus-adapters recipe (282.9M trainable params vs. 1.15G for full UNet training) is a practical efficiency win that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks. Open questions include scalability to long-duration videos, real-time inference speed, and whether the progressive disentanglement order generalizes to more complex multi-speaker or multi-emotion scenarios.
