## Problem

Inversion-based text-guided image editing with diffusion models suffers from three bottlenecks: the inversion process is time-consuming (up to 810s for StyleDiffusion), balancing consistency with editing faithfulness remains difficult even with optimization or dual-branch calibration, and existing methods are incompatible with efficient consistency sampling. The paper asks whether the inversion process can be eliminated entirely for editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241592/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of a pre-trained LDM (Stable Diffusion 1.4 or Latent Consistency Model), taking a source image encoded to latent z_0, a source prompt, a target prompt, and optional blend words that specify which tokens require local attention blending. The output is a single edited image decoded from the refined target latent z_0^tgt. The entire pipeline completes in under 3 seconds on a single A40 GPU (as few as 12 steps with LCM), compared to 50+ steps plus a separate inversion pass for baselines.

## Method

The key insight is that when the initial sample z_0 is known (as in editing), choosing the variance schedule σ_t = √(1−α_{t−1}) in the DDIM denoising step makes it identical in form to multi-step consistency sampling, yielding the Denoising Diffusion Consistent Model (DDCM). This 'Virtual Inversion' starts from random noise and directly computes the consistency noise ε^cons = (z_t − √α_t·z_0)/√(1−α_t) without any iterative inversion, guaranteeing exact reconstruction. For language-guided editing, the paper introduces Unified Attention Control (UAC), which unifies cross-attention control (for rigid changes like color/background) and mutual self-attention control (for non-rigid changes like pose/adding objects) through an additional layout branch that hosts structural composition. In the dual-branch sampling loop, InfEdit directly calibrates the predicted initial z_0^tgt using the difference between the consistency noise and the source-predicted noise, avoiding cumulative errors from calibrating intermediate z_t values.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, the best InfEdit variant (VI*+UAC with LCM backbone) achieves the highest PSNR (28.51), lowest LPIPS (47.58×10⁻³), lowest MSE (32.09×10⁻⁴), highest SSIM (85.66×10⁻²), and best CLIP similarity scores (25.03 whole / 22.22 edited) among all compared methods. It also runs in just 2.22 s with 12 sampling steps on a single A40 GPU, roughly an order of magnitude faster than optimization-based inversion baselines (e.g., Null-Text Inversion at 132 s inversion + 13 s forward, StyleDiffusion at 810 s inversion + 28 s forward). Even the SD-backbone variant (VI+P2P, 4.50 s, 32 steps) outperforms all inversion-based baselines on consistency metrics while eliminating the inversion phase entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfEdit (VI*+UAC) outperforms all six inversion-based baselines (DDIM, CycleD, NT, NP, StyleD, DI) on PSNR, LPIPS, MSE, SSIM, and both CLIP similarity scores simultaneously, while also being the fastest (2.22 s forward, 12 steps, no inversion). Against the closest competitor CycleD+P2P, InfEdit gains +0.26 PSNR, +1.35 CLIP Whole, and +1.35 CLIP Edited, though CycleD achieves a lower structure distance (6.06 vs 13.78) at the cost of weaker editing faithfulness. Compared to the optimization-heavy Null-Text Inversion, InfEdit eliminates the 132 s inversion phase entirely and improves LPIPS by 13.09×10⁻³ and SSIM by 0.95×10⁻². Against Direct Inversion (DI), InfEdit wins on PSNR (+1.29), LPIPS (−6.97×10⁻³), and SSIM (+0.90×10⁻²) while matching CLIP scores within 0.1. The SD-backbone variant (VI+P2P) already surpasses DDIM+P2P by +9.65 PSNR and −160.82 LPIPS, confirming that the inversion-free sampling itself provides substantial consistency gains independent of the attention control choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inversion-based editing paradigm (DDIM inversion, Null-Text Inversion, CycleDiffusion, Direct Inversion) by proving that when the initial sample z_0 is known—as in editing—the denoising step with a specific variance schedule σ_t = √(1−α_{t−1}) reduces to the same form as multi-step consistency sampling, eliminating the need for explicit inversion entirely. It builds on Consistency Models and Latent Consistency Models for the sampling framework, and on Prompt-to-Prompt (cross-attention) and MasaCtrl (mutual self-attention) for attention control, unifying both into a single tuning-free protocol (UAC) with an intermediate layout branch. The key conceptual departure is reframing editing as a forward-consistency problem rather than an inverse-then-forward problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the inversion bottleneck, InfEdit achieves sub-3-second editing on a single A40 GPU while remaining compatible with few-step LCM sampling, opening the door to real-time interactive editing applications. The DDCM proposition is a clean theoretical result that could influence how future editing methods are designed, potentially making the entire class of optimization-based inversion methods obsolete for the editing setting. Open questions include whether the virtual inversion approach extends to video editing or other conditioning modalities, whether the modest quality gap versus the best inversion-based methods (e.g., CycleDiffusion on structure distance) can be closed, and whether the UAC layout branch generalizes beyond the specific rigid/non-rigid taxonomy the authors define.
