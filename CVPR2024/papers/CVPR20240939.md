## Problem

Conventional cameras face an inherent trade-off at night: long exposure times needed for low light produce motion blur in dynamic scenes, and existing low-light enhancement methods cannot recover lost structural information. Event cameras offer microsecond temporal resolution and 120 dB dynamic range, but existing event-to-image reconstruction methods fail in real nighttime settings because they ignore two critical signal degradations: temporal trailing caused by reduced sensor cutoff frequency under low illumination, and spatially non-uniform event density under artificial lighting. Moreover, no high-quality paired dataset of low-light events and pixel-aligned ground-truth images existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams (polarity + timestamp per pixel) from a Prophesee EVK4 sensor (1280×720) captured under 0.5–1000 lux illumination, voxelized into B-bin tensors. It outputs grayscale intensity images (not color) at the same spatial resolution, and when run sequentially over a video, produces temporally consistent frame sequences. The RLED dataset provides 64,200 pixel-aligned image-event pairs collected via a co-axial system with an ND filter on the event camera, enabling supervised training with true low-light ground truth.

## Method

NER-Net is a U-shaped reconstruction network with two purpose-built modules addressing the two identified degradations. The Learnable Event Timestamps Calibration (LETC) module first uses a hand-crafted Event Trail Suppression (ETS) prior to identify and correct trailing events (consecutive same-polarity events with increasing inter-event intervals), then pre-trains a small CNN to learn timestamp weight redistribution end-to-end, producing sharp voxel grids before feeding the main network. The Non-uniform Illumination Aware Module (NIAM) stabilizes the spatially non-stationary event distribution using three sub-components: a Global Context Block (GCB) for long-range illumination-field dependencies, a Local Adaptation Gate (LAG) that modulates the ConvLSTM forget gate with event-density feedback to preserve weak-texture regions while suppressing noise, and a Spatiotemporal Aggregation Unit (SAU) that fuses hierarchical spatial features with long-term temporal memory via progressive upsampling. Training uses LPIPS perceptual loss plus a temporal consistency loss over 40-step sequences, with 160×160 patches on an A100 GPU for 200 epochs.

## Results

On the RLED dataset, NER-Net (trained on RLED) achieves MSE 0.011, SSIM 0.717, and LPIPS 0.309, outperforming the best competing method (E2VID+ trained on RLED: MSE 0.019, SSIM 0.688, LPIPS 0.393). On DSEC-night, NER-Net achieves LOE 1031.2 and SSIM 0.340, surpassing ET-Net trained on RLED (LOE 1227.6, SSIM 0.411). On MVSEC-night and VECtor-hdr, NER-Net similarly leads with LOE 1198.4/SSIM 0.205 and LOE 1139.8/SSIM 0.344 respectively. In the nighttime dynamic scene comparison (Table 3), NER-Net achieves the best LOE (92.81), NIQE (11.78), and user study score (6.02/10) against three low-light enhancement methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NER-Net outperforms all three event-based reconstruction baselines (E2VID+, ET-Net, DVS-Dark) across all four real-world datasets and all metrics when all methods are trained on RLED. On RLED, NER-Net reduces MSE by 42% (0.011 vs. 0.019) and LPIPS by 21% (0.309 vs. 0.393) over the strongest competitor E2VID+. On DSEC-night, NER-Net achieves the lowest LOE (1031.2 vs. 1165.1 for E2VID+), though ET-Net slightly edges it on SSIM (0.411 vs. 0.340), likely due to ET-Net's transformer-based global attention. On MVSEC-night and VECtor-hdr, NER-Net leads on all three metrics, with the largest margin on VECtor-hdr LOE (1139.8 vs. 1384.5 for E2VID+). In the separate nighttime dynamic scene experiment (Table 3), NER-Net also surpasses three frame-based low-light enhancement methods (KinD++, SCI, URetinex-Net) on LOE, NIQE, and user study scores, confirming that event-based reconstruction avoids the motion blur inherent to short-exposure conventional cameras.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based image reconstruction lineage (E2VID+, ET-Net, DVS-Dark) by identifying two nighttime-specific failure modes—temporal trailing due to reduced sensor cutoff frequency and spatial non-uniformity from artificial lighting—that prior methods implicitly ignore. The LETC module is directly motivated by Gehrig et al.'s event spike tensor (EST) representation, but repurposes it with a trail-suppression prior (ETS) as a pre-training signal rather than joint end-to-end learning. The NIAM module draws on GCNet's global context blocks and PredRNN's spatiotemporal memory, adapting them to handle the non-stationary event density caused by non-uniform illumination, a problem not addressed in prior event reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RLED dataset (64,200 pixel-aligned low-light event-image pairs spanning 0.5–1000 lux) fills a critical gap: no prior dataset provided high-quality ground truth for low-light events, forcing all previous methods to rely on simulation or daytime co-axial captures. The physical diagnosis linking photocurrent-dependent cutoff frequency to trailing events gives practitioners a mechanistic understanding of why sim-to-real transfer fails at night, beyond the usual 'simulation is imperfect' narrative. Open questions remain: the method fails below 0.5 lux where event density becomes too sparse, color reconstruction is unaddressed, and whether integrating frame-based camera input (as the authors suggest) could recover information lost in extreme darkness.
