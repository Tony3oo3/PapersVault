## Problem

Extracting physically-based rendering (PBR) materials from a single real-world image without knowledge of lighting, geometry, or viewpoint remains an open problem. Existing SVBRDF capture methods require multi-view setups, specialized hardware, or orthogonal close-up views, making them impractical for in-the-wild photographs. This paper formulates the novel task of extracting multiple distinct materials from arbitrary regions of a single image and provides an end-to-end solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242342/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a set of regions (user-drawn or produced by a segmenter such as SAM or Materialistic) as input, and outputs per-region SVBRDF maps (Albedo, Normal, Roughness) along with tileable RGB textures at arbitrary resolution (up to 1024px natively, higher via batch latent decoding with weighted blending). Unlike prior single-view decomposition that produces a single scene-wide albedo-shading split, Material Palette yields spatially varying BRDFs for each material region independently, directly usable in 3D rendering engines.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a Stable Diffusion model is finetuned via Dreambooth/LoRA on crops of each region to learn a concept token S* that encodes the material's fine-grained appearance; at inference, noise unrolling and Poisson seam-solving produce tileable textures at any resolution. Second, a multi-task CNN (UNet with ResNet-101 backbone) decomposes each generated texture into Albedo, Normal, and Roughness maps. To bridge the domain gap between synthetic SVBRDF libraries (AmbientCG) and diffusion-generated textures, the paper introduces TexSD (9,000 prompt-generated textures across 130 classes) and trains the decomposition network via unsupervised domain adaptation: pseudo-labels from the source-only model are used to jointly fine-tune on both the labeled source and the unlabeled target domain, with multi-view rendering losses under random illumination to enforce invariance.

## Results

On the SVBRDF decomposition task (Table 1), the proposed UDA method (ACG→SD) achieves the best MSE and SSIM across all three channels (Albedo, Normal, Roughness) on both PolyHaven and CGBookcase, in both In-Distribution and Out-of-Distribution settings. The relative improvement over the ACG source-only baseline is +5.87% on PH-ID, +2.62% on CGB-ID, and +5.24% on PH-OOD. On the end-to-end material extraction evaluation (Table 2), extracted materials using Materialistic segmentation achieve 50.89% CLIP top-1 and 86.82% top-5 zero-shot classification on ACG, surpassing the ACG ground-truth baseline (47.78%/85.88%), confirming that extracted materials retain coherent class-level characteristics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: Deep Materials [9] (an off-the-shelf single-image SVBRDF network trained on an in-house dataset) and SurfaceNet [55] (an adversarial SVBRDF estimation method adapted with the same architecture). Deep Materials is substantially outperformed across all settings, with MSE values roughly 3–10× higher (e.g., 0.264 vs. 0.069 on PH-ID Albedo), confirming that a domain-specific trained network is necessary. Against SurfaceNet, the proposed UDA method wins on PH-ID (+5.87% vs. +5.18% relative improvement) and PH-OOD (+5.24% vs. +3.94%), and critically, on CGB-ID where SurfaceNet actually degrades below the source-only baseline (−3.00%) while the proposed method still improves (+2.62%). This indicates that the TexSD-based pseudo-label adaptation generalizes more robustly to out-of-distribution material classes than the adversarial approach of SurfaceNet. The off-the-shelf Deep Materials baseline also serves as a sanity check: its poor performance underscores that generic SVBRDF networks cannot handle the domain shift to diffusion-generated textures without adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) single-image SVBRDF decomposition, which prior work (Deep Materials, SurfaceNet) addresses only under controlled close-up, orthogonal-view conditions; (2) texture extraction from natural images, which prior methods (scraping, quilting, GAN-based synthesis) perform without modeling light-material interaction; and (3) diffusion-based material generation (ControlMat, MatFuse), which the authors repurpose not for direct material synthesis but as a geometry/lighting disentangler via DreamBooth-style concept learning. The key conceptual departure is treating a finetuned diffusion model as a per-region 'material encoder' that produces tileable, lighting-free textures, then bridging the synthetic-to-diffusion domain gap with pseudo-label-based UDA rather than requiring matched-view training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formulating and solving the task of extracting multiple PBR materials from a single in-the-wild image without any prior knowledge of lighting, geometry, or viewpoint, this work removes the need for expensive multi-view or polarized capture apparatus, making physically-based material acquisition accessible from ordinary photographs. The practical demonstration of 3D scene editing with extracted materials (jaguar fur, brick, bamboo) shows immediate utility for content creators. However, the authors acknowledge that simple uniform materials cause concept collapse, shaded surfaces introduce color inconsistencies, and strong geometric distortion (perspective, lens effects) remains unaddressed—open problems that limit the method's robustness in unconstrained settings.
