## Problem

Existing synthetic image detectors (convolutional artifact detectors, CLIP-embedding classifiers) fail to generalize to current high-fidelity text-to-image models like DALL-E 3 and Imagen, even when retrained on better data. Moreover, prior evaluation protocols compare fakes against thematically and stylistically mismatched real images, masking whether a detector is truly generalizing or simply exploiting content/style biases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240963/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes a single RGB image (resized to 256px along the shortest side) and produces a binary fake/real logit. Beyond the raw image, the method internally derives two additional 512×512 RGB 'images' — a decoded DDIM noise map and a decoded DDIM reconstruction — by inverting the input through a frozen Stable Diffusion model conditioned on a BLIP-2-generated caption embedded via CLIP. These three images are concatenated channel-wise (9 channels total) and fed to a ResNet-50 classifier.

## Method

FakeInversion augments a standard CNN detector with text-conditioned DDIM inversion features extracted from a fixed, pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. Given an input image, it is encoded to latent space via SD's VAE, inverted to a noise map z_T via DDIM (conditioned on the CLIP embedding of a BLIP-2 caption), and then reconstructed to z_0 via DDIM sampling; both the noise map and reconstruction are decoded back to pixel space. A ResNet-50 trained from scratch on the 9-channel concatenation of [original, decoded noise map, decoded reconstruction] with binary cross-entropy loss learns to discriminate fakes from reals. The key insight is that these three signals together approximate a likelihood test under the SD distribution, enabling the detector to identify images that are unlikely under any diffusion model's learned distribution — a signal that transfers across unseen generators. The paper also introduces SynRIS, an evaluation protocol that pairs each fake image with a thematically and stylistically matched real image found via reverse image search, eliminating content/style bias from the benchmark.

## Results

FakeInversion achieves a mean AUCROC of 0.798 when trained on ProGAN+LSUN and 0.759 when trained on SD+LAION, outperforming all baselines by at least 4 percentage points on average. On the most challenging closed-source generators, the method reaches 0.807 AUCROC on Imagen (SD+LAION training) and 0.882 on Stable-Cascade (ProGAN+LSUN training). The method also remains robust to in-the-wild degradations (JPEG, blur, noise, crop), with AUCROC dropping by less than 0.02 in most cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FakeInversion outperforms all four baselines across both training configurations. Against UFD (CLIP-embedding-based, Ojha et al. 2023), the gap is largest under ProGAN+LSUN training (0.798 vs. 0.546 AUCROC, +25.2 pp) and remains substantial under SD+LAION training (0.759 vs. 0.661, +9.8 pp). Against DMDet (RGB-only with augmentations, Corvi et al. 2023), FakeInversion leads by +17.5 pp (ProGAN+LSUN) and +14.8 pp (SD+LAION). Against CNNDet (original GAN detector, Wang et al. 2020), the advantage is +29.4 pp and +6.2 pp respectively. Against DIRE (DDIM reconstruction residuals, Wang et al. 2023), FakeInversion leads by +30.5 pp on ProGAN+LSUN; DIRE is not reported for SD+LAION, but the paper's ablation using only reconstruction residuals (closest to DIRE) scores 0.650–0.677 AUCROC, well below the full method. Notably, on the academic benchmarks (DALL·E 2, Craiyon, LDM with mismatched real sets), UFD and DMDet perform competitively or even better, confirming that the RIS-based evaluation is harder and more discriminative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based fake image detection initiated by DIRE (Wang et al., ICCV 2023), which showed that DDIM reconstruction residuals carry discriminative signal. FakeInversion departs from DIRE by using the full internal state of a fixed Stable Diffusion model—the inverted latent noise map and the DDIM reconstruction—rather than just the image-space residual, and grounds this choice in a likelihood-estimation argument via the probability-flow ODE. It also builds on the broader artifact-detection lineage (CNNDet, DMDet) and CLIP-embedding-based detection (UFD), but replaces low-level convolutional traces or semantic embeddings with text-conditioned inversion features from a pre-trained diffusion model. The SynRIS evaluation protocol is a novel contribution that departs from prior benchmarks (which pair fakes with thematically unrelated real sets) by using reverse image search to enforce stylistic and thematic alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical need: as photorealistic text-to-image models are released behind closed APIs at increasing speed, detectors must generalize to unseen generators without retraining. The finding that prior SOTA methods (UFD, DMDet) degrade to near-random performance under thematically aligned evaluation reveals that much of their reported generalization was an artifact of distributional mismatch between real and fake evaluation sets—a concern for any deployment where fakes share content with real images. The SynRIS benchmark is likely to become a standard for evaluating GenAI detectors, and the observation that detection performance is 'far from saturated' on this harder benchmark signals substantial room for future work. Limitations include the computational cost of running DDIM inversion per image and the reliance on a fixed Stable Diffusion checkpoint, which may itself become outdated.
