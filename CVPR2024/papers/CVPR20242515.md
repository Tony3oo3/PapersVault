## Problem

Transformer-based super-resolution models rely on shifted rectangular windows for local self-attention, but this design suffers from boundary-level distortion (edge pixels have limited neighbor participation) and offers only a restricted set of unique shifting modes due to the isometric geometry of rectangles. These limitations constrain the model's ability to capture diverse spatial features and degrade reconstruction quality at window boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242515/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution RGB image of arbitrary spatial dimensions (H×W×C_in) and produces a high-resolution RGB image at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. During training, inputs are cropped into 64×64 patches from DIV2K and Flickr2K with geometric augmentation (flips and 90°/180°/270° rotations). The ×4 model is obtained by fine-tuning a pre-trained ×2 checkpoint, and the final upsampling is performed via a pixel-shuffle layer sandwiched between convolution layers.

## Method

CFAT introduces a triangular window attention mechanism that splits each M×M rectangular patch into four triangular sub-windows (upper, right, lower, left), enabling more unique shifting modes (0, 8, 16, 24) compared to the two available for rectangular windows. Triangular and rectangular window-based multi-head self-attention units are alternated in series within Dense-Hybrid Window Attention Blocks (D-HWAB), so that each window type mitigates the boundary distortion of the other. The body alternates three Dense Window Attention Blocks (DWAB) and three Sparse Window Attention Blocks (SWAB), each containing four D-HWAB or S-HWAB units with distinct shift sizes, plus a Channel-Wise Attention Block (CWAB) using depthwise-pointwise convolutions for global channel features. An Overlapping Cross-Fusion Attention Block (OCFAB) is appended at the end of each DWAB/SWAB to exploit overlapping spatial features via a sliding-window unfolding operation with configurable overlap ratio k. The full architecture uses 180 channels, 6 attention heads, 16-pixel window sizes, and is trained with L1 loss for 250K iterations using the ADAM optimizer with a stepwise learning-rate schedule.

## Results

CFAT achieves the best PSNR/SSIM across all five benchmark datasets (Set5, Set14, BSD100, Urban100, Manga109) at all three scale factors (×2, ×3, ×4). On the most challenging ×4 setting, CFAT reaches 28.17 dB / 0.7524 on BSD100, 33.19 dB / 0.9068 on Set5, and 28.11 dB / 0.8380 on Urban100, outperforming the previous best (HAT) by 0.14–0.17 dB. At ×2, the margin widens to 0.46 dB on Set5 (39.09 vs 38.63) and 0.56 dB on Urban100 (35.01 vs 34.45). The compact variant CFAT-S (144 channels) remains competitive with SwinIR and Swin2SR while using fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFAT outperforms all ten compared baselines (EDSR, HAN, SAN, IPT, SwinIR, Swin2SR, ACT, ART, EDT, HAT) on every dataset and scale factor. The closest competitor is HAT [6], which CFAT surpasses by 0.14–0.17 dB on ×4 (e.g., 28.17 vs 28.00 on BSD100) and by a larger 0.46–0.56 dB on ×2 (e.g., 39.09 vs 38.63 on Set5, 35.01 vs 34.45 on Urban100). Against the earlier SwinIR [25] and Swin2SR [8], the ×4 margin on BSD100 is 0.22–0.25 dB. The compact CFAT-S variant (144 channels) trails the full CFAT by ~0.5–1.2 dB but still matches or exceeds SwinIR and Swin2SR on most benchmarks. In terms of computational cost (Table 6), CFAT uses 22.07 M parameters and 90.59 GMac, slightly more parameters than HAT (20.8 M) but fewer multi-adds (90.59 vs 103.7 GMac) while delivering superior PSNR/SSIM.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFAT builds directly on the SwinIR lineage of shifted rectangular window attention and the HAT framework that added global channel attention and overlapping cross-fusion attention to the SR transformer. Its key departure is the introduction of a triangular window mechanism that operates in series with the rectangular window, motivated by the observation that rectangular windows suffer from boundary-level distortion and offer fewer unique shifting modes due to their isometric geometry. The architecture also inherits the dense/sparse attention duality from ART and the depthwise-pointwise convolution design for channel attention, but reorganizes these into a unified Dense/Sparse Window Attention Block structure where both window shapes appear in both dense and sparse configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The triangular window concept is a genuinely novel geometric primitive that, if it generalizes, could benefit any transformer-based CV task relying on rectangular window partitioning (segmentation, detection, restoration). However, the paper's evidence is confined to SR benchmarks, and the compact CFAT-s variant actually underperforms HAT by ~1 dB at ×3 and ×4 on Set5, raising questions about the robustness of the design at reduced capacity. Open questions include whether the triangular window's advantage persists at larger scales, whether the 0.7 dB gain over HAT justifies the additional architectural complexity in deployment, and whether the boundary-distortion argument holds for non-shifted or dilated window variants.
