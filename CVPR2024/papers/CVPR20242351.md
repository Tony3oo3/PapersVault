## Problem

Existing aesthetic QR code methods struggle to simultaneously satisfy user-defined visual customization and reliable scannability. Prior approaches either produce fixed-style outputs that cannot accommodate diverse user preferences, or rely on unstable generation pipelines requiring auxiliary control models and manual parameter tuning to maintain decoding robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242351/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (consumed by a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model to produce a 1024×1024 guidance image) and a message string encoded into a version-5 QR code (592×592 pixels, 37×37 modules of 16×16 pixels each). The output is a single RGB QR code image that preserves the semantic content and artistic style of the prompt while remaining decodable by standard mobile scanners at various sizes (3–7 cm) and angles (45°–90°).

## Method

Text2QR is a training-free, three-stage pipeline built on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. First, the QR Aesthetic Blueprint (QAB) module fuses the guidance image's luminance with the QR code structure via histogram polarization, module reorganization, and a novel Adaptive-Halftone blending step that dynamically adjusts each module's central block size to minimize decoding error while preserving image content; this blueprint drives a pre-trained ControlNet (QR-Monster) to condition the SD model. Second, the SD model produces an intermediate image that balances aesthetics with QR structure. Third, the Scannability Enhancing Latent Refinement (SELR) module iteratively optimizes the VAE latent code (400 Adam steps) under a composite objective combining a marker-region L2 loss, a module-based code loss (from ArtCoder's SSLayer), and a harmonizing loss computed as the L²-Wasserstein distance between VGG-19 feature maps of the refined output and the intermediate image, thereby enhancing scannability without degrading visual quality.

## Results

Text2QR achieves scanning success rates consistently exceeding 96% across all tested configurations (iPhone 14 Pro and Huawei P40, with native Scanner, TikTok, and WeChat apps, at 3 cm / 5 cm / 7 cm sizes and 45° / 90° angles), with the vast majority of entries at 100%. On aesthetic quality, the method outperforms all four baselines (QArt, Halftone QR, ArtCoder, Quick QR) in both a 24-subject user study (200 images) and the AesBench aesthetic scoring metric (0–100 scale), achieving the highest preference ratio and AesBench score across all evaluated aspects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text2QR is compared against four aesthetic QR code methods: ArtCoder [33], QArt [6], Halftone QR [5], and Quick QR [26]. On scanning robustness (Table 1), Text2QR maintains ≥96% success across all device/app/size/angle combinations, matching or exceeding ArtCoder's reported accuracy. On aesthetic quality, Text2QR wins in both the user study (highest preference ratio among 24 subjects over 200 images) and AesBench scoring. ArtCoder produces visible, undesired round spots from repaired modules that are visually distracting; Quick QR yields results inconsistent with the user's customized input image; QArt and Halftone QR are limited to predefined styles without text-guided personalization. The paper reports that Text2QR achieves superior performance across all evaluated aesthetic aspects, with modules nearly invisible and seamless integration with the user-specified guidance image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion + ControlNet paradigm for controllable image generation, applying it to the aesthetic QR code task that prior work (Halftone QR, QR Image, ArtCoder, QArt) addressed through image-to-image transformations, module reshuffling, or neural style transfer. The key departure from the closest prior method, QR-Monster [10], which also used ControlNet to modulate QR luminance, is the introduction of a QR Aesthetic Blueprint (QAB) module that generates a structured guidance image before diffusion, followed by a Scannability Enhancing Latent Refinement (SELR) stage that iteratively optimizes in VAE latent space with a multi-objective loss (marker, code, and Wasserstein-based harmonizing losses). The code loss mechanism is inherited from ArtCoder [33], while the adaptive halftone blending and histogram polarization in QAB draw on classical halftone QR code techniques [5] repurposed as a preprocessing step for diffusion guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing tension between user-defined aesthetic customization and scanning robustness in QR codes can be resolved through a two-stage diffusion pipeline, achieving near-perfect scanning success rates (≥96%) across diverse devices, sizes, and angles while producing visually rich, text-guided outputs. The QAB module's adaptive halftone blending offers a reusable technique for generating structurally-constrained guidance images for any ControlNet-based diffusion task, and the SELR latent refinement with L²-Wasserstein aesthetic preservation is a practical recipe for post-hoc constraint satisfaction in latent space. Limitations include the heavy multi-model pipeline (SD + ControlNet + VAE + VGG-19), the fixed QR version 5 evaluation, and the reliance on a pre-trained QR-specific ControlNet (QR-Monster), raising open questions about generalization to other barcode formats, real-time generation, and whether the approach scales to higher QR versions with more modules.
