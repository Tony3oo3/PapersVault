## Problem

Existing NeRF-based SLAM systems (e.g., ESLAM, Co-SLAM) rely on a single, fixed-capacity global radiance field that cannot scale to large indoor scenes or long video sequences, and their end-to-end pose networks accumulate significant drift over time. This results in degraded reconstruction quality and unreliable camera tracking in multi-room or extended environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240964/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes sequential RGB-D frames with known 3×3 camera intrinsics and produces per-frame camera poses (SE(3)), rendered colors, and an implicit TSDF field extractable as a 3D mesh via marching cubes. The tri-plane feature planes operate at 24 cm (coarse) and 6 cm (fine) resolution with 32 channels each, and the system runs in real-time (~0.14 s/frame on Replica, ~0.37 s/frame on ScanNet) on a single RTX 3090ti GPU.

## Method

PLGSLAM introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a progressive scene representation dynamically allocates new local tri-plane+MLP representations whenever the camera exits the current local bound, freezing prior representations and using inverse-distance-weighted fusion in overlap regions to maintain global consistency. Second, each local representation uses a joint parametric-coordinate encoding: two-scale tri-planes capture high-frequency geometric features while a one-blob MLP with positional encoding captures low-frequency coherence, reducing memory growth from O(L³) to O(L²) while enabling hole-filling in unobserved regions. Third, a local-to-global bundle adjustment maintains a global keyframe database and alternates between local BA (overlapping keyframes only) and global BA (randomly sampled historical keyframes), using both neural warping loss (differentiable rendering through the scene network) and SIFT-based reprojection error to suppress cumulative pose drift.

## Results

On the large-scale Apartment dataset (14.5m × 7.5m × 3.2m, 12,000+ images), PLGSLAM achieves the best reconstruction quality across all metrics: Depth L1 of 6.03 cm, Accuracy of 11.09 cm, Completion of 3.26 cm, and Completion Ratio of 85.36%, outperforming the closest competitor Co-SLAM (6.70 / 13.36 / 3.67 / 80.49%). For camera tracking, PLGSLAM achieves ATE Mean of 5.57 cm and ATE RMSE of 6.23 cm, beating Co-SLAM (6.18 / 6.89) and ESLAM (6.64 / 7.28), though NICE-SLAM reports slightly lower ATE (4.78 / 5.39) on this specific dataset. On the ScanNet long-sequence benchmark, PLGSLAM leads all methods on both reconstruction (Acc. 19.42 cm, Comp. 4.21 cm) and tracking (ATE Mean 6.12 cm, RMSE 6.77 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLGSLAM outperforms all five baselines on reconstruction metrics (Depth L1, Accuracy, Completion, Completion Ratio) across all three datasets. On the Apartment dataset, the closest competitor Co-SLAM is beaten by 10% on Depth L1 (6.03 vs 6.70 cm), 17% on Accuracy (11.09 vs 13.36 cm), and 4.9 points on Completion Ratio (85.4% vs 80.5%). ESLAM shows the largest gap, with PLGSLAM achieving 63% lower Depth L1 (6.03 vs 16.36 cm) and 13.7 points higher Completion Ratio. For camera tracking, PLGSLAM beats Co-SLAM and ESLAM on ATE across all datasets, but NICE-SLAM achieves slightly lower ATE on the Apartment dataset (4.78 vs 5.57 cm Mean), likely due to its simpler single-scene representation being less affected by the progressive switching overhead. On the ScanNet long-sequence benchmark, PLGSLAM dominates all methods on both reconstruction and tracking, with ATE RMSE of 6.77 cm vs 8.75 cm for Co-SLAM and 8.95 cm for ESLAM. iMAP and Vox-Fusion are consistently the weakest baselines, with iMAP achieving only 44.4% completion ratio on Apartment, indicating severe failure in large-scale scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLGSLAM sits at the intersection of two lines of NeRF-based SLAM work: the tri-plane SDF representation from ESLAM and the one-blob coordinate encoding from Co-SLAM, which it fuses into a joint parametric-coordinate architecture for local scene representation. Its key conceptual departure is from the single-global-representation paradigm used by iMAP, NICE-SLAM, ESLAM, and Co-SLAM, adopting instead a progressive, dynamically-allocated local representation scheme inspired by Mega-NeRF and LocalRF but made compatible with concurrent pose optimization. The local-to-global bundle adjustment further departs from purely end-to-end pose estimation by reintroducing traditional keyframe-based BA (with SIFT reprojection) alongside neural warping, bridging classical SLAM robustness with differentiable rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck for NeRF-SLAM: the inability of fixed-capacity global representations to scale to multi-room or long-sequence indoor scenes without accumulating pose drift. The progressive local representation and local-to-global BA offer a concrete, implementable path toward scalable neural SLAM, and the O(L²) memory growth (vs. O(L³) for voxel grids) is a meaningful efficiency gain. However, the system still requires RGB-D input, is validated primarily on indoor scenes up to ~14.5 m, and the progressive mechanism introduces bookkeeping complexity (freezing, re-activating, IDW-fusing local representations) that may complicate deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, and whether the hybrid traditional+neural BA can be further simplified or made fully differentiable.
