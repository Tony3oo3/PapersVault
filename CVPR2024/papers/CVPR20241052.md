## Problem

Existing event-camera object detection models based on RNN or Transformer architectures (e.g., RVT, GET) suffer two critical limitations: they train slowly due to sequential recurrent computation, and they degrade severely (20+ mAP) when deployed at inference frequencies higher than the training frequency. No prior method incorporates a learnable timescale parameter that would allow a single trained model to generalize across arbitrary sampling rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241052/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Events are binned into dense tensor representations over 50 ms windows (20 Hz sampling) split into T=10 discrete time bins, forming a temporal sequence fed into a 4-stage hierarchical backbone. The model outputs bounding boxes with class labels (cars, pedestrians, two-wheelers) via a YOLOX detection head. Crucially, at inference the SSM timescale parameter is rescaled by the ratio of target to training frequency, enabling deployment at 40–200 Hz without retraining, whereas RNN-based baselines require separate training runs per frequency.

## Method

The SSM-ViT block replaces the RNN in RVT's 4-stage hierarchical ViT backbone with a structured state-space model (S4, S4D, or S5) that operates as a parallelizable CNN during training (via unrolling or parallel scans) and as an efficient RNN at inference, yielding 33% faster training. Each stage combines a convolution, local window self-attention (Block-SA), dilated global attention (Grid-SA), and the SSM layer, with SSM states carried across timesteps for temporal memory. To counteract aliasing at higher frequencies, two strategies are introduced: (1) frequency-selective masking that zeros C-matrix entries for states whose effective frequency exceeds α/2 (α=0.5 optimal), and (2) an H₂-norm loss term that suppresses the continuous-time transfer function's magnitude beyond a chosen cutoff ω_min. The learnable timescale parameter in the SSM discretization is the key mechanism enabling arbitrary-frequency deployment from a single training run.

## Results

On the Gen1 dataset, S5-ViT-B achieves 47.4 mAP and S4D-ViT-B achieves 46.2 mAP, while on the 1 Mpx dataset they reach 47.2 and 46.8 mAP respectively, with inference times of 8.16 ms and 9.40 ms (Gen1) and 9.57 ms and 10.9 ms (1 Mpx). The headline advantage is in frequency generalization: SSM-based models suffer an average performance drop of only 3.31 mAP when tested at higher frequencies (40–200 Hz) than the 20 Hz training frequency, compared to 21.25 mAP for RVT and 24.53 mAP for GET. Training is 33% faster than the RVT baseline, and S5-ViT-B uses only 18.2M parameters versus 21.9M for GET-T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the standard 20 Hz evaluation, S5-ViT-B (47.4 Gen1 / 47.2 1 Mpx) matches RVT-B (47.2 / 47.4) within 0.2 mAP while being 33% faster to train and slightly faster at inference (8.16 ms vs 10.2 ms on Gen1). S5-ViT-B trails GET-T (47.9 / 48.4) by 0.5 and 1.2 mAP respectively, and ERGO-12 leads on Gen1 (50.4) but S5-ViT-B surpasses it on 1 Mpx (47.2 vs 40.6). The decisive advantage appears at higher frequencies: at 200 Hz, S5 retains 39.84 mAP on Gen1 (3.94 mAP drop) whereas RVT collapses to 8.35 (26.14 drop) and GET to 5.35 (29.25 drop). On 1 Mpx at 200 Hz, S5 holds 39.70 mAP (2.68 drop) versus RVT's 18.15 (16.36 drop) and GET's 15.44 (19.81 drop). Among older baselines (Asynet, AEGNN, Spiking DenseNet, Inception+SSD, RRC-Events, MatrixLSTM, YOLOv3Events, RED), S5-ViT-B outperforms all by at least 7 mAP on Gen1 and 4 mAP on 1 Mpx.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the RVT architecture (Gehrig & Scaramuzza, CVPR 2023) by replacing its LSTM-based recurrent memory with structured state-space model layers (S4, S4D, S5), while retaining the 4-stage hierarchical ViT backbone and event tensor representation. The conceptual leap is that SSMs are Linear Time-Invariant continuous-time systems whose discretization step is a learnable parameter, enabling inference at arbitrary frequencies without retraining—something RNNs fundamentally cannot do. The two aliasing-mitigation strategies (frequency-selective masking and H₂-norm regularization) are novel contributions not present in prior SSM literature, which had not been applied to high-dimensional visual data with significant temporal resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for event-camera deployment: a single trained model can operate at 20 Hz through 200 Hz with only ~3 mAP degradation, eliminating the need for frequency-specific retraining cycles that RNN and Transformer baselines require. The finding that a linear SSM matches the performance of a non-linear LSTM in this setting suggests that the added complexity of gated recurrent units may be unnecessary for event-based temporal aggregation. Open questions include whether SSMs can fully replace attention (the paper still uses Block-SA and Grid-SA), whether the aliasing strategies generalize to other event-based tasks like segmentation or tracking, and whether the approach scales to even higher resolutions or longer temporal contexts.
