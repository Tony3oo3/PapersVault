## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF methods with blend skinning (LBS, DBS) are designed for non-rigid deformable subjects like humans and animals, where movable parts are distinctly separated. They fail on general piece-wise rigid articulated objects (scissors, staplers, laptops) whose rigid components sit adjacent in their resting poses, producing bending artifacts, surface tearing, and corrupted motion. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a deformation model that enforces per-part rigidity while preserving smooth joint deformation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single casually captured monocular video (no controlled camera motion) plus approximately 4 sparse camera pose annotations per video for initialization. Preprocessing extracts object silhouettes via Track Anything, optical flow via VCN-robust, and pixel features via DINOv2. The output is a canonical NeRF encoding shape and texture, a set of dual-quaternion bone transforms parameterizing articulation, and a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes on a 128³ grid. No 3D ground truth or complete multi-view coverage is required.

## Method

REACTO models shape and appearance with a canonical NeRF (SDF-based density + color MLP with appearance code) and introduces Quasi-Rigid Blend Skinning (QRBS) as the deformation model. Three key design choices distinguish QRBS from prior DBS: (1) the rig is defined on bones placed at rigid-part centroids rather than at joints, so each component is strongly associated with a single bone; (2) skinning weights are made quasi-sparse via a temperature factor γ in the softmax, minimizing cross-bone influence on rigid parts while retaining flexibility at joints; (3) a geodesic point assignment algorithm (using exact geodesic distances on a marching-cubes mesh) correctly labels each point as belonging to a specific bone or to a joint, and a sparse skinning loss penalizes weights on non-assigned bones. Articulation is represented by per-bone dual quaternions predicted by an MLP from a latent pose code, and the full model is optimized end-to-end via volume rendering with RGB, mask, optical flow, feature, sparse-skinning, and regularization losses.

## Results

On the PartNet-Mobility synthetic dataset, REACTO achieves the best Chamfer Distance and F-scores across all three evaluated categories (USB, stapler, scissors). For USB, REACTO reaches CD 15.3 and F(10%) 78.6, outperforming the next-best MoDA (CD 17.1, F(10%) 74.9). For stapler, REACTO achieves CD 14.3 and F(10%) 75.5, surpassing PPR (CD 16.8, F(10%) 67.5) by a notable margin. For scissors, REACTO attains CD 14.0 and F(10%) 78.2, edging out MoDA (CD 14.8, F(10%) 77.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REACTO outperforms all three main baselines (BANMo, MoDA, PPR) on every metric across all three object categories. Against BANMo (linear blend skinning), REACTO reduces CD by 3.3–5.9 and improves F(10%) by 11.8–17.7 points, with the largest gap on stapler where BANMo struggles most (F(10%) 57.8 vs. 75.5). Against MoDA (dual quaternion blend skinning), REACTO consistently leads, with the most pronounced advantage on USB (CD 15.3 vs. 17.1) and stapler (F(10%) 75.5 vs. 64.2). Against PPR (dual quaternion with skin losses), REACTO achieves the largest F(10%) gain on stapler (75.5 vs. 67.5, +8.0) and USB (78.6 vs. 65.7, +12.9), while the scissors gap is smaller (78.2 vs. 71.4). In the ablation (Table 2), REACTO also beats the displacement field from NASAM and Real-NVP from CaDeX on all metrics, confirming that quasi-rigid blend skinning is a more suitable deformation model for piece-wise rigid articulated objects than generic displacement or invertible network approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the canonical NeRF + deformation model + volume rendering pipeline established by BANMo and MoDA, which were designed for humans, animals, or non-rigid deformable subjects. The key departure is targeting piece-wise rigid general articulated objects (scissors, staplers, laptops), where existing blend skinning approaches (LBS in BANMo, DBS in MoDA/PPR) produce bending artifacts, surface tearing, and corrupted motion. The proposed Quasi-Rigid Blend Skinning redefines the rig on bones rather than joints, introduces quasi-sparse skinning weights to enforce per-part rigidity, and uses geodesic distance on the evolving mesh for point-to-bone assignment—addressing the fundamental mismatch between smooth biological deformations and the discrete rigid-component structure of everyday objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a practical gap: dynamic NeRF methods that work well for humans and animals fail on the diverse range of piece-wise rigid objects encountered in robotics, AR/VR, and 3D content creation. The geodesic point assignment and bone-based rigging are simple but effective design choices that could be adopted by other deformation-modeling pipelines. A key limitation is that casual videos provide only partial views, degrading reconstruction on unseen sides; the method also requires per-object optimization (4000 iterations) and a small number of manual camera pose annotations, limiting scalability to large collections. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to objects with more than a handful of rigid components or to objects with continuous (non-piecewise) articulation.
