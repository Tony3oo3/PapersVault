## Problem

Regression-based human pose estimation methods are far more efficient than heatmap-based ones (e.g., 1/20000 FLOPs for the head), but all existing regression approaches are designed exclusively for static images and ignore temporal dependencies between video frames. When applied to video, they suffer substantial performance drops under occlusion, motion blur, and defocus. This paper presents the first regression-based framework for multi-person pose estimation in video sequences.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a top-down pipeline: a person detector identifies bounding boxes in the key frame, each box is expanded by 25% to crop the same individual across a temporal window of 2T+1 consecutive frames (T=1 or T=2 in practice). Input resolution is 384×288 for HRNet-W48 and 256×192 for other backbones. The output is a set of n=15 continuous 2D joint coordinates per person, regressed directly by a lightweight joint-wise FC head (0.02 GFLOPs) rather than decoded from high-resolution heatmaps.

## Method

DSTA decouples spatial and temporal modeling based on the observation that adjacent joints share spatial structure while each joint's temporal trajectory is largely independent. A Joint-centric Feature Decoder (JFD) converts the backbone's global feature map into n per-joint feature tokens via a single FC layer. The Space-Time Decoupling (STD) module then applies a local-awareness attention: the TD branch runs self-attention along the temporal axis for each joint independently across the 2T+1 frames, while the SD branch runs self-attention within spatially grouped joints (K=5 groups by body-part semantics) in the current frame only. The two updated tokens are concatenated and passed through a joint-wise FC network to regress coordinates. This decoupled attention reduces complexity from O(n²(2T+1)²) to O(n(2T+1)² + n²/K), yielding roughly a 45× speedup over global spatiotemporal attention. The entire network is trained end-to-end with the residual log-likelihood estimation (RLE) loss extended to the video setting.

## Results

On PoseTrack2017 validation, DSTA achieves 84.6 mAP with HRNet-W48 and 85.6 mAP with ViT-H, surpassing the best heatmap-based video method FAMI-Pose (84.8 mAP) by 0.8 points. Compared to the strongest image-based regression baseline Poseur, DSTA delivers an 8.9 mAP gain (79.3 → 84.6) with HRNet-W48. The regression head requires only 0.02 GFLOPs versus 11.0 GFLOPs for DCPose's heatmap head, while at 64×64 input resolution DSTA outperforms DCPose by 20.3 mAP (55.4 vs. 35.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against image-based regression baselines, DSTA substantially outperforms both RLE (75.7 → 84.6, +8.9 mAP) and Poseur (79.3 → 84.6, +5.3 mAP) with HRNet-W48 on PoseTrack2017, confirming that temporal cues are critical for video pose estimation. Among heatmap-based video methods, DSTA (HRNet-W48, 84.6 mAP) surpasses DCPose (82.8) by 1.8 points and DetTrack (83.8) by 0.8 points, while trailing FAMI-Pose (84.8) by a narrow 0.2 margin; however, with the ViT-H backbone DSTA reaches 85.6 mAP, exceeding FAMI-Pose by 0.8 points. In terms of efficiency, DSTA's regression head costs only 0.02 GFLOPs compared to 11.0 GFLOPs for DCPose and 156.7 GFLOPs for PoseWarper, a reduction of over 550×. At low input resolutions (64×64), DSTA maintains 55.4 mAP versus DCPose's 35.1 mAP, a 20.3-point gap that highlights the fragility of heatmap-based methods under resolution constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regression-based pose estimation line initiated by RLE [18] and Poseur [23], which demonstrated that direct coordinate regression can match heatmap-based accuracy on static images. The key departure is temporal: prior regression methods ignore inter-frame dependencies, while prior video-based methods (DCPose, FAMI-Pose, PoseWarper) rely on expensive heatmap representations. DSTA bridges this gap by introducing a joint-centric token representation (JFD) and a decoupled space-time attention mechanism that models spatial joint-to-joint structure and temporal joint-over-time trajectories separately, rather than conflating them in a single global self-attention over all joints across all frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first regression-based framework for multi-frame human pose estimation, DSTA opens a path toward real-time video pose estimation on edge devices, where the 1/550 head compute reduction (0.02 vs. 11.0 GFLOPs) relative to heatmap-based DCPose is practically significant. The decoupling insight—that each joint's temporal trajectory is largely independent of other joints'—provides a useful inductive bias that could inform future spatiotemporal modeling in other articulated-object tasks. Open questions include whether the top-down paradigm (requiring a separate person detector) limits end-to-end efficiency, whether the small temporal span (T=1–2) is sufficient for fast-motion scenarios, and whether the approach extends naturally to 3D pose or longer video sequences.
