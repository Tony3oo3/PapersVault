## Problem

Current spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) methods rely exclusively on the text query as the localization cue, which degrades in complex scenes containing distractors or severe appearance variations because the text is insufficient to distinguish the target. The paper asks whether visual information can be mined directly from the video itself to provide supplementary guidance for localization, without requiring additional external inputs or longer text descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240605/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (64 uniformly sampled frames resized to a short side of 420) and a free-form text query (up to 30 tokens) as input. It produces a spatio-temporal tube consisting of per-frame bounding boxes (center, width, height) and temporal start/end timestamps determined by the maximum joint start-end probability. The video is consumed through two parallel backbones—ResNet-101 for appearance features (2048-dim) and VidSwin-tiny for motion features (768-dim)—while the text is encoded by RoBERTa-base (768-dim), all projected to a shared 256-dim hidden space.

## Method

CG-STVG adopts a DETR-inspired encoder-decoder with a multimodal self-attention encoder and a 6-stage cascaded context-guided decoder, where each stage contains a spatial-decoding block (SDB) and a temporal-decoding block (TDB). The key mechanism is a progressive instance context loop: at each decoding stage, the spatial query features are passed through an Instance Context Generation (ICG) module that estimates foreground regions via a 3-layer MLP and extracts appearance and motion context using RoIAlign; this context is then refined by an Instance Context Refinement (ICR) module applying a two-level temporal-spatial joint filter (temporal confidence from the TDB output, spatial confidence from IoU-based MLP scores) to suppress irrelevant features. The refined instance context from stage k−1 is injected as a cross-attention guidance in the SDB of stage k, enhancing target-awareness in the spatial queries, which in turn produces better context for the next stage. Notably, instance context is applied only to the spatial branch, as ablation shows it causes slight temporal boundary blur when used in the TDB. Training is fully supervised with KL divergence, binary cross-entropy, smooth L1, and IoU losses across all decoding stages.

## Results

On HCSTVG-v1, CG-STVG achieves 52.8 m_tIoU, 38.4 m_vIoU, 61.5 vIoU@0.3, and 36.3 vIoU@0.5, setting new state-of-the-art on all four metrics. On HCSTVG-v2 (validation), it reaches 60.0 m_tIoU and 39.5 m_vIoU. On VidSTG, CG-STVG attains the best scores on all 8 metrics across both declarative (51.4 m_tIoU, 34.0 m_vIoU) and interrogative (49.9 m_tIoU, 29.0 m_vIoU) sentence types. The most notable gain is in vIoU@0.5, where CG-STVG improves by 4.0 points over its own baseline, indicating the instance context is particularly effective at refining low-IoU bounding boxes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-STVG outperforms all compared methods on HCSTVG-v1 across all four metrics. Against the strongest prior method STCAT [16], it gains +3.4 m_tIoU (52.8 vs 49.4) and +3.3 m_vIoU (38.4 vs 35.1). Against CSDVL [21] (CVPR 2023, winner of the 4th Person in Context Challenge), it improves m_vIoU by 1.5 points (38.4 vs 36.9) and vIoU@0.5 by 1.5 points (36.3 vs 34.8). On HCSTVG-v2, CG-STVG surpasses CSDVL by +1.9 m_tIoU (60.0 vs 58.1) and +0.8 m_vIoU (39.5 vs 38.7). On VidSTG, CG-STVG achieves the best results on all 8 metrics for both declarative and interrogative sentences, beating STCAT [16] by +0.6 m_tIoU and +0.9 m_vIoU on declarative sentences, and by +0.2 m_tIoU and +0.8 m_vIoU on interrogative sentences. The consistent gains over the ablated baseline (without ICG/ICR) of +2.4 m_tIoU and +4.0 vIoU@0.5 on HCSTVG-v1 confirm that the instance context guidance is the primary source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

CG-STVG builds directly on the DETR encoder-decoder paradigm, adapting its query-based iterative decoding to the spatio-temporal video grounding setting with separate spatial and temporal decoding blocks. It departs from all prior one-stage STVG methods (TubeDETR, STCAT, CSDVL) that rely exclusively on the text query as the localization cue, instead mining discriminative visual context from the video itself via RoIAlign-based feature extraction and a two-level temporal-spatial filtering mechanism. The multimodal encoder fuses ResNet-101 appearance features, VidSwin motion features, and RoBERTa text embeddings, initialized from MDETR weights, following the standard recipe in recent STVG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of text-only grounding—degradation under distractors and appearance variation—without requiring query reconstruction or external visual inputs, making the approach broadly applicable. The progressive context learning across decoder stages (where each stage's context refines the next) is a clean architectural idea that could transfer to other iterative localization tasks. However, the method introduces two manually tuned thresholds (θ^t=0.7, θ^s=0.8) and adds RoIAlign plus filtering overhead at every decoding stage, raising questions about scalability to longer videos and whether the thresholds can be learned end-to-end. The gains, while consistent across three benchmarks, are modest (1–4 points), and the approach has not been tested on more diverse or larger-scale video grounding settings.
