## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP learn 'bag-of-words' representations that fail to capture fine-grained compositional structure such as relations, attributes, and actions, because standard image-text contrastive learning on noisy web-crawled captions only requires distinguishing markedly different pairs. Existing hard-negative approaches (e.g., NegCLIP) add a single swapped-word negative but treat it as just another random negative in the contrastive denominator, failing to explicitly enforce fine-grained semantic discrimination between the positive and its near-identical counterparts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241827/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pretrained VLM (CLIP ViT/32-B or X-VLM-16M) on image-caption pairs from COCO (or COCO+CC3M+Visual Genome). For each caption, four hard negatives are automatically generated via spaCy POS parsing and RoBERTa infill: relation-swap (interchange two nouns), attribute/action/object replacement (mask and refill one adjective, verb, or noun). The model outputs cosine similarity scores between the image embedding and each caption embedding, which are used to rank the true caption above all four hard negatives in a zero-shot retrieval evaluation.

## Method

The paper extends the standard image-text contrastive loss (ITC) with two additional losses applied to the four generated hard negatives. First, an Intra-Modal Contrast (IMC) loss pushes the positive caption's text embedding away from all four hard-negative text embeddings, forcing the text encoder to encode nuanced semantic differences. Second, a Cross-Modal Rank (CMR) loss, built on a hinge formulation, enforces that the image–positive similarity exceeds the image–hard-negative similarity by at least a type-specific threshold Th_k. The threshold is adaptive: at each training step it is set to the batch-average similarity gap between true and hard-negative pairs from the previous step, creating a curriculum that progressively increases difficulty as the model improves. A per-type upper bound (u=10) prevents training collapse on relation-swap negatives, which produce grammatically implausible sentences and would otherwise drive the threshold unboundedly. The final loss is L = L_itc(hn) + α·L_imc + β·L_cmr with α=0.2, β=0.4, requiring no extra parameters or annotations.

## Results

On the ARO benchmark, CE-CLIP+ (151M params) achieves 83.6% on Relation and 77.1% on Attribute, improving over base CLIP by +24.3% and +14.2% respectively. On VALSE, CE-CLIP+ reaches 76.7% average accuracy (+11.4% over CLIP), and on VL-CheckList it scores 78.4% (+9.2%). On SugarCrepe, CE-CLIP+ attains 89.3% average (REPLACE), 78.0% (SWAP), and 94.4% (ADD), outperforming all VLM baselines. The method also yields modest gains on the already-strong X-VLM: +0.5% on ARO-Relation, +2.5% on ARO-Attribute, and +1.3% on VALSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CE-CLIP+ (151M) achieves the best ARO-Relation score (83.6%), surpassing NegCLIP (80.2%), CLIP-SVLC (80.6%), DAC-LLM (81.3%), and X-VLM-coco (73.4%) by 3.4–10.2 points. On VALSE, CE-CLIP+ leads all baselines at 76.7%, outperforming NegCLIP (71.6%) by 5.1 points and BLIP (70.0%) by 6.7 points. On VL-CheckList, CE-CLIP+ scores 78.4%, beating NegCLIP (73.4%), CLIP-SVLC (74.2%), and BLIP2 (77.8%). On SugarCrepe, CE-CLIP+ achieves 89.3% (REPLACE), 78.0% (SWAP), and 94.4% (ADD), exceeding NegCLIP (85.0/75.3/85.8) and all generative VLMs. The one area where CE-CLIP+ does not lead is ARO-Attribute (77.1%), where X-VLM-coco (86.8%) and BLIP (88.0%) are stronger, likely due to their richer pretraining data or multi-grained alignment objectives. Applying the method to X-VLM yields smaller but consistent gains (+0.5% to +2.5%), confirming the approach's generality across model architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hard-negative-augmented contrastive learning line initiated by NegCLIP [70] and SLVC [12], which generate a single swapped-word negative and fold it into the standard image-text contrastive loss as an additional random negative. The key departure is twofold: (1) four structured hard negatives (relation, attribute, action, object) are generated via POS parsing and RoBERTa infilling rather than random word swapping, and (2) two dedicated losses — intra-modal contrast between the positive and hard-negative captions, and cross-modal rank with an adaptive hinge threshold — explicitly model the partial-correctness structure of hard negatives instead of treating them as uniformly wrong. The method is applied as a parameter-free fine-tuning on top of CLIP [52] and X-VLM [71], positioning it as a lightweight complement to annotation-heavy approaches like scene-graph-guided training (SGVL, MosaiCLIP) or segmentation-based caption densification (DAC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple loss modification — no extra parameters, no scene graphs, no segmentation models — can close a large gap in compositional understanding (up to 24% on ARO-Relation), and that scaling up CLIP to ViT-G/14 on LAION-2B still fails to match the base-sized CE-CLIP on VL-CheckList, challenging the assumption that scale alone resolves compositionality. Limitations include only modest gains on the already-strong X-VLM (suggesting the method is most beneficial for models lacking fine-grained pre-training alignment) and a small drop in image classification accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger pre-training pipelines, whether additional hard-negative types (e.g., numerical, spatial) yield further gains, and whether the adaptive threshold strategy generalizes beyond the four POS-based perturbation types explored here.
