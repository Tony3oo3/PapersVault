## Problem

Prior RIR estimation methods are either audio-only, which approximates late reverberation as decaying filtered noise due to its noise-like structure, or visual-only, which lacks sufficient information such as 3D geometry and material absorption properties. No existing approach combines audio and visual cues to estimate the full RIR, leaving a significant gap in accurately capturing both early and late reverberation components from real-world reverberant speech.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240374/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 14400-sample reverberant speech segment, a panoramic RGB image of the source environment, and a 3-channel Geo-Mat feature map (constructed from frequency-dependent absorption coefficients at 125/500 Hz and 2000/8000 Hz plus a monocular depth map) as input. It produces a 4096-sample estimated RIR and dereverberated speech. At inference, CRIP retrieves a matching RIR from a synthetic datastore and replaces the late components (samples 2000–4000) of the estimated RIR to improve late reverberation fidelity.

## Method

AV-RIR employs a neural codec-based dual-branch architecture: a shared 1D CNN reverberant speech encoder feeds two parallel branches, where the RIR branch fuses S2IR-GAN-adapted audio features with ResNet-18 encodings of the Geo-Mat map, and the speech branch fuses audio features with ResNet-18 encodings of the panoramic image. Both branches pass through a 64-layer Residual Vector Quantizer (codebook size 8192, ~59 Kbps) before decoding via a modified SoundStream decoder (RIR) and HiFi-GAN vocoder (speech). Training uses multi-task learning with MSE loss for RIR, multi-resolution Mel-spectrogram and STFT losses for dereverberation, and adversarial losses from MPD/MSD discriminators. A key insight is that solving dereverberation as an auxiliary task forces the network to learn the decomposition of reverberant speech into anechoic speech and RIR, improving both tasks. CRIP, a CLIP-style contrastive model using HorizonNet (image) and FAST-RIR discriminator (RIR) encoders, is pre-trained to retrieve acoustically matching RIRs from a datastore, filling the late-reverberation gap at inference.

## Results

On the SoundSpaces dataset, AV-RIR achieves the best RIR estimation across all metrics: T60 error of 40.2 ms, DRR error of 1.76 dB, EDT error of 62.1 ms, EMSE of 82×10⁻⁵, and LMSE of 6×10⁻⁵, outperforming the best audio-only baseline S2IR-GAN by 36%, 42%, 63%, 89%, and 98% respectively. For speech dereverberation, AV-RIR achieves WER of 4.17% and EER of 2.02% on SoundSpaces, and RTE of 0.042 s on the real-world AVSpeech dataset, outperforming the SOTA audio-visual method AdVerb by 35% on EER and 60% on RTE. In perceptual evaluation, 56%–79% of participants rated AV-RIR-generated speech as closest to ground truth across six scenes of varying complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-RIR decisively outperforms all audio-only RIR estimation baselines: S2IR-GAN (T60=63.1 ms, DRR=3.04 dB) and FiNS (T60=87.7 ms, DRR=3.30 dB), with the largest gap on late reverberation (LMSE: 6 vs. 310 for S2IR-GAN, a 98% improvement). Against visual-only methods, AV-RIR dominates Image2Reverb (T60=131.7 ms) and FAST-RIR++ (T60=126.4 ms) by over 3× on T60 error, confirming that audio cues are essential for accurate RIR estimation. The CRIP-only retrieval baseline (T60=118.9 ms) shows that image-to-RIR retrieval alone cannot capture early components, validating the need for the full audio-visual pipeline. For speech dereverberation, AV-RIR (WER=4.17%, EER=2.02%, RTE=0.042 s) outperforms the SOTA audio-visual method AdVerb (WER=3.54%, EER=3.11%, RTE=0.101 s) on EER by 35% and RTE by 60%, while AdVerb retains a slight edge on WER (3.54% vs. 4.17%). All audio-only dereverberation baselines (VoiceFixer, SkipConvGAN, Kotha et al., WPE, DEMUCS, MetricGAN+, HiFi-GAN) are outperformed by AV-RIR on both WER and EER, with the largest margins over WPE (EER: 2.02% vs. 5.90%) and HiFi-GAN (WER: 4.17% vs. 9.31%).

## Relation to Prior Work

AV-RIR is positioned as the first audio-visual method for RIR estimation, departing from prior work that was either audio-only (S2IR-GAN, FiNS) or visual-only (Image2Reverb, FAST-RIR). Architecturally, it repurposes the SoundStream neural codec (RVQ + decoder) for RIR estimation rather than audio compression, adapts the S2IR-GAN encoder for the RIR branch, and builds CRIP on the CLIP contrastive pre-training paradigm with a FAST-RIR discriminator as the RIR encoder. The multi-task formulation—jointly solving RIR estimation and speech dereverberation—draws on the audio-visual speech codec literature (Yang et al., 2022) but applies it to a fundamentally different output space (impulse responses rather than waveforms).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in AR/VR where acoustic-visual mismatch causes the 'room divergence effect,' and the 36–63% improvement over the best audio-only baseline (S2IR-GAN) across T60, DRR, EDT, and MSE metrics is substantial for downstream applications like spatial audio rendering. The finding that a simple retrieval-based module (CRIP) can recover 86% of the late-reverberation error suggests that codec architectures have a structural limitation in modeling noise-like late components, pointing toward hybrid generative-retrieval designs as a broader pattern. Key limitations remain: the method assumes a stationary single speaker in a noise-free environment, and the Geo-Mat pipeline depends on a fixed acoustic material database and semantic matching that may not generalize to novel or mixed materials. Open questions include multi-channel RIR estimation, noisy multi-source scenes, and whether the retrieval datastore can be made environment-agnostic.
