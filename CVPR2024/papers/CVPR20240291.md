## Problem

Existing methods that leverage SAM's semantic priors for image restoration require SAM at both training and inference stages, imposing prohibitive computational overhead that negates the efficiency of smaller IR models. The paper addresses how to transfer SAM's rich semantic knowledge into existing IR models so that the inference pipeline remains identical to the original model's, with no additional segmentation module needed at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240291/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single degraded RGB image (rainy, motion-blurred, or noisy) and produces one restored RGB image using only the distilled student network f^IR1, with no SAM or segmentation input required. During training, SAM's automatic mode is applied to the student's preliminary output (not the degraded input, to avoid segmentation errors from heavy degradation) to obtain N-channel binary masks that guide the fusion and distillation process. The framework is model-agnostic and has been applied on top of RCDNet, Efficientderain, and Uformer across deraining, deblurring, and denoising tasks.

## Method

The framework cascades two copies of an IR model: a student f^IR1 and a teacher f^IR2. The Semantic Priors Fusion (SPF) scheme feeds the student's restored image and SAM's segmentation masks into the teacher via SPF units inserted at each building block, where a Semantic Selection Attention module highlights salient semantics through dot-product scoring. The Semantic Priors Distillation (SPD) scheme then transfers the teacher's capability to the student via a smooth L1 loss between their outputs, with a stop-gradient preventing the distillation loss from backpropagating through the teacher. A Semantic-Guided Relation (SGR) module further aligns the two networks by extracting VGG features, masking them with SAM's object masks, computing pairwise cosine-similarity relations across objects, and minimizing the L2 distance between the student's and teacher's relation matrices. At inference, only the student f^IR1 is deployed, preserving the original model's inference cost exactly.

## Results

On the deraining task, the proposed framework achieves an average PSNR improvement of +0.91 dB over RCDNet and +1.02 dB over Efficientderain across four datasets (Rain200L, Rain200H, DID, DDN), with the largest gain of +1.28 dB PSNR on Rain200L for Efficientderain. On GoPro deblurring, Uformer+ reaches 32.21 dB PSNR / 0.950 SSIM, surpassing all prior deblurring methods (best prior: DMPHN at 31.20 dB). On SenseNoise denoising, Uformer+ achieves 35.25 dB PSNR / 0.9151 SSIM, outperforming UNet (34.92) and CBDNet (35.00). All improvements are obtained without any change to the inference pipeline of the original IR model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The framework is applied as a plug-and-play enhancement on top of three existing IR backbones (RCDNet, Efficientderain, Uformer) and consistently improves each without altering their inference. On deraining, RCDNet+ (40.26 dB on R200L) surpasses the strongest standalone deraining baseline MPRNet (39.47 dB) by 0.79 dB, while Efficientderain+ (35.70 dB) still trails MPRNet but gains 1.28 dB over its own baseline. On GoPro deblurring, Uformer+ (32.21 dB) outperforms all six prior deblurring methods, with the closest competitor DMPHN at 31.20 dB (a 1.01 dB gap). On SenseNoise denoising, Uformer+ (35.25 dB) beats CBDNet (35.00 dB) and UNet (34.92 dB). The framework also improves downstream segmentation: on Cityscape-syn200, RCDNet+ raises IoU from 0.7701 to 0.7741 and Efficientderain+ from 0.7692 to 0.7770, both exceeding the 'No Rain' upper bound of 0.8020 in PSNR-adjacent quality while approaching it in segmentation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of prior work: (1) semantic-prior-based IR methods that either use semantic-aware losses (loss-level) or fuse segmentation features into IR networks (feature-level), and (2) recent SAM-for-IR approaches (e.g., SAM-Deblur, SAM-Dehaze) that require SAM at both training and inference. The key departure is repurposing self-distillation to transfer SAM's semantic knowledge into a standalone IR model, so that SAM is only needed during training. The SPF scheme (cascading a second IR model that receives SAM masks) and the SGR module (mask-guided VGG feature relation alignment) are new architectural and loss designs not present in prior semantic-prior IR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: SAM's inference cost is prohibitive for real-time IR deployment, and this framework removes that dependency entirely while retaining semantic benefits. However, the empirical gains are modest—around 0.1 dB PSNR for deblurring and denoising, and ~1 dB for deraining—raising the question of whether the added training complexity (a second IR model, frozen SAM, frozen VGG) is justified for such small improvements. Open questions include whether the distillation gap can be further closed, whether the framework extends to video restoration or other foundation models beyond SAM, and whether the SGR module's benefit generalizes beyond the tested architectures.
