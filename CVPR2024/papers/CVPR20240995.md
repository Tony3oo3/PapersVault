## Problem

Generalizable NeRF methods can synthesize novel views on unseen scenes without per-scene retraining, but they lack high-level semantic understanding. Existing semantic NeRFs either require scene-specific training with annotated segmentation maps, or (as in S-Ray) aggregate features from all points along a ray using hierarchical sampling, which introduces noisy features that degrade segmentation quality and increases computational cost. No prior method jointly achieves generalizable novel-view synthesis and semantic segmentation with an efficient, geometry-aware sampling strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes K multi-view RGB images with their camera poses and a target camera pose as input; during training, ground-truth depth maps and semantic segmentation labels of the target view are optionally available for supervision. It produces a rendered novel-view RGB image, a per-pixel semantic segmentation map, and an intermediate target-view depth map estimated by projecting and reprojecting source-view depth predictions. The model operates on-the-fly on unseen scenes without any per-scene fine-tuning, and can be trained either with GT depth supervision or purely self-supervised via cross-view depth consistency.

## Method

GSNeRF consists of two stages. The Semantic Geo-Reasoner uses a shared 2D CNN encoder, a cost-volume aggregator, and a semantic decoder to extract 2D image features, 3D volume features, semantic features, and per-source-view depth maps; the target-view depth is then estimated by a deterministic 3D reprojection of all source depth maps. The Depth-Guided Visual Rendering stage replaces conventional uniform or hierarchical sampling with a Gaussian distribution centered at the predicted target depth (with standard deviation scaled to the distance from scene bounds), concentrating samples near the first encountered surface. For volume rendering, all sampled points are fed to a renderer that aggregates reprojected 2D and 3D features from all source views plus global mean/variance statistics. For semantic rendering, only a single surface point per ray (at the predicted depth) is used, avoiding the noisy aggregation of all ray points that plagues S-Ray. Training combines image reconstruction loss, depth supervision (supervised or self-supervised via RC-MVSNet-style cross-view consistency), and multi-class cross-entropy semantic loss.

## Results

On ScanNet (with GT depth), GSNeRF achieves 58.30 mIoU for semantic segmentation and 31.33 PSNR / 90.73 SSIM / 12.53 LPIPS for novel-view synthesis, outperforming the primary baseline S-Ray (55.53 mIoU, 25.19 PSNR). Without GT depth supervision, GSNeRF still leads with 52.21 mIoU and 31.49 PSNR on ScanNet, and 51.23 mIoU and 31.71 PSNR on Replica. On Replica (with depth), GSNeRF reaches 51.52 mIoU and 31.16 PSNR, surpassing all baselines by a clear margin in both segmentation and image quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSNeRF outperforms S-Ray [17], the primary generalizable semantic NeRF baseline, by 2.77 mIoU on ScanNet (58.30 vs 55.53) and 6.22 mIoU on Replica (51.52 vs 45.30) with depth supervision, while also achieving substantially better LPIPS (12.53 vs 30.98 on ScanNet). Without GT depth, the gap widens: GSNeRF leads S-Ray by 4.52 mIoU on ScanNet (52.21 vs 47.69) and 7.96 mIoU on Replica (51.23 vs 43.27). Against GeoNeRF+semhead [14], GSNeRF gains 4.52 mIoU on ScanNet (58.30 vs 53.78) and 6.40 mIoU on Replica (51.52 vs 45.12), though GeoNeRF retains a slight PSNR edge on ScanNet with depth (32.55 vs 31.33). GSNeRF also dominates Neuray+semhead [18], MVSNeRF+semhead [3], and GNT+semhead [32] across all metrics on both datasets, with the largest margins over MVSNeRF (15.24 mIoU on ScanNet without depth). The consistent advantage in LPIPS (12.53–13.87 vs 20–35 for baselines) indicates substantially better perceptual quality in rendered novel views.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSNeRF sits at the intersection of two NeRF research threads: generalizable novel view synthesis (GeoNeRF, MVS-NeRF, NeuRay) and semantic-aware NeRF (Semantic NeRF, S-Ray, NeSF). It most directly builds on S-Ray, the first method to combine generalizable NeRF with semantic segmentation via cross-reprojection attention, but departs from S-Ray's hierarchical sampling by replacing it with a depth-guided Gaussian sampling strategy centered on a predicted target-view depth map. The Semantic Geo-Reasoning stage borrows cost-volume aggregation from GeoNeRF and depth supervision ideas from DS-NeRF, while the self-supervised depth loss follows RC-MVSNet, creating a unified pipeline that estimates geometry before rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for per-scene retraining and (optionally) GT depth inputs, GSNeRF moves semantic NeRF closer to practical deployment in robotic navigation and AR where scenes are unseen and depth sensors may be unavailable. The depth-guided sampling strategy is a simple but effective insight: focusing samples near the predicted surface reduces noise for segmentation and dramatically improves robustness to low sample counts. However, the method is validated only on indoor scenes (ScanNet, Replica), and the quality of the entire pipeline is bottlenecked by the accuracy of the predicted target-view depth map; open questions include scalability to outdoor or dynamic scenes, handling of transparent objects more rigorously, and whether the two-stage design can be unified end-to-end.
