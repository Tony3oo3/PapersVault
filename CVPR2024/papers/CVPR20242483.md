## Problem

Existing OOD robustness methods perform well on synthetic corruptions (e.g., ImageNet-C) but degrade significantly on real-world nuisances such as weather, texture, pose, and context changes, as measured by the OOD-CV benchmark. CompNets, a Bayesian generative model, handle partial occlusion well but are fully supervised and fail under OOD shifts. No prior method simultaneously addresses real-world OOD nuisances and occlusion in an unsupervised target-domain setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source-domain images and unlabeled target-domain images (containing OOD nuisances) as input, along with a frozen DCNN backbone (VGG16 or ResNet-50) for feature extraction and background images for estimating the occluder distribution. Target-domain images with partial occlusion are never seen during training and are used only at inference. The output is a single class label per image, produced by a forward pass through the learned generative model that computes P(F|y) for each candidate class y.

## Method

UGT (Unsupervised Generative Transition) extends CompNets by decoupling training into two stages: unsupervised learning of von Mises-Fisher (vMF) kernel dictionaries (representing object subparts) and supervised learning of spatial coefficients (representing where subparts appear). The key insight is that some vMF kernels are invariant across domains while others are not, enabling the construction of a transitional dictionary learned via regularized MLE on target features, initialized from source kernels and penalized to remain close to them. Spatial coefficients are then learned on the labeled source domain using the transitional dictionary, transferring object geometry knowledge to the target. A final pseudo-labeling step refines the spatial coefficients using high-confidence target predictions, and the entire model can be end-to-end finetuned with a generalized cross-entropy loss plus vMF and spatial regularization terms.

## Results

On the OOD-CV benchmark (Combined nuisances, 0% occlusion), UGT achieves 85.0% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baselines CST (84.0%) and MIC (83.7%). Under increasing occlusion (60–80%), UGT retains 50.1% accuracy versus 47.7% for CST and 43.4% for MCC, demonstrating substantially better occlusion robustness. On ImageNet-C corruptions (Pascal3D+), UGT reaches 90.9% on Gaussian Blur and 92.3% on Contrast at 0% occlusion, exceeding RPL (85.5%, 90.1%) and BNA (83.3%, 89.9%). In the Synthetic-to-Real setting (UDAParts → Pascal3D+), UGT achieves 99.2% at 0% occlusion and 75.3% at 60–80% occlusion, far surpassing BNA (95.0% / 35.6%) and CompNet (94.0% / 34.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGT outperforms all 14 baselines across every nuisance category and occlusion level on OOD-CV. Among UDA methods, CST [23] and DINE [22] are the strongest competitors at 0% occlusion (84.0% and 83.5% vs. UGT's 85.0% on Combined), but UGT pulls ahead sharply under occlusion: at 60–80% occlusion, UGT reaches 50.1% while CST drops to 47.7% and DINE to 44.3%. MIC [13] is an outlier that performs well under texture nuisance with occlusion (70.6% at 20–40%) but collapses on Combined (26.2% at 60–80%), whereas UGT maintains 50.1%. The robustness-specific baselines RPL [30] and BNA [32] perform worst overall (66.4% and 65.3% on Combined 0%), lagging UGT by ~19 points. The direct architectural baseline CompNet [21] achieves 72.0% on Combined 0% and degrades to 41.5% at 60–80% occlusion, while UGT improves both to 85.0% and 50.1%. On the Synthetic-to-Real task, UGT's advantage is most dramatic: at 60–80% occlusion it scores 75.3% versus 35.6% (BNA) and 34.7% (CompNet), a gap of over 40 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Compositional Neural Networks (CompNets), a Bayesian generative architecture that replaces the discriminative head of a CNN with a mixture-of-vMF model over spatial feature positions, which was previously shown to be robust to occlusion but not to OOD domain shifts. The key conceptual departure is decoupling CompNets' end-to-end supervised training into an unsupervised stage (learning vMF kernel dictionaries via EM clustering) and a supervised stage (learning spatial coefficients), which enables the construction of a 'transitional dictionary' that interpolates between source and target domain feature distributions. This positions UGT against a large body of UDA methods (CDAN, MDD, MCC, BNA, RPL, etc.) that rely on feature alignment, adversarial training, or pseudo-labeling but have been shown to degrade on real-world OOD nuisances (OOD-CV) and under occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing OOD-robustness methods perform well on synthetic corruptions (ImageNet-C) but fail on real-world nuisance factors (weather, texture, pose, context) and especially under partial occlusion, a combination that is common in deployed vision systems. The introduction of Occluded-OOD-CV as a benchmark is a useful contribution for the community. However, the method is tightly coupled to the CompNets/vMF generative formulation and the DCNN backbone, limiting immediate transferability to transformer-based or other architectures; the authors note extension to semantic segmentation as future work but provide only a qualitative proof of concept. An open question is whether the transitional-dictionary idea generalizes beyond vMF kernels to other feature representations.
