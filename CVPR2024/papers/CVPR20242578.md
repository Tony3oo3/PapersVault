## Problem

In Open-Set Source-Free Domain Adaptation, the target domain contains unknown classes absent from the source, and source data is inaccessible. Existing OS-SFDA methods group all unknown classes into a single cluster, which restricts thorough exploration of the target class space and simultaneously degrades both known-class knowledge transfer and unknown-class generalization. This paper argues that actively exploring and separating unknown classes can in turn improve identification of known ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen source pre-trained model (ResNet-50 backbone plus a classifier trained on known source classes) and a batch of unlabeled RGB target images, with no access to source data or any prior on the number of unknown classes. It produces per-sample class predictions over an inferred wider class space, the dynamically estimated target class number K, and soft cluster assignments from the unknown diffuser. The inferred K changes iteratively during training as the diffuser splits or merges clusters, and the final model outputs both known-class labels and unknown-class cluster identities.

## Method

The core component is an unknown diffuser (an MLP with a main network C and per-cluster sub-networks C_k^sub) that progressively infers the target class number K by alternating between (i) optimizing discriminative cluster assignments under the current K via k-means-guided alignment loss, and (ii) hierarchical class exploration where candidate clusters for splitting (far apart) or merging (close together) are selected by Euclidean distance of cluster centroids, and the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance ratio determines whether K increases or decreases. After the class space is explored, a contrastive constraint on the top-50% lowest-entropy target samples enhances the reliability of known-class knowledge from the source classifier. The final optimization jointly minimizes a cross-entropy loss on high-confidence known pseudo-labels and an alignment loss over the full wider class space, so that known samples receive supervised transfer while unknown samples are regularized through the alignment term, yielding simultaneous known-class transfer and unknown-class generalization without any source data or unknown-class-count prior.

## Results

On Office-31, the proposed method (with supervision) achieves 79.8% OS*, 80.3% UNK, and 79.7% HOS on average across six domain pairs, outperforming AaD (69.4% OS*, 87.8% UNK, 77.4% HOS) on HOS and SHOT (85.8% OS*, 54.6% UNK, 64.8% HOS) on UNK and HOS. On the more challenging Office-Home (12 tasks, 65 classes), the method reaches 61.6% OS*, 79.7% UNK, and 69.2% HOS, surpassing AaD (58.0%, 72.1%, 63.6%) on all three metrics. For class space exploration, the method infers target class numbers (K) and achieves ACC scores substantially higher than no-parameter clustering baselines (e.g., 71.2% ACC with K=25 vs. COMIC's 56.8% with K=22 on A→D in Office-31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SHOT, the proposed method dramatically improves UNK accuracy (80.3% vs. 54.6% avg on Office-31; 79.7% vs. 33.9% on Office-Home) while sacrificing some OS* (79.8% vs. 85.8%), yielding a much higher HOS (79.7% vs. 64.8%). Against AaD, the method wins on OS* (79.8% vs. 69.4% on Office-31; 61.6% vs. 58.0% on Office-Home) and HOS (79.7% vs. 77.4%; 69.2% vs. 63.6%), though AaD retains a slight edge on UNK for Office-31 (87.8% vs. 80.3%). The simple k-means clustering baseline (Cluster) is far inferior on all metrics, confirming that domain adaptation is essential. For class-space exploration (Tables 3–4), the method outperforms all three no-parameter clustering baselines (FINCH, COMIC, DenMune) on both ACC and inferred class number K across every task; for example, on Office-31 A→D the method achieves 71.2% ACC with K=25, versus COMIC's 56.8% (K=22), FINCH's 31.0% (K=8), and DenMune's 20.0% (K=5). The ablation (w/o s vs. w/ s) shows that adding reliable known-knowledge supervision consistently improves HOS and ACC on Office-Home (69.2% vs. 69.0% HOS; 61.4% vs. 60.5% ACC on Ar→Pr), confirming the benefit of the contrastive-constrained pseudo-labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) and open-set domain adaptation (OSDA), specifically targeting the OS-SFDA setting where only a pre-trained source model is available and the target domain contains additional unknown classes. It departs from prior OS-SFDA methods (SHOT, AaD, FS, OSHT, SF-PGL) that uniformly group all unknown classes into a single cluster, arguing instead that exploring the unknown class space via an M-H-based 'unknown diffuser' (inspired by no-parameter clustering and hierarchical clustering) simultaneously improves known-class transfer and unknown-class generalization. The paper explicitly contrasts with recent OSDA methods that explore unknown classes (e.g., [11, 23, 49]) by emphasizing that those require source data access and a prior on the unknown class count, whereas this work operates without either.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing—that actively exploring unknown classes can sharpen known-class discrimination rather than merely being a byproduct of separation—offers a useful perspective for the OS-SFDA community, and the M-H-based class-number inference provides a principled, parameter-free mechanism for discovering target class structure without source data. However, the approach relies on iterative k-means and M-H sampling that may not scale gracefully to very large class spaces or high-dimensional feature spaces, and the manually-set confidence threshold for selecting reliable known samples introduces a sensitivity that limits plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the hierarchical split/merge strategy generalizes to non-Euclidean feature spaces, whether the method extends to video or multi-modal OS-SFDA, and how performance holds when the unknown class count is very large relative to the known count.
