## Problem

Existing self-supervised and weakly supervised visual sound source localization methods struggle to disentangle one-to-one audio-visual correspondence in multi-source mixtures, where individual source sounds are unavailable during training. Prior approaches rely on audio-visual correspondence as guidance, which degrades significantly when multiple sources overlap in both modalities. The paper addresses this gap by introducing the text modality as an intermediate semantic guide to explicitly discriminate among multiple sounding sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240497/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single video frame (224×224 for single-source, 448×224 for multi-source with two concatenated frames) and a corresponding audio mixture spectrogram as input. It produces K per-source spatial localization heatmaps of size K×H×W, where K is the number of detected sounding classes. Training is weakly supervised with only video-level class labels (no bounding boxes or masks), and the framework supports a flexible number of sources at test time, including more sources than seen during training.

## Method

T-VSL exploits the tri-modal joint embedding space of AudioCLIP (ResNet-50 image encoder, ESResNeXt audio encoder, transformer text encoder) to disentangle multi-source mixtures. It first detects sounding class instances via cosine similarity between mean-pooled mixture features and N-class text embeddings, then uses the text representations of detected sources as coarse guidance in cross-attention conditioning blocks to extract categorical audio and visual patch tokens for each source. An audio-visual correspondence block further refines alignment between the disentangled categorical features via cross-attention and InfoNCE contrastive loss. The total training objective combines multi-label detection loss, class-conditioning cross-entropy loss, and audio-visual contrastive loss, with AudioCLIP backbones kept frozen. This text-guided disentanglement enables flexible source counts and zero-shot transfer to unseen classes by simply swapping text prompts.

## Results

On multi-source localization (the paper's primary task), T-VSL achieves CIoU@0.3 of 40.1% on VGGSound-Duet, 89.6% on VGGSound-Instruments (CIoU@0.1), and 43.2% on MUSIC-Duet, outperforming the best multi-source baseline AVGN by +2.3, +2.9, and +3.9 CIoU points respectively. On single-source localization, T-VSL reaches IoU@0.5 of 53.7% on VGGSound-Single, 69.5% on VGGSound-Instruments (IoU@0.3), and 68.5% on MUSIC-Solo, surpassing the SOTA single-source method Alignment by +2.9, +3.7, and +2.1 IoU points. The method also demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer, achieving CIoU@0.3 of 55.8% on MUSIC-Duet and CIoU@0.1 of 81.7% on VGGSound-Instruments when trained on only 50% of classes, outperforming self-supervised baselines by 4–5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-VSL outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest multi-source baseline AVGN [30], T-VSL gains +3.8 CAP and +2.3 CIoU@0.3 on VGGSound-Duet, +3.7 CAP and +2.9 CIoU@0.1 on VGGSound-Instruments, and +3.7 CAP and +3.9 CIoU@0.3 on MUSIC-Duet. Against the best single-source SOTA Alignment [42], T-VSL leads by +5.3 CAP and +4.3 CIoU@0.3 on VGGSound-Duet, +6.2 CAP and +4.7 CIoU@0.1 on VGGSound-Instruments, and +5.1 CAP and +4.9 CIoU@0.3 on MUSIC-Duet. The CLIP-based baselines (AudioCLIP, Wav2CLIP) serve as natural lower bounds; T-VSL exceeds AudioCLIP by +7.3 CAP and +5.2 CIoU@0.3 on VGGSound-Duet, confirming that the text-guided conditioning and audio-visual correspondence blocks add substantial value over raw tri-modal similarity. Notably, several single-source methods (OTS, LVS, EZ-VSL) degrade significantly in multi-source settings, sometimes falling below the AudioCLIP baseline, while T-VSL maintains consistent gains, highlighting the importance of explicit text guidance for mixture disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AudioCLIP's tri-modal (audio-visual-text) joint embedding space, repurposing it as a backbone for multi-source sound localization. It departs from prior multi-source methods (Mix-and-Localize, AVGN, DSOL) that attempt to learn audio-visual correspondence intrinsically from noisy mixtures without explicit single-source guidance, instead introducing text embeddings as a coarse supervisory signal to disentangle per-source features. The cross-attention conditioning blocks that use class-level text prompts to separate mixed audio and visual patch tokens represent a novel architectural contribution not present in prior CLIP-based localization work (e.g., the concurrent CLIP-based single-source method [34]).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-guided disentanglement strategy addresses a core limitation of weakly-supervised multi-source localization: the absence of clean single-source pairs during training. The demonstrated zero-shot transferability to unseen classes (outperforming self-supervised baselines by 4–5 points) and robustness to test-time source counts exceeding training conditions are practically valuable properties that prior weakly-supervised methods lack. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully beyond 4 sources, how it performs in real-world recordings with reverberation and overlapping speech, and whether the reliance on AudioCLIP's pretraining quality limits generalization to domains underrepresented in web-scraped data.
