## Problem

Existing medical self-supervised learning is confined to single modalities or small paired sets, and naive joint multi-modal pre-training suffers from 'modal data collision'—conflicting representation learning across disparate modalities—while also incurring prohibitive cost to integrate new data. No practical paradigm exists for building a scalable, universal pre-trained model across heterogeneous medical modalities (1D text, 2D images, 3D volumes) without degrading performance or requiring full retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes five input types through dimension-specific tokenizers: clinical report text (BPE tokens, sequence length 112), 2D X-ray and pathological images (224×224 patches), and 3D CT/MRI volumes (16×192×192 patches), all fed into a single shared ViT/B encoder. During pre-training, the output is a masked reconstruction (cross-entropy for text, MSE for visual patches); during fine-tuning, the encoder produces a shared representation embedding that is decoded into class labels (MLP head) or segmentation masks (convolutional decoder) depending on the downstream task.

## Method

MedCoSS replaces joint multi-modal pre-training with a sequential, stage-wise paradigm where each stage trains exclusively on one modality using masked modeling (BERT-style 15% masking for text, MAE-style 75% masking for visual data). To prevent catastrophic forgetting between stages, it employs a rehearsal-based continual learning strategy: a k-means sampling procedure selects 5% of each previous modality's data into a rehearsal buffer, a frozen copy of the prior-stage model provides feature distillation targets (MSE matching of embeddings on buffer samples, bypassing pretext tasks), and an intra-modal mixup augmentation (binary mask for text, per-sample continuous blending for images) expands the limited buffer. The architecture is dimension-agnostic, using three tokenizers (BPE, 2D patch, 3D patch) that feed a single Transformer encoder, enabling a unified representation space across all modalities.

## Results

MedCoSS achieves the best or second-best performance on 8 out of 9 downstream datasets across five modalities (Report, X-ray, CT, MRI, Pathological). Headline numbers include 94.31% ACC on ChestXR (X-ray classification), 95.76% ACC and 99.51% AUC on NCH (pathological classification), 90.12 Dice on VS (MRI segmentation), and 90.46 Dice on LA (MRI segmentation). On the only dataset where it is not best (PudMed20k, Report), MedCoSS scores 83.59% ACC versus 83.79% for the single-modal Report pre-training model, a marginal 0.2% gap. The model also demonstrates strong scalability, with the last-trained modality (Pathological) achieving near-single-modal performance (95.76% ACC on NCH vs. 95.62% for Path.-only pre-training).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four continual-learning baselines (EWC, ER, PackNet, CaSSLe), MedCoSS consistently outperforms all of them on 8 out of 9 datasets across all metrics. The largest margins appear on cross-modal transfer: on RICORD (CT classification), MedCoSS achieves 83.33% ACC versus 75.79% (EWC), 75.79% (ER), 71.43% (PackNet), and 76.59% (CaSSLe); on ChestXR (X-ray), MedCoSS reaches 94.31% ACC versus 90.48% (EWC), 85.32% (ER), 86.14% (PackNet), and 91.66% (CaSSLe). Against the two joint SSL variants (dimension-shared and modal-specific decoders), MedCoSS also wins on most datasets, e.g., 94.31% vs. 92.24% ACC on ChestXR and 83.33% vs. 77.78% on RICORD, while joint SSL retains a slight edge on PudMed20k (84.10% vs. 83.59% ACC). The only dataset where MedCoSS is not best is PudMed20k, where the single-modal Report pre-training model (83.79% ACC) and Joint SSL* (84.10% ACC) marginally outperform it. Among the CL baselines, CaSSLe is the strongest competitor, achieving 95.01% ACC on NCH and 89.66 Dice on VS, but MedCoSS still leads on NCH (95.76% ACC) and VS (90.12 Dice). ER and PackNet show the weakest cross-modal generalization, with ER dropping to 85.32% ACC on ChestXR and PackNet to 71.43% on RICORD, highlighting the limitations of standard rehearsal and architecture-based approaches for multi-modal SSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedCoSS builds on the Uni-Perceiver architecture [59] for its dimension-free backbone (multiple tokenizers feeding a shared ViT/B encoder) and adopts masked modeling pretext tasks from MAE [17] (visual) and BERT [12] (text). Its continual learning mechanism is a customized adaptation of the DER/ER rehearsal paradigm [5, 35], replacing reservoir sampling with k-means clustering, substituting logit-matching with feature distillation via a frozen encoder, and adding intra-modal mixup augmentation. The key conceptual departure is from joint multi-modal SSL (as in [7, 15, 46]) to a sequential, stage-per-modality pre-training scheme, motivated by the empirically observed 'modal data collision' where joint training degrades performance below single-modal baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in medical AI: building a single pre-trained model that generalizes across the heterogeneous modalities (text, 2D, 3D) encountered in real clinical workflows without requiring impractical paired datasets. The scalability argument—adding a new modality as an incremental stage rather than retraining from scratch—has direct implications for deploying foundation models in evolving hospital settings. Limitations include the fixed sequential order (though tested with one alternative), the 5% buffer size being a heuristic, and evaluation limited to five modalities and nine downstream tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly streaming data with unknown future modalities, how it scales beyond five modalities, and whether the modal data collision phenomenon persists with stronger backbones or longer training.
