## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods for face anti-spoofing focus on learning domain-invariant features during training, which fails when unseen target domains differ significantly from source distributions. The paper identifies that testing data is underutilized—treated only for evaluation rather than as a resource to actively improve generalizability—and that prior test-time adaptation approaches require costly model updates or large batches of target data, making them impractical in real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241214/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image as input and outputs a binary real/spoof classification label. During training, pseudo-depth maps generated by PRNet provide auxiliary pixel-level supervision (live faces get depth maps, spoof faces get zero maps), but at inference only the RGB image is required. The framework is backbone-agnostic, operating on channel-wise mean and variance statistics of intermediate features (C×H×W tensors) extracted by either a CNN or ViT-Base, with no architectural modifications needed to the backbone itself.

## Method

The TTDG framework projects unseen test sample styles into the known source style space without any parameter updates at test time. Test-Time Style Projection (TTSP) computes cosine-similarity-based weights between the test sample's channel-wise feature statistics (mean and variance) and N=64 learnable style bases, then reassembles the feature using a weighted combination of those bases. Diverse Style Shifts Simulation (DSSS) learns these style bases in a hyperspherical feature space guided by two losses: a style diversity loss enforcing orthogonality among bases, and a content consistency loss (contrastive) ensuring that style-projected features remain aligned with their original content. The theoretical motivation is to reduce the H-divergence gap γ between the real target domain and the ideal target within the source convex hull, pulling unseen data closer to the source distribution at test time rather than relying solely on training-time invariance.

## Results

On the four standard leave-one-out cross-domain FAS benchmarks (I&C&M→O, O&C&M→I, O&C&I→M, O&M&I→C), TTDG with a CNN backbone achieves HTER of 10.00%, 6.50%, 7.91%, and 8.14% with AUC of 95.70%, 97.98%, 96.83%, and 96.49%, respectively. With a ViT-Base backbone (TTDG-V), the method further improves to HTER of 10.00%, 9.62%, 4.16%, and 7.59% and AUC of 96.15%, 98.18%, 98.48%, and 98.18%. These results set new state-of-the-art across all four evaluation settings, outperforming the strongest prior method IADG by 1.45–4.60% in HTER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTDG (CNN) outperforms all 11 train-time DG FAS baselines across all four leave-one-out settings on both HTER and AUC. The closest competitor, IADG [121], is surpassed by 1.45% HTER on I&C&M→O, 4.54% on O&C&M→I, 0.54% on O&C&I→M, and 4.60% on O&M&I→C. Among test-time DG methods (Table 2), TTDG beats TF-Cal [113] by 2.79–5.81% HTER and Sty.-Pro [29] by 3.15–6.67% HTER across the four settings. For ViT backbones, TTDG-V dominates TTN-S [95] by 2.64–4.97% HTER and DiVT-V [47] by 2.03–10.47% HTER, with the largest gap on O&C&I→M (4.16% vs. 10.00% HTER). On limited source domains (Table 3, M&I→C and M&I→O), TTDG achieves 17.77% and 17.70% HTER, outperforming the best baseline AMEL [116] by 6.75% and 1.98% respectively, confirming robustness even with only two source domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time style shifting paradigm (Park et al., ICML 2023) from a single-nearest-domain projection to a weighted aggregation over N learnable style bases, and adapts the style-statistics representation (channel-wise mean/variance) established in FAS-specific DG work (Wang et al., CVPR 2022; Zhou et al., CVPR 2023). The key departure from prior test-time adaptation methods for FAS (Kim et al., 2022/2023; Wang et al., AAAI 2021) is that no gradient-based model updates are required at test time; instead, features are projected into a pre-learned style space. The theoretical grounding via H-divergence and source convex hull analysis (Albuquerque et al., 2019) reframes the problem as reducing the gap between the real target and the ideal target within the source hull, rather than expanding the hull through augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating test-time model updates, which removes the computational burden and catastrophic forgetting risks of TTA methods while requiring no domain labels or large target batches. The model-agnostic design (CNN and ViT) and the learnable-bases formulation make the approach broadly applicable beyond FAS to other domain-generalization tasks with style-driven shifts. Open questions include whether the simple mean/variance style representation captures all relevant domain shifts (e.g., texture, geometry), whether the convex-hull assumption holds for truly out-of-distribution attacks, and how the method scales to open-world settings with unknown attack types not represented in the learned style bases.
