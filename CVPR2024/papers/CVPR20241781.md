## Problem

Unsupervised video domain adaptation (VUDA) aims to transfer action recognition models to new domains using only unlabeled target videos, but existing approaches rely on adversarial alignment or contrastive objectives that can be unstable. This paper is the first to explore self-supervised masked distillation in the VUDA setting, asking whether a spatial (image) teacher can effectively guide a spatiotemporal (video) student to close the domain gap without source-target feature alignment losses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241781/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model consumes 8 uniformly sampled frames per video, each resized to 224×224, forming a 3×8×224×224 tensor processed by a ViT-B/16 with joint space-time attention. During training, the student receives an 80%-masked video view (attention-guided by the CLIP teacher's final-layer CLS token), while the teacher processes unmasked frames. At test time, predictions are averaged over 3 spatial crops and 4 temporal clips. The CLIP teacher additionally takes class-name text prompts (templated as 'A video of a person {class}.') for zero-shot pseudolabel generation.

## Method

UNITE is a three-stage pipeline: (1) UMT-style masked distillation on target videos, where a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 spatial teacher guides a ViT-B/16 spatiotemporal student to match teacher features at the last 6 layers via MSE on visible patches only; (2) supervised fine-tuning on source videos with a linear head and layer-wise learning rate decay (0.65) to preserve target-domain features; (3) collaborative self-training (CST) where the student and CLIP teacher jointly produce pseudolabels via a MatchOrConf scheme (agree, or one exceeds confidence threshold γ=0.1 while the other does not), and the target CE loss is applied to masked target videos with confidence-based weighting. The key insight is that masked pre-training and masked self-training are complementary: pre-training equips the student to extract robust features from occluded inputs, which then unlocks the benefits of enforcing the classification loss on masked target videos during self-training, yielding substantially larger gains than either stage alone.

## Results

On Sports-DA, UNITE achieves 94.0% average top-1 accuracy across 6 domain shifts, substantially outperforming the best prior method DALL-V (82.3%) and the CLIP zero-shot baseline (89.9%). On Daily-DA, UNITE reaches 59.2% average accuracy over 12 domain shifts, improving over DALL-V (51.4%) and Source Only (49.6%). On UCF↔HMDB_full, UNITE attains 93.8% average accuracy, slightly trailing UDAVT (94.6%) but exceeding CO²A (91.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNITE outperforms all adversarial and discrepancy-based UDA baselines (DANN, MK-MMD, TA³N) by a wide margin on every benchmark, with average gaps of 15–25 points on Sports-DA and Daily-DA. Among source-free video UDA methods, UNITE surpasses ATCoN (73.8% vs 94.0% on Sports-DA), EXTERN (83.2% vs 94.0%), and DALL-V (82.3% vs 94.0%), demonstrating that the collaborative masked pre-training and self-training pipeline substantially exceeds both adapter-based and consistency-based adaptation. The CLIP zero-shot baseline (89.9% on Sports-DA, 53.3% on Daily-DA) is also exceeded, confirming that the spatiotemporal student model benefits from temporal modeling beyond what the spatial image teacher provides. On UCF↔HMDB_full, UNITE (93.8% avg) trails UDAVT (94.6%) by 0.8 points overall, losing specifically on H→U (92.5% vs 96.8%) but winning on U→H (95.0% vs 92.3%); it still exceeds CO²A (91.8%) by 2.0 points. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the combination of UMT pre-training and collaborative self-training yields the largest gains (e.g., +18.3% on A→H), with neither stage alone matching the full pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNITE builds directly on the Unmasked Teacher (UMT) masked distillation framework [30], which trains a spatiotemporal student to match a frozen spatial teacher (CLIP) on visible patches of heavily masked video inputs. The paper repurposes this self-supervised pre-training objective—previously used for general video representation learning—as the first stage of a domain adaptation pipeline, and pairs it with a collaborative self-training stage that leverages CLIP's zero-shot classification to refine pseudolabels via the MatchOrConf scheme [69]. This departs from prior VUDA methods (DANN, MK-MMD, TA3N, CO2A, UDAVT) that rely on adversarial alignment or contrastive losses, and from source-free approaches (DALL-V, ATCoN, EXTERN) that adapt CLIP directly, by instead using CLIP as a spatial teacher to train a dedicated spatiotemporal student. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to explore self-supervised masked distillation within the unsupervised video domain adaptation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that masked modeling techniques, previously confined to self-supervised pre-training, can be directly harnessed for domain adaptation, opening a new design axis for VUDA beyond adversarial or contrastive alignment. The ablation showing that masked pre-training and masked self-training are synergistic (each contributing modestly alone but producing a much larger combined gain) suggests that invariance to masking is a useful property to enforce during adaptation. Limitations include the manually tuned confidence threshold γ, degradation on some harder classes after self-training, and dependence on CLIP's zero-shot quality for pseudolabel refinement. Open questions include developing principled threshold selection, extending the collaborative scheme to handle class imbalance during self-training, and whether the approach generalizes to source-free or few-shot video adaptation settings.
