## Problem

Video Paragraph Grounding (VPG) requires laborious temporal annotations for every sentence in a paragraph, and existing weakly-supervised grounding methods (multiple instance learning, reconstruction) are limited to single-sentence grounding with propose-and-rank pipelines that suffer from quadratic complexity and non-temporal-sensitive supervision. The weakly-supervised setting for VPG (WSVPG) has been largely unexplored, leaving a gap between the annotation cost of fully/semi-supervised methods and the limited expressiveness of prior weakly-supervised approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (sampled into T fixed-length clips encoded by a frozen C3D 3D-CNN, yielding a feature sequence of length T×256) and a paragraph query of N temporally ordered sentences (tokenized with frozen Glove embeddings and encoded by a BiGRU into N×256 sentence features). It outputs per-sentence temporal intervals (start and end timestamps) in normalized [0,1] space. During training, the augmentation branch additionally consumes a synthesized pseudo video formed by inserting the query video into a randomly sampled background video, while the inference branch processes the original video; at test time only the inference branch is retained.

## Method

SiamGTR is a siamese transformer with two weight-sharing branches that jointly learn cross-modal alignment and boundary regression in a single stage without proposals. The Augmentation Branch regresses the temporal boundaries of the complete paragraph within a pseudo video (constructed by inserting the query video at a random position into an irrelevant background video, with random re-sampling and boundary shifting to generate pseudo labels), using a self-consistent regression loss that selectively optimizes only samples with high attention over the pseudo ground-truth interval. The Inference Branch learns order-guided cross-modal correspondence by constraining decoder attention weights so that earlier sentences attend to earlier video regions, via an order-guided attention loss. Key architectural components include Modulated Positional Encodings in the encoder, Dynamic Anchor Boxes in the DETR-style decoder for position-aware iterative refinement, and a Conceptual Semantic Connector that bridges the semantic gap between paragraph-level and sentence-level queries through a learned concept dictionary. The framework is easily extended to semi-supervised learning by adding a standard regression-plus-attention loss on the subset of fully-annotated samples without modifying the network structure.

## Results

On ActivityNet-Captions, SiamGTR under the weakly-supervised setting achieves 52.32 mIoU and 57.23 R@0.5, surpassing the best prior weakly-supervised baseline WSTPN (38.54 mIoU) by 13.78% in mIoU. Under the semi-supervised setting, SiamGTR reaches 54.57 mIoU, outperforming the best semi-supervised baseline SVPTR (51.98 mIoU) by 2.59% and approaching the fully-supervised SVPTR (55.91 mIoU). On Charades-CD-OOD and TACoS, similar margins are observed, with SiamGTR (WS) achieving 37.21 and 20.48 mIoU respectively, consistently leading all weakly-supervised competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiamGTR decisively outperforms both weakly-supervised baselines: it exceeds WSTPN [75] by 13.78% mIoU (52.32 vs. 38.54) and WSSL [18] by 23.99% mIoU (52.32 vs. 28.33) on ActivityNet-Captions, with similar large gaps on Charades-CD-OOD (+3.72% over WSTPN) and TACoS (+11.16% over WSTPN). In the semi-supervised setting, SiamGTR (SS) surpasses SVPTR [31] (SS) by 2.59% mIoU (54.57 vs. 51.98) and VPTR [31] (SS) by 4.49% mIoU, while remaining only 1.34% behind the fully-supervised SVPTR [31] (FS) and 5.14% behind the strongest fully-supervised method HSCNet [67] (59.71 mIoU). The fully-supervised baselines 3D-TPN [101], DepNet [4], and PRVG [62] are all outperformed by SiamGTR under the weaker semi-supervised or weakly-supervised settings, demonstrating that the siamese framework extracts substantially more signal from limited annotations than prior approaches. On Charades-CD-OOD, SiamGTR (WS) also beats the fully-supervised DepNet [4] (29.30 mIoU) and approaches SVPTR [31] (FS) (36.01 mIoU) with no temporal labels at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Video Paragraph Grounding (VPG, Bao et al. 2021) into a weakly-supervised regime, departing from the proposal-based two-stage pipelines (multiple instance learning, reconstruction learning) that dominate weakly-supervised video sentence grounding. The closest prior work is Chen et al. (AAAI 2022), which used video composition for pseudo-boundary supervision in VSG, but the authors argue that sentence-level pseudo boundaries are too noisy and that adapting a fully-supervised proposal framework causes train-test discrepancy. SiamGTR repurposes the siamese network paradigm—typically used for contrastive self-supervised representation learning—into a joint alignment-and-regression framework, combining DETR-style dynamic anchor box decoding with a novel order-guided attention loss that exploits the temporal ordering of sentences within a paragraph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating temporal annotations entirely for VPG, matching or exceeding semi-supervised methods that still require ~10% labeled data, while using a simpler one-stage pipeline. The ablation revealing that order-guided cross-modal alignment (L_oga) contributes far more to weakly-supervised performance than direct boundary regression (L_screg) challenges the assumption that coordinate-level supervision is the primary signal needed for grounding. Limitations include reliance on frozen C3D features, the assumption that a paragraph serves as a unique referring expression for its video, and notably weaker results on TACoS (20.48 vs. 40.61 mIoU for FS), suggesting the pseudo-boundary strategy degrades with longer videos and more sentences. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond VPG to other multi-query grounding tasks and whether the pseudo-video construction can be made more robust to domain shift.
