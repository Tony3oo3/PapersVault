## Problem

LiDAR upsampling is critical for perception in autonomous systems, but existing range-image-based methods either use CNNs that smooth out the sharp object-boundary edges inherent to range images, or apply standard Swin Transformer architectures with square patches and windows that are poorly suited to the highly asymmetric (1:8 to 1:64) single-channel geometry of range images. This mismatch causes blurry 3D reconstructions and invalid floating points between objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241573/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution LiDAR point cloud (e.g., 16×1024 or 32×2048) and spherically projects it into a single-channel 2D range image where pixel values encode Euclidean distance. It produces a 4× vertically upsampled range image (e.g., 64×1024 or 128×2048) that is then inverted back into a 3D point cloud. Only vertical resolution is increased (horizontal resolution is preserved), reflecting the fact that rotating LiDARs have much lower vertical than horizontal beam density.

## Method

TULIP builds on a Swin-UNet encoder-decoder with skip connections but introduces two geometry-aware modifications tailored to range images: (1) row-based 1×4 patch partitioning instead of square patches, which preserves full vertical resolution at the tokenization stage and better captures boundary discontinuities between objects; and (2) non-square (rectangular) attention windows that are wider than tall, directing self-attention toward the information-rich horizontal dimension while maintaining the same computational cost as a large square window. Additional refinements include circular padding in the horizontal dimension to match the rotating LiDAR sensor model, patch unmerging and pixel shuffle layers for parameter-efficient upsampling (avoiding deconvolution smoothing), and Monte Carlo Dropout at inference to threshold and remove high-variance invalid points. The network is trained end-to-end with pixel-wise L1 loss on the range image.

## Results

On KITTI (4× upsampling, 64×1024 output), TULIP achieves MAE 0.4185, IoU 0.4174, and Chamfer Distance 0.1207, outperforming all baselines. On CARLA (128×2048), TULIP reaches MAE 0.7699, IoU 0.5152, and CD 0.1028. On the more challenging DurLAR dataset (128×2048, 45° FoV), TULIP attains IoU 0.3562 and CD 0.0648, with TULIP-L (larger variant) further improving to IoU 0.3654 and CD 0.0635. The larger TULIP-L model consistently improves over TULIP across all three datasets, e.g., reducing KITTI MAE from 0.4185 to 0.3708 and CD from 0.1207 to 0.0992.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TULIP outperforms all six baselines on all three datasets across the majority of metrics. The closest competitor is ILN [26], which achieves the highest IoU among baselines (0.5006 on CARLA, 0.3342 on KITTI, 0.3430 on DurLAR) but is consistently beaten by TULIP on all three metrics; on KITTI the gap is largest (IoU 0.4174 vs 0.3342, CD 0.1207 vs 0.2787). LIIF [10] is competitive on MAE (0.6143 on KITTI vs TULIP's 0.4185) but lags significantly on 3D metrics (IoU 0.3226 vs 0.4174, CD 0.1916 vs 0.1207), confirming that implicit image functions are unsuitable for geometric range data. LiDAR-SR [43] achieves the best baseline MAE on DurLAR (1.5312 vs TULIP's 1.5432) but produces the worst IoU on KITTI (0.1005) due to many invalid floating points. Among image super-resolution methods, SwinIR [29] and HAT [9] perform poorly on 3D metrics (CD 0.7347 and 0.2516 on KITTI respectively), while SRNO [51] is the weakest baseline overall, failing to even match bilinear interpolation on CARLA (MAE 2.4640 vs 1.8128). On DurLAR, where sensor noise and larger FoV make the task hardest, TULIP-L achieves the best CD (0.0635) and IoU (0.3654), with only a marginal MAE loss to LiDAR-SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

TULIP builds on the Swin Transformer's hierarchical windowed-attention architecture and the Swin-UNet encoder-decoder structure, adapting both specifically for LiDAR range image geometry. It departs from prior LiDAR upsampling methods (LiDAR-SR's CNN U-Net, ILN's implicit interpolation) by replacing convolutional or implicit backbones with a transformer, and from general image super-resolution methods (SwinIR, HAT, LIIF) by introducing row-based 1×4 patch partition, rectangular (non-square) attention windows, and circular horizontal padding to match the asymmetric, single-channel, vertically-sparse nature of range images. The paper explicitly positions itself as a geometry-aware modification of an existing architecture rather than a new architectural paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TULIP provides a strong, well-motivated baseline for LiDAR range image upsampling that outperforms both LiDAR-specific and general image SR methods across three datasets, with open-source code that lowers the barrier for follow-up work. The consistent finding that naive image SR (e.g., SwinIR) degrades on range images—sometimes below bilinear interpolation—highlights that geometric awareness in tokenization and attention is not optional for this task. Limitations include failure on irregular scenes, degraded performance beyond ~30 m where point density is extremely low, and restriction to vertical-only upsampling; whether the row-patch and rectangular-window ideas transfer to other panoramic or anisotropic sensing modalities remains open.
