## Problem

Existing one-shot 4D head synthesis methods train on monocular videos with 3DMM reconstruction providing pose and expression supervision, but monocular 3DMM estimation is inherently inaccurate, degrading geometry quality for large-pose reenactment. These methods also typically neglect independent eye-gaze and neck-pose control and cannot learn foreground-background separation. The paper asks whether large-scale synthetic multi-view data can replace this flawed monocular training pipeline entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source RGB portrait (appearance) and a single driving RGB portrait (motion/expression) as input, both at 512×512 resolution, and produces a feed-forward triplane NeRF (256×256×96) encoding a 3D head with the source's identity and the driving's motion. The triplane is rendered at arbitrary camera poses via volume rendering plus 2D super-resolution, yielding reenacted images with independent foreground animation and a separately predicted background. Inference runs at 10 FPS on a single A100 GPU with no per-image optimization.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, GenHead is a part-wise 3D GAN (StyleGAN2 backbone + FLAME-derived deformation fields) trained adversarially on monocular FFHQ images to synthesize photorealistic multi-view 4D head data with full control over face expression, eye gaze, jaw, and neck pose, using separate triplanes for the head and the eye/mouth regions. Second, Portrait4D is a transformer-based animatable triplane reconstructor that takes appearance features from two CNN encoders and identity-agnostic motion features from a pre-trained motion encoder, injecting motion via cross-attention layers in a canonicalization-and-reenactment module Φ. The key training insight is a disentangled learning strategy: at a fixed probability, all cross-attention outputs are zeroed out and the model is trained on static single-motion data, forcing the self-attention and MLP layers to learn pure 3D pose canonicalization while cross-attention layers handle all motion transfer. This prevents overfitting to synthetic identities and substantially improves generalizability to real images, with the entire pipeline trained end-to-end via self-reenacting synthetic identities against multi-view ground truth.

## Results

On VFHQ self-reenactment at 512², Portrait4D achieves the best FID (43.0 vs. 52.6 for Face-vid2vid), best head-region LPIPS (0.181), and best pose accuracy (APD 0.560), while remaining competitive on identity preservation (ID 0.790). Under large pose variation (yaw >30°), the advantage widens: FID drops to 43.0 overall with APD of 1.340, compared to 7.879 (HideNeRF) and 24.99 (Face-vid2vid). In cross-identity reenactment, Portrait4D attains the best FID (54.8), highest identity similarity (ID 0.620), and best pose accuracy (APD 1.020) among all six baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three 2D-based baselines, Portrait4D achieves substantially lower FID (43.0 vs. 52.6–67.3) and better pose accuracy (APD 0.560 vs. 0.778–1.392), while Face-vid2vid retains a slight edge on full-image LPIPS (0.290 vs. 0.320) and identity (0.848 vs. 0.790). The gap widens dramatically under large yaw differences (>30°), where Face-vid2vid's APD explodes to 24.99 and PIRenderer's to 9.326, versus 1.340 for Portrait4D. Against the three 3D-based baselines (ROME, OTAvatar, HideNeRF), Portrait4D dominates on every metric: FID improves by 37.5–69 points, LPIPS_h by 0.093–0.146, and APD by 0.40–2.75. In cross-identity reenactment (Table 2), Portrait4D leads on FID (54.8 vs. 78.0–107), ID (0.620 vs. 0.350–0.606), and APD (1.020 vs. 2.936–7.771), though PIRenderer and ROME achieve marginally lower AED (0.146/0.148 vs. 0.164) at the cost of identity leakage. The authors attribute the 3D baselines' weaker performance to reliance on imperfect monocular 3DMM reconstructions for geometry guidance, which Portrait4D avoids by training entirely on synthetic multi-view data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in one-shot 4D head synthesis (ROME, OTAvatar, HideNeRF), which relies on monocular 3DMM reconstruction to provide geometry and pose supervision during training. Instead, it introduces a two-stage pipeline: first training a part-wise 4D generative model (GenHead) on monocular images via adversarial learning to produce multi-view synthetic data with full motion control, then training a transformer-based triplane reconstructor on that synthetic data in a feed-forward manner. The architecture draws on EG3D's triplane NeRF representation, PanoHead's encoder-decoder structure, and a pre-trained identity-agnostic motion encoder, but the key conceptual shift is replacing 3DMM-derived supervision with GAN-generated multi-view ground truth, thereby eliminating the 3DMM error bottleneck at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 4D GAN can serve as a synthetic data engine for training a generalizable feed-forward 4D head reconstructor, this work opens a scalable path toward photorealistic head avatar creation that avoids the ill-posedness of monocular 3DMM estimation. The disentangled learning strategy (randomly zeroing cross-attention layers to isolate 3D reconstruction from reenactment) is a practical and broadly applicable training trick for improving generalizability from synthetic to real data. Open questions include whether this synthetic-data-then-reconstruct pattern extends to full-body avatars or other object categories, whether the domain gap can be further closed without the two-stage cost, and whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or more diverse identities beyond FFHQ.
