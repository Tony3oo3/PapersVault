## Problem

Neural radiance fields achieve state-of-the-art view synthesis quality but render slowly because volumetric rendering requires many samples per ray. Surface-based representations (e.g., SDFs) are far more efficient but struggle with semi-opaque, thin, and view-dependent structures. No prior method efficiently handles both surface-like and genuinely volumetric regions within a single model at real-time VR resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240196/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of high-resolution HDR RGB images (2K) with known camera poses from multi-view captures and produces a hybrid surface-volume 3D representation that renders novel views at 2K×2K (VR) resolution at ≥36 FPS. Colors are processed in PQ color space during training to handle wide dynamic range, then tonemapped to sRGB for evaluation. The representation encodes geometry as a signed distance field with spatially-varying surfaceness, stored in multi-resolution 3D feature grids and triplanes.

## Method

HybridNeRF's key insight is replacing the global surfaceness parameter β (which controls how sharply density concentrates around the SDF zero-level set) with a spatially-varying field β(x) stored in a 512³ voxel grid. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a VolSDF-like model is trained with a uniform β and a distance-adjusted Eikonal loss (η_i = 1/d_i²) that downweights far-field regions, allowing the same MLP to behave as a valid SDF in the foreground and a volumetric NeRF in the background without a separate background model. During finetuning, β(x) is increased per-voxel only where the accumulated Eikonal loss stays below a threshold, so most of the scene (>95%) becomes a thin surface needing few samples while challenging regions retain volumetric flexibility. At render time, sphere tracing is used where β(x) > 350 (valid SDF regions), hardware texture interpolation reduces feature lookups from 24 to 8 per sample, and the SDF MLP is distilled from 256 to 16 channels for speed.

## Results

On the Eyeful Tower dataset (the primary evaluation), HybridNeRF achieves 31.57 PSNR, 0.913 SSIM, and 0.198 LPIPS overall, while rendering at 45.78 FPS at 2K×2K resolution on an RTX 4090. This makes it the only method to simultaneously exceed the 36 FPS VR target and achieve top-tier quality, outperforming Zip-NeRF (31.75 PSNR) by a narrow margin on SSIM and LPIPS while rendering >400× faster (45.78 vs <0.1 FPS). On MipNeRF 360, HybridNeRF reaches 27.52 PSNR overall, competitive with Zip-NeRF (28.49) and 3DGS (27.16). On ScanNet++, it achieves 24.64 PSNR at 41.90 FPS, near-identical to Zip-NeRF (24.79 PSNR) while rendering >400× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridNeRF outperforms iNGP by 1.51 dB PSNR and 10× in speed (45.78 vs 4.55 FPS), and beats VolSDF by 1.29 dB PSNR and 3× in speed, confirming that adaptive surfaceness adds substantial value over a fixed-β SDF. Against MERF (baked), HybridNeRF gains 3.26 dB PSNR and 0.337 LPIPS improvement, though MERF is slightly faster (60.18 vs 45.78 FPS); MERF's baking pipeline sacrifices significant quality for speed. 3D Gaussian Splatting renders 3× faster (138 vs 45.78 FPS) but cannot handle fisheye projections and produces visible floaters from lighting inconsistencies, yielding only 27.42 PSNR on pinhole views versus HybridNeRF's 29.07. Zip-NeRF achieves marginally higher PSNR (31.75 vs 31.57) but is the only method HybridNeRF does not clearly surpass on quality; however, HybridNeRF wins on SSIM (0.913 vs 0.909) and LPIPS (0.198 vs 0.216) while rendering >400× faster. On MipNeRF 360, HybridNeRF (27.52 PSNR) is competitive with both real-time (3DGS: 27.16) and offline (Zip-NeRF: 28.49) methods, and on ScanNet++ it matches Zip-NeRF quality (24.64 vs 24.79 PSNR) at 41.90 FPS versus <0.1 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

HybridNeRF extends the VolSDF line of work (which converts SDF outputs to densities via a global temperature β) by making β spatially adaptive, guided by the Eikonal loss as a natural indicator of where the scene cannot be faithfully represented as a surface. It inherits the ray-casting paradigm from NeRF and the multi-resolution feature grid architecture from iNGP/K-Planes, but departs from both by using explicit 3D grids and triplanes (enabling hardware texture interpolation) and by employing sphere tracing instead of uniform or proposal-based sampling. The method also draws on MERF's baking protocol for the proposal network and occupancy grid, while explicitly positioning itself against rasterization-based approaches like 3D Gaussian Splatting that sacrifice view-dependent modeling for speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single neural model can simultaneously behave as a valid SDF in >95% of the scene and as a flexible volumetric field in challenging regions, eliminating the need for separate foreground/background models or mesh baking. This is practically significant for VR/AR applications where 2K×2K rendering at 36+ FPS is a hard requirement, and the 15–30% error reduction over prior real-time methods while matching offline quality is meaningful. However, a substantial speed gap remains relative to 3D Gaussian Splatting (45 vs. 138 FPS), and the dense grid storage imposes higher memory costs than hash-table-based methods. The authors explicitly identify combining the surface-volume representation with splatting-based rendering as a promising next step, and the memory/training-time trade-offs of dense multi-resolution grids remain an open practical concern.
