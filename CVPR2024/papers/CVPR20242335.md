## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised oriented object detection methods rely on horizontal box or image-level annotations that remain labor-intensive, while single point supervision—roughly 50% cheaper than HBB and 105% cheaper than OBB—has never been used to directly generate oriented bounding boxes. Moreover, the Multiple Instance Learning paradigm underlying point-supervised detection suffers from inherent instability in perceiving exact object scale and boundary, and learning object orientation from a single point without any angle supervision is an entirely unexplored problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes aerial RGB images (800×800 for DIOR-R, 1024×1024 cropped patches for DOTA) annotated with a single point per object, where the point is randomly sampled within a 10% height-and-width region around the OBB center to simulate realistic annotation noise. The output is a set of pseudo oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle, category) that are subsequently used to train standard fully-supervised oriented detectors such as Rotated FCOS or Oriented R-CNN at a 1× schedule.

## Method

PointOBB constructs three views—original, randomly resized (scale factor σ ∈ [0.5, 1.5]), and randomly rotated/flipped (95:5 ratio)—and switches between them via a three-stage progressive multi-view strategy. In stage 1, a Scale-Sensitive Consistency (SSC) loss aligns the score distributions of proposals across the original and resized views by reshaping scores into scale bins and minimizing cosine-distance, directly addressing MIL's tendency to over-confidently select proposals that do not match true object scale. In stages 2–3, a self-supervised angle branch on the rot/flp view learns orientation through a Self-Supervised Angle (SSA) loss that enforces the known affine relationship between views, with dense-to-dense sample assignment on feature-pyramid grid points around the ground-truth point. A Dense-to-Sparse (DS) matching strategy then hierarchically pairs feature-pyramid levels with proposal scale levels to aggregate dense angle predictions into per-proposal orientations, producing final OBBs through a refined MIL head.

## Results

On DIOR-R, PointOBB achieves mAP50 of 37.31% (Rotated FCOS) and 38.08% (Oriented R-CNN), with 8-mAP50 of 54.80% and 57.38% respectively, significantly outperforming the best point-supervised baseline (P2BNet+H2RBox-v2 at 23.61% mAP50) by 14.47%. On DOTA-v1.0, PointOBB with Oriented R-CNN reaches 33.31% mAP50 and 49.01% 7-mAP50, surpassing the best point-supervised cascade (P2BNet+H2RBox-v2 at 21.87%) by 11.44%. Notably, the 8-mAP50 of 57.38% on DIOR-R exceeds 85% of the fully-supervised Oriented R-CNN (69.88%), demonstrating the strong potential of single-point supervision for oriented detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointOBB decisively outperforms all point-supervised baselines: it beats P2BNet+H2RBox-v2 (the strongest point-supervised cascade) by 14.47% mAP50 on DIOR-R (38.08% vs. 23.61%) and by 11.44% on DOTA-v1.0 (33.31% vs. 21.87%), and surpasses Point2Mask-RBox by over 24% mAP50 on DIOR-R. It also substantially outperforms the image-supervised WSODet (22.20% mAP50) by 15.88% on DIOR-R. However, PointOBB still trails HBox-supervised methods (H2RBox at 57.80%, H2RBox-v2 at 57.64%) and RBox-supervised methods (Oriented R-CNN at 62.80%) on DIOR-R mAP50, reflecting the inherent information gap between point and box annotations. The paper highlights that despite using only single-point labels, PointOBB achieves over 85% of fully-supervised performance on the 8 major remote-sensing categories (8-mAP50: 57.38% vs. 69.88%), positioning it as a practical middle ground between expensive OBB annotation and weaker image-level supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointOBB extends the MIL-based point-supervised detection paradigm (OICR, P2BNet) from horizontal boxes to oriented bounding boxes, while borrowing the symmetry-based self-supervised angle learning strategy from H2RBox-v2 and adapting it to the absence of scale information. It departs from prior point-supervised methods (P2BNet, Click) that only produce HBBs, and from HBox-supervised oriented detectors (H2RBox, H2RBox-v2) that still require box-level annotations, by introducing a multi-view framework (original, resized, rot/flp) with a Scale-Sensitive Consistency loss and a Dense-to-Sparse matching strategy to jointly learn scale and orientation from a single point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a genuine gap in the weakly-supervised oriented detection literature by demonstrating that single point labels—roughly 50% cheaper than HBB and 105% cheaper than OBB annotations—can yield pseudo OBBs achieving over 85% of fully-supervised performance on major aerial categories. However, the method operates as a two-stage pipeline (pseudo-label generation followed by standard detector training), and performance remains suboptimal for categories with ambiguous boundaries (e.g., Bridge, Golf Field, Overpass), suggesting that contextual or structural features beyond local appearance are needed. Open questions include whether the multi-view switching strategy generalizes to other weakly-supervised settings and whether the pseudo-label quality can be further improved without additional annotation forms.
