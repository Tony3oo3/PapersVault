## Problem

Diffusion-based virtual try-on methods rely on additional image encoders (CLIP, ViT, or extra UNets) with cross-attention for garment-to-person texture transfer, which loses fine-grained textures and increases computational cost. Additionally, existing approaches use roughly estimated inpainting masks that remove garment-irrelevant body details such as tattoos and muscle structures, degrading the fidelity of synthesized results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image S, a reference garment image C*, a pose map c_p, and a dense pose c_d, all resized to 512×384 pixels. The garment and masked person images are concatenated along the spatial dimension (not channel dimension) before being fed into the UNet. Training uses paired data <S, C> where the person already wears the reference garment, while inference operates on unpaired <S, C*> data. The model outputs both a synthesized try-on image and a predicted inpainting mask via an additional output channel in the UNet.

## Method

The core contribution is a Self-Attention-based Texture Transfer (SATT) strategy that discards external garment encoders and cross-attention entirely. Instead, the reference garment and masked person image are concatenated along the spatial dimension and jointly fed into the Stable Diffusion denoising UNet, so that the UNet's inherent multi-resolution self-attention blocks capture long-range pixel correlations and transfer garment textures to the person area within a single forward pass. A second contribution, Decoupled Mask Prediction (DMP), adds an extra output channel to the UNet that predicts the new garment's clothing area on the person in a two-stage pipeline: the first stage denoises a coarse mask from random noise, and the second stage takes the union of this predicted mask with the human-parsing-derived original clothing area to form a precise inpainting mask. During training, the initial mask is augmented by random interpolation between the true clothing area and its bounding box, making the model robust to both coarse and accurate masks encountered across the two inference stages.

## Results

On VITON, TPD achieves SSIM 0.89, FID 9.58, and LPIPS 0.07, outperforming the best baseline PF-AFN (SSIM 0.87, FID 10.09, LPIPS 0.08). On VITON-HD, TPD achieves SSIM 0.90, FID 8.54, and LPIPS 0.07, surpassing the strongest diffusion-based competitor DCI-VTON (SSIM 0.88, FID 8.78, LPIPS 0.08). The method also demonstrates strong person-to-person try-on results on DeepFashion, where it preserves body details (fingers, arms, tattoos) that are lost by competing methods using coarse inpainting masks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPD outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric across both VITON and VITON-HD. Against the strongest warping-free diffusion baselines, TPD improves FID by 0.24 over DCI-VTON (8.54 vs. 8.78) and by 0.87 over LaDI-VTON (8.54 vs. 9.41) on VITON-HD, while also achieving the best SSIM (0.90 vs. 0.88) and LPIPS (0.07 vs. 0.08). Against warping-based methods, the margins are larger: TPD reduces FID from 10.09 (PF-AFN) to 9.58 on VITON and from 11.30 (PF-AFN) to 8.54 on VITON-HD. The diffusion-based inpainting method Paint-by-Example [21] lags behind TPD by 0.05–0.06 in SSIM and 3.5–3.6 in FID on VITON-HD, confirming that the self-attention-based texture transfer (SATT) and decoupled mask prediction (DMP) jointly contribute to the gains. Qualitatively, TPD preserves fine-grained textures (logos, text patterns) and body-part details (fingers, tattoos) that are distorted or lost by all compared methods, particularly under challenging poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework and the inpainting formulation from Paint-by-Example, but departs sharply from the prevailing warping-free try-on paradigm (DCI-VTON, LaDI-VTON, TryOnDiffusion) that relies on cross-attention with specialized garment encoders (CLIP image encoder, ViT, or an auxiliary UNet). Instead, TPD repurposes the self-attention blocks already present in the denoising UNet by spatially concatenating the masked person and reference garment images, turning the garment into in-image context for texture transfer without any additional encoder. The Decoupled Mask Prediction (DMP) component further distinguishes the work by integrating mask prediction into the diffusion denoising process itself, rather than relying on a fixed or roughly estimated inpainting mask as in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial-concatenation trick offers a simpler, lower-compute alternative to the cross-attention-plus-encoder pipeline that has become standard in diffusion-based try-on, and the same idea could transfer to other diffusion-based image editing tasks (inpainting, image-to-image translation) where a reference image must inform the generation. The DMP approach of predicting garment-specific masks through the diffusion process is a practical step toward preserving identity details (tattoos, fingers, body contours) that fixed masks inevitably destroy. Key open questions include performance on complex, non-uniform backgrounds (acknowledged as a limitation), scalability to full-body or multi-garment try-on, and whether the self-attention-based transfer generalizes to tasks with even larger spatial misalignment between reference and target.
