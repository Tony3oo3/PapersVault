## Problem

Textual Inversion-based face personalization suffers from severe overfitting, where the model reconstructs the input image rather than placing the individual in novel scenes or styles. The paper identifies the root cause through the lens of initialization: traditional super-category token initialization creates a gap of up to 100× in scale between the initial and learned embeddings, forcing many optimization steps that increase overfitting risk and degrade editability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 face image and a free-form text prompt (e.g., expression editing, background change, interaction with named individuals, or artistic style) as input, and produces personalized face images preserving the subject's identity while following the prompt. Personalization is performed on a single A800 GPU with batch size 8; the full method requires 320 optimization steps (~346 s), while a fast variant with a higher learning rate (0.08) completes in 25 steps (~26 s). Evaluation uses 200 CelebA-HQ test images with 20 prompts across four editing categories, generating 32 images per prompt.

## Method

Cross Initialization replaces the standard super-category token initialization by first computing a mean embedding v̄₆₉₁ from 691 well-known names (split into first- and last-name tokens), then feeding it through the CLIP text encoder E and using the output E(v̄₆₉₁) as the initial textual embedding v_init. This works because the learned embedding naturally converges toward the text encoder's output in both scale and orientation (due to LayerNorm and self-attention layers progressively normalizing the vector), so starting near that fixed point drastically reduces the optimization distance. The method optimizes the input vector v_init (not the encoder output directly), preserving cross-attention interaction with other prompt tokens for compositional editing. An L2 regularization term (λ=10⁻⁵) keeps the learned embedding close to v_init throughout training, which becomes effective precisely because Cross Initialization has already narrowed the initial-to-learned gap. Together these components reduce optimization from 5,000 to 320 steps while improving both identity preservation and prompt alignment.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ (200 test images, 20 prompts, 32 images per prompt), the full method achieves an identity similarity of 0.2517 and prompt similarity of 0.2859, outperforming all baselines on the combined metric. The fast variant (Ours-fast) completes personalization in just 26 seconds while still surpassing Celeb Basis and Textual Inversion on both identity and prompt scores. The full method reduces optimization time from 6,331 s (Textual Inversion) to 346 s (~18× speedup) while improving both reconstruction and editability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Textual Inversion, the proposed method wins on both identity (0.2517 vs. 0.2115) and prompt similarity (0.2859 vs. 0.2498) while being ~18× faster (346 s vs. 6,331 s). Against DreamBooth, the method achieves higher identity similarity (0.2517 vs. 0.2053) but slightly lower prompt similarity (0.2859 vs. 0.3015), reflecting DreamBooth's tendency to ignore the personalized concept in favor of the remaining prompt tokens. Against NeTI, the method trades identity (0.2517 vs. 0.3789) for substantially better prompt alignment (0.2859 vs. 0.2325), since NeTI overfits the input image and fails to incorporate other prompt tokens. Against Celeb Basis, the method wins on both identity (0.2517 vs. 0.2070) and prompt (0.2859 vs. 0.2683), and the fast variant is ~5× faster (26 s vs. 140 s). A user study (600 responses, 30 participants) confirms a strong human preference for the proposed method over all four baselines (73–91% preference).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Textual Inversion [Gal et al., 2022] by rethinking how the new-concept embedding is initialized, and borrows the 691-name mean embedding construction from Celeb Basis [Yuan et al., NeurIPS 2023] as the starting point for its Cross Initialization scheme. Rather than fine-tuning the diffusion model (DreamBooth) or training a separate encoder (NeTI), it stays within the lightweight textual-embedding-optimization paradigm but argues that the conventional super-category-token initialization is fundamentally misaligned with where the learned embedding ends up. The key conceptual departure is treating the CLIP text encoder's own output as a natural fixed point for initialization, thereby collapsing the optimization trajectory and making a simple L2 regularization term effective for the first time in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: personalization drops from ~106 minutes to 6 minutes (or 26 seconds in the fast variant) while simultaneously improving both identity preservation and prompt alignment over all four baselines. However, the authors explicitly note that Cross Initialization is less effective for general (non-face) concepts, leaving open whether the text-encoder fixed-point property generalizes beyond the human-face subspace. A broader question is whether the observed 100× scale divergence in standard Textual Inversion is specific to CLIP-based encoders or reflects a more general pathology in embedding-space optimization that could inform initialization strategies for other personalization or prompt-tuning tasks.
