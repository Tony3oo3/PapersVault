## Problem

Existing watermarking methods for diffusion models inevitably compromise generative performance: post-processing degrades image quality, fine-tuning-based approaches modify model parameters and add computational overhead, and latent-representation methods like Tree-Ring restrict sampling randomness. The fundamental question this paper tackles is whether watermarks can be embedded into diffusion model outputs without any measurable degradation in model performance, while remaining training-free and plug-and-play.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a k-bit binary watermark (up to 256 bits with default settings f_c=1, f_hw=8, l=1), and a secret stream key K, and produces a 512×512 watermarked image indistinguishable from a normally generated one. Extraction requires only the watermarked image and the stream key: the image is encoded back to latent space, inverted via DDIM (50 steps, empty prompt, guidance scale 1) to recover the initial latent representation, and the watermark bits are decoded by inverting the quantile mapping and decrypting with K. The watermark is replicated f_c·f_hw² times across the 4×64×64 latent tensor, and majority voting over copies yields the final bit sequence.

## Method

Gaussian Shading embeds watermarks through three stages applied to the initial latent representation z_T before denoising: (1) watermark diffusion replicates the k-bit watermark f_c·f_hw² times across the full latent tensor to spread information throughout image semantics; (2) watermark randomization encrypts the diffused watermark with ChaCha20 under key K, producing a pseudorandom bit stream m; (3) distribution-preserving sampling maps each l-bit group of m to a quantile interval of N(0,I) and samples z_T^s from the corresponding conditional distribution via the inverse CDF, so that the marginal distribution of z_T^s remains exactly N(0,I). The paper provides a proof by contradiction (reducing distinguishability of watermarked vs. clean images to breaking ChaCha20's pseudorandomness) establishing performance-losslessness under chosen-watermark tests. No model parameters are modified and no training is required; the watermark is extracted by DDIM inversion followed by the inverse quantile mapping and decryption, with majority voting over replicated copies for robustness.

## Results

Under adversarial noise (average of 9 noise types), Gaussian Shading achieves a TPR of 0.997–0.998 and bit accuracy of 0.972–0.975 across SD V1.4/V2.0/V2.1, substantially outperforming all baselines. In the detection scenario at FPR=10⁻⁶, TPR remains ≥0.99 for 8 of 9 noise types; in traceability with N=10⁶ users, accuracy is near-perfect for 7 of 9 noise types. The t-test on FID (t=0.3567) and CLIP-Score (t=0.6870) yields the smallest t-values among all methods, confirming the performance-lossless property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gaussian Shading dominates all four baselines on adversarial robustness. Against the strongest baseline RivaGAN (Bit Acc. Adv. ≈0.90), Gaussian Shading achieves ≈0.975, a ~7 percentage-point gain; against Tree-Ring (TPR Adv. ≈0.90), it reaches ≈0.997. The post-processing methods DwtDct and DwtDctSvd collapse under adversarial noise (TPR drops to 0.17 and 0.60 respectively), while Stable Signature (fine-tuning-based) also degrades to TPR ≈0.50. On performance preservation, Gaussian Shading's t-values (FID: 0.36, CLIP: 0.69) are the smallest, meaning its watermarked images are statistically indistinguishable from watermark-free ones, whereas RivaGAN (t=12.29) and DwtDctSvd (t=8.25) show significant FID shifts. Tree-Ring and Stable Signature have moderate t-values (≈2.5) but still exhibit measurable performance bias compared to Gaussian Shading.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and DDIM inversion (Song et al., ICLR 2020) as its operational backbone, but departs fundamentally from all prior diffusion-model watermarking approaches. Post-processing methods (DwtDct, RivaGAN) alter the image directly, fine-tuning methods (Stable Signature, backdoor-based) modify model parameters, and Tree-Ring reshapes the latent noise distribution in the frequency domain—each at the cost of model performance. Gaussian Shading instead preserves the exact Gaussian distribution of the initial latent noise by using an encrypted watermark to drive distribution-preserving sampling via the inverse CDF, drawing a conceptual link to steganographic security definitions (Hopper et al., CRYPTO 2002) for its formal proof of indistinguishability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably performance-lossless watermarking method for diffusion models, offering operators a plug-and-play, training-free mechanism for both copyright detection and user-level traceability (256-bit capacity) without any degradation in FID or CLIP-Score. Key limitations remain: extraction requires ODE-based samplers (excluding SDE-based ones), the model must remain non-public to resist white-box and forgery attacks, and the stream cipher introduces key-management overhead. Open questions include extending the approach to SDE samplers, developing more efficient inversion methods, and designing defenses against the demonstrated forgery attack where an attacker can frame an innocent user.
