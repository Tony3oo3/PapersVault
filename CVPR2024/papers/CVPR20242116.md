## Problem

Existing generalizable 6D pose refinement methods for novel objects (e.g., OSOP, MegaPose) only use the target object's 3D shape information indirectly—through surrogate 2D-2D matching losses or relative pose regression that ignores projective geometry—making them suboptimal. The paper asks whether a better refinement design exists that explicitly leverages the 3D shape while maintaining generalization to unseen objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB (or RGB-D) image, a 3D CAD model of the target object (unseen during training), and a coarse 6D pose hypothesis as input. The image crop and rendered image are both resized to 256×256 with adjusted intrinsics. It outputs a refined 6D pose (rotation and translation), dense 2D optical flow between the rendered and observed images, and confidence weights factorized into certainty (occlusion-aware) and pose sensitivity (texture-aware) components. The refinement is iterative: each outer update consumes the previous pose estimate and produces an improved one.

## Method

GenFlow is a shape-constrained recurrent flow refiner built on a ConvGRU-based module that iteratively updates optical flow, confidence weights, and 6D pose. The key mechanism is a correlation lookup indexed by pose-induced flow (computed by lifting rendered-image pixels to 3D via the known depth and reprojecting under the current pose) rather than the estimated flow, which embeds the target's 3D shape into the matching process without memorizing it. A differentiable Levenberg-Marquardt PnP solver converts weighted 2D-3D correspondences into a pose update, with a regularization trick (detached intermediate pose + one Gauss-Newton step) to enable stable end-to-end backpropagation. A cascade architecture chains two GenFlow modules at different feature pyramid resolutions for coarse-to-fine refinement, and the confidence weights are explicitly factorized into a certainty term (supervised by depth-consistency classification for occlusion robustness) and a pose-sensitivity term (highlighting texture-rich regions). The entire system is trained end-to-end on the 2M-image MegaPose synthetic dataset (ShapeNet + GSO) with flow L1 loss, disentangled pose loss, and binary cross-entropy certainty loss.

## Results

On the BOP challenge unseen object pose estimation benchmarks, GenFlow achieves a mean AR of 57.8 for RGB input and 67.2 for RGB-D input, outperforming MegaPose (54.9 and 62.8 respectively) by 2.9 and 4.4 points. For seen objects with RGB input, GenFlow reaches 71.3 mean AR without fine-tuning, competitive with ZebraPose (72.0) and surpassing MegaPose (67.5). For seen objects with RGB-D input, GenFlow achieves 79.4 mean AR, approaching the best seen-object method WDR-Pose+PFA (80.0) while exceeding MegaPose+Teaser++ (74.4) by 5.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For novel objects with RGB input, GenFlow (57.8 mean AR) outperforms MegaPose+MH (54.9) by 2.9 points and OSOP (which only reports partial results) substantially. For novel objects with RGB-D input, GenFlow+Kabsch+MH (67.2) beats MegaPose+MH+Teaser++ (62.8) by 4.4 points and ZeroPose+MegaPose (57.0) by 10.2 points. Among seen-object RGB baselines, GenFlow (71.3) surpasses MegaPose (67.5) by 3.8 points and CosyPose (57.0) by 14.3 points, but falls slightly short of ZebraPose (72.0) which uses a dedicated per-object training pipeline. For seen-object RGB-D, GenFlow (79.4) is within 0.6 points of the best method WDR-Pose+PFA+Kabsch (80.0) and exceeds MegaPose+Teaser++ (74.4) by 5.0 points. Notably, GenFlow achieves these seen-object results without any fine-tuning on target datasets, relying solely on the generalizable training on synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenFlow extends the shape-constraint recurrent flow framework of SCFlow [20]—originally designed for seen-object pose estimation—into the generalizable (novel-object) setting by replacing SCFlow's pose regressor with a differentiable PnP solver, thereby incorporating projective geometry that prior generalizable refiners (MegaPose, OSOP) lack. The recurrent flow architecture (ConvGRU, correlation volumes, convex-combination upsampling) is inherited from RAFT [61], while the iterative render-and-compare pipeline and coarse-to-fine refinement strategy follow the DeepIM/CosyPose/MegaPose lineage. The GMM-based hypothesis sampling and cascade multi-scale design are novel contributions that address the rendering cost and accuracy limitations of prior generalizable methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenFlow demonstrates that explicitly leveraging 3D shape information through a differentiable PnP layer is compatible with generalization to novel objects, resolving a trade-off that prior generalizable methods (MegaPose, OSOP) handled only indirectly. The practical impact is significant: it achieves SOTA on BOP unseen-object benchmarks for both RGB and RGB-D, and the GMM sampling reduces coarse-estimation renderings by ~64% while improving accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to CAD-model-free settings, whether the cascade depth can be increased further, and how the method scales to multi-object scenes with heavy occlusion beyond the single-object crop assumption.
