## Problem

Oriented object detectors require massive fully-annotated data with precise oriented bounding boxes, which is prohibitively expensive. Existing semi-supervised methods still demand full box annotations on a subset, while weakly supervised point annotations introduce ambiguity in depicting oriented objects (orientation-agnostic, location-ambiguous, scale-unconstrained). This paper tackles the gap of leveraging cheap point annotations on a large image pool to enhance oriented detection when only a small fraction of images carry full box annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242237/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes aerial images (1024×1024 patches from DOTA, 800×800 from DIOR-R) in two forms: a small subset with full oriented box + category annotations, and a large subset with only point locations + category labels. Point annotations are randomly sampled inside ground-truth oriented boxes and carry no orientation or scale information. The model outputs oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle) with category predictions, evaluated by mAP on held-out validation/test sets.

## Method

The framework uses a Mean Teacher-style teacher-student setup (Faster R-CNN + ResNet-50 + FPN) where the teacher generates pseudo annotations for point-annotated images and is updated via EMA (0.999). The core novelty is a Rotation-Modulated Relational Graph Matching loss: proposals centered on each annotated point form a cluster, RRoI features serve as graph vertices, and cosine similarities between vertices form edges; both vertex features and edge matrices are matched between teacher and student under different random-rotation augmented views, weighted by a modulated orientation difference to emphasize predictions with large angular disagreement. A complementary Relational Rank Distribution Matching loss aligns within-cluster rank distributions on classification logits and on pseudo RIoU-based regression scores. Finally, weakly supervised learning handles difficult points via multi-instance learning (bag-level cross-entropy) for clusters where all proposals are underrated, and regression refinement (extending/contracting box sides) for predictions that incorrectly enclose or exclude annotated points.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0 under the partially-annotated setting with only 5% fully-annotated images, the proposed method achieves 53.85 mAP, surpassing the closest weakly semi-supervised competitor Group R-CNN (51.24) by 2.61 mAP and the best semi-supervised method Soft Teacher (44.11) by 9.74 mAP. On the more challenging DOTA-v1.5 at 5% annotation, the gap widens dramatically to 46.29 mAP vs. Group R-CNN's 33.99 mAP (+12.30). Under the fully-annotated setting with additional point-annotated data, the method reaches 74.56 mAP on DOTA-v1.0 and 66.13 mAP on DOTA-v1.5, outperforming all baselines. On DIOR-R at 5% annotation, the method achieves 50.76 mAP, leading Group R-CNN (48.33) by 2.43 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting on both DOTA and DIOR-R. Against the two fully-supervised baselines (FCOS and Faster R-CNN), the gap is largest at low annotation ratios: at 5% on DOTA-v1.5, the method exceeds Faster R-CNN by 13.88 mAP (46.29 vs. 32.41). Among the three semi-supervised baselines (Dense Teacher, Soft Teacher, SOOD), the method leads by 3.74–11.27 mAP at 5% annotation on DOTA-v1.0, with the advantage narrowing at higher annotation ratios. The most direct comparison is against Group R-CNN, the only other weakly semi-supervised method using point annotations; the proposed method wins by 2.43–12.30 mAP depending on dataset and annotation ratio, with the largest margin on DOTA-v1.5 at 5% (46.29 vs. 33.99). Under the fully-annotated setting, the method still leads Group R-CNN by 1.46 mAP on DOTA-v1.0 and 0.55 mAP on DOTA-v1.5, confirming consistent gains even when abundant box annotations are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: semi-supervised object detection (Mean Teacher, Soft Teacher, Dense Teacher), weakly supervised detection with point annotations (Group R-CNN, Point-Teaching), and oriented object detection (RRoI Align, rotated Faster R-CNN). It departs from prior semi-supervised oriented detection (SOOD) by replacing pseudo-labels with point-annotated weak supervision, and from prior weakly semi-supervised generic detection (Group R-CNN) by introducing rotation-modulated relational graph matching to handle the specific ambiguity that point annotations are orientation-agnostic and scale-unconstrained for oriented objects. The teacher-student EMA framework and asymmetric augmentation strategy are inherited directly from the semi-supervised detection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical annotation-cost reduction for oriented object detection in aerial/remote sensing scenarios, where annotating rotated bounding boxes is expensive and point annotations are readily available. The relational graph matching idea—encoding contextual relations among proposals around a point to disambiguate weak supervision—is a transferable principle that could inform other weakly-supervised detection settings. However, the evaluation is confined to two aerial datasets (DOTA, DIOR-R) and a single base architecture (Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50/FPN), leaving open whether the relational matching generalizes to generic object detection, other backbones, or denser scenes. The multiple hand-tuned loss weights and thresholds (τ_m, λ factors) also raise questions about robustness across datasets.
