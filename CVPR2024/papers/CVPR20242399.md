## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods either require many views with known camera poses (optimization-based approaches like NeRF) or are limited to a fixed single input view with 2D generative priors (diffusion-based methods like Zero-1-to-3). No prior system can accept a variable number of unposed images and progressively improve its 3D understanding as more views of the object are provided, leaving a gap between single-view and multi-view reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242399/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an arbitrary number k ≥ 1 of unposed RGB images of a single object, with no camera pose information required. It produces a 3D reconstruction as an InstantNGP NeRF, novel views sampled directly from the adapted diffusion model, and refined relative camera poses (parameterized by azimuth, elevation, and scale — 3 DoF inherited from Zero-1-to-3). When k=1, the system reduces to standard Zero-1-to-3 with no test-time adaptation; for k≥2, all k(k−1) image pairs are used during fine-tuning.

## Method

SAP3D is a three-stage pipeline. First, coarse relative camera poses are estimated via RelPose++ retrained on Objaverse (~800K objects) for better generalization, and a pre-trained view-conditioned diffusion model (Zero-1-to-3, based on Stable Diffusion in VAE latent space) provides implicit 3D priors. Second, during test-time optimization, the diffusion model is fine-tuned on all input image pairs while simultaneously backpropagating gradients to refine the camera pose parameters; a CLIP-retrieved 3D prior preservation loss (50 nearest neighbors from Objaverse) regularizes against catastrophic forgetting. Third, the instance-specific diffusion model is distilled into a NeRF via score distillation sampling (SDS) loss combined with photometric data loss and 3D shape regularization, while novel view synthesis uses stochastic conditioning to sample a different input image and camera pose at each denoising step. The key insight is that joint fine-tuning of the generative prior and camera poses at test time creates an instance-specific model whose 3D understanding improves monotonically with the number of input views.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (20 objects), SAP3D's 3D reconstruction quality improves monotonically with more input views. At 5 views, SAP3D achieves LPIPS 0.11, PSNR 19.8 dB, SSIM 0.86, Chamfer Distance 0.019, and VolumeIoU 0.68, compared to the single-view Zero-1-to-3 baseline (LPIPS 0.23, PSNR 14.1, SSIM 0.82, CD 0.168, VolumeIoU 0.25). For 2D novel view synthesis, 5 views yield LPIPS 0.10, PSNR 19.5 dB, SSIM 0.85 versus 0.23/15.2/0.79 for the single-view baseline. The largest gain occurs between 1 and 2 views (where test-time adaptation is first applied), with diminishing returns saturating around 4 views for 3D reconstruction but not yet for 2D NVS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only explicit numerical baseline in the main results is Zero-1-to-3 [Liu et al., ICCV 2023], which serves as the 1-view reference (no test-time adaptation is possible with a single image). SAP3D at 5 views outperforms this baseline by a wide margin on every metric: PSNR improves from 14.1 to 19.8 dB (+5.7 dB), LPIPS drops from 0.23 to 0.11 (halved), Chamfer Distance drops from 0.168 to 0.019 (≈9× reduction), and VolumeIoU rises from 0.25 to 0.68 (nearly 3×). Even at just 2 views, SAP3D already surpasses the single-view baseline on all metrics (PSNR 18.0 vs. 14.1, CD 0.041 vs. 0.168, VolumeIoU 0.51 vs. 0.25), confirming that the test-time adaptation stage is the primary driver of improvement. The paper does not include direct numerical comparisons against other multi-view or few-view methods (e.g., PixelNeRF, SparseFusion, RelPose++) in the main tables; those are discussed qualitatively in Table 1 as a feature comparison rather than a quantitative benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAP3D builds directly on Zero-1-to-3's view-conditioned diffusion model and RelPose++'s sparse-view camera pose estimation, combining them with a test-time fine-tuning strategy inspired by DreamBooth. It departs from prior single-view diffusion-based 3D methods (Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer, MV-Dream) by adapting the generative prior to the specific instance at test time, and from optimization-based multi-view methods (PixelNeRF, SparseFusion) by incorporating learned 2D priors that fill in unseen geometry. The key conceptual departure is the joint optimization of diffusion model parameters and camera poses during test-time adaptation, which no prior method performs simultaneously for 3D reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAP3D provides a principled bridge between single-image diffusion-based 3D reconstruction and multi-view optimization, showing that a system can gracefully handle any number of unposed inputs and improve monotonically with more views. The 15-minute-per-object optimization cost and the 3-DOF camera parameterization inherited from Zero-1-to-3 are significant practical limitations that restrict real-time or large-scale deployment. The authors identify an important open direction: whether in-context learning (analogous to LLMs) could eliminate the need for per-instance fine-tuning entirely, which would be a major simplification if achievable.
