## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) requires adapting a pre-trained model to a sequence of shifting target distributions with no source data access and single-pass sample usage. Existing self-supervised CTTA methods—entropy minimization and teacher-student pseudo-labeling—produce miscalibrated predictions and noisy pseudo-labels under dynamic shifts, causing uncontrolled error accumulation and catastrophic forgetting. No prior work has leveraged masked reconstruction as a mechanism for continual adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single RGB images (384×384 for CIFAR-C, 224×224 for ImageNet-C, 960×540 for segmentation) in a strictly online, one-pass manner with no source data access. For classification it outputs a class label; for segmentation it outputs a pixel-level mask. Internally, each input is processed twice: once in full to estimate token-wise uncertainty via MC Dropout (m=10 forward passes on the first FFN layer), and once with 50% of tokens replaced by shared learnable [MASK] embeddings, so the effective per-sample compute is roughly 2× a standard forward pass plus a lightweight linear decoder.

## Method

ADMA introduces two coupled mechanisms into a ViT/SegFormer backbone. First, Distribution-aware Masking (DaM) computes per-token uncertainty via MC Dropout, selects the top 50% most uncertain tokens, replaces them with learnable mask embeddings, and enforces a cross-entropy consistency loss between the masked-input prediction and the original-input prediction. Second, a randomly initialized linear decoder projects the masked-token features to reconstruct hand-crafted HOG descriptors (9 orientation bins, 8×8 cells, 3 channels) at the masked positions, minimizing L2 distance; HOG's invariance to brightness, geometry, and local contrast prevents the reconstruction target from injecting target-domain shift. The total objective is L_con + 0.5·L_rec, and the method additionally injects the ViDA homeostatic adapter for parameter stability across domains.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-to-CIFAR-10C, ADMA achieves a mean classification error of 12.6%, reducing the source model's 28.2% error by 15.6 percentage points. On ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C, the method reaches 42.5% mean error versus 55.8% for the unadapted source. For the segmentation task (Cityscapes-to-ACDC), ADMA attains 61.8% mIoU, a 5.1-point gain over the source SegFormer model, and notably shows a 0.2% mIoU *increase* from round 1 to round 3, indicating resistance to error accumulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADMA outperforms all compared baselines across every benchmark. On CIFAR-10-to-CIFAR-10C, it reduces mean error to 12.6%, beating the strongest prior method ViDA (20.7%) by 8.1 points, TENT-continual (23.5%) by 10.9 points, and CoTTA (24.6%) by 12.0 points. On CIFAR-100-to-CIFAR-100C, ADMA achieves 26.4% mean error versus ViDA's 27.3% and TENT's 32.1%. On ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C, ADMA reaches 42.5% mean error, a 7.5-point improvement over VDP (50.0%) and a 13.3-point gain over the source model. For Cityscapes-to-ACDC segmentation, ADMA attains 61.8% mIoU, surpassing SVDP (61.3%) by 0.5 points and CoTTA (58.6%) by 3.2 points, while uniquely showing positive performance drift across three adaptation rounds (61.8% → 61.6% → 62.0% mIoU) compared to TENT's 1.7-point decline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the masked autoencoder (MAE) framework from pre-training into the continual test-time adaptation (CTTA) setting, a context where prior work has relied almost exclusively on entropy minimization (TENT) or teacher-student pseudo-labeling (CoTTA). The Distribution-aware Masking mechanism draws on MC Dropout for token-wise uncertainty estimation, while the HOG reconstruction target is directly inspired by MaskFeat's use of hand-crafted features in pre-training. The method also builds on ViDA's homeostatic domain adapter, which is injected into the model as a complementary regularization, positioning ADMA as a self-supervised update strategy layered on top of existing CTTA infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a simple, invariant hand-crafted descriptor (HOG) outperforms learned high-level features (CLIP, Mean-Teacher) as a reconstruction target in CTTA, the paper challenges the implicit assumption that more powerful learned representations are always preferable for domain-invariant reconstruction. The approach offers a practical, low-overhead alternative to teacher-student schemes that suffer from noisy pseudo-labels under distribution shift. Open questions include whether the method generalizes beyond corruption-based benchmarks to more semantically diverse domain shifts, whether the reliance on ViDA injection limits standalone applicability, and whether the MC Dropout uncertainty estimation (requiring 10 forward passes) scales efficiently to larger models or real-time deployment.
