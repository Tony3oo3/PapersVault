## Problem

Identity-aware movie captioning requires tracking characters across a videoset, but prior work either anonymizes all characters as 'someone' (losing identity continuity) or uses a two-stage pipeline that first generates captions and then fills in identities independently. The two-stage approach suffers from error propagation between stages, per-video face clustering that ignores cross-video identity, and BERT-based blank embeddings that fail to leverage visual face information (only 1.5% improvement from adding visual inputs).

## Input / Output

The model consumes a videoset of 5 consecutive clips (2–5 s each) and, in FITB mode, a corresponding captionset with person-id tokens replaced by blanks. Visual features are extracted at multiple granularities: CLIP embeddings from 50 sub-sampled frames per video (5 fps), I3D action features mean-pooled over 5 segments per video, and up to 300 ArcFace face detections across the entire videoset with spatial bounding boxes. Output is either a set of person-id labels (from a vocabulary of 11) filling the blanks, or a full id-aware captionset of up to 120 tokens generated over an augmented vocabulary of 30,522 words plus person-id labels.

## Method

MICap is a single-stage Transformer encoder-decoder (2 encoder layers, 3 decoder layers, d=512) that unifies fill-in-the-blanks and full caption generation. The encoder ingests four token types—BERT blank embeddings, CLIP semantic features, I3D action features, and ArcFace face detections—each projected to a shared 512-d space and augmented with type, video-index, segment, DBSCAN cluster-id, and bounding-box embeddings, enabling cross-video face association. The decoder autoregressively generates tokens with causal self-attention and cross-attention to the memory; in FITB mode it is teacher-forced with the ground-truth caption and only predicts at blank positions via a smaller person-id classifier, while in captioning mode it uses the full augmented vocabulary. Joint training runs two forward passes per batch (one per task) sharing the output classifier W^{V*}, so the same parameters serve both objectives and the model switches tasks at inference by deciding whether to teacher-force the decoder.

## Results

On the LSMDC validation set, MICap achieves 69.14% class accuracy on the FITB task, a 4.2% absolute improvement over the prior state-of-the-art FillIn (65.9%). For id-aware caption generation, MICap (joint training) scores 9.09 CIDEr, 12.47 METEOR, 13.30 SPICE, and 0.788 iSPICE, outperforming the two-stage FillIn pipeline (7.77 CIDEr, 10.68 METEOR). MICap also substantially outperforms adapted T5-Base and GPT-2 models on all captioning metrics (e.g., CIDEr 9.09 vs. 4.9 for T5, 3.6 for GPT-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline FillIn [29] (ECCV 2020), MICap wins on all four FITB metrics on the validation set: class accuracy improves from 65.9% to 69.14% (+3.2%), instance accuracy from 69.0% to 73.0% (+4.0%), and different-id accuracy from 68.4% to 72.9% (+4.5%). On the captioning task (Table 5), MICap's joint model outperforms FillIn's two-stage pipeline by +1.32 CIDEr (9.09 vs. 7.77), +1.79 METEOR (12.47 vs. 10.68), and +0.197 iSPICE (0.788 vs. 0.591 with same-id). Against the older LSMDC v2 baselines (Yu et al. [59], Brown et al. [2]), MICap's class accuracy of 69.14% exceeds theirs by 28.5 and 21.6 points respectively, though these were evaluated on the test set. For the VLM comparison (Table 6), MICap dominates both T5-Base and GPT-2 on every captioning metric (e.g., CIDEr 9.09 vs. 4.9/3.6; iSPICE 0.788 vs. 0.755/0.640), suggesting that integrating diverse visual tokens (CLIP, I3D, ArcFace) remains challenging for general-purpose VLMs fine-tuned on this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Park et al. [29], who formulated identity-aware multi-sentence video description as a two-stage pipeline: first generating captions with anonymized 'someone' tags via a multi-sentence captioning model [28], then filling in local person IDs via a BERT-based fill-in-the-blanks (FITB) module. MICap departs from this by unifying both stages into a single autoregressive Transformer encoder-decoder that can seamlessly switch between full caption generation (with person IDs in an augmented vocabulary) and FITB (teacher-forced captions with causal attention), and by replacing per-video face clustering with DBSCAN across the entire videoset plus learnable cluster embeddings. The iSPICE metric extends SPICE's scene-graph tuple matching by filtering to identity-bearing tuples, addressing a gap in evaluation that n-gram metrics (CIDEr, BLEU, METEOR) cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-stage formulation eliminates the error-propagation problem inherent in the two-stage pipeline, and the demonstrated positive transfer from joint training (both tasks improve) suggests that identity prediction and caption generation share useful representations. The iSPICE metric fills a practical gap: existing captioning metrics are nearly blind to identity swaps, making them unreliable for evaluating the core contribution of identity-aware systems. Limitations include evaluation only on the LSMDC validation set (test server unavailable), a fixed 5-clip videoset length, and a relatively small person-ID vocabulary (|P|=11); whether the approach scales to longer narratives, larger cast sizes, or open-world person identification remains open.
