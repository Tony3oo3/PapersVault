## Problem

SAM's ViT-H image encoder (632M parameters) dominates computational cost, making real-time deployment impractical. Existing lightweight alternatives like MobileSAM and FastSAM reduce complexity but sacrifice 4–8 AP in zero-shot instance segmentation. The paper asks whether a better pretraining strategy can produce small ViT encoders that close this accuracy gap while retaining SAM's promptable segmentation capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240072/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During SAMI pretraining, the model consumes 224×224 RGB images with 75% of patches randomly masked; the decoder's target is the corresponding feature embedding from a frozen SAM ViT-H encoder rather than raw pixel values. For the final EfficientSAM, input is a 1024×1024 RGB image plus a point or box prompt, and output is a segmentation mask. The pretrained encoders also emit general-purpose feature representations that transfer to classification, detection, and semantic segmentation without architectural changes.

## Method

SAMI (SAM-leveraged Masked Image Pretraining) replaces MAE's pixel-reconstruction objective with reconstruction of SAM ViT-H encoder features, transferring the foundation model's knowledge into a lightweight ViT backbone. A cross-attention decoder uses masked tokens as queries and unmasked encoder outputs as keys/values, treating the well-aligned unmasked features as anchors that guide reconstruction of masked positions. A linear projection head bridges the dimension mismatch between the MAE output and SAM features, and an L2 (MSE) loss drives end-to-end training for 400 epochs on ImageNet-1K. After pretraining, the decoder is discarded and the lightweight encoder (ViT-Tiny or ViT-Small) is paired with SAM's default mask decoder, then finetuned jointly on SA-1B for 5 epochs to yield EfficientSAM, which reduces SAM's parameter count and inference time by ~20× while losing only ~2 AP.

## Results

On zero-shot instance segmentation (the primary task), EfficientSAM-S achieves 44.4 AP on COCO and 42.3 AP on LVIS, outperforming MobileSAM (38.7/34.4 AP) and FastSAM (37.9/34.5 AP) by 5.7/7.9 and 6.5/7.8 AP respectively, while remaining within ~2 AP of full SAM (46.5/44.7 AP) with ~20× fewer parameters and ~20× faster inference. On ImageNet-1K classification, SAMI-pretrained ViT-Small reaches 82.7% top-1 accuracy, surpassing MAE (81.5%), DeiT (81.2%), and iBOT (82.3%). On COCO object detection, SAMI-B achieves 52.5 AP^box / 46.5 AP^mask, and on ADE20K semantic segmentation, SAMI-B reaches 51.8 mIoU, both outperforming MAE-pretrained counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full SAM model [31], EfficientSAM-S closes the gap to only ~2 AP on COCO (44.4 vs 46.5) and ~2.4 AP on LVIS (42.3 vs 44.7) while using ~20× fewer parameters (9.8M vs 632M for the image encoder) and ~20× faster inference. Compared to MobileSAM [68], EfficientSAM-S wins by 5.7 AP on COCO and 7.9 AP on LVIS, and EfficientSAM-Ti still leads by 3.6 AP on COCO and 5.5 AP on LVIS. Against FastSAM [71], the gains are even larger: 6.5 AP on COCO and 7.8 AP on LVIS for EfficientSAM-S, with EfficientSAM-Ti using only 9.8M parameters versus FastSAM's 68M. On the secondary pretraining benchmarks, SAMI consistently outperforms MAE [26] (e.g., +1.2% top-1 on ImageNet-1K for ViT-Base, +0.9 AP^box on COCO detection), DeiT [53] (+1.5% top-1 for ViT-Small, +2.6 AP^box on COCO), and iBOT [73] (+0.4% top-1 for ViT-Base). ViTDet-H [34] remains the strongest reference at 51.0 AP on COCO, but it is a supervised detector rather than a promptable segmentation model, so it serves as an upper bound rather than a direct competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the MAE masked autoencoder framework by replacing pixel-level reconstruction with feature-level reconstruction from SAM's ViT-H image encoder, creating a new pretraining objective (SAMI) that transfers knowledge from a vision foundation model into lightweight ViT backbones. It departs from prior efficient-SAM efforts (MobileSAM's decoupled distillation, FastSAM's CNN-based YOLOv8-seg architecture) by retaining the ViT architecture and instead investing in a stronger pretraining signal rather than architectural simplification. The cross-attention decoder, where unmasked encoder outputs serve as anchors for reconstructing masked tokens, is a targeted modification to the standard MAE decoder motivated by the asymmetric role of SAM features as a fixed teacher signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EfficientSAM-S achieves a ~20× reduction in inference time and parameter count relative to SAM with only ~2 AP loss on zero-shot instance segmentation, making promptable segmentation viable on resource-constrained hardware. The SAMI pretraining method generalizes beyond SAM to classification, detection, and semantic segmentation, suggesting that foundation-model feature reconstruction is a broadly useful pretraining signal for small ViTs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, CLIP) as teachers, whether the gains persist at even smaller scales (ViT-Nano), and whether the 5-epoch SA-1B finetuning can be further compressed for rapid adaptation to new domains.
