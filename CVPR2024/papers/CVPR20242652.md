## Problem

Imbalanced regression tasks (age estimation, crowd counting, depth estimation) suffer from long-tail target distributions where a single quantization scheme cannot simultaneously ensure balanced class samples and small quantization error. Existing approaches naively port classification balancing techniques (reweighting, logit adjustment) into regression losses, trading off head and tail performance without addressing the root cause of data insufficiency in tail classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242652/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a continuous scalar regression value (age in years, crowd count, or depth in meters). Internally, the continuous target range is quantized into C_H bins (121 for age, 100 for depth/counting) using linear or log-spaced intervals, and the predicted class index is mapped back to a representative value (mean of samples in that interval). The method operates with H=7 hierarchical classifiers, where the h-th classifier has C_h = 2^h classes with boundaries chosen to equalize sample counts per class.

## Method

HCA trains an ensemble of H hierarchical classifiers (each with 2^h classes, balanced by sample count) sharing a common feature backbone, then adjusts the finest classifier's logits with progressively coarser ones via logit summation (HCA-add) or log-logit summation (HCA-mul). For efficient single-model inference, HCA-d distills the ensemble into one classifier using KL divergence with a novel range-preserving alignment: instead of summing fine-class probabilities into coarse bins (which can shift the predicted range), it takes the maximum within each coarse group before normalizing, guaranteeing the fine prediction stays within the coarse-predicted range. A theoretical analysis shows the MAE upper bound of HCA is strictly lower than a vanilla classifier, with the gap growing as data becomes more imbalanced or insufficient per class.

## Results

On IMDB-WIKI-DIR (age estimation), HCA-d+LDS+ranksim achieves a bMAE of 11.92 overall, improving over the strongest classification baseline CLS+LDS+ranksim (12.33) and the best regression baseline Regression+LDS+ranksim (12.83), with the largest gains in the few-shot range (27.72 vs. 29.10 and 30.51). On SHTech (crowd counting), HCA-d reduces MAE from 58.2 (CLS) to 53.7 on Part A and from 7.0 to 6.8 on Part B, outperforming all prior counting methods. On NYUDv2-DIR (depth estimation), HCA-d+LDS+ranksim achieves MAE 0.895 and RMSE 1.321, marginally improving over CLS+LDS+ranksim (0.904 / 1.335), consistent with the theoretical prediction that gains shrink when per-class samples are sufficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCA-d consistently outperforms the regression baselines from Yang et al. [43] (Regression, Regression+LDS) across all shot ranges on IMDB-WIKI-DIR and NYUDv2-DIR; for example, HCA-d+LDS+ranksim reduces few-shot bMAE from 30.51 to 27.72. Against BalancedMSE [26], HCA-d achieves comparable few-shot bMAE (29.94 vs. 28.14) but substantially better many-shot performance (7.00 vs. 7.65), confirming that logit-adjustment-style methods sacrifice head accuracy. On SHTech crowd counting, HCA-d (MAE 53.7 / 6.8) beats all prior counting methods including CSRNet [20] (68.2 / 10.6), BL [23] (62.8 / 7.7), MNA [33] (61.9 / 7.4), OT [35] (59.7 / 7.4), GL [34] (61.3 / 7.3), and the authors' own DC-regression [40] (60.7 / 7.1), with the largest margin over CSRNet. The strongest classification baseline CLS+LDS+ranksim [12] is the closest competitor: HCA-d+LDS+ranksim improves overall bMAE by 0.41 and few-shot bMAE by 1.38, at a small cost to many-shot (6.88 vs. 6.70). On NYUDv2-DIR, where all classes have >10⁷ samples, HCA gains are modest (MAE 0.895 vs. 0.904), aligning with the theoretical remark that HCA benefits diminish under data sufficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant approach to imbalanced regression—extending reweighting or logit adjustment into regression losses (BalancedMSE, RankSim)—by instead reformulating the problem as a hierarchical classification ensemble with distribution-aware quantization. It draws on three distinct lineages: (1) distribution-aware quantization for regression (log-spaced bins from crowd counting and depth estimation), (2) hierarchical classification with coarse-to-fine supervision, and (3) knowledge distillation for merging ensembles into single models. The key conceptual departure is the range-preserving distillation: standard hierarchical classification aligns predictions via summation of softmax probabilities, which the paper shows catastrophically breaks range consistency, motivating a max-based alignment instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HCA provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to loss-based balancing for imbalanced regression, avoiding the inherent head-tail tradeoff that plagues reweighting and logit adjustment methods. The clean characterization (Prop. 3) that HCA's benefit scales with data insufficiency and imbalance gives practitioners a clear criterion for when to apply it. However, the practical impact is bounded: on NYUDv2-DIR where all classes have >10⁷ samples, improvements are marginal, and the method requires a two-stage training pipeline. Open questions include whether the hierarchical quantization scheme generalizes to non-ordinal or multi-dimensional regression targets, and whether the distillation stage can be made end-to-end.
