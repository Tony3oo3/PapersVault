## Problem

Contrastive vision-language pre-training aligns paired image-text embeddings but ignores whether the relative similarity structure between different samples is consistent across modalities. The authors identify this as the 'Affinity Inconsistency Problem': two images that look similar should have the same degree of similarity as their corresponding texts, yet in practice the cross-modal affinity correlation is extremely low. This inconsistency degrades retrieval and classification performance because the embedding space geometry is not jointly optimized across both modalities.

## Input / Output

The method operates on batches of N image-text pairs, where images are encoded by a vision encoder (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32, or ViT-B/16) and texts by a text encoder, both producing L2-normalized embeddings. The SaCo loss is computed entirely within a batch: for each sample, it forms an N-dimensional vector of image-image cosine similarities and a corresponding N-dimensional vector of text-text cosine similarities, then penalizes their L1 distance. No additional input beyond the standard image-text pairs is required, and the output remains the same dual-embedding representation used for downstream zero-shot tasks.

## Method

The SaCo loss minimizes the L1 distance between the image embedding similarity vector and the text embedding similarity vector for every sample in a batch, directly enforcing that the geometric structure of pairwise affinities is preserved across modalities. It is added as a weighted auxiliary term (α·L_SaCo) to the standard contrastive loss, making it drop-in compatible with existing VLP objectives. For pre-training from scratch, the authors introduce a pseudo-affinity mimicking objective (β·L_mimic) that aligns the in-training model's visual affinity with that of a frozen well-trained model (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14) to stabilize early training when affinities are noisy. For continue pre-training on an already-converged model, only the SaCo loss is needed, and even a small public dataset (LLaVA-595K) suffices to yield significant gains. The key insight from their simulation is that contrastive loss controls the radial distance between cross-modal embeddings but is insensitive to angular relationships, which are precisely what determine affinity consistency.

## Results

Pre-training from scratch on CC3M with the SaCo loss yields consistent gains across all CLIP variants: +6.4% top-1 accuracy on zero-shot ImageNet-1K (ViT-B/32: 11.9→18.3), +9.3% Recall@1 on Flickr30K image-to-text retrieval (16.0→25.3), and +6.1% Recall@1 on Flickr30K text-to-image retrieval (12.2→18.3). On the more challenging MS-COCO retrieval benchmark, improvements are +5.3% (I2T) and +3.7% (T2I) in Recall@1. Continue pre-training on LLaVA-595K from OpenAI's CLIP ViT-B/32 adds +7.9% Recall@1 on Flickr30K T2I retrieval and +1.0% on ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), against which the SaCo loss is compared on all downstream tasks. The method wins on every configuration: across three image encoders (R50, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16) and two pre-training scales (CC3M, YFCC15M), SaCo consistently improves zero-shot ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy by +4.6% to +6.8% and zero-shot retrieval Recall@1 by +2.7% to +12.8%. The second baseline, BLIP (Li et al., ICML 2022), is compared only on the affinity consistency diagnostic metric (Figure 3a), where both CLIP and BLIP show low cross-modal Pearson correlation (~0.3–0.4) while the SaCo-trained model reaches substantially higher values (~0.6+), confirming that the inconsistency is a shared limitation of contrastive pre-training rather than specific to one architecture. Notably, the retrieval gains are roughly 1–2× larger for image-to-text than text-to-image, suggesting the text embedding space had more room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLIP contrastive pre-training paradigm, which has been extended by a series of works adding masked modeling (FLIP, MaskCLIP), fine-grained alignment (FILIP, VoLTA), label smoothing (SoftCLIP), and text generation objectives (BLIP, CoCa). The key conceptual departure is identifying that all these objectives focus on cross-modal alignment (pulling paired embeddings together) while ignoring whether the *relative* affinity structure between samples is preserved across modalities. The paper draws on the broader consistency-supervision lineage (cycle consistency, temporal/spatial consistency) and repurposes it for cross-modal sample-wise affinity, positioning SaCo loss as complementary rather than competing with existing objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The affinity inconsistency problem is a fundamental diagnostic of how contrastive VLP shapes embedding spaces, and the simulation showing that contrastive loss is radially sensitive but angularly insensitive provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why this gap persists. The practical impact is significant: the SaCo loss is a simple additive term that can be plugged into any existing VLP model, and the continue pre-training strategy (a few epochs on a small dataset like LLaVA-595K) makes it accessible without expensive from-scratch training. Open questions include whether the gains hold at much larger scales (e.g., 400M+ pairs), whether the pseudo-affinity mimicking strategy generalizes to other multi-modal settings beyond vision-language, and whether the observed asymmetry (text space benefiting more than image space) reveals a deeper structural limitation in text encoders.
