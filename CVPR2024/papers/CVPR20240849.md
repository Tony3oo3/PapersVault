## Problem

Existing 3D facial landmark datasets derived from 3DMM-based registration suffer from a definitional misalignment with human-labeled 2D landmarks: for instance, 2D labels follow apparent brow boundaries while 3DMM vertices are fixed structurally above the eyes, and fine-scale details in the mouth and eyes are poorly captured. This gap limits the effectiveness of supervised 3D landmark methods and makes them inconsistent with the 2D definitions used in practice. The paper asks whether it is possible to learn 3D landmarks that align with 2D human labels without any ground-truth 3D dataset.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 224×224 RGB face crop as input and outputs 98 3D facial landmarks (predicted as offsets from a template), a 3D head pose (6D rotation representation plus translation), 2.5D projected landmarks, and per-landmark 2D anisotropic covariance matrices via predicted Cholesky factorization. The 3D landmarks are projected to 2.5D using the predicted pose and fixed camera intrinsics, enabling a unified 2D-3D supervision signal. A 68-landmark subset is extracted for comparison with prior methods that use fewer landmarks.

## Method

FaceLift is a semi-supervised pipeline that requires no ground-truth 3D labels. In a pre-processing stage, a 2D landmark detector generates pseudo-labels on both multi-view IDE-3D GAN renders and multi-frame CelebVHQ video frames; the multi-view 2D detections are then lifted to 3D pseudo-labels via an occlusion-aware masked reprojection optimization over a fixed set of camera views. The architecture is a ViT encoder (FaRL) feeding a transformer decoder with one token per landmark plus pose tokens, where landmark/pose tokens cross-attend to image tokens and self-attend among themselves before MLP heads predict 3D landmarks, covariances, and pose. Training jointly optimizes a multi-view loss (3D landmark MSE, translation MSE, 2.5D Laplacian Log-Likelihood, and geodesic rotation loss) on GAN samples and a multi-frame loss (pose-dependent masked 2D LLL loss) on video frames, combining the 3D geometric constraints of multi-view data with the in-the-wild diversity of video.

## Results

On DAD-3DHeads, FaceLift achieves a full-face NMLC of 1.91, outperforming the best non-starred baseline (3DDFAv2, 2.45) by 22% and the best overall baseline including those trained on evaluation data (DAD-3DNet+, 2.06) by 7%. On the photogrammetric Multiface dataset, FaceLift achieves 2.52 NMLC, beating the best baseline (FAN3D, 3.13) by 19%. The method also leads on every individual facial region (brows: 2.02, nose: 1.28, eyes: 1.33, mouth: 1.45 on DAD-3DHeads) and pose/expression/occlusion category, despite being trained without any ground-truth 3D landmark dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaceLift outperforms all six baselines on both DAD-3DHeads and Multiface across every facial region and category. Against the strongest 3DMM-based methods, 3DDFAv2 [15] (2.45) and FAN3D [5] (2.46) on DAD-3DHeads, FaceLift (1.91) achieves a 22% NMLC reduction; on Multiface the margins are 22% over 3DDFAv2 (3.25) and 19% over FAN3D (3.13). The 3D-aware GAN-based methods 3DDFA [60] and 3DDFA+ [49] are the weakest baselines, with FaceLift improving by 45% and 40% respectively on DAD-3DHeads. SynergyNet [42], which combines 3DMM and 3D landmarks, is beaten by 32% on DAD-3DHeads and 30% on Multiface. Notably, even DAD-3DNet+★ [49], which was trained on the very DAD-3DHeads evaluation samples (marked with ★), is surpassed by 7% on full-face NMLC, suggesting the semi-supervised 2D-to-3D lifting approach captures finer landmark definitions than 3DMM-based supervision. The largest relative gains appear in the nose and eyes regions, where 3DMM vertex definitions diverge most from human-labeled 2D positions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3DMM-based paradigm (3DDFA, DAD-3DNet, SynergyNet) that relies on mesh-topology-defined 3D landmark datasets, which the authors argue suffer from a fundamental definitional misalignment with human-labeled 2D landmarks. It builds on 2D-to-3D uplifting methods (FAN3D) and 3D-aware GANs (IDE-3D), but repurposes the GAN not as a reconstruction target or inversion source, but as a multi-view geometric prior for lifting 2D pseudo-labels into 3D via occlusion-aware reprojection optimization. The novel 3D transformer decoder that jointly trains on multi-view GAN samples (3D supervision) and multi-frame video samples (2D supervision) represents a new architectural formulation not seen in prior 3D landmark or pose estimation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that state-of-the-art 3D facial landmark accuracy is achievable without any ground-truth 3D dataset, challenging the field's assumption that 3D supervision is necessary. This opens a path toward 3D landmark definitions that truly align with human perceptual 2D labels, potentially improving downstream applications like talking-head generation and 3D face reconstruction. Key limitations include heavy dependence on 2D detector quality (failing on extreme expressions like puckers) and 3D GAN pose/expression distribution constraints, and the NMLC evaluation metric, while addressing cross-dataset definition bias, is somewhat self-referential since it optimizes over the model's own landmark-to-vertex mapping. Open questions include the asymptotic scaling of pseudo-label count and whether volumetric occlusion augmentation in GAN training could address remaining failure cases.
