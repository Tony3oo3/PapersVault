## Problem

Audio-visual segmentation (AVS) requires pixel-level localization of sounding objects in video, but existing methods address only one or two of three critical challenges: cross-modal alignment, temporal coherence, and precise visual feature extraction. Prior approaches either ignore temporal dependence, rely on implicit inter-frame relations that yield inaccurate associations, or naively concatenate frozen foundation models without proper knowledge transfer, resulting in sub-optimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short video clip of T RGB frames (resized to 224×224) and a corresponding audio clip (resampled to 16 kHz mono, converted to a mel spectrogram via STFT, then encoded by a frozen VGGish model). It produces per-pixel binary masks identifying sounding objects and, for the semantic variant, class labels per pixel. Notably, the S4 subset is trained semi-supervised (only the first of five frames is annotated), while MS3 and AVSS use full supervision across all frames. A SemanticSAM model generates class-agnostic masks offline, which are converted into RGB-like 'Maskige' images via a fixed random color encoding matrix to serve as visual prior knowledge.

## Method

COMBO is a transformer-based framework that simultaneously exploits three bilateral entanglements. (1) A Siam-Encoder Module (SEM) runs a siamese image encoder and Maskige encoder in parallel, then injects Maskige features into visual features at four scales via channel-weighted blocks (GAP + learned linear weight), transferring foundation-model knowledge without fine-tuning the frozen SAM. (2) A Bilateral-Fusion Module (BFM) performs bidirectional cross-attention where visual embeddings serve as queries and audio features as keys, with separate value projections; the single attention matrix is reused (transposed) to update both modalities, making the fusion memory-efficient and symmetric. (3) An adaptive inter-frame consistency loss weights the penalty on adjacent-frame mask discrepancy by exp(cosine_similarity − 1), so strongly dissimilar frames receive near-zero constraint while similar frames are encouraged to stay consistent. The full pipeline uses MSDeformAttn as pixel decoder, a standard transformer decoder (L=3, Nq=100) with fused audio features expanded as object queries, and is trained with a combined cross-entropy + BCE + Dice + adaptive consistency loss.

## Results

On AVSBench-object, COMBO with PVT-v2 backbone achieves 84.7 mIoU and 91.9 F-score on S4 (single-source), and 59.2 mIoU and 71.2 F-score on MS3 (multi-source), setting new state-of-the-art. On the more challenging AVSBench-semantic (AVSS), COMBO-PVT reaches 42.1 mIoU and 46.1 F-score, surpassing the previous best (AVSegFormer, 36.7 mIoU) by 5.4 mIoU. With ResNet-50, COMBO still leads by 3.7 mIoU on S4 and 8.4 mIoU on AVSS over the strongest prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMBO outperforms all 15 baselines across every evaluated subtask. On S4 with PVT-v2, COMBO (84.7 mIoU) beats the closest competitor AVSegFormer (82.1) by 2.6 mIoU and BAVS (82.0) by 2.7 mIoU. On MS3, COMBO (59.2 mIoU) edges out CATR (59.0) by 0.2 mIoU while gaining a larger 1.2 F-score advantage (71.2 vs. 70.0). The gap widens substantially on AVSBench-semantic: COMBO-PVT (42.1 mIoU) surpasses AVSegFormer (36.7) by 5.4 mIoU and BAVS (32.6) by 9.5 mIoU, indicating the bilateral entanglement design is particularly effective for semantic-level segmentation. Even with the lighter ResNet-50 backbone, COMBO leads AVSegFormer by 8.4 mIoU on AVSS, suggesting the architectural gains are not solely attributable to backbone capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMBO builds on the Mask2Former-style transformer decoder architecture (citation [10]) for pixel-level segmentation and extends the AVSBench benchmark framework. It departs from prior AVS methods (AVSegFormer, AVSBench, CATR) that perform only unidirectional audio-to-visual fusion by introducing a bilateral attention mechanism that updates both modalities simultaneously. It also moves beyond approaches that naively concatenate or fine-tune frozen foundation models (CSMF, AV-SAM, BAVS) by proposing a siamese encoder that distills SAM's class-agnostic masks into a lightweight 'Maskige' prior, decoupling the model from the foundation model at inference time. The adaptive inter-frame consistency loss is a novel addition not present in prior AVS work, which either ignored temporal coherence or relied on implicit inter-frame dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AVS is a young task (introduced in 2022), establishing strong, well-analyzed baselines is valuable for the community. The bilateral entanglement framing provides a useful conceptual lens, but the individual component gains are modest (0.6–1.4 mIoU each), suggesting the task may be approaching a plateau with current architectures. Open questions include whether the siamese SAM-prior approach generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-source scenarios, whether the bilateral fusion principle transfers to other multimodal dense prediction tasks, and whether the adaptive temporal loss can be made more principled rather than relying on hand-tuned λ values that behave inconsistently across subsets (e.g., λ=10 hurts AVSS).
