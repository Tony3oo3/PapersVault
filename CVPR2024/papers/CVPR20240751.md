## Problem

Foundation models like SAM generate class-agnostic segments at various granularities, but existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods struggle to classify these segments into text-defined categories. End-to-end methods cannot accept foundation-model segments as input, while two-stage methods like OVSeg are limited by sparse human annotations and fail to capture rich semantics (attributes, parts, context) at multiple granularities. The paper addresses this gap by building a data-centric framework that autonomously generates large-scale segment-text pairs and trains a lightweight embedding model aligned with diverse text descriptions.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 560×560 during training) and arbitrary segment masks as input, producing a single embedding vector per segment in the CLIP vision-language space. A key efficiency property is that the image encoder's patch-level output is computed once per image and reused across all segments, so the segment embedding head (mask pooling + linear projection) is the only per-segment computation. At inference, segment embeddings are compared via cosine similarity against text embeddings of target class names to assign labels, and pixel-level predictions are aggregated by taking the highest-probability class among all segments covering each pixel.

## Method

The USE framework has two components. The data pipeline autonomously curates segment-text pairs: CogVLM generates multi-granularity captions prompted to describe all visible object parts, Grounding DINO localizes referring expressions (expanded noun phrases with context) by querying with the full caption rather than individual phrases to preserve part-to-object relationships, and SAM converts bounding boxes to masks. The segment embedding model combines frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 and DINOv2 ViT-L/14 patch embeddings via multi-level feature merging (linear-layer-norm per block, learned weighted summation) plus cls tokens, then applies a lightweight mask-pooling head that computes area-weighted averages of patch embeddings within each segment and projects to the CLIP text space. Training uses a symmetric segment-text contrastive loss with only the LLN modules and block scales as trainable parameters, keeping the backbone frozen for efficiency at scale.

## Results

On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, USE+SAM (trained on COCO + Visual Genome) achieves an average mIoU of 30.9 across ADE20K and Pascal Context, outperforming the best end-to-end method SAN (29.4) and the best prior two-stage method OVSeg (26.6). On ADE-150, USE reaches 37.1 mIoU versus 32.1 for SAN and 29.6 for OVSeg. On open-vocabulary part segmentation (PartImageNet), USE trained only on COCO achieves 6.2 mAP@[0.5,0.95] over all 40 parts, surpassing VLPart trained on Pascal Part (4.5) and Pascal Part + ImageNet (5.4), though trailing VLPart with full part annotations (7.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

USE outperforms all seven prior open-vocabulary semantic segmentation baselines on the average mIoU metric (30.9 vs. 29.4 for the strongest end-to-end method SAN and 26.6 for the strongest two-stage method OVSeg). The margin is largest against OVSeg (+4.3 average mIoU) and SimSeg (+8.1), and most pronounced on ADE-150 where USE reaches 37.1 versus 32.1 (SAN) and 29.6 (OVSeg). The only per-dataset loss is on PC-459, where SAN edges out USE by 0.7 mIoU (15.7 vs. 15.0), though USE still leads on the other three benchmarks. Notably, OVSeg's performance degrades when paired with SAM segments (24.9 vs. 26.6), whereas USE consistently benefits from SAM's multi-granularity masks. On part segmentation, USE (6.2 mAP) beats VLPart trained on Pascal Part (4.5) and Pascal Part + ImageNet (5.4) without using any human-annotated part data, but falls short of VLPart with full ImageNet part annotations (7.8), which operates under a non-open-vocabulary setup with known target classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

USE follows the two-stage paradigm for open-vocabulary segmentation (generate class-agnostic segments, then classify via text matching), building directly on CLIP's vision-language joint space and DINOv2's self-supervised visual features. Its key departure from prior two-stage methods like OVSeg is a data-centric pipeline that leverages MLLMs (CogVLM), Grounding DINO, and SAM to autonomously generate multi-granularity segment-text pairs, rather than relying on limited human annotations or noun-only expressions. The image encoder adopts the multi-level feature merging strategy from COMM, combining CLIP and DINOv2 patch embeddings via learned block-scale weighted sums with LLN modules, while the segment embedding head uses a deliberately simple mask-pooling-plus-linear-projection design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's flexibility—accepting segments at any granularity from any segmentation model—makes it a practical drop-in classifier for diverse downstream tasks (semantic, instance, part segmentation, and text-based segment querying) without retraining. A notable limitation is the dependence on SAM's edge-oriented mask generation, which struggles with blurry part boundaries (e.g., head vs. neck), capping part-segmentation performance. The simple mask-pooling head, while effective, is acknowledged as a starting point; more sophisticated designs (prompt encoders, cross-attention) are left for future work, and the data pipeline's quality ceiling—determined by MLLM captioning accuracy and grounding model precision—remains an open question for scaling to billions of images.
