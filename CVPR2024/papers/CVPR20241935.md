## Problem

Biological microscopy images have highly variable channel counts (1–10), types, and imaging modalities across experiments, unlike the fixed 3-channel RGB format assumed by most vision models. Existing approaches such as Microsnoop encode each channel independently, producing variable-sized embeddings that prevent a unified representation space and block large-scale self-supervised pre-training across heterogeneous datasets. This limits cross-experiment comparisons and the transferability of pre-trained models in biological imaging.

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-channel microscopy images at 224×224 pixels with an arbitrary number of channels (1 to 10) from 7 different microscopy modalities. Regardless of the input channel count, it always produces a fixed 192-dimensional CLS embedding, in contrast to the One Channel baseline whose output dimension scales as n_i × 192. The architecture also yields channel-level self-attention maps that reveal inter-channel spatial correlations, which are not accessible from single-channel encoders.

## Method

ChAda-ViT patchifies each channel independently into 16×16 patches, then pads the token sequence to N_max × m (where m is patches per channel) with binary masking to exclude padding tokens from attention. Each patch receives both a shared positional embedding and a learnable channel-specific embedding, allowing the model to distinguish spatially co-located patches from different channels. On top of standard intra-channel (spatial) self-attention, the architecture introduces inter-channel attention that operates across the full padded token sequence, enabling the model to learn cross-channel relationships. The model is trained self-supervised with DINO for 400 epochs on the newly curated IDRCell100k dataset (104k multiplexed images from 79 experiments), using a ViT-Tiny backbone with 192-dim embeddings but processing 10× more tokens than a standard ViT-Tiny due to the per-channel patchification.

## Results

ChAda-ViT Moyen outperforms the One Channel ViT/16 Tiny baseline (Microsnoop) in 6 out of 8 downstream tasks using CLS token only, despite operating in a smaller fixed 192-dimensional embedding space versus the baseline's variable 384–576 dimensions. The evaluated tasks span classification (BloodMNIST, CyclOPS, BBBC048), regression (NF-kB Nuclear Translocation), and channel reconstruction (BBBC021), with metrics of top-1 accuracy and R² (normalized 0–100). In low-data linear probing regimes (10% and 1% of training data), ChAda-ViT maintains consistent relative performance trends across all tasks, confirming robustness in data-scarce biological settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the One Channel ViT/16 Tiny (Microsnoop [47]), which encodes each channel independently with a standard ViT and concatenates per-channel CLS tokens. ChAda-ViT Moyen wins on 6 of 8 tasks (BloodMNIST classification, CyclOPS classification, NF-kB Nuclear Translocation regression, BBBC021 channel reconstruction, and their clustering counterparts) while losing on BBBC048 classification and clustering. The BBBC048 loss is attributed to the dataset's cell-cycle features being predominantly intra-channel, combined with the One Channel ViT's larger 576-dim representation versus ChAda-ViT's 192-dim. An ablation variant (Inter-Channel only ViT Moyen, which treats each entire channel as a single 224×224 patch) underperforms both models, confirming that the combination of Intra-Channel and Inter-Channel Attention is necessary for optimal biological image representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision Transformer architecture (ViT) and DINO self-supervised pre-training to the specific challenge of heterogeneous multi-channel microscopy images, where channel count, type, and ordering vary across experiments. It departs from the dominant one-channel encoding paradigm (Microsnoop, CytoImageNet) that processes each channel independently and concatenates or averages results, by introducing Inter-Channel Attention that treats channels as first-class tokens alongside spatial patches, coupled with channel-specific embeddings and NLP-style token padding/masking to handle variable channel counts. The IDR-Cell100k dataset (79 experiments, 7 microscopy modalities, 1–10 channels) is positioned as the first collection explicitly curated for this heterogeneous-channel SSL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to project fundamentally different microscopy assays (e.g., 3-channel Cell Painting vs. 5-channel Bray) into a single fixed-size embedding space opens a path toward cross-experimental and cross-modal biological studies that were previously infeasible with per-channel or per-assay models. However, the cross-modal demonstration rests on only two datasets sharing 22 compounds, and the model underperforms on tasks where intra-channel information dominates (BBBC048 cell-cycle classification), revealing that inter-channel attention is not a universal substitute for spatial detail. Open questions include whether the approach scales to a true foundation model over millions of images, how to better balance intra- and inter-channel capacity, and whether the unified space supports more complex cross-dataset inference beyond linear separability.
