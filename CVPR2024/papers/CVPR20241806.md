## Problem

Coordinate networks (implicit neural representations) suffer from spectral bias: ReLU-based MLPs learn low-frequency signal components rapidly while high-frequency components converge extremely slowly, limiting reconstruction quality. This bias stems from a pathological NTK eigenvalue distribution where a few eigenvalues dominate and most are near zero. The paper identifies that batch normalization—a standard deep learning technique rarely applied in the coordinate network community—can fundamentally reshape this eigenvalue distribution to alleviate the bias.

## Input / Output

The method takes low-dimensional coordinate vectors (2D pixel coordinates for images/CT, 3D voxel coordinates for shapes/MRI, or 3D position + 2D viewing direction for NeRF) as input and outputs the corresponding signal attribute at each coordinate. For inverse problems (CT, MRI), supervision is indirect: the network is trained against sparse integral projections or Fourier-domain measurements rather than direct pixel values, with the batch size set to the full coordinate set so that BN statistics are computed over the entire signal. Outputs range from RGB pixel values and volume density fields to occupancy grids (extracted via marching cubes) and 5D radiance fields (RGB + density).

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical proof, via mean field theory, that inserting a batch normalization layer after each ReLU activation transforms the NTK from K ≈ (L−1)N·M + O(√N) (where M has a rank-1 structure with large off-diagonal entries) into K^BN ≈ (L−1)N·(κ₁−κ₂)/σ² · I + O(√N), i.e., approximately a scalar multiple of the identity. This reduces the eigenvalue variance from O(N³) to O(N^{3/2}) and the maximum eigenvalue from O(N²) to O(N^{3/2}) while preserving the O(N) mean, effectively shifting the bulk of eigenvalues upward so that high-frequency components receive larger learning rates. The analysis extends to positional-encoding-enhanced MLPs, where BN simultaneously alleviates the 'frequency-specified spectral bias' of Fourier features. Empirically, BN is applied as a single layer immediately after each ReLU in standard MLPs (with a larger initial learning rate of 1e−2 exploiting BN's insensitivity to LR), and validated across five tasks: image compression, CT reconstruction, 3D shape representation, MRI reconstruction, and novel view synthesis, where BN+PE consistently achieves the best PSNR/IoU among all compared methods.

## Results

On the 2D image compression task (Kodak), BN+PE achieves the highest PSNR across all network widths, reaching 30.84 dB at [13×49], outperforming the next best (SIREN at 29.42 dB) by 1.42 dB. On 3D MRI reconstruction (ATLAS brain), BN+PE reaches 34.56 dB at [4×256], surpassing PE (32.12 dB) and SIREN (27.89 dB). For 3D shape representation (Stanford 3D Scanning Repository), BN+PE achieves the best IoU of 0.992 at [2×256], compared to 0.968 for PE and 0.964 for SIREN. On 5D novel view synthesis (Blender Synthetic), BN+DINER attains the best PSNR (33.31), SSIM (0.968), and LPIPS (0.033) among all six methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BN+PE consistently achieves the highest PSNR/IoU across all four representation tasks (2D Image, 2D CT, 3D Shape, 3D MRI), beating the next-best method by 0.4–1.6 dB or 0.5–2.5% IoU depending on the task and network width. Plain BN (without positional encoding) already surpasses ReLU by up to 4.75 dB on 2D Image and 4.51 dB on 3D MRI, and outperforms PE, WIRE, and MFN on most configurations, though MFN narrowly edges out BN by 0.44 dB on 2D CT at [4×256]. SIREN degrades catastrophically on deeper CT networks (dropping to 16–18 dB at [2×256] and beyond), while WIRE similarly collapses at 8 layers (18.61 dB vs. 33.64 dB for BN+PE), indicating severe over-fitting to high frequencies. On 5D novel view synthesis, BN+DINER achieves the best PSNR (33.31), SSIM (0.968), and LPIPS (0.033), marginally outperforming DVGO (33.23 / 0.966 / 0.034) and the original DINER (33.11 / 0.967 / 0.034), while BN+NeRF improves over vanilla NeRF by 0.33 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant line of spectral-bias mitigation in coordinate networks, which has focused on frequency-specific architectural modifications (positional encoding, SIREN, MFN, WIRE) or signal re-organization via hash tables (DINER, InstantNGP). It departs from this lineage by introducing batch normalization—a technique ubiquitous in CNNs but largely absent from the implicit neural representation community—as a spectral-bias remedy. The theoretical analysis builds on the NTK framework (Jacot et al., 2018) and mean field theory (Karakida et al., 2019) to show that BN reduces the NTK eigenvalue variance from O(N³) to O(N^{3/2}), shifting the distribution toward higher values without altering the mean. This reframes spectral bias alleviation as a normalization problem rather than an activation-encoding problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single drop-in normalization layer, requiring no architectural redesign or frequency-specific tuning, consistently improves coordinate networks across five diverse tasks (image compression, CT, shape, MRI, NeRF), which is valuable for practitioners who want a simple, universal fix. The theoretical proof, however, is limited to BN on the last layer and relies on the mean field approximation; extending the analysis to multi-layer BN or other normalization variants (LayerNorm, GroupNorm) remains open. The modest gains over the strongest baselines in NeRF (0.2–0.33 dB) suggest BN is a complementary rather than transformative technique, and its interaction with hash-based or multi-scale representations is unexplored.
