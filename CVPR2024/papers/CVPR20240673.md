## Problem

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning guides LLMs through sequential, step-by-step logic, but this paradigm is ill-suited for creative tasks that require non-sequential associative thinking and conceptual leaps. The paper identifies that even state-of-the-art LLMs like GPT-4 and Qwen-VL lack sufficient 'Leap-of-Thought' (LoT) ability, and that CoT-style prompting can actually impair creative performance. Using the Oogiri humor game as a testbed, the paper asks whether LLMs can be trained to exhibit strong remote-association and creative generation abilities that go beyond sequential reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method accepts three types of multimodal, multilingual inputs: image-only (I2T), text-only (T2T), or image+text (IT2T), in English, Chinese, or Japanese. At inference, the LLM generates n candidate humor responses under n weakly-associated conditions (either empty or randomly sampled object nouns), then ranks them and selects the top-1 from the top-2 via a learned selection skill. The Oogiri-GO dataset contains over 130,000 samples, 77.95% of which carry human preference annotations (like counts) used to construct ranking evaluation questions.

## Method

CLoT comprises two LoT-boosting stages. Stage 1 (Associable Instruction Tuning) formulates the Oogiri-GO dataset into 500K+ instruction samples using a dual-purpose template that randomly inserts a noun clue from the ground-truth response or leaves the condition empty (with probability ρ_c), thereby training LoRA-adapted LLMs on both associable generation and associable discrimination (choice and ranking) abilities. Stage 2 (Explorative Self-Refinement) prompts the trained LLM to generate diverse creative responses under weakly-associated conditions drawn from a global object-noun set, ranks the candidates, mixes the top-2 with the ground truth, and selects the best; only samples where the selected response is not the ground truth are retained as new high-quality training data for a second round of LoRA fine-tuning. The key design insight is that weakly-associated (rather than strongly-associated) conditions force the LLM to build connections between seemingly unrelated concepts, enabling remote-association exploration that CoT's sequential exploitation cannot achieve.

## Results

On the Oogiri-GO benchmark (multilingual, multimodal), Qwen-VL+CLoT achieves average accuracies of 42.7% (IT2T), 40.5% (I2T), and 38.5% (T2T), improving over the vanilla Qwen-VL by +9.1%, +10.4%, and +8.2% respectively. On the non-multilingual CogVLM-17B, CLoT yields even larger gains: +21.7% on I2T (51.6% avg) and +16.4% on T2T (48.3% avg). On the English T2T task, CLoT-integrated models (43.4–44.2% avg) substantially outperform all single-modal LLMs including GPT-4 (32.0%) and GPT-3.5 (36.0%). CLoT also generalizes to Cloud Guessing Game (+8%) and Divergent Association Task (+5%) over the vanilla Qwen-VL baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLoT consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across every task and model family. Among multimodal multilingual models, Qwen-VL+CLoT beats the strongest baseline (mPLUG-Owl-Multilingual, 32.2% IT2T avg) by 10.5 points and GPT-4v (23.5%) by 19.2 points. On non-multilingual models, CogVLM-17B+CLoT (51.6% I2T, 48.3% T2T) surpasses InstructionBLIP (23.1/23.1) and Otter (20.5/15.1) by roughly 28 and 33 points respectively. On single-modal English T2T, CLoT-integrated models (43.4–44.2% avg) exceed GPT-4 (32.0%), GPT-3.5 (36.0%), LLaMA2-70B (27.4%), and Qwen-14B (30.3%) by 8–17 points. Critically, CLoT also outperforms CoT, CoT-SC, and prompted LoT (PLoT) reasoning frameworks (Fig. 8), demonstrating that sequential chain-of-thought prompting does not improve—and can even degrade—leap-of-thought creative performance, whereas CLoT's two-stage training paradigm reliably enhances it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by positioning Leap-of-Thought (LoT) as a distinct reasoning paradigm complementary to Chain-of-Thought (CoT), arguing that the sequential, step-by-step nature of CoT is fundamentally misaligned with creative and associative tasks. Methodologically, it repurposes standard instruction tuning (via LoRA) and self-refinement loops, but introduces the novel mechanism of weakly-associated conditions to force remote association during both training and inference. The Oogiri game is repurposed from a cultural entertainment format into a structured benchmark for LoT evaluation, bridging computational humor generation literature and the emerging LLM creativity research thread.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Oogiri-GO dataset (130K+ multilingual, multimodal samples with human preference annotations) provides the first large-scale benchmark specifically designed to probe associative/creative thinking in LLMs, filling a gap left by logical-reasoning-centric evaluations. The finding that CoT actively degrades creative performance challenges the near-universal assumption that more structured reasoning always helps, suggesting LLMs may need fundamentally different training paradigms for creative versus analytical tasks. Open questions include whether LoT ability scales with model size, whether the CLoT framework transfers to non-humor creative domains (e.g., scientific ideation), and how to move beyond multiple-choice evaluation toward open-ended creative quality assessment.
