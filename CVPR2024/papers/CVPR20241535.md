## Problem

Current egocentric video understanding relies on hyper-specialized single-task models or multi-task learning that suffers from negative transfer and requires supervision for all tasks simultaneously at training time. Neither approach can abstract task-specific knowledge into reusable perspectives that transfer to a novel task without access to all prior task labels. The paper asks whether a compact 'backpack' of task perspectives learned from known tasks can positively aid the learning of any new task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241535/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes egocentric video clips encoded as 1536-dimensional Omnivore Swin-L features (dense sampling over 32-frame windows, stride 16), mapped to temporal graphs where nodes represent segments and edges connect temporally close segments with a task-dependent threshold τ. Outputs are task-specific: verb/noun labels for action recognition, predicted future action sequences for long-term anticipation, binary state-change labels for OSCC, and temporal localization timestamps for PNR. The final prediction for a novel task is structured as a sum of pre-softmax logits from all carried task perspectives plus a dedicated new-task head, effectively letting each prior task cast a vote on the output.

## Method

EgoPack uses a unified GraphSAGE-based temporal GNN backbone shared across all tasks with minimal task-specific projection heads, enabling all egocentric tasks to be modeled as graph prediction with a single forward pass through the backbone. After multi-task pretraining, the model extracts frozen task-specific prototypes by aggregating head outputs per (verb, noun) action pair, creating a compact 'backpack' of abstracted knowledge. When learning a novel task, features are forwarded through all task heads, matched against the frozen prototypes via k-NN cosine similarity, and enriched through iterative message passing that updates task features while keeping prototypes fixed. The temporal backbone, task heads, and interaction GNNs are jointly trained using only the novel task's supervision, decoupling knowledge acquisition from knowledge transfer and avoiding the negative transfer and full-supervision requirements of standard multi-task learning.

## Results

On the Ego4D validation set, EgoPack achieves 25.10% verb and 31.10% noun top-1 accuracy on Action Recognition, 71.83% accuracy on OSCC, 0.728/0.752 edit distance on LTA (verbs/nouns), and 0.61 s localisation error on PNR. These results outperform the Ego4D official baselines across all four benchmarks and surpass the EgoT2s baseline on AR (by ~2 pp verbs, ~8 pp nouns) and LTA, while trailing EgoT2s by 0.86 pp on OSCC. On the Ego4D test set, EgoPack achieves SOTA on LTA (0.721 verb ED, 0.925 action ED) and matches EgoT2 on PNR (0.66 s), though it lags EgoVLP-based methods on OSCC (72.1% vs 75.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Ego4D official baselines [25], EgoPack wins on every benchmark: AR verbs improve from 22.18% to 25.10%, AR nouns from 21.55% to 31.10%, OSCC from 68.22% to 71.83%, LTA verb ED from 0.746 to 0.728, and PNR from 0.62 s to 0.61 s. Compared to EgoT2s [68], EgoPack substantially outperforms on AR (25.10% vs 23.04% verbs; 31.10% vs 23.28% nouns) and LTA (0.728 vs 0.731 verb ED), ties on PNR (0.61 s), but trails by 0.86 pp on OSCC (71.83% vs 72.69%). On the Ego4D test set, EgoPack achieves SOTA on LTA (0.721 verb ED, 0.925 action ED), beating EgoT2 [68] (0.722/0.935), HierVL [1] (0.724/0.928), and I-CVAE [43] (0.741/0.930). On PNR test, EgoPack matches EgoT2 at 0.66 s and slightly edges out EgoVLP [42] (0.67 s). On OSCC test, EgoPack (72.1%) trails EgoVLP-based methods (74.0–75.0%) but outperforms EgoT2(I3D) (71.0%) and the I3D baseline (67.6%), with the gap attributed to EgoVLP's full Ego4D pretraining advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoPack builds on the GraphSAGE message-passing architecture for its temporal backbone and positions itself against two main lines of work: standard multi-task learning (which suffers from negative transfer and requires all-task supervision at training time) and EgoT2, which uses an ensemble of separate task-specific models with a transformer-based translation step. The key departure is the introduction of task-specific prototypes as a frozen, reusable abstraction of multi-task knowledge, eliminating the need for the novel task to be known at pretraining time and avoiding the per-task forward passes required by EgoT2. The unified temporal graph formulation also departs from EgoT2's heterogeneous model ensemble by casting all egocentric tasks as graph prediction problems under a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prototype-based knowledge abstraction offers a practical path toward incremental skill acquisition in egocentric video understanding, where a model can learn a new task without retraining from scratch or requiring supervision for all previously seen tasks. However, the empirical gains are modest (EgoPack scores 0.441 vs. 0.437 for MTL+FT in the ablation), test-set results are mixed (SOTA on LTA but not OSCC), and evaluation is limited to four Ego4D benchmarks. Open questions include whether the prototype mechanism scales to larger task collections, whether it generalizes beyond egocentric video, and whether the modest improvements persist with stronger backbones pretrained on Ego4D itself.
