## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) had previously been used only in offline reconstruction pipelines that required accurate camera poses from a separate Structure-from-Motion system, making it inapplicable to live SLAM. Existing dense SLAM representations—voxel grids, meshes, surfel clouds, and neural fields—each suffer from memory constraints, topology difficulties, discontinuities, or expensive per-pixel ray marching that preclude real-time operation. This paper asks whether 3DGS can serve as the sole 3D representation for a fully online monocular SLAM system, unifying tracking, mapping, and high-fidelity rendering in one framework.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a live monocular RGB video stream (or RGB-D when a depth sensor is available) and incrementally builds a 3D Gaussian scene map while simultaneously estimating camera poses at 3 fps. No pretrained monocular depth predictor or external tracking module is used; the monocular mode relies purely on photometric residuals against the rendered Gaussians. Outputs include the evolving Gaussian map (position, covariance, colour, opacity per Gaussian), a camera pose trajectory, and photorealistic novel-view renderings produced by differentiable splatting rasterisation at up to 200 fps at 1080p.

## Method

The key technical contribution is an analytic Jacobian of the SE(3) camera pose with respect to the 3D Gaussians in the EWA-splatting rasterisation pipeline, derived on the Lie group to yield minimal 6-DoF parameters; this allows camera poses to be optimised jointly with Gaussian geometry via first-order gradient descent inside the same CUDA rasteriser used for rendering. To handle the ambiguities of incremental monocular reconstruction, the authors introduce an isotropic shape regularisation that penalises deviation of each Gaussian's scale vector from its mean, preventing elongation along the viewing ray that would otherwise create artefacts and destabilise tracking. A covisibility-based keyframe selection and pruning strategy—exploiting the natural depth ordering of splatted Gaussians to handle occlusions without extra heuristics—keeps the map compact and geometrically stable. The full SLAM loop alternates between a tracking phase (optimising only the current pose against the fixed Gaussian map) and a mapping phase (jointly optimising keyframe poses and newly inserted Gaussians with photometric, optional geometric, and isotropic losses), all rendered through the same differentiable splatting operator.

## Results

On the TUM RGB-D dataset, the method achieves a mean ATE RMSE of 3.96 cm in the monocular setting (without loop closure), outperforming DSO (11.0 cm), DROID-VO (7.73 cm), and DepthCov-VO (25.2 cm). In the RGB-D setting (without loop closure), it achieves 1.47 cm mean ATE RMSE, surpassing all baselines including ESLAM (2.00 cm) and Co-SLAM (2.17 cm). On the Replica dataset, the single-process implementation achieves 0.32 cm mean ATE RMSE, the best among all compared methods. For novel view rendering on Replica, the method achieves 38.94 dB PSNR and 0.070 LPIPS at 769 FPS, dramatically outperforming Point-SLAM (35.17 dB PSNR at 1.33 FPS) and NICE-SLAM (24.42 dB PSNR at 0.54 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the monocular setting (no loop closure), the proposed method (3.96 cm avg ATE) decisively outperforms DSO (11.0 cm), DROID-VO (7.73 cm), and DepthCov-VO (25.2 cm), and approaches the performance of loop-closure systems like DROID-SLAM (1.70 cm) and ORB-SLAM2 (1.60 cm) without using any loop closure or learned depth priors. In the RGB-D setting (no loop closure), it achieves 1.47 cm avg ATE, beating the closest competitor ESLAM (2.00 cm) by ~27%, and substantially outperforming iMAP (4.23 cm), NICE-SLAM (4.77 cm), DI-Fusion (4.07 cm), Vox-Fusion (10.34 cm), Co-SLAM (2.17 cm), and Point-SLAM (3.04 cm). On the Replica dataset, the single-process variant (0.32 cm) beats Point-SLAM (0.53 cm), ESLAM (0.63 cm), NICE-SLAM (1.07 cm), Vox-Fusion (3.09 cm), and iMAP (2.58 cm). For novel view rendering, the method achieves 38.94 dB PSNR at 769 FPS, surpassing Point-SLAM (35.17 dB at 1.33 FPS) by 3.8 dB and NICE-SLAM (24.42 dB at 0.54 FPS) by 14.5 dB, while being orders of magnitude faster. The method is competitive with loop-closure RGB-D systems (ORB-SLAM2: 1.00 cm, BAD-SLAM: 1.50 cm) despite not performing loop closure, narrowing the gap between map-centric and frame-centric SLAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to use 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the sole scene representation in an online SLAM system, extending 3DGS from its original offline, SfM-pose-dependent setting to continuous monocular and RGB-D tracking. It departs from neural-implicit SLAM methods (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, Co-SLAM) that rely on MLPs or hash grids with per-pixel ray marching, replacing them with explicit anisotropic Gaussians rendered via differentiable rasterisation. The key technical departure is the derivation of an analytic SE(3) Jacobian for camera pose optimisation against Gaussians (building on EWA splatting), which was not available in the original 3DGS pipeline, along with a novel isotropic shape regularisation to prevent viewing-direction elongation artefacts in incremental reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying tracking, mapping, and rendering in a single explicit representation, the system achieves 769 FPS novel-view rendering (hundreds of times faster than neural-implicit baselines) while capturing challenging materials like transparency and thin structures that voxel or surfel-based methods struggle with. The discovery that 3DGS maps have a substantially larger camera-pose convergence basin than hash-grid or MLP SDF representations (0.79–0.82 vs. 0.14–0.33 success rate) has direct implications for map-based localisation. Key limitations include the absence of loop closure (restricting large-scale operation), no explicit surface extraction, and a 3 fps SLAM rate; the authors identify loop closure integration and surface normal recovery as the most important future directions.
