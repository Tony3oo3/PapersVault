## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in text-to-image diffusion models applies a single global scale to the entire image, but the paper shows this causes spatial inconsistency: different semantic regions (e.g., 'astronaut' vs. 'sky') receive markedly different effective guidance strengths, leading to uneven detail and quality across the generated image. No prior work has addressed this by customizing guidance per semantic region during the denoising process.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step t, the method consumes the current latent image x_t, the text prompt c, and the self-attention and cross-attention maps extracted from the U-net backbone (averaged over the two lowest-resolution layers and all heads). It produces a per-patch CFG scale map γ_{t,i} that modulates the classifier score before computing the guided noise estimate ε̂_θ. The entire procedure is training-free and operates in the latent space (e.g., 64×64 for SD), requiring no access to the final decoded image.

## Method

S-CFG has two core components. First, a training-free semantic segmentation: the cross-attention map is renormalized along the spatial dimension and argmax'd over tokens to assign each patch to its most relevant text token, then the self-attention map is used as a graph transition matrix (R=4 propagation steps) to fill internal holes and sharpen boundaries. Second, an adaptive CFG scale: assuming semantic regions are approximately independent, the classifier score is decomposed per-region, and each region's scale γ_{t,i} is set so that its classifier score norm is rescaled to match a benchmark (the foreground region, identified as the complement of the START-token region). This yields a spatially varying scale map that equalizes guidance strength across semantic units while preserving the original CFG formulation as a special case when all scales are uniform.

## Results

On MSCOCO 256×256, S-CFG consistently shifts the FID-30K vs. CLIP Score trade-off curve toward the bottom-right (lower FID, higher CLIP) compared to original CFG across all three base models (SD-v1.5, SD-v2.1, DeepFloyd IF) and both samplers (DDIM, DPMSolver++). In human evaluation (80 prompts, 5 raters), S-CFG is preferred over CFG in image quality by 73.22% (SD-v1.5), 71.84% (SD-v2.1), and 67.61% (IF), and in image-text alignment by 76.80%, 68.15%, and 70.83% respectively. On the ControlNet downstream task (SD-v1.5, DPMSolver++, 50 steps, γ=3.0), S-CFG reduces FID from 8.670 to 8.382 (Canny) and 9.595 to 9.549 (Segmentation) while slightly improving CLIP Score (0.3006→0.3019, 0.3004→0.3017).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original global-scalar Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [Ho & Salimans, 2021], which applies a single γ uniformly across all spatial locations. S-CFG wins on every reported metric and model: in the FID-30K vs. CLIP Score trade-off (Figure 4), the S-CFG curve is consistently shifted toward lower FID and higher CLIP Score for all γ values tested (2.0–10.0) on SD-v1.5, SD-v2.1, and IF with both DDIM and DPMSolver++ samplers. In the human study (Table 1), S-CFG receives a clear majority of votes (67–77%) over CFG in both image quality and text-image alignment across all three models. On the ControlNet downstream task (Table 2), S-CFG improves FID by ~0.29 (Canny) and ~0.05 (Segmentation) with marginal CLIP Score gains, confirming the benefit transfers to spatially-conditioned generation. No comparison shows S-CFG losing to or tying with CFG; the improvement is consistent but the magnitude is modest in absolute FID terms, with the largest relative gains appearing in the pixel-space IF model and in human preference scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [11] and the extensive literature on exploiting cross-attention maps in diffusion U-Nets for semantic understanding (Prompt2Prompt, Attend-and-Excite, layout control). While prior works repurpose attention maps for layout conditioning, image editing, or text-alignment optimization, S-CFG repurposes them for a different purpose: spatially modulating the guidance strength itself. The self-attention-based region completion draws on graph feature propagation ideas [51] and the training-free segmentation approach of [41], but applies them to the novel problem of per-region CFG scale adaptation rather than segmentation per se.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S-CFG offers a training-free, plug-and-play improvement that works across latent-space (Stable Diffusion) and pixel-space (DeepFloyd IF) models, as well as downstream tasks like ControlNet and DreamBooth, making it broadly applicable to practitioners. The core assumption that semantic regions are approximately independent is a simplification that may break down for highly compositional or overlapping prompts, and the per-step attention extraction adds non-trivial computational overhead. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion, whether the independence assumption can be relaxed to handle interdependent regions, and whether the modest quantitative gains (FID ~0.3–0.5, CLIP ~0.001–0.003) scale to higher-resolution or longer-prompt settings.
