## Problem

Most multi-object tracking methods represent objects as coarse-grained bounding boxes or holistic appearance features, which degrade severely under the high dynamicity of open-world objects—severe deformation, fast 3D motion, and frequent occlusion distort internal object relationships and break temporal continuity. This fragility affects both association (reduced cross-frame similarity) and localization (inaccurate object-text correspondence for deformed or occluded instances), yet no existing framework explicitly addresses these dynamicity-induced failures.

## Input / Output

NetTrack consumes RGB video frames at 25 FPS and a text prompt (e.g., 'bird') for open-world tracking; the prompt can be augmented with LLM-generated descriptors (GPT-3.5) to inject professional or contextual knowledge for disambiguation. It outputs tracked bounding boxes with persistent track IDs alongside fine-grained point Nets—sets of points of interest (POIs) sampled on object surfaces and propagated across frames via a pretrained point tracker. The framework operates with a tracking stride of 8, retains lost tracks for 30 frames, and initializes POI sampling on a (3,3) grid per object, requiring no additional training for zero-shot deployment.

## Method

NetTrack replaces the coarse-grained object representation with a fine-grained Net of points of interest (POIs) on the object surface, whose local appearance and neighborhood relationships remain stable even when global geometry is distorted by deformation or fast motion. A fine-grained sampler uses importance sampling conditioned on Kalman-filter-estimated trajectories to select cross-frame POIs efficiently, and a matching method computes containment scores between tracked POIs and candidate detection boxes (with an area-ratio penalty for oversized boxes), combining these fine-grained scores with standard IOU-based coarse-grained similarity for Hungarian-algorithm association. For localization, NetTrack substitutes CLIP-style image-text correspondence with phrase-grounding pre-training (via Grounding DINO or GLIP), which learns word-level object-text alignment and supports embedded LLM descriptors for context-aware detection. The architecture is modular—detectors and point trackers (CoTracker, TAPIR, PIP) are interchangeable—and the entire pipeline operates zero-shot without task-specific finetuning, leveraging pretrained weights from TAP-Vid/Kubric (point tracking) and COCO/O365 (detection).

## Results

On the highly dynamic BFT benchmark (zero-shot, Grounding DINO detector), NetTrack achieves OWTA 72.5, HOTA 68.7, MOTA 78.9, and IDF1 77.0, surpassing the best finetuned closed-set tracker (TrackFormer, OWTA 67.4) by 5.1 points without any finetuning. On TAO-OW, NetTrack attains OWTA 62.7 on known classes (vs. 60.2 for OWTB) and 43.7 on unknown classes, with a notable 8.4-point improvement in detection recall over Video OWL-ViT. On TAO (open-vocabulary), NetTrack achieves TETA 32.6 on novel classes (vs. 27.8 for OVTrack) and a 11.8-point gain in classification accuracy (ClsA 13.3 vs. 1.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BFT zero-shot with Grounding DINO, NetTrack outperforms OC-SORT by 3.5 points on OWTA (72.5 vs. 69.0) and by 9.8 points on detection recall (80.7 vs. 70.9), while trailing OC-SORT on association precision (82.5 vs. 90.4) and detection precision (83.3 vs. 87.9) due to more false positives from the open-set detector. With YOLOX, NetTrack leads OC-SORT by 4.8 points on OWTA (63.3 vs. 58.5) and 12.9 points on D.Re. (70.6 vs. 57.7), confirming the fine-grained Net's benefit is detector-agnostic. On TAO-OW, NetTrack surpasses OWTB by 2.5 points on known-class OWTA (62.7 vs. 60.2) and Video OWL-ViT by 8.4 points on unknown-class D.Re. (58.7 vs. 53.4), though it trails Video OWL-ViT slightly on unknown-class OWTA (43.7 vs. 45.4). On TAO, NetTrack achieves a 4.8-point TETA gain on novel classes over OVTrack (32.6 vs. 27.8) and a dramatic 11.8-point ClsA improvement (13.3 vs. 1.5), but falls 2.5 points short on base-class TETA (33.0 vs. 35.5) due to higher false positives without finetuning. Across AnimalTrack and GMOT-40, NetTrack consistently leads on D.Re. (e.g., 42.0 vs. 32.9 on AnimalTrack known) while sacrificing a few points on A.Acc., reflecting the trade-off between recall and precision in open-world settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

NetTrack sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: open-world tracking-by-detection (building on ByteTrack's coarse-grained association and Grounding DINO/GLIP-style phrase grounding for localization) and physical point tracking (CoTracker, TAPIR, PIP). The key conceptual departure is repurposing point-level visual cues—previously used for tracking arbitrary image points—as a fine-grained association signal within a multi-object tracking pipeline, rather than relying solely on bounding-box IoU or global appearance features. The framework is deliberately modular, treating the point tracker and the grounding detector as interchangeable components, which distinguishes it from end-to-end tracking architectures like TrackFormer or TransTrack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BFT benchmark fills a notable gap: prior open-world MOT datasets (TAO, TAO-OW, AnimalTrack, GMOT-40) do not stress-test trackers against the combined challenges of fast 3D motion, severe wing-flap deformation, and flock-level occlusion simultaneously. The finding that a simple point-containment matching score, combined with coarse-grained IoU, yields consistent zero-shot gains across four diverse benchmarks suggests that fine-grained spatial cues are an underexploited signal in MOT association. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large flocks (hundreds of objects), whether the separate point-tracker module can be unified end-to-end with the detector, and how to mitigate the false-positive inflation that slightly degrades association precision.
