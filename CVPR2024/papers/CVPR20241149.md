## Problem

Token-based predictive learning frameworks built on VQGAN struggle to simultaneously preserve low-level spatiotemporal details and capture high-level dynamics. Specifically, vanilla vector quantization loses high-frequency visual information, and existing iterative decoding schemes are irreversible—once a token is decoded incorrectly, it cannot be corrected in subsequent iterations—and they ignore correlations between tokens in the codebook.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241149/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes past video frames (e.g., 4–12 frames) as a spatiotemporal sequence and produces predicted future frames (e.g., 4–20 frames) across resolutions ranging from 32×32 (TaxiBJ) to 2160×3840 (SJTU4K). Internally, input frames are encoded into two parallel streams of discrete tokens—coarse (low-frequency) and fine (high-frequency)—via a wavelet-based VQGAN tokenizer, and the final output is reconstructed by decoding these tokens back to pixel space through an inverse wavelet transform.

## Method

PredToken introduces a three-stage pipeline. Stage I employs a 'decomposition, quantization, reconstruction' (DQR) schema: discrete wavelet transform splits each frame into low-frequency (coarse) and high-frequency (fine) sub-bands, which are independently quantized using a dimension-aware model that applies self-attention across time, space, and channel dimensions with a shared codebook of 16,384 entries, then reconstructed via inverse DWT. Stage II trains a coarse-grained Transformer (CGT) and fine-grained Transformer (FGT) via masked token modeling, alongside a mask discriminator trained adversarially against CGT to distinguish masked from unmasked tokens. Stage III performs coarse-to-fine iterative decoding: coarse tokens are refined with dynamic soft decoding, where the mask discriminator acts as a guidance network to dynamically re-mask incorrectly decoded tokens using Gumbel-perturbed scores, while fine tokens are refined with static soft decoding that uses negative information entropy as a confidence score for weighted codebook averaging rather than hard categorical sampling.

## Results

PredToken achieves state-of-the-art results across all six spatiotemporal prediction benchmarks. On KTH (10→20 frames), it reaches PSNR 35.11 and LPIPS 0.1265, improving over the prior best TAU (34.13 / 0.2394) by 0.98 dB and 47% in LPIPS respectively. On TaxiBJ (4→4), it attains SSIM 0.985 and PSNR 39.79, and on WeatherBench (12→12) it achieves MAE 0.6347 and RMSE 1.042, both surpassing TAU (0.6810 / 1.106). On the high-resolution SJTU4K dataset (2160×3840), PredToken improves PSNR from 21.03 to 23.89 and reduces LPIPS from 65.21 to 61.37 at the t+3 frame compared to TAU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PredToken outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric across all datasets. The closest competitor is TAU (CVPR'23), which PredToken surpasses by 0.98 dB PSNR and 47% LPIPS reduction on KTH, by 0.29 dB PSNR on TaxiBJ, and by 6.8% MAE reduction on WeatherBench. Among recurrent-based methods, PredRNN2 (PAMI'22) and E3D-LSTM (ICLR'19) are the strongest, yet PredToken still leads by 0.41 dB PSNR on TaxiBJ and 1.78 dB on KTH. The recent non-autoregressive DMVFN (CVPR'23) achieves competitive LPIPS on KTH (0.1284) but trails PredToken (0.1265) and is significantly worse on PSNR (32.15 vs. 35.11). SwinLSTM (ICCV'23) and SimVP (CVPR'22) show moderate performance, with PredToken gaining 1.05 dB PSNR over SwinLSTM and 1.39 dB over SimVP on KTH. The earliest baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, MIM) are consistently 1.5–2.5 dB behind PredToken in PSNR, confirming that the DQR tokenizer and coarse-to-fine iterative decoding provide substantial gains over both recurrent and early non-autoregressive approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

PredToken sits within the token-wise video prediction paradigm initiated by Walker et al. (VQ-VAE + PixelCNN) and advanced by MaskViT (VQGAN + bidirectional window Transformer) and MAGVIT (3D VQGAN + masked modeling). It departs from this lineage in two targeted ways: (1) replacing the monolithic VQGAN tokenizer with a wavelet-decomposition-based DQR schema that explicitly separates low- and high-frequency components before quantization, and (2) augmenting the MaskGIT-style iterative decoding with a coarse-to-fine cascade that introduces soft probability sampling and a mask discriminator that operates at inference time as a token-correction guide. The dimension-aware quantization (attention over time, space, and channel) extends the spatial-only attention used in prior VQGAN variants to better capture inter-frame dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete bottlenecks in token-based video prediction—loss of high-frequency detail in the tokenizer and error propagation in iterative decoding—that have limited the fidelity of prior methods. The mask discriminator's dual role (adversarial training signal + inference-time correction network) is a practical design choice that could transfer to other masked-generation settings. However, the framework requires training and coordinating four separate networks (VQGAN encoder/decoder, CGT, FGT, mask discriminator), raising questions about scalability to longer sequences and whether the wavelet decomposition is strictly necessary compared to learned frequency separation. The approach also remains confined to relatively short prediction horizons (4–20 frames), leaving open whether the coarse-to-fine cascade degrades gracefully over much longer temporal spans.
