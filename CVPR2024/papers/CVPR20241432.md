## Problem

Camera-based 3D object detectors fail beyond the range of available 3D annotations (typically ~40–70 m) because distant 3D boxes are extremely costly to label due to sparse or absent LiDAR points. Existing methods either require expensive long-range LiDAR or labor-intensive multi-view stereo annotation, making long-range 3D detection impractical at scale. This paper asks whether distant 3D detection can be achieved using only the much cheaper 2D bounding box supervision for far objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241432/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular RGB images together with 2D bounding boxes; for close objects (<40 m) both 2D and 3D annotations are used during training, while distant objects (>40 m) provide only 2D box labels. At inference, the network outputs full 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) for all objects, with depth for distant objects recovered implicitly from their 2D box geometry rather than directly regressed. The framework is plug-and-play: it can be attached to monocular detectors (FCOS3D, FastRCNN3D) or used as a pseudo-label teacher for BEV detectors (BEVFormer).

## Method

The core component is the Implicit Projection Head (IP-Head), which models the inverse camera-projection mapping from a 2D box to depth as a small MLP f^(θ) whose weights are dynamically generated per-instance by a second MLP f_g conditioned on object features encoding size and orientation. This per-object dynamic weighting lets a single architecture produce the correct 2D-to-depth curve for objects of different sizes and orientations, rather than relying on a shared mapping. During training, only close 2D/3D pairs supervise f_g, and a projection augmentation strategy synthesizes additional (2D box, depth) pairs by varying depth in the forward projection equation to strengthen the learned mapping. For BEV-based detectors, LR3D operates as a long-range teacher: a monocular IP-Head detector generates pseudo 3D labels for distant objects, which are then combined with close 3D ground truth to train the BEV student, eliminating the need for any distant 3D annotations.

## Results

On KITTI (val), LR3D with IP-FCOS3D achieves 50.0% LDS and 46.7% mAP overall without any distant 3D annotations, surpassing the fully 3D-supervised FCOS3D baseline (48.0% LDS, 44.7% mAP). For distant objects (40m–Inf), LR3D reaches 36.2% LDS and 31.0% mAP versus only 4.9% LDS and 3.3% mAP for FCOS3D without distant 3D labels. As a long-range teacher, LR3D boosts DID-M3D to 56.3% LDS overall (vs. 56.1% with full 3D supervision) and 39.0% LDS on distant objects (vs. 38.9% fully supervised). On nuScenes, BEVFormer-S with LR3D teacher achieves 36.3% LDS overall, nearly matching its fully supervised counterpart (36.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LR3D (IP-FCOS3D) dramatically outperforms the FCOS3D baseline without distant 3D labels: 36.2% vs. 4.9% LDS on distant objects (40m–Inf), a 7.4× improvement. As a long-range teacher, LR3D recovers nearly all the performance lost by removing distant 3D annotations across all tested detectors: DID-M3D goes from 24.2% to 39.0% distant LDS (matching its fully supervised 38.9%), CaDDN from 5.5% to 20.9% (vs. 19.3% fully supervised), and MonoFlex from 8.4% to 35.8% (vs. 40.6% fully supervised). On nuScenes, BEVFormer-S with LR3D teacher achieves 18.3% distant LDS (40m–51.2m) versus 2.4% without, approaching the fully supervised 19.6%. The paper also reports 34.5% and 14.17% LDS improvements for FCOS3D on Cityscapes3D and Argoverse 2, and 7.09% for MV-FCOS3D++ on Waymo, confirming generality. Notably, in several cases (DID-M3D, CaDDN, GUPNet on KITTI), the LR3D-teacher-augmented model slightly exceeds the fully 3D-supervised counterpart on overall LDS, suggesting the pseudo-labels introduce useful regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the monocular 3D detection line (FCOS3D, DID-M3D, MonoFlex) by introducing the IP-Head, a lightweight MLP that learns an instance-specific implicit mapping from 2D box dimensions to depth, drawing conceptually from NeRF's use of MLPs to fit implicit functions. It departs from prior long-range detection work (e.g., Far3D, R4D) that still requires distant 3D annotations, instead showing that 2D box supervision alone suffices when combined with close-range 3D supervision. The teacher-student pipeline generalizes the approach to BEV-based detectors (BEVFormer), positioning LR3D as a pseudo-label generator that bridges monocular and multi-view 3D detection paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 3D annotation for distant objects is bottlenecked by LiDAR sparsity, and this work removes that bottleneck entirely, making long-range 3D detection scalable without expensive multi-sensor labeling. The LDS metric addresses a real evaluation gap where fixed-threshold mAP yields near-zero scores for distant objects, making prior comparisons uninformative. Open questions include whether the pseudo-labeling pipeline accumulates errors over multiple iterations, how the approach handles occluded or partially visible distant objects, and whether the 2D-box-to-depth mapping generalizes across camera intrinsics and object categories beyond vehicles.
