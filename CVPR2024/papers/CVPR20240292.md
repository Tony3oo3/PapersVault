## Problem

Prior work integrating geometric knowledge into stereo matching has relied exclusively on intra-view structures such as edges and surface normals, overlooking cross-view geometric factors like matching uniqueness and occlusion. This paper identifies that local feature matching—interest point detection combined with cross-view correspondence—naturally encodes both intra-view and cross-view geometric knowledge, and proposes a framework to transfer this knowledge into stereo matching networks without any inference-time overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240292/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of rectified stereo images (left and right views at standard resolutions, e.g., 1226×370 for KITTI 2012) and outputs a dense disparity map. During training, a pre-trained SuperPoint detector and SuperGlue matcher serve as frozen knowledge sources, but both auxiliary decoders are discarded at inference, so the deployed model is structurally identical to the base stereo network (GwcNet or IGEV-Stereo) with zero additional latency.

## Method

ICGNet augments any stereo matching backbone with two training-only decoders. An intra-view decoder (two bottleneck conv blocks) maps backbone features to interest point maps, supervised by focal loss against SuperPoint's detection outputs to inject intra-view geometric structure. A cross-view decoder (MLP encoder followed by four alternating self-attention and cross-attention layers) takes bilinearly-sampled interest point descriptors from the backbone features and produces an assignment matrix, which is supervised by two complementary losses: a soft cross-view loss (KL-divergence against SuperGlue's continuous assignment matrix) and a hard cross-view loss (negative log-likelihood against binary ground-truth match/unmatch labels derived by warping interest points via the disparity map). The total loss is a weighted sum of the base disparity loss, intra-view loss (weight 100), and both cross-view losses (weight 0.5 each), and the framework is demonstrated to be plug-and-play across GwcNet and IGEV-Stereo baselines.

## Results

On SceneFlow, ICGNet (with IGEV-Stereo backbone) achieves an EPE of 0.447 px, outperforming IGEV-Stereo (0.479) and GwcNet (0.765). On the KITTI 2015 benchmark (all pixels), ICGNet achieves D1-all of 1.57%, ranking 1st among published peer-reviewed methods at submission time, compared to IGEV-Stereo's 1.59%. For cross-domain generalization (SceneFlow→real), ICGNet improves EPE by 4.8% on KITTI 2012 (0.99 vs 1.04) and 4.1% on KITTI 2015 (1.16 vs 1.21) over the IGEV-Stereo baseline, and also leads on Middlebury 2014 (EPE 0.82, >2px 6.73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICGNet (IGEV-Stereo backbone) outperforms all 14 compared baselines across every reported metric on both KITTI 2012 and KITTI 2015. The closest competitor is IGEV-Stereo [55], which ICGNet beats by 0.01 on KITTI12 2-noc (1.70 vs 1.71), 0.02 on KITTI15 D1-fg (2.55 vs 2.67), and 0.02 on KITTI15-noc D1-all (1.47 vs 1.49). Among geometric-knowledge-based methods, ICGNet surpasses EdgeStereo [45] by a large margin (e.g., 0.62 on KITTI12 2-noc, 0.51 on KITTI15 D1-all) while incurring zero inference overhead since its geometric decoders are discarded at test time. On SceneFlow, ICGNet achieves 0.447 EPE vs. IGEV-Stereo's 0.479 and GwcNet's 0.765, and also beats DLNR [71] (0.48) and ACVNet [54] (0.48). For cross-domain generalization, ICGNet (0.99 EPE on KITTI12, 1.16 on KITTI15, 0.82 on Middlebury) consistently outperforms IGEV-Stereo (1.04, 1.21, 0.91) and all other domain-generalization baselines including CFNet [42] and DSMNet [66].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of geometric-knowledge-guided stereo matching (EdgeStereo, normal-assisted methods) by introducing cross-view geometric knowledge—matching uniqueness and occlusion—through interest point correspondences, whereas prior work relied solely on intra-view structures like edges and surface normals. It repurposes pre-trained local feature matching models (SuperPoint for detection, SuperGlue for matching) as frozen knowledge sources, injecting their geometric understanding into a stereo backbone via auxiliary decoders and constraints during training only. Unlike EdgeStereo or normal-assisted approaches that require additional branches at inference, ICGNet discards all geometric decoders at test time, achieving zero inference overhead while still improving disparity estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead property makes this approach practically attractive for deployment in latency-sensitive applications like autonomous driving, where prior geometric-guided methods added computational cost. The clean separation of intra-view (occluded-region) and cross-view (non-occluded-region) knowledge, validated by the ablation in Table 6, provides a principled framework that could be extended to other geometric knowledge sources beyond interest points. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other pre-trained knowledge sources (e.g., depth estimators, segmentation models), whether the specific choice of SuperPoint/SuperGlue is optimal versus newer alternatives, and whether the training-time complexity of maintaining two auxiliary decoders scales well to larger backbones.
