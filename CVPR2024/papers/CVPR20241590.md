## Problem

Robotics agents struggle to follow general multi-modal prompts in long-horizon manipulation because end-to-end VLMs accumulate compounding errors without intermediate guidance, while LLM-based text decomposition produces redundant, confusing sub-prompts that are harder to parse than the original instruction. The paper asks whether generating subgoal images step-by-step before acting can provide more expressive and grounded intermediate guidance than text sub-prompts for long-horizon task completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241590/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial RGB observation image x_0 and a multi-modal prompt P (which may combine text, reference images, or both) as input, and produces a chain of N progressively advancing subgoal images {x_i} followed by short-horizon action sequences of length T for each subgoal step. The subgoal images are generated in a receding-horizon loop: after executing actions toward subgoal x_i, the robot re-observes the scene and generates the next subgoal x_{i+1}, repeating until task completion. The low-level policy is a simple MLP that only needs basic single-object manipulation primitives since the high-level planner handles all long-horizon reasoning.

## Method

CoTDiffusion is a hierarchical framework that uses a conditional diffusion model as a high-level visual planner to implicitly decompose multi-modal prompts into chained subgoal images in a chain-of-thought manner, decoupling instruction understanding from action execution. The key technical component is a triple semantic alignment module S(x_0, x_i, P) that compares the current generated subgoal against the fixed initial observation and prompt via multi-stage cross-attention, producing aligned tokens that encode 'which step has been reached' and 'which step comes next' to anchor the generation chain. Training follows a coarse-to-fine pipeline: the alignment module is first pre-trained with a masked patch prediction pretext task (predicting spatial residuals between adjacent keyframes) to learn spatial reasoning without pixel generation, then integrated into the diffusion model for fine-grained pixel-level reconstruction. Two additional mechanisms enhance generation quality: bi-directional aligned generation (the aligned token guides both forward prediction of x_{i+1} and backward reconstruction of x_i, enforcing tighter instruction grounding) and frame concatenation (the current observation is concatenated with Gaussian noise as part of the diffusion input rather than merely a conditioning signal, providing strong visual continuity). The low-level policy is a simple MLP trained by imitation learning on paired subgoal images and expert trajectories, requiring only single-object manipulation primitives.

## Results

On VIMA-BENCH, CoTDiffusion achieves 64.6% overall success rate across three long-horizon manipulation tasks (visual rearrangement, visual reasoning, and visual constraints), outperforming the strongest baseline VIMA (49.5%) by 15.1 percentage points. Per-task, CoTDiffusion reaches 59.0% on rearrangement, 51.7% on reasoning, and 83.1% on constraints. In single-view robustness tests, CoTDiffusion suffers only a 13.2% relative performance drop versus 29.4% for VIMA, demonstrating the benefit of explicit visual subgoal planning under limited perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTDiffusion outperforms all four baselines on every task and the overall metric. Against the strongest abstract planner VIMA (49.5% overall), CoTDiffusion gains 15.1 points, with the largest margin on visual reasoning (+13.5) and constraints (+15.9). The end-to-end generalist agents Gato (11.4%) and Flamingo (18.9%) are far behind, confirming that without intermediate visual subgoals, compounding errors over long horizons cause catastrophic deviation. The visual planner SuSIE (10.0%) performs poorly without explicit step-wise sub-prompts; even when granted privileged decomposed sub-prompts (43.0%), it still trails CoTDiffusion by 21.6 points, highlighting the advantage of implicit chain-of-thought alignment over rule-based text decomposition. CoTDiffusion also shows superior robustness under single-view observation (56.0% vs. 34.9% for VIMA), with only a 13.2% relative drop versus 29.4% for VIMA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes diffusion models—previously used for text-to-image synthesis—as high-level visual planners for robot manipulation, departing from both end-to-end VLM action prediction (RT-2, VIMA, Gato) and LLM-based text subgoal decomposition (SayCan, EmbodiedGPT). The key conceptual departure is replacing text subgoals with generated visual subgoal images, motivated by the argument that complex manipulation scenes are poorly specified by text alone. The chain-of-thought framing is borrowed from LLM reasoning but extended to sequential image generation, with a novel semantic alignment module (coarse mask-prediction pretraining followed by fine-grained pixel reconstruction) to track progress along the generation chain. The concurrent SuSIE work also uses image editing for manipulation but lacks intrinsic CoT reasoning, requiring externally decomposed step-wise prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a simple MLP policy suffices when conditioned on well-generated visual subgoals suggests the primary bottleneck in long-horizon manipulation is planning rather than action execution, which could reshape how hierarchical robot architectures are designed. However, all experiments are confined to the VIMA-BENCH simulator with scripted oracle data, and the low-level policy is deliberately minimal, leaving open whether the approach transfers to real-world manipulation with richer dynamics and sensor noise. The authors note that incorporating commonsense knowledge from MLLMs like GPT-4V is a natural next step, and the framework's flexibility to swap in stronger goal-conditioned policies (transformers, diffusion policies) is acknowledged but not explored. A key open question is whether the semantic alignment module generalizes beyond the structured, object-centric scenes of Ravens to more open-ended real-world environments.
