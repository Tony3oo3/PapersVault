## Problem

Popular multi-label losses such as the asymmetric focal loss and ASY loss achieve state-of-the-art accuracy but produce severely miscalibrated probability estimates. The paper identifies the root cause theoretically: these losses lack the strictly proper property, a necessary condition for a loss to be uniquely minimized by the true posterior probability. This miscalibration becomes particularly damaging in multi-label retrieval, where errors across independently predicted label probabilities accumulate and degrade performance as query length increases.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images (or image+question pairs for VQA) and outputs a vector of T independent class-posterior probability estimates, one per label, via a per-label sigmoid-based inverse link. For the multi-label retrieval application, these per-label probabilities are combined by multiplication over desired and undesired labels to form a single ranking score per image. The SPA loss operates on real-valued logits rather than probability estimates directly, requiring a dedicated inverse link function to recover calibrated probabilities.

## Method

The paper introduces the Strictly Proper Asymmetric (SPA) loss, constructed as a composite loss ℓ_{±1,Ψ}(v) paired with a custom inverse link [Ψ]^{-1}(v) that is a ratio of weighted sigmoid terms. The positive partial loss reduces to standard BCE, while the negative partial loss uses hyperparameters (ζ⁻, k⁻, b⁻) to control the decay rate and overall weight of negative examples, enabling asymmetry without sacrificing strict properness (proven in Theorem 3). A Label Pair Regularizer (LPR) is added to the training objective, which computes pairwise probability ratios β^{tt'} for all label pairs with differing ground-truth values and penalizes miscalibration of these ratios via BCE, effectively adding O(T²) calibration constraints per example beyond the T individual label constraints. The paper also proves (Theorem 1) that both the focal and ASY losses are not strictly proper, and derives a bijective recovery map φ that can partially correct their miscalibration only when the probability margin m=0.

## Results

On MS-COCO with ECA-ResNet50-T, SPA+LPR achieves mAP@y of 77.7 and mAP@x of 88.6 while reducing average calibration error (ACE) to 4.2, compared to 30.6 for ASY and 17.2 for TWL. Across all four datasets and both architectures (ECA-ResNet50-T and ViT-B/32), SPA+LPR attains the best or near-best accuracy while achieving the lowest ACE and MCE in 25 of 36 dataset/model/metric configurations. On the most imbalanced dataset (VISPR, positive/negative ratio 0.08), SPA+LPR reduces ACE from 28.8 (ASY) to 3.0, a nearly 10× improvement, while matching ASY's mAP@y (52.7 vs 51.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ASY (Ridnik et al., ICCV 2021), SPA+LPR matches or slightly exceeds classification accuracy (e.g., COCO mAP@y 77.7 vs 77.5; VISPR mAP@y 52.7 vs 51.6) while reducing ACE by 5–10× (e.g., 4.2 vs 30.6 on COCO, 3.0 vs 28.8 on VISPR), demonstrating that the strictly proper property is the key differentiator. Against the asymmetric Focal loss (Lin et al., ICCV 2017), SPA+LPR wins on both accuracy and calibration across all datasets; even the post-hoc recovery mapping φ applied to Focal (Focal+φ) only partially closes the calibration gap (ACE 11.1 vs 4.2 on COCO). Against TWL (Kobayashi, CVPR 2023), a non-CPE loss, SPA+LPR achieves comparable or better mAP@y (77.7 vs 77.2 on COCO) while halving ACE (4.2 vs 17.2); TWL's non-probabilistic outputs require temperature scaling and still lag in calibration. The paper also shows that the post-hoc mapping φ (Theorem 2) improves ASY calibration but is only bijective when the probability margin m=0, limiting its applicability to the full ASY loss. Overall, SPA+LPR is the only method that simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art multi-label accuracy and the best calibration across all 32 dataset/architecture/metric configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the composite binary loss framework of Reid and Williamson (JMLR 2010), which expresses class-probability-estimation losses as functions of real-valued scores paired with an inverse link function. It departs from the dominant multi-label classification literature, where the focus has been on improving labeling accuracy via asymmetric losses (focal, ASY), by showing that these popular losses are provably not strictly proper and therefore fundamentally incapable of producing calibrated probabilities. The LPR regularizer extends the authors' earlier work on pairwise constraints for calibration to the multi-label setting, and the multi-label retrieval evaluation connects to the broader calibration literature (temperature scaling, Bayesian DNNs) that has so far been applied only to single-label classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the most widely adopted multi-label losses (ASY, focal) are provably uncalibrated has broad implications for any application relying on multi-label DNN probability estimates, from medical diagnosis to multi-condition retrieval. The SPA loss provides a principled, drop-in replacement that maintains state-of-the-art accuracy while fixing calibration, and the LPR offers a simple additional regularizer applicable to any multi-label loss. Key limitations include the independence assumption across labels (Assumption 1), which precludes modeling label co-occurrence, and the fact that strict properness only guarantees asymptotic calibration; the LPR partially addresses the finite-data gap but the joint-probability-estimation problem for correlated labels remains open.
