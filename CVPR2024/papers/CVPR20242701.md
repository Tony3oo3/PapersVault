## Problem

Building digital twins of unknown articulated objects from visual observations requires recovering both part geometries and joint articulations, but prior methods either rely on category-specific training priors (Ditto) that fail on out-of-distribution objects, or use per-object optimization (PARIS) that is unstable across random initializations and limited to two-part objects. No existing approach robustly handles arbitrary novel objects with multiple movable joints without shape or structure priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes approximately 100 multi-view RGB-D images with object masks at two articulation states, along with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics and the number of joints (joint types are unknown). It outputs per-part 3D meshes extracted via marching cubes, a soft part segmentation field, and for each joint: its type (revolute or prismatic, classified by rotation angle threshold), joint axis, and relative joint state between the two scans. The method is fully per-object optimized with no training phase, and part 0 is assumed static.

## Method

The approach factorizes reconstruction into two stages: (1) per-state object-level shape reconstruction using Neural Object Fields (SDF + appearance networks with multiresolution hash encoding) supervised by RGB-D rendering losses following BundleSDF, and (2) articulation model recovery where part segmentation is modeled as a probability distribution over parts and per-part SE(3) transformations are optimized jointly. The key insight is deriving a differentiable point correspondence field from the articulation model (analogous to linear blend skinning) and supervising it with three complementary losses: a consistency loss enforcing SDF/RGB/occupancy agreement between corresponding points across states, a matching loss using 2D pixel correspondences from LoFTR feature matching, and a collision loss that penalizes multiple source points mapping to the same target location. Shared motion constraints link the two articulation models (forward and inverse), and visibility-aware weighting handles partial observations from self-occlusion.

## Results

On the PARIS Two-Part Object Dataset (synthetic, averaged over 10 objects), the proposed method achieves 0.14° axis angle error, 0.01 (0.1m) axis position error, 0.10° part motion error, 2.10 mm CD-s, and 0.73 mm CD-m, substantially outperforming all baselines. On real-world data, it achieves 10.11° axis angle error, 1.03 mm part motion error, 6.69 mm CD-s, and 13.80 mm CD-m, again the best or near-best across all metrics. On the multi-part object dataset (Table 2), the method achieves 0.16°/0.10° axis angle errors and 0.11°/0.13° part motion errors for two joints, while PARIS*-m fails with 34.52°/15.91° axis angle errors and 86.21°/105.86° part motion errors. The method is notably stable across 10 random seeds (e.g., 0.14±0.0° axis angle) whereas PARIS exhibits large variance (10.17±12.5°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: Ditto (feed-forward, trained on 4 categories), PARIS (per-object NeRF optimization), PARIS* (PARIS + depth), CSG-reg (TSDF + CSG + ICP registration), and 3Dseg-reg (TSDF + PAConv segmentation + registration). On synthetic data, the proposed method dominates all baselines by large margins: axis angle error of 0.14° vs. 9.60–54.22° for baselines, and movable-part Chamfer distance of 0.73 mm vs. 39.72–78.36 mm. On real data, the method achieves the best CD-m (13.80 mm vs. 35.48–481.83 mm) and CD-s (6.69 mm vs. 9.44–48.56 mm), though Ditto achieves a slightly lower axis angle error (3.80° vs. 10.11°) on the two real objects, likely due to Ditto's training on the fridge category. The most striking comparison is with PARIS/PARIS*, which show extreme variance (±12.5° to ±58.0°) and frequent catastrophic failures, while the proposed method is stable (±0.0° to ±0.1°) across all 10 random seeds. On multi-part objects (Table 2), PARIS*-m fails to segment correctly (axis angle errors of 34–43°), while the proposed method achieves 0.10–0.88° errors, demonstrating clear scalability to multiple joints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BundleSDF for per-state neural object field reconstruction and positions itself against two prior lines: feed-forward articulation prior learning (Ditto, CARTO) that requires category-specific training data, and per-object optimization (PARIS) that avoids training but is limited to two-part objects and suffers from high optimization variance. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage decomposition—first reconstructing object-level SDF geometry independently at each state, then recovering articulation via an explicit point correspondence field supervised by three complementary losses (3D consistency, 2D LoFTR matching, and kinematic collision). This contrasts with PARIS's single image-rendering objective applied to separate static/mobile neural fields, which the authors show is insufficient for multi-part objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method democratizes digital twin construction for arbitrary articulated objects without category priors or large training sets, and extends the problem from the two-part setting to multi-joint objects—a practical gap for robotics and mixed reality applications. The dramatic stability improvement over PARIS (near-zero variance vs. ±30+ degree axis errors) makes the approach more deployable in practice. Open questions remain for unknown camera poses, unaligned base parts across states, and handling changing occlusion patterns, which the authors identify as future work.
