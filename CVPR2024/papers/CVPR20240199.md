## Problem

Existing diffusion-based HMR methods (e.g., HMDiff) add Gaussian noise to mesh vertices, destroying the physical body structure at intermediate steps and making it difficult to establish meaningful relationships between noisy meshes and input images. Direct vertex regression from a single image also suffers from inherent depth ambiguity, producing unrealistic results especially from side views. The paper reframes HMR as a structured posture transformation from a known SMPL T-pose to the target pose, preserving anatomical constraints throughout the multi-step process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240199/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (cropped and resized to 256×256) and the SMPL T-pose template mesh (6,890 vertices) as input. A frozen HRNet-W48 backbone estimates a 2D mesh of 431 points from the image, which serves as the conditioning signal. The output is a refined 3D human mesh of 6,890 vertices, produced by 10 reverse diffusion steps that progressively transform the T-pose toward the target posture, with a final MLP layer upscaling from 431 coarse to 6,890 fine vertices.

## Method

PostureHMR replaces the standard Gaussian-noise forward process with a forward-kinematics-based skeletal animation: pose changes are interpolated via bone rotation blending plus FK computation, while shape changes use linear interpolation of SMPL shape parameters, ensuring every intermediate mesh remains a physically valid body. The reverse process employs a Mesh-to-Posture (M2P) decoder built from L transformer layers with Graphormer blocks that model both non-local and topology-based neighborhood interactions among vertices, conditioned on the 2D mesh to guide 3D posture changes step by step. Training combines three losses: a posture loss supervised by the FK-generated intermediate labels, a 3D geometry loss (vertex, keypoint, surface), and a novel trimap-based IoU rendering loss that upweights edge regions where pixel misalignment is most severe. Unlike standard diffusion, the T-pose is a fixed deterministic starting point rather than random noise, eliminating sampling randomness and reducing the required iteration count to just 10 steps.

## Results

On Human3.6M, PostureHMR achieves 55.7 mm MPVE, 44.5 mm MPJPE, and 31.0 mm PA-MPJPE, setting new state-of-the-art on all three metrics. On 3DPW, it reaches 75.4 mm MPVE, 64.9 mm MPJPE, and 39.6 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming the previous best (Zolly: 76.3/65.0/39.8). On SURREAL, PostureHMR attains 42.1 mm MPVE, 35.3 mm MPJPE, and 27.4 mm PA-MPJPE, surpassing VirtualMarker by 2.6/1.6/1.5 mm respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PostureHMR outperforms all 24 compared baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. Against the closest diffusion-based method HMDiff [11], PostureHMR improves MPJPE by 4.8 mm (44.5 vs. 49.3) and PA-MPJPE by 1.4 mm (31.0 vs. 32.4) on H3.6M, and by 7.8 mm MPJPE (64.9 vs. 72.7) on 3DPW. Versus the strongest model-free baseline VirtualMarker [29], gains are 2.8 mm MPVE, 2.8 mm MPJPE, and 1.0 mm PA-MPJPE on H3.6M, and 2.5/2.6/1.8 mm on 3DPW. Against the best model-based method ProPose [10], PostureHMR achieves 1.2 mm lower MPJPE and 1.6 mm lower PA-MPJPE on H3.6M, and 3.4 mm lower MPJPE on 3DPW. On SURREAL, PostureHMR beats VirtualMarker [29] by 2.6 mm MPVE and 1.6 mm MPJPE, and surpasses the model-based PC-HMR [28] by 17.7 mm MPVE and 16.4 mm MPJPE, demonstrating the advantage of preserving body structure through kinematics-based interpolation over direct parameter regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

PostureHMR departs from HMDiff (Foo et al., ICCV 2023), which applies standard diffusion by adding Gaussian noise to mesh vertices, by replacing the noise-based forward process with a forward-kinematics-driven skeletal animation that preserves the physical structure of the human body at every intermediate step. The M2P decoder architecture directly adopts the Graphormer block design from MeshGraphormer (Lin et al., ICCV 2021), which also uses the SMPL T-pose as input, but PostureHMR extends this to a multi-step iterative refinement conditioned on a 2D mesh rather than a single-shot regression. The 2D mesh estimation pipeline and 3D geometry optimization losses are inherited from VirtualMarker (Ma et al., CVPR 2023), positioning PostureHMR as a diffusion-style refinement layer on top of that framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete structural limitation of diffusion-based HMR—noise destroys body topology—by showing that kinematics-based interpolation yields more physically plausible intermediate states and better final reconstructions. The trimap-guided rendering loss is a practical, lightweight mechanism for improving edge alignment that could transfer to other mesh-recovery pipelines. However, the method remains tied to the SMPL template and single-person setting, and the authors acknowledge that occluded or partially visible bodies still produce incorrect pose estimates in invisible regions, leaving multi-person and heavily occluded scenarios as open challenges.
