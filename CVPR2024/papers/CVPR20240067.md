## Problem

Data replay for incremental video learning has been limited to storing static frame exemplars borrowed from the image domain, ignoring the temporal structure that is critical for procedural videos. This paper introduces incremental temporal action segmentation (iTAS) as a new task and identifies that frame-exemplar replay fails to capture the natural evolution of action features over time, leading to poor retention of previously learned actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240067/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The segmentation backbone (MSTCN or ASFormer) consumes pre-computed I3D frame features at full temporal resolution and outputs per-frame action labels. The TCA generative model takes a frame feature x, a one-hot action label a, and a scalar coherence variable c ∈ [0,1] (the relative temporal position within a segment) as joint input, and outputs a reconstructed frame feature. Replay videos are generated top-down: a sequential structure (ordered action labels with durations) is sampled first, then per-segment features are synthesized and concatenated by timestamp.

## Method

The core contribution is a Temporally Coherent Action (TCA) model, a conditional VAE whose encoder and decoder are two-layer MLPs with a 256-D latent space, conditioned jointly on the action label a and a coherence variable c = (i−1)/(ℓ−1) that encodes the frame's relative progression within its segment. During replay generation, a single latent z is sampled per segment and held fixed while c is swept linearly from 0 to 1, producing temporally coherent feature trajectories; z governs scene context, a governs action semantics, and c governs temporal evolution. The sequential structure (action order and segment durations) is stored symbolically from training data and sampled uniformly, so replay videos are diverse at the segment level while remaining coherent within each segment. The segmentation model is then trained incrementally on the union of current-task real data and generated replay videos using a standard frame-wise cross-entropy plus smoothing loss, while a fresh TCA model is trained on each new task's data for future replay.

## Results

On the Breakfast dataset (10-task incremental setup), the proposed TCA method achieves 29.4% frame-wise accuracy with MSTCN and 34.2% with ASFormer, compared to 16.1% and 12.4% for the Exemplar baseline, respectively. In the 5-task setup, accuracy rises to 54.5% (MSTCN) and 57.2% (ASFormer), narrowing the gap to the original-data upper bound (60.4% and 65.1%). On YouTube Instructional (5 tasks), the method achieves 30.2% Acc / 25.0 Edit (MSTCN) and 25.2% Acc / 20.9 Edit (ASFormer), with segmental metrics (Edit, F1) consistently outperforming the static Exemplar baseline despite comparable or slightly lower frame-wise accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Exemplar method from Alssum et al. [3], which stores a single mean frame per action segment and replicates it to form static replay segments. On Breakfast (10 tasks), TCA outperforms Exemplar by 13.3 points in accuracy with MSTCN (29.4% vs. 16.1%) and by 21.8 points with ASFormer (34.2% vs. 12.4%), demonstrating that temporally coherent generated segments are far more effective than static frame replication. In the 5-task Breakfast setup, the gap narrows slightly (54.5% vs. 32.5% for MSTCN; 57.2% vs. 29.5% for ASFormer) but remains substantial. On YouTube Instructional, the comparison is more nuanced: with MSTCN, TCA's accuracy (30.2%) is marginally below Exemplar (30.8%), but its Edit score (25.0 vs. 19.7) and F1 scores are clearly superior, indicating better segment-level segmentation. With ASFormer on YTI, TCA wins on both accuracy (25.2% vs. 22.1%) and Edit (20.9 vs. 18.9). The authors attribute the smaller YTI gains to reduced segment diversity in the smaller dataset, and note that ASFormer is particularly prone to overfitting on static exemplar data, which explains its weaker Exemplar performance relative to MSTCN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative replay paradigm from image continual learning (Shin et al., NeurIPS 2017) to the video domain, but departs fundamentally from prior video continual learning work (Alssum et al., CVPRW 2023; Villa et al., CVPR 2022) that simply stores frame exemplars. The key architectural departure is the Temporally Coherent Action (TCA) model, a conditional VAE augmented with a coherence variable that encodes relative temporal progression within an action segment, enabling the generation of diverse yet temporally consistent replay features. The paper also introduces incremental temporal action segmentation (iTAS) as a new task formulation, positioning procedural activities as natural incremental units rather than treating individual action classes as the incremental step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of incremental learning for temporal action segmentation, a task that is a natural fit for intelligent assistants that must learn complex procedural skills over time. The finding that temporal coherence in replay data matters more than raw frame diversity (Ours_static outperforms Ours_random) challenges the assumption that maximizing diversity is always beneficial for video replay. However, the 13.1% gap to the original-data upper bound on Breakfast (10-task) and the modest gains on YTI suggest the generative replay approach still falls well short of storing real data, and the reliance on dense per-frame labels for TCA training limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether the coherence variable generalizes to longer or more complex procedural videos, and whether the approach scales to larger datasets with more diverse action vocabularies.
