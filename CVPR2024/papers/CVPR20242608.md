## Problem

Existing texture generation methods built on 2D diffusion priors (e.g., TEXTure, Text2Tex) produce pre-illuminated textures with baked-in shadows and lighting, making them incompatible with physically based rendering pipelines where textures must be re-lit. Additionally, 2D models lack comprehensive 3D shape understanding, leading to view inconsistency and incomplete coverage on complex geometries. The paper addresses how to generate high-quality, lighting-less UV textures that generalize across diverse object categories while preserving semantic consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D surface mesh (with vertices and triangular faces, UV-unwrapped via original coordinates or xatlas) plus either a text prompt or a reference image as appearance condition, and produces a 2K-resolution multi-channel UV texture map. The output is specifically illumination-free, meaning no baked lighting or shadows are embedded, making it directly usable in PBR rendering engines. The UV texture map is inherently fragmented into separate patches due to UV cutting, which the method must handle to maintain 3D surface continuity.

## Method

Paint3D employs a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline. The coarse stage progressively samples multi-view images from a pre-trained depth-conditioned Stable Diffusion v1.5 model (6 axis-aligned viewpoints, 2 images per symmetric pair) and back-projects them onto the mesh surface, with subsequent views using depth-aware inpainting to fill uncolored regions. The refinement stage operates in UV space with two specialized diffusion models: a UV Inpainting model that fills self-occlusion holes, and a UVHD model that removes illumination artifacts and enhances detail. Both refinement models share a novel position encoder (following ControlNet's zero-convolution architecture) that injects 3D surface coordinates as adjacency information to resolve UV fragmentation. Training freezes the pre-trained denoiser and optimizes only the position encoder on 105K Objaverse meshes with paired position maps and lighting-free texture maps, allowing the model to learn the illumination-free prior directly from data distribution.

## Results

On the text-to-texture task, Paint3D achieves a FID of 27.28 and KID×10⁻³ of 4.81, outperforming the strongest baseline Text2Tex (FID 38.93, KID 7.94) by 29.93% and 39.42% respectively. In the user study, Paint3D scores 4.45/5 on overall quality and 4.74/5 on text fidelity, compared to 3.57 and 4.27 for Text2Tex. On the image-to-texture task, Paint3D reduces FID from 40.83 to 26.86 and KID from 9.76 to 4.94 relative to TEXTure, while scoring 4.89/5 on image fidelity in the user study.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Paint3D outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric for text-to-texture. Against Latent-Paint [35], the improvement is the most dramatic: FID drops from 62.22 to 27.28 (56% reduction) and KID from 15.81 to 4.81, with user-study overall quality rising from 2.83 to 4.45. Against TEXTure [49], Paint3D reduces FID by 37% (43.13→27.28) and KID by 57% (11.13→4.81), while also producing illumination-free textures that avoid the pre-illumination artifacts visible in TEXTure's outputs. Against the strongest baseline Text2Tex [4], Paint3D still achieves a 29.93% FID improvement and 39.42% KID improvement, with a 1.25-point gain in user-rated overall quality. For image-to-texture, Paint3D similarly dominates TEXTure, cutting FID by 34% and KID by 49%, and scoring 4.89/5 on image fidelity versus 3.73/5 for TEXTure.

## Relation to Prior Work

Paint3D builds directly on the iterative multi-view texturing paradigm established by TEXTure and Text2Tex, which use pre-trained depth-to-image diffusion models to progressively paint textures from multiple viewpoints. Its key departure is the addition of a UV-space refinement stage (UV Inpainting + UVHD) that uses a ControlNet-style position encoder to perform shape-aware inpainting and illumination removal, trained on lighting-free texture data from Objaverse. This contrasts with 3D-data-based methods (PointUV, Mesh2tex) that achieve lighting-less textures but sacrifice generalization, and with optimization-based approaches (Latent-NeRF, Fantasia3D) that compromise geometry fidelity. The coarse-to-fine design effectively decouples the generalization problem (solved by 2D priors) from the illumination-removal problem (solved by UV-space diffusion).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lighting-less textures are essential for integration into modern PBR rendering pipelines used in gaming, film, and VR, where pre-illuminated textures cause incorrect shadows and lighting errors upon relighting. Paint3D's framework is practically important because it leverages off-the-shelf 2D diffusion models for diversity while producing textures compatible with standard graphics workflows. However, the method still suffers from the multi-face problem in the coarse stage, cannot generate PBR material maps (normal, roughness, metallic), and cannot edit geometry—limiting its applicability to full production pipelines. An open question is whether the UV-space refinement approach can be extended to multi-channel material map generation.
