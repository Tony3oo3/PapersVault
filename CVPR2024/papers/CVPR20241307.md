## Problem

Existing human grasp generation methods rely on complete 3D object models, yet real-world observation typically provides only a single viewpoint, yielding incomplete object point clouds mixed with scene noise. This incompleteness causes generated hands to penetrate invisible object regions, and scene points introduce collision risks that existing approaches cannot handle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241307/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view scene point cloud of 2,000 points (1,000 from the partial object, 1,000 from the tabletop) as input and produces 61-dimensional MANO hand parameters (10 shape, 45 pose, 3 rotation, 3 translation), which are rendered into a 778-vertex hand mesh. Notably, the input is not a clean object point cloud but a mixed scene containing both the partially visible object and surrounding tabletop geometry, and the output must simultaneously avoid penetration into unseen object regions and collision with the table plane.

## Method

S2HGrasp is an end-to-end framework with two modules. The Global Perception module uses multi-task learning (point cloud completion via a PCN-inspired GSP head and category classification via a GCP head) on PointNet++ + transformer features to imbue the scene representation with global shape awareness of the partial object; at test time, only the aligned latent features are used without explicit completion. The DiffuGrasp module is a conditional diffusion model that, during training, adds noise to normalized hand parameters, passes them through the differentiable MANO layer to obtain hand point clouds, encodes those into features, and feeds them with scene features into a transformer decoder to recover the original parameters. A key design choice is converting noised parameters into geometric point clouds before decoding (rather than decoding raw parameters), which injects shape and positional context and improves grasp naturalness. Training combines reconstruction, contact-map, penetration, and plane losses, where the penetration and contact losses use a filtered union of the input point cloud and GSP completion to penalize intrusion into invisible regions while avoiding errors from rough completions.

## Results

On the primary View-S2HGD test set, S2HGrasp achieves a penetration volume of 6.58 cm³ and penetration depth of 0.21 cm, roughly halving the penetration volume of GraspCVAE (13.54 cm³, 0.23 cm depth). On the Object-S2HGD split (unseen objects), S2HGrasp further reduces penetration volume to 5.58 cm³ versus 14.48 cm³ for GraspCVAE. On the external HO3D-object set, S2HGrasp achieves 4.87 cm³ penetration volume and 3.30 cm mean grasp displace, compared to 8.66 cm³ and 3.61 cm for GraspCVAE. S2HGrasp also attains the highest contact ratio (99.41% on View-S2HGD) and perceptual score (3.25/5) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2HGrasp outperforms GraspCVAE (the CVAE component of GraspTTA [25], used without TTA since TTA requires complete objects) on every metric across all four test sets. The most striking gain is in penetration volume: S2HGrasp reduces it from 13.54 to 6.58 cm³ on View-S2HGD and from 14.48 to 5.58 cm³ on Object-S2HGD, roughly halving the hand-object interpenetration caused by incomplete point clouds. On the external HO3D-object set, S2HGrasp cuts penetration volume from 8.66 to 4.87 cm³ and improves contact ratio from 97.62% to 99.06%. Against the two-stage PCN+GraspCVAE baseline, S2HGrasp achieves 5.58 cm³ penetration volume versus 11.34 cm³, and against PCN+DiffuGrasp it achieves 5.58 vs. 7.68 cm³, confirming the advantage of end-to-end training over sequential completion-then-grasp pipelines. Compared to the SceneDiffuser-style approach without a hand encoder [22], S2HGrasp's hand-encoder design yields lower grasp displace (2.73 vs. 3.13 cm) at the same penetration depth, indicating more stable and natural grasps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human grasp generation line of work (GraspCVAE/GraspTTA, which require complete 3D object models) to the more realistic setting of single-view scene point clouds containing partial objects and tabletop interference. Methodologically, it adapts PCN's encoder-decoder architecture into a Global Shape Perception module for partial point cloud completion, and repurposes DDPM's forward-reverse diffusion process into a DiffuGrasp module that conditions on scene features. A key departure from prior diffusion-based grasp work (e.g., SceneDiffuser) is the insertion of a MANO-based hand encoder that converts noised parameters into point clouds before decoding, rather than directly regressing parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world perception systems observe objects from a single viewpoint, yet prior grasp generators assume complete geometry, making them inapplicable without expensive multi-view capture or reconstruction. The S2HGD dataset (~99K single-view scene point clouds with grasp annotations) provides a benchmark that did not previously exist for this setting. Limitations include the synthetic, single-object, tabletop-only nature of the data, and the reliance on a fixed 36-viewpoint camera arrangement; extending to multi-object cluttered scenes, real-world depth sensors, and dynamic manipulation remains open. The finding that test-time adaptation (TTA) from GraspTTA fails on incomplete objects is a useful negative result that cautions against naively transplanting techniques designed for complete geometry.
