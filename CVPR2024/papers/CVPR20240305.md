## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks face a fundamental trade-off: uniform-trigger attacks are easily detected by trigger-inversion methods, while sample-specific invisible attacks evade detection but are fragile against simple mitigation like fine-tuning on 5% of clean data. LOTUS addresses this gap by designing an attack that is simultaneously evasive to detection and resilient to mitigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240305/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for CelebA, 224×224 for RImageNet) and outputs a class label. The trigger is a single-color polygon patch placed on the side or corner of the image, but critically, the trigger is partition-specific: each of n sub-partitions of the victim class has its own unique trigger, and only the matching trigger on the matching partition induces misclassification. The partitioning function and trigger assignments are secret to the attacker.

## Method

LOTUS first partitions victim-class samples into n sub-partitions using either explicit attributes or an implicit scheme (K-means/GMM clustering on encoder features followed by training a surrogate classifier for generalizable partition assignment). Each partition is assigned a unique polygon trigger, and data poisoning injects trigger-stamped samples labeled with the target class. The key technical contribution is a trigger focusing mechanism that adds two loss terms beyond standard data poisoning: a Label-specific Loss ensuring non-victim-class images are unaffected even with the correct trigger, and a Dynamic Loss that stamps each partition's samples with both individual wrong triggers T_j and paired triggers [T_i, T_j] (j≠i) labeled as the victim class. This paired-trigger strategy requires only 2n−2 combinations (linear in n) rather than the exponential 2^n−2 from the full combinatorial loss, yet effectively teaches the model that any trigger combination containing an extra trigger cancels the backdoor effect, restricting each trigger strictly to its own partition.

## Results

LOTUS achieves attack success rates (ASR) of 89.00%–94.30% across 4 datasets and 7 model architectures, with benign accuracy degradation within 1.07%. The trigger focusing mechanism limits ASR on wrong triggers/combinations to below 6.87% (average ~4%). Most critically, after backdoor mitigation (fine-tuning, fine-pruning, NAD, ANP with 5% clean data), LOTUS retains 34.90%–46.90% ASR, substantially outperforming all 7 compared attacks whose ASR drops to 0.17%–37.91%. LOTUS also evades all 4 trigger-inversion detection methods (Neural Cleanse, Pixel, ABS, FeatRE) where most baselines are detected by at least one.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the no-defense setting, LOTUS achieves 93.80% ASR, slightly below the 98–100% ASR of BadNets, Dynamic, LIRA, and DFST, reflecting a deliberate trade-off between raw attack strength and resilience. However, after any of the four mitigation methods, LOTUS dramatically outperforms all baselines: it retains 46.90% ASR under fine-tuning (vs. 0.17%–22.86% for others), 44.90% under fine-pruning (vs. 0.17%–22.03%), 42.30% under NAD (vs. 1.40%–24.66%), and 34.90% under ANP (vs. 0.32%–37.91%). The closest competitor is LIRA under ANP (37.91%) and DFST under fine-tuning (22.86%), but LOTUS leads by 14+ points in most settings. On detection evasion, LOTUS is the only attack that evades all four trigger-inversion methods (NC, Pixel, ABS, FeatRE), while BadNets, Dynamic, ISSBA, LIRA, and DFST are detected by at least NC/Pixel/ABS, and IA and WaNet are detected by FeatRE. The paper attributes LOTUS's resilience to the learned partition-trigger correlation being difficult to unlearn during retraining, whereas uniform or sample-specific triggers are more easily washed out.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOTUS departs from uniform-trigger backdoor attacks (BadNets, Dynamic) and sample-specific invisible attacks (LIRA, IA, WaNet, ISSBA) by introducing a sub-partitioning mechanism that assigns unique triggers to different groups within a victim class, combined with a novel trigger focusing training technique. While sample-specific attacks already broke the universal-trigger assumption to evade detection, they remained vulnerable to simple mitigation like fine-tuning; LOTUS addresses this gap by learning intricate input-trigger correlations through a linearly-scaled pair-trigger stamping loss (Eq. 3) rather than the exponential full-combination loss. The approach is conceptually related to subpopulation poisoning [27] but differs in objective (backdoor injection vs. availability degradation) and mechanism (trigger injection with focusing vs. no trigger).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LOTUS demonstrates that the evasiveness-resilience trade-off in backdoor attacks is not fundamental: an attack can simultaneously evade all four major trigger-inversion detectors (including FeatRE, which catches feature-space attacks) and retain 35–47% ASR after four mitigation methods, where all seven compared attacks drop below 25%. The trigger focusing technique is a practical, linearly-scaled training modification that practitioners can adopt. Open questions include how the attack scales to many partitions, whether adaptive defenders who can probe partition boundaries can recover the secret partitioning, and whether the approach transfers to non-CNN architectures or modalities beyond image classification.
