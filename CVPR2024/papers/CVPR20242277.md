## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction and generation methods produce clothed humans as a single-layer mesh where hair, clothing, and accessories are fused into one surface, making them unusable for virtual try-on or avatar customization. Decomposing such a mesh into separate animatable layers is difficult because occluded regions lack geometry and texture, and the resulting meshes are not normalized in pose or body shape, preventing coherent composition with novel identities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242277/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-layer clothed 3D human mesh (e.g., from RenderPeople scans or single-view reconstruction) along with a text prompt identifying the target object to separate and an OpenPose pose condition. It produces two complete 3D meshes in SMPL-X canonical space — the human body and the target object — each with full geometry (via DMTet) and vertex-color textures, plus LBS skinning weights that enable reposing to arbitrary poses. A final refinement step adjusts vertex positions along normals to eliminate inter-layer penetration before composition.

## Method

GALA decomposes the input mesh by lifting multi-view 2D segmentations (from SAM) onto the 3D surface, then initializes separate DMTet representations for the human and object layers in canonical space. The key technical contribution is a pose-guided SDS loss that conditions a pretrained latent diffusion model via ControlNet with OpenPose skeletons, enabling pose-aware geometry and texture completion. Crucially, this loss is applied in canonical space (not posed space) to avoid many-to-one mapping artifacts, while a reconstruction loss in posed space preserves visible-region fidelity. The pipeline optimizes geometry first (reconstruction + pose-guided SDS + segmentation losses), then textures (RGB reconstruction + pose-guided SDS on rendered vertex colors), and finally refines composition with a penetration-reduction loss that displaces human vertices along normals away from the object surface.

## Results

On the decomposition task (RenderPeople, 30 scans), GALA achieves a CLIP text-image direction similarity of 0.1117 and a pixel-wise object removal (POR) score of 0.1144, substantially outperforming Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (0.0621 / 0.4871) and Vox-E (0.0374 / 0.5583). On the canonicalization task (CAPE, 18 subjects × 100 scans), GALA attains 84.70% IoU and 0.821 cm Chamfer distance from a single scan, surpassing nearest-neighbor (83.93% / 0.845) and KNN (83.93% / 0.846) baselines, and approaching Fast-SNARF trained on 50 scans (86.87% / 1.000) while using only one scan.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For decomposition, GALA clearly wins over both text-guided 3D editing baselines: Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [27] achieves only 0.0621 CLIP similarity and 0.4871 POR score, while Vox-E [69] scores 0.0374 and 0.5583 respectively—GALA nearly doubles the CLIP score and reduces the POR score by roughly 4×, indicating far more complete and faithful object removal. For canonicalization, GALA (84.70% IoU, 0.821 cm) narrowly beats the NN baseline from ARCH [33] (83.93%, 0.845) and the KNN baseline from [84] (83.93%, 0.846), showing that the pose-guided SDS loss provides meaningful improvement over simple skinning-weight transfer. Against Fast-SNARF [15], GALA from a single scan dramatically outperforms Fast-SNARF with 1 scan (27.90% IoU) or 10 scans (41.56%), and comes within ~2% IoU of Fast-SNARF trained on 50 scans (86.87%), despite using 50× fewer observations. The paper notes that the original Fast-SNARF is trained on ~3000 scans, so GALA's single-scan result is competitive with a method using two orders of magnitude more data.

## Relation to Prior Work

GALA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) clothed human reconstruction via implicit representations (PIFu, PIFuHD, SCARF), which treats the clothed body as a single-layer mesh; (2) text-to-3D generation via Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion, Fantasia3D), which produces single-layer assets in isolation; and (3) canonicalization of articulated shapes (SNARF, Fast-SNARF), which requires multiple scans to learn a pose-invariant representation. The paper's core departure is the pose-guided SDS loss, which injects ControlNet-based pose conditioning into the SDS gradient to enable canonicalization from a single scan, and the use of 2D segmentation (Segment Anything) lifted to 3D to decompose the input into separate layers before inpainting each in canonical space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for virtual try-on and avatar customization pipelines, where artists currently hand-craft layered assets; GALA automates this from a single scan. However, the evaluation is limited in scale (30 RenderPeople scans, 18 CAPE subjects), the decomposition baselines (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, Vox-E) are text-guided editing methods rather than purpose-built decomposition approaches, and the method depends on a chain of pretrained models (SAM, ControlNet, latent diffusion) that may not generalize to out-of-distribution garments or extreme poses. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two layers, how it handles highly deformable or semi-transparent materials, and whether the pose-guided SDS loss can be made more efficient for real-time applications.
