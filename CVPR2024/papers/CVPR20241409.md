## Problem

Existing VLP-based video action recognition methods follow an 'adapt then align' paradigm that first adapts image encoders to video representations and then aligns them with action-name embeddings, overlooking the entity-to-region correspondences that underlie complex activity concepts. This limits generalization to unseen or unfamiliar action categories because the model cannot reuse the visual-semantic bindings of individual entities (body parts, objects, scenes) across different actions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (16 or 32 frames at 224×224 resolution) and an offline-constructed text corpus of K action-related entities (each with a name and description, generated via POS tagging, LLM prompting, WordNet, and word sense disambiguation) as inputs. It produces a single video representation vector that is matched against CLIP text-encoder embeddings of action labels (with prompt ensembling) for classification. The text corpus is fixed and reusable across all action categories, enabling zero-shot transfer without retraining.

## Method

ALT proposes an 'Align before Adapt' paradigm with two stages. First, a CLIP-ViT backbone augmented with ToMe token-merging modules produces region-aware image embeddings by clustering visually similar patches; these are matched to text-corpus entities via Gumbel-Softmax similarity with a differentiable one-hot trick to enforce a single dominant entity per region. Second, the text embeddings of the aligned entities serve as queries in a transformer-based video adapter that cross-attends to multi-frame visual embeddings (as keys/values), with self-attention and 1D-convolution modules for temporal modeling. The adapter compresses T frame-level entity queries into a single video vector via average pooling and an MLP, trained with cross-entropy against CLIP text embeddings of action labels. This design preserves VLP's visual-language alignment during adaptation and makes entity-to-region correspondences reusable across actions, yielding strong zero-shot and few-shot generalization at low computational cost (4947 GFLOPs for 88.1% top-1 on Kinetics-400).

## Results

On Kinetics-400 (fully supervised, 32 frames), ALT-L/14 achieves 88.1% top-1 accuracy with only 4947 GFLOPs, surpassing AIM-L/14 (87.5%) by 0.6% while using 55% fewer GFLOPs (4947 vs. 11208). The smaller ALT-B/16 variant reaches 85.5% top-1 at just 1308 GFLOPs, outperforming ViViT-H/16 (84.9%) with 30× less computation. In few-shot (2-shot) settings, ALT-B/16 achieves 64.3% on HMDB-51 and 93.2% on UCF-101, exceeding the previous best (ViFi-CLIP) by 7.1% and 12.5% respectively. In zero-shot evaluation, ALT-L/14 reaches 56.6% on HMDB-51 and 83.9% on UCF-101, setting new state-of-the-art across all three target datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among CLIP-400M-based methods on Kinetics-400, ALT-L/14 (88.1%) outperforms all prior approaches: AIM-L/14 (87.5%, +0.6%), EVL-L/14 (87.3%, +0.8%), ST-Adapter-L/14 (87.2%, +0.9%), and X-CLIP-L/14 (87.1%, +1.0%), while requiring 55% fewer GFLOPs than AIM. The only method exceeding ALT is MTV-H/16 (89.1%), which uses 70M video-text pairs for pretraining and 9× the GFLOPs (45537). In few-shot (2-shot) evaluation, ALT-B/16 surpasses ViFi-CLIP by 7.1% on HMDB-51 (64.3% vs. 57.2%) and 12.5% on UCF-101 (93.2% vs. 80.7%), and outperforms X-CLIP-B/16 by 11.3% and 16.8% respectively. In zero-shot settings, ALT-L/14 leads all baselines on HMDB-51 (56.6% vs. 51.3% for ViFi-CLIP), UCF-101 (83.9% vs. 76.8%), and Kinetics-600 (74.9% vs. 71.2%), with margins of 5.3%, 7.1%, and 3.7% over the strongest prior method. The gap over baselines widens substantially in low-data regimes, confirming the generalization benefit of the entity-to-region alignment paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'adapt then align' paradigm in VLP-based video action recognition (X-CLIP, EVL, ST-Adapter, AIM, ViFi-CLIP), which first adapts image encoders to video representations and then aligns them with action label embeddings. Instead, ALT inverts this order: it first establishes entity-to-region correspondences by matching region-aware image embeddings (obtained via repurposed ToMe token merging) against an offline-constructed text corpus of action-related entities, then uses the aligned entity text embeddings as queries in a transformer-based video adapter. The approach builds on CLIP's visual-language alignment, ToMe's soft bipartite matching (originally designed for ViT acceleration), and Gumbel-Softmax differentiable assignment, but recombines them into a new pipeline where semantic grounding precedes temporal aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entity-to-region alignment provides a principled mechanism for generalization: because entities (bodies, objects, scenes) recur across action categories, the learned correspondences transfer to unseen classes, yielding strong zero-shot and few-shot results that outperform prior SOTA by over 10%. The efficiency gains (55% fewer GFLOPs than AIM at comparable accuracy) make the approach practical for deployment. Open questions include whether the handcrafted text corpus pipeline scales to open-vocabulary or non-human-centric domains, whether the Gumbel-Softmax one-hot enforcement is necessary versus softer assignments, and whether the paradigm extends to other video understanding tasks beyond classification (e.g., temporal localization, video-text retrieval).
