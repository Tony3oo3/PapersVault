## Problem

Fine-grained image retrieval has relied exclusively on sketches to capture instance-level visual details, but sketches cannot express qualitative attributes like colour, texture, or contextual cues that text handles naturally. Existing sketch+text composed retrieval methods require expensive paired fine-grained textual descriptions during training and have been limited to category- or scene-level tasks, leaving the fine-grained instance-level setting unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242630/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a freehand sketch (sparse binary line drawing) and an optional short text phrase (e.g., 'red', 'with a woman riding') as input. The sketch is encoded via CLIP's frozen ViT-L/14 visual encoder and converted to a single pseudo-word token in CLIP's 768-d text embedding space; the text is tokenised via CLIP's BPE tokenizer. At inference, the composed query is a single 768-d embedding vector compared against pre-computed gallery image features, producing a ranked retrieval list. For the generation task, the same composed embedding drives a pre-trained StyleGAN2 to produce a photorealistic image.

## Method

The core mechanism converts a sketch into a pseudo-word token in CLIP's text embedding space via a 3-layer MLP visual-to-word converter, then composes it with user text through CLIP's frozen text transformer using connecting phrases, exploiting CLIP's implicit grammatical compositionality. The key training innovation is a compositionality constraint that uses the sketch-photo difference signal (Δ = |p⁺ − s|) as a proxy for the absent textual description, enforcing that the difference-enriched embedding is closer to the target photo than the sketch-only embedding. A neutral-text regulariser (100 GPT-generated generic phrases like 'with a line drawing') constrains the difference signal from disrupting CLIP's language manifold grammar. Training is multi-task, jointly optimising a standard triplet loss, the compositionality constraint, neutral-text regularisation, a text-to-text prompt generalisation loss (aligning learnable prompts with handcrafted English prompts), a region-aware triplet loss using patch-level attention, and an auxiliary UNet reconstruction loss for appearance guidance. Only the LayerNorm of the visual encoder, the visual-to-word converter, three learnable prompt vectors, and the UNet decoder are trained; all CLIP encoders remain frozen.

## Results

On fine-grained object-level composed retrieval, the proposed method achieves Acc.@5 of 47.3 on ShoeV2, 73.5 on ChairV2, and 30.6 on Sketchy, outperforming the strongest unsupervised baseline SEARLE by 8.9, 12.7, and 5.3 points respectively. On scene-level retrieval, it reaches Acc.@5 of 22.7 on FS-COCO and 33.4 on SketchyCOCO, surpassing SEARLE by 5.0 and 7.4 points. Average improvements over the best baseline range from +11.3 (FS-COCO Acc.@5) to +45.5 (ShoeV2 Acc.@10) across all five datasets. For domain attribute transfer on ImageNet-R, the method attains r@10 of 15.3, exceeding SEARLE (12.1) and Pic2Word (10.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six compared baselines across every dataset and metric. Among unsupervised competitors, SEARLE [2] (two-stage optimisation-based textual inversion) is the closest, with the proposed method exceeding it by 8.9 Acc.@5 on ShoeV2 (47.3 vs 38.4) and 12.7 on ChairV2 (73.5 vs 60.8), while Pic2Word [57] (3M-image-trained inversion network) trails by 12.6 and 17.8 Acc.@5 on the same datasets. Supervised methods that require paired sketch-text-photo triplets—Combiner [1], TASK-former [59], and SceneTrilogy [11]—are substantially outperformed, with SceneTrilogy [11] (the strongest supervised baseline) achieving only 29.1 Acc.@5 on ShoeV2 versus 47.3 for the proposed method. The naive CLIP-based composition baseline B-CLIP(S+T) [51] (mean of frozen sketch and text features) scores just 19.1 Acc.@5 on ShoeV2, confirming that simple feature averaging fails to capture fine-grained compositionality. Notably, the proposed method achieves these gains without any paired textual descriptions during training, whereas all supervised baselines rely on BLIP-generated captions or ground-truth annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the textual inversion paradigm (Pic2Word, SEARLE) from the photo+text composed retrieval setting to the sketch+text domain, where the visual-to-linguistic gap is substantially larger due to the sparsity of freehand sketches. It builds directly on CLIP's joint embedding space, repurposing the text encoder as the compositionality engine: a sketch is mapped to a pseudo-word token via a visual-to-word converter, then combined with user text through CLIP's transformer. The key departure from prior sketch+text retrieval work (Sangkloy et al. 2022, Chowdhury et al. 2023) is the elimination of paired sketch-text-photo triplets during training, using a sketch-photo difference signal as a proxy for the missing textual description instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: fine-grained sketch-text-photo datasets are extremely expensive to annotate, and this work removes that bottleneck entirely, making fine-grained composed retrieval feasible with only sketch-photo pairs already available in standard SBIR datasets. The compositionality constraint (sketch-photo difference as text proxy) is a generalizable training trick that could apply to other sparse-to-dense cross-modal settings. Open questions include whether a single pseudo-word token suffices for highly complex sketches (the authors note 98.35% Acc@1 but acknowledge multi-token variants), whether the approach transfers to other sparse visual inputs (e.g., point clouds, depth maps), and how well it scales to open-vocabulary settings beyond the evaluated datasets.
