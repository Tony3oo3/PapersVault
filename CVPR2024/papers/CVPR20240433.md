## Problem

Traditional aggregate-then-adapt federated learning suffers from client drift under strong cross-client data heterogeneity, where local fine-tuning causes clients to forget global knowledge and diverge from the global optimum. Existing aggregation-free FL methods that train the server directly on client-condensed data do not adequately leverage knowledge from other clients to improve condensation quality, and relying solely on condensed data for global training limits convergence and robustness.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local set of labeled images (grayscale 28×28 for FashionMNIST, RGB 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, RGB for DomainNet) and receives the global model parameters plus class-wise mean logits from the server. In return, each client uploads a small set of condensed synthetic images (50 per class) and temperature-scaled soft labels computed from its original data. The server outputs a single trained global classifier; no local model parameters are ever transmitted, distinguishing this from standard FedAvg-style updates.

## Method

FedAF replaces model aggregation with an aggregation-free pipeline built on two mechanisms. First, Collaborative Data Condensation (CDC) has each client optimize its condensed data by minimizing a combined loss: a standard Distribution Matching (DM) term in latent feature space plus a Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD) regularization that aligns the client's condensed-data logits with the server-averaged class-wise mean logits across all clients, giving each client a global perspective during condensation. Second, Local-Global Knowledge Matching (LGKM) trains the server's global model with a cross-entropy loss on the received condensed data augmented by a symmetric KL-divergence term that matches soft labels derived from the condensed data against averaged soft labels extracted from clients' original data, preserving knowledge from prior rounds and stabilizing updates. A model re-sampling step interpolates the global model with random parameters (γ=0.9) during local condensation to mitigate overfitting of the synthetic data.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with strong label-skew (α=0.02), FedAF achieves 65.15% global model accuracy, surpassing the best aggregate-then-adapt baseline FedDyn (45.73%) by 19.43% and the prior aggregation-free method FedDM (60.28%) by 4.87%. Similar gains are observed on CIFAR-100 (48.71% vs. 35.01% for FedDyn) and FashionMNIST (87.53% vs. 69.79% for FedDyn). FedAF also accelerates convergence substantially: on CIFAR-10 at α=0.02, it reaches 60% accuracy in 3 rounds versus 15 rounds for FedDM, an 80% speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAF outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. Against the strongest aggregate-then-adapt method, FedDyn, FedAF gains up to 19.43% on CIFAR-10 (65.15% vs. 45.73% at α=0.02), 13.70% on CIFAR-100, and 17.74% on FashionMNIST. The remaining aggregate-then-adapt baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedBN, MOON, FedGen) are all surpassed by wider margins, with FedAvg showing the largest gap (up to 25.44% on CIFAR-10). Against the closest aggregation-free competitor FedDM, FedAF consistently leads by 2.17–4.87% in accuracy and converges roughly 80% faster (3 rounds vs. 15 to reach 60% on CIFAR-10 at α=0.02). On the feature-skew DomainNet benchmark, FedAF achieves 54.68% average accuracy versus 52.62% for FedDM and 47.95% for FedDyn, ranking first or second in every individual domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedAF departs from the dominant aggregate-then-adapt FL paradigm (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedDyn, MOON, FedGen) by adopting an aggregation-free framework where the server trains directly on client-condensed synthetic data rather than averaging local model updates. It builds most directly on FedDM [34], which first demonstrated that distribution-matching-based data condensation can replace model aggregation in FL, and on the meta-knowledge condensation work [25] that explored sharing condensed data among clients. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a Sliced Wasserstein Distance-based collaborative regularization (CDC loss) that aligns each client's condensed data with a global logit-space distribution, and a KL-divergence-based local-global knowledge matching (LGKM) scheme that supplements condensed data with soft labels — neither of which was explored in prior aggregation-free FL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The aggregation-free paradigm eliminates client drift by construction, which is a structural advantage over all aggregate-then-adapt methods that must add regularization or control variates to mitigate drift. The practical trade-off is that clients must upload condensed images and soft labels rather than model gradients, raising different privacy and communication considerations that the paper acknowledges but does not fully quantify. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet), deeper architectures, or highly asymmetric client participation, and whether the SWD-based alignment generalizes beyond the logit-space matching used here. The finding that a modest random perturbation (γ=0.9) outperforms exact reuse of the previous global model (γ=1.0) for data condensation is a small but practically useful regularization insight.
