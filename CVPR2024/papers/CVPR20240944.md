## Problem

Existing saliency object ranking methods fail to explicitly model the saliency degree-related relationships between candidate objects. Specifically, they do not separately capture shape and texture interactions while suppressing task-irrelevant noise, they ignore cross-image relationships among objects sharing the same saliency level, and graph-based approaches rely on single-value edges that cannot represent the complex, diverse relationships underlying saliency ranking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240944/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 480×640 and produces a final saliency ranking map that combines per-object segmentation masks with continuous saliency scores. The number of object queries is dataset-dependent (5 for ASSR, 8 for IRSR), and the pipeline first detects candidate saliency objects via Mask2Former before applying graph-based ranking reasoning. Training uses backbones (ResNet-50 or Swin-L) pretrained on MS-COCO, with a fixed batch size of 8 over 36,000 iterations.

## Method

DSGNN is built atop Mask2Former and introduces two domain-separation modules. The Shape-Texture Graph Domain Separation (STGDS) module separately extracts shape and texture queries, constructs a shape graph and a texture graph where each edge is a multi-dimensional feature learned via cross-attention between node pairs, and encodes them with GGCN layers. A shared GNN encoder captures task-relevant (saliency-aware) features while orthogonal encoders capture task-irrelevant features, with a reconstruction loss preventing trivial collapse. The Cross Image Graph Domain Separation (CIGDS) module then learns a unified representation across a batch of images so that objects with the same saliency level share similar features regardless of scene, with image-specific noise obtained by subtraction. The key architectural novelty is the first use of multi-dimensional learned edge features in saliency object analysis, enabling the graph to encode complex, diverse, and ranking-relevant pairwise relationships rather than a single scalar weight.

## Results

On the ASSR dataset with a Swin-L backbone, DSGNN achieves MAE 0.047, SOR 0.853, and SA-SOR 0.761, improving SA-SOR by 5.2% over the previous best (OCOR, 0.687) and MAE by 40.5% (0.079→0.047). On the IRSR dataset with Swin-L, it reaches MAE 0.067, SOR 0.807, and SA-SOR 0.606, a 4.1% SA-SOR gain over IRSR [31]. Notably, the ResNet-50 variant (MAE 0.060, SA-SOR 0.716 on ASSR) already surpasses all prior Swin-L-based methods, demonstrating strong efficiency. The method does not achieve the best SOR score (OCOR reports 0.883 on ASSR), which the authors attribute to SOR's insensitivity to missed detections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSGNN (Swin-L) outperforms OCOR [49] on ASSR by 40.5% in MAE (0.047 vs. 0.079) and 7.4% in SA-SOR (0.761 vs. 0.687), though OCOR retains a higher SOR score (0.883 vs. 0.853) due to that metric ignoring missed targets. Against IRSR [31] (ResNet-50), DSGNN improves MAE from 0.105 to 0.060 on ASSR and from 0.085 to 0.073 on IRSR, while also gaining in SA-SOR (0.716 vs. 0.709; 0.568 vs. 0.565). The ResNet-50 variant of DSGNN surpasses the VOVNet-39-based SOR [13] method (MAE 0.060 vs. 0.081) and the ResNet-101-based ASSR [45] method (MAE 0.060 vs. 0.101, SA-SOR 0.716 vs. 0.667). On IRSR, DSGNN (Swin-L) achieves the best MAE (0.067) and SA-SOR (0.606) among all reported methods, while the SOR metric is marginally lower than IRSR [31] (0.807 vs. 0.806, essentially tied). The authors note that OCOR's reported results could not be directly compared due to discrepancies between their open-source code and published numbers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mask2Former's query-based instance segmentation as its detection backbone, extending the saliency object ranking (SOR) task formalized by ASSR [45] and IRSR [31]. It departs from prior SOR methods (ASSR, IRSR, SOR, OCOR) that model object-scene relationships via attention or single-value graph edges by introducing a domain separation framework (inspired by Bousmalis et al. [3]) applied to GGCN-based graphs, explicitly disentangling shape and texture cues and learning multi-dimensional edge features. The cross-image domain separation module is a novel addition not present in any prior SOR approach, aiming to create scene-invariant representations for objects at the same saliency level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-dimensional edge GNN and the explicit shape-texture domain separation provide a more principled way to model inter-object relationships in saliency ranking, with the ablations confirming each component contributes. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets derived from MS-COCO and SALICON, and the method's reliance on Mask2Former's detection quality means ranking errors are bounded by upstream segmentation failures. Open questions include whether the cross-image domain separation generalizes to more diverse scene distributions or larger object counts, and whether the approach transfers to video saliency ranking or other ranking tasks beyond static images.
