## Problem

Existing defenses against adversarial point cloud attacks—whether adversarial training or input-oriented filtering—overfit to specific attack patterns and fail to generalize to unseen attacks. The paper identifies that adversarial point clouds remain visually indistinguishable from benign ones to humans, suggesting that a causal modeling approach which isolates structural information from confounding noise could provide a more generalizable defense.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a 3D point cloud (uniformly sampled to 2,048 points, normalized to a unit sphere) and outputs a class label. It is model-agnostic, wrapping around existing classifiers (PointNet, DGCNN, PointCNN) that accept variable-length point sets. At inference, the input point cloud is processed through three parallel paths—FPS-based structural extraction, PU-Net upsampling with random subsampling and added noise for reconstruction, and a joint cross-attention fusion—before being fed to the pre-trained classifier's feature extractor and classification head.

## Method

CausalPC formulates point cloud classification through a causal graph with variables for the physical object O, observed point cloud X, structural information Z, label Y, and hidden confounders U (adversarial perturbations, sensor noise). A key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) applies front-door adjustment to eliminate the unobservable U, transforming the causal effect P(Y|do(X=x)) into an expectation over P(Z|x) and P(X|o), which is approximated via Monte Carlo sampling. Three sub-modules implement this: (1) Farthest Point Sampling extracts structural information Z as a sparse vertex-like subset; (2) a PU-Net-based upsampler followed by random subsampling and uniform noise simulates the point cloud generation process P(X|o); (3) a joint cross-attention module fuses features from Z, the reconstructed X, and their concatenation to model P(Y|Z,X), reusing pre-trained classifier backbones with only the attention and classification heads fine-tuned for a few epochs.

## Results

On ModelNet40 under untargeted attacks, CausalPC raises the average classification accuracy of DGCNN from 29.2% (vanilla) to 72.0%, far exceeding the best baseline (AT at 42.4%). For PointNet, average ACC improves from 40.9% to 67.4% (best baseline SOR: 54.8%), and for PointCNN from 59.6% to 66.6% (best baseline AT: 62.8%). Under targeted attacks, the average attack success rate drops dramatically: PointNet from 73.1% to 2.2%, DGCNN from 67.8% to 0.7%, and PointCNN from 16.7% to 2.0%, all outperforming the best baseline DUP-Net (7.3%, 2.0%, 2.9% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalPC outperforms all four baselines across every model and attack setting. Against the input-oriented defenses, SOR/DUP-Net (Zhou et al., ICCV 2019) achieve the best baseline average ACC of 54.8% for PointNet and 39.2% for DGCNN under untargeted attacks, while CausalPC reaches 67.4% and 72.0% respectively; DUP-Net also fails on the Drop attack (42.7% ACC for PointCNN vs. 71.7% for CausalPC) because it relies on outlier filtering. GvG (Dong et al., CVPR 2020) is model-specific (applied only to PointNet) and is particularly vulnerable to shape-invariant attacks, with ASR reaching 100% against GeoA3. The adversarial training baseline AT (Liu et al., ICIP 2019, using IFGM examples) achieves 42.4% average ACC for DGCNN but collapses to near-100% ASR against unseen attacks like KNN and GeoA3, whereas CausalPC maintains ASR below 5% on all shape-invariant attacks. PAGN (Liang et al., 2022), which trains in feature space, shows marginal improvement over AT but still lags CausalPC by 12–26 percentage points in average ACC across models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply causal inference—specifically front-door adjustment and do-calculus—to adversarial defense in point cloud classification. It departs from prior causal-robustness work in image classification (which models style or background as confounders) by identifying structural information (extracted via FPS) and hidden confounders (adversarial perturbations, sensor noise) as the relevant causal variables for 3D point clouds. On the defense side, it contrasts with input-oriented methods (SOR, DUP-Net, GvG) that target specific perturbation patterns and adversarial-training methods (AT, PAGN) that overfit to known attack distributions, instead proposing a model-agnostic, inference-time framework that approximates the causal effect P(Y|do(X)) through Monte Carlo sampling over reconstructed point clouds and structural summaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, model-agnostic defense that generalizes across attack types without requiring knowledge of specific perturbation patterns, addressing a key limitation of both input-oriented and adversarial-training defenses. The substantial gains (e.g., DGCNN on ModelNet40: 29.2% → 72.0% vs. best baseline 42.4%) across three architectures and ten attacks suggest the causal framing captures something fundamental about why DNNs fail on adversarial point clouds. However, the authors acknowledge a gap between the ideal causal model and its practical implementation (FPS approximates structure, PU-Net approximates resampling), and no analytical robustness lower bound is provided. Open questions include whether the approach extends to real-world LiDAR data, 3D detection, and whether the inference-time Monte Carlo cost is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployments.
