## Problem

Learning implicit shape representations from sparse, noisy, unoriented point clouds without ground-truth supervision remains challenging, and the community has predominantly relied on signed distance fields (SDFs) for this task. Binary occupancy fields, which encode only the sign of the SDF and impose no Eikonal constraint, are theoretically easier to learn but have not been explored for unsupervised sparse point cloud fitting. The core difficulty is that with only surface points available, there is no natural way to supervise an occupancy classifier that requires inside/outside labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse, noisy, unoriented point cloud (typically 1024 points with Gaussian noise σ=0.005) as input and fits an instance-specific MLP that maps 3D coordinates to a binary occupancy probability. No normals, pre-learned priors, or additional training data are required. The learned occupancy field is extracted as a watertight triangle mesh via Marching Cubes, and the entire fitting process takes roughly 5 minutes on a single GPU for a 1024-point input.

## Method

The key insight is to supervise the occupancy field's decision boundary through a margin uncertainty sampling loss: a margin function U_θ(x) = P(y=1|x,θ) − P(y=0|x,θ) is defined, and one step of Newton-Raphson root finding on this margin (initialized at query points near the surface) produces uncertain samples that are then pulled toward the nearest input point cloud sample via MSE. This simultaneously enforces correct occupancy sign on both sides of the boundary and acts as a smoothness constraint. An entropy polarization regularizer complements this by minimizing entropy over uniformly sampled space (pushing the field toward confident predictions) while maximizing entropy at the input points (concentrating uncertainty at the true surface), with its weight λ decaying exponentially during training. The MLP follows the N-Pull architecture with K=51 nearest neighbors for local scale estimation, and is initialized following the SAL scheme adapted to predict occupancy for an r-radius sphere.

## Results

On ShapeNet (1024 noisy points), the method achieves CD1=0.76, CD2=0.020, NC=0.88, and FS=0.83, outperforming the best SDF-based competitor G-Pull (CD1=1.07, CD2=0.032, NC=0.70, FS=0.74) by 29% in CD1 and 37% in CD2. On Faust human shapes, it reaches CD1=0.260, CD2=0.002, NC=0.952, FS=0.974, surpassing even the supervised feed-forward POCO (CD1=0.308, FS=0.981) in Chamfer distance while matching its F-Score. On the 3D Scene benchmark (100 pts/m²), the method achieves mean CD1=0.027 and CD2=0.003, roughly 3× better than NTPS (CD1=0.077) and N-Pull (CD1=0.097).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all unsupervised SDF-based baselines (N-Pull, G-Pull, NTPS, OG-INR, SPSR) across every metric on ShapeNet, with the largest gains over G-Pull (29% CD1 reduction) and SPSR (67% CD1 reduction). Against the closest competitor NTPS [15], it wins on CD1 (0.76 vs 1.11), CD2 (0.020 vs 0.067), and FS (0.83 vs 0.74) on ShapeNet, and on CD1/CD2/FS on Faust, though NTPS edges it slightly on NC for 3D Scene (0.897 vs 0.886). On Faust, the method also beats supervised feed-forward models POCO [8] (CD1 0.260 vs 0.308) and CONet [60] (CD1 0.260 vs 1.26), as well as the prior-based On-Surf [49] (CD1 0.260 vs 0.584), demonstrating that unsupervised occupancy learning generalizes better than ShapeNet-trained priors on out-of-distribution data. On the 3D Scene benchmark, it dominates NDrop [9] (CD1 0.027 vs 0.175), SAP [61] (CD1 0.027 vs 0.151), and NSpline [84] (CD1 0.027 vs 0.151) by 5–6× in Chamfer distance. On SRB, it matches DiGs [5] on Hausdorff distance (6.04 vs 6.05) while improving CD1 (0.49 vs 0.68) in the sparse setting, and slightly outperforms OG-INR [38] in the dense setting (CD1 0.20 vs 0.20, HD 3.94 vs 4.06).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of learning signed distance functions (SDFs) for unsupervised implicit shape reconstruction by proposing binary occupancy fields as a simpler, more tractable alternative. It builds directly on the MLP architecture and nearest-neighbor sampling strategy from Neural-Pull [48] and the initialization scheme from SAL [2], but repurposes them for a classification objective rather than continuous regression. The key conceptual shift is replacing the Eikonal-constrained SDF learning with a margin-based uncertainty sampling loss (one-step Newton-Raphson root finding) combined with entropy polarization regularization, a training strategy not previously applied to occupancy fields in the unsupervised sparse setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simpler representation (binary occupancy) with a carefully designed loss can outperform both SDF-based unsupervised methods and supervised generalizable models (POCO, CONet) on sparse, noisy inputs, suggesting that representation choice and loss design matter more than prior-based generalization in extreme sparsity regimes. The entropy regularization as a convergence stabilizer is a practical insight applicable beyond this specific task. However, the method remains instance-specific (per-shape MLP fitting), and the authors acknowledge that over-regularization can cause excessive smoothing, limiting normal consistency. Open questions include whether the occupancy-first approach extends to generalizable feed-forward settings or to even sparser inputs where the Newton-Raphson approximation may break down.
