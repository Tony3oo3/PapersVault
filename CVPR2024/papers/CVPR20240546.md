## Problem

Multi-view inverse rendering suffers from severe lighting-material ambiguity, and existing approaches either assume a single fixed lighting condition (yielding strong ambiguity) or require multiple far-field lighting setups (imposing high capture workload). WildLight introduces a flashlight but discards ambient-lighting observations for material estimation, leaving available data unused. No prior method efficiently combines both near-field and far-field lighting observations under a unified, lightweight model to disentangle geometry, material, and lighting while keeping the capture process practical.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with per-image foreground masks, where each image is annotated with a far-field lighting index and binary on/off states for each near-field light; the total number and types of lighting conditions must be specified a priori. It produces a textured triangle mesh directly importable into Blender, along with the underlying SDF geometry field, a spatially-varying GGX BRDF material field, far-field lighting parameterized as 128-lobe spherical Gaussians, and near-field lighting as point lights with 3D positions and anisotropic spherical-harmonic radiation profiles. The near-field lights can be either camera-collocated (moving with the camera) or stationary, and the method supports any combination of single/multiple far-field lights with zero or multiple near-field lights.

## Method

VMINer represents the scene with 3D implicit fields: an SDF for geometry (via multi-resolution hash encoding + MLP), a material MLP predicting SVBRDF parameters, and separate radiance MLPs per lighting condition that take light embeddings and view-direction encodings as inputs. The lighting model is lightweight yet inclusive: far-field lights are 128-lobe spherical Gaussians, and near-field lights are point sources with adjustable 3D positions and anisotropic SH radiation, enabling both camera-collocated flashlights and stationary lamps. Rendering is performed in two complementary ways per lighting condition—neural radiance field volume rendering and physically-based surface rendering (Monte Carlo integration for far-field, direct multiplication for near-field, with secondary visibility and indirect illumination via the radiance field). Training proceeds in two stages: first optimizing geometry and radiance fields without PBR, then jointly optimizing material and lighting with PBR. A key self-consistency loss enforces agreement between the neural radiance and PBR radiance under each individual lighting condition, which is critical for disambiguating contributions when multiple lights are simultaneously active in a single image.

## Results

On 6 synthetic scenes, VMINer with single far-field + flashlight (row 5) achieves 10.89° normal MAngE, 31.62 dB albedo PSNR, 32.09 dB view synthesis PSNR, and 30.75 dB FV relit specular PSNR, outperforming WildLight (11.49°, 28.86 dB, 29.87 dB, 27.71 dB) while training 24× faster (60 min vs. 1440 min on a single RTX 3090). In the best setting (two far-field + one near-field light, row 8), the method reaches 10.79° normal MAngE, 32.04 dB albedo PSNR, and 32.38 dB FV relit PSNR. Under single far-field lighting alone (row 3), VMINer matches or slightly trails TensoIR and NVDiffRecMC on albedo/view-synthesis PSNR but achieves substantially better normal accuracy (12.39° vs. 17.66°/16.24°) and trains 3–7× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WildLight [6] (the closest competitor using a flashlight), VMINer wins on every metric in the single far-field + flashlight setting: normal MAngE improves from 11.49° to 10.89°, albedo PSNR jumps from 28.86 to 31.62 dB, and FV relit specular PSNR rises from 27.71 to 30.75 dB, while training time drops from 1440 min to 60 min (24× speedup). Against TensoIR [10] under single far-field lighting, VMINer achieves substantially better normal accuracy (12.39° vs. 17.66°) and faster training (45 min vs. 300 min), but trails slightly on albedo PSNR (24.50 vs. 26.48 dB) and view synthesis PSNR (28.20 vs. 29.48 dB). Under two far-field lighting, VMINer again dominates on normal accuracy (11.70° vs. 16.24°) and FV relit PSNR (29.27 vs. 28.66 dB) while training 6.7× faster. Against NVDiffRecMC [9], VMINer is clearly superior on normal MAngE (12.39° vs. 16.24°) and FV relit specular PSNR (27.56 vs. 25.57 dB), with comparable view synthesis quality. The paper notes that adding a second far-field light (row 7 vs. row 3) yields gains similar to adding a flashlight, and combining both (row 8) gives the best overall results with only marginal improvement over the single far-field + flashlight setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

VMINer sits at the intersection of SDF-based implicit surface reconstruction (NeuS, Instant-NGP) and multi-view inverse rendering (TensoIR, NVDiffRecMC), but its key departure is a unified lighting model that simultaneously handles far-field spherical Gaussian lights and near-field point lights, whereas prior methods address only one type. It builds directly on WildLight's flashlight paradigm and mesh extraction pipeline, but extends it by also modeling and leveraging the ambient far-field lighting for material estimation rather than discarding it. The self-consistency loss between the neural radiance field and physically-based rendering is a novel training signal not present in prior inverse rendering frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in producing textured triangle meshes directly compatible with industrial renderers like Blender, bridging the gap between research-quality inverse rendering and production 3D asset pipelines. The 5–24× speedup over comparable methods (45–60 min vs. 300–1440 min) makes the approach viable for real-world capture workflows. Limitations include the requirement for foreground masks, a fixed tone-mapping curve, and the need to toggle near-field lights on/off during capture; open questions include whether the framework scales to complex multi-object scenes with inter-reflections and whether unknown ISP curves can be jointly estimated.
