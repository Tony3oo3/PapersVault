## Problem

Existing 3D diffusion models generate shape and appearance jointly without the ability to control them independently. Prior disentangled generation methods (GRAF, Disentangled3D) rely on GANs and lack the generation quality of diffusion models, while current diffusion approaches use representations (implicit fields, triplanes, voxel grids) that inherently couple shape and appearance in shared parameters, preventing separate manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242097/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model takes 512-point neural point clouds as its data space, where each point carries a 3D position and a high-dimensional feature vector encoding local geometry and appearance. For joint sampling, both positions and features start as Gaussian noise; for disentangled sampling, one modality is fixed (e.g., given point positions) while the other is denoised from noise. The output is a full neural point cloud that is rendered to 128×128 RGB images via ray marching through the learned radiance field, with the point positions defining coarse shape and features defining fine local detail.

## Method

The method is a two-stage pipeline: first, a category-level Point-NeRF autodecoder with shared MLPs is trained across all objects to map neural point features to density and color, regularized with total variation and KL divergence to eliminate many-to-one mappings between features and renderings; second, a Transformer-based DDPM denoiser is trained to jointly denoise point positions and features in this high-dimensional latent space. The key architectural insight is that the hybrid point-cloud-plus-radiance-field representation naturally separates coarse shape (positions) from local appearance (features), so disentangled generation is achieved by a RePaint-style mechanism: during reverse diffusion, the fixed modality is re-noised via the forward process at each step while the free modality is updated by the denoiser. Zero initialization of features combined with TV and KL regularization is shown to be critical for producing a coherent latent space amenable to diffusion.

## Results

On the disentangled generation task, NPCD achieves FID 28.38 / KID 17.62 on SRN Cars, FID 9.87 / KID 3.62 on SRN Chairs, and FID 14.45 / KID 5.40 on PhotoShape Chairs, reducing FID by 30–90% over the previous best disentangled methods (GRAF and D3D). Against non-disentangled 3D diffusion baselines, NPCD outperforms DiffRF (FID 14.45 vs 15.95 on PhotoShape Chairs) and Functa (FID 28.38 vs 80.3 on SRN Cars), but trails SSDNeRF (FID 28.38 vs 11.08 on SRN Cars). For shape-only point cloud generation, NPCD is competitive with dedicated point cloud generators, achieving CD 60.23 / EMD 52.41 on SRN Cars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPCD decisively outperforms both prior disentangled-generation baselines: on SRN Chairs it reduces FID from 37.19 (GRAF) and 45.73 (D3D) to 9.87, and on PhotoShape Chairs from 34.49 and 59.80 to 14.45. Against non-disentangled 3D diffusion methods, NPCD beats DiffRF on PhotoShape Chairs (FID 14.45 vs 15.95) and Functa on SRN Cars (FID 28.38 vs 80.3), but is outperformed by SSDNeRF on SRN Cars (FID 28.38 vs 11.08, KID 17.62 vs 3.47). In the shape-only point cloud comparison (Table 3), NPCD is competitive but not the best: it trails LION (CD 60.23 vs 53.41 on Cars) and PVD (EMD 52.41 vs 53.83 on Cars), though it remains within a few points of the top methods. The key differentiator is that NPCD is the only method in all these comparisons that supports independent control of shape and appearance during generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Point-NeRF from single-scene reconstruction to a category-level autodecoder, then applies DDPM-style denoising diffusion over the resulting high-dimensional latent space of point positions and features. It departs from prior 3D diffusion methods (DiffRF, Functa, SSDNeRF) that operate on implicit fields, voxel grids, or triplanes—representations that inherently couple shape and appearance—and from prior disentangled generators (GRAF, Disentangled3D) that rely on GANs. The key conceptual move is recognizing that a neural point cloud's positions and features form a natural shape/appearance split, enabling conditional sampling analogous to RePaint-style inpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based method to enable independent control of coarse 3D shape and local appearance, closing a gap that GAN-based methods (GRAF, D3D) addressed with substantially lower quality. The identification of many-to-one mappings in autodecoded latent spaces as a core obstacle for latent diffusion, along with the simple zero-initialization + TV/KL regularization remedy, is a practically useful diagnostic for anyone applying diffusion to backprop-optimized representations. Open questions include whether the disentanglement holds under conditional (e.g., text-guided) generation, whether 512 points suffice for high-frequency detail, and whether the approach scales beyond category-level to open-vocabulary or multi-object scenes.
