## Problem

Conventional deep unfolding networks for accelerated MRI attempt to recover all missing information in the entire null space at every iteration, which becomes increasingly unreliable under severe undersampling (e.g., 8× acceleration) due to cumulative errors. No prior unfolding framework decomposes the severe degradation into a sequence of moderate sub-problems, leaving a gap in how to progressively tackle highly ill-posed reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241694/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes zero-filled undersampled k-space measurements (384×384 for multi-coil with 15 coils, 320×320 for single-coil) at 4× or 8× acceleration with 4–8% central fraction, plus coil sensitivity maps in the multi-coil setting. It produces a single reconstructed MR image in the spatial domain. Unlike the original HUMUSNet backbone which accepts adjacent slices, PDAC uses only the central slice for fair comparison.

## Method

PDAC decomposes the severe subsampling matrix D into a product of T moderate diagonal matrices (D = D₀…D_T) and solves the resulting MAP problem via progressive half-quadratic splitting, where each iteration recovers only the information lost in one decomposed subsampling step. Each PDAC iteration performs three steps: (1) a data-consistency update, (2) a neural-network denoising step (HUMUSNet backbone), and (3) a projection onto the range space of the current decomposed degradation. A degradation predictor adaptively learns the subsampling mask M_t at each iteration by estimating per-column confidence from the current reconstruction, guided by an auxiliary probability loss against normalized reconstruction error. A severity conditioning module (adaLN) embeds the masked confidence m_t ⊙ p_t so the network is aware of the degradation pattern at each stage. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with an L1 pixel-wise loss plus the auxiliary probability loss, using a coarse-to-fine sampling budget schedule across T=8 iterations.

## Results

On 8× accelerated multi-coil MRI reconstruction, HUMUSNet+PDAC achieves 37.12 dB PSNR and 0.0085 NMSE on fastMRI knee, and 36.77 dB PSNR and 0.0166 NMSE on Stanford2D FSE, outperforming the HUMUSNet backbone by 0.28 dB and 1.34 dB respectively. On single-coil fastMRI knee, PDAC reaches 31.16 dB PSNR at 8× and 32.73 dB PSNR at 4×, setting new state-of-the-art in both PSNR and SSIM. The method also surpasses HUMUSNet* (which uses extra adjacent slices) in PSNR (37.12 vs 37.03 dB) while maintaining comparable SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDAC consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across both multi-coil and single-coil settings. On fastMRI knee multi-coil 8×, PDAC improves PSNR by 0.28 dB over HUMUSNet (37.12 vs 36.84) and by 0.31 dB over E2E-VarNet (37.12 vs 36.81), while also achieving the lowest NMSE (0.0085). On Stanford2D FSE, the gain is more pronounced: PDAC surpasses HUMUSNet by 1.34 dB in PSNR (36.77 vs 35.43) and E2E-VarNet by 0.29 dB. Notably, PDAC beats HUMUSNet* (which leverages adjacent slices) in PSNR (37.12 vs 37.03) despite using only a single slice as input, though it trails slightly in SSIM (0.8905 vs 0.8933). In the single-coil setting, PDAC achieves the best PSNR at both 4× (32.73 dB, tying ReconFormer) and 8× (31.16 dB vs HUMUSNet's 31.04 dB), and the best SSIM at 8× (0.6739). The classical CS baseline (BART) and ESPIRiT are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 8 dB in PSNR, confirming the advantage of learned unfolding over hand-crafted regularizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network (DUN) paradigm for accelerated MRI by introducing a progressive divide-and-conquer strategy that decomposes the severe subsampling degradation into a sequence of moderate degradations, each recovered in a dedicated iteration. It builds directly on half-quadratic splitting (Afonso et al., 2010) for the optimization derivation and uses HUMUSNet as its reconstruction backbone, but departs from conventional DUNs (ADMM-Net, E2E-VarNet, MoDL) that attempt to recover the entire null space in every iteration. The key conceptual departure is the adaptive learning of the decomposition itself via a degradation predictor and severity conditioning module, rather than using a fixed or hand-designed schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive decomposition addresses a genuine limitation of DUNs under high acceleration factors, where cumulative errors from recovering the full null space at once degrade quality. However, the performance gains are modest (0.2–1.3 dB over HUMUSNet) and the approach is currently restricted to Cartesian sampling masks and 2D reconstruction, leaving open whether the decomposition strategy generalizes to non-Cartesian trajectories, 3D volumes, or other inverse problems. The clean theoretical derivation and the plug-and-play nature of PDAC (it can be layered onto existing backbones) make it a practical building block, but the field still lacks a unified theory for optimally choosing the number and granularity of decomposition steps.
