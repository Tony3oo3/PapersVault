## Problem

Current multi-modality large language models (MLLMs) exhibit unsatisfactory accuracy on low-level visual tasks such as identifying distortions (noise, blur), evaluating visual dimensions (clarity, brightness), and predicting image quality, primarily because publicly available instruction tuning datasets focus almost exclusively on high-level visual abilities. No large-scale dataset existed that captured human reasoning over low-level visual attributes in natural language form.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images (18,973 images from seven sources including in-the-wild IQA datasets, AI-generated images, and artificially corrupted COCO images) paired with text queries as input. Outputs are structured as 200K instruction-response pairs spanning three formats: pathway reasoning (a detailed low-level attribute description of ~46 words followed by an overall quality conclusion), visual question answering (76K what/how questions with short adjective or noun answers, 57K balanced yes/no questions, plus multiple-choice variants), and 12K extended multi-turn conversations about causes, improvements, and recommendations related to low-level visual aspects.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage data construction pipeline: first, 39 trained human subjects in a controlled lab provide 'pathway feedbacks'—exhaustive natural-language descriptions of low-level attributes (noise, blur, clarity, color, brightness) with content/position context, followed by an overall quality conclusion—yielding 58K feedbacks (Q-Pathway); second, GPT transforms these feedbacks into diverse instruction-response pairs (VQA, yes/no, MCQ, extended conversations) to form the 200K-pair Q-Instruct dataset. For training, two strategies are validated: (a) mixing Q-Instruct with standard high-level instruction tuning datasets in a single stage, and (b) conducting a dedicated low-level tuning stage with only Q-Instruct after high-level tuning. Experiments across four MLLM architectures (LLaVA-v1.5 7B/13B, mPLUG-Owl-2, InternLM-XComposer-VL) show consistent improvements in perception accuracy (+5–9%), description relevance (+0.31), and IQA SRCC (+0.24–0.40) without any numerical quality score supervision during training.

## Results

On the IQA task (A3), Q-Instruct tuning yields dramatic SRCC gains across all four MLLMs: e.g., mPLUG-Owl-2 jumps from 0.196 to 0.911 on KonIQ-10k and from 0.541 to 0.698 on KADID-10K, while LLaVA-v1.5-7B improves from 0.463 to 0.809 on KonIQ-10k. Average SRCC improvements are +0.398 on 'Mostly Seen' datasets, +0.349 on 'Barely Seen', and +0.238 on 'Never Seen' (cross-domain) datasets, demonstrating strong generalization. On the Perception task (A1), overall MCQ accuracy on LLVisionQA-test improves by 6–9% (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5-7B: 60.1% → 69.3%), with the largest gains on Yes-or-No questions (+14%) and distortion-related items (+17%). On Description (A2), the GPT-scored sum metric rises by +0.52 on average (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5-7B: 3.21 → 3.86 out of 6), driven primarily by relevance (+0.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four MLLM baselines (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, LLaVA-v1.5-13B, mPLUG-Owl-2, InternLM-XComposer-VL) are consistently and substantially outperformed by their Q-Instruct-tuned counterparts across all three evaluation tasks. On IQA (SRCC), the tuned models surpass the traditional no-reference baseline NIQE on 7 out of 8 datasets; for example, mPLUG-Owl-2 (after) reaches 0.911 on KonIQ-10k versus NIQE's 0.316, and even on the hardest 'Never Seen' cross-domain set (KADID-10K), tuned models (0.68–0.70) far exceed NIQE (0.374). On Perception (LLVisionQA-test), the 'mix with high-level' strategy (a) generally edges out the 'after high-level' strategy (b) by 1–2% overall accuracy, with the largest gap on LLaVA-v1.5-7B (69.3% vs. 67.4%). The 13B LLaVA variant benefits slightly less in absolute terms than the 7B variant on Perception (e.g., +9.3% vs. +9.2% overall) but shows comparable IQA gains. Notably, the 'replace high-level' ablation (using only Q-Instruct without any high-level tuning data) underperforms both proposed strategies by 4–5% overall, confirming that high-level visual awareness is a prerequisite for effective low-level perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of low-level visual perception (IQA, distortion detection, attribute evaluation) and the MLLM instruction-tuning paradigm. Prior MLLM instruction tuning datasets (LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl, InternLM-XComposer) focus almost exclusively on high-level visual tasks (captioning, VQA), leaving low-level visual abilities underdeveloped. The paper repurposes the instruction-tuning framework by introducing the Q-Pathway annotation format—natural-language pathway feedbacks that capture human reasoning from low-level attributes to overall quality—and uses GPT to expand these into diverse instruction-response pairs (VQA, multi-choice, extended conversations). Conceptually, it extends the COCO-VQA-from-captions pipeline to the low-level domain and connects to the CLIP-based zero-shot IQA line of work, but departs from it by providing supervised, task-specific training data rather than relying on pre-trained alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale low-level visual instruction tuning dataset, and its 200K pairs are likely to become a standard resource for improving MLLMs' perceptual capabilities. The finding that purely text-driven instruction tuning (with no numerical MOS values in training) can align MLLMs with numerical quality scores—and generalize to completely unseen domains (CGI, artificially degraded images, video) with +0.243 average SRCC—suggests that low-level visual understanding in MLLMs is more transferable than previously assumed. Open questions include whether scaling beyond 200K samples yields further gains (the paper suggests performance is not yet saturated), how to improve description precision and completeness (the weakest gains), and whether the pipeline extends to more distortion types and finer-grained low-level dimensions beyond those covered in Q-Pathway.
