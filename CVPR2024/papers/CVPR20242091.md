## Problem

Existing post-hoc explanation methods for Vision Transformers treat attention weights as the sole measure of token contributions, ignoring how token transformations (changes in magnitude and direction) alter the actual influence of each token on the output. This oversight causes attention-based heatmaps to misrepresent foreground objects and highlight noisy background regions as rationales, undermining faithful interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained Vision Transformer (ViT or DeiT) processing a standard RGB image, given a target class label for which to generate an explanation. It produces a pixel-space contribution heatmap by extracting the [CLS] token row from the final layer's contribution map and reshaping it to the image's spatial dimensions (e.g., 16×16 patches for 224×224 inputs). The contribution map itself is an n×n matrix where each entry C_ij tracks the cumulative influence of the j-th input token on the i-th output token across all layers.

## Method

TokenTM reinterprets both MHSA and FFN layers as weighted sums of original and transformed tokens, then introduces a two-component transformation measurement: a relative length ratio (L2 norm of transformed token divided by original token) and a Normalized Exponential Cosine Correlation (NECC) that captures directional alignment between original and transformed tokens. These combine into transformation weights W that modulate the attention matrix, yielding update maps U for each layer (U = I + E_h[(∇_A p(c))^+ ⊙ (A·W)] for MHSA; U = I + I·W for FFN). An aggregation framework initializes the overall contribution map C⁰ as a diagonal matrix of input token lengths, then iteratively multiplies C^l ← U^l · C^{l-1} across all n_L layers, accumulating both transformation and contextualization effects. The final explanation is read from the [CLS] row of C^{n_L}, providing a faithful, object-centric heatmap that accounts for how tokens evolve through the network.

## Results

On ImageNet-Segmentation, TokenTM achieves 81.79% pixel accuracy, 86.45 mAP, and 64.22 mIoU, outperforming the strongest baseline GAE (79.05%, 85.71, 61.56) by 2.74 points in pixel accuracy and 2.66 in mIoU. On perturbation tests, TokenTM attains the best AUC across CIFAR-10 (74.90/75.05 Neg., 37.14/36.48 Pos.), CIFAR-100 (54.78/55.30 Neg., 16.07/15.89 Pos.), and ImageNet (58.12/59.36 Neg., 15.75/15.09 Pos.) for both predicted and ground-truth labels. On probability impact, TokenTM reaches 0.764 AOPC and −5.781 LOdds on ViT-B, and 0.726 AOPC and −5.755 LOdds on ViT-L, again surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokenTM outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation metric and dataset. The closest competitor is GAE [12], which also uses gradient-weighted attention; TokenTM edges it out by ~2.7 points in pixel accuracy and ~2.7 in mIoU on ImageNet-Seg, and by 0.5–3.2 AUC points on perturbation tests. Transformer Attribution [13] is the second-strongest baseline (79.17 Pix. Acc., 61.02 mIoU) but still trails TokenTM by 2.6 and 3.2 points respectively. Gradient-based Grad-CAM [37] and attribution-based LRP [9] / Conservative LRP [3] lag substantially, with LRP scoring only 50.96% pixel accuracy. Pure attention methods—Raw Attention [26], Rollout [1], and ATTCAT [35]—perform poorly (43.88–59.07% pixel accuracy), confirming the paper's central claim that attention weights alone are insufficient for faithful explanation. The consistent margin over GAE across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, ImageNet-Seg) and both ViT-B/ViT-L variants indicates that the token transformation measurement (length ratio + NECC) provides a meaningful and generalizable improvement over attention-only aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-based explanation paradigm for Vision Transformers established by Rollout (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020), Transformer Attribution (Chefer et al., 2021), and GAE (Chefer et al., 2021), which all treat attention weights as the sole proxy for token contributions. The key departure is a unified reformulation of both MHSA and FFN as weighted sums of original and transformed tokens, revealing that attention weights alone ignore how tokens are actually reshaped in magnitude and direction during processing. By introducing a token transformation measurement (length ratio + Normalized Exponential Cosine Correlation) and a cross-layer aggregation framework, the paper augments the existing attention-based pipeline rather than replacing it, positioning itself as a principled correction to a recognized blind spot in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap in ViT interpretability: attention maps can highlight background regions with high weights while missing foreground objects that undergo significant internal transformation. The consistent improvements across segmentation, perturbation, and probability metrics on multiple datasets and model sizes (ViT-B, ViT-L, DeiT) suggest the transformation-aware signal is genuinely informative rather than an artifact. However, the evaluation is limited to classification-oriented ViT/DeiT architectures; generalization to detection, segmentation, or multi-modal transformers (e.g., Swin, MAE, CLIP) remains untested. An open question is whether the length-and-direction measurement captures the full geometry of token evolution in deeper or wider models, and whether the aggregation framework scales efficiently to very deep stacks.
