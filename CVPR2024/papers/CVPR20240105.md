## Problem

Existing medical generative models are confined to single or two-modality pipelines (e.g., MRI-to-CT, text-to-X-ray), making it impossible to handle the multi-modality integration that real clinical diagnosis demands. Unifying multiple medical modalities (CT, MRI, X-ray, text) in one model is further hampered by the substantial inter-modality disparities, the risk of losing modality-specific clinical properties during alignment, and the absence of well-paired cross-modal medical datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of medical images (CT, MRI, X-ray at 64×64 latent resolution) and medical report text (768-dim latent), and can generate any of these modalities as output. A single unified model handles five distinct tasks—text-to-image, image-to-text, MRI multi-sequence synthesis, MRI-to-CT translation, and joint multi-modality generation—switching behavior via which diffusion flow is activated. Notably, the multi-flow training requires only three paired datasets (Text-Xray, Text-CT, CT-MRI) to enable generation across all four modalities, including pairs like MRI-Xray that have no direct training data.

## Method

MedM2G builds on Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) with three key innovations. First, a Central Alignment strategy uses text as a hub modality, aligning each imaging modality to text via InfoNCE contrastive loss in O(n) pairs rather than O(n²) pairwise alignment, implicitly aligning all modalities in a shared embedding space. Second, a Medical Visual Invariant Preservation module (inspired by Barlow Twins) minimizes off-diagonal elements of the cross-correlation matrix between two augmented views of each modality, disentangling the latent space feature-wise to retain modality-specific clinical knowledge. Third, a Latent Cross-guided Alignment Generation mechanism injects a Textual Inversion-style trainable adaptive parameter and a shared cross-attention sublayer from one modality's UNet into another's diffusion process, enabling flexible cross-modal conditioning. These components are trained via a multi-flow strategy of three sequential paired-training rounds (Text-Xray → Text-CT → CT-MRI) with progressive freezing, so the unified model acquires multi-modal generation capability without requiring complete pairwise data.

## Results

On medical report generation (Table 1), MedM2G achieves BLEU-1 of 0.533 and ROUGE-L of 0.416 on IU X-Ray, and BLEU-1 of 0.412 and ROUGE-L of 0.309 on MIMIC-CXR, outperforming the best baseline Kiut by 0.019 BLEU-1 and 0.024 ROUGE-L on MIMIC-CXR. For MRI synthesis (Table 2), MedM2G reaches PSNR 29.89 dB on BraTS (T2+T1ce+FLAIR→T1), exceeding CoLa-Diff by 1.63 dB. On chest X-ray generation (Table 4), MedM2G achieves FID 1.7 on MIMIC-CXR, surpassing UniXGen (FID 2.5) and SD-RadBERT (FID 6.0). For MRI-CT translation (Table 5), MedM2G attains PSNR 27.45 dB on T2→CT, outperforming SynDiff (26.86 dB) by 0.59 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedM2G consistently outperforms all 31 baselines across 5 generation tasks. In medical report generation, it surpasses the strongest baseline Kiut [21] by 0.019 BLEU-1 and 0.024 ROUGE-L on MIMIC-CXR, and by 0.008 BLEU-1 and 0.007 ROUGE-L on IU X-Ray, while also beating GAN-based (R2Gen, R2GenCMN) and VLP-based (Clinical-BERT, METTransformer, COMG) methods. For MRI synthesis, MedM2G exceeds the best diffusion baseline CoLa-Diff [23] by up to 1.63 dB PSNR on BraTS and 2.24 dB on IXI (T2+PD→T1), and outperforms GAN-based methods (MM-GAN, Hi-Net, ProvoGAN) by 2+ dB. In medical text-to-image generation (Table 3), MedM2G reduces FID by 1.32 over LLM-CXR [30] on ChestXray14 and by 1.20 over AdaMatch-Cyclic [4] on ACDC, while also beating general-domain diffusion models (GLIGEN, GCDP) and medical-specific models (RoentGen, UniXGen). For chest X-ray generation, MedM2G achieves FID 1.7 vs. UniXGen's 2.5 and SD-RadBERT's 6.0 on MIMIC-CXR. In MRI-CT translation, MedM2G outperforms the strongest diffusion baseline SynDiff [39] by 0.59 dB PSNR on T2→CT and 0.92 dB on accelerated T2→CT, and exceeds all GAN-based methods (UNIT, MUNIT, cGAN, AttGAN, SAGAN) by 1.5+ dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedM2G builds on Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) as its generative backbone, adapts Barlow Twins' redundancy-reduction objective for medical visual invariant preservation, and borrows the Textual Inversion mechanism for cross-modal guided adaptation. It departs from the dominant paradigm of single-flow, task-specific medical diffusion models (CoLa-Diff, SynDiff, MT-Diffusion) by unifying text-to-image, image-to-text, and multi-modal medical image generation (CT, MRI, X-ray) within one framework. The central alignment strategy is a practical departure from general multi-modal alignment approaches (ImageBind, Versatile Diffusion) that assume large well-paired cross-modal datasets, instead leveraging text as a hub to achieve O(n) rather than O(n²) alignment cost with limited medical paired data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuine practical bottleneck in medical AI: the need for separate, task-specific generative models for each imaging modality and generation direction. The unified multi-flow framework and central alignment offer a cost-efficient path toward a single model handling diverse clinical generation needs, which could streamline diagnostic workflows. However, the paper acknowledges that generated modalities serve only as auxiliary information for diseases lacking multimodal clinical features, and raises the ethical concern of fabricated medical images. Open questions include extension to 3D volumetric imaging, validation in clinical settings, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the three imaging modalities studied.
