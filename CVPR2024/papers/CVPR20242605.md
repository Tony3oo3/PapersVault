## Problem

Behavior cloning for autonomous driving lacks the corrective feedback that humans rely on to learn complex tasks, leaving models unable to understand why their predictions fail in out-of-distribution scenarios. Existing MLLM-based driving approaches are further limited by slow autoregressive token generation, reliance on closed-source GPT APIs, and the absence of a language interface for refining predictions from failure explanations, making real-time closed-loop evaluation infeasible.

## Input / Output

The sensorimotor agent consumes a single front camera RGB image (processed by CLIP ViT into 512 patch embeddings projected to 4096-dim space), ego vehicle speed, a categorical navigational command encoded as natural language, and a 2D GNSS goal coordinate. The privileged teacher additionally receives ground-truth BEV information—route centerline, object locations, and traffic light states—discretized onto a 96×96 grid as location tokens. Output is a set of K future 2D waypoints predicted directly from the hidden-layer features of K special waypoint tokens via an MLP head (bypassing sequential generation), plus corrective feedback reasoning text generated during the fine-tuning stage.

## Method

FeD builds on LLaVA-7B (CLIP ViT + LLaMA) with LoRA adapters and introduces three key mechanisms. First, waypoints are predicted in a masked-token fashion: K special tokens are embedded in the prompt, and an MLP head maps their last hidden-layer representations directly to 2D coordinates, achieving 2.7 FPS versus ~0.1 FPS for sequential generation. Second, training proceeds in two stages: (1) feature distillation from a privileged agent that has access to ground-truth BEV, using an L1 waypoint loss plus an L2 feature-matching loss between student and teacher hidden states; (2) feedback-guided fine-tuning where the model is trained to generate structured corrective text (covering five failure types—vehicle/pedestrian collision via kinematic models, traffic-light violation, expert deviation, and route deviation) and simultaneously predict corrected waypoints, supervised by cross-entropy on the feedback text plus L1 on the corrected waypoints. This two-stage design keeps memory and compute tractable while grounding the model in language-based failure reasoning.

## Results

On the CARLA closed-loop LAV benchmark, FeD achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 81.60%, Route Completion (RC) of 97.60%, and Infraction Score (IS) of 0.84, outperforming the best LiDAR-based method TransFuser++ (DS 70.00) by 16.6% while using only a single front camera. On nuScenes open-loop evaluation, FeD achieves an average L2 displacement error of 0.34 m (ST-P3 metrics) and 0.58 m (UniAD metrics), improving 8.1% over VAD (0.37 m) and reducing average collision rate from 0.17% (GPT-Driver) to 0.06% (a 57.1% reduction). The lightweight TinyFeD variant (1/7 model size) still achieves DS 74.65 at 10.3 FPS, outperforming TransFuser++ at comparable inference speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeD outperforms all 14 baselines across both evaluation settings. In CARLA closed-loop, FeD (DS 81.60) surpasses the strongest LiDAR-based method TransFuser++ (DS 70.00) by 16.6%, and the next-best camera-based method CaT (DS 66.70) by 14.9%, while achieving near-zero vehicle collisions (0.01 vs. 0.63 for TransFuser++). On nuScenes, FeD beats the non-LLM SOTA VAD (L2 0.37 m) by 8.1% and the LLM-based GPT-Driver (L2 0.44 m) by 22.7% in displacement error, while reducing average collision rate from 0.17% to 0.06% (57.1% improvement). Under UniAD metrics, FeD (L2 0.58 m, collision 0.19%) outperforms UniAD itself (L2 1.03 m, collision 0.31%) and GPT-Driver (L2 0.84 m, collision 0.44%). The lightweight TinyFeD variant (DS 74.65 at 10.3 FPS) still exceeds TransFuser++ (DS 70.00 at 11.5 FPS), demonstrating that the efficiency gains from masked-token waypoint prediction are preserved even at 1/7 model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

FeD builds directly on LLaVA's vision-language architecture (CLIP ViT encoder + LLaMA decoder) but departs from prior LLM-based driving methods (GPT-Driver, DriveGPT4) in three fundamental ways: it replaces slow autoregressive token generation with masked-token waypoint prediction, uses open-source weights instead of closed GPT-4 APIs, and introduces a feedback-guided fine-tuning stage where the model learns to generate and act on corrective language explanations of its own prediction errors. The knowledge distillation from a privileged BEV agent follows the teacher-student paradigm established by TCP and CaT, but is the first to apply it within an MLLM framework. Conceptually, the paper bridges the imitation learning literature (behavior cloning, privileged distillation) with the emerging LLM-for-control literature by showing that structured language feedback can serve as a richer supervision signal than auxiliary losses alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first LLM-based driving agent that supports efficient end-to-end closed-loop evaluation, removing a major barrier that limited prior GPT-4-based approaches to open-loop or simplified highway simulations. The finding that a single front-camera agent outperforms the best LiDAR-based method (TransFuser++) by 16.6% in driving score suggests that rich language priors in MLLMs can compensate for missing sensor modalities, which has implications for sensor cost and system design. Key limitations remain: 2.7 FPS is far from real-time driving requirements, the feedback fine-tuning requires hundreds of iterations, and all closed-loop results are in CARLA simulation. Open questions include whether the feedback mechanism generalizes to more diverse failure types, whether more efficient distillation can close the real-time gap, and whether the approach scales to multi-camera or multi-sensor inputs.
