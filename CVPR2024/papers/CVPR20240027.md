## Problem

Recovering the 3D structure and motion of a fast-spinning object from a static event camera (event-based Structure-from-Orbit, eSfO) is unsolved: existing event feature trackers fail under the periodic self-occlusion caused by rotation, and the orbital-constrained SfO problem has not been satisfactorily addressed in the literature. No prior work jointly estimates orbital motion parameters and sparse 3D structure from event data alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240027/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous event stream (pixel locations, timestamps, binary polarity) from a static DAVIS346 event camera plus the intrinsic calibration matrix. It outputs a sparse 3D point cloud of the object, 14 fixed orbital parameters (rotation frequency, axis normal, orbit radius, circle center, in-plane vectors, and a constant look-at rotation), and camera poses constrained to a circular trajectory. Unlike general SfM where camera pose parameters scale as 6N with the number of views, the orbital formulation keeps motion parameters at a fixed 14 regardless of the number of temporal windows, with only 3N_P parameters for landmarks.

## Method

The pipeline has a front-end (Event Tracking by Clustering, ETC) and a back-end (factor-graph optimization). ETC detects corner events via eFAST, filters them by a local spatio-temporal density score to handle polarity bias, clusters the remaining corners in spatio-temporal space using HDBSCAN, and merges nearby clusters via head-tail matching within a spatio-temporal hemisphere to form continuous tracks despite self-occlusion gaps. Tracks are extracted by averaging pixel locations within fixed-duration temporal windows. The back-end initializes with COLMAP (unconstrained SfM), fits a circle to the resulting camera centers to seed the orbital parameters, estimates initial frequency via Fourier transform of mean event x-locations, then refines all parameters through a factor graph where each orbit factor enforces the circular-trajectory camera pose model and minimizes reprojection error over the sparse point cloud.

## Results

On the Event Camera Dataset (threshold = 5 px), ETC achieves the lowest RMSE reprojection error among practically useful trackers: 1.26±1.21 px (shapes), 1.66±1.50 px (poster), and 1.56±1.50 px (boxes), while maintaining feature ages of 0.40–0.70 s. On the spinning-object TOPSPIN dataset, ETC yields a COLMAP reprojection error of 2.39 px versus 348.48 px for HASTE, a >145× improvement. In the back-end, eSfO refines the Fourier-transform frequency initialization to closely match ground-truth turntable rates, and the orbit-constrained optimization reduces point-cloud-to-CAD registration error relative to unconstrained COLMAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HASTE [3], ETC achieves lower RMSE reprojection error on all three Event Camera Dataset subsets (e.g., 1.26 vs 1.73 px on shapes at threshold 5) and dramatically outperforms it on spinning objects (2.39 vs 348.48 px COLMAP reprojection error), because HASTE's fixed planar template assumption breaks down under periodic self-occlusion. Against eCDT [25], ETC wins on accuracy across every subset (e.g., 1.56 vs 1.90 px on boxes), while eCDT produces longer feature tracks (1.31 s vs 0.40 s on shapes) at the cost of substantially higher reprojection error. Metavision [9] reports near-zero RMSE but with feature ages of only ~0.005 s, rendering it impractical for SfM; ETC balances accuracy and track length far better. In the back-end, eSfO's orbit-constrained optimization yields slightly higher reprojection error than unconstrained COLMAP [45] (as expected, since not all constraints can be fully resolved), but recovers a circular camera trajectory that is qualitatively and quantitatively superior to COLMAP's deviated path, and reduces point-cloud-to-CAD registration error after ICP alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating event-based Structure-from-Orbit (eSfO) as a joint motion-and-structure recovery problem, departing from prior turntable SfM work (Fitzgibbon et al., Fremont et al.) that assumed known angular rates and solved structure only. On the feature tracking side, it repurposes the eFAST corner detector but replaces template-based or learning-based trackers (HASTE, eCDT, Metavision) with a spatio-temporal HDBSCAN clustering approach (ETC) specifically designed to handle periodic self-occlusion from spinning motion. The back-end borrows COLMAP for initialization but then constrains camera poses to a parametric orbital model via a factor graph, a formulation the authors state is the first event-only solution in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The eSfO formulation and TOPSPIN dataset open a path toward vision-based 3D perception of naturally spinning objects in space (debris, defunct satellites) and on turntables for e-commerce/AR, where event sensors' high temporal resolution eliminates motion blur at high angular velocities. Key limitations include the single-axis rotation assumption, sparse (not dense) reconstruction, and monocular-only setting; extending to multi-axis tumbling or dense surface recovery remains open. The finding that eSfO's constrained optimization yields higher reprojection error than unconstrained COLMAP while producing a far more physically meaningful trajectory highlights a fundamental trade-off between constraint satisfaction and per-point fit that is worth further investigation.
