## Problem

Makeup transfer is inherently unsupervised because no real transferred ground truth exists for training. Existing methods synthesize pseudo ground truths (PGTs) via histogram matching or geometric warping, but these PGTs are sub-optimal and their imprecision degrades transfer performance. This paper eliminates PGT generation entirely by exploiting frequency-domain structure to decouple content from makeup style.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unpaired source face image (non-makeup) and a reference face image (with makeup) as RGB inputs. Before processing, each image undergoes face parsing (BiSeNet) to separate foreground from background, followed by Gaussian-kernel frequency decomposition into a low-frequency component (downsampled by factor d) and a high-frequency residual. The output is a single transferred face image that preserves the source's identity and structure while adopting the reference's makeup style.

## Method

CSD-MT assumes that the low-frequency component of a face image encodes makeup style while the high-frequency component encodes content details, validated empirically by showing LF MSE between source and transferred images far exceeds HF MSE. A semantic correspondence module computes a pixel-wise cosine-similarity correlation matrix between encoder features of the source and reference at LF resolution, which is then used to spatially deform the reference's LF component to align with the source. A U-Net-based makeup rendering module (with SPADE conditioning) fuses the source's background and HF residual with the deformed reference LF to produce the transferred image. Training is purely unsupervised, combining a transfer loss (L1 on LF for makeup consistency + Gradient Profile on HF for content preservation), cycle consistency, LSGAN adversarial loss, a self-augmented reconstructive loss (applying random spatial transforms to create pseudo-pairs), and a color contrastive loss using VGG19 Gram-matrix distances.

## Results

CSD-MT achieves the lowest FID scores across all three datasets: 37.56 on MT, 60.82 on Wild-MT, and 40.87 on LADN, outperforming the best baseline (SSAT) by 0.45, 9.71, and 12.97 FID points respectively. On the self-augmented reconstruction metric, CSD-MT attains the highest PSNR/SSIM on all datasets, e.g., 27.28/0.920 (Crop) and 26.68/0.915 (Rotate) on MT, surpassing the next-best method (EleGANt) by ~2.1 dB in PSNR. In the user study, CSD-MT is selected as the best result 55.9%–76.7% of the time across simple, complex, and extreme makeup styles, compared to at most 15.4% for the best baseline (EleGANt).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD-MT outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and every metric. On the MT dataset, it beats the closest competitor SSAT by 0.45 FID and ~3.3 dB PSNR (Crop), while EleGANt—despite its hybrid PGT strategy—lags by 16.5 FID. On Wild-MT, which features large pose and expression variations, CSD-MT's advantage widens to 9.71 FID over SSAT and 16.2 FID over SpMT, demonstrating the robustness of the frequency-decoupled approach to spatial misalignment. On the LADN dataset with extreme makeup, CSD-MT achieves 40.87 FID versus 48.18 for SpMT (best baseline), a 7.31-point gap, and leads in PSNR by over 2 dB. The histogram-matching baselines (BeautyGAN, PSGAN, SCGAN, SpMT) consistently underperform on color-diverse pairs, while the geometric-distortion baselines (LADN, SSAT) struggle with artifact generation and detail preservation. The ablation further confirms that replacing CSD-MT's unsupervised strategy with PGT-guided training (using PSGAN's or SSAT's PGTs) degrades performance substantially, validating that the gains come from the decoupling strategy rather than the network architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PGT-guided paradigm in makeup transfer, where prior methods (BeautyGAN, PSGAN, SCGAN, SpMT, LADN, SSAT, EleGANt) all synthesize pseudo ground truths via histogram matching or geometric warping to provide supervision. Instead, CSD-MT repurposes frequency decomposition—a technique well-established in super-resolution and style transfer—to decouple content (HF) from makeup style (LF) directly from input images, eliminating PGT generation entirely. The architecture itself is deliberately simple (U-Net with SPADE conditioning), and the training objectives draw on CycleGAN cycle consistency, LSGAN adversarial loss, and a novel color contrastive loss, but the core conceptual contribution is the frequency-based supervision strategy rather than any single architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the PGT bottleneck, CSD-MT addresses a fundamental limitation that has constrained all prior makeup transfer methods, and the ablation showing that the same architecture trained with PGTs performs substantially worse validates that the unsupervised strategy—not the network—is the key driver. The stated limitation that the HF-contains-content assumption fails for patterned makeup (e.g., geometric designs) suggests a clear boundary for the approach, and whether more sophisticated frequency banding or learned decompositions could close this gap remains open. The self-augmented evaluation protocol (crop/rotate + SSAT demakeup) is a practical contribution for a task lacking true ground truth, though it still relies on an external demakeup model.
