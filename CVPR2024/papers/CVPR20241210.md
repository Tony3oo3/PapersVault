## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods rely on limited paired text-motion annotations and fail to generalize to unseen open-vocabulary prompts that reference diverse characters, skills, or abstract motion traits. Text-pose alignment approaches avoid the pairing requirement but produce unrealistic motions because discrete poses cannot capture rich continuous motion characteristics. The paper tackles how to generate physically plausible, text-aligned motions from arbitrary natural-language descriptions without requiring paired training data for every possible prompt.

## Input / Output

The method takes free-form natural-language text prompts (phrases or full sentences) as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 text encoder into 768-dimensional token embeddings (max 77 tokens). It outputs 3D human motion sequences in over-parameterized SMPL skeleton format at 30 fps, with arbitrary sequence lengths (trained up to 300 frames via sliding random windows). At inference, DDIM sampling with 200 timesteps and classifier-free guidance (strength 4.5) combines the unconditional and conditional denoisers to produce the final motion.

## Method

OMG adopts a two-stage pretrain-then-finetune paradigm. In pre-training, an unconditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone is scaled from 88M to 1B parameters and trained on over 20M unlabeled motion instances from 13 datasets using a sliding random window strategy to support arbitrary-length generation. In fine-tuning, a Motion ControlNet freezes the pre-trained model and adds a trainable copy alongside Mixture-of-Controllers (MoC) blocks that inject n conditional residuals (one per text token) into the transformer output. Each MoC block uses cross-attention between motion features and CLIP text embeddings to simultaneously fuse modalities and determine which sub-motion range each token governs, then applies text-token-specific experts (a 2-layer FFN whose parameters are blended from a pool of K=12 experts via a gating network) to produce attention-masked residuals. This design aligns individual text tokens to their corresponding sub-motion segments in an MoE fashion, enabling fine-grained control over complex multi-token prompts.

## Results

On the zero-shot Mixamo benchmark, OMG achieves the best FID (1.164) and CLIP-score (0.588) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best MLD (FID 1.229, CLIP-score 0.556) by a clear margin. On the in-domain HumanML3D test set, OMG attains the highest R-Precision (0.784) and the best FID among diffusion-based methods (0.381), though T2M-GPT (FID 0.116) and MotionGPT (FID 0.232) report lower FID due to their autoregressive/LLM-based architectures. The diversity scores remain close to real motion (9.657 vs 9.503 on HumanML3D; 2.632 vs 2.665 on Mixamo), indicating no significant mode collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMG decisively wins on the zero-shot Mixamo benchmark across all three metrics: it achieves FID 1.164 vs. MLD's 1.229, MDM's 1.297, MotionGPT's 1.365, T2M-GPT's 1.420, MotionDiffuse's 2.363, and MotionCLIP's 2.542, and CLIP-score 0.588 vs. MLD's 0.556, MotionGPT's 0.552, T2M-GPT's 0.541, MDM's 0.536, MotionCLIP's 0.511, and MotionDiffuse's 0.505. On the in-domain HumanML3D set, OMG achieves the best R-Precision (0.784) and the best FID among diffusion-based methods (0.381 vs. MLD 0.473, MDM 0.544, MotionDiffuse 0.630, MAA 0.774), but trails the autoregressive T2M-GPT (FID 0.116) and LLM-based MotionGPT (FID 0.232) on FID, suggesting that in-distribution generation still favors tokenized/autoregressive approaches. The gap on zero-shot FID is most pronounced against text-pose alignment methods (MotionCLIP, MAA) and the earlier diffusion baselines (MDM, MotionDiffuse), confirming that the pre-training on 20M+ unlabeled frames plus the Mixture-of-Controllers conditioning is critical for open-vocabulary generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the pretrain-then-finetune paradigm that proved successful in text-to-image and text-to-video generation (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Sora) to the text-to-motion domain, using a DiT backbone scaled to 1B parameters on 20M+ unlabeled motion frames. The fine-tuning stage directly borrows the ControlNet architecture from image generation, but introduces a novel Mixture-of-Controllers block that replaces the standard cross-attention conditioning with text-token-specific experts drawn from a shared pool, inspired by Mixture-of-Experts literature. It departs from prior open-vocabulary motion methods (MotionCLIP, AvatarCLIP, MAA) that rely on discrete text-pose alignment, instead operating on continuous motion representations to preserve global rotation and translation. The sliding random window training strategy and Ada-IN conditioning on the CLIP <eos> token are additional design choices not present in prior motion diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OMG demonstrates that the image-generation playbook (large-scale unconditional pre-training + lightweight conditional fine-tuning) transfers effectively to 3D human motion, establishing a clear path toward open-vocabulary motion generation that was previously limited by paired-data scarcity. The MoC block's text-token-specific expert mechanism offers a reusable architectural pattern for aligning multi-token text conditions to different temporal segments of a generated signal, potentially applicable beyond motion. Limitations include dependence on CLIP's text understanding (capping semantic richness), fine-tuning on only HumanML3D (a relatively small paired dataset), and the substantial compute cost of pre-training (1500 A100 GPU-hours for the 1B model). Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person or scene-conditional generation, and whether the expert pool can be made more interpretable or controllable.
