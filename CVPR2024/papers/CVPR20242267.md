## Problem

Standard CNN convolutions use fixed kernel sizes, limiting their ability to adapt receptive fields to the highly variable scales of scene text. Meanwhile, existing segmentation-based detectors either segment only the text center (losing edge and direction information) or introduce iterative corrections and multi-stage decoding to recover accuracy, sacrificing inference efficiency. The paper aims to close this gap by achieving state-of-the-art detection accuracy with a simpler, more efficient single-stage architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242267/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image as input, resized to 640×640 during training and processed at original resolution during inference. During training, the network simultaneously predicts a distance map (per-pixel distance to the nearest text boundary), a binary segmentation map, and a direction map (per-pixel vector toward the nearest boundary point). At inference, only the distance map and segmentation map are produced; the distance map is binarized at a dataset-specific threshold to extract text center regions, which are then expanded to full text contours via the Vatti clipping algorithm, with the segmentation map used to filter false positives.

## Method

The architecture consists of a ResNet-based pyramid backbone, a plug-and-play Kernel Adaptive Convolution Module (KACM), and a detection head similar to BPN++. KACM replaces standard convolution with a parallel bank of n convolutions at increasing kernel sizes (1×1, 3×3, 5×5, 7×7 for n=4); a lightweight branch (5×5 conv → ReLU → 3×3 conv → Sigmoid → normalization) generates per-spatial-position weights that are broadcast-multiplied onto each convolution's output before concatenation and a 1×1 projection with residual connection. This makes the effective receptive field adaptive at every pixel rather than per-channel or per-instance as in Dynamic Convolution or CondConv. For detection, the network predicts a full text-region distance map supervised jointly by direction map and segmentation map as auxiliary priors during training, but only the distance and segmentation maps are needed at inference. Training uses a composite loss (cross-entropy for segmentation, L2 for distance, L2+angular for direction) with weights 1:3:1, initialized from BPN++ backbone weights and fine-tuned on target datasets.

## Results

On Total-Text, KAC-ResNet50 achieves 94.10% Precision, 88.07% Recall, and 90.98% F-measure at 25.0 FPS, surpassing the previous SOTA TextBPN++ (90.13% F, 13.2 FPS) by 0.85% F-measure and 89.4% in inference speed. On TD500, KAC-ResNet50 reaches 93.87% P, 88.14% R, 90.79% F at 18.1 FPS, outperforming TextBPN++ (90.10% F, 15.3 FPS) by 0.69% F-measure and 18.3% in speed. On CTW1500 and ICDAR19 ArT, KAC achieves competitive accuracy (86.84% and 80.0% F-measure, respectively) while delivering substantially higher efficiency (19.2 and 4.6 FPS vs. 16.5 and 1.9 FPS for TextBPN++).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Total-Text, KAC-ResNet50 (90.98% F) outperforms the previous SOTA TextBPN++ (90.13% F) by 0.85% F-measure while nearly doubling inference speed (25.0 vs. 13.2 FPS, +89.4%). On TD500, KAC (90.79% F, 18.1 FPS) beats TextBPN++ (90.10% F, 15.3 FPS) by 0.69% F-measure and 18.3% in speed. On CTW1500, DPText-DETR achieves higher accuracy (88.8% F vs. 86.84% F) but does not report FPS, whereas KAC runs at 19.2 FPS vs. TextBPN++'s 16.5 FPS. On ICDAR19 ArT, TextBPN++ marginally leads in F-measure (80.59% vs. 80.0%), but KAC is 142.1% faster (4.6 vs. 1.9 FPS). Overall, KAC achieves the best accuracy-efficiency tradeoff: it sets new SOTA on Total-Text and TD500 in both accuracy and speed, while remaining competitive in accuracy on CTW1500 and ArT with substantially better throughput than all two-stage or iterative baselines (TextBPN, TextBPN++, DPText-DETR, TextPMs, PCR).

## Relation to Prior Work

KAC builds directly on the BPN/BPN++ family of boundary-proposal networks, reusing their detection head architecture, loss formulation, and pyramid-ResNet backbone, while replacing the two-stage iterative boundary-point correction with a single-stage distance map prediction. The Kernel Adaptive Convolution Module (KACM) is conceptually positioned between Dynamic Convolution (channel-level kernel selection), CondConv (instance-level), and SENet (channel attention), but operates at the spatial-position level, providing per-pixel adaptive kernel-size weighting. The distance map supervision strategy draws from TextPMs [43] and BPN [41], but simplifies the multi-map iterative scheme into a single distance map plus segmentation map, trading some of the iterative refinement for inference speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff: KAC achieves SOTA F-measure on Total-Text and TD500 while running at roughly 2× the FPS of BPN++, making it more viable for real-time deployment where two-stage boundary-point methods are too slow. The plug-and-play nature of KACM (validated by a 1.6% F-measure gain when inserted into DB) suggests the module could benefit other segmentation-based detectors beyond scene text. Open questions include whether the distance-map formulation generalizes to end-to-end text spotting (the authors explicitly flag this as future work) and whether the position-wise kernel adaptation provides gains on tasks with less extreme scale variation than scene text.
