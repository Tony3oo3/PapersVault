## Problem

Monocular 3D human reconstruction is inherently ill-posed due to occlusion, depth ambiguity, and unobserved body parts, yet existing methods treat it as a one-to-one mapping and output a single deterministic solution. This causes predictions to regress toward the mean of the training distribution in uncertain regions (e.g., the back of a person), producing blurry, over-smooth geometry and colour. The paper addresses this by predicting a full probability distribution over plausible 3D reconstructions, enabling sampling of multiple diverse, input-consistent results.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image (with masked background) as input and produces a learned distribution over 3D human reconstructions, from which N diverse samples can be drawn at inference. Each sample is an implicit surface (SDF + albedo field) extractable as an explicit mesh via Marching Cubes, accompanied by surface albedo and a shaded colour render. The internal diffusion operates on a 6-channel observation set (front/back albedo, normals, and depth maps) rendered from the implicit surface under a fixed camera, with back-views generated by inverting the z-buffer.

## Method

DiffHuman implements a conditional DDPM that denoises pixel-aligned 2D observations of an underlying 3D implicit surface, where the denoising step is mediated by a noise-dependent SDF f_Θ^(t) conditioned on features g_Θ^(t)(x_t, I) extracted by a U-Net. The key architectural contribution is a dual-branch (hybrid) diffusion framework: alongside the standard render-based denoising loss, a separate generator U-Net h_Θ^(t) is trained to directly map pixel-aligned features to denoised observations, imitating the expensive implicit surface rendering. During inference, the generator handles most of the 100 DDIM steps while explicit rendering (Marching Cubes + rasterisation) is performed only at the final step to extract the 3D mesh, yielding a 55× speedup over pure diffusion-via-rendering. A timestep-dependent shading network s_Θ^(t) decouples albedo from illumination, and the model is trained end-to-end on ~450K synthetic HDRI-lit renders of real body scans.

## Results

On the PHORHUM test set, DiffHuman (N=10) achieves the best shaded-front PSNR of 27.15 dB and albedo-back PSNR of 21.24 dB, outperforming the retrained PHORHUM baseline (25.23 and 19.74 dB respectively). On 3D metrics, DiffHuman (N=10) attains a Chamfer distance of 1.09×10⁻³ and IoU of 0.73, matching or slightly exceeding the retrained PHORHUM (1.10×10⁻³, 0.73). The method shows the largest gains over deterministic baselines on unobserved (back) regions, where albedo-back PSNR improves by ~1.5 dB over PHORHUM-retrained and ~5.2 dB over S3F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffHuman (N=10) outperforms the strongest deterministic baseline, retrained PHORHUM, on shaded-front PSNR (27.15 vs. 25.23 dB), albedo-back PSNR (21.24 vs. 19.74 dB), and normals-back LPIPS (0.15 vs. 0.19), while PHORHUM-retrained retains a slight edge on normals-front angular error (18.41° vs. 18.91°). On 3D metrics, DiffHuman N=10 achieves a marginally lower Chamfer distance (1.09 vs. 1.10×10⁻³) and equal IoU (0.73) compared to retrained PHORHUM, with NC slightly lower (0.86 vs. 0.87). Against S3F, DiffHuman dominates across all reported metrics: albedo-front PSNR improves from 15.33 to 23.47 dB, shaded-front PSNR from 17.38 to 27.15 dB, and Chamfer distance drops from 2.35 to 1.09×10⁻³. Methods that only predict geometry (PIFu, PIFuHD, ICON, ECON, D-IF) lack albedo and shading outputs, making direct colour comparison impossible, but DiffHuman's normals-front angular error (18.91°) is competitive with or better than all of them. The key qualitative advantage is in unobserved back regions, where deterministic methods produce blurry, over-smooth reconstructions while DiffHuman samples retain fine geometric detail and correct colour without front-to-back cloning artefacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffHuman sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) implicit-surface-based monocular 3D human reconstruction (PIFu, PHORHUM, S3F), which uses pixel-aligned features to condition a signed-distance function, and (2) diffusion-via-rendering (e.g., [57, 59]), which integrates 3D rendering into the denoising loop of a 2D diffusion model. The paper departs from the deterministic paradigm of prior reconstruction methods by framing the output as a probability distribution over implicit surfaces, and from prior probabilistic 3D human work (ProHMR, 3D Multibodies, HuManiFlow) by extending distributions from sparse parameters (SMPL, keypoints) to full surface geometry. The key architectural departure is the dual-branch hybrid that replaces per-step implicit surface rendering with a learned generator network, a practical modification not present in prior diffusion-via-rendering approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic formulation directly addresses the well-known averaging/blurry-output problem of deterministic methods on ill-posed monocular reconstruction, enabling diverse, detailed samples in unobserved regions (e.g., the back of a person) and visualising prediction uncertainty. The 55× speedup from the hybrid generate/render strategy makes diffusion-via-rendering practically viable for 3D sampling, which could generalise to other implicit-representation generation tasks. A significant limitation is the reliance on synthetic 3D ground-truth scans for training, constraining data scale; the authors note that leveraging partial 2D/2.5D supervision is a key open direction. Quantitative gains over a retrained PHORHUM baseline are modest, suggesting the main value is qualitative (detail in uncertain regions) and the probabilistic framework itself rather than a large accuracy jump.
