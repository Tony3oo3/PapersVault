## Problem

Existing 2D portrait style transfer methods only handle limited forward-facing views and cannot maintain 3D view consistency, while 3D reconstruction methods produce photorealistic but un-stylized results. Naively combining the two fails because stylized 2D images create a biased image manifold that makes 3D representation learning ill-posed. This paper tackles 3D portrait stylization: generating a high-fidelity, view-consistent 3D stylized avatar from a monocular portrait video guided by a single exemplar image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242465/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short monocular portrait video (20–30 s, ~300 frames, person standing still) and a single stylized exemplar image. Camera intrinsics/extrinsics are recovered via COLMAP, foreground masks via a matting network, and depth priors via OpenMVS. The output is a 3D stylized avatar encoded as three neural implicit fields (SDF geometry, view-dependent appearance, view-independent albedo texture), from which view-consistent renderings at arbitrary novel viewpoints and explicit textured meshes can be extracted.

## Method

The core architecture is a Spatial Neural Representation (SNR) composed of three disentangled MLPs: a geometry field (SDF), a view-dependent appearance field, and a view-independent albedo texture field, all sharing geometric feature vectors. Training follows a progressive three-stage scheme: (1) MVS-guided prior learning reconstructs a photorealistic field using accumulated depth and surface losses from OpenMVS depth maps; (2) geometry adaption fine-tunes the SDF and appearance fields on few-shot stylized frontal views to learn deformed geometry; (3) albedo texture adaption introduces the texture field and jointly optimizes it with the appearance field, using a discriminator to match the reconstructed color distribution to the exemplar's posterized palette. The key insight is that learning a photorealistic prior first and then progressively adapting disentangled structures is far more stable than directly fitting inconsistent stylized observations, and separating geometry from texture enables accurate surface deformation and seamless multi-view-consistent albedo extraction.

## Results

On the Portrait360 dataset, 3DToonify achieves a FID of 57.6 and identity preservation (IP) score of 0.678, outperforming both 3D avatar stylization baselines (DeformToon3D: FID 66.5, IP 0.551; NeRF-Art: FID 78.8, IP 0.671). When compared against 2D portrait stylization methods, the 3D-consistent renderings also yield lower FID (57.6 vs. 86.9 for VToonify, 94.7 vs. 126.1 for DCT-Net) and achieve perfect 3D validity (1.00) versus 0.62 and 0.54 respectively, confirming full 360° view coverage without failure cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DToonify outperforms DeformToon3D on both metrics: FID improves from 66.5 to 57.6 (−13.4%) and IP jumps from 0.551 to 0.678, indicating substantially better style fidelity and identity preservation. Against NeRF-Art, the FID advantage is even larger (57.6 vs. 78.8), while IP is marginally higher (0.678 vs. 0.671), suggesting NeRF-Art preserves identity reasonably well but produces weaker stylization. In the 2D comparison (Table 2), 3DToonify's renderings beat both VToonify (FID 57.6 vs. 86.9) and DCT-Net (FID 94.7 vs. 126.1), and achieve 100% 3D validity versus 62% and 54% for the 2D methods, which fail on extreme side-view poses. Qualitatively, DeformToon3D only handles frontal views and over-exaggerates features, while NeRF-Art produces weakly-changed geometry with undesired textures; 3DToonify generates fine-grained full-head avatars with consistent geometry and exaggerated style across all viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NeRF/NeuS lineage of neural implicit representations (SDF + volume rendering) but repurposes them for a stylization task rather than photorealistic reconstruction. It departs from prior 3D avatar stylization methods (DeformToon3D, NeRF-Art, 3D-AvatarGAN) that rely on text prompts or generative priors, instead adopting an exemplar-guided domain-adaptation strategy inspired by 2D portrait stylization (DCT-Net, VToonify). The key conceptual departure is the progressive training scheme that first learns a photorealistic SDF prior via MVS-guided supervision, then sequentially adapts geometry and texture to the target style, rather than jointly optimizing all components from stylized observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D-consistent stylized avatars can be produced from a single monocular video and one exemplar image, a practical pipeline that could lower the barrier to 3D content creation for non-experts. However, the approach is constrained to static head/upper-body captures with 360° camera rotation, and the Portrait360 dataset (14 videos) is small, limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the progressive scheme extends to full-body or dynamic avatars, whether the MVS depth guidance is necessary with denser captures, and whether the GAN-based albedo discriminator can be replaced with more modern distribution-matching objectives. The 3D validity metric, while illustrative, is somewhat self-referential since 2D methods are structurally incapable of 3D consistency.
