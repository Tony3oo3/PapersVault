## Problem

Existing head avatar methods face a fundamental trade-off: mesh-based approaches (e.g., NHA) are limited in representing non-surface features like hair and accessories, while neural implicit methods (e.g., INSTA, NeRFBlendshape) achieve high fidelity but suffer from slow training and inference. The 'canonical + deformation' strategy used by dynamic 3D-GS works also fails to robustly model complex facial expressions. The paper asks whether a lightweight, mesh-guided Gaussian representation can simultaneously achieve photo-realistic quality, capture fine details and exaggerated expressions, and render at 300 FPS on a consumer GPU.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 1–3 minute monocular video (sub-sampled to 25 FPS, resized to 512×512) along with camera intrinsics, poses, and FLAME tracking results (expression coefficients, eye pose, jaw pose, and eyelid coefficients concatenated into a single code). It produces a compact 3D Gaussian field of only 13,453 Gaussians maintained in a 128×128 UV space, which can be animated by swapping expression codes and rendered at 300 FPS at 512×512 on a single RTX 3090. Training completes in a few minutes, and the resulting avatar supports free camera pose changes for novel view synthesis.

## Method

FlashAvatar initializes 3D Gaussians uniformly on the FLAME mesh surface via UV-space sampling (rasterizing the mesh to a 128-resolution UV map and sampling every head-region pixel), which yields a far more uniform position distribution than vertex-based initialization. A 5-layer MLP offset network (256 hidden dim) takes the positional encoding of each Gaussian's canonical mesh position and the tracked expression code, outputting small spatial residuals (position, rotation, scaling) that model non-surface regions (hair, teeth) and subtle facial dynamics. The mesh-attached Gaussians move rigidly with the deformed FLAME mesh, so the offset network only needs to learn small corrections rather than large deformations. Training uses a Huber photometric loss with 40× weighting on the mouth region plus LPIPS perceptual loss, optimized with Adam over 2000 randomly sampled frames per epoch. This design compresses the Gaussian count to the 10K level while preserving full expression range, enabling stable 300 FPS rendering without the densification explosion of standard 3D-GS.

## Results

On the public head-avatar benchmark (data from NeRFBlendshape, NHA, Neural Voice Puppetry, and INSTA), FlashAvatar achieves a PSNR of 32.33 dB, LPIPS of 0.0323, and SSIM of 0.942, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, INSTA, reaches 30.54 dB PSNR and 0.0376 LPIPS, while NHA and PointAvatar trail further behind at 28.80 and 27.03 dB PSNR respectively. FlashAvatar also renders at over 300 FPS at 512×512 on a single RTX 3090, roughly an order of magnitude faster than the implicit-representation baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashAvatar wins on all five metrics (MSE, L1, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) against all three baselines. Against the strongest baseline INSTA [62], FlashAvatar improves PSNR by 1.79 dB (32.33 vs 30.54) and reduces LPIPS by 14% (0.0323 vs 0.0376), while also rendering at 300+ FPS versus INSTA's much slower neural-implicit inference. Against NHA [13], the mesh-based explicit method, FlashAvatar gains 3.53 dB in PSNR and halves the LPIPS, largely because NHA's fixed mesh topology cannot model hair, glasses, or interior mouth. Against PointAvatar [60], which uses a deformable point cloud, FlashAvatar achieves 5.3 dB higher PSNR and 45% lower LPIPS while using far fewer primitives (13K Gaussians vs. tens of thousands of points) and requiring only a 24 GB GPU instead of 80 GB. Qualitatively, FlashAvatar recovers thin structures (eyeglasses, earphones, individual hair strands) and subtle expressions that INSTA smooths over and PointAvatar fails to capture even with its larger point budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashAvatar sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) and parametric face models (FLAME), departing from the dominant 'canonical + deformation' paradigm used by dynamic 3D-GS works (Deformable3DGS, 4DGS) by instead attaching Gaussians directly to the mesh surface and learning only small spatial offsets. This contrasts with implicit NeRF-based avatar methods (INSTA, NeRFBlendshape, AvatarMAV) that rely on volume rendering and neural primitives, and with explicit mesh-based methods (NHA, PointAvatar) that are constrained by fixed topology. The key methodological novelty is the UV-space sampling strategy for uniform Gaussian initialization, which compresses the field to ~13K Gaussians while preserving surface coverage, combined with a lightweight offset MLP conditioned on expression codes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 300FPS photo-realistic head avatar rendering on a single consumer-grade GPU (RTX 3090) with training in minutes opens practical pathways for real-time multimodal digital humans where remaining compute can support speech processing, text understanding, and cross-modal translation. The finding that the standard canonical+deformation strategy fails for complex facial expressions even with mesh initialization is a useful negative result that redirects the field toward mesh-embedded representations. Limitations include dependence on accurate FLAME tracking (global pose errors cause misalignment) and inability to model heavy non-rigid hair deformation, leaving open questions about robustness to in-the-wild conditions and extension to mobile/mixed-reality hardware.
