## Problem

While humans can naturally imagine plausible local motions (swaying vegetation, facial expressions, flickering flames) from a single static image, no large-scale model existed to computationally capture this motion distribution. Existing image-to-video approaches either generate full pixel-space video frames directly—making them computationally expensive and less controllable—or fail to leverage large-scale prior knowledge to abstract unified motion laws across diverse scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 reference image I_0 and an optional text prompt (encoded via ImageBind alongside I_0 features) as input. It produces a 40-frame video sequence at 512×512 resolution, along with intermediate structured outputs: a latent motion trajectory of shape 40×16×16×4, per-frame optical flows (2×H/4×W/4), and intention maps (H/4×W/4). The motion flow operates at quarter resolution to reduce computational cost while preserving local motion detail.

## Method

LVMM decomposes image animation into two stages: a Motion Rendering Model (Neural Image Renderer R, Motion Flow Predictor P, and a Motion VAE with encoder E and decoder D) that maps motion flows to rendered images, and a Motion Diffusion Model (a conditional 3D U-Net denoiser ε_θ trained with DDPM) that generates latent motion trajectories from Gaussian noise conditioned on the reference image. The key insight is that the latent motion vector z decomposes into a visual component v (captured by z_0 = E∘P(I_0, I_0)) and a pure motion component u = z_k − v_0, allowing the diffusion model to learn only motion-agnostic dynamics rather than entangled visual features. The motion flow itself is split into optical flow (low-frequency motion trends) and an intention map (high-frequency motion boundaries) to prevent gradient pollution from stationary regions. Training proceeds in four phases: joint training of R and P, VAE training with both motion-flow and pixel reconstruction losses, optional fine-tuning of R, and finally diffusion model training on precomputed latent trajectories.

## Results

On the MUG facial expression dataset, LVMM (fine-tuned from LVMM-Facial) achieves an FVD of 15.34, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline LFDM (27.57) by 44%. On MHAD (human actions), LVMM reduces FVD to 124.56 versus LFDM's 152.48, and on NATOPS (aircraft handling signals), it reaches 144.46 versus 160.84. Even when trained from scratch (LVMM†), the method surpasses all baselines on FVD across all three datasets, confirming the architectural advantage independent of pretraining. The fine-tuned model also achieves the best cFVD and sFVD on every dataset, indicating superior condition accuracy and subject relevance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVMM outperforms all five baselines on FVD across all three evaluation datasets. Against the closest competitor LFDM (latent flow diffusion), the fine-tuned LVMM achieves a 44% FVD reduction on MUG (15.34 vs. 27.57), 18% on MHAD (124.56 vs. 152.48), and 10% on NATOPS (144.46 vs. 160.84). The GAN-based ImaGINator is the weakest baseline, with FVD values 10–11× higher than LVMM, while VDM and LDM occupy the middle range. Notably, even LVMM trained from scratch (LVMM†) beats LFDM on FVD for all three datasets, isolating the architectural contribution from the pretraining benefit. Qualitatively, LVMM also surpasses the large-scale video generation models I2V-GenXL and Gen-2 in maintaining subject relevance (measured via CLIP feature similarity curves in Fig. 6), producing more detailed and controllable local motion while preserving the reference image content.

## Relation to Prior Work

LVMM builds on the latent diffusion paradigm (LDM, DDPM/DDIM) and optical-flow-based image animation (Siarohin et al.), but departs from prior image-to-video methods (ImaGINator, VDM, LFDM) by operating in a learned latent motion space rather than pixel or feature space. The key architectural departure is the Motion VAE that compresses optical flow plus an intention map into a compact latent vector, followed by a decomposition of that vector into visual and motion segments, so the diffusion model only needs to learn motion-agnostic dynamics. Conditioning leverages ImageBind for multimodal (image + text) guidance, extending the typical CLIP-only conditioning seen in prior I2V work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The visual/motion decomposition in latent space is a conceptually clean idea that could simplify motion generation, but its validation rests on only three simulated objects (golden leaf, brown leaf, avocado), leaving the generalizability of the decomposition largely unproven. The paper itself acknowledges the model cannot produce arbitrary motion zero-shot and requires domain-specific fine-tuning (e.g., CelebV-HD for faces), limiting its practical scope as a general-purpose animator. Open questions include whether the decomposition holds for large-amplitude or multi-object motions, whether the intention map generalizes beyond the 1/4-resolution setting, and how the approach scales to longer video sequences without temporal drift.
