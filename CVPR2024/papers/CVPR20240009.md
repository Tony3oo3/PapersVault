## Problem

Existing OOD detection algorithms (MSP, Energy, ODIN, KL) process each test sample independently and produce scores that are sub-optimal due to model over-confidence on individual inputs. The paper identifies an underexploited property: across diverse detectors and datasets, the OOD score is linearly related to the network's penultimate-layer features, yet no prior method leverages this inter-sample structure at test time to rectify individual score errors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch (or streaming mini-batches) of unlabeled test images, from which it extracts penultimate-layer features z and base OOD scores s from any pretrained classifier (Wide ResNet for CIFAR, BiT-S ResNetV2-101 for ImageNet). It outputs a calibrated OOD score per sample via the fitted linear model, which is then thresholded to yield an in/out-of-distribution decision. Unlike standard post-hoc OOD methods that score samples one at a time, RTL requires simultaneous access to multiple test samples, though this is the same batch requirement needed to compute FPR95 and AUROC evaluation metrics.

## Method

RTL exploits the empirically verified linear relationship between OOD scores and network features by fitting a closed-form linear regression β̂ = (ZᵀZ)†ZᵀS over the batch of (z_i, s_i) pairs at test time, then replacing each raw score with the calibrated prediction z_iᵀβ̂. RTL++ addresses cases where the base detector produces a few large errors by introducing a sparse incidental parameter γ_i and solving a LASSO problem to identify and exclude outlier samples before re-fitting β. An online variant updates the sufficient statistics ZᵀZ and ZᵀS incrementally as new mini-batches arrive, avoiding the need for full test-set access. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves that under sub-Gaussian error with moderate strength (t/ν exceeding a rank-dependent threshold), RTL recovers the correct ID/OOD ranking with probability at least 1−δ, and the bound is independent of sample size n.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1k benchmark (averaged over iNaturalist, SUN, Places, and Textures), RTL++ reduces FPR95 from 76.96% (MSP) to 45.97% and improves AUROC from 79.29% to 87.38%, a ~31-point FPR95 gain. On CIFAR-100, RTL++ with MSP achieves FPR95 of 43.73% and AUROC of 87.06%, compared to 80.21% and 75.67% for raw MSP. The online version of RTL (batch size 32) achieves FPR95 of 15.06% on CIFAR-10, nearly matching the full-batch RTL result of 12.30%, confirming practicality in streaming settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTL and RTL++ consistently outperform all four post-hoc OOD score baselines (MSP, Energy, ODIN, KL) across both CIFAR and ImageNet benchmarks. On ImageNet, RTL++ with MSP achieves FPR95 of 45.97%, beating GradNorm (54.70%) by 8.73 points and surpassing the best post-hoc baseline (ODIN at 72.99%) by over 27 points. The traditional outlier detection methods (Isolation Forest, LOF, GMM) perform substantially worse, with FPR95 values of 81.62%, 91.28%, and 92.53% respectively on ImageNet, because OOD samples do not form a small outlier cluster but rather scatter normally in feature space. On CIFAR-100, RTL++ with MSP (FPR95 43.73%, AUROC 87.06%) also outperforms GradNorm (86.55%, 57.56%) by a wide margin. The online version of RTL (batch size 32) retains most of the full-batch gains, e.g., FPR95 of 15.06% vs. 12.30% for full-batch RTL on CIFAR-10 with MSP, confirming that the improvement stems from test-time learning rather than access to the full test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the family of post-hoc OOD detection methods (MSP, ODIN, Energy, KL divergence, GradNorm) by treating their scores as noisy observations of an underlying linear function of network features. It positions itself within the test-time adaptation (TTA) literature but departs from standard TTA approaches that update model parameters (e.g., batch-norm statistics, entropy minimization); instead, RTL fits a lightweight linear regression between features and scores without touching the backbone. The method also draws on robust statistics (incidental parameters, LASSO) for the RTL++ variant, connecting OOD detection to the outlier-robust regression literature. Unlike outlier detection methods (LOF, Isolation Forest), RTL requires no access to training data and does not assume OOD samples are a small minority.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of a consistent linear trend between OOD scores and pre-classification features across multiple scoring algorithms and datasets provides a unifying lens on how these seemingly different detectors relate to the feature geometry, potentially guiding the design of future OOD methods. The practical appeal is strong: a single closed-form linear regression (or LASSO) at test time yields large FPR95 reductions (e.g., ~31% absolute on ImageNet with MSP) at negligible compute cost, and the online variant makes it deployable in streaming settings. Limitations include the assumption that the linear trend holds (validated on CNNs with ImageNet/CIFAR features but not extensively on transformers or very different architectures), the need for a batch of test data to fit the regression, and the fact that the method is inherently transductive—it cannot score a single sample in isolation. Open questions include whether the linear structure persists under domain shift, for generative models, or when the OOD distribution is highly structured rather than random.
