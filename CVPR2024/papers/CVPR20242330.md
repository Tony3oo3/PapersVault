## Problem

Existing implicit motion models like Dynamic Point Fields (DPF) learn per-frame deformation fields, causing model size to grow linearly with sequence length and producing temporal discontinuities between consecutive frames. Moreover, the SIREN-based translation field in DPF has limited representational power because its Jacobian contains only an identity matrix plus a bounded gradient term, constraining the motion complexity that can be captured in any infinitesimal neighborhood.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point x in the canonical frame and a scalar time step t (normalized to [-1,1]) as input to a single SIREN network, which outputs a 12-dimensional vector reshaped into a 3×3 affine matrix A and a 3D translation vector u. For the guided mesh alignment task, the input additionally includes SMPL-X guidance keypoints (10,475 points) and low-resolution target scans (40,000 points per frame), and the output is warped mesh vertices (~60K vertices) with recomputed normals. Training uses only 25% of observed point trajectories, with the remaining 75% held out for evaluation.

## Method

DOMA formulates the motion field as y = A(x,t)·x + u(x,t), where both A and u are produced by a shared SIREN network (sinusoidal activations, 2–3 hidden layers of 128 dimensions) that takes the 4D input (x, t). The key insight is that the Jacobian ∂y/∂x = A + ⟨∇A, x⟩ + ∇u(x) replaces the identity matrix in DPF's formulation, so additional degrees of freedom at the output layer (scaling, shearing, rotation) increase motion representational power without enlarging the hidden layers, keeping the model at ~200 KB versus DPF's ~8 MB for 30 frames. The authors provide a theoretical bound showing that the upper limit on ‖∇u‖₂ is governed by hidden-layer parameters, while the A term contributes unbounded DOFs. A Charbonnier-based smoothness regularization penalizes the Frobenius norm of spatial gradients of A and u to prevent overfitting on sparse trajectories, with analytical SIREN gradients computed for efficiency. Four variants (Trans, SE(3), Scaled SE(3), Affinity) are offered by constraining A to identity, rotation, scaled rotation, or full 3×3, respectively.

## Results

On the DeformingThings4D novel point motion prediction task, DOMA variants achieve EPE of 76.2–78.5 (×10⁻⁴), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline BoneCloud (136.1) and MLP-ReLU (222.4). DOMA-Affinity attains the best Chamfer distance (LCD = 1.266 ×10⁻⁴) and DOMA-Trans the best Chamfer normal distance (Ln = 0.215). On the Resynth guided mesh alignment task, DOMA-Affinity matches frame-wise DPF alignment accuracy (LCD 1.142 vs 1.149 ×10⁻⁴) while reducing model size from ~7.8 MB to ~211 KB and improving temporal smoothness (STD(V) 22.8 vs 24.6 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOMA consistently outperforms all four baselines on every metric in the DeformingThings4D evaluation. Against the strongest baseline BoneCloud (adapted from KeyTr [41]), DOMA-Scaled SE(3) reduces EPE from 136.1 to 76.2 (×10⁻⁴), a 44% improvement, while DOMA-Affinity cuts LCD from 1.993 to 1.266. The MLP-ReLU and DCT-NeRF baselines, which use standard ReLU activations or DCT coefficient outputs, are outperformed by roughly 3× on EPE (215–238 vs 76–78). BANMO, which relies on 3D Gaussian control proxies, performs worst overall (EPE 488.9), indicating that object-model-based priors are less effective for generic non-rigid motion than the implicit SIREN-based field. On the Resynth guided mesh alignment task, DOMA-Affinity matches frame-wise DPF [48] in alignment accuracy (LCD 1.142 vs 1.149) while achieving better temporal regularity (STD(V) 22.8 vs 24.6 mm) and a ~37× smaller checkpoint (211 KB vs 7.8 MB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Dynamic Point Fields (DPF, Prokudin et al., ICCV 2023) from a per-frame, two-frame deformation model to a single spatiotemporally continuous network by incorporating a 1D time input into the SIREN architecture (Sitzmann et al., NeurIPS 2020). The key conceptual departure is a Jacobian-matrix analysis drawn from continuum mechanics, which shows that adding degrees of freedom at the output layer (affine, SE(3), scaled-SE(3)) increases local motion complexity without enlarging hidden layers—contrasting with the straightforward capacity-scaling strategy of deeper/wider MLPs used in prior warping fields (Nerfies, D-NeRF, Neural Volumes). The paper also departs from object-model-based approaches (BANMO, KeyTr, bone-based skinning) by requiring no parametric proxy, and introduces a Charbonnier-based smoothness regularizer that differs from the elastic (rigidity-encouraging) loss in Nerfies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× model-size reduction (≈200 KB vs. ≈8 MB for a 30-frame sequence) combined with built-in temporal consistency makes DOMA practically attractive for applications where per-frame models are infeasible, such as real-time avatar animation or long-sequence mesh alignment. The Jacobian-based justification for output DOFs provides a reusable design principle for implicit motion fields beyond this specific architecture. Open questions remain: the method requires inter-frame correspondences, loss-weight balancing across four terms is non-trivial, the model is deterministic (no motion uncertainty), and its applicability to dynamic scene rendering or highly complex dynamics (e.g., fluids) is left as future work.
