## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D scene editing methods (e.g., Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, RePaint-NeRF) can only perform general edits and cannot incorporate a specific reference subject, while image-driven editing of NeRF scenes remains largely unexplored. Two core challenges are unresolved: (1) accurately manipulating only foreground regions in an implicit NeRF representation without disturbing the background, and (2) maintaining multi-view geometric consistency when the editing prompt is a single-view reference image (the Janus problem).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241809/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of well-calibrated RGB images of a real 3D scene plus either a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'a dog standing in the forest') or one or more reference images of a specific subject. In the image-driven setting, the reference image is first encoded via Custom Diffusion into a special token V* that is spliced into the text prompt to form a hybrid prompt. The output is an edited NeRF (Instant-NGP-based) that renders the scene with the foreground replaced per the prompt while the background geometry and appearance remain unchanged; editing takes 10 minutes to 1 hour on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

CustomNeRF introduces a foreground-aware NeRF that augments standard color and density outputs with an editing-probability mask field m(p,d), supervised by Grounded SAM pseudo-labels, enabling separate rendering of foreground (σ_f = m·σ) and background (σ_b = (1−m)·σ) regions. The core training procedure is a Local-Global Iterative Editing (LGIE) scheme that alternates between a local stage—where only the foreground is rendered and optimized via SDS loss with a foreground-only prompt—and a global stage—where the full image is rendered, the complete prompt is used, and background gradients are detached to prevent layout drift. For image-driven editing, a class-guided regularization removes the V* token from the local-stage prompt so that general class priors in the T2I model enforce geometrically consistent multi-view editing, while V* is retained only in the global stage to transfer the reference subject's appearance. Background preservation is further enforced by an explicit MSE loss between original and edited background renderings, yielding the total loss L = λ_SDS·L_SDS + λ_bg·L_bg.

## Results

On image-driven editing, CustomNeRF achieves a CLIP directional similarity of 20.07 and DINO similarity of 47.44, with 75.6% user preference votes across 8 real scenes (BlendedMVS, LLFF, IBRNet, Bear Statue). On text-driven editing, it reaches a CLIP directional similarity of 22.66 and 85.2% user votes. The method is evaluated with 4–6 text prompts per scene for text-driven editing and 3–5 reference images per scene for image-driven editing, with editing times ranging from 10 minutes to 1 hour on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In text-driven editing, CustomNeRF outperforms Instruct-NeRF2NeRF by 11.18 points on CLIP_dir (22.66 vs. 11.48) and by 74.6 percentage points in user preference (85.2% vs. 10.6%), with Instruct-NeRF2NeRF retaining the original bear's body shape rather than producing a convincing dog. Against RePaint-NeRF, CustomNeRF leads by 6.32 points on CLIP_dir (22.66 vs. 16.34) and by 81 percentage points in user votes (85.2% vs. 4.2%), as RePaint-NeRF tends to produce abnormal renderings and background style drift. In image-driven editing, CustomNeRF surpasses the Ours+Splice Loss baseline by 4.6 points on CLIP_dir (20.07 vs. 15.47) and by 65 percentage points in user votes (75.6% vs. 10.6%), since Splice Loss only transfers superficial color/texture without modifying geometry. Compared to RePaint-NeRF* (modified with Custom Diffusion), CustomNeRF wins on all three metrics: CLIP_dir (20.07 vs. 16.46), DINO_sim (47.44 vs. 38.74), and user votes (75.6% vs. 13.8%), with RePaint-NeRF* failing to preserve background content and lacking fine details such as hairstyle and clothing texture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Score Distillation Sampling framework from DreamFusion for optimizing NeRF with 2D diffusion priors, and on Custom Diffusion for embedding reference subjects into text prompts. It departs from prior text-driven NeRF editing methods (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, RePaint-NeRF) by introducing a foreground-aware NeRF with an explicit editing-probability field and a Local-Global Iterative Editing scheme, rather than relying on 2D semantic masks or iterative dataset updates. Unlike Vox-E and DreamEditor, which switch to explicit voxel or mesh representations for selective editing, CustomNeRF operates entirely within the implicit NeRF representation, using the learned mask field to modulate density during rendering. The unification of text-driven and image-driven editing under a single hybrid-prompt formulation is the key conceptual extension over prior work that handled only textual instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, unified pipeline for 3D scene editing that accepts either free-form text or a reference image, lowering the barrier for users who want specific (not just general) edits. The LGIE scheme and class-guided regularization are simple, modular additions that address two well-known pain points (background drift and the Janus problem) without requiring explicit 3D representations. Limitations include inherited DreamFusion artifacts (oversaturation), imperfect subject replication from Custom Diffusion, and restriction to text/image prompts; extending to other modalities (sketches, audio) or handling more complex multi-object edits remains open. The approach is also limited to single-scene editing and does not address large-scale scene modifications or temporal consistency.
