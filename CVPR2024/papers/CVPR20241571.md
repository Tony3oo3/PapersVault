## Problem

Existing adversarial makeup methods for facial privacy protection rely on GANs or coarse text prompts, resulting in poor visual quality, imprecise control over fine-grained makeup attributes (position, color shade, intensity), and low black-box transferability against unseen face recognition models. The core challenge is establishing a deterministic, fine-grained guidance signal that simultaneously controls both the direction and distance of makeup generation in a semantic space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image x, a reference makeup image y, and a target face image x* as inputs, and produces a protected face image x' that carries adversarial makeup style from y while fooling FR systems into recognizing x* as the identity. The pipeline operates in two sequential fine-tuning stages on pre-trained ADM diffusion models, using deterministic DDIM inversion and sampling with reduced time steps (T=60, S_inv=20, S_sam=6) to limit modifications to fine-grained makeup details while preserving identity, background, and other makeup-irrelevant attributes.

## Method

DiffAM is a two-stage diffusion-based framework. In the first stage (text-guided makeup removal), a pre-trained diffusion model is fine-tuned with a directional CLIP loss to remove makeup from the reference image y, yielding a non-makeup image ŷ; the CLIP-space vector between y and ŷ provides a deterministic direction from the non-makeup domain to the reference makeup domain. In the second stage (image-guided adversarial makeup transfer), a second diffusion model is fine-tuned to generate x' under three joint losses: a CLIP-based makeup direction loss that aligns the x→x' direction with the reversed makeup-removal direction, a pixel-level histogram-matching loss that constrains makeup intensity per facial region, and an ensemble attack loss averaging cosine similarity across K surrogate FR models to push x' toward a universal adversarial domain. The key insight is that the inverse of makeup removal provides a precise, image-grounded cross-domain alignment that text prompts cannot achieve, enabling fine-grained control over both the direction and distance of adversarial makeup generation.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ and LADN, DiffAM achieves an average black-box attack success rate (ASR) of 77.88% across four FR models (IRSE50, IR152, FaceNet, MobileFaceNet), outperforming the best noise-based method TIP-IM (50.06%) by ~28% and the best makeup-based method CLIP2Protect (64.90%) by 12.98%. In terms of image quality, DiffAM achieves FID 26.10, PSNR 20.53 dB, and SSIM 0.886, surpassing CLIP2Protect (FID 37.12, SSIM 0.603) and AMT-GAN (FID 34.44, SSIM 0.787) while remaining competitive with Adv-Makeup (FID 4.23), which only generates eyeshadow. On commercial APIs (Face++, Aliyun), DiffAM achieves the highest average confidence scores (~70 and ~50 respectively) with stable performance across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffAM outperforms all seven baselines on average ASR across both CelebA-HQ and LADN. Against the strongest noise-based method TIP-IM (50.06% avg ASR), DiffAM gains ~28 percentage points, and against the strongest makeup-based method CLIP2Protect (64.90%), it gains 12.98 percentage points. Among makeup-based methods, AMT-GAN (52.84%) and Adv-Makeup (14.72%) are substantially outperformed; Adv-Makeup's low ASR stems from generating only eyeshadow rather than full-face makeup. DiffAM also notably attacks FaceNet more effectively (64.67% vs. 41.72% for CLIP2Protect and 40.74% for TIP-IM), a model that is difficult to fool with other methods. In image quality (Table 2), DiffAM achieves the best FID (26.10) and SSIM (0.886) among full-face makeup methods, though Adv-Makeup scores higher on all quality metrics (FID 4.23, PSNR 34.52, SSIM 0.985) at the cost of drastically lower attack effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffAM sits at the intersection of three research threads: CLIP-guided diffusion editing (DiffusionCLIP), GAN-based makeup transfer (BeautyGAN's histogram matching, AMT-GAN's ensemble attack), and adversarial facial privacy protection. Its key conceptual departure is reframing the makeup transfer problem through its inverse—text-guided makeup removal—to establish a deterministic, image-based direction in CLIP space, replacing the coarse-grained textual prompts used by CLIP2Protect. Rather than proposing a new generative architecture, DiffAM repurposes a fine-tuned diffusion model as a controllable editor, combining the directional CLIP loss from DiffusionCLIP with a novel makeup direction loss and the ensemble attack strategy from AMT-GAN into a unified two-stage pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: social media users need imperceptible yet effective protection against commercial face recognition APIs, and DiffAM demonstrates strong black-box transferability (77.88% average ASR, ~70 confidence on Face++) while preserving visual quality better than prior makeup-based methods. However, the approach requires per-image fine-tuning of a diffusion model (6 epochs on an RTX 3090), limiting real-time or large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the targeted-attack formulation generalizes to dodging attacks, whether the method remains effective against FR models trained with adversarial robustness, and whether the reliance on a reference makeup image can be relaxed for broader usability.
