## Problem

Existing pipelines that convert LiDAR scans into ground-truth occupancy grids for training camera-based occupancy prediction models (e.g., Occ3D, SurroundOcc, OpenOccupancy) produce low-quality voxel maps with significant depth errors relative to raw LiDAR measurements. These errors stem from noisy measurements, sparse observations, incorrect densification, and imperfect motion compensation, yet current methods treat each voxel as simply occupied or free without accounting for observation uncertainty. The paper shows that these inaccuracies propagate into trained models, degrading prediction quality in safety-critical regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-frame LiDAR point clouds (sparse, distance-dependent), per-object scene flow annotations for dynamic objects, and ego-vehicle pose to aggregate observations over up to 50 frames within a 20 m displacement window. LiDAR measurements are first quantized into a spherical grid (Δρ=0.1 m, Δθ=Δφ=0.5°) before being warped into a Cartesian voxel grid (0.2 m or 0.4 m cells, spanning ±40 m in x/y and −1 to 5.4 m in z). The output is a per-voxel basic belief assignment over three hypotheses—occupied, free, and uncertain—yielding both a binary occupancy map (by comparing m(o) vs. m(f)) and a continuous observability score (1−m(Ω)) that can serve as a loss weight during training.

## Method

The core pipeline has three stages. First, each LiDAR frame is mapped into two spherical grids: a reflections grid (weighted point scattering of returns into neighboring voxels by volume overlap) and a transmissions grid (computed as the exclusive cumulative sum of reflections from far to near along each ray, encoding how many rays passed through each voxel). Second, these spherical grids from multiple time steps are warped into a common Cartesian grid at the reference frame by transforming voxel centers through known per-object scene flow, normalizing by the Cartesian-to-spherical volume ratio, and averaging over all considered frames. Third, a Dempster–Shafer basic belief assignment (Eq. 10) converts the aggregated reflection count r and transmission count q into belief masses m(o), m(f), and m(Ω) using sensor-specific false-negative and false-positive probabilities, explicitly assigning conflicting or missing evidence to the uncertain hypothesis. For training, the paper replaces the standard camera visibility mask with the per-voxel uncertainty weight (1−m(Ω)) and uses the BBA-derived target probability m(o)/(m(o)+m(f)) as the supervision signal, optionally extending to a three-class cross-entropy loss that also predicts the uncertainty mass.

## Results

On nuScenes at 0.4 m voxel size, the proposed evidential occupancy map achieves a depth MAE of 1.25 m against raw LiDAR, improving over Occ3D (1.80 m) by 30%. At 0.2 m voxel size, the MAE drops to 0.91 m, a 52% improvement over OpenOccupancy (1.89 m). On Waymo at 0.4 m, the MAE is 1.51 m versus Occ3D's 3.23 m (53% improvement). When BEVDet4D-Occ-R50 is fine-tuned on the proposed GT data, prediction MAE improves from 1.89 m to 1.42 m (25% reduction) relative to the Occ3D-trained baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against Occ3D [22], the closest competitor at 0.4 m voxel size, the evidential approach reduces MAE from 1.80 m to 1.25 m on nuScenes (30% gain) and from 3.23 m to 1.51 m on Waymo (53% gain), while also improving δ<1.25 accuracy by 6.5 and 15.0 percentage points respectively. Against OpenOccupancy [26] at 0.2 m, the MAE drops from 1.89 m to 0.91 m (52% improvement), with δ<1.25 rising from 75.0% to 92.6%. Scene as Occupancy [23] and SurroundOcc [27] (both at 0.5 m) show substantially larger errors (MAE 2.44 m and 2.36 m) and are outperformed by the proposed 0.4 m map by roughly 47–49% in MAE. In the downstream training experiment (Table 4), fine-tuning BEVDet4D-Occ-R50 on the proposed GT data with uncertainty-weighted CE loss yields a prediction MAE of 1.42 m, beating the Occ3D-GT baseline (1.89 m) by 25%, confirming that the data quality gain translates directly into better model predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evidential mapping framework from Yang and Aitken (2006) and Richter et al. (2023), which applied Dempster-Shafer theory to 2D top-view grid maps, to full 3D occupancy grid mapping from multi-frame LiDAR. It departs from the standard occupancy GT generation pipeline used by Occ3D, SurroundOcc, OpenOccupancy, and Scene as Occupancy, which rely on binary point-in-voxel occupancy and ray-casting for free space, by instead accumulating continuous reflection/transmission counts and resolving conflicts through a basic belief assignment that explicitly models an 'uncertain' hypothesis. For the downstream prediction task, the paper builds on BEVDet4D as a widely adopted baseline and introduces an uncertainty-weighted loss as a lightweight modification to the training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely-used occupancy benchmark ground truth (Occ3D, SurroundOcc, OpenOccupancy) deviates substantially from raw LiDAR measurements (30–53% MAE) challenges a foundational assumption in the field: that these datasets provide reliable supervision signals. The practical impact is significant for anyone training or evaluating occupancy prediction models, as the improved GT and uncertainty-weighted loss yield measurable gains without architectural changes. Limitations include the absence of semantic labels (only binary occupied/free/uncertain), a single prediction architecture tested (BEVDet4D-R50), and heuristically tuned sensor error parameters. The authors identify incorporating semantic information into the evidential pipeline as a key future direction.
