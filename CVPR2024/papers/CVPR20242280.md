## Problem

Existing monocular 3D building reconstruction methods require fully-annotated 3D labels (footprint, roof-to-footprint offset, and height) for supervised training, but such annotations are expensive and available only for limited single-city datasets. This restricts large-scale cross-city deployment despite the abundance of cheaper footprint-only or partial annotations from open map data. The paper asks whether a unified network can effectively combine samples at different annotation levels to reduce the demand on full 3D supervision while maintaining or improving reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single off-nadir RGB remote sensing image as input and produces, per building instance, a bounding box, a pixel-wise roof mask, a roof-to-footprint offset vector, and a real-world height value. A convolutional ROFE module then integrates the predicted roof mask and offset to directly predict a pixel-wise footprint mask (rather than performing a rigid translation at inference), and the footprint mask plus height are combined into a vectorized 3D building model. During training, the network consumes three tiers of samples—footprint-only, footprint+height, and full footprint+height+offset—each contributing different subsets of loss terms.

## Method

MLS-BRN extends the LOFT-FOA (Mask R-CNN-based) architecture with two novel modules and a multi-level training strategy. The Pseudo Building Bbox Calculator (PBC) predicts image-level off-nadir and offset angles, which—combined with instance-level height ground truth and a scale factor—yield a pseudo building bounding box that substitutes for the unavailable ground-truth bbox, thereby enabling RPN supervision on offset-unknown samples. The Roof-Offset guided Footprint Extractor (ROFE) is a small convolutional network that learns the roof-to-footprint translation as a differentiable operation, providing an indirect supervision pathway for the offset and roof heads even when no offset ground truth exists. A multi-level loss strategy assigns distinct loss combinations to three sample tiers: footprint-only samples contribute only the ROFE footprint loss; footprint+height samples additionally activate the RPN loss (via PBC pseudo bboxes) and a height regression loss; and fully-annotated samples activate all losses including offset, roof mask, and both angle-head losses. The backbone is ResNet-50 with FPN, and the total hybrid loss is the sum across all three sample levels, allowing the network to be trained end-to-end on a mixed dataset with minimal 3D-annotated samples.

## Results

On the BONAI dataset (BN_100), MLS-BRN achieves 66.36% F1-score for footprint segmentation and 4.76 EPE for offset prediction, improving over LOFT-FOA by 2.05% F1 and 0.18 EPE. On OmniCity-view3 (OC_100), the method reaches 72.25% F1 and 5.38 EPE, outperforming LOFT-FOA by 1.79% F1 and 0.70 EPE. In the multi-level supervised setting, training on only 30% fully-annotated samples plus 70% height-annotated samples (BN_30/70) yields 65.49% F1, surpassing LOFT-FOA trained on 100% fully-annotated samples (64.31% F1). For building height prediction, MLS-BRN achieves 10.90 MAE and 21.09 RMSE, the first method to predict instance-wise real-world building height.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLS-BRN outperforms all direct footprint segmentation baselines (PANet, HRNetv2, Mask R-CNN, CM R-CNN) by 5.4–8.3% F1 on BN_100, confirming that offset-based reconstruction is superior to direct footprint extraction for off-nadir images. Against the two offset-based methods, MLS-BRN improves F1 by 2.76% and EPE by 0.93 over MTBR-Net [19], and by 2.05% F1 and 0.18 EPE over LOFT-FOA [32] on BN_100; on OC_100 the gap over LOFT-FOA widens to 1.79% F1 and 0.70 EPE. In the multi-level setting, MLS-BRN trained on BN_30/70 (only 30% fully-annotated) achieves 65.49% F1, exceeding LOFT-FOA trained on the full BN_100 (64.31% F1), demonstrating that the additional height-annotated samples compensate for the missing offset annotations. For height prediction, MLS-BRN (10.90 MAE / 21.09 RMSE) beats SARPN [2] (15.23 / 28.69), DORN [7] (13.40 / 27.03), and the LOFT-FOA+H ablation (11.12 / 21.60), though the margin over LOFT-FOA+H is modest, suggesting the height head's main benefit is providing auxiliary supervision rather than standalone accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LOFT-FOA [32], which introduced the roof-to-footprint offset formulation for off-nadir building reconstruction, and extends it with two new modules (PBC and ROFE) plus a building height head. Conceptually, it draws from the weakly/semi-supervised 3D reconstruction literature in general computer vision [3, 11, 14, 26], but adapts those ideas to the specific geometric structure of buildings (roof, footprint, offset, height relationships) rather than generic 3D shapes. The key departure from prior monocular building reconstruction methods [19, 32, 4, 5] is the multi-level supervision strategy: instead of requiring every training sample to carry full 3D annotations (footprint + offset + height), the framework flexibly incorporates samples with only footprints or footprints + height, leveraging the geometric coupling between building components to transfer supervision across annotation levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: 3D-annotated building datasets are extremely limited (BONAI has only 3,000 training images), while 2D footprint annotations are available at massive scale from open map data. By showing that 30% fully-annotated samples plus 70% partially-annotated samples match or exceed 100% fully-annotated performance, this work opens a path toward cross-city 3D building reconstruction without prohibitive annotation costs. Limitations include the continued dependence on at least some 3D-annotated samples (the 30% floor), evaluation on only a handful of cities, and the fact that the geometric priors (planar roofs, vertical walls) may not generalize to highly irregular structures. Open questions include whether the framework can operate with zero 3D-annotated samples via self-supervision, and whether the multi-level strategy transfers to other 3D reconstruction domains beyond buildings.
