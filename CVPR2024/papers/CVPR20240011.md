## Problem

Unsupervised blind image deblurring lacks real paired sharp-blurry data, making it difficult to learn accurate blur-to-sharp mappings. Existing unsupervised methods either rely on complex geometric augmentation that inflates inference cost or fail to progressively improve the quality of synthetic pseudo-paired data used during training. The paper addresses how to synthesize increasingly realistic pseudo-paired datasets and enhance deblurring performance without altering network architecture or adding inference overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes unpaired sets of real blurry and real sharp RGB images (128×128 patches) from the GoPro dataset, with no paired correspondence required. At inference, a single blurry RGB image is fed into the NAFNet-based reconstructor to produce a deblurred sharp image of the same resolution. The framework is trained exclusively on GoPro and evaluated zero-shot on HIDE, RealBlur (R and J splits), and ReloBlur, demonstrating cross-dataset generalization.

## Method

The MGUD framework employs four ResNet-based generators and PatchGAN discriminators in a CycleGAN-inspired loop (sharp → synthetic blur → reconstructed sharp → secondary synthetic blur), with three Generator Complementary Constraint Modules (GECMs) adding adversarial constraints to stabilize training and improve synthetic image quality. A novel re-degradation principal component consistency (RPC²) loss enforces that multi-scale Gaussian-filtered principal components of the synthesized blurry image match those of the sharp image re-blurred by an estimated kernel, isolating the true degradation signal from content/color interference. The Self-Enhancement (SE) strategy iteratively freezes the previous round's reconstructor to guide the generator toward more realistic pseudo-paired data, then retrains the full pipeline, progressively upgrading the reconstructor (Rec₁ → Rec₂ → … → Recₙ) without any architectural change or additional inference cost. The total loss combines four adversarial terms, the RPC² loss (weighted by Φ=3), and a self-enhancement reconstructor loss that includes PSNR and SSIM terms against both the frozen previous reconstructor's output and the current reconstruction.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, SEMGUD achieves 29.06 dB PSNR and 0.927 SSIM, surpassing the best unsupervised baseline UAUD (26.12 dB, 0.869) by 2.94 dB in PSNR. On HIDE, it reaches 27.64 dB / 0.892 SSIM, and on RealBlur-R it attains 35.51 dB / 0.946 SSIM — matching or exceeding the supervised NAFNet (35.50 dB) on RealBlur-R. All models are trained solely on the GoPro dataset, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMGUD outperforms all 7 unsupervised baselines across all four test sets. The largest gap is on GoPro, where it leads the best unsupervised method UAUD [36] by 2.94 dB PSNR (29.06 vs 26.12) and 0.058 SSIM. On HIDE the margin is 3.27 dB over UAUD, and on RealBlur-R it gains 2.60 dB. Against supervised methods, SEMGUD still trails the strongest (UFPNet [9] at 34.06 dB on GoPro) by ~5 dB, but on RealBlur-R its 35.51 dB PSNR matches NAFNet [5] (35.50 dB) and comes within 0.74 dB of UFPNet (36.25 dB), indicating competitive performance with paired-data methods despite using no paired training data. The paper also notes that supervised models like NAFNet and UFPNet exhibit 'mode collapse' (anomalous pixel regions) when tested on RealBlur-J, whereas SEMGUD does not, suggesting better robustness to domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CycleGAN framework for unpaired image-to-image translation, adapting its cycle-consistency concept into a multi-generator deblurring pipeline (MGUD) with complementary constraint modules. It departs from prior unsupervised deblurring methods (UIDGAN, FCLGAN, USDF, UADU) by introducing two key innovations: a re-degradation principal component consistency (RPC²) loss that constrains synthesized blur to match re-degraded sharp images in principal component space, and a self-enhancement strategy inspired by iterative self-collaboration in GAN training [21]. The reconstructor backbone (NAFNet) and kernel estimation components are borrowed from existing supervised deblurring literature, positioning the contribution primarily in the training paradigm rather than network architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-enhancement strategy is practically significant because it closes much of the gap between unsupervised and supervised deblurring (2.94 dB over SOTA unsupervised, and competitive with NAFNet on RealBlur-R) without any inference-time overhead or architectural modification, making it deployable in resource-constrained settings. The demonstration that SE transfers across different reconstructors (NAFNet, FCLGAN, UFPNet) suggests it is a general-purpose training technique rather than a method-specific trick. Open questions include whether the iterative training cost scales favorably for video deblurring or other restoration tasks, and whether the approach can be extended to truly paired-data-free settings where even unpaired sharp images are unavailable.
