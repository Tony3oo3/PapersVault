## Problem

Existing 3D instance segmentation methods rely on expensive, dense manual annotations, and fully unsupervised approaches for 3D scenes are nearly absent. The few clustering-based alternatives (e.g., HDBSCAN, Felzenszwalb) achieve very low AP scores on cluttered indoor scenes because they lack learned semantic signals and cannot handle overlapping or spatially close objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240327/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB-D scans (color images plus registered depth maps and camera poses) to extract and back-project 2D self-supervised features onto the 3D mesh, alongside 3D self-supervised geometry features. At inference, only the 3D mesh geometry (vertices and face edges) is required—no color or depth input is needed. The output is a set of class-agnostic dense instance masks over mesh vertices, with no predefined category labels.

## Method

UnScene3D first coarsens each scene mesh into geometric primitives via a Felzenszwalb-style graph coarsening that clusters vertices with similar normals and colors, reducing the graph by orders of magnitude and regularizing high-frequency noise in self-supervised features. It then computes cosine-similarity adjacency matrices independently from 2D (DINO) and 3D (PointContrast) self-supervised features, averages them, and applies iterative Normalized Cut on the primitive graph to extract a sparse set of reliable pseudo masks, enforcing spatial contiguity by keeping only the connected component with the largest eigenvector magnitude. These pseudo masks supervise a sparse-voxel 3D transformer (Res16UNet34C) through four rounds of self-training, where each round adds the model's most confident new predictions (gated by an IoU threshold) to the training set, progressively densifying the instance set. The key design choices—operating on geometric primitives rather than raw points, using independent per-modality similarity matrices rather than direct feature fusion, and favoring a sparse but clean initial mask set—enable a >3× AP improvement over clustering baselines on ScanNet.

## Results

On ScanNet, UnScene3D achieves AP@25=58.5, AP@50=32.2, and AP=15.9 for unsupervised class-agnostic 3D instance segmentation, representing a ~3× improvement in AP over the strongest baseline (Felzenswalb at AP=5.0). On S3DIS Area-5, the method reaches AP@25=52.6, AP@50=40.3, and AP=21.4, again substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline AP=5.5). The method also transfers to ARKitScenes, producing qualitatively cleaner instance masks than baselines in complex indoor environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnScene3D decisively outperforms all baselines on both ScanNet and S3DIS. Against the strongest traditional clustering baseline (Felzenswalb, AP=5.0 on ScanNet), the method achieves 3.2× higher AP (15.9) and 2.5× higher AP@50 (32.2 vs 12.7). The learned-feature baseline of Nunes et al. (AP=2.3) is designed for sparse outdoor LIDAR and relies on physical object separation, making it poorly suited to cluttered indoor scenes; UnScene3D improves AP by ~7×. The 2D CutLER method projected to 3D (AP=0.3) fails dramatically due to view inconsistencies and occlusions, confirming the necessity of native 3D reasoning. In the pseudo-mask generation ablation (Table 3), UnScene3D's NCut-based approach with both modalities (AP=15.9 final) outperforms the FreeMask adaptation (AP=12.1 final), demonstrating that a sparser but cleaner initial mask set is more effective than a larger noisier one. For data-efficient pretraining (Figure 4), UnScene3D's self-trained features surpass both training from scratch and the CSC contrastive pretraining [18] on downstream ScanNet instance segmentation with limited annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

UnScene3D adapts the two-stage pseudo-mask-then-self-training paradigm established in 2D unsupervised instance segmentation (FreeSolo, CutLER) to the 3D domain, where it had not previously been applied. It builds on self-supervised 3D feature learning (PointContrast, CSC) for geometric representations and 2D DINO features for color, fusing them through a geometric graph coarsening step inspired by Felzenszwalb's oversegmentation. The key methodological departure from prior 3D work is the use of Normalized Cut on coarsened mesh primitives to generate initial pseudo-masks, followed by iterative self-training with a 3D transformer (Mask3D architecture), rather than relying on density-based clustering (HDBSCAN) or motion-based detection as in prior unsupervised 3D approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully unsupervised 3D instance segmentation method for cluttered indoor scenes, eliminating the need for expensive dense 3D annotations that have been a major bottleneck for the field. The 3× AP improvement over clustering baselines demonstrates that the 2D self-training paradigm transfers effectively to 3D when combined with geometric regularization, opening a path toward scalable 3D scene understanding. However, limitations remain: the mesh-based coarsening may miss very small objects, the fixed initial pseudo-mask set can reinforce noisy predictions across self-training iterations, and absolute AP scores (~16) are still far below supervised methods, leaving a significant gap to close.
