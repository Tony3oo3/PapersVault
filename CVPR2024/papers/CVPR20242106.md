## Problem

Existing dense long-range pixel tracking methods operate entirely in 2D image space, where the 3D-to-2D projection introduces occlusions, discontinuities, and spatially misleading neighborhoods that degrade tracking under complex motion and self-occlusion. The paper argues that the underlying 3D motion is often simpler and lower-dimensional than its 2D projection, and that tracking in 3D enables natural enforcement of geometric priors like as-rigid-as-possible constraints that are inexpressible or ill-posed in 2D.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB video (with depth estimated via ZoeDepth and focal length set to image width) or an RGBD video with known camera intrinsics, and produces dense long-range 2D pixel trajectories as its primary output, along with 3D trajectories, per-point visibility scores, and per-trajectory rigidity embeddings. Internally, each frame is encoded into triplane feature maps at h×w, w×d, and h×d resolutions (128 channels, depth discretized into 256 bins), and trajectories are predicted over sliding windows of 8 frames with 6 iterative transformer updates for 256 query points per batch.

## Method

SpatialTracker lifts 2D query pixels into 3D using monocular depth, then represents each frame's 3D content as triplane feature maps obtained by splatting depth-unprojected, feature-annotated point clouds onto three orthogonal planes followed by convolutional completion. A transformer with six blocks (each containing spatial and temporal attention) iteratively refines 3D trajectories within a sliding window, using correlation features computed against local triplane neighborhoods at each step. The key regularization is a learned ARAP loss: the model predicts a rigidity embedding per trajectory, and pairs of points with high cosine similarity in embedding space are penalized for changing their 3D inter-point distance over time, effectively grouping pixels into rigid parts and propagating motion from visible to occluded regions. The total training objective combines L1 trajectory supervision, cross-entropy visibility loss, and the ARAP term (weighted at 0.1), trained end-to-end on 11,000 synthetic RGBD sequences from TAP-Vid-Kubric.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid benchmark, SpatialTracker achieves an average AJ of 58.2, position accuracy of 73.3%, and occlusion accuracy of 88.2% across Kinetics, DAVIS, and RGB-Stacking, outperforming the strongest baseline CoTracker (57.4 / 72.2 / 87.8). On the long-horizon PointOdyssey dataset (~2000 frames), it reduces Median Trajectory Error to 28.3 px and raises survival rate to 78.6%, compared to CoTracker's 30.5 px and 76.1%. For 3D tracking on PointOdyssey, SpatialTracker achieves an ATE_3D of 0.22 m and δ_0.2 of 76%, substantially outperforming Chained RAFT-3D (0.70 m / 25%) and Lifted CoTracker (0.77 m / 64%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialTracker consistently outperforms CoTracker (the strongest 2D baseline) across all three TAP-Vid sub-datasets by 0.5–0.8 AJ points and 1.1 position-accuracy points on average, with the gap widening on DAVIS (61.1 vs. 60.6 AJ). Against TAPIR, the margin is larger: roughly 5 AJ points and 5.3 position-accuracy points on average, reflecting TAPIR's weaker handling of long occlusions. On the very long PointOdyssey sequences (~2000 frames), SpatialTracker reduces MTE from 30.5 to 28.3 px and improves survival rate from 76.1% to 78.6% over CoTracker, while TAPIR degrades severely (MTE 41.0, survival 67.0%). For 3D tracking, SpatialTracker's ATE_3D of 0.22 m is roughly 3× lower than both Chained RAFT-3D (0.70 m) and Lifted CoTracker (0.77 m), and its δ_0.2 of 76% exceeds Lifted CoTracker's 64% by 12 points. OmniMotion, which also tracks in 3D via test-time optimization, achieves the best RGB-Stacking score (69.5 AJ) but is not directly comparable due to its prohibitively expensive optimization-based pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'tracking any point' (TAP) lineage—TAP-Net, PIPs, TAPIR, CoTracker—by shifting the tracking computation from 2D image space to 3D space. It repurposes the triplane representation from 3D generative models (EG3D) as a compact scene encoding, borrows the ARAP rigidity prior from scene flow literature (RAFT-3D), and uses monocular depth (ZoeDepth) to unproject pixels, all within a CoTracker-style iterative transformer with sliding windows. The key conceptual departure is that feature correlation and motion prediction happen in 3D rather than 2D, which the authors argue provides more meaningful spatial context near occlusion boundaries and enables geometric regularization unavailable in 2D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D tracking formulation opens a path to incorporating richer geometric priors (rigidity, z-buffering, rotation parameters) that are natural in 3D but awkward in 2D, potentially improving robustness to self-occlusion and out-of-plane motion. A notable limitation is the dependence on off-the-shelf monocular depth quality: the ablation shows ZoeDepth substantially outperforms MiDaS and DPT, and the authors explicitly note that tracking performance will improve as depth estimators advance. An open question is whether depth estimation and trajectory prediction can be jointly learned end-to-end, removing the reliance on a frozen depth prior. The learned rigidity embedding also provides a useful byproduct—soft segmentation of rigid parts—without explicit supervision.
