## Problem

Full fine-tuning of large pre-trained models is memory-intensive and infeasible across many downstream tasks, while existing PEFT methods (Adapter, VPT, LoRA, SSF) introduce additional parameters that disrupt the original architecture, increase training and inference costs, and require architecture-specific designs that limit cross-model generalizability. No prior approach simultaneously avoids extra parameters, works uniformly across diverse architectures (Transformer, CNN, segmentation models), and adaptively selects task-relevant parameters without a fixed insertion strategy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (processed following SSF's protocol) and produces class labels for 24 image classification tasks or pixel-level segmentation masks for polyp segmentation. What is unusual is that the input/output interface is completely unchanged from the pre-trained model; GPS operates purely at the parameter level, selecting a sparse subset of existing weights (as few as 0.25% of total parameters) to update, so inference uses the identical forward pass with no reparameterization or additional modules.

## Method

GPS is a two-stage, parameter-free fine-tuning procedure. In the selection stage, it computes gradients of a Supervised Contrastive Loss (SCL, avoiding random head initialization) with respect to all pre-trained weights, then for each neuron selects the top-K input connections with the highest gradient magnitude, ensuring selected parameters are both task-important and distributed across all network depths. In the masked fine-tuning stage, a binary mask per weight matrix zeros out gradients of unselected parameters, so only the sparse subset is updated via standard cross-entropy optimization. The neuron-level granularity (rather than layer- or network-level) is critical: it lets every neuron adjust its activation state, and the approach is model-agnostic because it operates on individual neurons rather than architecture-specific modules. The paper further shows GPS is equivalent to sparse-regularized fine-tuning (L0 constraint on parameter deviation), which theoretically yields tighter generalization bounds than full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the FGVC benchmark (5 fine-grained classification tasks), GPS achieves 91.78% mean top-1 accuracy using only 0.77% trainable parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (88.54%, 100% params) by 3.24% and the previous SOTA SSF (90.72%) by 1.02%. On VTAB-1k (19 diverse visual tasks), GPS reaches 75.18% mean accuracy with just 0.25% trainable parameters, a 9.61% improvement over full fine-tuning (65.57%) and 1.11% over the prior best SPT-LoRA (74.07%). On medical polyp segmentation (Kvasir-seg) with SAM as backbone, GPS achieves 88.1% mDice and 72.5% mIoU, outperforming full fine-tuning (71.1%/55.7%) by 17% and 16.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPS outperforms all seven compared baselines on both FGVC and VTAB-1k. Against full fine-tuning, GPS gains 3.24% on FGVC and 9.61% on VTAB while tuning only 0.36% of parameters on average, demonstrating that selective parameter updates generalize better than updating all weights. Among addition-based PEFT methods, GPS beats SSF (90.72% → 91.78% on FGVC; 73.10% → 75.18% on VTAB), SPT-LoRA (90.04% → 91.78%; 74.07% → 75.18%), VPT-Deep (89.11% → 91.78%; 69.43% → 75.18%), LoRA (84.78% → 91.78%; 72.63% → 75.18%), and Adapter (85.66% → 91.78%; 55.82% → 75.18%). Among selection-based methods, GPS surpasses Bias fine-tuning (88.40% → 91.78% on FGVC; 62.05% → 75.18% on VTAB) and Linear probing (79.32% → 91.78%; 53.00% → 75.18%). On polyp segmentation with SAM, GPS (88.1% mDice) outperforms SAM-Adapter (84.8%) and SSF (87.3%), while using fewer parameters (4.22M vs. 4.26M for SSF). Notably, GPS also maintains its advantage across architectures, beating full fine-tuning on Swin-B (92.56 vs. 92.42) and ConvNeXt-B (93.32 vs. 93.04) where other PEFT methods like SSF underperform full fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPS departs from the dominant addition-based PEFT paradigm (Adapter, VPT, LoRA, SSF) that injects extra learnable parameters into the backbone, instead adopting a selection-based strategy that fine-tunes only a subset of the original model's weights. Conceptually, it draws on the sub-network/pruning literature (Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, SpotTune, Child-tuning) but repositions it for the PEFT setting by fixing a small task-specific subset and tuning only those parameters. The neuron-level gradient-based selection (top-K input connections per neuron) is a finer-grained criterion than the layer-level or network-level selection used in prior selection-based methods like BitFit or last-K-layers tuning, and the sparse-regularization equivalence (Eq. 3–4) connects the approach to the theoretical analysis of PEFT generalization bounds by Fu et al. [18].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-extra-parameter, model-agnostic design makes GPS immediately deployable across heterogeneous architectures (ViT, Swin, ConvNeXt, SAM) without architecture-specific engineering, which is a practical advantage over addition-based PEFT methods that require per-architecture insertion points. The finding that 0.36% of parameters can outperform full fine-tuning by up to 9.61% (VTAB) suggests that most pre-trained parameters are redundant for downstream adaptation, raising open questions about how this scales to much larger models (e.g., 1B+ parameter backbones) and whether the one-time gradient-selection pass remains tractable. A limitation is that the method requires a forward-backward pass on the target dataset before fine-tuning begins, and the optimal K (connections per neuron) must be tuned per task, which could be costly in multi-task or few-shot settings.
