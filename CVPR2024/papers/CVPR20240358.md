## Problem

Existing blind face restoration methods rely on capturing correlations between degraded and clean images, making them vulnerable to significant performance drops on unseen degradations. The paper addresses the gap of learning a degradation-unaware representation that allows a restoration network to generalize effectively to real-world, unknown degradation factors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB face image (resized to 512×512) as input and produces a restored high-quality face image of the same resolution. Degradations during training include Gaussian blur, downsampling (×0.8 to ×32), Gaussian noise, and JPEG compression, while test evaluations cover both synthetic degradations at ×16/×32/×64 and in-the-wild images from WIDER FACE, LFW, and WebPhoto-Test. The network operates entirely in a latent feature space, with the encoder producing preliminary features that are transformed before cross-attention and decoding.

## Method

PLTrans is an encoder-transformer-decoder architecture whose core contribution is transforming the query, key, and value vectors in a latent transformer to be degradation-robust. A latent diffusion-based regularization module (using a U-net for noise prediction with a standard DDPM schedule) iteratively denoises the encoder features into a degradation-unaware query, while injecting low-frequency components from the degraded features via 2D discrete wavelet transform at intermediate reverse-diffusion steps to preserve identity semantics. Separately, a prior-based key-value module quantizes the preliminary features against a latent dictionary learned via vector quantization from HQ face images, retrieves the top-d nearest neighbors, applies a learnable global mapping over them, and passes the result through a lightweight transformer to produce key and value. Cross-attention between the regularized query and the prior-based key/value is performed in each transformer block, and the output is decoded into the restored face. Training combines L1 and VGG perceptual losses with an adversarial objective, plus a KL divergence penalty on the latent variable to stabilize the diffusion process.

## Results

On CelebA-Test, PLTrans achieves the best scores across all four metrics (FID, LPIPS, PSNR, IDS) at every downsampling level (×16, ×32, ×64). At the most challenging ×64 setting, PLTrans obtains FID 39.58, LPIPS 0.4270, PSNR 19.36, and IDS 0.5264, outperforming the second-best methods by 5.54 in FID (vs. GPEN's 45.12) and 0.11 in PSNR (vs. DIL's 19.25). At ×16, PLTrans reaches FID 19.64 and IDS 0.7412, surpassing the next-best RestoreFormer (FID 24.32, IDS 0.7378). A user study on in-the-wild data (WIDER FACE, LFW, WebPhoto) further confirms PLTrans achieves the highest average human-rated score with the smallest variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLTrans outperforms all eight baselines on every metric at all three downsampling levels. Against the strongest GAN-prior methods, PLTrans beats GPEN by 5.54 FID at ×64 (39.58 vs. 45.12) and by 4.86 FID at ×32 (32.36 vs. 36.22), while also surpassing GPEN's IDS by 0.025 at ×64. Against the closest transformer-based competitor RestoreFormer, PLTrans improves FID by 4.70 at ×64 (39.58 vs. 46.28) and by 7.03 at ×32 (32.36 vs. 39.39), with a consistent IDS advantage of ~0.05. Among diffusion-based methods, PLTrans significantly outperforms DR2 (FID 39.58 vs. 49.64 at ×64) and ILVR (FID 39.58 vs. 107.83 at ×64), confirming that the task-specific latent diffusion module is more effective than applying a pre-trained DDPM in data space. Generic restoration methods (Restormer, DIL) achieve competitive PSNR but suffer severe identity loss (IDS 0.13–0.17 at ×64), whereas PLTrans maintains IDS of 0.5264. GFPGAN and Panini show the weakest overall performance, with GFPGAN's FID degrading to 159.24 at ×64.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLTrans sits at the intersection of three established lines: blind face restoration (BFR), vision transformers, and diffusion-based denoising. It departs from DR2, which applies a pre-trained DDPM in pixel space, by learning a task-specific latent diffusion module that regularizes encoder features into a degradation-unaware query. It also extends the latent dictionary idea from RestoreFormer: rather than using the dictionary to store clean face priors for direct lookup, PLTrans quantizes degraded features against the dictionary and learns a global linear mapping over the top-d nearest neighbors to construct key and value vectors. The DWT-based low-frequency injection during the reverse diffusion process is a novel combination that preserves input semantics while the diffusion removes degradation-specific high-frequency artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The degradation-unaware representation concept offers a principled way to improve generalization to unseen degradations, which is the central practical challenge in BFR. The paper validates this on in-the-wild datasets (WIDER FACE, LFW, WebPhoto) with a user study, strengthening the claim beyond synthetic benchmarks. However, the degradation model used for training (blur + downsample + Gaussian noise + JPEG) remains a simplified proxy for real-world corruption, and the latent diffusion module adds non-trivial computational overhead. Open questions include whether the latent-diffusion-as-query-regularizer idea transfers to non-face restoration tasks, and whether the dictionary-based key-value construction can be made more efficient at scale.
