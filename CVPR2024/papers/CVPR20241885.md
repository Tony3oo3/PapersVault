## Problem

While monocular two-hand reconstruction is well-studied, generating plausible and diverse two-hand interactions remains under-explored. Directly modeling the joint distribution of two articulated hands is intractable due to the combinatorial complexity of their coupled articulations, and existing generative methods for hand-object or two-human interaction do not transfer well to the higher-degree-of-freedom two-hand setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241885/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes Gaussian noise as input and produces 64-dimensional MANO parameters per hand (45-D pose, 10-D shape, 6-D root rotation, 3-D root translation), yielding two interacting 778-vertex hand meshes. For object-conditioned generation, an object point cloud is additionally fed through a PointNet++ branch. In the reconstruction application, the pretrained diffusion model is used as a frozen critic that regularizes a reconstruction network's predicted two-hand state, requiring no architectural modification to the baseline.

## Method

The core idea is to decompose the joint two-hand distribution p(x_l, x_r) = p(x_l)·p(x_r|x_l), reducing the combinatorial generation problem into two single-hand sub-problems. A single transformer-based diffusion network is trained via conditioning dropout to simultaneously model both the unconditional and conditional single-hand distributions, with left-right hand symmetry exploited through MANO mirroring to unify both hands in a shared right-hand parameter space. Inference proceeds as cascaded reverse diffusion: an anchor hand is sampled unconditionally, then the interacting hand is sampled conditionally using classifier-free guidance for fidelity-diversity control and an anti-penetration guidance term that penalizes inter-mesh penetration at each denoising step. The learned prior can be plugged into any optimization or learning pipeline as a regularization term (a single forward-reverse diffusion step) to reduce ambiguity in ill-posed reconstruction tasks.

## Results

On the InterHand2.6M two-hand interaction generation task, InterHandGen achieves FHID 1.00, KHID 0.15×10⁻², Diversity 3.59, Precision 0.86, Recall 0.85, and penetration volume 0.76 mm³, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the ARCTIC object-conditioned task, it reduces FHID from 22.56 (ContactGen*) to 12.91 and KHID from 1.58 to 0.55×10⁻¹. When used as a plug-and-play prior for monocular two-hand reconstruction (InterWild), it improves MRRPE by 10% on InterHand2.6M (29.29→26.56 mm) and 18% on HIC (31.35→26.63 mm), setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VAE prior from Zuo et al. [72], InterHandGen wins on every metric by a large margin: FHID drops from 8.18 to 1.00, Recall jumps from 0.02 to 0.85, and penetration volume decreases from 7.32 to 0.76 mm³. Against BUDDI* [43] (modified for two-hand generation), InterHandGen achieves 3.5× lower FHID (1.00 vs 3.48), 27× lower KHID (0.15 vs 4.10), and 1.8× higher Diversity (3.59 vs 2.71), while also matching its penetration volume (0.76 vs 0.82 mm³). On the object-conditioned ARCTIC task, ContactGen* [37] (modified for two-hand) performs poorly due to its contact-prior bias, yielding FHID 22.56 and Precision 0.21, whereas InterHandGen achieves FHID 12.91 and Precision 0.71. For the downstream reconstruction task, InterWild [40] without the prior achieves MRRPE 29.29 mm on InterHand2.6M and 31.35 mm on HIC; adding InterHandGen's diffusion prior as a regularizer reduces these to 26.56 mm and 26.63 mm respectively, confirming the prior's utility as a drop-in improvement without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework and classifier-free guidance with conditioning dropout, but its key conceptual departure is from BUDDI, which directly models the joint distribution of two interacting humans. The authors argue that direct joint modeling is suboptimal for two hands due to the combinatorial complexity of articulated hand shapes in close interaction, and instead decompose the joint distribution into an unconditional single-hand model and a conditional single-hand model learned via a shared network. This decomposition also distinguishes the work from hand-object interaction generation methods (e.g., ContactGen) that rely on physical contact priors, which do not transfer well to the more open-ended two-hand setting where contact is not guaranteed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first evaluation protocol and benchmark for two-hand interaction generation (FHID, KHID, diversity, precision-recall, penetration volume), filling a clear gap in the literature where only reconstruction methods existed. The plug-and-play diffusion prior that boosts monocular two-hand reconstruction by 10–18% in relative-root error without modifying the baseline architecture demonstrates practical utility beyond generation. Open questions include extending to the temporal dimension, training on heterogeneous datasets (single hand, hand-object, two-hand, two-hand-object) for a universal hand prior, and generalizing beyond hands to other articulated interaction domains.
