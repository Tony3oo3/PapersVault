## Problem

Naively enlarging 3D convolution kernels for LiDAR perception leads to cubically-increasing parameter and compute costs, making them impractical for resource-constrained autonomous systems. The prior 3D large-kernel method (LargeKernel3D) relies on Spatial-wise Group Convolution but retains redundant shared weights within each spatial group and degrades in performance beyond 7×7×7 kernels. The paper asks how to scale 3D kernels to 9×9×9 or larger while simultaneously reducing model size and FLOPs relative to a dense large-kernel baseline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes LiDAR point clouds voxelized at 5 cm resolution (SemanticKITTI) or 2 cm (ScanNet v2), processed exclusively with submanifold sparse convolution (no regular sparse convolution or aggressive downsampling) across four scales {2,4,8,16}. A parallel point-based branch preserves high-resolution features. For segmentation, output is a per-point class label (19 or 25 classes on SemanticKITTI, 20 or 200 on ScanNet); for detection on KITTI, the voxelized input has spatial shape {1600, 1408, 41} and the network outputs 3D bounding boxes evaluated at 11 recall positions.

## Method

LSK3DNet combines two complementary mechanisms applied to the large-kernel layers of a Modified SPVCNN backbone. Spatial-wise Dynamic Sparsity (SDS) initializes each spatial group of the 3D kernel with an Erdős-Rényi binary mask at a fixed sparsity (s=0.4), then every f_a=2000 iterations prunes the lowest-magnitude weights and regrows an equal number of random connections independently within each spatial group, maintaining constant sparsity throughout training. Channel-wise Weight Selection (CWS) expands the network width to 1.8×D during training, periodically sorts channels by L1 norm (every 6×f_a iterations), and at validation selects the top-D channels to recover the original model size. Together, SDS and CWS follow the principle of 'using spatial sparse groups, expanding width without more parameters,' achieving 70.2 mIoU on SemanticKITTI val with 9×9×9 kernels while reducing parameters by ~40% and FLOPs by ~60% compared to the naive dense 9×9×9 kernel.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI single-scan, LSK3DNet achieves 75.6% mIoU, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the previous best (SphereFormer, 74.8%) by 0.8 points, while running at 89 ms/scene. On the multi-scan track, it reaches 63.4% mIoU and 92.2% accuracy, outperforming 2DPASS (62.4% mIoU). On ScanNet v2, LSK3DNet scores 75.7% (val) / 75.5% (test) on ScanNet20 and 33.1% on ScanNet200, and on KITTI 3D detection it achieves 90.16/85.61/79.53 AP (Easy/Moderate/Hard). Compared to the naive dense 9×9×9 kernel baseline, LSK3DNet reduces model size by ~40% (28.8M vs. 47.9M params) and FLOPs by ~60% (763.6G vs. 1916.3G) while improving mIoU from 67.5% to 70.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSK3DNet outperforms all 40 compared baselines across the three datasets. On SemanticKITTI single-scan, it beats the closest competitor SphereFormer [24] by 0.8 mIoU (75.6 vs. 74.8) and 2DPASS [3] by 2.7 mIoU (75.6 vs. 72.9), while also being faster than 2DPASS (89 vs. 62 ms/s) and SphereFormer (89 vs. 123 ms/s). On the multi-scan track, LSK3DNet surpasses 2DPASS by 1.0 mIoU (63.4 vs. 62.4) and 0.8 Acc (92.2 vs. 91.4). On ScanNet v2, it edges out Point Transformer v2 [87] by 0.3 mIoU on val (75.7 vs. 75.4) and 0.3 on test (75.5 vs. 75.2), and clearly beats the prior 3D large-kernel method LargeKernel3D [18] by 2.2 mIoU on val (75.7 vs. 73.5). On KITTI detection, LSK3DNet improves over LargeKernel3D [18] by 0.64/0.54/0.21 AP on Easy/Moderate/Hard, and over its Voxel R-CNN [5] backbone by 0.75/1.09/0.60 AP respectively. Notably, LSK3DNet uses only 3D input (PV) yet surpasses 2DPASS which additionally leverages 2D image data.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSK3DNet extends the 3D large-kernel line initiated by LargeKernel3D (Chen et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced Spatial-wise Group Convolution but suffered from redundant shared weights and performance degradation beyond 7×7×7. The paper adapts the dynamic sparse training paradigm from 2D SLaK (Liu et al., 2022) to 3D spatial groups rather than depth-wise groups, and introduces Channel-wise Weight Selection to decouple sparsity from model width—a key departure from SLaK's trade-off between the two. The overall architecture builds on Modified SPVCNN (2DPASS, Yan et al., ECCV 2022) for segmentation and Voxel R-CNN (Deng et al., AAAI 2021) for detection, replacing their standard sparse convolution blocks with the proposed LSK Block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling large-receptive-field 3D perception on resource-constrained autonomous driving platforms: LSK3DNet achieves SOTA on SemanticKITTI while reducing model size by ~40% and FLOPs by ~60% relative to a naive 9×9×9 dense kernel, making large-kernel 3D CNNs deployable where they previously were not. An open question is whether the specific SDS hyperparameters (sparsity 0.4, prune rate 0.3, adaptation frequency 2000) transfer across architectures and kernel sizes, and whether the approach scales beyond 11×11×11 without the diminishing returns observed in the ablations. The method remains tied to submanifold sparse convolution and voxelized inputs, so its applicability to purely point-based or transformer-based 3D backbones is unexplored.
