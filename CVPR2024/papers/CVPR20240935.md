## Problem

Existing text-to-3D avatar generation methods are limited by their 3D representations: mesh-based approaches lack geometric expressiveness, NeRF-based methods are too slow for high-resolution interactive rendering, and naive Gaussian splatting fails for avatars due to animation incoherence, training instability over millions of unconstrained Gaussians, and the inability to capture fine surface geometry or extract usable meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240935/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar (up to 2.5M Gaussians organized into 4096 pose-driven primitives on an SMPL-X body) along with a textured 3D mesh extracted via DMTet. Renderings are produced at 1024×1024 resolution at 100 fps, with the Gaussian splatting pass itself taking only ~3 ms. The avatar supports arbitrary pose animation through SMPL-X parameters, and the extracted mesh carries a texture map derived from the learned Gaussian color field.

## Method

GAAvatar introduces a primitive-based implicit Gaussian representation where 3D Gaussians are defined in the local coordinate system of pose-driven primitives attached to an SMPL-X mesh, so that deforming the primitives coherently animates all associated Gaussians. To stabilize optimization of millions of Gaussians under the high-variance SDS objective, a neural implicit attribute field H_φ predicts each Gaussian's color, rotation, and scaling from its canonical rest-pose position, amortizing learning across spatially nearby Gaussians. A separate SDF-based opacity field O_ψ = K ∘ S_ψ parametrizes Gaussian opacity through a bell-shaped kernel applied to the signed distance to the surface, which both regularizes geometry (via Eikonal and alpha losses) and enables differentiable mesh extraction through DMTet. Training jointly optimizes Gaussian local positions, the attribute field, the SDF, primitive corrective networks, and SMPL-X shape parameters using a composite loss combining SDS, position regularization, Eikonal, alpha matching, normal SDS, and normal consistency terms.

## Results

The primary quantitative evaluation is an A/B user study with 42 participants and 1,512 responses across 24 prompts, measuring preference for geometry quality, appearance quality, and prompt consistency. GAvatar achieves 92.86–100% preference over all six baselines, with the strongest margins against DreamGaussian (100% geometry preference) and AvatarCLIP (98.81% geometry), and the smallest against TADA (61.90% geometry, 69.05% appearance). In terms of rendering efficiency, a generated avatar with 2.5 million Gaussians renders at 100 fps at 1024×1024 resolution, with the Gaussian splatting step alone taking ~3 ms (300+ fps), substantially faster than NeRF-based approaches that query neural fields per view.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAavatar wins decisively against all six baselines across every metric. Against DreamGaussian [40], the closest Gaussian-splatting-based competitor, GAvatar achieves 100% geometry preference and 98.81% on appearance and consistency, with the paper attributing the gap to DreamGaussian's lack of SDF-based geometry regularization and its noisier mesh extraction (Fig. 6). Against AvatarCLIP [9] and AvatarCraft [15], both NeRF-based methods, GAvatar leads by 96–100% on all three criteria, benefiting from higher-resolution (1024×1024 vs. 64×64 for DreamHuman) SDS optimization enabled by fast Gaussian rendering. Fantasia3D [4] is the strongest NeRF-based baseline, yet GAvatar still leads by 92.86% on geometry and appearance, with the paper noting Fantasia3D's inability to model complex avatars without degenerate geometry. The two contemporary mesh-based methods, DreamHuman [18] and TADA [22], show the largest gaps (61.90–73.81% preference), with DreamHuman limited by its 64×64 training resolution and TADA producing smoother but less detailed geometry with occasional degenerate shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAavatar extends the primitive-based volumetric representation from MVP [25] by replacing fixed cubic primitives with variable sets of 3D Gaussians, and builds its rendering pipeline on 3D Gaussian Splatting [17]. For optimization, it inherits the SDS objective from DreamFusion [29] and the SMPL-X body prior used by DreamAvatar [3] and DreamHumans [18], but departs from all prior avatar methods by replacing NeRF or mesh representations with a Gaussian-based one. The key conceptual departure from DreamGaussian [40], which first applied Gaussian Splatting to text-to-3D, is the introduction of pose-driven primitives for animation, neural implicit attribute fields for training stability, and SDF-based geometry learning for mesh extraction—three components that collectively address the animation, optimization, and surface-representation failures of naive Gaussian Splatting for articulated bodies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100 fps rendering at 1K resolution (vs. 64×64 for DreamHumans) and the ability to extract high-quality textured meshes from learned Gaussians make this approach practically relevant for interactive applications like VR, gaming, and synthetic data generation. The SDF-based opacity parametrization is a clean bridge between Gaussian splatting and traditional surface representations, potentially enabling hybrid pipelines that combine fast Gaussian rendering with mesh-based downstream tools. Open questions include whether the SDS-based color oversaturation and geometry-appearance misalignment can be resolved without sacrificing the speed advantage, and whether loose clothing animation can be handled without explicit temporal or physics supervision.
