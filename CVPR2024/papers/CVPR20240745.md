## Problem

Existing DNN pruning methods require complex multi-stage pipelines (pre-training, redundancy identification, fine-tuning) that demand substantial engineering expertise. While OTO and OTOv2 eliminate the fine-tuning step, their static pruning-group selection—fixed early in training—traps optimization in local optima and degrades final accuracy. This paper addresses how to perform fully automatic, end-to-end structural pruning from scratch with dynamic group selection and no post-training fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrained target model architecture (e.g., ResNet-18/34/50/56, MobileNetV2) and a standard image classification dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) as input. A small 5% subset of the training data is reserved exclusively for the controller network. The output is a directly deployable compressed model whose FLOPs are controlled by a target fraction hyperparameter p; no fine-tuning or retraining is needed after the single training run, and the final model simply has masked-out ZIGs removed.

## Method

ATO trains a target network and a lightweight controller network (bi-directional GRU + Gumbel-Sigmoid with straight-through estimator) jointly in an end-to-end fashion. The controller generates a binary mask over Zero-Invariant Groups (ZIGs), and group-specific regularization coefficients λ_g = λ(1−w_g) are applied during training so that masked-out groups are progressively penalized toward zero. The controller is trained alternately with the target model using a small data subset and an FLOPs-regularized loss, dynamically adjusting which groups to prune as weights evolve—avoiding the static-group limitation of OTO/OTOv2. The optimization is framed as a stochastic mirror descent problem, yielding a convergence guarantee (O(ε⁻⁴) complexity) for both non-adaptive (SGD) and adaptive (Adam) optimizers. After training, the controller is frozen and masked ZIGs are simply deleted, producing the final compressed model in one shot.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, ATO achieves 76.59% Top-1 accuracy (+0.46% over baseline) at 55.2% pruned FLOPs, the only method to improve over the unpruned model at that compression level. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-56, ATO reaches 93.74% pruned accuracy (+0.24% Δ-Acc) at 55.0% FLOPs, outperforming the second-best GNN-RL by 0.14%. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-34, ATO achieves 78.54% pruned accuracy (+0.11%) at 49.5% FLOPs, surpassing OTOv2 (76.31%) by 2.23 percentage points at identical FLOPs. On MobileNet-V2 ImageNet at 30.1% FLOPs, ATO is the only method to exceed the baseline (72.02% vs. 71.88%), while all other baselines degrade by 0.8–1.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATO outperforms OTOv2 across all shared settings: on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, ATO gains +0.10% Δ-Acc vs. OTOv2's −0.16% at nearly identical FLOPs (79.8% vs. 79.7%); on CIFAR-100 ResNet-34, ATO achieves 78.54% vs. OTOv2's 76.31% (a 2.23-point gap) at the same 49.5% FLOPs; and on ImageNet ResNet-50 at 61.7% FLOPs, ATO reaches 76.07% Top-1 vs. OTOv2's 75.20% (+0.87%). Against traditional multi-stage pruning methods (FPGM, DMC, SCOP, DCP, CCP, ABCP, DTP, Taylor), ATO consistently achieves the smallest accuracy drop or even a gain at comparable FLOPs on ImageNet ResNet-34 and ResNet-50. On MobileNet-V2 ImageNet at ~30% FLOPs, ATO is the only method to exceed the unpruned baseline (72.02% vs. 71.88%), while Uniform, AMC, CC, MetaPruning, and Random-Pruning all degrade by 0.8–2.0%. On CIFAR-10 ResNet-56, ATO (+0.24% Δ-Acc at 55.0% FLOPs) beats GNN-RL (+0.10% at 54.0%) and all other baselines (DCP-Adapt +0.01%, DMC +0.07%, SCP −0.46%, FPGM −0.66%, SFP −1.33%). The key advantage is that ATO requires no fine-tuning stage, matching or exceeding methods that rely on post-pruning retraining while using a simpler single-stage training pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the OTO/OTOv2 line of work, which introduced Zero-Invariant Groups (ZIGs) and the train-and-prune-from-scratch paradigm that eliminates the need for a separate fine-tuning stage. The key departure is replacing the static, pre-determined pruning group selection of OTO/OTOv2 with a lightweight GRU-based controller network that dynamically generates binary masks during training, and showing that this dynamic selection makes the specific projection operator (half-space vs. proximal gradient) largely irrelevant. The convergence analysis also departs from OTO's strong convexity assumption and OTOv2's full-gradient assumption, providing guarantees under standard stochastic optimization conditions for both SGD and Adam.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a genuinely simpler pruning pipeline: one training run, no fine-tuning, no manual selection of pruning groups, and minimal overhead (5% of training data for the controller, <5% extra compute). This lowers the barrier to adopting structural pruning for practitioners who lack the expertise to tune multi-stage pruning pipelines. Limitations include evaluation only on CNN architectures (ResNet, MobileNetV2) with no transformer or non-vision models, and the controller network's scalability to very large models (e.g., ViT, LLMs) is untested. An open question is whether the dynamic mask selection idea transfers to other compression tasks (quantization, low-rank factorization) or to architectures where ZIGs are less naturally defined.
