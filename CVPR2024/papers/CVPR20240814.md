## Problem

Pretrained text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion are trained at a single fixed resolution (512×512) and degrade severely at other sizes or aspect ratios, producing repeated textures, structural incoherence, or outright noise. Existing remedies either require expensive fine-tuning on multiple resolutions (SDXL, Any-Size-Diffusion) or rely on overlapping patches that work well for repetitive scenes but fail on single-object or face images where global structure matters. The paper seeks a training-free decoding strategy that generates coherent images at arbitrary sizes with a constant memory footprint.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a target image size (arbitrary H×W, including both larger and smaller than the model's 512×512 training resolution) and produces a single generated image at that exact resolution. All diffusion forward passes operate at the model's native 512×512 latent size, so peak memory stays constant (~8.6 GB) regardless of output dimensions; the number of forward calls per decoding step scales with the number of spatial patches (e.g., 9 calls for 1024×1024 vs. 1 for 512×512). The method supports arbitrary aspect ratios (9:16, 16:9, 3:4, 4:3, etc.) and can extend up to roughly 4× the training resolution before quality degrades.

## Method

ElasticDiffusion reinterprets classifier-free guidance by decomposing it into a global signal (the class-direction score Δc, which clusters along semantic regions and controls overall composition) and a local signal (the unconditional score, which lacks inter-pixel correlation and governs fine detail). The class-direction score is computed at the model's native resolution on a downsampled, padded reference latent, then upsampled to the target size; the unconditional score is estimated on non-overlapping local patches that are concatenated with context pixels from adjacent patches (implicit overlap), avoiding the expensive explicit-overlap averaging used by MultiDiffusion. Three auxiliary techniques refine the result: (1) iterative resampling that progressively replaces a fraction of reference-latent pixels and blends the recalculated class-direction score to increase its effective resolution, (2) Reduced-Resolution Guidance (RRG), which at each DDIM step nudges the high-resolution latent toward its downsampled counterpart's noise-free estimate to suppress artifacts, and (3) dynamic padding with a random constant-color background to keep all U-Net inputs at the trained 512×512 size. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates entirely within the pretrained model's latent space.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ at 1024×1024, ElasticDiffusion achieves FID 228.87 and CLIP 23.74, slightly outperforming SDXL (FID 230.21, CLIP 24.62) while using only 8.6 GB of memory versus SDXL's 27.5 GB. At 256×256, ElasticDiffusion dramatically improves over both SD_1.4 (FID 235.23 vs 258.43) and SDXL (FID 235.23 vs 368.06), demonstrating that the method re-enables quality generation below the training resolution. On LAION-COCO at 1024×1024, ElasticDiffusion achieves FID 27.76 and CLIP 26.07, competitive with SDXL (FID 25.58, CLIP 28.06) while requiring only ~31% of the memory. The method uses a constant 8.6 GB memory footprint across all resolutions, compared to SDXL's 18.5–27.5 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD_1.4 (the base model), ElasticDiffusion substantially improves FID at all non-native resolutions: 235.23 vs 258.43 at 256×256, 225.86 vs 238.87 at 768×768, and 228.87 vs 266.01 at 1024×1024 on CelebA-HQ, while maintaining comparable CLIP scores. Against MultiDiffusion, ElasticDiffusion achieves lower FID at 768×768 (225.86 vs 240.56) and 1024×1024 (228.87 vs 264.57) on CelebA-HQ, while requiring far fewer forward calls (17 vs 50, and 33 vs 162 per decoding step). Against SDXL, ElasticDiffusion is competitive at 1024×1024 (FID 228.87 vs 230.21 on CelebA-HQ) but uses only 8.6 GB vs 27.5 GB of memory; at 256×256, ElasticDiffusion dramatically outperforms SDXL (FID 235.23 vs 368.06), showing that SDXL degrades severely below its training resolution. On LAION-COCO at 1024×1024, SDXL retains a slight edge on both FID (25.58 vs 27.76) and CLIP (28.06 vs 26.07), but ElasticDiffusion narrows the gap to within ~2 FID points despite using a smaller base model and no fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ElasticDiffusion reinterprets classifier-free guidance [18] by empirically showing (via PCA) that the class-direction score encodes global semantic structure while the unconditional score captures local pixel-level detail, enabling their decoupled computation at different spatial scales. For local content, it extends the patch-based score estimation of MultiDiffusion [3] and StableSR [53] with an implicit-overlap strategy that replaces costly explicit patch averaging with context-pixel concatenation. For global content, it introduces an iterative resampling technique adapted from Repaint [29] to progressively refine the class-direction score resolution, plus a novel Reduced-Resolution Guidance term that aligns the high-res latent with its downsampled counterpart to suppress artifacts. Unlike SDXL [35] and Any-Size-Diffusion [62], which require expensive fine-tuning on fixed resolution sets, ElasticDiffusion operates entirely training-free with a constant memory footprint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, constant-memory approach to arbitrary-resolution generation is practically significant for deployment scenarios (billboards, automotive displays, mobile) where retraining or large memory budgets are infeasible. The global-local content separation insight opens a broader research direction: selectively manipulating global composition versus local detail, improving style transfer, or leveraging the rich semantic structure in class-direction scores for discriminative tasks. Key limitations remain: quality degrades beyond ~4× the training resolution, RRG introduces slight blurring, and the method is less effective for single-object images at extreme aspect ratios. An open question is whether the global-local separation can be made more principled or extended to video and 3D generation.
