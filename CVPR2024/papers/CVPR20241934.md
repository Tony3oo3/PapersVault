## Problem

Creating ground-truth alpha mattes for video matting datasets requires laborious manual annotation, constrained lighting setups, or chroma keying with extensive post-processing, making large-scale dataset creation impractical. Prior polarization-based matting (PTM) assumes all foreground-reflected light is unpolarized, producing erroneous fractional alpha values when the foreground reflects polarized light with a phase angle comparable to the backlight. PolarMatte addresses both gaps by providing a fully computational, single-camera method that explicitly handles polarized foreground reflections.

## Input / Output

The method takes four polarization-state images (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) of the target scene captured in a single shot by a CPFA camera, plus pre-captured polarization images of the polarized LCD background (captured 30 times to estimate mean and covariance for the Bayesian model). It outputs a per-pixel alpha matte and foreground color that can be directly composited onto arbitrary backgrounds without any manual correction. The setup requires only a single polarization camera and an off-the-shelf LCD monitor, operating at 30 fps for video.

## Method

PolarMatte formulates matting in the polarization channel via a polarimetric compositing equation that decomposes foreground and background into unpolarized and polarized components with phase angles, then converts four polarization images into Stokes parameters to obtain three compositing equations (s₀, s₁, s₂). Well-posed pixels are detected by minimizing polarized foreground intensity via a Bayesian MAP estimator (accounting for background drift) combined with phase-angle thresholding, yielding high-confidence alpha values. The complete matte is then extracted by solving a total optimization that combines a novel polarimetric matting Laplacian (built from Stokes parameter vectors instead of RGB intensities in the local linear model), hard constraints on well-posed pixels, and a weak least-squares supervision term. A PolarIC step applies a per-pixel linear correction to enforce the physical constraint I₀ − I₄₅ + I₉₀ − I₁₃₅ = 0 before all downstream processing.

## Results

On 12 static scene test data (fur, solid/translucent objects, optical blur), PolarMatte achieves MAE 7.65, MSE 0.28, and GRAD 0.72, outperforming all baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, PTM, scores MAE 8.38, MSE 0.33, and GRAD 1.03, while BGM and Keylight are substantially worse (MAE 137.75 and 84.45 respectively). The GRAD metric shows the largest relative improvement over PTM (0.72 vs 1.03, a 30% reduction), reflecting better edge fidelity from the polarimetric matting Laplacian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolarMatte outperforms PTM [15] on all three metrics (MAE 7.65 vs 8.38, MSE 0.28 vs 0.33, GRAD 0.72 vs 1.03), with the gap primarily attributable to PTM's failure to handle polarized foreground reflections that produce erroneously fractional alpha values in solid regions. Against BGM [13], PolarMatte is dramatically superior (MAE 7.65 vs 137.75, MSE 0.28 vs 110.43, GRAD 0.72 vs 21.96), as BGM operates on ordinary color images where matting is heavily ill-posed per pixel, especially for translucent objects and fine details. Keylight (Adobe After Effects chroma keying) also loses substantially (MAE 84.45, GRAD 6.13), struggling with fractional alpha values at object boundaries without manual post-processing. The paper additionally shows that replacing the polarimetric matting Laplacian with the original matting Laplacian degrades performance to MAE 9.89 and GRAD 2.22, confirming that polarization clues in the Laplacian are critical for the observed gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarMatte extends polarimetric triangulation matting (PTM) [15], which formulated matting in the polarization channel but assumed all foreground reflections are unpolarized. The key departure is explicitly modeling polarized foreground reflections via a four-polarization-state CPFA camera and a polarimetric compositing equation, then solving the resulting ill-posed problem with a well-posed pixel detection step and a polarimetric matting Laplacian adapted from Levin et al.'s [10] closed-form matting. The method also introduces PolarIC, a pixel-wise linear correction to enforce the Stokes-parameter constraint violated by real camera sensors, and a Bayesian MAP framework to robustly handle background drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the primary bottleneck in video matting research: the labor-intensive creation of ground-truth alpha mattes. By enabling fully computational extraction of high-quality mattes for dynamic scenes (including fine details, translucency, and motion blur) without manual post-processing, PolarMatte provides a practical pathway to large-scale matting datasets. Limitations include the requirement for a polarization camera and LCD backlight, tens-of-seconds processing time per frame, and failure when foreground reflections closely match the polarized backlight. Open questions include whether the approach can be accelerated for real-time use and whether it generalizes to capture setups beyond a single LCD monitor.
