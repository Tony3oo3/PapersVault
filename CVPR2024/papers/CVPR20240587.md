## Problem

Existing egocentric 3D human pose estimation methods rely on synchronously operating RGB cameras that suffer from motion blur, over/under-exposure, high power consumption, and constant data throughput demands—limitations that are particularly restrictive for head-mounted devices. No prior work has addressed 3D human motion capture from an egocentric monocular event camera with a fisheye lens, a configuration that introduces additional challenges including lightweight real-time processing and substantial background events generated by camera motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240587/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N=20 consecutive LNES frames of size 192×256×2, where each frame aggregates asynchronous per-pixel brightness-change events (from a DVXplorer Mini with a 190° fisheye lens) over a 15 ms time window into a compact 2D representation. It outputs device-centric 3D coordinates for 16 body joints (head, neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, feet), intermediate 2D joint heatmaps at 48×64×16, and a human body segmentation mask at 48×64×1, achieving a 140 Hz pose update rate on a 4 GB laptop GPU.

## Method

EventEgo3D comprises two modules: an Egocentric Pose Module (EPM) that estimates 2D joint heatmaps via a U-Net with Blaze blocks and then lifts them to 3D through a six-layer heatmap-to-3D network, and a Residual Event Propagation Module (REPM) that addresses the dominant challenge of background events in egocentric settings. REPM uses a segmentation decoder to produce a human body mask, a confidence decoder to generate spatially varying importance weights, and a frame buffer that element-wise multiplies the previous LNES frame by the prior confidence map before adding it to the current frame—thereby amplifying human-triggered events and retaining pose information when motion is minimal. The network is trained end-to-end with a weighted combination of joint MSE, heatmap MSE, and segmentation cross-entropy losses, first pre-trained on the 946-sequence synthetic EE3D-S dataset (6.21M poses rendered at 480 fps and converted to events via VID2E) and then fine-tuned on the 12-subject real EE3D-R dataset (4.64M poses captured with a 29-camera motion capture studio).

## Results

On the EE3D-R real-world dataset, EventEgo3D achieves a mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) of 107.30 mm and a Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE) of 79.66 mm, outperforming the closest competitor Rudnev et al. (114.52 / 84.87 mm) by 6.3% and 6.1% respectively. The method also achieves the lowest standard deviation of 3D errors (σ = 25.78 mm for MPJPE), indicating consistent accuracy across diverse motion activities. In terms of efficiency, EventEgo3D uses only 1.25M parameters and 416.84M FLOPs while sustaining a 140 Hz pose update rate on a laptop GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventEgo3D outperforms all three adapted baselines on both MPJPE and PA-MPJPE across the EE3D-R dataset. Against the closest competitor, Rudnev et al. (EventHands adapted for full-body pose), EventEgo3D improves MPJPE by 6.3% (107.30 vs. 114.52 mm) and PA-MPJPE by 6.1% (79.66 vs. 84.87 mm), with the gap widening on challenging motions like crawling (109.71 vs. 120.51 mm) and dancing (88.87 vs. 98.14 mm). Against Xu et al. (Mo2Cap2, an RGB-based egocentric method adapted to LNES input), EventEgo3D achieves a 19.6% MPJPE reduction (107.30 vs. 133.53 mm), with particularly large gains on crouching (163.84 vs. 153.53 mm) and crawling (109.71 vs. 187.95 mm). Against Tome et al. (XR-EgoPose, another RGB-based method), the improvement is 38.0% on MPJPE (107.30 vs. 173.01 mm). The paper attributes the advantage to the Residual Event Propagation Module, which highlights human-triggered events over background noise generated by head-mounted camera motion — a failure mode that visibly degrades all three baselines in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a genuinely new problem space by combining two previously separate threads: egocentric 3D human pose estimation (dominated by RGB fisheye camera methods like Xu et al. and Tome et al.) and event-based 3D reconstruction (exemplified by EventHands for hands). It adopts the LNES event representation from Rudnev et al. [27] and the two-stage heatmap-to-3D lifting pipeline from Pavlakos et al. [22], but departs from both by introducing the Residual Event Propagation Module (REPM) to handle the unique challenge of massive background events generated by a moving head-mounted camera. The authors explicitly note that no prior work addresses the egocentric event-camera configuration, and that RGB-based methods cannot be straightforwardly repurposed for asynchronous event streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end system for real-time 3D human pose from an egocentric event camera, and it ships with both a synthetic (946 sequences, 6.2M poses) and a real (12 subjects, 155 min) dataset that the field previously lacked. The 1.25M-parameter model achieving 140 Hz pose updates on a laptop GPU is practically significant for mobile HMD deployment. However, the real dataset is small in subject diversity and duration, the baselines are adapted rather than purpose-built event-based human pose methods, and generalization to multi-person scenes, fully unstructured outdoor environments, or longer sequences remains untested. The broader question of whether event cameras can replace RGB in egocentric 3D vision more generally is left open.
