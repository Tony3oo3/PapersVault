## Problem

Existing aerial object detection datasets are small-scale, low-resolution, and geographically concentrated in Asia and North America, while models trained on ground-view data or aerial data from one region fail to generalize to aerial imagery from different geographies due to differences in scene color content, object density, and solar conditions. The paper asks whether synchronizing ground and aerial views from the same geographic location can provide the contextual information needed to improve aerial visual perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242010/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 2.7K resolution (2700×1520) RGB video frames captured simultaneously by a drone-mounted camera (25–45 m altitude) and a tripod-mounted ground camera, with ~50 annotated bounding boxes per frame across 10 categories. In the semi-supervised setting, the model consumes both labeled frames (first 30 s of each synchronized clip) and unlabeled frames (the remainder), with the aerial view containing predominantly small objects (<32×32 px) due to the higher vantage point, while the ground view includes large objects and occluded instances recoverable from the wider aerial field of view.

## Method

The core contribution is a curriculum-based semi-supervised object detection pipeline built on a D-DETR teacher-student framework (Omni-DETR). The key insight is that pre-training the teacher on ground-view images from the same geography before aerial images induces geography-aware representations that substantially improve aerial detection—outperforming models pre-trained on MS-COCO or VisDrone by up to 270% relative improvement with only ~2K ground-view samples. During the burn-in stage, the teacher is trained sequentially on m labeled ground-view images followed by n labeled aerial images, ordered by a difficulty scoring function (average proposal confidence). In the semi-supervised stage, the teacher generates pseudo-labels on weakly augmented unlabeled aerial images, which supervise the student on strongly augmented counterparts via weak-to-strong consistency regularization, with the student also following a ground-then-aerial ordering for unlabeled data.

## Results

On the MAVREC aerial test set, D-DETR fine-tuned with MAVREC ground-view pre-training achieves 44.8 mAP (71.5 AP50, 42.9 AP_S, 72.4 AP_M), substantially outperforming the same model fine-tuned on MS-COCO (33.2 mAP) or VisDrone (20.9 mAP), and the open-world Grounding DINO (20.4 mAP). In the semi-supervised setting (Table 4), the proposed curriculum-based approach with 2,151 labeled ground + 8,605 labeled aerial images plus 8,605 unlabeled aerial images reaches 26.7 mAP on the aerial test set, improving over the aerial-only semi-supervised baseline (19.8 mAP) by 6.9 points. Supervised benchmarks (Table 2) show YoloV7 leads among from-scratch detectors on aerial images (31.9 mAP test), while MSCOCO-pretrained D-DETR dominates on ground view (58.6 mAP test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among from-scratch supervised detectors on the aerial test set, YoloV7 achieves the best mAP (31.9) over DETR (23.6), D-DETR (10.3), and Yolo-NAS (27.0), confirming that CNN-based architectures generalize better to aerial views without large-scale pre-training. However, when MSCOCO pre-training is applied, D-DETR* jumps to 33.2 mAP on aerial, surpassing all from-scratch CNN detectors. The paper's key comparison in Table 3 shows that D-DETR fine-tuned on geography-matched MAVREC ground-view images (44.8 mAP) outperforms the same model fine-tuned on MS-COCO (33.2 mAP) by 11.6 points, and outperforms VisDrone pre-training (20.9 mAP) by 23.9 points, demonstrating that geographic context matters more than dataset scale for aerial detection. Grounding DINO, despite being pre-trained on massive open-world corpora (O365, GoldG, Cap4M), achieves only 20.4 mAP on MAVREC aerial, underperforming even VisDrone pre-training and confirming a strong ground-view bias in open-world foundational models. In the semi-supervised setting, the curriculum-based approach (26.7 mAP test) beats the aerial-only semi-supervised baseline (19.8 mAP) by 6.9 points, and adding unlabeled ground images during the student phase yields further gains, reinforcing the geography-aware pre-training hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aerial object detection benchmarking line (VisDrone, DOTA, UAVDT, UAV123) by introducing the first large-scale, time-synchronized multiview (ground + aerial) drone dataset collected in a European geography, addressing the geographic and viewpoint homogeneity of prior UAV datasets. Methodologically, it builds on the Omni-DETR teacher-student semi-supervised framework and standard detectors (D-DETR, YoloV7, Yolo-NAS), but departs from the prevailing assumption that large-scale pre-training on diverse datasets (MS-COCO, VisDrone) is the optimal strategy for aerial detection, instead proposing a curriculum-based ground-to-aerial pre-training that learns geography-aware representations. The work also connects to the broader domain-adaptation and transfer-learning literature by showing that geographic context (color distribution, solar angle, population density) is a first-order factor in aerial perception that prior aerial detection work has largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAVREC fills a critical gap as the largest synchronized ground-and-aerial video dataset, enabling research on viewpoint transfer, occlusion recovery, and multiview fusion that was previously infeasible. The empirical finding that a small subset of geography-matched ground images outperforms large-scale pre-training on MS-COCO or VisDrone for aerial detection is practically actionable and challenges the 'bigger pre-training corpus is always better' heuristic in aerial vision. However, the evidence is confined to a single European geography and 10 object categories, leaving open whether the curriculum strategy generalizes across continents, seasons, and object taxonomies. The semi-supervised results also suggest a path toward reducing the heavy annotation burden in aerial detection, though the absolute mAP values remain modest, indicating substantial room for improvement.
