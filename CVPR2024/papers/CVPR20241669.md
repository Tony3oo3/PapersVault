## Problem

While prior work can estimate body composition quantities or individual structures (e.g., bones via OSSO) from the body surface, no method predicts the full 3D volumetric distribution of multiple internal tissue types (long bones, lean tissue, subcutaneous fat) simultaneously from surface observations alone. This is a novel problem complicated by the need for paired inside/outside data, the fact that MRI requires a prone position causing soft-tissue deformation not modeled by SMPL, and the challenge of designing a network that generalizes across subjects and poses.

## Input / Output

HIT takes SMPL shape parameters (β ∈ ℝ¹⁰), pose parameters (θ ∈ ℝ⁶⁹), and an arbitrary 3D query point x as input, and outputs a discrete tissue class (Bone Tissue, Lean Tissue, Subcutaneous Adipose Tissue, or Empty) at that point. Training data consists of 442 full-body 1.5T MRI scans (~110 slices, 256×192, ~2×2×10 mm voxels) segmented into five tissue classes, with SMPL meshes and tight-fits registered to the extracted body surface. At inference, the implicit function can be queried at arbitrary 3D locations to produce smooth volumetric tissue maps, and the predicted tissues can be reposed to any SMPL pose by bypassing the compression module.

## Method

HIT formulates internal tissue prediction as a multi-tissue classification problem in a canonical SMPL space, achieved by warping each query point through three learned modules: a Decompression module (MLP C) that predicts volumetric displacement to undo MRI-table compression, an Unposing module (MLPs B and W) that inverts linear blend skinning using learned per-point skinning weights, and a Deshaping module (MLP B) that removes shape offsets. The key insight is learning the compression field in the shaped space rather than the MRI space, since the shaped space provides natural shape consistency across subjects. A final MLP then classifies the canonical-space point into one of four tissue classes. Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training B and W on synthetic SMPL bodies, pre-training C on computed surface compression displacements, then jointly fine-tuning all networks with a weighted cross-entropy loss on the HIT dataset, using voxel-center sampling to avoid aliasing.

## Results

On the HIT dataset (442 full-body MRI scans), HIT achieves Dice scores of 77.8/57.7/45.5 (LT/SAT/BT) for females and 81.0/54.7/52.2 for males, substantially outperforming the Chance baseline (51.4/40.2/3.9 and 60.3/31.8/4.1 respectively). However, on the relative volume error metric (Δ%), HIT is on par with or slightly worse than Chance (e.g., 9.4% vs 6.7% for female SAT), indicating that HIT predicts average tissue quantities rather than individual-specific volumes. The paper also reports that HIT outperforms OSSO on bone prediction in terms of mean absolute error (Sup. Mat. 5.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's only direct baseline is a Chance predictor that assigns fixed tissue probabilities (e.g., 0.52 LT, 0.41 SAT, 0.04 BT for females) based on average training-set tissue percentages. HIT wins decisively on Dice scores across all six tissue-sex combinations: for example, female BT improves from 3.9 to 45.5, and male LT from 60.3 to 81.0. On the volume-accuracy metric (Δ%), however, HIT ties or slightly underperforms Chance (e.g., 9.4% vs 6.7% for female SAT), revealing that the model captures spatial structure well but predicts near-average tissue volumes, likely because 10 SMPL shape parameters cannot encode all individual body-composition variation. The authors also report that HIT outperforms OSSO [32] on bone prediction (mean absolute error between segmented and predicted bones), though this comparison is deferred to supplementary material. No other prior method directly addresses the full multi-tissue (LT + SAT + BT) prediction task, so no head-to-head comparison with a competing learned model is possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIT builds on the SMPL parametric body model for shape/pose parameterization and on gDNA's canonical-space warping via learned skinning weights, but repurposes this implicit-representation machinery for an entirely new target: internal tissue classification rather than surface occupancy. It departs from prior anatomy-inference work (OSSO for bones, Anatomy Transfer for organ deformation, SKEL for biomechanical skeletons) by predicting the full three-layer tissue composition (bone, lean, subcutaneous fat) as a multi-class volumetric problem, and from body-composition estimation work (which predicts scalar quantities from silhouettes or 3D scans) by providing spatially-resolved 3D tissue maps. The learned volumetric decompression field addressing MRI-table compression is a novel module not present in any prior implicit body model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to produce a 3D volumetric tissue map from surface observations alone, opening a path toward non-invasive anatomical digital twins for medicine, biomechanics, and graphics. The released dataset of 442 segmented full-body MRI scans paired with SMPL fits is a significant resource that was previously unavailable. Key limitations remain: the model predicts average tissue volumes rather than individual-specific ones (constrained by 10 SMPL shape parameters), internal decompression cannot be validated without additional data, and IMVAT localization is deferred. The privacy implication—that a single image could reveal health-risk-relevant body composition—raises important ethical questions for future deployment.
