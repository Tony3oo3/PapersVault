## Problem

Open-world detection requires recognizing objects specified by arbitrary class labels or free-form texts, but existing methods depend on expensive manually-annotated caption datasets for training. While synthetic captions from vision-language models offer a cheaper alternative, they suffer from hallucination—generating content unrelated to the actual image—which degrades learning when used with standard contrastive objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240448/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes an RGB image and a text query (a single class label like 'panda' or a free-form phrase like 'a black-and-white giant bear') and outputs bounding boxes with associated class labels or localization scores. At inference, the text query is encoded by a frozen 12-layer CLIP ViT/B16 text transformer, and the visual backbone is Swin-Tiny with CenterNetV2 as the region proposal network. For free-form text localization, a cross-modal attention module fuses language semantics into visual embeddings before computing similarity-based localization scores.

## Method

HyperLearner bootstraps dense synthetic captions using BLIP2 over three region sources (ground-truth boxes, high-objectness proposals, and grid crops) to provide rich open-vocabulary supervision without manual annotation. The key technical contribution is a hyperbolic vision-language learning objective that projects visual and caption embeddings onto a Lorentz hyperboloid via exponential mapping with learnable curvature, then computes a hyperbolic contrastive loss using Lorentzian distance. To specifically counter hallucination, the method imposes a 'caption entails object' hierarchy through an entailment cone: the true object embedding must lie inside the cone defined by its caption, while all other object embeddings must lie outside with a margin, ensuring hallucinated caption content is not directly aligned with visual features. A cross-modal attention module with positional encoding injects spatial awareness into the fused visual-language embeddings for precise free-form text localization.

## Results

On COCO, HyperLearner (pre-trained on O365 + GoldG) achieves 48.4 mAP zero-shot and 57.4 mAP fine-tuned, surpassing Grounding-DINO-T2 (48.1 / 57.1) while using only 90M parameters vs. 172M. On LVIS, the method achieves 31.3 mAP overall and 30.7 mAP on rare classes (APr), outperforming Grounding-DINO-T by +16.3% on APr. On ODinW, HyperLearner reaches 45.2 zero-shot mAP (vs. 44.9 for Grounding-DINO-T) and 68.9 full-shot mAP. On RefCOCO (fine-tuned), it attains 90.74 / 92.09 / 85.46 Top-1 accuracy on val/testA/testB, slightly ahead of Grounding-DINO-T (89.19 / 91.86 / 85.99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperLearner outperforms all traditional and closed-set detectors (Faster R-CNN, Deformable DETR, CenterNetV2, Dyhead, DINO) by a wide margin on COCO, with 57.4 fine-tuned mAP vs. 40.2–56.9 for those baselines. Against the strongest open-world competitors, HyperLearner (O365+GoldG) beats GLIP-T(C) by +1.7 mAP zero-shot and +2.3 fine-tuned on COCO, and surpasses Grounding-DINO-T2 by +0.3 / +0.3 mAP. The advantage is most pronounced on LVIS rare classes (APr), where HyperLearner leads Grounding-DINO-T by +16.3% and GLIP-T(C) by +13.0%, demonstrating superior novel-concept detection. On ODinW, HyperLearner edges out Grounding-DINO-T by +0.3 mAP zero-shot and achieves 68.9 full-shot (vs. 63.9 for GLIP-T(C)). On RefCOCO after fine-tuning, HyperLearner slightly trails Grounding-DINO-T on testB (85.46 vs. 85.99) but leads on val and testA, while substantially outperforming GLIP-T(C) by +20–28 points across splits. The model also matches Grounding-DINO-T3 (which uses the additional Cap4M manual caption dataset) on COCO zero-shot (48.4 vs. 48.4) using only machine-generated captions, highlighting the effectiveness of the synthetic caption bootstrapping strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of open-world detection (GLIP, Grounding DINO) and hyperbolic representation learning. It builds directly on the Lorentz model of hyperbolic geometry (Nickel & Kiela, 2018; Law et al., 2019) and the entailment cone formulation (Le et al., 2019), but repurposes these tools for a new cross-modal setting: imposing a 'caption entails object' hierarchy between visual embeddings and synthetic caption embeddings to mitigate VLM hallucination. Unlike prior hyperbolic vision-language work that models 'scene entails object' or 'text entails image' hierarchies, this paper specifically targets the noise structure of machine-generated captions in a detection pipeline, and unlike prior open-world detectors that rely on expensive manual annotations (Cap4M, Conceptual Captions), it bootstraps training knowledge from a frozen VLM (BLIP2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that machine-generated captions, when properly structured through hyperbolic geometry, can substitute for large-scale manual annotation in open-world detection, reducing data collection costs while matching or exceeding SOTA. The 'caption entails object' hierarchy offers a principled, geometry-grounded mechanism for handling hallucination that could generalize to other VLM-augmented vision tasks. Limitations include reliance on BLIP2 caption quality (the approach is agnostic to the VLM but not tested on stronger ones), validation primarily on Swin-T backbone, and the modest absolute gains over the strongest baselines (e.g., +0.3 mAP on COCO fine-tuning). Open questions include whether the hyperbolic formulation scales to larger models and whether the entailment structure remains effective as VLM hallucination rates decrease.
