## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods either rely on GAN-based pipelines that fail to generalize to arbitrary person images with complex backgrounds, or use pre-trained diffusion models that either lose spatial information from the clothing (e.g., CLIP-based textual inversion) or depend on external warping networks that introduce misalignment artifacts. The key unresolved challenge is how to learn semantic correspondence between clothing and the human body directly within the latent space of a pre-trained diffusion model, preserving fine clothing details while leveraging the model's generative prior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clothing image and a person image, from which it derives three auxiliary conditions: a clothing-agnostic person map, a corresponding binary mask, and a DensePose representation. These are concatenated into a 13-channel U-Net input (4+4+1+4 latent channels), while the clothing latent additionally feeds a spatial encoder and the CLIP image encoder for exemplar conditioning. The model is trained at 512×384 for fair baseline comparison but supports up to 1024×768 resolution, and outputs a single RGB try-on image.

## Method

StableVITON introduces a zero cross-attention block that conditions the U-Net decoder with intermediate features from a spatial encoder (initialized from the pre-trained U-Net weights) operating on the clothing latent, where the U-Net decoder provides queries and the spatial encoder provides keys and values. This replaces ControlNet's direct feature addition with an attention-based warping mechanism, enabling patch-wise alignment of clothing tokens to body regions without an external warping network. To sharpen the learned correspondence, the authors propose an attention total variation loss that penalizes dispersed attention centers within the clothing region, and apply random-shift augmentation to force the model to learn fine-grained semantic matching rather than relying on positional coincidence. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end in the latent space of Stable Diffusion, with only the zero cross-attention blocks and spatial encoder updated while the original U-Net blocks remain frozen.

## Results

In cross-dataset evaluation (Table 2), StableVITON achieves FID 12.581 and KID 1.70 on VITON-HD→DressCode, FID 10.104 and KID 1.72 on DressCode→VITON-HD, and FID 23.531 and KID 5.68 on VITON-HD→SHHQ-1.0, outperforming all six baselines by a large margin. With RePaint inference, paired-setting metrics improve substantially (e.g., SSIM 0.938, LPIPS 0.0470 on VITON-HD→DressCode), while unpaired FID/KID remain best-in-class even without RePaint. In single-dataset evaluation (Table 1), StableVITON achieves the best FID (8.698) and KID (0.88) on VITON-HD and the best KID (0.72) on DressCode upper-body.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableVITON outperforms all six baselines across every cross-dataset metric. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline DCI-VTON [8], StableVITON reduces FID from 23.076 to 12.581 (VITON-HD→DressCode) and KID from 12.03 to 1.70, while also improving paired SSIM from 0.903 to 0.911 and LPIPS from 0.1217 to 0.0603. LADI-VTON [19] achieves competitive FID (16.336) but lags significantly in KID (5.36 vs. 1.70) and paired similarity (LPIPS 0.1009 vs. 0.0603), reflecting its inability to preserve fine clothing details due to spatial information loss in the CLIP encoder. GAN-based methods (VITON-HD, HR-VITON, GP-VTON) suffer severe degradation on cross-dataset evaluation, with GP-VTON reaching FID 65.711 and KID 66.01 on VITON-HD→DressCode, confirming their poor generalizability to arbitrary person images with complex backgrounds. Paint-by-Example [32], a general inpainting method, achieves FID 16.398 but fails to preserve clothing identity, yielding higher LPIPS (0.0867) than StableVITON. The user study (40 participants) further confirms these quantitative findings, with StableVITON receiving overwhelming preference in the cross-dataset setting, particularly on clothing identity where LADI-VTON drops to only 35% preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableVITON builds on the Stable Diffusion / Latent Diffusion Model framework and the exemplar-based inpainting formulation of Paint-by-Example, but departs from prior diffusion-based try-on methods (LADI-VTON, DCI-VTON) that still rely on an external warping network to align clothing in RGB space before feeding it as a condition. The key architectural departure is the zero cross-attention block, which performs warping entirely within the latent space of the pre-trained U-Net by learning semantic correspondence between clothing tokens (key/value) and body tokens (query), drawing on the ControlNet paradigm of conditioning intermediate features but replacing its direct-addition mechanism with attention. The attention total variation loss and augmentation strategy are novel training components designed to sharpen the learned correspondence, addressing a failure mode (dispersed attention) not previously identified in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the independent warping network and performing alignment in latent space, StableVITON demonstrates that a pre-trained diffusion model's generative prior can subsume the warping step, yielding a simpler end-to-end pipeline that generalizes better to arbitrary person images with complex backgrounds. The cross-dataset evaluation (trained on VITON-HD, tested on SHHQ-1.0 and DressCode) provides stronger evidence of real-world applicability than prior work typically offers. Open questions include whether this latent-space warping approach extends to full-body or lower-body garments, whether the method can operate without paired training data, and whether the zero cross-attention block's alignment robustness transfers to other conditional generation tasks beyond try-on.
