## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation in dynamic scenes suffers from inherent scale ambiguity: when moving objects are present, pixel-wise motion estimation allows each pixel to adopt an arbitrary depth scale, making depth predictions for dynamic regions unreliable. Existing approaches that jointly optimize depth and motion with regularization terms cannot fully resolve this ambiguity, leaving a significant performance gap for moving objects compared to static background.

## Input / Output

During training, the pipeline consumes consecutive monocular RGB video frames with known camera intrinsics but unknown poses, producing scale-consistent pseudo depth labels at the pixel level. At inference, the final network takes a single monocular RGB image and directly outputs a dense depth map in one forward pass. The pseudo labels are constructed by compositing the static-region depth from the scene network with scale-aligned per-object depths, yielding a full-image depth map with a unified scale.

## Method

The framework decouples depth estimation into static and dynamic regions across three stages. First, a self-supervised scene network (following [19]) jointly estimates depth, camera pose, and pixel-wise object motion, providing reliable scale-consistent depth for static regions and identifying moving objects via the motion map. Second, a per-object network performs dense structure-from-motion on each moving object assuming rigid motion, yielding a unified-scale depth per object but with unknown absolute scale relative to the background. Third, a Depth Scale Alignment (DSA) module—a ResNet18 with FC heads—predicts a scalar scale ratio β for each object by learning on static objects where the correct scale is known, then generalizes to moving objects at inference. The resulting scale-consistent pseudo labels supervise a final end-to-end depth network trained with a scale-invariant L1 loss (using a per-pixel median ratio α) combined with photometric, smoothness, and motion-sparsity losses.

## Results

On Cityscapes, the best model (BrNet+Ours) achieves Abs Rel 0.084, Sq Rel 0.722, and δ<1.25 0.918 on all regions, while on dynamic regions the best model (DiffNet+Ours) achieves Abs Rel 0.113, Sq Rel 0.657, and δ<1.25 0.889. This represents a 52.6% reduction in Sq Rel on dynamic regions compared to the best prior method (Li et al., Sq Rel 1.654). On KITTI, the best model (DiffNet+Ours with semantic prior) achieves Abs Rel 0.143, Sq Rel 1.191 on dynamic regions, a 14.4% Sq Rel reduction over the best baseline (DiffNet, Sq Rel 2.072). With a pre-trained segmentation model (SEEM), the average dynamic-region Sq Rel reduction increases to 55.8% on Cityscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Cityscapes dynamic regions, all four of the paper's models (ResNet18, PackNet, DiffNet, BrNet + Ours) substantially outperform every prior method: the best prior method (Li et al. [19]) achieves Sq Rel 1.654, while the best proposed model (DiffNet+Ours) achieves 0.657, a 52.6% reduction. On all-region Cityscapes, BrNet+Ours achieves the best Abs Rel (0.084) and Sq Rel (0.722), beating RMDepth [15] (0.100 / 0.839) and DaCNN [13] (0.113 / 1.380). On KITTI dynamic regions, DiffNet+Ours with SEEM segmentation (Sq Rel 1.191) outperforms the best prior method (DiffNet [31], Sq Rel 2.072) by 14.4%, though on all-region KITTI the method does not surpass DaCNN [13] (Sq Rel 0.661 vs. 0.662), which the authors attribute to the small fraction of dynamic pixels (0.6%) in KITTI. RMDepth [15] and DaCNN [13] do not report dynamic-region-specific metrics, limiting direct comparison on that subset. The paper also shows that adding a pre-trained segmentation prior (SEEM) further improves dynamic-region Sq Rel by ~3–4% over the fully self-supervised mask pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of jointly optimizing depth and object motion under a photometric reconstruction loss (as in Li et al. [19], Lee et al. [17], and Godard et al. [8]) by arguing that the resulting scale ambiguity in dynamic regions is fundamentally unresolvable through regularization or sparsity constraints. Instead, it decouples static and dynamic region depth estimation into separate stages, leveraging the pixel-wise motion framework of [19] and the sequential depth-then-pose pipeline of [17] only as a first-stage tool for extracting object masks and initial depth. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a Depth Scale Alignment (DSA) module that explicitly resolves per-object scale mismatches against the static background, producing scale-consistent pseudo labels that serve as supervision for a final end-to-end network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52.6% Sq Rel reduction on Cityscapes dynamic regions (and 14.4% on KITTI) is substantial and consistent across four different backbone architectures, suggesting the scale-alignment principle is broadly applicable rather than architecture-specific. The modular design—where the pseudo-label pipeline can be plugged into any existing self-supervised depth framework—makes it a practical tool for practitioners working on dynamic-scene depth. A key limitation is the rigid-motion assumption for moving objects, which restricts generalizability to indoor scenes with humans; the iterative self-training also shows diminishing returns after one iteration, leaving open whether further gains require fundamentally different supervision signals for non-rigid motion.
