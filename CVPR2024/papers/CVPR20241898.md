## Problem

Monocular BEV semantic segmentation is difficult because the perspective-view RGB input and the bird's-eye-view target originate from fundamentally different coordinate systems, making direct point-to-point optimization hard. Existing end-to-end methods attempt to perform both source perception and target-view generation in a single step, producing distorted maps that violate real-world traffic patterns (e.g., discontinuous walkways, incorrectly shaped vehicles).

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image (900×1600, resized to 396×704 and cropped to 256×704 to remove sky/buildings) and produces a BEV semantic segmentation map over a [0,50]×[-25,25] m region at 0.25 m/pixel resolution (200×200 pixels). Internally, the BEV map is represented in polar coordinates to establish column-wise spatial correspondence with the RGB image; the final output is converted back to Cartesian coordinates. No camera intrinsic parameters or multi-scale features are required.

## Method

TaDe decomposes the end-to-end pipeline into two goal-oriented stages. Stage 1 trains a BEV autoencoder (encoder E and decoder D) to reconstruct polar-coordinate BEV segmentation maps from a heavily corrupted latent representation (50% Gaussian noise added to the 8×22 latent), forcing the decoder to internalize typical traffic scene priors independent of RGB input. Stage 2 freezes the autoencoder and trains a ResNet+FPN backbone followed by a column-wise transformer that maps RGB features into the BEV latent space, optimizing MSE alignment with the frozen encoder's output on ground-truth BEV maps. The column-wise transformer treats each image column as a sequence to handle the perspective-to-BEV scale discrepancy (objects shrink with distance in RGB but remain constant in BEV). A final fine-tuning step unfreezes only the BEV decoder with BCE loss to bridge the residual distribution gap between BEV maps and RGB-derived latents. The entire pipeline avoids multi-scale features and depth estimation, achieving lower FLOPs (48.5G) and higher FPS (51.4) than comparable methods.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, TaDe achieves a mean IoU of 29.2%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art GitNet (27.9%) by 1.3 mIoU and DiffBEV (25.9%) by 3.3 mIoU. On Argoverse, TaDe reaches 22.3 mIoU, outperforming PON (17.0%) by 5.3 mIoU. In terms of efficiency, TaDe uses 41.5M parameters and 48.5G FLOPs at 51.4 FPS, compared to TIM's 45.7M parameters, 268.3G FLOPs, and 18.1 FPS, while requiring only 80 GPU hours for training versus TIM's 160.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaDe outperforms all nine baselines on the nuScenes mIoU metric. Against the closest competitor GitNet [9] (27.9%), TaDe gains 1.3 mIoU and wins in 10 of 13 individual classes, with notable advantages on small objects like motorcycles (18.2 vs 15.0) and traffic cones (15.0 vs 11.6). Compared to DiffBEV [42] (25.9%), the improvement is 3.3 mIoU, and against EPOSH [8] (22.1%) it is 7.1 mIoU. On Argoverse, TaDe (22.3%) beats PON [31] (17.0%) by 5.3 mIoU and VPN [26] (13.9%) by 8.4 mIoU, with a particularly large gain on the Bus class (37.8 vs 11.0 for PON). For computational efficiency, TaDe (48.5G FLOPs, 51.4 FPS) is substantially faster at inference than TIM [34] (268.3G FLOPs, 18.1 FPS) while using fewer parameters (41.5M vs 45.7M) and half the training GPU hours (80 vs 160). TaDe also avoids the depth estimation dependency of OFT [32] and EPOSH [8], and the multi-scale feature overhead of PON [31], contributing to its efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

TaDe departs from the dominant end-to-end paradigm in monocular BEV segmentation (PON, VED, VPN, DiffBEV) by decomposing the cross-view prediction into a BEV autoencoder stage and an RGB-to-BEV latent alignment stage. It builds on the polar-coordinate alignment ideas from TIM and GitNet, but applies the Cartesian-to-polar transformation directly on label maps rather than in feature space, and introduces a noise-corrupted autoencoder to force the decoder to internalize traffic-scene priors. The column-wise transformer for perspective-to-BEV translation is adapted from TIM, while the backbone (ResNet + FPN) and the Lion optimizer are standard components. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the denoising-reconstruction objective from generative modeling as a regularizer for BEV map generation, a use not previously explored in this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The task-decomposition framing offers a clean and potentially transferable recipe for any cross-view prediction problem where the target view has strong structural priors: first learn what the target looks like, then learn how to map the source into that learned space. The method's efficiency (48.5 G FLOPs, 51.4 FPS, no multi-scale features, no depth estimation) makes it attractive for real-time autonomous driving deployment. Open questions include whether the 50% noise level and the fixed 8×22 latent bottleneck generalize to higher-resolution or multi-camera BEV settings, and whether the decomposition advantage persists when the BEV target is more complex (e.g., panoptic or instance-level segmentation). The paper does not explore temporal consistency or multi-frame fusion, which remain important for practical driving systems.
