## Problem

Most domain generalization methods for object detection require multiple labeled source domains, which is costly and often impractical. In the single-source setting, existing approaches are limited and underperform, and it remains unclear whether simple augmentation-based diversification alone can rival or surpass dedicated single-DGOD methods. Additionally, out-of-domain detectors tend to produce overconfident, poorly calibrated predictions, a problem that has received little attention in the object detection literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from a single source domain (Pascal VOC 2007+2012 trainval, or BDD-100k daytime-sunny subset) and, during training, each image is paired with an augmented version produced by randomly sampling one of 22 visual corruptions (ImageNet-C and Fourier-based) at one of 5 severity levels. The detector outputs the standard set of Z detections, each comprising a class probability vector and a 4-element bounding box. The alignment mechanism specifically operates on the per-proposal classifier logits and regressed box coordinates, requiring a 1-to-1 correspondence between proposals of the clean and augmented views, which is enforced by reusing the original image's RPN proposals to pool features from the augmented feature map.

## Method

The approach has two components. First, domain diversification: during training, every image in a minibatch is augmented with a randomly sampled visual corruption (excluding weather and constant-amplitude transforms), and both the clean and augmented versions are fed to the detector, establishing a strong baseline that already outperforms prior single-DGOD methods. Second, detection alignment: to enforce domain invariance (matching class distributions and box locations between the two views), the RPN proposals from the clean image are used to perform RoI-Alignment on the augmented image's feature map, yielding a spatially consistent set of proposals. A KL-divergence loss (L_cal) then minimizes the gap between the two views' classifier probability vectors, while an L2 loss (L_ral) minimizes the squared difference between their predicted bounding boxes. The total training objective is L_det + α·L_cal + β·L_ral, and this consistency regularization implicitly reduces overconfidence, improving out-of-domain calibration (D-ECE) without any explicit calibration module.

## Results

On the Urban Scene Detection benchmark (trained on Daytime Sunny, tested on four weather-shifted targets), the full method (diversification + alignment) achieves mAP@0.5 of 42.5% (Night Clear), 38.1% (Dusk Rainy), 24.1% (Night Rainy), and 37.2% (Daytime Foggy), outperforming the strongest prior single-DGOD method (Vidit et al.) by 5.6%, 5.8%, and 5.4% on NC, DR, and NR respectively. On the Real-to-Artistic benchmark (Pascal VOC → Clipart1k/Watercolor2k/Comic2k), the method reaches 38.9%, 57.4%, and 33.2% mAP@0.5, surpassing the Faster R-CNN baseline by 13.2%, 12.9%, and 14.3% points. The method also improves calibration, reducing D-ECE from 27.9% to 15.8% on Night Clear and from 31.5% to 29.3% on Night Rainy compared to the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Faster R-CNN baseline, the full method delivers large gains of 13.2%, 12.9%, and 14.3% mAP on Clipart1k, Watercolor2k, and Comic2k respectively, and 3.6–8.4% on the four urban-scene targets. Compared to the prior single-DGOD method of Wu et al. [60] (cyclic-disentangled self-distillation), the proposed method wins on all four target weather domains (e.g., 24.1% vs 16.6% on Night Rainy) but trails on the in-domain source (52.8% vs 56.1% on Daytime Sunny). Against Vidit et al. [54] (CLIP-based single-DGOD), the method achieves higher mAP on Night Clear (42.5 vs 36.9), Dusk Rainy (38.1 vs 32.3), and Night Rainy (24.1 vs 18.7), but is slightly lower on Daytime Foggy (37.2 vs 38.5). On the Real-to-Artistic benchmark, the method outperforms Normalization Perturbation [13] by 3.5%, 4.1%, and 4.3% on Clipart, Watercolor, and Comic. In the comparison with domain-adaptive detectors (Table 7) that assume access to unlabelled target data, the method still surpasses DBGL [4] on Watercolor (57.0 vs 53.8) and Comic (32.2 vs 29.7) despite having no target-domain access at training time, though it trails on Clipart (38.5 vs 41.6). The single-stage FCOS variant also benefits substantially, gaining 13.0%, 10.7%, and 15.8% over the FCOS baseline on the three artistic targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain diversification paradigm from single-source domain generalization in classification (adversarial augmentation, style synthesis) to object detection, where the challenge is compounded by the need to align both classification and localization outputs. It formalizes domain invariance for detection by building on the corruption-based domain transformation framework of Xiao et al. [61], extending it to jointly constrain class probability distributions (via KL divergence) and bounding box coordinates (via L2 loss) across augmented views. The approach deliberately departs from more complex single-DGOD methods like cyclic-disentangled self-distillation [60] and text-prompt-based adaptation [54], showing that carefully selected off-the-shelf visual corruptions plus a simple consistency loss suffice to match or exceed them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that a detector-agnostic augmentation-and-alignment recipe outperforms purpose-built single-DGOD methods on both artistic and weather-shift benchmarks—gives practitioners a low-cost, easy-to-integrate strategy for improving out-of-domain detection without target-domain access. The implicit calibration benefit (lower D-ECE without explicit calibration modules) is particularly relevant for safety-critical deployment where overconfident false positives are dangerous. Open questions include whether the fixed corruption set generalizes to more extreme or semantically different shifts, whether the alignment can be made adaptive per-domain, and whether the approach transfers to tasks beyond detection (e.g., segmentation, tracking).
