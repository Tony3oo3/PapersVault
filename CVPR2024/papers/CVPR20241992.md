## Problem

Acquiring and reconstructing high-quality dynamic 3D volumes in real time is hindered by the fundamental information gap between 2D sensor samples and 3D density volumes, compounded by the limited sampling budget imposed by temporal variation. Existing uncontrolled-illumination methods rely on priors to hallucinate missing information, while controlled-lighting methods suffer from long acquisition times or low SNR, making neither suitable for real-time volumetric capture of fast-changing phenomena.

## Input / Output

The method takes #p consecutive grayscale images per camera view, each captured under one of #p pre-optimized structured light patterns (1D vectors of 128 vertical-strip intensities projected by a consumer-grade projector onto a 96 mm³ valid volume). The decoder operates per-pixel: it consumes the #p scalar measurements at a single pixel location together with the resampled 1D local incident lighting profile, and outputs a 1D density distribution along the corresponding camera ray. All per-ray predictions are resampled into a 128³ 3D density volume per camera, which are then fused (via a 3D UNet) into a final volume when multiple cameras are used, achieving 40 volumes/second end-to-end.

## Method

The core contribution is a jointly optimized neural pipeline where the structured light patterns are learned as optimizable encoder weights (passed through sigmoid for physical plausibility) and the reconstruction decoder is a lightweight 4-layer MLP that predicts a 1D density distribution from single-pixel measurements and local incident lighting, rather than from raw camera indices or screen-space coordinates. This per-pixel, per-ray decoder design is generalizable and reusable across hardware configurations, with geometric transformations (resampling between light patterns and local lighting, and between 1D distributions and the 3D volume) handled by precomputed coefficients outside the network. In the multi-camera setting, a 3D UNet aggregation module fuses per-camera volumes in a common global space. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with supervised RMSE loss on synthetic fluid volumes generated by Mantaflow, with simulated Poisson and Gaussian measurement noise to ensure robustness on physical hardware.

## Results

The proposed framework achieves real-time volumetric reconstruction at 40 volumes per second with a spatial resolution of 128³, using only 6 optimized structured light patterns and 3 cameras. Reconstruction of a single volume from captured images takes 9.2 ms with unoptimized code. On both a real static object (Fig. 7) and a synthetic smoke sequence (Fig. 9), the method outperforms all compared baselines in SSIM, PSNR, and RMSE, with the speed advantage over [10] and [15] being several orders of magnitude (9.2 ms vs. 10–40 minutes per volume).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines on both a real static object and a synthetic smoke sequence, reporting SSIM/PSNR/RMSE×10⁻². Against the optical-sectioning baseline of Hawkins et al. [21] (projecting one of 128 light slices at a time), the proposed method produces visibly superior reconstructions with lower RMSE. Against the single-view compressive structured-light method of Gu et al. [19], the proposed approach also wins on all three metrics, benefiting from multi-view information and jointly optimized patterns. Against the two multi-view learning-based methods — Chu et al. [10] (physics-informed neural fields) and Franz et al. [15] (global transport with learned self-supervision) — the proposed method achieves higher SSIM and PSNR and lower RMSE on both the real object and the synthetic smoke sequence. The most striking difference is computational: [10] and [15] require roughly 10–40 minutes to reconstruct a single 3D volume, whereas the proposed decoder completes reconstruction in 9.2 ms, a speedup of several orders of magnitude. Notably, the proposed network is trained only on fluid simulations (Mantaflow) yet generalizes well to the non-fluid flower-like shape in the real-object experiment, attributed to the single-pixel decoder design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the illumination-multiplexing line of volumetric reconstruction (Gu et al. 2013, Kang et al. 2023) by jointly optimizing the projected light patterns as learnable encoder weights alongside the reconstruction network, rather than fixing patterns a priori. It departs from uncontrolled-illumination methods (heuristic, physics, or learned priors) by actively probing the scene with structured light to reduce the information gap, and from prior controlled-lighting work by replacing expensive scanning or complex optimization with a lightweight single-pixel 1D decoder. The 3D UNet aggregation module is borrowed from medical imaging, and training on Mantaflow fluid simulations follows the data-generation strategy of Xie et al. 2018 and Chu et al. 2022.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 40 volumes per second at 128³ resolution with an off-the-shelf projector and synchronized cameras makes real-time volumetric capture of dynamic phenomena practically accessible, removing the need for expensive scanning hardware or lengthy per-volume optimization. The joint hardware-software optimization framing and the elegant single-pixel decoder (which outperforms an end-to-end convolutional network) offer a reusable design principle for other structured-lighting tasks. Key limitations remain: the static-scene assumption during multi-pattern projection, the exclusion of complex optics (reflection, refraction, multiple scattering), and the reliance on fluid-simulation training data. Extending to lightfield projectors, neural representations, and more general optical models are natural next steps.
