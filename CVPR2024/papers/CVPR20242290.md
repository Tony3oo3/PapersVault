## Problem

Existing WSSS methods that incorporate SAM do so only during inference—either as post-processing on CAM-derived pseudo-labels or via zero-shot language-guided prompting—making the pipeline vulnerable to noise in the initial CAM seeds. Errors in CAMs propagate through SAM-based refinement and can even worsen, since SAM cannot recover from fundamentally incorrect prompts. The paper identifies that the most effective use of SAM is to transfer its segmentation knowledge into the classifier during training, thereby improving CAM quality at its source.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242290/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images with image-level multi-label class labels as the sole supervision signal. The classifier (WRN38 encoder + 1×1 conv head) produces class activation maps at reduced spatial resolution (h×w), which are then post-processed into pseudo-labels for training a DeepLab segmentation network. SAM (ViT-H) is queried twice per image: once in segment-everything mode for SSC and once with class-specific point prompts for CPM, with the encoder run only once and reused across all class decodes for efficiency.

## Method

S2C comprises two training-time modules that distill SAM's knowledge into the classifier. SAM-Segment Contrasting (SSC) runs SAM's segment-everything to obtain a segmentation map, computes per-segment prototypes by averaging the classifier's feature vectors within each segment, and applies an InfoNCE loss that pulls each pixel's feature toward its own segment prototype while pushing it away from all others—transferring segmentation structure at the feature level. CAM-based Prompting Module (CPM) extracts multiple local peaks from each class's CAM via a local maximum filter, feeds them as point prompts to SAM to obtain class-specific masks with stability scores, and aggregates overlapping masks into a unified self-supervision map using a confidence score that is the element-wise product of SAM stability and mean CAM activation. The unified map (including a derived background channel) supervises the CAMs via cross-entropy. The total loss is L_CLS + L_SSC + L_CPM, with CPM disabled in the first epoch to avoid noisy early prompts.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, S2C (WRN38) achieves 78.2% mIoU on the val set and 77.5% on the test set, surpassing the previous best (MAT, 73.0% val / 72.7% test) by over 5 points. On MS-COCO 2014 val, S2C reaches 49.8% mIoU, outperforming the prior best (Xu et al., 45.9%) by nearly 4 points. At the CAM level on VOC 2012 train, S2C scores 76.1% mIoU versus 70.8% for the next-best method (CLIP-ES), and at the pseudo-label (mask) level it reaches 81.7% versus 75.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2C outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every benchmark. On VOC 2012 val, the closest competitor is MAT (73.0%), followed by ACR (72.4%) and Xu et al. (72.2%); S2C leads by 5.2, 5.8, and 6.0 points respectively. On COCO val, Xu et al. (45.9%) and MAT (45.6%) are the nearest rivals, with S2C ahead by 3.9 and 4.2 points. Even when all baselines are augmented with SAM-based post-processing (Table 5), S2C's pseudo-labels (81.7%) still exceed the best SAM-enhanced method CLIP-ES (79.7%) by 2.0 points, and its final VOC val mIoU (78.2%) surpasses CLIP-ES (73.1%) by 5.1 points. The paper notes the comparison is not entirely fair since S2C leverages SAM as an additional information source, but the margin over SAM-enhanced baselines demonstrates that training-time knowledge transfer is more effective than inference-only SAM use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WSSS literature that incorporates external knowledge sources (saliency modules, CLIP, 3D data) by integrating SAM, but departs from prior SAM-based WSSS works [9, 10] that use SAM only at inference (post-processing or zero-shot). The SSC module is grounded in regional prototype-based contrastive learning [38], repurposing SAM's segment-everything as a clustering signal at the feature level rather than the logit level. The CPM module introduces a novel point-prompting strategy that converts CAMs into SAM prompts, bridging the gap between the classifier's score maps and SAM's promptable interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that transferring SAM's segmentation knowledge into the classifier during training is more robust than applying SAM as an inference-time post-processor—provides a principled framework for integrating foundation models into weakly supervised pipelines. The ~5–6% mIoU gain over prior SOTA on both VOC and COCO is substantial, and the ablation confirming that feature-level contrast far outperforms logit-level contrast (63.2 vs. 57.3) offers a useful design principle. However, the reliance on SAM's ViT-H backbone introduces significant computational overhead, and the comparison is acknowledged as not entirely fair since prior methods lack access to such a powerful external model. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other foundation models or to settings where SAM's automatic segmentation is less reliable (e.g., medical or satellite imagery).
