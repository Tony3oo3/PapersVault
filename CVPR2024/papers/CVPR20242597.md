## Problem

Existing dynamic scene graph generation methods rely on multi-stage pipelines (object detection, temporal association, multi-relation classification) that suffer from sub-optimal solutions due to independent optimization of separated stages, and from massive computational redundancy when enumerating all candidate subject-object pairs. Capturing temporal dependencies further requires additional trackers or handcrafted trajectories that are difficult to jointly train and prone to introducing noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242597/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence comprising one target frame and n reference frames, processed through a ResNet-50 backbone and Transformer encoder to produce per-frame visual features. It outputs a fixed set of N_q=100 predictions per frame, each containing subject and object bounding boxes with class labels plus a predicate label, forming a list of ⟨subject, predicate, object⟩ triplets that constitute the dynamic scene graph. At inference, duplicate triplets are filtered via NMS using the product of subject and object IoU as the overlap criterion.

## Method

OED reformulates DSGG as a one-stage set prediction problem by extending DETR across spatial and temporal dimensions, using pair-wise features to represent each subject-object pair. Spatial context is aggregated via two cascaded Transformer decoders: a Pair-wise Instance Decoder that extracts pair-wise instance features, and a Pair-wise Relation Decoder that uses those instance features as queries to capture relation-specific spatial context. For temporal context, the proposed Progressively Refined Module (PRM) performs multi-step cross-attention between the target frame's pair-wise features and Top-K selected reference frame features, with K progressively decreasing (80n→50n→30n) to filter background noise while retaining informative temporal signals. This eliminates the need for additional trackers or handcrafted trajectories, enabling fully end-to-end optimization. A matched predicate loss is applied only to predictions matched with ground truth to mitigate the impact of incomplete annotations in the Action Genome dataset.

## Results

On the Action Genome benchmark, OED achieves R@10/20/50 of 33.5/40.9/48.9 (With Constraint) and 35.3/44.0/51.8 (No Constraints) on the SGDET task, outperforming the second-best method (DSG-DETR: 30.3/34.8/36.1) by an average of 6.2% under With Constraint and 2.2% under No Constraints. On PredCLS, OED reaches 73.0/76.1/76.1 (With Constraint), surpassing the best prior method (TR²: 70.9/73.8/73.8) by ~2.1% on average. Under No Constraints PredCLS, OED (83.3/95.3/99.2) is marginally behind TPT (85.6/97.4/99.9) and TR² (83.1/96.6/99.9), which benefit from oracle tracks and additional pre-trained models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OED dominates the SGDET task across all 15 compared baselines under both With Constraint and No Constraints settings, with the largest gains over the closest competitors DSG-DETR (+3.2 R@10, +6.1 R@20, +12.8 R@50 under With Constraint) and TPT (+3.3 R@10, +4.4 R@20, +0.3 R@50 under No Constraints). On PredCLS under With Constraint, OED (73.0/76.1/76.1) outperforms all baselines, beating the strongest prior method TR² (70.9/73.8/73.8) by ~2.1% on average. However, under No Constraints PredCLS, OED (83.3/95.3/99.2) trails TPT (85.6/97.4/99.9) by 2.3/2.1/0.7 and TR² (83.1/96.6/99.9) by 0.2/1.3/0.7, which the authors attribute to TPT and TR² being tracking-based methods that can directly exploit oracle tracks provided in this protocol, plus TPT's multi-scale features and TR²'s CLIP pre-training. Earlier multi-stage methods (STTran, APT, TRACE, TEMPURA) and static SGG baselines (VRD, MSDN, GPS-Net, etc.) are all outperformed by substantial margins (5–15+ points on SGDET R@10). The one-stage end-to-end design consistently outperforms multi-stage pipelines, validating the paper's core claim that decoupling detection, tracking, and classification leads to sub-optimal solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

OED extends the DETR set-prediction paradigm from static scene graph generation (RelTR, Structured Sparse R-CNN) to the dynamic video setting, unifying spatial and temporal context aggregation in a single end-to-end framework. It departs from the dominant multi-stage DSGG pipeline (STTran, APT, TR², DSG-DETR, TPT) that chains object detection, temporal tracking, and predicate classification as separately optimized sub-problems. The key architectural departure is the Progressively Refined Module (PRM), which replaces explicit trackers and handcrafted trajectories with a multi-step Top-K selection over reference-frame pair-wise features, enabling joint end-to-end optimization. The cascaded decoder design (instance decoder feeding a relation decoder) draws on the observation that pair detection provides a strong spatial prior for predicate classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the multi-stage pipeline, OED removes the sub-optimality introduced by independent stage training and the combinatorial explosion of enumerated subject-object pairs, achieving a clear 6.2% average gain in SGDET over the best multi-stage baselines on Action Genome. However, the oracle-query experiment (Table 5) reveals a substantial 4-point gap between PRM and ideal reference selection, indicating that the progressive refinement strategy is far from optimal and that better cross-frame query matching remains an open problem. The method's inability to exploit oracle tracks in the PredCLS No-Constraints setting (where it trails TPT and TR²) highlights a fundamental tension between one-stage flexibility and the precision that tracking-based methods gain from ground-truth trajectories. Broader applicability beyond the single Action Genome benchmark and to longer video sequences remains untested.
