## Problem

Existing Blind Super-Resolution methods either predict blur kernels or degradation representations but remain agnostic to the essential content details that LR images inherently lack. Meanwhile, diffusion-based SR approaches that use the diffusion model as the full SR network suffer from expensive multi-step sampling and excessive generative diversity that introduces incorrect textures. The paper addresses both gaps by treating the diffusion model as a lightweight estimator for a compact prior rather than as the reconstruction network itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241661/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image with unknown degradation (various isotropic/anisotropic Gaussian blur kernels and additive Gaussian noise) and produces a high-resolution RGB image at scale factors ×2, ×3, or ×4. Internally, the diffusion module operates on a one-dimensional Content Degradation Prior vector of 256 channels rather than on full image tensors, requiring only T=4 reverse sampling steps at inference. The SR backbone is a Transformer-CNN hybrid with 6 residual groups of 3 CDRBs each (180 channels, 8×32 window size for the full model; 96 channels, 4×16 for the lightweight variant).

## Method

CDFormer employs a two-stage training strategy. In Stage 1, a GT encoder E_GT learns a Content Degradation Prior (CDP) from paired HR and LR images, while the SR network CDFormer_SR is jointly trained to reconstruct HR images guided by the CDP injected via learnable channel-wise affine transformations (CDIM) at four points per CDRB. In Stage 2, a diffusion model is trained to recreate the CDP from LR images alone: a conditional vector from E_LR is fused into each reverse denoising step, and the full T-step sampling is performed at every training iteration (unlike standard single-step DDPM training), with an L1 loss between the diffusion-generated CDP and the E_GT ground-truth CDP. Each CDRB combines Spatial Window Self-Attention and Channel-wise Self-Attention with depth-wise convolutions through an interflow mechanism (channel and spatial distillers) to adaptively model both high- and low-frequency information. The diffusion module is compact (~3M parameters) and efficient (T=4 steps) because it denoises a 1D vector rather than a full image.

## Results

On the primary ×4 blind SR setting with isotropic Gaussian kernels, CDFormer achieves 29.00 dB PSNR on Set14 (kernel width 0) and 27.21 dB on Urban100, outperforming the strongest KP baseline DCLS by 0.39 dB and 0.71 dB respectively. Under complex anisotropic kernels with noise (Table 3), CDFormer leads KDSR by 0.1–0.5 dB across all noise levels. In the diffusion-model comparison (Table 1), CDFormer (24.46 M params, 0.39 s/image) surpasses StableSR (1409 M params, 71 s) by 9.34 dB PSNR on Set14 while being ~180× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDFormer outperforms all seven compared baselines across every tested setting. Against the strongest KP method DCLS, CDFormer gains up to 0.71 dB on Urban100 (×4, kernel 0: 27.21 vs 26.50) and 0.39 dB on Set14 (29.00 vs 28.61), with the gap widening under heavier blur (e.g., 0.38 dB on Urban100 at kernel 3.6). Among DP methods, CDFormer beats KDSR by 0.33–0.51 dB and DASR by 0.50–0.71 dB on ×4 settings, confirming that jointly modeling content and degradation representations is more robust than degradation-only estimation. Against diffusion-based SR methods, the advantage is dramatic: CDFormer exceeds StableSR by 9.34 dB (Set14) and SR3 by 3.59 dB while using 1/60th the parameters and running ~180× faster, validating the paper's argument that treating the diffusion model as a CDP estimator rather than the full SR network avoids both the sampling cost and the excessive-diversity artifacts of prior DM-based approaches. RCAN (non-blind, known-kernel reference) is surpassed by CDFormer in all blind settings, e.g., 29.00 vs 28.87 on Set14 (kernel 0) and 25.72 vs 20.99 on Urban100 (kernel 3.6). DnCNN is used only as a denoising preprocessor for RCAN and DCLS in the noisy-kernel experiments (Table 3), not as a standalone SR baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDFormer extends the degradation prediction (DP) line of work (DASR, KDSR) by arguing that estimating degradation representations alone is insufficient and that content information must also be captured. It repurposes diffusion models in a fundamentally different role than prior diffusion-based SR methods (SR3, StableSR): rather than using the DM as the full generative SR network requiring 50–1000 sampling steps, CDFormer treats the DM as a lightweight estimator that denoises a single 1D Content Degradation Prior vector (T=4 steps, ~3M params). This design is conceptually closest to DiffIR's use of diffusion to estimate prior representations, but CDFormer extends it to jointly recover both degradation and content signals and injects them into a Transformer-based SR network via learnable affine transformations and an interflow mechanism between CNN and Transformer branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small diffusion-based estimator can outperform a ground-truth encoder (Stage 2 > Stage 1 in Table 5), suggesting that the generative prior of diffusion models can hallucinate plausible content details even when only LR input is available at inference. However, the gains over the strongest KP baseline (DCLS) are modest (≤0.71 dB), and performance degrades noticeably under high noise levels, indicating that the CDP approach still struggles when LR images carry very little recoverable information. Open questions include whether the CDP framework generalizes to real-world multi-degradation scenarios beyond synthetic Gaussian blur and noise, and whether the two-stage training strategy can be simplified or made end-to-end.
