## Problem

Deformable multi-modality medical image registration is hindered by the fact that statistical similarity measures (e.g., mutual information) are sensitive to locally varying noise and insensitive to subtle anatomical structures, while existing local structural descriptors (e.g., MIND) lack sufficient discriminability for complex anatomies, leading to ambiguous correspondence matching. No prior method learns discriminative, contrast-invariant structural representations from standalone images without requiring perfectly aligned training pairs or anatomical delineations.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D single-channel grayscale volumes (CT or MRI) as input and first produces a deep structural image representation (DSIR) — a compact 4D feature map of shape H×W×D×24 — via MASR-Net. This DSIR is then used as a modality-agnostic similarity metric: registration reduces to minimizing negated cosine similarity between the DSIRs of the fixed and warped moving volumes, yielding a dense deformation field. The DSIR is differentiable, so it can be plugged into either learning-based (LapIRN) or iterative optimization (3-level multiresolution IO) registration frameworks without modification.

## Method

MASR-Net extracts features with a 4-level encoder-decoder CNN (skip connections, BlurPool downsampling, trilinear upsampling, max stride 8), then computes Deep Neighbourhood Self-similarity (DNS) at the feature level rather than pixel level, using two neighbourhood layouts (direct and dilated 6-neighbourhood) and excluding the patch centre to capture long-range structural information while reducing noise sensitivity. A feature squeezing module (linear projection + two 3D conv layers) compresses the resulting 5D DNS map into a compact 4D DSIR. Training is fully self-supervised via anatomy-aware contrastive learning: stochastic non-linear intensity augmentation (random Bezier curves plus random inversion) simulates cross-modality contrast variation, and a contrastive loss pulls feature vectors at identical anatomical locations together while pushing apart those at different locations. The key insight is that computing self-similarity on learned features rather than raw intensities, combined with contrastive supervision, yields a representation that is simultaneously contrast-invariant and highly discriminative, reducing the multi-modality registration problem to a standard mono-modality one.

## Results

On the liver multiphase CT registration task (Pre-contrast ← Venous & Arterial), IO+DNS achieves the best tumour DSC of 80.43±13.72 and organ DSC of 94.26±3.32, outperforming the next-best DEEDs+MIND (79.65/93.90) and all MI-based methods. On the Abdomen MR-CT task, IO+DNS reaches 85.61±5.95 DSC, surpassing DEEDs+MIND (83.53) while MI-based methods (ANTs: 39.43, NiftyReg: 48.60) fail catastrophically. On Brain MR T1w-T2w, IO+DNS achieves 62.66/61.91 DSC (T1←T2 / T2←T1), on par with NiftyReg+MI (63.90/61.90) and slightly above DEEDs+MIND (61.47/60.70). The ablation confirms DNS contributes +11.12 DSC over a randomly initialized CNN backbone, and contrastive learning adds a further +0.63 DSC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ANTs+MI and NiftyReg+MI, the proposed IO+DNS wins decisively on liver CT (80.43 vs. 76.52/77.10 tumour DSC) and catastrophically on Abdomen MR-CT (85.61 vs. 39.43/48.60 DSC), confirming that MI-based similarity cannot handle large cross-modality deformations. Compared to DEEDs+MIND, IO+DNS gains +0.78 DSC on liver CT tumour alignment and +2.08 DSC on Abdomen MR-CT, while using a simpler gradient-descent optimizer rather than DEEDs' discrete convex optimization. Against LapIRN, swapping MIND/NMI for DNS yields +2.22 and +0.75 DSC gains on tumour and organ alignment respectively, demonstrating that the representation, not the registration framework, drives the improvement. Voxelmorph with either NMI or MIND trails IO+DNS by 4–5 DSC on liver CT tumour alignment, and the paper does not evaluate Voxelmorph on the MR-CT or brain tasks. On Brain MR T1w-T2w, IO+DNS (62.66/61.91) is on par with NiftyReg+MI (63.90/61.90) and slightly above DEEDs+MIND (61.47/60.70), indicating contrast invariance is preserved even with heterogeneous intensity distributions. Overall, DNS consistently outperforms MIND across all three registration frameworks tested (IO, LapIRN, DEEDs), validating its superior discriminability for multimodal correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the classical MIND descriptor (pixel-level local self-similarity) by computing self-similarity at the learned feature level (DNS) and augmenting it with anatomy-aware contrastive learning using stochastic non-linear intensity transformations (Bezier curves). It positions itself against both statistical-based methods (MI/NMI, which fail on large deformations and are noise-sensitive) and supervised deep learning registration methods (which require perfectly aligned pairs or anatomical labels). The key conceptual move is reducing multimodal registration to a monomodal problem by learning a shared, contrast-invariant structural space, then plugging that space into existing registration frameworks (LapIRN, iterative optimization) without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's self-supervised nature—requiring neither aligned training pairs nor anatomical segmentations—makes it broadly applicable across anatomies and modalities without retraining, which is a practical advantage over supervised DLIR approaches. A notable open question is whether the contrastive learning component generalizes to truly unseen modalities (e.g., ultrasound, PET) beyond the CT/MR pairs tested here, and whether the 24-channel DSIR is sufficient for more complex multi-organ or whole-body registration. The finding that DNS with simple gradient descent outperforms MIND with sophisticated convex optimization suggests the bottleneck in multimodal registration may lie more in the similarity metric than in the optimizer, redirecting future effort toward better structural representations.
