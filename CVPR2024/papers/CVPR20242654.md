## Problem

Existing visual feature extractors (ConvNets, ViTs, MLPs) universally represent images as rigid rectangular grids, a paradigm rooted in engineering convention rather than explicit modeling of data distribution. This grid-centric view fails to capture the organic, non-uniform structure of visual scenes and renders the forward process opaque, making it impossible to inspect how features are actually selected and grouped. The paper asks whether feature extraction can be reformulated as a transparent clustering process that automatically discovers the underlying pixel organization.

## Input / Output

FEC takes a standard RGB image (224×224 for classification, 512/800 shorter side for segmentation/detection) and first applies a 4×4 strided convolution to produce 56×56 pixel blocks as initial elements. Beyond the standard class label or detection/segmentation outputs, FEC produces explicit cluster assignments at every layer, forming a hierarchical pyramid of spatial segments (from 56×56 down to 7×7) that are directly viewable by humans. For dense prediction tasks, residual connections are added so each representative simultaneously encodes both a rectangular grid region and a data-driven cluster, preserving compatibility with anchor-based detectors.

## Method

FEC reformulates feature extraction as iterative representative selection via neural clustering, alternating between two operations: Clustering-based Feature Pooling (reduces spatial resolution by half by assigning pixels to O clusters and returning cluster representatives) and Clustering-based Feature Encoding (keeps resolution, redistributing cluster-center information back to pixels via modulated propagation). Cluster centers are initialized adaptively per input using adaptive average pooling on 1×1-convolved key and value projections; assignment is determined by argmax cosine similarity, and representatives are computed as the mean of assigned value features plus the center. Feature dispatching updates each pixel by adding an MLP of sigmoid(α·similarity + β)·representative, enabling intra-cluster communication. The four-stage architecture (resolutions 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32) is trained end-to-end with standard supervised losses, and the entire forward pass is mechanistically interpretable because every cluster assignment is an explicit, inspectable spatial partition rather than a hidden attention weight.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, FEC-Small (5.5M params) achieves 72.7% top-1 accuracy, outperforming ResNet18 (12M params, 69.8%) by 2.9% with less than half the parameters. FEC-Base (14.4M) reaches 78.1% top-1, and FEC-Large (28.3M) reaches 81.2% top-1, matching Swin-Tiny (29M, 81.3%) with fewer parameters. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, FEC-Base (18.0M) achieves 37.7% mIoU, surpassing ResNet50 (28.5M, 36.7%) by 1.0%. On COCO detection, FEC-Small (24.3M) achieves 35.6 AP^box, beating ResNet18 (31.2M, 34.0 AP^box) by 1.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEC consistently outperforms its closest clustering-based competitor CoC across all three model scales: FEC-Small beats CoC-Tiny by 0.9% top-1 (72.7 vs 71.8), FEC-Base beats CoC-Small by 0.6% (78.1 vs 77.5), and FEC-Large edges CoC-Medium by 0.2% (81.2 vs 81.0). Against convolutional baselines, FEC-Small surpasses ResNet18 by 2.9% top-1 while using less than half the parameters (5.5M vs 12M). Among MLP-based backbones, FEC-Base outperforms ResMLP-12 by 1.5% (78.1 vs 76.6) and PVT-Tiny by 3.0% (78.1 vs 75.1). FEC-Large is essentially tied with Swin-Tiny (81.2 vs 81.3) but uses fewer FLOPs (6.5G vs 4.5G) and fewer parameters (28.3M vs 29M). On dense prediction tasks, FEC-Small beats ResNet18 by 2.4% mIoU on ADE20K (35.3 vs 32.9) and by 1.6 AP^box on COCO (35.6 vs 34.0), while FEC-Large slightly trails CoC-Medium/49 on COCO detection (39.9 vs 40.6 AP^box) and ADE20K segmentation (40.5 vs 40.8 mIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

FEC departs from the dominant grid-style paradigm (ConvNets, ViTs, MLPs) that represents images as collections of rectangular regions, instead reformulating the entire feature extraction pipeline as iterative representative selection via neural clustering. It builds most directly on CoC (Image as set of points, ICLR 2023), which introduced clustering for information exchange within a backbone, but FEC extends this by making clustering the sole mechanism for both spatial reduction (pooling) and feature encoding, with gradually growing clusters replacing fixed-resolution patches. The feature dispatching mechanism draws on modulated propagation techniques from CoC and ClusterFormer (NeurIPS 2023), while the interpretability framing contrasts with post-hoc methods like Grad-CAM and white-box Transformer analyses (CRATE) by embedding transparency directly into the forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEC offers a conceptually clean alternative to the grid-based assumption that has underpinned visual backbones since the early deep learning era, and its cluster assignments provide a human-inspectable decomposition of the forward process without requiring gradient-based post-hoc explanations. However, the interpretability is qualified: visualizing the 49 final clusters requires an additional K-Means reduction step, and the exclusive (non-overlapping) clustering may limit expressiveness compared to soft attention. Open questions include whether set-prediction architectures like DETR can more naturally exploit the modeled representatives, whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video settings, and whether the emergent segmentation property generalizes beyond classification-trained models.
