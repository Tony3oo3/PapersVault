## Problem

Existing motion prediction models rely on symbolic perception outputs (bounding boxes, road graphs, traffic lights) that are vulnerable to detection failures on open-vocabulary obstacles and cannot capture scene context like road surface conditions. Fully end-to-end learning from raw sensors avoids these limitations but suffers from poor interpretability and prohibitive computational cost. The paper asks whether a compact, learned token representation of the visual scene can bridge this gap, providing sensor-grounded context without the full cost of raw-sensor processing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1 second of history (11 frames at 5 Hz) comprising 8 synchronized camera views, a LiDAR point cloud (75 m range), 3D bounding boxes with velocity, a road graph of polylines/polygons, and traffic signals. Camera images are pre-encoded into dense feature maps (256-dim SAM ViT-H embeddings or 512×32-dim VQ-GAN tokens per camera) and released as part of the augmented WOMD. The model outputs 6 most-likely future trajectories per agent over 8 seconds at 5 Hz, produced by fitting a GMM over decoder predictions and aggregating 2048 samples via K-means.

## Method

MoST decomposes each LiDAR frame into three disjoint scene-element types—ground tiles (10 m × 10 m via RANSAC), perception-detected agents, and open-set objects (connected-component analysis on residual points)—and assigns a unique token ID to every point. Pre-trained image foundation models (SAM ViT-H, CLIP, DINOv2, or VQ-GAN) produce per-pixel feature maps that are projected onto 3D points via camera-LiDAR calibration, then pooled by token ID into per-element image embeddings. An efficient multi-frame representation compresses temporal data using Kalman-filter tracking for open-set elements, cross-frame ground aggregation, and variable-length LiDAR downsampling. A spatial-temporal fusion module combines the pooled image features, fine-grained geometry (MLP-encoded point coordinates), coarse-grained geometry (MLP-encoded box attributes), and learned temporal embeddings through axial attention along time and element axes, yielding one compact vector per scene element. These scene-element tokens are concatenated with standard symbolic tokens (agent state, road graph, traffic signals) and processed by a Wayformer transformer backbone trained end-to-end for trajectory prediction.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset validation set, MoST-SAM_H-64 achieves minADE 0.5487, minFDE 1.1355, Miss Rate 0.1238, mAP 0.4230, and soft-mAP 0.4380, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. The variant MoST-SAM_H-6 (6 queries) achieves the best minADE of 0.5228. Compared to the single-decoder Wayformer baseline (minADE 0.5830, soft-mAP 0.4110), MoST delivers a 10.3% relative improvement on minADE and a 6.6% relative improvement on soft-mAP. The 3-decoder MoST-VQGAN-64 variant further improves soft-mAP to 0.4396 and Miss Rate to 0.1172.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoST outperforms all six compared baselines on the WOMD validation set. Against the primary baseline Wayformer (3-decoder, reproduced), MoST-VQGAN-64 improves minADE from 0.5494 to 0.5391, mAP from 0.4052 to 0.4201, and soft-mAP from 0.4239 to 0.4396, while also achieving the best Miss Rate (0.1172 vs. 0.1190). Against MTR (NeurIPS 2022), MoST-SAM_H-64 reduces minADE from 0.6046 to 0.5487 and raises mAP from 0.4164 to 0.4230. Against MotionLM (ICCV 2023), MoST improves minADE from 0.5702 to 0.5487 and soft-mAP from 0.4063 to 0.4380. The older baselines (SceneTransformer, MultiPath++, MotionCNN) are surpassed by large margins, with minADE gaps exceeding 0.15. Notably, MoST achieves these gains while using only a single decoder (MoST-SAM_H-64) or camera-only tokens (MoST-VQGAN-64), demonstrating that the multi-modality scene tokens compensate for the reduced model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoST positions itself as a middle ground between two established paradigms in motion prediction: symbolic perception-based models (e.g., Wayformer, MTR, MotionLM) that consume pre-processed bounding boxes and road graphs, and fully end-to-end systems (e.g., UniAD, ST-P3) that learn directly from raw sensors. It builds directly on Wayformer as the prediction backbone but augments it with learned scene tokens derived from pre-trained image foundation models (SAM, CLIP, DINOv2, VQ-GAN) and LiDAR point clouds, rather than replacing the symbolic interface entirely. The scene decomposition strategy—splitting the point cloud into ground tiles, perception-detected agents, and open-set objects via connected component analysis—repurposes standard geometric tools (RANSAC, Kalman filtering) in a novel tokenization pipeline that has no direct precedent in the motion prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a compact set of multi-modality scene tokens (a few hundred) can substantially reduce the vulnerability of motion predictors to perception failures and incomplete road graphs, without the interpretability and compute costs of fully end-to-end systems. The release of WOMD camera embeddings (both ViT-VQGAN tokens and SAM ViT-H embeddings) provides the community with a large-scale multi-modal benchmark that was previously unavailable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other driving datasets and sensor suites, how it scales to longer prediction horizons or interactive multi-agent prediction, and whether the reliance on a pre-trained image encoder's quality (SAM ViT-H outperforms others) limits applicability in resource-constrained settings.
