## Problem

The high downstream-task performance of large kernel CNNs (ConvNeXt, RepLKNet, SLaK) is commonly attributed to their large effective receptive fields (ERFs), but this claim lacks rigorous validation, especially on tasks sensitive to feature map quality. The paper uses weakly supervised object localization (WSOL) as a new diagnostic lens to test whether ERF size is truly the main driver of performance or whether other factors, such as improved feature map structure, are responsible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241103/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images (CUB-200-2011 bird images at 384×384 for fine-tuning, ImageNet-1K at 224–384 px) with only image-level class labels, i.e., no bounding-box annotations. A large kernel CNN backbone pretrained on ImageNet-1K is fine-tuned for 400 epochs on the target dataset; the final convolution layer produces 12×12 feature maps that are combined with GAP weights to yield a class activation map, which is thresholded into a bounding box and scored by MaxBoxAcc (V1 and V2).

## Method

The paper applies the classic CAM localization method directly to three modern large kernel CNNs (ConvNeXt, RepLKNet, SLaK) fine-tuned on CUB-200-2011, without any WSOL-specific training or post-processing, and achieves 90.99% MaxBoxAcc—comparable to state-of-the-art CNN-based WSOL methods. Through systematic analysis of feature maps, GAP weights, ERF measurements, and principal component decomposition, the authors show that the key factor is not ERF size but the global activation quality of feature maps: RepLKNet and ConvNeXt produce feature maps whose first principal component already encodes a clean, object-surrounding activation pattern, and whose negative-weight maps are either deactivated (RepLKNet) or spatially aligned with positive-weight maps (ConvNeXt), thereby neutralizing the two classic CAM failure modes. A simple PC1-based localization variant on RepLKNet reaches 93.6% MaxBoxAcc, exceeding the CNN-based SOTA by 0.43%. The authors further demonstrate via randomly-initialized weight experiments that the architectural design itself (not data augmentation like CutMix) predisposes ConvNeXt and RepLKNet to generate globally activated feature maps from initialization onward.

## Results

On the CUB-200-2011 dataset, the method achieves a MaxBoxAcc of 90.99 with a RepLKNet backbone, evaluated under the standard evaluation protocol. This beats all four pre-2021 baselines by 6.44–18.29 points, while among the four 2022 baselines it wins two (F-CAM+CAM by 0.69, CREAM by 0.56) and loses two (F-CAM+LayerCAM by 1.41, BridgeGAP by 2.18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is evaluated against eight baselines on the CUB-200-2011 dataset. It wins five of them: the four pre-2021 baselines by margins of 6.44 to 18.29 points, plus F-CAM(+CAM) by 0.69 and CREAM by 0.56. It loses three 2022 baselines: F-CAM(+LayerCAM) by 1.41, BridgeGAP by 2.18, and the VGG variant of F-CAM(+LayerCAM) by 1.01. The four pre-2021 baselines are all clearly beaten, confirming the method's advantage over earlier approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: the large-kernel CNN revival (ConvNeXt, RepLKNet, SLaK) and the long-standing WSOL/CAM literature. It departs from the dominant ERF-centric explanation for large kernel CNNs' downstream success by arguing that improved feature map quality—specifically globally activated feature maps that naturally avoid CAM's local-activation pathology—is the operative mechanism. The paper repurposes CAM, typically relegated to a weak baseline in WSOL, by showing that modern CNN backbones inherently resolve the two known failure modes (negative-weight suppression and activation-area/weight-size coupling) that motivated a decade of CAM improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ERF size is not the primary driver of downstream performance challenges a widely cited justification for large kernel architectures and redirects attention toward feature map structure as the key design target. Practically, it suggests that WSOL practitioners can achieve competitive results with minimal methodological complexity (CAM + modern backbone + basic augmentation) rather than elaborate training or post-processing pipelines. Open questions remain: the paper does not fully explain *why* RepLKNet and ConvNeXt produce global feature maps while SLaK does not despite larger kernels, and the ERF-performance analysis is correlational and limited to CUB-200-2011, leaving the generality of the findings across datasets and task types untested.
