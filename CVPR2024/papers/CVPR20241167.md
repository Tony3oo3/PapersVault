## Problem

Existing 3D occupancy prediction methods face a fundamental trade-off: compressed representations like Tri-Perspective View (TPV) lose critical 3D geometric information, while raw high-resolution occupancy (OCC) grids are computationally expensive and dominated by sparse free-space voxels. Additionally, the raw OCC representation suffers from poor semantic discriminability due to severe class imbalance, causing rare objects to be suppressed by dominant background classes during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241167/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes T-frame multi-view RGB images from N_c surround-view cameras (resized to 256×704) along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a dense 3D voxel occupancy map at 200×200×16 resolution (0.4 m voxel size, spanning −40 m to 40 m in X/Y and −1 m to 5.4 m in Z) with per-voxel binary occupancy and one of 17 semantic labels. During training, sparse LiDAR point clouds provide depth supervision for the explicit view transformation, but the model is purely camera-based at inference time.

## Method

COTR constructs a compact geometry- and semantic-aware OCC representation through two key modules. The Geometry-aware Occupancy Encoder first generates a high-resolution 3D OCC feature via explicit view transformation (depth-weighted voxel pooling), then downsamples it to a compact 1/16-size representation that serves as learned queries for implicit view transformation (3D deformable spatial cross-attention and self-attention), with the downsample-upsample path structured as a U-Net to recover geometric details lost during compression. The Semantic-aware Group Decoder reformulates occupancy prediction as mask classification (inspired by MaskFormer) and introduces a Coarse-to-Fine Semantic Grouping strategy that partitions the 17 categories into K groups by semantic granularity and sample count, applying group-wise one-to-many assignment so that each mask query receives balanced supervision across foreground, background, and rare classes, thereby alleviating the suppression of tail categories by dominant ones.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes, BEVDet4D+COTR with ResNet-50 (256×704) achieves 75.0% IoU and 44.5% mIoU, outperforming the original BEVDet4D (73.8% IoU, 39.3% mIoU) by 1.2% IoU and 5.2% mIoU. Scaling to SwinTransformer-B (512×1408) further pushes mIoU to 46.2%. The method also surpasses the prior SOTA FBOcc (42.1% mIoU with test-time augmentation) by 2.3% mIoU without TTA, and outperforms VoxFormer (40.7% mIoU, ResNet-101, 900×1600) by 3.8% mIoU while using a smaller backbone and image resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COTR is integrated into four mainstream occupancy backbones (BEVDet4D, TPVFormer, SurroundOcc, OccFormer) and consistently improves all of them: +5.1% mIoU over TPVFormer (34.2→39.3), +4.7% over SurroundOcc (34.6→39.3), +3.8% over OccFormer (37.4→41.2), and +5.2% over BEVDet4D (39.3→44.5). Against the strongest external baselines, BEVDet4D+COTR (44.5% mIoU) surpasses FBOcc (42.1%, which uses test-time augmentation) by 2.3% and VoxFormer (40.7%, ResNet-101 at 900×1600) by 3.8%, despite using a smaller ResNet-50 backbone at 256×704. The SwinTransformer-B variant reaches 46.2% mIoU, the highest reported on Occ3D-nuScenes in the paper. The paper also notes that the proxy experiment (replacing predicted semantics with ground-truth labels) yields ~95% improvement on rare classes, confirming that the semantic-aware group decoder addresses a genuine bottleneck rather than a geometric one.

## Relation to Prior Work

COTR extends the BEV-to-OCC perception lineage (BEVFormer, BEVDet4D, TPVFormer, SurroundOcc) by replacing the standard high-resolution occupancy feature with a compact 1/16-scale representation built through a U-Net-style explicit-implicit view transformation, and by borrowing the mask-classification formulation from MaskFormer and the group-wise one-to-many assignment from Group DETR. Its key conceptual departure from prior occupancy methods is twofold: (1) treating the sparsity of raw OCC features as a design constraint to be exploited via downsampling rather than a cost to be tolerated, and (2) reframing the semantic head as a coarse-to-fine grouping problem to counteract class imbalance, rather than relying on a single flat classifier. The method is explicitly positioned as a plug-in module that can be grafted onto existing backbones (BEVDet4D, TPVFormer, SurroundOcc, OccFormer) without architectural overhaul.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compact OCC representation offers a practical path toward real-time 3D occupancy prediction by cutting the attention computation by roughly 5× while preserving geometric fidelity, which is important for deployment on vehicle-grade hardware. The coarse-to-fine semantic grouping provides a lightweight, training-only remedy for the severe class imbalance in driving datasets (e.g., 6 background classes accounting for 93.8% of labels) without requiring re-weighting or data augmentation. Open questions include whether the 1/16 compression ratio generalizes to denser or larger scenes (e.g., urban maps beyond 80 m), whether the grouping strategy transfers to datasets with different class distributions (SemanticKITTI, Waymo), and whether the approach can be extended to streaming/online settings with limited temporal context. Evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (Occ3D-nuScenes), so cross-dataset robustness remains unverified.
