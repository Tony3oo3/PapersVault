## Problem

Existing short-term object interaction anticipation methods rely on neural networks to implicitly extract spatiotemporal action context from fixed-sized video chunks, without explicitly modeling what has happened in the past. This limits generalization, especially for rare or long-tailed interaction scenarios, and fails to leverage the vast world knowledge embedded in pre-trained vision-language foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240472/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single last-observed video frame (not a video clip) as visual input, combined with a textual action context summary consisting of up to Lc=3 verb-noun action segments (generated by an OFA image captioning model applied to past frames, with POS-tagged extraction at cutoff distance d=4) and up to k=3 CLIP-detected salient objects per frame. The output is a set of predicted next-active objects, each with a bounding box, noun label, verb label, and time-to-contact value, where the actual interaction occurs at least Δ ≥ 0.033s after the prediction frame.

## Method

TransFusion fuses visual and language modalities via a joint self-attention mechanism: a frozen ResNet-50 produces multi-scale feature maps from the prediction frame, which are split into P×P visual tokens and projected to a common dimensionality alongside SBERT-encoded language tokens; the concatenated sequence passes through M Transformer encoder layers, after which visual tokens are reshaped back into feature maps for a standard FPN and Faster R-CNN detector. The action context is generated offline by an OFA captioning model producing verb-noun pairs per frame, aggregated across frames into contiguous action segments to reduce noise, and supplemented with CLIP zero-shot salient object detection. Training uses the standard Faster R-CNN objective (box regression + objectness) plus cross-entropy losses for noun and verb classification and an L1 loss for time-to-contact, with a background class added to the verb head. The key insight is that language summaries from foundation models provide a compact, explicit, and generalizable representation of past action context that outperforms raw video features (e.g., SlowFast) by 21% in Noun-Verb mAP, particularly on long-tailed classes.

## Results

On Ego4D v1, TransFusion achieves 24.90 Noun mAP, 10.06 Noun-Verb mAP, 7.57 Noun-TTC mAP, and 3.54 overall (Noun-Verb-TTC) mAP, outperforming all baselines on N, N-V, and A metrics. On Ego4D v2, TransFusion reaches 30.43 Noun mAP, 13.45 Noun-Verb mAP, 10.38 Noun-TTC mAP, and 5.18 overall mAP, leading on N, N-T, and A while narrowly trailing GANOv2 on N-V (13.45 vs. 13.60). All metrics are Top-5 mean Average Precision conditioned on IoU ≥ 0.5 and TTC tolerance of 0.25 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Ego4D v1, TransFusion outperforms the official FRCNN+SF baseline [22] by +4.45 on Noun mAP and +3.28 on Noun-Verb mAP, and beats InternVideo [7] on N (24.90 vs. 24.60) and N-V (10.06 vs. 9.18) while trailing it slightly on N-T (7.57 vs. 7.64). Against StillFast [40], TransFusion leads on N (24.90 vs. 19.51), N-V (10.06 vs. 9.95), and A (3.54 vs. 3.49). On Ego4D v2, TransFusion achieves a substantial +4.76 Noun mAP gain over GANOv2 [46] (30.43 vs. 25.67) and +1.24 on N-T (10.38 vs. 9.14 StillFast), while GANOv2 edges out TransFusion on N-V by 0.15 points (13.60 vs. 13.45). Overall, TransFusion wins 3 out of 4 metrics on both dataset versions, with the only losses being marginal (≤0.07 mAP) on N-T (v1) and N-V (v2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in short-term object interaction anticipation, where prior methods (FRCNN+SF, InternVideo, StillFast, GANOv2) rely on video feature extractors (SlowFast, VideoMAE, X3D) to implicitly capture temporal context from past frames. TransFusion instead represents the past action context explicitly as concise language summaries—verb-noun pairs generated by an OFA image captioning model and salient objects detected via CLIP—then fuses these with the current frame through a transformer-based joint-attention module atop a Faster R-CNN detector. The multimodal fusion design draws on vision-language transformer architectures, but the key conceptual shift is treating language as a compact, denoised encoding of temporal history rather than a query or annotation signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a few words of language context can outperform high-dimensional video features (21% N-V boost over TransFusion-Video) challenges the assumption that temporal understanding in egocentric video requires explicit video modeling, and suggests language is a more sample-efficient and generalizable representation for action context. The approach shows particular gains on long-tailed noun classes, hinting that foundation-model knowledge transfers to rare scenarios poorly represented in training data. Limitations include a noted domain gap between generated captions and the training vocabulary, sensitivity to captioning quality, and evaluation on a single dataset (Ego4D). Open questions include whether this language-summarization strategy extends to longer prediction horizons, other video reasoning tasks, or benefits from richer multimodal inputs (e.g., optical flow, audio).
