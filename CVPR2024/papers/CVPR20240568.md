## Problem

2D lifting methods for text-to-3D generation achieve high fidelity and diversity but suffer from severe multi-view inconsistency (Janus problems) because text prompts alone cannot provide sufficient 3D geometric guidance. Conversely, 3D diffusion models ensure consistency but produce low-quality, limited-diversity results due to scarce 3D training data. The paper asks whether coarse 3D knowledge from an off-the-shelf 3D diffusion model can be exploited as guidance to bridge this gap without retraining a costly viewpoint-aware model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt as input and produces a textured 3D mesh encoded via DMTet at 128 resolution, compatible with standard graphics engines. The 3D prior is generated by Shap-E and the 2D diffusion backbone is Stable Diffusion 2.1 base; the entire pipeline runs on a single RTX 3090 (24 GB) in approximately 25 minutes. Camera sampling uses a fixed radius of 2.5 with 45° FOV, azimuth in [-180°, 180°], and elevation in [-30°, 30°].

## Method

Sherpa3D first sculpts a coarse 3D prior from Shap-E and encodes it in a DMTet network, then derives two guidance signals from the prior's rendered normal maps to steer the 2D lifting optimization: (1) a structural guidance that computes the magnitude of the first-order gradient of Gaussian-smoothed normals and penalizes deviation from the prior's structural descriptor, preserving geometric fidelity; and (2) a semantic guidance that extracts CLIP feature maps from the normal maps and enforces cosine similarity across views, maintaining high-level 3D coherence. A step annealing function γ(λ) = λ·exp(−β·max(0, n_cur − m)) progressively reduces the 3D guidance weight so that early optimization is strongly anchored to the prior while later steps let the 2D diffusion model enrich details. Geometry is optimized by feeding normal maps as shape conditioning into Stable Diffusion via SDS, and a separate appearance model is then refined with an additional SDS pass on rendered RGB images.

## Results

On the user study (120 prompts, 50 raters, 1–10 scale), Sherpa3D achieves 8.95/10 for multi-view consistency and 8.74/10 for overall generation quality, substantially outperforming all five baselines. On CLIP R-Precision (100 COCO prompts), Sherpa3D scores 72.3% (B/32), 75.6% (B/16), and 79.3% (L/14), approaching ground-truth image scores of 77.3% and 79.2% and surpassing Magic3D (71.5/73.8/76.1) and DreamFusion (70.3/73.2/75.0). The method runs in under 25 minutes on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sherpa3D wins decisively on both user-study metrics against all five quantitative baselines. The largest gap is versus Fantasia3D (consistency 8.95 vs 3.83; quality 8.74 vs 5.90), which suffers from severe multi-face artifacts. Against the strongest 2D-lifting baseline ProlificDreamer, Sherpa3D leads by 3.17 points in consistency (8.95 vs 5.78) and 1.72 in quality (8.74 vs 7.02). On CLIP R-Precision, Sherpa3D edges out Magic3D by 0.8–3.2 points across three CLIP variants and DreamFusion by 2.0–4.3 points, while Shap-E trails by over 30 points due to its coarse geometry. In a qualitative generalizability comparison (Figure 6), Zero123—fine-tuned on 3D data—degrades on open-vocabulary prompts, whereas Sherpa3D maintains coherent multi-view output without any 3D-data fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sherpa3D bridges two established but conflicting research lines in text-to-3D: 3D diffusion models (Shap-E, Point-E) that ensure multi-view consistency but suffer from limited quality and generalizability due to scarce 3D training data, and 2D lifting methods (DreamFusion, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer, Fantasia3D) that achieve high fidelity and diversity via Score Distillation Sampling but produce notorious multi-face Janus artifacts. Rather than retraining a viewpoint-aware diffusion model (as Zero123 or MVDream do) or relying on prompt engineering, the paper repurposes an off-the-shelf 3D diffusion model as a coarse geometric prior and derives two complementary guidance signals—structural (gradient-based normal descriptors) and semantic (CLIP features)—to steer the 2D lifting optimization. The geometry-appearance disentanglement follows Fantasia3D, the 3D representation uses DMTet, and the SDS loss follows DreamFusion, making the contribution primarily a new optimization strategy layered on existing components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single-GPU, 25-minute pipeline that produces textured meshes compatible with standard graphics engines, addressing the Janus problem without the cost of retraining viewpoint-aware models or the overfitting risk of fine-tuning on limited 3D datasets. The dual-guidance-plus-annealing recipe is a reusable pattern that could be transplanted to other 2D-lifting pipelines. However, quality is bounded by the backbone models (Shap-E and Stable Diffusion v2.1 base), and the authors acknowledge that larger backbones (SDXL, DeepFloyd) are needed for further gains. Open questions include whether the coarse-prior-guidance paradigm extends to text-to-4D generation, whether it generalizes to scene-level (rather than object-level) synthesis, and how sensitive the results are to the quality of the 3D diffusion prior.
