## Problem

While deep learning has advanced image deblurring through architectural innovations, data augmentation for deblurring remains underexplored. Existing GAN-based reblurring approaches (e.g., BGAN) produce uncontrollable and unstable results, limiting their utility as augmentation strategies. The paper addresses this by asking whether blur patterns can be implicitly parameterized in a continuous space to enable stable, controllable generation of diverse training pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242451/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sharp RGB image S and a pixel-wise blur condition map C = [x; y; z] ∈ R^{H×W×3} as input, where (x, y) encode normalized blur orientations and z encodes normalized blur magnitude at each pixel. The condition map is derived from bidirectional optical flows between consecutive sharp frames, normalized so z ∈ [0,1]. The output is a single blurred image B consistent with the specified per-pixel blur trajectories. Training uses 128×128 crops; inference generates full 1280×720 images in ~3.4 s on an RTX 3090.

## Method

ID-Blau parameterizes blur as a continuous pixel-wise condition field: bidirectional optical flows between consecutive sharp frames are averaged and decomposed into unit orientation vectors and a magnitude channel, yielding a 3-channel blur condition map. A conditional diffusion model (DDPM training with T=2000 steps, L1 noise-prediction loss) is trained to denoise a blurred image conditioned on the sharp image and the blur condition map, learning the mapping S, C → B. At inference, DDIM sampling with only 20 deterministic steps generates a blurred image from pure Gaussian noise given S and C. The key insight is that by sampling or perturbing blur conditions in the continuous field (e.g., rotating orientations, scaling magnitudes), one can synthesize diverse blurred images never seen in the original dataset, providing a stable and controllable augmentation pipeline that outperforms GAN-based reblurring by 0.47–1.24 dB in PSNR.

## Results

ID-Blau consistently improves four prominent deblurring models (MIMO-UNet+, Restormer, Stripformer, FFTformer) across GoPro, HIDE, and RealBlur datasets, with average PSNR gains of +0.45 dB (GoPro), +0.48 dB (HIDE), +0.46 dB (RealBlur-J), and +0.50 dB (RealBlur-R). The largest single-model gain is Stripformer on RealBlur-J (+1.29 dB PSNR) and RealBlur-R (+1.22 dB). With ID-Blau, FFTformer reaches 34.36 dB on GoPro and 31.94 dB on HIDE, while Stripformer achieves 33.77 dB on RealBlur-J and 41.06 dB on RealBlur-R, establishing new SOTA on GoPro and HIDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's direct head-to-head comparison against another augmentation method is with BGAN [42] (a GAN-based reblurring model) applied to the DBGAN deblurring network. ID-Blau (with diffusion) improves DBGAN's PSNR from 30.43 dB to 31.67 dB (+1.24 dB), while BGAN only achieves 30.92 dB (+0.49 dB), giving ID-Blau a 0.75 dB advantage over BGAN. Even the simplified ID-Blau without the diffusion process (ID-Blau†, 31.57 dB) outperforms BGAN by 0.65 dB, demonstrating that the continuous blur condition map itself provides substantial benefit over GAN-based synthesis. The paper attributes BGAN's inferiority to GANs' uncontrollable generation and poor mode convergence, which limit the diversity of synthesized blur patterns. In the broader context of Table 1, ID-Blau serves as a plug-and-play augmentation that improves all four tested deblurring architectures (MIMO-UNet+, Restormer, Stripformer, FFTformer) without modifying their architectures, with the largest gains observed on Stripformer (+1.29 dB on RealBlur-J) and MIMO-UNet+ on HIDE (+0.68 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

ID-Blau builds directly on the DDPM training framework (Ho et al., 2020) and DDIM accelerated sampling (Song et al., 2021), repurposing the conditional diffusion paradigm from image synthesis to the reblurring task. It departs from the GAN-based reblurring line (BGAN, Zhang et al., 2020) by replacing unstable adversarial training with a stable diffusion objective, and from the GoPro dataset's explicit frame-accumulation blur model (Nah et al., 2017) by introducing an implicit, pixel-wise continuous blur condition field parameterized by optical-flow-derived orientations and magnitudes. The key conceptual shift is treating blur as a sampleable continuous field rather than a discrete set of pre-computed kernels or accumulated frames, enabling controllable generation of unseen blur patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ID-Blau offers a practical, architecture-agnostic data augmentation plug-in that consistently improves four SOTA deblurring models by 0.45–0.50 dB without modifying their architectures, making it immediately adoptable for deblurring practitioners. The continuous blur condition field is a clean formulation that could generalize to other motion-related degradations (e.g., video deblurring, motion-compensated super-resolution). However, the method is limited to offline pre-generation of training pairs, relies on optical flow for blur condition computation (which may not capture all real-world blur physics), and has only been validated on GoPro-scale synthetic and semi-real datasets; whether the approach transfers to fully real-world, uncontrolled blur remains open.
