## Problem

Existing scene graph generation methods operate in closed-world settings with limited predicate categories, or address only simplified open-vocabulary subtasks such as novel entity detection or predicate classification with ground-truth entity pairs. No prior work provides an end-to-end framework that generates complete scene graphs with both seen and unseen predicate triplets directly from pixels, and previous open-vocabulary approaches often require costly additional pre-training of vision-language models for relation modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (384×384, as constrained by the BLIP ViT-B/16 backbone) and produces a scene graph containing entity bounding boxes, entity category labels, and relation triplets (subject, predicate, object). During inference, the VLM generates M diverse scene graph sequences via nucleus sampling (up to 1024 tokens each), from which a heuristic rule extracts triplets matching the pattern 'subject [ENT] predicate [REL] object [ENT]'. A post-processing step applies NMS and scores triplets by the product of entity and predicate prediction scores to yield the final graph.

## Method

PGSG formulates open-vocabulary SGG as an image-to-sequence generation task on a generative VLM (BLIP), introducing scene graph prompts with relation-aware tokens [ENT] and [REL] that encode the subject-predicate-object compositional structure. A dedicated Entity Grounding Module uses cross-attention between pooled hidden states of entity token sequences and image features (via a 6-layer transformer encoder-decoder) to predict bounding boxes, decoupling spatial localization from the autoregressive text generation. A parameter-free Category Conversion Module maps vocabulary-space token scores to target benchmark category spaces by indexing and averaging scores over tokenized category names, with a boost factor β for exact matches. Training uses a multi-task loss combining standard next-token language modeling and bounding box regression (GIOU + L1), requiring only fine-tuning on target SGG datasets without additional VLM pre-training. The unified generative formulation also enables direct transfer of relation-modeling knowledge to downstream VL tasks (VQA, grounding, captioning) by initializing their VLM parameters from the SGG-trained model.

## Results

On the open-vocabulary SGG benchmarks, PGSG achieves state-of-the-art performance. On Visual Genome SGDet, PGSG reaches mR@100 = 8.3 and R@100 = 19.1 (all categories), with novel-class mR@100 = 5.2, outperforming VS3 (mR@100 = 5.7) and SGTR (mR@100 = 5.4) which cannot predict novel predicates at all. On OpenImages-V6 SGDet, PGSG achieves mR@100 = 23.0 and R@100 = 43.3, surpassing SGTR (mR@100 = 16.7, R@100 = 38.4) by 6.3 and 4.9 points respectively. On the SGCls setting (VG), PGSG* improves mR@100 by 6.5 over SVRP (11.0 vs. 4.5) and R@100 by 5.7 (27.2 vs. 21.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGSG consistently outperforms all open-vocabulary SGG baselines. On VG SGDet, PGSG (mR@100 = 8.3) beats VS3 (5.7) by 2.6 and SGTR (5.4) by 2.9, and is the only method that predicts novel predicates (novel mR@100 = 5.2 vs. 0.0 for both baselines). On OIv6 SGDet, PGSG (mR@100 = 23.0) surpasses SGTR (16.7) by 6.3 points, a substantial margin. In the SGCls setting on VG, PGSG* (mR@100 = 11.0) improves over SVRP (4.5) by 6.5 points, and in PCls, PGSG* (13.9) edges out CaCao (12.6) by 1.3. In the close-vocabulary setting (Table 2), PGSG-c is competitive but slightly behind SGTR on PSG (mR@100: 22.1 vs. 23.5) and VG (12.7 vs. 15.2), which the authors attribute to lower input resolution (384×384 vs. 800×1333) and single-stage training. For downstream VL tasks, PGSG-initialized BLIP improves GQA overall accuracy by 1.7 (64.2 vs. 62.5) and RefCOCO val by 2.9 (86.0 vs. 83.1) over the initial pre-trained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BLIP's image-to-text generation paradigm and repurposes it for scene graph generation by formulating SGG as a sequence prediction problem, departing from the dominant two-stage detection-plus-classification architecture used by SGTR, SSRCNN, and similar methods. It extends the open-vocabulary SGG line (SVRP, CaCao, VS3), which had only addressed simplified sub-settings such as novel entity detection or predicate classification with ground-truth entities, to the more general end-to-end setting where both entities and predicates are generated freely from pixels. The plug-and-play entity grounding module and parameter-free category conversion module are the key architectural additions that bridge the gap between free-form text generation and structured scene graph output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of SGG and downstream VL tasks (VQA, visual grounding, captioning) under a single generative VLM framework offers a practical pathway for transferring explicit relational knowledge without task-specific pre-training, which prior open-vocabulary SGG methods required. However, the close-vocabulary SGG results remain below SOTA due to the lower input resolution (384×384) and single-stage training of the BLIP backbone, and the VL task improvements are modest (1–3 points), suggesting the relational knowledge transfer is real but not yet transformative. Open questions include whether higher-resolution VLMs or multi-stage training can close the gap on standard SGG benchmarks, and whether the framework generalizes beyond BLIP-family models to larger or more recent VLMs.
