## Problem

Non-Transferable Learning (NTL) was proposed to protect model IP by restricting generalization toward target domains, but the robustness of the resulting 'transferability barrier' has not been well studied. This paper reveals that NTL models exhibit generalization impairments on third-party domains that mirror target-domain impairments (over-confident predictions on an implicit target-domain class), making the barrier fragile and breakable with only a small fraction of source domain data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240969/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker requires a white-box NTL model and a small subset of source domain data (as little as 10%) with labels, plus a perturbation collection spanning additive (Gaussian/adversarial noise), multiplicative (speckle noise), and convolution (motion blur) operations. No target domain data is needed at any point. The output is a fine-tuned NTL model whose target domain accuracy is recovered (up to ~72% improvement) while source domain accuracy is largely preserved, effectively converting the NTL model back into a standard SL-like model.

## Method

TransNTL exploits the observation that slightly perturbed source domain data evokes the same impairment patterns (over-confidence and implicit target-domain class predictions) as the unavailable target domain, serving as a 'free lunch' for breaking the barrier. The method applies a group of diverse perturbations to the few source domain samples to generate third-party domains, then fine-tunes the NTL model under an impairment-repair self-distillation loss that minimizes KL divergence between the model's predictions on perturbed inputs and its predictions on clean source inputs, teaching the model source-domain-consistent behavior on shifted distributions. To counteract the dominant generalization of the non-transferable task (identified via loss-landscape analysis showing a sharp source-domain minimum and a flat target-domain minimum), TransNTL incorporates an ASAM-style sharpness-aware minimization term that flattens the loss landscape around source and third-party distributions. The full objective is a bi-level optimization: min_θ max_{‖T_θ^{-1}ε‖≤ρ} L_irft(θ+ε), where the inner maximization is solved in closed form via a gradient-direction parameter perturbation.

## Results

Using only 10% source domain data, TransNTL recovers target-domain accuracy by up to +49.2% (NTL on STL10→CIFAR10: 11.1→60.3%) and +55.0% (CUTI on STL10→CIFAR10: 10.2→65.2%), while maintaining source-domain accuracy within ~4% of the pre-trained model. Across all five benchmark tasks (Digits, CIFAR10/STL10, VisDA), TransNTL consistently outperforms every attacking baseline, with most baselines achieving less than +5% target-domain improvement. On the ownership verification task (VisDA-T→VisDA-T(P)), TransNTL recovers patched-data accuracy by up to +71.7%, effectively cracking the verification mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransNTL decisively outperforms all six attacking baselines across every task. FTAL and RTAL (backdoor-removal methods from [1]) are essentially ineffective, achieving ≤+2.8% target-domain improvement while often degrading source accuracy. FinePruning [37] and NAD [35] show only marginal gains (+0 to +3%) on most tasks, though NAD occasionally produces large but unreliable jumps (e.g., +40.2% on CUTI STL10→CIFAR10) at the cost of catastrophic source-domain collapse (9.0% on VisDA-T NTL). i-BAU [57] achieves moderate recovery on CUTI STL10→CIFAR10 (+26.3%) but fails on the corresponding NTL task (+0.0%). FT-SAM [61] is the strongest baseline, reaching +16.8% on CUTI CIFAR10→STL10, yet it frequently destroys source-domain accuracy (e.g., 12.0% on VisDA-T NTL, 28.4% on VisDA-T CUTI). In contrast, TransNTL achieves +39.6 to +55.0% target-domain recovery while keeping source accuracy within 4% of the pre-trained model, making it the only method that simultaneously breaks the transferability barrier and preserves source-domain utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first systematic attack on Non-Transferable Learning (NTL) models, building directly on the NTL [49] and CUTI [50] frameworks that it targets. Methodologically, it repurposes three established ideas—self-distillation (using source-domain predictions as teacher signals), perturbation-based data augmentation, and ASAM-style sharpness-aware minimization [31]—into a unified impairment-repair fine-tuning pipeline. Conceptually, it departs from prior NTL evaluations (which tested resistance to watermark removal with up to 30% source data) by showing that the transferability barrier is not a targeted degradation but a broad generalization impairment that extends to any distribution shift, enabling a 'free lunch' attack requiring no target-domain knowledge at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reveals that NTL-based IP protection (ownership verification and applicability authorization) is fundamentally fragile: a few source-domain images with simple perturbations can recover up to ~72% of target-domain accuracy, undermining the security guarantees that MLaaS providers would rely on. The proposed defense (R-CUTI) only partially mitigates the attack (still allowing 16.6% recovery on VisDA), leaving open the question of whether a fully robust NTL formulation exists without sacrificing the source-domain performance that makes NTL useful. More broadly, the loss-landscape analysis (sharp source minima vs. flat target minima) suggests that any method relying on asymmetric optimization pressure across domains may exhibit similar unintended generalization impairments, a concern that extends beyond NTL to other domain-specific regularization schemes.
