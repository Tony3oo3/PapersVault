## Problem

Existing unlearnable example (ULE) methods render data unlearnable by all networks, including those of the data owner, which is overly restrictive for legitimate use cases. The paper identifies the gap that data protection should be conditional: authorized users should retain the ability to train on their own data while unauthorized parties cannot, a capability no prior ULE framework provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes original RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 256×256 for TinyImageNet) along with the authorized network's architecture, initialization parameters, and a sampled training trajectory (intermediate epoch weights). It produces perturbed images with ℓ∞-bounded noise (ρ = 0.04) that are visually indistinguishable from the originals, plus a trained generator network G. The perturbation is irreversible: once UGEs are published, the original data is permanently replaced for all parties.

## Method

A generator G is trained to map original images to UGEs via three jointly optimized losses. The gradient matching loss (L_gm) aligns the cosine distance between gradients computed on original and UGE inputs at randomly sampled training epochs of the authorized network, preserving the authorized training trajectory. The feature distance loss (L_fd) leverages CLIP's pretrained image encoder as a shared feature space, pushing UGE features away from original features via MSE and further enforcing transferability through a triplet loss that uses CLIP's text encoder to pull UGE features toward the least-similar class text embedding. The undistill loss (L_ud) optimizes the data perturbation (not network parameters) to minimize KL-divergence-based distillation from the authorized network to an arbitrary hacker network, blocking a second attack vector. The key architectural choice is using CLIP's encoder as the common feature space, which makes the unlearnability perturbation transferable across diverse hacker network backbones without per-network optimization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the full UGEs framework maintains 93.89% accuracy on the authorized ResNet-18 (vs. 95.05% on original data, a 1.16% drop) while reducing hacker network accuracy by 57–69% (e.g., from 95.05% to 26.12% on ResNet-18, from 86.57% to 26.46% on CNN). On CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, authorized accuracy drops are 3.56% and 3.54% respectively, while hacker accuracy drops exceed 35–62%. In the direct ULE comparison (Table 3), the feature-distance loss alone (Ours L_fd) achieves 22.59% ULE accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 9.35% on CIFAR-100, placing it competitively among existing unlearnable-example methods.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its feature-distance loss (L_fd) against five existing ULE methods: EM, TAP, NTGA, REM, and CUDA. In pure unlearnability (lower ULE accuracy is better), EM [9] is the strongest baseline at 13.20% on CIFAR-10 and 1.60% on CIFAR-100, followed by NTGA [38] at 16.27% and 3.22%. The proposed Ours(L_fd) at 22.59%/9.35% is competitive but trails EM and NTGA on both datasets, while slightly outperforming REM [6] (27.09%/10.14%) on CIFAR-10 and matching TAP [5] (22.51%/13.75%) on CIFAR-100. CUDA [25] (18.48%/12.69%) sits between the proposed method and the top baselines. The critical distinction is that none of the five ULE baselines preserve authorized-network learnability; UGEs uniquely achieves conditional learnability (93.89% authorized accuracy on CIFAR-10) while simultaneously degrading hacker performance, a capability absent from all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Unlearnable Examples (ULE) paradigm of Huang et al. (ICLR 2020), which renders data unlearnable for all networks via bilevel optimization, by introducing conditional learnability: data remains learnable for a pre-defined authorized network while being unlearnable for others. The unlearnability mechanism departs from the traditional error-minimizing noise approach and instead leverages CLIP's shared image-text feature space with a triplet loss to achieve transferable unlearnability across diverse hacker architectures. The undistill loss component adapts the knowledge undistillation framework of Ma et al. (ICLR 2020) from protecting network IP to protecting the authorized network's knowledge from distillation-based extraction by hackers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in data privacy: existing ULE methods make data useless even for the legitimate owner, whereas UGEs enable authorized usage while blocking unauthorized training. Key limitations include performance degradation with multiple authorized networks (Table 2), restriction to classification tasks, and the assumption that the protector can pre-define the authorized network architecture and initialization. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based feature space approach generalizes to non-image modalities, how robust UGEs are against adaptive attacks where the hacker knows the generation process, and whether the framework can be extended to generative or multi-task settings.
