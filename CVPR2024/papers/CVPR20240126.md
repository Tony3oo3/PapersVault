## Problem

Sign Language Translation (SLT) requires cross-modal understanding of visual and linguistic cues, yet paired sign-text data remains scarce, making it difficult to train dedicated architectures effectively. While large language models have demonstrated strong multilingual translation by leveraging shared linguistic properties, no prior work has explored whether an off-the-shelf, frozen LLM can perform ELLT at all. The core obstacle is the large representational gap between continuous high-dimensional sign video features and the discrete text token space that LLMs operate in.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw sign video (a sequence of RGB frames) as input, segments it into short overlapping clips of 13 frames with a 4-frame stride, and converts each clip into a discrete sign token. A short text prompt is prepended to instruct the frozen LLM to produce the translation. The output is a natural-language sentence in the target language (German for Phoenix-2014T, Chinese for CSL-Daily), generated autoregressively by the LLM. Notably, the LLM itself is never updated; only the sign-to-token conversion modules and a small projection layer are trained.

## Method

SignLLM regularizes sign videos into language-like token sequences through two modules. The VQ-Sign module quantizes compact visual features (from a ResNet-18 + Conv3D encoderer) into discrete character-level tokens via a 256-entry codebook learned with a self-supervised context-prediction contrastive loss, avoiding costly video reconstruction. The CRA module then composes character tokens into word-level tokens by formulating the character-to-word assignment as an optimal transport problem solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm, selecting the codebook size that maximizes entropy reduction per token added. A Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) loss aligns both character- and word-level sign token embeddings toward the LLM's text embedding space. The resulting word-level sign sentence is projected into LLaMA-7B's embedding space and fed to the frozen LLM, which is fine-tuned only through a cross-entropy similarity loss on the generated text while keeping all LLM weights fixed.

## Results

On Phoenix-2014T (test), SignLLM achieves BLEU-1 of 45.21, BLEU-4 of 23.40, and ROUGE-L of 44.49, outperforming the previous best gloss-free method GFSLT-VLP (BLEU-1 43.71, BLEU-4 21.44, ROUGE-L 42.49) by 1.5, 1.96, and 2.0 points respectively. On CSL-Daily (test), SignLLM achieves BLEU-1 of 39.55, BLEU-4 of 15.75, and ROUGE-L of 39.91, surpassing GFSLT-VLP (BLEU-1 39.37, BLEU-4 11.00, ROUGE-L 36.44) by 0.18, 4.75, and 3.47 points. These are the best gloss-free results reported on both benchmarks, though gloss-based methods (e.g., TS-SLT) still lead by a wider margin due to their use of sign gloss annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignLLM outperforms all gloss-free baselines on both Phoenix-2014T and CSL-Daily across every reported metric (BLEU-1 through BLEU-4 and ROUGE-L). The closest competitor is GFSLT-VLP [72], which SignLLM beats by 1.5 BLEU-1 and 2.0 ROUGE-L on Phoenix-2014T, and by 0.18 BLEU-1 and 3.47 ROUGE-L on CSL-Daily. Earlier gloss-free methods such as GASLT [67] (BLEU-1 39.07 on Phoenix-2014T) and TSPNet [37] (BLEU-1 36.10) trail SignLLM by 6+ BLEU-1 points. Gloss-based methods (TS-SLT [14], MMTLB [13], SLTUNET [69]) still achieve substantially higher scores (e.g., TS-SLT reaches BLEU-1 54.90 on Phoenix-2014T), reflecting the advantage of using sign gloss annotations that SignLLM deliberately avoids. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the frozen LLaMA-7B backbone is critical: replacing it with a lightweight mBART generator drops BLEU-1 from 45.21 to 27.20, and substituting T5 reduces it to 44.03.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SLT paradigm of training dedicated encoder-decoder architectures (RNNs, Transformers, GCNs) by instead treating sign video as a 'language' and delegating translation to a frozen LLM (LLaMA-7B). The VQ-Sign module adapts the vector quantization mechanism from VQ-VAE [59] but replaces costly autoencoding with a contrastive context-prediction objective inspired by CPC [46], while the CRA module borrows optimal transport formulations from NMT vocabulary learning [65, 16] to compose character-level tokens into word-level tokens. The sign-text alignment via MMD [57] bridges the distributional gap between the learned sign codebook and the LLM's text embedding space, a step absent from prior SLT work that typically trains a task-specific decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a frozen, off-the-shelf LLM can perform SLT without fine-tuning its weights, opening a path toward leveraging ever-larger multilingual LLMs for sign language understanding as they improve. However, the empirical gains over the previous gloss-free SOTA (GFSLT-VLP) are modest (~1.5 BLEU-1 on Phoenix-2014T test), and the approach is validated on only two relatively small datasets with a single 7B LLM. Key open questions include whether the language-like regularization generalizes to larger sign vocabularies (e.g., How2Sign, BOBSL), whether scaling to larger LLMs yields disproportionate gains, and whether the optimal-transport codebook construction remains tractable as sign video complexity grows.
