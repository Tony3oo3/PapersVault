## Problem

Existing passport-based deep model IP protection methods require retraining the owner model to derive a new user model for every new passport issued, making them inflexible, non-scalable, and unable to support agile licensing. They also lack a mechanism to verify individual user licenses (accountability) and remain vulnerable to the advanced Expanded Residual Block (ERB) ambiguity attack that exploits the solution space of fully-connected layers to forge passports.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242014/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images for classification (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for Caltech) and produces class labels via the deployment branch. For verification, the owner-side passport images (randomly sampled from COCO test set) are fed through the model to derive affine factors for the verification branch, while the user-side passport—generated by hiding a user's ID image into the owner-side passport via the ISN—is combined with a private steganographic key (sampled from DTD) to reveal the hidden ID image. The model is trained once on the target dataset, and new users are admitted post-training without any retraining.

## Method

The Steganographic Passport decouples ownership verification from license verification using a key-based invertible steganographic network (ISN) that hides each user's ID image into the owner-side passport to produce a user-side passport, eliminating the need for per-user retraining. An irreversible SHA-512 hash maps the owner-side passport to a binary model signature that constrains the signs of passport-derived affine factors during training, coupling convolution and TLP weights to resist ERB attacks. The verification branch employs an activation-level obfuscation where passport-derived affine factors are composed with additional temporal affine factors before a Dynamic ReLU-like activation, making the branch highly sensitive to any passport or weight tampering while leaving the deployment branch unaffected. A balance loss explicitly aligns the composite affine factors of the verification branch with those of the deployment branch, ensuring negligible accuracy difference between the two branches so that post-deployment modifications are detectable via the absolute performance difference.

## Results

On ResNet-18, the proposed Steganographic Passport achieves a mean deployment/verification accuracy of 71.73%/71.73% across four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Caltech-101, Caltech-256) with both BN and GN, yielding an absolute difference (AD) of 0.00% between the two branches. On AlexNet, the mean is 67.50%/67.49% with AD of 0.01%. Under the ERB ownership ambiguity attack, the verification branch accuracy drops to 57.91% (ResNet-18) and 14.48% (AlexNet) with AD values of 16.20% and 52.66% respectively, the most severe degradation among all compared methods, confirming superior attack resistance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to DeepIPR [6], the proposed method substantially outperforms on both accuracy and branch consistency: on ResNet-18 the mean accuracy improves from 68.01% to 71.73% (deployment) while AD drops from 1.30% to 0.00%; on AlexNet the gap is even larger (60.14% → 67.50% mean, AD 3.07% → 0.01%). Against PAN [30], the proposed method trades a small accuracy margin (72.34% vs 71.73% mean on ResNet-18) for a dramatically lower AD (1.03% vs 0.00%), indicating far tighter coupling between deployment and verification branches. Against TdN [20], the accuracy difference is minimal (72.13% vs 71.73% on ResNet-18) but AD is reduced from 0.38% to 0.00%. Under the ERB ambiguity attack, the proposed method produces the lowest verification-branch accuracy (57.91% ResNet-18, 14.48% AlexNet) and highest AD (16.20%, 52.66%), meaning an attacker who forges the passport causes the most severe performance collapse, making successful forgery infeasible. Under fine-tuning removal attacks, the proposed method retains the second-highest signature accuracy (97.03% mean) while suffering the lowest post-attack deployment fidelity (67.28%), further deterring removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the passport-based deep model protection lineage (DeepIPR, PAN, TdN) by decoupling ownership verification from per-user license verification, a gap the authors identify as requiring costly retraining in prior methods. The steganographic component repurposes the key-based Invertible Steganographic Network (ISN) within the HiNet framework to embed user identity images into the owner-side passport, while the hash-based signature mechanism builds on TdN's trapdoor normalization concept. The activation-level obfuscation (Dynamic ReLU) and balance loss function are novel architectural additions designed to counter the ERB ambiguity attack, which prior passport methods were shown to be vulnerable to.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical scalability bottleneck in AIaaS model licensing: existing passport methods require retraining for each new user, making them non-scalable for complex models. By enabling ad-hoc user admission via steganographic ID embedding, the method offers a more flexible licensing workflow. However, experiments are limited to small architectures (AlexNet, ResNet-18) and small datasets (CIFAR, Caltech), leaving open whether the steganographic capacity and hash-based signature constraints scale to larger models like Vision Transformers or ImageNet-scale training. The single steganographic key as a point of failure and the reliance on PSNR thresholds for verification also raise practical security questions.
