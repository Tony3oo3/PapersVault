## Problem

Existing text-to-image customization methods either require test-time fine-tuning (taking 30 seconds to 30 minutes) or produce unsatisfactory tuning-free results, and all of them can only handle directive or descriptive prompts (captions, instructions) rather than ambiguous or conversational user input. This limits real-world usability where users may have only a vague creative intent and expect the system to infer their intention and explain its output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241194/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (any domain), a free-form text prompt that can be declarative, interrogative, or ambiguous (e.g., 'I want to generate something creative, can you help me?'), and optionally a second image whose CLIP embedding provides additional semantic conditioning. It produces a generated creative image in 2–5 seconds without any test-time fine-tuning, along with a natural-language explanation and elaboration of the generated content — a capability no prior customization method offers.

## Method

CAFE couples a Llama-2-13B-chat multimodal LLM with a modified Stable Diffusion 2 UNet. The reference image is encoded by DINOv2-Giant and injected into the LLM via a LLaVA-style vision projection; the LLM outputs both a natural-language explanation (via its LM head) and special tokens projected to a CLIP image embedding through a learned head H. The diffusion model receives both this CLIP embedding and the DINO patch features through separate cross-attention branches, combining high-level semantic guidance with fine-grained identity preservation. A key design choice is making H(s) a CLIP embedding, which decouples MLLM and diffusion model training (the diffusion model never appears in the MLLM loss) and enables flexible conditioning on arbitrary image embeddings. Training uses a two-stage self-improvement via distillation (SID) strategy: first train on ProFusion-generated samples filtered by CLIP/DINO similarity, then use the trained model itself to generate additional training data (controlling identity vs. semantics via a blending coefficient α), eliminating the need for expensive human filtering and enabling scalable dataset construction.

## Results

On DreamBench (object domain), CAFE achieves DINO 0.715, CLIP-I 0.827, and CLIP-T 0.294, making it competitive with the strongest tuning-free baselines (SuTI: 0.741/0.819/0.304; Kosmos-G: 0.694/0.847/0.287) while being the only method that also outputs text explanations. On the human face domain, CAFE achieves ID 0.464 and CLIP-T 0.297, outperforming all compared methods including ProFusion (0.432/0.293) and E4T (0.426/0.277). CAFE generates images in 2–5 seconds without any test-time fine-tuning, compared to 15–20 s for SuTI and up to 30 min for Textual Inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAFE clearly outperforms all fine-tuning-based methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion, BLIP-Diffusion) on identity preservation (DINO) and image-image similarity (CLIP-I), e.g., DINO 0.715 vs. 0.569 for Textual Inversion and 0.668 for DreamBooth, while remaining competitive on CLIP-T. Among tuning-free methods, CAFE beats ELITE (0.715 vs. 0.621 DINO) and Subject-Diffusion (0.827 vs. 0.787 CLIP-I) on most metrics, and edges out Kosmos-G on DINO (0.715 vs. 0.694) and CLIP-T (0.294 vs. 0.287) though Kosmos-G leads on CLIP-I (0.847 vs. 0.827). SuTI, built on the stronger Imagen backbone, slightly outperforms CAFE on DINO (0.741 vs. 0.715) and CLIP-T (0.304 vs. 0.294) but requires 15–20 s per generation versus CAFE's 2–5 s. On the human face domain, CAFE achieves the best ID score (0.464) and CLIP-T (0.297), surpassing ProFusion (0.432/0.293), E4T (0.426/0.277), DreamBooth (0.307/0.283), and Textual Inversion (0.210/0.257). Qualitatively, CAFE also outperforms PhotoVerse on identity preservation and detail fidelity (e.g., capturing a tiny microphone in a Funko Pop figure).

## Relation to Prior Work

CAFE builds on the LLaVA-style vision-to-language projection architecture [16] to inject image encoder features into a frozen Llama-2 backbone, and on Stable Diffusion [26] as the generation backbone, replacing its text encoder with cross-attention layers that consume CLIP global embeddings and DINOv2 patch embeddings. It departs from the dominant line of customization work (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion, and tuning-free variants like BLIP-Diffusion, ELITE, SuTI, Kosmos-G) by reframing the problem as a conversational assistant: rather than accepting only directive captions or instructions, the LLM infers user intent from ambiguous or interrogative prompts and additionally produces natural-language explanations for its outputs. The self-improvement via distillation (SID) strategy for dataset construction is a novel training pipeline that replaces expensive human curation with iterative self-generation and automatic filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAFE demonstrates that a tuning-free, LLM-augmented pipeline can match or exceed fine-tuning-based personalization methods (e.g., outperforming ProFusion and E4T on human-face identity) while running in 2–5 seconds, making interactive customization practical for real-world applications. The SID ablation showing that self-generated data (D3) outperforms human-filtered data (D2) on identity score challenges the common assumption that human curation is necessary for quality, and offers a scalable path to larger training sets. Open questions include whether the frozen LLM backbone limits the depth of multi-turn reasoning, how the approach generalizes to multi-concept or compositional generation, and whether the reliance on Stable Diffusion 2 (rather than newer architectures) constrains ceiling performance.
