## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language models still fail at compositional visual reasoning tasks such as counting, spatial reasoning, and multi-step knowledge retrieval. While LLM-generated visual programs that invoke specialized vision tools can decompose such tasks, they are error-prone, require loading multiple models at inference time, and empirically underperform end-to-end fine-tuned VLMs. The paper asks whether the reasoning structure of verified visual programs can be distilled into a single VLM that solves these tasks in one forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241532/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image and a free-form text query and produces either a short answer (single word/phrase or multiple-choice letter) or a step-by-step chain-of-thought rationale, controlled by a prompt suffix. During training, the synthesized data pairs each (image, query) with a ground-truth label and a natural-language CoT derived from the execution trace of a verified program, where intermediate steps include grounding information such as detected bounding boxes and tool outputs.

## Method

VPD is a two-stage distillation pipeline. First, an LLM (PaLM-2) samples k=5 candidate Python programs for each query; each program is executed against a suite of vision tools (PaLI-X for visual queries, OWLv2-based object detection, a depth API, and PaLM-2 for external knowledge), and only programs whose output matches the ground-truth label are retained. The execution trace of the surviving program is then rewritten by the LLM into a natural-language chain-of-thought rationale. Second, the target VLM (PaLI-3 5B or PaLI-X 55B) is fine-tuned with LoRA using a dual cross-entropy loss that separately optimizes prediction of the short answer and the CoT rationale, so the model internalizes both the final answer and the step-by-step reasoning structure. Sampling multiple programs and filtering by correctness is critical: it raises the success rate of finding a valid program by up to 45% over single-program sampling, directly increasing the volume of high-quality CoT training data.

## Results

PaLI-X-VPD (55B) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 10 evaluated benchmarks, with headline gains of +8.5 on MMBench (76.2 vs. 67.7 for LLaVA-1.5), +9.8 on TallyQA Complex (76.6 vs. 65.4), and +9.5 on A-OKVQA over the prior specialist SOTA. Compared to its own instruction-tuned baseline (PaLI-X-Instruct), VPD yields +1.6 on GQA, +1.2 on TallyQA Complex, and +1.2 on MMBench. The smaller PaLI-3-VPD (5B) also outperforms all prior generalist VLMs on VQAv2 (80.4), TallyQA Complex (70.9), POPE (88.6), and MMBench (69.0) despite being substantially smaller than 13B-parameter competitors. On Hateful Memes, PaLI-X-VPD (specialist) sets a new SOTA with 80.8% accuracy and 89.2% AUC-ROC in the supervised setting, and 69.7% accuracy in the zero-shot setting, surpassing supervised VisualBERT (69.5% acc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaLI-X-VPD (55B) outperforms all prior generalist VLMs on every reported benchmark, with the largest margins on MMBench (+8.5 over LLaVA-1.5's 67.7), TallyQA Complex (+9.8 over LLaVA-1.5's 65.4), and A-OKVQA (+9.5 over InstructBLIP's specialist SOTA). Against its own instruction-tuned counterpart PaLI-X-Instruct, VPD wins on 11 of 12 tasks, with the most notable gains on GQA (+1.6, 64.9 vs. 63.3) and TallyQA Complex (+1.2, 76.6 vs. 75.4), while narrowly losing on POPE (88.8 vs. 88.9). The 5B PaLI-3-VPD surpasses all larger prior models (13B Vicuna-based) on VQAv2, TallyQA, POPE, and MMBench, but trails on knowledge-intensive tasks (OK-VQA, A-OKVQA), which the authors attribute to the smaller 3B UL2 language backbone lacking sufficient world knowledge. On Hateful Memes, PaLI-X-VPD (specialist) achieves 80.8% accuracy and 89.2% AUC-ROC, beating the previous SOTA (78.8% / 86.7%) and approaching human-level accuracy (84.7%), while the zero-shot VPD variant (69.7% acc) already exceeds supervised VisualBERT (69.5% acc). The paper also compares against the raw generated programs (without distillation), showing that the distilled VLM matches or exceeds program-based inference while eliminating the need for multiple model invocations at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPD sits at the intersection of two research threads: visual programming (VisProg, ViperGPT), which uses LLMs to generate executable programs calling specialized vision tools at inference time, and chain-of-thought distillation (Distill Step-by-Step, PaD, SCOTT), which transfers LLM reasoning into smaller models via generated rationales. The key departure is using verified program execution traces as the source of CoT training data rather than free-form LLM text, and doing so only during training so that the resulting VLM solves tasks in a single forward pass. This contrasts with visual instruction tuning (LLaVA, InstructBLIP), which relies on LLM-generated instructions from image captions and lacks the grounding and verifiability that program execution provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VPD demonstrates that complex compositional visual reasoning (counting, spatial relations, multi-step knowledge retrieval) can be internalized into a single VLM without the latency and error-proneness of multi-tool inference pipelines, which is practically significant for deployment. The finding that sampling top-5 programs rather than top-1 yields a +45% increase in correct-program recovery rate highlights a simple but critical design choice for data synthesis efficiency. Limitations include dependence on the quality of LLM-generated programs and access to multiple specialized vision tools during training, and the paper acknowledges that better program generation (via agents, additional tools, or fact-checking) is the main lever for further gains. An open question is how well this approach transfers to domains where no labeled data exists for program filtering, though the Hateful Memes experiments offer preliminary evidence.
