## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from slow training and sampling due to the need to track extensive forward/reverse diffusion trajectories with a single large model across all noise levels. The paper identifies two specific sources of inefficiency: (i) parameter redundancy, where unified architectures over-parameterize at high noise levels while under-parameterizing at low noise levels, and (ii) gradient dissimilarity across noise levels that hinders gradient-descent training. Existing remedies—fully separate per-interval architectures—introduce overfitting, leaving no architecture that simultaneously addresses both issues.

## Input / Output

The model takes random Gaussian noise as input and generates synthetic RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10, CelebA) or 256×256 (CelebA via latent diffusion). During sampling, the reverse diffusion process is segmented: at each timestep the appropriate stage-specific decoder is invoked based on which interval the current noise level falls into, while the shared encoder processes the latent representation uniformly. The framework is applied to three SOTA solvers (DPM-Solver, EDM, LDM), with NFE ranging from 20 to 35 depending on the solver.

## Method

The proposed multi-stage framework has two core components. First, a multi-decoder U-Net architecture replaces the single decoder with interval-specific decoders (e.g., 192, 128, 16 channels for intervals [0,t₁), [t₁,t₂), [t₂,1]) while retaining one shared encoder across all timesteps; this allocates more parameters where the denoising task is harder (low noise) and prevents overfitting through shared encoder weights. Second, an optimal denoiser-based timestep clustering algorithm (Algorithm 1) determines the partition points t₁ and t₂ by maximizing the functional similarity of the optimal denoiser ε* within each cluster, using a threshold α to define homogeneity. The optimal denoiser is derived in Proposition 1, extending Karras et al.'s result to general noise perturbation kernels. The shared encoder maintains consistency in the h-space (semantic latent space) across timesteps, while the tailored decoders mitigate gradient dissimilarity and enable efficient parameter allocation per stage.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the Multistage DPM-Solver achieves FID 2.35 at 20 NFE, outperforming vanilla DPM-Solver (FID 2.73) and matching PFGM (FID 2.35) which requires 147 NFE. The Multistage EDM reduces FID from 2.05 to 1.96 at 35 NFE. In terms of training efficiency, the multi-stage framework reduces total training PFLOPs by 59% for DPM-Solver (7.94→4.66), 82% for EDM (10.06→8.28), and 30% for LDM (43.31→12.95) while maintaining or improving FID scores across CIFAR-10 and CelebA datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Multistage DPM-Solver (FID 2.35, 20 NFE) outperforms all same-NFE baselines: DPM-Solver (2.73), DEIS (2.86), and gDDIM (2.97), and matches PFGM's FID of 2.35 while requiring only 20 NFE versus PFGM's 147. It substantially surpasses DDIM (4.16) and DDPM (3.17) in both quality and sampling speed. The Multistage EDM (FID 1.96) improves upon vanilla EDM (2.05) at the same 35 NFE, and both outperform Score SDE (2.20) which requires 2000 NFE. For large-scale generation, the Multistage LDM on CelebA 256×256 achieves FID 8.38 versus 8.29 for vanilla LDM while reducing training iterations from 4.9×10⁵ to 1.7×10⁵ (35%) and GFLOPs from 88.39 to 76.19 (86%), a net 30% reduction in total training PFLOPs. The only comparison where the multi-stage approach does not strictly win on FID is LDM (8.38 vs 8.29), where the slight quality trade-off is offset by the large training cost reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two existing lines of diffusion model research: (1) the standard unified U-Net architectures of DDPM, Score SDE, and EDM that share all parameters across timesteps, and (2) the 'expert denoiser' approaches (Balaji et al., Lee et al., Go et al.) that use completely separate architectures per timestep interval. The key departure is a hybrid multi-decoder U-Net with a shared encoder and interval-specific decoders, which the authors argue captures the benefits of both regimes. Additionally, the paper introduces a novel timestep clustering criterion based on functional distance of the optimal denoiser (Proposition 1), departing from prior clustering methods that rely on simple timestep splits, SNR thresholds, or gradient-based task affinity scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: an 82% reduction in training FLOPs for EDM and 30% for LDM while maintaining or improving FID scores, making large-scale diffusion model training significantly more accessible. The framework is plug-and-play with existing fast solvers (DPM-Solver, DEIS, consistency models), broadening its applicability. However, the paper is limited to unconditional image generation on relatively small datasets (CIFAR-10 32×32, CelebA 32×32 and 256×256); open questions include whether the multi-stage strategy transfers to conditional generation, text-to-image, video, and inverse problem settings, and whether the optimal denoiser clustering criterion scales to very large latent spaces. The authors themselves note that extending to conditional DMs and large-scale stable diffusion models is future work.
