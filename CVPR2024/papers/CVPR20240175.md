## Problem

Existing PEFT methods (e.g., LoRA, Adaptformer) inject only a small number of parameters per transformer block, and the Hidden Markov Chain (HMC) along sequential layers restricts each block's update to interactions with adjacent layers alone. This prevents the substantial, coordinated adjustment of projection directions in the parameter space that segmentation tasks demand, since their output space is far larger and more varied than classification. The paper asks how to break this HMC constraint to enable effective PEFT for SAM on downstream segmentation scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a bounding box prompt (with random perturbation of 0–50 pixels) fed into SAM's prompt encoder, and produces a full dense pixel-level segmentation mask. It is applied on top of SAM's ViT-Base or ViT-Large image encoder while keeping the original backbone frozen, and the lightweight mask decoder is excluded from the trainable parameter count. Training uses a combined binary cross-entropy and dice loss over the dense label map.

## Method

SAM-COBOT is a plug-and-play module that adds cross-block orchestration to any existing PEFT method (LoRA, Adaptformer) with only ~1K extra parameters. The Inter-block Communication (IBC) module treats the diagonal coefficient sets of every PEFT block as frontal slices of a 3-D tensor and applies a learnable L×L relation matrix via a mode-3 (T-product) operation, so that each block's coefficients are adjusted by a weighted combination of all other blocks, explicitly breaking the HMC. To preserve the HMC's information-retention benefit, IBC uses dual coefficient sets: one updated under the standard sequential (HMC) constraint and one updated through the cross-block relation matrix. The Intra-block Enhancement (IBE) module then generates the projection-head weights from a hyper-complex layer, where Hamilton products on a suprasphere perform non-commutative rotations and scaling in the projection-direction space, amplifying the impact of each coordinated adjustment. The two branches (HMC-constrained and cross-block) are summed to produce the final adjusted feature map at each block.

## Results

SAM-COBOT is evaluated on 10 segmentation datasets spanning natural image (COCO, TRCAN), remote sensing (NWPU, SSDD, SONAR), and medical image (ADOME, SPLEN, MOMO, BRAST, SEGRAP) segmentation. With ViT-Base SAM backbone, Adaptformer+SAM-COBOT achieves 83.6% average (mIoU/DSC) across all 10 datasets, outperforming Adaptformer alone (82.4%) by +1.2% and LoRA+SAM-COBOT (81.8%) outperforming LoRA (81.1%) by +0.7%. The method adds only ~1K trainable parameters (148.3K vs. 147.4K for LoRA; 324.0K vs. 322.7K for Adaptformer). Gains are most pronounced in medical segmentation, e.g., +1.2% DSC on ADOME and +1.0% on SEGRAP with Adaptformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SAM-COBOT against two PEFT baselines: LoRA and Adaptformer, both applied to SAM's ViT-Base image encoder. SAM-COBOT consistently outperforms both baselines across all 10 datasets. Against LoRA, SAM-COBOT improves the average by +0.7% (81.8 vs. 81.1), with notable gains on SONAR (+1.8% mIoU) and SEGRAP (+1.4% DSC). Against Adaptformer, the improvement is larger at +1.2% average (83.6 vs. 82.4), with the strongest gains on ADOME (+1.2% DSC), SEGRAP (+1.0% DSC), and SPLEN (+1.6% DSC). The method also generalizes to ViT-Large backbone, where Adaptformer+SAM-COBOT achieves 93.0% DSC on ADOME vs. 91.6% for Adaptformer alone. Remarkably, these gains come from adding only 0.9K (LoRA) or 1.3K (Adaptformer) additional parameters, making SAM-COBOT a plug-and-play enhancement to existing PEFT pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEFT lineage (LoRA, Adaptformer) by introducing a cross-block orchestration mechanism specifically designed to overcome what the authors term the Hidden Markov Chain (HMC) limitation in sequential transformer layers. Conceptually, it departs from prior cross-block orchestration work (CLRNet, cross-layer correlation) by operating in the parameter space rather than the feature space, and it repurposes the T-product from tensor algebra to enable inter-block coefficient communication. The intra-block enhancement module draws on hyper-complex (quaternion) neural networks [29], applying the Hamilton product to generate projection head weights from a suprasphere, which is a first in the PEFT-for-segmentation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SAM-COBOT (adding ~1K parameters to existing PEFT methods) makes it a practical drop-in improvement for anyone fine-tuning SAM on downstream segmentation tasks, and the consistent gains across three domains (natural, remote sensing, medical) and two backbones (ViT-Base, ViT-Large) suggest reasonable generalization. However, the HMC argument motivating the design is somewhat hand-wavy and not rigorously validated; the ablation shows the relation matrix contributes most of the gain while the hyper-complex layer adds only ~0.4% over a simple linear layer, raising questions about whether the added algebraic complexity is justified. Open questions include whether the cross-block orchestration principle transfers to non-SAM architectures or to tasks beyond segmentation, and whether the modest absolute gains (~1%) would scale with larger models or more challenging scenarios.
