## Problem

Existing federated active learning (FAL) methods assume all clients draw data from the same domain, yet real multi-center medical data exhibits significant domain shifts that cause softmax-based uncertainty estimates to become overconfident and miscalibrated. Moreover, point-estimate predictions capture only aleatoric uncertainty while ignoring epistemic uncertainty arising from the model's limited knowledge of out-of-domain data, leading to unreliable sample selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240147/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides a pool of unlabeled medical images (skin lesion photos, histology patches, endoscopic frames, MRI slices, or retinal fundus images) along with its local Dirichlet-evidential model and the server's global model. Per FAL round, the method selects a fixed annotation budget (500 samples for classification, 50 or 20 for segmentation) from the unlabeled pool, producing a query set for expert annotation; the trained models then output class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks. The calibrated uncertainty score combines aleatoric uncertainty from both models with epistemic uncertainty from the global model, and diversity relaxation operates on local feature embeddings via cosine similarity.

## Method

FEAL replaces the standard softmax head with a Dirichlet-based evidential layer that outputs evidence parameters α, treating each prediction as a distribution over the probability simplex rather than a single point estimate. The Calibrated Evidential Sampling (CES) module computes aleatoric uncertainty as the expected entropy of the Dirichlet (Eq. 3) in both the global and local models, and epistemic uncertainty as the differential entropy of the Dirichlet (Eq. 4) in the global model; the final selection score is the product of summed aleatoric uncertainty and global epistemic uncertainty (Eq. 5), so that samples the global model is uncertain about due to domain shift receive higher priority. A diversity relaxation step (Alg. 1) then iterates over candidates sorted by descending calibrated uncertainty, rejecting samples whose local-model feature neighbors are already selected, balancing informativeness with coverage. For training, the Evidential Model Learning (EML) module minimizes the Bayes risk of cross-entropy (classification) or Dice loss (segmentation) under the Dirichlet posterior, augmented by an evidence regularization term (KL divergence to a uniform Dirichlet plus a confidence penalty) that suppresses spurious evidence and sharpens correct predictions.

## Results

On the segmentation datasets, FEAL achieves 80.18% Dice on Fed-Polyp (R5), 87.42% on Fed-Prostate (R5), and 90.58% average Dice on Fed-Fundus (R5), consistently outperforming all baselines and even exceeding full-supervision performance on Fed-Polyp (+2.00%) and Fed-Fundus. On classification, FEAL reaches 68.46% BMA on Fed-ISIC (R5), surpassing the second-best CoreSet(E) by 1.62%, and attains 99.40% of fully supervised accuracy on Fed-Camelyon with only ~3.43% of training samples annotated. The method's advantage grows with more FAL rounds, indicating that calibrated uncertainty selection becomes increasingly effective as the labeled set expands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEAL outperforms all seven baselines (Entropy, TOD, Gradnorm, CoreSet, BADGE, LoGo, KAFAL) across all five datasets in the final FAL round. The largest margins are on Fed-Polyp (+1.34% Dice over Gradnorm-L) and Fed-ISIC (+1.62% BMA over CoreSet-E), while on Fed-Prostate and Fed-Fundus the gains are smaller (+0.92% and +0.40% respectively). Notably, the FAL-specific methods LoGo and KAFAL, which were designed for non-IID class imbalance, underperform in these real multi-center settings, confirming that domain shift is a distinct and more challenging heterogeneity source. Among standard AL strategies, combining global and local models (E) generally helps over global-only (G), but without uncertainty calibration the ensemble does not consistently beat local-only (L), highlighting the overconfidence problem under domain shift. FEAL's calibrated evidential approach resolves this, achieving the best result in every dataset-round combination tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends federated active learning (FAL) methods such as LoGo and KAFAL, which combine local and global model knowledge for data selection but assume all clients share the same data domain. It departs from this assumption by introducing Dirichlet-based evidential deep learning to model predictions as distributions over the probability simplex, enabling simultaneous capture of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. The key conceptual departure is using the global model's epistemic uncertainty to calibrate the combined aleatoric uncertainty from both global and local models, addressing the overconfidence problem that arises when a deterministic softmax-based global model evaluates out-of-domain local data. The diversity relaxation strategy builds on core-set-style local constraints but operates in a federated, domain-shifted context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Domain shift across medical imaging centers is a pervasive and practically critical issue that prior FAL methods have largely ignored, making this work directly relevant to real multi-center deployment. The finding that purpose-built FAL methods (LoGo, KAFAL) degrade in genuine multi-center settings due to unmodeled domain shift highlights a gap between simulated federated benchmarks and real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the Dirichlet prior is the optimal distributional assumption, how the method scales to hundreds of clients with heterogeneous architectures, and whether the calibration strategy generalizes to non-medical federated settings. The modest but consistent gains (1–2% over second-best across five datasets) suggest the approach is sound but leaves room for further refinement of the uncertainty calibration mechanism.
