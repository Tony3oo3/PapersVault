## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF-based methods learn scene deformation implicitly through point-level MLPs or simple multi-scale feature interpolation, which cannot effectively capture local 3D geometric structure. This leads to geometrically incoherent deformations and unsatisfactory dynamic view synthesis, especially for complex non-rigid motions where neighboring points must move consistently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241080/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video of a dynamic scene with known camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and per-frame timestamps. For the D-NeRF synthetic dataset (800×800), 150,000 points are randomly initialized since no point cloud is provided; for HyperNeRF real scenes, the dataset's provided point cloud serves as initialization. The output is a rendered novel-view image at any desired viewpoint and time step, along with the full set of deforming 3D Gaussian distributions that enable explicit 3D dynamic reconstruction.

## Method

The architecture has two core components: a Gaussian canonical field and a deformation field. In the canonical field, the static scene is represented as 3D Gaussians, and a sparse 3D U-Net (sparse convolution on voxelized Gaussian positions) extracts local geometry-aware features at multiple receptive fields, which are fused with point-level MLP features via concatenation and a final MLP. The deformation field is an MLP that takes the fused geometry-aware features, positionally-encoded Gaussian position, and positionally-encoded timestamp to predict per-Gaussian position offset, 6D continuous rotation offset, and scale offset. Two key design choices distinguish this from prior work: (1) sparse 3D convolution replaces simple interpolation for geometry extraction, providing a larger effective receptive field and smoother local features, and (2) a continuous 6D rotation representation (V2M mapping) replaces quaternions to ensure the network learns smooth rotational trajectories. Density control is also modified to operate on the transformed Gaussians at the current timestamp rather than canonical-space parameters, with split/cloned Gaussians inverse-transformed back to canonical space.

## Results

On the D-NeRF synthetic dataset (8 scenes), the proposed method achieves the best PSNR across all scenes, with headline values of 32.27 dB (Hell Warrior), 41.43 dB (Mutant), 36.96 dB (Hook), and 43.59 dB (Bouncing Balls), outperforming the strongest competitor 4D-GS by 2.4–5.6 dB. On the HyperNeRF real dataset, the method achieves an average PSNR of 27.52, SSIM of 0.80, and LPIPS of 0.25 across four scenes, surpassing 4D-GS (26.98 / 0.78 / 0.31) and TiNeuVox-B (27.16 / 0.76 / 0.40). Training takes approximately 2 hours on a single RTX 4090 GPU, with a rendering speed of 12 FPS at a fixed viewpoint and a model size of ~48 MB (34 MB point cloud + 14 MB network).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the D-NeRF synthetic benchmark, the proposed method wins on all 8 scenes across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS against every baseline. The closest competitor is 4D-GS, which the method outperforms by 2.4 dB (Lego) to 5.6 dB (T-Rex) in PSNR; the gap is largest on scenes with complex non-rigid motion (T-Rex, Stand Up, Jumping Jacks). Static 3D-GS performs worst in dynamic scenes (e.g., 15.39 dB on Hell Warrior vs. 32.27 dB for the proposed method), confirming it cannot model deformation. On the HyperNeRF real dataset, the method achieves the best average PSNR (27.52) and LPIPS (0.25), but the advantage is narrower: it loses to TiNeuVox-B and FDNeRF on individual scenes like 3D Printer (22.04 vs. 22.75/22.80 dB) and Broom (20.90 vs. 21.27/21.91 dB), while clearly winning on Chicken (28.53 dB) and Peel Banana (25.58 dB). D-NeRF, the original canonical-field baseline, is consistently the weakest dynamic method, trailing the proposed approach by 4–10 dB on synthetic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent threads: the canonical-field-plus-deformation paradigm established by D-NeRF, Nerfies, TiNeuVox, and FDNeRF, and the explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting representation introduced by Kerbl et al. It departs from prior canonical-based methods (which rely on positional encoding or multi-scale interpolation for local geometry) by introducing a sparse 3D U-Net over voxelized Gaussians to extract geometry-aware features, and from 4D-GS (which projects 3D space onto 2D HexPlanes) by remaining fully in 3D. The 6D continuous rotation representation and the timestamp-aware density control are targeted modifications to the original 3D-GS pipeline to make it suitable for dynamic deformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves clear SOTA on the D-NeRF synthetic benchmark and competitive results on HyperNeRF, demonstrating that explicit 3D geometry (via sparse convolution over Gaussian point clouds) is a more effective inductive bias for deformation learning than the interpolation-based alternatives used by prior NeRF methods. However, the approach still assumes a fixed canonical topology (color and opacity are frozen over time), limiting it to non-topology-changing deformations, and the mixed results on real HyperNeRF scenes (losing on 3D Printer and Broom) suggest the canonical assumption is a real bottleneck for complex real-world motion. The authors note future work on explicit foreground/background motion segmentation as a natural next step.
