## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods built on plain transformer (ViT) backbones suffer from weak local spatial information, quadratic computational complexity with respect to input size, and inference cost that scales linearly with the number of categories. This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a simpler encoder-decoder that replaces the plain transformer with a hierarchical backbone and introduces a mechanism to reject non-existing categories early during inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 768×768) and an arbitrary set of category names as text input. Each category name is expanded into 80 prompt templates (e.g., 'a photo of a {T_n}') and encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. The output is a per-pixel segmentation map for each category, initially computed at 1/32 resolution (H/32 × W/32) and progressively upsampled through the decoder to full resolution. During inference, the category early rejection step reduces the active category set to at most 8 per pixel, shrinking the feature tensor dimensionality in subsequent decoder layers.

## Method

SED comprises two main components: (1) a hierarchical encoder-based cost map generation that uses ConvNeXt to extract multi-scale features at strides 4/8/16/32, aligns the deepest feature with text embeddings via an MLP, and computes a pixel-level image-text cost map via cosine similarity; and (2) a gradual fusion decoder that cascades Feature Aggregation Modules (9×9 depth-wise convolution + MLP for spatial fusion, linear self-attention along the category axis for class-level fusion) with Skip-Layer Fusion Modules (2× upsampling, concatenation with encoder features F2/F3/F4 and the cost map, followed by convolutions). A key design choice is stopping gradient back-propagation from skip connections to the image encoder to preserve open-vocabulary ability. At inference, a Category Early Rejection scheme applies a top-k (k=8) strategy on intermediate segmentation predictions to discard non-existing categories, yielding up to 4.7× speedup with negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

With ConvNeXt-B, SED achieves mIoU of 31.6% on A-150, 18.6% on PC-459, 11.4% on A-847, 57.3% on PC-59, and 94.4% on PAS-20, outperforming all comparable ViT-B-based methods on four of five benchmarks. With ConvNeXt-L, SED reaches 35.2% on A-150 and 22.6% on PC-459, setting new state-of-the-art on these two sets. Inference speed is 82 ms per image (ConvNeXt-B) and 98 ms (ConvNeXt-L) on a single A6000 GPU, substantially faster than CAT-Seg (362 ms) and FC-CLIP (285 ms) while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With the base model (ConvNeXt-B vs. ViT-B), SED outperforms SAN by 4.1% on A-150 (31.6 vs. 27.5), CAT-Seg by 4.4% (31.6 vs. 27.2), and OVSeg by 6.8% (31.6 vs. 24.8); on PC-459 it leads CAT-Seg by 2.0% and SAN by 6.0%. SED is slightly behind CAT-Seg on PC-59 (57.3 vs. 57.5) but ahead on all other base-model benchmarks. With the large model (ConvNeXt-L vs. ViT-L), SED surpasses SAN by 1.9% on A-150 (35.2 vs. 33.3) and FC-CLIP by 1.1% (35.2 vs. 34.1), and leads CAT-Seg by 3.7% on PC-459 (22.6 vs. 20.4). However, SED trails FC-CLIP on A-847 (13.9 vs. 14.8) and CAT-Seg on PC-59 (60.6 vs. 62.0) and PAS-20 (96.1 vs. 96.6) with the large model. In terms of speed, SED at 82 ms (base) and 98 ms (large) is roughly 4× faster than CAT-Seg (362 ms) and 3× faster than FC-CLIP (285 ms) while achieving higher mIoU on A-150.

## Relation to Prior Work

SED directly builds on CAT-Seg's pixel-level image-text cost map formulation for open-vocabulary segmentation, but departs from it in three key ways: replacing the plain ViT backbone with a hierarchical ConvNeXt encoder (for better local spatial information and linear complexity), introducing a gradual fusion decoder that fuses multi-scale hierarchical features via skip-layer connections, and adding a category early rejection mechanism for inference acceleration. Unlike two-stage methods (OVSeg, SimBaseline) that decouple mask generation from classification, SED follows the single-stage paradigm but eliminates the need for an additional spatial backbone that CAT-Seg required to compensate for ViT's weak local information. The paper also draws on large-kernel convolution (from ConvNeXt and GCNet) and linear self-attention for class-level aggregation within the decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SED offers a practically compelling speed-accuracy trade-off for open-vocabulary segmentation: 82 ms per image at 31.6% mIoU on A-150 with ConvNeXt-B, and the category early rejection scheme delivers up to 4.7× speedup with negligible accuracy loss, making the approach more deployable in real-time or resource-constrained settings. The finding that hierarchical backbones naturally provide the multi-scale spatial information needed for dense prediction—without auxiliary networks—simplifies the architectural recipe compared to prior ViT-based methods. However, the authors acknowledge that near-synonym category confusion remains an open challenge, and the absolute mIoU scores (31–35% on A-150) indicate substantial headroom, particularly on the harder A-847 and PC-459 benchmarks where performance drops sharply.
