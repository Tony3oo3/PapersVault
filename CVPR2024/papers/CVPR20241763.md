## Problem

Existing tuning-free text-guided image editing methods (e.g., P2P, PnP) modify cross-attention maps to control editing, yet frequently produce failures or incomplete transformations. The semantic content of cross-attention and self-attention maps in Stable Diffusion remains poorly understood: it is unclear whether these maps are purely spatial weight matrices or also encode category-level features, and which attention components are actually responsible for preserving structure versus introducing new semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241763/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image (real photograph or previously generated image) and a target text prompt as input, producing a single edited image at the same resolution. A key distinction is that for real images, no source prompt is required—only the target prompt and the image itself—whereas for generated images an optional source prompt can be supplied. For real images, a DDIM inversion step first reconstructs the latent trajectory before editing begins, and the VQ autoencoder reconstruction may lose fine facial details.

## Method

Through probing analysis with 2-layer MLP classifiers trained on attention maps, the paper shows that cross-attention maps encode the semantic category of their corresponding prompt tokens (e.g., 'orange' or 'sheep'), not merely spatial alignment weights, which explains why replacing them between source and target generation causes editing failures. In contrast, self-attention maps primarily encode spatial structure and shape contours, making them the appropriate channel for preserving layout. The proposed Free-Prompt-Editing (FPE) method exploits this by running a parallel denoising pass with the source image (or its DDIM-inverted latents) and, at each timestep, copying the self-attention maps from layers 4 through 14 of the source pass into the target-prompt pass, while leaving all cross-attention maps untouched. This single mechanism—self-attention hijacking in a restricted layer range—suffices to transfer structural information without injecting unwanted category semantics, eliminating the need for source-prompt alignment or cross-attention manipulation.

## Results

On the Wild-TI2I and ImageNet-R-TI2I benchmarks, FPE achieves the highest CDS (Clip Directional Similarity) on 3 of 4 splits: 0.3559 on ImageNet-R-TI2I fake, 0.2271 on ImageNet-R-TI2I real, and 0.2234 on Wild-real, while MasaCtrl edges ahead on Wild-fake CDS (0.3474 vs. 0.3116). FPE also attains the best CS (Clip Score) on ImageNet-R-TI2I fake (29.79) and is competitive elsewhere. Inference is fast at ~6.3 s per image (fake) and ~10.8 s (real) on an A100 GPU, comparable to P2P and MasaCtrl but roughly 50× faster than PnP (335.65 s / 384.26 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPE outperforms P2P on both CS and CDS across all four of the authors' own datasets (e.g., CDS 0.2659 vs. 0.2451 on Car-fake-edit; 0.2605 vs. 0.2288 on Car-real-edit), and also beats P2P on CDS for ImageNet-R-TI2I fake (0.3559 vs. 0.3394) and real (0.2271 vs. 0.2146). Against MasaCtrl, FPE wins CDS on ImageNet-R-TI2I fake (0.3559 vs. 0.3024) and Wild-real (0.2234 vs. 0.2101), but MasaCtrl leads on Wild-fake CDS (0.3474 vs. 0.3116) and ImageNet-R-TI2I real CS (31.40 vs. 29.05). FPE dramatically outperforms PnP on CDS for ImageNet-R-TI2I fake (0.3559 vs. 0.2318) and is ~50× faster (6.30 s vs. 335.65 s). DiffEdit and Pix2Pix-Zero trail FPE by a wide margin on CDS (e.g., 0.0748 and 0.2271 vs. 0.3559 on ImageNet-R-TI2I fake), while SDEdit and Shape-Guided are only evaluated on real-image splits and remain well below FPE on CDS (0.2171 and 0.1090 vs. 0.2271). InstructPix2Pix, a fine-tuning-based method, is shown qualitatively to lose structural fidelity compared to FPE's tuning-free approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the tuning-free text-guided image editing (TIE) literature, particularly P2P (which replaces cross-attention maps between source and target prompts) and PnP (which injects spatial features and self-attention from the source image). Its key departure is methodological: rather than proposing yet another editing recipe, it borrows probing analysis from NLP interpretability to empirically dissect what cross-attention and self-attention maps actually encode in Stable Diffusion. The resulting Free-Prompt-Editing (FPE) method is a simplification of PnP that discards cross-attention manipulation entirely and operates only on self-attention in layers 4–14, eliminating the need for a source prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cross-attention maps carry category-level semantic information (probing accuracy up to 98%) rather than merely spatial weights challenges the core assumption underlying P2P-style editing and explains why cross-attention replacement can cause editing failures. Practically, FPE offers a ~50× speedup over PnP (6.3 s vs. 335 s per image) while matching or exceeding its quality, making tuning-free editing more accessible. Open questions include whether the layer range (4–14) and the cross-attention semantic-encoding finding generalize to other diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the VQ autoencoder bottleneck for real-image reconstruction can be mitigated without retraining.
