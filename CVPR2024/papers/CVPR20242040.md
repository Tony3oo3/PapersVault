## Problem

Visual projectors in Multimodal LLMs have been underexplored despite being critical for both performance and efficiency. Existing linear projectors preserve local spatial context but are inflexible in the number of output tokens, while abstractors (resampler, Q-former) offer token-count flexibility but lose fine-grained local details essential for spatial understanding. Additionally, the optimal strategies for combining and utilizing multifaceted instruction datasets remain unclear.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 224×224 (7B) or 336×336 (13B) resolution along with a free-form text instruction, and produces an autoregressive text response. The CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder extracts region-level features from the second-last layer, which are projected into M visual tokens (144 or 256) that are concatenated directly with text tokens—no special image indicator tokens are used. The number of visual tokens M is a design-time hyperparameter that trades off spatial fidelity against LLM inference cost.

## Method

Honeybee introduces two locality-enhanced projectors that combine abstraction flexibility with local context preservation. The C-Abstractor stacks L ResNet blocks, applies adaptive average pooling to reshape features to any squared token count, then applies L more ResNet blocks, enabling flexible M while integrating neighboring spatial information. The D-Abstractor augments the standard resampler with deformable attention, where each learnable query samples features via 2-D coordinate-based offsets around reference points that are manually initialized to distribute uniformly over the feature map, ensuring comprehensive local coverage. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: (1) pre-training the projector alone on 200M image-text pairs with frozen vision encoder and LLM, then (2) jointly fine-tuning the projector and full LLM on instruction data. The paper further contributes a systematic 'hidden recipe' for instruction tuning, showing that per-dataset balancing with hand-crafted tuning, fine-grained single templates per dataset, and multi-turn examples with semantic deduplication collectively maximize benchmark performance.

## Results

Honeybee (13B, C-Abstractor, M=576) achieves MMB 73.6, MME^P 1661, MME 1977, SEED^I 68.6, and LLaVA^W 77.5, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art LLaVA-1.5 (13B) by +5.9, +130, +129, +0.5, and +6.8 respectively. At the 7B scale with only 144 visual tokens (vs. 576 for LLaVA-1.5), Honeybee (C-Abstractor) still surpasses LLaVA-1.5 on MMB (70.1 vs. 64.3), MME^P (1584.2 vs. 1510.7), and LLaVA^W (67.1 vs. 63.4), while using significantly fewer visual tokens and lower step time (2.23 s/step vs. 3.04 s/step for linear at M=256). The locality-enhanced projector enables Honeybee to outperform the linear projector by +3.7 Avg^N on spatial understanding tasks at the same M=256 budget, and even surpass it with fewer tokens (M=144: 53.5 vs. 52.6 Avg^N).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 7B scale, Honeybee (C-Abstractor, M=144) outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (M=576) on MMB (+5.8), MME^P (+73.5), and LLaVA^W (+3.7) while using only 144 visual tokens versus 576, demonstrating superior efficiency. It also surpasses Qwen-VL-Chat on MMB (+9.5) and MME^P (+96.7), though it trails slightly on SEED^I (64.5 vs. 65.4), a gap that closes when M is increased to 256 (65.5). Against InstructBLIP (Q-former, M=32), Honeybee leads by a wide margin on MMB (+34.1) and LLaVA^W (+6.2). At the 13B scale, Honeybee (C-Abstractor, M=256) dominates LLaVA-1.5 on MMB (+5.5), MME^P (+98), MME (+117.3), and LLaVA^W (75.7 vs. not reported), while matching SEED^I (68.2 vs. 68.1). The D-Abstractor variant achieves comparable or slightly higher MMB scores (70.8 vs. 70.1 at 7B; 73.5 vs. 73.2 at 13B) but trails the C-Abstractor on MME and SEED^I, suggesting the convolutional locality modeling is more effective for spatial tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM projector design space by identifying locality preservation as a critical but overlooked property, complementing the well-known flexibility requirement. It builds directly on BLIP-2's resampler architecture (modifying it with deformable attention from Deformable DETR to create the D-Abstractor) and introduces a convolutional alternative (C-Abstractor) that departs from the cross-attention paradigm entirely. The empirical study on instruction tuning recipes (dataset balancing, template granularity, multi-turn strategies) extends the practical guidance from LLaVA and InstructBLIP by systematically ablatting choices those papers left implicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard abstractors (resampler, Q-former) systematically degrade spatial understanding relative to linear projectors—despite being preferred for efficiency—challenges a prevailing design assumption in the MLLM community and offers a concrete fix (locality-enhanced abstraction) that recovers the spatial gap while retaining token-count flexibility. The comprehensive instruction-tuning ablations (e.g., fine-grained single templates beating diverse multi-template approaches) provide actionable recipes that practitioners can adopt immediately. Open questions include whether locality-enhanced projectors generalize to video, audio, or 3D modalities, and whether the benefits persist at substantially larger scales (e.g., 70B+ LLMs) where the projector's relative contribution may shift.
