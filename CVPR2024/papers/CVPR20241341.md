## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary detectors reuse classical detector architectures and inject language only at the final classification layer, leaving the inner workings (query selection, inter-class competition) unchanged. This limits generalization to unseen concepts and produces low-quality pseudo-labels on weakly-labeled data, where the detector cannot focus its proposals on the specific classes present in an image. The paper asks whether conditioning the entire detection pipeline on language embeddings can improve both open-vocabulary generalization and self-training on image-level annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241341/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In Phase 1 (conditioned detection), the model takes an RGB image and a set of class name strings, producing per-class binary presence scores and bounding boxes (300 queries per class, each predicting a single scalar score). In Phase 2 (open-vocabulary detection), the text input is the generic phrase 'an object' and the model outputs multi-class scores over all categories with associated boxes. The CLIP text encoder provides the language embeddings that parameterize the objectness function, and the detector runs at the same speed as vanilla Deformable DETR despite the per-class query expansion.

## Method

DECOLA modifies two-stage Deformable DETR by replacing the learned objectness function with a cosine similarity score between each region feature and the CLIP text embedding of the queried class, so that top-k query selection is performed independently per class, eliminating inter-class competition. A memory-efficient self-attention mechanism reshapes the decoder to isolate attention within each class, reducing memory from O(n²K²) to O(n²K). Training proceeds in two phases: Phase 1 trains the conditioned detector on fully-supervised data with per-class Hungarian matching and binary cross-entropy, optimizing for a strong pseudo-labeler; Phase 2 converts the model to an unconditioned open-vocabulary detector by conditioning on 'an object' and training jointly on human-labeled and offline pseudo-labeled data with standard DETR matching. The offline pseudo-labeling strategy—generating the most confident prediction per class from Phase 1—outperforms online self-training and requires no additional loss functions, distillation, or teacher models.

## Results

On open-vocabulary LVIS, DECOLA achieves 27.6 AP novel (ResNet-50) and 35.7 AP novel (Swin-B), outperforming the baseline+self-train by 4.4 and 4.9 AP novel respectively, while also improving frequent and common classes simultaneously. On the direct zero-shot transfer benchmark, DECOLA (Swin-T) achieves 32.8 AP rare on LVIS minival and 27.2 AP rare on LVIS v1.0, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art (MQ-GLIP) by 12.0 and 17.1 AP rare. On standard LVIS, DECOLA improves AP rare by 5.9 (ResNet-50) and 5.4 (Swin-B) over baseline+self-train. The largest Swin-L model reaches 46.9 AP novel on open-vocabulary LVIS, outperforming OWLv2 (45.9) and DITO (45.8) despite using far less training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECOLA consistently outperforms all compared methods across benchmarks. Against OV-DETR (the closest architectural competitor), DECOLA gains 9.6 AP novel with ResNet-50-1K (27.6 vs 18.0) and eliminates the quadratic query growth. Against OWLv2 and DITO (the strongest Swin-L-scale competitors using billion-scale web data), DECOLA achieves 46.9 AP novel with only ImageNet-21K as weakly-labeled data, beating OWLv2 by 1.0 AP novel and DITO by 1.1 AP novel. On the direct zero-shot transfer benchmark, DECOLA (Swin-T) surpasses MQ-GLIP by 12.0 AP rare on LVIS minival and 17.1 AP rare on LVIS v1.0, and outperforms GLIP and GroundingDINO by 19.3 and 13.3 AP rare with Swin-L. For R-CNN-based detectors, DECOLA labels (pseudo-labels from Phase 1) improve Detic by 2.8 AP novel (ResNet-50) and 1.8 AP novel (Swin-B) on open-vocabulary LVIS, and by 4.2 and 1.8 AP rare on standard LVIS. Notably, DECOLA improves frequent, common, and rare classes simultaneously, whereas baseline+self-train and other methods typically trade off frequent-class performance for novel-class gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECOLA builds on the two-stage Deformable DETR architecture but departs fundamentally from prior open-vocabulary detectors by conditioning the query selection mechanism itself on language embeddings, rather than injecting text only at the final classification head (as in ViLD, Detic, BARON) or expanding queries quadratically per class (as in OV-DETR). This design removes inter-class competition from both training and inference, allowing each queried concept to receive dedicated queries. The paper also repositions the self-training paradigm from Detic by replacing online pseudo-labeling with offline pseudo-labeling from a language-conditioned Phase 1 model, arguing that the conditioning mechanism produces substantially higher-quality labels that make the simpler offline approach superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DECOLA demonstrates that a single architectural change—replacing learned objectness with cosine similarity to CLIP text embeddings for query selection—yields large, consistent gains across backbones, architectures, and benchmarks while requiring only open-source data and academic-scale compute. The practical impact is significant: the released pseudo-annotations and checkpoints enable the broader community to build on high-quality open-vocabulary detection labels without billion-scale web data. Open questions include whether the two-phase training can be unified, whether the conditioning mechanism transfers to other tasks (segmentation, tracking, video), and whether the approach scales to truly open-ended vocabulary beyond the 1203 LVIS categories.
