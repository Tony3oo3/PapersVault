## Problem

Current generative models like StyleGAN produce realistic images but cannot selectively control scene lighting while preserving geometry and albedo. Existing relighting methods either require 3D morphable models, carefully curated light-stage data, or are limited to faces, and none can handle the complex inter-reflections, cast shadows, and glossy effects present in indoor scenes without altering the scene's material appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a StyleGAN w+ latent style code (obtained from random Gaussian noise via the pretrained mapping network) and produces a relit or resurfaced image by adding a precomputed direction vector d_i of the same shape to w+. Directions are discovered once using 2000 randomly generated images and then apply universally to any w+ without per-image search or fine-tuning. For resurfacing, the consistency and diversity losses are swapped so that albedo changes while shading is preserved, enabling material and color edits.

## Method

StyLitGAN searches for a set of latent directions d_i in StyleGAN's w+ space that change shading while preserving albedo, using a self-supervised intrinsic image decomposition (albedo × shading + gloss) to compute losses. Four losses guide the joint optimization of directions and a classifier: a Huber + VGG perceptual consistency loss on albedo, a log-determinant diversity loss on downsampled shading/gloss vectors, a cross-entropy loss from a classifier that must identify which direction was applied, and a saturation penalty. Because no single decomposition model yields all good directions, the method pools directions from 25 hyperparameter variants of three decomposition families, filters inadmissible models via a Pareto frontier on albedo change vs. diversity, and applies a greedy forward selection to pick the final 16 directions. The resulting directions exhibit persistent semantics across scenes (e.g., one direction consistently activates a bedside lamp) and enable smooth interpolation and scaling of lighting effects.

## Results

The primary quantitative result (Table 1) reports FID scores, which the authors explicitly frame as measuring distribution shift rather than realism: relighting increases FID from 5.01 to 14.23 on Bedroom and from 5.02 to 37.87 on Faces, confirming that StyLitGAN generates a strict superset of images beyond vanilla StyleGAN's distribution. For the downstream surface-normal task, fine-tuning Omnidata with 25 StyLitGAN relights per scene reduces angular error variance to lighting to a level comparable with fine-tuning on the true Multi-Illum dataset (30 scenes × 25 lightings), while 7 relights per scene is slightly worse. Qualitatively, the 16 forward-selected directions produce consistent, semantically meaningful relighting effects (e.g., switching on bedside lamps, increasing window light) across all tested scene categories with visible soft shadows, cast shadows, inter-reflections, and gloss changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAN-control [39], StyLitGAN clearly wins on indoor scenes: GAN-control, which represents lighting via spherical harmonics pretrained on a 3D face model, completely changes scene layout when applied to bedrooms (Figure 9), producing large albedo changes and inflated diversity scores due to structural alterations rather than genuine relighting. The paper attributes this failure to the attribute predictor being easily subverted—when the lighting representation cannot produce a desired effect (e.g., dark left side), GAN-control adjusts albedo instead. Against Yang et al. [48], StyLitGAN wins on albedo preservation: Yang et al. identify only a single relighting direction and aggressively change scene albedo, whereas StyLitGAN's forward-selected 16 directions maintain albedo consistency while producing diverse, physically plausible shading changes. StyLitGAN also extends to resurfacing (changing materials/colors while preserving lighting), a capability neither baseline supports. The comparison is largely qualitative since no standard relighting benchmark with ground-truth pairs exists for indoor scenes; the paper relies on albedo-change and diversity metrics (Figure 3) plus visual inspection.

## Relation to Prior Work

StyLitGAN extends the StyleGAN latent-space editing paradigm by introducing physics-informed search objectives (albedo consistency via intrinsic image decomposition, shading diversity, and direction distinctiveness) to discover relighting directions in w+ space. It departs from GAN-control and StyleFlow, which rely on parametric lighting models (spherical harmonics) and are limited to faces, and from Yang et al., which uses semantic labels to find a single direction. The method also builds on the self-supervised intrinsic image decomposition of Forsyth & Rock and Bhattad & Forsyth, repurposing it not as a standalone tool but as a loss signal to guide latent-space search. A forward selection process over multiple decomposition hyperparameter settings distinguishes it from prior single-model approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StyLitGAN demonstrates that physically plausible relighting of complex indoor scenes—complete with cast shadows, inter-reflections, and gloss—is achievable without labeled data, 3D models, or CGI, by simply adding learned directions to StyleGAN style codes. The downstream application showing that StyLitGAN-generated relights can substitute for scarce real multi-illumination data when fine-tuning surface normal predictors highlights practical value. Limitations include the out-of-distribution nature of generated images (elevated FID), reliance on qualitative evaluation, and the need for careful loss-weight tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond StyleGAN architectures, whether decomposition quality fundamentally bounds relighting fidelity, and whether the discovered directions compose predictably for arbitrary lighting configurations.
