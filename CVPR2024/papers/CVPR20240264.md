## Problem

Existing atom-level entity prediction methods for optical chemical structure recognition require rich bounding-box annotations for every atom, bond, charge, and stereocenter in training images, which are unavailable in important domains such as hand-drawn molecule sketches where only image-SMILES pairs exist. This prevents deploying the most interpretable and data-efficient class of OCSR models to precisely the domains where they are most needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240264/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single molecular structure image (hand-drawn or rendered) as input and produces four sets of labeled bounding boxes (atoms, bonds, charges, stereocenters) that are post-processed into a molecular graph and a SMILES string. During fine-tuning on a new domain, the only supervision required is the SMILES string paired with the image—no bounding boxes are needed. The graph constructor applies chemistry-aware validation (valence checks, bond removal) to ensure the output graph is chemically valid before SMILES conversion.

## Method

The architecture (AtomLenz) uses four separate Faster R-CNN object detection backbones—one each for atoms, bonds, charges, and stereocenters—pretrained on ~214K synthetically rendered images with full bounding-box annotations. A molecular graph constructor assembles the detected entities into a valid graph through filtering, node/edge creation, and chemistry validation. For domain adaptation with only SMILES supervision, the paper introduces EditKT*, which iteratively combines ProbKT* (probabilistic reasoning enabling backpropagation through the detection backbone using SMILES-derived constraints) with a graph edit-correction mechanism that finds minimal edits to make the predicted graph isomorphic to the SMILES-derived reference graph, generating pseudo-labels for rare atom/bond types. At inference, a ChemExpert module checks predictions from multiple models (AtomLenz + DECIMER fine-tuned) for chemical validity and returns the first chemically sound prediction.

## Results

On the hand-drawn test set, ChemExpert (the combination of DECIMER fine-tuned and AtomLenz+EditKT*) achieves 63.5% exact-match accuracy and 0.749 mean Tanimoto similarity, outperforming all baselines. AtomLenz+EditKT* alone reaches 33.8% accuracy and 0.484 Tanimoto, a substantial improvement over the non-fine-tuned AtomLenz (0.9% accuracy). On the out-of-domain ChemPix test set, ChemExpert scores 51.8% accuracy and 0.655 Tanimoto. In the data-efficiency experiment (Table 3), where all methods are retrained from scratch on only 4,070 hand-drawn samples, AtomLenz achieves 33.8% accuracy while all baselines fall below 1.3%, demonstrating a >25× data-efficiency advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChemExpert narrowly outperforms DECIMER fine-tuned [25] on both test sets: 63.5% vs. 62.2% accuracy (hand-drawn) and 51.8% vs. 50.8% (ChemPix), with corresponding Tanimoto gains of 0.022 and 0.012. Against the original DECIMER [24], the gap is dramatic (63.5% vs. 29.5% on hand-drawn), and all other baselines—Img2Mol [5] (8.4%), MolScribe [23] (10.2%), ChemGrapher [19] (0.2%), and OSRA [7] (0.6%)—are far behind on the hand-drawn domain. On atom-level localization (Table 2), AtomLenz achieves 0.801 mAP, vastly exceeding MolScribe (0.008) and ChemGrapher (0.002), while DECIMER provides no localization at all. In the critical data-efficiency test (Table 3), where every method is retrained from scratch on the same 4,070 hand-drawn samples, AtomLenz reaches 33.8% accuracy while the best baseline (MolScribe) manages only 1.3%, a 26× margin that underscores the architecture's sample efficiency. The paper attributes the complementary strengths to ChemExpert's design: AtomLenz+EditKT* excels on halogens (Cl, F) and phosphorus, while DECIMER fine-tuned handles bonds better, so their combination corrects each other's failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of optical chemical structure recognition (OCSR) and weakly supervised object detection. Prior OCSR methods split into two camps: SMILES-based sequence models (DECIMER, Img2Mol) that lack spatial localization, and atom-level entity predictors (MolScribe, ChemGrapher) that require rich bounding-box annotations unavailable in real-world domains like hand-drawn sketches. The paper bridges this gap by repurposing the ProbKT probabilistic knowledge-transfer framework (Oldenhof et al., ICLR 2023) and a graph edit-correction mechanism (Oldenhof et al., 2021) to fine-tune a Faster R-CNN object detector on new domains using only SMILES supervision, eliminating the need for per-atom bounding boxes at adaptation time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first demonstration that atom-level entity localization in OCSR can be achieved with only SMILES supervision opens a path to deploying interpretable, segmentable chemical structure recognizers in annotation-scarce settings (hand-drawn lab notes, patents, sketches). The released hand-drawn dataset with atom-level annotations is a practical resource for the community. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (63.5% on hand-drawn, 51.8% on ChemPix), and the authors acknowledge that higher performance is needed for wide adoption. Open questions include whether the weakly supervised fine-tuning strategy generalizes beyond hand-drawn to other low-resource domains (e.g., low-resolution scans, multi-molecule images) and whether the ChemExpert ensemble can be replaced by a single end-to-end model.
