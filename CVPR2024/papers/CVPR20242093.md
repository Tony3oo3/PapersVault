## Problem

No prior generative model could jointly synthesize both the 3D object shape and the human hand configuration during interaction, as existing approaches either required instance-specific object templates, operated in 2D image space, or modeled hand and object with incompatible representations (pose parameters vs. spatial fields). The paper asks what homogeneous 3D representation would allow a single diffusion model to capture the joint hand-object distribution and serve as a generic prior for downstream inference tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a CLIP-encoded text prompt (object category label) and produces a 64³ object SDF grid plus MANO hand pose parameters in a normalized hand-centric frame (palm-up). For reconstruction, it consumes a monocular video clip and outputs a time-persistent object implicit field, per-frame hand pose, and relative hand-object transformation. For grasp synthesis, it takes a single object mesh and outputs hand articulation and relative pose, with a scalar plausibility score for ranking. The internal interaction grid concatenates a 16³ VQ-VAE latent (from the 64³ SDF) with a 15-channel skeletal distance field, all spanning 30 cm in the hand-centric frame.

## Method

The core representation insight is encoding the hand as a 15-channel 3D skeletal distance field (squared distance to each of 15 MANO joints) rather than a 1D pose vector, making it spatially homogeneous with the object SDF so a single 3D diffusion model can reason about their interaction. A VQ-VAE compresses the 64³ object SDF into a 16³ latent, which is channel-concatenated with the skeletal field to form the interaction grid; a 3D-UNet with CLIP cross-attention denoises this grid under a standard DDPM objective. For downstream tasks, the trained prior is used via score distillation sampling (SDS) to approximate log-likelihood gradients, which are combined with task-specific losses (reprojection error for video reconstruction, mesh contact/collision penalties for grasp synthesis) in a test-time optimization loop. The model is trained jointly on seven real-world datasets spanning 155 object categories, and the joint (rather than conditional) modeling of hand and object is shown to improve both hand pose accuracy and object reconstruction over 2D or conditional baselines.

## Results

On HOI4D reconstruction, G-HOP achieves the best scores across all metrics: F@5mm=0.76, F@10mm=0.97, Chamfer distance=0.4mm, hand-object alignment CD_h=18.4, MPJPE=1.05, and AUC=0.79, outperforming DiffHOI (F@5mm=0.62, CD_h=48.7) by a wide margin. On HO3D grasp synthesis, G-HOP achieves the lowest average intersection depth (0.31mm) and simulation displacement (0.95mm) while maintaining 100% contact ratio and 0.23 contact area. On out-of-domain 3DW objects, G-HOP still achieves the best max intersection depth (0.74mm) and largest contact area (0.25), though with slightly lower contact ratio (0.93 vs. 1.00 for GraspTTA). In user studies, G-HOP grasps are preferred over all baselines including ground-truth annotations on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against iHOI [51] (single-view, no temporal consistency), G-HOP improves F@5mm from 0.42 to 0.76 and CD_h from 27.1 to 18.4, reflecting the benefit of multi-view video cues and a 3D prior. Against HHOR [21] (optimization without data-driven prior), G-HOP dramatically reduces CD_h from 165.4 to 18.4, showing the prior is essential for handling unobserved regions. Against the closest baseline DiffHOI [52] (2D image-space diffusion prior conditioned on hand pose), G-HOP improves F@5mm by 22% (0.76 vs. 0.62), halves CD_h (18.4 vs. 48.7), and reduces MPJPE from 1.12 to 1.05, demonstrating that a joint 3D hand-object prior outperforms a conditional 2D object-only prior. For grasp synthesis, G-HOP outperforms GraspTTA [22] on HO3D in average intersection depth (0.31 vs. 0.61mm) and simulation displacement (0.95 vs. 2.89mm), and on 3DW in max depth (0.74 vs. 0.87mm) and contact area (0.25 vs. 0.18), though G-HOP has higher intersection volume (11.46 vs. 5.25 on HO3D) due to more natural, less 'over-grasping' configurations. In user studies, G-HOP is preferred over GraspTTA and even ground-truth annotations on both HO3D and 3DW.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based HOI reconstruction framework of DiffHOI by replacing its 2D image-space prior with a 3D spatial diffusion model and, crucially, by jointly modeling hand and object rather than conditioning on hand pose. The core representation insight is the 'skeletal distance field' for the hand, which aligns the hand representation with the (latent) SDF used for objects, enabling a homogeneous 'interaction grid' that a single 3D-UNet can denoise. For inference, it repurposes score distillation sampling (from the SDS/Score Jacobian Chaining line of work) to extract log-likelihood gradients from the trained prior, unifying reconstruction and grasp synthesis under a single test-time optimization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

G-HOP is the first generative model to jointly produce both hand pose and object shape from a category label, and it demonstrates that this joint 3D prior outperforms task-specific methods on both video-based reconstruction and grasp synthesis. The skeletal distance field representation is a practical contribution that resolves the heterogeneity problem (1D pose vector vs. 3D SDF grid) that hinders joint diffusion. Key limitations remain: the model requires a category label (limiting scalability), provides no explicit contact guarantee, and is trained on a relatively small, long-tailed collection of 7 datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary generation, whether explicit contact constraints can be integrated, and whether the hand-centric frame assumption generalizes to two-handed or tool-mediated interactions.
