## Problem

Existing multimodal anomaly detection methods (BTF, M3DM) rely on large memory banks of stored nominal features, incurring substantial memory overhead and slow inference that limit industrial deployment. While the faster AST method avoids memory banks, it treats 3D data as a mere additional input channel without exploiting its spatial structure, resulting in inferior performance. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a lightweight crossmodal feature mapping paradigm that achieves state-of-the-art detection and segmentation with dramatically lower memory and faster inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image and a pixel-registered 3D point cloud (processed as 1024 groups of 32 points via farthest point sampling, with background removed by RANSAC plane fitting) as input. It produces a 224×224 pixel-level anomaly map computed as the pixel-wise product of modality-specific 2D and 3D anomaly maps, smoothed with a Gaussian kernel (σ=4), and a global anomaly score taken as the maximum value of the map. The 2D features are 768-dimensional (from DINO ViT-B/8) and 3D features are 1152-dimensional (from Point-MAE), both upsampled/interpolated to full image resolution before crossmodal mapping.

## Method

The core contribution is learning two lightweight 3-layer MLP mapping networks (M_{2D→3D} and M_{3D→2D}) that predict features of one modality from the other, trained jointly on nominal samples only by minimizing cosine distance between actual and predicted features. At inference, anomalies are localized by computing the Euclidean discrepancy between each modality's actual features and the crossmodal-predicted features, yielding modality-specific anomaly maps that are combined via pixel-wise product (a logical AND requiring both modalities to corroborate an anomaly). This crossmodal objective is shown to be more effective than intramodal reconstruction because it captures inter-modality relationships that anomalies inherently violate, including the multimodal-only case where individual modalities appear nominal but their co-occurrence is anomalous. A layer-pruning strategy further reduces cost by feeding features from shallower Transformer layers (e.g., layer 1 for Tiny, layer 4 for Small) into the mapping networks, cutting memory by up to half while retaining competitive performance.

## Results

On MVTec 3D-AD, the proposed method achieves a mean I-AUROC of 0.954 and AUPRO@30% of 0.971, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art M3DM (0.945 / 0.964) across all three mean metrics. Under the stricter AUPRO@1% threshold, the gap widens substantially: Ours reaches 0.455 versus 0.394 for M3DM, 0.398 for AST, and 0.383 for BTF. On Eyecandies, the method achieves AUPRO@30% of 0.887 and AUPRO@1% of 0.335, marginally outperforming M3DM on segmentation while trailing slightly on I-AUROC (0.881 vs 0.897). In terms of efficiency, the full model runs at 21.755 fps with 437.91 MB memory, compared to 4.966 fps for AST and substantially slower/larger memory-bank methods (BTF, M3DM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against M3DM (the prior state-of-the-art memory-bank method), the proposed crossmodal mapping approach wins on all three MVTec 3D-AD mean metrics: I-AUROC (0.954 vs 0.945), AUPRO@30% (0.971 vs 0.964), and AUPRO@1% (0.455 vs 0.394, a +0.061 gap). On Eyecandies the comparison is closer: M3DM edges ahead on I-AUROC (0.897 vs 0.881) and P-AUROC (0.977 vs 0.974), while the proposed method leads on AUPRO@30% (0.887 vs 0.882) and AUPRO@1% (0.335 vs 0.331). Against BTF, the proposed method dominates across every metric on both datasets, with the largest gap on I-AUROC (0.954 vs 0.865 on MVTec 3D-AD). AST is outperformed on all metrics as well, with particularly large margins on AUPRO@1% (0.455 vs 0.398). Critically, the proposed method also runs ~4.4× faster than AST (21.755 vs 4.966 fps) and uses far less memory than the memory-bank methods BTF and M3DM, making it the only approach that simultaneously achieves top accuracy and real-time inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant memory-bank paradigm in multimodal AD (BTF, M3DM) and the teacher-student approach (AST) by proposing crossmodal feature mapping: lightweight MLPs that predict 2D features from 3D features and vice versa, trained only on nominal samples. It reuses the same frozen Transformer extractors as M3DM (DINO ViT-B/8 for 2D, Point-MAE for 3D) but replaces the expensive feature bank with a simple discrepancy-based detection criterion. The conceptual lineage also connects to Deep Feature Reconstruction (DFR), which the authors explicitly contrast: their crossmodal mapping is shown to be a more effective learning objective than intramodal reconstruction (Table 7). The layer-pruning strategy for frozen encoders is a new technique not present in prior multimodal AD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial deployment: achieving SOTA on MVTec 3D-AD with a 3-layer MLP per mapping direction, 21.7 fps inference, and under 440 MB memory makes multimodal AD feasible on edge hardware where memory-bank methods are impractical. The introduction of AUPRO@1% as a stricter evaluation metric and the first few-shot multimodal AD benchmark open new directions for the field. A key limitation is the multimodal-only requirement—both RGB and 3D data must be available at training and test time—restricting applicability to settings where 3D sensors are deployed. Whether the crossmodal mapping paradigm generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., thermal + RGB, LiDAR + camera) or to different frozen backbone families remains an open question.
