## Problem

Neural radiance fields are sensitive to arbitrary scene scaling: halving the scene size doubles the required volumetric density, and vice versa. Existing NeRF architectures (Vanilla NeRF, TensoRF, DVGO, Plenoxels, Nerfacto) rely on ad-hoc heuristics—fixed interval sizes, hardcoded offsets, or aggressive learning rates—that work at one particular scale but fail at others, producing either insufficient opacity in small scenes or opaque 'cloudiness traps' in large scenes.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in parameterization recipe on top of existing NeRF architectures, consuming standard multi-view RGB images and camera poses. It produces novel-view RGB renderings, depth maps, and a learned volume density field. The key constraint is that the recipe must remain invariant when the scene is uniformly scaled by an arbitrary factor k (tested from 0.1 to 25), without modifying sampling strategies or other optimization hyperparameters.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary ingredients for achieving alpha invariance. First, it parameterizes both the ray interval length d and the volumetric density σ in log space, yielding the GumbelCDF form α = 1 − exp(−exp(x + log d + offset)), where the log d term naturally cancels scene scaling. Second, it derives a closed-form initialization offset μ = log(log(1/T′)) − log L − τ²/2 that guarantees a target average ray transmittance T′ (e.g., 0.99) at initialization, preventing the opaque-scene failure mode at large scales. The combined expression makes the local alpha depend only on the ratio d/L, which is invariant to uniform scene scaling. The recipe is discretization-agnostic and is validated across MLP (Vanilla NeRF), tensor (TensoRF), voxel (DVGO, Plenoxels), and hash-grid-MLP (Nerfacto) architectures, consistently outperforming each system's original heuristics across all tested scaling factors.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset with Nerfacto, the proposed high-transmittance initialization maintains PSNR within ~0.5 of the baseline at k=1.0 (e.g., bicycle 22.48 vs 22.62, room 28.91 vs 28.90) but dramatically outperforms it at k=10, where the baseline collapses (bicycle: 12.42, bonsai: 13.91, room: 11.27) while the proposed method retains quality (22.40, 28.50, 28.89). On the Blender synthetic dataset with TensoRF, the baseline completely fails at k=0.1 across all 8 scenes, whereas the proposed recipe achieves 26.0–37.0 PSNR. The method also eliminates random optimization failures in vanilla NeRF that occur at multiple k values in the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla NeRF (NeRF-Pytorch with ReLU), the baseline exhibits random optimization failures at multiple k values (e.g., lego drops to 9.45 at k=0.4, ship to 5.88 at k=2.5 and k=10), while the proposed GumbelCDF recipe with high-transmittance offset achieves consistent 27–34 PSNR across all k without any failures. Against TensoRF, the baseline completely fails at k=0.1 on all 8 Blender scenes (its hardcoded −10 softplus offset and ×25 interval multiplier are tuned for k≈1), whereas the proposed method achieves 26.0–37.0 PSNR; at k=10 the gap narrows since TensoRF's aggressive offset happens to help at large scales. Against Nerfacto (the most modern baseline, already using exp activation), both methods perform comparably at k≤2.5 (within ~0.5 PSNR), but at k=10 the baseline suffers catastrophic degradation due to the 'cloudiness trap' (bicycle: 12.42, bonsai: 13.91, room: 11.27) while the proposed high-transmittance initialization recovers to near k=1.0 quality (22.40, 28.50, 28.89), a gap of 8–17 PSNR. The paper also qualitatively demonstrates the method on Tanks and Temples at k=25, where the baseline produces cloudy floaters and the proposed method renders clean depth maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes and systematizes a property (alpha invariance) that is implicit in the volume rendering equation used by NeRF [20] and all subsequent radiance field methods. It builds directly on the exp activation introduced in Instant-NGP [21] but provides the missing theoretical justification via the Gumbel CDF connection, showing that log-space parameterization of both distance and density naturally cancels scaling factors. The paper positions itself as a diagnostic and corrective study: it surveys the ad-hoc heuristics (fixed interval sizes in DVGO, hardcoded offsets in TensoRF, aggressive learning rates in Plenoxels, scene contraction in Nerfacto) that existing systems use to cope with scene scaling, and replaces them with a unified, closed-form recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe (Gumbel CDF activation + high-transmittance offset) is simple to implement and makes a wide range of NeRF architectures robust to arbitrary scene scaling without per-scene tuning, which is valuable for practitioners who must handle scenes of unknown or varying size. The systematic ablation across MLP, voxel, and hash-grid architectures clarifies which components are truly necessary (exp activation for large densities, transmittance offset for large scenes) versus which are incidental. Open questions include whether the i.i.d. assumption on density samples along a ray holds well enough in practice for correlated representations like hash grids, and whether the recipe extends cleanly to dynamic or 4D radiance fields where temporal scaling introduces additional ambiguity.
