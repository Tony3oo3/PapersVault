## Problem

Existing text-to-SVG generation methods based on Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) produce vector graphics that lack editability (objects become entangled during synthesis), suffer from shape over-smoothing and color over-saturation, exhibit limited diversity due to mode-seeking optimization, and converge slowly. No prior method simultaneously addresses all four of these limitations while preserving the structural editability that makes vector graphics useful in design workflows.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a style specification that constrains the set of vector primitives (closed/open Bezier curves, square polygons) and their trainable parameters (fill colors, stroke colors, stroke widths, opacity). The output is an SVG file whose paths are semantically grouped into foreground objects and a background, each independently selectable and editable. Six distinct style presets (Iconography, Sketch, Pixel Art, Low-Poly, Painting, Ink) are supported, each governing which primitive types and attributes are optimized.

## Method

SVGDreamer combines two components. First, Semantic-Driven Image Vectorization (SIVE) extracts cross-attention maps from the Stable Diffusion model for each text token to initialize per-object control points, then optimizes them with an attention-mask loss that constrains each object's paths to its designated spatial region, yielding semantically separated and independently editable vector elements. Second, Vectorized Particle-Based Score Distillation (VPSD) replaces the single-parameter SDS objective with a particle-based variational formulation: k groups of vector parameters represent a distribution over SVGs, a LoRA-adapted diffusion network estimates the score of the current particle distribution, and the gradient is computed as the difference between the pretrained model's score and the LoRA-estimated score. A reward model (ReFL) re-weights particles by aesthetic score, filtering low-quality samples from LoRA updates and accelerating convergence by roughly 2×.

## Results

SVGDreamer (full pipeline with SIVE + VPSD + Reward Feedback) achieves the best score on all six metrics: FID 59.13, PSNR 14.54, CLIPScore 0.3001, BLIPScore 0.4623, Aesthetic 5.5432, and HPS 0.2685. Compared to the strongest baseline VectorFusion, SVGDreamer reduces FID by 41% (59.13 vs. 100.68) and improves PSNR by 81% (14.54 vs. 8.01), indicating substantially better diversity and reduced color over-saturation. Even the from-scratch variant (without SIVE initialization) already outperforms all baselines on every metric, with FID 84.04 and PSNR 10.48. Evaluation is conducted across six vector styles (Iconography, Sketch, Pixel Art, Low-Poly, Painting, Ink) with 10 prompts and 50 SVGs per prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVGDreamer outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against VectorFusion (the strongest baseline), SVGDreamer achieves a 41% lower FID (59.13 vs. 100.68), an 81% higher PSNR (14.54 vs. 8.01), and leads on CLIPScore (0.3001 vs. 0.2720), BLIPScore (0.4623 vs. 0.4291), Aesthetic (5.5432 vs. 4.9845), and HPS (0.2685 vs. 0.2450). Against CLIPDraw, the gap is even larger: FID drops from 160.64 to 59.13 and Aesthetic rises from 3.98 to 5.54, reflecting CLIPDraw's reliance on CLIP rather than a diffusion prior. Against DiffSketcher (re-implemented for RGB), SVGDreamer halves the FID (59.13 vs. 118.70) and more than doubles PSNR (14.54 vs. 6.75), confirming that VPSD's distributional modeling effectively mitigates the over-smoothing and over-saturation inherent in SDS-based approaches. The ablation rows further show that SIVE initialization (full vs. from-scratch) contributes an additional ~25 FID points and ~4 PSNR points, while Reward Feedback adds a smaller but consistent boost across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVGDreamer extends the text-to-SVG generation line initiated by VectorFusion and DiffSketcher, which adapted Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion to optimize differentiable vector graphics parameters. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) SIVE replaces LIVE's layerwise hierarchical vectorization with cross-attention-guided semantic decomposition of foreground objects and background, and (2) VPSD replaces point-optimization SDS with a distributional formulation inspired by ProlificDreamer's variational score distillation, using LoRA to model the distribution of control points and colors. The reward-feedback mechanism further differentiates it by re-weighting particles via a pretrained reward model, a technique borrowed from ReFL but applied to the vector particle setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in text-to-SVG tools: editability. By semantically separating foreground objects from background, designers can independently modify individual elements, which is critical for real-world poster and logo workflows. The VPSD formulation's distributional modeling directly tackles the over-smoothing and mode-seeking limitations of SDS that have plagued both 3D and 2D vector generation. Open questions remain around automatic determination of the number of control points per object, scalability to more complex multi-object scenes, and whether the editability ceiling imposed by the underlying T2I model's attention quality can be broken without architectural changes to the diffusion backbone.
