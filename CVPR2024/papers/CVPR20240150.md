## Problem

Existing visual grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO, ReferItGame, etc.) are confined to small-scale scenes with low resolutions (≤640×480) and a handful of objects, while modern imaging technology now produces gigapixel-level images spanning km²-scale scenes with hundreds of objects at vastly different scales. This gap limits the practical deployment of grounding models in real-world applications such as search-and-rescue, and no benchmark or model has been designed to handle the combined challenges of extreme resolution, significant scale variation, and multi-hop relational expressions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a gigapixel-level RGB image (typical resolution >25k×14k pixels) and a natural language expression (average 14.78 words, including multi-hop expressions that reference other objects) as input, and outputs a single bounding box localizing the referred object. In practice, the full-resolution image is consumed at two stages: a 640×640 downsampled thumbnail for coarse localization and a 1536×1536 adaptive crop for fine-grained grounding, with the final box coordinates mapped back to the original image space.

## Method

GlaZing employs a 'glance-to-zoom-in' cascading architecture inspired by human visual search. The Glance Grounding Module (GGM), built on ReSC's anchor-based recursive subquery construction, processes a 640×640 thumbnail to produce a coarse region-of-interest prediction. An Adaptive Cropping Module (ACM) then extracts the minimum rectangular region enclosing both the GGM prediction and a fixed-size center patch from the 1536×1536 image, resizing it to 640×640. The Zoomed-in Grounding Module (ZGM), based on TransVG's coordinate regression, refines the prediction within this high-resolution crop. Training combines YOLOv3 anchor-association and diversity losses for GGM with smooth L1 and generalized IoU losses for ZGM, and the adaptive cropping mechanism allows the model to handle objects spanning from ~110×330 to ~3000×1400 pixels without per-scale retraining.

## Results

On the GigaGrounding test set, GlaZing achieves 63.9% Precision@0.5 on direct expressions and 63.7% overall, substantially outperforming the best baseline (ReSC at 1536×1536) which reaches only 49.7% on direct expressions — a 28.6% relative improvement. No baseline exceeds 50% Precision@0.5, while human evaluators achieve 84% at 1536×1536 resolution. GlaZing operates at 110.2 ms inference time with 172.1 GFLOPs and 280.8M trainable parameters, comparable in efficiency to the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlaZing decisively outperforms all seven baselines on GigaGrounding. Against the strongest one-stage baseline ReSC at 1536×1536 (49.7% test direct), GlaZing gains 14.2 absolute points (63.9%), a 28.6% relative improvement. RefTR (41.0%) and SimREC (33.3%) are the next-best baselines but still trail GlaZing by 22.9 and 30.6 points respectively. The two-stage method MAttNet performs near random guessing (6.8% vs. 6.2% random), suggesting its frozen COCO/ImageNet-pretrained feature extractor is fundamentally inadequate for gigapixel scenes. Notably, GlaZing maintains consistent accuracy across direct (63.9%) and multi-hop (64.1%) expressions, whereas baselines show marked degradation on multi-hop expressions (e.g., ReSC drops from 49.7% to 37.0% overall). Even at 640×640 resolution where GlaZing's advantage is smaller, it still leads the best baseline (ReSC at 29.0%) by 34.9 points, confirming the glance-to-zoom-in strategy's robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual grounding task from small-scale scenes (RefCOCO, Flickr30K, ReferItGame, all ≤640×480) to gigapixel-level large-scale scenes, drawing on the PANDA dataset for imagery. Methodologically, GlaZing composes two existing one-stage grounding architectures—ReSC (anchor-based, for the glance stage) and TransVG (coordinate-regression, for the zoom-in stage)—with a novel adaptive cropping module, inspired by human 'glance-to-zoom-in' cognition. It also connects to the high-resolution deep learning literature (non-uniform downsampling, selective zooming, scanner networks) but applies these ideas specifically to the grounding task rather than detection or segmentation. The introduction of multi-hop expressions, which require relational reasoning over reference objects, departs from all prior VG benchmarks that focus on direct or appearance-based descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GigaGrounding is the first benchmark to expose the gap between current VG models and the demands of real-world large-scale scenes, revealing that no existing model exceeds 50% Precision@0.5 while humans achieve 62–84%. The finding that two-stage methods fail near random chance even with ground-truth proposals suggests a fundamental limitation in frozen feature extractors for large-scale scene understanding, pointing to a need for scene-aware backbones. Open questions include whether end-to-end multi-iteration glance-zoom pipelines can close the remaining ~20-point gap to human performance, and whether the multi-hop reasoning challenge generalizes to other spatially complex tasks. The benchmark's practical relevance to rescue, robotics, and surveillance makes it a potentially important testbed for future grounding research.
