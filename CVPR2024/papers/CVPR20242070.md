## Problem

Video understanding tasks such as action recognition, captioning, and object tracking each rely on distinct architectures and annotation formats (one-hot labels, free-form sentences, continuous coordinates), preventing a single unified model from addressing them all. The paper asks whether, analogous to how NLP unified diverse tasks under a shared text-sequence output space, video tasks can be unified by extending the language vocabulary with special time and box tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242070/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 video frames (32 frames for clip-based tasks, 160 frames at 1 FPS for dense video captioning, or 2 frames for tracking where the first frame is replaced by a cropped template) along with task-specific text prompts (a task name, a question for ViQA, or a discretized bounding box for VOT). All outputs are generated as autoregressive token sequences over a unified vocabulary of word tokens, 300 discrete time tokens, and 1000 discrete box tokens per coordinate dimension; continuous quantities like event timestamps and bounding-box coordinates are recovered by dequantizing the generated tokens.

## Method

OmniViD is an encoder-decoder framework in which a VideoSwin-Base video encoder extracts frame features, and a language encoder (BART-base) encodes the text prompts. A lightweight MQ-Former, inspired by BLIP-2's Q-Former but augmented with three query types (content, sentence, and box), aggregates per-frame features via cross-attention, after which a temporal transformer layer models inter-frame dynamics. An MLP visual translator projects the resulting queries into the text embedding space, where they are concatenated with prompt embeddings and fed to a BART-based autoregressive token decoder with causal self-attention. Training uses a single cross-entropy objective over the unified token vocabulary, and inference employs beam search; the key architectural insight is that discretizing spatial and temporal coordinates into learnable tokens lets one shared decoder handle classification, captioning, question answering, event localization, and tracking without task-specific heads.

## Results

OmniViD achieves state-of-the-art action recognition with 83.6% top-1 accuracy on Kinetics-400 and 71.3% on Something-Something V2, surpassing VideoSwin-B by 0.9 and 1.7 points respectively. For clip captioning, it reaches 56.6 CIDEr on MSRVTT and 122.5 on MSVD, outperforming the previous best (SwinBERT) by +2.8 and +1.9. On visual object tracking, OmniViD attains 88.9 normalized precision on TrackingNet, beating all prior methods including UNINEXT (88.2) and ARTrack (88.7). For dense video captioning, it achieves 5.6 SODA_c on ActivityNet, competitive with the pretrained Vid2seq* (5.8) while significantly outperforming the non-pretrained Vid2seq (5.4) in CIDEr (26.0 vs. 18.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On action recognition, OmniViD (83.6% K400, 71.3% SSV2) outperforms all 16 baselines, including the strongest prior methods VideoSwin-B (82.7/69.6) and UniFormer-B (82.9/71.2), with margins of +0.9/+1.7 on K400/SSV2. For clip captioning, it beats SwinBERT (53.8 CIDEr) by +2.8 on MSRVTT and (120.6) by +1.9 on MSVD, and also surpasses methods that rely on off-the-shelf object detectors (OA-BTG, ORG-TRL). On video question answering, OmniViD (42.3% MSRVTT, 47.7% MSVD) trails OmniVL (44.1/51.0) which uses 18M samples of video-language pretraining, but outperforms all non-pretrained baselines including JustAsk (39.6/41.2) and HCRN (35.6/36.1). For dense video captioning, OmniViD (5.6 SODA_c) is slightly below the large-scale pretrained Vid2seq* (5.8) but clearly above the non-pretrained Vid2seq (5.4) and all two-stage methods (PDVC 5.3, UEDVC 5.5). In visual object tracking, OmniViD achieves 88.9 Pnorm on TrackingNet (beating UNINEXT 88.2, ARTrack 88.7, MixFormer 88.1) but falls slightly short of UNINEXT on LaSOT (79.6 vs. 80.7 Pnorm), indicating the unified framework trades a small amount of tracking-specific performance for cross-task generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniViD draws its architectural backbone from BLIP-2's Q-Former, extending it with sentence and box queries to form the MQ-Former, and adopts a BART-based token decoder for autoregressive generation. Conceptually, it is inspired by NLP's unified text-sequence output space (GPT, Llama) and applies the same principle to video by discretizing timestamps and bounding-box coordinates into special tokens. It departs from prior unified video models (OmniVL, UNLoc, UniVTG, Unicorn, UNINEXT) that still rely on task-specific prediction heads, instead formulating all tasks as a single video-grounded token generation problem. The autoregressive tracking line (Pix2Seq, ARTrack, SeqTrack) is extended from images to video and from tracking-only to a multi-task setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that five structurally different video tasks (classification, captioning, QA, dense captioning, and tracking) can be handled by one shared encoder-decoder without task-specific heads, OmniViD offers a practical path toward scaling video understanding with diverse data, analogous to how unified text output enabled LLMs. The main limitation is that joint training degrades spatial-temporal localization performance relative to separate training, suggesting that multi-task optimization strategies remain an open problem. Future work could explore whether this token-unification paradigm scales to longer videos, more tasks (e.g., video segmentation, temporal grounding), or larger pretrained video encoders, and whether the modest per-task gains compound when trained on a single large multi-task corpus.
