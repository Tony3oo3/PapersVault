## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models can generate photorealistic images but cannot produce the instance-level bounding box annotations required to train object detectors, and off-the-shelf diffusion models tend to output images with only one or two objects on simplistic backgrounds, limiting their utility for realistic detection training. This paper addresses both gaps by augmenting a diffusion model with the ability to generate complex multi-object scenes and to simultaneously predict bounding boxes for each instance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241117/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The synthesizer takes a text prompt constructed from a template (e.g., 'a photograph of [category1] and [category2]') and a sampled noise vector as input, and produces a synthetic image together with instance-level bounding boxes and category labels. The grounding head operates on multi-scale dense visual features extracted from the diffusion model at denoising step t=1 (not on the final decoded image), and the CLIP text encoder provides category embeddings. The resulting synthetic dataset is combined with real annotated data to train a standard Faster R-CNN detector at 800×800 input resolution.

## Method

InstaGen consists of two stages. First, Stable Diffusion v1.4 is fine-tuned on random crops from a real detection dataset (COCO), using the category names within each crop as the text prompt, so that the model learns to generate images with multiple objects and complex contexts. Second, an instance grounding head—architecturally inspired by GroundingDINO with a channel-compression layer, six feature-enhancer layers (deformable self-attention + cross-attention), a language-guided query selection module, and a six-layer cross-modality decoder—is trained to align CLIP text embeddings with regional visual features and regress bounding boxes. Training proceeds in two phases: supervised learning on base categories using an off-the-shelf Mask R-CNN as an oracle labeler (score threshold α=0.8), followed by self-training on novel categories via a mean-teacher scheme (EMA momentum 0.999, score threshold β=0.4) where the teacher generates pseudo-labels from visual features alone. The final synthetic dataset, generated at scale with arbitrary category prompts, is combined with real data to train standard detectors, yielding +4.5 AP on open-vocabulary COCO and +1.2–5.2 AP in data-sparse settings.

## Results

On the open-vocabulary COCO benchmark (48 base / 17 novel classes), InstaGen achieves 42.3 AP50 on novel categories, surpassing the previous best (EdaDet, 37.8) by +4.5 AP and the second-best overall (CxORA, 35.1) by +7.2 AP. In data-sparse settings, training with synthetic data yields consistent gains from +1.0 AP at 100% real data to +5.2 AP at only 10% real data (23.3 → 28.5 AP). For cross-dataset transfer, InstaGen reaches 11.4 AP50 on Object365 and 23.3 AP50 on LVIS, outperforming CLIP-based methods that require additional target-domain data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstaGen outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every evaluation protocol. On the open-vocabulary COCO benchmark, the largest margins are over PromptDet (+15.7 AP novel), ViLD (+14.7), Detic (+14.5), and F-VLM (+14.3), while the closest competitor EdaDet (Deformable DETR + CLIP) trails by 4.5 AP on novel categories. Among CLIP-based methods that use more sophisticated detectors (CxORA with DAB-DETR, DK-DETR with Deformable DETR), InstaGen still leads by 7.2 and 10.0 AP respectively, indicating that the synthetic-data paradigm is orthogonal to detector architecture. On cross-dataset transfer, InstaGen beats VL-PLM by +0.5 AP on Object365 and +1.1 AP on LVIS, and substantially outperforms Gao et al. (+4.5 on Object365, +15.3 on LVIS); notably, InstaGen does not require any additional target-domain dataset, whereas both CLIP-based baselines need pseudo-labeled COCO images covering target categories. In data-sparse settings, the advantage grows as real data shrinks, reaching +5.2 AP at 10% COCO, confirming that synthetic data is most valuable when real annotations are scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Stable Diffusion as a data synthesizer for object detection by attaching a GroundingDINO-style grounding head to the diffusion model's intermediate features, rather than using the diffusion model purely for image generation. It departs from the dominant CLIP-based open-vocabulary detection paradigm (knowledge distillation, prompt tuning, pseudo-labeling on real images) by instead generating both images and bounding boxes from scratch, eliminating the need for real annotated data in the target categories. The self-training scheme with a mean teacher operating on diffusion features (rather than pixel-space images) is a notable adaptation, since standard data augmentation is unavailable in the latent feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that a diffusion model augmented with instance grounding can serve as a scalable, annotation-free data synthesizer for training object detectors, offering a practical path to expand detectable categories without manual labeling. The data-sparse results (+5.2 AP at 10% real data) suggest particular value in domains where annotation is expensive. However, the authors acknowledge that synthetic images still lack the contextual complexity of real scenes (occlusions, clutter, deformation), and the long-tail problem in diffusion models limits generation quality for rare categories—both of which remain open challenges for closing the synthetic-to-real gap in detection.
