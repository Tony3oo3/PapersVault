## Problem

While prior work can extract interpretable concepts from individual layers or attribute pixel-level contributions to model outputs, no existing method quantifies how distributed concepts at one layer contribute to concepts at another layer, leaving the hierarchical structure of concept assemblies across an entire network unexplored. This gap prevents understanding how many concepts a network encodes, how they connect across depths, and whether architecture choices shape the resulting concept hierarchy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of class exemplar RGB images, a pretrained deep network (CNN or transformer), and a user-selected subset of layer indices, then produces a topologically sorted directed acyclic graph whose nodes are k-means cluster centroids (concepts) at each layer, whose edges are ITCAV scalar weights measuring interlayer concept contribution, and whose each node is accompanied by up to four masked RGB segment exemplars. The graph includes the final class prediction as an additional terminal layer, and the entire construction is unsupervised and open-world, requiring no predefined concept dictionary or labelled concept images.

## Method

VCC construction proceeds in three steps: (1) top-down divisive segmentation in feature space, where maskSLIC clusters at layer n are conditioned on upsampled segment masks from layer n+1, ensuring spatial support respects each layer's receptive field; (2) layer-wise concept discovery by passing masked RGB segments through the model (zeroing out-of-segment regions to remove context) and clustering the resulting pre-segmented activations with k-means plus pruning; (3) interlayer edge weighting via ITCAV, which generalizes TCAV to arbitrary layer pairs by computing a CAV for the earlier concept and a gradient-based sensitivity score measuring how perturbing features along the CAV direction moves deeper-layer activations toward the target concept centroid, with the edge weight being the fraction of positive sensitivities. The l2-norm distance in the sensitivity computation suppresses noisy dimensions and makes the gradient computationally tractable compared to a full Jacobian. The approach handles superposition (multiple concepts per channel) and applies uniformly to both CNNs and transformers.

## Results

VCC segment proposals scale with the receptive field (RF) at each layer across ResNet50, VGG16, and ViT-b, whereas the ACE baseline produces segments with sizes invariant to the analyzed layer. Concept suppression experiments over 50 ImageNet classes show that perturbing in the opposite direction of a discovered concept reduces model accuracy faster than random perturbations for all tested architectures (ResNet50, VGG16, MobileNetv3, ViT-b, MViT). ITCAV validation confirms a positive correlation between average path strength (APS) and logit sum (LS) scores for both VGG16 and ViT-b, indicating that interlayer edge weights are predictive of class probability. Across all-layer VCCs, concept counts peak at ~20 (at 30–40% network depth) and converge to ~2 in the final layer, with branching factors ranging 5–15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is ACE (Ghorbani et al., NeurIPS 2019), compared in the segment proposal validation (Fig. 3a). ACE performs segmentation using a layer-independent feature (color) on the input image, producing concept segments whose sizes remain roughly constant regardless of which network layer is analyzed. In contrast, VCC's divisive clustering in the model's own feature space yields segments whose spatial extent scales monotonically with the receptive field of the corresponding layer—small patches at early CNN layers and large regions at later layers. Beyond this single direct comparison, the paper's broader empirical contribution is architectural: four-layer and all-layer VCC analyses reveal that CNNs exhibit a decreasing branching factor across depth while transformers maintain a near-constant branching factor until the final layer, where all models converge to approximately two concepts (foreground/background). Transformers also show higher final-layer edge-weight variance, suggesting more differentiated concept composition, and self-supervised (SimCLR) or adversarially trained (Stylized ImageNet) variants of ResNet50/VGG16 produce fewer low-level concepts and less compositionality than their supervised counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends concept-based interpretability from single-layer analysis to a full multi-layer, interlayer connectivity framework. It builds directly on TCAV (Kim et al., 2018) by generalizing the CAV-based sensitivity test from 'concept → class logit' to 'concept at layer j → concept at layer l' (ITCAV), and on ACE (Ghorbani et al., 2019) by replacing RGB-space segmentation with feature-space divisive clustering that respects each layer's receptive field. The key conceptual departure is the 'connectome' framing: prior work extracts concepts at one layer or attributes pixels to a single output, whereas VCC constructs a directed acyclic graph over all selected layers, yielding a structural description of how concepts compose hierarchically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VCC provides the first fully unsupervised, open-world method to quantify how concepts at one layer contribute to concepts at another, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature. The architectural comparison findings (CNNs show decreasing branching factor and rely heavily on final-layer concepts, while transformers maintain consistent branching and show higher final-layer edge weight variance) offer a concrete, quantitative lens for understanding why transformers may outperform CNNs. Limitations include high computational cost (~36 hours for all-layer VCCs), restriction to classification with a fixed set of exemplar images, and reliance on k-means clustering whose quality depends on the number of clusters chosen. Open questions include extension to dense prediction tasks, sensitivity to the choice of exemplar images, and whether the discovered concept hierarchies are stable across training runs.
