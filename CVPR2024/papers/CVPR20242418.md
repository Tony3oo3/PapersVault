## Problem

CLIP provides powerful image-level zero-shot recognition but lacks the pixel-level localization capability required for semantic segmentation. When CLIP is fine-tuned for segmentation, it suffers catastrophic forgetting—overfitting to seen classes while losing generalization to unseen ones. The paper identifies these two gaps (missing fine-grained regional semantics and overfitting during tuning) as the core obstacles to adapting CLIP for zero-shot semantic segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242418/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a set of class name strings as input. The image is encoded by a ViT-B/16 CLIP visual encoder augmented with 16 frozen region-level bridge tokens and deep prompt tuning tokens, while class names are encoded by CLIP's text encoder. The output is a pixel-level segmentation map of shape C×N (C classes × N patches), produced by averaging M region-specific predictions, each computed via cross-attention between region-conditioned text queries and aligned image features.

## Method

CLIP-RC introduces a Region-Level Bridge (RLB) that inserts M=16 frozen tokens into the ViT input, each attending to a fixed sub-region of the image via a structured attention mask, thereby extracting region-granularity category features as an intermediate between image-level and pixel-level representations. A Region Alignment Module (RAM) then upsamples the global [CLS] token and RLB features to patch resolution, concatenates them with the original image features, and constructs regional relationship descriptors by element-wise multiplying the fused region features with text embeddings. To combat overfitting, a training-only Recovery Decoder (architecturally identical to the segmentation decoder) is applied to the decoder outputs, and an L1 recovery loss pulls the recovered features back toward the original backbone features, balancing task-specific adaptation with preservation of CLIP's general knowledge. The total training objective combines a Non-mutually Exclusive Loss (sigmoid + BCE) for segmentation with the recovery loss, and the RLB gradients are explicitly frozen to prevent the region tokens from overfitting to seen classes.

## Results

In the inductive zero-shot setting, CLIP-RC achieves hIoU of 88.4% on PASCAL VOC 2012 (mIoU(U) 84.4%), 51.9% on PASCAL Context (mIoU(U) 57.3%), and 41.2% on COCO-Stuff 164K (mIoU(U) 41.6%), setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. In the transductive setting with self-training, it reaches hIoU 93.0% on PASCAL VOC 2012 and 60.8% mIoU(U) on COCO-Stuff 164K. The largest gains over the previous SOTA (ZegCLIP) appear on unseen classes, with a +6.6% mIoU(U) improvement on PASCAL VOC 2012.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-RC outperforms the previous one-stage SOTA, ZegCLIP [55], on every metric across all three datasets in the inductive setting, with the most substantial gain being +6.6% mIoU(U) on PASCAL VOC 2012 (84.4 vs. 77.8) and +4.1% hIoU (88.4 vs. 84.3). Against two-stage methods, CLIP-RC surpasses ZegFormer [7] by a wide margin (hIoU 88.4 vs. 73.3 on VOC) and ZSSeg [46] by +10.9% hIoU, while also exceeding MaskCLIP+ [52] (hIoU 87.4) and FreeSeg [33] (hIoU 86.9) in the transductive setting. On PASCAL Context, CLIP-RC leads ZegCLIP by +2.0% hIoU and +2.7% mIoU(U), and on COCO-Stuff 164K the margin is smaller (+0.4% hIoU, +0.2% mIoU(U)), suggesting the method's advantage is most pronounced on datasets with fewer classes. In the fully supervised upper-bound experiment (Table 3), CLIP-RC also exceeds ZegCLIP, confirming the gains are not solely from the zero-shot protocol. The method consistently wins on unseen-class metrics (mIoU(U)) more than on seen-class metrics (mIoU(S)), validating the region-level bridge's role in preserving generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIP-RC extends the one-stage ZS3 paradigm established by ZegCLIP, directly adopting its Relationship Descriptors and Non-mutually Exclusive Loss, while integrating Visual Prompt Tuning for parameter-efficient adaptation of CLIP's ViT encoder. The key conceptual departure is the Region-Level Bridge (RLB), which uses masked attention to create region-specific tokens that extract fine-grained category features at an intermediate granularity between image-level and pixel-level — a mechanism absent from prior one-stage methods. Unlike two-stage approaches (ZegFormer, ZSSeg, MaskCLIP+) that rely on separate class-agnostic mask generators, and unlike CLIPSelf which transfers regional features via knowledge distillation, CLIP-RC embeds regional reasoning directly within the CLIP encoder through frozen RLB tokens, and introduces a Recovery Decoder with L1 loss to counteract catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CLIP's pre-trained encoder already possesses strong region-level classification capability, suggesting that the gap between image-level and pixel-level understanding can be bridged with relatively lightweight architectural modifications rather than heavy two-stage pipelines. The recovery decoder addresses a practical and persistent problem in CLIP fine-tuning — catastrophic forgetting of unseen classes — with a simple L1-based constraint that outperforms L2 and knowledge-distillation alternatives. However, the method remains tied to CLIP's pre-training quality, and the computational cost grows substantially with finer region granularity (OOM at 1024 tokens), leaving open whether the region-level bridge concept can be made more scalable or generalized to other dense prediction tasks beyond segmentation.
