## Problem

Existing 3D human pose estimation and generation methods operate in narrow, task-specific pipelines that lack semantic understanding of scenes, human intentions, and body language. Text-driven pose generation requires explicit pose descriptions and cannot handle implicit or speculative queries. The paper asks whether multimodal LLMs can be taught to interpret 3D human pose as a modality, enabling them to reason about poses using their broad world knowledge in conversational settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241977/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a free-form text query (ranging from explicit pose descriptions to speculative questions like 'how would this person look if tired?' or person-identifying phrases like 'the woman near the stairs') and optionally an RGB image. When the LLM decides to output a pose, it emits a special <POSE> token whose hidden-state embedding is projected to 144-dimensional SMPL pose parameters (6D rotations for all joints, with shape β fixed to zero). These parameters are decoded via the standard SMPL forward function into a 3D triangulated body mesh, while the LLM simultaneously produces a natural-language textual response.

## Method

ChatPose builds on LLaVA-1.5V-13B (CLIP vision encoder + Vicuna-13B LLM) and introduces 3D body pose as a new output modality by adding a dedicated SMPL projection layer (MLP with dimensions 5120→5120→144) that maps the <POSE> token's language embedding to SMPL 6D rotation parameters. The vision encoder and vision projection are frozen; only the SMPL projection layer is trained from scratch and the LLM is fine-tuned via LoRA. Training uses a joint objective combining cross-entropy loss on the textual response and L1 loss on the predicted pose parameters, with data constructed from PoseScript text-to-pose pairs, cropped image-to-pose pairs from Human3.6M/MPI-INF-3DHP/COCO/MPII, and LLaVA-V1.5-MIX665K instruction-following data to preserve general multimodal capabilities. The key insight is that once the LLM can emit and interpret the <POSE> token, it can zero-shot reason about poses in multi-turn conversations—connecting high-level concepts (e.g., 'proposing marriage') to concrete 3D body configurations without additional task-specific training.

## Results

On classical 3D pose estimation, ChatPose achieves 163.6 mm MPJPE and 81.9 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW, outperforming all multimodal LLM baselines (LLaVA-S: 440.8/205.4, GPT4-S: 322.0/136.7) but trailing specialized regressors HMR 2.0 (91.0/58.4) and SPIN (102.9/62.9). On the novel Speculative Pose Generation benchmark, ChatPose achieves R^T2P@20 of 25.3, substantially outperforming PoseScript (7.2) by more than 3×. On Reasoning-based Pose Estimation, ChatPose attains 101.8 mm PA-MPJPE averaged across description types, beating both SPIN (107.3) and HMR 2.0 (105.7), as well as LLM+pipeline baselines (LLaVA+SMPLify: 203.7, LLaVA+PoseScript: 186.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against specialized pose regressors HMR 2.0 and SPIN, ChatPose underperforms on classical single-image pose estimation (163.6 vs. 91.0 and 102.9 mm MPJPE on 3DPW), which the authors frame as a first proof-of-concept. However, ChatPose decisively outperforms all multimodal LLM baselines: it beats LLaVA-S (440.8), GPT4-S (322.0), LLaVA-P (335.2), and GPT4-P (396.5) by 100–280 mm MPJPE on 3DPW. On the novel SPG task, ChatPose (R^T2P@20 = 25.3) surpasses PoseScript (7.2) by over 3×, demonstrating that LLM world knowledge enables reasoning about implicit pose queries that task-specific methods cannot handle. On RPE, ChatPose (101.8 PA-MPJPE) beats both SPIN (107.3) and HMR 2.0 (105.7), as well as LLM+pipeline combinations (LLaVA+SMPLify: 203.7; LLaVA+PoseScript: 186.8), showing that end-to-end pose token generation outperforms two-stage describe-then-optimize approaches. In GPT4-assisted evaluation of general multimodal capability, ChatPose (84.0) slightly lags LLaVA-V1.5-13B (86.5), confirming that pose training does not catastrophically degrade language and vision understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multimodal LLM architecture of LLaVA (CLIP vision encoder + Vicuna LLM) by introducing SMPL pose parameters as a new output modality, analogous to how NeXT-GPT aligns audio/video embeddings with language. It departs from task-specific pose estimation methods (HMR2.0, SPIN) and text-to-pose generators (PoseScript) by unifying both under a single LLM that reasons over world knowledge, and from prior LLM-based vision approaches (LLaVA, LISA) by adding a learned SMPL projection layer that maps a special <POSE> token embedding to continuous 3D rotation parameters via an MLP. The training strategy—freezing vision components, using LoRA for the LLM, and constructing QA pairs from existing pose datasets—follows standard multimodal instruction-tuning practice, but the pose-as-token formulation is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a first proof-of-concept that a general-purpose multimodal LLM can directly output 3D body pose parameters and, more importantly, reason about them using world knowledge (e.g., inferring a tired person's slumped posture from a vague query). The two new benchmarks—Speculative Pose Generation and Reasoning-based Pose Estimation—open a research direction that goes beyond pixel-level or parameter-level accuracy toward semantic understanding of human posture. Key limitations include accuracy well below specialized regressors (MPJPE ~164mm vs. ~91mm for HMR2.0 on 3DPW), a frozen vision encoder, and limited multi-turn pose conversation ability; future work on pose editing, body shape, and video input could substantially expand the framework's utility.
