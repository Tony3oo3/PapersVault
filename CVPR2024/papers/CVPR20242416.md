## Problem

Existing 3D garment recovery methods struggle with loose-fitting clothing that deviates substantially from the body shape. Template-based approaches have limited generality, point-cloud methods are not directly usable for animation or simulation, and watertight-mesh approaches (e.g., ECON) bind the garment to the body, precluding independent cloth dynamics. The paper addresses this by recovering separate, animation-ready garment meshes that faithfully capture large deformations such as flowing skirts and open jackets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image of a clothed person and derives all other inputs via off-the-shelf tools: normals are estimated with ECON, SMPL body parameters (β, θ) with HMR, and garment segmentation masks with SAM plus semantic parsing. The output is a triangulated 3D garment mesh in both its rest state (draped on a T-pose body) and its deformed state matching the input image, with the two states linked through the ISP UV parameterization so the mesh is directly usable for cloth simulation and re-animation without additional processing.

## Method

The approach extends the ISP garment model (which provides a shape prior via 2D SDF-defined panels and UV-to-3D mapping) with a learned deformation model that predicts per-UV-point occupancy and corrective 3D displacement. The deformation network consists of an MLP conditioned on pixel-aligned image features extracted by two ConvNeXt CNNs (one processing the normal map, the other processing body segmentation and vertex position maps), enabling the garment surface to deviate substantially from the body. Fitting to a new image is a two-stage optimization: first, the deformation model parameters are fine-tuned to minimize a composite loss (2D Chamfer distance to the segmentation, normal alignment, and a physics-based energy including strain, bending, gravity, and body-garment collision), then vertex positions are directly optimized to recover fine details like wrinkles. The key insight is that optimizing the deformation model's weights modifies the entire continuous deformation field globally, whereas direct vertex optimization only affects local regions, so the two stages are complementary.

## Results

On CLOTH3D, the proposed method achieves Chamfer Distance (CD ×10³) of 2.87 (skirt), 0.44 (shirt), and 0.28 (trousers), with corresponding IoU values of 0.940, 0.939, and 0.946, using normals estimated from images. With ground-truth normals, CD improves to 1.67/0.31/0.23 and IoU to 0.953/0.942/0.954. These results substantially outperform all baselines across all three garment categories on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on both CD and IoU across all three garment categories. Compared to ISP (the closest quantitative baseline), the method reduces CD by 60% on skirts (2.87 vs. 7.26), 78% on shirts (0.44 vs. 2.02), and 69% on trousers (0.28 vs. 0.91), while improving IoU by 23–27 percentage points. DrapeNet, which does not support skirts, still trails the proposed method on shirts (CD 1.54 vs. 0.44) and trousers (CD 0.84 vs. 0.28). SMPLicit and ClothWild produce watertight meshes that tightly bind body and garment, making them unsuitable for downstream animation; qualitatively they fail to capture loose-fitting shapes. BCNet, trained only on synthetic RGB, produces inaccurate body and garment predictions on in-the-wild images. The gap between 'Ours' and 'Ours-GT' is modest (e.g., CD 2.87→1.67 on skirts), indicating robustness to normal-estimation noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Implicit Sewing Patterns (ISP) framework [32], which provides a shape prior for garments in rest state via 2D SDF-defined panels and UV parameterization, by introducing a learned deformation model that allows the 3D surfaces to deviate substantially from the body. It departs from SDF-based approaches (SMPLicit, DrapeNet) that require wrapping non-watertight garment surfaces with a minimum thickness, and from point-based or single-watertight-mesh methods (ECON, Neural Point-based) that produce outputs not directly usable for cloth simulation or re-animation. The two-stage fitting strategy—first optimizing deformation network parameters, then refining vertex positions—draws on the exemplar fine-tuning paradigm used in human pose fitting, applied here to a garment-specific deformation field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to recover animation-ready, simulation-ready garment meshes from single in-the-wild images for both tight- and loose-fitting clothing addresses a practical gap that template-based and implicit-surface methods have not closed. The modest performance drop when using estimated normals versus ground-truth normals (e.g., CD 2.87 vs 1.67 for skirts) suggests the approach is robust to the inaccuracies of off-the-shelf normal estimators, making it deployable without specialized annotation. Open questions include generalization beyond the three evaluated categories (shirt, skirt, trousers), handling of complex topologies (e.g., multi-layered outfits, accessories), and the authors' stated future direction of extending to video with temporal consistency.
