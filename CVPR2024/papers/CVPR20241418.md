## Problem

Most geospatial pretrained models are restricted to a single sensor modality, and existing multisensor approaches (e.g., contrastive learning on Sentinel-2/SAR pairs) cannot leverage the abundant unpaired sensor data available in practice. The paper asks three unexplored questions: how to learn joint cross-sensor representations via masked image modeling, whether distilling features from natural-image models (ImageNet, CLIP) helps or hurts geospatial pretraining, and how to mitigate multisensor heterogeneity during training.

## Input / Output

The model ingests four sensor modalities at 192×192 resolution: 3-channel RGB, 14-channel Sentinel-2, 2-channel SAR (HV/VV), and 1-channel DSM, each processed through its own convolutional patch-embedding layer before entering a shared encoder. During pretraining, the output is a pixel-level reconstruction of masked patches, where paired sensors (SAR↔Sentinel-2, RGB↔DSM) may be cross-reconstructed at a 50% probability. During fine-tuning, the frozen encoder feeds task-specific heads producing class labels (BEN), segmentation masks (Vaihingen), cloud-free Sentinel-2 images (SEN12MS-CR), or high-resolution color images (SpaceNet pan-sharpening).

## Method

msGFM employs a Swin-Base backbone with per-sensor patch embeddings, a shared encoder, and per-sensor decoders, using SIMMIM as the pretraining objective. The key architectural innovation is cross-sensor masked image modeling: when two sensors are co-located, the model stochastically (50% of the time) reconstructs the paired sensor's masked patches rather than its own, forcing the shared encoder to align representations across modalities. To handle sensor heterogeneity within the shared encoder, sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts layers replace standard MLPs. A critical empirical finding is that pretraining from scratch substantially outperforms initializing from ImageNet-22k, CLIP, or EVA-style feature distillation, revealing a significant domain gap between natural images and geospatial sensors. The model is pretrained on GeoPile-2 (~2M images combining GeoPile, MillionAID, SEN12MS, and MDAS) for 800 epochs with a batch size of 2048.

## Results

msGFM achieves 92.9% mAP on 100% BigEarthNet scene classification, surpassing the strongest baseline (IN-22k at 89.5%) by 3.4 points, and 87.5% on 10% BEN (vs. 86.3% for GFM). On cloud removal (SEN12MS-CR), msGFM attains the best SAM of 4.87 and MAE of 0.026, outperforming dedicated cloud-removal models. For pan-sharpening (SpaceNet), msGFM reaches 22.850 PSNR and 0.668 SSIM, and for segmentation (Vaihingen) it achieves 75.8 mIOU, both setting new state-of-the-art among the compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

msGFM outperforms all geospatial pretraining baselines (GFM, SatMAE, SeCo, DINO-MC, MoCoV2) across every reported metric. Against the closest geospatial competitor GFM [39], msGFM gains +1.2 mAP on 10% BEN, +0.251 PSNR and +0.030 SSIM on SpaceNet, and +0.6 mIOU on Vaihingen. Compared to the strong ImageNet-22k pretrained Swin backbone (IN-22k), msGFM leads by +3.4 mAP on 100% BEN, +1.2 mAP on 10% BEN, +1.195 PSNR on SpaceNet, and +1.1 mIOU on Vaihingen, confirming that natural-image representations do not transfer well to multisensor geospatial data. On cloud removal, msGFM beats the dedicated specialist GLF-CR [65] on MAE (0.026 vs. 0.027) and SAM (4.87 vs. 7.65) but trails slightly on SSIM (0.842 vs. 0.885), indicating that while the multisensor pretraining captures spectral fidelity well, the structural similarity metric still favours task-specific architectures. The pan-sharpening results on SpaceNet (WorldView-3, a sensor absent from pretraining) demonstrate strong cross-sensor transferability, with msGFM exceeding GFM by +0.251 PSNR and +0.030 SSIM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked image modeling (MIM) paradigm for geospatial pretraining—established by SIMMIM and GFM—by introducing a cross-sensor reconstruction objective that allows the model to predict paired sensor images rather than only self-reconstructing. It departs from prior contrastive-learning approaches (SeCo, Liu et al., Chen & Bruzzone) that are inherently restricted to paired sensor modalities, and from single-sensor MIM models (SatMAE, GFM) that cannot exploit unpaired data. The architectural choice of per-sensor patch embeddings feeding a shared Swin Transformer encoder with Mixture-of-Experts layers draws on multi-source learning strategies from multilingual NLP (XLM, MUPPET) and vision-language MoE models (UniPerceiver-MoE), repurposing them for the geospatial sensor-heterogeneity problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified multisensor geospatial foundation model spanning four modalities (RGB, Sentinel-2, SAR, DSM) on a 2M-image dataset, offering a practical backbone for downstream tasks like cloud removal and pan-sharpening that inherently require multisensor input. A key open question it raises is whether temporal information can be effectively integrated into such spatial pretraining without prohibitive cost, which the authors flag as requiring 'profound rethinking of methodological design.' The finding that natural-image representations (ImageNet-22k, CLIP) fail to transfer to multisensor geospatial data challenges the common practice of initializing geospatial models from natural-image pretraining and suggests the field needs domain-specific foundation models rather than repurposed vision backbones.
