## Problem

Vision-Language foundation models struggle with fine-grained human action recognition because the large semantic gap between low-level visual features and high-level textual descriptions limits effective cross-modality alignment. The paper hypothesizes that the pose modality, which encodes body structure and joint movement as a latent semantic representation, can bridge this gap and enable more effective VPL joint learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240653/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 16-frame video clip at 224×224 resolution, 48-frame 2D body joint coordinates (from HRNet pose estimation), and a prompted text description (e.g., 'Leg separation 180 degree on the diagonal to the floor, a video of gymnastic action') as inputs. At test time, all label texts from a predefined set are fed to the model, and the predicted action is the label with the highest similarity score, determined via multi-view inference (3 spatial crops × 1 temporal clip) with averaged scores.

## Method

PeVL builds on a frozen CLIP backbone (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) with trainable bottleneck adapters on all three encoders. The Unsymmetric Cross-Modality Refinement (UCMR) Block performs bidirectional refinement: P2V uses a weighted pose mask to modulate video self-attention toward body-joint regions, while V2P uses cross-attention from pose tokens to video tokens to inject visual context into pose representations; a refinement supervision loss (NT-Xent) aligns pre- and post-refinement features to prevent cross-modal noise. The Semantic-Guided Multi-level Contrastive (SGMC) Module computes combined similarity across action-level video-text, pose-text, and sub-action-level frame-pose-to-word-text associations, then applies a KL-divergence-based loss where negative-sample pushing strength is scaled by the cosine discrepancy between the sampled text and ground-truth text, enabling elastic contrastive learning that accounts for semantic overlap among fine-grained action labels.

## Results

On Diving48, PeVL achieves 91.9% top-1 accuracy with ViT-B/16 and 92.5% with ViT-L/14, outperforming the closest competitor AIM by 3.0% and 1.9% respectively while using fewer video frames (16 vs. 32). On FineGym (Gym99), PeVL reaches 97.0% top-1 with ViT-L/14, surpassing the previous best RGBPose-Conv3D (95.6%) which also uses pose input. On HAA500 and Toyota-Smarthome, PeVL achieves 84.7% and 73.8% top-1/mean accuracy respectively, setting new SOTA on all four fine-grained action recognition benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PeVL consistently outperforms all 28 compared baselines across four fine-grained action recognition datasets. On Diving48, PeVL ViT-L/14 (92.5%) beats AIM ViT-L/14 (90.6%) by 1.9% and ORViT TimeSformer (88.0%) by 4.5% while using 51M fewer tunable parameters. On FineGym Gym99, PeVL (97.0%) surpasses RGBPose-Conv3D (95.6%), the only other method that also leverages pose, by 1.4%, and outperforms VTCE (94.6%) by 2.4%. On Gym288, PeVL ViT-L/14 (91.8%) edges out VTCE (90.1%) by 1.7%. On HAA500, PeVL (84.7%) improves over DC-TBAC-CSN (83.7%) by 1.0%, and on Toyota-Smarthome, PeVL (73.8%) beats PAAT (72.5%) by 1.3%. Notably, PeVL achieves these gains with lower computational cost (510 GFLOPs vs. 809 for AIM) by using fewer video frames (16) while compensating with higher-frame-rate pose input (48 frames).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based vision-language paradigm for video action recognition (as pursued by ActionCLIP, XCLIP, and FitCLIP) by introducing a pose modality as an explicit semantic bridge between low-level visual features and high-level textual descriptions. Architecturally, it inherits the bottleneck adapter design and multi-view inference strategy from AIM [59], but departs from prior video-pose methods (PoseConv3D, ViLP) by using raw 2D joint coordinates rather than pose heatmaps and by embedding pose within a VL contrastive framework rather than a separate 3D CNN pipeline. The UCMR block's unsymmetrical design—pose guiding vision via weighted spatial masks while vision enriches pose via cross-attention—represents a deliberate departure from symmetric fusion approaches, motivated by the inherent information asymmetry between rich visual tokens and concise skeletal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively lightweight pose modality (48 frames of 2D joints) can substantially close the vision-text semantic gap in VL models, yielding SOTA on four fine-grained benchmarks with notably fewer FLOPs (510 vs. 809 for AIM) and fewer tunable parameters. The multi-level contrastive learning with semantic-guided negative weighting (KL-divergence-based) is a transferable technique for any setting where text labels vary in granularity. Open questions include the dependency on an external pose estimator (HRNet) whose errors propagate into the UCMR block, whether the approach generalizes to 3D pose or hand skeletons as the authors note, and whether the gains persist when the VL backbone is replaced by a more recent model. The efficiency story—achieving SOTA with a small adapter-based retraining budget—makes this practically relevant for resource-constrained deployment.
