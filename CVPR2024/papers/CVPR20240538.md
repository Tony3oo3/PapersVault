## Problem

In online test-time adaptation under non-stationary environments, self-training with pseudo-labels suffers from error accumulation that progressively degrades label quality. Existing methods either update all batch normalization layers (risking modification of domain-invariant features) or generate pseudo-labels using hard augmentations that introduce additional domain gaps, both of which undermine stable long-term adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240538/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled RGB test images (32x32 for CIFAR, 224x224 for ImageNet) in batches of 200 or 64, along with their horizontally flipped counterparts. At each time step it outputs an ensemble class prediction (sum of student and teacher logits) and updated model parameters. The key I/O distinction is that entropy minimization only receives gradients for a pre-selected subset of blocks, while the paired-view consistency loss updates all parameters using the averaged teacher prediction over the original and flipped views.

## Method

DPLOT comprises two components. Before deployment, domain-specific block selection identifies which residual blocks handle domain-specific (low-level) feature extraction by measuring cosine similarity between class prototype vectors before and after entropy minimization on Gaussian-noise-perturbed source data; blocks whose prototypes remain highly similar (threshold gamma=0.75 after min-max scaling) are selected for test-time updates. After deployment, at each time step the student model's selected blocks are updated via Shannon entropy minimization on both the original and flipped test images, while an EMA teacher generates a pseudo-label by averaging its predictions on the original and horizontally flipped inputs. All model parameters are then updated via a symmetric cross-entropy loss between the student's predictions (on both views) and this averaged pseudo-label, and the teacher is refreshed by exponential moving average. The final prediction ensembles student and teacher logits.

## Results

On the gradual setting benchmarks (135 domain shifts), DPLOT achieves mean error rates of 8.8% (WRN28-10), 7.2% (WRN40-2A), and 6.3% (ResNet-18A) on CIFAR-10-C, 23.9% (ResNext-29A) and 27.3% (WRN40-2A) on CIFAR-100-C, and 30.8% (ResNet-50A) on ImageNet-C. In the continual setting (15 shifts), DPLOT reduces error by up to 5.4% on CIFAR-10-C, 9.1% on CIFAR-100-C, and 2.9% on ImageNet-C compared to the best baseline (RMT). DPLOT outperforms all baselines across nearly all architecture-dataset combinations, with the sole exception of ImageNet-C with ResNet-50 in the continual setting where RMT edges ahead by 0.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPLOT consistently outperforms the strongest baseline RMT across all CIFAR-10-C and CIFAR-100-C configurations in the gradual setting, with margins of 0.5–5.4% (e.g., 6.3% vs. 11.7% for ResNet-18A on CIFAR-10-C, and 23.9% vs. 26.4% for ResNext-29A on CIFAR-100-C). Against CoTTA, the advantage is even larger, reaching 4.6% on CIFAR-10-C (WRN28-10) and 5.5% on CIFAR-100-C (WRN40-2A). On ImageNet-C, DPLOT beats RMT by 2.7% (ResNet-50A) and 2.1% (ResNet-50) in the gradual setting, though in the continual setting RMT narrowly edges DPLOT by 0.4% on ResNet-50 (59.8% vs. 60.2%). Entropy-minimization-only methods (TENT, EATA, SAR) suffer severe error accumulation in the gradual setting—TENT's error on CIFAR-100-C ResNext-29A jumps from 60.9% (continual) to 74.8% (gradual)—while DPLOT's paired-view pseudo-labeling and block selection keep degradation minimal. AdaContrast performs poorly under gradual shifts (e.g., 66.3% on ImageNet-C ResNet-50), indicating that contrastive learning alone is insufficient for long-term adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two main TTA lineages: entropy minimization methods (TENT, EATA, SAR) that update batch normalization layers, and consistency regularization methods (CoTTA, RMT) that use teacher-generated pseudo-labels from augmented views. It departs from both by (1) replacing blanket BN updates with a pre-deployment block selection procedure that identifies domain-specific blocks via prototype similarity, and (2) replacing heavy multi-view augmentation (32 views in CoTTA) with a single horizontal flip for pseudo-label generation. The conceptual grounding draws on the domain-specific vs. domain-invariant feature decomposition from prior work on domain-adaptive detection [47–49], applied here to CNN block selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored problem—pseudo-label quality degradation in long-term online TTA—with a simple, architecture-agnostic solution that requires no source data at test time. The finding that a single flip outperforms 32 augmented views for pseudo-labels challenges the prevailing assumption that more augmentation improves self-training targets. Limitations include evaluation only on corruption-based benchmarks (CIFAR-C, ImageNet-C), sensitivity to the block-selection threshold γ, and the absence of source replay (which RMT uses), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to natural domain shifts or non-CNN backbones.
