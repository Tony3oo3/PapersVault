## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models rely on text prompts that are inherently ambiguous and cannot comprehensively capture delicate visual details like textures, styles, and fine-grained semantics. Even when combined with structural conditioning (e.g., ControlNet), users still face burdensome prompt engineering to control appearance, and the fundamental knowledge gap between language and vision limits what prompts can express. This paper eliminates the text prompt entirely, replacing it with a reference image as the sole semantic and stylistic conditioning signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image at arbitrary resolution (processed by SWIN-L's feature pyramid), an optional structural conditioning map (canny edge, depth, mlsd, or scribble via ControlNet), and an initial noise tensor. The reference image dimension need not match the output resolution, and the system supports multiple output scales (512², 512×768, 768×512). The output is a generated image that faithfully replicates the reference's textures, colors, style, and semantics while respecting the structural layout when provided.

## Method

The core contribution is SeeCoder (Semantic Context Encoder), a three-component network that replaces CLIP's text encoder: a SWIN-L backbone encoder producing multi-scale feature pyramids from arbitrary-resolution images, a transformer-based decoder that fuses multi-level features via convolutions and six multi-head self-attention modules, and a Query Transformer that distills visual cues into a fixed-size embedding using 4 global and 144 local queries with alternating cross-attention (visual features → local queries) and self-attention (local → global queries). These visual embeddings are fed directly into the frozen diffuser's cross-attention layers, exactly as CLIP text embeddings would be, requiring no architectural changes to the diffusion backbone. Training uses DDPM (T=1000) on Laion-2B and COYO-700M image collections with the diffuser weights frozen, and a position-aware variant (SeeCoder-PA) adds 2D spatial embeddings for improved quality without structural conditioning. A key property is reusability: a SeeCoder trained on one T2I model can be plugged into other open-source diffusers (SD1.5, OpenJourney, RealisticVision, etc.) with zero additional training.

## Results

This paper presents entirely qualitative evaluations with no numerical benchmark metrics. The headline finding is that Prompt-Free Diffusion matches the quality of the top two most sophisticated prompt-complexity levels in ControlNet+T2I (i.e., between 'semantic and style decorative prompt' and 'extra prompt engineering'), without requiring any text input. For Image-Variation, Prompt-Free Diffusion with SeeCoder-PA outperforms both ControlNet Shuffle and ControlNet Reference-Only in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings, though Versatile Diffusion (which requires model fine-tuning) retains the best overall performance. Additionally, a single pretrained SeeCoder is shown to be plug-and-play reusable across 7 different T2I models (SD1.5, OAM-V2, Anything-V4, OpenJourney-V4, Deliberate-V2, RealisticVision-V2, and an in-house model) with zero additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet (Zhang & Agrawala, 2023), Prompt-Free Diffusion wins on all three tested configurations: (1) for prompt-based generation, it matches the quality of the two most elaborate prompt levels without any text; (2) for Image-Variation, it beats both ControlNet Shuffle and ControlNet Reference-Only in both in-domain and out-of-domain tests, with Reference-Only outperforming Shuffle on in-domain samples and vice versa on out-of-domain, while Prompt-Free Diffusion maintains consistent quality across both; (3) for structural-guided generation (Dual-ControlNet comparison), Prompt-Free Diffusion shows superior texture quality, color consistency, and style replication in zoomed-in detail views. Against Versatile Diffusion (Xu et al., 2022), the paper acknowledges that VD has the best overall Image-Variation performance since it fine-tunes the diffusion model specifically for the task, but Prompt-Free Diffusion better replicates reference images in terms of semantics, texture, and background, and crucially requires no model fine-tuning—only a CLIP-to-SeeCoder swap. The paper also notes a persistent color-shift artifact in ControlNet+T2I that the authors could not resolve despite numerous attempts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the latent diffusion pipeline of Stable Diffusion and the structural conditioning mechanism of ControlNet, but departs from both by replacing the CLIP text encoder with a purpose-built visual encoder (SeeCoder) that ingests a reference image instead of a text prompt. Conceptually, it sits between exemplar-based generation (SPADE, CoCosNet) and image-variation (Versatile Diffusion), inheriting the reference-image conditioning of the former and the diffusion-based generation of the latter, while distinguishing itself through a drop-in, model-agnostic encoder design. The SeeCoder architecture draws on Swin Transformer for multi-scale feature extraction and a Query Transformer (analogous to segmentation decoders like Mask2Former/OneFormer) to distill multi-level visual features into a fixed-length embedding compatible with cross-attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in eliminating prompt engineering as a bottleneck for personalized generation and in the demonstrated reusability of a single SeeCoder across seven heterogeneous T2I models (photorealistic, anime, art-style) without retraining — a property prior exemplar-based or image-variation methods lack. However, the evaluation is almost entirely qualitative (side-by-side visual comparisons), with no quantitative metrics (FID, CLIP score, user studies), and the primary experiments rely on an undisclosed in-house diffuser, limiting reproducibility. Open questions include whether the quality gap versus well-crafted prompts truly closes at scale, how SeeCoder handles multi-reference or compositional inputs, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D generation as the authors briefly speculate.
