## Problem

Generating 3D articulated objects in a user-controllable manner remains unsolved. Existing generative approaches like NAP either fail to respect input constraints on part shape and articulation, or rely on fully connected graphs that scale poorly to objects with many parts. The paper addresses the gap of producing articulated objects that faithfully conform to user-specified conditions on category, part geometry, joint type, and joint axis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241248/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object category label (one of 8 categories), a directed adjacency matrix describing part interconnections (up to K=32 nodes), and optionally specific node attribute constraints (bounding boxes, joint types, or joint axes) as conditioning. The denoising target is a tensor of shape 5×K×M (M=6) encoding per-part bounding box corners, joint type, joint axis (direction + origin), joint range, and semantic label. The final 3D mesh is assembled via a two-step part retrieval strategy that matches generated part abstractions against 2690 parts from 527 training objects, prioritizing style consistency and motion compatibility.

## Method

CAGE is a DDPM-based generative model whose denoiser stacks 12 Attribute Attention Blocks, each containing three sequentially applied attention modules: Local Attention (intra-part attribute relations), Global Attention (all-pairs inter-part relations with key-padding masking), and Graph Relation Attention (parent-child relations masked by the input adjacency matrix). Conditions (timestep, category label) are injected via adaLN-Zero layers, while the graph structure is enforced as a hard attention mask in GRA, with the root node's self-connection enabling the model to infer edge direction. The key design insight is that representing parts as axis-aligned bounding boxes with explicit motion parameters (rather than detailed surface geometry) more effectively captures shape-motion correlations, and that incorporating actionable parts (handles, knobs) into an augmented action graph provides strong positional signals for plausible articulation. Training uses AdamW for 5000 epochs on a single A40 GPU, and the model is evaluated on PartNet-Mobility with metrics including MMD, Coverage, 1-NNA, and a custom Average Overlapping Ratio for physical plausibility.

## Results

On PartNet-Mobility with K=32 action graphs, CAGE achieves MMD-ID of 0.049 and MMD-AID of 0.816, outperforming NAP-large (0.067 and 0.860) on distribution similarity. Coverage is also higher (COV-AID: 0.852 vs. 0.716), indicating better diversity. The Average Overlapping Ratio drops from 0.034 to 0.008, reflecting far fewer physically implausible sibling-part collisions. In a human study, participants preferred CAGE-generated objects 82.61% of the time over NAP-large (17.39%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is NAP (Neural 3D Articulation Prior, NeurIPS 2023), compared in three variants: NAP (original representation, K=8), NAP-light (NAP with CAGE's data parameterization, K=8), and NAP-large (CAGE's parameterization, K=32). CAGE outperforms NAP-large on every metric in the primary K=32 comparison: MMD-ID improves from 0.067 to 0.049, MMD-AID from 0.860 to 0.816, COV-AID from 0.716 to 0.852, and AOR from 0.034 to 0.008. The human study preference rate is the most dramatic gap: 82.61% for CAGE versus 17.39% for NAP-large. In the simpler K=8 setting (Table 1), CAGE-light similarly beats NAP-light on MMD-ID (0.043 vs. 0.060) and AOR (0.007 vs. 0.062), while NAP-light edges out slightly on COV-ID (0.773 vs. 0.753). The paper attributes NAP's weaknesses to its fully connected graph (poor scalability with many parts) and lack of explicit graph-structure conditioning, which causes frequent violations of input graph topology.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAGE builds directly on the DDPM diffusion framework and the articulated object generation paradigm introduced by NAP (Lei et al., 2023), which first learned a generative prior over 3D articulated objects using a part relation tree. The key departure is architectural: where NAP uses a fully connected graph and a 128D latent surface code per part, CAGE replaces this with a sparse directed action graph and a high-level shape abstraction (axis-aligned bounding boxes plus joint parameters), coupled with a three-stage attention mechanism (local, global, graph-relation) that explicitly encodes kinematic hierarchy. The paper also draws on the broader conditional diffusion literature (adaLN-Zero, classifier-free guidance) but applies these conditioning techniques to structured graph-organized 3D geometry rather than images or unstructured shapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Controllable generation of articulated objects is practically important for robotics simulation, embodied AI, and 3D content creation, where users need to specify particular part arrangements, joint types, or axes rather than accepting whatever a generative model produces. The paper's main limitation is that final part geometry relies on retrieval from a finite training set (527 objects), capping diversity and occasionally producing unrealistic part combinations; motion attributes also remain less controllable than geometric ones. Open questions include whether the bounding-box abstraction can be replaced by learned part geometry generation, how the approach scales to categories with very different kinematic structures, and whether the graph-relation attention design transfers to other structured 3D generation tasks.
