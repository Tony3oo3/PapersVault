## Problem

Amodal segmentation ground truth on real images has always been produced by manual annotation, making it subjective and limiting evaluation to a small set of object categories. Additionally, existing amodal completion methods require the occluder mask as input, failing entirely when the occluder is unknown or unannotated in large-scale datasets. This paper addresses both gaps by generating authentic amodal ground truth from 3D data and proposing architectures that do not require an occluder mask.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241263/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and the binary modal (visible) mask of a target object at the same spatial resolution, with no occluder mask required. It outputs a binary amodal mask at the same resolution covering the full extent of the object including occluded regions. The two-stage OccAmodal variant additionally produces an intermediate occluder mask as a byproduct, while SDAmodal directly maps the image and modal mask to the amodal mask in one pass.

## Method

The paper introduces two architectures that eliminate the need for a provided occluder mask. OccAmodal is a two-stage pipeline: a lightweight U-Net first predicts the occluder mask from the image and modal mask, then a second U-Net (with ResNet image encoding at the bottleneck and five skip connections) takes the image, modal mask, and predicted occluder to produce the amodal mask. SDAmodal is a one-stage model that feeds the image into a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion network, extracts multi-scale features from the U-Net decoding layers at diffusion time step 181, concatenates them with multi-scale modal mask features, and forwards through additional decoding layers to predict the amodal mask. The key insight is that Stable Diffusion's pre-trained representations encode rich occlusion and shape information, enabling class-agnostic completion that generalizes zero-shot to unseen domains. Ground truth amodal masks are generated automatically by projecting individual 3D object meshes (amodal) and all meshes together (modal) from MatterPort3D onto real images, with a 1.2× area threshold to select occluded instances and a manual quality-check step.

## Results

SDAmodal achieves a new state-of-the-art mIOU of 90.7 and mIOU-inv of 71.6 on COCOA, surpassing the previous best (ASBU with occluder mask, 89.9 mIOU) without requiring the occluder mask as input. On the newly introduced MP3D-Amodal benchmark, SDAmodal reaches 76.4 mIOU and 38.5 mIOU-inv, outperforming the best prior method (Deocclusion Single Stage, 72.4 mIOU) by +4.0 mIOU and +10.5 mIOU-inv. When the occluder mask is withheld, the two-stage baselines (ASBU and Deocclusion Two Stage) collapse to ~69.9 mIOU with near-zero mIOU-inv, while SDAmodal maintains strong performance, demonstrating robustness to unknown occluders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior amodal completion methods: ASBU (Nguyen & Todorovic, ICCV 2021) and Deocclusion (Zhan et al., CVPR 2020). On COCOA, SDAmodal (90.7 mIOU) beats ASBU's reported result (89.9 mIOU) by +0.8 mIOU, and crucially does so *without* the occluder mask that ASBU requires. On MP3D-Amodal, SDAmodal (76.4 mIOU, 38.5 mIOU-inv) outperforms Deocclusion Single Stage (72.4 mIOU, 28.0 mIOU-inv) by +4.0 mIOU and +10.5 mIOU-inv, showing stronger zero-shot generalization to a different domain. The most striking comparison is when the occluder mask is withheld: both ASBU and Deocclusion (Two Stage) drop to ~69.9 mIOU with mIOU-inv near zero (0.006), meaning they cannot expand the modal mask at all, whereas SDAmodal retains 90.7 mIOU and OccAmodal retains 89.4 mIOU. The mIOU-inv gap on COCOA (71.6 vs. 65.3 for ASBU) indicates SDAmodal is substantially better at completing the specifically occluded regions, not just the overall mask.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the amodal completion line of work (ASBU, Deocclusion) by removing the assumption that occluder masks are provided, instead either predicting them (OccAmodal) or leveraging pre-trained Stable Diffusion features that implicitly encode occlusion structure (SDAmodal). It also departs from prior amodal datasets (COCOA, KINS, DYCE, OLMD) which rely on human-imagined or synthetic ground truth, by using 3D mesh projection from MatterPort3D to generate authentic amodal masks for real images. The use of frozen diffusion model features for a discriminative segmentation task builds on the observation in [49] that Stable Diffusion encodes 3D scene structure, repurposing a generative model's representations for amodal reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MP3D-Amodal benchmark is the first to provide authentic (3D-derived) ground truth amodal masks for real images across 427 semantic labels, addressing a long-standing gap where real-image amodal GT was always subjective human annotation. The class-agnostic, occluder-free completion capability (especially SDAmodal's zero-shot generalization from COCOA to MP3D-Amodal) moves amodal segmentation toward practical 'in the wild' deployment. Limitations include dependence on MatterPort3D's sometimes noisy/incomplete 3D meshes (requiring manual curation), restriction to indoor scenes, and the relatively small training split (1,100 images). Open questions include whether the 3D-projection pipeline extends to outdoor or dynamic scenes, and whether the diffusion features' occlusion understanding is truly geometric or merely correlational.
