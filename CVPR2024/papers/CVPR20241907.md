## Problem

Existing pedestrian trajectory prediction datasets cannot simultaneously provide wide field of view and high-resolution imagery, making minute-level long-term trajectory prediction in large-scale complex scenes unstudied. Narrow-FoV datasets (ETH/UCY) yield only seconds-long trajectories, while wide-FoV datasets (SDD/InD) lack the resolution to capture detailed pedestrian behavior and group interactions. This paper introduces GigaTraj, a gigapixel-level dataset covering ~4×10⁴ m², and demonstrates that current state-of-the-art methods are fundamentally inadequate for this setting.

## Input / Output

The prediction task uses a 30-second historical trajectory (60 frames at 2 FPS) expressed in world coordinates derived from homography matrices estimated via laser-scanned point clouds, combined with scene semantics as bird's-eye-view maps (9 semantic classes), group/interaction graphs (3 group types, 5 interaction types), and pixel-level bounding boxes in the 23972×13484 gigapixel image. The model outputs a 30-second future trajectory in world coordinates. Notably, the world coordinates achieve centimeter-level precision, and the bounding boxes span a 100× scale range due to the ~200 m imaging depth, creating a multi-scale detection challenge inherent to the input.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction pipeline and an empirical benchmark rather than a new prediction architecture. GigaTraj is built on the PANDA gigapixel camera array, with 6 additional scenes captured and 4 new scenes annotated; laser scanners reconstruct 3D scene geometry to estimate per-scene homography matrices mapping pixel coordinates to centimeter-accurate world coordinates. For the empirical study, six representative trajectory prediction methods (Vanilla-LSTM, Social-LSTM, SGAN, PECNet, Trajectron++, MemoNet) are re-implemented or run with official code under a unified 30s-in/30s-out protocol using GT coordinates and BEV maps. An ablation on PECNet tests concatenating scene semantics and interaction graphs as additional per-frame features, revealing that graph information yields significant improvement (ADE 2.18 vs. 3.85) while raw semantic IDs do not help without graph structure. The study further identifies severe collision artifacts in SOTA predictions and large performance drops on unseen scenes, establishing concrete failure modes for future research.

## Results

On the GigaTraj benchmark (30s input → 30s output), Trajectron++ achieves the best overall min ADE of 1.72 m and min FDE of 2.85 m, while MemoNet is the most consistent across scenes (2.04/2.87) without catastrophic per-scene failures. At the shorter 10s prediction horizon, Trajectron++ leads with 0.70/0.82, followed by MemoNet (0.89/1.00) and SGAN (1.03/2.84). All methods show errors an order of magnitude larger than on small-scale datasets (e.g., NSP-SFM achieves 0.17 ADE on ETH/UCY), confirming that minute-level long-term prediction in gigapixel scenes remains largely unsolved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Trajectron++ achieves the lowest overall ADE (1.72 m) at T=30s, but suffers a catastrophic failure on Scene 12 (22.40/53.67), inflating its mean; excluding that scene, it would be even stronger. MemoNet is the most robust baseline, maintaining ADE below 2.6 m on every individual scene and achieving the best FDE among consistent performers (2.87 m overall). Social-LSTM paradoxically performs worse than Vanilla-LSTM overall (5.71 vs. 4.40 ADE), indicating that naive social pooling fails to capture the complex multi-agent relationships in gigapixel scenes. SGAN and PECNet show intermediate performance but both exhibit large per-scene variance (e.g., PECNet jumps to 15.72 ADE on Scene 12). At the 10s horizon, the gap narrows considerably, with Trajectron++ (0.70 ADE) and MemoNet (0.89 ADE) clearly ahead of the GAN- and LSTM-based methods, suggesting that the difficulty scales super-linearly with prediction horizon in large-scale open scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PANDA gigapixel video dataset by adding homography matrices (via laser-scanner-based scene reconstruction), world coordinates, scene semantic segmentation, and six additional complex scenes, specifically to enable minute-level trajectory prediction. It positions itself against the trajectory prediction dataset lineage (ETH/UCY for small-scale, SDD/InD for wider FoV but low resolution) by arguing that no existing dataset simultaneously provides wide FoV, high resolution, and rich multimodal annotations needed for long-horizon prediction. The empirical evaluation repurposes established trajectory prediction methods (Social-LSTM, SGAN, Trajectron++, MemoNet, PECNet) as baselines rather than proposing new architectures, making the contribution primarily a benchmark and diagnostic study.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a clear gap: no prior resource supported minute-level (60 s) trajectory prediction across hundreds of pedestrians in ~4×10⁴ m² scenes, which is the regime relevant to smart-city and autonomous-system planning. The finding that social-interaction models (Social-LSTM, SGAN) underperform a vanilla LSTM on this benchmark challenges the working assumption that explicitly modeling pairwise interactions improves prediction, suggesting that at this scale the interaction structure is too complex for current graph/pooling mechanisms to exploit. Key open questions include how to effectively fuse multimodal inputs (scene semantics, group/interaction graphs, bounding-box appearance), how to generalize to unseen scenes, and how to prevent the severe inter-pedestrian collisions observed in current predictions.
