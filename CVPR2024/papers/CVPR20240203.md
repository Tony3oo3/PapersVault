## Problem

Existing blind motion deblurring methods rely on shift-invariant convolutional networks to reconstruct residuals in feature space, which limits their ability to capture spatially variable, large-scale real-world motions and provides little interpretability for how motion blur is actually removed. These approaches ignore the tailored handling of diverse motion patterns directly in image space, where filtering operators can explicitly model local motion geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurred RGB image (processed in 256×256 patches during training) and produces a deblurred sharp RGB image of the same resolution. No external motion prior, blur kernel, or additional input is required. The final output is the sum of a filtered image produced by the MISC Filter in image space and a residual image from a reconstruction network, both operating at full resolution.

## Method

The MISC Filter operates in image space with two sequential modules. First, a Motion-Guided Alignment (MGA) module uses a lightweight flow estimator and mask estimator (each a single convolution layer) to predict spatially-variant motion flow and occlusion mask, then performs bi-directional warping to align motion-induced blur to the motion midpoint along the predicted flow direction. Second, a Separable Collaborative Filtering (SCF) module uses three lightweight estimators to jointly predict a separable 2D kernel (approximated by a pair of 1D kernels of size n=7), spatial offsets for non-local pixel sampling, and softmax-normalized aggregation weights, applying them to the aligned image to produce the filtered output. The motion estimation network and residual reconstruction network share the same U-Net encoder-decoder structure with frequency-domain learning, and filtering is applied before residual addition—a coupling strategy shown via ablation to maximize performance at minimal parameter count (16M total).

## Results

On the real-world motion blur benchmarks, MISC Filter achieves 41.23 dB PSNR / 0.978 SSIM on RealBlur-R and 33.88 dB / 0.938 on RealBlur-J, setting new state-of-the-art results. On GoPro it reaches 34.10 dB / 0.969 and on HIDE 31.66 dB / 0.946, matching or exceeding prior SOTA while using only 16.0 M parameters and 0.07 s runtime (half the runtime of FFTformer). The method also generalizes to hardware-induced blur (T-OLED), achieving 38.42 dB / 0.9800 with 5.6 M parameters, competitive with dedicated UDC restoration models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest motion-prior-free baseline FFTformer [26], MISC Filter gains +1.12 dB on RealBlur-R (41.23 vs 40.11) and +1.26 dB on RealBlur-J (33.88 vs 32.62), while matching GoPro (34.10 vs 34.21, −0.11 dB) and slightly leading HIDE (31.66 vs 31.62), all with fewer parameters (16.0 M vs 16.6 M) and half the runtime (0.07 s vs 0.13 s). Against the strongest motion-prior-related baseline UFPNet [19], MISC Filter wins on RealBlur-R (+0.62 dB), RealBlur-J (+0.53 dB), and GoPro (+0.04 dB) while trailing slightly on HIDE (−0.08 dB), yet uses only 16.0 M parameters versus UFPNet's 80.3 M. Compared to other strong baselines such as NAFNet [7] (33.71 dB GoPro) and DeepRFT+ [40] (33.23 dB GoPro), MISC Filter leads by 0.39 dB and 0.87 dB respectively on GoPro. On the hardware-induced blur benchmark (T-OLED), MISC Filter (38.42 dB, 5.6 M params, 0.05 s) is competitive with dedicated UDC models like FSI [33] (38.60 dB) and BNUDC [25] (38.22 dB), though it does not fully address the low-light attenuation specific to under-display cameras.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in blind motion deblurring—reconstructing image residuals in feature space via deep networks—by instead handling motion blur directly in image space through an adaptive filtering operator. It synthesizes ideas from deformable separable convolution (Dai et al., 2017; Cheng & Chen, 2021), pixel-adaptive filters (Su et al., 2019), and motion-prior estimation (UFPNet, 2023) into a unified MISC Filter that jointly predicts flow, mask, kernels, weights, and offsets. The paper also draws on adaptive filtering successes in video frame interpolation and artifact removal, repurposing those filtering primitives for the deblurring task where they had not previously been applied in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The image-space filtering perspective offers a more interpretable and spatially differentiated mechanism for handling complex, spatially varying motion than black-box feature-space reconstruction, and the 16M-parameter model achieves SOTA on RealBlur with half the runtime of comparable methods. The systematic ablation of ten coupling strategies between the motion estimation and residual reconstruction networks provides practical guidance for multi-branch architecture design. However, the method still falls short on hardware-induced blur (UDC) due to its inability to address low-light attenuation, and the approach's generalizability to other degradation types (e.g., defocus, mixed blur) remains unexplored.
