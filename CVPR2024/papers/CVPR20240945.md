## Problem

Existing crack segmentation methods struggle with ambiguous labels in marginal non-crack regions, where low contrast and heterogeneous texture make it difficult to distinguish crack from non-crack pixels near boundaries. While prior work has focused on learning crack boundaries or edges, the ambiguity on the non-crack side of the boundary remains unaddressed, causing models to fail at extracting discriminative features in these regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240945/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB crack images (cropped to 256×256 during training) and produces a binary pixel-level segmentation mask. During training, an auxiliary std head outputs a per-pixel variance map used to localize ambiguous regions, but this head is removed at inference so the framework adds no extra computation or memory. The approach is plug-and-play and can be attached to any existing segmentation backbone (e.g., JTFN, Crackformer) without architectural modification.

## Method

CIRL is a two-phase framework. In Phase I, an ambiguity-aware segmentation loss (Aseg Loss) models each pixel's prediction as a Gaussian and minimizes the Wasserstein distance to a Dirac-delta ground truth, forcing the network to output a learned variance that flags ambiguous pixels in marginal non-crack regions (identified via morphological dilation of the ground-truth mask). In Phase II, a clustering-inspired loss (CI Loss) replaces supervised supervision for those ambiguous pixels with an unsupervised contrastive objective: for each feature, K-nearest neighbors in feature space form a close set that should receive consistent predictions, while all other features form a far set that should receive inconsistent predictions, implemented via a negative log-likelihood with a Jensen-inequality upper bound for efficiency. The overall loss combines standard BCE for non-ambiguous pixels with the CI Loss for ambiguous ones, and the std head is discarded at test time.

## Results

On the four evaluation datasets, JTFN+CIRL achieves F1 scores of 86.53% (CrackTree200), 67.62% (Crack500), 84.32% (DRIVE), and 60.08% (CrackSeg5k), consistently outperforming the base JTFN model by 1.5–2.3% F1 across all datasets. The method also surpasses all other state-of-the-art crack segmentation baselines (U-Net, VGG-UNet, TopoNet, DRU, Crackformer) on every dataset. In the loss-function comparison on CrackSeg5k, CIRL (60.08% F1) outperforms Focal Loss (58.85%), Poly Loss (59.56%), clDice Loss (59.32%), Robust Dice Loss (59.06%), and Robust T-Loss (58.84%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JTFN+CIRL outperforms all six architecture-level baselines in Table 2 across all four datasets; the largest gains are on CrackTree200 (+2.34% F1 over JTFN, +8.11% over U-Net) and Crack500 (+1.86% over JTFN, +5.79% over U-Net). Against the five alternative segmentation losses in Table 3 (all paired with JTFN on CrackSeg5k), CIRL achieves the highest F1 of 60.08%, beating Poly Loss (59.56%) by 0.52%, clDice Loss (59.32%) by 0.76%, Robust Dice Loss (59.06%) by 1.02%, Focal Loss (58.85%) by 1.23%, and Robust T-Loss (58.84%) by 1.24%. In the clustering-learning comparison (Table 5), the proposed CI Loss surpasses NNCLR [13] by 1.13% F1 (60.08% vs. 58.95%) on CrackSeg5k, attributed to its set-based contrastive formulation that avoids the class-collision issue of positive/negative pair contrastive learning. The method also demonstrates cross-domain transferability, improving F1 by 1.51% on the DRIVE blood-vessel segmentation dataset, confirming that the ambiguity-aware strategy generalizes beyond concrete crack imagery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the crack segmentation architecture lineage (DeepCrack, Crackformer, JTFN) and the broader loss-function design literature (Focal Loss, Dice Loss, clDice), but departs from the dominant strategy of exploiting crack boundaries or edges as auxiliary supervision. Its conceptual departure is identifying 'marginal non-crack regions'—a narrow band adjacent to cracks—as the primary source of label ambiguity, and addressing it through a two-phase pipeline: Wasserstein-distance-based variance learning (Aseg Loss) to localize ambiguous pixels, followed by a clustering-inspired loss (CI Loss) that replaces supervised supervision in those regions with an unsupervised nearest-neighbor contrastive objective. The clustering component draws on deep clustering and contrastive learning ideas (NNCLR, InfoNCE) but reformulates them for pixel-level segmentation by operating on feature sets rather than image-level positive/negative pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces CrackSeg5k, a high-resolution dataset from nuclear power plant inspections, which fills a gap in publicly available crack segmentation benchmarks with realistic industrial conditions. The plug-and-play nature of CIRL (no architectural changes at inference) makes it practically attractive for practitioners already using JTFN or Crackformer. However, the pre-process for identifying marginal regions relies on ground-truth dilation, limiting direct deployment without annotations, and the ~2% F1 gains, while consistent, are modest. Open questions include whether the variance-based ambiguity detection generalizes to other curvilinear segmentation tasks beyond cracks and whether the two-phase training schedule (1000-epoch warmup before CI Loss activation) is necessary or an artifact of the specific datasets tested.
