## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud diffusion models (PVD, LION) rely on convolutional UNet/PVCNN backbones originally designed for 2D images, whose limited receptive fields make them slow to establish global shape at early denoising steps. No prior work investigates how the relative importance of convolution (local detail) versus attention (global structure) shifts across diffusion timesteps, leaving a clear architectural gap for 3D point cloud generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a noisy 3D point cloud of N=2048 points (N×3 coordinates) at diffusion timestep t and predicts the corresponding noise (N×3) to produce the denoised point cloud at t−1. Internally, the point cloud is voxelized into an L×L×L grid and downsampled via furthest point sampling to M sparse points for efficient Transformer processing, then upsampled back to N points through trilinear interpolation in the decoder. Training uses the standard DDPM MSE loss on the predicted noise, evaluated with 1-NN accuracy under Chamfer Distance and Earth Mover's Distance on ShapeNetv2 (airplane, chair, car categories).

## Method

TIGER is a two-stream denoising backbone that processes the latent point cloud through a shallow PVCNN branch (local features) and a latent point cloud Transformer branch (global features) in parallel, then fuses them via a learnable timestep-dependent mask that adaptively reweights local versus global contributions. The mask is generated by encoding the diffusion timestep into a sinusoidal embedding and mapping it through MLPs to a channel-wise weight vector constrained to sum to 1 with its complement, so the network learns when to prioritize global shape (early timesteps, Transformer-dominant) versus local surface detail (late timesteps, CNN-dominant). Two novel 3D continuous position encodings—Phase-Shift Position Encoding (PSPE) using sine/cosine with axis-specific phase offsets, and Base-λ Position Encoding (BλPE) compressing 3D coordinates into a single scalar via a polynomial—are combined with a position-aware self-attention mechanism that multiplies the QK^T attention matrix by a learned position relationship map, preserving spatial structure through stacked Transformer layers.

## Results

On ShapeNetv2 (1-NN accuracy, where closer to 50% is better), TIGER achieves 67.21/56.26 (CD/EMD) on Airplane, 54.32/51.71 on Chair, and 54.12/50.24 on Car under the standard evaluation split. The EMD-based 1-NN accuracy for Airplane improves by 13.45% relative to PVD (64.81→56.26) and 8.12% relative to LION (61.23→56.26). TIGER trains in 164 GPU hours and infers in 9.73 s, roughly 1/3 the training time and 1/3 the inference time of LION (550 h / 27.12 s), while matching PVD's speed (142 h / 8.46 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIGER outperforms the closest diffusion baseline LION on 4 of 6 metrics (Airplane CD 67.21 vs 67.41, Airplane EMD 56.26 vs 61.23, Chair EMD 51.71 vs 52.34, Car EMD 50.24 vs 51.14), with the largest gap on Airplane EMD (≈5 points). Against PVD, TIGER improves Airplane EMD by 13.45% relatively (64.81→56.26) and Car EMD by 5.5 points (53.83→50.24), while PVD retains a slight edge on Chair CD (56.26 vs 54.32). All GAN, flow, and VAE baselines (r-GAN, PointFlow, DPF-Net, ShapeGF, SoftFlow, SetVAE) are substantially outperformed on every metric, with gaps of 10–40+ points. DPM [34], the earliest diffusion baseline, is far behind on EMD (e.g., 86.91 vs 56.26 on Airplane) but competitive on CD for some categories. In terms of efficiency, TIGER trains in 164 GPU hours—only 30% more than PVD (142 h) but 70% less than LION (550 h)—and infers in 9.73 s versus LION's 27.12 s, making it the best quality-to-cost trade-off among diffusion-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIGER extends the diffusion-based 3D point cloud generation line (DPM, PVD, LION) by replacing the standard PVCNN/UNet denoising backbone with a two-stream architecture that explicitly separates global (Transformer) and local (shallow CNN) feature extraction. The key departure is the introduction of a learnable, timestep-dependent mask that adaptively reweights these two streams, motivated by the observation that convolution-only backbones struggle to establish global shape at early diffusion timesteps. The paper also contributes novel 3D continuous position encodings (PSPE, BλPE) and a position-aware self-attention mechanism, building on but extending the position-encoding ideas from Point Transformer and image-domain sinusoidal embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that the denoising architecture in diffusion models for 3D generation is not interchangeable with 2D image architectures, and that the relative importance of local vs. global modeling shifts predictably across the diffusion schedule. This insight could inform architecture design for other diffusion-based 3D tasks (completion, upsampling, mesh generation). However, the model remains unconditional (no category control), requires two separate subnetworks for local and global features (reducing efficiency), and the performance gains over LION are modest in absolute terms. Open questions include whether the time-varying reweighting can be achieved within a single network and whether the approach generalizes to conditional or text-guided generation.
