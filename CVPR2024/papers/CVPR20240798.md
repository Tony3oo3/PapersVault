## Problem

Comparing a user's video execution of a procedural step against a reference how-to video is essential for AR/VR personalized assistance, yet existing video understanding methods operate on single videos and prior change-captioning work targets pixel-level differences in static scenes rather than semantic variations in tools, ingredients, and techniques. No prior approach can jointly reason across two video clips to describe, recognize, or rank fine-grained step differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240798/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two video clips (reference and candidate), each encoded from 8 uniformly sampled frames into 2048 spatiotemporal tokens by an InternVideo backbone, then projected to 32 tokens in the LLM's input space via a 2-layer Perceiver module. At inference, a text question (e.g., 'what is the main difference in the category g?') is interleaved with the visual tokens of both videos in a single prompt. Outputs are autoregressively generated text: free-form difference captions, a selected video-pair index for multiple-choice recognition, or a YES/NO likelihood score used to rank candidate videos by difference severity.

## Method

The approach has two key components. First, training data is automatically generated by pairing clips of the same keystep from distinct HowTo100M/HT-Step videos, synthesizing rich step descriptions from ASR narrations, filtered object detections, and action captions, then prompting Llama2 to produce comparative QA pairs. Second, a paired-video VCLM is instruction-tuned: a frozen Llama2-70B LLM is aligned with a frozen InternVideo encoder through a learnable 2-layer Perceiver projection, trained with standard cross-entropy on answer tokens only. The model is initialized from interleaved (video, ASR) pretraining weights and fine-tuned on both that data and the generated pair-QA data to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This design lets the model directly compare two raw video clips at inference without requiring external captions, detections, or narrations.

## Results

On the StepDiff benchmark, the proposed method achieves 54.1% top-1 accuracy on Difference Recognition (DiffMCQ), 0.181 Kendall's τ on Difference Ranking (DiffRank), and 0.215 ROUGE-L on Difference Captioning (DiffCap). These are state-of-the-art results across all three tasks, with the largest margin on DiffMCQ where it outperforms the strongest baseline (Interleaved AnyMAL) by 4.4 percentage points. The model uses a Llama2-70B LLM with an InternVideo encoder and a Perceiver projection module, fine-tuning only the projection layer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StepDiff outperforms all six baseline classes across all three evaluation tasks. On DiffMCQ, it achieves 54.1% accuracy, beating the strongest baseline Interleaved(AnyMAL) (49.7%) by 4.4 points and VCLM(AnyMAL) (47.1%) by 7 points; the VLEmbed baselines (CLIP 39.6%, InternVideo 45.1%) and Socratic baselines (33.2–39.2%) trail further behind. On DiffRank, StepDiff (τ=0.181) surpasses the best VLEmbed baseline InternVideo (τ=0.170) by 1.1 points and the best LLM-based baseline Interleaved(IDEFICS) (τ=0.081) by a wide margin of 10 points. On DiffCap, StepDiff leads in ROUGE-L (0.215) over Interleaved(IDEFICS) (0.210) and VCLM(LLaVA) (0.199), though the gap is smaller. The paper notes that Socratic models are fundamentally limited by caption quality and hallucination, while VCLM and Interleaved models, though stronger, lack explicit training for cross-video comparison — a gap that StepDiff's paired-video instruction tuning directly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm (LLaVA, AnyMAL) from single-video QA to paired-video comparison, and repurposes the image change-captioning literature by shifting from pixel-level differences in static scenes to semantic differences (tools, ingredients, technique) in procedural video clips. The data generation pipeline is inspired by LLM-based instruction tuning (Alpaca, MIMIC-IT) but applied to a novel paired-video setting, leveraging HowTo100M/HT-Step narrations and off-the-shelf captioning/detection models to synthesize training QA without manual annotation. It departs from prior VCLMs that handle one video at a time by introducing a learnable projection that encodes both reference and candidate videos into the LLM's token space simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark specifically for fine-grained video comparison in procedural activities, filling a gap between image change captioning and single-video QA that is critical for AR/VR personalized assistance (progress tracking, mistake detection). The automatic data generation pipeline is a reusable technique for creating paired-video supervision from existing narrated datasets, though the LLM-generated data is acknowledged as noisy and the evaluation is limited to cooking-themed videos. Open questions include generalization beyond cooking, handling more than two videos, real-time inference for interactive assistance, and whether the weakly-supervised training signal can be further improved to reduce hallucination.
