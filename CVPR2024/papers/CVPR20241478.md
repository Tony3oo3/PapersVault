## Problem

2D diffusion models used for instruction-guided 3D scene editing (via distillation into NeRF) produce multi-view-inconsistent images because each view is denoised independently from random noise with no cross-view context or 3D information. This forces the NeRF to converge to a blurred, averaged representation that loses fine-grained textures and completely fails on regular patterns like checkered or plaid designs. The paper identifies three root causes: single-view input lacking scene context, independently sampled Gaussian noise per view, and absence of 3D geometry in the diffusion input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained NeRF of the scene, a text editing instruction, and the current NeRF renderings as input. The diffusion model receives a composed 'surrounding view' image (one large main view surrounded by 4(k−1) small reference views) along with 3D positional embeddings (per-pixel depth and 3D coordinates queried through a learnable hash table) injected via a ControlNet branch. The output is an edited NeRF scene; the diffusion model generates edited images in the same surrounding-view layout, which are buffered and used to continuously retrain the NeRF in parallel.

## Method

ConsistDreamer introduces three synergistic components that make 2D diffusion 3D-consistent. (1) Structured noise: Gaussian noise is sampled once on a dense 3D point cloud (unprojected from NeRF depth), then rendered per-view into latent-space noise of shape (H/8, W/8, 4), so all views begin denoising from a shared 3D-consistent noise field. (2) Surrounding views: a single composed image tiles one large main view with multiple small reference views, exploiting the UNet's self-attention to propagate context and style across views simultaneously. (3) Consistency-enforcing training: a ControlNet adaptor injects 3D positional embeddings (hash-encoded depth + 3D points), and a self-supervised loss is computed by depth-warping all generated sub-views into each other's frame and taking a weighted average as the target; training uses full multi-step denoising with gradient checkpointing so consistency is enforced at every intermediate timestep, and multi-GPU parallel generation enforces cross-batch consistency. A VGG-based perceptual/stylization loss (rather than pixel-wise) preserves texture detail, and a coarse-to-fine strategy handles shape-changing instructions by first editing geometry without structured noise, then refining with full consistency.

## Results

The paper primarily evaluates through qualitative comparison, as the authors note that ConsistDreamer generates significantly sharper results making quantitative metrics less informative. The only quantitative metric is the Distillation Fidelity Score (DFS, lower is better), used in the ablation study: the full ConsistDreamer achieves DFS of 27.4±0.4 (Task A), 159.8±2.1 (Task B), 62.7±1.9 (Task C), and 132.8±1.2 (Task D), substantially outperforming all ablated variants (e.g., the 'IN2N' variant scores 91.0, 255.8, 90.9, 222.4 respectively). Qualitatively, ConsistDreamer is the first method to successfully edit complex plaid/checkered patterns and to produce recognizable results in large-scale ScanNet++ indoor scenes where IN2N fails.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baselines are IN2N [8] and ViCA-NeRF [6], both of which distill from the same 2D diffusion model [2]. ConsistDreamer clearly outperforms both in qualitative comparisons: it produces significantly sharper results with fine-grained textures (e.g., Van Gogh brushstrokes, Munch textures, marble grain) where IN2N and ViCA produce blurred, over-smoothed outputs. In the 'Lord Voldemort' editing task, ConsistDreamer is the only method that successfully produces the distinctive no-hair, peculiar-nose appearance; all baselines fail. ConsistDreamer also succeeds in editing plaid/checkered patterns (a common failure mode for IN2N) and in complex ScanNet++ indoor scenes where IN2N cannot achieve satisfactory edits. NeRF-Art [34], an earlier CLIP-based method, is shown to produce inferior results in Figure 3. The remaining baselines (CSD [15], DreamEditor [49], GaussianEditor [5], EN2N [31], PDS [17]) are compared only in supplementary material under a limited set of tasks due to code availability constraints, with no quantitative comparison provided in the main paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsistDreamer extends the IN2N pipeline (iterative NeRF fitting + 2D diffusion generation) by addressing the fundamental 3D-consistency gap that prior distillation-based editing methods (IN2N, ViCA-NeRF, CSD, ConsistNet) leave unresolved. It builds on the InstructPix2Pix diffusion model [2] as the editing backbone and augments it with a ControlNet for 3D-aware conditioning, but its core departure is three synergistic input/training modifications: 3D-consistent structured noise (replacing per-view independent Gaussian noise), surrounding-view composed images (exploiting the UNet's self-attention across tiled sub-images), and self-supervised consistency-enforcing training that enforces cross-batch consistency via depth-warped averaging. Unlike prior joint-distillation methods that only enforce within-batch consistency, ConsistDreamer explicitly targets cross-generation consistency through its structured noise and multi-GPU parallel training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that 3D consistency in diffusion-based scene editing is not merely an output-level problem but fundamentally requires consistent inputs (noise) and explicit training signals, a reframing that could inform future distillation-based 3D/4D generation. The ability to preserve complex regular patterns (plaid, checkered) and succeed on large-scale ScanNet++ indoor scenes where prior methods fail is practically significant for content creation pipelines. However, the method remains per-scene (requiring NeRF fitting and per-scene diffusion fine-tuning), relies on multi-GPU parallelization, and its evaluation is primarily qualitative with a novel but non-standard DFS metric, leaving open questions about generalizability, computational cost at scale, and whether the approach transfers to alternative scene representations like Gaussian Splatting.
