## Problem

Existing 3D animal reconstruction methods are category-specific, requiring separate models per species and failing to generalize to rare animals with only a handful of training images. Scaling to the 2.1 million animal species on Earth is infeasible with current pipelines that rely on expensive 3D scans, manual annotations, or per-category optimization. The paper tackles the challenge of learning a single pan-category deformable model for 100+ quadruped species using only unlabeled 2D Internet images as supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image of any quadruped animal (no category label, no multi-view, no 3D annotation) and produces a fully articulated, textured 3D mesh with skeletal pose in a feed-forward pass taking seconds. The mesh is parameterized by a quadrupedal skeleton with linear blend skinning, making it directly animatable. At test time, the texture network can optionally be fine-tuned for ~50 iterations (<10 s) to improve appearance fidelity, but the geometry and pose are predicted in one forward pass.

## Method

The core contribution is the Semantic Bank of Skinned Models (SBSM), a learned memory bank of K=60 key-value pairs that uses DINO-ViT image embeddings to automatically discover a small set of base animal shapes via cosine-similarity attention, eliminating the need for category labels while preserving evolutionary shape similarities across species. The base shape is predicted by a coordinate MLP in a hybrid SDF-mesh representation (extracted via DMTet), followed by an instance-specific deformation MLP and a pose network that drives a quadrupedal skeleton through linear blend skinning. A mask discriminator adversarially enforces that silhouettes rendered from random viewpoints remain realistic, countering the strong frontal-view bias in Internet photos that otherwise causes shape elongation. Training is fully self-supervised using RGB reconstruction, PCA-reduced DINO feature-field matching for semantic correspondences, and mask losses, organized in three stages: rigid base shapes, articulation with discriminator, and finally instance deformation.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC (horses), 3D-Fauna achieves a KT-PCK@0.1 of 0.539, substantially outperforming MagicPony (0.429), A-CSM (0.329), and UMR (0.284). On the multi-species PCK@0.1 metric, the method scores 0.782 on PASCAL VOC, 0.841 on APT-36K, and 0.901 on Animal3D, surpassing the strongest baseline MagicPony (0.756 and 0.867) and A-CSM (0.649 and 0.822) on all three datasets. The method also produces articulated, textured 3D meshes in a feed-forward manner within seconds, compared to 10–20 minutes of per-instance optimization required by LASSIE, Hi-LASSIE, and Zero-1-to-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-Fauna outperforms all quantitative baselines on every reported metric. Against MagicPony [60] (the closest competitor and architectural base), it improves KT-PCK@0.1 by 11 points on PASCAL VOC (0.539 vs. 0.429) and PCK@0.1 by 8.5 and 3.4 points on APT-36K and Animal3D respectively. A-CSM [28] trails by a wider margin (e.g., 0.841 vs. 0.649 on APT-36K), while UMR [29] is only evaluated on PASCAL and scores lowest (0.284). In qualitative comparisons (Fig. 5), MagicPony degrades severely on rare categories with fewer than 100 training images, producing severe artifacts; LASSIE [70] and Hi-LASSIE [71] yield coarser reconstructions with unstable viewpoint predictions despite per-instance optimization; and Zero-1-to-3 [31] frequently fails to reconstruct legs and does not model articulated pose. The key advantage of 3D-Fauna is that a single pan-category model generalizes to all 100+ species without per-category fine-tuning, whereas MagicPony requires category-specific training and the optimization-based methods require 10–20 minutes per instance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the skinned-model line of work for animal 3D reconstruction (MagicPony, DOVE, Farm3D) from category-specific to pan-category modeling of 100+ quadruped species. The key architectural departure is the Semantic Bank of Skinned Models (SBSM), which uses off-the-shelf DINO features to automatically discover a small set of base shapes via a learned memory bank, replacing the single fixed template used in prior skinned-model approaches. It also introduces a mask discriminator (rather than an RGB discriminator) to combat the strong frontal-viewpoint bias in Internet photos, and contributes the Fauna Dataset spanning 128 quadruped species. The method builds on MagicPony's hierarchical shape factorization (base shape → instance deformation → articulation) and its use of PCA-reduced self-supervised features for correspondence supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first feed-forward deformable model that reconstructs articulated 3D meshes for 100+ quadruped species from a single image without category labels, addressing a fundamental scalability bottleneck in animal 3D modeling. The Fauna Dataset (78K images, 128 species) and the SBSM mechanism are reusable assets for the community. However, the model is still restricted to quadrupeds sharing a single skeletal topology, and training images require manual curation, leaving the broader goal of modeling all 2.1 million animal species far from reach. Open questions include whether the semantic-bank approach generalizes to non-quadruped body plans (birds, fish, insects) and whether fully automatic image collection without manual filtering is feasible.
