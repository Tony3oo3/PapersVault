## Problem

Existing multimodal language models either project full modality embeddings into text space (inflating sequence length and computational cost) or rely on heavy external resamplers like Q-Former that introduce large parameter counts and require separate pretraining. The paper addresses how to efficiently bridge text-relevant multimodal information into a frozen pre-trained language model with minimal trainable parameters, without sacrificing the model's native token-fusion and representation capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242186/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10 sampled video frames (encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14 into per-frame embeddings) and 10 audio clips (encoded by CLAP into segment embeddings) alongside a text question. Only 1–2 learnable querying prompt tokens per modality are prepended to the text embedding sequence, keeping the LM input length nearly unchanged. For AVQA and VideoQA tasks the output is a multiple-choice answer; for Multimodal Sentiment Analysis the output is a continuous sentiment score in (−3, +3) evaluated via MAE, Pearson correlation, binary accuracy, and F1.

## Method

QaP introduces learnable Querying Prompts that serve a dual role: they act as prompts for text interaction within the frozen LM's self-attention, and as queries for extracting modality-specific information via a lightweight Text-Conditioned Resampler (TCR) inserted into the first 12 of 24 LM layers. The TCR operates in three steps: a shared linear projection aligns modality features to the LM's representation space, a parameter-free dot-product cross-attention uses the text-conditioned querying prompt as Query to sample text-relevant features from the modality, and a learnable gate (initialized to 1 in early layers, 0 in later layers) controls injection intensity. A parallel FFN Adapter handles domain adaptation. Because the LM's own MSA layer propagates text semantics into the querying prompts before the TCR samples, the resampler needs only a single shared linear layer for Key/Value, yielding just 40M trainable parameters (4.6% of the full model) while surpassing full-parameter fine-tuning on all four benchmarks.

## Results

On Music-AVQA (the primary task), QaP achieves 78.41% accuracy with only 40M trainable parameters (4.6% of the full model), surpassing full-parameter fine-tuning (77.73% with 890M params) and the best parameter-efficient baseline MAGMA (77.44%) by 0.97%. On How2QA, QaP reaches 94.5% accuracy, outperforming FrozenBilm (86.7%) which was pre-trained on additional multimodal data. On CMU-MOSEI, QaP achieves Corr of 0.825 and F1 of 90.87/90.90, exceeding the strongest baseline UniMSE by 1.4% on Corr and 5.1% on F1 (non-negative), while its MAE of 0.529 is within 0.6% of UniMSE's 0.523.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QaP outperforms all 19 baselines on the primary AVQA task: it beats the closest parameter-efficient competitor MAGMA [5] by 0.97% (78.41% vs 77.44%) and Lavish [24] by 1.24% (78.41% vs 77.17%), while also surpassing full-parameter fine-tuning (77.73%) with only 4.5% of the trainable parameters. On VideoQA, QaP achieves a decisive 7.8% gain over FrozenBilm [48] on How2QA (94.5% vs 86.7%) despite not using any extra pre-training data, but trails FrozenBilm by 2.2% on the larger TVQA dataset (80.19% vs 82.4%). On CMU-MOSEI, QaP wins on Corr (0.825 vs 0.773 for UniMSE [11]), ACC-2, and F1, but is slightly behind UniMSE on MAE (0.529 vs 0.523) and MMIM [7] on MAE (0.529 vs 0.526), indicating a small trade-off in regression accuracy for substantially better correlation and classification metrics. The projection-based baselines (Limber [29], PromptFuse [23], eP-ALM [35]) all underperform QaP by 2.7–5.6% on AVQA, confirming that the Text-Conditioned Resampler's query-based extraction is more effective than direct linear projection or [CLS] pooling for modality bridging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the querying mechanism from external resamplers (Q-Former, Flamingo) and embedding it directly inside a frozen language model's layers as learnable prompt vectors. It departs from projection-based MLMs (Limber, PromptFuse, MAGMA, eP-ALM) that inject long modality token sequences, and from querying-based MLMs that require heavy external resampler modules with separate pre-training. The key conceptual move is the dual role of the Querying Prompt: it simultaneously acts as a cross-attention query for modality extraction and as a self-attention prompt for text interaction, all within the LM's existing MSA layer, making the Text-Conditioned Resampler a parameter-free dot-product cross-attention plus a single linear projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or superior multimodal performance with only 4.6% trainable parameters and no external pre-training data, which lowers the barrier for adapting frozen LMs to new multimodal tasks. The modular design—adding a modality simply adds one query vector and one lightweight TCR—suggests easy extensibility to additional modalities (point clouds, depth) without architectural changes. However, the authors acknowledge validation is limited to a bidirectional LM (DeBERTa-V2) and do not explore decoder-only autoregressive LLMs, leaving open whether the self-attention-based text-to-query information transfer generalizes to causal attention settings. Scaling to larger LLMs and broader modality sets remains future work.
