## Problem

Prior open-vocabulary HOI detectors use a single level of feature maps to model human-object pairs with widely varying spatial distances, causing severe performance drops on large-distance interactions. Additionally, they rely solely on category names for interaction recognition, overlooking the rich contextual and fine-grained semantic information that language can provide—information that is especially critical for rare or unseen concepts where text embeddings from short names are unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at a fixed 224×224 resolution as input and produces a set of <human bounding box, object bounding box, interaction label> triplets, where the interaction label is an arbitrary open-vocabulary text string (action-object pair). The visual encoder (CLIP ViT-B/16) is fully frozen, and the text side encodes both the HOI category name (with learnable context tokens) and GPT-generated body-part state descriptions (without learnable tokens) through the frozen CLIP text encoder, with the final interaction score computed as a weighted sum of similarities to both text embeddings.

## Method

CMD-SE is a DETR-style end-to-end detector built on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder. Its first key component is conditional multi-level decoding: HOIs are decoded in parallel from multiple intermediate layers of the ViT encoder, and a soft constraint loss (L_d) added to the bipartite matching cost explicitly encourages low-level feature maps to assign to small human-object distance pairs and high-level maps to large-distance pairs. Its second key component is fine-grained semantic enhancement: a two-step GPT prompting pipeline first identifies which body parts are relevant to each HOI, then generates brief state descriptions for those parts; these descriptions are encoded by the CLIP text encoder and combined with the standard HOI-name embedding via learnable scalar weights to produce the final interaction prediction. Training uses the Hungarian algorithm for bipartite matching with a composite cost (box regression, IoU, classification, and the distance-constraint term), and inference applies a confidence exponent to suppress overconfident object predictions.

## Results

On the open-vocabulary SWIG-HOI dataset (400 actions × 1000 objects), CMD-SE achieves 15.26 mAP on the Full split, a 15.08% relative gain over the previous state-of-the-art THID (13.26 mAP). The gains are consistent across all splits: 21.46 (Non-rare), 14.64 (Rare), and 10.70 (Unseen) mAP. On the simulated zero-shot HICO-DET setting, CMD-SE achieves 16.70 mAP on unseen interactions, outperforming the prior open-vocabulary method THID (15.53 mAP), though it trails zero-shot methods like HOICLIP (23.48) that rely on COCO-pretrained DETR weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SWIG-HOI, CMD-SE outperforms both baselines across all four splits: it beats THID by +3.79 (Non-rare), +1.82 (Rare), +0.66 (Unseen), and +2.00 (Full) mAP, and beats QPIC by +4.51, +3.80, +4.49, and +4.14 mAP respectively. On HICO-DET under the simulated zero-shot setting, CMD-SE surpasses the only other open-vocabulary method THID on unseen interactions (16.70 vs. 15.53 mAP) but is marginally behind on seen (23.95 vs. 24.32) and full (22.35 vs. 22.38) splits. The zero-shot baselines (HOICLIP, GEN-VLKT, FCL, ATL, VCL) report substantially higher numbers (e.g., HOICLIP: 23.48 unseen, 32.26 full), but the authors note this comparison is not fair because those methods use DETR weights pretrained on COCO, which shares the same object label space as HICO-DET, giving them an inherent advantage. Among the compositional zero-shot methods (VCL, ATL, FCL), CMD-SE's unseen mAP of 16.70 exceeds all three (10.06, 9.18, 13.16), though again under different training assumptions. Overall, CMD-SE establishes a new state-of-the-art specifically among open-vocabulary HOI detectors that do not rely on detection pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary HOI detection line initiated by THID [56] and the SWIG-HOI benchmark [55], which first cast HOI detection as a language-supervised, end-to-end set-matching problem built on CLIP. It departs from prior vision-language HOI detectors (GEN-VLKT, HOICLIP, THID) in two ways: (1) replacing single-level feature decoding with parallel multi-level decoding guided by a distance-aware conditional matching constraint, and (2) augmenting CLIP text embeddings of HOI category names with GPT-generated descriptions of human body-part states, a use of LLMs in HOI that prior work had not explored. The architectural backbone follows the DETR-style query-based pipeline, and the CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder is kept frozen, consistent with the zero-shot/open-vocabulary framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two concrete failure modes of prior open-vocabulary HOI detectors—performance collapse at large human-object distances and poor discrimination among rare interaction concepts—making it a practical step toward scalable, language-driven interaction understanding. However, the HICO-DET results are mixed (slightly below THID on Seen and Full splits), and the GPT-generated body-part descriptions are static, pre-computed artifacts that do not adapt to the image at inference time, limiting the approach's flexibility. Open questions include whether the multi-level conditional matching generalizes to non-ViT backbones, whether on-the-fly LLM querying could replace the fixed description set, and whether the body-part semantic space can be extended beyond the six-part vocabulary used here.
