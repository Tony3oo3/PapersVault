## Problem

While adversarial attacks on individual vision models are well-studied, the impact of visual adversarial perturbations on Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has not been systematically examined. Prior work on LMM adversarial robustness focused primarily on safety and jailbreaking, leaving open the question of whether an attack crafted solely against the visual encoder component can compromise the full LMM's task performance across classification, captioning, and VQA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241481/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluated LMMs (LLaVA, BLIP2-T5, InstructBLIP) take an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produce a class label, a short caption, or a VQA answer depending on the task. Adversarial perturbations are generated exclusively with respect to the visual encoder (CLIP for LLaVA, EVA-CLIP for BLIP2/InstructBLIP) using PGD, APGD, or CW under L∞ or L2 constraints, leaving the LLM component entirely untouched. Evaluation spans COCO 2014val, ImageNet 2012val, VQA v2, ScienceQA, TextVQA, POPE, and MME, with both normal (ε=8/255) and strong (ε=0.2) perturbation budgets.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical study of LMM robustness by generating visual adversarial examples w.r.t. the image encoder alone and measuring downstream LMM accuracy across three task types. A key finding is that LMMs are highly vulnerable when the query directly targets the attacked object (e.g., captioning, classification without context), but exhibit significant resilience when the query concerns peripheral aspects of the image (e.g., VQA questions about background or reasoning). Building on this, the authors propose a context-augmented classification scheme called query decomposition, where a single classification query is split into multiple existence queries—each paired with a textual description of the candidate object—which leverages the LLM's ability to recover object identity from corrupted visual inputs by matching against provided context. This approach yields 10–20% smaller accuracy drops under attack compared to plain LMM classification, and substantially boosts pre-attack accuracy on ImageNet by providing the LLM with explicit class descriptions.

## Results

On COCO caption retrieval, all three LMMs suffer severe degradation: InstructBLIP's Top-1 recall drops from 37.82% to 1.9% (PGD-N) and 0.18% (PGD-S), closely matching its EVA-CLIP encoder's collapse, indicating the LLM adds no meaningful robustness. In contrast, on ScienceQA VQA, LMMs show remarkable resilience with only a 2–10% accuracy drop (e.g., LLaVA: 71.59% → 68.77% under PGD-N) while the corresponding visual encoders plummet by 77–98%. Adding a one-sentence object context to the prompt reduces the PGD-N accuracy drop for BLIP2-T5 from 67% to 10%, and the proposed query decomposition further narrows the COCO PGD-N drop from 45% (plain) to 34% (decomposed).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three LMMs (LLaVA, BLIP2-T5, InstructBLIP) against their respective visual encoders (CLIP for LLaVA; EVA-CLIP for BLIP2-T5/InstructBLIP) under identical adversarial perturbations. On caption retrieval (Table 2), all LMMs perform at or below their visual encoders post-attack, confirming the LLM layer provides no inherent robustness gain. On VQA (Table 3), LLaVA consistently outperforms BLIP2-T5 and InstructBLIP in post-attack accuracy (e.g., VQA V2 PGD-N: 64.38% vs. 50.60% vs. 56.33%), likely due to its simpler linear projector preserving more visual signal versus the Q-former's modality blending. CW attacks are markedly less effective than PGD/APGD across all models and tasks (e.g., LLaVA VQA V2: −2.1% for CW-N vs. −18% for PGD-N). The query decomposition approach (Table 5) improves LLaVA's COCO PGD-N accuracy from 48.25% (plain) to 65.30% (decomposed), a 17-point gain, and boosts ImageNet pre-attack accuracy from 28.10% to 90.62%, demonstrating that context-augmented prompting is a practical robustness strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established adversarial robustness evaluation paradigm (PGD, CW, APGD) from standard vision models to the LMM setting, with a deliberate focus on attacks targeting only the visual encoder rather than the full end-to-end pipeline. It departs from recent LMM adversarial work (Carlini et al., Qi et al.) that focused on safety and jailbreaking, instead systematically measuring task-accuracy degradation across classification, captioning, and VQA. The query decomposition defense repurposes the observation that prompt context mitigates visual adversarial effects into a practical closed-world classification scheme, connecting the robustness literature to prompt engineering practices in instruction-tuned models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study showing that LMMs are not inherently robust to visual adversarial perturbations when no textual context is provided, yet context in the prompt can dramatically reduce accuracy drops (e.g., 8.10% vs 99.73% on ScienceQA). The practical implication is significant: deploying LMMs in adversarial environments (e.g., security screening, autonomous systems) requires deliberate prompt design rather than relying on model architecture alone. Key open questions include whether findings generalize to end-to-end attacks on the full LMM pipeline, whether the context-mitigation effect persists as models scale, and whether the query decomposition approach scales beyond closed-world classification to open-ended tasks.
