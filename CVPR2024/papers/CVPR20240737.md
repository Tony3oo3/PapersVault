## Problem

Non-rigid point set registration without correspondences remains highly ill-posed, especially for shapes with large deformations. Most existing approaches rely on a two-step pipeline (shape matching followed by alignment) or are limited to the Gaussian kernel due to fast Gauss transform constraints, leaving direct correspondence-free registration for large deformations comparatively underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240737/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two unstructured point sets in any spatial dimension (2D or 3D) with no prescribed correspondences, typically 3,000–4,000 points after voxel-grid downsampling. The source set is internally treated as clustering centroids and the target as clustering members; the output is a smooth displacement field that deforms the source to match the target, evaluated via RMSE against ground-truth or nearest-neighbor correspondences. The method is dimension-independent, so the same formulation applies identically to 2D hand keypoints and 3D human/animal scans.

## Method

The core reformulation treats non-rigid registration as an unsupervised fuzzy clustering problem: source points are centroids whose positions are dynamically updated (i.e., deformed), while target points are cluster members, with the clustering objective adapted from the ℓ₂,ₚ-norm framework. Tikhonov regularization is applied via a sparsity-induced Laplacian kernel (exp(−γ‖yᵢ−yⱼ‖₁)) rather than the standard Gaussian RBF, yielding thicker tails and greater robustness to noise and outliers. All variables (membership matrix U, cluster sizes α, isotropic covariance σ², and displacement coefficients c) admit closed-form solutions per iteration, enabling efficient optimization. For scalability, a clustering-improved Nyström low-rank approximation replaces random sampling with Elkan k-means partitioning of the source set, reducing Gram matrix complexity to O(C²C′) and O(CC′) storage, with a rigorous Frobenius-norm error bound proven specifically for the Laplacian kernel.

## Results

On the FAUST human scan dataset (Table 2), the proposed method achieves an average RMSE of 0.0092 across all intra- and inter-subject settings, outperforming the closest neural-network-based competitor Nerfies (0.0129) by ~29% and the best classical method GBCPD (0.0265) by ~65%. On the 2D IMM hand dataset, the method achieves average RMSE of 0.0383–0.0879 across four subjects, beating the best baseline GMM (0.0547–0.0917) while running faster (0.1074s vs. 0.1140s). On the TOSCA animal dataset with large deformations, the method achieves an average RMSE of 1.5247, surpassing the best baseline NSFP (2.0710) by 26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. On the 2D IMM hand dataset, it beats GMM (best classical 2D method) by 17–33% in RMSE and ZAC by over 10×, while also being faster than MR-RPM and BCPD. On FAUST, the closest competitor is Nerfies (0.0129 avg RMSE), which the method surpasses by 29%; among classical methods, GBCPD (0.0265) is the strongest but still trails by 65%. On TOSCA with large animal deformations, the method leads NSFP (2.0710) by 26% and GBCPD (2.6523) by 43%. Notably, the method achieves the best accuracy while maintaining a runtime (2.37s) comparable to GBCPD (1.93s) and NDP (1.76s), and substantially faster than Nerfies (9.43s). Fast_RNRR and AMM_NRR, while fast, show large degradation on inter-subject (large-deformation) settings, whereas the proposed method maintains consistent accuracy across both intra- and inter-subject conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates non-rigid point set registration as an unsupervised fuzzy clustering problem, drawing directly on the l2,p-norm clustering framework of Chen et al. [9] and the Bayesian Coherent Point Drift (BCPD) formulation of Hirose [20]. The key conceptual departure is treating the source and target point sets as clustering centroids and members within a single holistic optimization, rather than as separate entities requiring explicit correspondences or probabilistic matching. The choice of an l1-induced Laplacian kernel for Tikhonov regularization replaces the standard Gaussian RBF used in CPD/BCPD, and the improved Nyström approximation [51] is coupled with Elkan k-means clustering (rather than random sampling) to achieve linear-complexity Gram matrix approximation with a provable error bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By delivering closed-form solutions within a dimension-independent framework, this method offers a practical, training-free alternative to neural network-based registration (Nerfies, NDP, NSFP) that outperforms them on both intra- and inter-subject deformations, which is notable for a non-learned approach. The Laplacian kernel's superior robustness to noise and occlusion, validated through ablation, suggests that the field's default reliance on Gaussian kernels may be suboptimal for large-deformation scenarios. Open questions include scalability to very large point clouds (beyond 3,000–4,000 points), handling of partial overlaps or outliers beyond the tested 20% occlusion, and whether the clustering-based Nyström approximation degrades gracefully as the approximation ratio drops further.
