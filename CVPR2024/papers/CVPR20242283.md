## Problem

End-to-end learning-based methods for multi-view 3D human pose estimation (e.g., MvP, VoxelPose) achieve strong in-domain accuracy but collapse to near-zero performance when tested on unseen camera arrangements or datasets, because learned priors overfit to training-time camera geometry. Pure geometric triangulation methods generalize well but fail under severe occlusion due to lack of learned appearance priors. The paper asks which sub-tasks in multi-view pose estimation should be handled geometrically (learning-free) versus by neural networks to achieve both accuracy and generalization.

## Input / Output

The model takes T synchronized RGB images from calibrated cameras along with their known 3×4 projection matrices. A frozen ResNet-50 backbone (pre-trained on COCO for 2D pose) extracts per-view feature maps. K=1024 compositional queries are initialized as T-pose skeletons uniformly sampled on the ground plane, each carrying a per-joint appearance feature vector and a per-joint 3D position. The output is a set of 3D joint positions per detected person, per-view 2D joint positions, and per-query confidence scores, with NMS applied to remove redundant queries attached to the same person.

## Method

MVGFormer alternates two modules across four decoder layers in a coarse-to-fine loop. The Appearance Module (AM) projects each query's 3D position into every view, uses deformable attention to sample local features around the projected 2D location, and predicts a 2D offset and confidence score via an MLP. The Geometry Module (GM) then performs differentiable algebraic triangulation over the refined 2D poses (weighted by confidence) to produce an updated 3D position, which is learning-free and immune to quantization errors. Queries use a hierarchical compositional embedding (instance + joint embeddings for appearance, explicit 3D coordinates for geometry), and multi-view features are fused via an MLP to update the appearance term each layer. Training uses anchor-based KNN matching (W=5 nearest queries per GT pose), L1 pose losses on both 2D and 3D predictions at every layer, and a cross-entropy classifier to suppress empty queries; the entire pipeline is end-to-end differentiable.

## Results

On the in-domain CMU Panoptic benchmark, MVGFormer achieves AP25 of 92.3 and MPJPE of 16.0 mm, matching MvP on AP25 and outperforming VoxelPose by 8.3 AP25 points. On Shelf and Campus, it reaches PCP of 98.0% and 96.7% respectively, tying or exceeding all baselines. In the out-of-domain cross-arrangement setting (Table 2), MVGFormer achieves an average AP25 of 74.7 and mAP of 90.6, while MvP drops to 0.0 and VoxelPose falls to 29.4/78.5, demonstrating a decisive generalization advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVGFormer ties MvP [36] on CMU Panoptic AP25 (92.3) but slightly trails on MPJPE (16.0 vs 15.8 mm); however, it surpasses MvP on Shelf (98.0 vs 97.4 PCP) and Campus (96.7 vs 96.6 PCP). Against VoxelPose [31], MVGFormer leads by 8.3 AP25 points on CMU Panoptic (92.3 vs 84.0) and by 1.0 PCP on Shelf (98.0 vs 97.0), while matching on Campus (96.7). The geometric baseline Dong et al. [8] is outperformed on Shelf (98.0 vs 96.9) and Campus (96.7 vs 96.3). The most striking gap appears in out-of-domain evaluation: when camera arrangements change (Table 2), MvP collapses to 0.0 AP25 and VoxelPose drops to 29.4 AP25, while MVGFormer retains 74.7 AP25; on cross-dataset transfer without finetuning (Table 3), MVGFormer achieves 87.9 PCP on Shelf versus 69.8 for VoxelPose and 8.7 for MvP. Lin et al. [20] and Faster VoxelPose [35] are outperformed on CMU Panoptic AP25 (92.3 vs 92.1 and 85.2) and MPJPE (16.0 vs 16.8 and 18.3 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

MVGFormer bridges two previously separate lines of multi-view 3D pose estimation: classical geometric pipelines (triangulation-based methods like Dong et al. [8]) that generalize well but fail under occlusion, and end-to-end learned approaches (VoxelPose [31], MvP [36]) that handle occlusion but overfit to training camera configurations. Architecturally, it builds on the DETR-style multi-view framework of MvP [36] (adopting its hierarchical query embedding) and deformable attention [41] for feature sampling, but inserts a learning-free differentiable triangulation module [16] as an explicit geometry step between appearance modules. This hybrid design departs from the fully-learned paradigm by treating 3D reconstruction as a closed-form geometric operation rather than a learned regression, while retaining learned 2D pose refinement for occlusion robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit multi-view geometry within a Transformer pipeline yields dramatically better out-of-domain generalization (74.7% avg AP25 on unseen camera arrangements vs. 0% for MvP) without sacrificing in-domain accuracy, suggesting a 'train once, deploy anywhere' path for multi-camera systems that is practically valuable. Limitations include single-frame evaluation (no temporal tracking), dependence on known camera calibration, and reduced performance on the outdoor Campus dataset due to appearance domain gap. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based tracking, whether the geometry module can be partially learned for even better occlusion handling, and how it scales to very large numbers of cameras or uncalibrated setups.
