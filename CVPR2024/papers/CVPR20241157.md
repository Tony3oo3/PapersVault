## Problem

Existing learning-with-noisy-labels methods handle instance reweighting and label reweighting separately: meta-learning approaches adjust per-sample importance but ignore label correction, while bootstrapping methods blend real and pseudo-labels with a fixed hyperparameter that is highly sensitive to noise level. No prior method concurrently and adaptively adjusts both the balance between real and pseudo labels and the contribution of individual training samples within a single unified objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241157/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy-labeled images (32×32 CIFAR, dermoscopic ISIC2019 patches, 144×144 axial MRI slices for PROMISE12) together with a small clean validation set (as few as 500 samples, or zero samples via a GMM-based online meta set drawn from the training data). For classification it outputs a single class label per image; for segmentation it outputs a pixel-wise mask, where the per-sample scalar weights α_i and β_i are replaced by spatial weight maps over the image grid.

## Method

L2B introduces a general loss (Eq. 3) that assigns each training sample two non-negative meta-learned weights: α_i scaling the cross-entropy against the observed (noisy) label and β_i scaling the cross-entropy against the model's own pseudo-label, with the critical relaxation that α_i + β_i need not equal 1. A bi-level meta-learning loop updates θ in the inner step using the weighted loss and then performs one gradient step on α, β in the outer step to minimize validation loss, followed by rectification (clipping to ≥ 0) and within-batch normalization. Because the sum constraint is dropped, the optimizer can simultaneously down-weight unreliable samples and up-weight the pseudo-label term when the network's prediction has already corrected a noisy annotation, achieving implicit relabeling without explicitly constructing new targets. The method is a drop-in plug-in: it wraps existing LNL pipelines (DivideMix, UniCon, C2D) and, when no clean validation set is available, constructs an online meta set from the training data via a Gaussian mixture model, requiring no additional architectural changes or training cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 50% symmetric noise, L2B achieves 64.5% test accuracy, outperforming the instance-reweighting baseline L2RW (55.6%) by 8.9% and the bootstrapping baseline (45.8%) by 18.7%. When integrated with C2D, L2B-C2D reaches 94.4% on CIFAR-10 and 60.7% on CIFAR-100 under 90% noise, surpassing all competing SOTA methods. On the real-world Clothing 1M dataset, L2B-Naive attains 77.5% accuracy (±0.2 over 3 runs), exceeding the best prior method MSLG (76.0%) by 1.5%. For prostate segmentation on PROMISE12 with ~60% label corruption, L2B achieves 80.83% Dice, 6.68 HD, and 2.10 ASD, outperforming the best baseline (NL reweighting: 76.64% Dice, 8.33 HD, 2.75 ASD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2B consistently outperforms all 28 compared baselines across every dataset and noise level tested. On CIFAR-100 at 50% symmetric noise, L2B (64.5%) surpasses L2RW [38] (55.6%) by 8.9%, Bootstrap [37] (45.8%) by 18.7%, and M-correction [2] (66.1%) is the closest competitor but still trails by 1.6%. At the extreme 90% noise setting, L2B-C2D (94.4% CIFAR-10, 60.7% CIFAR-100) dominates AugDesc [32] (91.9%, 41.2%), TCL [14] (89.4%, 54.5%), and DivideMix [22] (76.0%, 31.5%) by large margins. On the real-world Clothing 1M dataset, L2B-Naive (77.5%) beats MSLG [1] (76.0%), MLC [70] (75.8%), and DivideMix+SNSCL [51] (75.3%). For segmentation on PROMISE12, L2B (80.83% Dice) exceeds NL reweighting [30] (76.64%) by 4.2% Dice and UNet++ [72] (73.74%) by 7.1%. The ablation in Table 8 confirms that the unconstrained formulation (α,β≥0) is critical: restricting to α+β=1 drops CIFAR-100 accuracy from 71.8% to 64.8% at 20% noise, and setting β=0 (reducing to pure instance reweighting) yields only 63.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2B unifies two previously separate lines of work on noisy-label learning: the bootstrapping loss of Reed et al. (ICLR 2015), which blends real and pseudo labels with a fixed hyperparameter β, and meta-learning-based instance reweighting (L2RW, Ren et al. ICML 2018), which adaptively down-weights noisy samples. The key conceptual departure is a single meta-learned objective that jointly assigns per-sample weights to both the real-label loss (αᵢ) and the pseudo-label loss (βᵢ) without requiring αᵢ+βᵢ=1, thereby performing implicit relabeling and instance reweighting in one step. The paper also positions itself as a drop-in complement to semi-supervised LNL pipelines (DivideMix, UniCon, C2D) rather than a replacement, and shows that the clean validation set typically required by meta-learning can be replaced by an online GMM-based meta set drawn from the training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in L2B's plug-in compatibility: it consistently boosts existing SOTA LNL methods (e.g., +7.9% over C2D at 90% noise on CIFAR-10) without architectural changes, and it operates with zero or very few clean validation samples, making it applicable in settings where a held-out clean set is unavailable. The ablation showing that relaxing the convexity constraint (α+β=1 → α,β≥0) yields an 8+ point gain on CIFAR-100 is a clean, well-isolated result that reframes how one thinks about the bootstrapping objective. Open questions include whether the meta-learning overhead scales to very large datasets (e.g., ImageNet-scale), whether the GMM-based online meta set remains reliable under instance-dependent or class-dependent noise, and whether the approach extends naturally to tasks beyond classification and 2D segmentation (e.g., 3D medical segmentation, detection).
