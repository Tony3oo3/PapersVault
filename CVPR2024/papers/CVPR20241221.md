## Problem

Existing methods for animatable clothed human avatars from monocular video face a fundamental speed-quality tradeoff: NeRF-based approaches (HumanNeRF, ARAH) achieve high rendering quality but require 8+ GPU days of training and render at 0.1–0.2 FPS, while fast-training methods (InstantAvatar, Instant-NVR) sacrifice pose-dependent non-rigid cloth deformation or rendering fidelity. No prior method simultaneously delivers fast training, real-time inference, pose-dependent deformation, and state-of-the-art rendering quality from a single monocular video.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short monocular video sequence (tens of frames) with pre-fitted SMPL pose/shape parameters, calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and per-frame foreground masks. It outputs a canonical set of 50K 3D Gaussians plus three small networks (deformation, skinning, color) that together enable both novel view synthesis and novel pose animation at 50+ FPS on a single GPU, with total training time under 30 minutes. The SMPL parameters are further refined during training via differentiable optimization of per-frame translation, global rotation, and local joint rotations to correct fitting inaccuracies.

## Method

The method initializes 50K 3D Gaussians on the canonical SMPL mesh surface and decomposes human deformation into a pose-conditioned non-rigid component (a multi-level hash grid encoding 3D positions, concatenated with a hierarchical pose latent code, fed through a 2-layer MLP of width 128 to predict position/scale/rotation offsets and a 16-dim feature vector) and a rigid component (a learned skinning MLP predicting per-Gaussian weights for forward LBS with 24 bone transforms). Instead of per-Gaussian spherical harmonics, a tiny color MLP (single 64-dim hidden layer) decodes view-dependent color from the per-Gaussian feature, the pose-dependent deformation feature, a per-frame latent code for global lighting, and spherical harmonics of the canonicalized viewing direction. A key regularizer—applied to both Gaussian mean vectors and covariance matrices—enforces as-isometric-as-possible constraints over k=5 nearest neighbors, preventing the underconstrained deformation network from producing noisy, non-generalizable deformations under monocular supervision. The full system is trained end-to-end with photometric RGB loss, mask loss, skinning weight regularization, and the isometric terms, jointly optimizing the Gaussians and all three networks.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap (6 sequences), 3DGS-Avatar achieves an average LPIPS of 29.58 (×1000), significantly outperforming ARAH (36.13), HumanNeRF (31.73), and MonoHuman (37.51), while matching or exceeding their PSNR (~30.6 vs. 30.5 for ARAH) and SSIM (0.9703 vs. 0.9709 for ARAH). Training completes in 30 GPU minutes (0.5 h) versus 8 GPU days for ARAH/HumanNeRF (≈400× faster), and rendering runs at 50 FPS versus 0.1–0.2 FPS for those baselines (≈250–500× faster). On PeopleSnapshot, the method reaches 30.57 PSNR and 20.86 LPIPS on female-3-casual, beating InstantAvatar (27.66 PSNR, 21.00 LPIPS) while rendering 3× faster (50 vs. 15 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ARAH [57] and HumanNeRF [58], the two strongest NeRF-based baselines, 3DGS-Avatar achieves comparable PSNR (30.61 vs. 30.52 and 30.24) and SSIM (0.9703 vs. 0.9709 and 0.9680) but substantially lower LPIPS (29.58 vs. 36.13 and 31.73), which the authors argue is the most informative metric under monocular input due to stochastic cloth deformation. The speed advantage is dramatic: 30 min training vs. 8 GPU days (≈400×) and 50 FPS rendering vs. 0.1–0.2 FPS (≈250–500×). Against Instant-NVR [7] on the refined ZJU-MoCap split, 3DGS-Avatar wins on LPIPS (28.62 vs. 38.40) and SSIM (0.9711 vs. 0.9708) but trails slightly on PSNR (30.91 vs. 31.02), while rendering 17× faster (50 vs. 3 FPS). On PeopleSnapshot, the method outperforms InstantAvatar [12] on PSNR (30.57 vs. 27.66 on female-3-casual; 34.28 vs. 29.53 on male-3-casual) and LPIPS across all four sequences, while rendering 3× faster (50 vs. 15 FPS). NeuralBody [37] and MonoHuman [70] are clearly outperformed on all three metrics, with LPIPS gaps of 20+ points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from static scene reconstruction to animatable clothed human avatars, departing from the dominant NeRF-based paradigm (HumanNeRF, ARAH, NeuralBody, MonoHuman) that relies on large MLPs for radiance fields. It inherits the rigid-plus-non-rigid deformation decomposition from HumanNeRF and the as-isometric-as-possible geometric constraint from Dynamic Point Fields, but applies both to explicit Gaussian primitives rather than implicit fields. Compared to fast-training NeRF variants (InstantAvatar, Instant-NVR) that use hash grids, this work adds pose-dependent non-rigid cloth deformation and a learned color MLP in place of spherical harmonics, addressing the blurry-rendering and poor novel-view generalization those methods exhibit under monocular input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 400× faster training (30 min vs. 8 GPU days) and 250× faster inference (50+ FPS vs. 0.1–0.2 FPS) while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art LPIPS, this work makes high-quality animatable avatar creation from a single video practically deployable in VR, gaming, and e-commerce pipelines. The finding that a tiny 64-dim MLP outperforms spherical harmonics for view-dependent color in the monocular setting is a useful conceptual insight: with only one training view, SH coefficients are underconstrained, and a learned decoder conditioned on canonicalized viewing direction and local deformation features generalizes better. Open questions include whether the as-isometric-as-possible regularization (applied to both positions and covariances) scales to more extreme or out-of-distribution motions, and whether the method can be extended to multi-person scenes or uncalibrated inputs without the SMPL fitting and mask prerequisites.
