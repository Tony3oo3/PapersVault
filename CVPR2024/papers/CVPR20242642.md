## Problem

Existing Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) representations for oriented object detection suffer from inherent discontinuities: rotation discontinuity at angle boundaries, aspect-ratio discontinuity for square-like objects, and decoding discontinuities (Decoding Incompleteness and Decoding Ambiguity) that degrade prediction precision. No prior method has achieved a theoretically guaranteed continuous representation that simultaneously resolves all forms of encoding and decoding discontinuity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242642/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on aerial or remote sensing images (e.g., DOTA at 800×800 to 4000×4000 resolution) and outputs oriented bounding boxes with class labels. The OBB is encoded as a 9-dimensional vector: the outer horizontal bounding box (x_c, y_c, w, h), a sliding ratio r_s ∈ [0, 0.5], and four IoU scores that disambiguate among four geometrically distinct OBBs sharing the same base parameters. This representation is designed as a drop-in plugin, replacing only the OBB encoding/decoding module in existing two-stage detectors such as Faster R-CNN, RoI Transformer, and Oriented R-CNN.

## Method

COBB (Continuous OBB) represents each OBB via nine parameters derived from the outer HBB and OBB area, both of which vary continuously under geometric transformations. A sliding ratio r_s approximates the acreage ratio r_a through a continuous strictly-increasing map, and four IoU scores between the target OBB and four candidate OBBs (sharing the same x_c, y_c, w, h, r_s) resolve the remaining ambiguity without introducing Decoding Ambiguity. The paper formally defines six continuity metrics (target/loss rotation and aspect-ratio continuity, decoding completeness, and decoding robustness) and proves COBB satisfies all of them, which no prior method achieves. Two variants are provided: COBB-sig (sigmoid-bounded r_s) and COBB-ln (log-transformed r_s for better sensitivity to thin objects). The loss combines classification, Smooth L1 on the outer HBB, Smooth L1 on r_s, and Smooth L1 on the IoU scores, and the entire module can be swapped into any existing OOD detector without architectural changes.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, COBB-ln applied to Rotated Faster R-CNN achieves 74.44 mAP50, 44.08 mAP75, and 43.53 mAP50:95, improving over the baseline by 1.43%, 3.95%, and 2.20% respectively. On RoI Transformer, COBB-ln-ln reaches 76.53 mAP50 and 50.41 mAP75, surpassing the baseline by 0.94% and 1.87%. The method shows its strongest advantage in high-precision detection (mAP75), with a 13.77% mAP75 gain over Gliding Vertex on HRSC2016 for large-aspect-ratio ship objects. Across DOTA-v1.5, DIOR, and FAIR1M, COBB consistently improves all three baseline detectors by 1–2% in mAP50 and 1–4% in mAP75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COBB-ln on Faster R-CNN outperforms Gliding Vertex (the closest peer method) by 1.13% mAP50 and 2.46% mAP75 on DOTA-v1.0, and by a dramatic 13.77% mAP75 on HRSC2016 where large-aspect-ratio ships stress the sliding-ratio encoding. Against the two strongest two-stage baselines, COBB-ln-ln on RoI Transformer gains 0.94% mAP50 and 1.87% mAP75, while COBB-ln on Oriented R-CNN gains 1.14% mAP50 and 1.00% mAP75. Compared to ReDet (76.38/50.83), COBB-ln-ln on RoI Transformer (76.53/50.41) edges ahead on mAP50 but trails by 0.42% on mAP75, which the authors attribute to COBB's irregular parameter variation under rotation limiting its benefit for rotation-equivariant detectors. All single-stage baselines in Table 3 (CSL 67.99, KLD 68.75, GWD 69.02, KFIoU 68.99, RSDet 68.41, H2RBox 67.62, RetinaNet 68.18, FCOS 70.37, ATSS 72.44, S2A-Net 73.95) are substantially outperformed by COBB-ln on Faster R-CNN (74.44 mAP50), with the largest gaps over CSL-based and Gaussian-based methods that suffer from DI or DA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the OBB representation space by proposing COBB, a 9-parameter continuous representation (outer HBB + sliding ratio + four IoU scores) that provably eliminates both encoding discontinuity (rotation and aspect ratio) and decoding discontinuity (incompleteness and ambiguity) simultaneously. Prior methods—CSL and its variants, Gliding Vertex, PSC, FSTC, ACM, and Gaussian-based losses (GWD, KLD, KFIoU)—each address only a subset of these discontinuities or trade one for another. Rather than building on a single predecessor, COBB repurposes the geometric relationship between an OBB and its outer HBB as the foundation for a representation that is a drop-in replacement for any existing OOD detector's box regression head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee of full continuity for rectangle-based OBB representation is the first of its kind, providing a principled endpoint to years of ad-hoc boundary-fixing heuristics. Practically, COBB is a lightweight plugin (9 regression targets + 4 classification scores) that yields consistent mAP75 gains of 1–4% across Faster R-CNN, RoI Transformer, and Oriented R-CNN on DOTA, DIOR, HRSC2016, and FAIR1M. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the outer HBB and sliding ratio vary irregularly under rotation, which constrains COBB's benefit for rotation-equivariant detectors like ReDet. Open questions include whether the representation extends naturally to transformer-based or anchor-free OOD architectures, and whether the four-way IoU classification can be replaced by a fully continuous mechanism to further reduce parameter count.
