## Problem

Instruction-based diffusion editing methods like InstructPix2Pix (IP2P) frequently over-edit by altering unintended image regions and fail to execute multiple editing instructions harmoniously, as different instructions interfere with each other. Although IP2P exhibits a strong implicit grounding capability in its early cross-attention maps, no prior method has exploited this to precisely localize editing regions or reduce cross-instruction interference without additional training or test-time optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a composite text instruction decomposed into k sub-instructions, each containing a keyword identifying the target object or attribute. It produces one edited image in a single forward pass through the frozen IP2P U-Net (100 Euler ancestral denoising steps, effectively 80 since initial noise is 80% of the original latent). Mask extraction operates on 16×16 cross-attention maps at the first denoising step only, and the union mask is upsampled to latent resolution for the disentangle sampling stage.

## Method

FoI is a training-free, test-time-optimization-free pipeline built on frozen IP2P weights with three components. First, it extracts a binary mask per sub-instruction by iteratively squaring and min-max normalizing the 16×16 cross-attention map of the sub-instruction's keyword (γ=3 iterations), then thresholding at τ∈[0.4,0.7]. Second, cross-condition attention modulation replaces the instruction-conditioned attention in non-masked regions with the null-instruction attention (from ε_θ(z_t,t,I,∅_T)), while a timestep-scaled enhancement term ΔX (weighted by ξ(t)=0.05·t⁴ and a per-instruction coefficient α) boosts attention within the mask, confining each instruction to its region and reducing inter-instruction interference. Third, mask-guided disentangle sampling multiplies the text-guidance term in the score estimate by the upsampled union mask, so that editing and preservation directions are spatially separated; this is applied for the first 75% of denoising steps before switching to standard IP2P sampling for the remaining 25%.

## Results

On single-instruction editing, FoI achieves the best CLIP image similarity (0.9402), DINOv2 image similarity (0.9277), and PickScore (0.3901), outperforming the next-best MagicBrush (0.9178, 0.8702, 0.1780). On multi-instruction editing, FoI's advantage widens dramatically: PickScore jumps to 0.5077 versus 0.1591 for MagicBrush and 0.1325 for InsDiff, while CLIP image similarity reaches 0.9255 and DINOv2 similarity 0.9159. In the human preference study, over 80% of participants prefer FoI for both instruction alignment and image alignment in multi-instruction scenarios, compared to under 10% for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoI outperforms all five baselines on CLIP image similarity, DINOv2 image similarity, and PickScore in both single- and multi-instruction settings. The gap is most pronounced in multi-instruction editing, where FoI's PickScore (0.5077) is more than 3× that of MagicBrush (0.1591) and InsDiff (0.1325), reflecting the severe over-editing and instruction interference that plagues those methods. FoI scores lower on CLIP direction similarity than MagicBrush (0.1934) and InsDiff (0.2064) in single-instruction, but this is attributed to those methods over-editing (making larger, less targeted changes) rather than to FoI failing to follow instructions. In the human preference study, FoI receives 80.83% instruction-alignment and 81.33% image-alignment votes in multi-instruction editing, compared to at most 9.42% and 4.75% for MagicBrush, confirming a decisive qualitative advantage. Inversion-based methods (DiffEdit, NTI+P2P) consistently rank last on PickScore and human preference, struggling to execute multiple sub-instructions simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on InstructPix2Pix (IP2P) as a frozen backbone, departing from both inversion-based methods (NTI+P2P, PnP) that require test-time optimization and from prior mask-based editing approaches (DiffEdit, InstructEdit) that rely on external segmentation or noise-difference heuristics. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that IP2P's cross-attention maps in early denoising steps exhibit precise spatial grounding for instruction keywords — a capability the authors argue has been overlooked — which they exploit for training-free mask extraction. The cross-condition attention modulation and mask-guided disentangle sampling are novel post-hoc interventions on IP2P's denoising pipeline, extending the single-instruction editing paradigm to coordinated multi-instruction editing without any retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, single-forward-pass design makes this approach immediately deployable atop existing IP2P checkpoints, which is practically significant for practitioners who cannot afford retraining or per-image optimization. The >80% human preference in multi-instruction editing over all baselines highlights a substantial gap that prior methods (including the closely related EMILIE) have not addressed. However, the method is tightly coupled to IP2P's architecture and its specific cross-attention behavior; whether the grounding discovery and attention modulation generalize to other instruction-based or text-to-image diffusion models remains an open question. The heuristic nature of mask extraction (iterative squaring, fixed threshold range) also raises questions about robustness on atypical or highly compositional instructions.
