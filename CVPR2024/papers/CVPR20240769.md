## Problem

Text-to-video generation lags behind text-to-image generation largely because of the limited scale of publicly available video-text pairs (~10M in WebVid10M vs. ~5B image-text pairs in LAION), driven by the high cost of video captioning. While unlabeled video clips are trivially abundant on platforms like YouTube, existing methods either ignore them or rely on fragile two-step cascading pipelines. This paper asks whether a single unified diffusion model can be trained effectively on text-free videos and how performance scales with additional unlabeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240769/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a text prompt and a random noise sequence and produces a 16-frame video clip at 448×256 resolution (sampled at 4 FPS). During training, the content branch consumes image-text pairs from LAION-5B (single images treated as one-frame videos), while the motion branch consumes text-free video clips with no textual annotations; the center frame's CLIP image embedding serves as the sole conditioning signal for the motion branch. When integrated into the compositional synthesis paradigm, optional depth maps, sketches, or motion vectors can be added as additional spatial/temporal control signals.

## Method

TF-T2V decomposes video generation into two jointly optimized branches sharing a single 3D-UNet backbone: a content branch that learns text-conditioned spatial appearance from image-text pairs (using CLIP text and image encoders with random dropout), and a motion branch that learns temporal dynamics from text-free videos via image-conditioned video synthesis (center-frame CLIP embedding as condition). A novel temporal coherence loss is introduced that supervises the difference between adjacent predicted frames against ground-truth frame differences, explicitly enforcing inter-frame correlation beyond the standard per-frame v-prediction objective. The total loss combines the base v-prediction loss with the coherence term (λ=0.1), and the entire model is trained end-to-end with shared weights. The framework is plug-and-play: it can be dropped into existing compositional video synthesis pipelines and supports semi-supervised training by reintroducing labeled video-text pairs alongside text-free videos, yielding further gains (FID 8.19→7.64, FVD 441→366).

## Results

On MSR-VTT, TF-T2V trained on WebVid10M + Internal10M (text-free) achieves FID 8.19, FVD 441, and CLIPSIM 0.2991, outperforming all compared baselines on FID and FVD. In the semi-supervised setting (labeled WebVid10M + text-free Internal10M), performance further improves to FID 7.64, FVD 366, and CLIPSIM 0.3032. The model uses only 1.8B parameters, substantially fewer than CogVideo (15.5B) and Make-A-Video (9.7B), while achieving the best FID and FVD among all methods with reported values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-T2V (WebVid10M+Internal10M) achieves the best FID (8.19) and FVD (441) among all methods with reported values, beating the closest competitor PyCo (FID 9.73) by 0.54 FID and ModelScopeT2V (FID 11.09, FVD 550) by 2.9 FID and 109 FVD. On CLIPSIM, TF-T2V (0.2991) surpasses ModelScopeT2V (0.2930), VideoComposer (0.2932), and VideoLDM (0.2929), but remains slightly below Make-A-Video (0.3049), which uses a much larger 9.7B-parameter two-step pipeline. In the compositional video synthesis setting, TF-T2V also outperforms VideoComposer on depth error (0.209 vs. 0.217), sketch error (0.1146 vs. 0.1161), and motion EPE (1.88 vs. 1.98), as well as on all three human-preference dimensions (structure alignment 89.0% vs. 79.0%, visual quality 79.5% vs. 66.0%, temporal coherence 84.5% vs. 77.5%). The semi-supervised variant (TF-T2V-Semi) further narrows the CLIPSIM gap to Make-A-Video (0.3032 vs. 0.3049) while maintaining the best FID and FVD.

## Relation to Prior Work

TF-T2V builds directly on the latent 3D-UNet architecture from ModelScopeT2V and VideoLDM, and integrates into the compositional video synthesis framework of VideoComposer. It departs conceptually from the two-step cascade of Make-A-Video (which uses a ~1B-parameter diffusion prior to map text to image embeddings before video synthesis) by proposing a single unified model that decomposes learning into a content branch (trained on image-text pairs from LAION-5B) and a motion branch (trained on text-free videos). The key architectural departure from prior video diffusion models is the introduction of a temporal coherence loss that supervises frame-difference predictions, rather than applying the standard v-prediction loss independently to each frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data bottleneck in video generation: high-quality video-text pairs are expensive to produce, while text-free clips are trivially abundant on platforms like YouTube. By demonstrating that a unified text-to-video model can be trained effectively without any video-text annotations—and that scaling with text-free data yields consistent gains (FID 9.67→8.19, FVD 484→441)—it opens a practical path toward higher-resolution and longer video generation unshackled from the 360P WebVid10M constraint. Open questions include whether the content/motion decomposition generalizes to longer temporal horizons, more complex multi-object motion, and whether the semi-supervised gains persist at substantially larger scales. The plug-and-play design also suggests the approach could be adopted by other video diffusion frameworks beyond the specific 3D-UNet backbone used here.
