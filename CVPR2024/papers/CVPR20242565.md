## Problem

Existing face personalization methods for text-to-image diffusion models suffer from overfitting to training samples, which manifests as three distinct failure modes: locked camera poses, fixed face expressions, and rigid backgrounds. This overfitting creates a fundamental trade-off where methods that preserve identity similarity (e.g., CustomDiffusion) sacrifice text alignment, while methods that improve editability (e.g., FastComposer) degrade identity fidelity. The paper asks how to remove identity-irrelevant information from the training pipeline to achieve high editability without losing identity preservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single face image and a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'S* holding a dog in cherry blossoms') and produces a generated image containing that specific face identity in a scene aligned with the prompt. The face is encoded as a two-word embedding S* in CLIP text space via a lightweight mapping network, requiring no per-subject fine-tuning at inference (encoding time ~0.006s on A100). At inference, the expression feature is replaced with a learned unconditional vector to diversify expressions, while the identity feature remains fixed. Generation uses StableDiffusion-v1.4 with 30 denoising steps and classifier-free guidance at scale 7.0.

## Method

Face2Diffusion (F2D) introduces three components to disentangle identity-irrelevant information. (1) A Multi-Scale Identity (MSID) encoder pretrains a ViT-based ArcFace model with the ArcFace loss applied simultaneously across layers {3,6,9,12}, making shallow features as discriminative as deep ones (AUC improves from 93.99% to 99.46% on shallow layers) so that multi-scale extraction no longer leaks camera-pose information. (2) Expression guidance concatenates a 3D face reconstruction feature v_exp with the identity feature before the mapping network; during training, v_exp is dropped with probability 0.2 and replaced by a learnable unconditional vector, enabling expression control at inference. (3) Class-Guided Denoising Regularization (CGDR) constrains the predicted noise to match the class prompt 'a person' in background regions (via a face-parsing mask) while matching the true noise in the foreground, forcing the model to denoise backgrounds generically rather than memorizing training backgrounds. Only the two mapping networks (two-layer MLPs projecting into CLIP text space) are trained on FFHQ for 100K iterations; all other weights remain frozen, avoiding language drift and catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On 100 faces from FaceForensics++ with 40 diverse text prompts (4,000 generated scenes), Face2Diffusion ranks in the top-3 across five of six individual metrics and achieves the best harmonic mean (hMean = 0.1749) and geometric mean (gMean = 0.2252) of identity-fidelity (AdaFace, SphereFace, FaceNet) and text-fidelity (CLIP, dCLIP, SigLIP) scores. Specifically, F2D scores 0.3143 on AdaFace (3rd), 0.3856 on SigLIP (1st), and 0.2020 on dCLIP (2nd), balancing identity preservation with text alignment. The method also achieves the best FID (69.33) and fastest encoding time (0.006 s) among the top-performing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CustomDiffusion [24] achieves the highest identity scores (AdaFace 0.4537, SphereFace 0.5624, FaceNet 0.6458) but suffers severe overfitting, yielding the worst text-fidelity among strong methods (CLIP 0.2023, dCLIP 0.1490) and a much lower hMean (0.1078 vs. 0.1749). FastComposer [51] is the closest competitor on the combined metrics (hMean 0.1655, gMean 0.2120) with the best dCLIP score (0.2149), but its identity scores are substantially lower (AdaFace 0.1736 vs. 0.3143), reflecting its inability to represent face identities sufficiently. CelebBasis [54] leads on CLIP (0.2563) and SigLIP (0.3624) but has very weak identity preservation (AdaFace 0.1601), resulting in a lower hMean (0.1138). DreamIdentity [5] and DreamBooth [39] both trail F2D on the combined metrics (hMean 0.0849 and 0.0689 respectively), with DreamIdentity's multi-scale features trading text-fidelity for identity (CLIP 0.1924 vs. 0.2486). F2D's key advantage is its balanced performance: it is within ~0.01–0.03 of the best on each individual metric while achieving the best hMean and gMean, confirming superior overall quality in the identity–text trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of T2I personalization and face recognition, building directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion pipeline and ArcFace's identity embeddings. It departs from DreamIdentity's multi-scale feature extraction by retraining ArcFace so that shallow layers become identity-discriminative (removing pose/expression leakage), and from FastComposer's inference-time Delayed Subject Conditioning by instead using a training-time class-guided denoising regularization (CGDR) that constrains background denoising to follow the super-class 'a person.' The three-component decomposition of overfitting—camera poses, expressions, backgrounds—provides a more granular diagnostic than prior work, which typically treated overfitting as a monolithic problem addressed by a single regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

F2D offers a practically attractive recipe: sub-10ms encoding, no per-subject fine-tuning, and preservation of the original StableDiffusion weights (avoiding language drift and catastrophic forgetting). The CGDR technique—repurposing classifier-free guidance as a spatially-masked training objective—is a reusable idea that could extend to other personalization domains beyond faces. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-face scenes, video generation, or newer diffusion backbones (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based models), and whether the reliance on a 3D face reconstruction model for expression features limits robustness on low-quality or heavily occluded inputs.
