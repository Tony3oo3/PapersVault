## Problem

Vision-based CPR skill assessment is unexplored, and the core challenge is the 'Single-class Training & Multi-class Testing' dilemma: training data can only contain individual error actions, yet real assessment requires recognizing up to 8,191 possible composite error combinations simultaneously. No existing dataset or framework addresses this combinatorial explosion in the medical CPR domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241886/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes CPR compression videos captured from four 4K (4096×2160) cameras at 25 FPS, with three available modalities: RGB frames, TV-L1 optical flow, and AlphaPose 2D skeletons (average clip length 19.52 s). Output is a multi-hot vector over 14 single-error classes (1 correct + 13 errors) scored via BCE, evaluated on 74 unseen composite-error combinations using mAP and mmit mAP. During inference, a single video feature is replicated to satisfy the two-input fusion interface, a mismatch resolved by the replication trick.

## Method

ImagineNet is a modular, human-cognition-inspired wrapper that attaches to any existing HAR backbone (TSN, TSM, ST-GCN, MMNet) and operates in three stages: (1) feature extraction producing T×D video features, (2) an 'Imagine' fusion stage that combines two single-class features into a composite representation, and (3) BCE loss against multi-hot ground-truth labels. Three fusion mechanisms are provided—fully-connected (FC), self-attention (SA), and cross-attention (CA)—with SA yielding the best mAP. A MixUp-style random weighted summation (λ~U(0,1)) generates diverse feature combinations during training, improving generalization to unseen error pairings. The key insight is that a coach never memorizes all error combinations but reasons from single-error knowledge; ImagineNet mimics this by learning to compose single-class features into composite predictions, achieving up to 13.9% mAP improvement over direct model migration on Set-2.

## Results

On the single-class task (Set-1), TPN achieves the best CE-loss Top-1 accuracy of 98.02%, while MMNet achieves the best BCE-loss Top-1 of 97.72%. On the composite error recognition task (Set-2), direct migration of pre-trained models yields mAP scores of only 0.55–0.59. Adding ImagineNet-FC substantially improves performance: TSM gains +13.91% mAP (0.5662→0.7053) and +9.48% mmit mAP (0.6618→0.7566), while MMNet gains +9.79% mAP (0.5948→0.6927). Among the three ImagineNet variants, ImagineNet-SA achieves the best mmit mAP (0.7630) with only 0.068 GFLOPs overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Set-1 single-class recognition, TPN (98.02% CE Top-1) and MMNet (97.72% BCE Top-1) outperform all other baselines including TSM (90.67%), I3D (96.92%), and ST-GCN (92.46%). On Set-2 composite error recognition via direct migration, MMNet leads with 0.5948 mAP, followed by ST-GCN (0.5776), TSM (0.5662), and TSN (0.5598) — all showing severe degradation from Set-1. After applying ImagineNet-FC, TSM achieves the largest absolute gain (+13.91% mAP), surpassing MMNet's direct-migration score by a wide margin. In the cross-modality setting (Table 8), ImagineNet-CA (0.7110 mAP, 0.164 ms latency) outperforms BLOCK (0.7107 mAP, 1.294 ms) and CBP (0.7089 mAP, 0.304 ms) while being ~7.8× faster than BLOCK. Among the three ImagineNet variants, ImagineNet-SA (0.7011 mAP, 0.7630 mmit mAP) edges out ImagineNet-FC (0.7053 mAP, 0.7566 mmit mAP) on mmit mAP, while both substantially outperform ImagineNet-CA (0.6752 mAP, 0.7346 mmit mAP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Temporal Segment Network (TSN) as its base feature extractor and borrows the random weighted summation idea from MixUp for its feature aggregation strategy, but departs from both by repurposing them for a fundamentally different problem: recognizing composite multi-error actions when only single-class training data is available. In the medical action recognition literature, prior work has focused on surgical workflow recognition (laparoscopic, cataract, Da Vinci) and action quality scoring, but none has addressed fine-grained error identification in CPR or the combinatorial explosion of composite errors. The ImagineNet framework is positioned as a human-cognition-inspired solution to the 'Single-class Training & Multi-class Testing' gap, drawing on multi-label learning literature but under the stricter constraint that training samples contain only one positive label each.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CPR-Coach dataset is the first vision-based benchmark for CPR skill assessment, filling a clear gap where prior evaluation relied on expensive force-sensor dummies and human examiners. The 'single-class training, multi-class testing' formulation is a general problem that extends beyond CPR to any domain where atomic defects must be detected in combination (e.g., manufacturing QA, driving behavior), making the ImagineNet approach potentially transferable. Key limitations include the small participant pool (12 people), restriction to external cardiac compression only (no ventilation or full CPR cycles), and a deployment test on just 187 out-of-distribution videos. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-order combinations (beyond quadruple errors), whether it generalizes to other medical or safety-critical skills, and whether the modest performance gap between the simplest FC variant and attention-based variants indicates diminishing returns from architectural complexity in this regime.
