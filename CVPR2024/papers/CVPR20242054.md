## Problem

Existing one-stage scene graph generation models either introduce complex triplet detectors on top of object detectors or focus solely on triplet detection and miss objects without relations. Meanwhile, the rich inter-object relationship information already encoded in the multi-head self-attention weights of the DETR decoder has been largely neglected as a direct source for relation prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242054/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and produces a complete scene graph: N=200 object candidates each with a category label and 4D bounding box, plus an N×N×|C_p| relation tensor where each (i,j,k) entry is a sigmoid score for predicate k between object i (subject) and object j (object). At inference, final triplet scores are computed by multiplying the predicate score with the subject and object class scores, zeroing self-connections, and further gating by a connectivity score to filter implausible pairs.

## Method

EGTR extracts relations directly from the DETR decoder's self-attention by-products: at each layer l, the attention queries Q^l and keys K^l are linearly projected and pairwise concatenated to form relation representations R_a^l, which are combined with the final hidden-state representations R_z via a learned gated sum across all L layers before a shallow 3-layer MLP predicts the relation graph. No separate triplet queries or triplet detector are introduced. For training, the paper proposes adaptive smoothing that scales each ground-truth relation label by (1−u_i)(1−u_j), where u_i is derived from the bipartite matching cost, creating a continuous curriculum that defers relation learning until object detection is sufficiently reliable. A connectivity prediction auxiliary task (binary: does any relation exist between the pair?) is added to aid relation extraction, and hard negative/non-matching sampling addresses the extreme sparsity of the N×N ground-truth graph.

## Results

On Visual Genome (SGDet, graph-constraint), EGTR achieves the highest object detection AP50 of 30.8 among all one-stage models, with R@50=30.2 and mR@50=7.9, using only 42.5M parameters and 14.7 FPS — the fewest parameters and fastest inference among all compared one-stage SGG models. With logit adjustment (τ=0.7), EGTR reaches mR@50=17.8 and mR@100=21.7, matching or exceeding the state-of-the-art SSR-CNN with logit adjustment (mR@50=17.9, mR@100=21.4). On Open Image V6, EGTR scores 48.6 (micro-R@50=75.0, wmAPrel=42.0, wmAPphr=41.9), competitive with SSR-CNN's 49.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGTR achieves the highest AP50 (30.8) among all one-stage SGG models, surpassing Iterative SGG (27.7†), FCSGG (28.5), and SSR-CNN (23.8†) by a wide margin, demonstrating superior detection of objects without relations. In terms of efficiency, EGTR uses only 42.5M parameters (vs. 93.5M for Iterative SGG, 117.1M for SGTR, 274.3M for SSR-CNN) and runs at 14.7 FPS (vs. 4.0–13.4 FPS for competitors). On triplet detection, SSR-CNN leads with R@50=32.7 and mR@50=8.4, slightly ahead of EGTR's 30.2 and 7.9; however, with logit adjustment, EGTR (τ=0.7) reaches mR@50=17.8 and mR@100=21.7, matching SSR-CNN LA (τ=0.3) at 17.9 and 21.4 while using 6.5× fewer parameters. EGTR clearly outperforms Relationformer (R@50=28.4, mR@50=9.3), SGTR (R@50=24.6, mR@50=12.0), and FCSGG (R@50=21.3, mR@50=3.6) on both recall and mean recall. On Open Image V6, EGTR (score 48.6) trails SSR-CNN (49.4) by 0.8 points but leads on wmAPrel (42.0 vs. 41.5), confirming competitive cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

EGTR builds directly on DETR and Deformable DETR as its object detection backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior one-stage SGG models (RelTR, SGTR, SSR-CNN, Iterative SGG) by eliminating the need for separate triplet queries or triplet detectors. Instead of introducing new queries and complex cross-attention modules to fuse object and triplet information, EGTR repurposes the attention queries and keys already computed in the DETR decoder's multi-head self-attention layers as subject and object representations for relation prediction. The closest prior work is Relationformer, which also extracts relations from the object detector without a triplet detector, but EGTR extends this by leveraging attention by-products from all decoder layers rather than only the final hidden states, and introduces adaptive smoothing and connectivity prediction as training aids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: EGTR achieves comparable triplet detection to the state-of-the-art SSR-CNN with 6.5× fewer parameters and 3.7× faster inference, making scene graph generation substantially more deployable. The finding that self-attention by-products (Q and K) are more informative for relation extraction than the hidden states themselves challenges the assumption that only the final contextualized representations carry useful relational structure. Open questions include whether this attention-by-product strategy generalizes to non-DETR architectures or to denser/more complex scene graphs, and whether the fixed 200-query budget limits scalability to scenes with many interacting objects.
