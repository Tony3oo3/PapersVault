## Problem

Estimating relative camera pose from correspondences has required a two-step pipeline for over 40 years: first solving for the essential matrix, then disambiguating among four candidate poses via cheirality (positive-depth) checks. This paper demonstrates that the disambiguation step is unnecessary and can be eliminated by incorporating geometric validity constraints directly into the optimization, yielding a single-step, certifiably globally optimal solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241962/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n ≥ 5 pixel correspondences between two calibrated views, parameterized as unit bearing vectors (compatible with pinhole, fisheye, and omnidirectional cameras), and produces the essential matrix E, rotation R ∈ SO(3), unit translation t ∈ S², a boolean global-optimality certificate, and a pure-rotation detection flag. The translation is returned as a unit vector (scale-ambiguous), and the pure-rotation flag is derived from the slack variable s_t² in the optimization, eliminating the need for a separate posterior check.

## Method

C2P formulates relative pose estimation as a QCQP over the essential matrix E, translation t, and rotated translation q = R^T t, minimizing squared normalized epipolar errors subject to manifold and cheirality constraints. The key theoretical contribution is a novel characterization of the normalized essential manifold (Theorem 3.1): E ∈ M_E iff tr(EE^T) = 2 and Adj(E) = qt^T, which couples t and q without explicitly optimizing R. Cheirality is enforced via two quadratic constraints—a rotation disambiguation term (Ef₁)·([t]_×f₀) > 0 and a translation disambiguation term f₀^T t − f₁^T q ≥ 0—converted to equalities using slack variables and a homogenization variable. The QCQP is relaxed to an SDP via Shor's lifting, solved with SDPA, and tightness is certified by a rank condition (rank of the top-16 submatrix ∈ [1,3] with rank-1 sub-blocks). The rotation is then recovered in closed form as R = tq^T − ½([t]_×E + E[q]_×), and the dominant singular vector of the SDP solution is selected to satisfy the cheirality constraints.

## Results

C2P achieves certifiably globally optimal relative pose estimation without posterior disambiguation. In the small-correspondence regime (n ∈ [12, 30]), C2P and Garcia-Salguero et al. [23] both outperform Zhao [69] in rotation and translation accuracy, while C2P is faster. In the large-correspondence regime (n ∈ [10², 10⁴]), all methods achieve equivalent accuracy, but C2P and C2P-fast scale significantly better in runtime. For n > 10³ (typical of dense matchers), C2P achieves up to 4× and 35× speedups over the fastest and slowest competing methods, respectively, as the disambiguation step in prior work begins to dominate their runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zhao [69], C2P wins on accuracy in the small-n regime (R1) where redundant constraints help, and ties in accuracy in the large-n regime (R2). On runtime, C2P-fast matches [69]+M at low n but pulls ahead decisively for n > 10³, where the disambiguation step (triangulation or midpoint sign check) causes [69]'s runtime to grow with n while C2P's does not. Against Garcia-Salguero et al. [23], C2P ties on accuracy in both regimes (both outperform [69] in R1), but C2P is consistently faster, particularly at high correspondence counts where [23]'s larger constraint set increases SDP solve time. The key differentiator is that C2P eliminates the post-optimization disambiguation entirely, giving it a structural advantage that widens with n. All methods are certifiably globally optimal, so the comparison is purely on accuracy-at-equal-certification and runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QCQP-to-SDP relaxation framework for non-minimal relative pose estimation established by Briales et al. [10], Zhao [69], and Garcia-Salguero et al. [23], which all require a post-hoc disambiguation step to select the correct pose among four candidates satisfying the same epipolar geometry. The key departure is the incorporation of cheirality constraints (derived from Kneip et al. [37]) directly into the optimization, enabled by a novel necessary-and-sufficient characterization of the normalized essential manifold via the adjugate matrix (Theorem 3.1). This eliminates the 40-year-old two-step paradigm (estimate essential matrix, then disambiguate) that has been standard since Longuet-Higgins [42].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the disambiguation step, C2P achieves up to 4×–35× speedup over prior globally-optimal methods when the number of correspondences exceeds 10³, which is the regime typical of modern dense matchers (LoFTR, DKM, RoMa). The method also directly detects near-pure rotational motions via the slack variable s_t², removing another post-processing step. The main limitation is that C2P cannot handle outliers, which is explicitly left for future work; in practice this means it must still be embedded within RANSAC or GNC frameworks. The adjugate-matrix characterization of the essential manifold is a clean theoretical contribution that may find use beyond relative pose estimation.
