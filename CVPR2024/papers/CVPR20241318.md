## Problem

Prior multimodal social behavior research focuses on single-person behaviors or relies on holistic scene-level visual representations that are not aligned to individual utterances in multi-party settings, making it impossible to model fine-grained interaction dynamics among multiple people. This paper addresses this gap by introducing three new tasks—speaking target identification, pronoun coreference resolution, and mentioned player prediction—that explicitly require matching language references to specific individuals in a visual scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241318/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes approximately 3 seconds of video frames (0.4 s interval) from which AlphaPose extracts 17 body keypoints per player, along with a window of 11 utterances (target ± 5) where the target utterance contains a [MASK] token replacing the unknown player reference. All player names are anonymized to Player# labels, and positions are normalized relative to the speaker's nose point. The output is a single anonymized player identity (e.g., Player3) predicted via a softmax classification head over up to 6 players.

## Method

The proposed baseline establishes dense language-visual alignment by first matching AlphaPose tracking IDs to player references in transcripts, then encoding two visual streams: (1) speaker kinesics from 9 gaze/gesture-related keypoints normalized by the nose point and encoded via MLP, and (2) relative positions of all players (nose points offset from the speaker) encoded via a separate MLP with a speaker-label one-hot. These are fused through a transformer to produce time-resolved visual interaction features. In parallel, a pretrained language model (BERT/RoBERTa/ELECTRA) encodes the surrounding utterance window, extracting the [MASK]-position embedding as conversation context. The two streams are concatenated with an [AGG] token and processed by a multimodal transformer, whose [AGG] output feeds a classification head. A key training trick—player permutation learning—randomly shuffles the name-to-Player# mapping each iteration across both text and visual encodings, forcing the model to learn identity-agnostic interaction patterns.

## Results

On the speaking target identification task, the RoBERTa-based densely aligned baseline achieves 74.5% accuracy on YouTube and 66.5% on Ego4D, outperforming the language-only RoBERTa (72.4%/63.6%) by 2.1 and 2.9 points respectively. Across all three tasks (speaking target identification, pronoun coreference resolution, and mentioned player prediction) and both datasets, the proposed densely aligned multimodal baseline consistently outperforms language-only models and non-aligned multimodal baselines. The largest gains appear with BERT, where the proposed method improves YouTube accuracy by 6.9 points (65.8%→72.7%) for speaking target identification and 4.2 points (54.6%→58.8%) for mentioned player prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three categories of baselines: language-only models (BERT, RoBERTa, ELECTRA), non-aligned multimodal models combining language with DINOv2 or MViT visual features, and the prior social deduction game method by Lai et al. (Language Model + MViT). The proposed densely aligned baseline consistently outperforms all baselines across all three tasks and both datasets. For example, on speaking target identification (YouTube), the RoBERTa-based proposed method (74.5%) beats RoBERTa alone (72.4%), RoBERTa+DINOv2 (72.7%), and RoBERTa+MViT (73.1%). The non-aligned multimodal baselines (DINOv2, MViT) provide minimal or even negative gains over language-only models, confirming that holistic visual features without person-level alignment are insufficient for multi-party interaction tasks. The largest margins appear with BERT, where dense alignment adds 5.8–6.9 points over the best non-aligned multimodal variant, suggesting weaker language backbones benefit most from explicit visual grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior multimodal social behavior work (e.g., Lai et al. [28] on persuasion strategy prediction in Werewolf games) by replacing holistic scene-level visual features (MViT, DINOv2) with per-person pose tracking via AlphaPose, enabling dense alignment between individual player visuals and their language references. It also extends the social deduction game benchmarking line by introducing three referent-identification tasks that explicitly require multi-party spatial reasoning, rather than single-person behavior classification. The methodological lineage draws on standard transformer-based multimodal fusion and masked language models (BERT/RoBERTa/ELECTRA), but the key conceptual contribution is the alignment mechanism that synchronizes tracked player identities across visual and linguistic domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark to formalize fine-grained multi-party referent identification (addressee, pronoun, named entity) in video conversations with dense language-visual alignment, filling a gap between single-person gesture/gaze analysis and holistic scene understanding. The finding that holistic visual features (DINOv2, MViT) provide negligible gains over language-only baselines while per-person aligned features consistently improve performance validates the dense-alignment hypothesis. Limitations include restriction to social deduction games with at most 6 players, reliance on AlphaPose tracking accuracy, and third-person camera views only; open questions include generalization to larger groups, first-person/egocentric settings, and real-time deployment.
