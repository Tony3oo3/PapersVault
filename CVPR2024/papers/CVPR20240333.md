## Problem

Existing autonomous driving datasets are collected from a single vehicle's one-time pass of a location, lacking both multiagent interactions (multiple vehicles simultaneously present at the same place) and repeated traversals of the same area under varying conditions. This absence prevents research on collaborative perception, memory-based scene understanding, and multitraversal 3D reconstruction, which require synchronized multi-vehicle data or temporally diverse observations of identical scenes.

## Input / Output

Each vehicle provides 6 RGB cameras (3 narrow-angle at 720×464, 3 fisheye at 620×364, all 10Hz) covering 360°, a 128-channel LiDAR (360° horizontal, 40° vertical FoV at 10Hz), plus GPS and IMU. For VPR benchmarks, images are resized to 400×224 and point clouds downsampled to 1024 points; for reconstruction, 100 frames from the three front cameras plus LiDAR per traversal serve as input. Outputs include global descriptors for place matching (evaluated via Recall@K with a 20m spatial threshold) and rendered novel-view images with depth maps for reconstruction.

## Method

The core contribution is the MARS dataset, curated from May Mobility's fleet of four Toyota Sienna shuttles operating daily on a 20+ km route. Two subsets are extracted: a multitraversal subset (67 locations of 50m radius, 5,757 traversals totaling 1.4M+ frames across 26 days) capturing the same scene under different lighting, weather, and traffic; and a multiagent subset (53 scenes of 30 seconds where 2–3 vehicles remain within 50m for >9 seconds) providing synchronized overlapping sensor views. Benchmark experiments evaluate six VPR models (NetVLAD, MixVPR, GeM, ViT, PointNetVLAD, CoVPR) and six reconstruction models (iNGP, 3DGS, EmerNeRF, PVG, RobustNeRF, SegNeRF) on these subsets, establishing baselines for multitraversal place recognition and dynamic/environment neural reconstruction. The fleet's continuous real-world operation naturally produces hundreds of traversals per location and up to four simultaneous agents, setting records for both traversal and agent counts in existing driving datasets.

## Results

For visual place recognition (VPR) on the multitraversal subset, MixVPR achieves the best Recall@1 of 71.73%, outperforming NetVLAD (63.51%), PointNetVLAD (66.45%), GeM (61.00%), and Plain ViT (53.33%). In the multiagent setting, CoVPR achieves Recall@1 of 92.27%, marginally surpassing single-agent NetVLAD (91.85%). For single-traversal dynamic scene reconstruction, PVG attains the best SSIM (0.900) and LPIPS (0.197), while EmerNeRF leads in PSNR (29.63 dB). For multitraversal environment reconstruction, iNGP achieves the best overall fidelity (PSNR 26.04, SSIM 0.759, LPIPS 0.346), though it retains dynamic objects, whereas RobustNeRF performs worst (PSNR 16.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among VPR baselines on the multitraversal subset, MixVPR (Recall@1 71.73%) clearly outperforms NetVLAD (63.51%), GeM (61.00%), and Plain ViT (53.33%), with PointNetVLAD (66.45%) in between, confirming that feature-mixing pooling and point-cloud inputs both help over plain CNN or transformer backbones. In the multiagent setting, CoVPR (92.27%) edges out single-agent NetVLAD (91.85%) by ~0.4%, demonstrating a small but consistent collaborative gain. For single-traversal reconstruction, PVG dominates in structural fidelity (SSIM 0.900, LPIPS 0.197) over EmerNeRF (0.839, 0.237), iNGP (0.821, 0.256), and 3DGS (0.867, 0.235), while EmerNeRF leads in PSNR (29.63 vs. 29.28 for PVG). For multitraversal reconstruction, iNGP (PSNR 26.04) outperforms SegNeRF (24.44) and RobustNeRF (16.17) in overall fidelity, but RobustNeRF is specifically designed to suppress dynamic objects at the cost of static detail, and SegNeRF offers the best visual quality after shadow artifacts. Overall, no single method dominates all metrics, highlighting the open challenge of multitraversal reconstruction that the dataset is designed to address.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARS carves new ground in the autonomous driving dataset lineage by being the first to simultaneously provide multiagent, multitraversal, and multimodal recordings from a real-world fleet. Prior datasets address at most one of these dimensions: V2V4Real offers multiagent data but with only front/back cameras, Ithaca365 provides multitraversal data but with front-view cameras only, and mainstream datasets like nuScenes and Waymo cover multimodal surround-view but from single-vehicle, single-pass traversals. Rather than proposing a new method, the paper repurposes existing VPR models (NetVLAD, MixVPR, CoVPR, PointNetVLAD) and neural reconstruction approaches (iNGP, EmerNeRF, PVG, 3DGS, RobustNeRF) as benchmarks to characterize the new data regime and expose its challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARS fills a clear gap by enabling research on collaborative perception, memory-based (retrospective) perception, and multitraversal 3D reconstruction—directions that were previously infeasible with single-vehicle, single-pass datasets. The benchmark results reveal that existing methods transfer poorly to the multitraversal reconstruction setting: RobustNeRF's robust-loss approach to ignoring dynamic objects collapses (PSNR 16.17 vs. iNGP's 26.04), and semantic segmentation fails to remove car shadows, indicating that multitraversal environment reconstruction remains an open problem. The paper also highlights future directions—unsupervised perception with accumulated scene priors, neural simulation from multiagent/multitraversal data, and camera-only multitraversal 3D reconstruction—that are currently unaddressed. A limitation is the absence of dense annotations for online perception tasks (e.g., semantic occupancy), which the authors note as future work.
