## Problem

Diffusion models incur substantial computational cost due to their sequential denoising process, and existing compression methods (pruning, distillation, quantization) all require retraining, making them costly and impractical for large pre-trained models like Stable Diffusion. The paper asks how to significantly reduce per-step computational overhead at inference time without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240654/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is applied at inference time to pre-trained diffusion models (DDPM, LDM-4-G, Stable Diffusion v1.5), taking either a text prompt (Stable Diffusion at 512×512) or a class label (DDPM/LDM at 32×32 or 256×256) and producing a synthetic image. It is compatible with fast samplers (DDIM, PLMS) and operates as a drop-in modification to the denoising loop, requiring no changes to the model weights or training pipeline.

## Method

DeepCache exploits the temporal redundancy of high-level features across adjacent denoising steps by leveraging U-Net's skip-connection structure. At designated full-inference steps, the entire U-Net is executed and the high-level features from the main branch (output of up-sampling block U_{m+1}) are cached. At subsequent partial-inference steps, only the shallow downsampling block D_m is computed, and the cached features are retrieved and concatenated with D_m's output to feed U_m, bypassing all deeper blocks. This 1:1 caching extends to a 1:N strategy where cached features are reused for N−1 consecutive steps, and further refined by a non-uniform 1:N schedule that concentrates full-inference steps at timesteps where feature similarity drops (e.g., around 40% of the denoising process for LDM), yielding up to 7× speedup with minimal quality loss.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with LDM-4-G (250 DDIM steps), DeepCache achieves a 4.12× speedup (N=5) with FID 3.59 and sFID 5.16, compared to the unaccelerated LDM-4* baseline (FID 3.37, sFID 5.14). At a more aggressive 6.96× speedup (N=10), the non-uniform strategy yields FID 4.27 and sFID 5.42. On Stable Diffusion (50-step PLMS, 512×512), DeepCache achieves 2.15× speedup with CLIP Score 29.46 on PartiPrompts and 30.23 on COCO2017, versus 29.51 and 30.30 for the full 50-step PLMS. On CIFAR-10 (100 DDIM steps), N=2 gives FID 4.35 at 1.40× speedup, outperforming Diff-Pruning's FID 5.29 at 1.37×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diff-Pruning [10] (the main retraining-based baseline), DeepCache achieves substantially better FID at equal or higher speedup: at ~1.5× speed, Diff-Pruning yields FID 9.27 while DeepCache N=2 achieves FID 3.39 at 1.88× speed, and this gap widens at higher speedups. Against Spectral DPM [68], DeepCache N=5 (FID 3.59, 4.12×) dominates Spectral DPM (FID 10.60) by a large margin despite comparable MACs. On Stable Diffusion, DeepCache (CLIP 29.46, 2.15×) outperforms all three BK-SDM variants [23] (CLIP 28.88/27.94/27.36 at 1.49×/1.75×/1.81×) on both quality and speed. Compared to PLMS [35] under matched throughput, DeepCache achieves a slightly lower CLIP Score (29.46 vs 29.51 for 50-step PLMS) but at 2.15× speed, and when combined with 20-step PLMS the MSE to the original output decreases. Against DDIM [60] at the same throughput (Table 4), DeepCache matches or marginally improves FID (3.59 vs 3.59 at 0.733 throughput; 3.44 vs 3.46 at 0.471 throughput), confirming that the caching mechanism adds no quality penalty relative to simply reducing steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepCache carves out a distinct niche in diffusion model acceleration by exploiting temporal redundancy in U-Net features rather than reducing sampling steps (DDIM, PLMS, DPM-Solver) or permanently shrinking the model (pruning, distillation, quantization). It builds on the structural property of U-Net skip connections—where the main branch carries high-level features and the skip branch carries low-level features—to selectively cache the expensive main-branch computation while still updating the cheap skip-branch computation each step. Unlike Diff-Pruning or BK-SDM, which require retraining (even if reduced), DeepCache is entirely training-free and operates as a runtime inference strategy, making it directly applicable to frozen, large-scale pre-trained models like Stable Diffusion without any modification to weights or architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 2.3× speedup on Stable Diffusion with only a 0.05 CLIP score drop, and up to 7× on LDM-4-G, achieved with zero retraining cost, makes this immediately deployable for practitioners who cannot afford to retrain large diffusion models. The finding that DeepCache can match or slightly exceed DDIM/PLMS quality at the same throughput suggests that per-step computation reduction and step-count reduction are complementary rather than competing axes of acceleration. Key limitations include dependence on U-Net architecture (unclear applicability to transformer-based diffusion models like DiT), constrained speedup when the shallowest skip branch carries a large fraction of computation, and noticeable quality degradation at large caching intervals (N≥20). Open questions include whether analogous temporal redundancy exists in attention-based architectures and whether adaptive, content-aware caching schedules could push the speedup-quality tradeoff further.
