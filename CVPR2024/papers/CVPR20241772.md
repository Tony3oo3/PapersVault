## Problem

Deterministic precipitation nowcasting models produce increasingly blurry forecasts and underestimate high-value echoes because they ignore local stochastic variations, while probabilistic generative models capture realistic details but place rainfall in inaccurate positions because they model the entire system stochastically. The root cause is that chaotic precipitation evolution—coupled global motion trends and local particle growth/decay—is not properly decomposed in existing approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241772/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 5 initial radar echo frames at 128×128 spatial resolution (1 channel) and autoregressively predicts 20 future frames (10 for the CIKM dataset), with temporal resolution of 5–6 minutes per frame. The output is structured as a deterministic motion prediction μ plus a stochastically generated residual r̂, where the residual is produced segment-by-segment (segment length K) via iterative denoising conditioned on the previous segment and a global motion prior.

## Method

DiffCast decomposes precipitation evolution into a global deterministic motion trend and a local stochastic residual. Any deterministic backbone (SimVP, Earthformer, ConvGRU, MAU, PhyDnet) produces the motion prediction μ, and the residual r = y − μ is modeled by a temporal residual diffusion network (GTUnet) that predicts each residual segment autoregressively via a T-step denoising process. The GTUnet incorporates three multi-scale temporal conditions: a GlobalNet that extracts motion priors from μ, channel-concatenated previous-segment conditioning with position embeddings for segment-level consistency, and temporal attention blocks for inter-frame dependency within each segment. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a combined loss (weighted deterministic MSE + denoising noise-prediction loss), allowing the backbone and diffusion components to co-adapt rather than operating as a frozen predictor-corrector pair.

## Results

On SEVIR, DiffCast_SimVP achieves CSI 0.3077 (+15.59% over SimVP), CSI-pool16 0.5683 (+64.63%), and LPIPS 0.1812 (53.70% lower than SimVP's 0.3914). On MeteoNet, the CSI-pool16 improvement reaches 72.70% (0.7155 vs. 0.4143 for SimVP) and LPIPS drops by 65.99% (0.1198 vs. 0.3523). Across all four datasets, the framework yields 2–20% gains in CSI/HSS and 16.9–66.7% reductions in LPIPS over the corresponding deterministic backbone, while also surpassing all probabilistic baselines (MCVD, PreDiff, STRPM) on both accuracy and visual quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against deterministic baselines, DiffCast consistently improves every backbone: CSI gains range from +7.70% (PhyDnet) to +15.59% (SimVP), while CSI-pool16 gains are far larger at +41.42% to +89.23%, and LPIPS drops by 50–56%. Against probabilistic baselines, DiffCast_SimVP achieves CSI 0.3077 vs. MCVD 0.2148, PreDiff 0.2304, and STRPM 0.2512 on SEVIR, and CSI-pool16 0.5683 vs. 0.4706, 0.4028, and 0.4959 respectively. On visual quality (LPIPS), DiffCast_SimVP (0.1812) also outperforms all three probabilistic methods (0.2170, 0.2851, 0.2577), demonstrating that the residual-diffusion decomposition yields both higher positional accuracy and sharper appearance than modeling the entire system stochastically. The only metric where some DiffCast variants slightly underperform their backbone is SSIM (e.g., DiffCast_ConvGRU 0.6188 vs. ConvGRU 0.6532), but this is offset by large gains in all other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffCast sits at the intersection of two established lines of precipitation nowcasting research: deterministic spatio-temporal predictors (SimVP, Earthformer, ConvGRU, MAU, PhyDnet) that capture global motion but produce blurry, low-contrast forecasts, and probabilistic generative models (MCVD, PreDiff, STRPM) that yield realistic textures but lose positional accuracy. The key conceptual departure is a residual decomposition: rather than modeling the entire evolving system as either deterministic or fully stochastic, DiffCast assigns a deterministic backbone to predict the global motion trend and a conditional diffusion model (GTUnet) to predict only the local stochastic residual, trained end-to-end. This 'predictor-corrector' framing repurposes DDPM/DDIM machinery in a way that prior diffusion-based nowcasters (which denoise the full frame) do not, and the plug-and-play design means any existing deterministic architecture can serve as the backbone without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, drop-in upgrade path for the precipitation nowcasting community: any published deterministic backbone can be wrapped with the residual diffusion module to gain both accuracy and visual fidelity, as demonstrated across four datasets and five backbones. The ablation showing that removing the deterministic loss causes the model to degenerate into a pure stochastic generator (worse than the plain backbone) underscores that the decomposition itself—not merely the addition of diffusion—is the critical ingredient. Open questions include whether the residual-diffusion paradigm transfers to other chaotic spatio-temporal tasks (e.g., ocean currents, traffic), whether it scales to 3D volumetric radar or longer lead times beyond 100 minutes, and how the framework behaves under extreme-weather regimes where the deterministic/stochastic split may be less clean.
