## Problem

Existing parallel-inference ASD methods that process all frames in one pass for efficiency largely ignore multi-speaker interactions, while sequential-inference methods that model context are computationally expensive due to sliding-window strategies. The paper identifies that ASD benefits from two complementary but distinct contexts—long-term intra-speaker temporal dependencies and short-term inter-speaker interactions—that prior methods capture only partially or not at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241046/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes stacked visual face crops from S=3 speakers (target plus 2 context speakers) over T=200 frames, each resized to 112×112, along with a 4T×M audio Mel-spectrogram that is repeated S times to align with the speaker dimension. It produces a per-frame binary speaking-activity prediction for the target person across all T frames in a single parallel-inference pass, avoiding the sliding-window overhead of sequential methods.

## Method

LoCoNet interleaves two context-modeling modules within N stacked LSCM blocks: Long-term Intra-speaker Modeling (LIM) applies multi-head self-attention along the temporal dimension for long-range intra-speaker dependencies and dual audio-visual cross-attention for cross-modal interaction, while Short-term Inter-speaker Modeling (SIM) uses small s×k convolutional blocks to capture local inter-speaker patterns within a 7-frame receptive field. The visual encoder combines a 3D convolution, ResNet-18, and V-TCN; the audio encoder is a novel VGGFrame that modifies pretrained VGGish by removing temporal downsampling and adding a deconvolutional upsampling layer to yield per-frame audio features. Training is end-to-end with multi-supervision (shared FC heads at each LSCM block), and inference reuses speaker features to reduce computation by a factor of S.

## Results

On AVA-ActiveSpeaker, LoCoNet achieves 95.2% mAP and 98.0% AUC, surpassing the previous best end-to-end method Light-ASD (94.1% mAP) by 1.1% and the best multi-stage method SPELL+ (94.9% mAP) by 0.3% while using 38× fewer average-FLOPs (0.51G vs 19.6G). On Talkies, LoCoNet reaches 97.2% mAP (+2.7% over EASEE) under the AVA-pretrained + Talkies-finetuned setting. On Ego4D's Audio-Visual benchmark, LoCoNet achieves 68.4% mAP, outperforming the challenge winner by 7.7% and TalkNet by 16.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoCoNet outperforms all 11 compared baselines across all three datasets. On AVA-ActiveSpeaker, it beats the strongest multi-stage method SPELL+ [48] by 0.3% mAP (95.2 vs 94.9) while requiring 38× fewer FLOPs and ~32% fewer parameters, and surpasses the best end-to-end competitors Light-ASD [45] and EASEE [3] (both 94.1% mAP) by 1.1%. On Talkies, LoCoNet leads EASEE [3] by 2.7% mAP (97.2 vs 94.5) and Light-ASD [45] by 2.2% (96.1 vs 93.9) when trained on Talkies alone. On Ego4D, LoCoNet (68.4% mAP) exceeds the challenge winner [47] by 7.7% and TalkNet [65] by 16.7%, demonstrating strong generalizability to egocentric video. In the challenging multi-speaker scenario (3 visible faces), LoCoNet gains +3.0% mAP over the previous best (Light-ASD), and for small faces (<64 px) it achieves 77.8% mAP vs 77.5% for Light-ASD, confirming the benefit of inter-speaker context modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoCoNet positions itself at the intersection of two existing ASD paradigms: parallel-inference methods (TalkNet, ASD-Transformer, Light-ASD) that are fast but ignore multi-speaker interactions, and sequential-inference methods (ASC, MAAS, SPELL) that model context but require expensive sliding-window inference. The key architectural departure is the interleaved Long-Short Context Modeling (LSCM) design, where self-attention-based LIM captures long-range intra-speaker temporal dependencies (building on TalkNet's temporal modeling and ASC's self-attention) while convolution-based SIM captures short-range inter-speaker interactions — a combination not present in prior parallel-inference ASD methods. The VGGFrame audio encoder repurposes VGGish (Hershey et al., ICASSP 2017) pretrained on AudioSet by removing temporal downsampling and adding a deconvolutional layer, addressing the common problem that pretrained audio classifiers produce overly downsampled features unsuitable for per-frame classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving state-of-the-art ASD accuracy (95.2% mAP on AVA-ActiveSpeaker) at 38× lower FLOPs than the previous best multi-stage method, making real-time multi-speaker detection more feasible for applications like speech diarization and human-robot interaction. The +7.7% mAP on Ego4D and +3.0% in multi-speaker scenarios demonstrate that the long-short context decomposition generalizes to challenging real-world settings. However, the method is limited to an 8-second context window, and the authors acknowledge that scaling to longer contexts and incorporating additional modalities (e.g., eye gaze) remain open problems. The VGGFrame technique of adapting pretrained audio classifiers for per-frame feature extraction may have utility beyond ASD in other audio-visual tasks.
