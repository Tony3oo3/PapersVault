## Problem

Non-parametric classifiers with fixed hyperspherical prototypes (e.g., ETF-based) improve metric space utilization but enforce a static label-to-prototype assignment, forcing all class pairs to have equal similarity regardless of semantic relatedness. This static mapping cannot capture inter-class dependencies and, in the ETF case, requires the metric space dimension to be at least c−1, limiting practical applicability. The paper asks: can the label-to-prototype assignment itself be optimized during training while keeping prototypes fixed?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240425/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN/STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a single class label per image. A key design detail is that the metric space dimension d can be set well below the number of classes c (e.g., d=50 for c=100), since the fixed equidistributed prototypes do not require d ≥ c−1 as ETF does. The pre-computed prototype matrix W depends only on d and c, so the same W is reused across different datasets with identical class counts and dimensions.

## Method

The method decouples classification into three subtasks: (1) pre-computing c equidistributed prototypes on S^{d−1} by minimizing a Gaussian-potential-kernel uniformity loss (independent of data and architecture), (2) solving a bipartite matching problem via the Hungarian algorithm to assign each class to its best-matching prototype based on momentum-averaged class representatives, and (3) training the backbone with a regression loss L_IPM that pulls each sample's feature toward its assigned prototype. The assignment A is re-solved every τ iterations (typically once per epoch), making it a dynamic optimization variable rather than a fixed mapping. This two-step procedure—alternating gradient descent on backbone parameters and combinatorial reassignment of labels to prototypes—requires no auxiliary information (unlike HPN's word embeddings) and generalizes ETF by removing the d ≥ c−1 constraint while converging to ETF structure when that constraint is satisfied.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-32, the proposed method achieves 66.22% top-1 accuracy at d=50, surpassing HPN (65.0%), PSC (62.1%), and Word2Vec (57.6%). On ImageNet-200, it reaches 46.85% at d=100, outperforming HPN (44.7%) by 2.15% and PSC (33.1%) by 13.75%. On ImageNet-1K, the method achieves 77.47% (ResNet-50), 79.63% (ResNet-101), and 77.26% (Swin-T), consistently outperforming PSC and prior non-parametric classifiers. Notably, the best performance is achieved when d < c, demonstrating superior metric space exploitation compared to methods requiring d ≥ c−1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and backbone tested. Against PSC (He et al., CVPR 2016), it gains +4.12% on CIFAR-100 (66.22 vs 62.1), +13.75% on ImageNet-200 (46.85 vs 33.1), and +0.96% on ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50 (77.47 vs 76.51). Against HPN (Mettes et al., CVPR 2022), which uses privileged word-embedding information for label-to-prototype assignment, the method achieves +1.22% on CIFAR-100 and +2.15% on ImageNet-200 while requiring no auxiliary information. Against ETF (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022), the method surpasses it by 0.88% on CIFAR-100 and 9.70% on ImageNet-200 (Table 2, at d=c/2 where ETF is infeasible). Against DNC (Wang et al., ICLR 2022) and Goto et al. (WACV 2024) on ImageNet-1K, the method leads by +0.98% (ResNet-50) and +1.36% (ResNet-101) respectively. Against Kasarla et al. (NeurIPS 2022), the advantage is +2.67% on ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50. The method also matches or exceeds ETF on long-tail tasks (CIFAR-10, SVHN, STL-10) when d > c, confirming convergence to ETF structure under that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of non-parametric classifier research that replaces the standard parametric softmax (PSC) with fixed prototypes, building directly on the ETF approach of Yang et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and the equidistributed hyperspherical prototypes of Mettes et al. (HPN, CVPR 2022). The key conceptual departure is making the label-to-prototype assignment a dynamic optimization variable during training rather than a static mapping, solved via bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) at each epoch. For prototype distribution, it adapts the Gaussian potential kernel uniformity loss from Wang & Isola (ICML 2020), originally designed for contrastive representation learning, to precompute a dataset- and architecture-independent prototype configuration. The paper positions itself as a generalization of ETF: when d ≥ c, the optimized prototypes converge to the ETF structure, but the dynamic assignment removes the d ≥ c−1 constraint that limits ETF's practical applicability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that fixed-prototype classifiers become viable at metric space dimensions well below the number of classes (d = c/2), which is critical for high-dimensional classification where ETF is inapplicable. The method also addresses long-tail classification without auxiliary information or loss reweighting, alleviating the 'minority collapse' problem inherent in learnable classifiers. Limitations include the O(c³) cost of the Hungarian algorithm (though negligible per epoch) and the fact that performance gains, while consistent across architectures and datasets, are moderate (1–2% on CIFAR-100, ~2% on ImageNet-200). An open question is how the dynamic assignment behaves at very large scale (e.g., 100k+ classes) where the matching cost and the diminishing returns of inter-class relationship modeling may shift the tradeoff.
