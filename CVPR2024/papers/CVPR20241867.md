## Problem

Text prompts alone cannot accurately specify the detailed appearance of a desired subject in video generation, as it is difficult to enumerate all visual attributes in text and models struggle to capture them faithfully. While image-prompt-based customization has been explored for text-to-image generation, extending this to video generation introduces the additional challenge of maintaining temporal consistency while preserving subject appearance across frames. VideoBooth is the first framework to address video generation with image prompts in a tuning-free, feed-forward manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single image prompt (a segmented subject image specifying appearance) and a text prompt (describing the scene, action, and context) as input, and produces a temporally consistent video clip containing the specified subject performing the described action. The image prompt is processed at multiple spatial scales corresponding to different U-Net stages, and the text prompt must contain a target subject token whose word embeddings are replaced by the image-derived embedding. Inference requires no per-subject fine-tuning—only feed-forward passes through the trained model.

## Method

VideoBooth embeds image prompts in a coarse-to-fine manner. At the coarse level, a pretrained CLIP image encoder extracts visual features that are mapped via trained MLP layers into the text embedding space, replacing the target subject's word embeddings in the cross-attention conditioning. At the fine level, the image prompt's VAE latent representation (with corresponding diffusion noise added) is injected as additional keys and values into cross-frame attention layers at multiple scales; the first frame's values are updated using the image prompt's K/V, and then the updated first frame values propagate to subsequent frames to maintain temporal consistency. The two modules are trained sequentially—first the coarse image encoder with cross-attention K/V projections, then the fine attention injection module—because joint training causes the encoder to learn degenerate representations. The framework is built on a pretrained text-to-video diffusion model with inflated 3D convolutions and cross-frame attention, requiring no weight updates at inference time.

## Results

On the VideoBooth test benchmark (650 image-text prompt pairs from WebVid), VideoBooth achieves the best image alignment scores: CLIP-Image 74.80 and DINO 65.10, outperforming all baselines. Text alignment (CLIP-Text) is comparable at 30.10, slightly below DreamBooth (30.69) but above ELITE (30.09) and Textual Inversion (29.97). A user study with 25 participants confirms VideoBooth is preferred on all three dimensions: image alignment, text alignment, and overall quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoBooth outperforms all three baselines on both image-alignment metrics (CLIP-Image and DINO). Against Textual Inversion, VideoBooth gains +5.0 on CLIP-Image and +19.8 on DINO, reflecting far superior preservation of subject appearance. Against DreamBooth, VideoBooth leads by +3.6 on CLIP-Image and +12.1 on DINO, though DreamBooth scores higher on CLIP-Text (30.69 vs. 30.10) — the authors note this is an artifact of DreamBooth inserting an extra token rather than replacing word embeddings, inflating text similarity without improving image fidelity. Against ELITE, VideoBooth improves CLIP-Image by +1.0 and DINO by +6.1, with a marginal CLIP-Text gain. In the user study, VideoBooth is preferred by the majority of users across all three evaluation dimensions, confirming the quantitative advantage translates to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoBooth extends the line of encoder-based image customization methods (ELITE, Taming Encoder) from text-to-image to text-to-video generation, adapting the CLIP-image-to-text-embedding mapping to a video diffusion backbone. The key architectural departure is the fine-level attention injection, where multi-scale latent representations of the image prompt are appended as additional keys and values in cross-frame attention layers—a mechanism absent from prior image-customization work. The coarse-to-fine training strategy (train the image encoder before the attention injection module) is a novel design choice motivated by the observation that joint training causes the encoder to collapse into meaningless representations. Unlike prior video-personalization attempts (Make-A-Protagonist, VideoDreamer) that require per-subject fine-tuning, VideoBooth operates entirely in a feed-forward, tuning-free manner at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first tuning-free framework for video generation conditioned on a single image prompt, making subject-specified video creation practical without per-subject optimization. The coarse-to-fine decomposition (high-level semantics via encoder + spatial details via attention injection) offers a reusable design pattern for injecting visual references into generative models. However, the model is limited to image prompts with views similar to the target video (e.g., a back-view prompt cannot produce a front-view video), and the dataset covers only a narrow set of moving-object categories. Open questions include generalization to multi-subject scenes, arbitrary viewpoint changes, and whether the attention injection mechanism transfers to newer video diffusion architectures.
