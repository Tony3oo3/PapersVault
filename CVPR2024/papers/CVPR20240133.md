## Problem

Slot attention and most object-centric learning methods require a predefined number of slots K, a dataset-dependent hyperparameter that must be set before training. This is problematic because real scenes contain variable object counts, so a fixed K inevitably causes under-segmentation (too few slots) or over-segmentation (too many slots), and the optimal K is unknown without ground-truth annotations. The paper asks whether the slot number can be determined adaptively per instance based on scene complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, encodes it with a frozen DINO ViT/B-16 backbone into a feature map, and produces per-object segmentation masks, slot representations, and a binary keep/drop vector Z that determines how many of the K_max candidate slots are actually used for reconstruction. For toy datasets (CLEVR10) the reconstruction target is raw RGB pixels, while for MOVi-C/E and COCO it is DINO features, following the DINOSAUR protocol. K_max is set to 11, 24, and 33 for MOVi-C, MOVi-E, and COCO respectively, and the model learns to retain a variable subset of these.

## Method

AdaSlot first generates K_max slots via standard slot attention, then applies a lightweight two-layer MLP to each slot to predict a keep/drop probability. To make the discrete selection differentiable and computationally tractable, the paper uses a mean-field factorization that decomposes the 2^K subset selection into K independent binary decisions, each resolved with Gumbel-Softmax and Straight-Through Estimation. A masked slot decoder suppresses dropped slots by zeroing their alpha masks before the softmax normalization (rather than zeroing slot representations), which fully removes their contribution to the mixture. A complexity-aware regularization term L_reg = E[Σ Z_i] penalizes retaining too many slots, and the overall objective balances reconstruction loss against this sparsity penalty via a hyperparameter λ.

## Results

On MOVi-C, AdaSlot achieves ARI 75.59, F1 84.25, mBO 35.64, and CorLoc 76.80, outperforming the best fixed-slot DINOSAUR (K=6: ARI 73.23, F1 82.56, mBO 33.85, CorLoc 73.86) across all metrics. On MOVi-E, AdaSlot reaches ARI 76.73, F1 81.42, and CorLoc 91.03, surpassing the best DINOSAUR variant (K=9: ARI 76.01, F1 81.16, CorLoc 87.45). On the real-world COCO dataset, AdaSlot (ARI 39.00, mBO 27.36) is marginally below the best fixed-slot DINOSAUR (K=6: ARI 41.89, mBO 27.46) but dramatically outperforms the K=33 model (ARI 20.83, mBO 24.09), demonstrating that adaptive slot selection mitigates the severe degradation caused by over-allocation on complex scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOSAUR (fixed-slot slot attention with DINO features), AdaSlot wins on MOVi-C and MOVi-E across all five object-grouping metrics (ARI, F1, Purity, AMI, NMI) and both localization metrics (mBO, CorLoc). On MOVi-E, AdaSlot achieves CorLoc 91.03 vs. 90.02 for the best DINOSAUR (K=13) and ARI 76.73 vs. 76.01 for K=9, while also maintaining higher recall (80.31 vs. 87.83 for K=9 but 80.31 vs. 68.36 for K=18), effectively balancing precision and recall that no single fixed K can achieve. On COCO, the best fixed-slot DINOSAUR (K=6) slightly edges out AdaSlot on ARI (41.89 vs. 39.00) and F1 (70.66 vs. 68.37), but AdaSlot dramatically outperforms the K=33 model (ARI 20.83) by ~18 points, showing that the adaptive mechanism prevents the catastrophic over-segmentation that fixed large-K models suffer on cluttered real-world scenes. Against GENESIS-V2 (variable-slot via stochastic stick-breaking), AdaSlot wins by a large margin on every dataset: on MOVi-C, ARI 75.59 vs. 39.65 (K=6) and 26.63 (K=11); on MOVi-E, ARI 76.73 vs. 48.19 (K=9); on COCO, ARI 39.00 vs. 25.39 (K=6). The gap is particularly stark in localization (CorLoc 76.80 vs. 1.29 on MOVi-C), indicating GENESIS-V2's clustering approach struggles with spatial precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) by replacing its fixed slot count with a learned, instance-adaptive selection mechanism, while adopting DINOSAUR's (Seitzer et al., 2022) DINO feature-reconstruction pipeline as the base architecture. The closest prior work on variable slot numbers is GENESIS-V2, which uses a stochastic stick-breaking process to output a variable number of objects; AdaSlot instead frames the problem as a differentiable subset-selection task solved via Gumbel-Softmax under a mean-field factorization, paired with a complexity-aware sparsity regularizer. The masked slot decoder (zero-mask strategy) is a novel architectural component that cleanly suppresses unselected slots during mixture decoding, addressing a gap not addressed by prior variable-slot methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the need to predefine slot count eliminates a dataset-dependent hyperparameter that is particularly problematic for in-the-wild data where object-count statistics are unknown, making object-centric learning more practical and generalizable. However, the paper acknowledges that on complex real-world scenes (COCO) with incomplete annotations, cluttered compositions, and part-whole hierarchies, the adaptive mechanism does not yet clearly surpass the best fixed-slot configuration, and performance on small dense objects remains limited. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, 3D scenes, or compositional generation, and whether the mean-field independence assumption (which ignores inter-slot relations) becomes a bottleneck at higher slot counts.
