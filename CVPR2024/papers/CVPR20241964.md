## Problem

Existing sparse-IMU motion capture methods require tight on-body fixation (straps) for reliable sensing, which is uncomfortable for everyday use. Loose-wear clothing introduces secondary motion between skin and garment that corrupts IMU readings, but collecting diverse real-world loose-wear data across body shapes, poses, and wearing styles is impractical, creating a critical training-data scarcity for pose estimation networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241964/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes 30 Hz readings from four Xsens MTI-3 IMUs (left forearm, right forearm, back, waist) embedded in a loose polyester jacket, producing a 36-dimensional vector per frame (3-axis acceleration + 6D rotation per IMU, normalized relative to the waist IMU). The two-stage LSTM pipeline outputs 3D positions and rotation angles for 10 upper-body joints; notably, pelvis rotation is estimated separately rather than read directly from the waist IMU, since loose coupling decouples garment orientation from body orientation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Secondary Motion AutoEncoder (SeMo-AE), which synthesizes diverse loose-wear IMU signals from tight-wear IMU signals by modeling secondary motion as additive Gaussian noise in a learned latent space. Two key techniques enable this: (1) noise-guided latent space learning, where the encoder is trained so that E(ŷ)−E(y) follows a Gaussian distribution (enforced via reparameterization + MMD loss with batch normalization to avoid over-constraining the solution space), and (2) a temporal coherence scheme that linearly interpolates noise vectors across n=128 frames to capture the smooth temporal evolution of secondary motion and reduce jitter. SeMo-AE is trained on paired simulated loose/tight IMU data generated via TailorNet on AMASS, then concatenated with a two-stage LSTM pose estimator that is trained on the SeMo-AE's on-demand synthetic data, with a controllable looseness parameter (η=2) to extrapolate beyond the training distribution.

## Results

On the real-world loose-wear testing dataset (5 subjects, 212,496 frames at 30 fps), Loose Inertial Poser achieves a mean angular error of 19.83° ± 6.79° and a mean positional error of 10.58 cm ± 6.24 cm across all subjects and wearing styles. This represents a clear improvement over the best SOTA baseline (PIP) of 22.28° ± 6.28° angular error and 15.74 cm ± 7.80 cm positional error. The method maintains sub-20° mean joint rotation error even in the more challenging unzipped condition (20.98°), where all baselines degrade further.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Loose Inertial Poser outperforms all five SOTA sparse-IMU baselines (DIP, IMUPoser, TransPose, TIP, PIP) on both angular and positional error across every subject and wearing style. The largest gap is against DIP (26.38° vs. 19.83° angular error, a 6.55° improvement) and IMUPoser (17.78 cm vs. 10.58 cm positional error, a 7.2 cm improvement). Even the strongest baseline, PIP, which incorporates physics-aware optimization, is outperformed by 2.45° in angular error and 5.16 cm in positional error. The paper notes that PIP's physics-aware motion optimizer produces abnormal joint position estimates under loose-wear conditions, further widening the gap. In the ablation, replacing SeMo-AE with a cGAN for synthetic data generation degrades angular error to 25.06° (vs. 19.83°), confirming that the noise-guided latent space learning is critical for generating diverse and realistic secondary motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse IMU pose estimation line (DIP, TransPose, TIP, PIP) by addressing a setting those methods explicitly exclude: IMUs mounted on loose-wear clothing where secondary motion between garment and skin corrupts sensor readings. The pose estimation backbone follows TransPose's two-stage LSTM pipeline, but the core novelty is the SeMo-AE data generator, which repurposes the autoencoder + reparameterization + MMD framework (borrowed from VAE and domain-adaptation literature) to synthesize diverse loose-wear IMU signals from a small simulation dataset. Conceptually, it departs from prior work by treating secondary motion as learnable additive Gaussian noise in a *learned* latent space rather than as an unmodelled disturbance, and by using TailorNet-based clothing simulation to produce the limited paired training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: motion capture becomes viable in everyday clothing without straps, opening doors to long-term health monitoring and ubiquitous body-centric interaction that tight-wear systems preclude. However, several limitations temper the impact: only a single garment was produced (no size/fabric variation study), the jacket is not washable, arm-twisting rotation is poorly captured, and the simulation covers limited wearing styles (zipped/unzipped only), with the unzipped case showing noticeably higher error. Open questions include whether the SeMo-AE approach generalises to full-body capture, different garment types, and whether the data-scarcity bottleneck can be further allevated as more diverse simulation or real-world data becomes available.
