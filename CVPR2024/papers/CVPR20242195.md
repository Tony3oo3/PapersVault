## Problem

Existing generalizable 6D pose estimation methods require either a CAD model, a video sequence, or multiple reference views of the novel object at test time, making them inapplicable when the object is not physically available. This paper introduces the open-vocabulary setting where the object of interest is specified solely by a free-form text prompt, and the pose must be estimated across two different scenes with no shared background.

## Input / Output

The method takes two full-scene RGBD image pairs (anchor and query, no prior cropping or detection) and a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'brown open laptop') as input. It outputs binary segmentation masks localizing the prompted object in each scene and the relative 6D pose T_A→Q between the two viewpoints. The internal feature maps operate at 192×192 resolution with 32-dimensional descriptors, and depth maps are required for 3D back-projection before registration.

## Method

Oryon uses a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 to encode both images and N text prompt templates into a shared feature space. A text-visual fusion module computes a cost volume via cosine similarity between prompt features and each spatial location in the visual feature maps, then refines it with two Swin Transformer-based cost aggregation layers (intra-window and shifted-window self-attention) enriched by guidance features from a pretrained Swin backbone. A three-stage upsampling decoder (also guided by Swin features) produces high-resolution 192×192 feature maps used jointly for matching and segmentation. Training combines a contrastive loss with hardest-negative mining on ground-truth correspondences and a Dice loss on segmentation masks, optimizing both objectives simultaneously. At inference, predicted masks filter the feature maps, nearest-neighbor matching yields 2D correspondences that are back-projected to 3D using depth and intrinsics, and PointDSC registration recovers the final relative pose while rejecting spurious matches.

## Results

On REAL275, Oryon with its own predicted masks achieves AR=32.2 and ADD(S)-0.1d=24.3, outperforming SIFT (AR=24.4, ADD=12.8) and ObjectMatch (AR=22.4, ADD=13.2). On Toyota-Light, Oryon reaches AR=30.3 and ADD=20.9, substantially exceeding ObjectMatch (AR=8.3, ADD=3.8) and SIFT (AR=27.2, ADD=9.9). The segmentation quality (mIoU) of Oryon's own masks is 66.5 on REAL275 and 68.1 on TOYL, compared to 56.4/75.5 for OVSeg. With oracle masks, Oryon achieves AR=46.5 on REAL275 and AR=34.1 on TOYL, indicating that segmentation errors account for a portion of the remaining gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Oryon outperforms both baselines on both datasets across all metrics. Against SIFT, Oryon gains +7.8 AR and +11.1 ADD on REAL275, and +3.1 AR and +11.0 ADD on Toyota-Light; the smaller AR gap on TOYL is attributed to SIFT's scale-invariant design handling the large apparent-size variation in that dataset. Against ObjectMatch, Oryon's advantage is more pronounced: +9.8 AR and +11.1 ADD on REAL275, and a substantial +22.0 AR and +17.1 ADD on Toyota-Light, where ObjectMatch's sensitivity to domain shift (different lighting conditions between scene pairs) causes severe degradation. The paper notes that ObjectMatch's reliance on SuperGlue for matching makes it particularly vulnerable to the cross-scene lighting variations present in TOYL, whereas Oryon's learned features are more robust to such shifts. Oryon's own segmentation masks (mIoU 66.5/68.1) also outperform OVSeg (56.4/75.5) on REAL275, contributing to the overall pose estimation advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Oryon departs from the dominant paradigm in generalizable 6D pose estimation, where methods like Gen6D, OnePose, and MegaPose require a CAD model or a video sequence of the novel object at test time. Instead, it repurposes the CLIP vision-language alignment framework and the cost-aggregation strategy from Cat-Seg to fuse a free-form textual prompt with local visual features, jointly performing open-vocabulary segmentation and keypoint matching. The final registration step relies on PointDSC, a robust point-cloud registration method, rather than a learned pose head, positioning the work at the intersection of vision-language models, open-vocabulary segmentation, and point cloud registration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing the need for CAD models or multi-view video with a simple text prompt, Oryon opens a practical pathway for 6D pose estimation in settings where the object is not physically available for onboarding (e.g., remote manipulation, AR). The new cross-scene benchmark on REAL275 and Toyota-Light is a useful resource, and the ablation showing that pose quality plateaus around 0.7 mIoU suggests the registration step is forgiving of segmentation errors. Key limitations remain: the method requires depth maps, and the prompt ablation reveals that generic or missing object names cause segmentation to collapse entirely (mIoU drops from 66.5 to 3.0), making the system brittle to imprecise user descriptions. Extending the approach to RGB-only inputs via depth prediction is identified as a natural next step.
