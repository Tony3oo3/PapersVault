## Problem

Intraoperative 2D/3D registration of portable X-rays to preoperative CT scans is critical for image-guided surgery, yet existing methods fail in practice: gradient-free optimization is prohibitively slow and trapped in local minima, supervised pose regressors cannot generalize to new patients without impractically large labeled datasets, and landmark-based approaches require expert annotation for every new anatomy. No prior method achieves consistent sub-millimeter accuracy across patients and surgical domains without manual supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single intraoperative 2D X-ray (preprocessed from raw attenuation to absorption via log-transform, cropped to 256×256) and a preoperative 3D CT volume of linear attenuation coefficients. It outputs a 6-DOF rigid camera pose T ∈ SE(3) (rotation and translation) expressing the X-ray scanner's position relative to the CT. Notably, the method is patient-specific: a CNN is trained from scratch on ~1M synthetic X-rays rendered from that patient's own CT before any real image is processed, and the final pose is refined by 250 iterations of gradient-based test-time optimization.

## Method

DiffPose is a two-stage self-supervised framework. First, a ResNet18 encoder is pretrained to regress camera poses by rendering synthetic X-rays from the patient's CT at random poses sampled via the exponential map from se(3), trained with a composite loss combining sparse multiscale NCC (averaging global and local patch correlations) and two geodesic losses on SE(3). Second, at test time, the encoder's initial pose estimate is refined by differentiable X-ray rendering (DiffDRR), where gradient-based optimization in the se(3) tangent space maximizes sparse multiscale NCC between the real and rendered X-rays. Key design choices include parameterizing poses jointly in the Lie algebra se(3) rather than separate rotation/translation components, using sparse patch-based rendering (100 patches of size 13) to accelerate the similarity computation, and sampling training poses as random perturbations from the patient's isocenter to cover all clinically relevant views.

## Results

On the DeepFluoro pelvic dataset (6 subjects, 366 X-rays), DiffPose achieves 87% sub-millimeter success rate (SMSR) with a median mTRE of 0.9±2.8 mm at 2.2±1.2 s per registration. On the Ljubljana neurovascular dataset (10 patients, 20 X-rays), DiffPose achieves 85% SMSR with a median mTRE of 0.2±10.6 mm, compared to 25% SMSR and 263.7±193.9 mm for DiffDRR initialized at the PA pose. The encoder alone (without test-time optimization) achieves only 1% SMSR, highlighting the critical role of differentiable refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPose (87% SMSR) outperforms all five baselines on DeepFluoro. The closest competitor, PnP-Regularizer [25], achieves 69% SMSR but shows high inter-subject variability (median mTRE ~10 mm on Subjects 3, 4, and 6 where DiffPose remains sub-millimeter). The unsupervised intensity-based methods—xReg [24] (20% SMSR) and DiffDRR [22] (37% SMSR)—both fail to reach surgical viability when initialized at the PA pose, confirming that test-time optimization alone is insufficient. The landmark-based PnP [25] (1% SMSR) and supervised PoseNet [29] (0% SMSR) are essentially non-functional on this dataset due to limited training data and out-of-distribution failures. On the Ljubljana neurovascular dataset, DiffPose (85% SMSR, 0.2 mm median mTRE) dramatically outperforms DiffDRR (25% SMSR, 263.7 mm median mTRE), demonstrating generalizability across surgical domains without any hyperparameter changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffPose builds directly on DiffDRR [22], a differentiable X-ray renderer previously demonstrated only on synthetic data, and extends it into a full clinical registration pipeline. It departs from the dominant paradigm of landmark-based PnP registration (which requires expert annotation per patient) and supervised pose regression (which suffers from memorization and fails to generalize) by introducing patient-specific self-supervised training on unlimited synthetic X-rays rendered from the patient's own CT. The paper also introduces a conceptual shift in optimization: performing test-time refinement in the Lie algebra se(3) with geodesic and multiscale NCC losses, rather than in separate rotation/translation parameterizations or with gradient-free search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffPose is the first 2D/3D registration framework to achieve consistent sub-millimeter accuracy without any manual landmark annotation, outperforming fully supervised baselines (87% vs 69% SMSR) while remaining clinically deployable at ~2 seconds per image. Key limitations include the ~12-hour per-patient pretraining (impractical for emergency surgery) and the rigid-body assumption, which precludes deformable anatomy. Open questions include whether meta-learning (e.g., MAML) can reduce pretraining to minutes, whether piecewise-rigid extensions can handle deformable structures, and whether the se(3) optimization insight generalizes to other registration problems beyond X-ray/CT.
