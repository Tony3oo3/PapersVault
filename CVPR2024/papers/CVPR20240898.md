## Problem

Seven distinct methods for constructing 1-Lipschitz convolutional layers have been proposed in the literature, each with very different computational profiles in training time, memory usage, and inference overhead. No prior work provides a unified comparison across these practical dimensions, making it difficult for practitioners to select the best method given their hardware constraints and latency requirements. This paper fills that gap with both a theoretical complexity analysis and controlled empirical measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240898/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All methods consume standard RGB images at resolutions matching their respective datasets (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, 224×224 for Imagenette) and output a class label along with a certified robustness margin computed as the gap between the top two class scores. The certified robust accuracy is evaluated at a fixed radius ε = 36/255, where an input is certified robust if this margin exceeds ε√2. Because the networks are 1-Lipschitz by construction, a single forward pass at inference suffices to certify robustness, unlike randomized smoothing which requires up to 100k evaluations.

## Method

The paper implements and benchmarks seven 1-Lipschitz layer parameterizations—BCOP, Cayley, SOC, AOL, LOT, CPL, and SLL—within an identical CNN architecture using MaxMin activations and Lipschitz-margin training loss. A theoretical analysis derives the MACS and memory complexity of each method's input and parameter transformations, revealing that methods like Cayley and LOT scale as O(s²c³) while AOL and SLL scale as O(c³k⁴), and that SOC's multiple convolutions make it the most expensive at inference. Empirically, all methods are trained under matched time budgets (2h, 10h, 24h on a single A100 GPU) across four model sizes (1.5M–100M parameters) and four datasets, with hyperparameters tuned via random search on a validation split. The key finding is that CPL offers the best trade-off of accuracy and resource efficiency, SOC achieves the highest certified robust accuracy when compute is abundant, and AOL/BCOP are preferable when inference latency is critical since they add no overhead beyond a standard convolution.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (24h training, M model size), SOC achieves the highest certified robust accuracy at 66.3% (ε=36/255), followed closely by CPL at 65.1%. On CIFAR-100, SOC leads with 34.9% robust accuracy, while on Tiny ImageNet, LOT achieves the best result at 21.9% (S size). On Imagenette, CPL clearly dominates with 82.4% robust accuracy (M size), outperforming all other methods by at least 2.3 points. The paper concludes that CPL offers the best overall trade-off between certified robustness, training time, and memory usage, while SOC is preferred when computational resources are abundant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOC [36] achieves the highest certified robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 (66.3%) and CIFAR-100 (34.9%) at M size, but requires ~3× the training time of a standard convolution due to 5 sequential convolutions. CPL [31] is the strongest overall performer, ranking top-2 on all four datasets and clearly dominating on Imagenette (82.4% vs. 80.1% for BCOP), while requiring only 2 convolutions per layer. LOT [46] achieves the best Tiny ImageNet robust accuracy (21.9% at S size) but is too memory-hungry to train at L size on an 80GB GPU. AOL [34] and BCOP [27] have zero inference overhead (standard convolution speed) but trail the top methods by 3–6 points in robust accuracy on CIFAR-10. SLL [3] shows strong class-count sensitivity, performing well on CIFAR-10 (62.8%) but poorly on CIFAR-100 (29.9%). Cayley [40] is consistently outperformed by all other methods on robust accuracy across every dataset, despite having moderate training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the 1-Lipschitz neural network literature, which has produced several independent parameterization strategies (BCOP, Cayley, SOC, AOL, LOT, CPL, SLL) over the past few years, each proposed to solve the hard problem of constructing orthogonal or near-orthogonal Jacobians for convolutional layers without explicit Jacobian computation. Prior work has evaluated these methods in isolation, typically reporting certified robust accuracy under different architectures, training budgets, and hyperparameter choices, making cross-method comparison unreliable. This paper departs from that fragmented evaluation by imposing a unified protocol—identical architecture, loss, optimizer, and wall-clock training budget—across all seven methods, and additionally provides a theoretical complexity and memory analysis (Table 1) that no prior paper had consolidated. It also positions 1-Lipschitz networks within the broader certifiable-robustness landscape (verification, randomized smoothing) to clarify the trade-off of single-pass inference guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, multi-metric comparison that practitioners can use to select a 1-Lipschitz construction method based on their actual constraints (GPU memory, inference latency, training budget, target dataset resolution). The finding that CPL is the best all-around choice, while SOC edges ahead when compute is abundant and AOL/BCOP win when inference speed is critical, gives concrete decision rules that were previously scattered across incompatible experimental setups. Open questions include whether these rankings hold at ImageNet scale, for deeper or non-convolutional architectures, and whether the observed advantage of skip-connection-inherent layers (CPL, SLL, SOC) over purely orthogonal ones will persist as network depth increases. The practical observation that LOT-L exceeds 80 GB GPU memory also highlights that some theoretically elegant methods are simply infeasible on current hardware.
