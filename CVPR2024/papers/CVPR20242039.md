## Problem

Existing document image restoration systems treat dewarping, deshadowing, appearance enhancement, deblurring, and binarization as independent tasks, each requiring a separately designed and trained model. This fragmentation prevents multi-task learning synergies and creates complex, hard-to-maintain pipelines. Moreover, prior visual prompt approaches for unifying image-to-image tasks rely on ViT with fixed resolutions or require additional input/output pairs at inference, making them unsuitable for high-resolution, variable-size document images.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB document image and a task label as input. A DTSPrompt generator extracts task-specific prior features (e.g., document masks, background estimates, gradient maps) directly from the input image, and these are channel-concatenated with the original image to form a 6-channel tensor fed into the restoration network. Outputs are task-dependent: a restored RGB image for deshadowing/appearance enhancement/deblurring, a backward deformation map for dewarping, or a binary foreground mask for binarization. The architecture supports inputs up to 1600×1600 pixels and handles variable resolutions without architectural changes.

## Method

DocRes introduces Dynamic Task-Specific Prompts (DTSPrompt), where each task's prompt is constructed from distinct prior features extracted from the input image itself: document segmentation mask plus x/y coordinate maps for dewarping, a dilated-and-median-filtered background for deshadowing, the absolute difference between the image and its background for appearance enhancement, a triplicated gradient map for deblurring, and Sauvola binarization output plus threshold map plus gradient map for binarization. These prompts serve a dual role—identifying which task to perform and supplying task-relevant auxiliary information—while being inherently resolution-adaptive since they are derived from the input. The prompt is simply channel-concatenated with the input image and fed into an unmodified Restormer backbone, avoiding ViT's fixed-resolution constraint. Training uses supervised multi-task learning with equal sampling weights across five tasks, L1 loss (cross-entropy for binarization), and a 50K-step dewarping pre-training phase to stabilize the unified optimization.

## Results

DocRes achieves competitive or superior performance across all five document restoration tasks using a single unified model. On deblurring (TDD), DocRes attains SSIM 0.9723 and PSNR 27.35, surpassing both DocDiff (0.9559/24.00) and DE-GAN (0.9226/22.24). On appearance enhancement (RealDAE), DocRes reaches PSNR 24.65, exceeding GCDRNet (24.42), and on DocUNet* it outperforms UDoc-GAN by a large margin (SSIM 0.7598 vs. 0.6833). On binarization (DIBCO'18), DocRes achieves FM 89.82 and pFM 94.33, closely trailing the dedicated SOTA GDB (91.09/94.57) while substantially outperforming DE-GAN and DocDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On dewarping (DIR300), DocRes achieves the best AD score (0.241) but trails DocGeo [11] on MS-SSIM (0.6264 vs. 0.6380) and LD (6.83 vs. 6.40), while clearly outperforming Li et al. [23] on all three metrics. For deshadowing, DocRes surpasses DocShadow [25] on all three test sets (Jung, Kligler, OSR) in both SSIM and PSNR, but remains below BGSNet [71] on Kligler (SSIM 0.9005 vs. 0.9480) and OSR (PSNR 21.64 vs. 22.64), though it wins on Jung (SSIM 0.9089 vs. 0.9040, PSNR 23.02 vs. 17.34). On appearance enhancement, DocRes beats UDoc-GAN [59] by a wide margin on both DocUNet* and RealDAE, and edges out GCDRNet [70] on RealDAE PSNR (24.65 vs. 24.42) while slightly trailing on SSIM (0.9219 vs. 0.9423). On deblurring (TDD), DocRes decisively outperforms both DocDiff [61] (SSIM 0.9723 vs. 0.9559, PSNR 27.35 vs. 24.00) and DE-GAN [50] (SSIM 0.9723 vs. 0.9226, PSNR 27.35 vs. 22.24). For binarization (DIBCO'18), DocRes (FM 89.82, pFM 94.33, PSNR 19.35) narrowly trails the dedicated SOTA GDB [62] (91.09/94.57/19.92) but substantially outperforms both DE-GAN [50] and DocDiff [61] across all three metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: task-specific document image restoration (dewarping, deshadowing, appearance enhancement, deblurring, binarization, each with dedicated SOTA models) and the emerging line of all-in-one image restoration models that use visual prompts (ProRes, Painter, PromptIR). It departs from prior visual prompt methods in two key ways: rather than using learnable parameter matrices or input/output sample pairs as prompts, DTSPrompt extracts task-specific prior features (document masks, background estimates, gradient maps, Sauvola binarization) directly from the input image; and rather than being confined to ViT-based architectures with fixed resolutions, it plugs into CNN-based restoration networks like Restormer, enabling high and variable resolution inputs. The prior features themselves are repurposed from existing document restoration literature, where they were used as auxiliary inputs to task-specific models, here reinterpreted as the mechanism for task disambiguation in a unified model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DocRes demonstrates that a single model with a simple channel-concatenation fusion can match or exceed task-specific SOTA across five document restoration tasks, eliminating the need to maintain and deploy five separate models in a document AI pipeline. The generalization finding—that multi-task training on photographed-document tasks (dewarping, deshadowing, appearance enhancement) improves out-of-domain performance on binarization and deblurring of photographed documents—is practically significant for real-world deployment where inputs are rarely clean. Open questions include whether more sophisticated prompt fusion mechanisms could close the remaining gaps to task-specific SOTA (notably binarization), whether DTSPrompt extends naturally to additional restoration tasks (e.g., denoising, JPEG artifact removal), and whether the reliance on external models for prior feature extraction (document segmentation, Sauvola) introduces a practical dependency chain.
