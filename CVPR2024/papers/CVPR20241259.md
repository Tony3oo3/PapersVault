## Problem

Existing infrared-visible image fusion methods produce a single fixed output and cannot adapt to degraded source images (low light, noise, overexposure, low contrast) without manually switching between separate restoration models. They also lack interactivity, meaning users cannot specify desired fusion characteristics or task priorities. The paper asks whether a single model can simultaneously handle arbitrary degradations and produce user-customized fusion results through natural language guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241259/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a visible RGB image (H×W×3), a single-channel infrared image (H×W×1), and a free-form English text prompt describing the scene, degradation type, and/or desired fusion emphasis. Images are cropped to 96×96 during training. The text prompt is consumed by a frozen CLIP text encoder, so users can write natural-language descriptions (e.g., 'address low light degradation in the visible images') without any structured format. The output is a single fused infrared-visible image whose content, brightness, contrast, and color balance are modulated by the text semantics.

## Method

Text-IF couples a Restormer-based Transformer image fusion pipeline with a text interaction guidance architecture. The image pipeline uses separate Transformer encoders for visible and infrared inputs, a cross-attention layer that exchanges queries between modalities for spatial interaction, and a multi-stage decoder. The text side uses a frozen CLIP text encoder to extract semantic features, which are mapped through MLPs into per-layer scale (γ) and bias (β) parameters. These parameters modulate fusion features at each decoder stage via the Semantic Interaction Guidance Module (SIGM): F̂ = (1+γ)⊙F + β, enabling dense text-to-image coupling. The training loss combines intensity, SSIM, maximum gradient, and color consistency terms, with their weights dynamically regulated by the text semantics, so the same model handles all degradation types and task preferences without architectural changes.

## Results

Without text guidance, Text-IF achieves the best scores on all five metrics (SCD, SD, EN, VIF, Q^AB/F) on both MSRS and LLVIP datasets, with particularly large margins on VIF (1.046 vs. 0.726 for MetaFusion on MSRS; 1.011 vs. 0.833 for MetaFusion on LLVIP). On RoadScene, Text-IF leads on EN (7.332), VIF (0.739), and Q^AB/F (0.578) but trails DDFM on SCD (1.572 vs. 1.864). With text guidance on degraded images, Text-IF attains the best overall performance across MSRS, LLVIP, MFNet, DN-MSRS, and RoadScene, e.g., achieving the lowest NIQE (3.342) and BRISQUE (29.021) on RoadScene and the highest SD (43.933) and EN (6.683) on MFNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text-IF outperforms all seven baselines (UMF-CMGR, TarDAL, ReCoNet, MURF, U2Fusion, MetaFusion, DDFM) on every metric for MSRS and LLVIP without text guidance, with the largest gaps on VIF (1.046 vs. 0.726 MetaFusion on MSRS; 1.011 vs. 0.833 MetaFusion on LLVIP) and Q^AB/F (0.676 vs. 0.478 MetaFusion on MSRS). On RoadScene, Text-IF wins on four of five metrics but DDFM achieves a higher SCD (1.864 vs. 1.572), suggesting DDFM preserves slightly more contrast in that specific setting. When text guidance is applied to degraded images, Text-IF achieves the best or near-best scores across all five degradation datasets (low-light, low-contrast, noise, overexposure), e.g., the lowest NIQE of 3.342 and BRISQUE of 29.021 on RoadScene, and the highest SD of 43.933 on MFNet, while the best restoration-plus-fusion baselines (eir.+MetaFusion, eir.+DDFM) trail by 0.1–0.3 on NIQE and 2–8 points on SD. For downstream object detection on LLVIP, Text-IF attains the highest mAP@0.50 (0.941) and mAP@0.50:0.95 (0.602), surpassing the next-best MURF (0.926 / 0.599) and MetaFusion (0.916 / 0.590).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the text-guided image processing paradigm (CLIP, Style-CLIP, DiffusionCLIP) for the image fusion domain, where prior work has treated fusion as a fixed mapping F(I_vis, I_ir) with no user-controllable input. It extends the Restormer/Transformer-based fusion pipeline (building on U2Fusion's all-in-one philosophy and DDFM's diffusion-based approach) by inserting a Semantic Interaction Guidance Module that modulates fusion features via text-derived scale and bias parameters. The key conceptual departure is integrating degradation restoration directly into the fusion objective rather than requiring a separate restoration-then-fusion pipeline, and making loss hyperparameters functions of the text prompt rather than fixed constants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world IR-visible fusion routinely encounters degraded sources (low light, noise, overexposure), and current methods either ignore this or require manual switching between restoration models. The interactive text interface lowers the barrier for non-expert users to specify fusion preferences. However, the evaluation is limited to 96×96 crops on four datasets, the text guidance mechanism (frozen CLIP encoder → MLP → scale/bias) is relatively simple, and it remains unclear how well the approach generalizes to other fusion modalities (e.g., multi-focus, multi-exposure) or to more complex, open-ended text instructions. The 'open-set multi-point mapping' framing for loss selection is conceptually appealing but underdeveloped in practice.
