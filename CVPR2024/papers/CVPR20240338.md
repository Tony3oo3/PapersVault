## Problem

Sparsely-supervised monocular depth estimation methods trained on LIDAR data produce poorly localized depth edges (discontinuities), which severely degrades downstream applications like augmented reality occlusion and novel view synthesis. The core difficulty is that accurate depth edges ground truth is unavailable in sparse LIDAR-based outdoor scenes, since LIDAR measurements are themselves sparse near edges and suffer from alignment errors between RGB and LIDAR acquisition. This paper is the first to directly address depth edge localization in sparsely-supervised MDE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MDE network takes a single RGB image and produces a per-pixel depth map, while the auxiliary Depth Edge Estimation (DEE) network takes both an RGB image and the corresponding sparse LIDAR measurement and outputs a probabilistic depth edge map. The Edge Detection Block (EDB) within the MDE network computes a directional gradient of the predicted depth (perpendicular to the estimated edge direction) and converts it into a per-pixel edge probability via a sigmoid. All depth and edge metrics are evaluated in the bottom 60% of the image where LIDAR is typically available.

## Method

The method uses a three-stage training pipeline: (1) a DEE network is trained on the synthetic GTA-PreSIL dataset with dense depth GT (edges extracted via Canny) to predict depth edge probability maps from RGB+LIDAR input; (2) the trained DEE infers approximate depth edge labels on the real training set (KITTI or DDAD); (3) the MDE network is fine-tuned with an additional Edge Detection Block that computes the depth gradient in the direction perpendicular to the estimated edge (derived from DEE predictions) and an Edge Loss (Balanced Binary Cross Entropy) against the DEE-inferred labels. The total loss is a weighted sum of the standard multi-scale depth loss and the edge loss. The key insight is that MDE networks under-focus on edge localization because edges occupy a tiny image fraction and LIDAR is sparse near discontinuities, so explicit edge supervision—transferred from synthetic data—corrects this bias without requiring dense depth GT in the target domain.

## Results

On the KITTI-DE evaluation set, the proposed Edge Loss (EL) improves depth edge AUC by 30–40% relative across all three MDE baselines: Packnet-SAN+EL achieves 61.87% AUC vs. 47.56% for Packnet-SAN, AdaBins+EL reaches 53.47% vs. 41.23%, and PixelFormer+EL reaches 46.23% vs. 32.79%. Per-pixel depth accuracy (ARE) remains comparable, with Packnet-SAN+EL at 3.61% vs. 3.45% baseline and PixelFormer+EL at 2.94% vs. 3.00%. On DDAD-DE, Packnet-SAN+EL achieves 48.32% AUC vs. 31.52% for the baseline (~50% relative improvement) with ARE of 8.99% vs. 8.89%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three SOTA sparsely-supervised MDE baselines (Packnet-SAN, AdaBins, PixelFormer) and two depth-merging methods (BoostingDepth [29] and GradientFusion [11]). The proposed Edge Loss consistently wins on edge AUC across all baselines: Packnet-SAN+EL (61.87%) outperforms Packnet-SAN (47.56%) by 14.3 points, AdaBins+EL (53.47%) beats AdaBins (41.23%) by 12.2 points, and PixelFormer+EL (46.23%) surpasses PixelFormer (32.79%) by 13.4 points. Per-pixel ARE remains within ~0.2–0.5% of the baselines, confirming that edge improvement does not sacrifice depth accuracy. Against BoostingDepth, the proposed method achieves substantially better AUC (61.87% vs. 46.04% original / 36.19% KITTI-trained) and ARE (3.61% vs. 9.32% / 7.24%), as the merging approach degrades per-pixel accuracy due to its GAN-based training. GradientFusion also underperforms on both AUC (44.51%) and ARE (5.93%), likely because its self-supervised guided-filter training is sensitive to the low resolution of KITTI images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by addressing depth edge localization specifically in sparsely-supervised (LIDAR-based) outdoor MDE, a setting where prior edge-refinement work (SharpNet, DCTNet, segmentation-guided losses) has been confined to dense indoor GT or self-supervised regimes. Rather than requiring additional inputs like stereo images or panoptic segmentation maps, the method repurposes a synthetic-to-real transfer paradigm: a Depth Edge Estimation (DEE) network trained on GTA-PreSIL generates pseudo edge labels that supervise a lightweight Edge Detection Block (EDB) and Balanced BCE loss during MDE training. The approach is conceptually distinct from multi-resolution merging methods (BoostingDepth, GradientFusion), which the paper shows actually degrade edge quality on KITTI due to domain mismatch and resolution sensitivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of manually annotated depth edge benchmarks (KITTI-DE, DDAD-DE) fills a genuine evaluation gap, since standard LIDAR-based metrics (ARE, ORD) are insensitive to 2D edge localization errors that critically affect AR occlusion and novel view synthesis. The finding that edge quality is poorly correlated with per-pixel depth accuracy underscores that optimizing ARE alone is insufficient for edge-sensitive applications. Key limitations include the domain gap between GTA-trained DEE and real scenes, a small ARE degradation on some datasets, and the reliance on Canny thresholds for edge extraction during evaluation. Future work could close the domain gap via unsupervised adaptation or by incorporating target-domain LIDAR into DEE training.
