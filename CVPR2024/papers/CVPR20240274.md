## Problem

Existing novel class discovery methods assume i.i.d. data and balanced class distributions, assumptions that break down in biomedical imaging where inconsistent acquisition protocols across cohorts introduce distribution shifts and rare phenotypes create severe long-tail imbalance. Without explicit geometric constraints on the embedding space, learned representations suffer from semantic ambiguity and uncontrolled open-space risk, making it impossible to reliably discover and group unseen biomedical concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240274/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled base set of biomedical images (e.g., chest X-rays, histology patches, dermoscopy images, fundus photographs) with known class labels and an unlabeled set drawn from a different distribution that contains both base and novel classes. A ViT-B/16 backbone with DINO pre-trained weights extracts 768-dimensional [CLS] token embeddings. The output consists of cluster assignments for each unlabeled instance (base or novel class), an estimated number of novel classes derived from spectral graph Laplacian eigenvalue gaps, and per-instance von Mises-Fisher distributions on a unit hypersphere encoding both semantic direction and representation uncertainty.

## Method

The core contribution is a geometry-constrained probabilistic framework that models each instance's latent embedding as a marginal von Mises-Fisher distribution on a unit hypersphere, decoupling discriminative semantic context from task-irrelevant distributional bias via the concentration parameter as an instance-wise uncertainty measure. Four geometric inductive biases are jointly enforced: (1) uniform proxies pre-positioned on the hypersphere via Riesz s-kernel energy minimization provide equidistributed anchors; (2) base space bounding minimizes KL divergence between each instance's vMF posterior and a Dirac delta at its class proxy, shrinking base classes to bounded regions; (3) open space dispersion pushes instances with maximal probabilistic deviation from base proxies toward remaining proxies, enforcing large angular margins; and (4) open space structuring minimizes KL divergence between vMF distributions of unlabeled instance pairs that share top-k proxy overlaps, promoting uniform yet semantically coherent clustering of novel classes. A spectral graph-theoretic estimator identifies the number of novel classes by locating the largest eigenvalue gap in the normalized graph Laplacian, supporting taxonomy-adaptive granularity without recursive clustering.

## Results

Across four biomedical discovery tasks (pneumonia organisms, cell nuclei, skin lesions, diabetic retinopathy), the proposed method consistently achieves the best performance on all metrics. On pneumonia infectious organisms, it reaches 78.51% overall accuracy and 66.67% novel-class accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline DCCL (72.73% / 60.32%) by ~6 points. On diabetic retinopathy severity, it attains 58.41% overall accuracy and 34.22% novel F1, outperforming the next-best BYOP (51.67% / 25.56%) by a substantial margin. The largest relative gains appear on novel-class F1 scores, where the method improves over the best baseline by 9–10 points across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all four biomedical tasks. DCCL [36] is the strongest competitor on pneumonia (72.73% Acc-all) but lags by 5.8 points overall and 6.4 points on novel-class accuracy; its self-training paradigm struggles with the non-i.i.d. distribution shift. BYOP [48] is the closest rival on skin cancer (65.79% vs. 69.62% Acc-all) and diabetic retinopathy (51.67% vs. 58.41%), yet still trails by 4–7 points, with the gap widening to ~9 points on novel F1. GCD [44] and UNO+ [16] assume balanced class distributions and i.i.d. data, leading to 10–15 point deficits on novel-class metrics. RankStats+ [21] and SLF [50] perform weakest overall, with SLF particularly poor on cell nuclei (F1-novel of only 18.42%). The consistent advantage on novel-class metrics (Acc-novel, F1-novel) across all tasks confirms that the geometry-constrained probabilistic modeling specifically addresses the open-space risk that plagues existing self-training-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Novel Class Discovery (GNCD) framework by explicitly addressing two assumptions that prior GNCD methods (GCD, DCCL, UNO+) rely on but that fail in biomedical imaging: i.i.d. data distribution and balanced class frequencies. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes the von Mises-Fisher distribution—previously applied in face recognition and OOD detection—as a probabilistic embedding mechanism on a hyperspherical manifold, and introduces geometric inductive biases (boundness via base-space bounding and uniformity via Riesz-energy proxy placement) that have not been systematically exploited in the NCD literature. The spectral graph-theoretic class number estimation departs from the recursive clustering strategy in GCD, offering a computationally efficient and taxonomy-adaptive alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for novel class discovery under the distribution shifts and long-tail imbalances that are endemic to real biomedical data, a setting where prior self-training-based methods (SLF, BYOP) show volatile performance. The geometric inductive biases yield interpretable embedding layouts and provable error bounds on open-space risk, which could inform broader open-world learning beyond biomedical imaging. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large unlabeled pools, whether the vMF concentration parameter can be leveraged for downstream confidence calibration in clinical deployment, and whether the spectral estimation generalizes to hierarchical taxonomies with more than two granularity levels.
