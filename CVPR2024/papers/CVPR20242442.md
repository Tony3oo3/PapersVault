## Problem

Existing MLLM frameworks either require massive multi-stage training with feature-level alignment (adaptor training, multi-modal pre-training) or sacrifice flexibility for training-free agent pipelines. The paper asks whether one can combine the training efficiency of LLM-as-agent methods with the input flexibility of adaptor training, while eliminating the complex multi-stage feature-level alignment that plagues current adaptor-based MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242442/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary combinations of a text instruction with one or more non-textual inputs (image, video clip, audio clip, or any mixture thereof), encoded via ImageBind into a unified embedding space. Rather than directly producing non-text outputs, the LLM emits a structured meta-response (e.g., {model: "text-to-image", prompts: "a photo of a cat"}) that is parsed at inference time to invoke a frozen text-to-x generative model (Stable Diffusion, AudioLDM, VideoCrafter) for the final output. This decoupling means the generative models are never run during training.

## Method

ModaVerse introduces an Adaptor+Agent paradigm: on the input side, three trainable linear projection layers map ImageBind embeddings into the LLM's text space; on the output side, the LLM (Vicuna, fine-tuned with LoRA) is treated as an agent that generates language-level meta-responses specifying which external model to call and what prompt to use. The key technical contribution is I/O Alignment, an instruction-following tuning strategy that simultaneously fits the input projection and teaches the LLM to produce prompts in the stylistic register expected by generative models (e.g., concise descriptive phrases with quality tags like '4k' rather than literary paragraphs). Training data is constructed by prompting ChatGPT with seed examples, candidate descriptions from paired datasets, and language references from generative model training sets to synthesize instruction-invocation pairs. The entire pipeline is trained in a single stage on 2M samples with 4 GPUs for 20 hours, compared to multi-stage pipelines requiring 13M+ samples and hundreds of GPUs in prior work.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (COCO-caption), ModaVerse achieves a FID of 11.24, matching NExT-GPT (11.28) and Stable Diffusion (11.21). On image-to-text (COCO-caption), it scores B@4 43.9, METEOR 31.8, and CIDEr 151.4, slightly trailing NExT-GPT (44.3/32.9/156.7) and OFA (44.9/32.5/154.9). For text-to-audio (AudioCaps), ModaVerse obtains FD 23.40 and IS 8.22, and for text-to-video (MSR-VTT), FID 13.35 and CLIPSIM 0.3014, all within ~1–2% of the best baselines. Crucially, ModaVerse achieves these results with a single-stage training on only 2M samples (4 GPUs × 20 h), versus 82M+ samples and multi-stage pipelines for competitors like Emu and NExT-GPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ModaVerse is compared against 19 baselines spanning dedicated generators (Stable Diffusion, GLIDE, CogView, AudioLDM, CogVideo, MakeVideo, Latent-Shift) and multimodal LLMs (NExT-GPT, CoDi, BLIP-2, OFA, mPLUG-2, GIT, BART, AL-MixGen, ORG-TRL, Oscar, DiffSound). On text-to-image, ModaVerse (FID 11.24) edges out NExT-GPT (11.28) and CoDi (11.26) but trails Stable Diffusion (11.21) by 0.03. On image-to-text, it outperforms CoDi (B@4 40.2) and BLIP-2 (43.7) but is narrowly behind NExT-GPT (44.3) and OFA (44.9). For text-to-audio, ModaVerse (FD 23.40, IS 8.22) beats NExT-GPT (23.58, 8.35) on FD but loses to CoDi (22.90, 8.77) on both metrics. On audio-to-text, it surpasses CoDi (SPIDEr 0.480) but trails NExT-GPT (0.521). For text-to-video, ModaVerse (FID 13.35, CLIPSIM 0.3014) is close to NExT-GPT (13.04, 0.3085) and MakeVideo (13.17, 0.3049). On video-to-text, it beats CoDi (B@4 52.1) and mPLUG-2 (57.8) on METEOR but loses to NExT-GPT (58.4, 38.5). Overall, ModaVerse is consistently within 1–3% of the best method on every benchmark while requiring an order-of-magnitude less training data and a single training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

ModaVerse synthesizes two established MLLM paradigms — adaptor training (BLIP-2, LLaVA, NExT-GPT) and LLM-as-agent (HuggingGPT, MM-React) — into a unified Adaptor+Agent framework. Its key departure from prior adaptor-based methods is the I/O Alignment mechanism, which aligns the LLM's output with generative model inputs at the natural language level (via meta-responses) rather than at the latent feature level, eliminating the need for output-side projection layers and multi-stage training. The input side follows the simplified linear-projection approach of MiniGPT-4 and PandaGPT, using ImageBind as a unified multi-modality encoder, while the output side repurposes the LLM-as-agent idea to invoke frozen text-to-x models (Stable Diffusion, AudioLDM, VideoCrafter) without any additional trainable components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in training efficiency: ModaVerse achieves comparable performance to multi-stage methods like NExT-GPT and Emu using a single training stage, 2M data samples (less than 2% of Emu's 82M), and 4 GPUs for 20 hours, making multi-modal LLM training substantially more accessible. The plug-and-play output design means new generative models can be swapped in without retraining, which is valuable as the generative model landscape evolves rapidly. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: the model cannot perform in-place image editing (it generates entirely new images), and it struggles without explicit language instructions at input time. Open questions include whether language-level I/O alignment scales to more complex or compositional generation tasks, and whether the reliance on ChatGPT-generated training instructions introduces systematic biases.
