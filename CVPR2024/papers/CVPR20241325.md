## Problem

Existing 3D facial makeup estimation methods (Yang-Ext and Yang-Res) suffer from high computational cost (56–63 s per image), poor robustness on self-occluded faces, and limited applicability beyond basic reconstruction. The paper addresses this by introducing dedicated makeup prior models that extend 3DMMs with a 4-channel (base + alpha matte) makeup representation, enabling both fast and high-fidelity estimation across a broader set of applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241325/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB face image and produces a 256×256 four-channel UV map (RGB makeup base plus alpha matte) parameterized either by a 100-dimensional PCA coefficient vector or a 7×512 StyleGAN2 W+ latent code. The estimated makeup is composited onto a bare-skin albedo via differentiable alpha blending to yield a final makeup-applied albedo, which is then rendered into a 3D face reconstruction compatible with FLAME topology. The 4-channel representation (as opposed to the 3-channel residual used by Yang-Res) is what enables downstream operations like transfer, interpolation, and user-friendly editing.

## Method

Two makeup prior models are constructed from 3,070 extracted UV maps: a PCA linear model (100 principal components) with a ResNet-50 encoder for coefficient regression, and a StyleGAN2 generative model with a pSp encoder for GAN inversion. The estimation network comprises three modules—a frozen FLAME reconstruction module, a makeup estimation module (encoder + prior-model decoder), and a novel makeup consistency module that enforces cyclic coefficient agreement when estimated makeup is transferred to random bare faces, thereby decoupling makeup from identity and expression. Training is self-supervised, minimizing L1, VGG perceptual, cyclic-consistency, and L2-regularization losses. For the StyleGAN2 variant, an additional refinement stage optimizes the latent code with a soft bilateral-symmetry loss and an alpha-matte L1 regulation loss to suppress over-fitting artifacts on less common makeup styles.

## Results

On the Wild dataset, the StyleGAN2-based method achieves the best reconstruction quality with RMSE 0.0517, SSIM 0.6240, and LPIPS 0.0608, outperforming Yang-Res (RMSE 0.0543, SSIM 0.6036, LPIPS 0.0674) and DECA (RMSE 0.0876, SSIM 0.3651, LPIPS 0.0871). On BeautyFace, the StyleGAN2 method leads in HM(eyes) (0.0031), HM(lips) (0.0068), SSIM (0.5134), and LPIPS (0.0673), though Yang-Res achieves a slightly lower RMSE (0.0545 vs. 0.0650). In terms of speed, the PCA-based method runs in 0.31 s per image (~180× faster than Yang-Res at 56.12 s), while the StyleGAN2 method takes 18.13 s (~3× faster).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Yang-Res [84], the StyleGAN2-based method wins on 4 of 5 metrics on BeautyFace (HM-eyes, HM-lips, SSIM, LPIPS) and all 5 metrics on Wild, with the sole exception being RMSE on BeautyFace where Yang-Res is slightly lower (0.0545 vs. 0.0650). The PCA-based method is competitive with Yang-Res, matching or exceeding it on SSIM and HM-lips but trailing on RMSE for both datasets. Against DECA [24] (which lacks any makeup modeling), both prior models yield substantial improvements: integrating the StyleGAN2 prior into DECA reduces RMSE from 0.0876 to 0.0807 on Wild and from 0.0636 to 0.0568 on BeautyFace, while the full StyleGAN2 pipeline further narrows the gap to 0.0517 and 0.0650 respectively. The key practical advantage over Yang-Res is robustness: the regression-based estimation with the makeup consistency module handles self-occluded faces and large poses where Yang-Res's analysis-by-synthesis approach fails, and the PCA variant runs ~180× faster (0.31 s vs. 56.12 s), enabling real-time use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Yang et al. [84], who first extracted 3D makeup UV maps via illumination-aware decomposition (Yang-Ext) and constructed a residual-based PCA prior (Yang-Res) using analysis-by-synthesis. The key departure is replacing the 3-channel residual formulation with a 4-channel alpha-blended representation and shifting from iterative optimization to a regression-based estimation network (ResNet-50 for PCA, pSp encoder for StyleGAN2), while introducing a novel makeup consistency module that enforces coefficient invariance across randomly re-rendered bare faces. The generative prior leverages StyleGAN2's W+ latent space and the pSp encoder [60], following the GAN-inversion-with-refinement paradigm established in [21, 54], and the reconstruction backbone is the FLAME model [47] with DECA [24] as the compatible rendering framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 180× speedup (PCA) and 3× speedup (StyleGAN2) over the prior analysis-by-synthesis approach make 3D makeup estimation practical for interactive applications, and the alpha-blended 4-channel formulation unlocks makeup transfer, interpolation, and user-friendly editing that were infeasible with Yang-Res's tightly-coupled residual representation. The makeup consistency module addresses a genuine robustness gap (self-occluded faces) that limited prior methods. Open questions include whether the StyleGAN2 prior's latent disentanglement can be further exploited for fine-grained makeup manipulation (e.g., independently editing eyeshadow vs. lipstick), and whether the PCA model's inability to benefit from refinement (due to limited degrees of freedom) can be overcome with a richer linear or hybrid model.
