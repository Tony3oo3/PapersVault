## Problem

Despite widespread use of synthetic 3D scene datasets for training embodied navigation agents, no systematic analysis has examined the tradeoff between dataset scale (number of scenes, total navigable area) and dataset realism (visual fidelity, correlation to real-world object statistics). Procedural generation enabled near-infinite scale, but whether scale alone suffices for zero-shot generalization to real-world scanned environments, or whether scene quality and realism are the true bottleneck, remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a 224×224 RGB frame, GPS and compass sensor readings, an integer goal category index (one of six: bed, chair, sofa, tv, plant, toilet), and the previous action. The RGB frame is encoded by a frozen CLIP ResNet-50 into a 2048-dim feature vector, while the goal category and previous action are mapped through learned 32-dim embedding matrices. The output is a discrete action from {STOP, MOVE_FORWARD, TURN_LEFT, TURN_RIGHT, LOOK_UP, LOOK_DOWN} with 0.25 m forward steps and 30° turn angles, executed by a LoCoBot (0.18 m radius, 0.88 m height).

## Method

The paper contributes two main components: (1) HSSD-200, a dataset of 211 human-authored 3D interiors built in Floorplanner from real house layouts, containing 18,656 unique objects across 466 WordNetCo semantic categories, with objects created by professional 3D artists matching real-world brands and compressed via quadric mesh simplification and Basis supercompression for 12.4× size reduction; and (2) a systematic empirical study that disentangles scale from realism by training identical DD-PPO + VER agents on scale-matched subsets (HSSD-60/122 vs. ProcTHOR-60/122/10K) and evaluating zero-shot generalization to HM3DSem and MP3D. The agent architecture follows Khandelwal et al., using a frozen CLIP ResNet-50 visual encoder feeding a 2-layer LSTM that also consumes GPS, compass, goal, and action embeddings to predict the next action. The key finding is that 122 high-quality HSSD scenes outperform 10,000 procedurally generated ProcTHOR scenes on zero-shot ObjectNav (19.15% vs. 12.53% success on HM3DSem), demonstrating that scene realism and object co-occurrence statistics matter more than raw dataset scale for generalization.

## Results

The headline result is that agents trained on only 122 HSSD scenes outperform agents trained on 10,000 ProcTHOR scenes in zero-shot ObjectNav generalization to real-world scans: 19.15% success and 7.71 SPL on HM3DSem (vs. 12.53% and 5.26 for ProcTHOR-10K), and 14.44% success and 5.17 SPL on MP3D (vs. 8.26% and 2.96). In the scale-matched comparison (122 scenes each), HSSD-122 achieves 19.15% success vs. 9.33% for ProcTHOR-122 on HM3DSem, and 14.44% vs. 5.47% on MP3D, confirming that realism—not scale—drives generalization. After fine-tuning on the target dataset, the gap narrows substantially (HSSD-122: 48.23% success / 23.1 SPL vs. ProcTHOR-10K: 48.32% / 21.8 SPL on HM3DSem), indicating the advantage is specific to zero-shot transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProcTHOR-10K (the primary baseline), HSSD-122 wins decisively on zero-shot transfer: 19.15% vs. 12.53% success on HM3DSem and 14.44% vs. 8.26% on MP3D, despite using ~80× fewer scenes. Against iTHOR (120 single-room scenes), HSSD-122 also wins on both real-world targets (19.15% vs. 6.16% on HM3DSem; 14.44% vs. 6.18% on MP3D). In the scale-matched ablation, HSSD-122 beats ProcTHOR-122 by roughly 2× on success rate for both HM3DSem (19.15 vs. 9.33) and MP3D (14.44 vs. 5.47), isolating realism as the driving factor. HSSD-122 loses to agents trained directly on MP3D (32.07%) or HM3DSem (48.10%), which is expected since those agents are evaluated in-distribution. The key takeaway is that the 10,000-scene ProcTHOR dataset provides no meaningful advantage over 122 high-quality HSSD scenes for zero-shot generalization, and in fact performs worse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ObjectNav literature by introducing HSSD-200, a human-authored synthetic 3D scene dataset that departs from the dominant trend of using procedurally generated scenes (ProcTHOR) or noisy real-world scans (HM3DSem, MP3D). The agent architecture builds directly on Khandelwal et al.'s CLIP-embedding-plus-LSTM design, but the core contribution is a systematic empirical study of the scale-versus-realism tradeoff that prior work had treated as a black box. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the assumption that near-infinite procedural scale (ProcTHOR-10K) is the primary lever for improving generalization, arguing instead that visual fidelity and correlation to real-world object statistics matter more.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 122 high-quality scenes outperform 10,000 procedurally generated scenes in zero-shot transfer to real scans challenges the field's default assumption that more training data is always better, with direct practical implications for how embodied AI researchers allocate effort between dataset scale and quality. The HSSD-200 dataset itself is a significant resource: fully human-authored interiors with 18,656 real-world objects, fine-grained WordNetCo semantics, and compressed assets enabling high-throughput simulation. Key limitations include the study being restricted to a single monolithic RL agent architecture and the ObjectNav task; whether the quality-over-scale finding holds for modular approaches, imitation learning, or longer-horizon tasks like rearrangement remains open.
