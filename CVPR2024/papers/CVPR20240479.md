## Problem

Most multimodal sentiment analysis methods assume all modalities are available during both training and inference, yet real-world deployments face uncertain modality missingness (frame-level gaps within a modality or entire modalities being absent) that severely degrades performance. Existing missing-modality solutions rely on single-sample reconstruction or joint learning but fail to exploit cross-sample correlations, cross-category feature structure, and fine-grained distributional alignment between teacher and student networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240479/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes word-aligned multimodal video segments with three modalities: language (300-d GloVe embeddings), audio (74-d COVAREP features including MFCCs and glottal parameters), and visual (35-d Facet action units), where missing portions are zeroed out. During training, a Modality Random Missing (MRM) strategy simultaneously applies intra-modality frame dropout and inter-modality removal to generate incomplete samples for the student network. The output is a sentiment classification: binary positive/negative (weighted F1) for MOSI and MOSEI, or four-class emotion labels (happy, sad, angry, neutral) for IEMOCAP.

## Method

CorrKD is a teacher-student knowledge distillation framework where the teacher is trained on complete modalities and the student on randomly missing modalities, both sharing a 1D temporal convolution + Transformer encoder + GAP fusion architecture. Three distillation mechanisms decouple different levels of correlation: (1) Sample-level Contrastive Distillation (SCD) applies a mini-batch contrastive loss that pulls same-sample teacher-student representations together while pushing different-sample representations apart, transferring holistic cross-sample structure; (2) Category-guided Prototype Distillation (CPD) computes per-category prototypes within each batch and aligns the cosine-similarity matrices between teacher and student to preserve intra- and inter-category feature variations; (3) Response-disentangled Consistency Distillation (RCD) splits softmax outputs into Target Category Response and Non-Target Category Response, then maximizes mutual information (estimated via a Jensen-Shannon divergence-based neural estimator) between homogeneous response pairs to sharpen decision boundaries and encourage distributional alignment. The total loss combines standard cross-entropy with all three distillation terms.

## Results

On MOSI, CorrKD achieves an average F1 of 74.69% across six inter-modality missing conditions and 83.94% under complete-modality testing, outperforming the strongest missing-modality baseline GCNet (73.84% avg.) by 0.85 points. On MOSEI, CorrKD reaches 74.02% average F1 and 82.16% complete-modality F1, surpassing GCNet (73.54% avg.) by 0.48 points. On IEMOCAP, CorrKD attains the highest average F1 across all four emotion categories (e.g., 76.6% for Sad, 76.1% for Happy) under missing conditions, with the largest margin over GCNet in the Sad category (+1.2 points). Under complete-modality testing, CorrKD remains competitive with complete-modality methods (e.g., 83.94% vs. Self-MM's 84.64% on MOSI) while substantially outperforming them under any missing-modality condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorrKD outperforms all seven baselines on the average F1 across six inter-modality missing conditions on both MOSI (74.69 vs. GCNet's 73.84, SMIL's 72.64) and MOSEI (74.02 vs. GCNet's 73.54, SMIL's 70.65). Against the strongest missing-modality baseline GCNet, CorrKD gains +0.85% on MOSI and +0.48% on MOSEI average F1, with the largest single-condition margin of +3.72% on MOSI when only audio and visual are available ({a,v}: 73.74 vs. 70.02). Compared to joint-learning methods (MCTN, TransM), CorrKD leads by 6.38 and 3.37 points on MOSI average, respectively, and by 4.64 and 3.75 points on MOSEI. Against generative methods (SMIL, GCNet), the advantage is more modest but consistent across all six missing conditions. Under complete-modality testing ({l,a,v}), CorrKD is slightly behind Self-MM (83.94 vs. 84.64 on MOSI) and DMD (82.16 vs. 84.78 on MOSEI), but surpasses all missing-modality baselines, confirming that the distillation framework does not sacrifice full-modality performance. On IEMOCAP, CorrKD achieves the best average F1 in every emotion category under missing conditions, with the most notable gain over GCNet in the Sad category (76.6 vs. 75.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

CorrKD extends the teacher-student knowledge distillation paradigm for missing-modality MSA (as in [13, 18, 33, 40, 47]) by decomposing the distillation signal into three correlation levels: cross-sample (via contrastive learning), cross-category (via prototypical networks borrowed from few-shot learning), and cross-response (via mutual information estimation with Jensen-Shannon divergence). It departs from prior joint-learning methods (MCTN, TransM) and generative methods (SMIL, GCNet) that model modality interactions within a single sample, arguing that holistic inter-sample and inter-category structure is underexploited. The 'correlation-decoupled' framing is the paper's conceptual contribution, repurposing well-established tools (contrastive losses, category prototypes, MI maximization) into a unified distillation objective for the missing-modality setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-level correlation decomposition offers a structured and interpretable recipe for knowledge transfer under modality missingness, and the consistent (if modest) gains across MOSI, MOSEI, and IEMOCAP validate that cross-sample and cross-category structure carries sentiment-relevant information beyond what single-sample reconstruction captures. Limitations include the reliance on a frozen teacher trained on complete data (doubling training cost), the MRM missingness strategy that may not reflect all real-world degradation patterns, and evaluation restricted to three word-aligned datasets. Open questions include whether the correlation-decoupling principle generalizes to other multimodal tasks (e.g., video understanding, medical imaging) and whether the teacher-student setup can be replaced by self-distillation to reduce overhead.
