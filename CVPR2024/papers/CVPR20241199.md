## Problem

Existing scene graph generation methods assume access to clean, undisturbed images, yet real-world observations are frequently degraded by corruptions such as fog, sun glare, water drops, and rain. No prior work has introduced image corruptions into the SGG setting or proposed a method that can generate accurate scene graphs under such domain shifts without explicit training on corrupted data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241199/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (clean or corrupted with one of 20 procedurally generated corruption types) and produces a directed scene graph consisting of object nodes (each with a bounding box and one of 150 object classes) and predicate edges (one of 50 predicate classes) forming subject-predicate-object triplets. Object proposals are obtained via a frozen Faster-RCNN detector with a VGG-16 backbone, and predicate features are extracted from the union bounding box of each object pair. Evaluation follows the standard PredCls and SGCls protocols with both constrained (one relation per object pair) and unconstrained settings, reporting top-k mean triplet recall.

## Method

HiKER-SGG constructs a hierarchical commonsense knowledge graph by adding superclass entity (CXE) and superclass predicate (CXP) nodes above the standard commonsense nodes, with hierarchies discovered automatically via hierarchical clustering over a weighted combination of GloVe semantic similarity and MotifNet confusion-matrix pattern similarity. A scene graph is initialized from Faster-RCNN detections, then bridged to the knowledge graph via bidirectional edges whose weights are updated iteratively through GGNN-style message passing with GRU updates. At inference, the model performs hierarchical classification from coarse superclass to fine-grained subclass (e.g., animal → cat), constraining the search space at each level so that corruption-robust coarse decisions guide subsequent fine-grained predictions. An adaptive refinement module dynamically updates a row-normalized transition probability matrix each epoch to convert general predicate predictions into more specific ones, and the entire system is trained exclusively on clean Visual Genome images yet generalizes zero-shot to all 20 corruption types in the new VG-C benchmark.

## Results

On the Visual Genome PredCls task, HiKER-SGG achieves mR@20 of 42.1 (UC) / 33.4 (C), mR@50 of 57.9 / 39.3, and mR@100 of 69.2 / 41.2, outperforming the strongest baseline EB-Net+EOA (39.8/30.8, 54.9/36.7, 66.3/39.2) by 2.3–3.0 points across all k values. On SGCls, HiKER-SGG reaches mR@20 of 22.6/18.2, mR@50 of 30.0/20.3, and mR@100 of 36.7/21.4, again leading EB-Net+EOA (19.6/14.9, 26.7/17.3, 32.5/18.3). On the corrupted VG-C benchmark, HiKER-SGG shows the smallest performance degradation (−17.8% average mR@20 C) compared to EB-Net (−22.4%) and GB-Net (−27.3%), demonstrating zero-shot robustness to 20 types of image corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiKER-SGG outperforms all 18 baselines on both PredCls and SGCls tasks on clean Visual Genome images. Against the closest competitor EB-Net+EOA [9], it gains +2.3 mR@20 (C), +2.6 mR@50 (C), and +2.0 mR@100 (C) on PredCls, and +3.3/+3.0/+3.1 on SGCls respectively. The knowledge-graph-based GB-Net [85] is substantially outperformed, with HiKER-SGG achieving roughly double the constrained mR@20 (33.4 vs. 15.3 on PredCls). Among non-knowledge-graph baselines, the strongest is PE-Net+SIL [69] at 26.9/33.1 mR@20 (C) on PredCls, still 6.5 points below HiKER-SGG. On the corrupted VG-C benchmark, HiKER-SGG maintains the smallest average performance drop (−17.8% mR@20 C) versus EB-Net (−22.4%) and GB-Net (−27.3%), confirming its zero-shot robustness advantage. The method also surpasses PCPL [77] (50.6/35.2 mR@50 C) and MotifNet+RTPB [6] (35.3 mR@50 C) by a wide margin on PredCls.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge-graph-based SGG line (GB-Net, EB-Net) by injecting hierarchical superclass nodes and edges into the commonsense knowledge graph, enabling a coarse-to-fine prediction process rather than flat classification. It builds on GGNN-style message passing to bridge the scene graph and knowledge graph, but departs from prior work by discovering hierarchies automatically via hierarchical clustering over GloVe embeddings and MotifNet confusion matrices, rather than relying on manually curated taxonomies. The paper also introduces the first corruption-robust SGG formulation, positioning itself against the broader robustness literature (ImageNet-C, adversarial training) while noting that no prior work has addressed corruptions in the SGG setting specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VG-C benchmark (20 procedurally generated corruptions) fills a clear gap in SGG evaluation and provides a standardized platform for future robustness research. The zero-shot corruption robustness—trained exclusively on clean images yet generalizing to unseen corruptions—suggests that hierarchical decomposition acts as an implicit inductive bias that preserves higher-level semantic features under degradation, a principle potentially transferable to other structured prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger class taxonomies or more complex multi-level hierarchies, whether the gains hold on datasets beyond Visual Genome, and whether the moderate per-component ablation gains (0.2–1.0% mR@k) compound meaningfully in downstream applications like VQA or embodied planning.
