## Problem

Plug-and-play (PnP), RED, and Langevin-sampling algorithms that rely on implicit denoising priors frequently exhibit instability—divergence, geometric artifacts, and sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning—limiting their transferability across imaging tasks. While prior work has addressed this by constraining denoiser architectures or modifying optimization schedules, these approaches trade reconstruction quality for stability. The paper identifies equivariance to geometric transformations (rotations, reflections, translations) as an underexplored lever that simultaneously improves stability and reconstruction quality without architectural constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a linear forward operator A (e.g., convolutional blur, Fourier mask for MRI, downsampling for super-resolution), noisy measurements y, and a frozen pretrained denoiser (DnCNN, DRUNet, SCUNet, SwinIR, DiffUNet, or GSNet) along with a chosen group G of unitary transformations. At each iteration, a single random group element is sampled and applied to the denoiser input with its inverse applied to the output, requiring no retraining or architectural modification. Outputs are reconstructed images (for PnP/RED) or posterior mean and variance estimates (for ULA sampling), evaluated on grayscale and color images at 64×64 to 256×256 resolutions.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Monte Carlo approximation of group averaging: at each algorithm iteration, a random transformation T_g is sampled from group G, the denoiser is evaluated as D̃_G(x) = T_g^{-1} D(T_g x), and this equivariant estimate replaces the standard denoiser call in PnP, RED, or ULA updates. Theoretically, the paper proves that group averaging (i) guarantees equal or better denoising performance when the signal distribution is G-invariant, (ii) symmetrizes the denoiser's Jacobian (ensuring an explicit prior exists for linear denoisers equivariant to shifts and reflections), and (iii) strictly reduces the Lipschitz constant when the dominant singular vector is not equivariant. Additionally, when the forward operator A is not G-equivariant, the equivariant denoiser's Fourier-diagonal spectrum becomes incoherent with A^T A, further reducing the effective Lipschitz constant of the PnP composition. The approach is denoiser-agnostic and requires only a single forward pass of the original network per iteration, making it a drop-in modification to any existing PnP/RED/ULA pipeline.

## Results

On the PnP algorithm, enforcing equivariance dramatically improves stability and reconstruction quality. For example, DRUNet-based PnP on motion deblurring (Set3C) jumps from 10.1±1.5 dB to 18.0±6.0 dB, and on BSD10 from 16.5±9.8 dB to 28.4±2.3 dB. DnCNN-based PnP diverges entirely on MRI ×8, while its equivariant counterpart achieves 24.6±2.9 dB. On Gaussian deblurring (BSD10), Eq.DnCNN reaches 33.0±0.6 dB versus 29.6±4.6 dB for standard DnCNN, surpassing the TGV baseline (30.5±0.9 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 1-Lipschitz DnCNN (Pesquet et al. [30]), the equivariant version yields only marginal gains (e.g., 31.1 vs 30.9 dB on motion deblur BSD10), as the Lipschitz constraint already ensures stability. Against standard DnCNN (Zhang et al. [49]), equivariance brings large improvements: 33.0 vs 29.6 dB on Gaussian deblur BSD10, and it rescues the divergent MRI ×8 case (24.6 dB vs. divergence). Against DRUNet/DPIR (Zhang et al. [50]), equivariant DRUNet dramatically reduces variance (e.g., 28.4±2.3 vs 16.5±9.8 dB on motion deblur BSD10) but still trails the fine-tuned DPIR algorithm (35.0 dB) on most tasks. Against TGV (Bredies et al. [3]), Eq.DnCNN outperforms on Gaussian deblur BSD10 (33.0 vs 30.5 dB) and MRI ×4 (30.1 vs 28.5 dB), while TGV remains more stable on MRI ×8 (25.4 vs 24.6 dB). Against GSPnP (Hurault et al. [17]), the equivariant PnP methods still lag on deblurring (e.g., 30.9 vs 33.5 dB on motion deblur BSD10), confirming that purpose-built convergent algorithms with architectural constraints retain an edge over the plug-and-play approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the plug-and-play (PnP), RED, and Langevin sampling frameworks by inserting a group-averaged (equivariant) denoiser in place of the standard one, building directly on the algorithmic formulations of Venkatakrishnan et al. (2013), Romano et al. (2017), and Laumont et al. (2022). It departs from prior stability-focused work (Lipschitz-constrained denoisers, modified optimization schedules) by requiring no architectural changes or retraining: equivariance is enforced at test time via a single random group transform per iteration. The idea connects to geometric ensembling in super-resolution (Timofte et al., 2016) and symmetrization of linear denoisers (Milanfar, 2013), but the paper is the first to systematically analyze the role of equivariance for implicit denoising priors across multiple algorithm families and provide theoretical guarantees on Jacobian symmetry and Lipschitz reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a one-line modification (apply random rotation/reflection/translation to the denoiser input, invert on output) stabilizes algorithms that otherwise diverge, without sacrificing reconstruction quality, and opens the door to reliable posterior sampling with implicit priors. However, the approach remains insufficient for strongly non-equivariant backbones like SCUNet, and the Monte Carlo approximation introduces per-iteration variance that may matter in high-precision settings. Open questions include whether the technique extends to non-linear forward operators, whether larger group sizes or structured sampling further reduce variance, and whether equivariance can be combined with other stabilization strategies (e.g., adaptive stepsizes) to handle the remaining failure cases.
