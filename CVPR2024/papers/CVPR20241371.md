## Problem

Federated Domain Generalization (FedDG) requires training a global model across clients, each holding a single source domain, that generalizes to unseen domains without centralizing data. Existing FedDG methods either share partial client data (frequency-space amplitudes, style vectors), introducing privacy leakage risks, or impose substantial extra communication and computation costs (e.g., O(N²) classifier ensembles, doubled client memory). The paper asks whether a purely architectural modification—operating only on model parameters—can achieve competitive domain-invariant representation learning while fully respecting FL privacy and efficiency constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 224×224 (PACS, Office-Home) or 96×96 (Camelyon17 histology patches) and outputs a single class label per image (7-way for PACS, 65-way for Office-Home, binary for Camelyon17). In the federated protocol, each client owns exactly one domain and trains locally for one epoch per round; the global model is produced by weighted averaging of client parameters, with the sole exception that BN-side parameters of PerXAN layers are excluded from server-to-client broadcasting and remain client-specific. Evaluation follows a leave-one-domain-out protocol where the held-out domain is entirely unseen during training.

## Method

gPerXAN replaces Batch Normalization layers in the first blocks of a CNN backbone (ResNet-50 or DenseNet-121) with an eXplicitly Assembled Normalization (XAN) module that computes a learnable weighted sum of IN and BN output activations (Eq. 2), rather than mixing their statistics as in prior work. The personalization twist (PerXAN) splits aggregation: IN-side parameters are globally averaged via FedAvg alongside all other weights, while BN-side parameters are updated locally and never broadcast, giving each client a domain-adapted normalization branch. A guiding regularizer then freezes the global classifier h_g and adds an auxiliary cross-entropy loss ℓ(h_g(g_i(x)), y) to each client's local objective, forcing client feature extractors to produce representations directly compatible with the shared global classifier—effectively an implicit domain-alignment signal without any inter-client data exchange. The full training loop (Algorithm 1) requires no additional communication beyond standard FedAvg parameter uploads and no extra computation at the server, distinguishing it from methods that exchange style vectors, frequency amplitudes, or maintain per-domain classifier ensembles.

## Results

On the PACS dataset (leave-one-domain-out), gPerXAN achieves 87.94% average accuracy across unseen domains, outperforming the second-best FedDG-GA (86.92%) by 1.02%. On Office-Home, it reaches 71.01% average accuracy, surpassing FedDG-GA (69.86%) by 1.15%. On the real-world medical dataset Camelyon17, gPerXAN attains 94.1% average accuracy, exceeding FedDG-GA (92.3%) by approximately 2%. The method also eliminates the privacy risk and extra communication/computation overhead present in prior FedDG approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

gPerXAN outperforms all seven baselines on both PACS and Office-Home. Against the strongest prior FedDG method FedDG-GA, it gains +1.02% on PACS (87.94 vs 86.92) and +1.15% on Office-Home (71.01 vs 69.86). The information-sharing methods ELCFS and CCST show competitive PACS results (83.19% and 86.52%) but underperform on Office-Home (69.23% and 68.84%), and both carry privacy risks from partial data leakage. The architectural method COPA (85.89% PACS, 68.21% Office-Home) incurs O(N²) communication overhead from its ensemble of domain-specific classifiers. Centralized DG methods (MixStyle, RSC) integrated into FedAvg are inconsistent and sometimes worse than the vanilla FedAvg baseline, confirming they require inter-domain access unavailable in FL. On Camelyon17, gPerXAN leads by ~2% over FedDG-GA (94.1% vs 92.3%), while ELCFS and CCST degrade significantly (91.3%, 91.0%), suggesting their style/frequency interpolation is ineffective for sensitive medical imaging features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IBN-Net (Pan et al., 2018) and DSON (Seo et al., 2020) line of work on combining Instance and Batch Normalization for domain generalization, but replaces their implicit statistics-mixing with an explicit output-level mixture that cleanly separates the two normalization paths. This explicit design is what enables the key architectural move: aggregating IN parameters globally while keeping BN parameters local (personalized) within the FedAvg framework, a strategy not available to prior implicit combinations. In the FedDG setting specifically, the paper departs from ELCFS and CCST, which share partial data information (frequency amplitudes or style vectors) among clients, by relying solely on model parameter updates plus a lightweight regularizer that uses the frozen global classifier to guide local feature extractors toward domain-invariant representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's main practical value is achieving competitive FedDG accuracy with zero additional communication or computation overhead and no data leakage, making it more deployable in privacy-sensitive or resource-constrained federated settings than prior approaches. The ablation showing that centralized DG methods (MixStyle, RSC) can actually degrade performance under FedAvg highlights a non-trivial gap between centralized and federated DG that the community should account for. However, the method is limited to normalization-based CNN architectures, and the paper acknowledges that vulnerability under adversarial attacks remains unexplored; whether the explicit IN/BN split generalizes to transformer-based or other non-normalization-dependent backbones is an open question.
