## Problem

Generic Face Image Quality Assessment (GFIQA) aims to predict perceptual quality of face images, but existing approaches either rely on biometric recognizability (BFIQA) that misses perceptual degradation, or depend on generative priors like StyleGAN2 that degrade on non-standard inputs. A key technical limitation is that prior degradation learning assumes uniform degradation across image patches, which fails for real-world faces with spatially varying artifacts (motion blur in one region, defocus in another). Additionally, existing GFIQA datasets suffer from severe imbalances in skin tone and gender representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB face image (512×512 in the CGFIQA-40k dataset) and produces a single perceptual quality score (MOS). Rather than resizing the image to fit the pre-trained ViT's fixed input dimensions—which would distort quality predictions—the image is cropped into multiple patches, each processed independently, and the resulting per-patch MOS predictions are averaged. The model also outputs a regional confidence map that weights local quality estimates, with facial landmark information guiding which regions receive higher confidence.

## Method

The architecture combines a pre-trained ViT feature extractor, a Squeeze-and-Excitation channel attention block, and a Swin Transformer block, followed by a Transformer Decoder that integrates degradation features via cross-attention (inspired by Stable Diffusion). The key novelty is Dual-Set Degradation Representation Learning (DSL): a self-supervised contrastive learning scheme that trains a degradation encoder using two image sets—Set S (one high-quality face with diverse synthetic degradations) and Set R (real-world faces with natural degradations)—connected via soft proximity mapping to form positive/negative pairs, enabling global (non-patch-wise) degradation representation that is content-independent. A landmark-guided mechanism uses 3DMM facial landmarks with NeRF-style positional encoding (fixed identifiers rather than coordinates) to modulate the regional confidence head, ensuring the model prioritizes perceptually salient regions like eyes and mouth. Training uses Charbonnier loss for outlier robustness, and the degradation encoder is pre-trained separately before being frozen during core GFIQA network training.

## Results

DSL-FIQA achieves the best PLCC and SRCC across all three GFIQA benchmarks: 0.9745/0.9740 on GFIQA-20k, 0.7370/0.7333 on PIQ23, and 0.9873/0.9880 on CGFIQA-40k. On GFIQA-20k, it outperforms the strongest GFIQA baseline StyleGAN-IQA (0.9673/0.9684) by ~0.7% PLCC and the best generic IQA method VCRNet (0.9672/0.9679) by ~0.7% PLCC. On PIQ23, the margin is larger, with DSL-FIQA surpassing MANIQA (0.7202/0.7180) by ~1.7% PLCC. On the newly introduced CGFIQA-40k, DSL-FIQA leads StyleGAN-IQA (0.9822/0.9821) by ~0.5% PLCC and ~0.6% SRCC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSL-FIQA outperforms all 18 baselines on every dataset and metric. Among GFIQA-specific methods, it beats StyleGAN-IQA by 0.72% PLCC on GFIQA-20k, 3.57% PLCC on PIQ23, and 0.51% PLCC on CGFIQA-40k; it also surpasses IFQA by 1.44% PLCC on GFIQA-20k and 0.82% on CGFIQA-40k. Among generic IQA methods, the closest competitors are VCRNet (0.9672/0.9679 on GFIQA-20k) and MANIQA (0.7202/0.7180 on PIQ23), both of which are outperformed by DSL-FIQA by 0.73% and 1.68% PLCC respectively. BFIQA methods (ArcFace, MegaFace, CR-FIQA) achieve high scores on GFIQA-20k and CGFIQA-40k but drop sharply on PIQ23 (e.g., ArcFace falls to 0.5913 PLCC), confirming their limited generalizability to perceptual quality assessment. The largest absolute gains appear on PIQ23, where DSL-FIQA's 0.7370 PLCC exceeds the previous best (MANIQA at 0.7202) by a notable margin, suggesting the dual-set degradation learning and landmark guidance are particularly effective on diverse, in-the-wild portrait images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Re-IQA's [52] self-supervised degradation extraction paradigm but departs from its patch-based contrastive learning by proposing Dual-Set Degradation Representation Learning (DSL), which operates on entire images and uses soft proximity mapping between synthetic and real degradation sets to avoid the uniform-degradation assumption. For the GFIQA task itself, it moves beyond the generative-prior approach of StyleGAN-IQA [59], which is limited by StyleGAN2's training distribution, toward a transformer architecture combining ViT, Swin Transformer, and cross-attention (inspired by Stable Diffusion [49]). The landmark-guided mechanism repurposes NeRF-style positional encoding [38] and 3DMM [12] to inject spatial context into the quality regression head, a design choice motivated by the perceptual salience of facial features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CGFIQA-40k dataset is a practically important contribution that corrects significant skin-tone and gender imbalances in prior GFIQA benchmarks (e.g., PIQ23 is 94% male, GFIQA-20k is 81.6% light skin tone), which prior work has shown to degrade model fairness and generalization. The DSL mechanism offers a more principled alternative to patch-based degradation learning that could transfer to other quality assessment or restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the multi-module pipeline (separate degradation encoder, landmark detector, and core network) scales efficiently at deployment, how performance holds on extreme or out-of-distribution degradations beyond the tested set, and whether the 512×512 resolution constraint limits applicability to high-resolution portrait photography.
