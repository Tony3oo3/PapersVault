## Problem

Existing text-based person retrieval datasets (CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, RSTPReid) have coarse-grained text annotations averaging only 23–37 words, which degrades the task toward attribute-based retrieval and introduces identity-binding ambiguity during training. Moreover, standard evaluation sets fix domain, textual granularity, and style, and discrete rank-based metrics cannot sensitively distinguish models that produce different continuous similarity distributions under identical rank conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image (resized to 384×128) and a natural-language text description (up to 168 tokens) as input. In UFine6926, each image is paired with two manually written descriptions averaging 80.8 words—three to four times longer than prior benchmarks—capturing fine-grained details like clothing material, exact garment length, and accessory placement. At inference, the model outputs a ranked list of gallery images by computing cosine similarity between the global visual embedding and the global textual embedding; all fine-grained decoder and matcher modules are discarded at test time.

## Method

CFAM builds on CLIP pre-trained ViT visual and text encoders and introduces a shared cross-modal granularity decoder: a 2-layer transformer that takes K=16 fixed learnable granularity queries and interacts with both the visual and textual token sequences to produce fine-grained representations of equal length K for each modality. Cross-modal alignment is then computed as the mean cosine similarity across the K paired query outputs, optimized with an SDM loss. A hard negative matching module performs binary classification on whether the fine-grained representations belong to the same pair, using the highest-similarity unpaired text (or image) as the hard negative to encourage discriminative representations. The total training objective combines global similarity loss, local fine-grained similarity loss, the matching loss, and a cross-modal identity classification loss, all weighted equally. At inference, only the global CLIP embeddings are compared, making the approach deployment-efficient despite the richer training signal.

## Results

On the new UFine6926 test set, CFAM with CLIP-ViT-L/14 achieves 89.61% R@1, 96.78% R@5, 98.89% R@10, 88.19% mAP, and 69.45% mSD, outperforming the strongest baseline IRRA (83.53% R@1, 82.79% mAP, 66.35% mSD) by over 6 points in R@1. On the cross-setting UFine3C evaluation, CFAM(L/14) reaches 64.62% R@1 and 48.34% mSD, again surpassing IRRA (56.34% R@1, 42.92% mSD). On the standard CUHK-PEDES benchmark, CFAM(L/14) attains 76.71% R@1 and 68.47% mAP, exceeding the previous best IRRA (73.38% R@1, 66.13% mAP) and PLIP (69.23% R@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFAM(L/14) achieves the best R@1, R@5, R@10, mAP, and mSD across all four standard benchmarks (UFine6926, UFine3C, CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, RSTPReid), outperforming every listed baseline. On CUHK-PEDES, CFAM(L/14) leads IRRA [12] by 3.33 points in R@1 (76.71 vs. 73.38) and 2.34 in mAP (68.47 vs. 66.13), and surpasses PLIP [55] by 7.48 points in R@1 (76.71 vs. 69.23). On ICFG-PEDES, CFAM(L/14) edges out PLIP [55] by 2.33 points in R@1 (66.58 vs. 64.25) and IRRA [12] by 3.12 points (66.58 vs. 63.46). On RSTPReid, CFAM(L/14) beats IRRA [12] by 3.34 points in R@1 (63.54 vs. 60.20) and 3.31 in mAP (50.48 vs. 47.17). With the lighter CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone, CFAM remains competitive with IRRA on CUHK-PEDES (73.67 vs. 73.38 R@1) and ICFG-PEDES (63.57 vs. 63.46 R@1) but trails PLIP [55] on ICFG-PEDES (63.57 vs. 64.25 R@1), indicating the L/14 setting is critical for full superiority. Older baselines such as MIA [20], TIMAM [28], TDE [21], LapsCore [45], TIPCB [3], CAIBC [42], Dual Path [53], CMPM/C [48], ViTAA [41], DSSL [54], and LBUL [43] are all substantially outperformed by CFAM across all datasets, with gaps exceeding 10 points in R@1 on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a corrective to the text-based person retrieval line of work initiated by CUHK-PEDES (2017) and extended by ICFG-PEDES and RSTPReid, arguing that all three effectively degenerate into attribute-based retrieval due to their coarse textual annotations (23–37 words average). Methodologically, CFAM builds on CLIP's pre-trained vision-language encoders and the SDM contrastive loss from IRRA, but introduces a shared cross-modal granularity decoder with fixed learnable queries to perform fine-grained local alignment, plus a hard-negative binary matching objective. The evaluation protocol is novel in leveraging LLMs (Qwen-14B, Llama2-70B) to augment textual diversity, a repurposing of generative language models for benchmark construction that departs from purely manual annotation pipelines used in prior datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that training on a smaller but ultra-fine-grained dataset (6,926 identities) generalizes substantially better to cross-domain, cross-granularity, cross-style evaluation than training on larger coarse-grained datasets (13,000+ identities)—challenges the implicit assumption that more training data always helps in this task. The asymmetry (fine→coarse transfer works, coarse→fine does not) suggests that granularity is a more important axis of representation quality than dataset size for this task. Open questions include whether the mSD metric is stable across model scales and whether the LLM-augmented evaluation set introduces systematic biases that could be gamed. The benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for the text-based person retrieval community, but the field remains narrow and the practical deployment challenges (latency, gallery scale) are not addressed.
