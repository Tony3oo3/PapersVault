## Problem

Learning generalizable human-to-robot (H2R) handover is hindered by three compounding bottlenecks: existing simulators (e.g., HandoverSim) provide only ~1000 scenes with 20 objects, real-world demonstration collection is prohibitively expensive, and naively generated robot demonstrations often produce vision-irrelevant actions that are difficult to distill into closed-loop visuomotor policies. The paper tackles all three by scaling up simulation assets, automating distillation-friendly demonstration generation, and designing an imitation learning objective that exploits the vision-action correlation.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a sequence of L₁ past point clouds captured by a wrist-mounted RGB-D camera on a Panda 7-DoF arm, processed as 4D spatial-temporal data via ICP-computed flow features between frames. It outputs a single 6D egocentric gripper action (position + orientation) per timestep, which is executed as a target for the end effector. The point cloud representation is chosen over RGB images for richer geometric information and a smaller sim-to-real gap.

## Method

GenH2R comprises three components. First, GenH2R-Sim procedurally generates 1M+ handover scenes by combining ShapeNet objects (3,266 categories), DexGraspNet grasp poses (~1M), and multi-segment Bezier curve trajectories with consistent object rotations, surpassing HandoverSim by three orders of magnitude in scene count. Second, a distillation-friendly demonstration pipeline uses the OMG planner with a landmark planning strategy: it trains a pose-forecasting network to identify points where future motion becomes unpredictable, partitions trajectories at those endpoints, and replans grasp/motion every P steps toward the next landmark—balancing foresight with vision-action correlation. Third, the forecast-aided 4D imitation learning method encodes sequential point clouds using ICP-derived flow features fed into PointNet++, and augments the standard L1 action loss with an auxiliary prediction loss on future object poses (L₂ frames ahead), which reinforces the vision-action correlation exploited during demonstration generation.

## Results

The paper's best configuration (Landmark Planning trained on GenH2R-Sim 't0') achieves 86.57% success rate on s0(Sequential), 85.65% on s0(Simultaneous), 41.43% on t0, and 68.33% on t1 (HOI4D real-world), with corresponding Average Success (AS) scores of 28.0, 42.8, 22.3, and 27.9. In the real-world user study, the method achieves 90.0% success in the simple setting and 70.0% in the complex setting, compared to 56.7% and 33.3% for Handover-Sim2real. The method consistently outperforms all baselines by at least 10% success rate across every test set, with the largest gains (up to ~60%) on the simultaneous and real-world benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Handover-Sim2real (Christen et al., CVPR 2023), the paper's Landmark Planning method trained on GenH2R-Sim achieves +11.34%, +16.90%, +12.26%, and +15.93% success rate improvements on s0(Sequential), s0(Simultaneous), t0, and t1 respectively. Against the reproduced Handover-Sim2real* in the true simultaneous setting, gains are even larger: +22.22% on s0(Sequential) and +59.96% on s0(Simultaneous), highlighting that the original benchmark's 'simultaneous' evaluation was less challenging than intended. Against GA-DDPG (Wang et al., CoRL 2022), a goal-auxiliary DPG method designed for static grasping, the paper's method wins by +31.81% on s0(Sequential), +40.97% on s0(Simultaneous), +17.38% on t0, and +42.83% on t1. Against the OMG Planner (Wang et al., RSS 2020), which only supports ground-truth state evaluation on s0(Sequential), the paper's method achieves +24.07% success rate. The paper also shows that training on the large-scale synthetic GenH2R-Sim (1M scenes) substantially outperforms training on the small-scale real-world HandoverSim (720 scenes), with the same Landmark Planning method gaining +8.79% to +14.13% across test sets when switching from s0 to t0 training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HandoverSim benchmark [9] (1000 mocap scenes, 20 objects) by three orders of magnitude in scene count and two in object diversity, replacing motion-captured human hands with procedurally generated Bezier-curve trajectories and DexGraspNet-derived grasp poses. For demonstration generation, it builds on the OMG planner [41] but introduces a landmark-planning strategy that interpolates between the foresighted (destination-based) and shortsighted (per-step replanning) extremes, motivated by the observation that vision-action correlation is the critical factor for distillability into closed-loop visuomotor policies. The learning method combines ICP-based 4D flow features with PointNet++ encoding and a forecasting auxiliary loss, drawing on recent 4D point cloud understanding work but keeping the architecture lightweight for real-time robotic control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GenH2R-Sim benchmark (1M scenes, 3266 objects) provides a much-needed large-scale resource for H2R handover research, and the distillation-friendly demonstration generation pipeline offers a practical recipe for automatically producing high-quality robot demonstrations without human teleoperation. The sim-to-real transfer achieving 90% success in simple settings without any real-world demonstrations is a strong signal that synthetic scaling can substitute for costly real data collection. Open questions include whether this approach extends to bimanual or multi-object handovers, different robot morphologies beyond the Panda arm, and whether the ICP-based flow representation can handle more aggressive or occluded human motions that the Bezier-curve generation may not fully capture.
