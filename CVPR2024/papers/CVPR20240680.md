## Problem

GAN-based naturalistic adversarial patches (e.g., NAP) operate in a limited latent space, making it difficult to simultaneously achieve high attack success rate, visual stealthiness, and robustness to multiple real-world transformations. Moreover, existing physical attacks assume static objects and ignore non-rigid deformations (creases and wrinkles) that arise when a printed patch is worn on a moving person's clothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240680/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target benign image (any predefined natural pattern) and a frozen victim object detector (YOLOv2/v3/v3tiny/v4/v4tiny/v7 or Faster R-CNN at 416×416 input) as inputs, and produces a printable 2D adversarial patch (20.5 cm × 21.5 cm for T-shirt deployment) optimized directly in pixel space. Unlike GAN-based approaches that constrain generation to a learned latent manifold, DAP freely manipulates patch pixels, giving it a much larger search space while still being guided toward the target image's appearance.

## Method

DAP iteratively optimizes patch pixel values via Adam (lr=0.001) with all detector parameters frozen, minimizing a composite loss L_total = α·L_det + β·L_sim + γ·L_tv (α=1, β=4, γ=0.5). The detection loss minimizes the product of objectness and person-class probability across all anchor boxes; the key novelty is a similarity loss defined as the negative squared cosine similarity between the patch and a target benign image, where the squaring acts as a non-linear brake to prevent the similarity term from converging too quickly and stalling the detection loss. For robustness, an EOT block applies random rotation (±20°), affine, scale, noise, brightness, and contrast during training, and a novel Creases Transformation (CT) block models non-rigid clothing deformations by randomly selecting a point and 2D direction vector on the patch, then displacing pixels along that vector with a distance-dependent multiplier (Eq. 4) to emulate natural wrinkle intensity variation. A total variation loss enforces spatial smoothness for physical printability.

## Results

On the INRIA dataset, DAP achieves a mAP of 6.54% against YOLOv3tiny (82.28% attack success rate) and 17.72% against YOLOv7 (82.28% ASR) without transformations. In the physical world, the printed DAP T-shirt achieves a 92.01% True Positive Rate (i.e., 92.01% of missed detections are attributable to the patch) against YOLOv3tiny, compared to 57.22% for the GAN-based NAP baseline. In a user study with 20 participants, DAP receives a naturalness score of 93.12%, substantially higher than NAP (47.75%), UPC (18.15%), and AdvYOLO (15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN-based Naturalistic Adversarial Patch (NAP) [13], DAP achieves lower mAP (i.e., stronger attack) on YOLOv3 (32.63% vs. 34.93%) and a large margin on YOLOv7 (17.72% vs. 60.78%), but NAP outperforms DAP on YOLOv2, YOLOv4, and YOLOv4tiny in raw attack performance. Against Adversarial YOLO [33], DAP wins only on YOLOv3tiny (6.54% vs. 8.74%) while Adv. YOLO achieves substantially lower mAP on the other detectors, though its patches are visually conspicuous. Against UPC [14], DAP is outperformed on all shared detectors in raw mAP, as UPC prioritizes attack strength over naturalness. The paper's key differentiator is the trade-off: DAP sacrifices some peak attack performance to achieve a 93.12% naturalness score (vs. 47.75% for NAP, 18.15% for UPC, 15% for AdvYOLO) and far superior robustness to non-rigid clothing deformations (e.g., only 8.9% ASR drop under creases vs. ~59% drop for NAP on YOLOv3tiny).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the GAN-based naturalistic adversarial patch line (NAP [13], TNT [8]) by replacing the GAN's learned image manifold with direct pixel-space optimization guided by a cosine-similarity loss to a target benign image. It builds on the EOT robustness framework [3] and total variation regularization [22] already used in prior physical-attack work (Thys et al. [33], UPC [14]), but introduces a novel Creases Transformation block to model non-rigid fabric deformations, addressing a gap that static-object assumptions in [13, 14, 33] left unaddressed. The Adversarial T-shirt work [37] also targets dynamic objects but via thin-plate-spline warping without any stealthiness constraint, whereas DAP simultaneously optimizes for effectiveness, naturalness, and robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, GAN-free recipe for generating printable adversarial patches that remain effective under realistic clothing deformations, which is directly relevant to physical security of surveillance and autonomous-driving systems. However, the physical-world success rate (65% ASR on YOLOv3tiny) remains moderate, and the approach is evaluated only on the INRIA pedestrian dataset with a single target class (person) in the main experiments. Open questions include whether the similarity-loss formulation generalizes to 3D-printed patches or multi-object scenes, and whether the CT block captures the full distribution of real-world fabric deformations beyond the simplified sinusoidal model.
