## Problem

Existing video stabilization methods face a fundamental trade-off: 2D-based approaches can generate full frames but distort 3D structure (especially parallax regions), while 3D-based approaches preserve geometry but produce heavily cropped frames due to relying on single-frame information. The paper addresses the unresolved challenge of simultaneously achieving full-frame generation and structure preservation in stabilized video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a shaky video sequence along with pre-estimated depth maps and optical flow (both from a pre-trained Deep3D model) and a camera trajectory smoothed via Gaussian filtering. It renders each stabilized frame using a sliding window of 13 temporally neighboring frames, projecting them into 3D space under the smoothed pose. The output is a full-frame stabilized video sequence with no cropping, evaluated at resolutions up to 720p on the DeepStab dataset.

## Method

RStab's core is Stabilized Rendering (SR), a volume rendering module that projects a target pixel's ray into 3D space, samples L=3 spatial points along an adaptive depth range, and fuses multi-frame features via an MLP to predict per-point volume densities while aggregating colors via weighted linear combination. Two auxiliary modules address dynamic-region failures: Adaptive Ray Range (ARR) constrains the sampling interval to [M−S, M+S] around object surfaces using temporally-weighted multi-frame depth maps (projected and splatted onto the stabilized frame), and Color Correction (CC) refines the geometrically-projected color sampling positions using optical flow from the target frame to neighboring frames. The feature extraction network and MLP are trained end-to-end with MSE loss on LLFF and IBRNetCollected, while depth and flow are provided by a frozen pre-trained Deep3D model, making the color aggregation path training-free at inference.

## Results

On the NUS dataset, RStab achieves a Cropping Ratio of 1.00 (full-frame), Distortion Value of 0.91, and Stability Score of 0.94, outperforming all 2D baselines on D and S while matching their C. On the challenging Selfie dataset (large camera motion, dynamic regions), RStab reaches C=1.00, D=0.92, S=0.95, substantially surpassing the best 2D method (Zhao et al.: D=0.87, S=0.87) and the 3D method Deep3D (C=0.35, D=0.70). On the high-resolution DeepStab dataset, RStab attains C=1.00, D=0.98, S=0.92, tying Deep3D on D and S while achieving full-frame generation (vs. Deep3D's C=0.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six 2D baselines, RStab achieves the best Distortion Value on all three datasets (e.g., 0.91 vs. 0.90 for Zhao et al. on NUS; 0.92 vs. 0.87 on Selfie) while matching their Cropping Ratio of 1.00, confirming that 3D constraints improve structure without sacrificing full-frame coverage. The strongest 2D competitor, Zhao et al. [42], trails RStab by 0.01–0.05 in D and 0.01–0.08 in S across datasets. Against the sole 3D baseline Deep3D [11], RStab matches or exceeds D and S (e.g., D=0.91 vs. 0.90 on NUS; S=0.95 tied on Selfie) while dramatically improving C from 0.66/0.35/0.75 to 1.00, resolving the full-frame limitation of single-frame 3D methods. In the ablation (Table 2), the coarse-to-fine range strategies from ENeRF [15] and IBRNet [34] yield substantially lower PSNR (13.45 and 28.31) and distortion (0.80 for IBRNet) compared to RStab's Adaptive Ray Range (PSNR 40.01, D=0.90), validating that depth-prior-guided sampling is critical for handling dynamic regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

RStab sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: 3D-based video stabilizers (Deep3D, content-preserving warps) that enforce epipolar geometry but operate on single frames, and multi-frame 2D stabilizers (DIFRINT, FuSta) that achieve full-frame output but lack physical structure constraints. The paper's core departure is importing volume rendering from the neural view-synthesis literature (IBRNet, ENeRF) into the stabilization pipeline, fusing multi-frame color and feature information in 3D space rather than blending in 2D. Two auxiliary modules—Adaptive Ray Range (depth-prior-guided sampling) and Color Correction (optical-flow-refined projection)—are introduced specifically to handle dynamic regions that violate the epipolar assumptions underlying both the rendering and the depth estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing trade-off in video stabilization: prior methods could either generate full frames (2D multi-frame) or preserve 3D structure (single-frame 3D), but not both simultaneously. By demonstrating that volume rendering with adaptive depth-constrained sampling achieves both, RStab offers a principled framework that could be extended to other geometry-aware video editing tasks. Limitations include dependence on a pre-trained depth model (Deep3D) and optical flow as preprocessing, a fixed 13-frame sliding window, and training on static-scene datasets (LLFF, IBRNetCollected) rather than video-specific data, which may limit robustness to highly dynamic or long-sequence scenarios. Open questions include real-time feasibility, scalability to longer temporal neighborhoods, and whether the volume-rendering formulation can be made training-free or amortized for online use.
