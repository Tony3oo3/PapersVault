## Problem

Existing pipelines that adapt pre-trained image-language models (e.g., CLIP) to video recognition address the visual/temporal gap with additional temporal learners but overlook a critical textual discrepancy: web-scaled descriptive narratives in CLIP pre-training differ substantially from concise action category names in video datasets. This results in a less separable semantic space where category embeddings cluster densely (validated via t-SNE and pairwise cosine similarity), diminishing the efficacy of category embeddings as decision planes in cross-modal matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip sampled as 8 or 32 frames (224×224) encoded by CLIP ViT-B/16 into frame-level features V ∈ R^{T×d}, combined with offline-generated Spatio-Temporal Descriptors (4 spatial + 4 temporal per class, produced by GPT-3.5 and encoded by the same CLIP text encoder). At inference, the Optimal Descriptor Solver computes per-class matching scores by fusing pooled cosine similarities with entropy-regularized OT transport scores, yielding a final logit vector over all K classes. A linear weight-space ensemble (ratio 0.2) between the fine-tuned and original CLIP weights is applied to mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

## Method

OST has two core components. First, Spatio-Temporal Descriptors are generated by prompting GPT-3.5 (temperature 0.7) to produce 4 spatial descriptors (static visual elements like settings and objects) and 4 temporal descriptors (step-by-step action decomposition) per category; temporal descriptors are further conditioned on the category name via a hard prompt to counteract CLIP's insensitivity to verbs. Second, the Optimal Descriptor Solver formulates video-text matching as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem: a cost matrix C = 1 − cos(V, D_k) is constructed between T frame-level tokens and N descriptor embeddings, and the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm iteratively solves for the optimal transport plan P* ∈ R^{T×N}. The matching score is computed as the Frobenius inner product of P* with the cosine similarity matrix, and the final logit averages four terms (pooled spatial, pooled temporal, OT spatial, OT temporal). Training uses a symmetric KL-divergence contrastive loss over video-to-text and text-to-video logits, fine-tuning CLIP ViT-B/16 on Kinetics-400.

## Results

OST achieves a state-of-the-art zero-shot Top-1 accuracy of 75.1% on Kinetics-600 (32 frames), surpassing the previous best MAXI (71.5%) by 3.6% and ViFi-CLIP (71.2%) by 3.9%. On HMDB-51 and UCF-101, OST reaches 55.9% and 79.7% Top-1 accuracy respectively, outperforming all prior zero-shot methods. In few-shot settings (K=2, fine-tuned on K400), OST achieves 64.8% on HMDB-51 and 90.3% on UCF-101, exceeding MAXI by 6.8% and 3.5% respectively. In fully-supervised settings, OST consistently improves over Text4Vis by 0.1–0.8% across Kinetics-400, Something-Something V2, and ActivityNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OST outperforms all 11 compared baselines across the three zero-shot benchmarks. Against the strongest tuning-based competitors, OST (32 frames) beats ViFi-CLIP by 4.6% on HMDB-51 (55.9 vs 51.3), 2.9% on UCF-101 (79.7 vs 76.8), and 3.9% on K600 (75.1 vs 71.2), and beats MAXI by 3.6% on HMDB-51, 1.5% on UCF-101, and 3.6% on K600. Against temporal-learner-based methods, OST surpasses DiST by 0.5% on HMDB-51 (55.9 vs 55.4) and 7.4% on UCF-101 (79.7 vs 72.3), and outperforms XCLIP by 11.3% on HMDB-51 and 9.9% on K600. Notably, even with only 8 frames, OST already exceeds ViFi-CLIP's 32-frame results on K600 (73.9 vs 71.2), demonstrating the efficiency of the descriptor-based approach. In few-shot settings, OST (fine-tuned on K400) leads MAXI by an average of ~5% on HMDB-51 and ~3% on UCF-101 across all shot counts. In fully-supervised settings, OST provides consistent but modest gains of 0.1–0.8% over Text4Vis across all encoder/frame configurations on K400, SSv2, and ActivityNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of adapting CLIP to video recognition by shifting focus from the visual/temporal side (where prior work like XCLIP, DiST, and ViFi-CLIP add temporal learners) to the textual side, arguing that concise category names create a less separable semantic space in CLIP's embedding. It builds on the line of LLM-augmented visual recognition (CuPL, Menon et al., MAXI) that uses generated descriptions to enrich class representations, but introduces a key distinction: descriptors are split into Spatio and Temporal components and matched to video frames via an entropy-regularized Optimal Transport solver rather than pooled into a single global embedding. The OT formulation draws on the broader optimal transport literature in ML (domain adaptation, prompt learning) but is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application to frame-level video-text matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that the semantic density of video category names in CLIP's text space is a meaningful bottleneck for recognition, not just the temporal modeling gap—reframes how practitioners think about adapting vision-language models to video. The OT-based descriptor matching offers a principled, architecture-agnostic mechanism for instance-level text-visual alignment that could transfer to other open-vocabulary settings. However, the gains are modest (1–4% over strong baselines like ViFi-CLIP and MAXI), the method depends on LLM descriptor quality (hallucination limits descriptor count to ~4), and the approach has not been validated on very long-form or open-world video understanding tasks. Open questions include whether the OT formulation scales efficiently to thousands of classes or very long videos, and whether the Spatio-Temporal decomposition generalizes beyond action recognition to other video understanding tasks.
