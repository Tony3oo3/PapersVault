## Problem

Existing unlearnable example methods for protecting images against personalized text-to-image generation (e.g., DreamBooth) rely heavily on high-frequency adversarial noises that are easily filtered by standard image compression (JPEG, WebP) applied by public platforms. This paper identifies that directly restricting perturbations to the low-frequency domain without additional guidance also fails to protect images, and asks: which pixels actually influence the denoising ability of diffusion models during fine-tuning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (portrait or artwork, 512×512 RGB) and produces a watermarked version by adding a protective noise δ* = x̃₀ − x₀ constrained within an L0 ball of radius Δ. The noise is structured to reside primarily in the low-frequency DCT subspace (top-left ηd×ηd coefficients per channel), making it visually subtle (PSNR ~35 dB) while being robust to JPEG/WebP compression at quality 0.75. The watermark is generated using a pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 2.1) and a set of class-preservation images, requiring no access to the adversary's fine-tuning setup.

## Method

InMark embeds protective watermarks into pixels identified as most influential on the diffusion model's denoising loss, using two complementary mechanisms. First, a DCT-based binary mask confines the PGD search space to the low-frequency subspace (top-left ηd×ηd coefficients), ensuring the noise survives compression. Second, an influence-function-guided update (self-influence I(x₀) = −∇θ SE^T H_{θ*}^{-1} ∇x₀∇θ SE) directs the noise toward pixels that maximally increase the training loss after convergence, rather than merely maximizing the inference-time loss. The optimization alternates between standard masked PGD steps (even epochs) and influence-function steps (odd epochs), with the Hessian computed only on odd epochs to balance cost. This dual strategy yields watermarks that are both visually imperceptible and robust to real-world modifications, outperforming prior methods in protective effectiveness (BRISQUE, FDFR, ISM) while maintaining the best image quality (highest PSNR, lowest RMSE).

## Results

On VGGFace2 image variations, InMark achieves the best visual fidelity (PSNR 34.96, RMSE 4.55) while simultaneously delivering the strongest protection: BRISQUE 43.51 (vs. 35.77 for ASPL and 34.67 for AdvDM) and FDFR 0.48 (vs. 0.26 for ASPL and 0.07 for AdvDM). Under JPEG compression (quality 0.75), InMark is the only method that still degrades generated portraits, whereas all baselines (ASPL, FSMG, AdvDM, and their low-frequency variants) fail completely. InMark also generalizes across personalized techniques: when optimized with DreamBooth loss, it still protects against Textual Inversion and LoRA fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InMark outperforms ASPL [37] and FSMG [37] (Anti-DreamBooth) on all three protection metrics: BRISQUE 43.51 vs. 35.77/35.84, FDFR 0.48 vs. 0.26/0.24, while also preserving better visual quality (PSNR 34.96 vs. 34.42/34.70). Against AdvDM [39], InMark achieves a 7.8-point BRISQUE advantage (43.51 vs. 34.67) and a 6.9× higher FDFR (0.48 vs. 0.07), indicating far stronger disruption of the denoising process. The low-frequency variants (LF-ASPL, LF-FSMG, LF-AdvDM) confirm that merely restricting the search space to the DCT low-frequency subspace without influence functions is insufficient: their BRISQUE scores (22.95–28.30) are actually lower than the clean baseline's protection level, and their FDFR drops to 0.03–0.04. On ISM, InMark (0.32) is competitive with ASPL (0.28) and FSMG (0.33) but slightly worse than AdvDM (0.36), suggesting identity leakage is a shared limitation across methods. Critically, under JPEG/WebP compression, InMark remains effective while all five baselines fail entirely, highlighting the robustness gap that motivates the influence-function design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unlearnable-examples line of work for diffusion models (AdvDM, ASPL, FSMG) by replacing the standard projected-gradient-descent adversarial noise with an influence-function-guided watermark confined to the DCT low-frequency subspace. Conceptually, it departs from prior methods that treat unlearnable examples as inference-time adversarial examples (maximizing loss w.r.t. a fixed model) by instead using self-influence to identify pixels whose perturbation most increases the *training* loss after convergence, bridging robust statistics (Cook & Weisberg; Koh & Liang) with adversarial optimization (Madry et al.). The DCT subspace constraint borrows from low-frequency adversarial perturbation (Guo et al.), but the paper shows that this constraint alone is insufficient without the influence-function component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical gap addressed—prior unlearnable examples collapse under ubiquitous JPEG/WebP compression on public platforms—is a critical barrier to real-world deployment of image protection, and InMark's survival under compression is a meaningful step forward. However, the method requires iterative model fine-tuning during watermark generation (including Hessian computation), which limits scalability to very large diffusion models, and robustness is demonstrated only at a single compression quality (0.75) on two datasets. Open questions include whether the influence-function guidance generalizes to more aggressive or unknown modifications (e.g., super-resolution, style transfer by platforms), and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation pipelines where the threat of unauthorized personalization is equally acute.
