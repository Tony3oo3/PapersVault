## Problem

Density-based low-shot counters achieve accurate total counts but provide no individual object locations or sizes, while detection-based counters output bounding boxes yet fall far behind in count accuracy. Both paradigms also suffer from false positives in multi-class scenes due to the specificity-generalization tradeoff: high recall requires broad appearance matching, which activates on incorrect object categories. No prior method simultaneously delivers accurate counts, precise detections, and robustness to distractor classes across few-shot, zero-shot, and text-prompt settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image resized so that the mean exemplar width/height falls between 10 and 50 pixels, along with three exemplar bounding boxes (few-shot), a CLIP text prompt (prompt-based), or nothing (zero-shot, where a two-pass bootstrap estimates object sizes). It outputs per-object bounding boxes, a total count estimated by summing an updated location density map, and the density map itself. The density map is refined post-detection by zeroing all values outside the retained bounding boxes, so the final count can differ from the number of detections and compensate for missed objects in dense regions.

## Method

DAVE introduces a two-stage detect-and-verify pipeline. In the detection stage, it reuses LOCA's ResNet-50 + transformer backbone to produce a high-recall density map, extracts centers via NMS, then fuses multi-scale backbone features with the prototype-correlation similarity tensor through a Feature Fusion Module (FFM) before a dense box-regression head predicts per-pixel edge distances. In the verification stage, a shallow projection network φ(·) extracts appearance features for each candidate and exemplar; spectral clustering on cosine similarities partitions candidates, and only clusters containing at least one exemplar (or, in zero-shot, clusters ≥45% of the largest; in prompt-based, clusters with ≥85% of the best text-prompt similarity) are retained. Training is two-phase: the FFM and box head are supervised with GIoU loss on the three exemplar boxes, then φ(·) is trained with a contrastive cosine-similarity loss on stitched pairs of images from different classes. This design lets DAVE inherit LOCA's strong density estimation while adding detection output and outlier rejection, yielding simultaneous improvements in both count MAE and detection AP.

## Results

On the FSC147 few-shot density-based counting benchmark, DAVE achieves MAE 8.91 (val) / 8.66 (test) and RMSE 28.08 (val) / 32.36 (test), outperforming the previous best LOCA by 13% MAE (val) and 20% MAE (test), and by a remarkable 43% RMSE on the test set. On the FSCD147 detection benchmark, DAVE reaches AP 24.20 / 26.81 and AP50 61.08 / 62.82 (val/test), surpassing C-DETR by 40% AP and 45% AP50 on validation. In detection-based counting (count = number of boxes), DAVE achieves MAE 9.75 / 10.45, beating C-DETR by 38% MAE and also outperforming all density-based counters including LOCA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAVE wins against all 17 baselines across every evaluation setting. In few-shot density-based counting on FSC147, DAVE outperforms the long-standing top method LOCA [6] by 13% MAE (val) and 20% MAE (test), and by 43% RMSE on the test set; it also beats CounTR [16], SAFECount [38], BMNet+ [28], VCN [24], CFOCNet [37], MAML [10], FamNet [26], and GMN [20] by wide margins. In few-shot detection on FSCD147, DAVE surpasses C-DETR [22] by 40% AP and 45% AP50 (val), and by 18% AP and 24% AP50 (test), while also outperforming adapted detectors FSDetView [34] and AttRPN [9] by even larger gaps. In detection-based counting, DAVE^box (MAE 10.45 test) beats C-DETR (MAE 16.79) by 38% and also outperforms all density-based counters, a first for a detection-based method. In zero-shot counting, DAVE_0-shot beats LOCA [6] by 11% MAE (val) and 7% MAE (test), and outperforms RCC [13], RepRPN-C [25], and CounTR [16]. In prompt-based counting, DAVE_prm outperforms CounTX [1] by 12% MAE (val) and 5% MAE (test), and beats CLIP-Count [15] and ZeroClip [36] by larger margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAVE re-purposes the LOCA [6] architecture for its initial density estimation stage, then departs from the density-based paradigm by appending a bounding-box regression head and a spectral-clustering verification stage that jointly improve recall and precision. It also departs from the recent detection-based counter C-DETR [22], which performs direct few-shot detection, by instead using density estimation as a high-recall candidate generator and clustering as a precision filter. The detect-and-verify framing unifies these two previously separate lines of work (density-based and detection-based counting) into a single pipeline, and extends naturally to zero-shot and CLIP-based text-prompt settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAVE bridges a long-standing applicability gap: density-based counters give accurate totals but no object locations, while detection-based counters give locations but worse counts. By showing that detection-based counting (DAVE^box) can outperform all published density-based counters in MAE, the paper challenges the assumption that density summation is inherently superior for count estimation. The main open limitation is performance degradation in extremely dense regions with very small objects, where the detection stage still misses objects; the authors defer this to future work. The clean two-stage paradigm (high-recall detection + appearance-based verification) is generalizable and could inform other class-agnostic perception tasks.
