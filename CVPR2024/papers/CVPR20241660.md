## Problem

Previous co-speech gesture generation methods produce 2D/3D skeletons that entirely discard appearance information, requiring labor-intensive post-processing (e.g., Blender rendering or separate image synthesizers) for visualization. Directly generating gesture videos is challenging because existing video diffusion models use VAE-derived latent features with ill-defined semantics that fail to preserve human motion structure, and they are limited to fixed-length outputs that cannot capture the inherent temporal dependencies between gestures and speech of arbitrary duration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source image of the speaker (cropped to 256×256) and a speech audio clip (4–15 seconds, resampled at 25 fps) as input, and produces a temporally aligned co-speech gesture video at 256×256 resolution. The source image provides all appearance information (identity, clothing, background), while the audio drives motion; the output video can be extended to arbitrary length via the optimal motion selection module, which generates and selects among multiple candidate segments to maintain coherence and consistency across segment boundaries.

## Method

The framework decouples motion from video using a nonlinear thin-plate spline (TPS) transformation: a keypoint predictor estimates K groups of N adaptive boundary keypoints per frame, which define TPS transformations to estimate optical flow and warp the source image, yielding compact latent motion features (K×N×2 per frame) that encode both motion trajectories and region shapes. A transformer-based diffusion model (DDPM with x₀-prediction) then generates these latent motion sequences conditioned on WavLM + hand-crafted audio features, using self-attention for intra-motion temporal interactions and cross-attention for speech-motion alignment, with geometric velocity and acceleration losses for physical plausibility. An optimal motion selection module extends generation to arbitrary lengths by sampling P candidate segments per chunk and selecting the one minimizing a score based on keypoint position continuity and velocity direction consistency. Finally, a UNet-like refinement network with residual blocks, guided by MobileSAM segmentation of hands and face plus occlusion masks, recovers missing fine-grained details in a separate pass to avoid disrupting the motion modeling balance.

## Results

On the PATS test set (4 speakers, 1,200 clips each), the proposed method achieves FGD of 18.13, Diversity of 5.63, and FVD of 2058.19, substantially outperforming ANGIE (FGD 55.66, FVD 2965.29) and MM-Diffusion (FGD 41.63, FVD 2656.06). In subjective MOS evaluation, the method scores 3.79/5 on Realness, 3.91/5 on Diversity, 3.90/5 on Synchrony, and 3.77/5 on Overall quality, compared to ANGIE's 2.00–2.53 range and MM-Diffusion's 1.47–2.02 range. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is BAS (ANGIE: 0.1504 vs. Ours: 0.1273), which the authors attribute to ANGIE's audio-synchronized tremors rather than genuine gesture alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: ANGIE (NeurIPS 2022), the prior SOTA for audio-driven co-speech gesture video generation using MRAA + VQ-VAE + GPT-like prediction, and MM-Diffusion (CVPR 2023), a general multi-modal diffusion model for joint audio-video generation. The proposed method decisively outperforms both on FGD (18.13 vs. 55.66 for ANGIE and 41.63 for MM-Diffusion), FVD (2058 vs. 2965 and 2656), and all four MOS dimensions (e.g., Overall 3.77 vs. 2.00 and 1.47). ANGIE's linear MRAA motion representation produces coarse, ellipse-like hand deformations and autoregressive error accumulation, while MM-Diffusion struggles to capture body structure, often generating missing or malformed hands. The sole exception is BAS, where ANGIE scores marginally higher (0.1504 vs. 0.1273) due to audio-synchronized tremors that inflate the beat-alignment metric without reflecting true gesture-speech synchrony, as confirmed by the much lower Synchrony MOS (2.19 vs. 3.90).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the co-speech gesture generation line from skeleton-based outputs (GANs, VAEs, Flows, diffusion models on pose sequences) to direct video synthesis, building most closely on ANGIE [23], which first formulated audio-driven co-speech gesture video generation using the linear MRAA motion representation. The key methodological departure is replacing MRAA's linear affine model with a nonlinear thin-plate spline (TPS) transformation that preserves region shape and appearance information, and replacing ANGIE's VQ-VAE + GPT pipeline with a transformer-based DDPM operating in a purpose-built latent motion space. The diffusion model's x0-prediction formulation follows Ramesh et al. [28], the optical flow and warping pipeline follows Zhao & Zhang [55], and WavLM features [9] are adopted for semantically rich audio conditioning, distinguishing this work from general-purpose video diffusion methods (MM-Diffusion [30], LaMD [17]) whose VAE latents lack well-defined motion semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework that generates co-speech gesture videos end-to-end without any structural human skeleton prior, addressing a practical gap where skeleton-based methods require labor-intensive avatar binding or produce inter-frame jitters. The TPS-based motion decoupling offers a reusable principle—using a small set of adaptive keypoints to parameterize a nonlinear deformation field—that could transfer to other motion-conditioned video tasks. However, evaluation is limited to four speakers at 256×256 resolution and 25 fps, and only two baselines are compared; open questions include scalability to diverse populations, higher resolutions, real-time inference, and whether the optimal motion selection module generalizes beyond the 4–15 second gesture unit assumption.
