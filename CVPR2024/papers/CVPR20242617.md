## Problem

Existing visual hand pose tracking methods model the hand as a joint-actuated multi-body system, directly applying joint torques to produce motion. This simplification ignores the underlying musculoskeletal system (30+ muscles and tendons) that physiologically constrains hand dynamics, leading to unnatural or infeasible robot-like movements. The paper asks whether integrating a full musculoskeletal model into a learnable parametric hand model can impose realistic biomechanical constraints to improve tracking accuracy and temporal consistency.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes an RGB video sequence and the per-frame MANO joint poses and shape parameters from an off-the-shelf estimator. Velocities are computed via finite differences (wrist joint excluded, yielding 15×3 vectors). The IDNet receives two consecutive (pose, velocity) pairs (16×3×4 = 192-d input) and outputs 31 muscle excitation signals; the Refine Net then takes the predicted pose (48-d) and the simulator's reference pose (45-d, wrist ignored) to produce a 48-d refined pose. Inference adds only ~9 ms over the base estimator.

## Method

MS-MANO integrates the MyoHand muscle-tendon model (from OpenSim's MoBL and 2nd-Hand anatomical data) into the MANO parametric hand by converting bone-centric muscle attachment points to a joint-centric representation, so muscles adapt to shape variations. BioPR operates as a simulation-in-the-loop: an IDNet trained with PPO reinforcement learning learns inverse dynamics (mapping pose/velocity transitions to muscle excitation signals), supervised by matching muscle torques against a PD-controller reference. The excitation signals drive a forward dynamics simulation in RFUniverse (Hill-type muscle model with 31 hand + 8 wrist muscles) to produce a biomechanically valid reference pose. A lightweight MLP (Refine Net, single 64-d hidden layer) then fuses the predicted and reference poses to output the final refined pose, trained with a simple L1 loss against ground truth. The key insight is treating the musculoskeletal simulation not as a replacement for visual observation but as a biomechanical prior that regularizes per-frame predictions.

## Results

On DexYCB, BioPR applied to gSDF reduces MPJPE from 14.4 mm to 12.81 mm and improves AUC from 89.1 to 89.7, while Deformer+BioPR achieves the best AUC of 90.4 with MPJPE 12.92 mm. The framework adds only 0.11M total parameters (0.1M for IDNet, 0.01M for RefineNet) and incurs just 9 ms of additional inference cost. On the anatomic accuracy evaluation, MS-MANO produces ring-finger bending trajectories closely matching MyoSuite, with a slightly faster response to muscle stimulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioPR consistently improves both modern transformer-based baselines: gSDF's MPJPE drops from 14.4 to 12.81 mm (−1.59 mm) and Deformer's from 13.64 to 12.92 mm (−0.72 mm), while AUC improves by 0.6 and 0.8 points respectively. Against the older baselines (VIBE, TCMR, MeshGraphormer), the BioPR-enhanced methods lead by 3.2–4.1 mm in MPJPE and 20+ points in AUC, confirming a substantial gap over non-temporal or graph-based approaches. For anatomic fidelity, MS-MANO is validated against MyoSuite (the MyoHand model); the ring-finger joint trajectories under FDS4_R excitation show similar movement patterns, with MS-MANO exhibiting a marginally faster activation response. The paper notes that BioPR's gains are most visible under occlusion and motion blur, where the biomechanical prior compensates for degraded visual features. No comparison shows BioPR degrading performance relative to its base method on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work integrating musculoskeletal systems into parametric body models (BASH for SMPL, RCareWorld for SMPLX) by specifically targeting the hand, where prior full-body systems lack detailed muscle modeling. It departs from the dominant joint-actuated paradigm in visual hand tracking (gSDF, Deformer, VIBE) by replacing direct joint torques with Hill-type muscle-tendon dynamics, and introduces a simulation-in-the-loop refinement (BioPR) that uses a physics simulator as a biomechanical prior within a learning pipeline. The MS-MANO model itself is constructed by adapting bone-centric MyoHand/MyoSuite muscle data into the joint-centric MANO framework, a non-trivial mapping that had not been done for a learnable hand model before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MS-MANO provides a reusable, differentiable musculoskeletal hand model that could benefit a range of downstream tasks beyond pose tracking (motion generation, robotics, animation). The BioPR framework demonstrates that a tiny plug-and-play refinement (0.1M params, 9 ms inference) can consistently improve strong baselines, suggesting biomechanical priors are underexploited in visual hand analysis. However, the approach remains dependent on base-model quality, struggles with extremely slow motions, and has only been validated on two datasets with single-hand scenarios; open questions include bimanual extension, real-time deployment, and generalization to diverse object interactions.
