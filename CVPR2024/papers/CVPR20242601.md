## Problem

Diffusion models excel at generating realistic textures for image restoration but introduce temporal flickering when applied to video super-resolution due to their inherent sampling randomness. Existing temporal consistency mechanisms (3D convolutions, temporal attention, cross-frame attention) only constrain short local sequences and fail to maintain low-level texture coherence across longer videos, leaving a gap in both local and global temporal consistency for real-world VSR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242601/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution video with unknown mixed degradations (noise, blur, compression, flickering) and produces a ×4 upscaled high-resolution video. Two optional user controls modulate the output: a text prompt that steers texture generation (e.g., 'animal fur') via Classifier-Free Guidance, and a noise level injected into the input that trades off fidelity (low noise) against detail generation (high noise). The model was trained on 80×80 spatial crops of length 8 frames, and can operate with or without text prompts since it was trained on both labeled and null-prompt data.

## Method

Upscale-A-Video builds on the pretrained Stable Diffusion ×4 Upscaler by inflating 2D convolutions to 3D and fine-tuning only inserted temporal layers (temporal attention with RoPE and 3D residual blocks) in the U-Net, then separately fine-tuning the VAE-Decoder with 3D residual blocks and a Spatial Feature Transform layer for color fidelity. For global consistency across segments, a training-free flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module uses RAFT optical flow at latent resolution, validates flow via forward-backward consistency error as an occlusion mask, and bidirectionally warps and fuses predicted latents (ẑ₀) across frames with a fusion weight β=0.5. This propagation is applied only at user-specified diffusion steps (T*), allowing it to be scheduled early for minor jitter or late for severe artifacts. The approach is trained via transfer learning on WebVid10M and a newly collected YouHQ dataset (37K 1080p clips) using v-prediction loss, with the VAE-Decoder additionally trained with L1, LPIPS, and temporal PatchGAN adversarial losses.

## Results

Upscale-A-Video achieves the highest PSNR on all four synthetic benchmarks: 25.32 on SPMCS, 30.79 on UDM10, 24.41 on REDS30, and 25.83 on YouHQ40, outperforming the strongest CNN-based baseline RealBasicVSR by 0.81 dB on SPMCS and 1.19 dB on UDM10. On the real-world VideoLQ dataset, it attains the best no-reference scores (CLIP-IQA 0.530, MUSIQ 57.99, DOVER 7.811), and on AIGC30 it leads in CLIP-IQA (0.674) and DOVER (11.67). For temporal consistency, it records the lowest flow-warping error on UDM10 (0.446) and competitive scores on SPMCS (0.367) and YouHQ40 (0.737), surpassing all diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Upscale-A-Video achieves the best PSNR on all four synthetic datasets, beating the strongest CNN baseline RealBasicVSR by 0.81 dB (SPMCS) and 1.19 dB (UDM10), and the strongest diffusion baseline StableSR by 1.69 dB (SPMCS) and 4.26 dB (UDM10). On temporal consistency (E*warp), it outperforms all diffusion-based methods (SD×4, ResShift, StableSR) by large margins but trails the CNN-based RealVSR on SPMCS (0.367 vs 0.294), REDS30 (1.278 vs 0.378), and YouHQ40 (0.737 vs 0.485), while winning on UDM10 (0.446 vs 0.531). On the real-world VideoLQ benchmark, it leads all baselines in every no-reference metric (CLIP-IQA 0.530 vs 0.430 ResShift; MUSIQ 57.99 vs 55.33 RealBasicVSR; DOVER 7.811 vs 7.562 RealBasicVSR). On AIGC30, it achieves the best CLIP-IQA (0.674) and DOVER (11.67) but is slightly behind RealBasicVSR on MUSIQ (57.66 vs 58.87). The image-only baselines (Real-ESRGAN, SD×4 Upscaler) are consistently outperformed, with SD×4 showing particularly poor temporal consistency (E*warp 5.008 on SPMCS) due to its lack of video-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image diffusion-based super-resolution line (StableSR, ResShift) to the video domain by fine-tuning the pretrained Stable Diffusion ×4 Upscaler with temporal layers, following the ModelScope paradigm of inflating 2D convolutions to 3D and training only the new temporal components while freezing spatial layers. Its key departure from prior video diffusion work is the explicit local-global temporal strategy: local consistency is enforced via temporal attention and 3D convolutions in both the U-Net and VAE-Decoder, while global consistency across segments is achieved through a novel training-free, flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module that warps and fuses latents bidirectionally. This addresses two gaps identified in prior methods—persistent texture flickering at the low-level (solved by VAE-Decoder fine-tuning) and limited temporal reach of attention mechanisms (solved by long-range latent propagation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first practical diffusion-based framework for real-world VSR that simultaneously handles complex unknown degradations, maintains temporal coherence over long sequences, and offers user-controllable generation via text prompts and noise levels. The finding that fine-tuning the VAE-Decoder (not just the U-Net) is critical for eliminating low-level flickering is a useful insight for the broader diffusion-for-restoration community. Open questions include scalability to very long videos (the propagation module's cost grows with sequence length), generalization to other diffusion backbones beyond SD ×4 Upscaler, and whether the training-free propagation can be replaced by learned mechanisms for even better long-range coherence. The 32×A100 training requirement also limits immediate adoption.
