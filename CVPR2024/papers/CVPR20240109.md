## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained deep networks on small target datasets suffers from overfitting, and existing regularization methods either require auxiliary source information (source labels or datasets) that is often unavailable (e.g., CLIP's WebImageText) or incur heavy additional computation. The simpler random feature regularization (RandReg) avoids these requirements but uses a fixed class-agnostic prior whose effectiveness depends heavily on the pre-training method, can underperform plain L2 regularization, and degrades feature norms and entropy, thereby limiting mutual information between features and labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images as input and produces a K-class label via a linear classification head on top of a pre-trained feature extractor (ResNet-50 or ViT-B). The regularization operates on the d-dimensional feature vectors g_φ(x) produced by the backbone; class-conditional Gaussian priors are initialized by forwarding the target dataset through the frozen pre-trained model to compute per-class mean and variance statistics, requiring no source dataset or labels beyond the target task.

## Method

AdaRand replaces RandReg's fixed class-agnostic prior with a mixture of class-conditional diagonal Gaussians N(μ_k, σ_k²I), where reference vectors z^i are sampled from the component matching the input's label y^i. The prior means μ are dynamically updated during fine-tuning by minimizing two losses: an intra-class term ℓ_intra that pulls each μ_k toward the exponential moving average of the current feature vectors for class k, and an inter-class term ℓ_inter that maximizes pairwise cosine distances between class means to enforce separability. This adaptive update prevents the feature norm and entropy collapse observed with fixed priors, preserving the gradient magnitude of the cross-entropy loss and the mutual information I(g_φ(x); y). The overall training objective is the standard cross-entropy loss plus λ‖g_φ(x) − z‖², with the prior parameters updated on a separate schedule (step size ξ) alongside the model parameters (step size η).

## Results

On the Cars dataset with ResNet-50 pre-trained on ImageNet classification, AdaRand achieves 91.17% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best RandReg variant (90.61%), FNP (90.27%), and DR-Tune (90.38%). Across six fine-grained datasets (Aircraft, Birds, Cars, DTD, Flowers, Pets), AdaRand attains the best or near-best accuracy in every setting, with gains of 1.5–3.5% over plain fine-tuning. On CLIP-pretrained ViT-B/16, AdaRand reaches 92.84% on Cars, surpassing RandReg (91.33%) and FNP (91.24%) by over 1.5 points. Even with only 10% of the Cars training data, AdaRand achieves 27.55% accuracy versus 26.45% for the best RandReg variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaRand consistently outperforms all RandReg variants (U(0,1), N(0,1), N(μs,σs²), and conditional-prior without update) across all six datasets, with the largest margin on DTD (+2.3% over best RandReg) and Aircraft (+2.5%). Against FNP [10], AdaRand gains 1.4–3.5% across datasets, confirming that adaptive class-conditional priors are strictly better than simple ℓ₂ feature norm penalty. DR-Tune [38] is the only baseline that beats AdaRand on one dataset (Pets: 93.24% vs. 92.34%), but AdaRand dominates on the other five datasets. Among methods requiring auxiliary source information, AdaRand surpasses Co-Tuning [33] (91.17% vs. 90.66%) and UOT [21] (91.17% vs. 90.82%) on Cars without needing source labels or datasets. Compared to methods with heavy computation, AdaRand outperforms Core-tuning [36] (91.17% vs. 90.01%) and DR-Tune [38] (91.17% vs. 90.38%) on Cars while using less GPU memory (8,585 MiB vs. 24,223 MiB for Core-tuning) and less time per epoch (13.8s vs. 25.5s for DR-Tune). L2SP [18], DELTA [19], and BSS [5] all trail AdaRand by 1.5–2.7% on Cars, confirming that parameter/output-level regularization is less effective than feature-space regularization with adaptive priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RandReg (Zhong & Maki, CVPR 2020), which regularizes fine-tuning by penalizing the ℓ₂ distance between feature vectors and random reference vectors drawn from a fixed class-agnostic prior. The key departure is replacing that fixed prior with class-conditional Gaussian distributions whose mean parameters are adaptively updated during training to track the evolving feature statistics and maximize inter-class separation. The paper also positions itself against a broader family of fine-tuning regularizers that either require auxiliary source information (L2SP, DELTA, Co-Tuning, UOT) or incur heavy additional computation (Core-tuning, DR-Tune), arguing that AdaRand achieves comparable or better accuracy without either requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaRand offers a drop-in regularization term that works across supervised, self-supervised, and CLIP pre-training with both CNN and ViT backbones, requiring no source labels, source datasets, or extra stored features—addressing a practical gap for models like CLIP where source data is unavailable. The diagnosis that RandReg's fixed prior silently degrades feature norms and entropy (thereby limiting mutual information) is a useful mechanistic insight for understanding why naive random regularization can underperform. Limitations include evaluation restricted to classification tasks and a modest absolute gain (~0.5–1.5%) over the best RandReg variant; the authors note extending to generative tasks (e.g., diffusion models) as future work, and the method's interaction with more complex downstream objectives remains unexplored.
