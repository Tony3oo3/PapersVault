## Problem

Existing saliency ranking detection (SRD) datasets rely on mouse-trajectory data as a proxy for human visual attention, which is fundamentally different from real eye-fixation: mouse pointing is voluntary while saccadic eye movements are reflexive, and the two modalities are processed in different neural reference frames. Additionally, these datasets inherit MS-COCO annotation errors (missing, merged, or incorrect objects) and impose arbitrary caps on the number of salient instances, limiting their ability to model complex multi-object scenes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 1024×1024 and produces three outputs: pixel-level saliency ranking maps, instance segmentation masks, and per-instance ranking scores. Unlike prior methods that constrain outputs to a fixed maximum number of objects, QAGNet predicts rankings for up to 200 salient instances per scene with no arbitrary cap, retaining only predictions exceeding a 0.7 confidence threshold at inference.

## Method

QAGNet builds on Mask2Former's query-based transformer detector, extracting 9 salient instance query representatives (3 decoder layers × 3 feature scales) that serve as regional proposals. These queries are organized into a novel tri-tiered nested graph: Single-Scale Graphs (SSGs) aggregate features within one scale per instance, Multi-Scale Graphs (MSGs) combine across scales, and a Global Relationship Graph (GRG) models inter-instance relationships. A Representative Aggregation (RA) stage refines features bottom-up through this hierarchy, while a Representative Feedback (RF) stage feeds ranking-aware cues back top-down, enabling bi-directional information flow. Graph Attention Networks with 256-dim features perform all edge and node updates, and the model is trained end-to-end with a combination of mask loss, saliency classification loss, and pair-wise SRD ranking loss.

## Results

On the three SRD datasets (ASSR, IRSR, SIFR), QAGNet with Swin-L backbone achieves the best SA-SOR scores of 0.7873, 0.6468, and 0.6206 respectively, surpassing the next-best non-QAGNet method (OCOR-L at 0.6474 on ASSR, IRSR-L at 0.5648 on IRSR, IRSR-L at 0.5585 on SIFR) by 14.0, 8.2, and 6.2 percentage points. On MAE, QAGNet-SwinL-L achieves 0.0478, 0.0767, and 0.0416, again the best across all datasets. Notably, QAGNet uses substantially fewer parameters (218.8M for Swin-L) than OCOR (401.7M) while outperforming it on SA-SOR and MAE, though OCOR retains a slight edge on the SOR metric (0.8937 vs 0.8535 on ASSR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QAGNet decisively outperforms all five baselines on SA-SOR and MAE across all three datasets. On ASSR, QAGNet-SwinL-L achieves SA-SOR 0.7873 vs. OCOR-L's 0.6474 (+14.0 pp) and IRSR-L's 0.7090 (+7.8 pp); on SIFR, it reaches 0.6206 vs. IRSR-L's 0.5585 (+6.2 pp). The SOR metric tells a more nuanced story: OCOR-L achieves the highest SOR on ASSR (0.8937) and IRSR (0.8184), slightly edging out QAGNet (0.8535 and 0.8240), because SOR does not penalize missing or low-quality segmentation—only rank order among matched instances. The paper explicitly notes this limitation, arguing SA-SOR (which incorporates IoU-based matching) is a more discriminative metric. In terms of efficiency, QAGNet-SwinB (110.2M params) matches or exceeds OCOR (401.7M params) on SA-SOR and MAE, and QAGNet-SwinL (218.8M) uses roughly half the parameters of OCOR while achieving superior segmentation-aware ranking. The weakest baselines are ASSR (Siris et al.) and RSDNet (Islam et al.), which trail by 10–25 percentage points on SA-SOR, reflecting their older architectures and reliance on mouse-trajectory-derived ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mask2Former query-based transformer architecture for salient instance detection, repurposing its 9-layer decoder queries as node features for a novel tri-tiered nested graph network (QAGNet). It departs fundamentally from all prior SRD datasets (ASSR, IRSR, COCO-SalRank), which derive saliency rankings from mouse-trajectory data in SALICON, by instead using genuine eye-tracking fixations recorded with a Tobii Pro Nano. The method also extends prior SRD approaches (RSDNet, ASSR, IRSR, SOR, OCOR) that constrain outputs to a fixed number of objects, by ranking up to 200 instances per scene without an arbitrary cap, arguing that less salient objects still carry ranking-relevant information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SIFR dataset challenges a foundational assumption in the SRD literature—that voluntary mouse pointing is an adequate proxy for reflexive saccadic eye movements—and provides the first large-scale benchmark grounded in actual human visual attention. The substantial SA-SOR gains (11–14.5% over prior SOTA) suggest that data-source alignment with the perceptual process being modeled matters as much as architectural innovation. Open questions include whether the 8-participant, 3-second free-viewing protocol generalizes to longer or more interactive viewing, whether the mouse-vs-fixation distinction holds when models are trained and tested on the same data source, and whether the tri-tiered graph design scales beyond 200 queries without prohibitive compute.
