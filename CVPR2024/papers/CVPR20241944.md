## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detection methods represent all background proposals with a single learnable 'background' embedding, which discards the diverse implicit knowledge about unseen categories contained within those proposals. This leads to loss of crucial background information, incomplete representations of novel categories, and model overfitting toward base classes due to the abundance of fully supervised foreground data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241944/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images annotated only with base-class bounding boxes (no novel category names or additional supervision). At inference, it additionally receives the names of novel categories to compute detection probabilities. The detector outputs bounding boxes with class labels for both base and novel categories, and can also produce instance segmentation masks. The framework is built on Faster R-CNN with a frozen CLIP model providing both image and text encoders for knowledge distillation.

## Method

LBP introduces three modules to exploit implicit knowledge in background proposals without any prior knowledge of novel categories. First, Background Category-specific Prompt (BCP) estimates the number of underlying background categories via k-means clustering on CLIP features of background proposals, then learns category-specific context vectors as prompts for each estimated category, replacing the single background embedding with multiple learned embeddings and a relaxed loss that activates when a proposal's probability mass on estimated categories falls below a threshold. Second, Background Object Discovery (BOD) operates online during training, using k-means cluster centers as embedding references to generate pseudo-labels for background proposals, enabling the detector to learn from implicit objects and reduce base-class bias. Third, Inference Probability Rectification (IPR) resolves conceptual overlap between estimated background categories and novel categories at inference by re-estimating the background contribution in the softmax denominator using a sample-agnostic conditional probability derived from cosine similarity between category embeddings, preventing underestimation of novel-class scores.

## Results

On OV-COCO (V-OVD setting), LBP achieves AP50^n=35.9, AP50^b=60.8, and AP50=54.3, outperforming the strongest baseline BARON† (35.8/58.2/52.3) by +0.1/+2.6/+2.0 respectively. With a smaller batch size (LBP†), novel-class AP50^n further rises to 37.8. On OV-LVIS, LBP† reaches APr=24.1 for object detection and APr=23.7 for instance segmentation, surpassing BARON† (23.2/22.6) by +0.9/+1.1 points on rare-category AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the primary V-OVD setting on OV-COCO, LBP outperforms all compared baselines: it beats BARON† by +0.1 on AP50^n and +2.6 on AP50^b, surpasses ViLD by +8.3 on AP50^n, and exceeds OADP by +5.9 on AP50^n. The LBP† variant (batch size 16) further pushes AP50^n to 37.8, a +2.0 gain over BARON†. On OV-LVIS, LBP† improves upon BARON† by +0.9 on APr for object detection and by +1.1 on APr for instance segmentation, while also leading on APc and APf. Against DetPro [4] on OV-LVIS, LBP† gains +3.3 on APr (24.1 vs. 20.8) for detection. The paper notes that LBP's advantage is most pronounced in the V-OVD setting (no external knowledge), where it achieves the best AP50 overall (54.3), confirming that the background-prompt learning strategy is effective without relying on novel-class names or weakly-supervised annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge-distillation-based OVD line (ViLD, BARON, DetPro) by rethinking how background proposals are interpreted. Rather than using a single learnable background embedding or a uniform soft background loss, LBP estimates multiple underlying background categories via k-means clustering on CLIP features and learns category-specific prompt contexts for each, drawing on prompt tuning ideas from CoOp/CoCoOp. The Background Object Discovery module adapts the pseudo-labeling strategy from VL-PLM to operate online during training, while the Inference Probability Rectification module is a novel correction for conceptual overlap between estimated background categories and novel classes at test time. The method is designed as a plug-in module that integrates with existing OVD frameworks like BARON and ViLD rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underexplored failure mode in OVD: treating all background proposals as a single class discards implicit structural knowledge about novel categories that are present in the training data. The plug-and-play design (no prior knowledge of novel class names, no additional datasets) makes the approach broadly applicable to existing OVD pipelines. However, the improvements over strong baselines like BARON are modest (≈2% on key metrics), the k-means estimation of the number of background categories is heuristic, and the IPR module relies on a sample-agnostic conditional probability assumption that may not hold in all regimes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large vocabularies beyond LVIS and whether the background category estimation can be made more principled than k-means.
