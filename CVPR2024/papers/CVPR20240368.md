## Problem

Most group robustness strategies for mitigating spurious correlations require access to group labels in training or validation data, which is impractical in real-world settings where underlying spurious attributes are unknown. Existing unsupervised approaches, such as GEORGE, cluster raw feature representations to infer pseudo-labels, but these representations are highly entangled and do not isolate the features actually driving predictions. The paper asks whether a more targeted representation—explainability heatmaps that highlight only task-relevant pixels—can yield better pseudo-labels for worst-group robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240368/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen ERM base model (ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50) and a validation set of RGB images with class labels but no group labels. It produces pseudo group labels (class × estimated spurious attribute) for the validation data, which are then fed into an off-the-shelf group robustness retraining strategy (e.g., DFR or JTT) to yield a retrained classifier with improved worst-group accuracy. The pseudo-labels can be generated for the training set by simply repeating the same procedure, making the approach compatible with any strategy that consumes group labels.

## Method

ExMap is a two-stage, strategy-agnostic mechanism. In stage one, LRP (Layer-wise Relevance Propagation) is applied to the frozen ERM model on validation images to produce pixel-level relevance heatmaps that capture which input regions the model actually uses for its prediction. In stage two, these heatmaps are clustered globally (G-ExMap) using spectral clustering with an eigengap heuristic to estimate the number of underlying model strategies, yielding pseudo spurious-attribute labels that are combined with class labels to form pseudo group labels. The key insight is that heatmaps, unlike raw penultimate-layer features, discard entangled irrelevant information and focus exclusively on task-relevant pixel regions, making the resulting clusters more aligned with true spurious-correlation groups. The generated pseudo-labels are then used in place of ground-truth group labels during retraining with any existing group robustness method (DFR, JTT, Group-DRO), and the approach is further shown to generalize to the multiple-shortcut setting where mitigating one spurious attribute can exacerbate reliance on another.

## Results

On Waterbirds, DFR+ExMap achieves 92.5% worst-group accuracy (WGA) and 96.0% mean accuracy, outperforming the closest unsupervised baseline GEORGE(DFR) (91.7% WGA) and approaching the partially supervised DFR (92.1% WGA). On CelebA, DFR+ExMap reaches 84.4% WGA and 91.8% mean accuracy, surpassing GEORGE(DFR) (83.3% WGA) and remaining within 5% of the fully supervised Group DRO (88.9% WGA). On C-MNIST, Urbancars(BG), and Urbancars(CoObj), DFR+ExMap consistently outperforms GEORGE(DFR) by 0.8–2.5% WGA while also improving mean accuracy on Urbancars variants (93.2% vs. 83.6% and 91.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExMap's primary competitor is GEORGE(DFR), the closest unsupervised method that clusters raw penultimate-layer features instead of explainability heatmaps. DFR+ExMap outperforms GEORGE(DFR) on every dataset: +0.8% WGA on Waterbirds (92.5 vs. 91.7), +1.1% on CelebA (84.4 vs. 83.3), +0.8% on C-MNIST (72.5 vs. 71.7), +2.3% on Urbancars(BG) (71.4 vs. 69.1), and +2.3% on Urbancars(CoObj) (79.2 vs. 76.9). Against the partially supervised DFR (which uses validation group labels), ExMap matches or exceeds it on Waterbirds (92.5 vs. 92.1 WGA) and remains within 2.5% on CelebA (84.4 vs. 86.9). Compared to the fully supervised Group DRO, ExMap closes the gap to within 5% on CelebA (84.4 vs. 88.9 WGA) and 1.1% on Waterbirds (92.5 vs. 91.4, actually exceeding it). Among other unsupervised baselines, ExMap substantially outperforms MaskTune (86.4% WGA on Waterbirds), DivDis (81.0%), and the unsupervised variants of JTT (62.5%), LfF (44.1%), and CVar DRO (62.0%). In the multi-shortcut UrbanCars setting, DFR+ExMap avoids the Whac-A-Mole dilemma entirely (no gap increases vs. ERM), whereas GEORGE(DFR) exhibits a 1.08× increase in CoObj Gap, confirming ExMap's robustness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the group robustness literature (Group-DRO, DFR, JTT) and the explainability/shortcut-learning literature (LRP, SPRAY). Its closest conceptual predecessor is GEORGE, which generates pseudo-group labels by clustering penultimate-layer features in a UMAP-reduced space; ExMap departs by clustering pixel-attribution heatmaps (LRP) instead, arguing that input-space relevance scores more cleanly separate task-relevant from spurious features. The method is explicitly strategy-agnostic, plugging into any existing group-robustness retraining procedure (DFR, JTT), and extends the evaluation to the recently introduced multi-shortcut (Whac-A-Mole) setting where supervised methods are known to struggle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ExMap addresses a practical bottleneck in group robustness: the need for group labels that are rarely available in real-world deployments. By showing that a simple two-stage pipeline (LRP heatmap extraction + spectral clustering) can close most of the gap to fully supervised methods across four datasets, it offers a low-cost, model-agnostic drop-in for existing robustness toolkits. Limitations include reliance on a single explainability method (LRP), a single backbone (ResNet-50), and image classification only; whether the heatmap-clustering principle transfers to other modalities, architectures, or more complex spurious structures remains open. The multi-shortcut results, while encouraging, are demonstrated on a single dataset (UrbanCars), leaving the generality of the Whac-A-Mole avoidance untested.
