## Problem

Manual CNC programming is a labor-intensive bottleneck requiring expert knowledge, and adapting machines to new tasks demands extensive reprogramming. The core challenge is that finding optimal sequential machining operations is NP-hard, so no ground-truth operation sequences exist to supervise a neural network. Existing 3D CAD reconstruction methods (e.g., CSG-based approaches) model static shape assembly rather than the dynamic, sequential material-removal process inherent to CNC machining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 3D CAD model as input, voxelized at 64×64×64 resolution, with the initial raw material implicitly defined as the axis-aligned bounding box of the target shape. It outputs a sequence of up to 20 milling operations (each specifying a parametric 2D path, a discrete tool radius from {0.025, 0.05, 0.075, 0.1}, and X/Y rotation angles) followed by up to 20 drilling operations (each specifying a 3D tip coordinate, a discrete radius from {0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04}, and rotation angles). The final reconstructed shape is extracted via marching cubes at 256 resolution for evaluation.

## Method

CNC-Net is a self-supervised framework that learns to generate sequential CNC operations solely from the target shape, without any labeled operation sequences. An encoder (shared with CAPRI-Net) extracts features from the voxelized input, which are concatenated with bounding-box dimensions and fed into a 2-layer LSTM (hidden size 256) that autoregressively predicts operation parameters at each step. Three decoders per operation type produce rotation angles (tanh-scaled to [−π, π]), tool radius (softmax over a discrete set), and path/tip coordinates (ResNet-based with 5 blocks of 256 channels). Each step applies a CSG subtraction: the workpiece is rotated, the cylindrical tool (milling path or drill point) is subtracted via a max operation on implicit functions, and the result is inverse-rotated. Training uses four complementary losses—milling shape matching, drilling region removal, interior preservation, and Chamfer-distance-based tool-tip localization—enabling zero-shot training on a single sample without pre-training.

## Results

On the ABC dataset, CNC-Net achieves the best volume-based metrics among all compared methods: IoU of 0.824 and F1 of 0.901, outperforming the next-best baseline (CSG-Stump, IoU 0.787) by 4.7%. On ShapeNet, CNC-Net similarly leads in IoU (0.740) and F1 (0.850), surpassing CAPRI-Net (IoU 0.700) by 4.0%. However, CNC-Net underperforms on surface-based metrics (CD and NC) on both datasets, as its carving-from-bounding-box approach produces less smooth surfaces than methods that assemble smooth primitives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CNC-Net outperforms all four baselines on volume-based metrics (IoU and F1) on both ABC and ShapeNet. On ABC, it improves IoU by 4.7% over CSG-Stump (0.824 vs. 0.787), 7.2% over ExtrudeNet (0.824 vs. 0.769), 7.3% over CAPRI-Net (0.824 vs. 0.768), and 6.2% over SECAD-Net (0.824 vs. 0.776). On ShapeNet, CNC-Net achieves IoU 0.740 vs. 0.700 (CAPRI-Net), 0.697 (CSG-Stump), 0.650 (SECAD-Net), and 0.607 (ExtrudeNet). However, CNC-Net consistently loses on surface-based metrics: CAPRI-Net achieves the best CD (0.312) and NC (0.914) on ABC, while CNC-Net records CD 0.509 and NC 0.893. The authors attribute this gap to the fundamental difference in approach—CNC-Net carves material from a bounding box (producing faceted surfaces), whereas baselines assemble smooth CSG primitives (yielding smoother reconstructed surfaces). On ShapeNet, CNC-Net's CD of 1.562 is better than SECAD-Net (2.405) and ExtrudeNet (0.918) but worse than CAPRI-Net (0.447) and CSG-Stump (0.521).

## Relation to Prior Work

CNC-Net builds directly on CAPRI-Net's encoder architecture and pre-training/fine-tuning protocol, but departs fundamentally from the assembly-based paradigm of prior CSG reconstruction methods (CSG-Stump, ExtrudeNet, CAPRI-Net, SECAD-Net) by framing shape reconstruction as sequential material removal from a bounding box. While prior self-supervised CAD reconstruction methods construct shapes through unions of primitives, CNC-Net inverts this logic to model subtraction operations (milling and drilling with cylindrical tools) plus workpiece rotation, aligning the learned process with actual manufacturing workflows. The use of an LSTM to sequentially predict operation parameters and implicit cylindrical primitives for tool representation is a novel architectural choice within the implicit-representation family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges the gap between 3D shape reconstruction research and practical manufacturing by demonstrating that a DNN can autonomously generate sequential CNC operations without any labeled toolpaths, offering a cost-effective alternative to expert CAM programming. Key limitations include the restriction to cylindrical tools and only milling/drilling/rotation operations, the use of a bounding box as the sole input material, and the absence of ground-truth operation sequences (an NP-hard problem) that prevents assessing whether learned operations are optimal. The inferior surface metrics (CD, NC) compared to assembly-based methods highlight a fundamental trade-off: carving from a cube preserves volume accuracy but sacrifices surface smoothness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to diverse tool geometries (broaches, gear cutters), additional operations (grinding, turning), and non-convex raw materials.
