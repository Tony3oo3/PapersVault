## Problem

Multi-modal semantic scene completion methods suffer from two underexplored limitations: (1) joint training causes individual modality encoders to learn weaker representations than their single-modal counterparts, and (2) deep SSC models trained on limited indoor scene data are prone to severe overfitting, where training and validation performance diverge after a certain epoch. The paper addresses both issues by reframing the problem as one of optimizing gradient updates across modalities and across training steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a depth map converted to a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) volume as input. The RGB features are extracted by a 2D encoder (SegFormer-B2 or DeepLabv3) and projected into 3D space via per-pixel depth values, while the TSDF is encoded with 3D convolutions. The output is a 3D voxel grid at 60×36×60 resolution with C+1 channels, where the extra channel encodes occupancy (empty vs. occupied) and the C channels encode semantic class labels for each voxel.

## Method

AMMNet introduces two core mechanisms. First, a cross-modal modulation module replaces the baseline's element-wise addition of RGB and TSDF features with a scale-bias recalibration: two 1×1×1 convolutions on the TSDF feature produce a scale and bias that multiplicatively and additively modulate the RGB feature, creating interdependent gradient flows so that each modality's encoder is stimulated by the other's context. This modulation is applied at the encoder fusion point and at the last two decoder layers. Second, a lightweight adversarial discriminator (built from DDR layers) is trained in a minimax game against the voxel predictor, with two types of perturbed ground truths fed as additional fake samples: geometrically incomplete voxels (randomly erasing non-empty voxels) and semantically shuffled voxels (randomly remapping category labels). This dynamic gradient competition provides continuous adaptive supervision that prevents overfitting without architectural changes. The total loss combines a smooth cross-entropy SSC loss (3D voxel + 2D image supervision) with the adversarial loss weighted by β=0.005.

## Results

On the NYU dataset, AMMNet with DeepLabv3 backbone achieves 56.1% SSC-mIoU and 76.3% SC-IoU, outperforming the previous best method CVSformer (52.6% SSC-mIoU) by 3.5%. On the synthetic NYUCAD dataset, AMMNet DLabv3 reaches 67.2% SSC-mIoU and 83.3% SC-IoU, surpassing CVSformer DLabv3 (63.9%) by 3.3%. With the default SegFormer-B2 backbone, AMMNet achieves 48.5% SSC-mIoU on NYU, exceeding the same-architecture baseline CleanerS (47.7%) by 0.8% without requiring clean CAD data or a complex distillation pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMMNet DLabv3 decisively outperforms all prior multi-modal methods on both datasets: it beats CVSformer DLabv3 by 3.5% SSC-mIoU on NYU (56.1 vs 52.6) and by 3.3% on NYUCAD (67.2 vs 63.9), and surpasses SISNet DLabv3 by 3.7% on NYU. Against the closest same-architecture baseline CleanerS [34] (which shares the identical RGB+TSDF encoder-decoder structure), AMMNet with the default backbone gains 0.8% SSC-mIoU (48.5 vs 47.7) on NYU while avoiding CleanerS's reliance on clean CAD data and a complex distillation pipeline. On NYUCAD, AMMNet DLabv3 leads PCANet by 7.2% SSC-mIoU (67.2 vs 59.6) and 3D-Sketch by 12.0% (67.2 vs 55.2). Single-modal depth-only methods (SSCNet, CCPNet) trail by 18–32% SSC-mIoU, confirming the value of multi-modal inputs. Notably, AMMNet's gains are consistent across both noisy real (NYU) and clean synthetic (NYUCAD) data, with the advantage being larger on NYUCAD where cross-modal modulation can fully exploit noise-free representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMMNet builds directly on the CleanerS [34] encoder-decoder architecture (RGB encoder + TSDF encoder + 3D decoder with element-wise fusion), but departs from it by replacing the simple addition with a cross-modal modulation that creates interdependent gradient flows between modalities. Conceptually, the modulation resembles channel recalibration (à la SE-Net) applied across modalities rather than within one, while the adversarial training scheme repurposes GAN-style minimax dynamics as a regularizer rather than a generative objective. The paper positions itself against the broader multi-modal SSC lineage (3D-Sketch, DDRNet, PCANet, SISNet, CVSformer) by arguing that these methods overlook two specific failure modes—insufficient single-modal representation learning and overfitting—that prior multi-modal fusion strategies do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that jointly-trained multi-modal encoders produce *worse* single-modal representations than their unimodal counterparts challenges a core assumption in multi-modal learning and provides a concrete diagnostic for practitioners. The 3.5% SSC-mIoU gain on NYU and 3.3% on NYUCAD over prior SOTA, achieved with a simpler pipeline than CleanerS's distillation approach, suggests that gradient-level interventions can outperform complex auxiliary training stages. However, the method is validated only on small indoor benchmarks (NYU, NYUCAD) with a fixed voxel resolution, leaving open whether the cross-modal modulation and adversarial regularization transfer to outdoor scenes, larger-scale 3D understanding, or other multi-modal fusion settings. The added discriminator also introduces extra training compute and a new hyperparameter (β) that requires careful tuning.
