## Problem

Video-language models that project video frames into language tokens suffer from hallucination: as generated text lengthens, the relative distance between visual tokens and newly generated text tokens grows, causing RoPE-based attention to diminish the visual signal and produce content unrelated to the video. Additionally, standard frame-by-frame projection ignores temporal relationships between consecutive frames, further degrading video comprehension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip (sampled at multiple timestamps, each frame encoded into 32 visual tokens via a Q-Former) concatenated with a text question prompt, and produces an open-ended natural language answer. Evaluation is conducted in a zero-shot setting on four VideoQA benchmarks (NExT-QA, MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA) using GPT-3.5-turbo as an LLM-assisted judge that returns a binary correctness label and a 0–5 relevance score, avoiding brittle word-matching for variable-length responses.

## Method

VISTA-LLAMA introduces two mechanisms on top of a frozen LLaVA backbone (frozen LLaMA-7B + frozen EVA-CLIP-g or ViT-L encoder), training only the Q-Former visual projector. First, EDVT-Attention modifies the attention computation so that RoPE is applied only to text-text token pairs, while visual-text token attention weights are computed without rotary position embedding, effectively giving every visual token equal positional influence on any generated text token regardless of distance. Second, a sequential visual projector generates each frame's visual tokens by using the previous frame's projected tokens as queries in the Q-Former, encoding temporal context without additional parameters and enabling sparse frame sampling to represent longer videos within the LLM's context window. The model is fine-tuned for 3 epochs on 100K video instruction pairs with a learning rate of 2e-5 on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On zero-shot open-ended video question answering, VISTA-LLaMA achieves 60.7% accuracy on NExT-QA, 65.3% on MSVD-QA, and 60.5% on MSRVTT-QA, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. On ActivityNet-QA, it reaches 48.3% accuracy. In the video-based text generation evaluation (ActivityNet-200), VISTA-LLaMA scores 2.44 on Correctness, 2.31 on Consistency, 2.64 on Detail Orientation, 3.18 on Contextual Understanding, and 2.26 on Temporal Understanding (scale 0–5), outperforming Video-ChatGPT on four of five aspects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISTA-LLaMA achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on three of four VideoQA benchmarks. On NExT-QA, it surpasses VideoChat (56.2%) by 4.5 points and Video-ChatGPT (54.6%) by 6.1 points. On MSRVTT-QA, the margin is largest: VISTA-LLaMA (60.5%) outperforms the second-best MovieChat (49.7%) by nearly 11 points. On MSVD-QA, it edges out Video-ChatGPT (64.9%) by 0.4 points. The sole loss is on ActivityNet-QA, where MovieChat (51.5%) exceeds VISTA-LLaMA (48.3%) by 3.2 points, though VISTA-LLaMA still beats all other baselines by a wide margin (next-best: LLaMA Adapter at 34.2%). In the text-generation evaluation, VISTA-LLaMA outperforms Video-ChatGPT on Correctness (2.44 vs 2.40), Detail Orientation (2.64 vs 2.52), Contextual Understanding (3.18 vs 2.62), and Temporal Understanding (2.26 vs 1.98), but trails slightly on Consistency (2.31 vs 2.37).

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA-LLAMA extends the video-language model paradigm established by Video-ChatGPT, MovieChat, and LLaVA, where video frames are projected into language token space and processed by a frozen LLM. Its key architectural departure is the EDVT-Attention mechanism, which selectively removes rotary position embedding (RoPE) from visual-text attention pairs while retaining it for text-text pairs, directly addressing the diminishing visual influence problem as generated text lengthens. The sequential visual projector builds on BLIP-2's Q-Former but introduces temporal dependency by conditioning each frame's projection on the previous frame's projected tokens, a lightweight alternative to explicit temporal encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EDVT-Attention modification is a minimal, architecture-agnostic change (removing RoPE for one attention subset) that yields consistent gains across multiple video QA benchmarks, making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing RoPE-based video LMs. The sequential projector offers a parameter-free way to inject temporal context, enabling longer video encoding with fewer tokens. However, the approach is limited to frozen-LLM fine-tuning of the projector, and the paper acknowledges that applying these designs during pre-training or evaluating on substantially longer videos remains open. Generalization beyond LLaMA/Vicuna-based architectures and to other position encoding schemes is untested.
