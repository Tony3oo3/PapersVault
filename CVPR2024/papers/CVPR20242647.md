## Problem

Adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models like CLIP to few-shot generalization tasks faces a discrimination-generalization dilemma: task-specific knowledge must be fine-tuned while general knowledge must be preserved, yet existing uni-modal adapters neither exploit the cross-modal relationship between text and image representations nor account for the differing discriminability and generalizability of features at different transformer layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242647/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt (template: 'a photo of a <category>') as input, encoding them through frozen ViT-based CLIP encoders with lightweight adapters inserted at higher transformer layers. The output is a class label determined by cosine similarity between the projected image embedding and per-class text embeddings in a shared vision-language space. All experiments use a 16-shot setting (16 training examples per base category), and the model is evaluated on base classes, novel classes, cross-dataset transfer, and out-of-distribution domain shifts.

## Method

MMA introduces a multi-modal adapter unit consisting of modality-specific 'Down' and 'Up' projection layers plus a shared 'Shared' projection layer that aggregates dual-modal signals into a common feature space, enabling gradient communication between vision and language branches. Based on a systematic dataset-level recognition analysis across three CLIP variants, the paper finds that higher transformer layers encode discriminable dataset-specific knowledge while lower layers encode generalizable knowledge, and that text features are more discriminable than visual features with larger cross-modal gaps in lower layers. Consequently, MMA is inserted only from the k-th layer (k=5 for base-to-novel, k=9 for cross-dataset) to the final layer of both encoders, with a scaling factor α=0.001 balancing task-specific adaptation against preservation of pre-trained general knowledge. Only the adapter parameters (with 32-dimensional shared projections) are optimized via SGD; the entire pre-trained CLIP backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the Base-to-Novel Generalization task (16-shot, 11 datasets), MMA achieves the best average harmonic mean of 79.87%, with 83.20% base accuracy and 76.80% novel accuracy, outperforming all 9 compared methods. In the Cross-Dataset Evaluation setting, MMA attains the best average accuracy of 66.61% across 10 target datasets. For Domain Generalization, MMA achieves the highest accuracy on 3 out of 4 out-of-distribution ImageNet variants (ImageNet-V2: 64.33%, ImageNet-A: 51.12%, ImageNet-R: 77.32%), with a near-tie on ImageNet-Sketch (49.13% vs. MaPLe's 49.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMA outperforms all 9 baselines on the average harmonic mean across 11 datasets. Against the strongest prompt-learning baselines, MMA beats LASP-V (79.48 HM) by +0.39 and LASP (78.61 HM) by +1.26, while also surpassing the multi-modal prompt method MaPLe (78.55 HM) by +1.32. On novel-class accuracy specifically, MMA leads LASP-V by +0.69 and MaPLe by +1.66, indicating better generalization to unseen categories. The zero-shot CLIP baseline (71.70 HM) is surpassed by +8.17 in HM, though CLIP retains a slight edge on novel-class accuracy alone (74.22 vs. 76.80 is actually MMA winning; CLIP's novel advantage is dataset-specific, e.g., Flowers102). In the Cross-Dataset setting, MMA (66.61 avg) edges out MaPLe (66.30) and PromptSRC (65.81), and in Domain Generalization it leads on ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-A, and ImageNet-R while tying MaPLe on ImageNet-Sketch.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMA extends the residual adapter paradigm established by AdaptFormer and CLIP-Adapter, which add lightweight projection modules to frozen transformer blocks, by making the adapter explicitly multi-modal: separate down/up projections handle each branch while a shared middle projection layer enables gradient communication between vision and language encoders. A key departure from prior adapter and prompt-learning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, LASP) is the selective placement of adapters only in higher transformer layers, motivated by a dataset-level recognition analysis showing that lower layers encode generalizable knowledge while higher layers encode discriminable, dataset-specific knowledge. This contrasts with AdaptFormer's all-layer adapters and MaPLe's lower-to-upper prompt insertion, positioning MMA as a targeted, layer-aware adaptation strategy rather than a uniform one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer-wise discriminability/generalizability analysis provides a practical, data-driven principle for deciding where to insert adapters in any transformer-based VLM, which could inform future parameter-efficient fine-tuning designs beyond CLIP. The shared projection mechanism offers a simple yet effective way to align cross-modal representations without the per-pair feature interactions of methods like MaPLe, reducing computational cost. However, the evaluation is limited to ViT-B/16 CLIP at 16-shot, and the performance gains over strong baselines (LASP-V, MaPLe) are modest (~0.4–1.3 HM points), leaving open whether the layer-selection strategy and shared-projection design transfer to larger models, different architectures, or lower-shot regimes where the discrimination-generalization trade-off is even more acute.
