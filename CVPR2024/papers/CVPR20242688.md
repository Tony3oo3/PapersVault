## Problem

2D-lifting text-to-3D methods suffer from the Janus problem (inconsistent geometry such as multi-face artifacts) due to two root causes: viewpoint bias inherited by 2D diffusion models from Internet data, and overfitting of the SDS optimization objective to the highest-probability view. Existing remedies that inject 3D priors into 2D diffusion models compromise texture fidelity and generalizability because they rely on limited, cartoonish 3D training data.

## Input / Output

The primary input is a free-form text prompt; for controllable tasks, the method additionally accepts a loose 3D sketch (e.g., a simple animal silhouette or a coarse multi-view layout reconstructed via Differentiable Blocks World) or a template skeleton for animation. The output is a full 3D NeRF scene with consistent geometry and high-fidelity texture, rendered from arbitrary viewpoints. Internally, the 3D prior is converted into an edge map (via HED detector) for ControlNet conditioning and a normal map for the conditional LoRA, so the user never needs to supply explicit geometric conditions.

## Method

DreamControl is a two-stage 2D-lifting framework. Stage 1 optimizes a coarse NeRF as a 3D self-prior using SDS, but with two key modifications: (a) adaptive viewpoint sampling that aligns the 3D camera distribution with the 2D diffusion model's inherent view bias by computing per-view-range probabilities via CLIP similarity, and (b) a boundary integrity metric that terminates optimization once the density boundary between foreground and background becomes clear, preventing overfitting. Stage 2 performs control-based score distillation: the prior's rendered edge mask conditions a ControlNet (scribble variant) as the pre-trained term, while a conditional LoRA—conditioned on the current NeRF's normal map rather than a raw camera pose—serves as the variational term. A weighted score with a linearly increasing coefficient λ (0.5→0.75 over 5,000 iterations) gradually blends from pure SDS toward full VSD, stabilizing early optimization. This design maintains the prior's geometry while allowing the diffusion model to synthesize fine-grained textures without requiring external 3D training data.

## Results

On 30 text prompts, DreamControl achieves a Janus Rate (JR) of 10.00%, tying with MVDream for the lowest inconsistency rate, while substantially outperforming SDS-based methods (DreamFusion 36.67%, Magic3D 53.33%, ProlificDreamer 56.67%). On texture quality, DreamControl scores 27.46% PickScore (PS) and 28.14% CLIP-Score (CS), the best among all compared methods, surpassing the next-best MVDream (PS 17.70%, CS 26.17%) by nearly 10 points on PS. The results demonstrate that DreamControl simultaneously achieves high geometry consistency and high texture fidelity, whereas prior methods trade off one for the other.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamControl ties with MVDream on geometry consistency (JR 10.00%) but decisively wins on texture fidelity, scoring 27.46% PS vs. MVDream's 17.70% and 28.14% CS vs. 26.17%. Against the SDS-based baselines (DreamFusion, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer), DreamControl reduces the Janus Rate by 26–47 percentage points while also improving PS by 7–17 points, showing that the two-stage self-prior + control-based distillation resolves the overfitting that plagues single-stage SDS. Zero-1-to-3 achieves a moderate JR (16.67%) but has the worst texture quality (PS 7.89%, CS 21.25%), confirming the paper's argument that 3D-prior-trained diffusion models sacrifice texture fidelity due to cartoonish training data. Overall, DreamControl is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best (or tied-best) score on all three metrics, validating the claim that geometry consistency and texture fidelity need not be in tension.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamControl sits at the intersection of three threads: SDS-based 2D-lifting (DreamFusion), variational score distillation with LoRA (ProlificDreamer), and conditional diffusion via ControlNet. Its key departure from prior work is a reframing of the Janus problem: rather than attributing inconsistency solely to the lack of 3D view-awareness in 2D diffusion models (the motivation behind Zero-1-to-3, MVDream, SweetDreamer), the authors decompose it into viewpoint bias and optimization overfitting, and address both with a training-free coarse NeRF self-prior plus a control-based second stage. This avoids the need for large-scale 3D training data that limits the generalizability of view-aware diffusion approaches, while the conditional LoRA and weighted score are targeted modifications to ProlificDreamer's VSD to stabilize optimization under the weaker boundary-mask condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage self-prior framework offers a practical route to high-fidelity, geometry-consistent 3D generation without requiring curated 3D datasets, and the control-based guidance naturally extends to downstream tasks like user-guided generation and skeleton-conditioned 3D animation. The boundary integrity metric as an automatic termination criterion addresses a long-standing practical pain point (manual early stopping) in SDS-based pipelines. However, the method still fails when the coarse prior is view-ambiguous (e.g., symmetric objects), and the reliance on ControlNet's scribble variant with a boundary mask means the geometric constraint is relatively weak—stronger conditions (depth, normal) would tighten geometry but sacrifice the diversity that motivates the VSD-style optimization. Open questions include whether the self-prior stage can be made more robust to symmetric or highly repetitive geometries, and whether the framework scales to scene-level generation beyond single objects.
