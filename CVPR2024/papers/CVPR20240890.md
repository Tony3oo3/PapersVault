## Problem

Existing Domain Generalized Semantic Segmentation methods rely on Domain Randomization that only diversifies image style (photometric and geometric transformations) but cannot introduce content variation, leaving the model unable to cover the full range of possible target distributions. Foundation Models such as CLIP, SAM, and Diffusion offer complementary capabilities for robust features, content generation, and mask refinement, yet none have been integrated into DGSS. The paper addresses how to collaboratively assemble these FMs to overcome the style-only limitation of traditional DR and the class-agnostic output problem of SAM.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and produces a class-specific semantic segmentation mask via Mask2Former's mask classification decoder, which treats segmentation as region classification rather than per-pixel classification. During training, the model consumes both the labeled source domain (GTA at 1914×1052 or SYNTHIA at 1280×760) and approximately 5000 unlabeled photorealistic images generated by Stable Diffusion. Segmentation targets 19 classes for GTA or 16 shared classes for SYNTHIA, evaluated across Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary, and ACDC weather scenarios.

## Method

CLOUDS assembles three foundation models in a collaborative pipeline: a frozen CNN-based CLIP encoder extracts robust features, a Mask2Former decoder performs mask classification, and training proceeds in two phases. The first phase is supervised training on the source domain with the CLIP backbone frozen to preserve its generalization and image-text alignment. The second phase is self-training on diffusion-generated images, where a teacher (EMA of the student decoder) produces pseudo labels that are refined by SAM: per-class binary masks are extracted, connected components are labeled via the Hoshen-Kopelman algorithm, random points within each component prompt SAM for precise masks, and overlapping predictions are marked as an 'unlabeled' class to prevent erroneous supervision. Content diversification is achieved by conditioning Stable Diffusion on LLM (Llama-2) prompts that vary class names, synonyms, and environmental factors (weather, lighting) to cover plausible target distributions.

## Results

On the GTA → {Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary} benchmark, CLOUDS achieves 53.3% average mIoU with ResNet-50, 54.7% with ResNet-101, 58.1% with MiT-B5, and 61.5% with ConvNeXt-Large, outperforming the strongest DGSS baseline (MoDify, 46.8%) by +7.6% and the strongest FM-based method (FC-CLIP, 57.0%) by +4.5%. On SYNTHIA → {Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary}, CLOUDS reaches 46.5% (ResNet-101) and 52.1% (ConvNeXt-L), surpassing the prior best (MoDify, 41.7%) by +4.8%. On the ACDC adverse-weather benchmark, CLOUDS with ConvNeXt-L achieves 45.1/65.3/64.4 mIoU on Night/Snow/Rain, beating P0DA by +4.4/+8.2/+7.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLOUDS outperforms all 17 baselines across every evaluation setting. Among traditional DGSS methods, it beats the strongest style-randomization baselines MoDify [31] (+8.1% with ResNet-50, +7.6% with ResNet-101) and TLDR [33] (+8.2%, +7.6%) on GTA→{C,B,M}, and surpasses MoDify by +4.8% on SYNTHIA→{C,B,M}. Against the UDA-derived HRDA [26], CLOUDS gains +14.6% (ResNet-101) and +2.2% (MiT-B5) on GTA. Among foundation-model-based methods, CLOUDS with ConvNeXt-L (61.5% avg) outperforms FC-CLIP [77] (57.0%), ODISE [73] (55.5%), CAT-Seg [11] (48.2%), and GroundingSAM [35,41] (43.8%), with the largest margin over text-prompt-dependent methods that struggle with ambiguous classes like 'pole' and 'terrain'. On the ACDC weather benchmark, CLOUDS (ResNet-50) beats P0DA [18] by +4.4/+8.2/+7.4% on Night/Snow/Rain and CLIPStyler [36] by +8.1/+11.1/+11.0%, confirming that content-level diversification via diffusion models generalizes better than style-only transfer under adverse conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLOUDS departs from the dominant DGSS paradigm of style-based Domain Randomization (SHADE, MoDify, TLDR) by introducing content diversification through a text-to-image diffusion model guided by LLM-generated prompts. It builds on the student-teacher EMA framework from HRDA [26] and repurposes SAM [35] not as a standalone segmentation model but as a pseudo-label refiner within a self-training loop. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as orthogonal to both traditional DGSS (which hardens features against style shifts) and open-vocabulary segmentation (which relies on text prompts for class identification), instead leveraging the source dataset's visual information through a frozen CLIP backbone while using generative models to cover content modes of potential target distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that an assembly of foundation models can outperform both traditional DGSS and open-vocabulary methods by a non-trivial margin (5.6–6.7% mIoU), suggesting a viable path forward for DGSS in the FM era. However, the ablation reveals that the diffusion-based content diversification contributes only ~0.7% mIoU, with the bulk of gains coming from the CLIP backbone and SAM refinement—undermining the paper's central narrative about content diversification. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving scenes, the computational cost of SAM-based pseudo-label refinement at scale, and whether the self-training loop can be made more stable without the SAM crutch.
