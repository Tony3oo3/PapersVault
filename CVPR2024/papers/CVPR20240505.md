## Problem

Open-world 3D scene understanding requires recognizing unseen object categories from point clouds, but dense 3D semantic annotations are scarce and hard to scale. Existing approaches either distill 2D dense features into 3D backbones (incurring high storage and training costs) or rely on sparse image-captioning supervision that limits vocabulary coverage. The paper identifies that diverse 2D vision-language models offer complementary region-level cues, yet naive data-level or loss-level fusion of their outputs fails to improve 3D learning due to redundancy and semantic conflicts in overlapping regions.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a 3D point cloud and a text query (category name) as input, producing per-point semantic scores via similarity between point embeddings and CLIP text embeddings, plus per-point instance IDs via SoftGroup. During training, multi-view 2D images of the scene are projected to 3D to associate region-level captions from five 2D VL model streams (Detic, OFA, GRiT, Kosmos-2, and sliding-window prompts) with partial point sets, creating dense 3D-language pairs without any human 3D annotation. When integrated with an LLM (RegionGR), the system additionally accepts free-form user queries and returns grounded 3D reasoning responses with object localization.

## Method

RegionPLC builds a 3D open-world backbone (SparseUNet + CLIP text encoder as classifier) trained with dense region-level 3D-language supervision. A Supplementary-oriented Fusion (SFusion) strategy selects a primary caption source and iteratively merges candidate sources only where 3D point-set overlap (IoU) falls within a threshold band [T_l, T_h], filtering redundant or conflicting descriptions while preserving spatially complementary cues. The training objective replaces CLIP-style pooled contrastive loss with a region-aware point-discriminative contrastive loss (L_rpdc): point-wise embeddings are individually matched against all caption embeddings, log-probabilities are pooled within each region, and a normalization factor α_r = n_t·card(p̂)/Σcard(p̂_i) equalizes gradient magnitude across regions of varying size, preventing large regions from dominating optimization and enabling each point to learn discriminative features independent of neighboring points from unrelated categories.

## Results

On open-world 3D semantic segmentation, RegionPLC achieves an average 17.2% improvement in unseen-category mIoU over the previous state-of-the-art PLA across ScanNet, nuScenes, and ScanNet200 (e.g., mIoU^N of 66.6 vs. 45.9 on ScanNet B12/N7, and 56.0 vs. 35.4 on nuScenes B12/N3). In the annotation-free setting on ScanNet, RegionPLC with SparseUNet32 reaches 59.6% mIoU† and 77.5% mAcc†, surpassing OpenScene-3D (57.8/70.3) while using only 17% of its training time and 5% of its storage. For instance segmentation, RegionPLC gains 4.3%–12.3% in mAP50^N over PLA across three ScanNet partitions. On the long-tail ScanNet200 dataset, RegionPLC still delivers 4.2%–5.1% mIoU^N gains over PLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionPLC decisively outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and partition. Against the closest competitor PLA [7], RegionPLC gains 8.3%–21.8% in mIoU^N on ScanNet and nuScenes (e.g., 66.6 vs. 45.9 on B12/N7) and 4.2%–5.1% on the long-tail ScanNet200. The earlier zero-shot methods 3DGenZ [22] and 3DTZSL [4] are far behind, with RegionPLC achieving 30.6%–42.7% higher mIoU^N. OVSeg-3D [19], which extends 2D LSeg to 3D, scores near-zero mIoU^N (0.0–0.9), indicating it cannot generalize to unseen categories at all. In the annotation-free setting (Table 4), RegionPLC (59.6% mIoU†) surpasses OpenScene-3D (57.8%) and MaskCLIP (23.1%) while requiring only 13.0 h of training and 5.5 GB storage versus OpenScene's 25.3 h and 117.3 GB. RegionPLC can also be combined with OpenScene-3D to further push mIoU† to 63.6%, demonstrating complementary strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionPLC builds directly on PLA [7], which established the paradigm of replacing 3D classification heads with CLIP text embeddings for open-vocabulary 3D understanding, but departs from it by replacing sparse view/entity-level captions with dense region-level language supervision sourced from multiple 2D foundation models (Detic, OFA, GRiT, Kosmos-2). It also contrasts with feature-distillation approaches like OpenScene [23] that require expensive pixel-aligned 2D feature extraction and storage, instead learning from lightweight language pairs. The SFusion strategy and region-aware point-discriminative contrastive loss are the paper's core methodological contributions, addressing redundancy and conflicts when combining heterogeneous caption sources and enabling point-wise discriminative learning for dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or superior open-world 3D segmentation (59.6% mIoU annotation-free) while consuming only 5% of OpenScene's storage and 17% of its training cost, making large-vocabulary 3D understanding far more accessible. The plug-and-play design (RegionPLC + OpenScene yields further gains) and seamless LLM integration for grounded 3D reasoning position the framework as a building block for embodied AI. Open questions include whether the SFusion overlap thresholds generalize beyond the tested datasets, how the method scales to even larger vocabulary spaces or more diverse 2D foundation models, and whether the reliance on multi-view 2D supervision during training limits deployment in single-view or real-time settings.
