## Problem

SAM's segmentation quality degrades significantly under common imaging degradations such as low lighting, noise, blur, adverse weather, and compression artifacts, undermining its utility as a structural prior for downstream restoration tasks. Existing remedies—preprocessing with image restoration networks or directly fine-tuning SAM—either do not guarantee segmentation improvement or cause catastrophic forgetting that destroys SAM's zero-shot generalization. The paper addresses how to make SAM robust to diverse degradations while fully preserving its promptable, zero-shot segmentation capability.

## Input / Output

At inference, RobustSAM takes a single degraded RGB image and a user prompt (point or bounding box) and outputs a binary segmentation mask, requiring no separate restoration pre-processing step. During training, the model additionally consumes a paired clear image (the pre-degradation version) to compute consistency losses, and the training set is augmented with 15 types of synthetic degradations plus an identity mapping to prevent performance loss on clean inputs. The model operates at the same resolution as SAM (1024×1024 ViT-H image encoder) and supports both sparse prompts (points, boxes) and dense prompts (masks) through the inherited prompt encoder.

## Method

RobustSAM freezes the entire original SAM (image encoder, prompt encoder, mask decoder) and adds two lightweight modules: the Anti-Degradation Mask Feature Generation (AMFG) block and the Anti-Degradation Output Token Generation (AOTG) block, plus a fine-tuned Robust Output Token (ROT). AMFG processes degraded mask and complementary features through parallel Instance Normalization and Batch Normalization branches, fuses them via attention and squeeze-and-excitation channel attention, then applies a Fourier Degradation Suppression module that removes amplitude components (style/degradation) while preserving phase (structure) to yield degradation-invariant features. AOTG refines the ROT with stacked Instance Normalization layers followed by a single MLP. Training enforces Mask Feature Consistency Loss and Token Consistency Loss between the refined degraded-image features and the corresponding features extracted from the paired clear image by the frozen original SAM, combined with a Dice+Focal segmentation loss. Only 403 MB of new parameters are optimized (versus 1250 MB for SAM), trainable in 30 hours on 8 A100 GPUs, making the approach accessible to typical research labs.

## Results

On the BDD-100k and LIS datasets (unseen, real-world degradations) with point prompts, RobustSAM achieves 0.3717 IoU and 0.8926 Dice, substantially outperforming SAM (0.3056 IoU, 0.3837 Dice) and all restoration-then-SAM baselines. On unseen synthetic-degradation datasets (NDD20, STREETS, FSS-1000), RobustSAM reaches 0.8216 average IoU and 0.9780 PA, compared to SAM's 0.8000 IoU and 0.9565 PA. For downstream SAM-prior tasks, RobustSAM improves single-image dehazing PSNR from 21.677 to 23.159 and deblurring PSNR from 27.491 to 29.351 over using vanilla SAM as the prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustSAM outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against vanilla SAM, the most striking gain is on real-world degraded images (BDD-100k + LIS) with point prompts, where Dice more than doubles from 0.3837 to 0.8926 and IoU improves from 0.3056 to 0.3717. Compared to HQ-SAM (a high-quality SAM variant), RobustSAM leads by 0.0774 IoU on point-prompt real-world evaluation and by 0.0114 average IoU on unseen synthetic-degradation datasets. The two-stage restoration baselines (AirNet+SAM and URIE+SAM) offer only marginal gains over SAM (e.g., AirNet+SAM reaches 0.3245 IoU vs SAM's 0.3056 on BDD-100k+LIS point prompts), while RobustSAM achieves 0.3717, confirming that direct feature-level anti-degradation is more effective than preprocessing with general-purpose restoration networks. On bounding-box prompts, RobustSAM also leads all baselines (0.8958 IoU vs 0.8877 for HQ-SAM), though the margin is smaller than for point prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

RobustSAM is built directly on the frozen SAM architecture (Kirillov et al., 2023), adding lightweight anti-degradation modules (AMFG, AOTG, ROT) on top of the ViT image encoder and mask decoder rather than fine-tuning SAM itself. It borrows the Feature Fusion block design from HQ-SAM (Ke et al., 2023) and draws on the dual IN/BN normalization strategy from URIE (Son et al., 2020) and the Fourier-domain degradation suppression idea from domain adaptation work (FDA, RDA). The key departure from prior robust segmentation approaches (QualNet, FIFO, URIE) is that those target CNN-based segmentation with task-specific training, whereas RobustSAM preserves SAM's zero-shot generalization by freezing all original parameters and supervising new modules via consistency losses against clear-image features. It also contrasts with the two-stage restoration-then-segmentation pipeline (AirNet+SAM, URIE+SAM), arguing that restoration optimized for human perception does not guarantee better segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since SAM is increasingly used as a structural prior in downstream restoration tasks (dehazing, deblurring, super-resolution), improving its robustness on degraded inputs has broad practical impact beyond segmentation alone, as demonstrated by the gains in SOTS and GoPro experiments. The 688K-pair Robust-Seg dataset and the 30-hour/8-GPU training recipe make the contribution accessible to typical research labs, lowering the barrier for robust foundation-model adaptation. Open questions include how well the 15 synthetic degradations cover truly novel real-world corruptions, whether the approach extends to video segmentation or 3D, and whether the consistency-loss paradigm generalizes to other foundation models beyond SAM.
