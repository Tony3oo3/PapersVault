## Problem

Existing audio-visual pre-training methods such as Audio-CLIP and CAV-MAE align global features across modalities but neglect fine-grained region-level features and positional information, resulting in poor performance on dense prediction tasks like audio-visual source localization and segmentation. The paper asks how to incorporate region-aware, fine-grained cross-modal interaction into a pre-training strategy that also preserves strong global alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242111/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single video frame (224×224) and its paired audio spectrogram (1024×128), tokenizes both with 16×16 patches, and applies 75% random masking before encoding. During pre-training it produces two types of reconstruction targets: pixel-level reconstructions of the unmasked patches and embedding-level reconstructions of the complete feature vectors of the other modality. After pre-training, the learned encoders output audio-visual features that are fine-tuned for classification (mAP/accuracy), retrieval (R@k), or dense prediction (CIoU/AUC localization maps).

## Method

CrossMAE introduces a dual-level masked autoencoding framework combined with audio-visual contrastive loss. At the pixel level, Cross-Conditioned Reconstruction has each modality reconstruct its own unmasked pixels conditioned on Attentive Tokens (the top-25% tokens most attended by [CLS] in the other modality) injected via a cross-attention layer in the decoder. At the embedding level, Cross-Embedding Reconstruction uses DETR-inspired Learnable Queries—where each query encodes a specific position rather than an object—to guide unmasked tokens of one modality to generate the complete feature sequence of the other modality through a cross-attention decoder. The overall objective is L = λ·L_contrast + L_cross-cdt + L_cross-emb, where contrastive loss ensures global alignment while the two reconstruction losses enforce region-level cross-modal interaction. The key design insight is that Attentive Tokens carry rich positional semantics of salient regions, and Learnable Queries predefine the spatial/frequency structure of the reconstruction target, together enabling the model to learn coordinated, position-aware representations across modalities.

## Results

On audio-visual classification, CrossMAE achieves 55.3 mAP (A-V) on AudioSet-2M and 67.0% accuracy (A-V) on VGGSound-200K, outperforming the strongest prior method CAV-MAE (51.2 / 65.5) by 4.1 and 1.5 points respectively. On audio-visual source localization (a dense prediction task), CrossMAE reaches 73.20 CIoU and 42.61 AUC on Flickr-SoundSource, surpassing CAV-MAE (50.80 / 30.83) by 22.4 and 11.8 points. For audio-visual retrieval on Flickr-SoundNet, CrossMAE attains R@1 of 16.8 (A→V) and 18.2 (V→A), improving over CAV-MAE (12.2 / 18.2) and AV-MAE (not reported for retrieval).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossMAE outperforms all six compared baselines across every reported metric. Against Audio-CLIP [17], CrossMAE gains +14.1 mAP on AudioSet-2M A-V (55.3 vs 41.2) and +11.2 on VGGSound-200K A-V (67.0 vs 55.8), reflecting the advantage of region-aware pre-training over global contrastive alignment alone. Versus CAV-MAE [14], the closest prior audio-visual MAE method, CrossMAE improves A-V classification by +4.1 mAP (AudioSet-2M) and +1.5% (VGGSound-200K), and dramatically leads on dense prediction: +22.4 CIoU on Flickr-SoundSource AVSL (73.20 vs 50.80) and +11.8 AUC (42.61 vs 30.83). Compared to AV-MAE [11], CrossMAE achieves +3.5 mAP on AudioSet-2M A-V and +2.0% on VGGSound-200K A-V, while also reporting retrieval results where AV-MAE is absent. Against MBT [34] (a supervised multimodal transformer), CrossMAE surpasses it by +3.2 mAP on AudioSet-2M A-V and +2.9% on VGGSound-200K A-V despite using only self-supervised pre-training. Perceiver [21] and Attn AV [8] are weaker baselines (44.2 and 46.2 mAP on AudioSet-2M A-V) that CrossMAE exceeds by 11.1 and 9.1 mAP respectively, confirming that the dual-level cross-modal reconstruction (Cross-Conditioned + Cross-Embedding) is the key differentiator over prior global-alignment-only approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossMAE extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE, AudioMAE) into the audio-visual domain by introducing dual-level cross-modal reconstruction, building directly on CAV-MAE's combination of contrastive learning and MAE for joint pre-training. The key departure from prior audio-visual pre-training (Audio-CLIP, CAV-MAE, AV-MAE, MAViL) is the explicit targeting of region-level alignment rather than global feature matching, achieved through two novel mechanisms: Attentive Tokens (top-K attention to [CLS] from the counterpart modality) for pixel-level conditioning, and Learnable Queries adapted from DETR's object queries to encode positional information for embedding-level cross-modal feature generation. Unlike AV-MAE and MAViL which use joint masked autoencoding or two-stage training, CrossMAE employs a separate dual-level structure with end-to-end pre-training, and unlike prior methods that treat MAE as a self-reconstruction task, CrossMAE makes reconstruction explicitly cross-modal at both levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies and quantifies a significant gap: existing audio-visual pre-trained models, despite strong global alignment, fail at dense prediction tasks like source localization, suggesting that global contrastive objectives alone are insufficient for region-level understanding. The mutual reinforcement between contrastive (global) and cross-modal MAE (local) objectives, validated through careful ablations, provides a principled recipe for combining these paradigms. Open questions include whether the region-aware formulation generalizes beyond two modalities, whether the fixed 75%/25% masking and attentive-token ratios are optimal across domains, and whether the approach transfers to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., audio-visual segmentation) without task-specific architectural modifications. The method's reliance on paired audio-visual data during pre-training also limits applicability to unpaired or single-modality settings.
