## Problem

In the pretraining-finetuning paradigm, selecting a small subset of unlabeled data for annotation (active finetuning) produces a training set whose distribution deviates significantly from the full data pool, leading to overfitting. Existing methods like ActiveFT match the subset's distribution to the pool but fail to exploit the implicit category structure embedded in the large volume of unlabeled pretrained features, leaving the selected subset's per-class distribution uncalibrated and biased.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240273/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large unlabeled RGB image pool (resized to 224×224) and a small oracle-labeled subset selected via ActiveFT, with all features extracted by a DINO-pretrained DeiT-Small backbone. It produces an extended labeled pool that augments the oracle-labeled samples with pseudo-labeled samples drawn from the unlabeled pool, which is then used for supervised finetuning to output Top-1 class predictions. The pseudo-labels are assigned by matching generated features to real features via cosine similarity in FAISS, so no additional human annotation is required beyond the initial budget.

## Method

ActiveDC builds on ActiveFT's continuous-space distribution-matching selection and adds a Distribution Calibration Module that operates on the pretrained feature pool. It first applies Tukey's Ladder of Powers transformation (λ=0.5) to render the feature distribution more Gaussian-like, then clusters the transformed features with K-Means (K = number of true categories) and assigns pseudo-labels by aligning cluster centers with the labeled subset. Per-category mean and covariance are calibrated by blending the robust pseudo-category center (90% trimmed) with the labeled-data center, where the blend weight β = 1 − exp(−αr) increases with sampling ratio r, and a dispersion term ξ is added to the covariance. Pseudo-features are sampled from these calibrated Gaussians (2× the labeled count), and the most similar real features are retrieved via FAISS cosine search; a filtering step using Earth Mover's Distance removes generated features that would push the extended pool's distribution away from the full pool.

## Results

ActiveDC is evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1k using top-1 accuracy as the metric, with a DeiT-Small backbone pretrained via DINO. At the lowest sampling ratios, ActiveDC achieves 61.3% on CIFAR-10 (0.1%), 34.5% on CIFAR-100 (1%), and 50.9% on ImageNet (0.5%), consistently outperforming the strongest baseline ActiveFT by 14.2, 8.4, and 14.1 percentage points respectively. At higher sampling ratios the gap narrows (e.g., 90.3% vs. 90.1% on CIFAR-10 at 2%), confirming that distribution calibration is most impactful when the labeled budget is tight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveDC outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and sampling ratio where comparisons are valid. Against the strongest baseline ActiveFT [41], the gains are largest at low budgets: +14.2 pp on CIFAR-10 (0.1%), +17.3 pp on CIFAR-100 (5%), and +14.1 pp on ImageNet (0.5%), while at higher budgets the margin shrinks to 0.2–2.9 pp. The five traditional active learning methods (CoreSet, VAAL, LearnLoss, TA-VAAL, ALFA-Mix) are not applicable at the smallest budgets (marked '–') and, where applicable, trail ActiveDC by 3–15 pp; for example, on CIFAR-100 at 5%, ALFA-Mix reaches 50.4% versus ActiveDC's 71.9%. Among the baselines, K-Means is the closest competitor at very low CIFAR-10 budgets (40.3% at 0.1%) but still lags ActiveDC by 21 pp. The paper also demonstrates in Table 2 that the Distribution Calibration module can be plugged into any selection strategy (Random, FDS, K-Means, ActiveFT), yielding consistent 3–19 pp improvements, confirming that the calibration step is the primary source of the gain rather than the selection strategy itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ActiveFT (Xie et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced the active finetuning task and a distribution-matching selection strategy in continuous space. The key departure is the addition of a Distribution Calibration Module that exploits implicit category structure in unlabeled pretrained features—information that ActiveFT leaves underutilized. The calibration mechanism draws on ideas from few-shot distribution calibration (Yang et al. [43], Farquhar et al. [13]) and Tukey's Ladder of Powers transformation [14], repurposing them for the active finetuning setting. The paper also positions itself against traditional active learning methods (CoreSet, VAAL, etc.), which prior work has shown to underperform in the pretraining-finetuning paradigm due to iterative retraining and batch-selection biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the substantial accuracy gains (up to 10%) at very low sampling ratios, where annotation budgets are most constrained and distribution bias is most severe. The Distribution Calibration Module's demonstrated generality—plugging into Random, FDS, K-Means, and ActiveFT selection strategies—suggests it could serve as a broadly applicable post-selection refinement step. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with a single pretrained architecture (DeiT-Small/DINO), and the filtering process details are deferred to supplementary materials. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to dense prediction tasks, scales to larger pretrained models, and how sensitive it is to the quality of the initial clustering.
