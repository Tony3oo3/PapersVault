## Problem

Self-supervised learning in computational pathology has largely been confined to patch-level embeddings, and scaling SSL to produce meaningful slide-level representations from gigapixel whole-slide images remains difficult due to the computational cost of constructing slide views, unclear visual invariances at large scale, and intra-slide heterogeneity that weakens training signals. This paper asks whether complementary molecular information from gene expression profiles can serve as a stronger, task-agnostic training signal for slide representation learning than slide-only augmentations or masking.

## Input / Output

The method consumes gigapixel H&E whole-slide images (up to 150,000×150,000 pixels, processed at 20× magnification / 0.5 μm per pixel) paired with bulk transcriptomic measurements (RNA-seq for TCGA, microarrays for TG-GATEs), which are log2-fold-change normalized relative to a control group and reduced to the top 1,000 genes. The WSI is tessellated into patches encoded by a frozen pre-trained vision encoder, and the output is a single fixed-dimensional slide embedding vector that encodes morphological manifestations of the corresponding molecular signatures, usable directly for linear probing, prototyping, or cosine-similarity retrieval without further training.

## Method

TANGLE employs modality-specific encoders aligned via a symmetric cross-modal contrastive objective: a vision encoder (iBOT-Tox, a ViT-Base trained from scratch on 15M rat H&E patches, or CTransPath for human tissue) extracts patch embeddings that are pooled into a slide embedding by an attention-based MIL (ABMIL) module, while a 3-layer MLP encodes the gene expression vector into an expression embedding. The two embeddings are projected to a shared dimension and aligned by maximizing cosine similarity of matched (slide, expression) pairs while pushing apart mismatched pairs within a batch. The key insight is that gene expression provides a dense, high-dimensional molecular description of tissue that is complementary to the sparse spatial information in H&E, yielding a much stronger contrastive training signal than intra-modality slide augmentations. Ablations show that replacing the contrastive loss with L1/L2 regression degrades performance by ~7% AUC, and adding an intra-modality slide SSL objective (INTRA) actually hurts performance, confirming that the cross-modal signal is the primary driver of representation quality.

## Results

TANGLE achieves 84.7% Macro-AUC at k=10 for rat liver lesion classification (TG-GATEs, n=4,584), 84.8% for human breast cancer subtyping (in-house, n=1,265), and 91.5% for human lung cancer subtyping (in-house, n=1,946), evaluated via linear probing. These represent absolute gains of +5.9%, +11.0%, and +6.2% over the strongest supervised MIL baseline (ABMIL) at k=10, respectively. In slide retrieval, TANGLE retrieves 2.88/4 relevant slides at Recall@10 and 3.44/4 at Recall@20, outperforming all baselines. In prototype-based classification, TANGLE with k≥3 prototypes surpasses a fully supervised ABMIL trained on 18,552 slides.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANGLE outperforms all five baseline families across every dataset and k value. Against supervised MIL, TANGLE beats ABMIL by +5.9% (liver), +11.0% (breast), and +6.2% (lung) at k=10, and consistently surpasses TransMIL, which underperforms ABMIL in these low-data regimes. Against the simple average-patch-embedding baselines, TANGLE gains +23.2% over ResNet50+Avg. and +13.5% over CTransPath+Avg. in breast at k=10, confirming that domain-specific encoders (iBOT-Tox for rat, CTransPath for human) are critical but insufficient without the (S+E) alignment signal. Against the slide-level SSL baseline HIPT, TANGLE achieves +13.8% (breast) and +17.4% (lung) at k=10, demonstrating that intra-modality slide SSL provides a much weaker training signal than transcriptomics-guided contrastive alignment. The intra-modality variant INTRA performs comparably to HIPT, further validating that the cross-modal expression signal is the key driver of TANGLE's advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANGLE extends the multimodal contrastive learning paradigm popularized by CLIP-style vision-language models to the histology–gene expression domain, replacing text with transcriptomic profiles as the alignment signal. For slide-level pooling it relies on the well-established ABMIL architecture, and for patch encoding it builds on iBOT and CTransPath, but departs from prior slide-level SSL work (HIPT, Giga-SSL, INTRA) by using cross-modal alignment rather than intra-modality augmentations or masked reconstruction as the training signal. The paper also introduces iBOT-Tox, the first large-scale SSL vision encoder for rodent tissue, filling a gap in the CompToxPath literature where all prior SSL models were trained exclusively on human cancer samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that gene expression provides a substantially stronger task-agnostic training signal for slide embeddings than visual augmentations or masking, with consistent gains across three organs, two species, and three downstream task types. This opens a practical pathway for drug-safety assessment in preclinical toxicology, where paired expression data are routinely generated but slide-level AI tools have been limited to supervised MIL. Key limitations include the requirement for paired (S+E) data during pre-training, evaluation on only three tissue sites, and the observation that reconstruction-based variants (TANGLE-REC) suffer from dimensionality collapse. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to organs without readily available expression profiles, whether multimodal reconstruction objectives (e.g., masked expression recovery) can further improve performance, and whether the framework extends to spatial transcriptomics.
