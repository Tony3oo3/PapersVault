## Problem

The element-wise multiplication ('star operation') has shown consistent empirical superiority over summation in recent architectures (FocalNet, VAN, HorNet, etc.), yet its foundational rationale remains unexplored—existing explanations (modulation, high-order features, convolutional attention) are largely intuitive and lack rigorous analysis. This paper asks: why does this seemingly simple operation deliver such strong representational power, and can this understanding be leveraged for efficient network design?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240372/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard 224×224 RGB images and produces a single class label over 1000 ImageNet-1K categories. Internally, the star operation consumes two linearly projected feature vectors of width d and produces their element-wise product, which the paper shows implicitly spans a feature space of approximately (d/√2)² dimensions without any explicit widening. The network operates in a compact low-dimensional space throughout, with high-dimensional representation emerging implicitly through the multiplication structure.

## Method

The paper rewrites the star operation (W₁ᵀX * W₂ᵀX) as a sum of (d+2)(d+1)/2 ≈ (d/√2)² distinct non-linear terms, revealing that a single star layer implicitly maps d-dimensional inputs into a quadratic-order non-linear feature space—functionally analogous to a polynomial kernel. Stacking l layers compounds this effect, yielding implicit dimensions of (d/√2)^(2ˡ), which approaches infinity with just a handful of layers. Building on this insight, StarNet is a deliberately minimal 4-stage hierarchical CNN where each block applies two 1×1 convolutions, an element-wise multiplication, a depth-wise convolution, and ReLU6, with a fixed channel expansion factor of 4 and no reparameterization, attention, or SE-blocks. The architecture is scaled only by varying embedding width and block depth across four sizes (S1–S4), and is trained from scratch for 300 epochs with AdamW under a standard DeiT recipe, achieving competitive ImageNet accuracy with substantially lower latency than comparable efficient models.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, StarNet-S4 achieves 78.4% top-1 accuracy with 7.5M parameters and 1075M FLOPs, outperforming EdgeViT-XS (77.5%) by 0.9% while running 3× faster on iPhone 13 (1.0 ms vs. 3.5 ms). StarNet-S1 reaches 73.5% top-1 at just 2.9M params and 425M FLOPs, surpassing MobileOne-S0 (71.4%) by 2.1% at the same 0.7 ms mobile latency. The star operation consistently outperforms summation by 3.6–6.5% top-1 across varying widths and depths in the DemoNet ablation (Tables 2–3), and removing all activations causes only a 1.2% drop for star vs. 33.8% for summation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StarNet-S1 (73.5%) beats MobileOne-S0 (71.4%) by 2.1% at identical 0.7 ms iPhone 13 latency, and outperforms MobileV3-S (67.4%) and GhostNet0.5 (66.2%) by 6+ points at similar parameter budgets. StarNet-S4 (78.4%) surpasses EdgeViT-XS (77.5%) by 0.9% while being 3× faster on mobile (1.0 ms vs. 3.5 ms) and 2× faster on GPU (3.7 ms vs. 12.1 ms), and edges out MobileOne-S2 (77.4%) by 1.0% with fewer FLOPs (1075M vs. 1299M). StarNet-S3 (77.3%) matches or exceeds EfficientNet-B0 (77.1%) with comparable parameters (5.8M vs. 5.3M) and lower mobile latency (0.9 ms vs. 1.6 ms). On CPU, StarNet variants show a small latency penalty (e.g., 9.4 ms vs. 8.4 ms for StarNet-S4 star vs. sum), but the paper argues this is negligible given the 3+ point accuracy gain. StarNet-S2 (74.8%) outperforms EdgeViT-XXS (74.4%) by 0.4% while being 2.6× faster on mobile (0.7 ms vs. 1.8 ms) and 4.5× faster on GPU (2.0 ms vs. 8.9 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper takes the element-wise multiplication operation that has appeared independently across many recent architectures (FocalNet, HorNet, VAN, Monarch Mixer, Conv2Former) and provides a unified theoretical explanation for its effectiveness, reframing it through the lens of polynomial kernel functions from classical machine learning. Rather than building on a single prior method, it carves new conceptual ground by showing that the star operation implicitly constructs a feature space of approximately (d/√2)² linearly independent dimensions per layer, growing exponentially with depth — a property previously attributed only to kernel tricks or explicit width expansion. The resulting StarNet architecture deliberately departs from the taxonomy of efficient networks (depth-wise convolution, feature reuse, re-parameterization, NAS) by leveraging implicit high-dimensionality as its sole design principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a foundational rationale for a widely-used but poorly-understood operation, potentially redirecting future architecture design away from handcrafted heuristics toward principled exploitation of implicit dimensionality. The finding that removing all activations causes only a 1.2% drop (vs. 33.8% for summation) challenges the near-universal assumption that explicit non-linearity is indispensable, opening a promising but unexplored research direction. Open questions remain about optimizing coefficient distributions in the implicit space, the precise relationship between star operations and matrix multiplication in self-attention, and whether activation-free networks can be made fully competitive at scale. StarNet serves as a proof-of-concept rather than a final architecture, and the authors explicitly leave engineering optimizations to future work.
