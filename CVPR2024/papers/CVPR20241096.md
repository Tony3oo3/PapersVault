## Problem

Density-based crowd counting methods rely on broad Gaussian kernels to construct ground truth density maps, causing background noise accumulation and loss of density in congested regions. Narrowing the kernel would mitigate these issues, but regression-based networks fail to learn from narrow-kernel density maps, and existing GAN-based generative approaches still use broad kernels while discarding the stochastic multi-realization capability of generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241096/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB crowd image (trained at 256×256) as input and produces a crowd density map via a reverse diffusion process conditioned on the image. At inference, four stochastic density map realizations are generated, each thresholded into a binary dot map (crowd map), and these are fused into a single compound map whose dot count serves as the final crowd count. An auxiliary regression branch that directly predicts the count from U-Net features is active only during training and discarded at inference.

## Method

CrowdDiff formulates crowd density map generation as a conditional denoising diffusion process: a U-Net denoising network (initialized from ImageNet-pretrained super-resolution weights) predicts the noise added at each of 1,000 timesteps, conditioned on the crowd image, using a hybrid loss with SNR-based weighting. The key architectural insight is using extremely narrow Gaussian kernels (1×1 or 3×3, σ=0.5) for ground truth density maps, which keeps pixel value distributions tight and enables counting via thresholding and dot detection rather than density summation, thereby eliminating background noise accumulation. During training, an auxiliary regression branch estimates the count from globally-pooled encoder-decoder features at each timestep to improve feature learning under noisy conditions. At inference, DDIM sampling generates multiple stochastic density map realizations, which are thresholded into dot maps and fused in ascending order of cumulative SSIM similarity, with a rejection-radius mechanism (β=0.85, k=4 nearest neighbors) to suppress duplicate points across realizations.

## Results

CrowdDiff achieves state-of-the-art MAE on all six public crowd counting benchmarks: 47.3 on JHU-Crowd++, 47.4 on ShanghaiTech A, 5.7 on ShanghaiTech B, 160.8 on UCF-CC-50, 68.9 on UCF-QNRF, and 57.8 on NWPU-Crowd. The largest gains appear on dense, large-image datasets (UCF-QNRF: 68.9 vs. 74.3 for STEERER; NWPU-Crowd: 57.8 vs. 63.7 for STEERER), where narrow-kernel density thresholding avoids background noise accumulation. On ShanghaiTech B, CrowdDiff ties CrowdHat on MAE (5.7) but improves MSE from 9.4 to 8.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrowdDiff outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against the strongest recent methods, it beats STEERER (ICCV'23) by 6.0 MAE on JHU-Crowd++, 7.1 on UCF-QNRF, and 5.9 on NWPU-Crowd; and beats CrowdHat (CVPR'23) by 5.0 on JHU-Crowd++, 5.2 on UCF-QNRF, and 10.9 on NWPU-Crowd. On UCF-CC-50, CrowdDiff improves over GauNet (CVPR'22) by 25.5 MAE (160.8 vs. 186.3), the largest single-dataset gain in the table. On ShanghaiTech B, CrowdDiff ties CrowdHat on MAE (5.7) but reduces MSE from 9.4 to 8.2. The gains are most pronounced on dense, high-resolution datasets (UCF-QNRF, NWPU-Crowd, JHU-Crowd++), consistent with the paper's argument that narrow-kernel thresholding avoids the background-noise accumulation that penalizes density-summation methods on large images.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrowdDiff repurposes the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020; Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) and the SNR-based loss weighting scheme (Choi et al., 2022) for the first time in crowd density map generation, departing from both regression-based density estimation and GAN-based density prediction. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between density-based and localization-based crowd counting by using narrow Gaussian kernels with a generative model, circumventing the known failure of narrow kernels under regression (Xu et al., 2022) while avoiding the density heuristics required by point-query methods. The stochastic multi-realization fusion mechanism has no direct analogue in prior crowd counting pipelines, which are deterministic and produce a single density map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing trade-off between kernel width and counting accuracy in density-based methods can be resolved by switching from regression to a generative formulation, offering a principled path to noise-robust counting without density summation. The 4× MAE gap between thresholding and summation on the same density map (Table 8) highlights how much of prior density-based error was an artifact of the counting operation rather than the density map itself. Open questions include whether the iterative inference cost (even with DDIM) is acceptable for real-time deployment, whether the narrow-kernel + diffusion paradigm transfers to other dense object counting domains (e.g., cells, vehicles), and whether the multi-realization fusion strategy generalizes beyond four samples.
