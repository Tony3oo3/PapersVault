## Problem

In source-free universal domain adaptation (SF-UniDA), the key challenge is identifying target-private unknown categories without access to source data. Existing approaches rely on hand-crafted global thresholds (sub-optimal across scenarios) or iterative K-means clustering (computationally expensive and unstable in high dimensions due to the curse of dimensionality), leaving a gap for a principled, efficient mechanism to separate common from private data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained source model (feature extractor g_θ^s and classifier h_θ^s) plus a batch of unlabeled target RGB images. The classifier weights W_cls ∈ R^{C×D} are extracted for SVD-based subspace construction, while only the target feature extractor g_θ^t is updated during adaptation. Output consists of per-sample pseudo-labels (one-hot common class or 'unknown') and the adapted feature extractor, with no additional C+1 class introduced for private data (a uniform distribution is used instead).

## Method

LEAD performs orthogonal feature decomposition via SVD on the frozen classifier weights, splitting the D-dimensional feature space into a source-known subspace (span of the first C right-singular vectors) and a source-unknown subspace (remaining D−C vectors). Normalized target features are projected onto both subspaces, and the L2 norm of the source-unknown projection ‖z_{i,unk}^t‖₂ serves as a private-data indicator, whose bimodal distribution is modeled by a two-component GMM. Rather than a fixed global threshold, LEAD constructs instance-level decision boundaries ρ_{i,c} using a 'common score' ε_{i,c} that fuses (via geometric mean) the cosine-distance-based proximity to both a target prototype (top-K sampling) and a source anchor (classifier weight vector), yielding category- and instance-adaptive thresholds. Training combines a Student's-t-weighted cross-entropy loss on pseudo-labels, a feature decomposition regularizer encouraging private data to dominate in the source-unknown subspace, and a nearest-neighbor consensus regularizer, all optimized jointly with a single trade-off parameter λ.

## Results

In the most challenging OPDA scenario, LEAD achieves 87.8% H-score on Office-31, 50.8% on DomainNet, and 76.6% on VisDA, outperforming the strongest source-free baseline GLC by 3.5% on VisDA while matching it on Office-31. When integrated with UMAD, LEAD boosts the H-score from 70.1% to 78.0% on Office-Home (+7.9%) and from 58.3% to 67.2% on VisDA (+8.9%). LEAD also reduces the runtime for deriving pseudo-labeling decision boundaries by 75% on VisDA (0.11s vs 0.46s) and by over 99% on DomainNet (0.29s vs 897.65s) compared to GLC's iterative clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-source-free baselines, LEAD (87.8% on Office-31, 76.6% on VisDA) surpasses all methods including GATE (87.6%/56.4%), UniOT (91.1%/57.3%), and DCC (80.2%/43.0%), with the largest gaps over DANCE (80.3%/42.8%) and CMU (73.1%/32.9%). Among source-free baselines, LEAD dramatically outperforms SHOT-O (75.0%/44.0%) and UMAD (87.0%/58.3%), with the VisDA gap over UMAD reaching 18.3%. Against the strongest SF-UniDA baseline GLC, LEAD matches on Office-31 (87.8% vs 87.8%), wins on VisDA (76.6% vs 73.1%, +3.5%), but trails on DomainNet (50.8% vs 55.1%); however, integrating LEAD into GLC recovers and slightly exceeds GLC's DomainNet score (55.4%). The complementary nature of LEAD is most evident when added to UMAD, yielding +7.9% on Office-Home and +8.9% on VisDA, demonstrating that LEAD's instance-level decomposition-based decision boundary addresses a different failure mode than UMAD's thresholding approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAD builds on the source-free universal domain adaptation (SF-UniDA) framework established by GLC and UMAD, which freeze the source classifier and adapt only the feature extractor. It departs from prior private-data identification strategies—hand-crafted entropy/confidence thresholds and iterative K-means clustering—by introducing an orthogonal feature decomposition (via SVD of the classifier weights) that separates features into source-known and source-unknown subspaces. This repurposes the classical ICA/SVD decomposition idea, previously applied to content-style disentanglement in domain adaptation, toward the distinct goal of identifying target-private categories rather than achieving domain invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 75% reduction in decision-boundary derivation time and the demonstrated complementarity to existing SF-UniDA methods (improving both UMAD and GLC without architectural changes), making LEAD a drop-in module for the broader source-free adaptation community. A notable limitation is the slight PDA degradation when combined with GLC, hinting at gradient conflicts between decomposition regularization and clustering objectives that remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the orthogonal-complement assumption holds for non-linear or transformer-based classifiers, and whether the GMM-based bimodal distribution assumption generalizes to highly imbalanced or multi-modal private categories.
