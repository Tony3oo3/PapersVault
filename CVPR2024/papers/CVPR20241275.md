## Problem

NeRF-based novel view synthesis on street scenes suffers from sparse, nearly collinear camera trajectories and low-texture road surfaces, making geometry and appearance reconstruction ill-posed. While Lidar provides explicit geometric cues, prior works either use it only as a sparse depth supervision signal or rely solely on point cloud features (yielding low-resolution, incomplete rendering), leaving its potential largely untapped for high-quality street scene rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241275/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized front-camera RGB images and spinning Lidar point clouds with known camera poses; dynamic objects are masked via bounding boxes and instance segmentation. Lidar points are accumulated across adjacent frames to increase density, and colorized point clouds are projected onto synthetically perturbed camera positions to generate augmented training views. The output is photorealistic novel view renderings (evaluated under both interpolation and lateral lane-shift extrapolation of 2 m and 3.7 m) along with per-pixel depth maps from volume rendering.

## Method

LidaRF fuses two complementary scene representations: a 3D sparse UNet encodes geometric features from voxelized, temporally accumulated Lidar point clouds, and these features are queried at each ray sample via fixed-radius nearest-neighbor search (K=6 within 0.3 m) and aggregated by inverse-distance weighting; the resulting Lidar encoding is concatenated with the Instant-NGP hash-grid encoding and decoded by small MLPs for density and color. For depth supervision, a curriculum-learning scheme progressively expands the valid depth range from near to far field while filtering occluded points whose ground-truth depth exceeds the NeRF prediction by a decaying offset, combined with an exact CDF-based line-of-sight loss. Additionally, colorized accumulated Lidar points are projected onto stochastically perturbed camera views to synthesize augmented training images, addressing the limited angular coverage of forward-driving trajectories.

## Results

On Pandaset (interpolation), LidaRF achieves 27.255 PSNR, 0.812 SSIM, and 0.224 LPIPS, outperforming all baselines. On the extrapolation (lane shift) setting, LidaRF attains FID of 106.5 at 2 m and 126.0 at 3.7 m, substantially better than Nerfacto (116.7 / 151.0) and UniSim (118.5 / 141.3). On NuScenes, LidaRF reaches 31.162 PSNR and 0.211 LPIPS, surpassing S-NeRF (29.377 PSNR, 0.349 LPIPS) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LidaRF outperforms all five baselines on every metric in Table 1. Against the strongest implicit baseline Nerfacto, LidaRF gains +0.13 PSNR, +0.008 SSIM, and −0.044 LPIPS on interpolation, with a more pronounced FID improvement of −10.2 (2 m) and −25.0 (3.7 m) on lane shift. UniSim, which also uses Lidar depth supervision, is surpassed by LidaRF by +1.24 PSNR and −0.118 LPIPS on interpolation, and by −12.0 / −15.3 FID on extrapolation. The gap widens dramatically against Mip-NeRF 360 (−3.56 PSNR) and Instant-NGP (−2.97 PSNR), which lack any Lidar integration. On NuScenes (Table 3), LidaRF beats S-NeRF by +1.79 PSNR and −0.138 LPIPS, confirming the advantage generalizes beyond Pandaset. On Argoverse, qualitative comparison with NeRF-LiDAR-cGAN [9] shows LidaRF produces high-resolution, complete renderings where the point-cloud-only baseline yields blank regions outside Lidar coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

LidaRF builds directly on the Nerfacto framework (hash-grid encoding, proposal networks, fused MLPs) and extends it with a hybrid representation that fuses Lidar-derived geometric features—extracted via a 3D sparse UNet inspired by BEVFusion-style architectures—with the implicit hash grid, departing from prior point-only approaches like Point-NeRF and NeRF-LiDAR-cGAN that rely solely on point features for radiance decoding. For depth supervision, it adapts the line-of-sight loss from URF but introduces a curriculum-based occlusion-aware filtering scheme that replaces the depth-completion-network approach of S-NeRF with temporal accumulation of raw Lidar frames. The augmented-view strategy, which projects colorized accumulated Lidar onto perturbed camera poses to synthesize additional training views, has no direct precedent in the street-scene NeRF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: NeRF-based simulators for autonomous driving struggle with the sparse, near-collinear camera trajectories inherent to forward-driving data collection, and the extrapolation (lane-shift) setting is where simulation fidelity matters most for safety testing. The demonstrated FID improvement in extrapolation (151→126) is the most consequential result, suggesting that deeper Lidar integration specifically helps where prior methods fail. A key limitation is that only static backgrounds are handled; the authors note that extending the insights to dynamic objects is an open direction. The curriculum-based occlusion filtering and the finding that offline hidden-point-removal preprocessing is inferior to online NeRF-informed filtering offer transferable lessons for any depth-supervised implicit reconstruction pipeline.
