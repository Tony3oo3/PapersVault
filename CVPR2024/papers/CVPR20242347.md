## Problem

The Hierarchical Volume Sampling (HVS) strategy in NeRF approximates the weight function w(t) using piecewise constant functions, which keeps fine-stage sampling points uniformly distributed within each interval regardless of where the surface actually lies. This coarse approximation fails to exploit the L₀-like binary structure of w(t) (near-zero everywhere except at the surface), resulting in wasted samples in empty space and slower convergence toward accurate geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242347/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same inputs as standard NeRF pipelines: multi-view RGB images with known or SfM-recovered camera parameters. It produces rendered novel-view images (evaluated via PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and, when integrated with surface-reconstruction frameworks like NeuS, 3D meshes (evaluated via Chamfer distance). Crucially, it is a parameter-free drop-in replacement for the fine-stage sampling step, requiring only ~10 lines of code modification and introducing no additional network parameters or computational overhead beyond the original HVS.

## Method

L₀-Sampler replaces the piecewise constant interpolation of w(t) in HVS with a piecewise exponential function ŵ(s) = a(b/a)^s over each normalized interval [0,1], where a and b are the endpoint weights from the coarse stage. This function is monotonic, adaptively steepens when the endpoint weight ratio b/a is large (concentrating samples toward the surface), and degenerates toward linear when weights are similar (preserving uniform sampling in ambiguous regions). The integral and inverse CDF both have closed-form solutions, enabling efficient inverse transform sampling with no extra computation. The authors also incorporate the maxblur technique from mip-NeRF to smooth the coarse weights before interpolation, broadening peak regions so the steep exponential can rebalance sampling density across intervals. The approach generalizes HVS (piecewise constant is a special case) and is validated as a plug-in improvement across NeRF, mip-NeRF, Instant NGP, NeRF++, and NeuS.

## Results

On the Blender dataset, L0-Sampler consistently improves PSNR across all three NeRF frameworks: NeRF gains +0.3 to +0.6 dB (e.g., Lego 31.39→31.97, Mic 33.18→33.61), mip-NeRF gains +0.02 to +0.59 dB (e.g., Ficus 31.87→32.46), and Instant NGP gains +0.27 to +0.98 dB (e.g., Hotdog 34.68→35.66, Chair 32.07→33.05). LPIPS also decreases in most cases, indicating better perceptual quality. For 3D surface reconstruction, applying L0-Sampler to NeuS reduces the mean Chamfer distance on DTU from 0.848 to 0.810. All improvements are achieved with approximately the same training time and only ~10 lines of code modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRF, L0-Sampler wins on all 8 Blender scenes in PSNR (average gain ≈ +0.45 dB) and on 7/8 scenes in LPIPS, with the largest PSNR gain on Mic (+0.43 dB) and the largest LPIPS reduction on Lego (0.0400→0.0346). Against mip-NeRF, L0-Sampler wins on 7/8 scenes in PSNR (Ficus shows the largest gain at +0.59 dB) and on 6/8 in LPIPS; Ship and Mic show near-ties. Against Instant NGP, L0-Sampler wins on all 8 scenes in PSNR (Hotdog +0.98 dB, Chair +0.98 dB are the largest) and on 7/8 in LPIPS, though Drums LPIPS slightly worsens (0.1469→0.1616). For surface reconstruction, L0-Sampler applied to NeuS reduces the mean DTU Chamfer distance from 0.848 to 0.810, with the largest per-scene improvement on DTU-40 (0.545→0.475). In a direct comparison with sparsity loss (Table 2), L0-Sampler outperforms it on all four tested scenes (e.g., Lego 31.97 vs. 31.58 PSNR) while being parameter-free and requiring no extra loss computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends NeRF's Hierarchical Volume Sampling (HVS) by replacing its piecewise constant interpolation of the weight function w(t) with piecewise exponential interpolation, motivated by the observation that a well-trained w(t) closely resembles the L₀ distance between points and the surface. It also incorporates the maxblur smoothing from mip-NeRF to refine the coarse-stage weights before interpolation. Conceptually, it departs from sparsity-loss approaches (e.g., [51, 62]) that penalize volume density, instead using the L₀ model to directly shape the sampling distribution without modifying the density network. The original HVS is recovered as a special case of the proposed framework, positioning L₀-Sampler as a strict generalization of the most widely used NeRF sampling strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in its extreme simplicity: roughly ten lines of code, no additional parameters, and no extra computational overhead make it a nearly free drop-in upgrade for any HVS-based NeRF pipeline (NeRF, mip-NeRF, Instant NGP, NeuS). However, the performance gains are modest (typically 0.2–0.5 dB PSNR) and not universal—some scenes show slight degradation. The authors acknowledge that the method only improves fine-stage sampling and leave the coarse stage unaddressed, which is a natural but open direction. For practitioners already using HVS, the cost-benefit ratio is favorable, but the contribution does not change how the broader NeRF pipeline is structured.
