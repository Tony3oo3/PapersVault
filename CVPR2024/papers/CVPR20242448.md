## Problem

Promptable segmentation models like SAM enable effortless one-click extraction of any object from any image, posing serious copyright and privacy risks. Existing adversarial attacks on these models are either prompt-specific (overfitting to particular prompts and failing to generalize) or untargeted feature disruptions that point outside the image manifold and thus transfer poorly across architectures. The paper introduces the 'Anything Unsegmentable' task, requiring a single perturbation that degrades segmentation across all prompt types and model parameterizations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a quasi-imperceptible adversarial perturbation r constrained to an L-infinity ball of radius ε (typically 8/255 or 12/255), yielding an adversarial image I+r. The perturbation is crafted on a source model (SAM-B) and must transfer to unseen models (SAM-L, SAM-H, FastSAM) and unseen prompt types (points, boxes, free-form text). Evaluation uses mIoU, ASR@50, and ASR@10 averaged over randomized prompts (5 point samples per mask, 3 box size variations) on the SAM-1B dataset.

## Method

UAD is a two-stage differentiable optimization. Stage 1 (Deformation) optimizes a flow-field parameter w to warp the original image into a target deformed image Î, balancing three losses: SSIM (maximizing structural dissimilarity), a control loss combining total variation and variance of the flow field (preserving local natural patterns), and a fidelity loss that uses a proxy adversarial sample to ensure the deformed target remains within feasible feature distance of the perturbation budget. Stage 2 (Feature Simulation) then optimizes the adversarial perturbation r to minimize cosine feature distance to the deformed target while maximizing distance from the original image features. The key insight is that targeted feature attacks pointing inside the image manifold (toward a natural-image-like deformed target) transfer far better than untargeted attacks pointing outside it, because the deformation target provides a consistent, recognizable update direction across different segmentation architectures.

## Results

On the SAM-1B dataset (1000 images, 98,875 masks), UAD achieves the lowest mIoU across all four target models: 51.53 on SAM-B (white-box), 66.07 on SAM-L, 68.96 on SAM-H, and 28.83 on FastSAM. Attack success rates are also the highest, with ASR@50 reaching 43.89% on SAM-B and 69.95% on FastSAM, and ASR@10 reaching 20.79% and 59.63% respectively. All adversarial examples are crafted on SAM-B (smallest model) and transferred to the other three as black-box targets, demonstrating strong cross-model and cross-prompt generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAD outperforms all six baselines across every model and metric. The closest competitors are the targeted feature attacks AA [19] and PATA/PATA++ [59], which achieve mIoU around 61 on SAM-B; UAD reduces this to 51.53, a gap of roughly 10 mIoU points and 11 ASR@50 points. The untargeted feature attacks TAP [60] and ILPD [26] perform slightly worse than the targeted ones (mIoU ~63 vs ~61 on SAM-B), confirming the paper's finding that perturbations pointing inside the image manifold transfer better than those pointing outside it. Attack-SAM-K [56], the prompt-specific baseline, is the weakest overall (mIoU 68.07 on SAM-B, ASR@50 only 24.10%), validating the paper's observation that prompt-specific adversarial noises overfit and lack generalizability. On FastSAM, UAD achieves the largest absolute gains (mIoU 28.83 vs 32.64 for AA), likely because FastSAM's limited training data (2% of SAM-1B) makes it more susceptible to structural deformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-space adversarial attack lineage (AA, TAP, ILPD) by introducing a differentiable deformation function to synthesize the target image rather than relying on a fixed or randomly sampled one. It departs from prompt-specific attacks on SAM (Attack-SAM, PATA) by targeting the image encoder's feature space directly, motivated by the observation that prompt-specific perturbations overfit and fail to transfer across prompts. The two-stage pipeline—first optimizing a flow-field deformation to create a structurally dissimilar yet manifold-feasible target, then aligning adversarial features to that target—repurposes spatial transformer networks [20] in a novel adversarial context and builds on the targeted feature perturbation framework of Inkawwhich et al. [19].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and timely concern: protecting digital assets, artwork, and personal photos from effortless extraction via foundation segmentation models like SAM. The finding that targeted feature perturbations (toward natural-image-like targets) transfer far better than untargeted ones (away from original features) for foundation segmentation models challenges the default assumption from classification literature and suggests that the robustness of SAM-like models to random corruptions specifically negates out-of-manifold perturbations. Open questions include whether the deformation-based target selection generalizes beyond the SAM family, whether the attack remains effective against future models with stronger corruption robustness, and whether the moderate mIoU reductions (~50–68) are sufficient for real-world protection or whether stronger perturbations are needed.
