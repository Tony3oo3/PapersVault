## Problem

Existing MIL-based methods for weakly-supervised audio-visual video parsing tend to focus on the most discriminative segments, resulting in incomplete temporal localization and inconsistent labeling of visually similar frames. Additionally, the single weight vector per event class in the frame-level classifier is insufficient to capture intra-class variation across time, modalities, and training examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240662/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 10-second unconstrained YouTube videos split into T non-overlapping temporal segments, each with a 128-d audio feature (VGGish) and a 512-d visual feature (ResNet-152 + R(2+1)D-18). Training uses only video-level binary labels indicating which of C=25 events are present, while at test time the model must predict per-segment, per-modality binary event labels (audio and visual independently), effectively providing temporal localization of each event across both streams.

## Method

The method constructs multiple prototype features per event class by first selecting high-confidence positive and negative segments from MIL model predictions, then applying k-means clustering (k=10) on features from a momentum encoder to obtain cluster centroids as prototypes. Pseudo labels are assigned to all training segments via softmax similarity to positive vs. negative prototypes (soft labeling with temperature τ=0.1), and the model is re-trained with a combined loss of MIL cross-entropy, pseudo-label cross-entropy, and a contrastive loss that pulls segments sharing predicted events closer in feature space. Pseudo labels are re-estimated every epoch, and the authors show this iterative procedure is equivalent to an EM algorithm with segment-level labels as latent variables, providing convergence guarantees. The use of multiple prototypes per class (rather than a single classifier weight vector) and the contrastive regularization are the key mechanisms that improve temporal consistency and full-extent localization over standard MIL.

## Results

On the LLP dataset, the proposed method achieves 65.9% segment-level F1 for audio events, 66.7% for visual events, and 61.9% for audio-visual events, outperforming the previous best (CMPAE) by 1.7, 0.3, and 2.7 points respectively. Averaged over all ten metrics, the method improves by 1.86 points over CMPAE, with a particularly strong 2.6-point gain on audio-visual event-level F1 (54.3% vs. 51.8%). On the THUMOS14 TAL task, the method achieves 56.4% mAP at tIoU [0.1:0.5], matching the SOTA DELU (56.5%) while surpassing it at tIoU 0.7 (20.1% vs. 15.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines on the LLP dataset across every reported metric. The closest competitor, CMPAE [11], is surpassed by 1.7 points on Audio Seg. F1 (65.9 vs. 64.2), 2.7 points on AV Seg. F1 (61.9 vs. 59.2), and 2.5 points on AV Event F1 (54.3 vs. 51.8). Earlier MIL-based methods (HAN, MA, CVCM-MA, MGN-MA) trail by 5–13 points on audio-visual metrics, confirming that the prototype-based pseudo-labeling addresses the core limitation of MIL focusing only on discriminative segments. On the TAL task (THUMOS14), the method matches DELU [3] at tIoU [0.1:0.5] (56.4 vs. 56.5 mAP) but clearly wins at stricter thresholds: 20.1% vs. 15.3% at tIoU 0.7, and 37.8% vs. 37.4% at [0.3:0.7]. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that naive pseudo-labeling (Base+NPL) actually degrades performance, while prototype-based soft pseudo-labeling (Base+PPLS) yields the full 6.4-point gain over the MIL baseline on Audio Seg. F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the HAN architecture (Tian et al., ECCV 2020) and the instance-level MIL framework (Ilse et al., ICML 2018) that underpin most prior weakly-supervised AVVP methods. Its key departure is replacing the single weight-vector classifier with multiple k-means-derived prototypes per event class, then using feature similarity to these prototypes to generate segment-level pseudo labels for retraining. This contrasts with prior pseudo-labeling approaches for temporal tasks (e.g., EM-MIL, Luo et al. ECCV 2020) that rely on the model's own segment predictions or attention weights, and with other AVVP methods that add cross-modal or cross-video supervision signals. The paper further distinguishes itself by providing an EM-theoretic interpretation of the iterative pseudo-label retraining loop and by incorporating contrastive learning to structure the feature space around pseudo labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quality of pseudo labels (not merely their existence) is the critical factor in weakly-supervised AVVP, with prototype-based soft labels substantially outperforming both naive pseudo-labeling and hard prototype labels. The EM interpretation provides convergence guarantees and clarifies why soft labeling is necessary, offering a principled lens for future pseudo-labeling designs. Limitations include sensitivity to the negative-segment threshold β, a fixed number of prototypes across all events regardless of intra-class variation, and the possibility of EM converging to local optima. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond AVVP and TAL to other weakly-supervised video prediction tasks, and whether adaptive prototype counts per class could further improve performance.
