## Problem

Human-object contact estimation and 3D human-object reconstruction have been studied as separate tracks: contact methods ignore 3D geometry of both human and object surfaces, while reconstruction methods either heuristically pre-define contact regions or fail to exploit contact information meaningfully. This paper unifies both tasks into a single end-to-end framework that uses 3D-guided contact estimation to drive accurate joint reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242485/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 5-channel input tensor (RGB image concatenated with human and object segmentation masks from DetectronV2) and produces a refined 3D human mesh (431 downsampled SMPL+H vertices), a refined 3D object mesh (64 vertices), and per-vertex binary contact maps for both the human (431 values) and object (64 values). The reconstruction target region is cropped using a ground-truth bounding box during both training and inference, and the human mesh is downsampled from the full SMPL+H topology to reduce computational cost.

## Method

CONTHO is a three-stage pipeline: (1) an initial reconstruction module (based on Hand4Whole) predicts SMPL+H body/hand parameters and 6DoF object pose from a ResNet-50 backbone; (2) ContactFormer extracts 3D vertex features by grid-sampling image features at projected vertex positions and concatenating per-vertex 3D coordinates, then applies cross-attention Transformers between human and object vertex features to predict contact maps; (3) CRFormer refines the meshes by first applying contact-based masking (zeroing out non-contact vertex features) before cross-attention between human and object, combined with self-attention on unmasked features for non-contact parts. The key insight is that contact-based masking forces the refinement network to aggregate only features from physically contacting regions, which both spotlights contact as the primary signal and prevents the network from learning spurious correlations (e.g., object pose always tracking human head pose). The entire system is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy on contact maps, L1 vertex/edge losses on refined meshes, and parameter/joint/box losses on initial predictions.

## Results

On BEHAVE, CONTHO achieves a Chamfer distance of 4.99 cm for 3D human reconstruction and 8.42 cm for 3D object reconstruction, with contact precision of 0.628 and recall of 0.496 from the reconstructed meshes. On InterCap, it reaches 5.96 cm (human) and 9.50 cm (object) Chamfer distance with 0.661 contact precision and 0.432 recall. For human-object contact estimation, CONTHO attains 0.754 precision / 0.587 recall on BEHAVE and 0.660 / 0.612 on InterCap, outperforming all prior contact estimation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONTHO substantially outperforms both reconstruction baselines. On BEHAVE, it reduces human Chamfer distance from 12.17 cm (PHOSA) and 5.58 cm (CHORE) to 4.99 cm, and object Chamfer distance from 26.62 cm (PHOSA) and 10.66 cm (CHORE) to 8.42 cm; on InterCap the gains are similarly large (5.96 vs. 11.20 vs. 7.01 cm for human, 9.50 vs. 20.57 vs. 12.81 cm for object). For contact estimation, CONTHO wins on both datasets against all three baselines: on BEHAVE it achieves 0.754/0.587 precision/recall versus BSTRO (0.615/0.527), DECO (0.638/0.337), and POSA (0.514/0.299); on InterCap it reaches 0.660/0.612 versus DECO (0.635/0.479), BSTRO (0.506/0.427), and POSA (0.561/0.333). The largest margin is against PHOSA in reconstruction, where the optimization-based approach suffers from imperfect 2D silhouette targets, and against DECO in contact estimation, where the lack of 3D geometric guidance limits recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONTHO unifies two previously separate research tracks—human-object contact estimation (POSA, BSTRO, DECO) and 3D human-object reconstruction (PHOSA, CHORE, D3D-HOI)—into a single end-to-end learning framework. For initial reconstruction, it builds on Hand4Whole's 3D body and hand pipeline and standard 6DoF object pose estimation. The key conceptual departure is using intermediate 3D mesh geometry as explicit guidance for contact estimation (rather than relying solely on 2D image features) and introducing contact-based masking in CRFormer to prevent the network from learning spurious human-object pose correlations that optimization-based methods avoid by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification and mitigation of undesired human-object correlation (e.g., a monitor always facing the human head regardless of image evidence) is a useful diagnostic insight for any joint human-object learning system, and the sensitivity-test protocol provides a practical tool for detecting such issues. However, the method still depends on external segmentation (DetectronV2), a fixed SMPL+H parametric model, and a single object template, limiting applicability to open-vocabulary or multi-object scenes. Open questions include whether the contact-based masking strategy generalizes to video sequences, articulated objects, or scenarios where contact is ambiguous or absent, and whether the end-to-end approach can match optimization-based methods in physically constrained settings.
