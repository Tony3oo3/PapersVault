## Problem

Existing 3D hand-object pose estimation methods rely on explicit intermediate representations (2D keypoints, 3D mesh vertices) that only encode local shape information near an initial prediction, making them vulnerable to severe occlusions. The paper argues that implicit signed distance fields (SDFs) provide global, dense shape constraints over the entire reconstruction volume, but prior SDF-based methods use them only as final outputs for mesh reconstruction rather than as intermediate representations to guide pose regression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240170/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces MANO hand parameters (16 joint angles + 10 shape parameters β), a 3D object pose (rotation vector and translation vector), and a hand mesh. The SDF is regressed in the original camera space rather than a pose-aligned canonical space, deliberately prioritizing global plausibility over local fine-grained detail. Object pose is obtained by averaging per-point predictions across all sampled object query points, while hand pose uses both MANO regression and dense local offset regression from each query point to its joints.

## Method

HOISDF comprises two end-to-end trained modules: (1) a global SDF learning module that uses a U-Net (ResNet-50 backbone) to extract hierarchical pixel-aligned image features, then regresses hand and object signed distances via 3-layer MLP decoders conditioned on Fourier positional encodings of 3D query points; (2) a field-guided pose regression module that samples query points near predicted surfaces using SDF values, augments point features with volume-density scalars derived from the SDF (σ = α⁻¹·sigmoid(−d/α)), introduces cross-field interaction by querying each target's SDF at the other target's points, and applies multi-head self-attention across all point features to capture global 3D shape consistency before regressing poses. The key distinction from prior work is that SDFs serve as an intermediate representation whose global information is actively extracted and injected into the pose regression pipeline, rather than being the final output.

## Results

On the DexYCB split, HOISDF achieves MJE 10.1 mm, PAMJE 5.31 mm, OCE 18.4 mm, MCE 27.4 mm, and ADD-S 13.3 mm, outperforming all prior methods on every metric. On HO3Dv2, HOISDF reaches MJE 23.6 mm, STMJE 22.8 mm, PAMJE 9.6 mm, OME 48.5 mm, and ADD-S 17.8 mm (without synthetic data), and further improves to MJE 19.0 mm and OME 35.5 mm when co-trained with synthetic renders. The gains are largest on global metrics (MJE, STMJE, object metrics), consistent with the SDF providing global shape constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOISDF outperforms all 15 compared baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. Against the strongest hand-only methods (Lin et al. [32], Park et al. [39], Xu et al. [52]), HOISDF reduces MJE by 3.9–5.1 mm and PAMJE by 0.5–1.7 mm. Against the closest hand-object competitors, HOISDF beats Lin et al. [33] by 1.8 mm MJE and 21.4 mm OCE, and Wang et al. [49] by 2.6 mm MJE, 8.9 mm OCE, and 2.6 mm ADD-S. The SDF-based baselines (Chen et al. [12, 13]) that use SDFs as output meshes are substantially outperformed (MJE 10.1 vs. 14.4–19.0), confirming the benefit of using SDFs as intermediate representations rather than final outputs. On HO3Dv2, the only metric where a baseline edges out HOISDF is PAMJE (Lin et al. [33]: 8.9 vs. 9.6 mm), but HOISDF dominates all other metrics including OME (48.5 vs. 64.3 mm) and ADD-S (17.8 vs. 32.4 mm), showing a more balanced performance profile.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOISDF departs from the two dominant paradigms in joint hand-object pose estimation—direct lifting (2D feature filtering) and coarse-to-fine refinement (explicit mesh/vertex intermediates)—by introducing signed distance fields as a global implicit intermediate representation. While prior SDF-based methods (AlignSDF [12], gSDF [13], Grasping Field [26]) use SDFs as the final output for mesh reconstruction, HOISDF repurposes them as a mid-pipeline signal that informs pose regression through field-guided sampling, feature augmentation, and cross-target interaction cues. The attention mechanism is applied to field-guided 3D query points rather than 2D pixel features or mesh vertices, drawing on the transformer architecture popularized by Hampali et al. [19] but operating in a fundamentally different representational space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDF-as-intermediate-representation paradigm offers a principled way to inject global 3D shape constraints into pose estimation, addressing a core limitation of explicit representations that only provide local information near an initial guess. The substantial margins over SOTA on both DexYCB and HO3Dv2 (e.g., OCE 18.4 vs. 27.3 mm) suggest that global implicit constraints are underexploited in current methods. Open questions include whether this paradigm generalizes to other articulated-body or multi-object pose estimation tasks (the authors hint at this), whether it extends to video-based settings, and whether the reliance on MANO limits applicability to non-human hands or novel object categories beyond YCB.
