## Problem

Text-driven motion generation is well-studied for humans with large paired text-motion datasets, but no comparable animal text-motion dataset exists, and animal skeletons differ fundamentally from human ones in joint count, definitions, and offsets. This data scarcity and representational mismatch make it infeasible to directly apply human motion generation methods to animals, leaving text-driven animal motion generation essentially unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241643/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes paired text captions (minimum 5 words) with 3D animal skeletal motions in SMAL format (35 joints, 6D rotations) alongside human motions in SMPL format (22 joints). At inference, a free-form text description is encoded via CLIP and a human motion sequence (from a generative model or ground truth) is encoded through the human autoencoder; together they condition the animal decoders to produce a 3D animal skeletal motion sequence with per-frame joint rotations and global translation. The output is structured as a sequence of poses in the SMAL skeleton, with temporal length matching the input human motion.

## Method

OmniMotionGPT jointly trains two transformer-based motion autoencoders (human and animal) that share a common latent space defined by primal joints—joints whose graph degree is not 2—enabling dimensional coherence across different skeleton topologies. A text-aware cross-modal module encodes the latent motion into CLIP feature space and decodes back via a causal-attention transformer conditioned on CLIP text embeddings. Three cross-domain losses regularize the transfer: a CLIP similarity loss that enforces subject-invariance in the human latent code (by matching it to both the original and animal-substituted sentence embeddings), a latent consistency loss that penalizes round-trip drift through the animal decoder-encoder, and an end-effector loss that matches velocities at skeleton terminal nodes via forward kinematics. At inference, a human motion latent and a CLIP text embedding are fed into the animal text decoder and motion decoder, which sample a new animal latent and decode it into the target animal's SMAL skeleton, effectively transferring human motion knowledge to generate both in-distribution and out-of-distribution animal behaviors.

## Results

On out-of-distribution (OOD) prompts, OMGPT achieves R-Precision Top-1 of 0.850, Top-2 of 0.935, and Top-3 of 0.964, substantially outperforming the best diffusion baseline MotionDiffuse (0.407/0.614/0.733) and the GPT-based baselines T2M-GPT (0.089) and MotionGPT (0.148). On the in-distribution AnimalML3D test set, OMGPT attains the best FID of 0.223 and MM-Dist of 0.348, while also achieving the highest Diversity (37.487) and MModality (17.487) among all methods. The OOD advantage is particularly pronounced, with OMGPT's R-Precision Top-1 exceeding the next-best method (MotionDiffuse) by over 44 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMGPT decisively outperforms all four baselines on OOD R-Precision, achieving 0.850 Top-1 versus MotionDiffuse's 0.407, MDM's 0.336, MotionGPT's 0.148, and T2M-GPT's 0.089 — a gap the authors attribute to the inability of baselines to jointly train with human data due to skeleton representation differences. On in-distribution AnimalML3D, OMGPT leads in FID (0.223 vs. MotionDiffuse's 0.401), MM-Dist (0.348 vs. 0.421), Diversity (37.487 vs. 25.194), and MModality (17.487 vs. 7.081), while MotionDiffuse edges it slightly on R-Precision Top-1 (0.505 vs. 0.539). The GPT-based baselines (T2M-GPT, MotionGPT) perform poorly on both settings due to VQVAE overfitting on the small 922-sample animal dataset and a large 512-entry codebook causing repetitive or noisy outputs. MDM and MotionDiffuse, while more robust to small datasets than the GPT baselines, still fall short of OMGPT on text-motion alignment (MM-Dist) and multimodality, and cannot leverage human motion knowledge to handle OOD prompts effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the text-driven human motion generation paradigm (autoencoder + CLIP alignment, as in T2M, MotionCLIP, and MDM) for the entirely new domain of animal skeletal motion, where no prior text-motion pipeline exists. It departs from human motion generators by introducing a cross-domain knowledge transfer mechanism: a shared primal-joint latent space that bridges SMPL (human) and SMAL (animal) skeletons, coupled with three purpose-built losses (latent consistency, CLIP subject-invariance, end-effector velocity matching) that have no analogue in single-domain human motion synthesis. The GPT-style architecture is inspired by MotionGPT and T2M-GPT, but the paper explicitly avoids their VQVAE codebook approach, which it shows fails on the sparse animal data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that text-driven animal motion generation is feasible, and the AnimalML3D dataset (1,240 sequences, 36 identities, 3,720 captions) provides the community with a previously nonexistent benchmark. The large OOD performance gap (0.850 vs. 0.407 R-Precision Top-1) suggests human motion priors are a powerful inductive bias for under-resourced animal animation, with implications for game and VFX pipelines where artists currently hand-animate creatures. Key limitations include the small training set (922 pairs), restriction to SMAL-compatible species, and reliance on a single human motion source; open questions include whether the approach scales to more diverse species, longer sequences, or real-time generation, and whether the end-effector loss generalizes beyond the specific SMAL joint definitions used here.
