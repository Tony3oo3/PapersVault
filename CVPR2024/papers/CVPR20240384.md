## Problem

Existing token pruning methods for Vision Transformers (e.g., DynamicViT, A-ViT) require computationally expensive fine-tuning after pruning, and each distinct pruning configuration demands separate re-training. This is impractical for edge deployment where compute resources are scarce and hardware constraints may vary across devices. The paper asks whether tokens can be pruned from pre-trained Transformers in a zero-shot manner—without any fine-tuning—while preserving accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240384/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (224×224 px for most backbones, 384×384 px for SWAG) that are split into non-overlapping patches forming a token sequence. Pruning layers are inserted between existing Transformer blocks and progressively reduce the token count through the network. The final output is a class label (e.g., ImageNet 1000-class prediction); the intermediate pruned token sequence is passed to subsequent blocks. No architectural modification to the backbone is required, and the method is applied off-the-shelf to pre-trained models.

## Method

Zero-TPrune treats the attention matrix of each pre-trained Transformer block as the adjacency matrix of a directed attention graph and applies a Weighted PageRank (WPR) algorithm to iteratively propagate importance scores across tokens, reducing noise from semantically weak tokens. Cross-head aggregation uses a root-mean-of-sum-of-squares (EIR) to preserve tokens that are highly informative in specific heads, while a Variance-based Head Filter (VHF) discards heads whose score distributions are degenerate. A second S-stage then partitions tokens into two groups guided by their importance rank and prunes the most similar token pairs (cosine similarity on Key vectors) from the less important group, eliminating redundant representations. The stages are ordered as I′-stage → S-stage → I-stage so that similar tokens are removed before importance scoring, preventing background tokens from crowding out object tokens. The entire pipeline is training-free and can switch between pruning configurations at zero computational cost.

## Results

On DeiT-S (ImageNet, 224px), Zero-TPrune reduces FLOPs by 34.7% (4.55G → 2.97G) with only 0.4% top-1 accuracy loss (79.8% → 79.4%) and a 45.3% throughput increase (1505.9 → 2188.4 img/s on A100). Across other backbones at matched GFLOPS budgets, Zero-TPrune achieves 80.22% on AugReg, 81.5% on LV-ViT-S, 82.93% on MAE, and 85.17% on SWAG, consistently outperforming both ATS and ToMe. The method requires no fine-tuning after pruning, and switching between different pruning configurations incurs zero additional computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fine-tuning-required methods, Zero-TPrune without any fine-tuning outperforms both DynamicViT and A-ViT (which rely on random token drops without training) by approximately 1% top-1 accuracy, and matches their fine-tuned performance with only ~0.1% accuracy loss at a 3.5 GFLOPS budget on DeiT-S. Against fine-tuning-free methods, Zero-TPrune consistently wins: on DeiT-S it reduces accuracy loss by 33% versus ATS (0.4% vs 0.6%) and by 56% versus ToMe (0.4% vs 0.9%) at matched ~3 GFLOPS. The advantage widens on medium-sized backbones—on AugReg, Zero-TPrune achieves 80.22% versus 79.21% (ATS) and 79.30% (ToMe) at ~2.8 GFLOPS, and on LV-ViT-S it reaches 81.5% versus 80.4% (ATS) and 79.8% (ToMe) at 3.5 GFLOPS, reducing accuracy loss by up to 49% over the best baseline. On large models (MAE, SWAG) the margin narrows (e.g., 85.17% vs 85.09% on SWAG), and the authors note that aggressively pruning large models (50% FLOPS reduction) is generally inferior to simply using a smaller pretrained backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Zero-TPrune adapts the PageRank algorithm (Brin, 1998) to the attention graph of pre-trained Vision Transformers, treating the attention matrix as a directed adjacency matrix and iteratively propagating importance signals via a Weighted Page Rank (WPR) scheme. It departs from fine-tuning-required token pruning methods (DynamicViT, A-ViT) by eliminating the need for learned pruning modules and post-pruning training, and extends fine-tuning-free baselines (ATS, ToMe) by jointly exploiting both token importance (via the full attention graph) and token similarity (via Key-vector cosine distance), whereas ATS relies solely on CLS-token attention and ToMe relies solely on embedding similarity without importance guidance. The key conceptual contribution is the observation that the attention graph of a pre-trained model is a sufficient information source for identifying prunable tokens without any additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot nature of Zero-TPrune is practically significant for edge deployment: it eliminates the 150+ GPU-hour fine-tuning cost of methods like DynamicViT and enables switching between pruning configurations at zero computational cost, which is critical when hardware constraints vary across devices. The method generalizes across multiple backbones (DeiT, LV-ViT, AugReg, MAE, SWAG) and reduces accuracy loss by up to 49% compared to fine-tuning-free baselines on medium-sized models. However, the paper acknowledges that aggressive pruning of large models (50% FLOPS reduction) does not outperform baselines, suggesting the attention graph signal becomes less reliable under extreme compression. Open questions include applicability to segmentation, generation, and reconstruction tasks, and whether the method's advantages persist as model scale increases further.
