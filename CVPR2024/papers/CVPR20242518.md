## Problem

Single-shot novel view synthesis from a single RGB image remains challenging because conventional NeRF methods require multi-view supervision to learn geometry, while 3D GAN-based approaches demand test-time optimization (e.g., GAN inversion) for each specific image and tend to produce geometry collapse due to the absence of multi-view constraints. The paper asks whether geometry priors can be extracted from an off-the-shelf 3D GAN trained on single-view data alone, enabling feed-forward novel view synthesis without per-image fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242518/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (e.g., a face or cat face at 512×512) as input and produces a rendered novel view from an arbitrary camera pose along with a corresponding depth map. At inference time, the model operates in a feed-forward manner (≈1.1 s per view on an RTX A800), requiring no per-image optimization. Outputs are generated at 64×64 natively and upscaled to 512×512 via a super-resolution module, with camera poses sampled from a distribution matched to the training data's pose statistics.

## Method

G-NeRF operates in two stages. First, Geometry-guided Multi-View Synthesis (GMVS) leverages a pre-trained 3D GAN (EG3D) to generate triplets of synthetic multi-view data (two images from different viewpoints plus a depth map), applying a latent truncation (ψ=0.5) that pulls sampled codes toward the center of the W-space to trade diversity for geometry quality. Second, Depth-aware Training (DaT) trains a NeRF-based generator on a mixture of synthetic multi-view pairs and real single-view images using a reconstruction loss (L1 + SSIM + VGG perceptual), while a depth-aware discriminator—conditioned on camera pose and trained with R1 regularization—distinguishes EG3D-generated depth maps from the model's own depth predictions, providing explicit geometry supervision even for real images lacking ground-truth depth. The total objective is L_recon + λ_g · L_gan, and the depth discriminator is additionally trained with randomly sampled camera poses beyond the input pose to enforce multi-view depth coherence.

## Results

On FFHQ at 64², G-NeRF achieves FID 26.04, KID 2.09, Depth 0.35, and ID 0.43, outperforming Pix2NeRF (FID 32.44, KID 2.37, Depth 0.40, ID 0.25) across all metrics. At 512², G-NeRF reaches FID 40.24 and KID 2.72 on FFHQ, substantially better than Pix2NeRF's FID 75.04 and KID 5.97. On ShapeNet Chairs and Cars at 128², G-NeRF (SSIM 0.87, PSNR 19.87, LPIPS 0.10 average) outperforms Pix2NeRF (SSIM 0.81, PSNR 16.99, LPIPS 0.16) but remains slightly behind multi-view methods like VisionNeRF (SSIM 0.92, PSNR 23.37, LPIPS 0.08). Inference cost is 1.1s per image versus 76.9s for PTI-based GAN inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-NeRF consistently outperforms Pix2NeRF [5] on every metric across all single-view datasets: on FFHQ 64² it reduces FID from 32.44 to 26.04 and improves ID from 0.25 to 0.43; on AFHQv2-Cats it achieves FID 18.64 vs. 25.34, and notably Pix2NeRF fails entirely on this dataset due to limited pose variation. On ShapeNet Chairs and Cars (128²), G-NeRF (SSIM 0.87, PSNR 19.87, LPIPS 0.10) substantially beats Pix2NeRF (SSIM 0.81, PSNR 16.99, LPIPS 0.16) but trails multi-view methods: VisionNeRF* [30] achieves SSIM 0.92 / PSNR 23.37 / LPIPS 0.08, CodeNeRF* [23] reaches SSIM 0.91 / PSNR 23.73 / LPIPS 0.12, and PixelNeRF [52] attains SSIM 0.91 / PSNR 23.45 / LPIPS 0.13. ENR [16] and SRN [44] are comparable to CodeNeRF* on ShapeNet (SSIM ~0.89–0.91, PSNR ~22.5–22.9). Against PTI [41] (GAN inversion), G-NeRF is 70× faster (1.1s vs. 76.9s per image) and achieves better depth accuracy (0.35 vs. 0.37) and side-face depth (0.53 vs. 0.55), while being slightly worse on FID (40.24 vs. 35.53) and KID (2.72 vs. 2.30) at 512².

## Relation to Prior Work

G-NeRF sits at the intersection of two research threads: NeRF-based single/few-shot view synthesis (Pix2NeRF, PixelNeRF, SinNeRF) and 3D GAN-based geometry-aware generation (EG3D, StyleNeRF). It departs from prior NeRF methods by eliminating the need for multi-view training data or per-image optimization, instead repurposing a pre-trained 3D GAN as a synthetic multi-view data source to bootstrap geometry priors. The depth-aware discriminator introduces a new adversarial loss that provides geometry supervision through depth maps without requiring ground-truth depth for real images, distinguishing it from reconstruction-only approaches like Pix2NeRF and from GAN-inversion methods like PTI that require test-time fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving single-shot novel view synthesis at 1.1s inference (vs. 76.9s for PTI) while matching or exceeding geometry quality, making the approach viable for real-time applications like digital humans and AR. However, the method is constrained to object classes where a suitable pre-trained 3D GAN already exists (faces, cats, chairs, cars), limiting generalization to arbitrary scenes. The acknowledged one-to-many ambiguity and the modest depth-accuracy gains from the discriminator (0.53 vs. 0.59 on side faces) suggest that explicit multi-view supervision remains the gold standard, and the approach may struggle with highly occluded or novel object categories.
