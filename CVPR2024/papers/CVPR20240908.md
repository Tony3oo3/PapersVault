## Problem

GAN-based super-resolution models inevitably hallucinate high-frequency details and produce structural artifacts, and existing perceptual losses (VGG/LPIPS) are insufficient to suppress them. The fundamental question is whether a model can learn to distinguish genuine image details from artifacts during training. This paper shows that wavelet-domain losses, which explicitly separate image features by scale and orientation, enable the discriminator to make this distinction far more effectively than RGB-domain or Fourier-space losses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240908/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4× downsampled low-resolution RGB image and produces a single high-resolution super-resolved image (deterministic, one sample per input). During training, 32×32 RGB patches are randomly cropped from DIV2K images; the SWT decomposition is applied to the Y channel after YCbCr conversion, and only the detail subbands (LH, HL, HH) are fed to the discriminator. The generator remains in the RGB domain, so inference time is identical to the underlying RRDB network.

## Method

WGSR replaces the conventional RGB-domain l1 fidelity loss and RGB-domain discriminator of ESRGAN with wavelet-domain counterparts. The fidelity loss is a weighted sum of l1 distances across SWT subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH), with per-subband weights (λ_LL=0.1, λ_LH=λ_HL=0.01, λ_HH=0.05) that control how much each scale/orientation of detail is preserved. The discriminator is a 9-layer CNN that receives only the concatenated HF detail subbands rather than full RGB images, making its task of separating genuine details from hallucinated artifacts simpler and more stable. The perceptual loss uses DISTS instead of LPIPS, which the ablation shows improves both PSNR (+0.3 dB) and perceptual quality simultaneously. The generator uses the standard RRDB backbone from ESRGAN, and the entire framework is generic—any GAN-SR generator/discriminator pair can be plugged in with the wavelet-domain loss schedule.

## Results

On the five standard ×4 SR benchmarks, WGSR (1-level SWT) achieves the best no-reference perceptual scores (NRQM, PI, NIQE) on Set5, BSD100, Urban100, and DIV2K, while WGSR (2-level SWT) leads on Set14. For example, on Urban100 WGSR (2-lvl) reaches NRQM 7.406 and PI 3.112, outperforming the next-best SROOE (6.552 / 3.695) and FxSR (6.608 / 3.603). On DIV2K, WGSR (1-lvl) attains PSNR 29.188 dB, NRQM 6.870, and PI 3.107, simultaneously exceeding ESRGAN+ (26.770 / 6.721 / 3.126) and RankSRGAN (27.196 / 6.828 / 2.891) in perceptual quality while matching or surpassing them in fidelity. Both WGSR variants satisfy the LR-PSNR > 45 dB consistency criterion on all five datasets, whereas ESRGAN-FS, ESRGAN+, SPSR, and RankSRGAN fail on at least one.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WGSR clearly wins on no-reference perceptual metrics: it achieves the best NRQM on 4 of 5 datasets (Set5: 8.252, BSD100: 8.793, Urban100: 7.406, DIV2K: 6.870) and the best PI on all 5 datasets (e.g., Urban100 PI 3.112 vs. SROOE 3.695 and FxSR 3.603). On full-reference distortion (PSNR), PDASR and LDL remain the strongest, with PDASR leading on Set5 (31.728 dB) and Urban100 (26.279 dB); WGSR (2-lvl) is competitive on DIV2K (29.857 dB, actually best) but trails PDASR by ~0.3–0.8 dB on other sets. The key differentiator is LR-consistency: WGSR exceeds the 45 dB LR-PSNR threshold on every dataset, while ESRGAN-FS, ESRGAN+, SPSR, and RankSRGAN all fall below it on at least one benchmark (e.g., RankSRGAN drops to 36.39 dB on Urban100). Against the closest GAN-SR competitors (ESRGAN+, RankSRGAN, SROOE), WGSR improves NRQM by 0.1–0.9 points and PI by 0.3–0.6 points while maintaining comparable or higher PSNR. On DISTS, WGSR is mid-pack (e.g., 0.101 on Urban100 vs. LDL's 0.082), indicating that the wavelet-domain losses primarily benefit no-reference perceptual quality rather than full-reference structural similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ESRGAN's RRDB generator architecture but fundamentally rethinks the training objective by replacing conventional RGB-domain fidelity and adversarial losses with Stationary Wavelet Transform (SWT) subband losses. It departs from prior frequency-domain approaches (Fourier-space discriminators, focal frequency losses) by arguing that wavelet decompositions uniquely provide joint scale-and-orientation sensitivity needed to distinguish genuine high-frequency details from hallucinated artifacts. Unlike prior wavelet-based SR methods that predict wavelet coefficients directly, WGSR keeps the generator in the pixel domain and uses wavelets only as a loss-space, making it a drop-in training scheme for any existing GAN-SR backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a generic, plug-and-play training framework that any GAN-SR model can adopt to improve its perception-distortion trade-off without architectural changes, which has broad practical utility. However, the empirical weight selection across subbands remains manual and task-dependent, and the authors acknowledge that standard quantitative metrics (PSNR, DISTS, NIQE) fail to capture the visual artifact suppression that is the method's primary qualitative advantage. An open question is whether the subband weighting scheme can be learned automatically or generalized across degradation types and scale factors without re-tuning.
