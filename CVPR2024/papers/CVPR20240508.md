## Problem

SRe2L achieves the first data condensation on full 224×224 ImageNet-1k but relies on a single 'local-match-global' matching (BatchNorm channel statistics from one backbone at one layer), which limits the generalization of the distilled dataset. The paper argues that sufficient and diverse matching across multiple backbones, layers, and statistics types is more precise and effective than any single matching, and that this 'generalized matching' perspective has not been explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240508/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the complete training dataset (e.g., full 224×224 ImageNet-1k with 1.28M images) and a set of pre-trained convolutional backbones (ResNet18, MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, ShuffleNetV2-0.5). It produces a small synthetic dataset (IPC 10–100) where each image is paired with a soft label generated by a backbone ensemble. Notably, the complete dataset is traversed once in a gradient-free forward pass to record global Convolution statistics (channel and patch mean/variance per layer), which are stored on disk and reused during synthesis, avoiding any secondary computation over the full data.

## Method

G-VBSM comprises three modules built on SRe2L's Squeeze-Recover-Relabel pipeline. (1) Data Densification (DD) enforces intra-class linear independence by minimizing KL divergence between a temperature-scaled and the raw eigenvalue distribution of per-class Gram matrices, ensuring the synthetic images carry diverse information. (2) Generalized Statistical Matching (GSM) extends matching beyond BatchNorm to Convolution layers by recording global channel and patch mean/variance via a single gradient-free forward pass, then matching them during synthesis using an EMA-based 'score distillation sampling' form that accumulates statistics across batches. (3) Generalized Backbone Matching (GBM) randomly samples one backbone from the candidate set per iteration for data synthesis and generates soft labels via a parallel backbone ensemble (optionally with logit normalization), multiplying the number of local-match-global constraints by the number of backbones. The evaluation-phase loss replaces SRe2L's KL divergence with MSE + 0.1×ground-truth cross-entropy to avoid numerical instability at high temperature while improving generalization.

## Results

On the full 224×224 ImageNet-1k under IPC 10, G-VBSM achieves 31.4% top-1 accuracy with ResNet18, surpassing SRe2L (21.3%) by 10.1% and DataDAM (6.3%, on 64×64) by 25.1%. On Tiny-ImageNet (IPC 50, ResNet18), G-VBSM reaches 47.6%, outperforming SRe2L (41.1%) by 6.5%. On CIFAR-100 (IPC 10, 128-width ConvNet), G-VBSM attains 38.7%, exceeding DataDAM (34.8%) by 3.9% and MTT (33.1%) by 5.6%, making it the first algorithm to achieve SOTA on both small-scale and large-scale benchmarks simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-VBSM consistently outperforms SRe2L, the closest and most relevant baseline, across all datasets and IPC settings: by 10.1% on ImageNet-1k IPC 10 (R18), 6.5% on Tiny-ImageNet IPC 50 (R18), and 7.1% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10 (R18). Against the classical trajectory-matching method MTT, G-VBSM gains 19.6% on Tiny-ImageNet IPC 50 and 5.6% on CIFAR-100 IPC 10. Compared to DataDAM (attention matching), G-VBSM leads by 23.7% on ImageNet-1k IPC 10 (full 224×224 vs. 64×64), 18.9% on Tiny-ImageNet IPC 50, and 3.9% on CIFAR-100 IPC 10. On CIFAR-10/100, G-VBSM also surpasses gradient-matching DC (+30.9% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10), distribution-matching DM (+9.3% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10), CAFE (+9.4% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10), and KIP (+3.3% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10). TESLA, which operates on 64×64 ImageNet-1k, is outperformed by 23.7% under IPC 10. Notably, G-VBSM is the only method that achieves competitive results on both the full-resolution ImageNet-1k and the small-scale CIFAR-100 benchmark, whereas SRe2L underperforms on CIFAR-100 and classical methods cannot scale to full-resolution ImageNet-1k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SRe2L, the first method to achieve data condensation on full 224×224 ImageNet-1k via lightweight BatchNorm statistical matching, by generalizing its single 'local-match-global' matching into a multi-backbone, multi-layer, multi-statistic framework. Where SRe2L relies on one backbone (ResNet18), one layer type (BatchNorm), and two statistics (channel mean/variance), G-VBSM introduces Convolution-layer statistics (channel and patch level), random backbone sampling from a pool of four architectures, and a data densification regularizer to ensure intra-class diversity. The broader dataset distillation lineage (MTT, FTD, DC, DM, CAFE, DataDAM, TESLA) is positioned as computationally prohibitive at ImageNet scale, motivating the continued reliance on lightweight statistical matching rather than gradient or trajectory unrolling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

G-VBSM is the first dataset condensation method to achieve strong performance simultaneously on small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) and the full-resolution ImageNet-1k, closing a gap where prior methods excelled on one scale but not the other. The cross-architecture generalization results—particularly 29.43% on DeiT-Tiny and 57.40% on Swin-Tiny, architectures never encountered during synthesis—suggest the distilled data captures genuinely transferable structure rather than backbone-specific artifacts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN architectures (ViTs without BatchNorm), detection/segmentation tasks (acknowledged as future work), and whether the gains from additional backbones saturate beyond four candidates.
