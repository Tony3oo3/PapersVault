## Problem

Implicit neural representations (e.g., NeRF) for talking head synthesis suffer from visual artifacts, head jitters, and motionless mouths because the implicit definition of 3D geometry prevents analytical control over deformation and effective disentanglement of static appearance from motion. Explicit 3DMM-based methods avoid these issues but are limited by fixed topology, failing to capture personalized details like hairstyle, glasses, and inner mouth. The paper bridges this gap by encoding dynamic meshes within neural networks to combine geometric consistency with personalized expressivity.

## Input / Output

Training requires a short frontal video (~6500 frames at 25 FPS, cropped to 512×512) of a single person, from which 3DMM expression coefficients and camera poses are extracted; a 10:1 train/test split is used. At inference, the model accepts 3DMM expression coefficients (either extracted from driving frames or converted from audio via an off-the-shelf Audio2Exp model) and camera parameters to render photorealistic head images at 512×512 (up to 1024×1024) at 46 FPS. A key byproduct is the explicit dynamic triangular mesh with consistent topology across all frames, enabling downstream 3D asset integration.

## Method

DynTet parameterizes a 128³ tetrahedral grid with two coordinate-based MLP branches: f₁ predicts SDF values and per-vertex elastic scores (independent of the talking signal, ensuring topological invariance), while f₂ predicts deformation offsets conditioned on a 27-frame window of 3DMM expression coefficients, scaled by the elastic scores to restrict non-rigid motion to regions like the mouth while keeping the forehead rigid. Marching Tetrahedra decodes the deformed grid into triangular meshes with consistent connectivity, which are then rendered via a differentiable rasterizer using a physically-based material model (hash-grid-encoded albedo, roughness, metalness) and image-based lighting. A canonical projection maps pixel coordinates into a mean-expression 3D space to simplify texture learning, and 3DMM-derived losses (normal distance and facial deformation) inject geometric priors to compensate for the limited depth information in frontal talking videos. Frequency positional encoding is used for geometry (outperforming hash encoding which produces noisy surfaces), and the total model has only 1.41M parameters.

## Results

On the self-driven head reconstruction task (Table 1), DynTet achieves the best PSNR (35.15 dB), lowest AUE (1.833), lowest LMDm (2.418), and highest FPS (46) among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest NeRF baseline ER-NeRF† by 2.65 dB in PSNR and 34% in AUE. With LPIPS supervision (DynTet†), it further achieves the best LPIPS (0.0223) and FID (4.72). On cross-driven lip synchronization (Table 2), frame-driven DynTet attains the lowest AUE on both test sets (3.672 and 6.029), though ER-NeRF achieves higher SyncNet scores (6.877 vs. 5.055 on Testset A). Training time is 2 hours on a single V100, matching ER-NeRF, while inference runs at 46 FPS at 512×512 and maintains 46 FPS even at 1024×1024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynTet outperforms all three NeRF-based baselines (AD-NeRF, RAD-NeRF, ER-NeRF) on PSNR, AUE, LMDm, and inference speed, with the largest gap over ER-NeRF† (the strongest NeRF method): +2.65 dB PSNR, −0.94 AUE, and 2× FPS (46 vs. 23). On perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID), DynTet† with LPIPS supervision surpasses ER-NeRF† (0.0223 vs. 0.0345 LPIPS; 4.72 vs. 6.44 FID), while the base DynTet is slightly behind. Against 2D-based methods (Wav2Lip, PC-AVS), DynTet dominates on reconstruction fidelity (PSNR, FID, CSIM) and geometric accuracy (LMDm, LMDe, AUE), but Wav2Lip retains an edge on SyncNet lip-sync score (8.256 vs. 7.407) owing to its large-scale pretraining. In the cross-driven setting, frame-driven DynTet achieves the best AUE on both test sets (3.672 and 6.029), beating ER-NeRF by 0.54 and 0.64 respectively, yet ER-NeRF scores higher on SyncNet (6.877 vs. 5.055 on Testset A), suggesting DynTet's explicit geometry improves expression accuracy but not yet temporal audio-visual alignment. The audio-driven variant (Audio2Exp + DynTet) achieves competitive results with SadTalker, confirming flexibility across driving modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynTet extends the static tetrahedral representation line (Deep Marching Tetrahedra, GET3D, Nvdiffrec) to the dynamic domain by decoupling SDF-based topology from signal-driven deformation, a separation that prior static methods did not need. It also departs from the dominant NeRF-based talking head paradigm (AD-NeRF, ER-NeRF, RAD-NeRF) by replacing volumetric rendering with differentiable rasterization over explicit meshes, and from 3DMM-based methods by learning personalized topology end-to-end rather than relying on a fixed template. The 3DMM prior is repurposed not as the rendering model but as a geometric regularizer in SDF space, and a canonical projection space is introduced to simplify texture learning—both enabled by the explicit mesh structure that implicit NeRF representations cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DynTet demonstrates that explicit dynamic meshes decoded from tetrahedra can match or exceed NeRF-based avatars in fidelity while delivering 2× real-time rendering speed (46 FPS at 1024×1024 vs ~23 FPS for NeRF baselines), and it outputs editable meshes directly usable in AR/VR pipelines and game engines. Limitations include reliance on frontal-only training data, a single-person setting, and 3DMM coefficient extraction as a driving signal, which constrains generalization. Open questions include whether the elastic-score mechanism generalizes to full-body or multi-person dynamics, whether the approach scales to longer sequences without temporal drift, and whether the tetrahedral resolution (128³) can be pushed higher without prohibitive memory costs.
