## Problem

Mixed-precision quantization (MPQ) typically follows a searching-then-retraining pipeline, where the retraining stage to fit the discovered bit-width policy incurs substantial cost (e.g., ~200 GPU-hours for a single ResNet18 policy). Prior work focuses on accelerating the search stage while largely ignoring this retraining bottleneck, hindering practical deployment across diverse devices. Additionally, weight-sharing quantization models that could enable retraining-free search suffer from a previously uncharacterized bit-width interference problem that destabilizes training when ultra-low bit-widths are included.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (ImageNet at 224×224, CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Oxford Pets) and produces class labels during both training and inference. The key output is a per-layer mixed-precision bit-width policy (e.g., 4MP/4MP) that can be directly deployed from the single trained weight-sharing model without any additional retraining epochs. The search operates purely in inference mode, evaluating candidate policies by forward-passing through the shared weights at different quantization levels.

## Method

The paper proposes a one-shot training-then-searching paradigm where all N^{2L} bit-width configurations are coupled within a single set of shared latent weights, trained once via Monte-Carlo sampling over policies. To address the identified bit-width interference (where low-bit-width quantization noise destabilizes higher-bit-width convergence), a dynamic bit-width scheduler periodically freezes the most unstable layers using rounding-error proximity to quantization bounds as an instability proxy, with a cosine schedule to progressively freeze more layers. An Information Distortion Mitigation (IDM) loss, inspired by the information bottleneck principle, aligns the normalized outputs of low-bit-width sub-networks to their high-bit-width counterparts via a learnable affine transformation. Finally, a bidirectional greedy search (O(2L) candidates per iteration) selects the optimal per-layer bit-width policy in inference-only mode, balancing validation loss and BitOPs with a trade-off hyperparameter, requiring zero retraining epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet18, the proposed method achieves 71.0% top-1 accuracy at 31.6G BitOPs with zero retraining epochs under a 4MP/4MP constraint, outperforming the second-best SEAM (70.8%, 33.7G, 90 epochs). On MobileNetV2, it reaches 70.7% top-1 at 5.5G BitOPs (4MP/4MP) and 67.8% at 3.6G BitOPs (3MP/3MP), both with no retraining. On EfficientNetLite-B0, it attains 73.2% top-1 at 6.9G BitOPs (4MP/4MP) and 70.4% at 4.5G BitOPs (3MP/3MP), again with zero retraining cost. Transfer learning to CIFAR-100 and Oxford Pets shows the quantized weights retain near full-precision accuracy (e.g., 79.5% vs. 79.4% on CIFAR-100 at 4MP/4MP for ResNet18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms or matches all 21 baselines while eliminating retraining entirely. On ResNet18 at 4MP/4MP, it beats SEAM (70.8%) and LIMPQ (70.8%) by 0.2% in accuracy while reducing BitOPs from 33.7G/35.0G to 31.6G and cutting retraining from 90 epochs to zero. At 3MP/3MP, it surpasses SEAM (70.0%) by 0.2% and LIMPQ (69.7%) by 0.5% with no retraining. On MobileNetV2 at 4MP/4MP, it edges out QBR (70.4%) and GMPQ (70.4%) by 0.3% while matching their BitOPs and avoiding 40–90 retraining epochs; at 3MP/3MP it beats QBR (67.4%) by 0.4%. On EfficientNetLite-B0 at 4MP/4MP, it nearly matches QBitOPT (73.3%) at 73.2% while reporting explicit BitOPs (6.9G) and zero retraining versus QBitOPT's 30 epochs. The only case where a baseline slightly edges out the proposed method is EfficientNet 4MP/4MP (QBitOPT 73.3% vs. 73.2%), but QBitOPT uses channel-wise quantization and does not report BitOPs, making the comparison not fully apples-to-apples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-sharing quantization framework introduced by LIMPQ (Tang et al., ECCV 2022) and ABN (Tang et al., ACM MM 2022), which jointly optimize all bit-width configurations within shared latent weights via Monte-Carlo sampling. However, it departs fundamentally from the standard 'searching-then-retraining' pipeline used by prior MPQ methods (HAQ, EdMIPS, GMPQ, SEAM, LIMPQ) by inverting the order to 'training-then-searching': a single training run produces a weight-sharing super-network, and the final policy is extracted via an inference-only bidirectional greedy search with zero retraining. The paper also identifies and formalizes the 'bit-width interference' phenomenon—where highly coupled shared weights cause destabilizing quantization noise superposition across bit-widths—that prior weight-sharing quantization works either bypassed by dropping ultra-low bits or left unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of retraining cost (0 epochs vs. 30–150 epochs for competing methods) is a significant practical win for deploying mixed-precision models across diverse hardware with different bit-width constraints, since each new device configuration previously required a full retraining cycle. The bit-width interference analysis and the information distortion mitigation technique (inspired by the information bottleneck principle) provide a principled understanding of why naive weight-sharing quantization fails at high compression ratios, which could inform future dynamic-precision and elastic-network designs. Limitations include evaluation on only three CNN architectures at ImageNet scale, the greedy search's O(2L) per-iteration cost for very deep networks, and the fact that the one-shot training itself still requires substantial compute; whether the approach extends to transformers or non-convolutional backbones remains open.
