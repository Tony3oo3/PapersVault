## Problem

Existing multi-concept customization methods for diffusion models either degrade individual concept fidelity when merged, require joint access to all concepts during training (making the process unscalable and raising privacy concerns), or rely on computationally expensive merging procedures like gradient fusion (~15–20 minutes for three concepts). The paper introduces Modular Customization: a setting where concepts are fine-tuned independently and privately, then merged at inference with trivial compute while preserving each concept's identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During fine-tuning, each concept is represented by ~16 reference images and encoded as two separate text tokens; the method is applied to all linear layers of a Stable Diffusion (ChilloutMix) checkpoint with LoRA rank r=20. At inference, the user provides a text prompt referencing any subset of the merged concepts, and the model generates a single image containing those concepts jointly. Merging itself requires no additional training data or compute beyond summing the stored weight residuals (sub-1-second operation).

## Method

Orthogonal Adaptation modifies standard LoRA fine-tuning by freezing the B matrix (Δθ_i = A_i B_i^T) and optimizing only A_i, where B_i is drawn as a random subset of columns from a shared large orthogonal matrix. This enforces approximate orthogonality between the residual subspaces of different concepts (B_i^T B_j ≈ 0), which analytically guarantees that the crosstalk term Δθ_j X_i vanishes on the projected data subspace, so each concept's mapping is preserved after summation. Because r ≪ m, the orthogonal complement of any single B_j covers most of R^m, making the projection loss negligible. Merging is then simply θ_merged = θ + Σ λ_i A_i B_i^T, enabling near-instant, plug-and-play combination of any number of independently trained concepts without retraining.

## Results

On the custom 12-concept identity dataset, Orthogonal Adaptation achieves the highest post-merging Image Alignment (0.741) and Identity Alignment (0.745) among all methods, and is the only method where Identity Alignment actually *increases* after merging (+0.005). Pre-merging, it matches Mix-of-Show on Image Alignment (0.748 vs. 0.745) and slightly exceeds it on Identity Alignment (0.740 vs. 0.728), confirming that the orthogonality constraint does not sacrifice single-concept expressivity. Merging is near-instantaneous (<1 s), compared to ~15 minutes for Mix-of-Show with Gradient Fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mix-of-Show (FedAvg), the closest efficient baseline, Orthogonal Adaptation wins on both Image Alignment (0.741 vs. 0.735) and Identity Alignment (0.745 vs. 706) after merging, while matching it on Text Alignment. Against Mix-of-Show (Gradient Fusion), the quality leader, Orthogonal Adaptation matches or exceeds it on all three metrics (Image 0.741 vs. 0.729; Identity 0.745 vs. 0.717) while reducing merge time from ~15 minutes to under 1 second. Against DB-LoRA (FedAvg), the method avoids the catastrophic identity collapse seen in that baseline (Identity 0.745 vs. 0.098 post-merge). Against Custom Diffusion, Orthogonal Adaptation substantially outperforms on both Image (0.741 vs. 0.623) and Identity (0.745 vs. 0.408) alignment after merging. Against P+, which preserves identity trivially by not modifying weights, Orthogonal Adaptation achieves far higher single-concept fidelity (Identity 0.740 vs. 0.515) while still preserving identity after merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA's low-rank decomposition for diffusion model fine-tuning, but departs from the standard practice of jointly optimizing both factor matrices by freezing B and sampling it from a shared orthogonal basis, thereby constraining each concept's learned residual to an orthogonal subspace. Conceptually, it reframes the model-merging problem (previously addressed by FedAvg's naive averaging or Mix-of-Show's computationally expensive gradient fusion) as a fine-tuning-time constraint problem: if residuals are orthogonal by construction, summation-based merging becomes interference-free. The work also positions itself against token-embedding approaches (Textual Inversion, P+) that avoid weight modification entirely but sacrifice expressivity, and against Custom Diffusion's limited cross-attention-only fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-instant (<1s) merging of independently fine-tuned concepts, with identity alignment that actually improves post-merging, offers a practical path toward scalable, privacy-preserving multi-concept personalization at consumer scale. Key limitations remain: complex multi-concept compositions and interactions are still poorly handled, existing LoRA checkpoints cannot be retrofitted with orthogonality, and the evaluation is confined to a small 12-concept face dataset on a single Stable Diffusion checkpoint. Open questions include whether the orthogonality constraint generalizes to non-face concepts, larger concept pools (hundreds+), and whether the randomized orthogonal basis can be replaced by learned or structured alternatives that further reduce residual crosstalk.
