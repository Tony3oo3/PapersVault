## Problem

SAM's pipeline separates image preprocessing from interactive inference, which prevents dynamic zoom-in strategies from refocusing on the target object during interaction and limits the lightweight decoder's ability to deeply fuse interactive click information with precomputed image embeddings. This causes severe instability in challenging scenarios (e.g., camouflaged objects), where a single additional click can drop IoU from 88.59 to 12.78.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized and padded to 1024×1024) and a sequence of positive/negative clicks as input, producing a binary segmentation mask at the original image resolution. The image is encoded once into 256-dimensional embeddings at 1/16 spatial resolution before any interaction begins. The focus refiner activates after the second click (K=2) and produces a refined image embedding that replaces the original for all subsequent clicks on that object, adding only ~10% computational overhead to SAM's decoder-only inference.

## Method

FocSAM introduces a focus refiner (12 alternating plain/shift blocks) that refines SAM's image embeddings once per object using two novel components. Dwin-MSA partitions embeddings into 16×16 windows, selects only those intersecting the bounding box derived from the previous mask, and performs windowed self-attention with a shifting strategy for long-range patch interactions, avoiding redundant computation on irrelevant background. P-DyReLU replaces standard ReLU with a pixel-wise dynamic activation whose coefficients are derived from the dot product between image and click-fused query embeddings, enhancing object-related channels while suppressing unrelated ones. The refiner is trained with a two-stage strategy (fine-tune SAM decoder, then train refiner with frozen decoder) using normalized focal loss plus a point loss on click locations, with SAM's ViT-Huge encoder and prompt encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

FocSAM-ViT-H achieves a mean NoC@90 of 5.38 across six datasets (GrabCut, Berkeley, SBD, DAVIS, MVTec, COD10K), matching the previous state-of-the-art SimpleClick-ViT-H (5.40) while requiring only 0.39 SPC on CPUs versus SimpleClick's 6.99 SPC (~5.6% of the inference time). FocSAM attains the best NoC@90 on 5 of 6 datasets, with particularly strong gains on SBD (4.69 vs. 4.70 for SimpleClick-ViT-H) and COD10K (8.91 vs. 9.13). Compared to the base SAM-ViT-H (mean 6.85), FocSAM reduces the mean NoC@90 by 21% while adding only ~10% computational overhead to SAM's pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FocSAM outperforms all earlier CNN-based and transformer-based baselines (f-BRS, RITM, CDNet, EdgeFlow, PseudoClick, FocalClick, InterFormer) by a wide margin on every dataset where they report results, with mean NoC@90 improvements of 0.4–1.6 clicks. Against the closest competitor SimpleClick-ViT-H, FocSAM achieves a marginally better mean (5.38 vs. 5.40) and wins on 5 of 6 individual datasets (GrabCut 1.32 vs. 1.50, Berkeley 1.47 vs. 1.75, SBD 4.69 vs. 4.70, DAVIS 4.77 vs. 4.78, COD10K 8.91 vs. 9.13), while trailing slightly on MVTec (11.14 vs. 10.56). The most striking advantage is inference speed: FocSAM runs at 0.39 SPC on CPUs, roughly 18× faster than SimpleClick-ViT-H (6.99 SPC), because it reuses pre-extracted image embeddings rather than running full model inference per click. Compared to the base SAM-ViT-H, FocSAM reduces mean NoC@90 from 6.85 to 5.38 (−21%) and dramatically improves stability, as shown by fewer significant IoU drops across 100 simulated clicks on SBD, MVTec, and COD10K.

## Relation to Prior Work

FocSAM extends SAM's encoder-decoder pipeline by inserting a lightweight focus refiner that dynamically refines pre-computed image embeddings during interaction, addressing SAM's inability to perform image-level zoom-in strategies. The Dwin-MSA module adapts Swin Transformer's windowed and shifting attention to operate only on object-relevant windows, while P-DyReLU repurposes DyReLU's input-dependent activation to fuse click information into image embeddings in a pixel-wise manner. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of efficient attention (windowed, shifted) and interactive segmentation, departing from prior methods like SimpleClick and InterFormer that perform full-model inference at each click by instead refining a fixed set of embeddings with minimal additional computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM has become a foundational tool for interactive annotation across many domains, so improving its stability and efficiency directly benefits a large practitioner base. The demonstrated instability (IoU collapsing from 88.59 to 12.78 with a single additional click) is a practical failure mode that limits SAM's reliability in real annotation workflows, and FocSAM's stability analysis provides useful diagnostic evidence. Open questions include whether the focus refiner generalizes to video segmentation or other SAM-based downstream tasks, whether the approach can be extended to handle multiple simultaneous objects more gracefully, and whether the 12-block refiner can be further compressed without quality loss. The method remains dependent on SAM's pre-trained ViT-H encoder, inheriting its domain-specific limitations.
