## Problem

Existing text-to-video diffusion models face a fundamental tension: directly training large spatiotemporal models demands enormous GPU resources and data, while simpler strategies of inserting temporal layers into pre-trained text-to-image models produce videos with poor appearance fidelity and temporal coherence. The paper argues that appearance generation and motion modeling are distinct sub-problems that should be decoupled rather than learned jointly in a single network.

## Input / Output

The system operates in two stages: first, a text prompt is fed to any off-the-shelf text-to-image model (e.g., SDXL) to produce a key frame; second, that key frame (serving as the center frame of the output) together with the original text prompt conditions the image&text-to-video model. The base model outputs a 320×320, 9-frame clip at 2 fps, which is then upscaled to 32 fps via a fourfold temporal super-resolution module and further enhanced by a spatial super-resolution model. The reference image is replicated across all N output frames as a dense appearance condition rather than being used as a single-frame boundary.

## Method

MicroCinema extends Stable Diffusion 2.1's 2D U-Net into a 3D architecture by inserting zero-initialized 1D temporal convolutions after each spatial conv and 1D temporal attention after each spatial attention, with skip connections to preserve pretrained spatial weights. Two core mechanisms inject appearance: (1) an Appearance Injection Network (AppearNet) that mirrors the encoder and middle block of the 3D U-Net, takes the replicated center frame as input, and fuses its multi-scale features into both the encoder and decoder of the main branch via SPADE-style de-normalization of GroupNorm parameters; and (2) an Appearance Noise Prior that replaces the standard i.i.d. Gaussian noise with ε^i = λz^c + ε_n^i during training (and optionally adds γz^c at inference), so the diffusion process is conditioned on the reference frame's appearance from the very first denoising step. The model is fine-tuned on filtered WebVid-10M with a noise-prediction loss, using a 10× lower learning rate for spatial weights than temporal weights, and achieves SOTA zero-shot FVD of 342.86 on UCF-101 and 377.40 on MSR-VTT.

## Results

MicroCinema achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot FVD of 342.86 on UCF-101 and 377.40 on MSR-VTT, trained exclusively on the WebVid-10M dataset. On UCF-101, it also reports an Inception Score of 37.46, and on MSR-VTT a CLIPSIM of 0.2967. Notably, these FVD results surpass methods that leverage additional training data beyond WebVid-10M, such as PYoCo (FVD 355.19) and Make-A-Video (FVD 367.23) on UCF-101, and SimDA (FVD 456.00) and Show-1 (FVD 538.00) on MSR-VTT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MicroCinema achieves the lowest FVD on both UCF-101 (342.86) and MSR-VTT (377.40), outperforming all 14 baselines on this primary metric. On UCF-101, it beats the closest competitor PYoCo (355.19) by ~12 FVD points and Make-A-Video (367.23) by ~24 points, despite PYoCo and Make-A-Video using additional training data beyond WebVid-10M. On MSR-VTT, the margin is even larger: MicroCinema's 377.40 FVD surpasses SimDA (456.00) by ~79 points and Show-1 (538.00) by ~161 points. However, MicroCinema does not lead on all metrics: PYoCo achieves a higher IS (47.76 vs. 37.46) on UCF-101 and a higher CLIPSIM (0.3204 vs. 0.2967) on MSR-VTT, suggesting that while MicroCinema excels at temporal coherence (FVD), it is slightly less competitive on per-frame quality and text-image alignment. Among methods trained on WebVid-10M only, MicroCinema's FVD advantage over the next-best (Show-1 at 394.46 on UCF-101, SimDA at 456.00 on MSR-VTT) is substantial, confirming the effectiveness of the divide-and-conquer strategy with Appearance Injection Network and Appearance Noise Prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

MicroCinema extends the Stable Diffusion 2D U-Net into a 3D architecture by interleaving zero-initialized temporal convolutions and attention layers, following the factorized space-time design established by Align Your Latents and Make-A-Video. Its AppearNet for appearance injection is structurally inspired by ControlNet and employs SPADE-style denormalization for multi-scale feature fusion into the main branch. The key conceptual departure is the explicit divide-and-conquer decomposition: rather than training a monolithic text-to-video model, the pipeline separates appearance generation (delegated to any off-the-shelf T2I model like SDXL) from motion modeling, with a lightweight appearance noise prior (adding a scaled center-frame latent to the diffusion noise) to preserve the pretrained 2D generation capability during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The divide-and-conquer framing is practically significant because it decouples video quality from the video model's own appearance-generation capacity, allowing the system to automatically benefit from future T2I model improvements without retraining. Achieving SOTA FVD on both UCF-101 and MSR-VTT using only WebVid-10M—outperforming methods trained on additional data—demonstrates that architectural design can partially compensate for data limitations. Limitations include the modest 320×320 base resolution, the 9-frame/2fps output requiring a separate temporal super-resolution stage, and dependence on WebVid-10M's noisy alt-text captions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer, higher-resolution videos and whether the appearance noise prior generalizes to other diffusion backbones or conditioning modalities.
