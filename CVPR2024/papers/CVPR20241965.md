## Problem

Tracking object instances in 3D from egocentric videos is a largely unexplored problem: existing egocentric tracking datasets only support 2D tracking, and no prior benchmark provides the multi-modal sensor data (depth, camera pose) needed for 3D instance tracking in real-world scenes. The paper argues that 3D world-coordinate tracking is fundamentally easier than 2D tracking in the egocentric setting because stationary objects maintain a fixed 3D position while their 2D projections shift drastically with head motion, yet this opportunity has not been systematically studied.

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized RGB (720×1280), depth (480×640), and per-frame camera pose streams from HoloLens2 at 30 fps, plus an object enrollment signal: either a single 2D bounding box (≥500 px²) in the first fully-visible frame (SVOE) or 25 high-resolution (2124×2832) object-centric photos captured from multiple elevations (MVPE). The output is the 3D center of each tracked instance in a predefined world coordinate frame at every timestamp, evaluated only during stationary intervals (when the object is not being handled). Evaluation uses L2 distance thresholds at 0.25–1.5 m for precision/recall, plus continuous L2 (meters) and angular (radians) errors following the Ego4D VQ3D protocol.

## Method

The proposed SAM+DINOv2 pipeline is a non-learned, tracking-by-detection approach: SAM generates exhaustive per-frame 2D proposals from RGB frames, DINOv2 encodes both proposals and the enrolled instance image(s), and cosine similarity (threshold 0.6) selects the matching proposal. The selected 2D center is lifted to 3D via the depth map and camera pose (o = T·z·K⁻¹·c), and a Kalman filter models the object's stationary position as piecewise-constant-velocity motion, with an L2 distance heuristic (0.15 m) detecting interaction-induced motion changes and resetting the filter. A size-1 memory stores the most recent valid 3D position to bridge frames where the object is occluded or outside the field of view. The key insight is that exhaustive proposals combined with high-quality frozen features provide strong generic re-identification ability, and operating in 3D world coordinates exploits the prior that uninteracted objects are stationary—yielding substantially better results than repurposed SOT trackers that rely on temporal propagation in 2D.

## Results

On the IT3DEgo benchmark (SVOE setting), the proposed SAM+DINOv2 method achieves 23.3% precision and 24.9% recall at the 0.25 m threshold, 59.4% precision at 1.5 m, with an L2 error of 1.35 m and angular error of 0.81 rad — outperforming the best SOT baseline (VITKT_M: 21.5% prec@0.25 m, L2=1.55 m) by a clear margin. In the MVPE setting, SAM+DINOv2 achieves 56.0% precision at 0.25 m and 74.3% at 1.5 m, with L2=0.67 m and angular error=0.40 rad, roughly doubling the best SOT baseline (EgoSTARK^T: 18.9% prec@0.25 m, L2=1.67 m). Adding a Kalman filter (SAM+DINOv2_KF) yields marginal gains (e.g., L2 drops from 1.35 m to 1.32 m in SVOE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed SAM+DINOv2 method outperforms all six baseline categories in both SVOE and MVPE settings. Against short-term RGB trackers (ToMP, MixFormer, ARTrack), SAM+DINOv2 achieves roughly 2× higher precision at 0.25 m (23.3% vs. 5.6–9.1%) and lower L2 error (1.35 m vs. 1.92–2.11 m). Against RGB-D trackers (SAMF, MixForRGBD, ViPT), the advantage is similar, confirming that encoding depth as an auxiliary visual feature does not help; SAM+DINOv2's L2 of 1.35 m beats ViPT's 2.02 m by 0.67 m. Against long-term trackers (mixLT, mlpLT, VITKT_M), SAM+DINOv2 leads by 1.8–7.7 points in precision@0.25 m and by 0.2–0.5 m in L2 error. The egocentric-specific EgoSTARK tracker (L2=1.70 m) is also clearly outperformed. In MVPE, the gap widens dramatically: SAM+DINOv2 reaches 56.0% precision@0.25 m and L2=0.67 m, while the best SOT baseline (EgoSTARK^T) only reaches 18.9% and L2=1.67 m, indicating that SOT trackers cannot effectively exploit multi-view pre-enrollment images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the tracking-by-detection paradigm from the SOT literature but replaces learned temporal trackers with a non-learned pipeline built on two foundation models: SAM for per-frame proposal generation and DINOv2 for appearance matching via cosine similarity. It extends the egocentric video understanding line (Ego4D, EgoTracks) by moving from 2D bounding-box tracking or discrete 3D location queries to continuous 3D instance tracking in world coordinates, leveraging camera pose and depth to lift 2D detections into 3D. The key conceptual departure from prior SOT work is the exploitation of the allocentric prior that stationary objects retain fixed world coordinates, which makes the problem fundamentally different from 2D tracking with a moving camera.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark enabling systematic study of 3D instance tracking from egocentric video, filling a gap between 2D egocentric tracking datasets and 3D tracking benchmarks designed for third-person or autonomous-driving settings. The finding that a simple non-learned SAM+DINOv2 pipeline outperforms all learned SOT trackers (including egocentric-specific ones) suggests that strong pretrained features may be more important than temporal modeling for this task, though the small dataset (50 recordings) limits generalizability. Open questions include accurately detecting object motion-state changes (when the user picks up or moves an object), better fusion of 3D scene geometry with instance appearance, and scaling the benchmark to diverse environments and demographics.
