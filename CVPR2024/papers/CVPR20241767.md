## Problem

Existing generative data augmentation methods for instance segmentation do not explain why synthetic data helps from a distribution perspective, rely on manually designed prompts that cannot scale to millions of images, and often require web-crawled data alongside generative data. The paper asks: what is the actual role of generative data in mitigating overfitting, and what properties of generative data are necessary to maintain performance gains as data scale increases?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241767/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generative pipeline takes text prompts (category names combined with attribute descriptions and a 'white background' constraint) and produces single-object images at 512×512 (Stable Diffusion V1.5) or 256×256 (DeepFloyd-IF Stage II). SAM is prompted with four corner points to extract background masks, which are inverted to yield foreground instance masks. The final annotated instances are pasted onto real LVIS training images during model training, and the trained CenterNet2 model outputs per-instance bounding boxes, pixel-level masks, and category labels for 1,203 LVIS categories.

## Method

DiverGen proposes a four-stage generative pipeline (generation, annotation, filtration, augmentation) whose core innovation is Generative Data Diversity Enhancement (GDDE) at three levels: category diversity (adding 250 extra ImageNet-1K categories selected by WordNet similarity, with their classification-head parameters truncated at inference), prompt diversity (using ChatGPT to generate 32–128 diverse prompts per category describing different attributes, combined with manually designed templates), and generative model diversity (mixing outputs from Stable Diffusion and DeepFloyd-IF). For annotation, SAM-background uses corner-point prompts to obtain the background mask and inverts it, exploiting the single-object white-background constraint. For filtration, CLIP inter-similarity compares image embeddings between generative and real data to remove low-quality samples, replacing X-Paste's CLIP text-image score. The paper demonstrates that diversity, not raw quantity, is the critical factor: without GDDE, scaling from 600k to 1.2M images degrades performance, while with GDDE the same scale yields +1.2 box AP and +1.0 mask AP.

## Results

On the LVIS validation set, DiverGen achieves 51.2 box AP and 45.5 mask AP across all categories, and 50.1 box AP and 45.8 mask AP for rare categories, using a Swin-L backbone with CenterNet2. This represents a +3.7 box AP and +3.2 mask AP improvement over the CenterNet2 baseline, and a +1.1 box AP / +1.1 mask AP gain over the strongest prior method X-Paste. For rare categories specifically, DiverGen improves over X-Paste by +1.9 box AP and +2.5 mask AP, and over the baseline by +8.7 box AP and +9.0 mask AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiverGen outperforms all five compared methods on LVIS val. Against the direct baseline CenterNet2 (Swin-L), it gains +3.7 box AP and +3.2 mask AP overall, with much larger gains on rare categories (+8.7 box AP, +9.0 mask AP). Against the strongest prior generative-data method X-Paste (also Swin-L), DiverGen achieves +1.1 box AP and +1.1 mask AP across all categories, and +1.9 box AP and +2.5 mask AP on rare categories; notably, X-Paste uses both generative and web-retrieved data while DiverGen uses only generative data. Compared to Detic (Swin-B), DiverGen leads by +4.3 box AP and +3.8 mask AP. Against the older Copy-Paste and Tan et al. baselines (which use different, smaller backbones), DiverGen leads by roughly 9–10 box AP and 7–8 mask AP, though these comparisons are less directly comparable due to backbone differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiverGen builds directly on X-Paste, which established a pipeline for instance segmentation data augmentation using Stable Diffusion-generated images, CLIP-based filtering, and copy-paste augmentation. The key conceptual departure is reframing the role of generative data from the class-imbalance/data-scarcity perspective (used by X-Paste, DiffuMask, and others) to a distribution-discrepancy perspective, arguing that generative data expands the learnable distribution and mitigates overfitting. Methodologically, DiverGen extends the X-Paste pipeline with a three-level diversity enhancement strategy (category, prompt, and generative model diversity) and replaces X-Paste's multi-model annotation and CLIP-score filtering with SAM-background annotation and CLIP inter-similarity filtering, while notably eliminating the need for web-retrieved data entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-discrepancy framing provides a more principled explanation for why generative data augmentation works, shifting the field's focus from 'compensating for missing data' to 'broadening the learned distribution.' The finding that diversity is more important than raw quantity (1.2M images without diversity enhancement underperform 600k images with it) offers a practical guideline for scaling generative data pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to LVIS with a single backbone (Swin-L) and only two generative models, leaving open whether the diversity strategies generalize to other datasets, tasks, or newer generative architectures. The approach also remains computationally expensive due to large-scale image generation, and the reliance on ChatGPT for prompt generation introduces a dependency on external LLM services.
