## Problem

Omnidirectional (360°) videos inevitably capture dynamic objects and the photographer, causing ghosting artifacts in novel view synthesis. Existing methods either require constrained capture conditions, dedicated hardware, manual motion masks, or model dynamic objects in 3D space across time, which is not scalable for long 360° videos. No prior approach can automatically remove dynamic objects, inpaint occlusions, and perform large-scale omnidirectional view synthesis from casual 360° videos without manual interaction or pretrained segmentation models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a casual omnidirectional 360° video (equirectangular format, e.g., 5760×2880 from an Insta360 camera) as input, using half-resolution frames with no precomputed camera poses. It produces photorealistic rendered novel views of the static scene with dynamic objects removed and occluded regions inpainted, along with estimated camera poses and per-frame motion masks. The progressive local optimization processes 125 frames (every 4th from the first 500) in sliding temporal windows, with 13 held-out test frames for evaluation.

## Method

OmniLocalRF formulates multiple local TensoRF blocks in a sliding temporal window and introduces a bidirectional optimization scheme that exploits the omnidirectional property: distant frames in a 360° video contain substantial geometric information about the current scene (unlike perspective cameras), enabling mutual refinement between the current and previously converged RF blocks via projected ray supervision. A multi-resolution neural feature plane module (4 resolution levels, 128 base height, 4 channels per frame) decoded by a global MLP predicts per-ray dynamic color and motion mask alpha, jointly optimized with the radiance fields through L1 photometric loss, Gaussian-noise-supervised dynamic color for unique factorization, and TV/binary regularization on the mask. The forward step refines the current block using prior frames outside its window, while the backward step refines prior blocks using the current window, with valid-ray filtering based on depth consistency between blocks to avoid occlusion conflicts. This self-supervised pipeline requires no pretrained segmentation model, no manual masks, and no camera pose priors.

## Results

On the real 360° dataset (6 outdoor scenes), OmniLocalRF achieves 27.73 PSNR / 0.8165 SSIM / 0.3297 LPIPS without camera priors, outperforming Mip-NeRF360 (26.88 PSNR), EgoNeRF (25.95 PSNR), and LocalRF (26.56 PSNR) by 0.9–1.8 dB. On the synthetic dataset (3 scenes), the gap widens to ~4.3 dB over Mip-NeRF360 (29.93 vs. 25.59 PSNR). The method also achieves the best pose estimation accuracy (ATE 0.00165) among all compared methods, matching or exceeding OpenMVG and LocalRF even in the presence of dynamic objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniLocalRF outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both datasets. The largest gains are over D²NeRF (20.95 PSNR) and RobustNeRF (20.78 PSNR) on the real dataset, where the ~7 dB gap reflects their inability to scale to long 360° videos due to single-module temporal parameterization and patch-wise IRLS limitations, respectively. Against the strongest scalable baselines—Mip-NeRF360 (26.88 PSNR) and LocalRF (26.56 PSNR)—OmniLocalRF leads by ~1 dB, attributable to its bidirectional optimization and self-supervised motion mask that eliminate ghosting artifacts these methods cannot handle. Notably, OmniLocalRF without camera priors (27.73 PSNR) marginally exceeds its pose-guided variant (27.72 PSNR), demonstrating that the bidirectional refinement compensates for pose uncertainty. On the synthetic dataset, the advantage over Mip-NeRF360 widens to 4.3 dB (29.93 vs. 25.59 PSNR), confirming that dynamic-object removal is the primary source of improvement. For pose estimation, OmniLocalRF achieves the lowest ATE (0.00165) and RPE_t (0.00081), surpassing both OpenMVG and LocalRF even when dynamic objects are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LocalRF's progressive local radiance field framework to the omnidirectional setting, combining it with a self-supervised multi-resolution motion mask module inspired by DynIBaR's Bayesian segmentation approach. The key conceptual departure is the bidirectional optimization scheme, which exploits the unique property of omnidirectional cameras—where every frame observes the full 360° scene—to refine both the current and previously converged radiance blocks using distant frames, something that is infeasible with perspective cameras where distant frames provide minimal new information. Unlike D2NeRF and RobustNeRF, which model dynamic objects in 3D space or down-weight outliers globally, OmniLocalRF segments dynamics per-frame via lightweight feature planes, making it scalable to long videos without pretrained segmentation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniLocalRF removes two major practical barriers to 360° view synthesis: the need for manual motion masks and precomputed camera poses, making it applicable to casual omnidirectional video capture. The bidirectional optimization insight—that omnidirectional cameras inherently provide global multi-view constraints even from distant frames—offers a principled way to improve local radiance field quality that could inform future omnidirectional reconstruction methods. Open questions include handling completely occluded regions (requiring generative inpainting), incorporating global bundle adjustment and loop closure for long trajectories, and addressing polar oversampling in the equirectangular mask prediction space.
