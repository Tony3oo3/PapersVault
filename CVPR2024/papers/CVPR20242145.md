## Problem

Scaling diffusion models to high-resolution video generation breaks their end-to-end nature: latent diffusion requires a separate autoencoder with extensive hyperparameter tuning, and cascaded diffusion introduces multiple independently trained stages that complicate downstream tasks like editing and distillation. Full-resolution training is computationally infeasible for videos, and prior patch-wise diffusion approaches (PatchDiffusion, MaskDIT) still retain 50% of original pixels or rely on full-resolution steps, preventing true scalability to high-resolution video.

## Input / Output

The model takes either a class label (UCF-101) or a text prompt encoded via T5-11B (text-to-video) and produces high-resolution videos: 64×256² for class-conditional generation and 64×288×512 (or 16×576×1024) for text-to-video. During training, only a single patch per pyramid level is processed, retaining as little as ~0.7% of the original video pixels per optimization step. Inference proceeds hierarchically via tiled patch-by-patch generation with 50% coordinate overlap between neighboring patches, using MultiDiffusion-style score averaging in overlapping regions.

## Method

HPDM replaces full-resolution diffusion with a hierarchical pyramid of patches (e.g., 16×64² → 32×128² → 64×256²) built on a Recurrent Interface Network (RIN) backbone, where each level's patch is spatially nested inside the previous level's. Two key techniques enable this: (1) Deep Context Fusion grid-samples and averages feature activations from all lower pyramid levels at positionally aligned coordinates, concatenating them with patch coordinates into each RIN block's input, which allows gradients from high-resolution denoising loss to flow back and shape low-level features; (2) Adaptive Computation assigns only a subset of RIN blocks to process higher-resolution patches (e.g., load [1,1,2,2,3,4] across 6 blocks), concentrating capacity on coarse structure and cutting compute by ~60%. The model is trained end-to-end in pixel space with v-prediction and LAMB optimizer, and can be rapidly fine-tuned from a low-resolution 36×64 base generator (SnapVideo) in just 15K steps for high-resolution text-to-video synthesis.

## Results

On UCF-101 16×256² class-conditional video generation, HPDM-L achieves a state-of-the-art FVD of 66.32 and Inception Score of 87.68, surpassing the previous best non-pretrained method (PVDM, FVD 343.6) by more than 100% in FVD. The smaller variants also perform strongly: HPDM-M reaches FVD 143.1 / IS 84.29, and HPDM-S reaches FVD 344.5 / IS 73.73. For text-to-video, HPDM-T2V fine-tuned from a 36×64 base generator for only 15,000 steps achieves FVD 299.3 at 72×128 resolution, competitive with larger foundational models like PYoCo (FVD 355.2) and Make-A-Video (FVD 367.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPDM-L (FVD 66.32, IS 87.68) outperforms all 13 compared baselines on UCF-101 16×256². Against the strongest non-pretrained competitor PVDM [71] (FVD 343.6, IS 74.4), HPDM-L improves FVD by ~81% and IS by ~18%. Against Make-A-Video [51] (FVD 81.25, IS 82.55), which benefits from large-scale text-to-video pretraining, HPDM-L still achieves a lower FVD (66.32 vs. 81.25) and higher IS (87.68 vs. 82.55) despite being trained end-to-end without pretraining. The gap over GAN-based baselines is even more pronounced: HPDM-L reduces FVD by 90–96% compared to MoCoGAN-HD (700), StyleGAN-V (1431.0), and DIGAN (1630.2). In the zero-shot text-to-video setting (Table 5), HPDM-T2V at 72×128 resolution (FVD 299.3) beats PYoCo (355.2), Make-A-Video (367.2), and VideoFactory (410.0), though it trails HPDM-T2V-1K at 576×1024 (FVD 447.5) which required 100K fine-tuning steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions hierarchical patch diffusion as a third paradigm alongside latent diffusion (LDM) and cascaded diffusion (CDM), departing from both by operating entirely in pixel space without a separate autoencoder and by maintaining a single end-to-end model rather than a pipeline of independently trained generators. It builds directly on the patch-wise training idea from PatchDiffusion [64] but removes its reliance on full-resolution optimization steps, and borrows the coarse-to-fine cascade structure from CDM [23] while unifying it into one jointly-trained network. The base architecture is RIN [27], whose uniform block structure enables the proposed adaptive computation, and the overlapped tiled inference adopts the MultiDiffusion [2] averaging strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a purely patch-wise model retaining only ~0.7% of original pixels can achieve state-of-the-art class-conditional video generation while being 5× more efficient than full-resolution diffusion is a meaningful efficiency result for the field, and the ability to fine-tune from a low-resolution base generator to 64×288×512 in 15K steps offers a practical path to high-resolution video synthesis without prohibitive training costs. However, the text-to-video results remain preliminary (zero-shot, modest IS scores), and the authors acknowledge open issues including dead-pixel artifacts, the need for stronger inter-patch dependence enforcement, and better context conditioning strategies. The techniques (deep context fusion, adaptive computation) are general and could transfer to other patch-wise generative paradigms beyond diffusion.
