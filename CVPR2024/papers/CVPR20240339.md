## Problem

Diffusion models like Stable Diffusion XL achieve state-of-the-art text-to-image generation but require enormous computation per denoising step, dominated by attention blocks. Existing efficiency methods either reduce denoising steps (without reducing per-step memory) or compress the architecture through expensive retraining (thousands of A100 GPU hours). Training-free architectural compression of diffusion models is largely unexplored, and the one prior approach (Token Merging) performs poorly on SD-XL due to its significantly different architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image via the standard SD-XL pipeline (EulerEDMSampler, 50 denoising steps, CFG scale 7.0). The key operational detail is that token pruning is applied dynamically at runtime within each denoising step, with an adjustable FLOPs budget (default 4.1T per step vs. 6.7T for the full model, a 38.8% reduction). The pruning ratio (ρ=63% by default) and the number of early prune-less steps (τ=15) are the main tunable parameters that control the speed-quality trade-off.

## Method

AT-EDM accelerates SD-XL inference at runtime through two complementary mechanisms. First, within each denoising step, it prunes redundant tokens from attention blocks using a Generalized Weighted Page Rank (G-WPR) algorithm that treats the self-attention map as a directed graph adjacency matrix and iteratively propagates importance scores across nodes (O(M×N) complexity per head); pruned tokens are then recovered via a similarity-based copy that uses the attention map to find the most similar retained token for each pruned position, ensuring compatibility with downstream ResNet convolution layers. Second, a Denoising-Steps-Aware Pruning (DSAP) schedule exploits the observation that early-step attention maps have low variance and are less informative for identifying redundant tokens, so it leaves certain attention blocks unpruned for the first τ=15 steps and prunes more aggressively in later steps. The entire framework requires no retraining and is orthogonal to existing sampling or distillation-based acceleration methods.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017 (5k images, 1024×1024), AT-EDM achieves a FID of 28.04 and CLIP score of 0.3209 at 4.1 TFLOPs per step (38.8% FLOPs reduction vs. the full SD-XL at 6.7T), compared to ToMe's FID of 35.27 at the same budget. At a slightly higher budget of 4.5T, AT-EDM-d reaches FID 27.23 and CLIP 0.3245, actually surpassing the full SD-XL sweet spot (FID 27.30, CFG=4) while using only 67% of its FLOPs. In terms of latency, AT-EDM† achieves up to 1.53× speed-up (15.3s vs. 18.0s per image) with fused operations under a desired xformers implementation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMe (the only prior training-free token-merging method for diffusion models), AT-EDM wins decisively on FID at every FLOPs budget tested: at 4.1T, AT-EDM achieves FID 28.04 vs. ToMe's 35.27 (a 7.23-point gap), and at 4.5–4.6T, AT-EDM-d reaches 27.23 vs. ToMe-d's 32.46. AT-EDM also edges out ToMe on CLIP score at the 4.1T budget (0.3209 vs. 0.3198), indicating better text-image alignment. Compared to the full SD-XL sweet spot (FID 27.30 at 6.7T), AT-EDM-c narrows the FID gap to just 0.74 while cutting FLOPs by 38.8%, and AT-EDM-d actually surpasses the full model (27.23 vs. 27.30) at only 67% of the compute. The paper also shows that the DSAP schedule, when applied to ToMe, improves ToMe's quality as well, suggesting the schedule is a broadly applicable component rather than one tied to AT-EDM's specific pruning algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

AT-EDM directly generalizes the Weighted Page Rank (WPR) token-pruning algorithm from ZeroTPrune (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), extending it from self-attention in ViTs to both self- and cross-attention in diffusion U-Nets via the G-WPR formulation. It positions itself against ToMe (Bolya & Hoffman, CVPR 2023), the only prior training-free efficiency method for diffusion models, by replacing embedding-similarity-based token merging with attention-map-driven token pruning and a similarity-based recovery step. The DSAP schedule draws conceptual inspiration from non-uniform denoising-step literature (OMSDPM, DDSM, AutoDiffusion, DiffPruning) but requires no training, fine-tuning, or evolutionary search, making it orthogonal to and stackable with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free architectural compression of diffusion models is a largely unexplored area, and AT-EDM provides the first systematic framework that achieves ~39% FLOPs reduction on SD-XL with a negligible FID gap (0.7 vs. 8.0 for ToMe), making it practically relevant for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The DSAP schedule's transferability to other pruning methods (demonstrated with ToMe) suggests it could serve as a general-purpose quality-boosting component for the broader efficient-DM literature. Key limitations include evaluation only on SD-XL, the xformers current-implementation bottleneck that caps real-world speedup at ~15%, and the method's dependence on U-Net architectures with ResNet blocks, leaving open questions about applicability to DiT-based or video diffusion models.
