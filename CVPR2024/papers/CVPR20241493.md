## Problem

Generalizable novel-view synthesis without per-scene optimization remains ill-posed, with existing methods (GeoNeRF, MVSNeRF, NeuRay) producing inaccurate geometry and severe artifacts in textureless regions and thin structures. No prior generalizable view synthesis approach explicitly exploits the fixed-baseline constraint of stereo-camera images, which enables more reliable dense correspondence and geometry estimation than arbitrary multi-view stereo.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241493/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N pairs of undistorted, stereo-rectified stereo-camera images (left-right) with known camera poses as input; in practice, three stereo pairs (six images) at viewpoints nearest to the target are used. Real images are 1792×896 and synthetic images are 864×448. The output is a rendered novel-view image at an arbitrary target camera pose along with a corresponding depth map, synthesized via volume rendering over a neural radiance field.

## Method

StereoNeRF integrates a pre-trained stereo estimation network (UniMatch) into a GeoNeRF-based generalizable view synthesis pipeline through three components. First, a stereo feature extractor uses weight-shared CNN encoders and stereo attention modules (SAM) to fuse left-right features along horizontal epipolar lines, augmented with stereo-correlated features from the pre-trained stereo network to inject geometric knowledge. Second, a depth-guided plane-sweeping (DGPS) constrains cost-volume construction to a dynamic search range around the stereo-estimated depth rather than the full scene depth range, reducing correspondence-matching errors in textureless regions. Third, a stereo depth loss supervises depth predictions from the stereo network, MVS network, and neural renderer against pseudo-ground-truth depths from the frozen pre-trained stereo network, while the stereo estimation network itself is partially fine-tuned with multi-view supervision for consistency. Training combines a color MSE loss with a composite depth loss (self-supervised + stereo), using Adam with cosine annealing over 250K iterations.

## Results

On StereoNVS-Real, StereoNeRF achieves 28.44 PSNR, 0.9000 SSIM, 0.1396 LPIPS, and 0.4868 ABS, outperforming the closest baseline GeoNeRF (28.01 PSNR, 0.5064 ABS) across all four metrics. On StereoNVS-Synthetic, StereoNeRF reaches 33.45 PSNR and 0.9336 SSIM, the best image-quality scores among all methods, though GeoNeRF+D achieves a lower ABS (0.0782 vs. 0.1056) and LPIPS (0.1171 vs. 0.1203) on that dataset. The method's advantage is most pronounced on real-world scenes, where depth-aware baselines (GeoNeRF+D, NeuRay) degrade substantially (e.g., GeoNeRF+D drops to 25.65 PSNR and 0.8693 ABS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StereoNeRF outperforms all five baselines on every metric for StereoNVS-Real, with the largest margins over SRF (+7.32 PSNR) and GNT (+2.36 PSNR). Against the strongest baseline GeoNeRF, StereoNeRF gains +0.43 PSNR and reduces ABS from 0.5064 to 0.4868 on Real. On StereoNVS-Synthetic, StereoNeRF achieves the best PSNR (33.45) and SSIM (0.9336), but GeoNeRF+D— which explicitly consumes depth maps at inference—edges ahead on LPIPS (0.1171 vs. 0.1203) and ABS (0.0782 vs. 0.1056). The paper attributes this to GeoNeRF+D's sensitivity to depth errors in real-world images, where it drops to 25.65 PSNR and 0.8693 ABS, far below StereoNeRF's 28.44/0.4868. NeuRay, which also requires depth inputs, shows a similar pattern: competitive on Synthetic (32.26 PSNR) but degraded on Real (26.51 PSNR, 0.6147 ABS). GNT underperforms all other methods on both datasets, which the authors attribute to its data-hungry transformer backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

StereoNeRF builds directly on GeoNeRF's generalizable view synthesis pipeline (MVS-based cost volume construction and neural rendering) and extends it by integrating stereo matching as an explicit geometric prior. It departs from prior generalizable NVS methods (MVSNeRF, GeoNeRF, NeuRay, IBRNet) that treat all input views symmetrically, instead exploiting the fixed-baseline, rectified structure of stereo pairs through a stereo attention module (adapted from NAFSSR) and a pre-trained stereo estimation network (UniMatch). The key conceptual shift is that stereo matching's constrained geometry (two views, known baseline) provides more reliable depth than unconstrained MVS, which the paper leverages for both feature extraction and depth-guided plane-sweeping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first dataset (StereoNVS) specifically designed for stereo-camera-based novel-view synthesis, filling a gap in the generalizable NVS literature where stereo inputs were treated as arbitrary multi-view pairs. The finding that stereo depth outperforms MVS depth as pseudo-ground-truth supervision (despite using fewer input images) suggests that the fixed-baseline constraint of stereo matching is a more effective geometric prior than additional views. However, the method remains limited in sparse-view settings where it produces blurry results, and the overall performance gains over GeoNeRF on real scenes are modest, leaving open whether the stereo-specific architecture is necessary or whether simpler depth-conditioning suffices.
