## Problem

Existing large multimodal models (e.g., LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) are limited to whole-image understanding and lack the ability to process region-specific information in complex scenes. Prior region-referencing approaches rely on textual coordinates, learned positional embeddings, or ROI features that are rigid in format and not intuitive for natural user interaction. This paper addresses the gap by enabling models to interpret arbitrary, free-form visual prompts (arrows, scribbles, circles, masks) that users naturally draw on images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240896/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 336×336 by CLIP) with one or more visual prompts alpha-blended directly onto the pixel space, along with a text instruction. Visual prompts come in 8 shapes (rectangle, ellipse, point, triangle, mask, mask contour, arrow, scribble) with randomized color, thickness, transparency (α ∈ [0.5, 1]), scale, and direction. The output is a free-form text response generated autoregressively by the LLM, enabling open-ended region-specific dialogue rather than fixed-format predictions.

## Method

The key insight is that CLIP inherently attends to visual markers, so no separate region-encoding module is needed: visual prompts are simply alpha-blended onto the image (X̂v = α·Pv + (1−α)·Xv) and fed through the standard CLIP encoder. To capture both low-level geometric details of the prompt and high-level scene semantics, the model extracts features from multiple CLIP layers (layer 6 for shapes; layers 15, 18, 21, 24 for semantics), concatenates them, and passes through LayerNorm and a 2-layer MLP connector into Vicuna-7B/13B. Training is three-stage: (1) pretrain the MLP connector on 558k BLIP captions, (2) instruction-tune on LLaVA-1.5 data plus 520k region-level visual prompting pairs (from RefCOCOg, PointQA, Visual Genome, Flickr30k Entities, VCR, Visual7W) with randomly assigned prompt types and attributes, and (3) optionally fine-tune on 13k GPT-4V-curated region-level instruction triplets to improve open-world conversational quality.

## Results

On Visual7W, ViP-LLaVA-13B achieves 87.91% accuracy, surpassing the previous best generalist model Shikra-13B (85.33%) and the specialist GPT4ROI-13B (84.82%). On PointQA-LookTwice, ViP-LLaVA-Base-13B reaches 72.15% accuracy, outperforming Shikra-13B (70.30%) and LLaVA-1.5-13B (57.93%). On VCR, ViP-LLaVA-Base-7B achieves 87.66% Q→A accuracy, edging out GPT4RoI-7B (87.4%). On the newly introduced ViP-Bench, ViP-LLaVA-7B scores 48.4 (synthesized prompts) and 48.3 (human-drawn prompts), second only to GPT-4V-turbo (60.7/59.9) among all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViP-LLaVA outperforms all specialized region-encoding baselines on Visual7W: it beats GPT4ROI-13B by 3.09 points (87.91 vs 84.82) and Shikra-13B by 2.58 points (87.91 vs 85.33), while also surpassing older specialist methods like 12in1 (83.35) and CMNs (72.53) by large margins. On PointQA-LookTwice, ViP-LLaVA-Base-13B (72.15%) exceeds Shikra-13B (70.30%) and LLaVA-1.5-13B (57.93%) by 1.85 and 14.22 points respectively, and outperforms the specialist Point-and-Ask (60.20%) by 11.95 points. On VCR, ViP-LLaVA-Base-7B (87.66% Q→A) narrowly beats GPT4RoI-7B (87.4%) and substantially outperforms prior vision-language models like VILLA-L (78.45%) and ERNIE-ViL-L (78.52%). On ViP-Bench, ViP-LLaVA-7B (48.4 synthesized / 48.3 human) surpasses all open-source baselines including LLaVA-1.5-13B (41.8/42.9), Qwen-VL-Chat (39.2/41.7), InstructBLIP-13B (35.8/35.2), Shikra-7B (33.7), GPT4ROI-7B (35.1), and Kosmos-2 (26.9), but trails GPT-4V-turbo-detail:high (60.7/59.9) by roughly 12 points. Notably, ViP-LLaVA is a generalist model (not fine-tuned on the target benchmark) yet outperforms specialist models that are explicitly trained on those datasets, highlighting the effectiveness of the visual prompt overlay approach over learned positional embeddings or ROI features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP encoder + MLP connector + Vicuna LLM) by exploiting CLIP's inherent ability to attend to superimposed visual markers, rather than building dedicated region-encoding modules as in GPT4ROI, Shikra, or Kosmos-2. Prior region-understanding approaches rely on textual coordinate strings, learned positional embeddings, or ROI feature extraction, which constrain visual prompts to fixed geometric formats; ViP-LLaVA departs from this by simply alpha-blending arbitrary free-form prompts (arrows, scribbles, circles, masks) directly onto the RGB image and letting the existing CLIP encoder process them. The multi-layer CLIP feature extraction (early layer for geometric detail + deeper layers for semantics) is a targeted modification to the standard single-layer LLaVA pipeline, motivated by the need to preserve low-level shape information of the overlaid prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical takeaway is that a trivially simple interface—drawing on an image—can replace complex region-encoding pipelines without sacrificing performance, lowering the barrier for user-facing multimodal applications. ViP-Bench fills a genuine evaluation gap by testing arbitrary visual prompt interpretation across six cognitive dimensions, and its results reveal that even GPT-4V struggles with region-specific localization under visual prompts, suggesting the problem is far from solved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution inputs (OCR and math scores remain weak), whether CLIP's marker-attention property transfers to other vision encoders, and whether the 8-prompt training set is sufficient for truly open-ended user drawings.
