## Problem

Existing multi-person human reconstruction methods focus on pose accuracy, penetration avoidance, or coarse spatial ordering, but fail to model close physical interactions between individuals. The core difficulty is that monocular observation of closely interacting humans suffers from severe depth ambiguity and inter-person occlusion, leaving insufficient visual information to recover plausible poses, natural proxemic relationships, and accurate body contacts simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of two interacting people along with per-frame 2D keypoint detections and bounding boxes as input. It outputs two sets of Gaussian distributions {N^a, N^b} over SMPL parameters (6D pose θ∈R^144, shape β∈R^10, translation τ∈R^3) for each individual, explicitly modeling the uncertainty of the ill-posed single-view reconstruction. The translation is expressed in absolute camera coordinates (via CLIFF-style estimation) rather than root-relative, which is necessary to capture inter-person spatial relationships.

## Method

The approach formulates close-interaction reconstruction as a distribution adaption process rather than direct regression. A dual-branch VQ-VAE learns a discrete interaction prior where each branch encodes one individual's motion state (SMPL params, joint positions, velocities, and relative positions to the counterpart) into a separate codebook, with cross-attention enabling inter-person information sharing. A ViT-based network first regresses coarse initial Gaussian distributions from image features and bounding boxes, avoiding the information-poor early timesteps of standard diffusion. The key modification to the diffusion process is diffusing toward these initial distributions (q(x_t|x̂_0) = x + √(α̂_t)(x̂_0 − x) + √(1−α̂_t)ε, ε~N(0,σ)) rather than toward a standard Gaussian, so early denoising steps already contain meaningful pose structure. At each reverse diffusion timestep, the current sample is refined by (1) snapping to the nearest codebook entries in the interaction prior to enforce proxemic plausibility, (2) computing a BVH-based penetration loss via local 3D distance fields as a physical condition, and (3) incorporating projection-loss gradients and image features as additional guidance, all concatenated as the conditioning signal c for the dual-branch denoiser.

## Results

On the Hi4D dataset (the primary evaluation), the proposed method achieves 63.1 mm MPJPE, 47.5 mm PA-MPJPE, 76.4 mm MPVPE, and 81.4 mm interaction error, outperforming all five baselines on every reported metric. On 3DPW (outdoor, in-the-wild), the method achieves 70.6 mm MPJPE and 100.3 mm interaction error, the best among all methods that report interaction metrics, though it trails single-person methods (Human4D: 49.1, BEV: 48.5, GroupRec: 48.7) on PA-MPJPE. The method also produces qualitatively better physical contacts and proxemic relationships on CHI3D compared to BUDDI and BEV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning single-person and multi-person reconstruction. On Hi4D, the proposed method wins decisively on all four metrics: it reduces MPJPE by 9.0 mm over the closest single-person baseline Human4D (63.1 vs. 72.1) and by 33.7 mm over BUDDI (63.1 vs. 96.8), the most relevant close-interaction method. BUDDI, despite being specifically designed for close interactions, suffers the worst PA-MPJPE (70.6 mm) because its optimization overfits to noisy 2D pose detections and is highly sensitive to depth ambiguity. CLIFF and BEV, which estimate absolute positions for multi-person scenes, show large interaction errors (141.5 and 131.0 mm respectively) due to depth–shape coupling and lack of physical constraints. GroupRec, the strongest multi-person baseline, still trails by 19.3 mm in MPJPE and 17.4 mm in interaction error. On 3DPW (outdoor), the method achieves the best MPJPE (70.6 mm) and interaction error (100.3 mm) but is slightly behind Human4D, BEV, and GroupRec on PA-MPJPE (51.4 vs. 48.5–49.1 mm), indicating that root-relative accuracy in unconstrained outdoor settings remains a challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE discrete representation learning paradigm to a dual-branch architecture where each branch models one individual's motion and the two branches exchange information via cross-attention, enabling the learned codebooks to capture proxemic interaction patterns rather than just individual motion. It also modifies the standard DDPM forward process to diffuse toward task-specific initial distributions (regressed from image features) rather than standard Gaussian noise, reframing reconstruction as a 'distribution adaption' problem. Conceptually, it departs from BUDDI's optimization-based fitting by treating the interaction prior and physical constraints as conditions within a generative diffusion loop, and from multi-person methods (BEV, GroupRec) by explicitly modeling body contacts and proxemic relationships rather than coarse spatial ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely underexplored regime—close physical interactions with severe mutual occlusion—where standard multi-person reconstruction methods fail due to depth ambiguity and pixel-level ambiguity. The distribution adaption formulation offers a principled way to incorporate multiple heterogeneous signals (discrete interaction prior, penetration metrics, projection gradients) as iterative conditions rather than competing loss terms. Key limitations include restriction to two-person interactions (scaling to N people would require combinatorially more codebooks) and degraded performance in outdoor/in-the-wild settings, leaving open the question of whether the approach generalizes to larger social groups or uncontrolled environments without dedicated close-interaction training data.
