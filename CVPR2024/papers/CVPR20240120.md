## Problem

Motion-guided object discovery (MGOD) relies on binary motion masks derived from optical flow, which are inherently noisy (merged objects, spurious background segments from camera motion) and sparse (static objects are entirely absent from supervision). Existing methods like BMOD process all noisy labels in a single round, forcing a trade-off between capturing objects and suppressing noise, and cannot recover the missing static-object category. The paper asks whether framing MGOD as learning from noisy and sparse labels—specifically through iterative self-distillation—can simultaneously filter noise and enrich supervision with previously undiscovered objects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes short video sequences (T=5 frames for TRI-PD/KITTI, T=6 for MOVi-E) encoded by a ResNet-18 backbone (or DINOv2 ViT-S14 in the DIOD* variant) with a convGRU module, alongside M binary motion masks per frame extracted from optical flow (no confidence scores provided). It produces K slot attention maps (45 for TRI-PD/KITTI, 24 for MOVi-E) representing individual object instances, a learned foreground map W_fg, and a background map W_bg; the slots are also decoded via weighted summation to reconstruct the input sequence. During teacher-student training, the teacher's binarized attention maps are decomposed into connected regions (object candidates) that supervise multiple student slots in a one-to-many configuration, while filtered motion masks provide a fixed regularization signal.

## Method

DIOD places the slot-attention mechanism inside a two-phase self-distillation framework: a burn-in stage trains both models on noisy motion masks (with full-map regularization on W_fg rather than only non-object regions, since distillation will recover attenuated objects), followed by teacher-student training where the teacher is an EMA of the student (rate 0.996) and only the student receives gradient updates. The key mechanism is a one-to-many attention connection: the teacher's attention maps are binarized, decomposed into connected regions, scored by mean activation (threshold p=0.9), and high-confidence candidates supervise multiple student slots via a confidence-weighted BCE loss, enabling recovery of static objects and previously missed instances. A second, fixed label source—motion masks filtered by the teacher's learned foreground attention used as an objectness map—regularizes training against confirmation bias. The global loss combines the teacher pseudo-label BCE, the filtered motion-mask BCE, the foreground/background modeling loss, and an MSE reconstruction term, with normalization by the number of objects per frame.

## Results

On TRI-PD, DIOD achieves 70.3 all-ARI and 35.4 F1@50, substantially outperforming the previous best BMOD (28.6 all-ARI, 14.4 F1@50) by over 2× on both metrics. On KITTI, DIOD reaches 61.6 all-ARI and 18.0 F1@50 versus BMOD's 17.8 and 9.3. With DINOv2 pre-training (DIOD*), the gains extend to 74.1/41.5 (TRI-PD) and 81.6/23.2 (KITTI). On the standard fg-ARI metric, DIOD scores 66.1 (TRI-PD) and 73.5 (KITTI), surpassing MoTok (55.1/64.4) and BMOD (53.9/54.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIOD dominates all motion-mask-based baselines by large margins on both full-image metrics: on TRI-PD it improves all-ARI from 28.6 (BMOD) to 70.3 and F1@50 from 14.4 to 35.4, and on KITTI from 17.8/9.3 to 61.6/18.0. The earlier motion-guided methods (Bao et al., MoTok) score below 7 all-ARI on TRI-PD, indicating severe background over-segmentation that DIOD's distillation-based filtering eliminates. On the standard fg-ARI metric (Table 1), DIOD still leads MoTok (55.1→66.1 on TRI-PD; 64.4→73.5 on KITTI) and BMOD (53.9→66.1; 54.7→73.5). On MOVi-E (Table 2), DIOD* with DINOv2 achieves 82.2 fg-ARI, edging out SLOV (80.8) and VideoSAUR (78.4), while the non-pretrained DIOD is not directly listed there. The DINOv2 variant (DIOD*) consistently adds 3–4 points over the base DIOD, confirming scalability, and on KITTI all-ARI the gap widens from 61.6 to 81.6, surpassing BMOD* (21.7) by a factor of nearly 4×.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIOD builds directly on BMOD's background-aware motion-guided object discovery architecture and the slot-attention mechanism, while importing the EMA teacher-student distillation and burn-in protocol from the semi-supervised object detection literature (Unbiased Teacher). The paper's conceptual contribution is unifying two previously separate research threads—motion-guided object discovery (MGOD) and learning from noisy labels (LNL)—by observing that MGOD's optical-flow-derived masks are inherently noisy and sparse, making the slot-attention model a natural candidate for iterative pseudo-label refinement. This departs from prior MGOD methods (Bao et al., MoTok, BMOD) that treat all motion masks equally in a single training round, and from prior LNL methods that discard or re-annotate noisy labels rather than recovering missing object categories (static objects) through empty slots.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIOD demonstrates that a self-distillation loop can simultaneously filter optical-flow noise and recover static objects that are entirely absent from the initial motion supervision, yielding more than a 2× improvement in F1@50 over the prior state-of-the-art (BMOD) on TRI-PD. The proposed F1@50 metric and the one-to-many attention connection (avoiding semantic-segmentation drift) offer practical guidance for future MGOD work. Open questions include whether the fixed regularization strength α can be made frame-adaptive, whether confidence scoring can incorporate object completeness (IoU-like), and whether the framework extends to end-to-end motion integration without pre-computed flow maps.
