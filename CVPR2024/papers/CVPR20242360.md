## Problem

UnCLIP text-to-image models (DALL-E-2, Karlo, Kandinsky) rely on a ~1 billion parameter diffusion-based T2I prior trained on 115M–250M image-text pairs, raising the question of whether this heavy diffusion prior is truly necessary for strong compositional generation. The paper investigates whether the diffusion formulation of the prior actually helps (it does not, and slightly hurts) and whether a far smaller, non-diffusion prior can match SOTA compositionality with a fraction of the parameters and data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242360/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt, encodes it via a frozen CLIP text encoder into a text embedding z_y, and the ECLIPSE prior (a 33–34M parameter time-independent PriorTransformer) maps z_y to a vision embedding ẑ_x in the CLIP vision latent space. This ẑ_x is then fed to a frozen diffusion image decoder (Karlo-v1-alpha or Kandinsky v2.2) along with z_y to produce the final RGB image. The prior itself is non-diffusion: it takes a single Gaussian noise vector ε and the text embedding as input and outputs the vision embedding in one forward pass, requiring no iterative denoising.

## Method

ECLIPSE replaces the standard diffusion T2I prior with a compact non-diffusion PriorTransformer and trains it with a two-term objective: a projection loss (MSE between the predicted vision embedding and the ground-truth CLIP vision embedding) plus a CLIP contrastive loss that maximizes cosine similarity between the predicted vision embedding and the correct text embedding while minimizing it against in-batch negatives. The key insight is that the diffusion prior's iterative denoising in the compressed CLIP latent space accumulates errors and provides no benefit, while the contrastive term distills CLIP's pre-trained cross-modal semantic alignment into the prior, acting as a regularizer that improves generalization beyond the training distribution. Empirically, the prior needs only 33M parameters (3.3% of the 1B baseline) and as few as 0.6M–5M image-text pairs (2.8% of typical training data) to match or exceed the compositionality of full-size unCLIP models, with a hyperparameter λ=0.2 balancing the two loss terms.

## Results

ECLIPSE trains a 33–34M-parameter non-diffusion T2I prior using only 0.6M–10M image-text pairs (2.8% of Kandinsky's 177M training set) and achieves competitive composition scores: with the Kandinsky v2.2 decoder on 5M LAION-HighRes pairs, it reaches Color 0.6119, Shape 0.5429, Texture 0.6165, Spatial 0.1903, and Non-Spatial 0.3139 on T2I-CompBench, while attaining a ZS-FID of 19.16 on MS-COCO 30k. Under resource-limited settings (same data and model size as baselines), ECLIPSE achieves a 71.6% average PickScore preference over Projection and Diffusion-Baseline priors. Against SOTA large models, the best ECLIPSE variant (Kandinsky v2.2 + 5M HighRes) achieves a 63.36% average PickScore preference, using only 3.3% of the parameters and 2.8% of the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Diffusion v1.4 and v2.1 (LDM, 900M params, 400M–2000M data), ECLIPSE (33M params, 0.6M–10M data) substantially outperforms on all five T2I-CompBench composition metrics (e.g., Color 0.5965 vs. 0.3765 for SDv1.4; Texture 0.6136 vs. 0.4156), though it has a higher ZS-FID (23.67 vs. 16.31 for SDv1.4). Against Würstchen (1B params, 1420M data), ECLIPSE wins on every composition category by a wide margin (e.g., Spatial 0.1574 vs. 0.0696). Against Karlo (1B params, 115M data), ECLIPSE with the same Karlo decoder outperforms on Color (0.5965 vs. 0.5127), Texture (0.6136 vs. 0.5887), Spatial (0.1574 vs. 0.1337), and Non-Spatial (0.3235 vs. 0.3112), while being slightly lower on Shape (0.5063 vs. 0.5277). Against Kandinsky v2.2 (1B params, 177M data), ECLIPSE with the Kandinsky v2.2 decoder and 5M HighRes data matches or exceeds on Color (0.6119 vs. 0.5768), Shape (0.5429 vs. 0.4999), and Texture (0.6165 vs. 0.5760), with comparable Spatial (0.1903 vs. 0.1912) and Non-Spatial (0.3139 vs. 0.3132). Against DALL-E-2 (4.5B total params, 250M data), ECLIPSE is competitive on compositions (Color 0.6119 vs. 0.5750; Texture 0.6165 vs. 0.6374) but trails on Shape (0.5429 vs. 0.5464) and has a higher FID (19.16 vs. 10.65), reflecting the trade-off of using far less data and parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unCLIP two-stage architecture (T2I prior + diffusion image decoder) established by DALL-E-2, Karlo, and Kandinsky, but departs from the standard diffusion-based prior formulation by replacing it with a non-diffusion PriorTransformer trained via CLIP contrastive learning. It positions itself against both LDMs (Stable Diffusion) and the full unCLIP stack, arguing that the prior component—rather than the decoder—is the key to compositional quality. The contrastive distillation strategy repurposes CLIP's image-text alignment as a regularizer for the prior, a use of CLIP that goes beyond its typical role as a frozen feature extractor in these pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 33M-parameter non-diffusion prior trained on 2.8% of the data can match or exceed 1B-parameter diffusion priors on composition benchmarks challenges the implicit assumption that scale and diffusion formulation are necessary for strong T2I priors. The empirical finding that additional diffusion prior steps and added noise actually degrade performance is a useful negative result for the field. However, the FID gap relative to original models (e.g., 23.67 vs. 20.64 for Karlo) and the restriction to the unCLIP framework limit broader applicability, and the paper leaves open whether these gains transfer to LDM architectures or whether improved VLMs will further close the quality gap.
