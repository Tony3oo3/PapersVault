## Problem

After 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization, the millions of tiny Gaussians are unorganized and do not align with the scene surface, making mesh extraction from them extremely challenging. No prior method could efficiently recover an editable mesh from a 3DGS representation, while existing approaches based on Neural SDFs require at least 24 hours on a single GPU and multiple GPUs for speedup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242220/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB training images of a scene along with an SfM point cloud for Gaussian initialization, and produces a 3D triangle mesh with surface-aligned flat Gaussians bound to its triangles. The full pipeline (regularized optimization, mesh extraction, and optional joint refinement) completes in minutes to an hour on a single GPU, and the resulting mesh-Gaussian pair supports real-time rendering, editing, animation, and compositing through standard mesh manipulation tools.

## Method

SuGaR introduces a regularization term that encourages Gaussians to be flat and well-distributed over the scene surface by minimizing the L1 difference between an ideal SDF (derived under the assumption of flat, opaque, non-overlapping Gaussians) and the actual SDF estimated from depth maps rendered by the splatting rasterizer. For mesh extraction, instead of Marching Cubes (which fails on the sparse density produced by millions of tiny Gaussians), the method samples points on a density level set by tracing rays through training-viewpoint depth maps and applying linear interpolation, then runs Poisson reconstruction on these points with their analytical normals. An optional refinement stage binds new flat Gaussians (with 2D scaling and rotation) to mesh triangles at predefined barycentric coordinates and jointly optimizes both the mesh vertices and Gaussian parameters through Gaussian splatting rendering, yielding higher rendering quality than prior mesh-based methods while enabling intuitive scene editing.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, R-SuGaR-15K achieves an average PSNR of 27.27, SSIM of 0.820, and LPIPS of 0.253 across indoor and outdoor scenes. Among methods that recover an editable mesh, SuGaR outperforms BakedSDF (27.06 PSNR, indoor only), NeRFMeshing (23.15 PSNR), and Mobile-NeRF (21.95 PSNR, outdoor only). SuGaR also surpasses no-mesh methods INGP-Big (26.75 PSNR) and Plenoxels (23.62 PSNR), while remaining slightly below vanilla 3DGS (28.69 PSNR) and Mip-NeRF360 (29.09 PSNR). Mesh extraction completes in 5–10 minutes on a single GPU, compared to 24+ hours for SDF-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among mesh-based methods, R-SuGaR-15K clearly dominates: it beats BakedSDF by 0.21 dB PSNR (27.27 vs. 27.06) while reporting full indoor+outdoor results (BakedSDF reports indoor only), and outperforms NeRFMeshing by 4.12 dB (27.27 vs. 23.15) and Mobile-NeRF by 5.32 dB (27.27 vs. 21.95). Against no-mesh baselines, SuGaR surpasses INGP-Big (27.27 vs. 26.75 PSNR) and Plenoxels (27.27 vs. 23.62 PSNR), but trails vanilla 3DGS by 1.42 dB (27.27 vs. 28.69) and Mip-NeRF360 by 1.82 dB (27.27 vs. 29.09). The paper argues this small gap is acceptable given that SuGaR additionally produces an editable, animatable mesh in minutes, whereas 3DGS and Mip-NeRF360 provide no explicit geometry. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that SuGaR's Poisson-based level-set extraction (24.87–24.91 PSNR) substantially outperforms Marching Cubes (23.91 PSNR) and naive Poisson on Gaussian centers (23.76 PSNR) for mesh quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuGaR extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by introducing a surface-alignment regularization term during optimization, then departs from the dominant NeRF/SDF-based mesh extraction pipeline (NeuS, Neuralangelo, BakedSDF, NeRFMeshing) that relies on Marching Cubes over a learned signed distance function. The key conceptual shift is replacing Marching Cubes with Poisson reconstruction on level-set points sampled from Gaussian depth maps, which is both faster and more robust to the sparse, unstructured density produced by 3DGS. The optional refinement stage—binding flat Gaussians to mesh triangles and jointly optimizing them—follows the 'baking' paradigm of Mobile-NeRF and BakedSDF but uses Gaussian splatting rendering instead of neural feature interpolation, inheriting the speed advantage of the 3DGS rasterizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing mesh extraction from hours (SDF-based methods) to minutes on a single GPU, SuGaR makes the 3DGS-to-mesh pipeline practical for interactive content creation, enabling traditional mesh editing, animation, and compositing tools to operate on Gaussian Splatting scenes. The finding that surface-aligned Gaussians bound to a mesh outperform traditional UV textures for rendering (Table 3) suggests a new texturing paradigm that could influence how appearance is stored on meshes. Open limitations include geometry distortion in highly specular regions (Gaussians 'cheat' depth to fake specular highlights) and the fundamental difficulty of representing volumetric or semi-transparent materials with a surface-aligned model.
