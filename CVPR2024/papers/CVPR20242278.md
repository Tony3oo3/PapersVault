## Problem

Existing 3D face stylization methods cannot simultaneously produce faces in a desired topology compatible with CG pipelines, achieve stylization beyond the limited 3DMM shape space, and generate results animatable via blend shapes. No prior approach satisfies all three requirements, leaving a gap between research-quality stylization and practical avatar production workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a deformation target mesh of arbitrary topology (e.g., FLAME, Loop-subdivided, simplified, CoMA, or ICT-FaceKit meshes) and a single manually-crafted style exemplar mesh paired with an identity exemplar. It outputs a stylized 3D face mesh in a user-specified target topology that preserves the deformation target's identity while adopting the exemplar's style, and remains animatable through standard 3DMM blend shape parameters (shape β ∈ R³⁰⁰ and expression ψ ∈ R¹⁰⁰).

## Method

The core pipeline trains a surface deformation network D_S on FLAME in a self-supervised manner using a Surface-Intensive Mesh Sampling (SIMS) strategy that samples ~4× more surface points than vertices, then fine-tunes it into a target-style network D_T via one-shot domain adaptation with a paired exemplar. Fine-tuning employs a hierarchical rendering scheme (multiple views at multiple zoom levels including close-ups of nose, eyes, lips) combined with directional CLIP losses (in-domain L_in and across-domain L_across) to transfer style while preserving identity. A novel style loss L_style compares surface normals using a pseudo-pair construction (concatenating the reference expression with the sampled shape) to enforce style adherence without collapsing expression diversity. At inference, a Mesh Agnostic Encoder (MAGE) built on NFR's pre-trained DiffusionNet identity and expression encoders plus MLP latent mappers projects any-topology input meshes into D_S's latent space, enabling topology-agnostic stylization.

## Results

On the mesh stylization benchmark (8 identities × 3 topologies), LeGO achieves an overall average CLIP score of 0.9491 (CLIP-SP 0.9753, CLIP-IP 0.9228), outperforming Deformation Transfer (0.9477), X-mesh (0.8680), and Text Deformer (0.8461). In a user study with 34 participants across 8 styles, LeGO was selected 60.65% of the time versus 38.17% for Deformation Transfer and <1% for both text-based methods. The SIMS sampling strategy reduces reconstruction loss by ~23% over hybrid sampling and ~53% over vertex-only sampling across four mesh resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeGO outperforms Deformation Transfer [39] by a narrow margin (0.9491 vs. 0.9477 overall average), with the gap most visible on the FLAME topology (0.9607 vs. 0.9572) where Deformation Transfer introduces surface artifacts from direct displacement transfer. Both text-based baselines—Text Deformer [7] (0.8461) and X-mesh [22] (0.8680)—trail LeGO by roughly 8–10 points in overall CLIP score, primarily because text prompts fail to faithfully reproduce the geometric style of a specific exemplar mesh. In the user study, LeGO was preferred 60.65% of the time, nearly double Deformation Transfer's 38.17%, while both text-based methods received less than 1% selection, confirming that example-based guidance is far more effective than text for matching a target style. The advantage over Deformation Transfer is consistent but small in aggregate metrics; the qualitative difference (absence of concavities and surface anomalies) is more pronounced in visual comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the template-deformation paradigm of DD3C (Jung et al., 2022) by training the surface deformation network on the FLAME 3DMM and then transferring its domain to a target style via a single paired exemplar, rather than learning caricature from scratch. It repurposes NFR's (Qin et al., 2023) topology-agnostic identity and expression encoders into MAGE to handle arbitrary input mesh topologies, and adapts the CLIP directional domain-gap losses from Zhu et al. (2022) — originally designed for 2D GAN adaptation — into a 3D mesh stylization setting via a hierarchical differentiable renderer. The key departure from prior 3D face stylization work (3D-aware GANs, text-guided 3DMM texture methods, text-based mesh deformation) is the simultaneous satisfaction of three constraints: desired output topology, geometric stylization beyond the 3DMM shape space, and animatability via blend shapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in the stylized avatar pipeline: no prior approach simultaneously produces a mesh in a user-specified topology, achieves geometric stylization beyond 3DMM limits, and retains 3DMM-compatible animation — making it directly usable in existing CG workflows without re-rigging. The MAGE component and the pseudo-pair style loss (which decouples style adherence from expression freedom) are reusable design patterns for other mesh-domain adaptation tasks. Open questions include the two-stage inference cost for same-topology output, whether the single-exemplar domain transfer generalizes to highly abstract or non-human styles, and how the approach scales to full-body or multi-character stylization.
