## Problem

Text-guided 3D domain adaptation and avatar generation remain challenging due to the lack of 3D training data and the difficulty of modeling highly variable geometry and appearance. Adversarial fine-tuning methods require large stylized datasets with aligned poses and suffer from mode collapse, while SDS-based methods (e.g., DreamFusion) produce unstable results with diversity loss, Janus artifacts, and poor texture fidelity. No existing approach simultaneously achieves stable, high-fidelity 3D generation and flexible text-guided adaptation by leveraging both 3D GAN priors and diffusion models.

## Input / Output

For domain adaptation, the method takes a text prompt and random noise (sampled from N(0,1)) along with camera parameters, and outputs a finetuned EG3D-based triplane generator that produces stylized, view-consistent images. For text-to-avatar, it takes a text prompt, performs CLIP-guided latent searching to select and refine a fixed latent code, and outputs a textured 3D mesh (via marching cubes and UV unwrapping) representing the avatar. The progressive texture refinement stage operates on explicit UV texture maps initialized from multi-view renderings at 2k+2 azimuths, producing final high-resolution textures.

## Method

DiffusionGAN3D is a two-stage framework that finetunes an EG3D-based 3D GAN using SDS loss from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, with only the triplane generator trainable while the mapping network and decoder remain frozen. Three key losses address specific failure modes: a relative distance loss that preserves inter-sample diversity in triplane space without constraining the domain shift magnitude, a diffusion-guided reconstruction loss that uses the SDS gradient magnitude as an adaptive mask for local editing, and a multi-scale total variation loss that regularizes a case-specific learnable triplane added to the generator output for text-to-avatar. The second stage extracts an explicit mesh via marching cubes, initializes a UV texture through an adaptive blend module (differentiable rendering with MSE and TV losses), then progressively refines it by rotating the mesh and applying ControlNet-guided inpainting (rather than image-to-image) at each view to maintain cross-view texture consistency.

## Results

On the 3D domain adaptation task (EG3D-FFHQ and EG3D-AFHQ), DiffusionGAN3D achieves a FID of 28.5, substantially outperforming StyleGAN-Fusion (53.6) and StyleGAN-NADA* (136.2). In the user study, the proposed method scores 3.725/5 on text-image correspondence, 3.758/5 on image quality, and 3.416/5 on diversity, leading all baselines on every dimension. For text-to-avatar generation, user preference voting shows the method is favored 80.5% of the time for head generation and 66.6% for full-body generation, far exceeding the next-best competitor (ProlificDreamer at 11.7% and 8.3% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionGAN3D decisively outperforms both domain-adaptation baselines: it reduces FID by 47% over StyleGAN-Fusion (28.5 vs. 53.6) and by 79% over StyleGAN-NADA* (28.5 vs. 136.2), while also leading on all three user-study dimensions. StyleGAN-Fusion's directional regularizer creates a trade-off between diversity and stylization strength, whereas the proposed relative distance loss avoids this by only constraining relative inter-sample distances. In text-to-avatar, the method dominates: DreamFusion suffers from Janus and over-saturation (1.1% head preference), ProlificDreamer and Magic3D improve texture but still produce inaccurate geometry (11.7% and 6.7% head), and DreamHuman—leveraging SMPL priors—reaches 18.9% body preference but still lags behind DiffusionGAN3D's 66.6%. DreamAvatar and AvatarVerse are shown qualitatively in Figure 7 but are excluded from the quantitative table due to unavailable code; the paper notes their SDS-based approaches still exhibit suboptimal texture fidelity and multi-view inconsistency compared to the GAN-anchored framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: 3D GAN-based generation (EG3D, PanoHead, AG3D) and diffusion-prior-guided 3D optimization (DreamFusion's SDS, ProlificDreamer's VSD, Magic3D). It departs from adversarial fine-tuning approaches (StyleGAN-NADA, 3D-AvatarGAN) that require large pose-aligned stylized datasets, and from pure NeRF-based SDS methods that suffer from Janus problems and geometric instability. The key conceptual move is using a frozen 3D GAN as a structural prior while letting SDS guide only the triplane generator, supplemented by a relative distance loss to preserve diversity and a progressive texture refinement stage that exploits 2D diffusion models on explicit UV textures via ControlNet-guided inpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for text-guided 3D avatar generation that avoids the instability of pure SDS-on-NeRF while retaining the flexibility of text conditioning, which is valuable for production pipelines in games and film. However, it is constrained to categories covered by the pretrained 3D GAN (heads, bodies) and relies on a multi-stage pipeline (SDS fine-tuning → marching cubes → UV unwrapping → progressive inpainting) that is computationally non-trivial. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to arbitrary object categories, whether the progressive texture refinement can be made differentiable end-to-end, and how it scales to higher-fidelity or animatable outputs beyond static avatars.
