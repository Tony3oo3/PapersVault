## Problem

Generating realistic, long-term oscillatory motion (e.g., trees swaying, candles flickering) from a single still image remains challenging: direct video generation methods produce temporally incoherent artifacts, while prior animation techniques require external driving signals or user annotations. No learned prior over image-space motion exists that can predict dense, long-range pixel trajectories from a single image and support both seamless looping and interactive dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240787/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (256×160 for quantitative evaluation, up to 512×288 for qualitative results) and outputs a 4K-channel spectral volume (K=16 frequency bands, each with complex Fourier coefficients for x- and y-displacement per pixel). This spectral volume is inverse-FFT'd into a motion texture of T per-pixel displacement fields, which drives an image-based rendering module to produce future video frames. The same spectral volume can alternatively be interpreted as an image-space modal basis for interactive force-response simulation.

## Method

The core contribution is a frequency-coordinated latent diffusion model that predicts spectral volumes (Fourier-domain representations of per-pixel motion trajectories) conditioned on a single image. Rather than predicting all K frequency bands jointly in one U-Net (which over-smooths) or independently (which produces uncorrelated, unrealistic motion), the architecture interleaves 2D spatial layers with cross-frequency attention layers that reshape the batch dimension to coordinate predictions across all K bands simultaneously. A frequency adaptive normalization (per-frequency 95th-percentile scaling followed by a square-root transform) addresses the exponential decay of spectral coefficient magnitudes, preventing higher-frequency terms from collapsing to zero. The predicted spectral volume is rendered into video via a feature pyramid softmax splatting module that uses motion magnitude as a depth proxy for occlusion handling, trained with VGG perceptual loss. For seamless looping, a motion self-guidance signal enforces position and velocity matching between start and end frames during the denoising process.

## Results

On the test set of 256×128 natural oscillation scenes, the proposed method achieves FID 4.03 and KID 0.08 for image synthesis, and FVD 47.1, FVD32 62.9, DTFVD 2.53, and DTFVD32 6.75 for video synthesis — substantially outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest single-image animation baselines (Endo et al. and Holynski et al.) achieve FID ~10–11 and FVD ~166–179, roughly 3–4× worse on video coherence metrics. In a user study against large video diffusion models (AnimateDiff, ModelScope, Gen-2), users prefer the proposed method 80.9% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven quantitative baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest single-image animation baselines — Endo et al. [29] (FID 10.4, FVD 166.0) and Holynski et al. [46] (FID 11.2, FVD 179.0) — the gap is roughly 2.5–3× on FID and 3.5–4× on FVD, indicating substantially better temporal coherence from the global spectral volume representation. Direct video-generation baselines (TATS, Stochastic-I2V, MCVD, LFDM, DMVFN) are far worse on both image and video metrics, with FVD values of 187–266 versus 47.1 for the proposed method. In a separate user study against three large video diffusion models (AnimateDiff [36], ModelScope [97], Gen-2 [31]), users prefer the proposed method 80.9% of the time, with the large models exhibiting color drift, content inconsistency, or failure to adhere to the input image. The sliding-window FID/DTFVD analysis further confirms that the proposed method does not degrade over time, unlike autoregressive baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the spectral volume representation from Davis et al. [23]—originally designed for interactive dynamics simulation from video—as the output target of a latent diffusion model [73], shifting the motion prediction problem from the time domain to the frequency domain. The frequency-coordinated denoising architecture (interleaving spatial U-Net layers with cross-frequency attention) is a novel adaptation of video diffusion techniques [7] to the spectral axis, while the rendering module builds on softmax splatting [67] and the interactive dynamics application extends image-space modal bases [22] from video inputs to single images. The key departure from prior image-to-video animation methods (Endo et al. [29], Holynski et al. [46]) is generating a compact frequency-domain motion prior rather than time-domain flow fields or raw frames, enabling long-term coherence without autoregressive drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By predicting motion in the Fourier domain, the method achieves a compact representation (16 frequency bands vs. hundreds of frames) that naturally enforces temporal periodicity and long-range coherence, enabling applications like seamless looping and interactive dynamics that are difficult with direct frame generation. The 80.9% user preference over large general-purpose video diffusion models (AnimateDiff, Gen-2) on oscillatory scenes suggests that explicit motion modeling can outperform implicit frame prediction for structured dynamics. However, the approach is limited to low-frequency oscillatory motions (trees, flowers, candles) and struggles with thin objects, large displacements, or non-oscillatory dynamics; extending to learned motion bases or higher frequencies remains an open question. The frequency-coordinated denoising and adaptive normalization techniques may transfer to other multi-channel or multi-scale diffusion prediction tasks.
