## Problem

Local appearance editing of NeRFs remains underexplored: existing methods for recoloring or stylization either introduce background artefacts, lack support for truly local edits, require excessive memory, or cannot combine photorealistic recoloring with non-photorealistic stylization in a single interactive framework. No prior approach enables simultaneous style transfer and interactive recoloring of arbitrary 3D regions while maintaining multi-view consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242452/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained iNGP-based NeRF, a user scribble on the 2D screen projection (mapped to a 128³ voxel edit grid with region growing), an optional style image, and a user-modified color palette. It produces a distilled, edited radiance field that renders the selected region with the new appearance while preserving the background, along with a learnable palette of 8 base colors that can be interactively modified post-training for real-time recoloring. Previews are available after ~20 seconds of optimization.

## Method

LAENeRF reduces the editing problem to a lightweight, NeRF-like module that maps estimated ray termination points (x_term) to output colors via a palette-based decomposition: a multi-resolution hash grid encodes each x_term, and shallow MLPs predict per-ray barycentric weights and view-dependent offsets (conditioned on spherical harmonics of the viewing direction), which are composed with a learnable color palette using additive layer mixing. Region selection leverages NeRFShop's voxel grid with a growing queue to model smooth transitions at boundaries via distance-based palette interpolation. Training uses a content loss, an optional VGG19 gram-matrix style loss, and two novel geometry-aware regularizers—a depth-guided total variation loss that suppresses noise in flat regions and a depth-discontinuity loss that encourages gradients at geometric edges—plus palette sparsity and offset losses. After optimizing LAENeRF for 100k iterations, a distillation step blends the edited region with the original training set (using per-ray alpha weights) and fine-tunes the full NeRF for 70k iterations, keeping total processing under 5 minutes on a single GPU.

## Results

For photorealistic recoloring on LLFF, LAENeRF achieves a mean background MSE of 0.0007, reducing error by 59% compared to PaletteNeRF with semantic guidance (0.0017) and by 91% compared to ICE-NeRF (0.0075). On mip-NeRF 360, LAENeRF maintains a background MSE of 0.0016 after recoloring, matching its unmodified baseline and outperforming PaletteNeRF's recolored result (0.0033). For non-photorealistic stylization, LAENeRF achieves an average background MSE of 0.0048 versus 0.0270 for Ref-NPR, with particularly large gains on NeRF-Synthetic (0.0071 vs. 0.0466). Processing time is 3 minutes for recoloring (vs. 13.5 min for PaletteNeRF) and 2 minutes for stylization (vs. 2.5 min for Ref-NPR), with interactive previews available after ~20 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PaletteNeRF [20], LAENeRF consistently achieves lower background MSE on both LLFF (0.0007 vs. 0.0017 with semantic guidance) and mip-NeRF 360 (0.0016 vs. 0.0033 for recolored), while also being 4.5× faster (3 min vs. 13.5 min). PaletteNeRF's reliance on distilled semantic features limits its ability to capture small regions or perform multi-color edits, and it introduces noticeable background artefacts. Against ICE-NeRF [22], LAENeRF reduces background MSE by 91% on LLFF (0.0007 vs. 0.0075) and supports arbitrary multi-palette edits, whereas ICE-NeRF is restricted to a single target color and struggles with unbounded 360° captures. Against Ref-NPR [49] for stylization, LAENeRF achieves a 5.6× lower average background MSE (0.0048 vs. 0.0270), with the gap widening to 6.6× on NeRF-Synthetic (0.0071 vs. 0.0466) due to Ref-NPR's sensitivity to occlusions. LAENeRF also completes stylization in 2 min versus Ref-NPR's 2.5 min, and uniquely supports interactive recoloring of the stylized output.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAENeRF sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of NeRF editing research: palette-based recoloring (PaletteNeRF, ICE-NeRF, RecolorNeRF) and non-photorealistic stylization (Ref-NPR, StyleRF, ARF). It inherits the voxel-grid region selection and growing-queue mechanism from NeRFShop, and the multi-resolution hash encoding from Instant-NGP, but departs from both by operating on a single estimated ray-termination point per ray rather than full volumetric sampling, drastically reducing memory. The key conceptual departure is unifying photorealistic recoloring and style transfer under one palette-based decomposition supervised by a switchable style loss, whereas prior methods treat these as disjoint problems requiring separate pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework that enables interactive, local, recolorable stylization of NeRFs in a single pass (~5 min total, previews in ~20 s), addressing a practical gap that limited prior methods to either global edits or slow, non-interactive workflows. The geometry-aware losses (depth-guided TV and depth-discontinuity alignment) are a useful recipe for preserving fine 3D structure during stylization that could transfer to other NeRF editing tasks. However, the approach is constrained to post-hoc palette modification for stylized regions, relies on a separate distillation step that adds latency, and inherits NeRF's known weakness of trading geometric fidelity for visual quality—limiting edit quality on complex real-world scenes. Whether the point-per-ray formulation can be extended to handle topology changes or material-level edits remains open.
