## Problem

Prior work treats long-term action forecasting and 3D human pose forecasting as separate tasks, with pose forecasting requiring paired 3D ground-truth data that is prohibitively expensive to capture for complex multi-step behaviors (e.g., cooking, assembly). No existing method jointly predicts future action labels and their 3D pose realizations from readily available 2D video, and no dataset provides both 3D pose and action annotations for complex action sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240323/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB frames from which 2D poses are extracted by a frozen OpenPose estimator (9 upper-body joints, centered at the neck), along with one-hot action and object labels. It autoregressively outputs a sequence of M future action labels (M=10 for MPII Cooking II, M=5 for IKEA-ASM) and corresponding 3D characteristic poses as J×3 joint coordinate sets. At each autoregressive step, the 2D projection of the previously predicted 3D pose and the predicted action label are fed back as input to the next prediction, enabling long-horizon sequence generation.

## Method

The architecture encodes 2D poses (via a linear projection and three residual blocks), action labels, and object labels (via MLPs) into a shared latent code z, from which parallel MLP decoders produce the next action class and 3D characteristic pose. Training uses three losses: cross-entropy on action labels, a differentiable 2D projection loss (projecting predicted 3D joints into the image plane with fixed camera parameters and matching ground-truth 2D poses via MSE), and a Wasserstein GAN adversarial loss where a critic network—conditioned on kinematic statistics (bone lengths and joint angles via Ψ = BᵀB)—distinguishes predicted poses from a database of ~800K uncorrelated 3D poses from Human3.6M, AMASS, and GRAB. The key design choice is predicting characteristic poses (the most distinctive moment of each action) rather than arbitrary time-point poses, which provides semantically meaningful intermediate representations that stabilize autoregressive prediction over long sequences. This eliminates the need for any 3D-to-2D correspondence in training data, requiring only 2D action video and an unrelated 3D pose database.

## Results

On MPII Cooking II, the method achieves 50 px 2D MPJPE, 0.55 3D quality, and 29% top-1 / 51% top-3 action accuracy, outperforming all individual-task baselines on both pose and action metrics. On IKEA-ASM, it reaches 40 px 2D MPJPE, 0.31 3D quality, and 29% top-1 / 50% top-3 action accuracy. The joint approach beats the best 3D pose forecasting baseline (RepNet+DLow: 72 px / 45 px) by 22 px and 5 px on 2D MPJPE respectively, and surpasses the best action anticipation baseline (FUTR: 27% / 20% top-1) by 2 and 9 percentage points on the two datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four 3D pose forecasting baselines (DLow, GSPS, STARS, EqMotion) on 2D MPJPE by 18–22 px on MPII Cooking II and 5–15 px on IKEA-ASM, while matching DLow's 3D quality score (0.31) on IKEA-ASM and trailing it on MPII (0.55 vs. 0.72). Against the two action anticipation baselines (AVT and FUTR), the joint approach achieves the highest top-1 and top-3 action accuracy on both datasets (29%/51% on MPII vs. FUTR's 27%/49%; 29%/50% on IKEA vs. AVT's 23%/47%). Compared to the only prior joint 2D pose-and-action method (Zhu et al. [95]), the proposed 3D formulation improves 2D MPJPE by 5 px (MPII) and 4 px (IKEA) while also boosting top-1 action accuracy by 2 and 7 percentage points respectively. The key finding is that joint 3D pose and action prediction is complementary: each task benefits from the other, and the characteristic pose representation (vs. middle or random poses) further improves both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diller et al.'s (CVPR 2022) characteristic pose forecasting framework by coupling it with action label prediction in a unified autoregressive system, and adapts RepNet's weakly supervised adversarial 3D pose estimation (including its kinematic statistics matrix Ψ) as a 3D regularization loss. It departs from prior 3D pose forecasting (RNN, GAN, VAE, diffusion methods) which all require paired 3D ground-truth sequences, and from prior action anticipation (AVT, FUTR) which predicts only discrete labels without spatial grounding. The key conceptual departure is that 2D video supervision and an uncorrelated 3D pose database can jointly constrain predictions without any correspondence between the two data sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that long-term 3D human behavior forecasting is feasible without expensive mocap or 3D-annotated action sequences, opening the door to deployment in settings where only RGB cameras are available (kitchens, assembly lines, monitoring). The complementary nature of joint action-pose prediction—where each task improves the other—is a useful design principle for multi-modal forecasting. Limitations include the restriction to 9 upper-body joints, reliance on a separate frozen 2D pose estimator rather than end-to-end learning, and the absence of fine-grained hand or body-surface modeling needed for contact-rich interactions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-body prediction, multi-person scenes, or end-to-end formulations that exploit richer visual signals.
