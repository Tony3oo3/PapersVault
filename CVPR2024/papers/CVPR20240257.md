## Problem

2D diffusion-based texture generation methods suffer from self-occlusion artifacts in multi-view approaches and the combinatorial complexity of UV mapping, while point cloud-based methods are constrained by GPU memory to sparse sampling that produces rough, low-detail textures. TexOct addresses both limitations by enabling dense point cloud processing through an efficient octree representation, allowing high-quality texture synthesis directly in 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240257/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh as geometric input, from which 100K surface points are sampled and quantized into an octree of depth 12; optionally, a text prompt or single-view reference image is encoded via CLIP to condition generation. The output is a colored point cloud (recovered via 'reverse octree') that is projected back onto the original mesh to produce a textured mesh. The octree depth of 12 and 100K-point sampling were selected as a trade-off between reconstruction fidelity, GPU memory cost, and generalization performance.

## Method

TexOct represents the dense point cloud as a hierarchical octree (depth 12) and applies a DDPM denoising process directly on octree nodes using a U-Net with ResNet blocks built from O-CNN octree convolution operators. The U-Net operates across four stages at tree depths 12, 11, 10, and 9 to capture multi-scale texture information with global consistency. For conditional generation, an octree-based multi-head cross-attention module (adapted from OctFormer) is inserted after each ResNet block (except the first) to inject CLIP text/image embeddings. Training follows the standard DDPM objective but predicts clean signals rather than noise for stability, using AdamW at lr=1e-4 over 2000 epochs with batch size 128. At inference, pure Gaussian noise is assigned to each point, amplified at t=1000, and iteratively denoised over finite DDPM steps before the octree is reversed to recover the final colored point cloud.

## Results

On ShapeNet (chair, car, table, bench), TexOct achieves an average FID of 14.75 and KID of 0.13 (×10²) in unconditional generation, outperforming the strongest baseline Point-UV (2-Stage) by 2.56 FID and 0.17 KID. Under the same 20-view rendering protocol as Text2Tex, TexOct achieves FID 38.11 and KID 0.14, surpassing Text2Tex (FID 53.22, KID 0.79) by 15.11 FID and 0.65 KID. In the user study, TexOct is preferred over Point-UV in 80.7% of pairwise comparisons across 100 render pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexOct outperforms all four baselines across every category and metric. Against Point-UV (2-Stage) [43], the closest competitor, TexOct improves average FID from 17.31 to 14.75 and KID from 0.30 to 0.13, with the largest gap on the Table category (FID 7.92 vs. 9.63). Against Point-UV (1-Stage) [43], which uses only 4096 points, TexOct's advantage is dramatic (FID 14.75 vs. 57.69), demonstrating the benefit of dense point clouds via octree representation. Against Text2Tex [8] under the matched 20-view protocol, TexOct reduces FID by 15.11 (38.11 vs. 53.22) and KID by 0.65 (0.14 vs. 0.79), largely because direct 3D-space generation avoids self-occlusion artifacts. Against Texture Fields [24] and Texturify [32], TexOct achieves substantially lower FID (14.75 vs. 70.07 and 28.80/73.16 respectively), though these older methods were not designed for the same evaluation protocol. In the user study, TexOct is preferred over Point-UV in 80.7% of pairwise comparisons, with the highest preference on Chair (87.8%) and Bench (86.2%) and the lowest on Car (66.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TexOct sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: octree-based 3D representation (O-CNN [37], OctFormer [36]) and denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM [17]). It departs from multi-view texture synthesis methods (Text2Tex, TexFusion) that are fundamentally limited by self-occlusion, and from UV-space approaches that struggle with the non-uniqueness of UV mappings. The key conceptual departure from prior point-based texture methods (notably Point-UV [43]) is the use of octree structure to efficiently encode dense point clouds (100K points) within GPU memory, enabling a single-stage diffusion process rather than a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By moving texture generation entirely into 3D space via octree-encoded point clouds, TexOct eliminates the self-occlusion artifacts that plague multi-view methods and the resolution bottleneck of sparse point sampling. However, the evaluation is limited to four ShapeNet categories and a single dataset, and the authors acknowledge that incorporating additional geometric cues (normals, curvature, Laplace-Beltrami operator) could further improve results. Open questions include whether the octree-based diffusion approach scales to more complex or open surfaces, and whether the depth-12 sweet spot generalizes across object categories and resolutions.
