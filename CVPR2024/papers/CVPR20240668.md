## Problem

Existing 3D shape generation methods (neural fields, point clouds, voxels) require post-hoc iso-surfacing to produce meshes, yielding dense, over-tessellated outputs with oversmoothing and bumpy artifacts that fail to capture the compact, sharp-edged triangulation patterns characteristic of artist-created meshes. Direct autoregressive mesh generation remains underexplored, with prior work like PolyGen relying on separate vertex and face generators that are vulnerable to error accumulation.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a learned start token and autoregressively produces a sequence of codebook indices (D=6 per face) until a stop token is emitted; for shape completion, a partial mesh is encoded and the transformer continues the sequence. The output is a sequence of triangles whose vertex coordinates are discretized into 128 classes per axis (128³ grid), initially forming a 'triangle soup' that requires a simple vertex-merging post-processing step (e.g., MeshLab) to yield a watertight mesh. Training meshes are constrained to fewer than 800 faces post-decimation to fit within the 4608-token context window.

## Method

MeshGPT uses a two-stage pipeline. First, a graph convolutional encoder (stack of SAGEConv layers) processes mesh faces as nodes with neighborhood edges, extracting geometrically enriched features that incorporate face normals, edge angles, area, and positional encodings; these features are quantized via residual vector quantization (RQ, depth 2) into D=6 codebook embeddings per face, with quantization performed per-vertex (D/3 codes per vertex) rather than per-face to produce sequences that are easier for the transformer to learn. A 1D ResNet34 decoder then reconstructs face coordinates from the quantized embeddings, trained with cross-entropy over discretized coordinates and a commitment loss. Second, a GPT-2-medium decoder-only transformer (context window 4608) is trained to predict the next codebook index autoregressively, using learned positional encodings for both face position and intra-face embedding index. The key architectural insight is that graph-convolutional neighborhood aggregation plus per-vertex RQ compression yields geometrically rich yet short token sequences, enabling a single unified transformer to generate compact meshes with sharp edges in one pass.

## Results

On ShapeNet (Chair, Table, Bench, Lamp categories), MeshGPT achieves the best COV (43.28–55.23), lowest MMD (1.44–3.94×10⁻³), and lowest FID (6.24–19.91) across all four categories, outperforming all baselines. The method also produces the most compact meshes, with average face counts of 187–291 versus 430–27,537 for baselines. The paper reports an average 9% relative increase in shape coverage and a 30-point improvement in FID over the strongest baseline per category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshGPT outperforms all four baselines on COV, MMD, FID, KID, and compactness across all four categories. Against PolyGen (the closest autoregressive baseline), MeshGPT improves FID by 32–43 points (e.g., 18.46 vs. 61.10 on Chair) and COV by 2–6 points, while producing meshes with roughly half the face count. Against GET3D (neural field + marching tetrahedra), MeshGPT achieves dramatically lower FID (6.24 vs. 93.93 on Table) and far fewer faces (187 vs. 27,537), though GET3D retains a slight edge on 1-NNA. Against BSPNet and AtlasNet, the gap is even larger: MeshGPT's FID is 20–180 points lower, and its meshes are 5–20× more compact. The one metric where MeshGPT trails is 1-NNA (65–75 vs. 83–96 for baselines), which the authors note is expected since 50% is the optimal value for unconditional generation and lower values indicate greater diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshGPT adapts the decoder-only transformer paradigm from NLP (GPT-2) to 3D mesh generation, treating a mesh as an autoregressive sequence of geometric tokens. It builds most directly on PolyGen, which also generates meshes autoregressively, but departs by replacing PolyGen's separate vertex-then-face generators with a single transformer over learned per-triangle embeddings produced by a graph convolutional encoder and residual vector quantization. In contrast to neural field methods (GET3D, DiffusionSDF) that require post-hoc iso-surfacing to extract meshes, MeshGPT generates triangle faces directly, and in contrast to voxel or point-cloud generators, it outputs a representation that is natively compatible with rendering pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating compact, artist-like triangulations directly rather than dense iso-surfaced meshes, MeshGPT addresses a practical gap between generative 3D models and downstream graphics applications. The 30-point FID improvement and 9% coverage gain over prior methods, combined with user-study preference, suggest the sequence-of-learned-tokens formulation is a viable and potentially extensible paradigm for mesh generation. Key open questions include scaling to larger transformer backbones (the authors are limited to GPT-2-medium), extending to scene-level or conditional generation, and reducing the 30–90 second sampling time that currently limits practical use.
