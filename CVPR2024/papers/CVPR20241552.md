## Problem

Appearance-based person re-identification is sensitive to background and environmental variations, and existing multi-modal approaches that add body shape or gait encode these modalities in separate branches, incurring extra parameters and computational cost while failing to establish a tight pixel-level connection with appearance. This paper asks whether 3-D body shape can be leveraged as implicit pixel-level supervision—rather than as an additional input—to enhance identity feature extraction at zero inference overhead.

## Input / Output

For frame-based re-identification, the input is a single RGB image resized to 256×128; for video-based, it is a sequence of frames (8 frames sampled per clip for MARS, all available frames for LS-VID). The output is a single identity feature vector obtained by global average pooling over the encoder's last convolutional feature map, which is then matched against gallery features via cosine similarity. During inference the shape extractor is entirely discarded, so the model's parameter count and FLOPs are identical to the plain ResNet-50 or PSTA backbone.

## Method

SEAS attaches a trainable deconvolutional extractor (a stack of UpConv blocks) to the identity encoder's last convolutional feature map, upscaling it to pixel resolution and decoding per-pixel body-shape features. These decoded features are supervised with smooth L1 loss against pixel-level implicit shape features from a frozen pretrained PIFu encoder, forcing the identity feature map to encode shape-aware information at every pixel. For video-based re-identification, an additional calibration loss projects per-pixel features from multiple frames onto a shared SMPL body model via SPIN-fitted shapes, then minimizes the variance of features at the same 3-D body vertex across frames to enforce temporal consistency. The total training objective combines the standard re-identification loss (triplet + cross-entropy), the shape reconstruction loss (λ₁=1), and the cross-frame calibration loss (λ₂=0.001 for video). Because the extractor is removed at inference, the approach adds no parameters or FLOPs to the deployed model while yielding a 1.4% rank-1 error reduction on frame-based and 2.5% on video-based benchmarks.

## Results

On frame-based person re-identification, SEAS with ResNet-50 achieves 98.6% Rank-1 and 98.9% mAP on Market1501, and 91.7% Rank-1 and 92.8% mAP on MSMT17, outperforming the previous best (SOLIDER at 96.9% Rank-1 on Market1501 and 90.7% Rank-1 on MSMT17). On video-based re-identification, SEAS with PSTA reaches 95.1% Rank-1 and 96.7% mAP on MARS, and 90.5% Rank-1 and 93.4% mAP on LS-VID, surpassing the prior best (CAViT at 90.8% Rank-1 on MARS and 89.2% Rank-1 on LS-VID). The method reduces rank-1 errors by 1.4% (frame-based) and 2.5% (video-based) on average, with no additional inference cost since the shape extractor is discarded at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAS (ResNet-50) outperforms all 18 frame-based baselines on both Market1501 and MSMT17 across Rank-1 and mAP. The largest gap is on MSMT17 mAP, where SEAS reaches 92.8% versus SOLIDER's 77.1% (+15.7 points), suggesting shape-aligned supervision is particularly effective under heavy occlusion. On Market1501, SEAS improves Rank-1 by 1.7 points over SOLIDER (98.6% vs. 96.9%) and mAP by 5.0 points (98.9% vs. 93.9%). The two explicit 3-D shape methods (ASSP at 95.0% and 3DInvarReID at 95.1% Rank-1 on Market1501) are substantially outperformed, confirming that shape-as-supervision yields stronger appearance-shape integration than shape-as-input. For video-based re-identification, SEAS (PSTA) beats the strongest baseline CAViT by 4.3 Rank-1 points on MARS (95.1% vs. 90.8%) and 1.3 points on LS-VID (90.5% vs. 89.2%), while also improving mAP by 9.5 and 14.2 points respectively. SEAS generalizes to other encoders: adding it to BoT lifts Market1501 mAP from 85.9% to 97.5%, and to STMN lifts MARS mAP from 84.5% to 94.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior multi-modality ReID methods (ASSP, 3DInvarReID) that encode 3-D body shape in a separate branch alongside appearance, instead repurposing implicit body shape representations (PIFu, SMPL) purely as a training-time supervision signal. The identity encoder architecture is standard (ResNet-50 for frames, PSTA for video), and the novel contribution is the shape-aligned supervision loss that forces the encoder's feature map to implicitly encode pixel-level body geometry. The video-based extension introduces a cross-frame calibration loss using SMPL vertex correspondence, building on the implicit representation idea from PIFu but applying it to enforce temporal consistency rather than single-frame reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that shape-aware features can be obtained at zero additional inference cost by using shape as supervision rather than input, and the technique generalizes cleanly to multiple existing encoders (BoT, LDS, STMN, BiCnet-TKS) without architectural modification. The limitation that performance depends on the quality of the pretrained shape encoder (PIFu/SPIN) means the approach inherits their failure modes on low-quality or heavily occluded ReID imagery. An open question is whether this supervision paradigm—using a frozen pretrained model's pixel-level output to shape an encoder's internal representations—transfers to other recognition tasks beyond person ReID, such as fine-grained object or animal identification where shape is similarly informative.
