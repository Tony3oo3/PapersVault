## Problem

Visual Instruction Tuning has demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization in NLP but remains unexplored for vision emotion understanding, where no benchmarks or guidelines exist for constructing emotion-specific instruction data. Existing large multimodal models (e.g., GPT-4) produce incorrect emotion predictions when queried directly, and the scarcity of annotated emotion instruction data has prevented the community from leveraging instruction-tuned LLMs for affective tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242120/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image paired with a natural-language emotion instruction (one of three types: Categorical, Conversation, or Reasoning) and produces a structured response containing a predicted emotion class label, a free-text explanation of the visual cues driving the decision, and optionally a humorous caption. Instructions are instance-wise and tailored to each image's multi-level attributes (brightness, colorfulness, scene type, object class, facial expression, human action), rather than using a single fixed template across all images.

## Method

EmoVIT builds on InstructBLIP and fine-tunes only the Instruction-aware Q-Former module while keeping the image encoder and LLM frozen. The key data-generation pipeline uses GPT-4 in a few-shot in-context learning setup to produce instance-wise emotion instruction data in three progressive types—Categorical (basic classification), Conversation (multi-turn dialogue about emotion attributes), and Reasoning (complex multi-step affective reasoning)—from 51,200 EmoSet images annotated with multi-level visual attributes. During training, emotion instruction tokens and learnable queries are jointly processed through self-attention layers in the Q-Former, enabling instruction-conditioned visual feature extraction that aligns image content with the LLM's instruction-following requirements. The generated data is model-agnostic and also improves LLaVA when used for fine-tuning, demonstrating transferability beyond the InstructBLIP backbone.

## Results

On the primary EmoSet test set, EmoVIT achieves 83.36% accuracy, a 41.16-point improvement over the InstructBLIP baseline (42.20%) and a 36.57-point gain over BLIP-2 (46.79%). Across all eight held-out emotion datasets, EmoVIT attains the best accuracy in every case, with particularly large margins on FI (68.09% vs. 57.72% for BLIP-2) and Emotion6 (57.81% vs. 50.00%). On the UnbiasedEmo robustness benchmark, EmoVIT reaches 74.72% accuracy, surpassing the strongest supervised baseline (Curriculum Learning at 74.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoVIT outperforms all four Visual Instruction Tuning baselines (Flamingo, LLaVA, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP) on every one of the eight held-out emotion datasets. The most striking margin is on EmoSet, where EmoVIT (83.36%) nearly doubles the InstructBLIP baseline (42.20%) and exceeds BLIP-2 (46.79%) by over 36 points, reflecting the benefit of emotion-specific instruction data. On FI and Emotion6, EmoVIT leads BLIP-2 by 10.37 and 7.81 points respectively, while Flamingo trails all other methods by a wide margin on every dataset (e.g., 29.59% on EmoSet vs. 83.36%). On the UnbiasedEmo robustness benchmark, EmoVIT (74.72%) edges out the best supervised baseline, Curriculum Learning from Panda et al. (74.27%), and surpasses Direct Learning, Self-Directed Learning, and Joint Learning (all 71.64–72.45%) by 2.3–3.1 points, confirming that the instruction-tuning approach generalizes well to subtle, bias-prone emotion categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Instruction Tuning paradigm (established by LLaVA, BLIP-2, and InstructBLIP) into the emotion recognition domain, building directly on InstructBLIP's Instruction-aware Q-Former architecture. It departs from prior template-based instruction data generation (used uniformly across images in LLaVA and InstructBLIP) by introducing an instance-wise, GPT-4-assisted pipeline that produces diverse Categorical, Conversation, and Reasoning instructions tailored to each image's emotion attributes. The work also draws on EmoSet's multi-level emotion attribute taxonomy (low/mid/high-level visual cues) to ground the generated instructions in perceptually meaningful features, bridging the gap between general-purpose VIT models and the nuanced demands of affective understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of Emotion Visual Instruction Tuning, providing both a data generation pipeline and a benchmark that the field lacked. The finding that less than 50% of EmoSet images, processed through GPT-4-generated instructions, outperforms models trained on full datasets suggests that instruction quality and diversity may matter more than raw data volume for emotion tasks. Open questions include whether the GPT-4-generated instructions introduce systematic biases, whether the approach generalizes to fine-grained or continuous emotion spaces beyond 8-class categorization, and whether the large EmoSet gap (partly due to held-in pretraining on the same dataset) holds under stricter cross-dataset evaluation. The paper also acknowledges the broader criticism that instruction tuning may capture surface patterns rather than deep understanding, a concern that remains unresolved here.
