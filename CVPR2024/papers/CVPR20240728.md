## Problem

Existing controllable T2I diffusion methods like ControlNet require training a separate auxiliary module for every combination of spatial condition type, model architecture, and checkpoint, making them costly and poorly scalable. Training-based methods also tend to prioritize the spatial condition over the text prompt and cannot handle conditions that are difficult to infer from images (e.g., meshes, point clouds). Training-free alternatives such as PnP suffer from appearance leakage, where visual details from the guidance image bleed into the output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240728/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a guidance image of arbitrary modality (depth map, Canny/HED edge, sketch, normal map, human pose, segmentation mask, natural image, 2D point-cloud or mesh projection, Blender/AutoCAD viewport, etc.) and produces a single generated image that respects both the text semantics and the spatial layout of the guidance image. It operates on any Stable Diffusion version (1.5, 2.1, XL 1.0) and customized checkpoints (DreamBooth, LoRA) without retraining. The guidance image is consumed via DDIM inversion to extract intermediate diffusion features, and a cross-attention-derived mask can restrict structure control to the foreground.

## Method

FreeControl's key insight is that the leading principal components of self-attention key features in the U-Net decoder form a modality-invariant semantic structure basis. In the analysis stage, it generates N_s seed images for the target concept, runs DDIM sampling to collect time-dependent features, and performs PCA to obtain a linear basis B_t. In the synthesis stage, it DDIM-inverts the guidance image, projects its features onto B_t to obtain semantic coordinates S_t^g, then applies two guidance terms during denoising: a structure guidance that attracts the current features' coordinates S_t toward S_t^g (with a backward term to suppress spurious background structure), and an appearance guidance that matches weighted spatial means of features between the controlled image and an uncontrolled sibling generated from the same seed. This PCA-based disentanglement of structure from appearance, combined with the sibling-image appearance transfer, avoids the appearance leakage problem of prior training-free methods while preserving strong text conditioning.

## Results

On the ImageNet-R-TI2I benchmark (30 images, 10 categories), FreeControl achieves the highest CLIP scores across all five condition types (0.321–0.322), surpassing the best training-based baseline ControlNet (0.291–0.314) and all training-free methods. It reports competitive Self-Similarity distances (0.078–0.090) versus training-based methods (0.040–0.079 for ControlNet) while substantially outperforming training-free baselines (PnP: 0.074–0.158; P2P: 0.078–0.194). FreeControl also attains the highest LPIPS scores (0.561–0.724), indicating richer appearance details than any baseline. Inference takes 176 s per image (A6000), 4.2× slower than PnP but 7.4× faster than Pix2Pix-zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-based methods, FreeControl trades a small amount of structure fidelity for substantially better text alignment: on Canny, ControlNet achieves Self-Sim 0.042 vs. FreeControl's 0.080, but FreeControl's CLIP score (0.322) exceeds ControlNet's (0.300) by ~7%, and its LPIPS (0.724 vs. 0.665) indicates richer appearance. Uni-ControlNet and T2I-Adapter show similar patterns—lower Self-Sim but weaker CLIP and LPIPS. Against training-free baselines, FreeControl dominates on all three metrics: PnP achieves Self-Sim 0.074 on Canny (slightly better than FreeControl's 0.080) but its CLIP (0.282) and LPIPS (0.417) are far below FreeControl's (0.322, 0.724); P2P is even weaker (CLIP 0.253, LPIPS 0.298 on Canny). SDEdit variants suffer from poor structure preservation (Self-Sim 0.108–0.165) and low LPIPS. In runtime, FreeControl (176 s) is 4.2× slower than PnP (42 s) and 1.8× slower than P2P (99 s) but 7.4× faster than Pix2Pix-zero (1302 s); with basis reuse, the gap to PnP narrows to 1.1×.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeControl extends the line of training-free attention manipulation for controllable diffusion (PnP, P2P, SDEdit) by replacing direct feature injection with a PCA-based semantic basis extracted from self-attention keys, which disentangles structure from appearance. It draws on DDIM inversion for feature extraction and on DSG's weighted spatial mean formulation for appearance guidance, but departs from both by operating in a learned low-dimensional subspace rather than raw feature space. Conceptually, it positions itself against the training-based paradigm (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, Uni-ControlNet) by arguing that the feature space of a pretrained model already encodes sufficient structural information to enable zero-shot spatial control without any auxiliary module training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a single training-free pipeline replaces the need to train and maintain separate ControlNet modules for every condition type × architecture × checkpoint combination, which is increasingly costly as the ecosystem of custom Stable Diffusion checkpoints (DreamBooth, LoRA) grows. The ability to accept conditions where training pairs are infeasible (point clouds, meshes, Blender/AutoCAD viewports) opens new use cases in design preview and sim-to-real transfer. The main limitation is inference cost (4.2× PnP, 1.8× P2P) due to DDIM inversion and dual sampling, and the method's reliance on U-Net self-attention features raises open questions about generalization to non-U-Net architectures (e.g., DiT-based models).
