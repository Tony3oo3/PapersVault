## Problem

Existing zero-shot image captioning methods face a fundamental trade-off: training-free approaches (e.g., ZeroCap, ConZIC) produce hallucinated content not present in the image, while text-only-training methods (e.g., DeCap, MAGIC) overfit to the training corpus and lose the world knowledge encoded in the pre-trained LM. No prior method simultaneously suppresses hallucination and preserves generalization to out-of-domain images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and an external textual memory corpus (e.g., CC3M captions or a dataset's training captions) as input, and produces one natural-language caption as output. The memory is preprocessed offline into CLIP text embeddings for fast retrieval (~0.05 s on GPU for CC3M). The framework operates in two modes: MeaCap_TF (fully training-free, using only the frozen CBART) and MeaCap_ToT (CBART fine-tuned on the corpus text with AdamW), both sharing the same memory-augmented retrieval pipeline.

## Method

MeaCap introduces a retrieve-then-filter module that extracts key visual concepts from an external textual memory: Top-N descriptions are retrieved via CLIP image-text similarity, parsed into subject-predicate-object triplets by TextGraphParser, merged into clusters via Sentence-BERT cosine similarity (threshold τ), filtered by concept-cluster frequency (>0.5), and finally reduced to one representative concept per cluster by CLIP image-concept similarity. These ordered key concepts are fed into CBART, a lexically constrained keywords-to-sentence LM that iteratively refines a sentence via copy/replacement/insertion actions. The key innovation is a memory-augmented visual-related fusion score that replaces CBART's standard LM sampling during replacement and insertion: p^fusion = α·p^lm + β·p^ITs + γ·p^TTs, where p^ITs is CLIP cross-modal image-sentence similarity and p^TTs is Sentence-BERT in-modal similarity between the candidate sentence and the retrieved memory descriptions. This dual-similarity scoring grounds generation in both the image and the retrieved textual context, reducing hallucination while retaining world knowledge.

## Results

On the zero-shot MSCOCO Karpathy-test split, MeaCap_TF (training-free) achieves B@4=7.1, METEOR=16.6, CIDEr=42.5, SPICE=11.8, and BLIP2-Score=0.81, substantially outperforming all prior training-free baselines (e.g., ConZIC: B@4=1.3, CIDEr=13.3). The text-only-training variant MeaCap_ToT further improves to B@4=9.0, CIDEr=48.3, SPICE=12.7 on MSCOCO and achieves an overall NoCaps CIDEr of 45.1, surpassing DeCap (39.7) by 5.4 points. On NoCaps validation, MeaCap_TF reaches an overall CIDEr of 40.2 versus 17.5 for ConZIC and 39.7 for DeCap, demonstrating strong generalization to novel objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-free baselines (ZeroCap, Tewel et al., ConZIC), MeaCap_TF wins by large margins on all reference-based metrics (e.g., CIDEr 42.5 vs. 14.6/17.2/13.3) and on NoCaps overall CIDEr (40.2 vs. 16.6/16.9/17.5), but scores lower on CLIP-Score (0.84 vs. 0.87/0.74/1.00), which the authors attribute to CLIP's insensitivity to hallucination. Compared to the retrieval-only baseline CLIPRe, MeaCap_TF improves CIDEr from 25.6 to 42.5 and NoCaps overall from 28.2 to 40.2, showing that retrieve-then-filter concept extraction is far more effective than raw caption retrieval. Against the strongest text-only-training baseline DeCap, MeaCap_TF is competitive (CIDEr 42.5 vs. 42.1; NoCaps 40.2 vs. 39.7) without any LM fine-tuning, while MeaCap_ToT clearly surpasses DeCap on every reference-based metric (CIDEr 48.3 vs. 42.1; NoCaps 45.1 vs. 39.7). In the in-domain setting (Table 2), MeaCap_ToT achieves CIDEr 84.8 on MSCOCO and 50.2 on Flickr30K, outperforming MAGIC (49.3/20.4) and ZEROGEN (55.4/26.4) by wide margins. In cross-domain transfer (Table 3), MeaCap_ToT leads on CIDEr in both directions (MSCOCO→Flickr30K: 40.3 vs. 17.5 for MAGIC; Flickr30K→MSCOCO: 51.7 vs. 18.3 for MAGIC), confirming the memory-augmented design's robustness to domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeaCap sits at the intersection of two established zero-shot image captioning paradigms: training-free methods (ZeroCap, ConZIC) that use CLIP to guide a frozen LM, and text-only-training methods (MAGIC, DeCap, ViECap) that fine-tune a LM on captioning corpus text. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the captioning corpus as an external retrieval memory rather than a training signal, combined with a retrieve-then-filter pipeline (TextGraphParser triplets → Sentence-BERT clustering → CLIP-based concept selection) that extracts key concepts to condition a keywords-to-sentence LM (CBART). The memory-augmented visual-related fusion score, which blends CLIP cross-modal similarity with Sentence-BERT text-text in-modal similarity against retrieved memory, extends the standard CLIP-guided scoring used by prior training-free methods with an additional in-modal consistency signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent and complementary failure modes of zero-shot IC—hallucination in training-free methods and world-knowledge forgetting in text-only-training methods—within a single unified framework that is plug-and-play across LM backends (demonstrated with both CBART and CLIP-invert decoders). The observation that CLIPScore is insensitive to hallucinated content (Fig. 1a) and the introduction of BLIP2Score as a complementary metric are practically useful for the community. Open questions include how the approach scales to much larger or more diverse memory corpora, whether the TextGraphParser-based concept extraction generalizes to non-English or highly compositional scenes, and whether the framework benefits from newer LLM-based captioners beyond the CBART/InvLM tested here.
