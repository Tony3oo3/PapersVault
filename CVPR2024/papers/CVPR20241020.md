## Problem

Training image super-resolution on real-world unpaired data is difficult because existing methods must synthesize pseudo LR images to create training pairs, and the large image space makes it hard to balance the realness and diversity of these synthesized images. This paper circumvents the image-generation bottleneck by instead associating unpaired HR and LR images in a compact latent code space learned through coupled dictionaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241020/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes unpaired HR images (256×256 patches) and LR images (64×64 patches) at ×4 scale, with no content alignment required between them. During inference, only a single LR image is fed through the 18-layer LR encoder and HR decoder pipeline (129G MACs on 128×128 input), producing a ×4 upscaled HR image. The LR image is first decomposed into spatial index codes over the LR content and degradation dictionaries, and the corresponding HR dictionary entries are retrieved for reconstruction.

## Method

DictSR constructs three coupled dictionaries — an HR content dictionary D^HR, an LR content dictionary D^LR, and a degradation dictionary D_deg — and learns them jointly within an autoencoder framework with three branches. The key coupling mechanism uses downscaled HR images as a bridge: the same spatial index i retrieved from D^LR for a downsampled HR embedding simultaneously indexes D^HR, tying the two content dictionaries together without requiring paired data. The degradation dictionary operates hierarchically on top of D^LR, where each content entry has K=32 sub-entries capturing degradation variations around that content. Discretization uses a VQ-style lookup with a linear projection to a 4-dimensional latent space and straight-through estimator for end-to-end optimization. The overall loss combines L1 and VGG perceptual reconstruction losses across all three branches with discretization losses, and the resulting 18-layer network (vs. 350+ layers in GAN-based competitors) achieves state-of-the-art LPIPS on both AIM-RWSR and NTIRE-RWSR benchmarks.

## Results

On AIM-RWSR (×4 SR), DictSR achieves the best LPIPS (0.259) among all unpaired methods and competitive PSNR (22.46) and SSIM (0.629) with paired methods, while using only 18 layers and 129G MACs versus 350 layers and 291G MACs for most baselines. On NTIRE-RWSR, DictSR attains the best unpaired performance on all three metrics: LPIPS 0.204, PSNR 25.90, and SSIM 0.711, surpassing the previous best unpaired method DeFlow (LPIPS 0.218, PSNR 25.87, SSIM 0.710). The method also outperforms most paired SR methods (e.g., BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN) on NTIRE-RWSR in LPIPS and SSIM despite its much shallower architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DictSR outperforms all unpaired baselines on AIM-RWSR across all three metrics, with the largest gap over PCR-ESRGAN (LPIPS 0.259 vs. 0.321) and DeFlow (LPIPS 0.259 vs. 0.349), while matching or exceeding the best paired methods (BSRGAN: PSNR 22.46 vs. 22.47; Real-ESRGAN: SSIM 0.629 vs. 0.622). On NTIRE-RWSR, DictSR beats the strongest unpaired competitor DeFlow on all metrics (LPIPS 0.204 vs. 0.218, PSNR 25.90 vs. 25.87, SSIM 0.711 vs. 0.710) and surpasses paired methods BSRGAN and Real-ESRGAN in LPIPS (0.204 vs. 0.265/0.251) and SSIM (0.711 vs. 0.669/0.687), though IKC retains a PSNR advantage (26.50 vs. 25.90). The method achieves these results with only 18 layers and 129G MACs, roughly half the compute of the 350-layer/291G baselines. Among unpaired methods, the GAN-based approaches (CycleGAN, CinCGAN, FSSR, DASR) show substantially worse perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.336–0.476) and lower SSIM, confirming the paper's argument that pseudo-LR synthesis in image space is inferior to dictionary-based coupling in a compact latent space.

## Relation to Prior Work

DictSR extends the classical coupled dictionary framework of Yang et al. (2012) from paired sparse coding to the unpaired setting, and adapts the VQ-GAN discretization machinery (Esser et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2021) with a straight-through estimator to learn dictionary entries end-to-end. The key conceptual departure from prior unpaired SR work (CinCGAN, DeFlow, PCR-ESRGAN, diffusion-based methods) is that instead of synthesizing pseudo LR images in the high-dimensional image space—which suffers from the diversity-fidelity tradeoff—DictSR learns the LR-to-HR mapping entirely in a compact 4-dimensional latent code space spanned by coupled dictionaries. This also builds on Maeda's deep dictionary for SR (2022), which demonstrated the viability of learnable dictionaries in CNN pipelines but was restricted to paired data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency: an 18-layer network with 129G MACs matches or beats 350-layer, 291G-MAC competitors, suggesting that the LR-to-HR mapping can be captured in dictionary space without deep network capacity. This challenges the implicit assumption in unpaired SR that large generative models (GANs, diffusion) are necessary to bridge the LR-HR gap. Limitations include evaluation only on ×4 SR and two benchmark datasets, and the method's reliance on a fixed dictionary size (4096 entries) that may not scale to more complex degradation distributions. Open questions include whether the hierarchical content+degradation dictionary decomposition generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring) and to higher scale factors.
