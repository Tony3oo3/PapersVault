## Problem

Existing motion learning methods develop specialized architectures for optical flow or scene depth in isolation, ignoring the underlying connections between different motion tasks and their potential for unification. Additionally, photometric inconsistencies (shadows, illumination changes) introduce uncertainty in pixel-wise feature matching, degrading motion representation robustness. The paper asks whether a single prototypical paradigm can unify disparate motion learners while simultaneously mitigating matching uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For optical flow, the model takes a pair of RGB video frames and outputs a dense 2D displacement map; for depth, it takes a single RGB image and outputs a per-pixel depth map. Both tasks share the same backbone and are distinguished only by their adaptation heads, which iteratively refine predictions by estimating residuals over T=12 steps. The learned motion features are also transferable: freezing the backbone and fine-tuning only the task-specific head enables adaptation to downstream 2D and 3D tasks (video object detection, segmentation, 3D detection) without retraining the core architecture.

## Method

ProMotion is a prototypical Transformer with two core stages. The Feature Denoiser applies subspace denoising (reformulating self-attention as a weighted sum of linear projections via Tweedie's formula) followed by large-margin projection using ISTA with an orthogonal dictionary, mapping tokens into compact, denoised subspaces along orthogonal directions to enforce intra-class compactness and inter-class separability. The Prototypical Learner then clusters these denoised features into K=100 semantic prototypes via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport, iteratively updating prototype centers to capture object-level motion patterns rather than relying on noisy pixel-wise correspondences. A universal adaptation head with iterative residual refinement produces the final flow or depth prediction, and the same trained features transfer to downstream tasks by swapping in task-specific heads while freezing the motion backbone.

## Results

On the Sintel optical flow benchmark (full training on C+T+S+K+H), ProMotion achieves 1.04 AEPE on the Clean pass and 2.01 AEPE on the Final pass, reducing RAFT's error by 35.4% and 29.7% respectively. On KITTI, it achieves 4.30 Fl-all AEPE, a 15.7% reduction over RAFT (5.10). For monocular depth estimation, ProMotion attains 0.54 Abs Rel on Sintel and 0.054 Abs Rel on KITTI, outperforming the prior SOTA P3Depth by 16.9% and 10.0% in Abs Rel on those datasets respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMotion outperforms all 18 compared baselines on both Sintel and KITTI optical flow. Against the strongest recent Transformer-based methods, it reduces Sintel Clean AEPE by 0.16 over FlowFormer (1.20→1.04) and by 0.29 over MatchFlow (1.33→1.04), while on Sintel Final it improves by 0.11 over FlowFormer (2.12→2.01) and by 0.63 over MatchFlow (2.64→2.01). On KITTI Fl-all, ProMotion (4.30) beats FlowFormer (4.68†), MatchFlow (4.72), and RAFT (5.10) by 0.38, 0.42, and 0.80 respectively. For depth estimation, ProMotion (0.54 Abs Rel on Sintel) surpasses DPT-Hybrid (0.62), P3Depth (0.65), and AdaBins (0.73), with the largest margin over AdaBins at 26.0% relative reduction. On KITTI depth, it achieves 0.054 Abs Rel vs. AdaBins (0.058), DPT-Hybrid (0.059), and P3Depth (0.060), a 10.0% improvement over the prior best. The generalization setting (C+T only) also shows consistent gains: 0.91 AEPE on Sintel Clean vs. FlowFormer's 1.01 and MatchFlow's 1.14, confirming the architecture's robustness without task-specific training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProMotion departs from the dominant paradigm of task-specific motion architectures (separate flow models like FlowFormer/MatchFlow and depth models like DPT/AdaBins) by proposing a single prototypical Transformer framework that handles both optical flow and scene depth. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from denoising theory (Tweedie's formula, ISTA-based large-margin projection) and optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering) into a motion-learning context, framing motion as a set of semantic prototypes rather than pixel-wise correspondences. The paper does not build on a single prior method but synthesizes the Transformer backbone lineage (RAFT → FlowFormer → MatchFlow) with prototype-learning literature from few-shot and zero-shot classification, positioning itself as the first work to unify motion tasks through a prototypical lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified prototype-based paradigm offers a principled path toward a single model for multiple motion tasks, with demonstrated transferability to 2D and 3D downstream tasks (video detection, segmentation, 3D detection). However, the 'unification' is currently limited to just flow and depth, and the downstream task results are reported only in the figure caption without full experimental detail, leaving open whether the prototype representation truly generalizes to a broader set of motion-related tasks. The theoretical framing (Propositions 1 and 2) is stated without formal proof, and it remains unclear whether the denoising formulation provides genuine inductive bias beyond a reparameterization of standard attention. A key open question is whether adding more motion tasks (e.g., 3D point tracking, ego-motion) under the same prototype framework yields compounding benefits or requires task-specific modifications.
