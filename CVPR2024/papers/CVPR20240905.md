## Problem

Standard cost aggregation in learning-based MVS networks (e.g., 3D convolutions) combines adjacent pixel costs that may carry geometrically inconsistent depth cues due to local geometry variation, leading to suboptimal depth predictions. Existing remedies either rely on learned aggregation that does not guarantee geometric plausibility or refine the final depth in 2D image space, neither of which addresses inconsistency within the cost volume itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240905/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference RGB image plus N source images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and outputs a per-pixel depth map for the reference view that is subsequently fused into a 3D point cloud. Training uses 5 input images at 512×640 (DTU) or 9 images at 576×768 (BlendedMVS fine-tuning), while evaluation scales to 864×1152 (DTU), 960×1920 (Tanks & Temples), and 1152×1536 (ETH3D). A three-stage cascade samples depth at 48, 32, and 8 hypotheses per pixel with intervals of 4, 1, and 0.5 respectively.

## Method

GoMVS replaces each standard 3D convolution in the MVSNet 3D U-Net with a Geometrically Consistent Aggregation (GCA) operator built around a Geometrically Consistent Propagation (GCP) module. GCP first collects depth hypotheses and surface normals over a k×k spatial window, then computes an analytic depth ratio between the reference pixel and each adjacent pixel under a local planar assumption (using the surface normal to relate their 3D positions). Adjacent cost volumes are warped into the reference pixel's depth space by scaling depth hypotheses with this ratio, and costs are read out via linear interpolation at the mapped locations. The resulting k²M-channel propagated costs are aggregated with a 1×1×k convolution, matching the parameter count of a conventional k×k×k 3D conv. For surface normals, the paper ablates depth-derived, cost-derived, and off-the-shelf monocular (Omnidata) normals, finding that monocular normals—applied via a divide-and-conquer patch strategy for high resolution—yield the best estimated-normal performance and complement multi-view ambiguities.

## Results

On DTU, GoMVS achieves the best completeness (0.227 mm) and overall score (0.287 mm), outperforming the previous best MVSFormer (0.251/0.289) by a large margin on completeness. On Tanks & Temples, GoMVS attains the highest F-score on both the Intermediate set (66.44) and the Advanced set (43.07), ranking 1st on the official TNT Advanced benchmark. On ETH3D, GoMVS achieves the best F-score on both the validation set (79.16%) and the test split (85.91%), surpassing the previous SOTA EPNet (79.08%/83.72%) by a notable margin on the test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DTU, GoMVS substantially outperforms all 14 compared methods on completeness (0.227 vs. 0.251 for MVSFormer, 0.253 for ET-MVSNet) and overall score (0.287 vs. 0.289 for MVSFormer), though it trades a small accuracy increase (0.347 vs. 0.327 for MVSFormer). On Tanks & Temples, GoMVS achieves the best mean F-score on both the Intermediate set (66.44 vs. 66.37 for MVSFormer) and the significantly harder Advanced set (43.07 vs. 41.52 for GeoMVSNet), with particularly large gains on individual scenes like Auditorium (35.52 vs. 30.23) and Ballroom (47.15 vs. 46.53). On ETH3D, GoMVS surpasses the previous best EPNet by 2.19 points on the test F-score (85.91% vs. 83.72%) and 0.08 points on the validation F-score (79.16% vs. 79.08%), while also outperforming PVSNet (82.62%) and Vis-MVSNet (83.46%) on the test split. The method consistently beats traditional baselines (COLMAP, Gipuma) by wide margins across all three benchmarks, confirming the advantage of learned geometrically consistent aggregation over classical approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoMVS builds directly on the MVSNet 3D U-Net architecture and the CasMVSNet three-stage cascade framework, replacing standard 3D convolutions with a geometrically consistent aggregation (GCA) operator. It departs from prior normal-assisted MVS methods (GeoNet, NAPV-MVS, NR-MVSNet) that refine or regularize depth in 2D image space by instead enforcing geometric consistency in the 3D cost volume, using surface normals to compute depth correspondences between adjacent pixels and propagate matching costs accordingly. The paper also positions itself against learned aggregation schemes (PatchMatchNet, AA-RMVSNet) that rely on network capacity to select relevant neighbors without geometric guarantees, and against sparse or window-based cost aggregation that ignores local depth variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By shifting geometric consistency enforcement from the 2D depth output to the 3D cost volume, GoMVS addresses a fundamental limitation of prior normal-assisted methods that cannot correct inconsistencies already baked into the cost volume. The finding that an off-the-shelf monocular normal estimator (Omnidata) outperforms both depth-derived and cost-derived normals is practically useful, suggesting that monocular priors complement multi-view geometry in regions where multi-view matching is ambiguous. Open questions include whether the local planar assumption degrades on highly curved or non-planar surfaces, and whether the GCP module's computational overhead scales favorably to very high-resolution inputs or larger depth hypothesis ranges.
