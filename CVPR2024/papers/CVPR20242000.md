## Problem

State-of-the-art VLMs struggle with 3D spatial reasoning tasks such as estimating metric distances between objects or comparing their sizes, because the image-caption training data they are trained on contains limited spatial information. The authors hypothesize this is a data scarcity problem rather than an architectural limitation, and aim to demonstrate that Internet-scale synthetic spatial VQA data can close this gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a free-form spatial reasoning text query (e.g., 'How far is object A from object B?') and produces a natural-language text answer. Qualitative answers express spatial relations (left/right, above/below, bigger/smaller), while quantitative answers provide metric-scale estimates in units like meters or centimeters. In the chain-of-thought setting, an external LLM decomposes a complex question into multiple simple spatial queries, each answered by the VLM, and synthesizes the final response.

## Method

SpatialVLM builds a data synthesis pipeline that lifts 10 million real-world images into object-centric 3D point clouds by combining CLIP-based scene filtering, open-vocabulary detection, Zoedepth metric depth estimation, semantic segmentation, and FlexCap fine-grained captioning. A template-based generator then produces 2 billion spatial VQA pairs (38 question types, ~20 templates each, 50% qualitative / 50% quantitative) with ambiguity resolved via CLIP-similarity clustering of object captions. The model uses the PaLM-E architecture with a PaLM 2-S backbone, trained with 5% of tokens on the synthetic spatial data mixed with the original PaLM-E corpus, and benefits from unfreezing the ViT encoder for fine-grained distance estimation. When paired with an LLM coordinator (text-davinci-003), the VLM serves as a spatial reasoning submodule enabling multi-hop chain-of-thought queries and dense reward annotation for robotics.

## Results

On the human-annotated spatial reasoning benchmark, SpatialVLM achieves 75.2% accuracy on binary predicate spatial questions, outperforming all baselines including GPT-4V (68.0%) and LLaVA-1.5 (71.3%). For quantitative spatial estimation, SpatialVLM outputs a valid numeric answer 99.0% of the time (vs. 1.0% for GPT-4V and 20.9% for LLaVA-1.5), and 37.2% of its distance estimates fall within the half-to-twice range of ground truth (vs. 0.0% for GPT-4V and 13.0% for LLaVA-1.5). On general VQA, co-training with spatial data improves VQA v2 by +2.4% over the PaLM 2-E baseline while maintaining comparable OKVQA performance (−0.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialVLM outperforms all five external baselines on both qualitative and quantitative spatial reasoning. On binary predicate accuracy, it beats LLaVA-1.5 (71.3%) by 3.9 points and GPT-4V (68.0%) by 7.2 points, with the largest margin over PaLM-E (50.2%) and PaLI (60.7%). On quantitative estimation, the gap is even more dramatic: GPT-4V outputs a numeric answer only 1.0% of the time and never lands in the acceptable range, while SpatialVLM achieves 99.0% valid output and 37.2% in-range accuracy. Among the closest competitors, PaLM 2-E (the same architecture without spatial data) reaches 33.9% in-range, showing that the synthetic spatial VQA data contributes a 3.3-point gain. On general VQA, SpatialVLM slightly degrades OKVQA (−0.4%) but improves VQA v2 (+2.4%) over PaLM 2-E, indicating no meaningful trade-off from spatial co-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PaLM-E's architecture and training procedure (replacing the PaLM backbone with PaLM 2-S) and extends the Socratic Models / LLM-as-coordinator paradigm for multi-step reasoning. It departs from prior spatial reasoning work that relies on explicit scene graphs or pathfinding by encoding 3D spatial understanding implicitly in VLM weights through large-scale synthetic training data. Unlike previous data generation efforts that render photorealistic scenes with bounded expressivity, SpatialVLM extracts spatial information from real-world internet images using off-the-shelf vision models (open-vocabulary detection, metric depth, segmentation, captioning), positioning the core contribution as a data-synthesis pipeline rather than an architectural innovation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that VLMs' spatial reasoning deficit is primarily a data problem, not an architectural one, by showing that fine-tuning on 2 billion synthetic spatial QA pairs closes a large gap with GPT-4V on both qualitative and quantitative spatial tasks. The quantitative metric estimation capability unlocks practical applications in robotics (dense reward annotation) and chain-of-thought spatial reasoning that were previously infeasible. Key limitations include inheriting biases from the monocular depth estimator (best performance in the 1–10 m range), reliance on template-based question generation that limits compositional diversity, and evaluation on a self-constructed benchmark of modest size (331 qualitative + 215 quantitative pairs). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex geometric primitives and whether improved depth estimators would further close the quantitative gap.
