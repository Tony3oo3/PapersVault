## Problem

Existing text-to-video generation methods either train large models from scratch or fine-tune entire T2I models with billions of parameters, making them prohibitively expensive in both training cost and GPU memory. The paper asks whether a strong T2I model can be adapted to video generation by tuning only a tiny fraction of its parameters while preserving temporal consistency and generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240767/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For T2V generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces a 16-frame video at 256×256 (latent size 32×32), optionally upscaled to 1024×1024 via a second-stage 4× super-resolution model. For one-shot video editing, it takes an input video plus an edited text prompt and produces a new video with modified content while preserving motion, requiring only ~2 minutes of tuning on a single video. Training operates in the latent space of a pretrained autoencoder and fits within 8 GB GPU memory at 16×256×256 resolution.

## Method

SimDA freezes the 1.1B-parameter Stable Diffusion U-Net and adds only 24M trainable parameters (≈2%) in the form of lightweight adapters. Spatial adapters (bottleneck FC layers with GELU) are inserted after attention and FFN blocks to transfer spatial knowledge, while temporal adapters use depth-wise 3D convolutions in a reduced-dimensional space to model inter-frame dynamics at low cost. The key architectural innovation is Latent-Shift Attention (LSA), which performs patch-level shifting along the temporal axis so that keys and values are formed by concatenating the current frame's tokens with those shifted from preceding frames, reducing attention complexity from O(L²N²) to O(2LN²) while capturing adjacent-frame relationships. A two-stage pipeline (256×256 generation followed by a 4× LDM upsampler) extends output resolution to 1024×1024, and the same adapter architecture is reused for text-guided video editing via DDIM inversion, cutting training time by 3× compared to Tune-A-Video.

## Results

On MSR-VTT (zero-shot), SimDA achieves an FVD of 456 and CLIPSIM of 0.2945 with only 1.08B total parameters (25M tunable), outperforming LVDM (FVD 742), CogVideo (FVD 1294), and VideoComposer (FVD 580) on FVD while matching or exceeding their CLIPSIM scores. On the WebVid validation set, SimDA reaches FVD 363.98 and CLIPSIM 0.3054, surpassing LVDM (FVD 455.53) and ModelScope (FVD 414.11). Inference speed is 11.20s per video on a single A100 GPU, roughly 39× faster than CogVideo (434.53s) and 2× faster than Latent-Shift (23.40s). For text-guided video editing, SimDA achieves 68.8% user preference (vs. 31.2% for Tune-A-Video) with 3.7× faster training (2.5 min vs. 9.3 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimDA achieves the best FVD (456) among all methods trained on WebVid-10M, beating VideoComposer (580), VideoFusion (581), and LVDM (742) by 21–63%, while using fewer parameters (1.08B vs. 1.16–1.85B). On CLIPSIM, SimDA (0.2945) surpasses Video-LDM (0.2929), VideoComposer (0.2932), and Latent-Shift (0.2773), but trails Make-A-Video (0.3049) and VideoFactory (0.3005), which leverage additional large-scale HD-VILA/HD-VG datasets. In the user study (Table 4), SimDA is preferred over VDM (85.2%), Latent-Shift (81.5%), ModelScope (78.3%), and LVDM (83.4%) in both video quality and text-video faithfulness. For video editing, SimDA outperforms Tune-A-Video on user votes (68.8% vs. 31.2% for frame consistency; 60.5% vs. 39.5% for textual alignment) while requiring only 24.9M tuned parameters versus 74.4M, with 3.7× faster training (2.5 min vs. 9.3 min) and lower GPU memory (28.6 GiB vs. 31.3 GiB). In inference speed, SimDA (11.20s) is the fastest among all compared T2V methods, roughly 2× faster than Latent-Shift (23.40s) and LVDM (21.23s), and 39× faster than CogVideo (434.53s).

## Relation to Prior Work

SimDA builds directly on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as its frozen T2I backbone, but departs sharply from prior T2V approaches (Make-A-Video, Imagen Video, Video LDM, Latent-Shift) that either train from scratch or fine-tune billions of parameters. The key conceptual move is transplanting parameter-efficient adapter techniques from NLP (LoRA, prefix-tuning) into the video diffusion setting, adding only 24M tunable parameters (≈2%) via spatial and temporal bottleneck adapters. The proposed Latent-Shift Attention (LSA) extends the temporal-shift lineage (TSM, TPS, Latent-Shift) by operating at the attention level and using all tokens from shifted frames as keys/values, which the authors argue guarantees temporal consistency better than direct patch shifting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: training requires <8 GB GPU memory and inference is 39× faster than the autoregressive CogVideo, making video diffusion accessible to labs without multi-node clusters. As the first parameter-efficient video diffusion baseline, SimDA establishes a reference point for future work on efficient T2V adaptation. However, the base resolution remains 256×256 (requiring a separate SR stage for 1024×1024), training relies on the noisy WebVid-10M dataset, and the editing extension, while faster than Tune-A-Video, still requires per-video tuning. Open questions include whether the adapter paradigm scales to longer videos, higher resolutions natively, or more diverse motion patterns without the SR cascade.
