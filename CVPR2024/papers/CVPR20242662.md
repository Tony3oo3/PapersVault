## Problem

Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) efficiently adapts frozen Vision Transformers to downstream tasks, but the paper identifies for the first time that VPT inherits and amplifies bias from the pre-trained ViT backbone, leading to significant unfairness across sensitive attribute groups (e.g., gender). The pre-trained ViT, rather than the learnable prompts or classification head, is shown to be the principal source of this unfairness, yet no prior method addresses bias removal within the prompt-tuning paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224 for ViT-B/16 patchification) and produces a single class label for a target attribute (e.g., Attractive, Race, Age, or bird type). During training, the input sequence is augmented with M=50 learnable prompt tokens (768-dim) prepended before the image patches; during inference, the cleaner prompts are masked out so only target prompts and image patches contribute to the final classification. Fairness is evaluated with respect to a separate sensitive attribute (e.g., Gender) using equalized odds, balanced accuracy, and group-wise accuracy.

## Method

Fair-VPT splits the M learnable prompts into α target prompts and (M−α) cleaner prompts, then encodes the class token in two parallel streams through the frozen ViT: a standard MSA stream (all prompts visible) trained to predict the target label, and a masked MSA stream (target prompts masked with −inf attention scores) trained to predict the sensitive attribute. This dual encoding forces sensitive-attribute information into the cleaner prompts while confining target-relevant information to the target prompts. A supervised contrastive disentanglement loss further decorrelates the two prompt representations by treating same-label/same-attribute samples encoded via different streams as negatives. At inference, the cleaner prompts are masked out, so the downstream classifier operates only on target prompts and image patches, effectively removing the biased information absorbed during training.

## Results

On CelebA (Attractive target, Gender sensitive), Fair-VPT achieves 78.6% accuracy, 76.3% balanced accuracy, and 12.0 equalized odds, outperforming all baselines on fairness while maintaining competitive accuracy. On UTK Face (Race target), it reaches 90.9% BAcc and 4.9 EO, and on bFFHQ (Age target) it achieves 80.7% BAcc and 37.1 EO. On Waterbirds (background bias), Fair-VPT lifts the worst-group accuracy to 78.9% (vs. ~50% for baselines), yielding 84.3% BAcc and 18.7 EO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fair-VPT decisively outperforms VPT on fairness (EO 12.0 vs. 32.1) while retaining 97% of VPT's accuracy (78.6% vs. 81.7%) and surpassing it in balanced accuracy (76.3% vs. 75.0%). The two fairness-oriented baselines, VPT+AT and VPT+FSCL+, improve EO over VPT (24.0 and 20.6 respectively) but at a severe accuracy cost, dropping to 67.6% and 69.3%—a 12–14 point loss—making them impractical for deployment. On UTK Face, Fair-VPT achieves the best BAcc (90.9%) and EO (4.9), whereas VPT+AT and VPT+FSCL+ offer only marginal EO improvements (11.6 and 9.9) with lower BAcc. On the extreme-bias bFFHQ dataset (0.5% minority), Fair-VPT reaches 62.3% worst-group accuracy versus 58.7% for VPT+AT, confirming its advantage under severe imbalance. On Waterbirds, where the smallest group has only 56 training samples, Fair-VPT lifts worst-group accuracy to 78.9% (vs. ~50–55% for all baselines), demonstrating generalizability beyond facial-attribute bias to background bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) by Jia et al. (ECCV 2022) with a fairness-aware mechanism, positioning itself at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and fairness-aware classification. It draws on supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020) to design a disentanglement loss that decorrelates two categories of prompts. Conceptually, it departs from prior fairness methods (adversarial training, fair contrastive learning) by showing that naively grafting them onto VPT causes significant accuracy degradation, and instead proposes a structural solution—categorizing prompts into 'cleaner' and 'target' roles with masked self-attention—that addresses the root cause identified as the biased pre-trained ViT backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic investigation of unfairness in Visual Prompt Tuning, revealing that the pre-trained ViT backbone—not the learnable prompts or classification head—is the principal source of bias, a finding that has implications for all parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods built on biased pre-trained models. The practical relevance is high for practitioners deploying VPT in sensitive domains (hiring, lending, healthcare), as it offers a drop-in fairness improvement without retraining the backbone. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-attribute or non-binary sensitive attributes, scales to larger ViT variants (ViT-L, ViT-H), and whether the cleaner/target prompt split remains effective when the target and sensitive attributes are highly entangled (as partially explored on bFFHQ).
