## Problem

Existing NeRF-based neural SLAM methods are designed for well-lit, static scenes and fail under practical conditions such as motion blur and lighting variation, producing view-inconsistent artifacts, tracking drifts, and distorted maps. No prior work has integrated the high dynamic range and high temporal resolution of event cameras into an implicit neural SLAM framework for dense 3D reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240534/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes two asynchronous sensor streams: RGBD frames (color image + depth map) from a frame-based camera and an event stream (pixel-level polarity and timestamp) from an event camera, each with its own intrinsic matrix. It outputs per-frame camera poses in SE(3), a shared multiresolution implicit scene representation (geometric and color feature grids), and can render RGB color, HDR luminance, and depth at arbitrary viewpoints. The event stream is not frame-aligned; instead, events are accumulated over time windows between two queried poses to form the supervision signal.

## Method

EN-SLAM learns a single shared radiance field e(x) and geometry hidden feature h(x)^g from both modalities, then decomposes the radiance into RGB color c(x) and event luminance l(x) via two differentiable Camera Response Function (CRF) tone-mapping operators Ψ_c and Ψ_l that model each camera's distinct imaging pipeline in the log domain. For optimization, the Event Temporal Aggregating (ETA) strategy renders log-brightness differences between two camera poses and matches them against the accumulated event polarity sum (the Event Generation Model constraint), providing a differentiable event loss that avoids direct luminance supervision. An adaptive forward-query mechanism selects the most reliable prior frame for the event constraint, and a probability-weighted sampling strategy uses RGB patch losses to guide which rays to sample in the event plane, reducing computational cost while focusing on high-uncertainty regions. The full system alternates between tracking (10 iterations, 1024 rays) and global bundle adjustment (every 5 frames, 2048 rays from all keyframes) at 17 FPS.

## Results

EN-SLAM achieves an average tracking ATE of 8.56 cm on DEV-Indoors (9 scenes), outperforming the next-best method ESLAM (14.66 cm) by 41% and remaining stable (fluctuation <0.8 cm) across all blur and dark variants. On the real-captured DEV-Reals dataset, EN-SLAM reaches 18.97 cm average ATE, 1.63× lower than CoSLAM (30.95 cm), while ORBSLAM and NICE-SLAM crash entirely. On the Vector benchmark, EN-SLAM achieves 1.67 cm average ATE versus USLAM's 2.84 cm and ESLAM's 7.19 cm, with CoSLAM degrading to 38.45 cm under fast motion. The system runs at 17 FPS with only 1.95M parameters, matching the speed of ESLAM (14.77 FPS) while using fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EN-SLAM wins decisively against all NeRF-based SLAM baselines. Against iMAP [52], the improvement is dramatic (8.56 vs. 214.57 cm avg ATE on DEV-Indoors), as iMAP completely diverges in blur and dark scenes. Against NICE-SLAM [71], EN-SLAM is 3.8× more accurate (8.56 vs. 32.47 cm) and never crashes, whereas NICE-SLAM fails on 3 of 9 DEV-Indoors sequences and 2 of 8 DEV-Reals sequences. Against CoSLAM [58] and ESLAM [23], EN-SLAM achieves 46% and 41% lower ATE on DEV-Indoors respectively, and the gap widens on DEV-Reals (18.97 vs. 30.95 and 31.27 cm). On the Vector dataset, EN-SLAM (1.67 cm) outperforms the event-based USLAM [57] (2.84 cm) by 41%, while CoSLAM [58] degrades catastrophically to 38.45 cm under fast motion and EVO [43]/ESVO [69] crash on 7 of 8 sequences. ORBSLAM [36] fails on all 8 DEV-Reals sequences, confirming the difficulty of the real-world evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EN-SLAM sits at the intersection of two research threads: NeRF-based implicit SLAM (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, CoSLAM, ESLAM) and event-based visual SLAM (EVO, USLAM, ESVO). It departs from prior NeRF-SLAM methods by introducing a differentiable CRF rendering pipeline (inspired by HDR-NeRF's tone-mapping) that decomposes a shared radiance field into both RGB color and event luminance, addressing the fundamentally different imaging mechanisms of the two sensors. It also departs from prior event-based SLAM, which has been limited to sparse feature tracking or visual odometry, by integrating events into a dense implicit neural representation with a novel temporal aggregating optimization that exploits the consecutive-difference structure of events for joint tracking and global bundle adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify event and RGBD streams within an implicit neural SLAM system, directly addressing the practical failure modes (motion blur, lighting variation) that have limited deployment of NeRF-based SLAM beyond well-lit indoor scenes. The two introduced datasets (DEV-Indoors, DEV-Reals) fill a notable gap in the literature, as no prior benchmark simultaneously provides RGBD, event streams, ground-truth meshes, and trajectories under challenging conditions. Key limitations remain: the method requires depth input (unavailable in many real deployments), is validated only on indoor scenes, and may struggle with boundless long trajectories. Extending to outdoor environments, removing the depth dependency, and scaling to large-scale scenes are the natural next steps.
