## Problem

Unifying diverse computer vision tasks (segmentation, keypoint detection, image editing, enhancement) under a single model is harder than NLP unification because of heterogeneous output formats (masks, coordinates, images), differing methodologies, and continuous data that resists discretization. Prior generalist approaches either quantize outputs into discrete tokens (introducing information loss) or rely on implicit in-context learning without explicit task instructions, limiting flexibility and open-set generalization.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 source RGB image concatenated with a natural-language instruction (e.g., 'Mark the pixels of the cat in the mirror to blue and leave the rest unchanged') and produces a 3-channel RGB image whose pixel content encodes the task result. For understanding tasks, a lightweight U-Net post-processor extracts binary masks or multi-channel heatmaps from the generated image; for generative tasks (editing, deblurring, watermark removal), the output image is used directly. All output types—masks, keypoints, edited images—are unified as 3-channel images, avoiding the quantization errors inherent in discrete-token representations.

## Method

InstructDiffusion builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and casts every vision task as instruction-guided image editing in latent pixel space. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) pretraining adaptation fine-tunes the diffusion model on augmented captions with random patches/circles to shift the output distribution toward mask- and keypoint-style images; (2) task-specific training injects the source image by concatenating it with the noise input (InstructPix2Pix-style), using 10 diverse instruction templates per task so the model learns detailed action semantics rather than treating task names as fixed indicators; (3) human alignment fine-tunes on 1,000 human-selected samples from 20 classifier-free guidance settings. A key design choice is encoding all outputs (binary masks, keypoint circles, edited pixels) as natural 3-channel images, eliminating quantization. Joint multi-task training across ~955K samples (keypoints, segmentation, enhancement, editing) is shown to improve open-set generalization over single-task training.

## Results

On keypoint detection, InstructDiffusion achieves 71.2 AP on COCO val, 51.4 on HumanArt, and 15.9 on AP-10K, outperforming Painter (70.2/12.4/15.3) and Unified-IO (25.0/15.7/7.6) across all three benchmarks. For semantic segmentation, it reaches 53.17 mcIoU on COCO-Stuff and 72.55 on Pascal VOC, substantially exceeding Painter (11.91/4.67) and Unified-IO (22.52/31.46). On referring segmentation, it attains 67.36 cIoU on gRefCOCO val, surpassing Unified-IO (17.31) by a wide margin. For image editing, it achieves 30.19/4.90 CLIP-Sim/AP on the Replace task, competitive with MagicBrush (30.50/4.94) and InstructPix2Pix (29.61/4.99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against specialized keypoint models (PCT, ViTPose), InstructDiffusion trails by ~9–11 AP on COCO val and ~12 AP on HumanArt but wins on the unseen AP-10K animal dataset (15.9 vs. 14.6/14.7), demonstrating cross-domain generalization. Among generalist models, it decisively outperforms Unified-IO on every task (e.g., 71.2 vs. 25.0 AP on COCO keypoint, 53.17 vs. 22.52 mcIoU on COCO-Stuff) and Painter on most benchmarks (e.g., 51.4 vs. 12.4 on HumanArt, 72.55 vs. 4.67 on VOC), with the sole exception of ADE-150 where Painter (54.50) benefits from having been trained on that dataset. For referring segmentation, InstructDiffusion beats Unified-IO by 40+ cIoU on gRefCOCO (67.36 vs. 17.31) and surpasses specialized models LAVT and ReLA on RefClef (40.34 vs. 47.08/45.94 — actually loses here), while remaining competitive on RefCOCO val (61.74 vs. 72.73/73.21). On image editing, it is broadly competitive with MagicBrush and Null-text Inversion (within ~0.5 CLIP-Sim) but slightly behind on Replace (30.19 vs. 30.71) and Remove (28.88 vs. 29.69). For low-level enhancement, it loses to specialized restoration models (NAFNet 33.71/40.30 PSNR, WDNet 40.24) but outperforms other editing-oriented methods on denoising (34.26 vs. 24.48 EDICT) and watermark removal (23.60 vs. 19.88 EDICT), with the gap to specialized models attributed to VAE reconstruction loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based image editing line (Stable Diffusion, InstructPix2Pix) by repurposing the continuous pixel-space output of a latent diffusion model as a universal representation for diverse vision tasks, including segmentation masks and keypoint indicators. It departs from discrete-token unification approaches (Pix2Seq, Unified-IO) that suffer from VQ-VAE quantization error, and from in-context learning approaches (Painter, PromptDiffusion) that rely on implicit task specification via example images, by instead using explicit natural-language instructions that directly describe the desired pixel manipulation. The key conceptual move is reframing understanding tasks (segmentation, keypoint detection) as a special case of instructional image editing, where the 'edit' is to paint a mask or draw a circle, unifying them with generative tasks under a single training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a meaningful step toward a single model that handles both understanding and generative vision tasks, and the finding that multi-task joint training improves open-set generalization (e.g., to unseen animal keypoints or novel object categories) suggests a path toward more robust generalist vision models. However, the model still trails specialized methods on most standard benchmarks (e.g., 71.2 vs. 82.0 AP on COCO keypoints), is constrained to 256×256 resolution, and the VAE bottleneck limits low-level task quality. Open questions include whether the instruction-based formulation scales to higher resolutions and more task types, whether the VAE information loss can be mitigated, and whether self-supervised or unsupervised pretraining can reduce the need for large curated datasets.
