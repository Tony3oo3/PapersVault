## Problem

State-of-the-art latent diffusion shape generators (e.g., 3DS2VS) perform distribution alignment in latent space but do not explicitly model geometric feasibility or physical stability, resulting in synthetic shapes with disconnected components, unrealistic geometric patterns, and thin hanging structures that are physically unstable. Existing approaches cannot easily enforce these properties under representations that support varying topologies, such as implicit surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240856/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a category label and Gaussian noise in the latent space of a pre-trained 3DS2VS decoder as input, and produces a 3D shape as an implicit neural field decoded by the 3DS2VS backbone. During training, the quality checker consumes point-cloud surfels (positions + normals) for the geometric feasibility branch and eigenvalues of the volumetric stiffness matrix for the physical stability branch, both evaluated on decoded shapes from sampled latent codes.

## Method

GPLD3D extends the EDM latent diffusion framework by introducing a regularized training loss that combines the empirical data distribution with a continuous regularization distribution induced by a quality checker. The quality checker scores each synthetic shape via two components: a PointNet++-based classifier trained on user-labeled synthetic shapes for geometric feasibility, and a two-layer MLP that maps stiffness-matrix eigenvalues to a worst-case stress score for physical stability. The key theoretical contribution is two variance bounds (Theorems 1 and 2) showing that the data-distribution variance scales as O(1/σ⁴) while the regularization variance remains bounded at small σ, yielding a principled trade-off ratio λ_reg/λ_data = c_ratio/(σ⁴(c_off+σ)²) that makes regularization dominate at low noise levels. Modified EDM hyperparameters (c_in, c_out, c_skip, λ_data, λ_reg) are derived from four balancing principles, recovering standard EDM when c_ratio=0.

## Results

On ShapeNet-v2 (7 categories: chair, table, airplane, sofa, rifle, lamp, car), GPLD3D achieves the best FPD and KPD scores across all categories. Averaged over all 55 categories, GPLD3D reaches FPD 0.56 and KPD 0.41, reducing FPD by 16.5% and KPD by 31.7% over the strongest baseline 3DS2VS (FPD 0.67, KPD 0.60). Per-category improvements over 3DS2VS range from 7.7%/21.9% (car) to 15.9%/33.3% (chair) in FPD/KPD, with larger gains on topologically complex categories. GPLD3D also improves physical stability (PSS) and reduces disconnected shapes (PDS) by 13.1% and 24.5% respectively over 3DS2VS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPLD3D is built on 3DS2VS [56] as its backbone and outperforms it on every reported category in both FPD and KPD, with the largest relative gains on complex-topology categories (chair: 15.9%/33.3% FPD/KPD reduction; table: 12.2%/28.6%). Against LAS [59], GPLD3D achieves substantially lower FPD/KPD wherever LAS reports results (e.g., chair: 0.53 vs 1.07 FPD; rifle: 0.59 vs 1.23 FPD). Against NW [18], GPLD3D wins on all three shared categories (chair, table, airplane), with the most dramatic gap on chair (0.53 vs 1.24 FPD). Against 3DILG [55], GPLD3D dominates across all seven categories, with FPD reductions of 64–82% (e.g., table: 0.43 vs 2.37; sofa: 0.53 vs 2.28). The paper also reports that GPLD3D improves physical stability (PSS) and reduces disconnected shapes (PDS) by 13.1% and 24.5% over 3DS2VS, confirming that the quality checker addresses structural issues beyond distributional fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion paradigm for 3D shape generation (as established by 3DS2VS, LAS, NW, and 3DILG) by introducing a quality checker that regularizes the diffusion training objective. It builds on the EDM framework for principled hyperparameter design and on worst-case structural analysis for physical stability scoring, but departs from prior work by converting a discrete empirical data distribution and a continuous quality-induced distribution into a unified training loss with theoretically grounded trade-off parameters (Theorems 1–2). Unlike classifier-guidance diffusion, which requires gradients between a classifier and latent codes, GPLD3D only needs a scalar score function, making it applicable to non-differentiable quality metrics like stiffness-matrix eigenvalue analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode of 3D shape generators—disconnected components and physically unstable structures—that cannot be resolved by better neural representations alone. The quality checker's demonstrated generalization across unseen object categories and different generative backbones suggests a reusable component for future shape generation pipelines. Key limitations remain: the method cannot guarantee quality (the latent space is still sparsely sampled), decoder reconstruction errors cap performance, and the approach has not been extended to 3D scenes or to incorporating topological/part/semantic priors. Open questions include whether local approximations of the quality checker can be integrated into the diffusion process and whether geometric/physical priors can be baked into the decoder itself to reduce latent dimensionality.
