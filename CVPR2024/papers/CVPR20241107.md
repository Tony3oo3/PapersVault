## Problem

High-fidelity reconstruction and photorealistic rendering of dynamic humans from multi-view video remains challenging, particularly for loose clothing, complex poses, and multi-person interactions. A key bottleneck is the absence of large-scale, high-detail datasets that capture these difficulties under markerless multi-view settings, making it hard to evaluate and advance NeRF-based human modeling methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241107/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes dense multi-view video (56–60 synchronized cameras at 1920×1080 or 3840×2160, 25 FPS), binary foreground masks generated by BGMv2, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics estimated via COLMAP SfM. Outputs include novel-view renderings, SDF/mesh geometry, refined foreground segmentation with normal maps, and per-frame dynamic reconstructions. Sequences are stratified by human width-to-image ratio (>0.4, 0.2–0.4, <0.2) to isolate difficulty levels from scale, appearance diversity, and pose complexity.

## Method

The paper contributes the PKU-DyMVHumans dataset (8.2M frames, 32 subjects, 45 scenarios spanning dance, kungfu, sport, and fashion) together with an off-the-shelf benchmark pipeline that converts COLMAP SfM output into formats directly consumable by NeuS, Instant-NGP, NeuS2, and Tensor4D. Three benchmark tasks are defined: (1) static novel view synthesis evaluated with PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across width-ratio sectors, (2) dynamic human modeling under both dense-view (48–52 training cameras, NeuS2/Instant-NGP) and sparse-view (16 front-facing cameras, Tensor4D) settings, and (3) self-supervised neural scene decomposition using Surface-SOS to separate foreground geometry from background without annotations. The key differentiator is the combination of 4K capture, 56+ cameras, and deliberately challenging content (loose garments, thin structures, multi-person occlusion, smoke, and complex lighting) that exposes failure modes of current hash-encoding and SDF-based methods.

## Results

On the PKU-DyMVHumans benchmark for static novel view synthesis (Table 2), Instant-NGP achieves the best average PSNR of 32.02, SSIM of 0.987, and LPIPS of 0.019, outperforming NeuS (PSNR 23.93) and NeuS2 (PSNR 30.75). The best single-scene reconstruction reaches PSNR 36.20 (1080_Dance_Banquet_Single_c23 with Instant-NGP), while the most challenging scene drops to PSNR 20.38 (4K_Studios_Show_Single_f16 with NeuS). For dynamic reconstruction (Table 3), NeuS2 on short dense-view sequences achieves the highest average PSNR of 31.20, while Tensor4D on sparse views lags at 29.38 PSNR. For foreground segmentation (Table 4), Surface-SOS improves over BGMv2 by reducing average MSE from 0.422 to 0.224 and increasing mIoU from 0.881 to 0.908.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For static novel view synthesis, Instant-NGP dominates all three metrics on average (PSNR 32.02 vs. NeuS2's 30.75 and NeuS's 23.93), with a particularly large gap over NeuS (~8 dB PSNR) attributed to NeuS's explicit SDF integration being less effective at capturing high-frequency details. NeuS2 is competitive with Instant-NGP on the 4K fashion show scenes (PSNR 34.33 vs. 34.49) but trails on complex multi-person and interaction scenes (WidthRatio > 0.4). For dynamic reconstruction, NeuS2 on short dense-view sequences achieves the best average PSNR (31.20), surpassing Instant-NGP on long dense sequences (29.82) and Tensor4D on sparse views (29.38); however, Tensor4D degrades most on the WidthRatio > 0.4 category (PSNR 27.59), indicating difficulty with complex interactions under sparse views. For foreground segmentation, Surface-SOS consistently outperforms BGMv2 across all width-ratio bins, with the largest gain in the (0.2, 0.4) range (mIoU 0.936 vs. 0.891), confirming that neural surface-based refinement is most beneficial for mid-scale human subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground as a data resource rather than a methodological advance: it introduces PKU-DyMVHumans, a dense multi-view video dataset (8.2M frames, 56–60 cameras, 32 subjects, 45 scenarios) that fills a gap left by prior human-centric datasets (Human3.6M, THuman, DNA-Rendering, DynaCap, etc.), which typically lack the combination of high-resolution appearance detail, complex multi-person interactions, and diverse motion categories (dance, kungfu, sport, fashion). The benchmark pipeline repurposes existing NeRF-based methods (NeuS, Instant-NGP, NeuS2, Tensor4D) and a self-supervised segmentation approach (Surface-SOS) as evaluation baselines, positioning the dataset as a common testbed for the neural implicit representation family rather than proposing a new reconstruction algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset is practically valuable for the dynamic human rendering community because it simultaneously stresses appearance fidelity (satin ribbons, classical headwear textures), motion complexity (loose/oversized clothing, kungfu choreography), and scene complexity (multi-person interactions, lighting, shadows, smoking) in a single benchmark, which no prior dataset covers comprehensively. The experiments confirm expected trends (hash-encoding methods outperform NeuS; complex interactions degrade quality) but also surface open challenges: multi-person/subject modeling with interactivity, monocular self-rotating video rendering, and faithful reconstruction of thin structures and facial expressions under sparse views. A key limitation is that all baselines are single-subject or short-sequence methods, so the dataset's multi-person and long-sequence potential remains largely unexploited, leaving room for future work on scalable 4D representations.
