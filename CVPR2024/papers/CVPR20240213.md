## Problem

Creating animation-ready 3D characters with accurate skinning traditionally requires extensive manual artist work, and existing automated methods either predict only static skinning weights (which produce severe artifacts under articulated poses) or require dense 3D scans/point clouds in multiple poses. No prior method has demonstrated that pose-dependent skinning weights can be learned purely from 2D multi-view video without any manual rigging, skinning, or dense geometry supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240213/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB video (as few as 10 calibrated cameras) plus skeletal pose from an off-the-shelf markerless mocap system, with precomputed foreground masks and distance transforms. It outputs a fully rigged, textured 3D template mesh (via NeuS + Marching Cubes) together with a continuous pose-dependent skinning weight field defined over canonical 3D space, which can be queried at arbitrary mesh resolution rather than being locked to a fixed vertex count. The posed geometry is obtained by applying Linear Blend Skinning with the predicted weights to the canonical template.

## Method

The core contribution is SkinNet, a coordinate-based MLP that maps a 3D point in canonical space concatenated with a normalized skeletal pose (global translation and yaw removed) to a per-point skinning weight vector, enabling both pose-dependent deformation and multi-resolution sampling. A canonical template is acquired via NeuS implicit surface reconstruction converted to an explicit mesh, with initial static weights from Pinocchio's heat diffusion. Supervision is weakly learned from video through a four-stage curriculum: first SkinNet is trained with silhouette loss only, then AlbedoNet (static per-point color) and ShadowNet (pose- and view-dependent scalar shading) are trained sequentially with a differentiable rendering loss, and finally SkinNet is refined with the full rendering loss. Additional regularizers include a Laplacian smoothness term, a geodesic-distance-based skinning constraint preventing non-local weight leakage, and a part-wise loss anchoring rigid skin vertices to their initial weights.

## Results

On three subjects (D2, D5, V6), VINECS achieves Chamfer distances of 3.034 cm, 4.512 cm, and 2.993 cm respectively, outperforming all skinning-only baselines (Pinocchio, RigNet, SCANimate*) across every metric. Compared to SCANimate (with pose-aware shape), VINECS wins on D5 (4.512 vs 4.982 Chamfer) and V6 (2.993 vs 3.154 Chamfer) but trails on D2 (3.034 vs 2.842). SNARF, which requires dense point clouds, achieves slightly lower Chamfer distances (2.591, 4.320, 2.760) but VINECS matches or exceeds it on S2M for D5 (2.070 vs 2.032) and V6 (1.274 vs 1.263) while using only multi-view video as input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VINECS clearly outperforms all skinning-only baselines: Pinocchio (Chamfer 3.760/5.077/3.358), RigNet (3.599/4.989/3.369), and SCANimate* (3.750/5.453/3.502) on all three subjects, confirming the benefit of pose-dependent over static skinning weights. Against SCANimate with pose-aware shape, VINECS wins on D5 (Chamfer 4.512 vs 4.982) and V6 (2.993 vs 3.154) but loses on D2 (3.034 vs 2.842), likely because D2's tight clothing is easier for SMPL-based methods. SNARF achieves the best absolute Chamfer distances (2.591/4.320/2.760) but requires dense 4D point clouds for training, whereas VINECS is supervised solely from multi-view video. The paper positions VINECS as the best method that does not require dense 3D/4D scans, with the gap to SNARF narrowing on S2M metrics (e.g., 1.274 vs 1.263 on V6). Notably, VINECS also handles loose clothing (D5 skirt) better than SCANimate and SNARF, which rely on SMPL and struggle with non-body-topology garments.

## Relation to Prior Work

VINECS builds on three key prior components: Pinocchio's heat-diffusion process for initial static skinning weights, NeuS for implicit surface reconstruction of the canonical template, and the differentiable renderer from Habermann et al. for multi-view supervision. It departs from prior dynamic skinning methods (SCANimate, SNARF) that require dense 3D point clouds or range scans, and from static skinning predictors (RigNet, NeuroSkinning, SkinningNet) that produce pose-agnostic weights. The core departure is formulating skinning as a coordinate-based MLP (SkinNet) conditioned on pose, enabling multi-resolution queries, and supervising it entirely through 2D multi-view imagery via a novel auxiliary appearance field (AlbedoNet + ShadowNet) rather than requiring any 3D geometric ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully automated pipeline producing an animation-ready, rigged, and pose-dependently skinned character from multi-view video alone, removing the manual rigging/skinning bottleneck in character creation. The coordinate-based formulation enables multi-resolution skinning and could benefit downstream tasks currently limited by SMPL's body-topology assumption (e.g., loose apparel modeling). Key limitations include the inability to model high-frequency deformations (skin folding, cloth wrinkles) that pure skinning cannot capture, lack of facial expression control, and per-vertex MLP query cost. Open directions include combining with surface tracking for residual deformations, adding parametric facial models, and exploring more efficient architectures like hash grids.
