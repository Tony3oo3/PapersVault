## Problem

Synthesizing multi-view consistent novel views from a single reference image is highly ill-posed, as unobserved regions admit many plausible explanations. Existing autoregressive approaches suffer from error accumulation over long trajectories, while methods that directly sample from 3D scene distributions are computationally expensive and limited to low resolutions. The paper tackles the gap of generating high-quality, pixel-aligned novel views along arbitrary camera trajectories that extend well beyond the reference viewpoint, without requiring ground-truth geometry or multiple input views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240262/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image and a user-defined free-form camera trajectory (a sequence of N camera poses relative to the reference camera) as input, and jointly produces a sequence of N novel view images at 128×128 or 256×256 resolution. Unlike autoregressive baselines that generate frames sequentially, MultiDiff synthesizes all target frames simultaneously in a single diffusion pass, reducing inference to ~1 second per 128px frame versus ~45 s for PhotoNVS. The camera trajectory can deviate substantially from the reference view, including large viewpoint changes and long-term extrapolation.

## Method

MultiDiff builds on VideoCrafter's pretrained 3D U-Net, repurposing its temporal attention layers as correspondence attention that jointly operates across all generated views to learn pixel-wise 3D correspondences. Geometric conditioning is achieved by warping the reference image to each target view using monocular depth (ZoeDepth) and injecting the warped images plus validity masks into the U-Net via a ControlNet-inspired parallel branch with zero-initialized convolutions. A key novelty is a structured noise distribution: the initial Gaussian noise is warped to all target views using the same depth-based warping, producing correlated 3D noise that enforces multi-view consistency during denoising. The model is trained with a standard variance-preserving diffusion objective (MSE on predicted noise) over posed video sequences, and at inference uses DDIM sampling to generate all frames jointly, optionally refining results by re-running with the closest generated frame as the warp source.

## Results

On RealEstate10K at 256px, MultiDiff achieves the best FID (25.90 short-term, 30.15 long-term), KID (0.004/0.006), FVD (105.9), and mTSED (0.855) among all compared methods. At 128px, MultiDiff improves short-term FID from 36.37 (DFM) to 25.30 and long-term FID by 33% over PhotoNVS (42.99→28.25), while DFM retains an edge on pixel-level PSNR (18.10 vs 16.41) and mTSED (0.972 vs 0.941). On the more challenging ScanNet dataset at 256px, MultiDiff outperforms all baselines on FID (43.84 vs 50.18–63.21), KID, and FVD (114.9 vs 134.2–230.1). Inference is ~45× faster than PhotoNVS (~1s vs ~45s per 128×128 frame) and ~17× faster than DFM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFM [66], MultiDiff wins decisively on perceptual quality (FID 25.30 vs 36.37 short-term at 128px; FVD 94.37 vs 120.2) but concedes pixel-level fidelity (PSNR 16.41 vs 18.10) and trajectory adherence (mTSED 0.941 vs 0.972), which the authors attribute to DFM's PixelNeRF representation enforcing pixel alignment at the cost of sharpness. Against PhotoNVS [78], MultiDiff dominates on every metric at both resolutions and both datasets, with long-term FID improving by 33% on RealEstate10K (28.25 vs 42.99) and FVD halved on ScanNet (114.9 vs 134.2). Against Text2Room [27], MultiDiff achieves substantially better FID (25.90 vs 35.41 at 256px) and FVD (105.9 vs 178.6) while matching or exceeding PSNR. Against MVDiffusion [65] on ScanNet, MultiDiff outperforms on all metrics (FID 43.84 vs 50.18, FVD 114.9 vs 230.1, mTSED 0.576 vs 0.361) despite not using ground-truth geometry, whereas MVDiffusion relies on predefined pixel correspondences from 3D meshes. The structured noise ablation confirms its contribution: removing it degrades FID from 25.90 to 29.49 and FVD from 105.9 to 116.4 on RealEstate10K.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiDiff repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model (VideoCrafter) by converting its temporal attention layers into correspondence attention, thereby leveraging temporal consistency as a proxy for 3D multi-view consistency. It departs from autoregressive NVS methods (PhotoNVS, Look Outside the Room) that suffer from error accumulation by jointly generating all target frames in a single diffusion pass, and from DFM which directly samples from 3D scene distributions at high computational cost. The geometric grounding comes from monocular depth warping (ZoeDepth) injected via a ControlNet-style branch, while a novel structured noise distribution correlates noise across views to enforce consistency. This positions the work as a synthesis of latent diffusion, video priors, and depth-based warping into a unified single-image NVS pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup over DFM (~1s vs ~17s per frame) and the ability to perform multi-view consistent editing without task-specific retraining make this approach practically attractive for interactive 3D content creation and AR/VR applications. However, the method remains limited to 256px resolution, relies on potentially noisy monocular depth estimates (particularly for outdoor or textureless scenes), and is evaluated primarily on indoor environments. Open questions include whether the video-diffusion-as-3D-prior strategy generalizes to large-scale outdoor scenes, whether higher resolutions can be achieved without prohibitive memory costs, and how the method behaves under extreme viewpoint changes where reference warping provides no overlap.
