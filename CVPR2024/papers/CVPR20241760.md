## Problem

Semi-supervised domain generalization (SSDG) requires learning domain-generalizable features from multiple source domains where only a small fraction of data is labeled, yet existing DG methods cannot exploit the abundant unlabeled data and SSL baselines still fall well short of fully-supervised DG performance. The core difficulty is generating accurate pseudo-labels when unlabeled data span heterogeneous domain shifts, a problem further aggravated by extreme label scarcity that increases overfitting risk.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241760/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from multiple source domains, sampled in minibatches of 16 labeled and 16 unlabeled images per domain, and outputs a class label from a fixed set of C classes for an unseen target domain. Labeled images are merged with unlabeled ones (labels dropped) for all unsupervised loss computations, following the StyleMatch protocol. The backbone is an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 with a single-layer MLP classifier head, and the method introduces no additional learnable parameters.

## Method

The approach adds two parameter-free modules to any SSL-based SSDG baseline. First, Feature-based Conformity (FBC) builds domain-aware class prototypes by averaging penultimate-layer features of labeled images per (domain, class) pair, then for each unlabeled feature computes cosine similarities against same-domain and randomly chosen different-domain prototypes, converts them to posteriors via softmax, and aligns both posteriors with the model's pseudo-label through cross-entropy losses. Second, a Semantics Alignment (SA) loss regularizes the feature-space layout by maximizing similarity to the assigned prototype while repelling hard non-assigned prototypes (top-N nearest neighbors excluding the assigned one) in the same domain, and additionally aligning the feature to the same-class prototype in a randomly selected different domain. The total loss is the sum of the baseline's supervised and unsupervised losses plus L_FBC and L_SA, making the method plug-and-play and model-agnostic.

## Results

On the 10-labels-per-class SSDG setting, the method (applied to FixMatch) achieves 78.2% on PACS, 59.0% on OfficeHome, 72.2% on VLCS, 70.4% on DigitsDG, and 34.7% on TerraIncognita, improving over the FixMatch baseline by 1.6%, 1.2%, 2.2%, 4.0%, and 4.2% respectively. With StyleMatch as the backbone, the method reaches 80.5% on PACS and 60.3% on OfficeHome, the best figures across all domains in the 10-label setting. Under the more challenging 5-labels-per-class setting, gains are amplified: FixMatch+Ours improves by up to 6.0% on DigitsDG and 4.3% on TerraIncognita over the FixMatch baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is integrated into four SSL/SSDG baselines (FixMatch, FlexMatch, FreeMatch, StyleMatch) and consistently improves all of them across all five datasets in both 10-label and 5-label settings. The largest absolute gains appear on DigitsDG (+4.0% with FixMatch, +4.2% with FlexMatch at 10 labels; +6.0% with FixMatch at 5 labels) and TerraIncognita (+4.2% with FixMatch at 10 labels; +4.3% at 5 labels), where domain shifts include corruption, illumination, and perspective changes that pixel-space methods like StyleMatch struggle with. StyleMatch+Ours achieves the best overall accuracy on PACS (80.5%) and OfficeHome (60.3%) in the 10-label setting, while FixMatch+Ours leads on DigitsDG (70.4%) and TerraIncognita (34.7%). Traditional DG baselines (ERM, EntMin, MeanTeacher) are substantially outperformed, confirming that exploiting unlabeled data via SSL is critical under label scarcity. Notably, StyleMatch fails to improve over its FixMatch backbone on DigitsDG and TerraIncognita (where no style shift is present), whereas the proposed feature-space method delivers consistent gains regardless of shift type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FixMatch's pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization pipeline and on StyleMatch's extension of FixMatch to the semi-supervised domain generalization (SSDG) setting. The key conceptual departure is moving consistency enforcement from the input pixel space (where StyleMatch operates under a style-variance assumption) to the feature space, where domain-aware class prototypes enable more flexible alignment across heterogeneous domain shifts. The proposed feature-based conformity (FBC) and semantics alignment (SA) modules are parameter-free regularizers that complement, rather than replace, the baseline's supervised and unsupervised losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSDG addresses a highly practical scenario (healthcare, autonomous driving) where labeled data is scarce across multiple source domains, and the plug-and-play, parameter-free nature of FBC+SA makes it easy to adopt alongside existing SSL baselines. A notable finding is that pixel-space methods like StyleMatch degrade on non-style shifts (corruption, background, illumination) while the feature-space approach maintains gains across all shift types, suggesting feature-space consistency is more robust to diverse real-world distribution changes. The authors acknowledge that adapting the method to semi-supervised single-source DG would require significant modifications, and the evaluation is limited to ResNet-18 on five medium-scale benchmarks, leaving open questions about scalability to larger architectures and datasets.
