## Problem

Web-collected vision-language datasets used for contrastive pre-training suffer from two under-addressed flaws: semantically near-duplicate images or captions are incorrectly treated as negative pairs, and raw captions are noisy, repetitive, and often irrelevant to their images. While prior work has partially acknowledged these issues, the paper argues that the full potential of fixing them—particularly through multi-positive training—has not been realized.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized/cropped to 224×224) and text captions (truncated to 77 tokens), but critically augments each image with up to 5 synthetic pseudo-captions generated offline by BLIP-2 via beam search. The output is a pair of image and text embeddings in a shared space, used for zero-shot classification (via prompt templates) and bidirectional image-text retrieval. The assignment matrix M, computed on-the-fly from a pre-trained model's features, dynamically determines which image-caption pairs within a batch are positive versus negative, yielding a variable number of positives per image.

## Method

FFF addresses false negatives by constructing an assignment matrix M from three cosine similarity matrices (image-text, image-image, text-text) using threshold-based logical rules (Eq. 1), re-labeling near-duplicate pairs as true positives. It addresses low caption quality via batch text augmentation: all 5 pseudo-captions per image are packed into the same training batch as simultaneous positives, increasing caption diversity and allowing ensemble correction of individual caption errors. Because both mechanisms produce a variable number of positives per sample, the standard contrastive (InfoNCE) loss is unsuitable; instead, the paper adopts a sigmoid (BCE) loss with a binary mask, which natively supports arbitrary positive counts and is robust to residual noise. A learnable scalar β is initialized via grid search over sampled batches to minimize the initial loss, preventing the model from being forced to learn incorrect assignments early in training.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across 11 datasets (pre-trained on YFCC15M with ViT-B/32), FFF achieves 50.8% average accuracy, surpassing the previous best HiDeCLIP (44.6%) by 6.2 points and setting a new state-of-the-art on ImageNet at 51.1%. For zero-shot retrieval, FFF achieves 85.3% R@1 on Flickr30k text retrieval and 67.6% R@1 on Flickr30k image retrieval, outperforming the prior best ALIP by 14.8 and 18.7 points respectively. On MSCOCO, FFF reaches 61.7% R@1 (text) and 44.3% R@1 (image), improving over ALIP by 14.9 and 15.0 points. With a ResNet-50 backbone on CC3M/CC12M, FFF also leads on ImageNet zero-shot (33.4% and 47.4% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFF outperforms all 13 compared baselines on the 11-dataset zero-shot classification benchmark, with the largest margin over the previous best HiDeCLIP (+6.2% average) and ALIP (+9.1% average). Notably, FFF achieves the best result on 10 out of 11 individual datasets, with particularly large gains on Stanford Cars (16.3% vs 5.4% for HiDeCLIP) and Pets (59.8% vs 43.6%). For retrieval, FFF dominates ALIP (the closest competitor using synthetic captions) by 14.8–18.7% R@1 on Flickr30k and 14.9–15.0% R@1 on MSCOCO, while also surpassing HiDeCLIP by 14.4–18.7% R@1 on Flickr30k image retrieval. Compared to the original CLIP baseline, FFF improves average classification accuracy by 19.0 points and Flickr30k text retrieval R@1 by 50.4 points. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that fixing incorrect negatives contributes +2.7 to +3.5 points depending on caption configuration, while batch text augmentation with 5 pseudo-captions adds a further +7.8 points over a single caption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's contrastive image-text pre-training framework and adopts the sigmoid (BCE) loss from SigLIP as its training objective. It departs from prior noise-handling approaches (soft-label distillation in SoftCLIP, optimal transport in [48], progressive self-distillation in [1]) by instead using hard multi-positive labels constructed via an online mining algorithm over image-text, image-image, and text-text cosine similarities. Compared to ALIP, which adds a single synthetic pseudo-caption with a consistency gate, FFF introduces batch text augmentation—training simultaneously with five pseudo-captions per image in the same batch—and combines this with false-negative correction, a combination not explored in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the vision-language pre-training community has been leaving substantial performance on the table by treating noisy web-collected data at face value; fixing false negatives and improving caption diversity through simple pipeline changes (no architectural modifications) yields gains of ~6% on classification and ~19% on retrieval, which is unusually large for this area. The finding that jointly training with multiple pseudo-captions in one batch substantially outperforms random sampling of a single caption (7% drop on YFCC) suggests that the ensemble effect within a batch is critical and non-obvious. Open questions include how the approach scales to much larger datasets (LAION-5B scale), whether the mining thresholds transfer across model sizes, and whether the pre-trained model used for similarity computation introduces a circularity that could limit gains at scale.
