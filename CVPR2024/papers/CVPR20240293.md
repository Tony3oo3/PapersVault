## Problem

The standard DomainBed evaluation protocol for domain generalization suffers from two sources of test data information leakage: supervised pretraining on ImageNet (which resembles many test domains) and oracle model selection on a single test domain. This means reported OOD generalization performance may reflect exploitation of leaked test information rather than true generalization from training domains, making algorithm comparisons unreliable.

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes RGB images from unseen test domains across six benchmark datasets (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraInc, DomainNet, NICO++) and produces class labels via standard image classification. The key protocol detail is that for DomainNet and NICO++, the model is evaluated on two test domains simultaneously (leave-one-group-out) rather than a single domain, while smaller datasets retain the leave-one-domain-out strategy. Pretrained weights (MoCo-v2 ResNet-50 or MoCo-v3 ViT-B/16) serve as initialization, and the model is fine-tuned on training domains before testing.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical analysis showing that linear-probing (relying on pretrained features) outperforms full fine-tuning (relying on training data) when test domains are similar to ImageNet, with the gap correlating with FID distance to ImageNet—evidence that supervised pretrained weights leak test information. It then demonstrates that evaluating on multiple test domains (K=2 or K=3) reduces oracle model selection leakage by 30–79% compared to single-domain evaluation. Based on these findings, the authors propose a revised protocol using self-supervised pretrained weights (MoCo-v2 for ResNet-50, MoCo-v3 for ViT-B/16) or training from scratch, combined with multi-domain testing, and re-evaluate ten representative algorithms (ERM, SWAD, RSC, GroupDRO, Fishr, CORAL, MMD, SagNet, IRM, Mixup) to produce new leaderboards that reveal significant ranking shifts compared to the original DomainBed protocol.

## Results

Under the revised protocol with MoCo-v2 pretrained ResNet-50, SWAD achieves the highest average accuracy of 69.0% across PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, NICO++, and DomainNet, followed by RSC (67.9%) and CORAL (67.8%). Under MoCo-v3 pretrained ViT-B/16, SWAD leads with 73.8% average, RSC follows at 72.0%, and CORAL at 71.9%. The paper further shows that self-supervised pretraining leaderboards correlate more strongly with training-from-scratch rankings (Spearman ρ = 0.576 for MoCo-v2, 0.600 for MoCo-v3) than supervised pretraining does (ρ = 0.261), confirming that self-supervised weights better isolate true OOD generalization from test-data leakage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWAD (Cha et al., NeurIPS 2021) is the only algorithm that consistently ranks 1st across all three leaderboards (supervised, MoCo-v2, MoCo-v3), improving over ERM by 3.3–4.0 points in average accuracy, confirming its robustness to evaluation protocol changes. RSC (Huang et al., ECCV 2020) shows the largest positive rank shift (+5), jumping from 7th under supervised pretraining to 2nd under both self-supervised protocols, suggesting its self-challenging mechanism captures genuine OOD generalization rather than exploiting pretrained features. Conversely, Fishr (Rame et al., ICML 2022) drops from 3rd to 8th (−5) under MoCo-v2, and SagNet (Nam et al., CVPR 2021) falls from 4th to 9th (−5), indicating their apparent superiority under the old protocol was partly an artifact of supervised pretraining leakage. MMD (Li et al., CVPR 2018) improves from 10th to 5th (+5) under MoCo-v2, while CORAL (Sun et al., ECCV 2016) drops only slightly (2nd→3rd), and ERM (Vapnik, 1991) remains stable at 6th. The paper concludes that algorithms relying on intrinsic generalization properties (flatness in SWAD, dropout in RSC) are less sensitive to test-data leakage and better reflect true OOD generalization ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DomainBed [19], which established the standard training and evaluation protocol for domain generalization (model backbone, data splits, hyperparameter search, oracle model selection). It departs from this protocol by identifying two specific sources of test data information leakage: supervised ImageNet pretraining and single-test-domain oracle model selection. The pretraining analysis connects to the broader self-supervised learning literature (MoCo, SimCLR, MoCo-v3) and to Kumar et al.'s [37] theoretical observation that fine-tuning can distort pretrained features under distribution shift. The multi-test-domain recommendation extends the evaluation framing from DomainBed's single-test-domain optimization target (Equation 1) toward a more realistic multi-domain generalization setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that algorithm rankings shift dramatically under the revised protocol (e.g., RSC jumps from 7th to 2nd, while recent SOTA methods Fishr and SagNet drop 5 positions) implies that a substantial portion of published domain generalization results may reflect exploitation of pretrained weights rather than genuine OOD generalization ability. The new leaderboards with MoCo-v2/v3 pretraining and multi-test-domain evaluation provide a more faithful comparison baseline for future work. Open questions include whether self-supervised pretraining is the optimal compromise between leakage mitigation and practical relevance, whether the protocol is sufficient for very large pretrained models (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2), and the need for larger datasets to support more test domains and training-from-scratch evaluation beyond DomainNet and NICO++.
