## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP achieve strong zero-shot performance on third-person datasets but drop dramatically on egocentric videos (e.g., 8.8% noun and 5.9% verb on Epic-Kitchens), revealing a significant domain gap. Existing adaptation methods (prompt learning, uni-modal adapters) do not account for egocentric-specific characteristics such as hand-object interactions and camera motion, and cross-dataset generalization for fine-grained action recognition remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240367/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric video clip (16 frames sampled during training, uniform sampling at inference), a set of fine-grained text class labels (nouns and verbs), and per-frame hand bounding boxes from the 100DOH detector. For each frame, both the full frame and a hand crop are encoded, producing a 2×D intra-frame sequence. The output is a predicted noun or verb class, determined by matching the frozen video representation against a per-video adapted text classifier in the shared VL embedding space.

## Method

X-MIC introduces a lightweight video adapter that produces a single X-MIC-vector per video, which is added directly to each frozen text class embedding to form a video-specific classifier—requiring no gradient propagation through either the text or visual encoders. The adapter comprises an Ego-Spatio-Temporal Attention Module with two transformer blocks: a spatial block (b_S) applies self-attention between full-frame and hand-crop representations to emphasize hand-object interactions, and a temporal block (b_T) aggregates information across frames. A second frozen visual encoder V_II (which can differ from the primary encoder, e.g., DINO instead of CLIP) provides the intermediate frame features fed into the adapter, decoupling domain-specific temporal and spatial modeling from the frozen VL backbone. This late-fusion, cross-modal design means each video receives its own adapted text classifier, enabling efficient per-instance conditioning and improved cross-dataset generalization without fine-tuning the pre-trained encoders.

## Results

On the primary cross-dataset evaluation (trained on Ego4D, evaluated on both Ego4D and Epic-Kitchens), X-MIC achieves a harmonic mean of 21.06% for nouns and 27.65% for verbs, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines (Vita-CLIP: 16.11% nouns hm, 24.13% verbs hm). Adding DINO as the secondary visual encoder (X-MIC+DINO) further boosts noun recognition to 24.80% hm and verb recognition to 28.86% hm. On the reverse direction (trained on Epic-Kitchens), X-MIC reaches 17.57% hm for nouns and 26.58% hm for verbs, again leading all baselines. Zero-shot CLIP performs very poorly in this egocentric setting, with noun accuracy as low as 5.89% and verb accuracy of 2.18% on Ego4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-MIC outperforms all six baselines on the cross-dataset noun harmonic mean when trained on Ego4D (21.06% vs. Vita-CLIP's 16.11%, a 5-point gap) and on verb harmonic mean (27.65% vs. CLIP-Adapter*'s 24.54%). Within-dataset noun accuracy on Ego4D is essentially tied with Vita-CLIP (33.54% vs. 33.52%), but X-MIC's cross-dataset noun score on Epic-Kitchens (15.35%) exceeds Vita-CLIP (10.61%) by nearly 5 points, confirming the generalization advantage of the cross-modal adapter design. For verbs, X-MIC leads within Ego4D (28.93%) over CLIP-Adapter* (27.32%) and A5 (26.31%), and leads cross-dataset on Epic-Kitchens (26.48%) over Vita-CLIP (25.81%). Early-fusion prompt methods (CoOp, CoCoOp) consistently underperform on cross-dataset evaluation, with CoCoOp dropping to 9.51% nouns on Epic-Kitchens versus X-MIC's 15.35%. Zero-shot CLIP is far below all adapted methods, confirming the significant domain gap between web-scale pre-training and egocentric video. The X-MIC+DINO variant further extends the lead, reaching 24.80% noun hm and 28.86% verb hm when trained on Ego4D, demonstrating that swapping the secondary visual encoder for a self-supervised model (DINO) captures complementary visual features beyond CLIP's object-centric representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-MIC extends the late-fusion adapter paradigm of CLIP-Adapter by replacing uni-modal feature blending with cross-modal instance conditioning: a video-specific vector (the X-MIC-vector) is added directly to frozen text embeddings in the shared VL space, rather than adapting the visual encoder or appending learnable prompts. The temporal aggregation module draws on the self-attention design of A5, but decouples it entirely from the frozen visual backbone, so temporal modeling is learned in a lightweight adapter rather than through gradient propagation through the encoder. This positions X-MIC as a 'late fusion + cross-modal' method, distinct from early-fusion prompt learning (CoOp, CoCoOp) and from video-specific prompt methods (Vita-CLIP, A5) that still require backpropagation through the text encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-dataset generalization is essential for deploying egocentric recognition in real-world AR and robotics settings where data from every new environment cannot be collected, yet prior work has focused almost exclusively on within-dataset evaluation. The paper's finding that an egocentric-pretrained backbone (Lavila) substantially improves noun recognition but not verb recognition—contrary to expectations—suggests that the object-centric bias of CLIP-style pre-training is deeply embedded and not easily corrected by domain-specific contrastive training. A stated limitation is that X-MIC only supports video classification and does not extend to text-to-video retrieval, which would require inverting the conditioning direction. Whether the cross-modal instance conditioning principle transfers to other domain gaps (e.g., third-person to egocentric, or across sensor modalities) remains an open question.
