## Problem

SAM is class-agnostic and requires manual prompts, making it difficult to adapt to diverse downstream segmentation tasks without full fine-tuning. Existing adaptation strategies—PEFT (adapter/LoRA), in-context learning, and text-guided prompting—suffer from requiring gradient access to frozen backbones, heavy data dependence, or failure on implicit semantics like saliency. The paper seeks a unified, parameter-frozen, sample-efficient framework that automatically generates informative prompts for both explicit and implicit semantic tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes an 800×800 RGB image (divided into 10×10 patches of 80×80 for the action space) and, for explicit-semantic tasks, a short text prompt (e.g., 'shadow', 'glass', or a category name) processed by CLIP-Surgery. It produces a binary segmentation mask from SAM's frozen mask decoder, refined over T=15 iterative prompting steps. The Semantic Recalibration Module additionally outputs a per-prompt binary label (foreground vs. background) that determines whether each selected point is added as a positive or negative prompt.

## Method

AlignSAM formulates automatic prompting as a sequential decision-making problem solved by a PPO-based RL agent (actor-critic) that iteratively selects point prompts from 100 patch-center candidates over 15 timesteps, with SAM's parameters kept entirely frozen. The state at each step is the product of SAM's image-encoder features and the previous mask prediction, and the reward is +1/−1 depending on whether the chosen point falls on ground-truth foreground. A Semantic Recalibration Module (SRM) converts the coarse RL policy into fine-grained prompt labels via a switchable dual-branch design: an implicit branch (two conv blocks on visual state only) for abstract tasks like saliency, and an explicit branch (three conv layers + self-attention fusing CLIP text-attention and visual state) for concrete object segmentation. The SRM is trained with Dice + BCE loss, while the RL agent is optimized with PPO (γ=0.99, ε=0.20, 50 episodes × 20 epochs), requiring only 50 training samples per task and no gradient flow through the frozen backbone.

## Results

On four diverse segmentation benchmarks, AlignSAM achieves the best performance across all reported metrics: 68.47 mIoU on blur detection (CUHK), 30.78 mIoU on shadow detection (SBU), 45.44 mIoU on glass detection (MSD), and 0.082 MAE on saliency detection (DUTS). On Pascal-VOC 2012 semantic segmentation, AlignSAM reaches 62.09 mean IoU, surpassing the next-best method (Painter) by 2.82 points. All experiments use only 50 training samples per task, demonstrating strong sample efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignSAM outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task. Against the PEFT methods SAMed [59] and MSA [51], AlignSAM gains +13.03 mIoU on blur detection (68.47 vs 55.44) and +14.97 mIoU on Pascal-VOC (62.09 vs 47.12), while also requiring no gradient access to the frozen backbone. Against the in-context learning method PerSAM [60], the margin is +12.63 mIoU on shadow detection (30.78 vs 18.68) and +14.26 mIoU on glass detection (45.44 vs 31.18). Against the foundation segmentation models SEEM [68] and Painter [48], AlignSAM leads by +11.26 mIoU on shadow (30.78 vs 19.52) and +36.83 mIoU on glass (45.44 vs 8.62). The only metric where a competitor edges ahead is saliency Eφ, where Painter scores 81.06 vs AlignSAM's 78.21, though AlignSAM still dominates on the complementary MAE metric (0.082 vs 0.113).

## Relation to Prior Work

AlignSAM departs from the dominant paradigm of adapting SAM via parameter-efficient fine-tuning (SAM-Adapter, LoRA) or in-context similarity matching (PerSAM, Painter), both of which require gradient access to the backbone or abundant training data. Instead, it borrows the model-free spirit of reinforcement learning (as in RAM and reinforced active segmentation) and reframes automatic prompting as a sequential decision-making problem solved with PPO, keeping SAM entirely frozen. The semantic recalibration module draws on CLIP-Surgery's attention maps for explicit semantics and on SAM's own iterative predictions for implicit semantics, unifying two previously separate adaptation strategies under one switchable architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a small RL agent trained on only 50 samples per task can outperform gradient-based PEFT and in-context learning methods across diverse binary segmentation tasks, suggesting that the bottleneck in adapting foundation models may be prompt quality rather than parameter adaptation. However, the approach still requires ground-truth masks to compute the binary reward during training, limiting its applicability to fully unsupervised or open-vocabulary settings. Open questions include whether the RL formulation generalizes to multi-class or instance-level segmentation, whether it transfers to other foundation models beyond SAM, and whether the 50-sample regime scales to more complex or long-tail distributions.
