## Problem

Existing HOI datasets and capture methods are restricted to single-person, single-object interactions, leaving a critical data gap for modeling the multi-person, multi-object social interactions that dominate real-world settings. Accurate 3D tracking of multiple humans and objects simultaneously is further hampered by severe occlusion and the absence of a robust, scalable capture pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240136/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the monocular capture task, a single 512×512 RGB image is consumed to produce per-person SMPL parameters (θ, β) with absolute camera-centric root depth, and per-object 6D pose (R∈R³ˣ³, T∈R³) with center depth, all in a single forward pass. For the generation task, the input is a set of pre-scanned object geometry point clouds plus integer counts of people (≤5) and objects (≤10), and the output is a 500-dimensional motion vector encoding 5 people (88 dims each: pose, shape, translation, orientation) and 10 objects (6 dims each: translation, pose). The underlying dataset provides 42 synchronized 4K/60fps RGB views plus per-object IMU streams as ground-truth supervision.

## Method

The paper contributes two baselines. The monocular capture method is a one-stage network that jointly predicts a human-object center heatmap, a human mesh map (regressing SMPL θ, β and FoV-normalized root depth), and an object mesh map (regressing 6D rotation and center depth) from a shared backbone, supervised by a weighted sum of parameter L1, 2D/3D keypoint, heatmap MSE, and depth losses. The dataset's ground-truth labels are produced by a multi-view pipeline: ViTPose 2D detection, cross-view matching, SMPL fitting via Easymocap for humans, and an IMU-aided per-frame optimization for objects that minimizes a composite energy of differentiable-rendering mask loss, offscreen penalty, collision constraint, and IMU smoothness. The generation method is a conditional DDPM whose conditions are object geometry features extracted by PointNet and person/object count embeddings passed through an MLP; the denoising network learns to reconstruct the 500-dim motion vector from pure noise over N diffusion steps, trained with an L1 reconstruction loss on x₀.

## Results

On the monocular multiple HOI capture benchmark (Table 2), the proposed method achieves 68.5% PCKrel and 5.9% PCKabs for human pose, and 235.0 Chamfer distance / 297.8 V2V for object pose (All setting). For the generation task (Table 3), the diffusion model yields a joint FID of 36.906±0.087 and a joint penetration rate (Pene) of 9.265% across 20 samples. The proposed method is the only one able to report PCKabs, as the single-HOI baselines rely on a weak-projection camera model that precludes absolute depth estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art single-HOI monocular capture methods: PHOSA [65] and CHORE [52]. The proposed method decisively outperforms both on every reported metric. On human pose, it achieves 68.5% PCKrel versus 43.9% for PHOSA and 10.4% for CHORE (All setting), a gap of 24.6 and 58.1 percentage points respectively. On object pose, the Chamfer distance drops to 235.0 from 1454.3 (PHOSA) and 465.8 (CHORE), roughly a 6× and 2× improvement. Notably, neither baseline can report PCKabs because their weak-projection camera model does not support absolute depth, whereas the proposed method reports 5.9% PCKabs. The authors note that PHOSA and CHORE were adapted by selecting the best-IOU human-object pair per frame, and that these methods require the number of objects to be preset, so their 'All' and 'Matched' columns are identical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HOI capture and generation literature from single-person, single-object settings (BEHAVE, InterCap, GRAB, NeuralDome) to the multi-person, multi-object regime, which the authors argue has been entirely absent from prior 3D MoCap datasets. The capture pipeline builds on established multi-view triangulation, SMPL fitting via Easymocap, and IMU-aided tracking, while the generation baseline adapts DDPM-style diffusion (as in Ego-Ego) to a joint multi-entity representation. The monocular capture method draws on single-shot multi-person pose estimation (SMAP, ViTPose) and differentiable rendering, but is the first to jointly regress both human SMPL parameters and object 6D poses in a unified one-stage framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOI-M³ is the first large-scale real-world dataset (181M frames, 42 synchronized 4K/60fps cameras, 90 objects, 31 subjects) providing accurate 3D tracking for multiple humans and multiple objects simultaneously, unblocking data-driven research on social interaction modeling that was previously infeasible due to data scarcity. The preliminary baselines confirm both tasks remain highly challenging (PCKabs ≈ 6%, joint penetration ≈ 9.3%), signaling substantial room for future work. Key limitations include indoor-only settings, five fixed scenes, controlled illumination, and hardware cost constraints that limit outdoor generalization, leaving open whether the capture and generation methods transfer to unstructured real-world environments.
