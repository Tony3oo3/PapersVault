## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to faithfully render all elements of a multi-concept prompt, exhibiting missing objects, incorrect attribute binding, and miscounting. Existing remedies (Attend & Excite, Divide & Bind, etc.) each target a single failure mode with a bespoke objective, making them brittle on complex prompts that combine multiple issues simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language text prompt (e.g., 'a green dog and a white clock') and outputs a single RGB image, operating entirely at test time without any retraining. It intervenes on the 16×16 cross-attention maps extracted from the UNet denoiser at selected backward timesteps, and is compatible with both latent-space models (Stable Diffusion v1.5) and pixel-space models (Imagen with T5-XXL text encoder). Each generation takes roughly 20 seconds on an NVIDIA L4 GPU using 50 optimization iterations.

## Method

CONFORM formulates prompt fidelity as a contrastive learning problem over cross-attention maps: tokens belonging to the same object-attribute group (e.g., 'green' and 'dog') are positive pairs, while all cross-group token pairs are negatives. An InfoNCE loss with cosine similarity is computed on the attention maps at consecutive timesteps t and t+1, which also enforces temporal consistency and prevents attention scattering during the backward pass. The latent code is then updated via gradient descent on this single contrastive term (z'_t = z_t − α_t ∇_{z_t} L) at iterations 0, 10, and 20, with optimization halted after iteration 25 to avoid artifacts. The approach is training-free, model-agnostic, and requires only a scale factor α=20 and temperature τ=0.5, making it a drop-in enhancement for any cross-attention-based diffusion pipeline.

## Results

On the TIFA faithfulness metric, CONFORM achieves 0.95/0.94/0.88/0.74 across the four Stable Diffusion benchmark categories (Animal-Animal, Animal-Obj, Obj-Obj, Multi-Obj), outperforming Attend & Excite (0.92/0.91/0.82/0.72) and Divide & Bind (0.93/0.91/0.83/0.73). For Imagen, CONFORM reaches 0.84/0.94/0.91/0.76, improving over the Imagen baseline (0.84/0.93/0.88/0.73) particularly on Obj-Obj and Multi-Obj. In the user study with 25 participants, CONFORM receives 72–94% of votes for Stable Diffusion and 96–98% for Imagen, far exceeding all baselines. On CLIP text-text similarity (via BLIP captions), CONFORM scores 0.82/0.85/0.82, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONFORM outperforms Attend & Excite [6] on all four TIFA benchmark categories for Stable Diffusion, with the largest gap on Obj-Obj (0.88 vs. 0.82) and Multi-Obj (0.74 vs. 0.72). It also beats Divide & Bind [22] across all SD categories (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.93 on Animal-Animal, 0.88 vs. 0.83 on Obj-Obj). Against the Imagen baseline [37], CONFORM ties on Animal-Animal (0.84) but improves on Animal-Obj (0.94 vs. 0.93), Obj-Obj (0.91 vs. 0.88), and Multi-Obj (0.76 vs. 0.73). ComposableDiffusion [23] and StructureDiffusion [10] are only compared on CLIP text-text similarity (Table 1), where CONFORM leads (0.82/0.85/0.82 vs. 0.69/0.77/0.76 and 0.76/0.78/0.76 respectively). In the user study, CONFORM dominates overwhelmingly: 72–94% preference for SD and 96–98% for Imagen, compared to at most 14.75% for A&E and 8.25% for D&B on SD. A-Star [1] is acknowledged as a competitor but excluded from quantitative comparison due to unavailable code.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONFORM builds on the test-time latent optimization paradigm established by Attend & Excite [6] and A-Star [1], both of which update the diffusion latent via gradient steps on attention-related losses. However, it departs from prior work by replacing the collection of tailored, problem-specific objectives (one for missing objects, another for attribute binding, another for counting) with a single unified contrastive (InfoNCE) loss that simultaneously repels unrelated tokens and attracts related ones in the cross-attention space. A further distinction is the incorporation of cross-timestep attention consistency, pulling attention maps from consecutive denoising steps together to counteract the observed scattering of attribute attention during the backward process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By subsuming multiple fidelity failure modes under one training-free, model-agnostic contrastive objective, CONFORM offers a simpler and more general recipe than the patchwork of bespoke losses in prior work, and its applicability to both latent-space (Stable Diffusion) and pixel-space (Imagen) diffusion models broadens its practical reach. The paper acknowledges that the method struggles when the initial attention map already omits a key object entirely, and that in Imagen the optimization can sometimes over-separate objects spatially. Open questions include whether the contrastive formulation extends to video diffusion, longer/more complex prompts with many interacting subjects, or settings where the text encoder's tokenization further complicates attribute-object pairing.
