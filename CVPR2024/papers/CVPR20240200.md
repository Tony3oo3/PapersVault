## Problem

Pre-trained deblurring networks fail on images captured by cameras whose blur characteristics differ from their training data, and collecting paired blurry-sharp training data for a new camera requires expensive dual-camera hardware with beam splitters and precise synchronization. Existing unsupervised deblurring methods that work with unpaired data struggle to reconstruct fine details lost to real-world motion blur, leaving a gap for practical, camera-specific deblurring without paired supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240200/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single blurry RGB image from the target camera and produces a deblurred image via a two-stage pipeline: a blur translator converts the unknown-blur image to a known-blur image, which is then fed to a pre-trained deblurring network (NAFNet or Restormer). For training the blur translator, the user only needs unpaired sets of blurry images (B) and sharp images (S) captured by the same camera, plus a reference paired dataset (e.g., GoPro) to define the target blur domain. Training uses 256×256 crops with rotation, flip, and color-jitter augmentation.

## Method

The key insight is decomposing the hard blur-to-sharp mapping into an easier blur-to-blur translation G (from unknown domain C to known domain C′) followed by a pre-trained deblurring network G*_{C′}. The blur translator G is a MIMO-UNet trained with a WGAN-GP-style adversarial loss (gradient penalty for Lipschitz constraint) to match the target blur distribution, combined with a multi-scale VGG19 perceptual loss to preserve image content. Crucially, the known-blur training set K is synthesized by applying a Blur Kernel Extractor [37] to transfer blur kernels from a reference paired dataset onto the user's own sharp images S, ensuring K and B share all camera-specific characteristics (color, noise, resolution) except the blur kernel, so the discriminator learns to distinguish blur patterns rather than other artifacts. This plug-and-play framework requires no paired data from the target camera and can be combined with any pre-trained deblurring backbone.

## Results

On three real-world deblurring benchmarks (RB2V_Street, REDS, RSBlur), Blur2Blur combined with NAFNet achieves PSNR/SSIM of 26.98/0.812, 28.11/0.893, and 29.00/0.857 respectively, improving over the same backbone trained directly on GoPro by +2.20, +2.31, and +2.67 dB. With Restormer, the gains are +2.63, +2.12, and +2.91 dB over the GoPro-trained baseline. The method approaches the upper bound of fully supervised training on the source domain (e.g., 26.98 vs. 28.72 PSNR on RB2V_Street with NAFNet) while requiring only unpaired blurry and sharp images from the target camera.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Blur2Blur+NAFNet decisively outperforms all unpaired baselines: CycleGAN (21.21/23.92/23.34 PSNR) and DualGAN (21.02/23.50/22.78 PSNR) trail by 4.2–6.2 dB across the three datasets, confirming that direct blur-to-sharp translation without paired data is far more difficult than the proposed blur-to-blur reformulation. Against generalized deblurring methods, Blur2Blur+NAFNet surpasses BSRGAN by 1.7–3.7 dB and RSBlur+NAFNet by 1.8–3.6 dB, showing that augmentation-based generalization strategies fail on unseen real-world blur. Compared to the same backbone trained directly on GoPro paired data (NAFNet w/GoPro: 24.78/25.80/26.33), Blur2Blur adds 2.2–2.7 dB, demonstrating that the blur-kernel translation step is more effective than simply applying a pre-trained model to an out-of-domain camera. The method also beats NAFNet trained on synthetic motion blur (22.10/25.07/23.53) by 2.2–5.5 dB, highlighting the inadequacy of synthetic degradation for real-world blur. Overall, Blur2Blur+NAFNet narrows the gap to the fully supervised upper bound (trained on source-domain pairs) to within 1.7–4.1 dB, making it the strongest unsupervised approach evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the blur kernel transfer technique of Tran et al. (CVPR 2021), which provides the Blur Kernel Extractor used to synthesize the known-blur training set K. Conceptually, it departs from both supervised deblurring (which requires paired data from a dual-camera rig) and prior unsupervised methods like CycleGAN and DualGAN (which attempt direct blur-to-sharp translation) by introducing an intermediate blur-to-blur translation step. The method composes a GAN-based blur translator (using MIMO-UNet with adversarial and perceptual losses, in the spirit of Pix2Pix) with a frozen pre-trained deblurring backbone (NAFNet or Restormer), effectively repurposing supervised deblurring models within an unsupervised framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach addresses a genuinely practical gap: deploying effective deblurring for a specific camera without the expensive paired-data collection pipeline. The plug-and-play design means any strong pre-trained deblurring network can be adapted to a new camera domain with only unpaired blurry and sharp captures. Limitations include the need for camera access to collect data, dependence on the quality of the chosen known-blur domain, and the assumption that a suitable pre-trained deblurring model exists for the target blur. Open questions include how well the method generalizes when the unknown and known blur domains differ substantially in character (e.g., defocus vs. motion), and whether the approach extends naturally to video deblurring.
