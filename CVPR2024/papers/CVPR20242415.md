## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods suffer from a 'Semantic-Geometric Coupling' gap: they neglect explicit geometric attributes (distance, orientation, shape) of 3D objects, generate object queries without fine-grained language guidance, and perform vision-text alignment in Euclidean space, which cannot capture the non-linear hierarchical relationships inherent in both 3D scenes and free-form text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with N points carrying F-dimensional auxiliary features (RGB, normals, or multi-view features) and a free-form text description of L words as input, and outputs a 3D bounding box (center, height, width) for the referred object. It operates in both one-stage mode (directly from raw point cloud) and two-stage mode (leveraging pre-computed 3D object proposals), with the point cloud tokenized via PointNet++ into n vision tokens and the text encoded by a pre-trained RoBERTa into t text tokens.

## Method

G³-LQ is a DETR-based framework with three novel components. The PAGE module extracts local geometric priors (relative position, distance, orientation) via KNN-based point proxies, applies position-adaptive normalization, and feeds them into a geometric self-attention encoder where geometric features serve as query/key and semantic features as value, followed by cross-attention with text. The Flan-QS module parses the utterance into a language scene graph via syntactic dependency parsing, performs graph convolution with attention to produce context-aware fine-grained text embeddings, and uses these to guide top-K query selection from candidate visual tokens. The PSA loss projects both vision and text features onto a Poincaré ball via exponential mapping and applies contrastive learning on geodesic distances to enforce semantic-geometric consistency, exploiting hyperbolic space's natural capacity to model tree-shaped hierarchies.

## Results

On ScanRefer (two-stage, 3D-only input), G³-LQ achieves 56.90% and 45.58% overall accuracy at IoU 0.25 and 0.5, surpassing the previous SOTA EDA by 2.31% and 2.34% respectively. In the one-stage setting (no external 3D detector), G³-LQ reaches 55.95% and 44.72%, still outperforming EDA one-stage by 2.12% and 3.02%. On Nr3D/Sr3D, G³-LQ attains 58.4% (Overall) and 73.1% (Overall) accuracy, improving over 3D-VisTA by 0.9% and 3.5% respectively, with gains of 1.3% and 2.7% on the harder subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G³-LQ outperforms all 18 compared baselines on both ScanRefer and Nr3D/Sr3D. On ScanRefer (two-stage), it surpasses the closest competitor EDA [43] by 2.31% (IoU 0.25) and 2.34% (IoU 0.5) overall, and by 2.35% on the challenging Multiple subset (51.48% vs. 49.13% at IoU 0.25). Among 2D-assisted methods, G³-LQ (3D-only input) beats 3D-SPS [31] by 8.08% and 3D-VLP [21] by 5.49% overall at IoU 0.25, demonstrating that explicit geometric modeling compensates for the absence of 2D image priors. Among DETR-like one-stage methods, G³-LQ one-stage exceeds EDA one-stage by 2.12% (IoU 0.25) and 3.02% (IoU 0.5), and BUTD-DETR one-stage by 6.19% and 7.67%. On Nr3D/Sr3D, G³-LQ leads 3D-VisTA [51] by 0.9% (Nr3D Overall) and 3.5% (Sr3D Overall), with the gap widening to 1.3% and 2.7% on the Hard subsets, indicating stronger disambiguation of geometrically similar objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the EDA framework [43], adopting its DETR-like architecture, cross-modal decoder, and contrastive alignment structure, while departing in three key directions: (1) explicit geometric feature extraction via KNN-based point proxies (PAGE), which prior 3D VG methods largely overlook; (2) fine-grained query selection guided by a language scene graph constructed via dependency parsing, replacing the coarse sentence-level text features used in EDA and BUTD-DETR; and (3) a Poincaré ball-based alignment loss (PSA) that replaces the standard Euclidean contrastive loss, motivated by the hierarchical nature of both 3D scenes and natural language. The geometric modeling draws on point cloud literature (DGCNN, RSCNN, HGNet), while the hyperbolic embedding draws on hyperbolic neural network research (Nickel & Kiela, Ganea et al.), repurposing these ideas for cross-modal 3D grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit geometric-semantic coupling framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for 3D VG: it identifies that prior methods conflate shape, spatial layout, and linguistic reference in a single undifferentiated feature space. The Poincaré ball alignment is an interesting but modestly validated idea—its individual ablation gain is small (~0.4–0.8%), and it remains unclear whether hyperbolic geometry offers advantages beyond what a well-tuned Euclidean contrastive loss achieves at scale. Open questions include whether the language scene graph approach generalizes to longer or noisier instructions, whether the geometric priors transfer to dynamic or large-scale scenes, and whether the hyperbolic alignment scales to multi-object or multi-turn grounding settings.
