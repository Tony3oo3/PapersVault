## Problem

Existing 3D human-scene interaction (HSI) synthesis methods rely on supervised learning from large-scale 3D interaction datasets, which are prohibitively expensive to capture and annotate (e.g., PROX contains only 12 indoor scenes and 11 object categories). This data dependency severely limits generalization to diverse scene types and arbitrary actions. The paper asks whether plausible 3D HSI can be synthesized in a fully zero-shot manner, without any 3D interaction training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242154/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene mesh, a natural language text prompt describing the desired interaction (e.g., 'sitting on a bench'), and a coarse 3D point location indicating where the interaction should occur. Text prompts are automatically augmented with a fixed prefix ('a woman') and suffix ('wearing a white shirt and blue pants, full body') to constrain generation. The output is a posed SMPL-X body mesh fully parameterized by global orientation (6D rotation), translation, VPoser latent body pose (32-dim), and shape coefficients (10-dim), placed in the scene with physical contact and no penetration.

## Method

GenZI distills interaction priors from a latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion Inpainting) by rendering k=16 views of the scene and inpainting a human into each view using a dynamic masking scheme that leverages cross-attention maps to automatically determine the inpainting region at each denoising step, eliminating manual mask specification. The inpainted 2D images are then processed by AlphaPose to extract 2D joint positions and confidence scores. A robust multi-view 3D optimization lifts these 2D hypotheses to a consistent SMPL-X pose by jointly optimizing view-consistency weights (with a Geman-McClure robust kernel) alongside regularization terms for body pose, shape, scene penetration (via SDF), and self-penetration (via BVH). An iterative refinement step re-renders the optimized 3D human silhouette as a precise mask for a second inpainting pass, improving cross-view consistency. The entire pipeline requires no 3D interaction data and operates in approximately 3 minutes on an A100 GPU.

## Results

On the Sketchfab dataset (8 scenes, 38 actions), GenZI achieves the best semantic consistency (CLIP 0.2710), diversity entropy (2.7304), and non-collision score (0.9767) among all methods. In the binary perceptual study with 30 participants, GenZI is preferred over all baselines more than 87% of the time, and in the unary realism test it scores 3.6/5 while all baselines remain below 2.0. The contact score is near-parity (0.9868 for GenZI vs. 1.0000 for the single-view ablation), and the full multi-view approach trades a small amount of cluster-size diversity for substantially better semantic coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenZI outperforms Hassan et al. [10] (single-view 3D human estimation with scene constraints) on every metric except contact: CLIP improves from 0.2598 to 0.2710, non-collision jumps from 0.8824 to 0.9767, and entropy rises from 2.7014 to 2.7304, while contact drops slightly (0.9669 → 0.9868). Against COINS [51] (compositional CVAE trained on the PROX indoor dataset), GenZI wins decisively in the perceptual study (>87% preference) and qualitatively generalizes to outdoor scenes and unseen object geometries where COINS fails due to its closed-set training vocabulary. The single-view ablation (Ours-SV) shows that restricting to one inpainted view yields higher cluster-size diversity (1.5813) but lower semantic consistency (CLIP 0.2613) and slightly worse non-collision (0.9765), confirming that multi-view robust lifting is critical for coherent 3D synthesis. Overall, GenZI is the only method that simultaneously achieves top semantic consistency, competitive physical plausibility, and strong perceptual realism without any 3D interaction training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the learning-based HSI synthesis lineage (PiGraphs, PLACE, POSA, COINS), all of which require supervised training on scarce 3D interaction datasets like PROX (12 scenes, 11 object categories). Instead of learning a 3D interaction prior, GenZI repurposes the 2D generative capacity of latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion Inpainting) as a proxy for interaction semantics, distilling VLM-imagined 2D human-scene compositions into a 3D parametric body via robust multi-view optimization. It also extends the 3D human estimation line (SMPLify-X, Hassan et al.) by replacing single-view fitting with a view-consistent multi-view formulation that simultaneously selects the most informative views, and connects to the broader trend of distilling VLM knowledge for 3D tasks (Text2Room, 3D semantic segmentation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenZI removes the primary bottleneck in HSI synthesis — the need for large-scale 3D interaction capture — by showing that 2D VLM priors are sufficient to guide plausible 3D interaction generation, opening the door to arbitrary scenes and actions without domain-specific training. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 scenes and 38 actions, and the method inherits the slow inference of iterative diffusion (~3 min/sample on A100) and the quality ceiling of current inpainting models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic/temporal interactions, more complex multi-person scenes, or higher-fidelity contact modeling, and whether faster VLMs or alternative distillation strategies can close the gap to learned methods in quality while retaining zero-shot flexibility.
