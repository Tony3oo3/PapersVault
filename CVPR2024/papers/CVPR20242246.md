## Problem

DCNN-based SAR object detectors rely on intensive computation partly because standard convolutions extract redundant, pattern-similar features within a single layer, yet existing compression or lightweight-design strategies degrade detection accuracy. General-purpose convolutions also fail to exploit two SAR-specific priors: the need for object-adaptive receptive fields to handle diverse scales, and the importance of frequency-domain features for distinguishing objects from speckle noise and clutter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242246/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector consumes a 640×640×3 SAR image and directly regresses oriented bounding boxes with class labels from densely sampled grid anchors in a one-stage fashion (no region proposals). During training, an object-level gradient-induced learning (OGL) strategy applies Canny edge detection within ground-truth bounding boxes to emphasize texture cues, but this is discarded at inference so it adds no computational overhead. The network operates at a detection resolution of H/8 × W/8 × 4C after two upsampling stages.

## Method

SFS-Conv implements a shunt-perceive-select strategy within a single convolutional layer. Input channels are split by ratio α into a spatial branch and a frequency branch. The Spatial Perception Unit (SPU) partitions channels into n groups, each convolved with progressively larger kernels (3×3, 5×5, …) connected by hierarchical residual links to dynamically expand the receptive field; the Frequency Perception Unit (FPU) modulates learned convolutional kernels with precomputed Fractional Gabor Transform filters to capture multi-scale, multi-orientation texture while suppressing speckle noise. A parameter-free Channel Selection Unit (CSU) then fuses the two branches via global average pooling followed by a softmax-based channel-wise soft attention, preserving the most discriminative features without adding learnable parameters. Stacking four CBR+SFS-Conv stages yields SFS-CNet (1.86 M params, 6.9 G FLOPs), which matches or exceeds YOLOv8s accuracy at only 18% of its size and 25% of its FLOPs.

## Results

On HRSID, SFS-CNet† achieves 96.2% AP50 and 93.0% AP75 with only 1.86M parameters and 6.9G FLOPs (8.6 ms/image), matching YOLOv8s (96.2% AP50) while using just 18% of its parameters and 24% of its FLOPs. On SAR-AIRcraft-1.0, SFS-CNet† reaches 89.7% mAP, surpassing YOLOv8s (89.6%) and YOLOv5s (89.0%). On SSDD, SFS-CNet† attains 99.6% AP50, outperforming YOLOv8s (99.4%) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFS-CNet† (1.86M params) matches YOLOv8s (10.65M) on HRSID AP50 (96.2% vs 96.2%) while using only 18% of the parameters and 24% of the FLOPs, and slightly edges it on AP75 (86.8% vs 87.2%—actually a small loss) and F1 (93.0% vs 92.9%). Against the lighter YOLOv8n (3.01M), SFS-CNet† gains +2.5% AP50 and +3.2% AP75. Compared to YOLOv5s (7.21M), SFS-CNet† improves AP50 by +0.8% and AP75 by +1.3% with fewer parameters. On SAR-AIRcraft-1.0, SFS-CNet† achieves 89.7% mAP, narrowly beating YOLOv8s (89.6%) and clearly surpassing YOLOv5s (89.0%) and YOLOv8n (88.4%). On SSDD, SFS-CNet† reaches 99.6% AP50, outperforming YOLOv8s (99.4%) and YOLOv5s (98.3%). Against two-stage detectors like Faster R-CNN (41.3M, 138.5G FLOPs), SFS-CNet† gains +9.7% AP50 on HRSID while being ~22× smaller in parameters and ~20× faster in FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the efficient convolution design lineage (MobileNet, GhostNet, SCConv) by shifting focus from suppressing channel redundancy to actively generating more diverse features within a single convolutional layer. The Frequency Perception Unit directly adapts the Gabor-convolutional kernel modulation from Luan et al. [26] and the Fractional Gabor Transform [47], repurposing them for SAR-specific texture and orientation extraction. Conceptually, it departs from prior SAR detectors (YOLO-FA, DAPN) that bolt on separate spatial or frequency attention modules, instead unifying both perspectives into a single shunt-perceive-select convolution that avoids the parameter overhead of additional modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.7× parameter reduction relative to YOLOv8s at matched accuracy (96.2% AP50 on HRSID) is a practically significant efficiency result for deployment in resource-constrained SAR monitoring scenarios. The parameter-free channel selection unit is an elegant design choice that avoids the cost of learned gating. However, the method's generalizability beyond SAR imagery remains untested, and the reliance on a Canny-edge-based OGL training strategy introduces a domain-specific heuristic that may not transfer cleanly. An open question is whether the FrGT-based frequency branch provides genuine gains over simpler multi-scale frequency filters, or whether the spatial multi-scale SPU alone would suffice in other imaging domains.
