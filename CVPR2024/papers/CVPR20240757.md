## Problem

Existing blind image quality assessment methods either ignore score relations between images entirely or exploit ranking information only during training, not at test time. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly encoding both ordering relationships and score differences between images into a geometric embedding space that is actively used during inference to estimate continuous quality scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240757/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single RGB image (resized to 384 px on the short side, aspect ratio preserved) together with N−1 auxiliary images sampled uniformly from score intervals of the training set; auxiliary features are pre-extracted so only the test image requires forward encoding. The output is a continuous quality score obtained by projecting the test image's feature vector onto the line segment between its two nearest score pivots in the embedding space, then linearly interpolating between the pivots' assigned scores.

## Method

QCN uses a ResNet50 encoder to map N input images into a token matrix, then applies three stacked Comparison Transformers (CTs), each containing four attention modules: masked feature self-update (FSU), feature-to-pivot cross-update (FPCU), pivot self-update (PSU), and pivot-to-feature cross-update (PFCU). M learnable score pivots (default 101, assigned non-uniformly via Lloyd-Max quantization) serve as quality centroids that guide the arrangement of feature vectors. Training minimizes four losses computed on the final CT output: an order loss aligning consecutive pivots collinearly, a metric loss enforcing global pivot spacing, a center loss pulling each feature vector toward its linearly interpolated pivot, and a smooth MAE loss on the projected score estimate. At inference, the test image's feature vector is located between its two nearest pivots, projected onto their connecting line, and the score is read off by linear interpolation.

## Results

On KonIQ10K, QCN achieves SRCC 0.934 and PCC 0.945, the best among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest network-design baseline MUSIQ (0.916/0.928) by 1.97%/1.83% and the strongest pre-training baseline QPT (0.927/0.941) by 0.76%/0.43%. On FLIVE, QCN attains PCC 0.741 (best) and SRCC 0.644 (second to MUSIQ's 0.646). In cross-dataset evaluation (Table 2), QCN achieves the best SRCC in all six train/test combinations, e.g., 0.886 when training on CLIVE and testing on KonIQ10K, surpassing the second-best HyperIQA by 1.55%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QCN outperforms all non-pre-training network-design baselines (MUSIQ, HyperIQA, DB-CNN, TReS, UNIQUE, SFA, etc.) in 9 out of 10 dataset-metric tests, with the largest margin over MUSIQ on KonIQ10K (+1.97% SRCC, +1.83% PCC). Against the three pre-training baselines (CONTRIQUE, Re-IQA, QPT), QCN wins 5 out of 10 tests; it surpasses QPT on KonIQ10K (SRCC 0.934 vs 0.927) and FLIVE PCC (0.741 vs 0.677), but trails QPT on CLIVE (SRCC 0.875 vs 0.895) and SPAQ SRCC (0.923 vs 0.925). On FLIVE SRCC, QCN (0.644) is marginally below MUSIQ (0.646) and Re-IQA (0.645), though it leads in PCC. In cross-dataset evaluation, QCN achieves the best SRCC in all six train/test combinations, with the most notable gain of 1.55% over HyperIQA when training on CLIVE and testing on KonIQ10K. Compared directly with the original GOL algorithm [14], QCN improves SRCC by 1.6% on KonIQ10K and 1.5% on SPAQ, confirming that the transformer-based adaptation is essential for continuous score estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Geometric Order Learning (GOL) from Lee et al. (NeurIPS 2022), originally designed for discrete rank estimation tasks like facial age estimation, to the continuous-score setting of blind image quality assessment. It departs from prior BIQA methods in two key ways: unlike network-design approaches (MUSIQ, HyperIQA) that regress scores independently per image, and unlike ranking-based methods (TReS, UNIQUE) that exploit ordering only during training, QCN constructs an explicit embedding space where both ordering relationships and score differences between images are geometrically encoded at test time. The Comparison Transformer architecture with learnable score pivots is a novel mechanism for enforcing this geometric structure, and the four-term loss (order, metric, center, MAE) jointly shapes the embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QCN demonstrates that a well-structured embedding space with explicit geometric constraints can match or exceed pre-training-based BIQA methods without the substantial computational overhead of contrastive or quality-aware pre-training, and it generalizes strongly across datasets (best in all 6 cross-dataset tests). This suggests that relational structure in the feature space may be a more transferable signal than dataset-specific pre-training. Open questions include whether the auxiliary-image mechanism (N=18 images at test time) can be reduced, whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal quality assessment, and whether the foreground/background focus mismatch identified in failure cases can be addressed through attention or content-aware weighting.
