## Problem

Inferring 3D keypoints from 2D images has been largely restricted to settings that rely on strong priors such as multi-view images of the same object, 3D shape templates, skeleton structures, spatio-temporal video consistency, or 2D/3D keypoint annotations. No prior method can learn 3D keypoint structure in an unsupervised manner from a single inhomogeneous collection of category-specific images (different instances of the same object category) without any additional information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242635/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image from a category-specific collection (e.g., different faces, birds, or horses) and produces K 2D keypoints extracted from predicted heatmaps, K 3D keypoints in canonical pose, and a per-instance rigid body pose (rotation and translation). The 3D keypoints are parameterized by a shared linear shape space model (mean shape M of size 1×3K and basis B of size n×3K) with per-image coefficients α, so the 3D structure is globally consistent across the collection while allowing intra-class variation. The 2D keypoints are normalized to [-1,1]² pixel coordinates, and the 3D keypoints are projected via a learned orthographic projection with per-image scaling.

## Method

The method extends the AutoLink 2D keypoint auto-encoder by jointly training a linear 3D shape space model: an MLP maps the 2D keypoint matrix to shape coefficients α, while the mean shape M and basis B are global trainable parameters, yielding canonical 3D keypoints as mat(M + αB). A second MLP predicts the rigid body pose (translation T and rotation angles Θ converted via the Rodrigues formula) that transforms canonical 3D keypoints to the posed frame. Training is fully unsupervised, combining three losses: the AutoLink reconstruction loss (edge map + masked input → VGG feature matching), a re-projection loss that penalizes the Frobenius distance between orthographically projected 3D keypoints and 2D heatmap predictions, and a repulsion loss that prevents degenerate collapse of all keypoints to a single point. The key insight is that the shared 3D shape space model acts as a geometry-aware regularizer, forcing the 2D keypoint predictor to produce spatially consistent structure across different instances of the same category, which simultaneously improves 2D keypoint accuracy and yields plausible 3D geometry.

## Results

On CELEBA WILD (K=8), the method achieves 5.21% NME for unsupervised 2D keypoint inference, outperforming AutoLink (5.39%) and all other unsupervised baselines (next best: GANSeg at 6.18%). The advantage grows with more keypoints: at K=32, the method reaches 3.48% NME versus AutoLink's 3.77%. For 3D keypoint inference on AFLW2000-3D (trained on 300W-LP), the method achieves 8.48% NME, beating the two-stage unsupervised baselines AutoLink+MiDaS (9.23%) and AutoLink+Unsup3d (11.47%), though remaining behind the supervised 3DDFA (4.94%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 13 unsupervised 2D keypoint baselines on CELEBA WILD. The closest competitor is AutoLink [12] (5.39% vs. 5.21% at K=8), with the gap widening to 0.29% at K=32 (3.48% vs. 3.77%). GANSeg [13] (6.18%) and LatentKeypointGAN-tuned [11] (5.63%) are the next closest, both clearly outperformed. Methods relying on video or multi-view inputs (IMM [19] at 8.74%, Huang et al. [16] at 8.4%) and those using GAN-based or auto-encoder frameworks (Zhang et al. [67] at 40.82%, Thewlis et al. [49] at 31.30%) are surpassed by large margins. For 3D keypoint inference, the method (8.48%) beats both two-stage unsupervised baselines—AutoLink+MiDaS (9.23%) and AutoLink+Unsup3d (11.47%)—but remains 3.54% behind the supervised 3DDFA [71] (4.94%), highlighting the remaining gap between unsupervised and supervised 3D keypoint detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends AutoLink [12], an unsupervised 2D keypoint auto-encoder that uses keypoints as bottleneck latent codes and reconstructs images from edge maps, by coupling it with a learnable linear 3D shape space model and a self-supervised re-projection loss. It departs from prior 3D keypoint inference methods (which rely on 2D/3D annotations, multi-view geometry, video temporal consistency, or shape templates/skeletons) by requiring only an inhomogeneous collection of single-view images from the same category. The pose estimation component uses a standard SE(3) rigid transformation parameterized via the Rodrigues formula, while non-rigid deformations are absorbed by the shape basis, distinguishing it from non-rigid structure-from-motion approaches that require multiple views of the same object.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to infer 3D keypoints from category-specific image collections without any additional supervisory signal or geometric prior, opening a path toward 3D structure-aware generative models and reconstruction from web-scale image collections. However, the quantitative 3D evaluation is limited to a single dataset (AFLW2000-3D), and the method fails on strongly articulated objects (e.g., HUMAN3.6M), indicating that domain-specific kinematic priors remain necessary for such cases. The modest 2D improvement (5.21% vs 5.39% NME) and the persistent gap to supervised methods (8.48% vs 4.94%) suggest that fully unsupervised 3D structure inference from inhomogeneous collections remains an open challenge, particularly for large pose variations and articulated categories.
