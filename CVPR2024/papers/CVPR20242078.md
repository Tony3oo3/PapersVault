## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods face two intertwined failures: cascaded base+super-resolution pipelines irreversibly lose high-frequency garment details (text, logos, fine textures) during downsampling, and clothing-agnostic person representations erase identity cues (tattoos, body shape, muscle definition) that the network must then hallucinate. Furthermore, nearly all prior work targets single-garment try-on, leaving multi-garment scenarios—where pixel budgets triple and agnostic representations preserve even less identity—largely unsolved.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a full-body person photo, up to three garment images (upper-body, lower-body, full-body; each may be a layflat product shot or a garment worn by a different person), and an optional free-text layout instruction (e.g., 'rolled sleeves, shirt tucked in'). All images are cropped and resized to 1024×512, and 2D pose keypoints are extracted for both person and garments. The output is a single 1024×512 RGB try-on image synthesized in one diffusion pass with no super-resolution stage.

## Method

M&M VTO introduces VTO-UDiT, an architecture that pairs fully convolutional UNet encoders (for noisy image, person, and garment features) with DiT transformer blocks for denoising, fully disentangling conditional feature encoding from the denoising process. This separation enables a 6 MB person-specific finetuning of only the person encoder (vs. 4 GB for DreamBooth-style full-model finetuning) to recover identity lost in the clothing-agnostic step, using 150 synthetic pairs generated by the pretrained model across poses and body sizes. To avoid the detail loss of cascaded SR pipelines, the model is trained progressively: first at 512×256, then continued at 1024×512 with the identical architecture, letting lower-resolution priors guide high-frequency learning. Garment layout control is achieved by finetuning PaLI-3 on just 1,500 labeled images to extract structured layout attributes, which are injected as text-conditioned FiLM modulations alongside the diffusion timestep embedding. Garment warping is handled implicitly via cross-attention between garment features and the noisy image features, while v-prediction stabilizes high-resolution generation.

## Results

On the authors' 8,300-triplet test set, M&M VTO achieves FID 18.145 and KID 15.227, outperforming TryOnDiffusion (FID 19.459, KID 17.617). On the DressCode test set, M&M VTO achieves FID 14.019 and KID 2.772, beating TryOnDiffusion (FID 15.944, KID 5.363) and GP-VTON (FID 38.392, KID 33.909). In the user study, M&M VTO is selected as best 6,512 times vs. 1,526 for TryOnDiffusion on the internal test set, and 2,945 vs. 951 (TryOnDiffusion) and 1,327 (GP-VTON) on DressCode, corresponding to a 78.5% preference rate reported in the abstract.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M&M VTO outperforms TryOnDiffusion [59] on both test sets across all three metrics (FID, KID, and user study), with the largest gap in user preference (6,512 vs. 1,526 on the internal set; 2,945 vs. 951 on DressCode). GP-VTON [54], evaluated only on DressCode, is substantially outperformed (FID 14.019 vs. 38.392; KID 2.772 vs. 33.909), though GP-VTON was trained on layflat garments only and thus not directly comparable in training setup. For garment layout editing, M&M VTO is compared qualitatively against six text-guided editing baselines: P2P + Null Inversion [19, 36], InstructPix2Pix [5], Imagen Editor [53], DiffEdit [10], and SDXL inpainting [40]. The paper reports that M&M VTO interprets garment layout concepts more effectively, producing more precise edits of the targeted region (e.g., rolling sleeves, tucking shirts) without degrading other areas, whereas the editing baselines either fail to preserve original garment details or require explicit inpainting masks that are impractical for VTO tasks like 'tuck this shirt in.' No quantitative editing metrics are provided in the main text; the comparison is supported by qualitative examples in Figure 5 and supplementary material.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends TryOnDiffusion's Parallel-UNet architecture by replacing it with VTO-UDiT, which combines lightweight UNet encoders with DiT transformer blocks to disentangle person-feature encoding from the denoising process. The single-stage high-resolution design draws on Simple Diffusion's end-to-end approach, while the garment layout conditioning repurposes PaLI-3 as a vision-language captioning model rather than training separate per-attribute classifiers. The key conceptual departure from prior VTO work is reframing the problem from single-garment to multi-garment try-on, which forces a rethink of how clothing-agnostic representations, warping capacity, and identity preservation interact when multiple garments change simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-garment VTO with layout editing addresses a practical gap in e-commerce and fashion visualization that single-garment methods cannot fill, and the 6MB-per-person finetuning (vs. 4GB for DreamBooth-style approaches) makes identity preservation economically viable at scale. The finding that cascaded super-resolution is actively detrimental for VTO—because the base model's downsampling irreversibly destroys garment details—challenges the standard text-to-image pipeline and may influence other detail-sensitive generation tasks. Open questions include whether the method can incorporate size/fit information, handle rare garment combinations (e.g., long coat over skirt), and generalize to upper-body layering (shirt under jacket from separate images), all of which the authors acknowledge as current limitations.
