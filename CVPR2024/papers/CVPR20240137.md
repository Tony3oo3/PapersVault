## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods rely solely on initial text prompts or 2D reference images, offering no mechanism for users to directly manipulate intermediate 3D outputs during generation. This limits controllability to the confines of a single prompt or image and introduces artifacts during 3D lifting, making it impossible for users to iteratively refine geometry, add/remove components, or apply localized semantic edits in a true 3D space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as the global generation guide plus direct 3D interactions at arbitrary optimization checkpoints: users select source/target point pairs with a local radius for dragging, define parts via 2D SAM segmentation masks projected into 3D or by direct 3D point selection, and specify new text prompts for local semantic editing. The denoising step t in the SDS loss is user-adjustable (e.g., 0.98 for coarse shapes, 0.3 for fine-tuning). Output progresses from a Gaussian Splatting representation (Stage I) through NeRF distillation into an InstantNGP radiance field (Stage II), from which a 3D mesh can be extracted.

## Method

Interactive3D is a two-stage cascading framework exploiting the complementarity of explicit and implicit 3D representations. Stage I optimizes a Gaussian Splatting representation under SDS loss while supporting five interaction types: part add/remove via set concatenation/deletion, bounding-box geometry transformations, deformable and rigid dragging (using a motion supervision loss plus a rigid constraint loss that preserves inter-point distances), and local semantic editing that restricts gradient flow to a selected subset of Gaussian blobs. An interactive SDS loss with adaptive camera zoom-in renders only the modified region for efficient local optimization. Stage II distills the Gaussian blobs into InstantNGP via L1 rendering loss, then applies a novel Interactive Hash Refinement module that extracts coarse 32³ occupancy grids, intersects them with user-selected regions, and constructs part-specific multi-level hash tables with learnable residual features fed through lightweight MLPs—overcoming both the finite capacity bottleneck and hash-conflict degeneration of the base InstantNGP representation.

## Results

On 50 prompts from Cap3D, Interactive3D achieves a CLIP R-Precision of 0.94, substantially outperforming DreamFusion (0.67) and ProlificDreamer (0.83). In terms of efficiency, Interactive3D completes generation in approximately 50 minutes, compared to 1.1 hours for DreamFusion and 3.4 hours for ProlificDreamer. The speed advantage is attributed to using fast Gaussian Splatting in Stage I to initialize Stage II optimization, and incorporating user interactions at checkpoints without requiring extra training steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Interactive3D is compared against two optimization-based text-to-3D baselines: DreamFusion and ProlificDreamer. On CLIP R-Precision, Interactive3D (0.94) outperforms ProlificDreamer (0.83) by 0.11 and DreamFusion (0.67) by 0.27, demonstrating significantly better alignment between generated 3D objects and text prompts. In terms of generation time, Interactive3D is roughly 2.2× faster than DreamFusion (50 min vs. 1.1 h) and 4.1× faster than ProlificDreamer (50 min vs. 3.4 h). Qualitatively, the paper notes that ProlificDreamer produces geometry that is 'totally wrong' and mismatches the text prompt, while Interactive3D yields more accurate geometry and texture. The efficiency gain stems from the two-stage design: Gaussian Splatting provides a fast, editable base that initializes the InstantNGP refinement, and user interactions are applied at optimization checkpoints without additional training overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDS-based text-to-3D optimization paradigm (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer) by inserting direct user manipulation into the optimization loop, rather than relying solely on an initial text prompt. The two-stage design is the key architectural departure: Stage I leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting (following Chen et al.) for its explicit, per-blob editability, while Stage II converts to InstantNGP via NeRF distillation to recover the geometry quality and mesh extractability that Gaussian Splatting lacks. The dragging interaction adapts DragGAN's motion-supervision loss from 2D GAN space to 3D point clouds, and the Interactive Hash Refinement module introduces part-specific hash tables to overcome the hash-conflict bottleneck inherent in InstantNGP's shared feature encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental controllability gap in text-to-3D generation: current methods offer no mechanism for users to correct or redirect the output mid-optimization, forcing acceptance of artifacts or re-running with modified prompts. The two-stage representation strategy (explicit for editing, implicit for geometry) is a practical design pattern likely to be adopted by subsequent interactive 3D methods. However, evaluation is limited to 50 prompts with a single CLIP R-Precision metric, and the method still requires per-object optimization (~50 min), leaving open whether the interactive paradigm can scale to scene-level generation or be partially automated. The reliance on a pretrained 2D diffusion model as the SDS prior also inherits its biases and failure modes.
