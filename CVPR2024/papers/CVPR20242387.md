## Problem

Text-guided image editing faces a dual challenge: faithfully transforming image content per a text prompt while preserving contextually relevant regions of the original image. Existing methods either require domain-specific fine-tuning, rely on manually or automatically generated masks at inference time (making them sensitive to mask quality), or treat editing as inpainting without leveraging masked content, failing to address both transformation and preservation simultaneously in a generalizable, self-supervised manner.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB input image and one target text prompt, producing a manipulated image with localised edits—no masks or paired target images are required. During training, the input image is paired with a single source prompt (cleaned via BLIP from noisy LAION-5B captions), which is augmented into N target prompts; the LDM generates N latent images per iteration, and the contrastive loss operates on these latent representations in the UNet's feature space. Evaluation uses 135 images (Stable Diffusion v1.4 generated + COCO + ImageNet real images) with CLIPScore, FID, SSIM-M, and CLIP-R-Precision metrics.

## Method

The method fine-tunes a pretrained Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion v1.4) with a self-supervised objective combining the standard LDM noise-prediction loss and a novel Contrastive Loss (CL). Prompt augmentation generates N target prompts per source prompt by masking a random noun/adjective and filling it with BERT-suggested replacements plus NLTK-derived synonyms, antonyms, and co-hyponyms. An on-the-fly binary attention mask is computed at training time by thresholding the mean absolute difference of the UNet's noise estimates across all prompt pairs, delineating regions to edit versus preserve. The CL has two terms: a dissimilarity term that pushes masked regions of different augmented-prompt images apart, and a preservation term that pulls unmasked regions together. The Soft-CL refinement modulates the dissimilarity term by the cosine similarity of CLIP prompt embeddings, so semantically close prompts (e.g., 'orange cake' vs. 'mandarin cake') receive less separation pressure, while Soft-PA selects augmented prompts at varying similarity levels to enrich training diversity.

## Results

On a combined evaluation set of 135 images (Stable Diffusion v1.4 generated + COCO + ImageNet), the proposed method achieves a CLIPScore of 78.19%, FID of 133, SSIM-M of 70.39%, and CLIP-R-Precision of 80.69%. In a user study on Microworkers, the method is preferred over all four baselines, with win rates of 76.3% (vs. SDEdit), 73.8% (vs. DiffEdit), 68.3% (vs. DALL-E 2), and 62.2% (vs. InstructPix2Pix). The method does not require paired training data or inference-time masks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDEdit, the proposed method wins on every metric: CLIPScore 78.19 vs. 76.76, FID 133 vs. 174, SSIM-M 70.39 vs. 63.07, and CLIP-R-Precision 80.69 vs. 75.91. Against DiffEdit, the method achieves a substantially higher CLIPScore (78.19 vs. 72.77) and CLIP-R-Precision (80.69 vs. 67.60), but DiffEdit retains advantages in FID (85 vs. 133) and SSIM-M (85.14 vs. 70.39) due to its inference-time mask guidance. Against InstructPix2Pix, the method edges ahead on CLIPScore (78.19 vs. 77.66) and CLIP-R-Precision (80.69 vs. 77.45) while trailing on FID (133 vs. 123) and SSIM-M (70.39 vs. 81.71). Against DALL-E 2, the method is slightly behind on CLIPScore (78.19 vs. 78.47), SSIM-M (70.39 vs. 96.74), and CLIP-R-Precision (80.69 vs. 81.83), but achieves a lower FID (133 vs. 151); the authors note that DALL-E 2's inpainting-based approach sacrifices fidelity to masked content, a weakness confirmed by the human study where the proposed method is still preferred 68.3% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts DiffEdit's inference-time noise-difference mask generation to training time, repurposing it as a self-supervised signal for localised editing. It draws on the prompt augmentation lineage from Specialist Diffusion and iEdit, but uniquely uses augmented prompts to derive dynamic attention masks and to construct a novel Soft Contrastive Loss weighted by CLIP-embedding similarity between prompts. The contrastive learning component extends prior work on contrastive losses for image synthesis (PatchNCE, Cntr-GAN) by introducing a soft-assignment variant that accounts for the continuous spectrum of editing demands, a direction only briefly explored in visual localisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised formulation eliminates the need for paired editing datasets or user-provided masks at inference, making the approach more scalable to diverse editing scenarios. However, the SSIM-M score (70.39) trails DiffEdit (85.14) and DALL-E 2 (96.74) substantially, indicating that background preservation remains a weakness; the method also requires fine-tuning a full LDM, unlike inference-time alternatives. Open questions include whether the prompt-augmentation strategy generalises to structural or compositional edits (adding/removing objects) beyond attribute changes, and whether the soft-contrastive weighting can be extended to multi-object or multi-region manipulations.
