## Problem

Existing 3D generative models for humans rely on implicit representations (NeRF, SDF) that are incompatible with standard graphics engines, or model only geometry without texture. No prior method generates explicit, articulable 3D meshes with realistic clothing textures from unpaired 2D and 3D data, despite the fact that large 2D fashion image datasets and medium-scale 3D scan datasets are both available but not paired.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241872/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 512×512 geometry latent, a 512×512 texture latent, a 69-dimensional SMPL pose vector, a 6-dimensional one-hot clothing type label, and a 512-dimensional CLIP text embedding for clothing color. It produces a 6890-vertex 3D mesh on fixed SMPL topology (with pose-dependent vertex displacements) and a 256×256×3 UV texture map in the SMPL template's UV space. The mesh is directly compatible with existing 3D rendering engines and game pipelines, and the texture can be rendered under arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

SCULPT uses two StyleGAN3-based generators operating in SMPL's UV space: a geometry generator that outputs pose-dependent displacement maps (trained on CAPE 3D scans with adversarial loss) and a texture generator that outputs UV color maps (trained unsupervised on 2D fashion images via adversarial losses on differentiable renders). The key architectural coupling is that intermediate feature blocks from the geometry generator are progressively added to the corresponding blocks of the texture generator at each synthesis level, ensuring texture conforms to geometry (e.g., sleeve boundaries, wrinkles). Both generators receive attribute conditioning—clothing type for geometry and clothing color for texture—to disentangle pose from clothing attributes; these labels are automatically extracted from 2D images using BLIP (VQA for color) and CLIP (zero-shot classification for type). Training proceeds in two stages: the geometry generator is first trained to convergence on CAPE, then frozen while the texture generator is trained on 16,362 Zalando fashion images using a dual discriminator (global + 64×64 patch-based) with R1 regularization and adaptive augmentation.

## Results

On the SCULPT dataset, SCULPT achieves a FID of 9.85 and KID of 0.0063, with a precision/recall of 0.53/0.22. EG3D attains the best FID (7.38) and KID (0.0036) but with the lowest recall (0.14), while EVA3D (retrained at 256×256) lags significantly with FID 44.11 and KID 0.0387. SCULPT achieves the highest recall (0.22) among all compared methods, indicating greater diversity in generated samples. Qualitatively, the paper reports that SCULPT's explicit mesh geometry substantially surpasses the implicit representations of EG3D and EVA3D in terms of clothing deformation detail and articulation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EG3D [Chan et al., CVPR 2022], SCULPT trades a modest FID gap (9.85 vs. 7.38) and KID gap (0.0063 vs. 0.0036) for substantially higher recall (0.22 vs. 0.14) and, critically, explicit mesh geometry with pose articulation that EG3D's implicit SDF representation cannot provide; the paper argues EG3D's geometry is of low quality for articulated humans. Against EVA3D [Hong et al., ICLR 2023], SCULPT clearly wins on all quantitative metrics (FID 9.85 vs. 44.11, KID 0.0063 vs. 0.0387, recall 0.22 vs. 0.03), though EVA3D was retrained at 256×256 resolution on SCULPT's data for a fair comparison. Against StylePeople [Grigorev et al., CVPR 2021], SCULPT produces sharper renders and accurate clothing geometry compatible with standard 3D renderers, whereas StylePeople relies on 2D neural rendering with generated neural textures superimposed on minimally clothed SMPL-X meshes. Against GET3D [Gao et al., NeurIPS 2022], SCULPT offers pose articulation and clothing-type/color control that GET3D lacks (GET3D generates only canonical-pose bodies), though GET3D can model looser topologies like skirts that SCULPT's fixed SMPL topology cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCULPT departs from the dominant implicit-representation paradigm (NeRF, tri-planes) used by EG3D, EVA3D, GET3D, and StylePeople by generating explicit SMPL-based meshes with UV displacement maps and texture maps, making outputs directly compatible with standard graphics engines. It builds on the StyleGAN3 architecture and the CAPE dataset for geometry, but introduces a key architectural departure: the texture generator is conditioned on intermediate features of the geometry generator, enabling unpaired learning where geometry is trained on 3D scans and texture on 2D fashion images without requiring paired 3D-2D data. The use of CLIP and BLIP for automatic clothing-type and color labeling is a practical repurposing of vision-language models as a labeling pipeline, replacing manual annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit mesh output addresses a critical practical gap: implicit 3D human generators (NeRF-based) cannot be directly inserted into game engines or classical rendering pipelines, whereas SCULPT's SMPL-compatible meshes can. However, significant limitations remain: poor back-side texture quality due to frontal-view dataset bias, degradation on out-of-distribution poses, topological constraints preventing loose-fitting garments (skirts, dresses), and demographic homogeneity in training data. Open questions include whether multi-view or video-based training can resolve the view/pose diversity issue, and whether the unpaired geometry-texture coupling strategy generalizes to more complex clothing topologies beyond SMPL's fixed mesh.
