## Problem

Reconstructing a relightable avatar from a single portrait image is ill-posed: geometry is a one-to-many mapping, and reflectance/light are entangled. Existing methods either rely on linear 3DMMs that limit geometric expressiveness, estimate only diffuse/specular/normal maps (omitting ambient occlusion and translucency needed by modern engines), or train exclusively on light stage data, resulting in poor generalization to in-the-wild images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241417/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB portrait image and produces a non-linear morphable 3D face mesh along with six 4K texture maps (diffuse, specular, normal, ambient occlusion, translucency, and displacement) that are directly compatible with modern PBR rendering engines. The geometry is unbounded (not constrained to a linear PCA space), and the reflectance maps are fully disentangled so the avatar can be relit under arbitrary lighting conditions.

## Method

MoSAR uses a part-based GNN auto-encoder to generate detailed neutral geometry, combined with a ResNet-50 scene encoder that regresses identity, expression, reflectance, light, and camera parameters. The key technical contribution is a novel differentiable shading formulation that extends the Microfacet BRDF with spherical-harmonic-based ambient occlusion (multiplicative term in the diffuse lobe) and translucency (frequency-dependent subsurface scattering term), making the full rendering equation differentiable with respect to all texture maps. Training is semi-supervised: light stage data (890 subjects with ground-truth geometry and reflectance) provides supervised signals, while 70k in-the-wild FFHQ images provide self-supervision through photo-consistency, landmark, and regularization losses. A U-Net light normalization step removes in-the-wild lighting before intrinsic map estimation, and GAN-based image-to-image networks (with per-map discriminators) predict the five reflectance maps jointly, with the differentiable shading loss enabling gradient-based disentanglement of diffuse from ambient occlusion and specular from translucency.

## Results

On the REALY benchmark (100 multi-view scans from HeadSpace), MoSAR achieves a mean reconstruction error of 1.500 mm across all face regions, ranking second overall behind only HiFace (1.275 mm, trained with 200k synthetic images). MoSAR outperforms all other baselines including HRN (1.537 mm), Deep3D (1.657 mm), and DECA (2.010 mm). For texture estimation, MoSAR achieves the highest SSIM on the diffuse map (0.754) compared to FitMe (0.720) and Relightify (0.704), and is the only method reporting specular (0.515), ambient occlusion (0.766), and translucency (0.806) maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the REALY geometry benchmark, MoSAR (1.500 mm) outperforms all linear-3DMM-based methods (MICA 2.134, DECA 2.010, PSL 1.882, AlbedoGan 1.746, Deep3D 1.657, HRN 1.537) and its own linear-3DMM baseline (1.890 mm), confirming the benefit of the non-linear GNN morphable model. The only method beating MoSAR is HiFace (1.275 mm), which leverages 200k additional synthetic images with ground-truth meshes; without that synthetic data, HiFace (1.558 mm) is slightly worse than MoSAR. For full avatar reconstruction, MoSAR achieves higher diffuse SSIM (0.754) than both FitMe (0.720) and Relightify (0.704), and uniquely provides specular, ambient occlusion, and translucency maps that the other two methods do not estimate. Visually, MoSAR captures finer geometric details (wrinkles, folds) than DECA, HRN, Deep3D, and FaceVerse, and avoids baking wrinkle shadows into the albedo as FitMe and Relightify do. The paper notes that for darker skin tones, MoSAR tends to produce lighter tones due to demographic imbalance in the FFHQ training set (69% white, 4% black).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoSAR repurposes the part-based GNN auto-encoder architecture from Aliari et al. [1] (originally designed for artist-controlled face editing) as a non-linear morphable model for monocular reconstruction, combining it with the self-supervised differentiable rendering framework established by Tewari et al. [53] and Dib et al. [17]. The key methodological departure is a novel differentiable shading formulation that extends the Microfacet BRDF [56] to incorporate ambient occlusion and translucency within a spherical harmonics lighting model, enabling semi-supervised training that disentangles these maps from in-the-wild images. The pipeline also draws on patch-based displacement estimation [7] and UV texture completion [14], but the semi-supervised combination of light-stage supervision with in-the-wild self-supervision through the new shading equation is the central contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing 4K reflectance maps (diffuse, specular, normal, ambient occlusion, translucency) compatible with modern rendering engines, MoSAR bridges the gap between research-grade face reconstruction and practical content-creation pipelines. The released FFHQ-UV-Intrinsics dataset (10k subjects with full intrinsic maps) is a significant resource for the community. However, the method exhibits a clear bias toward lighter skin tones due to FFHQ's demographic imbalance, and it does not model external occluders like hair or glasses, which limits its applicability to fully realistic avatar generation. Future work on balanced datasets and occluder modeling remains an open challenge.
