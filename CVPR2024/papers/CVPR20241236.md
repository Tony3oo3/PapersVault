## Problem

Deep learning-based HDR tone mapping methods require paired HDR-LDR training data that is extremely difficult to capture, and existing unpaired approaches rely on adversarial training where competing naturalness and structure-preservation objectives dilute each other. This paper addresses the data scarcity challenge by proposing a zero-shot framework that transfers a model trained entirely on LDR images to the HDR domain without any paired training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single HDR image as input and produces a tone-mapped LDR image. During inference, the HDR image is first divided into overlapping patches of fixed size (matching training resolution), each processed independently and blended via weighted summation. The HDR image is decomposed into an MSCN structural map and a Gaussian-blurred luma map (after applying a global log tone curve) that serve as dual conditional inputs; color channels are recovered post-generation via a luminance-scaling formula. The same pipeline generalizes to VIS-NIR image fusion without retraining.

## Method

The core insight is that MSCN coefficients (mean-subtracted contrast-normalized pixel values) exhibit nearly identical distributions across HDR and LDR domains (JS divergence 0.002), making them a transferable structural representation. The method decomposes images into tonal information (local mean and standard deviation maps) and structural information (MSCN), then trains a conditional diffusion model on Flickr2K LDR images using MSCN maps as fine-scale control and low-pass luma maps as coarse-scale control, both injected at different decoder scales of a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone. A structure-refinement operation (SRO) is integrated into the DDIM reverse sampling process: at each iteration before a critical timestep t₀, the intermediate latent is decoded, its MSCN is replaced with the original image's MSCN, and the result is re-encoded, explicitly enforcing structural fidelity while preserving the predicted tonal style. This dual-control architecture with SRO achieves superior TMQI (0.8915) and NIQE (2.340) on HDRPS compared to prior state-of-the-art methods.

## Results

On the HDRPS benchmark (105 HDR images), the proposed zero-shot diffusion model achieves a TMQI of 0.8915 and NIQE of 2.340, outperforming all six baselines on both metrics. The method is the only one that is HDR-free, requiring no HDR images during training (trained solely on Flickr2K LDR images). The largest margin is on NIQE, where the proposed method (2.340) substantially outperforms the closest competitor Vinker et al. (3.174), indicating markedly better perceptual naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on both TMQI and NIQE. Against the closest competitor Vinker et al. [33] (unpaired GAN-based tone mapping), it gains +0.0054 TMQI (0.8915 vs 0.8861) and a substantial −0.834 NIQE improvement (2.340 vs 3.174), indicating far better naturalness. Compared to DeepTMO [25] (pseudo-paired scheme), the gain is +0.0115 TMQI and −0.179 NIQE. Against LA-Net [37], the advantage is +0.0156 TMQI and −0.184 NIQE. The traditional DL-free methods (Liang et al. [16], Shibata et al. [30]) and ETMO [32] trail by 0.014–0.026 in TMQI. Notably, the proposed method is the only zero-shot approach (HDR-free), meaning it requires no HDR training data at all, while all DL-based baselines require either paired or pseudo-paired HDR data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as a fixed-weight generation backbone and modifies its DDPM/DDIM reverse sampling process with a structure-refinement operation. It departs from prior DL-based tone mapping methods (Vinker et al., Rana et al., LA-Net) that rely on adversarial training with paired or pseudo-paired HDR data, instead adopting a zero-shot transfer paradigm that trains entirely on LDR images. The key conceptual departure is the use of MSCN coefficients (Mittal et al., IEEE TIP 2012) as a domain-invariant structural representation, enabling the pre-trained LDR model to process HDR inputs without any HDR training data. The dual-control architecture draws on the ControlNet/T2I-Adapter line of work for conditioning diffusion models, but applies it to a novel dual-scale (fine MSCN + coarse luma) scheme specific to tone mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the fundamental data scarcity bottleneck in HDR tone mapping by eliminating the need for paired or even unpaired HDR training data, which is a practical constraint that has limited the field. The observation that MSCN coefficient distributions are nearly identical across HDR and LDR domains (JS divergence 0.002) provides a principled justification for the zero-shot transfer, and the generalization to VIS-NIR fusion without retraining suggests broader applicability to structure-preserving image enhancement tasks. Limitations include the computational overhead of the SRO (requiring a decoder-encoder round-trip in each early iteration), reliance on a fixed Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone, and the modest quantitative gains over the strongest baseline (TMQI 0.8915 vs. 0.8861). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video tone mapping, whether the SRO can be made more efficient, and how the method performs on extreme HDR content beyond the benchmark datasets.
