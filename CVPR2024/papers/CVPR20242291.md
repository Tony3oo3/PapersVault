## Problem

Flow-based super-resolution models suffer from three inherent deficiencies during inference: grid artifacts from independently sampled patches, exploding inverses when out-of-distribution latent codes are fed to the invertible network, and suboptimal quality from a fixed sampling temperature that cannot adapt to local image content. These issues stem from the reliance on random Gaussian sampling to generate latent codes, which ignores the specific structure of the input low-resolution image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242291/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image as input and produces a super-resolved high-resolution image as output. For the arbitrary-scale LINF variant, the latent module processes 96×96 LR patches and the flow model generates 3×3 HR patches at continuous scales (trained on 1×–4×, tested up to 8×). For the fixed-scale SRFlow variant, the latent module operates on 160×160 HR-sized inputs at a fixed 4× scale. The key distinction from standard flow-based SR is that no sampling temperature hyperparameter is needed at inference; the latent code is deterministically predicted from the LR image.

## Method

The proposed framework introduces a latent module that predicts a conditional learned prior (a latent code) from the LR image in a single forward pass, replacing the conventional random Gaussian sampling in flow-based SR inference. The latent module comprises two five-layer dense-block feature encoders—one extracting image-space features from the LR image and one extracting latent-space features from an 'initial prior' (the latent code of the bilinearly upsampled LR image)—whose concatenated features are processed by a latent generator (UNet, EDSR-baseline, or Swin-T) to produce the final latent code. This latent code is then fed to a frozen, pre-trained flow model (LINF or SRFlow) to produce the SR image, requiring no modification to the flow model's architecture or weights. Training uses a VGG perceptual loss in image space and, for SRFlow, an L1 latent-space loss as a regularizer to prevent out-of-distribution predictions that cause exploding inverses. The learned prior simultaneously addresses all three issues: it captures global LR context to reduce grid artifacts, stays within the training distribution to avoid exploding inverses, and eliminates the need for a fixed sampling temperature by directly predicting content-appropriate latent codes.

## Results

On the DIV2K 4× SR task, RRDB-LINF-LP achieves the best fidelity and perceptual scores among all flow-based methods: 28.00 dB PSNR, 0.78 SSIM, 0.105 LPIPS, and 47.30 dB LR-PSNR. SRFlow-LP improves over SRFlow (τ=0.9) by +0.42 dB PSNR and −0.012 LPIPS while also eliminating exploding inverses (0% vs. 0.8%). For arbitrary-scale SR, RRDB-LINF-LP consistently outperforms RRDB-LINF across all in-distribution (2×, 3×, 4×) and out-of-distribution (6×, 8×) scales on Set5, Set14, B100, and Urban100, with the largest LPIPS gain of 0.023 on Urban100 8× SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two flow-based baselines it directly augments, the learned prior yields consistent gains: SRFlow-LP improves over SRFlow (τ=0.9) by +0.42 dB PSNR and −0.012 LPIPS, while RRDB-LINF-LP improves over RRDB-LINF (τ=0.8) by +0.67 dB PSNR and −0.007 LPIPS. Among non-flow generative SR methods, RRDB-LINF-LP surpasses ESRGAN, RankSRGAN, and HCFlow++ on all four metrics, and outperforms SRDiff on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS (though SRDiff retains a higher LR-PSNR of 55.21 vs. 47.30). SROOE achieves a slightly lower LPIPS (0.096 vs. 0.105) but at the cost of lower PSNR (27.69 vs. 28.00) and SSIM (0.79 vs. 0.78), indicating the proposed method pushes the perception-distortion frontier further. For arbitrary-scale SR (Table 2), RRDB-LINF-LP dominates RRDB-LINF at every scale from 2× to 8× on all four benchmark datasets, with the advantage widening at OOD scales (e.g., 0.023 LPIPS gain on Urban100 8×). The framework also outperforms other arbitrary-scale baselines (MetaSR, LIIF, LTE) by a large margin, confirming that the learned prior is not limited to flow-based models but provides a general boost to patch-based SR pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends flow-based super-resolution (SRFlow, LINF) by replacing the standard Gaussian sampling at inference with a deterministic learned prior predicted by a lightweight latent module conditioned on the LR image. Conceptually, it draws on the learned-prior literature from improved VAEs, where neural networks replace fixed Gaussian priors to close the train-test gap, and repurposes this idea for the invertible flow setting. The key departure from prior flow-based SR work is that the latent module operates entirely at inference time without modifying the flow model's architecture or pretrained weights, positioning it as a plug-and-play enhancement rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses three concrete, previously-unaddressed failure modes of flow-based SR (grid artifacts, exploding inverses, suboptimal fixed temperature) in a unified way, and the plug-and-play design means it can be retrofitted onto any existing flow-based SR model. However, the evaluation is limited to two flow models (LINF and SRFlow) and two backbone variants, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other flow architectures or to diffusion-based SR. The moderate but consistent gains (~0.01–0.02 LPIPS) and the finding that even a 1.4M-parameter module suffices suggest the learned prior is a practical, low-cost addition, but the broader question of whether learned priors can replace stochastic sampling in other generative SR frameworks remains unexplored.
