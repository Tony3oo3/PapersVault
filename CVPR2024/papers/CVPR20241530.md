## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for human pose estimation (e.g., TTP, TTT) rely on multi-task learning where an auxiliary task drives weight updates during inference, but this offers no guarantee that self-supervised learning improves the primary pose estimation task, and the number of iterations required for convergence is large and uncertain, leading to catastrophic forgetting. The paper addresses the need for a deterministic, fast, and reliable test-time adaptation mechanism that preserves source-domain knowledge while adapting to domain shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241530/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (128×128 or 256×256) as input and produces 2D keypoint heatmaps at one-quarter spatial resolution (H/4 × W/4 × J) from which 2D joint coordinates are extracted via post-processing. During adaptation, the input image is additionally consumed by the auxiliary network in a masked form, where a body-specific region (30×30 or 60×60 pixels) is removed based on the predicted heatmap likelihood, and the auxiliary network reconstructs the missing patch guided by self-supervised heatmaps. Only the Y-shaped head of the primary network is updated during test-time adaptation; the backbone remains frozen.

## Method

MeTTA employs a three-stage meta-auxiliary learning pipeline: (1) joint training of a pose estimation primary network (SimpleBaseline or HRNet with a Y-shaped dual-head) and a body-specific image inpainting auxiliary network on the source domain; (2) MAML-style meta-training where the inner loop updates weights via the inpainting loss and the outer loop minimizes the pose estimation loss on those updated weights, learning a weight initialization that makes few-step adaptation effective; (3) meta-testing where only 5 SGD iterations of self-supervised inpainting on the target image adapt the model. The key design choice is the body-specific mask, generated by sampling pixel locations proportional to the max-over-joints heatmap value, which focuses the inpainting task on human body semantics rather than background. This ensures the auxiliary task is tightly coupled to the primary task, and the meta-optimization guarantees that minimizing the auxiliary loss during inference always improves pose estimation accuracy.

## Results

On MPII, MeTTA with HRNet-W32 backbone achieves 91.4% mean PCK, surpassing all conventional pose estimation methods (best: DARK at 90.6%) and the test-time adaptation baseline TTT (89.4%). On Penn Action and Human3.6M, MeTTA with meta-optimization reaches 87.60% and 90.16% PCK respectively, outperforming TTT (85.86% and 88.01%) and approaching the video-based TTP† (87.75% and 91.70%). For inter-dataset domain shift (SURREAL→LSP and SURREAL→Human3.6M), MeTTA achieves 82.5% and 78.5% PCK@0.05, comparable to the best source-free method SP+TR (83.2% and 78.7%) while supporting online per-image adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MPII, MeTTA (HRNet-W32) achieves 91.4% mean PCK, outperforming all conventional methods including DARK (90.6%), Poseur (90.5%), HRNet (90.3%), and TokenPose (90.2%), as well as the test-time adaptation baseline TTT (89.4%). On Penn Action, MeTTA with meta-optimization (87.60%) slightly trails the video-based TTP† (87.75%) but clearly beats TTP (87.27%) and TTT (85.86%); on Human3.6M, MeTTA (90.16%) lags TTP† (91.70%) by 1.54 points but surpasses TTP (89.43%) and TTT (88.01%). For inter-dataset shift (SURREAL→LSP), MeTTA (82.5%) is within 0.7 points of SP+TR (83.2%) and substantially ahead of POST (80.3%), RegDA-SF (66.5%), and MMT (71.5%); on SURREAL→Human3.6M, MeTTA (78.5%) is nearly tied with SP+TR (78.7%) and beats POST (76.7%) by 1.8 points. A key practical advantage is that MeTTA supports online per-image adaptation with a fixed 5-iteration budget, whereas SP+TR and POST require access to the full target domain or multiple passes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeTTA extends the meta-auxiliary learning paradigm (Chi et al., CVPR 2021) from deblurring and dehazing to human pose estimation, replacing the multi-task learning framework of TTP (Li et al., NeurIPS 2021) with MAML-style inner-outer loop optimization. The key conceptual departure is that the auxiliary task—body-specific image inpainting guided by predicted heatmaps—is explicitly coupled to the primary task through meta-training, ensuring that self-supervised updates during inference always improve keypoint localization rather than drifting toward auxiliary-task overfitting. The backbone architectures (SimpleBaseline, HRNet) and the test-time adaptation setting itself are inherited from prior work; the novelty lies in the meta-optimization wrapper and the heatmap-driven mask design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: existing test-time adaptation for pose estimation requires an uncertain number of iterations and risks catastrophic forgetting, while MeTTA makes adaptation deterministic (fixed K=5 updates) and preserves source-domain knowledge via meta-learned initialization. However, the inter-dataset results (SURREAL→LSP, SURREAL→Human3.6M) show MeTTA is comparable to but not clearly superior to SP+TR, and the intra-dataset gains over strong baselines are modest (~1–2 PCK points). Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D pose estimation or multi-person settings, whether freezing the backbone limits adaptation capacity for large distribution shifts, and whether the meta-training overhead is justified given the small number of test-time iterations.
