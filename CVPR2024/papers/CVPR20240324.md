## Problem

Existing color constancy methods are designed for well-lit, single-illuminant conditions and fail on nighttime scenes due to complex multi-light configurations and substantial low-light noise. A significant domain gap exists between daytime/indoor training datasets and nighttime imagery, and no prior work has specifically addressed nighttime color constancy with pixel-level ground truth.

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw nighttime image in camera RGB space (resized to 256×256) and produces a white-balanced surface color image, a pixel-wise illuminant (light color) map, and a pixel-wise uncertainty map. The U-Net architecture (27.4M parameters) runs at 16.9 ms per image. Evaluation uses sparse pixel-level annotations from achromatic paper placed on scene surfaces, with MAE computed only over the masked paper regions.

## Method

NightCC uses a mean-teacher framework for unsupervised domain adaptation: a teacher generates pseudo labels on unlabeled nighttime images while a student learns from both labeled daytime data and those pseudo labels, with the teacher updated via EMA. Two key mechanisms address nighttime-specific challenges. First, Adaptive Channel Masking computes feature covariance matrices on daytime images and their Night-Augmented versions (simulating multi-light via inverse von Kries transform plus Tukey-lambda sensor read noise), derives a per-channel sensitivity score, and randomly masks the most sensitive channels in the student to force learning of illumination-invariant features. Second, Light Uncertainty performs a two-pass prediction cycle: the teacher's surface-color output is converted back to camera RGB and re-fed to the teacher, and the cosine similarity between the second-pass light estimate and white light yields a pixel-wise confidence that down-weights the unsupervised loss (weighted by 1−U) to prevent learning from incorrect pseudo labels.

## Results

On the nighttime dataset, NightCC achieves a mean MAE of 4.82° and median MAE of 4.69°, outperforming all 16 baselines. This represents a 21.5% reduction in mean MAE over the best multi-illuminant method (MIMT, 6.14°) and a 27.1% reduction over the best single-illuminant method (CLCC, 6.61°). On the LSMI multi-illuminant indoor dataset, using only 10% labeled data plus 90% unlabeled data, NightCC achieves a mean MAE of 3.04°, approaching the fully-supervised MIMT (2.48°) while using 90% fewer labels, and a 33.8% improvement over its own 10%-labeled-only variant (5.71°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NightCC outperforms all 16 baselines on the nighttime dataset, with the largest margins over statistical single-illuminant methods (Gray-Edge: 12.18°, Quasi-U: 10.18°) and multi-illuminant statistical methods (HussainWP: 12.36°, Gijsenij: 11.88°). Among deep-learning baselines, it beats the best single-illuminant method CLCC (6.61°) by 1.79° and the best multi-illuminant method MIMT (6.14°) by 1.32° in mean MAE. On the LSMI indoor dataset, NightCC with 10% labels + 90% unlabeled data (3.04°) narrows the gap to fully-supervised MIMT (2.48°) to within 0.56°, while substantially outperforming fully-supervised AngularGAN (4.69°) and PatchCNN (4.82°) despite using 90% fewer labeled samples. The ablation confirms that both adaptive channel masking and light uncertainty contribute meaningfully: removing both degrades MAE from 4.82° to 6.95°, and removing only light uncertainty still costs 0.58°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-teacher unsupervised domain adaptation framework [38] to the color constancy domain, repurposing it to bridge the gap between labeled daytime data and unlabeled nighttime images. The adaptive channel masking mechanism draws on feature covariance analysis from RobustNet [12] for domain generalization, but applies it in a novel way: rather than whitening features, it uses covariance differences under augmentation to identify and selectively mask channels, forcing the student to develop more robust surface-color representations. The light uncertainty mechanism departs from standard MC-dropout or ensemble approaches by exploiting the self-consistency property of color constancy (a correctly white-balanced image should yield white light on reprocessing), providing a lightweight pixel-wise confidence signal without additional forward passes or network ensembles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Nighttime color constancy is a practically important but previously unaddressed gap: existing methods trained on daytime or indoor datasets suffer significant domain shift under complex multi-illuminant, noisy nighttime conditions. The paper's semi-supervised formulation (labeled daytime + unlabeled nighttime) is attractive because pixel-level nighttime ground truth is expensive to obtain, and the introduced dataset, while small (720 unlabeled + 64 labeled images from a single camera), provides the first dedicated benchmark for this setting. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across camera sensors and sensor noise profiles, whether the 64-image evaluation set is sufficient for reliable conclusions, and whether the adaptive channel masking strategy transfers to other low-light or domain-shift tasks beyond color constancy.
