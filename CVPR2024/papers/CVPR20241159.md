## Problem

Existing visual-tactile datasets lack spatially aligned vision and touch signals because the touch sensor and its holder occlude the scene, and they contain only sparse, unregistered touch probes whose 3D relationships are unclear. This paper asks whether one can build a scene-level representation that unifies vision and touch in a shared 3D space and use it to estimate touch at locations never directly probed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method queries a trained NeRF at a novel 3D location (the touch sensor pose translated 0.4 m upward with a 50° FOV) to render an RGB image and a depth map, and additionally supplies a background image of the DIGIT sensor's elastomer gel (captured with no contact) to inform the model of gel defects. The output is a 64×64 latent tactile image decoded by a VQ-GAN, representing the deformation pattern of the gel as seen by the sensor's internal camera. All examples shown in evaluation are at least 10 cm away from any training probe.

## Method

The TaRF pipeline has two key components: a low-cost capture procedure and a conditional latent diffusion model. For capture, a DIGIT touch sensor is mounted on an iPhone 14 Pro via a camera rod; the relative pose between the two is calibrated by synthesizing novel NeRF views at the camera locations (rotated 90° to face the touch sensor), annotating pixel correspondences on a braille board, and solving a resectioning problem. For generation, training proceeds in two stages: first, a ResNet-50 visual-tactile contrastive encoder is pre-trained with InfoNCE loss to produce shared 32-dim embeddings; second, a Stable Diffusion-based latent diffusion model (with a frozen VQ-GAN at 64×64 spatial resolution) is conditioned on the contrastive encoding of the rendered RGB-D image and the background image, pre-trained unconditionally on YCB-Slide, then fine-tuned end-to-end on the 19.3k spatially aligned TaRF pairs. At inference, 16 samples are generated and re-ranked by the contrastive model to select the final tactile prediction.

## Results

On the dense touch estimation task, the proposed diffusion model achieves a FID of 28.97 and CVTP of 0.80, substantially outperforming the VisGel GAN baseline (FID 130.22, CVTP 0.03) and the L1 pixel-loss baseline (FID 97.05, CVTP 0.01). On low-level metrics, the L1 baseline slightly edges out the proposed method (PSNR 24.34 vs. 22.84; SSIM 0.82 vs. 0.72), as expected for a model trained with a pixel-wise loss. On the 3D tactile localization downstream task, combining real and estimated touch samples yields the highest mAP (60.61 at r=0.1 m), surpassing real-only (57.15) and chance (21.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VisGel [35] (a GAN-based cross-modal predictor), the proposed diffusion model achieves a 4.5× lower FID (28.97 vs. 130.22) and a 27× higher CVTP score (0.80 vs. 0.03), indicating far better capture of the tactile data distribution, though it trails on pixel-level PSNR/SSIM. Against the L1 pixel-loss baseline, the gap on FID is 3.3× (28.97 vs. 97.05) and CVTP is 80× (0.80 vs. 0.01), while L1 retains a small PSNR advantage (24.34 vs. 22.84) due to its direct pixel supervision. On the material classification downstream task (Table 5), pretraining with the TaRF dataset combined with Touch and Go [56] yields 59.0% material accuracy and 86.1% rough/smooth accuracy, outperforming ObjectFolder 2.0 [17] + Touch and Go (54.6% / 84.8%) and VisGel [35] + Touch and Go (53.1% / 83.6%), confirming that the spatially aligned scene-level data provides broader material coverage than object-centric or tabletop datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on NeRF for 3D scene representation and latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion) for cross-modal generation, combining them in a novel pipeline where NeRF-rendered RGB-D images condition a diffusion model to synthesize tactile signals. It departs from the closest prior work, Zhong et al.'s 'Touching a NeRF,' by replacing expensive robotic proprioception with a simple multi-view geometry calibration that exploits the fact that vision-based touch sensors are themselves cameras. The contrastive pre-training stage follows Yang et al. (2023), but the key conceptual departure is the use of spatially aligned, scene-scale visual-tactile pairs rather than object-scale or unaligned data, enabling the model to exploit scene-specific geometry for touch imputation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 19.3k-pair scene-scale dataset of spatially aligned vision-tactile pairs is the first of its kind and provides a valuable resource for the visual-tactile learning community. The practical pipeline (smartphone + selfie stick + DIGIT sensor) makes data collection accessible without robotics expertise, potentially accelerating the field. Key limitations include centimeter-scale SfM errors causing pixel-level misalignments and the assumption that scenes do not deform under touch, which restricts applicability to inelastic surfaces. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to deformable or dynamic scenes, whether the diffusion model can operate in real time for robotic control, and how to handle the inherent ambiguity in touch (e.g., all similar edges feel the same).
