## Problem

Efficient Vision Transformers still contain unaddressed computational redundancy at two levels: early stages process an excessive number of spatially redundant tokens (e.g., 3136 tokens at 224×224 with 4×4 patches), and later stages employ multi-head attention where many heads are highly similar and individually dispensable. Prior work has focused on designing cheaper attention variants rather than questioning how tokens are constructed or whether multiple heads are necessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240222/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 224×224 or 256×256 for classification (ImageNet-1K, 1000 classes) and 512×512 crops for detection/segmentation (COCO). A key architectural detail is the 16×16 overlapping patchify stem (four strided 3×3 convolutions) that produces compact token representations with large receptive fields from the very first stage, reducing the feature map size by up to 16× compared to standard 4×4 patching. Outputs are class logits for classification, bounding boxes with class scores for RetinaNet detection, and instance masks for Mask R-CNN segmentation.

## Method

SHViT combines two memory-efficient design principles. At the macro level, it replaces the conventional 4×4 patch / 4-stage layout with a 16×16 overlapping patchify stem and a 3-stage hierarchy, where the first stage uses depthwise convolution (exploiting spatial inductive bias) and the remaining two stages use Single-Head Self-Attention (SHSA). At the micro level, SHSA splits the input channels into a partial attention subset (ratio 1/4.67) and a residual subset; single-head attention is applied only to the partial subset, the two are concatenated, and a full-channel output projection propagates attention features to all channels. This design inherently eliminates multi-head redundancy, reduces memory-bound operations (reshape, LayerNorm) to a fraction of the channels, and allows stacking more blocks with wider channels within the same compute budget. Four variants (S1–S4) scale depth and width, with S4 trained at 256×256 achieving 79.4% top-1 on ImageNet at 14,283 img/s on A100 GPU.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, SHViT-S4 achieves 79.4% top-1 accuracy with 986M FLOPs and 16.5M parameters, delivering 14,283 images/s on A100 GPU and 509 images/s on Intel CPU (ONNX). It is 3.3×, 8.1×, and 2.4× faster than MobileViTv2×1.0 on GPU, CPU, and iPhone 12 respectively, while being 1.3% more accurate. For object detection and instance segmentation on COCO with Mask R-CNN, SHViT-S4 achieves 39.0 AP^box and 35.9 AP^mask, matching FastViT-SA12 (38.9/35.9) while exhibiting 3.8× and 2.0× lower backbone latency on GPU and mobile device.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHViT dominates across all 19 compared baselines in the speed-accuracy tradeoff. At the ~79% accuracy tier, SHViT-S4 (79.4%) is 3.3× faster than MobileViTV2×1.0 (78.1%) on GPU and 8.1× faster on CPU, while also being 1.3% more accurate; it is 2.9×/3.3× faster than EfficientNet-B1 (79.1%) and 3.4×/5.5× faster than FastViT-T12 (79.1%) on GPU/CPU. At the ~77% tier, SHViT-S3 (77.4%) uses 3× fewer FLOPs than PoolFormer-S12 (77.2%) and is 3.8× faster on GPU and 6.1× faster in ONNX format, while outperforming FasterNet-T1 (76.2%) by 1.2% accuracy with 15.1% higher GPU throughput. At the ~75% tier, SHViT-S2 (75.2%) is 2.2×/2.5× faster than ShuffleNetV2×2.0 (74.9%) on GPU/CPU and 3.8× faster in ONNX. For downstream COCO tasks, SHViT-S4 as a Mask R-CNN backbone achieves 39.0 AP^box / 35.9 AP^mask, matching FastViT-SA12 (38.9/35.9) with 3.8× lower GPU latency (0.28ms vs 1.06ms) and 2.0× lower mobile latency (3.3ms vs 6.5ms), while outperforming EfficientViT-M4 (32.8/31.0) by 6.2 AP^box and 4.9 AP^mask.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of efficient ViT research (MobileViT, FastViT, EfficientViT, EdgeViT) that primarily focuses on designing cheaper token-mixing operations (sparse attention, separable attention, low-rank approximation) while retaining the conventional 4×4 patchify stem and 4-stage hierarchy. Instead, SHViT argues that the macro design—how tokens are constructed via patch embedding size and stage count—is the more critical lever for speed-accuracy tradeoff, and that a 16×16 overlapping patchify stem with only 3 stages eliminates most spatial redundancy at negligible accuracy cost. At the micro level, it builds on the growing evidence of multi-head redundancy (Michel et al., Voita et al.) but takes a different route: rather than pruning heads post-hoc, it designs a single-head attention that operates on only a fraction of channels, inherently preventing redundancy while reducing memory-bound operations like reshaping and layer normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that aggressive macro design (16×16 stem, 3 stages) outperforms sophisticated attention variants for latency-constrained deployment reframes where practitioners should invest architectural effort, and the partial-channel single-head attention offers a drop-in replacement for MHSA that is consistently faster across GPU, CPU, and mobile hardware. A key open question is whether the 16×16 stem's reduced early-stage spatial resolution becomes a bottleneck for tasks demanding fine-grained localization (e.g., small-object detection or high-resolution segmentation), and whether the fixed partial ratio (1/4.67) can be adapted per stage or learned. The paper's consistent speedups on ONNX and CoreML runtimes suggest the technique is well-suited for production edge deployment, though the single-head design may limit expressiveness for very large-scale or multi-task settings.
