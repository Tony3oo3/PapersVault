## Problem

Personalizing text-to-image diffusion models for specific concepts typically requires regularization images to prevent overfitting, involves finetuning a large number of parameters, and struggles to recontextualize the target concept into novel backgrounds without degrading unrelated regions. The paper addresses these limitations by reducing the parameter count, eliminating the need for regularization images, and introducing a sampling mechanism that preserves the base model's generative prior outside the concept region.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242377/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (typically a few) depicting the target concept and a text prompt containing a unique identifier token (e.g., 'V*') paired with a macro class noun (e.g., 'dog'). Training operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4's VAE, requiring only 150 iterations at batch size 4 on a single A100 GPU (~3 minutes). At inference, the model produces a standard generated image where the personalized concept appears in a novel scene specified by the prompt, optionally with LAG sampling to restrict residual application to the concept's localized region.

## Method

The approach learns low-rank residuals (LoRA-style, rank r_i = 0.05·m_i, totaling ~1.2M parameters or ~0.1% of the base model) for the 1×1 output projection conv layers following each cross-attention layer in the transformer blocks, rather than targeting the cross-attention key/value weights as in Custom Diffusion. The key insight is that these localized output projections capture finer identity details than global cross-attention operations, and the low-rank constraint inherently prevents overwriting the generative prior, eliminating the need for regularization images. The second contribution, Localized Attention-Guided (LAG) sampling, uses cross-attention maps at each denoising timestep to binarize (via median threshold) the spatial regions corresponding to the concept tokens, then computes each block's output as a spatially-weighted blend of features from the original weights and the personalized residual weights, applying the learned identity only where the concept is localized while delegating all other regions to the unmodified base model.

## Results

On CustomConcept101, the proposed method (1.2M trainable parameters, ~3 min training on a single A100) achieves the best identity preservation scores: CLIP image similarity 0.7594 and DINO image similarity 0.5671, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin on DINO (next best: DreamBooth at 0.5212). CLIP text alignment is 0.7193, lower than Custom Diffusion (0.7664) and DreamBooth (0.7536), though the user study shows human text-alignment preference is comparable to Custom Diffusion (50.99% vs. 50%). With LAG sampling, CLIP image drops slightly to 0.7424 and DINO to 0.5411, but the method still outperforms all baselines on image alignment while using 16× fewer parameters than Custom Diffusion and 820× fewer than DreamBooth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Textual Inversion (768 params), the proposed method wins decisively on all three metrics (CLIP text +0.104, CLIP image +0.034, DINO image +0.097), confirming that learning weight residuals is far more effective than optimizing a single token embedding. Against ViCo (51.3M params), the method wins on both image-alignment metrics (CLIP image 0.7594 vs. 0.7111; DINO 0.5671 vs. 0.4678) but trails slightly on CLIP text (0.7193 vs. 0.7403). Against DreamBooth (983M params), the method achieves higher CLIP image (0.7594 vs. 0.7424) and substantially higher DINO image (0.5671 vs. 0.5212) while using 820× fewer parameters and ~3 min training versus DreamBooth's full-model finetuning. Against Custom Diffusion (19M params), the method wins on both image metrics (CLIP image 0.7594 vs. 0.7074; DINO 0.5671 vs. 0.4669) but has lower CLIP text (0.7193 vs. 0.7664); however, the AMT user study shows human text-alignment preference is essentially a tie (50.99%), suggesting the CLIP text gap does not reflect perceived quality. Overall, the method achieves the best identity preservation with the fewest parameters and no regularization images, at the cost of a modest CLIP text-alignment gap that appears to be a metric artifact rather than a true quality deficit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LoRA (originally designed for large language model adaptation) to the text-to-image personalization setting, applying low-rank residuals to the output projection conv layers of Stable Diffusion's transformer blocks rather than the cross-attention key/value weights targeted by Custom Diffusion or the full model weights updated by DreamBooth. It departs from the standard personalization pipeline by eliminating the need for regularization images entirely, arguing that the low-rank constraint on a small subset of weights is sufficient to prevent overwriting the generative prior. The localized attention-guided (LAG) sampling further distinguishes the work by merging personalized and original model features spatially at inference time using cross-attention maps, a mechanism not present in prior personalization or attention-guided editing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 1.2M trainable parameters (~0.1% of the base model) and ~3 minutes of training on a single GPU make concept personalization dramatically cheaper than DreamBooth (983M params) or Custom Diffusion (19M params), while removing the often-fragile dependency on hand-picked regularization images. LAG sampling offers a clean, training-free mechanism to combat background overfitting, though the authors acknowledge it is not universally beneficial (e.g., color changes) and depends on attention map quality. Open questions include extending LAG to multi-concept generation, reducing sensitivity to macro class selection, and whether the approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion v1.4 to newer architectures.
