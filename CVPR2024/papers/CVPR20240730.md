## Problem

Creating diverse, interactive 3D environments for Embodied AI training currently relies on manual design, 3D scanning, or hard-coded procedural rules, all of which are labor-intensive and limit scene diversity. Existing text-to-3D generation methods produce non-interactive representations (meshes, NeRFs) with artifacts, while LLM-based layout methods that directly output numerical coordinates frequently produce physically implausible scenes with object collisions and boundary violations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240730/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language prompt (e.g., 'a 1b1b apartment of a researcher who has a cat' or 'three professors' office connected to a long hallway') and produces a complete interactive 3D scene within AI2-THOR, including floor plans with wall structures, material assignments (from 236 materials × 148 colors), doorways/windows (40 door styles, 21 window types), and placed 3D objects retrieved from a 51,464-asset Objaverse library. Because GPT-4 is probabilistic, the same prompt can yield multiple valid layouts with different asset selections and spatial arrangements.

## Method

HOLODECK is a multi-module pipeline that queries GPT-4 sequentially: a Floor & Wall Module generates room coordinates and material choices, a Doorway & Window Module adds connections, and an Object Selection Module retrieves assets from Objaverse using LLM-proposed descriptions and dimensions. The key technical contribution is the Constraint-based Layout Design Module, which avoids the collision and boundary errors of direct coordinate prediction by having GPT-4 output spatial relational constraints (10 types across 5 categories: global, distance, position, alignment, rotation) forming a scene graph. A DFS-based constraint satisfaction solver then places objects autoregressively from an LLM-identified anchor, enforcing hard constraints (no collisions, in-bounds) while softly satisfying relational constraints, and returns the layout maximizing total constraint satisfaction within a 30-second budget.

## Results

On zero-shot ObjectNav over NOVELTYTHOR (5 novel scene types, 92 unique object types), +HOLODECK achieves 20.40% average Success and 0.127 SPL, substantially outperforming the PROCTHOR-pretrained baseline (4.11% Success, 0.015 SPL) and the +OBJAVERSE ablation (17.02% Success, 0.093 SPL). In the comparative human evaluation on residential scenes (680 participants), annotators prefer HOLODECK over PROCTHOR on Asset Selection (59.8%), Layout Coherence (56.9%), and Overall Preference (64.4%). CLIP scores confirm that HOLODECK-generated scenes exceed PROCTHOR by large margins and closely approach the quality of human-designed iTHOR scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PROCTHOR (the sole prior system generating complete interactable scenes), HOLODECK wins decisively across all evaluation axes: in the 680-annotator human study, HOLODECK is preferred 64.4% of the time overall on residential scenes, and its CLIP scores exceed PROCTHOR by wide margins while approaching human-designed iTHOR. On the NOVELTYTHOR ObjectNav benchmark, the PROCTHOR-pretrained agent achieves only 4.11% average Success (nearly indistinguishable from random at 3.71%), whereas the HOLODECK-finetuned agent reaches 20.40% Success and 0.127 SPL — roughly a 5× improvement in Success. The +OBJAVERSE ablation (same asset selection but without HOLODECK's constraint-based layout) achieves 17.02% Success, confirming that the spatial layout design contributes an additional ~3.4 points. HOLODECK's advantage over +OBJAVERSE is largest on Music Room (31.08% vs 25.69% Success) and Daycare (15.61% vs 8.99%), where commonsense object proximity matters, while the two methods perform comparably on Gym and Arcade. Against the human-designed iTHOR upper bound, HOLODECK's CLIP scores are close but not yet matching, indicating room for further improvement in visual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOLODECK builds directly on the AI2-THOR simulation platform and extends PROCTHOR's procedural generation framework by replacing hard-coded rules with GPT-4-guided design decisions. It departs from prior LLM-for-layout work (e.g., LayoutGPT) by not having the LLM output absolute coordinates, which the paper shows leads to collisions and boundary violations; instead, the LLM generates spatial relational constraints that a DFS-based constraint satisfaction solver resolves. For 3D assets, it moves beyond category-specific datasets (3D-FRONT, etc.) by leveraging the 51K+ annotated Objaverse collection, and it contrasts with text-to-3D scene generation methods (SceneEscape, Text2Room, Text2NeRF) that produce non-interactive meshes or NeRFs unsuitable for embodied AI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOLODECK demonstrates that LLM commonsense priors can be harnessed to automate the creation of diverse, interactive 3D environments for embodied AI, reducing the manual design bottleneck that has limited scene diversity in prior benchmarks. The NOVELTYTHOR benchmark and the finding that agents fine-tuned on HOLODECK-generated scenes transfer to expert-designed novel environments (e.g., music rooms, daycares) open a path toward general-purpose embodied agents without per-scene-type human annotation. Limitations include struggles with complex multi-room layouts (restaurants) and assets absent from Objaverse (e.g., dental x-ray machines), and the constraint solver's 30-second time budget may not scale to very large scenes. Future work could incorporate more assets, richer constraint types, and broader embodied AI tasks beyond ObjectNav.
