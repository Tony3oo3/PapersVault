## Problem

PSF engineering is a well-established technique for encoding depth information into CMOS sensor images, but it has not been adapted to neuromorphic event cameras, which respond to log-intensity changes rather than absolute intensity. Without event-specific information-theoretic bounds, it is unclear whether existing optimal PSF designs (e.g., the Fisher phase mask) remain optimal for the fundamentally different measurement model of event sensors, particularly for tracking moving point sources at kilohertz rates.

## Input / Output

For mask design, the optimization consumes sampled 3D positions and Brownian-motion direction vectors across 11 depth planes and produces continuous phase masks or binary amplitude masks parameterized by small MLPs (~2× fewer parameters than pixel-wise grids). For tracking, the decoder CNN takes 256×256 single-channel binned event frames (accumulated over 16 refresh cycles, yielding 1000 frames per second) and outputs a normalized 3D position vector; ground truth is the emitter location at the end of each 16-frame bin.

## Method

The paper first derives the Fisher Information matrix and Cramér Rao Bound for event camera measurements by modeling the log-intensity difference as a ratio of Poisson variables approximated by a Normal distribution, yielding a closed-form 6×6 FI matrix for two consecutive time steps. To optimize optical elements under this non-convex CRB objective, the authors introduce implicit neural representations: a SIREN-style 4-layer MLP (hidden size 128, sin activation) for phase masks (NPM) and a 4-layer MLP with SoftPlus activations followed by a sigmoid for binary amplitude masks (NAM), both mapping 2D spatial coordinates to mask values. Masks are trained for 10,000 epochs with Adam (lr=1e-3), minimizing the summed CRB over 3 random orthogonal Brownian motions sampled per epoch across 11 depth planes. A separate 5-block CNN with ELU activations and max pooling is then trained for 100 epochs on 16,000-step Brownian trajectories to decode 3D position from binned event frames, supplemented by 2000 random position-motion pairs.

## Results

On 3D Brownian-motion tracking over an 8×8×4 μm volume, the proposed Neural Phase Mask (NPM) achieves 51.2 nm 3D RMSE and 39.2 nm depth L1 error, outperforming the Fisher phase mask (52.6 nm / 44.2 nm) and the open aperture (617 nm / 936 nm). The Neural Amplitude Mask (NAM) achieves 66.0 nm 3D RMSE and 49.2 nm depth L1, dramatically outperforming the Levin et al. coded aperture (764 nm / 1036 nm). In terms of Cramér-Rao Bound, NPM achieves 33.1 nm average CRB versus 36.3 nm for Fisher and 80.8 nm for the open aperture, while NAM achieves 50.5 nm versus 263.3 nm for Levin et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two CMOS-optimized optical designs: the Fisher phase mask [52] (optimal for static blinking emitters on CMOS) and the Levin et al. coded aperture [33] (depth-from-defocus for CMOS). Against the Fisher mask, the proposed NPM achieves a modest 2.7% improvement in 3D RMSE (51.2 vs 52.6 nm) but a more meaningful 11.3% improvement in depth recovery (39.2 vs 44.2 nm L1), confirming that the Fisher mask is near-optimal for static localization but sub-optimal for tracking moving sources. Against the Levin et al. amplitude mask, the proposed NAM is dramatically superior, reducing 3D RMSE from 764 nm to 66.0 nm (an 11.6× improvement) and depth L1 from 1036 nm to 49.2 nm (a 21× improvement), demonstrating that the CMOS-optimized binary aperture is fundamentally ill-suited for event-camera tracking. The open aperture (no coding) serves as a reference and is outperformed by all coded designs in tracking, though it has a slightly lower CRB near the focal plane.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fisher PSF design framework (Shechtman et al., 2014), which found optimal phase masks for 3D single-emitter localization with CMOS sensors, to the fundamentally different measurement model of neuromorphic event cameras that record log-intensity changes rather than absolute photon counts. It also builds on the 2D event-camera SMLM measurement model of Cabriel et al. (2023) by generalizing from blinking (static) particles to moving emitters, requiring a new CRB derivation that accounts for the ratio-of-Poisson structure in consecutive event frames. Methodologically, the paper adapts SIREN-style implicit neural representations (Sitzmann et al., 2020) to parameterize both phase and binary amplitude masks, replacing the restricted search spaces used in prior coded-aperture design (Levin et al., 2007) with a differentiable, continuous optimization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first information-theoretic (CRB) framework for 3D tracking with event cameras, this work provides a principled design criterion for future event-camera optics and clarifies when existing CMOS-optimized masks are sufficient versus when event-specific redesign is needed. The neural-representation approach to mask design is generalizable beyond event cameras to other non-convex optical design problems. Key limitations include the single-emitter assumption, the idealized noise-free event model, and the use of binned frames that do not fully exploit the asynchronous, per-pixel timing of event sensors; extending to multi-emitter scenes and hardware-realistic noise models remains open.
