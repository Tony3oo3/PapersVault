## Problem

Existing image editing methods—whether general instruction-based models or region-based approaches—cannot perform object-level editing when multiple objects coexist in a scene. General models lack the discriminative ability to locate a specific target object from a referring expression, while region-based methods require users to manually provide masks or bounding boxes. This paper introduces Referring Image Editing (RIE), a task that demands a single model simultaneously identify the target object via a text prompt and generate a natural edit while preserving all unrelated regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241777/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 480×480 source image and a single natural-language text prompt that combines a referring expression (e.g., 'the cow in the back') with an editing instruction (e.g., 'replace with a panda'). The output is a full-resolution edited image where only the referred object is modified and the remainder of the scene is preserved. Notably, the editing region is not strictly bounded by the source object's segmentation mask—the model is expected to produce spatially natural outputs even when the target object's shape differs substantially from the source (e.g., replacing a person with a truck).

## Method

ReferDiffusion builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with InstructPix2Pix and introduces a Region-Embedder Module (REM) that disentangles the prompt into three specialized embeddings: a vision embedding (aggregated from image features within the predicted source-object mask), a referring embedding (encoding spatial location of the target), and an editing embedding (encoding the modification instruction). The referring embedding extractor jointly predicts the source-object mask via an MLP-filtered image feature, supervised by ground-truth masks, thereby grounding the model's discriminative ability. A Region-Control Loss applies stop-gradient latent supervision to pixels outside the edited area at every diffusion step, explicitly penalizing background alteration. Training follows a two-stage mixed-supervision strategy: first, self-supervised learning on synthetic samples (objects covered with random colors, mask used as referring cue) to teach region-constrained generation; second, full supervision on the RefCOCO-Edit dataset to learn the complete identify-and-edit pipeline.

## Results

On the RefCOCO-Edit dataset, ReferDiffusion achieves the best background consistency (L2bg = 0.028) and the highest foreground CLIP similarity (CLIP-Simfg = 25.06) among all compared methods, while maintaining a competitive aesthetic score (AP = 4.60, second only to MagicBrush's 4.62). In the user study, ReferDiffusion also attains the highest consistency rating (3.63/5) with a substantial margin over all baselines (3.02–3.27), confirming its superior ability to edit only the referred object while preserving the rest of the scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReferDiffusion outperforms all four baselines on the two task-specific metrics: it achieves the lowest L2bg (0.028 vs. 0.031–0.044 for instruction-based methods) and the highest CLIP-Simfg (25.06 vs. 23.40–24.32), indicating it both preserves the background better and edits the correct target object more faithfully. Against the region-based baseline Blend Diffusion [3], ReferDiffusion leads on both AP (4.60 vs. 4.47) and CLIP-Simfg (25.06 vs. 23.40), demonstrating that simply providing a ground-truth mask to a region-based editor is insufficient for natural object-level editing. Among the instruction-based methods, MagicBrush [55] edges out ReferDiffusion on AP (4.62 vs. 4.60) but trails on both L2bg (0.034 vs. 0.028) and CLIP-Simfg (24.32 vs. 25.06), suggesting it occasionally edits unintended regions. InstructPix2Pix [4] and InstructDiffusion [19] are clearly outperformed on all three metrics, with InstructDiffusion showing the worst background consistency (L2bg = 0.044). The user study further confirms that ReferDiffusion's consistency advantage (3.63 vs. ≤3.27) is the most pronounced gap, validating its discriminative capability in locating and editing only the referred object.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of referring expression segmentation (RES) and diffusion-based image editing, explicitly positioning RIE as the missing generative step that prior RES frameworks (VLT, LAVT, etc.) could not provide. On the editing side, it departs from region-based methods (Blended Latent Diffusion, Paint-by-Example) that require manual masks, and from general instruction-based editors (InstructPix2Pix, InstructDiffusion, MagicBrush) that lack the discriminative ability to target a specific object among many. The key architectural departure is the Region-Embedder Module, which disentangles a single prompt into separate referring and editing embeddings, combined with a Region-Control Loss inspired by DragGAN/DragDiffusion to preserve unedited regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing RIE and releasing RefCOCO-Edit, the paper gives the community a concrete benchmark for the previously informal goal of 'edit only the object I point to with words,' a capability that general-purpose editors demonstrably lack. However, the dataset is very small (400 images, most with ≥2 samples), the discrimination component remains weaker than dedicated RES models, and the method inherits the generative limitations of Stable Diffusion v1.5. Open questions include whether the mixed-supervision strategy scales to larger, more diverse datasets, whether the REM can handle multi-object or compositional referring expressions, and whether the task generalizes beyond the RefCOCO visual domain to video or 3D scenes.
