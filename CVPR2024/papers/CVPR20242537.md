## Problem

Linear Probe (LP) has been widely reported as a weak baseline in few-shot CLIP adaptation, motivating extensive research on complex prompt-learning and adapter methods. However, standard LP completely discards CLIP's text-encoder knowledge and relies on intensive validation-set searches for learning rates, both of which are suboptimal. This paper asks whether a principled generalization of LP, combined with convex-optimization insights typically overlooked in deep learning, can close or close the gap to state-of-the-art methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed, l2-normalized image features (D=1024-dim vectors from CLIP's frozen ResNet-50 encoder) and pre-computed text embeddings (from CLIP's frozen text encoder using templates like 'a photo of a [class_k]'). It operates entirely in a black-box fashion, requiring no access to encoder internals or gradient back-propagation through them. Given a support set of S labeled images per class, it outputs a class label per query image via softmax over the blended logits f_i^T(w_k + α_k t_k).

## Method

LP++ generalizes the standard linear probe by making the classifier logits learnable functions of the text embedding: l_ik = f_i^T(w_k + α_k t_k), where w_k are visual class prototypes and α_k are trainable class-wise blending parameters. The objective (cross-entropy) is minimized via a Block Majorize-Minimize (BMM) descent algorithm in which step sizes are implicit and data-driven, derived from approximate Lipschitz constants of the loss (computed as the max eigenvalue of a single D×D matrix rather than the full K(D+1)×K(D+1) Hessian). This eliminates the need for validation-set learning-rate searches. Additionally, the loss is decomposed into convex components whose closed-form minima (hard and soft means of features and cosine similarities) provide data-informed initializations for w and α, yielding substantially lower initial loss than random initialization and even outperforming training-free Tip-Adapter in the zero-update setting.

## Results

On the average of 11 classification benchmarks, LP++ achieves 63.43% top-1 accuracy in the 1-shot setting, 69.16% in 4-shot, and 74.42% in 16-shot, outperforming the standard LP baseline (36.10% at 1-shot) by over 27 points. LP++ matches or exceeds the best adapter (Tip-Adapter-F*, 74.37% at 16-shot) and prompt-learning methods (PLOT, 74.35% at 16-shot) while running in ~2 seconds versus ~50 minutes for Tip-Adapter-F* and ~17 hours for CoOp. The method also exhibits the lowest standard deviation across 10 sampled tasks (e.g., ±0.90 at 1-shot vs. ±3.11 for CoOp), indicating more stable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LP++ outperforms the standard LP baseline by 27.3 points at 1-shot (63.43% vs. 36.10%) and 3.9 points at 16-shot (74.42% vs. 70.56%), demonstrating that incorporating text embeddings via learnable blending parameters is critical. Against prompt-learning methods, LP++ beats CoOp by 3.8 points at 1-shot and PLOT by 1.9 points, while also being orders of magnitude faster (~2s vs. ~10–17h) and compatible with black-box settings. Among adapters, LP++ surpasses Tip-Adapter-F (fixed hyper-parameters) by 2.1 points at 1-shot and CLIP-Adapter by 3.1 points; it edges out Tip-Adapter-F* (grid-searched) by 0.35 points at 16-shot while being ~25× faster. The only setting where LP++ trails is 2-shot, where Tip-Adapter-F* leads by 0.27 points (66.47% vs. 66.20%). Overall, LP++ achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all shot regimes without any hyperparameter search, using data-driven Lipschitz-based step sizes instead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the standard Linear Probe (LP) baseline from CLIP [23], which had been widely reported as a weak baseline in few-shot CLIP adaptation, by introducing learnable class-wise blending parameters that mix visual prototypes with pre-trained text embeddings. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant research trajectory of prompt learning (CoOp, PLOT, KgCoOp, ProGrad) and adapter methods (CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter) that build increasingly complex non-linear transformations, instead applying convex-optimization tools—specifically Majorize-Minimize descent with Lipschitz-derived data-driven step sizes—to a simple linear objective. The positioning is explicitly adversarial to the field's assumption that LP's weakness stems from its simplicity, arguing instead that the deficiency was the omission of text knowledge and the use of ad-hoc learning-rate search rather than principled optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LP++ provides the few-shot CLIP community with a strong, black-box-compatible baseline that runs in seconds rather than hours, fundamentally changing the bar that prompt-learning and adapter methods must clear to demonstrate meaningful progress. The convex-optimization perspective (implicit step sizes, data-informed initialization from hard/soft feature means) is a transferable toolkit that could inform other VLM adaptation settings beyond classification. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks requiring richer output structure (detection, segmentation), whether the convexity assumptions hold for larger or more heterogeneous support sets, and whether the speed advantage persists when the number of classes or feature dimensionality grows substantially.
