## Problem

Existing single-view 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: SDS-based approaches (e.g., DreamFusion, Magic123) produce detailed geometry but require tens of minutes of per-shape optimization and suffer from multi-face inconsistencies, while direct 3D generative models (e.g., Point-E, Shap-E) are fast but generalize poorly due to limited 3D training data. Prior multi-view image generation methods (SyncDreamer, MVDream) improve consistency but rely solely on color images, which encode geometry ambiguously and struggle to recover fine surface details without expensive additional optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image as input, along with a CLIP text embedding and camera parameters for six fixed views (front, back, left, right, front-right, front-left). It produces multi-view normal maps and corresponding color images at 256×256 resolution, which are then fused into a textured 3D mesh via SDF optimization in approximately 2–3 minutes. The domain switcher selects whether the diffusion model outputs normals or colors, so both modalities are generated from the same input in separate forward passes.

## Method

Wonder3D extends a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model to jointly model the distribution of multi-view normal maps and color images through a cross-domain diffusion scheme. A lightweight domain switcher (a 1D vector encoded via positional encoding and concatenated with the time embedding) is injected into the UNet, allowing the same network to generate either normals or colors without perturbing pre-trained weights. Multi-view self-attention is made global across views to enforce inter-view consistency, while a novel cross-domain attention layer combines keys and values from both the normal and color domains within each transformer block to ensure geometric-visual coherence. For mesh extraction, a geometry-aware normal loss weights each ray's normal supervision by the cosine of the angle between the generated normal and viewing direction (discarding back-facing rays and up-weighting oblique views), and an outlier-dropping strategy discards the top percentage of color errors per iteration to prevent isolated surface artifacts. The model is fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion Image Variations on ~30k objects from the Objaverse LVIS subset for 30k steps at 256×256 resolution.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset, Wonder3D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0199 and Volume IoU of 0.6244, outperforming all seven baselines on both metrics. The closest competitor, SyncDreamer, records a Chamfer Distance of 0.0261 and Volume IoU of 0.5421, meaning Wonder3D improves CD by ~24% and IoU by ~15% relative to the best prior method. For novel view synthesis (Table 2), Wonder3D also leads with PSNR 26.07, SSIM 0.924, and LPIPS 0.065, substantially ahead of SyncDreamer (PSNR 20.05, SSIM 0.798, LPIPS 0.146).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wonder3D outperforms all seven baselines on both Chamfer Distance and Volume IoU. Against the strongest baseline SyncDreamer (CD 0.0261, IoU 0.5421), Wonder3D reduces Chamfer Distance by 0.0062 and increases Volume IoU by 0.0823, reflecting substantially sharper geometric detail. Zero123 (CD 0.0339, IoU 0.5035) is the second-best but still trails Wonder3D by 41% in CD. The 3D generative models Point-E (CD 0.0426) and Shap-E (CD 0.0436) produce incomplete and distorted meshes, yielding the worst IoU scores (0.2875 and 0.3584 respectively). One-2-3-45 (CD 0.0629) captures only coarse geometries due to inheriting inconsistencies from Zero123's multi-view outputs, while RealFusion (CD 0.0819) and Magic123 (CD 0.0516), both SDS-based per-shape optimization methods, suffer from the Janus problem and low geometric fidelity. Qualitatively, the paper notes that SyncDreamer's shapes are roughly aligned but lack fine details and have subpar textures, whereas Wonder3D recovers high-frequency geometric structures and coherent textures in 2–3 minutes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Wonder3D extends the multi-view diffusion paradigm introduced by SyncDreamer and MVDream by adding a second generation domain (surface normals alongside color images) through a lightweight domain switcher and cross-domain attention layers, all built on top of pre-trained Stable Diffusion. It departs from SDS-based per-shape optimization methods (DreamFusion, RealFusion, Magic123) by replacing iterative optimization with a single forward pass of the diffusion model, and from 3D-native generative models (Point-E, Shape-E) by operating in 2D space for better zero-shot generalization. For the reconstruction stage, it builds on instant-NGP SDF optimization but introduces a geometry-aware normal loss with view-dependent weighting and an outlier-dropping strategy to handle the sparsity and imperfections of generated views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3 minute end-to-end pipeline (vs. tens of minutes to hours for SDS methods) makes high-fidelity single-view 3D reconstruction practically usable for content creation and downstream applications. The cross-domain formulation—jointly modeling normals and colors as a single diffusion target—is a clean architectural idea that could transfer to other multi-modal 2D generation tasks. However, the current 6-view limitation constrains reconstruction of thin structures and heavily occluded objects, and scaling to denser view sets would require more efficient multi-view attention. An open question is whether the cross-domain attention mechanism generalizes beyond the normals+colors pairing to other geometric cues (depth, curvature) without the convergence issues observed with naive channel expansion.
