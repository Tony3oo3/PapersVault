## Problem

Generating 360° panorama images from text prompts is hindered by the scarcity of text-panorama training pairs and the geometric domain gap between equirectangular-projection panoramas and the perspective images on which pre-trained diffusion models are trained. Existing multi-view stitching approaches like MVDiffusion lack global understanding, producing repetitive elements and semantic inconsistencies across the panorama.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240778/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (and optionally a room layout rendered as a distance map) and produces a 360° horizontal × 180° vertical FOV equirectangular panorama at 512×1024 resolution (2:1 aspect ratio). Internally, the panorama branch operates at full resolution while the perspective branch works at 256×256 with 20 camera views sampled on an icosahedron at 90° FOV, and the final output is decoded from the panorama latent map through the pre-trained SD decoder.

## Method

PanFusion is a dual-branch latent diffusion model where a panorama branch (global layout, loop-consistent via circular padding and latent rotation) and a perspective branch (local detail, exploiting SD's perspective prior) share the same UNet backbone with separate LoRA adapters. The two branches are coupled through an Equirectangular-Perspective Projection Attention (EPPA) module that uses spherical positional encoding to align corresponding pixels across projections and a soft attention mask to focus on geometrically relevant regions, enabling bijective cross-attention without interpolation loss. Joint latent map initialization projects the panorama noise into each perspective view so overlapping regions start from correlated noise. Training jointly optimizes noise-prediction losses on both branches while keeping the SD UNet frozen, and a ControlNet on the panorama branch enables layout-conditioned generation.

## Results

On the Matterport3D dataset, PanFusion achieves the best panorama realism with FAED 6.04 and FID 46.47, outperforming SD+LoRA (FAED 7.19, FID 51.69) and Pano Branch (FAED 7.90, FID 50.40). In the 20-view perspective evaluation, PanFusion attains the lowest FID of 17.04, compared to 19.32 for SD+LoRA and 20.10 for Pano Branch. In the horizontal 8-view setting (matching MVDiffusion's protocol), PanFusion achieves FID 19.88 and IS 6.50, surpassing MVDiffusion's FID 25.27. For layout-conditioned generation, PanFusion reaches 3D IoU 68.46 and 2D IoU 71.82, exceeding MVDiffusion (61.06 / 64.43) and approaching the GT upper bound (74.31 / 77.15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MVDiffusion [47], PanFusion substantially reduces FID in the 8-view setting from 25.27 to 19.88 (a 21% improvement) while also improving IS from 6.90 to 6.50; MVDiffusion retains a slight CS advantage (26.34 vs. 24.98), but the authors note this metric inherently favors MVDiffusion since it evaluates its native outputs without interpolation. Compared to the SD+LoRA baseline, PanFusion improves FAED from 7.19 to 6.04 and FID from 51.69 to 46.47 in the panorama domain, and reduces 20-view FID from 19.32 to 17.04, confirming that the dual-branch design with EPPA adds meaningful quality beyond simple LoRA fine-tuning. Versus the Pano Branch (SD+LoRA with circular padding for loop consistency), PanFusion further lowers FAED from 7.90 to 6.04 and 20-view FID from 20.10 to 17.04, demonstrating the value of the perspective branch. PanFusion's IS is marginally lower than baselines (4.36 vs. 4.54), which the authors attribute to reduced generation of spurious objects; similarly, CS is slightly lower (28.58 vs. 28.83), likely because baselines' repetitive elements artificially inflate text-image alignment scores. In the layout-conditioned setting, PanFusion outperforms MVDiffusion+ControlNet on both 3D IoU (68.46 vs. 61.06) and 2D IoU (71.82 vs. 64.43) while also achieving better FID (22.58 vs. 28.83), showing that the global panorama branch enables stronger layout adherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanFusion builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture, repurposing its UNet backbone, encoder, and decoder as the foundation for both the panorama and perspective branches. It departs from MVDiffusion's strategy of generating multiple perspective views and stitching them (which causes repetitive elements and loop inconsistency) and from iterative inpainting approaches like PanoGen (which suffer from error accumulation). The key architectural departure is the dual-branch design with the EPPA cross-attention mechanism, which explicitly models the equirectangular-to-perspective projection geometry during denoising rather than treating the two domains independently. Practical contributions include circular padding for loop consistency and joint latent noise initialization to maintain cross-branch correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Text-to-360° panorama generation is a prerequisite for immersive applications in VR/AR, autonomous driving, and visual navigation, yet prior methods struggled with global consistency and geometric distortion. PanFusion's dual-branch design offers a principled way to combine the global layout coherence of panorama-space generation with the rich detail of perspective-space generation, and its extensibility to layout-conditioned generation (via ControlNet) opens a path toward controllable indoor scene synthesis. However, the higher computational cost of running two UNet branches simultaneously, the occasional failure to generate room entrances, and the reliance on a single indoor dataset (Matterport3D) limit immediate broad applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or mixed scenes at scale, and whether the dual-branch overhead can be reduced through distillation or architectural simplification.
