## Problem

Self-attention in LLMs incurs quadratic computational cost that scales poorly with sequence length, yet directly applying sparse attention to pre-trained models fails due to the large gap between sparse and full attention used during pre-training. The paper further asks whether the heavy computation in full attention is actually necessary, or whether some of it is redundant or even harmful to performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241142/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For LLMs, the input is a text token sequence (up to 16,384 tokens for long-context experiments); for multi-modal LLMs, it is an RGB image paired with a text prompt. During inference, only the top s% (e.g., 30–50%) of key-value pairs per query are retained for the actual attention computation, selected via a low-rank (r=8) approximation of the full attention map. The output is a standard generated text response, but the internal attention computation is sparsified, reducing self-attention FLOPs by more than half without altering the model's output format.

## Method

LoRA-Sparse introduces two additional low-rank linear projections (W_q̂, W_k̂ ∈ R^{d×r}, r≪d) that compress queries and keys into a low-rank space, where an approximate attention map is computed at much lower cost. This approximate map is used to rank and select the top-s% most relevant Q-K pairs, after which full d-dimensional attention is computed only for those selected pairs. The low-rank layers are trained with an Order Mimic Loss—a soft-margin objective that pushes the lowest-scoring positive (top-s%) samples above the highest-scoring negative samples in the low-rank space—combined with a magnitude loss that constrains the dynamic range of the predicted attention scores to match the original. Because the method adds only small projection layers and does not modify pre-trained weights or model structure, it can be applied to existing LLMs (LLaMA, LLaVA) via standard fine-tuning without from-scratch training.

## Results

On multi-modal benchmarks, LoRA-Sparse (Vicuna-7B) achieves 68.4% on ScienceQA-Image, 62.4% on GQA, 86.8% on POPE, and 58.8% on SEED-Bench, outperforming the LLaVA-1.5 baseline (67.6%, 62.0%, 85.9%, 58.6%) while using only 50% of the self-attention computation. On LLM benchmarks, the method improves arc-challenge from 45.6% to 47.3% and SST from 70.9% to 75.8% over the full-attention LLaMA 7B baseline. For long-context language modeling, LLaMA 7B+Ours maintains perplexity of 10.80–11.01 across 4096–16384 token contexts, far exceeding LLaMA's native 2048-token limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct LLaVA-1.5 baseline (same Vicuna-7B backbone), LoRA-Sparse wins on GQA (+0.4), ScienceQA-Image (+0.8), POPE (+0.9), and SEED-Bench (+0.2), ties on TextVQA (58.2) and LLaVA-Bench (63.4), all while halving self-attention FLOPs. Against larger or differently-architected multi-modal models, LoRA-Sparse outperforms BLIP-2 (Vicuna-13B) by 1.4 points on ScienceQA and 17.4 on GQA, and beats InstructBLIP (Vicuna-13B) by 5.3 on ScienceQA and 12.9 on GQA. On LLM benchmarks (Table 6), the sparse method improves over the full-attention LLaMA 7B baseline on 11 of 13 tasks, with the largest gain on SST (+4.9) and arc-challenge (+1.7), while showing only marginal drops on arc-easy (−0.2) and ReCoRD (−0.4). Compared to the deformable attention mechanism (Table 5), LoRA-Sparse retains near-baseline accuracy (e.g., 73.2 vs 73.4 on arc-easy) whereas deformable attention collapses to 24.4, confirming that discrete token interpolation is unsuitable for LLM attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of efficient attention work (fixed patterns, learnable patterns, low-rank methods, kernels) by reframing sparsity as a plug-and-play module for pre-trained LLMs rather than a structural redesign. It borrows the magnitude-loss idea from CLIP [36] to stabilize low-rank attention training and uses positional interpolation [6] to extend context windows, but its core departure is the Order Mimic Loss, which trains low-rank projections to replicate the *ordering* of full-attention scores rather than their values. Unlike prior sparse-attention methods that were designed as discrete modules for new architectures, LoRA-Sparse operates without modifying the base model's weights or structure, making it directly applicable to frozen pre-trained LLMs like LLaMA and LLaVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that removing ~50% of self-attention computation can *improve* accuracy (not merely preserve it) challenges the implicit assumption that full attention is necessary for optimal LLM performance, suggesting that redundant attention acts as noise. Practically, this offers a low-cost path to halving attention FLOPs for long-context and multi-modal inference without retraining from scratch. Key limitations include modest absolute gains (≤1.7%), evidence confined to the LLaMA/LLaVA family, and the authors' own note that GPU acceleration is limited at lower sparsity ratios. Open questions include whether the redundancy finding generalizes to other architectures (e.g., GPT, Mistral) and whether the optimal sparsity ratio is task- or domain-dependent.
