## Problem

Neural Architecture Search is prohibitively expensive: search spaces exceed 10^10 candidate architectures and each evaluation requires full training, while existing cost-reduction methods like OFA supernetworks still demand massive one-time GPU investments and are dataset-specific. The paper asks whether one can learn to *build* a good architecture from interpretable module-level knowledge rather than exhaustively *search* for one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241919/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of evaluated architectures (as few as 68 for Stable Diffusion, ~3000 for ImageNet) with scalar performance labels (accuracy, latency, FID, PQ) and a macro-level search space definition specifying stages, permitted layer types, and their configurations. Architectures are encoded as sequence-like DAGs where nodes represent layers with features for operation type, kernel size, channel expansion ratio, and positional information. The output is interpretable importance scores at multiple granularities—individual node features (0-hop), single operations (1-hop), and multi-layer subgraph sequences (multi-hop)—which can directly construct architectures or prune the search space to a manageable subset for subsequent NAS.

## Method

AutoBuild trains a GNN on architecture graphs using a hop-level ranking loss that enforces Spearman rank correlation between graph embedding norms and ground-truth performance labels at every hop level, creating a Magnitude Ranked Embedding Space where higher-performing subgraphs receive larger embedding norms. A Feature Embedding MLP at hop 0 decomposes node embeddings into per-feature-category scalars, enabling interpretable importance ranking of individual design choices (e.g., kernel size vs. channel expansion) without additional annotations. Cross-hop score comparison is achieved via distribution shifting that aligns embedding norm distributions to the target label statistics at each hop level, accounting for the performance bias of larger subgraphs. The differentiable SRCC computation from Blondel et al. makes the ranking loss end-to-end trainable. These scores are then used to select top-K subgraphs per stage, either directly constructing architectures or forming a reduced search space (K^|stages| candidates) for evolutionary NAS.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4 Inpainting (Places365), AutoBuild selects top-4 architectures with an average FID of 10.13 and best FID of 9.96, substantially outperforming Exhaustive Search (10.82 / 10.29) and the 68 labeled architectures (22.13 / 10.54). On ImageNet macro-search spaces (MobileNetV3 and ProxylessNAS), AutoBuild-NAS produces superior accuracy-latency Pareto frontiers across all tested hardware (GPU, CPU, NPU), notably achieving >79% accuracy at <5 ms latency on MBv3-NPU. For Panoptic Segmentation on MS-COCO, AutoBuild-constructed MBv3 architectures reach PQ ≥ 39, exceeding the best fully fine-tuned architectures reported by AIO-P [27].

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Layer-Insight [25] (Mills et al., CIKM 2021), which reduces the search space via statistical sampling of thousands of architectures plus human expert knowledge, AutoBuild-NAS achieves strictly superior Pareto frontiers on all MBv3 and PN-GPU configurations with only 3k labeled samples and no manual insight. The gap is most pronounced at the low-latency end of the frontier, where Layer-Insight's gains are described as 'quite small and inconsistent.' Against AIO-P [27] (Mills et al., AAAI 2023), AutoBuild-constructed MBv3 architectures for Panoptic Segmentation surpass the PQ-FLOPs Pareto frontier of AIO-P's fully fine-tuned models, particularly with the balanced target y=√PQ−√FLOPs. In the Stable Diffusion Inpainting setting, AutoBuild also outperforms an ensemble-predictor-based Exhaustive Search baseline (trained on the same 68 architectures) by 0.69 FID on average and 0.33 FID on the best architecture, demonstrating that interpretable subgraph scoring provides a meaningful advantage over pure predictor-guided search even under extreme data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoBuild departs from the dominant NAS paradigm of traversing or predicting over vast search spaces by instead learning interpretable importance scores for architecture modules via a hop-level ranking loss on GNN embeddings. It builds on the GNN-based architecture representation used by predictors like GennaPE and PINAT, but introduces a Magnitude Ranked Embedding Space constraint (inspired by SPMiner's ordered embedding space) that forces embedding norms to correlate with performance labels, enabling direct module scoring. Unlike NAS-BOWL's WL-kernel motif extraction or sampling-based profiling methods that require large datasets, AutoBuild operates on macro-search sequence graphs and learns from limited labeled architectures. The hop-level ranking loss is conceptually related to CT-NAS's pairwise ranking but applied at every GNN layer to produce per-subgraph scores rather than pairwise comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoBuild offers a practical path to reducing or eliminating the search cost in NAS by directly constructing architectures from learned module importance scores, demonstrated across ImageNet classification, panoptic segmentation, and Stable Diffusion inpainting. The ability to work from as few as 68 labeled architectures (for SDv1.4) is practically significant given the extreme cost of evaluating diffusion model variants. Key limitations include the manual design of target equations (e.g., acc/lat, √PQ−√FLOPs) to focus on different Pareto regions, the restriction to macro-search spaces, and the need for a GNN predictor to be trained before scores are available. Open questions include automating the equation-creation process, extending to micro-level cell-based search spaces, and validating whether the learned module scores transfer across tasks or hardware targets.
