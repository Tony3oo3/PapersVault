## Problem

Existing point cloud completion methods rely on thousands of input points and degrade severely when only tens of points are available, a scenario common in real-world sensing with limited range and resolution. No prior work systematically investigates how few points suffice for shape recovery or designs an architecture specifically for this extreme sparsity regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse 3D point cloud of 64 to 2048 points (X ∈ R^{n×3}) as input, where points are generated by back-projecting 2.5D depth images into 3D and downsampling. It produces a completed dense 3D point cloud (Y ∈ R^{m×3}) with a fixed output resolution. The minimum viable input is 64 points, below which all methods (including FSC) show sharp performance degradation, as confirmed by both FPFH Shannon entropy analysis and empirical evaluation.

## Method

FSC employs a dual-branch encoder: an extensive branch (two-layer stacked MLPs with max pooling, mirroring PCN) ensures every input point contributes to the global feature, while a salient branch (MLPs augmented with offset attention and cascaded attention modules) dynamically reweights point importance. A two-stage WGAN-based revision network then refines both the latent feature vector (7-layer FC generator vs. 4-layer attention-enhanced discriminator) and the coarse point cloud (3-layer generator vs. 4-layer discriminator), aligning them with ground-truth distributions. The revised feature and point cloud are decoded via PointNet++ with multi-head external attention for local-global fusion, followed by FoldingNet to produce the final detailed point cloud. Training combines EMD on the coarse output, Chamfer Distance on the detailed output, and adversarial losses at both the feature and point-cloud levels.

## Results

On the primary few-point task (64 input points, 8 seen ShapeNet categories), FSC achieves an average CD-ℓ₁ of 7.89, substantially outperforming the next-best method SVDFormer (11.22) and PCN (12.11). On the standard 2048-point setting, FSC also leads with CD-ℓ₁ = 7.02 on ShapeNet and CD-ℓ₂ = 6.66 on Completion3D, narrowly beating ASFM-Net (6.68). On the real-world KITTI dataset, FSC achieves MMD = 0.239, more than 38% lower than the next-best AdaPoinTr (0.392).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSC outperforms all 22 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. On the headline 64-point ShapeNet task, FSC (CD-ℓ₁ = 7.89) beats SVDFormer (11.22) by 29.7%, PCN (12.11) by 34.8%, and GRNet (17.61) by 55.2%, with the largest per-category gains on Airplane (3.47 vs 7.21) and Car (6.70 vs 11.00). On the standard 2048-point ShapeNet benchmark, FSC (7.02) edges out SnowFlakeNet (7.21) and NSFA (8.06), and on Completion3D it marginally beats ASFM-Net (6.66 vs 6.68). On KITTI, FSC achieves MMD = 0.239, a 38.8% improvement over AdaPoinTr (0.392) and a 54.6% improvement over PoinTr (0.526), demonstrating strong generalizability to real LiDAR data. On unseen ShapeNet categories (64 points), FSC (12.90) still leads SVDFormer (13.60) and PoinTr (14.92), confirming robustness to novel object shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PCN encoder-decoder architecture as its backbone, incorporating PointNet++ for local-global feature fusion and FoldingNet for detailed point cloud generation, but departs fundamentally from prior completion methods by targeting an extreme sparsity regime (64 points) where each input point carries disproportionate information. Where voxel-based methods like GRNet fail due to missing neighborhoods and standard point-based methods like PCN treat all points uniformly, FSC introduces a dual-branch encoder (MLP-based extensive branch + transformer-based salient branch with offset and cascaded attention) coupled with a two-stage WGAN revision network operating on both feature and point-cloud spaces. The Shannon entropy analysis using FPFH histograms provides a quantitative information-theoretic justification for the 64-point threshold, a framing absent from prior completion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: LiDAR and depth sensors in autonomous driving and robotics frequently produce extremely sparse returns, and this paper demonstrates that meaningful shape recovery is possible from as few as 64 points, with a clear empirical boundary below which all methods degrade sharply. Limitations include reliance on synthetic ShapeNet training with zero-shot transfer to KITTI, and the comparison baselines at 64 points were not originally designed for this regime, making the performance gap partly a function of task mismatch rather than pure method superiority. Open questions include whether the 64-point threshold generalizes across object complexity, whether the WGAN revision stage is necessary at higher point counts, and how the method handles real-world sensor noise and non-uniform sampling patterns.
