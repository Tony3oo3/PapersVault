## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate object hallucinations—plausible but incorrect objects not present in the input image—driven by statistical biases in pretraining data and over-reliance on language priors from the LLM decoder. Existing mitigations require additional training, hallucination-specific datasets, or external pretrained models, making them costly and impractical to deploy. This paper seeks a training-free, tool-free decoding-time intervention that directly targets both root causes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a free-form text query as input, and produces auto-regressively generated text (captions, VQA answers) as output. Internally, it also constructs a distorted copy of the image by iteratively adding Gaussian noise (following a diffusion forward process with step size γ=0.1), which serves as an auxiliary contrastive input during decoding. No additional parameters, external models, or modified architectures are required; the only overhead is one extra forward pass per token on the noisy image.

## Method

Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) computes a contrastive logit distribution at each decoding step: (1+α)·logit_θ(y_t | v, x, y_<t) − α·logit_θ(y_t | v′, x, y_<t), where v is the original image and v′ is the Gaussian-noise-distorted version. The key insight is that visual uncertainty amplifies both language priors and statistical co-occurrence biases, so the distorted-input distribution over-weights hallucination-prone tokens; subtracting it selectively suppresses those tokens while preserving visually grounded ones. An adaptive plausibility constraint (threshold β on the original-input distribution) prevents the contrastive penalty from eliminating valid high-confidence tokens, ensuring linguistic coherence is maintained. The method is entirely training-free, requires no external models, and is applied as a drop-in replacement for standard sampling at inference time, with α=1 and β=0.1 as default hyperparameters.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, VCD achieves up to +7.4 F1 and +5.8 accuracy over regular decoding across all three LVLM families (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) under random, popular, and adversarial sampling settings. On the MME hallucination subset, VCD yields a +18% total score improvement for LLaVA-1.5 (565.33 → 604.66) and a +17.7% gain for InstructBLIP (380.33 → 447.67), with the largest relative gains in object-level Existence and Count tasks. In GPT-4V-aided open-ended evaluation on LLaVA-Bench, VCD improves accuracy from 3.23 to 4.15 (LLaVA-1.5) and from 4.76 to 6.69 (Qwen-VL) on a 10-point scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is regular (direct) decoding, since VCD is a training-free decoding-time intervention that requires no retraining or external models. VCD consistently outperforms regular decoding on POPE across all three LVLM families and all three sampling settings (random, popular, adversarial), with F1 gains ranging from +1.5 to +7.4 points. On the MME hallucination subset, VCD wins on Existence, Count, and Color sub-tasks for all three models, with the largest absolute gain being +27.33 on InstructBLIP's Existence score (141.00 → 168.33). The one exception is the Position sub-task, where VCD shows minimal or slightly negative change (e.g., Qwen-VL drops from 131.67 to 128.00), reflecting the inherently weak spatial reasoning of current LVLMs. In the GPT-4V-aided open-ended evaluation, VCD improves accuracy for all three models (e.g., Qwen-VL: 4.76 → 6.69) while also boosting detailedness, confirming that the contrastive mechanism enhances both faithfulness and informativeness without degrading general language quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the contrastive decoding framework from text generation (Li et al., 2022) to the vision-language setting, repurposing the forward diffusion noise schedule (Ho et al., 2020) as a mechanism to create distorted visual inputs that amplify hallucination-prone behaviors. It departs from prior LVLM hallucination mitigation approaches—hallucination-targeted fine-tuning datasets, post-hoc revisors, and factually augmented RLHF—by requiring no additional training, no external pretrained models, and no new data, positioning itself as a purely inference-time correction. The adaptive plausibility constraint is directly borrowed from Li et al.'s contrastive decoding to prevent the contrastive mechanism from promoting implausible tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VCD's training-free, tool-free nature makes it immediately deployable as a drop-in decoding strategy for any LVLM, which is practically significant given the computational cost of prior mitigation approaches. The finding that a simple Gaussian noise mask suffices to expose and correct both statistical bias and language-prior over-reliance suggests that hallucination is more of a decoding-calibration problem than a fundamental architectural deficiency. Open questions include whether finer-grained distortions (object-level blurring, semantic perturbation) yield further gains, whether the approach extends to video understanding, and whether it scales to larger or differently-architected LVLMs beyond the 7B models tested.
