## Problem

Existing 3D texture synthesis methods rely on extrinsic representations—either rendering to 2D images (suffering from view inconsistencies and SDS limitations) or rasterizing to 3D grids (aliasing fine details)—which sacrifice textural fidelity. No prior diffusion model operates intrinsically on mesh surfaces with isometry-equivariance, preventing seamless replication of details across locally similar regions and blocking generative texture transfer between shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240250/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single textured triangle mesh (~30K vertices) as input, with optional coarse semantic labels or binary inpainting masks for user-controlled generation. Textures are encoded as 8-dimensional tangent vector fields at mesh vertices (Field Latents) rather than UV maps or 3D feature grids, and the FL-VAE is pre-trained on flat planar meshes overlaid with 512×512 images from OpenImagesV4 so it generalizes to arbitrary 3D geometries. Output is a new synthesized texture decoded from sampled latent vector fields, which can be applied to the original mesh or to a different approximately-isometric mesh for texture transfer.

## Method

The framework has two components: a Field Latent VAE (FL-VAE) and a Field Latent Diffusion Model (FLDM). The FL-VAE encoder uses eight VN-Transformer layers over tangent vector features sampled from one-ring neighborhoods to produce per-vertex latent distributions, while the decoder is a neural field that queries a logarithm-map coordinate function (Eq. 5) to interpolate latent features across faces—yielding richer reconstruction than barycentric interpolation. The FLDM is a DDPM operating on tangent vector fields, with a shallow two-level U-Net denoiser built from field convolutions extended to interleave scalar embeddings (timestep, labels) directly into the convolution filters, preserving isometry-equivariance without the instability of multiplicative conditioning. Both components commute with distance-preserving deformations, enabling the model to replicate textural details across locally similar regions regardless of 3D embedding and to transfer textures between different meshes by simply sampling the trained FLDM on a new geometry.

## Results

For texture reconstruction (Table 1), the FL-VAE achieves PSNR 22.38, DSSIM 0.51, and LPIPS 1.02 on 30K-vertex meshes from Google Scanned Objects, substantially outperforming INFs (PSNR 18.86, DSSIM 1.16, LPIPS 2.15) and the barycentric FL-VAE ablation (PSNR 21.33, DSSIM 0.66, LPIPS 1.31). For unconditional texture generation (Table 2), FLDMs trained on single meshes from Objaverse and Scanned Objects produce higher-fidelity samples than Sin3DM, as measured by lower SIFID, while Sin3DM's extrinsic representation inflates its LPIPS diversity score through geometry changes rather than genuine textural variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against INFs [27] (Koestler et al., ECCV 2022), the FL-VAE wins decisively on all three reconstruction metrics at both resolutions: PSNR improves from 18.86 to 22.38 (+3.52 dB), DSSIM drops from 1.16 to 0.51, and LPIPS drops from 2.15 to 1.02 on 30K-vertex meshes. The gap is attributed to INFs' reliance on Laplacian eigenfunctions, which may not align with regions of fine textural detail, and to the FL-VAE's logarithmic coordinate function providing a richer interpolant than barycentric blending. Against Sin3DM [58] (Wu et al., 2023) on unconditional single-mesh generation, FLDMs produce higher-fidelity textures (lower SIFID) because they operate intrinsically on the surface, whereas Sin3DM rasterizes geometry and texture to a 3D grid before encoding, aliasing fine details. Qualitatively, Sin3DM's extrinsic representation entangles texture with 3D position, causing synthesized details to appear as repetitions or extrusions along major mesh axes and preventing novel textural changes without geometry modification. FLDMs, being isometry-equivariant, replicate textural details across locally similar regions regardless of their 3D configuration, enabling generative texture transfer to new meshes that Sin3DM cannot perform.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-image diffusion paradigm (SinDiffusion, SinGAN) to the 3D surface setting by constructing a fully intrinsic latent diffusion model built on field convolutions and VN-Transformer layers. It departs from the dominant extrinsic approaches (SDS-based optimization, triplane rasterization, UV-atlas diffusion) by operating directly on tangent vector fields over the mesh, and from prior intrinsic representations (INFs, Manifold Diffusion Fields) by using locally-supported convolutions with logarithm-map coordinate functions rather than global attention or Laplacian eigenfunctions. The isometry-equivariant design, inherited from the field convolution framework, is the key architectural departure that enables the generative texture transfer capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The isometry-equivariant formulation provides a principled solution to the orientation ambiguity problem on surfaces and yields the practical capability of generative texture transfer—sampling a model trained on one mesh onto a geometrically similar mesh—without explicit correspondence or retraining. Limitations include the single-textured-mesh scope (no multi-asset or category-level generation), relatively long training times, and an acknowledged inability to capture directional textural information. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multi-mesh or category-conditioned settings, how to incorporate directional conditioning, and whether the pre-trained-on-flat-meshes FL-VAE generalizes to highly curved or non-manifold geometries.
