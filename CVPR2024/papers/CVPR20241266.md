## Problem

Existing neural human avatar methods are designed for either animation (pose/viewpoint synthesis) or relighting, but none simultaneously support full customization across pose, shape, lighting, texture, and shadow. This limits their practicality for applications like the metaverse and 3D games where avatars must be freely editable in all aspects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular or sparse multi-view videos (as few as a single camera) along with estimated SMPL parameters for pose alignment. It produces three disentangled neural fields—SDF for geometry, albedo for intrinsic color, and shadow for self-occlusion—each decoded by a separate MLP, plus a learnable 16×32×1 latitude-longitude light probe representing the environment. This decomposition allows each attribute to be independently modified at test time (e.g., swapping albedo between subjects, replacing the light probe for relighting, or zeroing the shadow field).

## Method

NECA represents humans in complementary dual spaces: a pose-aware feature in canonical space encoded by per-pose factorized tri-planes (compressed via CP decomposition from O(L²) to O(RL) memory), and a subject-level feature in surface space built from tangent-space local coordinates and UV latent codes on the SMPL mesh, which resolves the many-to-one mapping problem of pure UV volumes. Three distinct MLPs decode these features into SDF, shadow, and albedo respectively, while a separate light probe is optimized jointly. The entire network is trained self-supervisedly with photometric losses (L2 + LPIPS), a binary cross-entropy mask loss, an eikonal regularization on the SDF, and a novel normal-alignment loss that penalizes normals facing away from the nearest SMPL surface normal to counter SMPL's inherent symmetry.

## Results

On the ZJU-MoCap dataset (9 subjects), NECA achieves the best novel pose synthesis PSNR of 25.0 dB and LPIPS of 0.091, outperforming all baselines. For novel view synthesis, it reaches 28.5 dB PSNR and 0.067 LPIPS, also the best among compared methods. The model uses 18.37M parameters, substantially fewer than ARAH (87.09M) and PoseVocab (24.08M), while delivering superior rendering quality. Qualitative relighting comparisons against Relighting4D show NECA produces more physically realistic results under novel poses and viewpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NECA outperforms all five pose/view synthesis baselines on PSNR and LPIPS for both novel view and novel pose synthesis. The closest competitor is ARAH [56], which achieves 28.4 dB PSNR on novel view (vs. NECA's 28.5) and 24.5 dB on novel pose (vs. NECA's 25.0), but at 87.09M parameters—nearly 5× larger than NECA. PoseVocab [30] is competitive on LPIPS (0.068 vs. 0.067 for novel view) but trails on PSNR by 1.0 dB for novel pose. Neural Body [42] and Animatable NeRF [41] are clearly weaker, with PSNR gaps of 1.3–2.7 dB on novel pose. For relighting, NECA qualitatively surpasses Relighting4D [9], which fails to produce natural-looking results under dramatic motion, while NECA maintains physically consistent shadows and albedo under novel poses and viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

NECA unifies two previously separate research threads—animatable human avatar reconstruction (Neural Actor, ARAH, PoseVocab) and neural human relighting (Relighting4D, Sun et al.)—into a single framework that supports all five customization axes simultaneously. It inherits the dual-space (canonical + surface) representation from Neural Actor [31] and the learnable light-probe rendering equation from Relighting4D [9], but departs from both by introducing a factorized tri-plane for pose-aware features (with CP decomposition for memory efficiency) and a tangent-space local coordinate that resolves the many-to-one UV mapping problem identified in prior UV-volume methods. The disentangled MLP architecture (separate SDF, albedo, and shadow heads) is the key architectural departure that enables independent editing of each attribute, whereas prior methods entangle geometry and appearance in a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NECA addresses a practical gap highlighted in its own comparison table: no prior method supported all five editing dimensions (pose, lighting, shadow, shape, texture) in one model, which is a stated requirement for metaverse, telepresence, and game applications. The main limitations are per-person optimization (no cross-subject generalization), sensitivity to SMPL parameter estimation, potential shadow errors under extreme novel poses, and blurred retexturing with large pose variance. Open questions include whether the disentangled representation can be made amortized (single forward pass without per-scene optimization), whether explicit visibility modeling could improve shadow fidelity, and whether the framework extends to multi-person or clothed-with-accessories scenarios.
