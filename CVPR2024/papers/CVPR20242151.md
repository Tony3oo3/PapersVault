## Problem

Domain-generalized stereo matching networks pre-trained on synthetic data exhibit strong robustness to unseen domains, but fine-tuning them on real-world ground truth seriously degrades this generalization ability. The paper identifies two root causes: learning new knowledge in GT-PL inconsistent regions without sufficient regularization from consistent regions, and overfitting fine-grained GT details in consistent regions. No prior work has addressed how to fine-tune stereo networks while preserving their domain generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes rectified stereo image pairs (left and right RGB images) and produces a dense continuous disparity map. It operates at the level of continuous disparity values rather than discrete cost volumes, making it compatible with both 2D/3D cost aggregation architectures and iterative optimization networks. During fine-tuning, the Student is supervised by both improved GT disparity maps (with sparse annotations, e.g., KITTI) and improved pseudo labels from a frozen Teacher, with a 3-pixel threshold τ used to partition pixels into consistent and inconsistent regions.

## Method

DKT employs three identically initialized networks: a frozen Teacher that predicts pseudo labels, an EMA Teacher that tracks the Student's evolving predictions via momentum-based weight averaging, and a Student trained jointly on improved GT and PL. The Filter-and-Ensemble (F&E) module uses the EMA Teacher's predictions to dynamically modify supervision: F&E-GT probabilistically drops the inconsistent region of GT (proportional to its fraction in the valid region) and ensembles GT with EMA predictions in the consistent region, truncating to ±1 pixel to prevent overfitting; F&E-PL removes PL regions inconsistent with the EMA Teacher and ensembles the remaining PL with EMA predictions to progressively improve PL accuracy. The EMA Teacher is re-initialized from the Student after 5k fine-tuning steps, enabling it to provide increasingly accurate region boundaries. The training loss is a weighted sum of disparity losses against the improved GT and improved PL within their respective valid masks.

## Results

On the KITTI benchmark (Table 3), DKT-IGEV achieves 1.56% D1 error on KITTI 2012 (all) and 1.72% on KITTI 2015 (all), slightly behind the base IGEV-Stereo (1.44% and 1.59%) but dramatically outperforming it on unseen domains: 2.22% avg on DrivingStereo (vs. 2.70%), 7.53% on Middlebury (vs. 11.87%), 4.23% on ETH3D (vs. 24.28%), and 15.30% on Booster (vs. 18.33%). On the Booster fine-tuning benchmark (Table 5), DKT-RAFT achieves 10.32% D1 on Booster while maintaining 3.69% on KITTI 2012 and 2.57% on ETH3D, whereas GT fine-tuning of RAFT-Stereo degrades to 17.02% on KITTI 2012 and 89.05% on ETH3D. DKT-IGEV similarly achieves 14.11% on Booster with 3.76% on KITTI 2012 and 3.13% on ETH3D, compared to 51.52% and 79.98% for CFNet fine-tuned with GT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DKT-IGEV slightly underperforms the base IGEV-Stereo on target KITTI domains (1.56% vs. 1.44% on 2012-all, 1.72% vs. 1.59% on 2015-all) but dominates on all unseen domains: DrivingStereo avg 2.22% vs. 2.70%, Middlebury 7.53% vs. 11.87%, ETH3D 4.23% vs. 24.28%, and Booster 15.30% vs. 18.33%. DKT-RAFT similarly trades a small target-domain cost (1.85% vs. 1.66% on KITTI 2012-all) for large cross-domain gains over RAFT-Stereo (DrivingStereo 2.52% vs. 3.80%, ETH3D 2.28% vs. 40.43%, Booster 15.35% vs. 23.87%). On the Booster fine-tuning benchmark (Table 5), DKT-RAFT matches the best GT fine-tuned target-domain result (10.32% vs. 10.73% for RAFT-Stereo-ft) while achieving 3.69% on KITTI 2012 and 2.57% on ETH3D, compared to catastrophic degradation to 17.02% and 89.05% for RAFT-Stereo-ft. CFNet-ft and PCVNet-ft show even more severe generalization collapse (51.52%/79.98% and 35.54%/75.21% on KITTI 2012/ETH3D respectively), whereas DKT-IGEV maintains 3.76% and 3.13%. Among all published SOTA methods in Table 3, DKT is the only one that generalizes well to Middlebury, ETH3D, and Booster simultaneously after fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 'dark knowledge' concept from classification knowledge distillation [9,14,57] to the stereo matching domain, using the pixel-wise difference between ground truth and pseudo labels as a structured signal for fine-tuning. It builds on the line of domain generalized stereo matching work [7,21,55] that achieves robustness during synthetic pre-training, but deliberately addresses the underexplored downstream step of real-world fine-tuning, where prior methods' robustness is silently lost. The DKT framework is architecture-agnostic and plugs into iterative stereo matching backbones (IGEV-Stereo, RAFT-Stereo) without modifying their structure, positioning it as a training-time intervention rather than a new network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying stereo matching in autonomous driving or robotics, the finding that standard GT fine-tuning can catastrophically destroy domain generalization (e.g., 52% D1 on KITTI after Booster fine-tuning vs. 5% zero-shot) is a critical practical warning. The DKT framework offers a drop-in solution that preserves cross-domain robustness while maintaining competitive target-domain accuracy, but it triples inference-time memory during training (three networks) and the probability-based filtering in F&E-GT introduces a heuristic that may not transfer cleanly to other dense prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the consistent/inconsistent region decomposition generalizes to optical flow or monocular depth, and whether the EMA Teacher's role can be replaced by lighter-weight uncertainty estimates.
