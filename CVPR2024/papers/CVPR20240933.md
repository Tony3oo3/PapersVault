## Problem

In federated learning with non-IID data, the classifier (final layer) becomes biased toward local label distributions, causing client drift and degraded global model performance. Prior methods either regularize local models toward the global model (which may itself be biased) or freeze the classifier entirely, but none directly encourage local models to emulate the IID training dynamic. Additionally, most prior work has not been evaluated under feature-shift heterogeneity and suffers from poor scalability due to extra forward passes or weight-distance computations at every batch.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (96×96×3 for feature-shift datasets like PACS, HAM10000, and Office-Home; standard resolutions for STL-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet) and produces a class label via a CNN backbone (small CNN, ResNet-18, or ResNet-50 depending on dataset scale) followed by a non-linear projector. The two regularization terms operate on intermediate activations: the penultimate-layer representation matrix (row vectors = per-sample features) and the final-layer softmax probability matrix (column vectors = per-class probabilities across the mini-batch), requiring no access to weight parameters or additional forward passes.

## Method

FedUV augments the standard cross-entropy loss with two lightweight regularization terms computed from forward-pass activations only. (1) Classifier Variance (L_V): a hinge loss that penalizes any class whose column-wise standard deviation in the softmax probability matrix falls below a fixed threshold c (the mean class-wise std of an identity matrix, representing an ideal balanced mini-batch), thereby forcing the classifier to produce balanced probability distributions regardless of local label skew. (2) Hyperspherical Uniformity (L_U): an RBF-kernel pairwise Gaussian energy over the penultimate-layer representations that encourages features to spread uniformly across the hypersphere, preventing the encoder from collapsing into a local-feature subspace and giving the classifier more directions in which to increase variance. Crucially, FedUV does not use the global model as a regularization source, requiring no extra forward passes, no weight-parameter access, and no inter-model distance calculations, with fixed hyperparameters (λ = D/4, μ = 0.5) across all datasets.

## Results

FedUV achieves the highest test accuracy across most of the six evaluation datasets, with the largest margins in extreme non-IID (α=0.01) and feature-shift settings. On CIFAR-100 (α=0.01), FedUV reaches 55.7% accuracy, outperforming FedAvg (51.6%), MOON (48.2%), and FedProx (50.2%) by 4–7 points. On the feature-shift benchmark Office-Home, FedUV scores 45.4% versus 42.2% for FedAvg and 41.9% for both FedProx and MOON. In the less extreme label-shift setting (α=1.0), Freeze or MOON can match or slightly exceed FedUV on smaller datasets (e.g., Freeze 72.1% vs. FedUV 68.5% on STL-10), but FedUV still leads on Tiny ImageNet (43.2%) and all feature-shift datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedUV outperforms FedAvg on 7 of 9 evaluation settings, with the largest gains in extreme non-IID (α=0.01) and feature-shift scenarios (e.g., +4.1 pts on CIFAR-100 α=0.01, +3.2 pts on Office-Home). Against FedProx and MOON, FedUV wins on all α=0.01 label-shift settings and all three feature-shift datasets (PACS, HAM10000, Office-Home), while FedProx and MOON actually underperform FedAvg in the most extreme non-IID cases because their global-model-based regularization amplifies bias. The Freeze baseline (randomly initialized, frozen classifier) is competitive in mild label-shift (α=1.0) on small datasets—achieving 72.1% on STL-10 vs. FedUV's 68.5%—but degrades sharply in extreme non-IID (23.7% on STL-10 α=0.01) and feature-shift settings, where FedUV leads by 3–4 points. MOON is the strongest competitor in the α=1.0 regime (60.5% on CIFAR-100, 70.0% on STL-10), but FedUV matches or exceeds it on Tiny ImageNet and all feature-shift benchmarks. Overall, FedUV is the only method that consistently leads across both label-shift and feature-shift heterogeneity without relying on the global model for regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedUV builds on the FedAvg framework and the line of local-training regularization methods (FedProx, MOON, FedDyn) that address client drift in non-IID federated learning. However, it departs conceptually from the dominant paradigm of using the global model as a regularization anchor: rather than pulling local models toward a potentially-biased aggregate, FedUV directly promotes IID emulation through two novel terms — a hinge-loss classifier variance regularizer and an RBF-kernel hyperspherical uniformity regularizer on encoder representations. The SVD-based diagnostic of classifier weight degeneration in non-IID settings provides a new mechanistic explanation that motivates the design, and the approach is positioned against both global-model-dependent methods and the frozen-classifier approach of FedBABU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedUV offers a simple, efficient, and scalable regularization that requires no extra forward passes, no weight-space comparisons, and no access to the global model, making it attractive for resource-constrained edge devices. The ablation clearly shows that the two regularizers address complementary failure modes (label-shift vs. feature-shift), providing practitioners with a unified method for both heterogeneity types. Limitations include performance degradation at high local epochs (E=40) where local models overfit to local optima, and the lack of a convergence proof for the non-smooth hinge-loss variance regularizer. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities, whether smooth surrogate losses can provide theoretical guarantees, and whether the fixed hyperparameter choices (λ=D/4, μ=0.5) generalize beyond the tested architectures.
