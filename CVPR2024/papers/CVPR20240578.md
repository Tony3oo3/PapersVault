## Problem

Learning discriminative instance embeddings for multiple object tracking has traditionally required large amounts of in-domain video annotations, which are expensive to collect and limit cross-domain generalization. Existing self-supervised approaches that learn from raw images focus on frame-level similarity and fail to exploit instance-level information in complex multi-object scenes. The paper asks whether universal, zero-shot instance association can be learned purely from unlabeled static images by leveraging a foundation segmentation model's exhaustive object proposals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240578/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes unlabeled RGB images (500K subsampled from SA-1B) and applies two different strong augmentations to each image to create paired views; SAM is run on the original image to generate dense instance masks that define positive/negative pairs. At inference, the MASA adapter receives frozen backbone features (from SAM, Detic, or Grounding-DINO) plus bounding box prompts—either from a detector or from its own learned detection head—and outputs per-instance tracking embeddings via RoI-Align and a lightweight track head. When paired with SAM, the detection head also produces box prompts that feed the SAM mask decoder, yielding segmentation masks alongside tracking assignments, and replaces SAM's ~1000-point uniform sampling with sparse box prompts for ~10× faster auto-mask generation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a self-supervised contrastive training pipeline where SAM's exhaustive segmentation of unlabeled images yields dense instance proposals; two different augmentations (φ and φ) applied to the same image establish pixel-level correspondence that, when transferred through SAM's masks, becomes instance-level correspondence for InfoNCE-style contrastive learning. The MASA adapter transforms frozen backbone features into tracking-discriminative representations using a multi-scale feature pyramid (FPN for hierarchical backbones, transpose convolution + max-pooling for ViT), deformable convolution for cross-location and cross-scale aggregation, and task/scale-aware attention (Dyhead) for SAM-based models. A joint object-prior distillation branch (standard RCNN head) learns to predict bounding boxes tightly enclosing SAM's masks, simultaneously strengthening the tracking features and providing efficient box prompts that accelerate SAM's everything mode. The full training objective combines the contrastive association loss with the detection distillation loss, and the resulting adapter is plug-and-play: it can be attached to any frozen detection or segmentation foundation model to enable zero-shot tracking of any detected object across diverse domains.

## Results

On the TAO TETA benchmark, MASA's unified Detic model achieves TETA 46.3 and AssocA 44.1, surpassing the best fully-supervised in-domain method TETer (TETA 34.6, AssocA 36.7) by over 11 points in TETA. With matched detections, the zero-shot Grounding-DINO variant reaches TETA 34.9 and AssocA 37.6, still exceeding TETer. On BDD100K MOTS, MASA-SAM-H achieves the best mIDF1 of 49.7 and AssocA of 54.5, outperforming the fully-supervised UNINEXT-H (mIDF1 48.5, AssocA 53.2). On the Open-vocabulary MOT benchmark, the unified Detic model leads on all metrics for both base (TETA 47.0) and novel (TETA 40.8) splits, beating OVTrack by 10.7 and 8.8 TETA points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASA's zero-shot association consistently outperforms all fully-supervised in-domain trackers: on TAO TETA with matched detections, Grounding-DINO (AssocA 37.6) beats TETer (36.7) and QDTrack (27.4); on BDD MOTS, SAM-H (mIDF1 49.7, AssocA 54.5) surpasses UNINEXT-H (48.5, 53.2) and PCAN (45.1, 46.7); on BDD MOT, Detic (IDF1 71.7, AssocA 52.9) edges out ByteTrack (70.4, 51.5) by ~1.4 points. The unified Detic model achieves a new SOTA on TAO TETA (46.3 vs. 34.6 for TETer) and Open-vocabulary MOT (TETA 47.0 base / 40.8 novel vs. OVTrack's 36.3 / 32.0), demonstrating that coupling MASA with a strong detector transfers detection quality into tracking. Against VOS-based methods, MASA-SAM-H (AssocA 54.5 on BDD MOTS, 36.4 on TAO) substantially outperforms Deva (46.8, 22.4) and SAM-PT (UVO AR100 31.8 vs. 37.5), particularly in scenes with frequent object entry/exit. Compared to self-supervised appearance methods trained on the same raw images, MASA-SAM-B (BDD MOT AssocA 51.9, TAO AssocA 35.8) dramatically outperforms MoCo v2 (42.7, 30.7), VFS (29.2, 19.1), and DINO (23.1, 12.9), confirming that instance-level contrastive learning with SAM proposals is far more effective than frame-level self-supervision for multi-instance tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

MASA builds directly on SAM's dense instance segmentation to generate self-supervised training signals, departing from prior association-learning methods (QDTrack, TETer, UNINEXT) that require in-domain video annotations. It also extends the self-supervised contrastive learning line (MoCo, DINO, UniTrack) by converting frame-level similarity into instance-level correspondence via SAM's masks, addressing the key limitation that frame-level methods fail in multi-instance scenes. The MASA adapter architecture draws on deformable convolution and Dyhead attention for dynamic feature fusion, and the object-prior distillation branch repurposes a standard RCNN head to accelerate SAM's everything mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for video tracking annotations entirely, MASA removes the primary bottleneck for cross-domain tracking generalization and provides a plug-and-play adapter that can be attached to any frozen detection or segmentation foundation model. The 10× speedup for SAM's auto-mask generation via learned box prompts has practical implications for real-time tracking pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach degrades on extremely long-term tracking with heavy occlusion, whether the SAM dependency limits applicability to domains where SAM's segmentation quality is poor, and whether the adapter can be further compressed for edge deployment.
