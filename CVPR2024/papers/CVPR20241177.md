## Problem

Neural fields are implicit continuous functions for which classical Fourier-based frequency decomposition is not directly applicable, yet effective filtering is critical for level-of-detail processing and for operations like marching cubes that sample fields on regular grids. Existing approaches to frequency decomposition of neural fields either rely on heuristics, require specialized architectures (e.g., multiplicative filter networks), or need pre-filtered supervision signals that are unavailable in inverse problems like 3D reconstruction from 2D images.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either 2D images (256²), 3D SDF point clouds (500k sampled points), or multi-view 2D images as supervision, and produces a cascade of K neural fields where each level k encodes only the frequency band [ω_k, ω_{k+1}]. In 3D, fields are trained at resolutions ranging from 32³ to 512³, and meshes are extracted at each level via marching cubes. The full signal is recovered by superposition of all levels, while any subset yields a valid anti-aliased approximation at that LOD.

## Method

The core insight is that sampling a neural field on a uniform lattice and interpolating the samples with a band-limited kernel (e.g., a triangle/linear kernel) is mathematically equivalent to low-pass filtering the field's output. By wrapping the field's output in this interpolation operation during the training loop, the optimization naturally constrains the learned signal to a bounded frequency band, requiring no architectural modification. Cascaded training then proceeds analogously to a Laplacian pyramid: level 0 is trained to approximate the full signal through the low-pass constraint, and each subsequent level k is trained on the residual (ground truth minus the sum of all previous levels), capturing the next frequency band. The approach is architecture-agnostic, working atop iNGP hash grids, dense grids, or pure MLPs, and the choice of interpolation kernel (linear vs. sinc) directly controls the sharpness of the frequency cutoff.

## Results

On the Synthetic NeRF Dataset (inverse rendering), BANF reduces average Chamfer Distance (×10⁻²) from 17.3 to 11.4 at 1/8× scale, from 10.5 to 8.19 at 1/4×, and from 8.01 to 7.74 at 1/2×, while matching NeUS exactly at full resolution (7.45). On the real MobileBrick dataset, BANF cuts average Chamfer Distance (×10⁻³) from 15.5 to 7.39 at 1/4× scale. For direct SDF fitting, BANF achieves Chamfer-L2 (×10²) of 1.23 vs. iNGP's 1.97 on Asian Dragon at 32³ resolution, with the gap narrowing to 0.32 vs. 0.32 at 128³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeUS [42] on inverse rendering, BANF achieves substantially lower Chamfer Distance at all coarser scales: 11.4 vs. 17.3 (34% reduction) at 1/8×, 8.19 vs. 10.5 (22% reduction) at 1/4×, and 7.74 vs. 8.01 at 1/2× on the Synthetic NeRF Dataset, while tying exactly at full resolution (7.45). On the real MobileBrick dataset, the advantage is even more pronounced at 1/4× (7.39 vs. 15.5, a 52% reduction), narrowing to 5.38 vs. 5.81 at 1/2×. Against iNGP [30] on direct SDF fitting, BANF wins at coarse resolutions (1.23 vs. 1.97 on Asian Dragon at 32³; 1.41 vs. 1.83 on Thai Statue) but converges to similar quality at 128³ where the Nyquist rate is exceeded. Against BACON [23] on 2D image decomposition, the comparison is primarily qualitative: both methods approximate low-pass filtering, but BANF avoids the Gibbs ringing artifacts visible in BACON's lower-frequency reconstructions, while requiring no specialized multiplicative filter network architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

BANF builds on the NeUS surface reconstruction pipeline and iNGP hash-grid backbone, but its core contribution departs from prior frequency-decomposition approaches for neural fields. Where BACON and Polynomial Neural Fields require specialized multiplicative filter network architectures, and Mip-NeRF-style integrated positional encoding demands explicit downsampled supervision, BANF achieves band-limited decomposition through a minimal training-time modification: sampling the field on a regular lattice and interpolating with a band-limited kernel, which is shown to be equivalent to low-pass filtering during optimization. This positions the work as an architecture-agnostic alternative to both MFN-based and positional-encoding-based frequency control, extending the classical signal-processing insight that interpolation kernels act as filters to the neural field training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, drop-in mechanism for anti-aliased level-of-detail extraction from any neural field representation, directly addressing aliasing artifacts that plague marching-cubes-based surface reconstruction at coarse scales. Its architecture-agnosticism (validated across hash grids, dense grids, and pure MLPs) and lack of requirement for pre-filtered supervision make it broadly applicable to inverse rendering and SDF fitting pipelines. Key limitations include memory intensity at high grid resolutions, restriction to uniformly sampled (bounded) signals rather than the contracted representations common in NeRF, and unresolved challenges in decomposing view-dependent color signals. Extending the approach to unbounded scenes and developing CUDA-efficient querying strategies are natural next steps.
