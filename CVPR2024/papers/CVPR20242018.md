## Problem

Existing NeRF-based active mapping methods estimate uncertainty via density or predicted RGB variance but fail to account for visibility: regions never observed in training views yield inherently unreliable NeRF color predictions, yet prior approaches assign them no elevated uncertainty. Additionally, these methods lack a principled mechanism for compositing position-based uncertainty into ray-based observation uncertainty, relying on ad hoc weighted averages or sums without theoretical grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242018/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB training images (512×512, rendered in Blender) with known camera poses, plus a set of uniformly sampled candidate viewpoints within a coarse bounding box. The NVF model outputs per-point volume density, color mean, color covariance, and a visibility probability; these are composed along each ray into a GMM whose entropy yields a per-pixel uncertainty map. The final output is the next-best-view pose selected by maximizing total image entropy (with a spatial correlation correction), which drives iterative scene reconstruction.

## Method

NVF models NeRF's volume rendering as a hybrid Bayesian network where the observed ray color is a Gaussian Mixture Model, with each component corresponding to a sampled point's emitted color weighted by its occlusion probability. A binary visibility variable V_i switches the color distribution between the NeRF-predicted Gaussian (for observed points) and a broad prior Gaussian (for unobserved points), while a conditional probability table adjusts the occlusion likelihood for invisible points using a hyperparameter β. The NVF is an Instant-NGP backbone augmented with heads for color covariance (trained via negative log-likelihood of the GMM) and visibility (trained via cross-entropy against ground-truth visibility computed from all training rays). For active mapping, the per-pixel GMM entropy is upper-bounded analytically, corrected for spatial correlation via expected depth, and summed to score candidate views; the highest-entropy view is selected as the next observation.

## Results

NVF achieves the best performance across all three scenes on every metric. On the NeRF Assets average, NVF reaches 23.90 PSNR and 0.685 Completion Ratio, outperforming the best baseline (ActiveRMAP at 20.03 PSNR, 0.510 CR) by ~4 dB and 0.175 CR respectively. The gap is most dramatic on the challenging Room scene, where NVF scores 22.83 PSNR versus AIR's 15.19 PSNR (a 7.6 dB margin), and on Hubble where NVF attains 27.99 PSNR and 0.681 visual coverage versus NeurAR's 25.19 PSNR and 0.537 Vis. NVF also achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.106, 0.100, 0.156) and highest SSIM (0.890, 0.919, 0.943) across all scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NVF outperforms all five baselines (WD, ActiveRMAP, AIR, ActiveNeRF, NeurAR) on every metric across all three scenes. The largest margins appear on the Room scene: NVF achieves 22.83 PSNR vs. AIR's 15.19 (best baseline, +7.6 dB) and 0.586 visual coverage vs. AIR's 0.498, while ActiveNeRF and NeurAR actually perform worse than random selection on PSNR (10.69 and 12.23 vs. 12.95), indicating their uncertainty formulations actively mislead exploration in complex multi-room settings. On Hubble, NeurAR achieves the highest PSNR among baselines (25.19) but still trails NVF by 2.8 dB, and its visual coverage (0.537) is notably lower than NVF's (0.681), suggesting NeurAR revisits already-explored regions. On the NeRF Assets average, the gap is more moderate (~4 dB PSNR) but NVF still leads by a wide margin on LPIPS (0.106 vs. 0.219–0.286) and Completion Ratio (0.685 vs. 0.433–0.510). The ablation in Table 2 confirms that removing the visibility term drops PSNR from 24.42 to 21.11 and Vis from 0.546 to 0.382, validating that visibility is the critical differentiator over prior methods that omit it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NeRF's volume rendering formulation by recasting it as a hybrid Bayesian network, providing a principled probabilistic bridge between position-based uncertainty (color variance, density) and ray-based observation uncertainty. It positions all prior NeRF uncertainty methods (ActiveNeRF, NeurAR, ActiveRMAP, AIR, WD) as special cases that omit the visibility factor, arguing that regions never seen in training views should inherently carry high uncertainty regardless of what the network predicts. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating visibility as a first-class, learned field (the Neural Visibility Field) rather than an afterthought, and using GMM entropy as the uncertainty metric rather than ad hoc weighted averages of position-based quantities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying Bayesian network framework gives the field a common theoretical language for NeRF uncertainty, making it clearer what each prior method approximates and what it misses. The ablation revealing that visibility dominates over learned color variance (PSNR drops from 24.42 to 21.11 without visibility, but only to 23.77 without variance) challenges the implicit assumption that NeRF's predicted RGB uncertainty is the primary signal for exploration. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real-world (non-synthetic) scenes, how it scales to large environments where the visibility field must be maintained across many views, and how to integrate trajectory and collision constraints into the entropy-maximizing planner, which the authors explicitly leave as future work.
