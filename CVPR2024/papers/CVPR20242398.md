## Problem

Existing multi-view multi-label learning methods either sidestep missing views and labels entirely or address only one type of incompleteness, and they largely neglect the complementary information between multi-view features and category semantics. This paper tackles the double incomplete multi-view multi-label classification (DIMVMLC) problem by simultaneously handling missing views and incomplete labels while exploiting view-category interaction to improve discriminative power.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242398/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six pre-extracted image feature views (GIST, HSV, HUE, LAB, RGB, SIFT) per sample, a binary missing-view indicator matrix, and a partially observed binary label matrix where absent labels are masked. Missing views are imputed in-embedding-space before transformer processing, and the final output is a per-sample probability vector over all L categories, with a masked cross-entropy and asymmetric loss applied only to observed label entries.

## Method

VIST employs a two-layer, four-block transformer architecture that alternates view and category processing: the first layer uses Transformer_sv to enrich view embeddings, fuses them into a shared view vector via attention-weighted aggregation, then feeds this into Transformer_sc alongside randomly initialized category vectors; the second layer projects the shared view feature into category space and vice versa, enabling advanced cross-modal interaction. A KNN-style missing view generation module identifies the k nearest category vectors in embedding space for each sample, fits a multivariate Gaussian over those neighbors, and samples to fill absent view embeddings. A view-category consistency guided embedding enhancement module applies contrastive learning to align same-sample views, pushes apart cross-sample views with disjoint labels, and enforces correlation-matrix alignment between view-feature and label spaces via a cross-entropy auxiliary loss. The overall objective combines masked cross-entropy, a masked asymmetric loss for class imbalance, and the contrastive enhancement loss.

## Results

On the five standard multi-view multi-label datasets (Corel5k, Pascal07, Espgame, IAPRTC12, Mirflickr) with 50% missing views and 50% missing labels, VIST achieves the best AP, 1-RL, and AUC across all datasets. Headline numbers include AP of 41.5 on Corel5k (vs. 38.4 for LMVCAT), 53.9 on Pascal07 (vs. 53.6 for DDINet), and 60.4 on Mirflickr (vs. 59.8 for DDINet). The AUC gains are particularly pronounced on Pascal07 (88.3 vs. 87.6 for DICNet) and IAPRTC12 (88.6 vs. 87.4 for DICNet). Under full-view/full-label settings (Table 2), VIST also leads, e.g., AP 55.4 on Corel5k and 70.4 on Mirflickr.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIST outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all five datasets. The closest competitor is LMVCAT (a transformer-based DIMVMLC method), which VIST surpasses by 3.1 AP points on Corel5k (41.5 vs. 38.4) and 1.6 AP points on IAPRTC12 (33.9 vs. 31.7). Among deep-learning baselines, DICNet is the strongest on 1-RL for Corel5k (88.2) but VIST still leads by 2.0 points; DDINet is competitive on Pascal07 AP (53.6 vs. 53.9) yet trails VIST by 4.9 points on 1-RL (80.7 vs. 85.6). Traditional methods (lrMMC, MVL-IV, iMSF, GLOCAL, iMvWL, NAIML) are consistently 5–15 AP points behind VIST, with iMSF performing worst overall (e.g., 10.1 AP on IAPRTC12). The gap between VIST and the best baseline is largest on Pascal07 1-RL (85.6 vs. 81.1 for LMVCAT, a 4.5-point margin), suggesting the view-category interaction mechanism is most beneficial when label space is smaller and view complementarity is stronger.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIST positions itself within the line of deep learning methods for double incomplete multi-view multi-label classification (DIMVMLC), following DDINet, DICNet, and LMVCAT. It departs from prior work by introducing an explicit view-category interaction mechanism via a two-layer transformer that treats categories as first-class tokens alongside views, rather than using labels only as supervision signals. The KNN-style missing view generation with multivariate Gaussian sampling repurposes a classical imputation idea in a deep embedding space, and the contrastive embedding enhancement module draws on instance-level contrastive learning (as in DICNet) but extends it with category-correlation alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The view-category interaction framing offers a principled way to exploit complementary information between features and labels, which prior DIMVMLC methods largely overlooked. However, the approach's reliance on existing views for imputation limits its robustness at high missing-view ratios, and evaluation is confined to five image datasets with six hand-crafted feature views, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to learned representations or other modalities. The modest but consistent gains over LMVCAT suggest the interaction mechanism is useful, but the practical impact is tempered by the narrow experimental scope.
