## Problem

Existing composed video retrieval (CoVR) frameworks, such as CoVR-BLIP, rely solely on visual embeddings to represent query videos, which fails to preserve query-specific contextual information because visual inputs are high-dimensional with many irrelevant details and lack non-visual contextual cues. The resulting multi-modal representation becomes biased toward the change text, causing the model to lose the broader context of the query (e.g., surrounding objects, scene layout) that is essential for correctly interpreting what the change text should modify.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a reference video (middle frame sampled and encoded by a frozen ViT-L), a detailed language description of that video (generated by MiniGPT-4 with hallucination filtering, encoded by a frozen BERT-based text encoder), and a short change text (tokenized). The output is a joint multi-modal embedding formed by pairwise summation of three cross-attention-grounded sub-embeddings, which is matched against a target video database via cosine similarity. The same framework applies to composed image retrieval by substituting the video with a single image, and at inference time any subset of the three inputs can be used.

## Method

The core mechanism uses a BLIP-2-based multi-modal encoder f (with cross-attention layers) that processes pairs of inputs at a time: f(q,t) grounds the change text to the visual query, f(q,d) grounds the detailed description to the visual query, and f(e(d),t) grounds the change text to the description embedding. These three sub-embeddings are summed pairwise to form the joint multi-modal embedding f̃(q,d,t). Training employs hard-negative contrastive loss (HN-NCE) against three distinct target databases—visual embeddings, multi-modal embeddings, and text-only embeddings of target videos—with learnable scaling weights (λ, μ, δ) optimized on validation data. Only the multi-modal encoder f is trained; the vision encoder (ViT-L) and text encoder (BERT-base) remain frozen, and no additional parameters are introduced beyond the baseline CoVR-BLIP architecture.

## Results

On the WebVid-CoVR test set (primary task), the proposed method achieves 60.12% Recall@1, 84.32% Recall@5, 91.27% Recall@10, and 98.72% Recall@50 when trained on WebVid-CoVR with cross-attention fusion and 15 frames, outperforming the CoVR-BLIP baseline (53.13% R@1) by 6.99 points. Without any training on WebVid-CoVR, the method still achieves 47.52% R@1 vs. 45.46% for the baseline. On the CIRR zero-shot CoIR benchmark (pretrained on WebVid-CoVR), the method reaches 40.12% R@1, surpassing CoVR-BLIP (38.48%) by 1.64 points. On FashionIQ zero-shot, the method gains 2.6% average R@10 over CoVR-BLIP across Dress, Shirt, and Toptee categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CoVR-BLIP [43], the most recent CoVR framework built on BLIP-2. The proposed method consistently outperforms CoVR-BLIP across all three datasets and both zero-shot and transfer-learning settings: +6.99% R@1 on WebVid-CoVR (60.12 vs. 53.13), +1.64% R@1 on CIRR zero-shot (40.12 vs. 38.48), and +2.6% average R@10 on FashionIQ zero-shot. On CIRR transfer learning (trained on CIRR), the method achieves 51.03% R@1 vs. 49.69% for CoVR-BLIP with WebVid-CoVR pretraining, and 49.18% vs. 48.84% without pretraining. Against other CoIR baselines on CIRR (CASE, CompoDiff, Pic2Word, Combiner, LF-BLIP, etc.), the method's zero-shot R@1 of 40.12 surpasses all zero-shot competitors including CASE (35.40) and Pic2Word (23.90), while in the transfer-learning setting it is competitive with CASE (49.35) and Combiner (33.59). On FashionIQ, the method outperforms all zero-shot baselines (Pic2Word: 20.00/26.20/27.90 R@10) and transfer-learning methods (FashionVLP: 32.42/31.89/38.51, CASE: 47.44/48.48/50.18) in the zero-shot and transfer settings respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CoVR-BLIP (Ventura et al., AAAI 2024), which introduced the WebVid-CoVR benchmark and the first composed video retrieval framework using BLIP-2's image-grounded text encoder. The key departure is twofold: (1) it augments the visual query with detailed language descriptions generated by a multi-modal conversational model (MiniGPT-4) to preserve query-specific context that visual embeddings alone fail to capture, and (2) it replaces the single visual target embedding with three discriminative embeddings (vision, multi-modal, and text-only) to improve alignment. The architecture reuses BLIP-2's frozen ViT-L vision encoder and BERT-based text encoder, training only the cross-attention multi-modal encoder with a hard-negative contrastive loss, so no new parameters are introduced beyond the baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoVR is a nascent task with only one prior benchmark and method, so establishing a stronger baseline with clear ablations is valuable for the field. The approach's reliance on an external LLM for description generation introduces a practical dependency and potential hallucination risk (mitigated here by a cosine-similarity threshold), and the single-middle-frame sampling limits how well it captures temporal dynamics in longer videos. Open questions include whether the pairwise summation fusion is optimal versus learned fusion, whether the method scales to multi-frame or longer video inputs, and whether the zero-shot CoIR transfer gains hold as CoVR benchmarks mature and grow in diversity.
