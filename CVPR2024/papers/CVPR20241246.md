## Problem

In low-precision neural networks (≤4-bit), non-quantized elementwise operations in batch normalization, parameterized activations, and quantization scaling dominate inference cost (up to 90%), yet standard efficiency metrics like ACE ignore them entirely. This blind spot drives researchers to optimize only MAC precision while leaving expensive high-precision elementwise operations unaddressed, resulting in models that appear efficient by conventional metrics but consume far more energy in practice.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs a 1000-class ImageNet label. All arithmetic is performed in mixed low-precision: pointwise convolutions at 4-bit (PoT quantizer), depthwise convolutions at 8-bit (linear quantizer), with batch normalization and quantization scales also quantized rather than kept in FP32. The architecture deliberately avoids parameterized activations and skip connections to minimize elementwise overhead and memory traffic.

## Method

PikeLPN is a MobileNetV1-based separable convolution architecture that quantizes both MAC and elementwise operations. Three key techniques enable this: (1) QuantNorm rewrites batch normalization as x·s − b where s = Q(γ/√(σ²+ε)) and b = Q(β) − Q(μ)·s, performing the division at high precision during training but using only quantized multiply-add at inference; (2) Double Quantization quantizes the quantization scale factors themselves (e.g., a PoT scale for the linear depthwise quantizer), reducing per-channel scaling from FP32 multiply to a cheap shift; (3) Distribution-Heterogeneous Quantization assigns a PoT quantizer to pointwise convolutions (95% of MACs, weights near ±0.1) and a linear quantizer to depthwise convolutions (weights near ±2), resolving the distribution mismatch that causes accuracy collapse when a single quantizer is used for both. The paper also introduces ACEv2, an extension of the ACE metric that accounts for elementwise multiplications, additions, and shifts, achieving 0.991 correlation with measured 45nm CMOS energy.

## Results

On ImageNet, PikeLPN-1× achieves 67.55% Top-1 accuracy with an ACEv2 cost of 8.50×10⁹ and 34.98 mJ arithmetic energy, while PikeLPN-3× reaches 71.95% Top-1 at 33.70×10⁹ ACEv2 and 139.59 mJ. The largest variant, PikeLPN-6×, attains 73.59% Top-1 accuracy at 58.74×10⁹ ACEv2. Across all variants, elementwise operations contribute less than 5% of total ACEv2 cost (vs. 31–96% for competing models), and the family achieves up to 3× efficiency improvement over SOTA low-precision models at comparable or higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PikeLPN-1× outperforms MobiNet [34] by 13.2% Top-1 accuracy (67.55% vs. 54.4%) while being 1.5× more efficient in both ACEv2 (8.50 vs. 12.64) and energy (34.98 vs. 50.66 mJ). Against PokeBNN-0.5× [46], PikeLPN-1× achieves 2.35% higher accuracy at roughly 4× lower ACEv2 cost (8.50 vs. 33.58). PikeLPN-3× surpasses PokeBNN-0.75× by 1.5% Top-1 accuracy (71.95% vs. 70.5%) while being 35% more efficient (33.70 vs. 50.61 ACEv2). At the high-accuracy end, PikeLPN-6× matches PokeBNN-1× accuracy (73.59% vs. 73.4%) with 14% lower ACEv2 (58.74 vs. 68.56) and 18% lower energy (243.85 vs. 298.44 mJ). Compared to ReActNet [28] (69.4%, 83.24 ACEv2), PikeLPN-2× achieves comparable accuracy at 5.4× lower ACEv2. The key differentiator is that PikeLPN's elementwise operations contribute <5% of total cost, whereas competing models like PokeBNN and ReActNet have elementwise costs of 73–96%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ACE efficiency metric from PokeBNN [46] into ACEv2, which accounts for elementwise operations (multiplications, additions, shifts) that prior metrics like ACE, CPU64, and FA-count entirely excluded. It departs from the dominant design philosophy in SOTA low-precision models (PokeBNN, ReActNet, MobiNet) that aggressively reduces MAC precision while retaining batch normalization, parameterized activations, and quantization scaling in FP32. Architecturally, PikeLPN builds on MobileNetV1's separable convolution blocks but deliberately removes skip connections and parameterized activations that prior binary networks added to compensate for quantization error, instead addressing the root cause through targeted quantization techniques (QuantNorm, Double Quantization, Distribution-Heterogeneous Quantization).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that non-quantized elementwise operations can account for up to 90% of inference cost in binary models fundamentally reframes how practitioners should evaluate and design low-precision networks, and ACEv2 provides a more hardware-aligned metric to guide future work. The practical techniques (QuantNorm, Double Quantization, Distribution-Heterogeneous Quantization) are broadly applicable to any quantized CNN pipeline. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ImageNet classification with CNNs only, energy estimates based on 45nm CMOS technology that may not reflect modern hardware, and the absence of analysis on whether these techniques transfer to transformer architectures or non-visual tasks. Open questions include whether the elementwise-dominance finding holds at different model scales and whether the 3× efficiency gap persists on actual edge hardware with different memory hierarchies.
