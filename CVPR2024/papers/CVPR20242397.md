## Problem

Existing monocular human mesh recovery methods perform well on standard benchmarks but fail in dance and martial arts scenarios, where they tend to recover hand-wrist and foot-ankle joints to a T-pose. This failure stems from a domain gap: commonly used datasets (Human3.6M, MPI-INF-3DHP, COCO) contain almost no dance or martial arts movements, and even dance-focused datasets like AIST++ have inaccurate hand/foot annotations resembling rest poses. The paper addresses this by constructing a large-scale dataset specifically targeting these hardcase scenarios.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes raw video sequences as input, crops individual person images using YOLOv8 bounding boxes, and produces per-frame 2D keypoints (23 body + 42 hand joints via RTM-pose) and 3D SMPL parameters (pose θ and shape β) as output. The resulting HardMo dataset contains over 7 million annotated images from 1,500 sequences spanning 15 dance and 14 martial arts categories, with two curated subsets (HardMo-Foot >500K, HardMo-Hand >400K) where SMPL labels are further refined through optimization. Samples are filtered by keypoint confidence thresholds (0.5 for general, 0.65 for foot, 0.7 for hand) to ensure annotation reliability.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage automatic annotation pipeline: (1) Normal Annotation generates pseudo-labels using RTM-pose for 2D keypoints and 4DHumans for initial SMPL parameters; (2) Angle-based Hardcase Mining identifies foot-hardcase samples where the ankle rotation deviates more than 63° from 90° and hand-hardcase samples where the wrist rotation deviates less than 52° from 90°, computed from 2D keypoint geometry; (3) Label Optimization refines SMPL annotations using a learning-based approach rather than classical SMPLify/EFT, which would produce physically implausible poses. For foot-hardcase, a model is trained with a joint loss combining 2D body/foot reprojection and SMPL parameter regularization, then used to generate refined pseudo-labels. For hand-hardcase, a two-stage optimization first handles body/foot, then optimizes hand joints while regularizing non-hand SMPL pose parameters (θ₀:₁₉) to prevent degradation of other body parts.

## Results

On the HardMo benchmark, HardMo-HMR (trained solely on HardMo) achieves an MPJPE of 36.0 mm and PA-MPJPE of 25.0 mm, surpassing the SOTA 4DHumans^b (36.6 mm MPJPE) and outperforming HMR trained with InstaVariety (61.1 mm) by 25.1 mm. The full HardMo-4DHumans model further reduces MPJPE to 26.0 mm and PA-MPJPE to 18.0 mm, with PCK@0.01 reaching 0.60. On the HardMo-Foot subset, HardMo-4DHumans achieves a foot MPJPE of 29.3 mm, roughly 3× better than 4DHumans^b (88.1 mm) and 7× better than ProHMR (213.6 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HardMo-HMR (36.0 mm MPJPE) outperforms ProHMR [22] (113.8 mm) by 3.2× and CLIFF [24] (93.3 mm) by 2.6× on the HardMo benchmark, demonstrating that the dataset alone is sufficient to close the domain gap. Compared to HMR [18] trained with InstaVariety (61.1 mm), HardMo-HMR reduces MPJPE by 25.1 mm, showing HardMo is substantially more representative of real-world dance and martial arts scenarios. Against the SOTA 4DHumans^b [10] (36.6 mm), HardMo-HMR achieves a marginal 0.6 mm improvement, while HardMo-4DHumans (26.0 mm) delivers a 10.6 mm gain, confirming that the dataset benefits even the strongest architecture. On the HardMo-Foot benchmark, HardMo-4DHumans achieves 29.3 mm foot MPJPE versus 88.1 mm for 4DHumans^b and 213.6 mm for ProHMR, and on HardMo-Hand it reaches PCK@0.01-hand of 0.54 versus 0.11 for 4DHumans^b and 0.03 for ProHMR, validating the hardcase-specific subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-driven paradigm for monocular human mesh recovery, building directly on 4DHumans [10] as the backbone for generating initial SMPL pseudo labels. It departs from prior dataset construction efforts (rendered datasets like AGORA/BEDLAM, marker-based like Human3.6M, markerless like AIST++) by proposing an automatic pipeline that crawls online dance and martial arts videos, applies off-the-shelf 2D pose estimation (RTM-pose) and 3D mesh recovery (4DHumans), and then mines hardcases via an angle-based criterion on ankle and wrist rotations. The key conceptual departure from 4DHumans' use of InstaVariety is the explicit identification and targeted correction of two failure modes—hand-wrist and foot-ankle misalignment—through a learning-based label optimization rather than classical SMPLify fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HardMo provides the first large-scale (7M images) dataset specifically targeting the domain gap between benchmark performance and real-world deployment in dance and martial arts, with dedicated subsets (HardMo-Foot, HardMo-Hand) that isolate and address the two most common failure modes. The practical impact is significant: the automatic pipeline annotates 1M images in three days, making domain-specific data collection feasible without expensive mocap rigs. However, the paper explicitly limits its scope to hand/foot misalignment, leaving other challenges like self-occlusion, multi-person interactions, and temporal consistency unaddressed. Whether the angle-based mining thresholds generalize beyond the specific dance and martial arts categories studied remains an open question.
