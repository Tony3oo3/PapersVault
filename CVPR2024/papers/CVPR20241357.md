## Problem

Existing LiDAR-camera BEV fusion methods (e.g., BEVFusion) generate modality-specific BEV features independently and concatenate them without any cross-modality interaction, leaving the camera stream without reliable depth information and preventing global spatial relevance between the two modalities. This paper addresses the lack of LiDAR-to-camera guidance and the absence of adaptive, interaction-aware fusion in the BEV representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized LiDAR point clouds (voxelized at 0.075m × 0.075m × 0.2m) and 6 surround-view RGB images at 448×800 resolution covering 360° horizontal FOV, sampled at 2 Hz key frames. It outputs 3D object bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via nuScenes mAP and NDS. Temporal fusion additionally ingests the previous frame's BEV features from a memory buffer, and the LiDAR stream requires raw point coordinates for sparse depth projection during guidance.

## Method

GAFusion employs a dual-stream architecture where the LiDAR branch uses VoxelNet with two extra downsampling layers (strides 16, 32) and sparse height compression to aggregate multi-scale sparse features into a rich BEV representation. The camera branch is guided by two LiDAR-derived signals: Sparse Depth Guidance (SDG) projects LiDAR points onto images to produce sparse depth maps that are concatenated with 2D features before view transformation, and LiDAR Occupancy Guidance (LOG) predicts a 3D occupancy volume from LiDAR BEV features and element-wise multiplies it with the camera 3D feature volume to suppress unreliable depth regions. A Multi-Scale Dual-Path Transformer (MSDPT) then enlarges receptive fields via a local path (horizontal slicing with windowed self-attention) and a global path (BEV-pooled features), merging them with height-direction gating. Finally, the LiDAR-Guided Adaptive Fusion Transformer (LGAFT) fuses the two BEV features through a sigmoid-gated cross-attention where LiDAR features modulate the query and camera features serve as keys/values, followed by a temporal fusion module that aligns and adds the previous frame's BEV features before a standard BEV encoder and detection head.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, GAFusion achieves state-of-the-art 3D object detection performance with 73.6% mAP and 74.9% NDS, surpassing the previous best multi-modality method MSMDFusion (71.5% mAP, 74.0% NDS) by 2.1% mAP and 0.9% NDS. On the nuScenes validation set, GAFusion reaches 72.1% mAP and 73.5% NDS, outperforming all compared methods without using any test-time augmentation or multi-model ensemble. The model is trained on two 3090 GPUs with a batch size of 4, using a two-stage training protocol (20 epochs for LiDAR backbone, then 6 epochs joint training).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAFusion outperforms all 17 compared baselines on both the nuScenes test and validation sets. On the test set, it leads the closest competitor MSMDFusion [13] by 2.1% mAP (73.6 vs 71.5) and 0.9% NDS (74.9 vs 74.0), and beats FocalFormer3D [6] by 2.0% mAP and 1.0% NDS. Against the BEVFusion family [22, 26], GAFusion gains 2.3–3.4% mAP and 1.6–2.0% NDS, demonstrating the benefit of LiDAR-guided interaction over simple concatenation. On the validation set, GAFusion (72.1% mAP, 73.5% NDS) surpasses SparseFusion [39] by 1.6% mAP and 0.7% NDS, and DeepInteraction [42] by 2.2% mAP and 0.9% NDS. The largest per-category gains are on Truck (65.3 vs 61.5 for CMT) and Pedestrian (92.3 vs 91.7 for DeepInteraction), suggesting the guidance and larger receptive fields particularly help with smaller or partially occluded objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAFusion is built directly on the BEVFusion dual-stream architecture (NeurIPS 2022 / ICRA 2023), retaining its lift-splat-shoot view transformation for the camera stream and sparse convolution for the LiDAR stream, but departs from BEVFusion's simple concatenation fusion by introducing a LiDAR-guided adaptive fusion transformer (LGAFT) that enables cross-modality interaction at the BEV level. The key conceptual departure is the LiDAR guidance module (SDG + LOG), which uses raw point-cloud depth and a learned occupancy head to inject geometric information into the camera stream before fusion, addressing the depth-estimation weakness that prior BEV-based methods (BEVFusion, CMT, TransFusion) leave unaddressed. Temporal fusion follows BEVDet4D's memory-buffer scheme, and the multi-scale dual-path transformer borrows from hierarchical attention designs, while the occupancy-guided component repurposes ideas from the 3D occupancy prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly guiding camera features with LiDAR depth and occupancy information before fusion yields a meaningful SOTA improvement (73.6% mAP / 74.9% NDS on nuScenes test) without any augmentation, suggesting that the bottleneck in multi-modal BEV detection is not fusion capacity but the quality of the camera stream's geometric representation. The additional downsampling + sparse height compression module is shown to be transferable to other architectures (TransFusion, BEVFusion), making it a practical building block. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset, temporal fusion restricted to two frames, and reliance on a pre-trained LiDAR backbone; open questions include whether the guidance mechanism generalizes to datasets with different sensor configurations and whether scaling to more temporal frames yields diminishing returns.
