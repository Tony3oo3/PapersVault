## Problem

Existing NeRF-based SLAM systems build only RGB maps that are unsuitable for downstream tasks like navigation, while prior semantic NeRF approaches require hours of offline training and are impractical for real-time SLAM. Additionally, appearance, geometry, and semantic features are inherently interrelated yet are processed independently in prior work, losing cross-modal correlations that could improve robustness when any single attribute is degraded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a real-time RGB-D frame stream where RGB images are processed by a pretrained DINOv2 backbone with a segmentation head to extract semantic features, and depth values drive ray sampling for geometry feature extraction. Outputs include a hierarchical 3D semantic map (coarse-to-fine feature planes with 16-channel vectors), rendered RGB and depth images, camera pose estimates, and TSDF-based surface reconstruction. The system achieves 16 FPS tracking and 2.48 FPS mapping on an RTX 4090, with semantic rendering using a separate learnable density parameter (α_s) decoupled from geometric rendering (α_g).

## Method

SNI-SLAM fuses geometry, appearance, and semantic features via cross-attention: geometry serves as Query, appearance as Key, and semantic as Value to produce a fused semantic feature, then geometry and fused semantic act as Query/Key with appearance as Value to produce a fused appearance feature, which is concatenated with the original appearance and passed through a fusion MLP to preserve fine-grained details. The scene is represented by multi-scale axis-aligned feature planes at two spatial resolutions (coarse and fine) for hierarchical semantic encoding, enabling top-down structured semantic mapping. A fusion-based decoder concatenates geometry with appearance and semantic features before an MLP to produce SDF, RGB, and semantic values, enforcing one-way correlation that prevents mutual interference between modalities. A novel feature loss directly supervises intermediate feature-plane values against extracted features, providing higher-level optimization guidance beyond pixel-level RGB/depth/semantic losses. Rendering uses StyleSDF-style SDF-to-density conversion with separate learnable sharpness parameters for geometric and semantic volume rendering, with gradient detachment on the semantic path to isolate its optimization.

## Results

On the Replica dataset (8 scenes), SNI-SLAM achieves the best reconstruction and tracking metrics among all NeRF-based SLAM methods: Depth L1 of 0.766 cm, Accuracy of 1.942 cm, Completion Ratio of 96.62%, ATE Mean of 0.397 cm, and ATE RMSE of 0.456 cm, outperforming the closest competitor ESLAM by 19% in ATE RMSE. For semantic segmentation, SNI-SLAM reaches 87.41% mIoU on average across 4 Replica scenes, surpassing NIDS-SLAM (82.37%) by over 5 percentage points. On the real-world ScanNet dataset, SNI-SLAM achieves 6.54 cm tracking RMSE (10% improvement over ESLAM), and on TUM RGB-D it reaches 4.35 cm RMSE, a 41% improvement over Point-SLAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNI-SLAM outperforms all six non-semantic NeRF-based SLAM baselines (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, Vox-Fusion, Co-SLAM, ESLAM) on every reconstruction and tracking metric in Table 1, with the largest gains over iMAP (32% relative improvement in ATE RMSE) and NICE-SLAM (82% reduction in ATE RMSE). Against the closest competitor ESLAM, SNI-SLAM improves ATE RMSE from 0.678 to 0.456 cm (33% reduction) and Depth L1 from 0.945 to 0.766 cm. For semantic segmentation, SNI-SLAM beats the only semantic NeRF-SLAM baseline NIDS-SLAM by 5.04 mIoU points on average (87.41% vs. 82.37%), with per-scene wins of 5.97, 3.35, 9.17, and 1.69 points. On TUM RGB-D tracking, SNI-SLAM (4.35 cm RMSE) substantially outperforms Point-SLAM (8.92 cm), Vox-Fusion (11.31 cm), and NICE-SLAM (15.87 cm). In terms of runtime, SNI-SLAM runs at 2.15 FPS with 6.2M parameters, faster than NIDS-SLAM (0.86–2.13 FPS, 12.6M params) while maintaining comparable speed to non-semantic methods like ESLAM (3.02 FPS, 6.85M params).

## Relation to Prior Work

SNI-SLAM extends the NeRF-based dense SLAM lineage (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, ESLAM, Vox-Fusion, Co-SLAM) by integrating real-time semantic mapping, a capability previously demonstrated only in offline NeRF semantic models (Semantic-NeRF, iLabel) or in the limited real-time system NIDS-SLAM. The key architectural departure is a cross-attention mechanism that fuses appearance, geometry, and semantic features bidirectionally, combined with a hierarchical (coarse-to-fine) feature-plane representation for semantics and a one-way-correlation decoder that prevents mutual interference between modalities during decoding. The system also introduces a feature-level loss that supervises intermediate representations rather than only pixel-level outputs, building on the observation that low-level losses can cause the network to overfit to insignificant details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNI-SLAM demonstrates that real-time semantic NeRF-SLAM can simultaneously achieve SOTA tracking and reconstruction accuracy without sacrificing semantic quality, addressing a gap where prior NeRF-SLAM systems produced only RGB/geometry maps unsuitable for downstream navigation tasks. The hierarchical semantic representation (coarse-to-fine feature planes) is a practical design choice that could transfer to other implicit-representation systems needing multi-scale understanding. However, the system is validated only on small indoor scenes (Replica, ScanNet, TUM), relies on a frozen DINOv2 backbone for 2D semantic features, and the 6.2M parameter count and ~2 FPS mapping rate raise open questions about scalability to large-scale or outdoor environments and to hardware-constrained deployment.
