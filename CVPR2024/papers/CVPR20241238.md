## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for 3D medical images (rotation reconstruction, multi-view restoration, masked autoencoding) learn primarily low-level, augment-invariant features that lack high-level semantics, which heavily limits downstream task performance. The paper identifies that 3D medical images contain relatively consistent contextual position information—stable geometric relations between organs across scans—that has not been explicitly exploited as a semantic prior in pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241238/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single 3D medical volume (CT or MRI) and internally partitions it into a 4×4×1 grid of non-overlapping base crops plus randomly sampled sub-volumes; no external annotations or paired views are required. The pretrained backbone (Swin-UNETR or 3D UNet) produces volumetric feature representations that are then fine-tuned with a standard segmentation head (producing per-voxel masks) or a classification head (producing a single label), evaluated across six downstream datasets including both CT and MRI modalities.

## Method

VoCo formulates a contextual position prediction pretext task via prototype-level contrastive learning. An input volume is cropped into n=16 non-overlapping base volumes whose backbone features (projected to 2048-d) serve as spatially-defined prototypes; a randomly cropped sub-volume is then assigned to a base by computing cosine similarity to each prototype, with soft position labels derived from the proportional overlap area between the random crop and each base. The prediction loss L_pred is an entropy-based distance between predicted similarity logits and these soft labels, while a regularization loss L_reg enforces near-orthogonality among the base features to ensure discriminative, linearly independent prototypes. Unlike prior position-prediction methods that use a black-box linear classifier, VoCo predicts positions through explicit volume contrast, and unlike SwAV-style methods that require online clustering or iterative prototype updates, the prototypes are directly defined by spatial partitioning—requiring no clustering step and no annotation.

## Results

On the BTCV dataset (13-organ abdominal CT segmentation), VoCo achieves 83.85% average Dice Score, surpassing the best prior method GL-MAE (82.01%) by 1.84% and the scratch Swin-UNETR baseline (80.53%) by 3.32%. On unseen datasets, VoCo reaches 96.52% Dice on LiTs (liver), 96.34% on MSD Spleen, 90.54% on MMWHS, 78.53% on BraTS 21 (MRI, cross-modality), and 90.83% accuracy on CC-CCII (COVID-19 classification), consistently outperforming all compared SSL methods by 1–2.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoCo outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all six downstream datasets. On BTCV, VoCo (83.85% avg Dice) beats the strongest reconstruction-based method GL-MAE (82.01%) by 1.84%, the geometric-similarity method GVSL (81.87%) by 1.98%, and the position-prediction methods Jigsaw (81.35%) and PositionLabel (81.09%) by 2.5% and 2.76% respectively. General SSL methods perform notably worse: MoCo v3 (79.54%) and SimCLR (73.85%) lag behind VoCo by 4.3% and 10%, attributed to impractical batch-size requirements and inappropriate negative-sample assumptions for 3D medical volumes. On unseen datasets, VoCo leads by 0.8% over Rubik++ on LiTs (96.52% vs 95.72%), by 0.87% over GVSL on MSD Spleen (96.34% vs 95.47%), by 2.27% over GVSL on MMWHS (90.54% vs 88.27%), by 0.97% over SwinMM on BraTS 21 (78.53% vs 77.56%), and by 1.22% over SwinMM on CC-CCII classification (90.83% vs 89.61%). The consistent 1–2.3% margins across segmentation and classification tasks, including cross-modality transfer from CT to MRI, indicate that the contextual-position contrastive pretext task provides broadly transferable high-level semantic representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoCo extends prototype-level contrastive learning (SwAV, Caron et al. 2020) by replacing the random initialization and online update of prototypes with spatially grounded base crops, leveraging the consistent anatomical geometry inherent to 3D medical volumes. It also inherits the projector, cosine similarity, and stop-gradient machinery from SimCLR (Chen et al. 2020) for feature extraction and contrast computation. The key conceptual departure from prior position-related SSL (Jigsaw, PositionLabel) is that VoCo predicts contextual positions through volume contrast against learned bases rather than through a black-box linear classifier, and it exploits the domain-specific prior that organ positions are anatomically consistent across patients—something that does not hold in natural images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VoCo addresses a recognized gap in 3D medical SSL—reconstruction-based pre-training captures low-level information but lacks high-level semantic structure—by encoding contextual position priors into representations without any annotations. The consistent gains across six diverse downstream tasks (including cross-modality CT-to-MRI transfer) suggest the learned representations carry transferable anatomical semantics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger pre-training corpora, how it performs in semi-supervised or few-label regimes, and whether the reliance on consistent spatial anatomy limits applicability to highly variable or non-anatomical 3D volumes.
