## Problem

Tensor network structure search (TN-SS) is an NP-hard problem that requires finding both the topology and rank of a TN to achieve compact tensor representation. Existing methods rely on a 'sampling-evaluation' framework that samples thousands of candidate structures and evaluates each by solving an iterative optimization, leading to prohibitively high computational costs (e.g., TNGA requires ~1000s of evaluations for a single 5th-order tensor). The paper asks whether the TN structure can be optimized simultaneously with the TN cores, eliminating repeated evaluations entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes an Nth-order tensor (e.g., 5th-order light fields of size 40×60×3×9×9 or 4th-order videos of size 144×176×3×50) and a sparsity parameter γ, and produces N TN cores, N(N−1)/2 diagonal factors, and the resulting TN structure (topology with pruned edges and per-edge ranks). For tensor completion, the input is an incomplete observation tensor with a specified missing ratio (90%), and the output is the fully reconstructed tensor. The method starts from a fully-connected topology and progressively prunes rank-1 edges as diagonal factors become sparse, so the output structure is data-adaptive rather than pre-defined.

## Method

SVDinsTN inserts a diagonal factor S_{t,l} between every pair of TN cores in a fully-connected topology, extending the SVD 'core–diagonal–core' pattern to higher-order tensors. The structure search is cast as a regularized optimization: minimize reconstruction error plus Tikhonov regularization on cores and ℓ₁-norm sparsity on diagonal factors, solved via proximal alternating minimization (PAM). Cores are updated in closed form (Eq. 4), while each diagonal factor is updated via a 5-iteration ADMM loop with soft-thresholding that drives small entries to zero; zero entries are then deleted, shrinking the effective rank and pruning edges. A novel initialization scheme uses truncated SVD of mode-(t,l) slices with a shrink operation to set small initial ranks, avoiding the high cost of starting from a dense fully-connected topology. The paper proves convergence to a critical point (Theorem 1) and establishes an upper bound on the TN rank (Theorem 2), which directly motivates the initialization design.

## Results

On fifth-order light field data (40×60×3×9×9) at RE bound 0.05, SVDinsTN achieves compression ratios of 6.92% (Bunny), 5.64% (Knights), and 6.42% (Truck) with run times of 0.029–0.152 ×1000 s, compared to 4.26–5.77% CR but 18–180 ×1000 s for the best TN-SS baselines (TNALE, TNLS, TNGA), yielding a 100–1000× speedup. In the tensor completion task at 90% missing ratio, SVDinsTN attains the highest MPSNR across all four color videos: 32.40 dB (Bunny), 31.64 dB (News), 31.68 dB (Salesman), and 32.71 dB (Silent), surpassing the best baseline (TW) by 0.9–1.7 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pre-defined topology methods (TRALS, FCTNALS), SVDinsTN achieves substantially lower CR (e.g., 6.92% vs. 17.4% on Bunny at RE 0.05) and faster run times, confirming that the learned structure is more compact than fixed topologies. Among TN-SS baselines, SVDinsTN trades a modest CR increase (≈1–2 percentage points) for a dramatic 100–1000× reduction in run time: on Knights at RE 0.05, SVDinsTN runs in 0.104 ×1000 s versus 140.2 (TNGA), 75.51 (TNLS), and 25.05 (TNALE) ×1000 s. TNGreedy is the closest TN-SS competitor in speed (1.0–1.4 ×1000 s) but still 10–40× slower than SVDinsTN. In the tensor completion task, SVDinsTN outperforms all six baselines (FBCP, TMac, TMacTT, TRLRF, TW, TNLS) on MPSNR by 0.9–3.6 dB, with the largest margin over FBCP and TMac; TNLS, while competitive in MPSNR (30.69 on Salesman), requires 76,053 s versus SVDinsTN's 769 s, a 100× overhead. Overall, SVDinsTN wins on speed across all comparisons and on representation quality (CR or MPSNR) against every baseline except TNGA/TNLS/TNALE on CR, where the gap is ≤2 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVDinsTN extends the Fully-Connected Tensor Network (FCTN) decomposition [41] by inserting diagonal factors between every pair of cores, drawing a direct analogy to the 'core–diagonal factor–core' structure of SVD. It departs fundamentally from the dominant 'sampling-evaluation' paradigm used by TNGreedy [12], TNGA [15], TNLS [17], and TNALE [18], which treat TN structure search as a combinatorial problem requiring thousands of candidate evaluations. Instead, the paper reframes structure search as a single regularized optimization problem solved via PAM and ADMM, where L1 sparsity on the diagonal factors implicitly determines the topology and rank. The truncated-SVD initialization scheme, grounded in a proven upper bound on the FCTN rank (Theorem 2), is a novel practical contribution that addresses the high initial cost of starting from a fully-connected topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100–1000× speedup over state-of-the-art TN-SS methods, validated across multiple light-field datasets and tensor orders up to 10 (where FCTNALS and TNGA run out of memory), makes tensor network structure search practically feasible for higher-order data in computer vision and signal processing. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that a theoretical guarantee of the *optimal* TN structure remains an open problem—convergence to a critical point does not ensure global optimality. The method's reliance on a single hyperparameter γ and its sensitivity to the shrink threshold in initialization also leave room for further theoretical and practical refinement. Broader impact includes enabling TN-based representations in settings where the combinatorial search cost was previously prohibitive, such as real-time or large-scale tensor completion.
