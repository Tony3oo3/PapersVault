## Problem

Existing open world object detectors exhibit low recall on unknown objects and tend to misclassify them into known classes, largely because their objectness predictions are correlated with class-specific information. This dependency means the detector over-relies on known-class cues (e.g., shape) to estimate objectness, overlooking general objectness signals for visually dissimilar unknown objects, and produces correlated decision boundaries that increase confusion across incremental learning phases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and uses randomly sampled bounding boxes (no region proposal network) to extract RoI-pooled features via a ResNet-50 backbone. Outputs include an objectness score, a class probability vector over C known classes plus a special unknown category, and localization predictions. The unknown class probability is not learned directly but derived heuristically as the complement of the maximum softmax probability over known classes, with remaining probabilities rescaled to sum to one.

## Method

The paper enforces orthogonality at three levels to decouple objectness from class information. First, each object feature is decomposed in a polar coordinate system: its magnitude feeds the objectness head while its unit direction feeds the classification head, making the two prediction pathways operate on orthogonal feature components. Second, a decorrelation loss penalizes the squared Pearson correlation between each class probability and the objectness score across a mini-batch, further reducing statistical dependence in prediction space. Third, a cross-task calibration layer applies task-specific affine transformations to features, with a Gumbel-Softmax routing mechanism (using class prediction confidence to estimate task membership) selecting the appropriate transform per proposal, thereby maintaining feature orthogonality and mitigating catastrophic forgetting during incremental learning of new object classes.

## Results

On M-OWODB, the proposed method achieves U-Recall of 24.6/26.3/29.1 and mAP (both) of 47.0/41.3/37.9 across tasks 2–4, outperforming the previous best (CAT) by up to 7.3% in U-Recall and 1.4–2.5% in mAP. On S-OWODB, it reaches U-Recall of 24.6/27.9/31.9 and mAP (both) of 51.3/48.8/46.2, surpassing baselines by 0.6–3.8% in mAP. On PASCAL VOC incremental detection, the method achieves overall mAP of 72.3/74.7/75.6 across three settings (10+10, 15+5, 19+1), exceeding the strongest baselines by 1.8–4.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all open-world baselines on U-Recall: it exceeds CAT [49] by 0.9/7.2/4.7 points and PROB [85] by 5.2/8.9/9.5 points across tasks 1–3 on M-OWODB, while also surpassing RandBox [71] (the strongest known-class detector) by 14.0/20.0/21.3 points in U-Recall. On mAP, the method leads CAT by 2.9/6.5/8.0 points and PROB by 3.0/5.3/9.8 points on tasks 2–4, though it trails RandBox by 0.5 points on Task 1 mAP (61.3 vs 61.8) before surpassing it on all subsequent tasks. On S-OWODB, the advantage is even more pronounced, with U-Recall gains of 10.6/4.9/7.3 over CAT and mAP (Both) gains of 0.6/3.8/3.4. For incremental detection on PASCAL VOC, the method beats the strongest baseline CAT by 4.6/2.5/1.8 mAP points across the three settings, and improves over its own base model by 2.5/2.3/2.2 mAP, confirming that the calibration layer mitigates catastrophic forgetting. The only consistent trade-off is a slightly higher wilderness impact (WI) than RandBox, though it remains competitive with all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on RandBox [71], a Fast R-CNN-like architecture that removes the region proposal network, and positions itself within the open world object detection paradigm introduced by Joseph et al. [30]. It departs from prior decoupling approaches (CAT [49], PROB [85]) by explicitly diagnosing the statistical correlation between objectness and class predictions as the root cause of poor unknown-class recall, then enforcing orthogonality at three levels: polar-coordinate feature decomposition, a prediction-space decorrelation loss, and a cross-task calibration layer inspired by lifelong learning calibration [66]. The orthogonalization techniques themselves draw on a broader literature of Stiefel-manifold constraints and soft regularizers [1, 35, 77], repurposed here for the specific problem of disentangling objectness from category in an open-set detection setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic explanation (Pearson correlation between objectness and class scores) for why existing open world detectors fail on unknown objects, and shows that systematic orthogonalization at multiple levels consistently improves both unknown recall and known-class retention across incremental tasks. However, the unknown-class discrimination remains a heuristic (confidence threshold on a unit hypersphere), and the evaluation is limited to 80-class COCO/VOC splits, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to truly large-scale open-world scenarios with thousands of evolving categories. The calibration layer, while effective, introduces task-specific parameters and a routing mechanism whose complexity grows with the number of incremental stages, raising scalability questions for long-horizon continual learning.
