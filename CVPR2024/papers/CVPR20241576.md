## Problem

No-Reference Image Quality Assessment (NR-IQA) models are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations that cause large score changes while remaining imperceptible to humans, yet no defense method specifically designed for NR-IQA tasks had been proposed. This paper fills that gap by establishing a theoretical link between score-change magnitude and the ℓ₁ norm of the input gradient, then using that link to design a training-time regularization that improves robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241576/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a single distorted RGB image (no reference image required) and outputs a scalar quality score in the MOS range [0, 100]. During training, the NT strategy additionally consumes a finite-difference probe: the model is evaluated at x and at x + h·sign(∇_x f(x)), where h is a small step size (0.01 in experiments), to approximate the gradient ℓ₁ norm without double backpropagation. The perturbation threat model is ℓ∞-bounded with ε chosen so that SSIM between clean and adversarial images stays above 0.9.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that under ℓ∞-bounded perturbations, the worst-case score change is approximately ε‖∇_x f(x)‖₁, meaning a smaller input-gradient ℓ₁ norm directly implies greater robustness. To exploit this, the authors add a gradient-norm regularization term to the standard IQA loss: L = L_IQA + λ·‖∇_x f(x)‖₁². Because computing the gradient of this term with respect to model parameters would require double backpropagation (infeasible for large DNNs), they approximate ‖∇_x f(x)‖₁ via a finite-difference estimate |f(x + h·d) − f(x)|/h with d = sign(∇_x f(x)), yielding a single-forward-pass regularization. The strategy is model-agnostic and is applied to four diverse NR-IQA baselines (HyperIQA, DBCNN, LinearityIQA, MANIQA) with per-model λ values, consistently reducing score changes under both white-box (FGSM, Perceptual Attack) and black-box (UAP, Korhonen) attacks at the cost of a small drop in clean-image performance.

## Results

On the LIVEC dataset, the NT strategy reduces the RMSE between predicted scores before and after FGSM attacks by 59% for HyperIQA (19.17→7.89), 42% for DBCNN (32.78→19.07), and 58% for MANIQA (15.55→6.56). Under the Perceptual Attack, DBCNN's score-change RMSE drops dramatically from 63.99 to 14.52, and HyperIQA's from 6.36 to 0.13. Clean-image performance degrades only marginally (e.g., DBCNN SROCC drops from 0.866 to 0.856), confirming a small robustness–accuracy trade-off. The NT strategy is most effective against white-box attacks (FGSM, Perceptual) and less so against black-box attacks (UAP, Kor.), where baseline models are already relatively robust.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies the NT strategy to four NR-IQA baselines and evaluates robustness via RMSE and SROCC between predicted scores before and after four attacks. **DBCNN** benefits the most: under the Perceptual Attack, score-change RMSE drops from 63.99 to 14.52 (−77%), and under FGSM from 32.78 to 19.07 (−42%). **HyperIQA** also shows large gains, with FGSM RMSE falling from 19.17 to 7.89 and Perceptual Attack RMSE from 6.36 to 0.13. **LinearityIQA** improves under FGSM (48.13→36.99) and Perceptual Attack (115.73→80.86), though absolute robustness remains the weakest among the four. **MANIQA** is the exception: it is already highly robust to the Perceptual Attack (SROCC = 1.000 for both baseline and NT), so the NT version shows a negligible RMSE increase (0.08→0.19); however, under FGSM the NT version still improves substantially (15.55→6.56). Across all four baselines, clean-image SROCC degrades by at most 0.04 (HyperIQA: 0.899→0.859), confirming the robustness gain comes at a small accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the gradient-norm regularization idea from adversarial robustness in classification (e.g., Finlay & Oberman's scalable input gradient regularization) and adapts it to the fundamentally different objective of NR-IQA, where the goal is score stability rather than correct classification. The theoretical justification (Theorem 1) is a direct first-order Taylor expansion argument linking ℓ∞-bounded perturbations to the ℓ₁ gradient norm, and the finite-difference approximation to avoid double backpropagation is borrowed from Ilyas et al.'s bandit-based attack framework. The paper carves new ground by being the first to formalize and defend against adversarial attacks specifically on NR-IQA models, building on existing attack methods (Perceptual Attack, UAP, Kor. Attack) but introducing no prior defense counterpart.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first defense method for NR-IQA adversarial robustness, filling a clear gap identified by prior attack papers that highlighted vulnerabilities without proposing solutions. The practical relevance is significant for media industry applications (advertising, streaming quality control) where manipulated quality scores could have real economic consequences. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (LIVEC), a modest but consistent drop in clean-image performance, and the absence of a theoretical explanation for the observed SROCC robustness gains. Open questions include extending the approach to FR-IQA, developing defenses that preserve prediction monotonicity (SROCC) rather than just score magnitude, and whether the finite-difference approximation remains effective for larger-scale or transformer-based IQA models.
