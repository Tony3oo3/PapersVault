## Problem

Prompt learning for Vision-Language Models (e.g., CoOp) relies on few-shot labeled datasets, but high-level or complex label noise (up to 75% Pairflip) causes severe accuracy degradation that existing robustness analyses have not adequately addressed. No systematic framework existed to handle label noise specifically within the prompt learning paradigm for VL-PTMs, where the small sample size makes clean/noisy separation particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with a 16-shot labeled dataset per class, where labels may be corrupted by Symflip (random reassignment) or Pairflip (adjacent-class reassignment) noise at ratios from 12.5% to 100%. Within the CoOp framework, only M learnable context vectors are optimized while CLIP's ResNet-50 image encoder and 63M-parameter text Transformer remain frozen; the output is a standard class label obtained via cosine similarity between the prompted text embedding and the image embedding, with a temperature-scaled softmax over all L classes.

## Method

JoAPR is a two-stage iterative framework: (1) Data Partitioning fits a two-component GMM to the per-sample loss distribution (averaged over 5 epochs) and applies two joint adaptive thresholds—θ₁ derived analytically from minimizing Gaussian overlap, and θ₂ dynamically adjusted from the previous epoch's partition count—to separate clean from noisy samples without manual tuning; (2) Label Refurbishment replaces noisy labels entirely with model predictions (averaged over K augmentations) and probabilistically blends clean labels with model predictions using a confidence score p_i = p(g_clean|L)·p_softmax(Y_i), which down-weights misclassified noisy samples early in training. A warmup phase with confidence penalty and a compensation term (L_Divide = L_CE + H) sharpens the loss distribution separation, and retraining incorporates MixMatch (sharpened pseudo-labels + Mixup) plus a uniformity regularization to prevent collapse under extreme noise.

## Results

On 10 datasets with 16-shot training, CoOp+JoAPR/JoAPR* dramatically outperforms baseline CoOp under high and complex label noise. At 75% Pairflip noise, CoOp drops to 16.23% on Caltech101 and 20.33% on ImageNet, while CoOp+JoAPR maintains 84.43% and 53.67% respectively. On the real-world noisy Food101N dataset, CoOp+JoAPR* achieves 73.87% accuracy versus CoOp's 69.50%. In the extreme 100% mislabeled setting, CoOp+JoAPR* recovers 84.80% on Caltech101 and 72.40% on OxfordPets, compared to near-zero accuracy (1.30% and 4.70%) for unassisted CoOp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline CoOp, JoAPR/JoAPR* achieves massive gains at high noise: at 75% Pairflip, improvements range from +33.3 pp (ImageNet: 53.67 vs 20.33) to +68.2 pp (Caltech101: 84.43 vs 16.23), while at low noise (12.5%) the two are roughly comparable. Against CoCoOp (Table 3, EuroSAT), JoAPR similarly rescues performance at 75% Pairflip (59.67% vs 24.97% for CoCoOp alone), confirming the method generalizes beyond CoOp. Against the robust-loss baseline GCE (Fig. 5), JoAPR clearly wins at high noise; notably, on EuroSAT and DTD at 75% Pairflip, CoOp+GCE underperforms even plain CoOp, while JoAPR maintains 39–46% accuracy. Against DivideMix (Fig. 5), JoAPR outperforms in most settings, with DivideMix even falling below CoOp+GCE on Caltech101-Symflip, indicating that standard LNL sample-selection methods transfer poorly to the few-shot prompt-learning regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

JoAPR sits at the intersection of two research threads: prompt learning for vision-language models (CoOp, CoCoOp) and learning with noisy labels (LNL). It directly builds on CoOp's frozen-encoder prompt optimization paradigm and adapts the divide-and-refurbish pipeline from DivideMix (Li et al., 2020), which was designed for large-scale semi-supervised settings. The key departure from prior LNL work is the introduction of joint adaptive thresholds (a GMM-derived loss threshold θ₁ and a posterior-probability threshold θ₂) that eliminate manual hyperparameter tuning, which is critical in the few-shot regime where sample counts are too small for reliable threshold calibration. Unlike Wu et al. (ICCV 2023), who characterized why prompt learning is *more* robust than fine-tuning under mild noise, JoAPR targets the regime where that robustness breaks down—high noise ratios and structured (Pairflip) noise—and provides a corrective mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic framework for handling label noise in prompt learning for VL-PTMs, and the 100%-noise experiment (achieving ~82–85% on Caltech101 with all labels wrong) suggests that CLIP's pre-trained prior can essentially override entirely corrupted supervision, potentially reducing annotation costs to near zero in few-shot transfer. However, the method's applicability is currently limited to the CoOp/CoCoOp paradigm at 16-shot, and the added pipeline (GMM fitting, K augmentations, MixMatch retraining) introduces non-trivial computational overhead that may not be justified in low-noise regimes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other prompt-learning variants (e.g., KAPT, PLOT), to noise types beyond Symflip/Pairflip (e.g., instance-dependent or open-set noise), and to larger few-shot budgets where the memorization effect weakens.
