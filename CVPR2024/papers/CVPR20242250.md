## Problem

Joint video super-resolution and deblurring (VSRDB) has received far less attention than either task individually, and existing joint methods rely on 2D CNNs with spatially-equivariant, input-independent filters that cannot effectively model spatio-temporally-variant motion blur. Conventional dynamic filtering, while adaptive, requires large kernels to capture large inter-frame motions, causing exponential growth in computation and memory. The paper addresses how to handle small-to-large motions with compact, motion-aware filters in a unified VSRDB framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurry low-resolution video sequence of T=3 frames (one center frame plus one past and one future frame) at 180×320 resolution and produces a single sharp high-resolution center frame at 4× scale (720×1280). The degradation is assumed to be a combination of motion blur and spatial downsampling, and the network learns both the degradation model and the restoration jointly. Evaluation uses PSNR, SSIM, and a temporal consistency metric (tOF) across REDS4, GoPro, and YouTube test sets.

## Method

FMA-Net comprises a degradation learning network (Net^D) that predicts motion-aware spatio-temporally-variant degradation kernels and a restoration network (Net^R) that uses these kernels for joint SR and deblurring. The core mechanism is Flow-Guided Dynamic Filtering (FGDF), which warps input features using learned optical flow before applying pixel-wise dynamic filter coefficients, allowing small kernels (k_d=20, k_r=5) to effectively capture large motions without the exponential cost of large fixed-position filters. Both networks share stacked FRMA blocks that iteratively refine temporally-anchored features, warped features, and multi-flow-mask pairs (n=9) through center-oriented and degradation-aware attention, learning self-induced optical flow with occlusion masks rather than relying on a pre-trained flow estimator. A novel Temporal Anchor (TA) loss sharpens unwarped features while constraining them to their corresponding frame indices, and a two-stage training strategy (pre-train Net^D with RAFT pseudo-GT flow supervision, then jointly fine-tune) further improves convergence.

## Results

On REDS4 (×4 VSRDB), FMA-Net achieves 28.83 PSNR / 0.8315 SSIM / 1.92 tOF with 9.6M parameters and 0.427 s runtime, outperforming the best prior joint method RVRT* (27.80 / 0.8025 / 2.40) by 1.03 dB PSNR and the best VSR baseline BasicVSR++* (27.06 / 0.7752 / 2.70) by 1.77 dB. On GoPro and YouTube test sets, FMA-Net reaches 27.65 / 0.8542 and 26.02 / 0.8067 PSNR respectively, yielding average gains of 2.08 dB over RVRT* and 1.93 dB over GShiftNet*. FMA-Net also achieves the best temporal consistency (lowest tOF) across all three test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMA-Net outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three test sets. Against the only prior joint VSRDB method HOFFR, FMA-Net gains 1.59 dB PSNR (28.83 vs. 27.24) and 0.0445 SSIM while using fewer parameters (9.6M vs. 3.5M) at comparable runtime. Among retrained single-task models, FMA-Net beats RVRT* by 1.03 dB PSNR and 0.029 SSIM, BasicVSR++* by 1.77 dB, Restormer* by 1.54 dB, and GShiftNet* by 3.06 dB on REDS4. Sequential cascade approaches (e.g., SwinIR+Restormer at 24.33 dB) suffer 3–4 dB losses due to error propagation, confirming that joint learning is essential. On the generalization test sets (GoPro, YouTube), FMA-Net maintains its lead with 2.08 dB over RVRT* and 1.93 dB over GShiftNet* in average PSNR, while also achieving the best tOF (3.31 on GoPro, 2.63 on YouTube), indicating superior temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

FMA-Net extends the Dynamic Filter Network [33] paradigm from images to video by introducing Flow-Guided Dynamic Filtering (FGDF), which warps input features via learned optical flow before applying position-wise dynamic kernels, avoiding the need for prohibitively large filters to capture large motions. It departs from the first deep VSRDB method HOFFR [18], which relied on standard 2D CNNs with spatially-equivariant filters and struggled with spatio-temporally-variant blur. The iterative multi-flow-mask refinement draws on deformable alignment [8] and many-to-many splatting [28], while the multi-attention module adapts transposed attention from Restormer [73] with a novel center-oriented query design and a degradation-aware attention that conditions on predicted blur kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Joint VSRDB remains significantly underexplored relative to its individual components, and FMA-Net establishes a strong new SOTA with well-ablated evidence that flow-guided sampling fundamentally outperforms larger fixed kernels for large-motion video restoration. The FGDF concept—using learned flow to determine where to sample rather than enlarging the receptive field—is a practical and generalizable design principle applicable beyond VSRDB. Limitations include evaluation only at ×4 scale with T=3 frames and a single training dataset (REDS), leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, higher magnifications, and diverse real-world degradation types beyond motion blur.
