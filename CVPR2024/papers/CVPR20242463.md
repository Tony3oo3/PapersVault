## Problem

Existing methods for improving worst-group accuracy in the presence of spurious correlations either require explicit spurious attribute labels during training or validation, or rely on unsupervised clustering that degrades on complex features like human faces. This paper addresses the underexplored setting where the spurious attribute is not captured by any pre-existing label and must be defined externally, making manual annotation of in-distribution data impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242463/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of out-of-distribution, task-agnostic concept images (as few as 4 total, often AI-generated via Stable Diffusion with simple text prompts) alongside standard labeled training and validation images. The concept images need not come from the training distribution or be relevant to the classification task; they only need to exhibit the spurious attribute (e.g., gender presentation, background type). The output is a robust classifier trained with worst-group loss, along with inferred spurious attribute labels for every training and validation sample.

## Method

Concept DRO (CDRO) is a two-stage procedure. In Stage 1, an ERM model is trained on the task, then used as a frozen feature extractor; a linear classifier (Concept Activation Vector) is trained on the ERM features of the curated concept set versus a contrastive set, yielding a direction in feature space corresponding to the spurious attribute. Cosine similarity between each data point's ERM representation and the CAV produces a scalar score, and a Gaussian Mixture Model with m components (m = number of spurious attribute values) partitions scores into groups, assigning inferred spurious attribute labels. In Stage 2, the inferred group labels (pairs of class label and spurious attribute) are fed into Group DRO to minimize worst-group loss. The key insight is that the ERM model's penultimate layer already encodes spurious attribute information, so even a handful of out-of-distribution images suffice to identify the relevant feature direction, decoupling concept curation from the training data entirely.

## Results

The key metric is worst-group accuracy, which measures performance on the most disadvantaged subgroup. CDRO achieves 88.8% worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds and 88.0% on CelebA, outperforming ERM (62.6% and 47.7%) and GEORGE (76.2% and 54.9%) by up to 33.1 percentage points. On CMNIST, CDRO reaches 69.3% worst-group accuracy, slightly below GEORGE (76.4%) where features are trivially clusterable. CDRO also matches or exceeds methods requiring validation group labels: it beats JTT by 5.0% on Waterbirds and 6.5% on CelebA, and outperforms the 5%-validation variants of SSA and AFR by 1.3–10.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two no-label baselines, CDRO dominates on real-world data: it beats GEORGE by 12.6 points on Waterbirds (88.8 vs 76.2) and 33.1 points on CelebA (88.0 vs 54.9), though GEORGE edges ahead on CMNIST (76.4 vs 69.3) where color clusters are trivially separable. Against label-requiring methods, CDRO outperforms JTT by 5.0% on Waterbirds and 6.5% on CelebA, and beats CNC by 0.3% on Waterbirds while trailing by 0.8% on CelebA. Compared to the 5%-validation variants of SSA and AFR, CDRO leads by 1.7%/1.3% and 2.2%/10.5% on Waterbirds/CelebA respectively. CDRO remains within ~1 point of full Group DRO (which uses ground-truth group labels in both train and validation) on Waterbirds (88.8 vs 89.9) and CelebA (88.0 vs 88.9), while falling 5.4 points short on CMNIST. Overall, CDRO achieves near-state-of-the-art worst-group accuracy using only ~100 out-of-distribution concept images, eliminating the need for any manual group labeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established ideas—Concept Activation Vectors (Kim et al., 2018) for representing a concept as a direction in feature space, and Group DRO (Sagawa et al., 2019) for worst-group loss minimization—into a new pipeline that eliminates the need for any manual spurious-attribute labeling. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (JTT, CNC, SSA, AFR) by replacing validation group labels with labels inferred from a small set of out-of-distribution concept images, and from GEORGE by using a CAV-based similarity score plus GMM clustering rather than unsupervised feature-space clustering. The key conceptual departure is that the concept images need not come from the training distribution or even be task-relevant, decoupling bias correction from dataset-specific annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a practitioner can correct a model's gender or background bias using a handful of AI-generated images (as few as 4) without ever touching the training or validation set, dramatically reducing the annotation cost that has limited adoption of group-robustness methods. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets with mostly binary spurious attributes, and the 'distant' distribution ablation shows performance can collapse (53.1% on Waterbirds), so the robustness claim has meaningful boundaries. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-attribute or continuous spurious attributes, whether it works for non-vision modalities, and how sensitive it is to the quality of the ERM backbone.
