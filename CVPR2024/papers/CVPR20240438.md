## Problem

Existing audio-visual saliency prediction (AVSP) methods rely on customized architectures with built-in inductive biases and task-specific loss functions, limiting their generalizability and making effective multi-modal interaction difficult to achieve. The paper asks whether a unified, generalized diffusion-based framework can simultaneously address both the need for effective audio-visual feature interaction and a flexible, non-task-specific network structure for saliency prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240438/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 16-frame RGB video clip of size 224×384×3 and a corresponding audio signal converted into 9 log-Mel spectrogram slices of 112×192 as joint conditions. At inference, it starts from a random Gaussian noise map and iteratively denoises it over 4 DDIM steps to produce a single-channel saliency map at the original spatial resolution. The framework is modular: the video and audio encoders can be independently enabled or disabled, allowing the same architecture to operate in audio-only, video-only, or full audio-visual mode without architectural changes.

## Method

DiffSal reformulates AVSP as a conditional generative task: a forward diffusion process corrupts the ground-truth saliency map with Gaussian noise, and a Saliency-UNet learns to predict the clean map (x₀-prediction parameterization) conditioned on spatio-temporal video and audio features. The Saliency-UNet consists of 4 ResNet encoder stages and 4 Multi-Modal Attention Modulation (MAM) decoder stages; each MAM stage combines an Efficient Spatio-Temporal Cross-Attention (ECA) that compresses high-resolution spatio-temporal features via strided 3D convolutions to reduce memory from 5.32G to 1.20G, and a Multi-Modal Interaction Module (MIM) that fuses audio, video, and noise features through element-wise products followed by softmax temporal pooling to highlight key audio-visual activity regions. Video features are extracted by a pretrained MViTv2 backbone and audio features by a pretrained VGGish network augmented with a temporal transformer for inter-frame consistency. Training uses a simple MSE loss between the predicted and ground-truth saliency maps, and inference applies DDIM sampling over 4 steps, enabling iterative refinement without retraining.

## Results

DiffSal achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six audio-visual saliency benchmarks (AVAD, Coutrot1, Coutrot2, DIEM, ETMD, SumMe) using four standard metrics (CC, NSS, AUC-J, SIM). On AVAD, DiffSal reaches CC 0.738 and SIM 0.571, surpassing the previous best CASP-Net (CC 0.691, SIM 0.528) by 6.8% and 8.1% respectively. On Coutrot1, the improvement is even more pronounced: CC 0.638 vs. 0.561 (+13.7%) and SIM 0.515 vs. 0.456 (+12.9%) over CASP-Net. The paper reports an average relative improvement of 6.3% over the previous state-of-the-art across all metrics and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffSal outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against the strongest prior method CASP-Net [58], DiffSal gains the largest margins on Coutrot1 (CC +0.077, SIM +0.059) and SumMe (CC +0.073, SIM +0.060), where audio-visual correspondence is most challenging. On AVAD, the improvement over CASP-Net is CC +0.047 and SIM +0.043, while on DIEM the gap is smaller (CC +0.005, SIM tied at 0.543), suggesting DIEM's non-natural audio-visual pairing limits the benefit of multi-modal fusion. Compared to the 3D-convolution-based ViNet [30], DiffSal leads by CC +0.064 on Coutrot1 and +0.064 on AVAD. The localization-based STAViS [50] and earlier methods (ACLNet, TASED-Net) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 0.10 in CC on Coutrot1 and SumMe. Notably, DiffSal achieves these gains with fewer FLOPs (187.31G) than CASP-Net (283.35G) despite having more parameters (76.57M vs. 51.62M), thanks to the efficient spatio-temporal cross-attention design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of audio-visual saliency prediction (AVSP) work—localization-based methods (STAViS) and 3D convolution-based encoder-decoder architectures (ViNet, CASP-Net)—by reformulating the task as a conditional generative problem using a diffusion model. It draws on the x₀-prediction parameterization from DiffusionDet and the DDIM sampling strategy, while leveraging off-the-shelf MViTv2 and VGGish encoders rather than task-specific backbones. The key conceptual departure is replacing both customized network inductive biases and sophisticated task-tailored losses (CE, KL) with a generalized Saliency-UNet architecture supervised by a simple MSE objective within the diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first successful application of diffusion models to saliency prediction, demonstrating that a unified generative formulation with a simple loss can outperform purpose-built architectures across six benchmarks. The framework's ability to generalize to audio-only, video-only, and audio-visual settings, and to perform iterative refinement without retraining, offers practical flexibility. However, the 76.57M parameter count and 187.31G FLOPs remain higher than several competitors, and the 4-step DDIM inference, while efficient, still adds latency compared to single-pass methods. Open questions include whether the diffusion formulation is truly necessary or whether the gains come primarily from the Saliency-UNet architecture and multi-modal attention design, and how the approach scales to longer video sequences or real-time deployment.
