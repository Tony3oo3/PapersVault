## Problem

Dense neural RGBD SLAM methods either lack loop closure, causing accumulated drift and duplicated geometry, or rely on grid-based representations (e.g., multi-resolution hash grids) that require storing the entire history of input frames and performing expensive gradient-based updates to correct the map after a loop closure. Point-SLAM demonstrated that a neural point cloud representation is efficient for tracking and mapping but could not handle loop closure. The paper addresses how to implement globally consistent loop closure with a point-based neural scene representation without re-integration or gradient updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242425/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a real-time RGBD frame stream (color image plus depth map per frame) and produces per-frame camera poses, a globally consistent neural point cloud map organized into submaps (each point carrying a 32-dim geometric and 32-dim color feature), a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes, and rendered depth/color images for evaluation. Tracking is performed frame-to-model by re-rendering depth and color from the active submap and minimizing an L1 re-rendering loss against the sensor reading, with a variance-normalized depth term. Submaps are created dynamically based on camera motion thresholds (translation and rotation) rather than at fixed intervals.

## Method

Loopy-SLAM anchors neural features in point cloud submaps that grow iteratively in a data-driven manner, using MLP decoders with learnable Gaussian positional encodings to render occupancy and color from interpolated point features. When a submap is completed, global place recognition via a DBoW3 database detects loop closures, and a pose graph is built with odometry edges and loop edges whose constraints are computed by a coarse-to-fine dense surface registration (FPFH global alignment followed by ICP on TSDF-fused point clouds). The pose graph is optimized with a robust line process that jointly estimates loop edge weights to reject outliers, using a dense surface registration objective rather than relative pose residuals. Because the representation is point-based, map corrections after PGO are applied as simple rigid shifts of all points in each submap, avoiding the need to store input frames or perform gradient-based re-integration. At the end of capture, overlapping submap regions are fused by averaging corresponding point features, followed by a final color and geometry refinement pass.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, Loopy-SLAM achieves an average Depth L1 of 0.35 cm and F1 of 90.8% for reconstruction, outperforming Point-SLAM (0.44 cm, 89.8%) and ESLAM (1.18 cm, 79.1%) by 20% and 70% respectively on the depth metric. For tracking, Loopy-SLAM reports 0.29 cm average ATE RMSE on Replica (vs. 0.35 for GO-SLAM and 0.52 for Point-SLAM), 3.85 cm on TUM-RGBD, and 7.7 cm on ScanNet. On rendering, it achieves 35.47 dB PSNR, 0.981 SSIM, and 0.109 LPIPS, marginally surpassing Point-SLAM (35.17 dB PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Loopy-SLAM outperforms all dense neural SLAM baselines without loop closure (Point-SLAM, ESLAM, NICE-SLAM, Vox-Fusion) on both reconstruction and tracking across all three datasets. Against Point-SLAM (its direct predecessor), it improves average Depth L1 from 0.44 to 0.35 cm on Replica and ATE RMSE from 8.92 to 3.85 cm on TUM-RGBD, while using only 14% more scene points. Against GO-SLAM (the closest competitor with loop closure), Loopy-SLAM achieves substantially better reconstruction (0.35 vs. 4.68 cm Depth L1, 90.8% vs. 26.3% F1 on Replica) and slightly better tracking on Replica (0.29 vs. 0.35 cm ATE), though GO-SLAM edges ahead on ScanNet tracking (7.0 vs. 7.7 cm ATE). Against traditional dense SLAM methods (ORB-SLAM2, BundleFusion, ElasticFusion), Loopy-SLAM is competitive on TUM-RGBD tracking (3.85 cm vs. ORB-SLAM2's 1.98 cm) but remains behind on the shortest sequences, while its reconstruction quality far exceeds these methods. On rendering, Loopy-SLAM marginally beats Point-SLAM (35.47 vs. 35.17 dB PSNR) and substantially outperforms ESLAM (27.8 dB) and NICE-SLAM (24.42 dB); GO-SLAM's rendering results were unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

Loopy-SLAM directly extends Point-SLAM's neural point cloud representation by adding online loop closure via pose graph optimization, inheriting its data-driven submap growth and frame-to-model tracking. The key architectural departure from competing dense neural SLAM methods (GO-SLAM, Orbeez-SLAM, NEWTON) is the use of a point-based rather than hash-grid-based scene representation, which allows map corrections to be performed as simple rigid point shifts without storing the entire history of input frames or performing expensive gradient-based re-integration. The robust PGO formulation with dense surface registration constraints and line-process outlier rejection follows Choi et al. [8], and the coarse-to-fine loop edge computation (FPFH global registration + ICP refinement) is a standard classical pipeline repurposed for neural submaps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in scalability: by avoiding the need to store all mapped frames for post-loop-closure corrections, the method is better suited to large-scale scenes where grid-based re-integration becomes infeasible. The 57% ATE improvement over Point-SLAM with only 14% more scene points demonstrates that loop closure is the critical missing piece for globally consistent neural point cloud SLAM. However, the system lacks relocalization, is not optimized for real-time operation (Python/PyTorch), and the tracker could benefit from frame-to-frame queues for robustness on noisy real-world data. Open questions include whether learned place recognition can replace BoW for more reliable loop detection, and whether the approach scales to multi-room or outdoor environments.
