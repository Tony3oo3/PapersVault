## Problem

Full-body co-speech gesture generation (face, body, hands, global motion) remains fragmented: existing datasets lack unified mesh-level representations, and prior models either handle face and body separately or cannot accept partial gesture inputs for completion. No prior framework jointly generates all body segments from audio while also supporting spatial-temporal gesture inpainting from a few seed frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241941/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EMAGE takes speech audio (encoded via onset/amplitude rhythm features and pretrained word embeddings for content) plus a partially or fully masked gesture sequence (as few as 4 seed frames in Rot6D joint format) and produces four disentangled outputs: FLAME facial parameters (100 expression + 3 jaw dims), upper-body SMPL-X pose, hand pose, and lower-body pose, each decoded from a separate VQ-VAE codebook, plus global translations estimated by a pretrained Global Motion Predictor from the lower-body output. The masked input allows arbitrary subsets of joints and frames to be visible, enabling flexible gesture customization.

## Method

EMAGE employs a Masked Audio Gesture Transformer trained jointly on two paths: Masked Gesture-to-Gesture (MG2G) reconstruction and Audio-to-Gesture (A2G) generation. A spatial convolutional encoder plus temporal self-attention encodes body hints from partially visible joints, while a Content Rhythm Attention (CRA) module adaptively fuses speech rhythm (onset, amplitude) with semantic content (word embeddings) via a learned softmax gate. A switchable temporal cross-attention layer merges body hints with audio features for the A2G path, and a separate concatenation-based decoder handles the face (which is weakly coupled to body motion). Four compositional VQ-VAEs (face, upper body, hands, lower body) provide discrete latent priors that preserve audio-correlated motions during reconstruction, and the progressive masking schedule (0→95% over training) ensures the model learns robust spatial-temporal gesture priors that transfer to audio-conditioned generation.

## Results

On BEATv2, EMAGE achieves the best FGD of 5.512 (×10⁻¹) for body gesture realism, outperforming the closest competitor TalkSHOW (6.209) by 11%. For speech-motion synchrony (BC), EMAGE scores 7.724 (×10⁻¹), surpassing TalkSHOW (6.947) and CaMN (6.769). On facial metrics, EMAGE attains MSE 7.680 (×10⁻⁸) and LVD 7.556 (×10⁻⁵), beating FaceFormer (7.787/7.593), CodeTalker (8.026/7.766), and TalkSHOW (7.791/7.771). In user preference studies, EMAGE achieves a 52.7% holistic win rate versus 34.9% for TalkSHOW and 12.4% for Habibie et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMAGE outperforms all body-gesture baselines on FGD: it beats TalkSHOW (6.209) by 11%, CaMN (6.644) by 17%, DiffStyleGesture (8.811) by 37%, Habibie et al. (9.040) by 39%, DisCo (9.417) by 41%, HA2G (12.32) by 55%, and Trimodal (12.41) by 56%. On speech-motion synchrony (BC), EMAGE (7.724) edges out Habibie et al. (7.716) and substantially surpasses TalkSHOW (6.947), CaMN (6.769), and DisCo (6.439). For facial generation, EMAGE achieves the lowest MSE (7.680) and LVD (7.556), beating FaceFormer (7.787/7.593), CodeTalker (8.026/7.766), and TalkSHOW (7.791/7.771). The only metric where EMAGE trails is Diversity (13.06 vs. TalkSHOW's 13.47), a marginal 3% gap. In the user preference study (Table 5), EMAGE dominates with 52.7% holistic win rate versus 34.9% for TalkSHOW and 12.4% for Habibie et al., with a 23% advantage over TalkSHOW on face specifically.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMAGE builds directly on the discrete-token gesture generation paradigm established by TalkSHOW [65] and the transformer-based facial animation line (FaceFormer [19], CodeTalker [61]), but departs from TalkSHOW's fully auto-regressive design by introducing a masked gesture reconstruction objective borrowed from BERT/MAE-style representation learning. The four compositional VQ-VAEs extend TalkSHOW's two-token approach to explicitly disentangle face, upper body, hands, and lower body, addressing the observation that a single codebook conflates audio-correlated and audio-agnostic motions. The Content Rhythm Attention mechanism generalizes prior additive fusion of rhythm and content features into an adaptive, frame-level selection, and the BEAT2 dataset extends the original BEAT [39] from skeleton/ARKit to unified SMPL-X/FLAME mesh parameters via MoSh++ and handcrafted blendshape optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEAT2 (60h, mesh-level, holistic) fills a critical gap as the largest motion-captured co-speech dataset in a community-standard format, enabling fair cross-task comparison and vertex-level losses that were previously impossible with BEAT's BVH/ARKit format. The masked-gesture completion capability opens a practical use case for digital human animation where artists can specify key poses and let the model fill in audio-synchronized motion. Limitations include evaluation on a single-speaker subset (Speaker2, 85/7.5/7.5 split), a 4-frame seed requirement, and the fact that multi-dataset training still requires separate VQ-VAEs per source, limiting true cross-dataset generalization. Open questions include whether the masked modeling objective scales to longer sequences or multi-speaker settings, and whether the hard-coded physical priors in BEAT2 generalize to other mocap systems.
