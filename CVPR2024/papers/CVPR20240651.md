## Problem

Existing urban scene reconstruction methods either require expensive inputs (LiDAR, ground-truth 3D bounding boxes), handle only static scenes, or cannot achieve real-time rendering. No prior approach can jointly optimize geometry, appearance, semantics, and motion from posed RGB images alone while remaining robust to the noisy 3D bounding box predictions that are the only practical source of dynamic object poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240651/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB images along with noisy auxiliary predictions: 2D semantic labels (from InverseForm), optical flow (from Unimatch), and monocular 3D bounding boxes with tracking IDs (from QD-3DT), where the latter are only available for training views. At inference it renders novel-view RGB images at ~93 fps on a single RTX 4090, 2D semantic maps, a 3D semantic point cloud (Gaussian centers with class labels), optical flow fields, and per-object trajectories parameterized by the unicycle model. Test-view bounding boxes are either linearly interpolated or computed from the unicycle dynamics rather than requiring additional tracking inputs.

## Method

HUGS decomposes the scene into static 3D Gaussians in world coordinates and N rigidly moving dynamic objects, each represented by Gaussians in a canonical frame transformed via a unicycle model (position x,y, yaw θ, forward velocity v, angular velocity ω). The unicycle constraint is enforced through a regularization loss that penalizes deviations from the discrete kinematic equations, making the optimization far more robust to noisy 3D bounding boxes than per-frame pose optimization. Each Gaussian additionally carries semantic logits (S-dim) and a color vector; a key design choice is applying softmax in 3D space before α-blending rather than in 2D, which eliminates semantic floaters. Camera exposure is modeled as a per-frame affine transform (3×3 matrix + 3-vector) predicted by a small MLP from camera extrinsics, compensating for auto-exposure/white-balance variation. Training jointly minimizes L1+SSIM image loss, cross-entropy semantic loss, L1 flow loss, bounding-box position loss, unicycle consistency loss, and velocity smoothness regularization over 30k iterations.

## Results

On dynamic scenes with noisy 3D bounding boxes (Table 1), HUGS achieves 25.42 PSNR / 0.821 SSIM / 0.092 LPIPS on KITTI Scene02 and 26.21 PSNR / 0.911 SSIM / 0.040 LPIPS on vKITTI Scene02, consistently outperforming NSG and MARS by 2–5 dB in PSNR. On the KITTI-360 static leaderboard (Table 2), HUGS reaches 23.38 PSNR and 85.64 mIoU_cat for semantic synthesis, surpassing MARS (23.09 PSNR) and PNF (22.07 PSNR, 84.97 mIoU_cat). For 3D semantic reconstruction (Table 3), HUGS achieves 0.233 chamfer accuracy and 0.505 mIoU_cls, dramatically outperforming Semantic Nerfacto (1.508 accuracy, 0.055 mIoU_cls).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUGS outperforms NSG [27] by 2.4–4.8 dB PSNR across all four dynamic scene splits (KITTI and vKITTI), with the largest gap on vKITTI where noisy bounding boxes are more severe (e.g., 26.21 vs 21.40 PSNR on vKITTI Scene02). Against MARS [40], HUGS gains 2.1–3.1 dB PSNR, demonstrating that the unicycle-model-constrained pose optimization is more robust to noisy 3D tracking than MARS's approach. On the KITTI-360 static leaderboard, HUGS edges out MARS by 0.29 dB PSNR (23.38 vs 23.09) and surpasses PNF [19] by 1.31 dB PSNR while achieving comparable semantic mIoU (85.64 vs 84.97 mIoU_cat). For 3D semantic reconstruction, HUGS dramatically outperforms Semantic Nerfacto [34] with 6.5× lower chamfer accuracy (0.233 vs 1.508) and 9× higher mIoU_cls (0.505 vs 0.055), highlighting the advantage of explicit Gaussian-based 3D representation over implicit NeRF density fields for extracting 3D semantics. mip-NeRF [2] is the weakest baseline, trailing HUGS by 1.84 dB PSNR and 18.19 mIoU_cls on KITTI-360, confirming that 3D Gaussian Splatting is a superior representation for urban scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from static scenes to dynamic urban environments by decomposing the scene into static Gaussians and N individually tracked dynamic objects, each parameterized by a unicycle model that enforces physical motion constraints. This departs from prior dynamic scene methods (NSG, MARS) that require ground-truth 3D bounding boxes, and from PNF which performs naive per-frame pose optimization without physical regularization. The paper also extends the Gaussian representation to carry per-Gaussian semantic logits and to model per-camera exposure via an affine transform, unifying appearance, semantics, and optical flow rendering under a single volume-rendering framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring only posed RGB images and noisy off-the-shelf predictions (monocular 3D tracking, 2D segmentation, optical flow), HUGS removes the need for expensive LiDAR or manually annotated 3D boxes, making holistic urban scene understanding far more accessible. The ~93 fps real-time rendering (vs. <1 fps for NeRF-based baselines) and robustness to 20% bounding-box noise make the approach practically viable for autonomous driving simulation. Key limitations include restricted rotational degrees of freedom for dynamic objects and the absence of lighting control; future work on category-level priors and more expressive motion models could address these gaps.
