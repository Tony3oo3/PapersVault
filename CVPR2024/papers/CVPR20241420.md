## Problem

Edge detection suffers from two intertwined data issues: severe class imbalance (only 1–7% of pixels are edges) and label uncertainty from disagreement among multiple human annotators. Existing methods address imbalance with class-balanced cross-entropy or Dice loss and handle uncertainty by simply thresholding to majority-vote pixels, but neither approach is principled or jointly optimized. The paper asks whether a unified ranking-based formulation can simultaneously resolve both problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 320×320 RGB image crops and produces a per-pixel edge probability map (which is post-processed with NMS and skeleton thinning to yield a binary edge map). For multi-label datasets (BSDS500, Multi-cue), it additionally computes a pixel-wise certainty map c ∈ [0,1] by measuring, within a Manhattan-distance tolerance d, how many of the n annotators' annotations cover each candidate edge pixel. The backbone is a Swin-Transformer (base) initialized with ImageNet-22k weights, and the model is trained with batch size 1 for 140k–200k iterations.

## Method

RANKED replaces the standard classification loss with two ranking-based objectives. The first, L_Rank, adapts AP-Loss from object detection: it maximizes a differentiable approximation of Average Precision by ranking positive (edge) pixel scores above negative (non-edge) scores, using a smoothed step function H(·) with a δ-approximation to make the rank counting differentiable. The second, L_Sort, adapts RS-Loss to sort positive pixels by their annotation certainty: it penalizes the model when a high-confidence prediction falls on a low-certainty pixel while a lower-confidence prediction falls on a high-certainty one, effectively prioritizing pixels that most annotators agree on. The overall loss is L_Rank + α·L_Sort (α=0 for single-annotation datasets like NYUDv2). Gradients are computed via an error-driven update mechanism rather than autograd, and the authors provide fully-vectorized implementations that are ~100× faster than the original for-loop-based AP-Loss/RS-Loss code.

## Results

On BSDS500 (multi-scale), RANKED (Ranking + Sorting) achieves 0.911 AP, 0.837 ODS, and 0.855 OIS, setting a new state-of-the-art in AP. On NYUD-v2 (RGB), RANKED (Ranking only) achieves 0.826 AP, 0.780 ODS, and 0.793 OIS, outperforming all prior methods on all three metrics. On the Multi-cue dataset, RANKED (R+S) achieves 0.973 AP for edge detection and 0.995 AP for boundary detection, surpassing the second-best (UAED) by +2.4 and +6.8 AP points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RANKED outperforms all 28 compared baselines on AP across all three datasets, with the sole exception being BSDS500 SS+VOC where it trails UAED by 0.1 AP points (0.901 vs. 0.902). On BSDS500 multi-scale, RANKED (R+S) beats the previous best UAED by 1.4 AP points (0.911 vs. 0.897) and EDTER by 1.5 points. On NYUD-v2, RANKED (Ranking only) surpasses EDTER by 2.9 AP points (0.826 vs. 0.797) and BDCN by 5.6 points. The largest margins appear on Multi-cue: for boundary detection, RANKED (R+S) achieves 0.995 AP versus UAED's 0.927 (+6.8 points) and EDTER's 0.919 (+7.6 points); for edge detection, it reaches 0.973 AP versus UAED's 0.949 (+2.4 points). The sorting component (L_Sort) consistently adds 0.3–1.1 AP points over ranking alone on multi-label datasets (BSDS, Multi-cue), confirming that prioritizing high-certainty edge pixels during training yields measurable gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

RankED adapts two ranking-based losses originally developed for object detection — AP Loss (Chen et al., CVPR 2019) for ranking positives over negatives, and RS Loss (Oksuz et al., ICCV 2021) for sorting positives by quality — to the edge detection setting, where they had not previously been applied. It departs from the standard edge detection loss landscape (class-balanced cross-entropy and Dice Loss) by replacing pixel-wise classification objectives with a unified ranking framework. The paper also diverges from the sole prior uncertainty-aware edge detector (UAED, Zhou et al., CVPR 2023), which modeled label uncertainty via multi-variate Gaussians and weighted high-uncertainty pixels more heavily; RankED instead computes a certainty map from multi-annotator agreement and uses a sorting loss that prioritizes high-certainty pixels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified treatment of class imbalance and label uncertainty in a single loss framework is a clean conceptual contribution, and the uncertainty-aware evaluation protocol (UaR) provides the field with a more informative way to assess edge detectors on multi-annotator datasets. The counterintuitive finding that prioritizing low-uncertainty (high-certainty) pixels outperforms the opposite strategy used by UAED is well-supported by ablations and could reshape how future methods handle annotation disagreement. Limitations include high GPU memory requirements (45 GB for full vectorization), dependence on Swin-Transformer as the backbone, and the small size of the Multi-cue dataset (100 images), which limits generalizability claims. Open questions include whether ranking-based losses can be extended to other evaluation objectives and whether post-processing steps (NMS, thinning) can be folded into the training loop.
