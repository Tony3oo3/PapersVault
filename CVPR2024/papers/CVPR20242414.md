## Problem

Existing NeRF-based multi-sensor calibration methods (e.g., MOISST) fuse all sensor observations into a single implicit scene representation, which can overfit to regions visible from only one sensor and converge to poor local minima without enforcing consistency on overlapping regions. This makes the calibration fragile and prone to divergence, especially in unconstrained outdoor driving scenes where sensor fields of view do not fully overlap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242414/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from multiple cameras (downscaled by 4×–6×), undistorted LiDAR point clouds, a known vehicle trajectory from a reference camera (obtained via IMU, SLAM, or SfM), and initial sensor pose priors perturbed with known noise (50 cm translation, 5° rotation, 100 ms time offset). It produces per-sensor 4×4 extrinsic calibration matrices relative to the reference camera, per-sensor temporal offset scalars, and a set of camera-specific NeRF scene representations. A key constraint is that the reference camera's trajectory must be accurate, and the method requires near scene structures for precise translation recovery.

## Method

SOAC replaces the single shared NeRF with one dedicated NeRF per camera, then alternates between two optimization steps: (1) scene representation training, where each NeRF is trained only on its own camera's rays plus all LiDAR scans, and (2) cross-NeRF calibration, where each sensor's extrinsic and temporal parameters are optimized by comparing its captured frames against the NeRFs of all *other* cameras in a round-robin fashion. A boolean visibility grid, built during scene training from each camera's rays, is used during calibration to filter out rays that fall outside the trained region of each target NeRF, ensuring only overlapping geometry contributes to the loss. LiDARs are calibrated against all camera NeRFs simultaneously since they provide only geometric (depth) information. Additional stabilizing mechanisms include a NeRF delaying schedule (the reference camera's NeRF is trained first, others are staggered by overlap) and sigmoid-bounded correction outputs that confine learned translation to car-scale and time offset to physically plausible ranges.

## Results

On KITTI-360 spatio-temporal calibration (50 cm / 5° / 100 ms initial noise), SOAC achieves a median error of 0.21° rotation, 5.24 cm translation, and 3.95 ms time offset, roughly 10× better than MOISST (2.24° / 56.34 cm / 27.07 ms). For LiDAR/Camera calibration, SOAC reaches 0.3° / 7.8 cm on KITTI-360 and 1.3° / 29.4 cm on Pandaset, outperforming MOISST (0.2° / 10.0 cm and 2.8° / 56.4 cm) and substantially beating the supervised LCCNet (1.9° / 95.8 cm and 14.3° / 370 cm). In dynamic scenes with semantic filtering, SOAC reduces LiDAR calibration error to 0.41° / 7.79 cm versus 2.36° / 79.17 cm without filtering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOAC is compared against three baselines: MOISST (the closest NeRF-based spatio-temporal method), LCCNet (a supervised cost-volume network), and Pandey et al. (a feature-based targetless method). Against MOISST, SOAC wins decisively on all metrics: on KITTI-360 spatio-temporal calibration the median error drops from 2.24°/56.34 cm/27.07 ms to 0.21°/5.24 cm/3.95 ms, and on LiDAR/Camera calibration SOAC achieves 7.8 cm vs. 10.0 cm translation on KITTI-360 and 29.4 cm vs. 56.4 cm on Pandaset. Against LCCNet, SOAC is dramatically better (e.g., 7.8 cm vs. 95.8 cm on KITTI-360), with the paper attributing LCCNet's poor transfer to its supervised, setup-specific training. Pandey et al. could not produce convincing calibration results on the tested sequences, confirming that feature-based targetless methods struggle in unconstrained outdoor driving scenes. The paper also shows that removing the NeRF delaying schedule causes calibration accuracy and stability to plummet on 5-camera nuScenes rigs, and that semantic filtering of dynamic objects is critical for robust calibration in scenes with moving vehicles and pedestrians.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOAC extends the authors' own prior work MOISST, which used a single shared NeRF to jointly optimize all sensor extrinsics and time offsets. The key architectural departure is replacing that single NeRF with one NeRF per camera, coupled with a boolean visibility grid that restricts calibration losses to overlapping regions, and an alternating optimization schedule between scene representation training and sensor co-registration. This positions SOAC within the broader NeRF-based calibration lineage (INF, AsyncNeRF, MOISST) while explicitly addressing the overfitting-to-partial-geometry failure mode that plagues single-NeRF approaches. It also contrasts with supervised deep-learning calibrators (LCCNet, RegNet) and feature-based targetless methods (Pandey et al.) by being fully self-supervised and targetless.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Targetless, self-supervised spatio-temporal calibration is essential for large-scale autonomous driving deployment where hand-placed targets are impractical and on-the-fly re-calibration may be needed. The visibility-grid mechanism offers a principled way to enforce multi-sensor consistency only where observations actually overlap, which is a practical insight applicable beyond this specific architecture. However, the method still requires a reference sensor with a known trajectory (from SLAM or IMU), degrades in open scenes with long ray distances, and training time scales exponentially with the number of cameras, limiting scalability. Open questions include removing the reference-sensor dependency, handling dynamic scenes more gracefully than simple semantic filtering, and whether the multi-NeRF strategy can be made more efficient for 6+ camera rigs.
