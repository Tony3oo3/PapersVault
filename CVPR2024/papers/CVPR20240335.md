## Problem

Existing deep learning point cloud denoising methods either regress geometric features to 3D displacements in Euclidean space (suppressing feature expressiveness and causing surface collapse) or operate in high-dimensional feature space with constrained architectures like affine-coupling normalizing flows that require dimension partitioning and lack sufficient local geometric feature extraction. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a latent-space denoising framework that explicitly disentangles noise into dedicated channels while maintaining an unconstrained, expressive invertible architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240335/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy 3D point cloud (normalized to unit sphere, processed as 1K-point patches via FPS and k-NN) and produces a denoised point cloud of the same cardinality. Unlike displacement-based methods that predict per-point offsets, the output is obtained by zeroing noise-specific channels in the latent code and applying the inverse of the invertible transformation—no separate decoder is required. Supervision uses Earth Mover's Distance to enforce bijective point-to-point matching with ground truth, avoiding the uniformity degradation caused by nearest-point assignment.

## Method

The core architecture combines an invertible neural network (INN) built on invertible monotone operators with a multi-level graph convolution (MLGC) module. The INN enforces a 1-Lipschitz constraint via spectral norm regularization on linear layers, enabling a bijective mapping between the augmented point space (3+D_a dimensions) and a prior latent space where noise components are separated into dedicated channels—no dimension partitioning is needed. The MLGC module uses EdgeConv with dense connections across two hierarchical stages to capture local-to-global geometric structure, and its features are injected additively at each invertible layer to guide noise disentanglement. Dimension augmentation (concatenating learned neighbor features to raw 3D coordinates) maintains a high-dimensional feature space throughout the encoding process. Training uses only an EMD reconstruction loss without Jacobian computation, and the heavy version stacks three denoising modules for iterative refinement.

## Results

On the PUNet benchmark, the heavy version of the proposed method achieves the best CD and P2M across all noise levels and point densities. For 10K points at 2% noise, it reaches CD=24.41 and P2M=7.58 (×10⁵), outperforming the closest competitor IterativePFN (CD=30.43, P2M=8.45) by ~20% in CD. On 50K points at 1% noise, it achieves CD=4.70 and P2M=2.95, again leading all baselines. The method also demonstrates superior uniformity (Table 2), with values roughly 2–5× lower than IterativePFN and PD-flow across all noise scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on both CD and P2M across every noise level and point density tested. The closest competitor, IterativePFN [9], is beaten by 13–20% in CD on 10K points (e.g., 24.41 vs. 30.43 at 2% noise) and by ~15–20% on 50K points (e.g., 6.46 vs. 8.03 at 2% noise). PD-flow [35] and Score [33] perform well at low noise (1%) but degrade sharply at 2.5% noise, where the proposed method maintains a clear margin. DMRDenoise [32] excels on dense 50K point clouds but is the worst performer on sparse 10K clouds (CD=47.12 at 1% noise, worse than the noisy input). PCN [43] and GPDNet [39] are consistently outperformed, with PCN showing almost no improvement over the noisy input on 10K points. The paper attributes its advantage to the bijective EMD-based supervision, which avoids the non-uniform point allocation used by displacement-based methods like IterativePFN and Pointfilter [52].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work on normalizing-flow-based point cloud denoising (PD-flow) by replacing Glow-style affine coupling layers with invertible monotone operators (Ahn et al., 2022), which enforce a global Lipschitz constraint via spectral norm rather than requiring dimension partitioning. It further departs from displacement-based denoising (PCN, GPDNet, IterativePFN) by operating entirely in a high-dimensional latent space where noise is explicitly disentangled into dedicated channels, rather than regressing 3D offsets. The multi-level graph convolution module with dense connections (building on DGCNN/EdgeConv and DensePoint) is integrated directly into the invertible network to supply geometric context, a design choice that distinguishes it from prior flow-based methods that used graph features only as conditioning signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent-space disentanglement framing offers a conceptually clean denoising mechanism—masking noise channels and inverting—without requiring a separate decoder or distributional prior, and the EMD-based supervision addresses a real uniformity deficiency in nearest-point-matching losses that affects many displacement-based methods. However, evaluation is limited to synthetic Gaussian noise on PUNet and a single qualitative real-world scene, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to non-Gaussian artifacts, outliers, or large-scale LiDAR data. The requirement that the invertible network be sufficiently deep to create adequate latent dimensionality for disentanglement (ablation shows 1 layer is insufficient) also raises practical questions about scaling to very large point clouds.
