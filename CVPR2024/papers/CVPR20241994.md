## Problem

Existing LRF-based rotation-invariant methods perturb local geometric relationships because the Gram-Schmidt construction of reference frame axes introduces an asymmetry that breaks local consistency between neighboring points. This inconsistency causes the network to lose relative pose information in intermediate layers, degrading both classification and segmentation accuracy under arbitrary rotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241994/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes raw 3D point cloud coordinates (1,024 points for classification, 2,048 for segmentation) without requiring normals or other auxiliary geometry. It produces a single class label for shape classification or per-point part labels for segmentation. The architecture is a two-branch fusion network: an invariant branch (DGCNN backbone) that builds rotation-invariant features via LCRF, and an equivariant branch (VN-DGCNN backbone) that supplies rotation-equivariant features for both LCRF construction and relative pose recovery, with attention-based fusion at the output.

## Method

LocoTrans introduces two key mechanisms. First, the Local-consistent Reference Frame (LCRF) replaces the asymmetric Gram-Schmidt axis construction with a symmetric definition based on the angular bisector of two equivariant feature vectors, ensuring both axes of the LRF maintain consistent orientation across neighboring points; this is further optimized via an orthogonality loss and a consistency loss that encourage the equivariant features to be orthogonal and locally aligned. Second, the Relative Pose Recovery (RPR) module uses equivariant features (transformed by the LRF) to encode the relative pose between adjacent patches, replacing hand-crafted features like rotation matrices or point pair features. The total training loss combines cross-entropy from three classifiers (invariant, equivariant, and fused branches) with the orthogonality and consistency regularization terms.

## Results

On ModelNet40, LocoTrans achieves 91.6% accuracy under both z/z and z/SO(3) settings and 91.5% under SO(3)/SO(3), outperforming all rotation-invariant baselines. On the real-world ScanObjectNN (OBJ_BG), it reaches 85.0% under z/SO(3), surpassing the next best method (Li et al., 84.3%) by 0.7%. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, LocoTrans attains 84.0% instance mIoU under z/SO(3), the best among all compared methods, and improves over its DGCNN backbone by 42.7% in the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ModelNet40 z/SO(3), LocoTrans (91.6%) edges out the closest rotation-invariant competitor PaRI-Conv (91.4%) by 0.2% and surpasses AECNN (91.0%) and PaRot (91.0%) by 0.6%; the gap widens to 3.2% over OrientedMP (88.4%), the method whose Gram-Schmidt-based LRF the paper directly modifies. On ScanObjectNN z/SO(3), the advantage is more pronounced: LocoTrans (85.0%) beats Li et al. (84.3%) by 0.7%, PaRot (82.1%) by 2.9%, and PaRI-Conv (77.8%) by 7.2%, with the latter's hand-crafted LRF degrading significantly due to background noise sensitivity. On ShapeNetPart z/SO(3), LocoTrans achieves the best instance mIoU (84.0%) over PaRI-Conv (83.8%) and Li et al. (81.7%), while its class mIoU (80.1%) is marginally below AECNN (80.2%). Rotation-sensitive baselines (PointNet, DGCNN) collapse under z/SO(3) to 16–29% accuracy, confirming the necessity of explicit invariance. Rotation-equivariant methods (VN-DGCNN, SVNet-DGCNN) maintain consistent accuracy across settings but remain 1–2% below the best invariant methods due to their constrained linear architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LRF-based rotation invariance line initiated by OrientedMP (Luo et al., CVPR 2022), which uses equivariant features and Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization to construct local reference frames. The key departure is identifying that Gram-Schmidt creates an inherent asymmetry between the two LRF axes, causing local inconsistency that perturbs geometric relationships; LCRF resolves this by defining both axes symmetrically around the angular bisector of equivariant features. For relative pose recovery, the paper replaces the hand-crafted features used by PaRI-Conv and AECNN with equivariant network features, arguing they better encode complex geometric relationships. The overall architecture is a two-branch fusion (invariant DGCNN + equivariant VN-DGCNN) with attention-based merging, a design pattern borrowed from feature fusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local-consistency insight addresses a real weakness in LRF-based methods that prior work acknowledged but did not systematically fix, and the two-step optimization (orthogonality loss + consistency loss) provides a clean, interpretable formulation. However, the practical impact is tempered by the significant computational overhead: the two-branch design roughly triples parameters (6.27M vs. 1.85M for PaRI-Conv) and FLOPs (7998M vs. 1938M), which the authors themselves flag as a limitation. The modest accuracy gains over the strongest baselines (0.2–0.6% on ModelNet40) and the 0.5% sensitivity to training augmentation on ScanObjectNN suggest the approach is not yet a drop-in replacement. An open question is whether the equivariant branch can be distilled or replaced by a lighter mechanism to retain the LCRF/RPR benefits without the cost penalty.
