## Problem

Existing video tracking methods face a fundamental trade-off: dense optical flow estimators provide spatially complete tracking but only over short temporal spans, while long-range point trackers (e.g., TAPIR, CoTracker) extend temporal coverage at the cost of spatial sparsity or prohibitive per-point computation. The paper asks whether off-the-shelf dense optical flow can be extended to long-range tracking by selectively chaining confident flow predictions and compensating for errors when they arise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241668/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of T frames (evaluated at 256×256 or 384×512) and densely sampled query points in the first frame. Forward and backward optical flow between all adjacent frame pairs is pre-computed once using a modified RAFT with 4-stride feature maps, after which per-point trajectory extraction is nearly free. The output is a per-point trajectory (2D position per frame) and a per-frame visibility probability indicating occlusion status, with a temporal window of 8 frames and motion sampling stride of 8 governing the internal processing granularity.

## Method

FlowTrack alternates between two stages: (1) flow chaining, which sequentially accumulates adjacent optical flow displacements and filters confident predictions via cycle-consistency and feature-matching thresholds, and (2) an error compensation module that corrects unconfident predictions. The error compensation module is a Transformer decoder that iteratively refines positions using a local correlation map (analogous to RAFT's cost volume) over a temporal window, with K=4 refinement iterations. The key insight is that confident trajectories extracted from optical flow serve as a spatio-temporal motion prior: a motion encoder (alternating spatial and temporal Transformer layers) processes these trajectories to capture co-movement structure and spatial smoothness, and the resulting embeddings are injected into the error compensation module via cross-attention. This allows the model to infer motion in occluded or textureless regions by referencing nearby confident motion, maintaining spatial coherence without per-point exhaustive search.

## Results

On the primary TAPVid-DAVIS benchmark (256×256), FlowTrack achieves AJ 63.2, <δx_avg 76.3, OA 89.2, and TC 0.53, outperforming TAPIR (AJ 61.3, TC 1.01) and OmniMotion (AJ 51.7, TC 0.74) while matching or exceeding their occlusion accuracy. At 384×512 resolution, FlowTrack reaches AJ 66.0 and TC 0.50 on DAVIS, surpassing CoTracker (AJ 64.8, TC 0.92). On RGB-Stacking, FlowTrack attains AJ 66.2 and OA 94.3, beating TAPIR (AJ 62.7) and TAP-Net (AJ 59.9), though OmniMotion remains stronger (AJ 77.5). In dense tracking (10⁴ points), FlowTrack completes inference in 19.8 s versus 28.1 s for TAPIR, and >36,000 s for PIPs, CoTracker, and OmniMotion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowTrack outperforms TAPIR [6] on DAVIS by +1.9 AJ and nearly halves the temporal coherence error (0.53 vs 1.01 TC), while TAPIR retains a slight edge on Kinetics (AJ 57.2 vs 56.4), likely due to scene cuts disrupting optical-flow-based motion priors. Against CoTracker [20] at 384×512, FlowTrack gains +1.2 AJ and improves TC from 0.92 to 0.50, while also being dramatically faster in dense settings (19.8 s vs >36,000 s for 10⁴ points). On RGB-Stacking, FlowTrack surpasses TAPIR (+3.5 AJ) and TAP-Net (+6.3 AJ) but trails OmniMotion [41] by 11.3 AJ, which the authors attribute to the static camera easing dynamic neural field optimization. Compared to the raw RAFT [37] baseline, FlowTrack more than doubles AJ on DAVIS (63.2 vs 30.0), demonstrating the substantial contribution of the error compensation module. PIPs [11] and COTR [18] are significantly outperformed across all DAVIS metrics, with FlowTrack achieving +21.2 AJ over PIPs and +27.8 AJ over COTR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant point-tracking paradigm (TAPIR, CoTracker, PIPs) that processes each query point independently across the full video, and instead revisits dense optical flow (RAFT) as the primary tracking mechanism. The key conceptual bridge is chaining short-range flow predictions for efficiency while introducing a learned error compensation module—conditioned on spatio-temporal motion priors extracted from confident flow chains—that activates only when cycle-consistency or feature-matching checks flag unreliable predictions. This positions the work between classical optical flow (which handles two-frame displacement) and modern long-range trackers (which handle arbitrary temporal spans but at per-point cost), and is complementary to multi-frame flow methods that could further improve the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~30× speed advantage over TAPIR for dense tracking (65k+ points) makes long-range dense tracking practically viable for applications like edit propagation and pose estimation, where prior methods were prohibitively slow. The finding that optical flow's inherent spatial smoothness yields superior temporal coherence (TC 0.50 vs. 0.92 for CoTracker) suggests that flow-based methods carry a structural advantage for smooth trajectory estimation that point-tracking architectures lack. However, the method's reliance on optical flow makes it vulnerable to scene cuts and disruptive effects (Kinetics results lag TAPIR), and the error compensation module's effectiveness on heavily occluded or textureless regions remains an open question. Integrating multi-frame flow estimators or adapting the framework to handle scene transitions would be natural next steps.
