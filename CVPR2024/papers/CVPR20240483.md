## Problem

Existing 3D-to-2D projection strategies for indoor point cloud segmentation assume a homogeneous medium, causing projection rays to travel in straight lines and permanently occluding points behind front-facing objects. In densely populated indoor scenes, this results in severe semantic occlusion and sparse, incoherent projection images that lack sufficient information for fine-grained point-level feature learning from 2D pre-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240483/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a raw 3D point cloud with per-point RGB color as input—no additional 2D images, camera poses, or extrinsic annotations are needed. Internally, it generates 12 synthetic projection images (4 view directions × 3 mirage parameters κ∈{0,1,2}) at a fixed resolution, processes them through a frozen Swin-Tiny backbone, and outputs per-point semantic class predictions across 13 categories (S3DIS) or 20 categories (ScanNet V2).

## Method

The core contribution is a parametric 'mirage projection' that models the 3D space as a heterogeneous medium with a height-dependent refraction rate n(z)=1+ρ₀e^(−kz), causing projection rays to follow parabolic trajectories rather than straight lines. A single scalar parameter κ controls the ray curvature, and by sweeping κ over a group of values across 4 orthogonal view directions, previously occluded points are progressively exposed, increasing point occupancy from ~20% to ~35% on S3DIS. A frozen Swin-Tiny (ImageNet-pretrained) backbone with an FPN extracts multi-scale 2D features from the 12 projection images. A masked reprojection module then performs 2D-to-3D cross-attention: each 3D point queries its corresponding 2D pixel neighbourhood (k=5 neighbours) across all 12 images, with a binary mask zeroing out contributions from images where the point is occluded or on the wrong side of the projection plane. The overall architecture is a U-Net-like encoder-decoder with three encoder layers (channels 96/192/384) that progressively downsample 3D points while fusing multi-scale 2D features via the masked cross-attention, totaling only 5.8M learnable parameters.

## Results

On S3DIS Area 5, MirageRoom achieves 91.3% OA, 78.2% mAcc, and 72.0% mIoU, surpassing the previous best PTv2 (91.1% OA, 77.9% mAcc, 71.6% mIoU) across all three metrics. On ScanNet V2 validation, MirageRoom reaches 74.9% mIoU with only 5.8M learnable parameters, outperforming StraFormer (74.3%, 8.0M) and matching PTv2 (75.4%, 12.8M) with roughly 60% fewer parameters. Notably, MirageRoom uses only 3D point cloud input (no additional 2D images or camera poses) yet outperforms the 2D+3D method BPNet (73.9% mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On S3DIS, MirageRoom (72.0% mIoU) outperforms all 17 compared methods, with the closest competitor being PTv2 (71.6% mIoU) by a 0.4-point margin; it also beats PMeta (71.3%), PointNeXt (70.5%), and PTv1 (70.4%). On ScanNet V2, MirageRoom (74.9% mIoU, 5.8M params) surpasses StraFormer (74.3%, 8.0M) and all other 3D-only methods except PTv2 (75.4%, 12.8M), which edges it out by 0.5 points but requires more than double the parameters. Against 2D+3D methods that require additional real-world images and camera poses, MirageRoom (74.9%) beats BPNet (73.9%) and MVPNet (65.0%) while using only 3D point cloud input. The ablation baseline (MirageRoom w/o 2D model, 70.4% mIoU) confirms that the 2D pre-trained Swin Transformer backbone contributes a +1.6 mIoU gain, validating the mirage projection's role in enabling effective 2D-to-3D feature transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D-to-2D projection paradigm for leveraging 2D pre-trained models in point cloud segmentation, departing from prior straight-line projection methods (RangeNet++, PointCLIP, MVTN) that suffer from severe occlusion in dense indoor scenes. Rather than changing viewpoints or requiring additional 2D images with camera poses (as in MVPNet, BPNet, 3DMV), it introduces a physically-motivated 'mirage projection' that curves projection rays through a heterogeneous medium, parameterized by a single coefficient κ that generalizes straight-line projection (κ=0). The masked reprojection module with 2D-to-3D cross-attention is a new mechanism for precise feature transfer, complementing the projection strategy within a U-Net-like architecture built on a frozen Swin Transformer backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mirage projection offers an elegant, single-parameter solution to the occlusion bottleneck that has limited 3D-to-2D projection methods in indoor scenes, with occupancy improvements of 54–76% over straight-line projection. However, the final segmentation gains are modest (1.6 mIoU on S3DIS, 0.5 mIoU over PTv2 on ScanNet), and the authors acknowledge that complete occlusion resolution remains non-trivial. The physical analogy, while creative, reduces to a simple quadratic z-offset in practice, raising questions about whether more complex ray-tracing or learned distortion functions could yield further gains. The approach is limited to indoor scenes with axis-aligned projection planes and may not generalize to outdoor or arbitrary-geometry settings.
