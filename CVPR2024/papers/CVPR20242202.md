## Problem

Existing large-scale annotated perception datasets are biased toward autonomous driving scenarios, rely on costly classical rotating lidars, and are carefully curated to mask real-world issues like motion distortion and extreme lighting. Meanwhile, the emerging low-cost NRE (Non-Repetitive Epicyclic) lidar modality remains largely unexplored in semantic segmentation research, and prior datasets provide only discrete-time ground truth with limited accuracy due to ICP matching failures. MCD addresses these gaps by introducing a diverse multi-campus dataset with NRE lidar annotations and a novel continuous-time ground truth.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 18 sequences (6 per campus across NTU, KTH, and TUHH) containing over 200k lidar scans (both CCS and NRE), 1500k camera frames, high-frequency IMU, and UWB range data. The key outputs are 29-class point-wise semantic annotations on 59k sparse NRE lidar scans and a continuous-time ground truth trajectory parameterized by B-splines, which allows arbitrary time and density sampling to directly undistort raw lidar point clouds—unlike prior datasets that only offer discrete keyframe poses or pre-processed scans.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a continuous-time ground truth generation pipeline that directly registers raw (undistorted) lidar-inertial data against survey-grade prior maps via a factor-graph optimization. The trajectory is parameterized as a B-spline on Lie groups, and the cost function jointly optimizes pose priors, point-to-plane lidar-to-map matching, and IMU gyro/accelerometer factors, explicitly modeling the deskew process during optimization rather than relying on a separate SLAM step. This avoids the observational bias that propagates from imperfect SLAM-based undistortion in prior work (e.g., NCD, Hilti-SLAM-Challenge). The paper further benchmarks nine SOTA SLAM/odometry methods and seven 3D semantic segmentation architectures on MCD, revealing that no single method consistently excels and that cross-campus domain shift causes over 70% relative mIoU degradation.

## Results

On the MCD NTU sequences (same-domain evaluation), S-Former achieves the best mIoU of 59.0% for 3D semantic segmentation on NRE lidar, followed by WaffleIron (50.2%) and SPVCNN (48.2%); all methods suffer a relative mIoU drop of 21–44% compared to their SemanticKITTI performance. For lidar-inertial SLAM, SLICT is the most consistent method with ATE below 2m on 15/18 sequences, while LIO-SAM ranges from 0.103m to 40.566m depending on sequence. Cross-campus transfer to MCD TUHH causes a catastrophic collapse: all segmentation methods lose over 67% relative mIoU (S-Former drops from 59.0% to 13.8%), and visual-inertial SLAM methods diverge or exceed 20m ATE on most nighttime sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For lidar-inertial SLAM, SLICT [28] outperforms LIO-SAM [36], Fast-LIO [46], and DLIO [7] on the majority of sequences, achieving the lowest ATE on 12/18 sequences (e.g., 0.084m on tuhh_day_04 vs. 0.077m for Fast-LIO, 0.232m for DLIO, 0.142m for LIO-SAM); LIO-SAM is highly inconsistent, excelling on slow HHS sequences (0.103–0.524m) but diverging or exceeding 12m on high-speed ATV and some nighttime sequences. Among visual-inertial methods, OpenVINS [15] is the most robust (completes all daytime sequences), while VINS [33] and KIMERA [35] diverge or exceed 20m ATE on most sequences, particularly at night. For range-aided localization, VRO [26] substantially outperforms RO [42] (e.g., 0.903m vs. 6.599m on tuhh_day_04), benefiting from visual factors when UWB line-of-sight is lost. In semantic segmentation, S-Former [19] and WaffleIron [32] are the only methods exceeding 50% mIoU on NRE lidar, while cylindrical-representation methods (Cylinder3D [48]) and projection-based methods (SalsaNext [10]) suffer the largest drops (−37% and −44% relative) due to mismatched point cloud geometry. Cross-campus transfer to TUHH is catastrophic for all methods, with SalsaNext [10] dropping to 5.8% mIoU and even S-Former [19] falling to 13.8%, confirming severe domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCD extends the dataset construction paradigm from autonomous-driving-centric benchmarks (KITTI, SemanticKITTI, NuScenes) to multi-campus environments with diverse sensing modalities, and builds directly on the Newer College Dataset's use of survey-grade prior maps for ground truth. However, it departs from NCD's discrete ICP-based registration by introducing a continuous-time B-spline optimization framework that jointly fuses lidar, IMU, and pose priors, eliminating the observational bias from imperfect SLAM preprocessing. On the perception side, the paper repurposes existing 3D segmentation architectures (SalsaNext, SPVCNN, WaffleIron, S-Former, etc.) and SLAM methods (LIO-SAM, Fast-LIO, SLICT, VINS, KIMERA) as baselines, but applies them to a previously untested modality—sparse NRE (Livox) lidar—rather than the dense CCS point clouds these methods were designed for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset exposes a critical gap: existing 3D segmentation methods suffer >25% relative mIoU drops simply from switching CCS to NRE lidar, and >70% drops when generalizing across campuses, revealing that domain adaptation for 3D point cloud segmentation is far harder than previously appreciated. The continuous-time ground truth, derived from survey-grade prior maps several times larger than prior public maps, sets a new accuracy standard for SLAM evaluation and enables direct motion undistortion at arbitrary time resolution. Open questions include whether the cross-campus domain gap can be closed with domain adaptation or test-time adaptation techniques, how to design architectures robust to NRE lidar's irregular sparsity, and whether the real-world noise classes (sun glare, glass reflection) that MCD explicitly preserves can be handled without sacrificing clean-scene performance.
