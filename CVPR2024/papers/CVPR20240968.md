## Problem

In text-video retrieval, text captions are typically short and concise, making a single deterministic text embedding insufficient to capture the redundant and diverse semantics present in a paired video. This asymmetry creates an alignment gap in the joint embedding space where the text point cannot adequately bridge to the richer video representation, limiting retrieval accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240968/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (short caption, subtitle, or hashtag) and a video clip (12 uniformly sampled frames resized to 224×224) as input, processed through a CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 backbone producing 512-dimensional embeddings. At inference, the method draws M=20 stochastic samples from the learned text mass and selects the sample with the highest cosine similarity to the video embedding as the final text representation for ranking. Output is a retrieval ranking evaluated via R@1, R@5, R@10, MdR, and MnR on both text-to-video and video-to-text directions.

## Method

T-MASS replaces the single-point text embedding with a stochastic 'text mass' via reparameterization: t_s = t + R·ε where ε ~ N(0,1) and R is a learned radius vector. A similarity-aware radius module computes R = exp(SW) from per-frame text-video cosine similarities S, making the mass scale adaptive to each text-video pair. Training uses a stochastic symmetric cross-entropy loss L_s computed between sampled t_s and video v, plus a support text regularization L_sup that anchors a proxy vector t_sup = t + (v−t)/|v−t|·R on the mass surface to jointly control position and scale. The total loss is L_total = L_s + α·L_sup (α=1.2), and the original deterministic text loss L_ce is deliberately omitted to avoid biased mass learning. At inference, the best of M stochastic samples is selected per video candidate, making the text embedding adaptive to the specific video being matched.

## Results

T-MASS achieves state-of-the-art text-to-video retrieval on five benchmarks (MSRVTT, LSMDC, DiDeMo, VATEX, Charades) using CLIP ViT-B/32 and ViT-B/16 backbones. On MSRVTT (ViT-B/32), T-MASS reaches R@1 = 50.2, R@5 = 75.3, R@10 = 85.1, improving over the X-Pool baseline (R@1 = 46.9) by 3.3%. On DiDeMo (ViT-B/16), it achieves R@1 = 53.3, a 6.0% gain over X-Pool (47.3). On Charades (ViT-B/16), T-MASS attains R@1 = 26.7 versus X-Pool's 20.7, a 6.0% improvement. Overall, T-MASS improves the baseline by 3%–6.3% at R@1 across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-MASS outperforms the X-Pool baseline on all five datasets and both CLIP backbones, with R@1 gains ranging from +3.3% (MSRVTT B/32: 50.2 vs 46.9) to +6.0% (DiDeMo B/16: 53.3 vs 47.3; Charades B/16: 26.7 vs 20.7). Against CLIP-ViP, T-MASS wins on LSMDC (28.9 vs 25.6, B/32; 30.3 vs 29.4, B/16), DiDeMo (50.9 vs 48.6, B/32; 53.3 vs 50.5, B/16), and VATEX (63.0 vs –, B/32; 65.6 vs –, B/16), but loses on MSRVTT ViT-B/16 (52.7 vs 54.2), which the authors attribute to CLIP-ViP's use of additional post-pretraining data (WebVid-2.5M, HD-VILA-100M). T-MASS also surpasses UATVR on all reported metrics (e.g., MSRVTT B/32: 50.2 vs 47.5; VATEX B/16: 65.6 vs 64.5), DiffusionRet (MSRVTT B/32: 50.2 vs 49.0; DiDeMo B/32: 50.9 vs 46.7), and TEFAL (MSRVTT B/32: 50.2 vs 49.4; LSMDC B/32: 28.9 vs 26.8). On video-to-text retrieval (MSRVTT), T-MASS achieves R@1 = 47.7 (B/32) and 50.9 (B/16), outperforming UATVR (46.9 / 48.1), TS2-Net (45.3 / 46.6), and CenterCLIP (42.8 / 47.7). On Charades, T-MASS improves over CLIP4Clip by +4.3% (B/32: 14.2 vs 9.9) and +10.7% (B/16: 26.7 vs 16.0) at R@1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on X-Pool [17], retaining its CLIP backbone and cross-modal feature fusion module ψ, but departs fundamentally from the prevailing focus on video-side improvements (temporal modeling, fine-grained alignment, post-pretraining with additional data). Instead, it repurposes the reparameterization trick from variational autoencoders [27] to model text as a stochastic 'mass' rather than a single point, introducing a similarity-aware radius module and a support text vector regularization to control the mass's scale and position. This positions T-MASS as a text-side complement to the extensive video-side literature, arguing that the expressiveness bottleneck lies in the text embedding rather than the video representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that short text captions are inherently less expressive than their paired videos, and that a single deterministic embedding cannot bridge this gap—reframes a problem the field has largely addressed from the video side. The practical implication is significant: T-MASS achieves state-of-the-art results on five benchmarks without requiring additional multi-modal data or post-pretraining, making it a lightweight drop-in improvement over existing CLIP-based pipelines. Limitations include one scenario (MSRVTT with ViT-B/16) where CLIP-ViP's data-augmented post-pretraining still prevails, and the inference cost scales linearly with the number of stochastic samples M. Open questions include whether the text-mass concept generalizes to other asymmetric modality pairs (e.g., audio-video) and whether the radius module's design choices transfer across domains.
