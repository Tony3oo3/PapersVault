## Problem

NeRF-based novel view synthesis degrades severely under sparse supervision, and this problem is particularly acute in aerial scenes where UAVs face energy and perspective constraints that limit view acquisition. Existing few-shot NeRF methods are designed for indoor or synthetic scenes and do not exploit the intrinsic geometric regularities of aerial imagery—namely, predominantly overhead perspectives aligned with planar surfaces and consistent geometric appearance across the landscape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242501/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3 or 5 sparse aerial RGB images along with their camera intrinsics and relative poses, and produces photorealistic novel view renderings from previously unseen viewpoints. During training, the MPI branch processes source-target image pairs to generate 16 discrete depth planes per frustum, while the NeRF branch is additionally supervised at randomly sampled pixels from a third unseen view. The SwinV2 Transformer feature extractor is frozen and operates on the source image to provide multiscale features that are fused into the MPI representation.

## Method

MPNeRF couples a standard NeRF branch with an MPI branch that uses a frozen SwinV2 Transformer (pre-trained via SimMIM) as a feature extractor feeding an encoder-decoder MPI generator. The key training mechanism is a multiplane prior loss (L_Mul) that matches NeRF's rendered colors to the MPI branch's homography-warped renderings at randomly sampled pixels from an unseen viewpoint, implicitly distilling the MPI's perspective-friendly inductive biases into NeRF without requiring accurate depth or explicit geometric supervision. The NeRF branch is optimized with standard photometric MSE plus the multiplane prior loss, while the MPI branch is trained with L1, SSIM, and LPIPS losses on source-target pairs. Ablations confirm that the concurrent MPI branch, pre-trained SwinV2 weights, and multi-scale feature connections are each necessary, and that simpler alternatives like depth matching or KL-divergence ray matching underperform the straightforward color-matching prior.

## Results

On the LEVIR-NVS dataset with 3 training views, MPNeRF achieves a mean PSNR of 21.72 dB, SSIM of 0.80, and LPIPS of 0.19, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, FreeNeRF, reaches only 15.54 dB PSNR, 0.27 SSIM, and 0.60 LPIPS — meaning MPNeRF triples SSIM and reduces LPIPS by roughly 3×. With 5 training views, MPNeRF further improves to 22.65 dB PSNR, 0.84 SSIM, and 0.19 LPIPS, while the best baseline (FreeNeRF) reaches 19.04 dB PSNR and 0.44 SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPNeRF dominates all seven baselines across every metric in both the 3-view and 5-view settings. The strongest baseline is FreeNeRF (15.54 dB PSNR, 0.27 SSIM, 0.60 LPIPS in 3 views), which MPNeRF surpasses by +6.2 dB PSNR, +0.53 SSIM, and −0.41 LPIPS. DietNeRF (15.71 dB, 0.24 SSIM) and PixelNeRF with fine-tuning (15.21 dB, 0.29 SSIM) are the next closest but remain far behind. PixelNeRF without fine-tuning collapses catastrophically (7.23 dB PSNR, 0.01 SSIM), confirming the domain gap between DTU-pretrained features and aerial imagery. Dense-view methods (NeRF, Mip-NeRF) and regularization-based methods (InfoNeRF, RegNeRF) all cluster around 13–15 dB PSNR and ~0.18–0.20 SSIM, indicating that generic few-shot strategies fail to capture the geometric regularity of aerial scenes. The paper attributes MPNeRF's large margin to the multiplane prior's ability to preserve high-frequency local structure (via the SwinV2 encoder) while avoiding the collapse and floater artifacts that plague pure NeRF under sparse supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NeRF framework for continuous volumetric scene representation and the Multiplane Image (MPI) paradigm for discrete, frustum-confined layered geometry, combining them into a dual-branch architecture where an MPI generator (backed by a frozen SimMIM-pretrained SwinV2 Transformer) produces pseudo-labels that guide NeRF training via a simple color-matching loss on unseen views. It departs from prior few-shot NeRF methods (DietNeRF, InfoNeRF, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF) that impose generic 3D regularizations (sparsity, smoothness, frequency) by instead exploiting domain-specific geometric regularities of aerial imagery—predominant planarity and aligned overhead perspectives—that make MPI's planar decomposition a natural inductive bias. The approach also differs from pure MPI-based view synthesis (e.g., LEVIR-NVS) by using MPI only as a training-time prior rather than the final rendering representation, thereby retaining NeRF's continuous 3D modeling capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first method purpose-built for few-shot aerial scene rendering, MPNeRF demonstrates that domain-specific geometric priors can dramatically outperform generic few-shot regularizations when the scene class has strong structural regularity, with SSIM jumping from ~0.27 (best baseline) to 0.80 and LPIPS from ~0.57 to 0.19 on LEVIR-NVS. The 63.5% data-efficiency gain has direct practical implications for UAV operations constrained by energy and flight time. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset of 16 scenes, all baselines are non-aerial methods (making the large gap partly a domain-mismatch artifact), and the authors acknowledge that the guiding strategy design is underexplored—uncertainty-aware priors and grid-based representations are flagged as open directions.
