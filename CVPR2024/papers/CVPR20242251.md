## Problem

Open-vocabulary recognition requires the model to recognize categories it has never seen during training. Existing approaches either fine-tune on few-shot data (time-consuming, degrades generalization) or rely on a single modality—textual descriptions that are ambiguous and lack visual detail, or exemplar images of inconsistent quality affected by domain gaps and cluttered backgrounds. No prior method robustly combines both textual and visual exemplar cues while adapting to the variable quality of each modality per category.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each novel category, the method takes a textual description (e.g., a category name or phrase) and M exemplar images (typically 16-shot, randomly crawled from the Internet or drawn from the dataset), plus a target image to classify. The exemplar images serve a dual role: they provide visual cues for classifier generation and act as a held-out validation set for the preference-based fusion step. In the detection variant, the same multi-modal classifiers replace the standard classification head in a CenterNet2 detector, producing bounding boxes with class labels on LVIS rare classes.

## Method

OVMR consists of two modules. First, a lightweight visual token generator—comprising P class-agnostic learnable queries and four transformer blocks with global self-attention—extracts visual tokens from exemplar images; these tokens are concatenated with textual tokens and passed through CLIP's frozen language encoder to produce a multi-modal classifier weight vector. The visual token generator is pre-trained on ImageNet21k-OVR (18,631 categories, 1.1M images) using a strategy that randomly splits K-shot images into non-overlapping exemplar and target subsets with random path and channel dropout to ensure generalizability. Second, a parameter-free preference-based fusion module evaluates the text-based, vision-based, and multi-modal classifiers on the exemplar images (using F1 score), converts the scores into softmax weights with a temperature parameter, and linearly combines the three classifiers. This two-stage design—adaptive multi-modal fusion followed by performance-weighted ensemble—allows OVMR to degrade gracefully when either modality is low quality, without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On 11 open-vocabulary classification datasets, OVMR achieves 82.34% average accuracy without any extra training, matching CoOp (82.69%) which requires per-dataset fine-tuning, and surpassing CoCoOp (80.47%) and MaPLe (82.28%). When integrated with CoOp's prompt tokens (OVMR†), it reaches 83.72% average, exceeding CoOp by 1.03%. On LVIS open-vocabulary detection with Swin-B backbone, OVMR achieves 34.4% mask AP_r on rare classes, outperforming Detic (33.8%) by 0.6% and DITO (32.5%) by 1.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the prompt learning setup, OVMR (82.34% avg) matches CoOp (82.69%) without any per-dataset fine-tuning, while clearly outperforming CoCoOp (80.47%) by 1.87% and MaPLe (82.28%) by 0.06%. In the few-shot setup, OVMR surpasses the best training-based method URL (82.13%) by 0.21% and the training-free PN (77.32%) by 5.02%, with a particularly large 11.0% gain over PN on ImageNet. For open-vocabulary detection on LVIS, OVMR with ResNet-50 outperforms MM-OVOD by 1.9% mask AP_r (21.2 vs. 19.3) using the same 5-shot exemplar images, and with Swin-B backbone it exceeds Detic (33.8%) by 0.6% and DITO (32.5%) by 1.9% on mask AP_r. OVMR also outperforms MQ-Det's text-guided fusion in both classification (82.34% vs. 62.60% multi-modal) and detection (21.2 vs. 15.4 mask AP_r), demonstrating that dynamic preference-based fusion is substantially more robust than static or text-prioritized fusion strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

OVMR extends the multi-modal classifier paradigm introduced by MM-OVOD, which first showed that exemplar images can complement text-based classifiers in open-vocabulary detection. However, MM-OVOD uses a static arithmetic mean to fuse vision and text classifiers, while MQ-Det assumes text is the dominant modality and uses it as a query. OVMR departs from both by introducing a lightweight visual token generator (pre-trained on ImageNet21k-OVR) that embeds exemplar images into the language encoder's space, and a parameter-free preference-based fusion that dynamically weights text, vision, and multi-modal classifiers based on their measured performance on the exemplars themselves. The detector backbone and training recipe follow Detic's CenterNet2 architecture, and the base model is CLIP's ViT-B/16.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free-at-inference nature of OVMR is practically significant: it matches or exceeds fine-tuned prompt learning methods (CoOp, MaPLe) without any task-specific optimization, and works with uncurated internet-crawled exemplar images. The preference-based fusion addresses a real deployment concern—low-quality exemplars or ambiguous text descriptions—by adaptively down-weighting unreliable modalities per category. Limitations include a small performance drop in very low-shot (2-shot) settings where preference estimation is noisy, and the visual token generator still requires a one-time pre-training phase (30 epochs on a single GPU). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, 3D, or other modalities, and whether the F1-based preference metric is optimal across all task types.
