## Problem

Existing 3D style transfer methods for radiance fields focus almost exclusively on transferring colors and textures, leaving geometric parameters unchanged. While shape and geometric patterns are essential to stylistic identity in art, prior approaches either ignore geometry entirely or adjust density without proper guidance from the style image, failing to reflect geometric cues from the style reference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241550/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained TensoRF radiance field (with color grid G_c and density grid G_σ) and an RGB-D style pair, where the depth map is derived from the RGB style image via a zero-shot depth estimator (Zoedepth) and replicated to 3 channels for VGG feature extraction. The output is a stylized radiance field with a new deformation grid G_Δ and updated appearance grid G_c', while the density grid remains fixed, enabling coherent rendering of both modified geometry and appearance from arbitrary viewpoints. The method also integrates with Panoptic Lifting to support partial, class-specific stylization at runtime.

## Method

The core insight is that directly optimizing the density grid to match a depth style map causes color-geometry misalignment, since appearance values remain tied to the original surface. To resolve this, the paper introduces a deformation grid G_Δ that predicts a 3D displacement vector for each point, mapping stylized positions back to canonical (original) surface locations for sampling both color and density, thus preserving appearance coherence. For joint RGB-D stylization, a geometry-aware nearest-neighbor matching loss concatenates VGG features from both modalities to find correspondences, then computes cosine-distance style loss separately per modality. A patch-wise optimization scheme partitions feature maps into k×k patches to capture spatial context and preserve local geometric structure. Finally, a perspective style augmentation strategy downsamples the style image at multiple scales corresponding to depth bins, mapping larger patterns to closer surfaces and smaller ones to distant surfaces to enhance depth perception.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset (trex and fern scenes), the method achieves the lowest SIFID across all three evaluation metrics (RGB, Grayscale, Depth) for both scenes. On fern, the headline SIFID scores are 0.81 (RGB), 0.37 (Gray), and 0.28 (Depth), outperforming the best baseline ARF by 0.30, 0.11, and 0.08 respectively. On trex, the scores are 1.43 (RGB), 0.58 (Gray), and 0.44 (Depth). In a user study with 22 participants across 12 scenes, the method achieves the best average ranking of 1.55 (lower is better) and is selected as the best in 162 out of 264 total responses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all three baselines on every SIFID metric for both scenes. Against ARF (the strongest baseline), the gains are most pronounced on the fern scene: 0.81 vs. 1.11 SIFID on RGB (a 27% relative improvement) and 0.28 vs. 0.36 on Depth. Against SNeRF, which updates density but lacks geometry-style guidance, the method improves fern RGB SIFID from 1.32 to 0.81. Ref-NPR, which relies on a single reference stylized view, performs worst on fern RGB (1.75) and is beaten by a large margin. In the user study, the method's average rank of 1.55 substantially surpasses ARF (2.58), SNeRF (2.70), and Ref-NPR (3.17), confirming that geometry-aware stylization is perceived as more visually appealing. The qualitative results further show that only this method reproduces complete, well-formed style patterns on narrow structures (e.g., fern leaves), which requires actual shape deformation rather than color-only transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ARF [83] framework for stylizing radiance fields via nearest-neighbor matching loss, but introduces a fundamentally new style dimension: geometry. Where prior 3D style transfer methods (SNeRF, ARF, Ref-NPR) treat geometry as fixed and transfer only color/texture, this work uses a depth map as a style guide to deform the scene's shape, borrowing the deformation-field concept from D-NeRF [61] to keep appearance and shape coherent during optimization. The underlying representation is TensoRF [6], and the partial-stylization application integrates with Panoptic Lifting [68], but the core contribution is the geometry-aware matching, patch-wise optimization, and perspective style augmentation that make depth-based style transfer practical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing depth maps as a valid style guide and showing that geometric deformation can be coherently coupled with appearance in radiance fields, this work opens a new axis of control for 3D stylization that was previously unexplored. The deformation-field trick (keeping the canonical surface intact while sampling from it) is a clean, generalizable solution to the shape-appearance misalignment problem that would arise in any geometry-modifying NeRF pipeline. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to bounded scenes by TensoRF, relies on a single style image (making 360° stylization ill-posed), and the depth estimation from RGB style images introduces potential noise. Future work on multi-view or 3D style guides and unbounded scene representations would address these gaps.
