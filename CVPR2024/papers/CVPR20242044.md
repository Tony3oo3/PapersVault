## Problem

Descriptor-free 2D-3D matching avoids the memory, maintenance, and privacy costs of storing visual descriptors, but existing methods like GoMatch rely solely on local geometric cues from points and their immediate neighbors, making them unable to distinguish geometrically similar structures. This results in a significant performance gap compared to descriptor-based matchers. The paper asks whether color information and global geometric context can be exploited to close this gap while retaining the privacy and efficiency advantages of descriptor-free approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242044/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes up to 1024 SIFT or SuperPoint keypoints with their per-pixel RGB values from a query image, and 3D points with RGB colors from the top-k retrieved database views (k=1 or 10). Camera intrinsics are required to up-lift 2D pixels into bearing vectors, which unifies both 2D and 3D points in a shared camera-coordinate space. The output is a set of 2D-3D correspondences (matched keypoint-to-point pairs) that can be fed into PnP-RANSAC for 6-DoF camera pose estimation.

## Method

DGC-GNN is a global-to-local GNN pipeline with three stages. First, two ResNet-style encoders separately embed bearing vectors (position) and RGB colors per point, which are summed into local features. Second, points are clustered into X=10 groups, and a GNN on cluster centers encodes both Euclidean distance (k-NN edges with max-pooling) and angular relations (sinusoidal embeddings on triplets) to produce rotation-invariant global geometric embeddings. Third, local and global features are concatenated and processed by a two-level hierarchical cluster-based attention module (inspired by ClusterGNN) that restricts message passing within correlated groups, followed by Sinkhorn optimal transport for initial matching and a classification network for outlier rejection. The key insight is that incorporating per-point color (which preserves privacy since sparse RGB cannot reconstruct scenes) and global angular-geometric context substantially improves matching over GoMatch's local-only geometric approach, nearly doubling AUC scores.

## Results

On MegaDepth with k=10 retrieved views, DGC-GNN achieves 15.30/51.70/60.01% reprojection AUC at 1/5/10 px thresholds, nearly doubling GoMatch's 8.90/35.67/44.99%. On ScanNet with k=10, DGC-GNN reaches 1.76/31.74/48.11% AUC versus GoMatch's 0.91/18.98/31.12%. For visual localization, DGC-GNN reduces the median pose error on Cambridge Landmarks to 54 cm / 2.23° (vs. GoMatch's 173 cm / 5.87°) and on 7Scenes to 15 cm / 4.47° (vs. GoMatch's 22 cm / 5.77°). Notably, DGC-GNN with k=1 surpasses GoMatch with k=10, indicating strong single-view matching capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGC-GNN decisively outperforms GoMatch [53], the strongest descriptor-free baseline, across all datasets and settings: on MegaDepth k=10 it nearly doubles the AUC@10px (60.01% vs. 44.99%) and reduces the median rotation error from 1.29° to 0.26°. On ScanNet k=10, the AUC@10px more than doubles (48.11% vs. 31.12%) and the median rotation error drops from 4.94° to 1.49°. BPnPNet [4] is far inferior, achieving near-zero AUC on both datasets, confirming that differentiable PnP alone is insufficient for descriptor-free matching. For visual localization, DGC-GNN beats GoMatch by a wide margin on Cambridge Landmarks (54 cm / 2.23° vs. 173 cm / 5.87° median error) and 7Scenes (15 cm / 4.47° vs. 22 cm / 5.77°). Against descriptor-based methods (SuperPoint+SuperGlue, Active Search), DGC-GNN still lags in absolute accuracy but requires no descriptor storage or maintenance, and its gap is substantially smaller than GoMatch's. The ablation confirms that color encoding contributes the single largest gain (+2.55/3.88/2.55% AUC), followed by the global geometric embedding and angular embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGC-GNN builds directly on GoMatch, which established the descriptor-free 2D-3D matching paradigm using bearing vectors and attention-based correspondence. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a global-to-local hierarchy: GoMatch relies solely on local geometric cues from each point and its immediate neighbors, while DGC-GNN adds a cluster-based global geometric graph that encodes both Euclidean distances and angular relations across the scene. The local matching stage adapts the cluster-based attention mechanism from ClusterGNN to constrain message passing within correlated feature groups, and the angular embedding is borrowed from the Geometric Transformer for rotation-invariant cues. The incorporation of per-point RGB color as an explicit matching signal is a novel addition absent from all prior descriptor-free 2D-3D matchers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By doubling the matching accuracy of the previous state-of-the-art descriptor-free method and substantially narrowing the gap to descriptor-based pipelines, DGC-GNN makes descriptor-free 2D-3D matching a more practical option for privacy-sensitive and memory-constrained localization systems. The ablation revealing that color information contributes a non-trivial share of the improvement (comparable to the global geometric embedding) challenges the implicit assumption that geometry alone suffices for cross-domain matching. The primary open question is whether the remaining gap to descriptor-based methods can be closed, particularly in scenes with repetitive or geometrically ambiguous structures where the authors acknowledge the method still struggles.
