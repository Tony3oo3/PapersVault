## Problem

Existing VQA benchmarks predominantly evaluate simplistic visual tasks (counting, color, object detection) that do not require cross-modal reasoning or entity alignment between image and text. Even newer multimodal QA datasets like MuMuQA and MultiModalQA suffer from limited entity diversity, automated data generation, or question patterns that reduce the need for genuine cross-media inference. This paper introduces VTQA, a dataset where every question mandates multi-hop reasoning that cannot be resolved from either the image or the text alone.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (from MSCOCO, with regional features extracted via frozen FasterRCNN), a human-annotated text passage (minimum 100 Chinese words, averaging ~238 words), and a natural language question. The output is an open-ended answer categorized into three types: yes/no (YN), extracted-from-text (E), or generated-from-pair (G). A critical constraint is that all 23,781 questions were verified in a second annotation round to be unanswerable from image or text in isolation, ensuring genuine cross-modal dependency.

## Method

KECMRN (Key Entity Cross-Media Reasoning Network) follows a modular design where each KECMR module contains one Key Entity Extract (KEE) layer followed by multiple Cross-Media Reason (CMR) layers. The KEE layer applies question-aware multi-head attention to text and image features, scores each feature via a fully-connected layer, and selects the top-k (16) as key entities. Each CMR layer gathers these key entities, applies self-attention among them plus global cross-attention against the full feature set, then scatters the updated entities back into the original feature positions. The network uses bottom-up attention for image encoding, a one-layer LSTM for text and question encoding, and an attention-reduce fusion layer that projects the combined multimodal representation into an N-way answer classifier trained with binary cross-entropy.

## Results

On the VTQA test set (English version), KECMRN achieves the best Exact Match score of 57.95% and YN-ACC of 74.99%, outperforming all other baselines. Among multimodal large language models, GPT4V attains the highest E-F1 (70.24%) and G-F1 (48.37%), surpassing KECMRN's 68.91% and 44.25% respectively, but trails on EM (48.93% vs. 57.95%). On the Chinese version, KECMRN leads with 51.32% EM and 77.59% YN-ACC. Overall, even the strongest pretrained models (GPT4V, VLMo) show substantial performance degradation compared to their VQAv2 scores, highlighting the difficulty of the VTQA task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KECMRN achieves the highest EM (57.95%) and YN-ACC (74.99%) among all baselines on the English VTQA test set, edging out Trans-CA (57.38% EM) by 0.57 points and MCAN-region (56.09% EM) by 1.86 points. However, GPT4V outperforms KECMRN on E-F1 (70.24% vs. 68.91%) and G-F1 (48.37% vs. 44.25%), indicating that the MLLM's open-ended generation capability remains stronger for extracted and generated answers despite its lower overall EM. Among the non-pretrained VQA architectures, Trans-CA is the closest competitor, while Trans-SA (54.39% EM) underperforms, confirming the need for independent processing of image, text, and question streams. The MLLMs (LLaVA at 39.90% EM, MMICL at 38.76% EM) and pretrained VLMs (BEiT3 at 41.02%, VLMo at 51.85%) all show significant degradation relative to their VQAv2 performance, underscoring that VTQA's multi-hop cross-media reasoning demands exceed what these models were trained for. On the Chinese version, KECMRN similarly leads with 51.32% EM and 77.59% YN-ACC, maintaining its advantage over Trans-CA (49.87% EM) and MCAN-grid (50.22% EM).

## Relation to Prior Work

The KECMRN architecture builds directly on the MCAN framework (Yu et al., CVPR 2019), replacing its modular co-attention layers with a custom KECMR module that adds an explicit key-entity extraction step and a dedicated text stream. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multimodal QA datasets (MuMuQA, MultiModalQA) that rely on extracted text from news or Wikipedia, instead pairing real COCO images with human-annotated passages to force genuine cross-modal entity alignment. The attention-based reasoning layers draw on the standard transformer building blocks (multi-head attention, feed-forward) from Vaswani et al., but reorganize them into a gather-scatter pattern specifically designed for iterative multi-step reasoning over aligned entities rather than sequential token processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VTQA provides a more realistic and diverse benchmark for cross-modal reasoning than prior datasets, covering varied object categories and human-written text rather than extracted news or textbook passages. The finding that even GPT4V (48.93% EM) and LLaVA (39.90% EM) perform far below their VQAv2 scores highlights a genuine gap in current MLLMs' ability to perform entity-level alignment and multi-hop inference across modalities. However, the dataset is relatively small (23,781 questions), the proposed KECMRN achieves only a modest improvement over Trans-CA (57.95% vs 57.38% EM), and the authors acknowledge the need for larger scale, reasoning annotations, and better evaluation metrics beyond exact match. Open questions include whether the entity-alignment formulation generalizes to longer documents or more complex multi-entity scenes, and whether the modest gains from KEE/CMR layers would hold at larger scale.
