## Problem

Existing transformer-based 3D human pose estimation methods derive Q, K, V vectors in their self-attention mechanisms solely through simple linear mapping, treating all joints and frames uniformly. This leads to 'attention collapse,' where attention weights concentrate on a limited subset of tokens while ignoring others, degrading spatial and temporal modeling. The paper asks whether injecting known anatomical and motion-trajectory prior knowledge into the attention computation can mitigate this issue without altering the transformer architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D pose sequence of shape T×N×2 (T frames, N joints, 2D x-y coordinates) as input, where 2D keypoints are either detected by CPN or provided as ground truth. It produces a full 3D pose sequence of shape T×N×3 via a seq2seq pipeline, predicting all frames simultaneously rather than a single central frame. In its diffusion-based variant, KTPFormer serves as the denoiser within a D3DP-style iterative refinement loop, receiving noisy 3D poses and outputting denoised estimates over multiple hypothesis-aggregation iterations.

## Method

KTPFormer introduces two lightweight plug-and-play prior attention modules placed in front of the multi-head self-attention (MHSA) layers of a vanilla transformer. Kinematics Prior Attention (KPA) constructs a learnable kinematics topology by combining a fixed skeletal-structure adjacency matrix with a fully-connected learnable global topology, then applies this topology via element-wise multiplication with a learnable weight matrix to modulate spatial tokens before the spatial MHSA. Trajectory Prior Attention (TPA) analogously builds a joint motion trajectory topology from a fixed temporal-local adjacency (same joint across consecutive frames) plus a learnable temporal-global topology, and stacks two TPA blocks with residual connections to modulate temporal tokens before the temporal MHSA. The overall pipeline is: KPA → Spatial MHSA → TPA → Temporal MHSA → L stacked alternating spatio-temporal encoder layers → linear regression head, trained with a combined WMPJPE + temporal consistency + velocity loss. The modules add only ~0.0016M parameters and ~21M FLOPs over the baseline, and can be dropped into other transformer-based estimators (PoseFormer, MixSTE, STCFormer, etc.) to yield consistent MPJPE improvements of 0.6–2.6 mm.

## Results

On Human3.6M with CPN-detected 2D poses, KTPFormer (diffusion-based, T=243) achieves 33.0 mm MPJPE and 26.2 mm P-MPJPE, outperforming D3DP by 2.4 mm and 2.5 mm respectively under identical settings. Without the diffusion process, KTPFormer (T=243) achieves 40.1 mm MPJPE, the best among non-diffusion methods. On MPI-INF-3DHP, KTPFormer (T=81) achieves 98.9% PCK, 85.9% AUC, and 16.7 mm MPJPE, surpassing the previous best (STCFormer, T=81) by 6.4 mm in MPJPE. On HumanEva, KTPFormer (T=27) achieves 15.3 mm average MPJPE, the best result on this smaller dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KTPFormer (diffusion-based) outperforms D3DP [31] by 2.4 mm MPJPE (33.0 vs 35.4) and 2.5 mm P-MPJPE (26.2 vs 28.7) on Human3.6M with CPN 2D poses, confirming KTPFormer as a stronger denoiser backbone. Among non-diffusion methods, KTPFormer (T=243) beats STCFormer (T=243) by 0.4 mm MPJPE (40.1 vs 40.5) and MixSTE (T=243) by 0.8 mm (40.1 vs 40.9). On MPI-INF-3DHP, KTPFormer (T=81) achieves 16.7 mm MPJPE, a 6.4 mm improvement over STCFormer (T=81, 23.1 mm) and 11.4 mm over D3DP (T=243, 28.1 mm), while also matching the best PCK (98.9%) and exceeding all methods in AUC (85.9%). On HumanEva, KTPFormer (T=27) achieves 15.3 mm, outperforming TCN (T=81, 18.9 mm) and PoseFormer (T=43, 21.6 mm). The plug-and-play experiments (Table 7) show that adding KPA+TPA to existing backbones (PoseFormer, StridedFormer, MHFormer, MixSTE, STCFormer) consistently reduces MPJPE by 0.6–2.6 mm with negligible parameter overhead (<0.06M), validating the modules' generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

KTPFormer positions itself against two lines of work in transformer-based 3D pose estimation: (1) standard transformer encoders (PoseFormer, MixSTE, STCFormer) that derive Q, K, V via simple linear projections, and (2) graph-transformer hybrids (GraFormer, PoseGTAC, DiffPose, POT) that embed graph convolutions into the transformer structure. The key departure is that rather than modifying the transformer architecture or replacing MLPs with GCNs, KTPFormer introduces two lightweight prior-attention modules (KPA and TPA) that inject anatomical kinematics and joint trajectory topology as learnable affinity matrices *before* the MHSA, preserving the vanilla transformer structure. For the diffusion-based variant, it builds directly on D3DP by substituting KTPFormer as the denoiser backbone while keeping D3DP's diffusion schedule and multi-hypothesis aggregation unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the plug-and-play design: KPA and TPA add only ~0.002M parameters and ~21M FLOPs yet yield consistent 0.6–2.6 mm MPJPE improvements when grafted onto five architecturally distinct transformer backbones (Table 7), making them a low-cost drop-in enhancement for existing pipelines. The identification of 'attention collapse' as a failure mode of uniform linear projection in pose estimation, and the demonstration that structured prior topologies mitigate it, offers a reusable diagnostic for other token-based attention systems. Open questions include whether the fixed skeletal topology generalizes to non-human articulated objects or variable joint counts, and whether the learnable global topology components could be further regularized to avoid overfitting on small datasets like HumanEva.
