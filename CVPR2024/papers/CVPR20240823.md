## Problem

Existing concealed object detection (COD) methods and datasets focus on animals or humans employing active camouflage strategies, leaving the agricultural domain unaddressed. Dense agricultural scenes present fundamentally different challenges—passive concealment of numerous small crops amid complex backgrounds, severe mutual occlusions, and high object density—that current COD models cannot handle effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240823/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired RGB image and depth map (both 1080×1920 from a ZED2i camera, downsampled to 704×704 for training) and produces a binary segmentation mask localizing all concealed crops. The depth map is converted to a 3-channel grayscale image, and the two modalities are concatenated along the batch dimension (rather than the channel dimension) so the encoder processes them jointly while preserving shared spatial alignment. The model outputs three intermediate coarse predictions (one per iteration) plus a final refined mask.

## Method

RISNet combines three modules: (1) a Concealed Feature Encoder using PVT with ASPP to capture multi-scale receptive fields for objects of varying sizes; (2) a Depth-Guided Feature Decoder that fuses RGB and depth features via element-wise addition (complementarity) and multiplication (commonality) followed by dual channel-spatial attention, then decodes through a residual-in-residual cascaded decoder with residual channel attention to handle dense objects at multiple scales; (3) an Iterative Feature Refinement stage where each coarse prediction is fed back through a Guidance-based Gated Attention module to progressively sharpen localization of small objects over 3 iterations. Training uses weighted BCE + IoU loss with per-iteration weighting (γ=0.2), and the final prediction fuses the bottom-level feature with the last coarse map through a Feature Adaptive Fusion module.

## Results

On the proposed ACOD-12K (CCD) benchmark, RISNet achieves Sα=0.866, Fβω=0.803, and Eθ=0.967, outperforming the best COD baseline HitNet (Sα=0.853, Fβω=0.787, Eθ=0.955) by 1.45% on average and the best RGB-D SOD baseline XMSNet (Sα=0.844, Fβω=0.754, Eθ=0.961) by 4.9% on Fβω. On classic COD benchmarks, RISNet also sets new state-of-the-art: CAMO (Sα=0.870, Fβω=0.827, Eθ=0.922), COD10K (Sα=0.873, Fβω=0.799, Eθ=0.931), and NC4K (Sα=0.882, Fβω=0.834, Eθ=0.925), surpassing the previous best (DaCOD, HitNet, FSPNet respectively) on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RISNet outperforms all 11 COD baselines and all 8 RGB-D SOD baselines on the primary CCD benchmark (ACOD-12K). Among COD methods, the closest competitor is HitNet [26] (AAAI'23), which RISNet surpasses by +1.3% Sα, +1.6% Fβω, and +1.2% Eθ; PopNet [61] (ICCV'23) is second-closest at Sα=0.844. Among RGB-D SOD methods, XMSNet [62] (MM'23) is the strongest baseline, yet RISNet exceeds it by +2.2% Sα and +4.9% Fβω, confirming that the multi-scale depth fusion strategy is more effective than prior RGB-D saliency approaches for dense concealed crops. On classic COD benchmarks (Table 3), RISNet also achieves the best scores across all three datasets: on CAMO it beats DaCOD [57] (Sα 0.855→0.870) and FSPNet [28] (Sα 0.856→0.870); on COD10K it surpasses HitNet [26] (Sα 0.868→0.873, Fβω 0.798→0.799); and on NC4K it edges out FSPNet [28] (Sα 0.879→0.882, Fβω 0.816→0.834). Notably, FSPNet [28] (CVPR'23) performs poorly on CCD (Sα=0.719), suggesting that transformer-based COD methods without depth guidance struggle with the dense, small-object agricultural setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concealed Object Detection (COD) line of work, which has predominantly addressed animal camouflage (active concealment of single objects), to the agricultural domain where the challenge is passive concealment of dense, small crops amid complex backgrounds and mutual occlusion. Methodologically, RISNet adapts HitNet's (AAAI 2023) iterative feedback mechanism for small-object refinement and borrows the batch-concatenation and element-wise add/multiply fusion strategy from the Siamese RGB-D SOD framework (TPAMI 2021), wrapping both within a PVT encoder augmented with ASPP multi-scale receptive fields and a Residual-In-Residual cascaded decoder. The key conceptual departure from prior COD is the explicit argument that unimodal RGB is insufficient for dense agricultural scenes, motivating the first multimodal (RGB-D) COD formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACOD-12K is the first multimodal COD dataset and the largest-scale COD dataset to date (6092 images at 1080×1920 with depth), filling a clear gap for agricultural vision research and enabling future work on crop monitoring, automated harvesting, and weed control. The CCD benchmark exposes a genuine limitation of existing COD methods (e.g., FSPNet drops from ~0.85 Sα on COD10K to 0.719 on CCD), suggesting that camouflage strategies in agriculture are fundamentally different from animal mimicry and that depth cues may be essential rather than optional. Open questions include whether the depth modality generalizes across seasons and crop types, whether the 3-iteration overhead is acceptable for real-time field deployment, and whether the findings transfer to other dense-scene detection tasks beyond agriculture.
