## Problem

Most scanpath prediction models are designed to capture either top-down (goal-directed visual search) or bottom-up (free-viewing) attention control, but not both, and they rely on coarse grid discretization of fixations that causes information loss especially at high resolution. This paper asks whether a single unified architecture can predict scanpaths across target-present search, target-absent search, and free-viewing while avoiding fixation discretization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241646/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

HAT takes an RGB image (resized to 320×512), a task label (one of 18 target categories for visual search or a single free-viewing task), and an optional initial fixation (default: image center). At each autoregressive step it outputs a dense per-pixel fixation heatmap Y ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} and a scalar termination probability τ ∈ [0,1], with predictions for all tasks produced in parallel. Unlike prior methods that predict a probability distribution over a coarse grid of fixation cells, HAT outputs a full-resolution heatmap, and the next fixation is sampled via L1-normalization of the heatmap.

## Method

HAT is a transformer-based architecture with four modules: a feature extraction module (ResNet-50 encoder + MSDeformAttn decoder) producing a 4-level feature pyramid; a foveation module that constructs a dynamic working memory from peripheral tokens (flattened low-resolution P1 features) and foveal tokens (P4 features at previous fixation locations), enriched with 2D sinusoidal spatial embeddings and learnable temporal embeddings; a transformer decoder aggregation module with task-specific queries that cross-attend to the working memory; and a fixation prediction module that convolves an MLP-transformed query with P4 to yield the dense heatmap. The key insight is that the foveation module simulates a simplified foveated retina, creating a dynamically-updating visual working memory that replaces RNNs (sacrificing interpretability) and static multi-resolution simulations (missing temporal/spatial dynamics). Training uses behavior cloning with pixel-wise focal loss on the fixation heatmap and weighted binary cross-entropy on the termination label, formulating scanpath prediction as sequential dense prediction with per-pixel supervision.

## Results

On the target-present (TP) test set of COCO-Search18, HAT achieves SemSS 0.543, SS 0.470, cIG 2.399, cNSS 5.086, and cAUC 0.977, outperforming all prior methods on nearly all metrics. On target-absent (TA) search, HAT reaches cNSS 3.103 (94% improvement over the previous SOTA) and SS 0.402, surpassing human consistency (0.381) for the first time. On free-viewing (COCO-FreeView), HAT attains cIG 1.485, cNSS 3.382, and cAUC 0.965, improving cNSS by 104% over the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAT outperforms all learned baselines on cNSS across all three settings: 5.086 vs. 2.606 (Chen et al. [10], +95%) on TP, 3.103 vs. 1.600 (Chen et al. [10], +94%) on TA, and 3.382 vs. 1.655 (Chen et al. [10], +104%) on FV. On TP, HAT slightly trails GazeFormer [42] in SS (0.470 vs. 0.489) but leads in SemSS (0.543 vs. 0.499), indicating better semantic alignment of predicted fixations. On TA, HAT is the first method to exceed human consistency in SS (0.402 vs. 0.381), while FFMs [63] and IRL [62] remain well below. In free-viewing, HAT dominates cIG (1.485) over FFMs (0.329, +351%) and DeepGaze III [32] (0.140), and leads cAUC (0.965) over all baselines. Heuristic baselines (detector, fixation density) perform competitively on TP SS but lag substantially on conditional saliency metrics, confirming that inter-fixation dependencies matter. GazeFormer [42] reports only SemSS and SS (no cIG/cNSS/cAUC), limiting direct comparison on next-fixation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAT extends the transformer-based scanpath prediction line initiated by Gazeformer (Mondal et al., CVPR 2023) and FFMs (Yang et al., ECCV 2022), but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it replaces RNN-based temporal state or multi-resolution foveation simulations with a transformer encoder that maintains a dynamic working memory of peripheral and foveal tokens, and (2) it formulates scanpath prediction as sequential dense prediction (per-pixel heatmaps) rather than classification over a coarse fixation grid. The architecture draws on Deformable DETR's pixel decoder and Mask2Former's query-based aggregation, while the foveated working memory conceptually builds on earlier foveation simulations at pixel, feature, and semantic levels. Unlike prior methods that trained separate models for target-present, target-absent, and free-viewing settings, HAT unifies all three under a single task-query mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying top-down and bottom-up attention prediction in one model and avoiding fixation discretization, HAT addresses a practical bottleneck for high-resolution and 360° AR/VR applications where grid-based methods lose precision. The interpretability afforded by the attention-weight contribution maps (peripheral vs. foveal) opens a window into how computational models allocate gaze, which is valuable for cognitive science validation. Open questions include whether the working-memory approach scales to video scanpaths, whether the fixed two-eccentricity foveation is optimal, and whether the behavior-cloning training paradigm can be replaced with reinforcement learning to better capture long-horizon dependencies. The paper does not extensively explore failure modes or computational cost at inference time for very long scanpaths.
