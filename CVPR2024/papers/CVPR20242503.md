## Problem

Existing assistive robot systems either passively wait for the user to fail or aggressively take over entire tasks, ignoring the user's residual capabilities and emotional well-being. No prior work formulates the problem of selectively helping only with the specific sub-goals that exceed a user's capability while letting them complete the rest independently, nor provides a 3D household environment and benchmark for evaluating such adaptive, empathetic assistance.

## Input / Output

The helper receives symbolic observations from AI2-THOR (object types, positions, weights, 6 binary attributes, parent receptacle indices, and agent states including position, rotation, held object, last action, and success/failure flag) processed through a sliding window of size w. The model outputs a 6-dimensional capability vector in [0,1]⁶ (reach height, lift weight, open, close, toggle-on, toggle-off), a goal specification (target state, object, parent receptacle), and a single intentional action from a 373-action space (e.g., PickUp, ToggleOn, Put). The main agent's true goals and capabilities are hidden from the helper, making this a partially observable setting.

## Method

The architecture has two modules: (1) an opponent modeling module using transformer-based object and agent encoders with a learnable object token, a sliding window of state features fed into MLPs, and separate feature extractors for capability (via a transformer layer) and goal (via transformer + MLP layers for target state, object, and receptacle); and (2) an actor-critic helping policy with an LSTM for context, a Policy MLP for action selection, and a Value MLP for state valuation. Training is two-stage: first, supervised pre-training of the opponent module with four auxiliary classifiers (cross-entropy, Adam, lr=1e-6); second, PPO-based RL training of the helping policy with a progressive learning schedule—840 epochs of warm-up with λ_e=0 (encouraging all helpful actions) followed by 240 epochs with λ_e=1.0 (penalizing unnecessary help by −30 when the helper completes a goal the main agent could handle independently). The key design insight is the reward function R_h = R^{g_m} + λ_e·R^{e_m}, which explicitly encodes the emotional cost of over-assistance, combined with the progressive schedule that first teaches basic task completion before refining the selective-help strategy.

## Results

On the Smart Help benchmark (10 held-out AI2-THOR rooms, 14 task-capability pairs), the full model (BaseModel-PL-λe=1.0) achieves a Task Success Rate (SR) of 0.483, Goal Success Rate (GSR) of 0.548, Helping Necessity (HN) of 0.498, and Helping Rate (HR) of 0.506, with an average Episode Length (EL) of 24.65 steps and SPL of 0.200. The oracle upper bound MCTS_TG (which knows the true goal) reaches SR=0.593, GSR=0.804, HN=0.610, and HR=0.678, indicating a meaningful but non-trivial gap. The full model outperforms all non-oracle baselines on SR, GSR, HN, and HR while maintaining competitive EL and SPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLM baseline (GPT-3.5-turbo-instruct), the full model wins on all four primary metrics: SR (0.483 vs. 0.345), GSR (0.548 vs. 0.506), HN (0.498 vs. 0.382), and HR (0.506 vs. 0.381), though the LLM achieves a slightly shorter episode length (22.91 vs. 24.65 steps) and comparable SPL (0.210 vs. 0.200). Against the MCTS baseline, the full model dominates across every metric, with the largest gap in HN (0.498 vs. 0.266) and HR (0.506 vs. 0.328), reflecting the advantage of learned opponent modeling over pure search. Against the Watch-and-Hep-inspired MCTS_TG oracle (which has access to the true goal), the full model trails on all metrics (SR 0.483 vs. 0.593, GSR 0.548 vs. 0.804, HN 0.498 vs. 0.610, HR 0.506 vs. 0.678), but the gap narrows substantially on HN, suggesting the learned capability inference partially compensates for not knowing the goal. The MCTS-heuristic variant (MCTS + task-specific rules) is the strongest non-oracle baseline, yet the full model still leads by 0.209 in SR, 0.143 in HN, and 0.106 in HR. Overall, the results confirm that the joint goal-and-capability opponent modeling plus progressive learning with emotional reward (λe=1.0) is the key to achieving smart, selective assistance rather than indiscriminate help-taking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the Watch-and-Help and NOPA line of work, which studies cooperation between agents of equal capability, by introducing an asymmetric assistance setting where the helper must infer both the main agent's goals and their heterogeneous capabilities (e.g., weight limits, reach height) to provide selective, non-intrusive help. It repurposes opponent modeling techniques (transformer-based feature extraction, sliding-window inference) from competitive multi-agent settings and extends them to jointly estimate goals and capabilities, a combination absent in prior assistive-robotics literature. The progressive learning strategy—first training a basic helping policy with λe=0, then fine-tuning with the emotional penalty—draws on curriculum learning ideas but applies them specifically to the transition from 'help everything' to 'help only bottlenecks.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in assistive AI: most prior systems either take over tasks entirely or rely on fixed rules, ignoring the user's emotional acceptance and desire for autonomy. The Smart Help benchmark and AI2-THOR-based environment provide a concrete testbed for future work on empathetic, adaptive assistance, and the six evaluation metrics (especially Helping Necessity and Helping Rate) offer a more nuanced assessment than raw task completion. However, the significant performance gap to the oracle MCTS_TG baseline (SR 0.483 vs. 0.593) and the restriction to three simple household tasks in a simulation limit near-term practical impact. Open questions include scaling to more complex tasks, richer capability models, and real-world deployment where partial observability and safety constraints are far more severe.
