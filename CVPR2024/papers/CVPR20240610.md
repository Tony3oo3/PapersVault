## Problem

Normalizing flows have been widely applied to density estimation in Euclidean space, but their extension to rotational manifolds like SO(3) remains underexplored. Existing probabilistic human pose models either parameterize joint rotations in Euclidean space (e.g., 6D representations) and thus lack a continuous bijective mapping to SO(3), or they learn non-normalized densities that cannot provide exact likelihoods. This paper addresses the gap by constructing a normalizing flow that operates natively on the high-dimensional product space of SO(3) manifolds, enabling normalized joint densities over human joint rotations.

## Input / Output

The model conditions on 3D or 2D positions of the 21 SMPL joints (or a subset thereof when joints are occluded), which are processed through an MLP to produce a context vector injected into every flow layer. The output is a full joint rotation distribution over N=19 SO(3) manifolds (after removing static joints), from which one can draw pose samples, compute exact log-likelihoods, or obtain 3D poses via forward kinematics. During training, each joint's observation is independently masked with a learned probability, so the model handles arbitrary subsets of visible joints at inference without retraining.

## Method

HuProSO3 composes K flow layers, each consisting of a Möbius coupling layer (which warps one column of the rotation matrix on S² while preserving orthogonality via cross-product reconstruction) followed by a quaternion affine transformation (which applies a learned global rotation and shift on the 3-sphere double-cover of SO(3)). The N=19 SO(3) manifolds are linked through a randomly permuted autoregressive decomposition p(R)=∏p(R_i|R_{1:i-1},c), where the conditioning is nonlinear: an MLP maps the 6D rotation representations of all preceding manifolds to parameters of both the Möbius transformation and the affine matrix W, replacing the Gaussian conditionals used in Euclidean masked autoregressive flows. Permutations of the conditioning order across layers ensure all three rotational degrees of freedom are modeled. Training maximizes the exact log-likelihood of target rotations propagated through the flow (NLL loss), and the model is trained on the AMASS database with the SMPL body model.

## Results

On the inverse kinematics task (AMASS test split), HuProSO3 achieves MPJPE of 8.2 mm (single sample) / 5.5 mm (10-sample mean) and MGEO of 0.163 / 0.121 rad, outperforming all baselines on both metrics. For 2D-to-3D pose uplifting, it reaches MPJPE of 32.8 / 23.4 mm and MGEO of 0.209 / 0.153 rad, again the best among all compared methods. As an unconditional pose prior, HuProSO3 attains the lowest cumulative geodesic distance for both precision (4.24 mean) and recall (3.44 mean) on the AMASS test set, indicating it captures both the mode and the diversity of the human pose distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HuProSO3 outperforms all four baselines on the MGEO (rotational accuracy) metric across every evaluated task. Against HF-AC [32], the closest probabilistic competitor, HuProSO3 reduces MGEO from 0.308 to 0.163 rad (single sample) on IK and from 0.346 to 0.209 rad on 2D-to-3D uplifting, while also halving MPJPE (33.2→8.2 mm and 74.4→32.8 mm). Pose-NDF [34] achieves a near-perfect MPJPE of 0.2 mm on full IK because its optimization-based pipeline can match the given 3D keypoints exactly, but it suffers the worst MGEO (0.289 rad) since it models a manifold of feasible poses rather than a probability distribution over rotations. GAN-S [4] is competitive on MPJPE for certain occlusion patterns (e.g., 16.8 mm for left-leg occlusion vs. 13.9 mm for HuProSO3) but consistently trails on MGEO (0.240 vs. 0.165 rad). VPoser [25] is the weakest baseline, with MPJPE of 26.6 mm and MGEO of 0.230 rad on IK, reflecting its lack of a learned pose prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two key ingredients from prior work: the Möbius coupling layer and quaternion affine transformation from Liu et al. [18] for SO(3)-native flow layers, and the masked autoregressive conditioning strategy from Papamakarios et al. [23] for learning joint densities. It departs from prior probabilistic human pose models (GAN-S, Pose-NDF, HuManiFlow) that either operate in Euclidean space with 6D rotation representations, model only a feasible-pose manifold without normalized densities, or learn only ancestor-conditioned single-manifold distributions. The key conceptual advance is lifting the autoregressive product-space construction to SO(3) manifolds with nonlinear (non-Gaussian) conditioning and random permutation of the conditioning order, yielding a true normalized joint density on the full product space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HuProSO3 is the first model to provide a normalized joint density on the product space of SO(3) manifolds for human pose, enabling exact likelihood evaluation and principled uncertainty quantification that GAN-based or manifold-based approaches cannot offer. This opens the door to applications in human-robot interaction (risk-aware planning), filtering/estimation with partial observations, and Bayesian model comparison. Key limitations include the autoregressive structure's dependence on permutation for capturing dependencies, linear scaling of sampling cost with the number of joints, and restriction to SO(3) (excluding other rotational manifolds like SO(2) for planar joints). Whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or heterogeneous manifold products remains an open question.
