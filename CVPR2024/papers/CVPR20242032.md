## Problem

Zero-shot descriptor ensembling methods (e.g., GPT descriptors, WaffleCLIP) are untrainable and thus sub-optimal when even a small amount of few-shot training data is available, while existing few-shot soft prompt tuning methods (e.g., CoOp, MaPLe) require many continuous parameters and backpropagation. The paper asks whether one can select discrete textual descriptors that maximize few-shot training accuracy, thereby achieving higher OOD target accuracy with far fewer stored parameters than soft prompt tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242032/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes 16-shot ImageNet-1K labels to greedily select descriptors or word chains; at test time, it takes a single RGB image and produces a class label via cosine similarity between the image embedding and L2-normalized centroids of text embeddings (class name + selected descriptor). The descriptors are discrete token sequences (not continuous vectors), so only the token indices need to be stored rather than 512 parameters per soft prompt token. No LLM is required at test time, and the vision encoder remains frozen throughout.

## Method

Descriptor soup greedily selects m descriptors from a pool of ~1,200 unique GPT-generated descriptors by iteratively adding the descriptor that most reduces 0-1 loss on the training set, then classifies using the L2-normalized centroid of the selected descriptors' text embeddings. Word soup generalizes this by greedily assembling chains of words from a 10,000-word dictionary: it initializes with a top-accuracy single word and appends words only if they increase training accuracy, with randomization and a patience parameter to encourage diversity across the m descriptors. A key additional contribution is a diversity loss (a knowledge-distillation-style regularizer with hyperparameter λ) that biases each descriptor's prediction toward its initial (pre-finetuning) prediction, preventing the text prototypes from collapsing to the same location during gradient-based finetuning. This loss allows word soup to be seamlessly stacked on top of CoOp, MaPLe, or CLIPood, yielding ~1% additional OOD accuracy over the best prior few-shot methods.

## Results

On the cross-dataset (XD) benchmark (10 target datasets), word soup with score-mean evaluation achieves 67.43% mean accuracy using only 8 descriptors, outperforming CLIP ZS (65.02%), CoOp (66.52%), MaPLe (66.30%), and ProDA (66.23%). On the domain generalization (DG) benchmark (4 target datasets), word soup achieves 61.32% mean accuracy, surpassing CLIPood (60.47%), MaPLe (60.30%), and CoOp (59.25%). When stacked on top of CE finetuning, word soup reaches 67.43% XD and 61.32% DG, while descriptor soup with the token offset trick reaches 67.51% XD and 61.01% DG. The method achieves these gains with only 8 discrete tokens (8 parameters) versus 512 parameters per soft token in CoOp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Word soup (8 descriptors) outperforms all gradient-based few-shot baselines on both XD and DG: it beats CoOp by +0.91% XD and +2.07% DG, MaPLe by +1.13% XD and +1.02% DG, and CLIPood by +0.93% XD and +0.85% DG. Against prompt-ensembling methods, word soup surpasses ProDA (32 prompts) by +1.20% XD and +2.49% DG, ProGrad by +0.95% XD and +2.36% DG, and KgCoOp by +1.27% XD and +2.68% DG, while using far fewer parameters (8 discrete tokens vs. 32 soft prompts or 512 parameters per token). Compared to zero-shot descriptor methods, word soup with only 8 descriptors outperforms WaffleCLIP (16 random descriptors) by +1.85% XD and +2.24% DG, and GPT score mean (5.8 descriptors) by +2.01% XD and +2.90% DG. When stacked on top of existing finetuning methods (CE, CoOp, MaPLe, CLIPood), word soup consistently adds 0.8–1.0% target accuracy, demonstrating complementarity rather than replacement. The descriptor soup with the token offset trick (100 descriptors) achieves the best XD mean (67.51%) but at higher computational cost, while word soup matches this performance with only 8 descriptors and no additional computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of descriptor-based zero-shot classification (Menon & Vondrick, WaffleCLIP) and few-shot OOD prompt tuning (CoOp, MaPLe, CLIPood). It departs from the soft-prompt-tuning paradigm by replacing continuous prompt vectors with discrete text tokens selected via a greedy procedure inspired by model soups (Wortsman et al.). The key conceptual bridge is WaffleCLIP's observation that ensembling drives ZS gains rather than descriptor content, which the authors extend by making the selection procedure trainable on few-shot data. The diversity loss for finetuning builds directly on CoOp-style training but adds a knowledge-distillation-style regularization to preserve descriptor diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a handful of discrete tokens (1 parameter each) can match or exceed soft prompts (512 parameters each) for OOD generalization, offering a simpler and more memory-efficient alternative to PEFT methods. The finding that word soup with m=2 already outperforms all ZS baselines up to m=64 challenges the assumption that large ensembles are necessary. Limitations include the greedy selection being approximate, the method still requiring labeled training data (not truly zero-shot), and modest absolute gains (~1%) over the strongest baselines. Open questions include whether the discrete-token approach generalizes to non-CLIP architectures and whether the diversity loss transfers to other ensembling settings.
