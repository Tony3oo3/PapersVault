## Problem

Existing 2D avatar systems either restrict generation to the facial region (GAN-based talking-head methods) or produce full-body videos with poor temporal consistency, inaccurate hand gestures, and degraded facial details (diffusion-based methods like DreamPose and DisCo). No prior system can generate full-body, temporally-coherent, identity-preserving anchor-style videos from just a one-minute source video while supporting arbitrary-length output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241072/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For training, the system requires a single anchor-style video clip (≥1 minute) of one person, from which the body region is cropped to 512×512 pixels. At inference, it takes a sequence of SMPL-X 3D mesh poses (rendered as 2D images) and a randomly sampled appearance frame from the source video as CLIP image features; the face enhancement stage operates at 256×256. The output is a temporally-consistent 2D human video of arbitrary length (experiments use 300-frame, 30 fps, 10-second clips), with full-body motions including torso, hands, and facial expressions. The system can also accept audio or motion-capture video as indirect input via external audio-to-motion pipelines.

## Method

The core architecture is a Structure-Guided Diffusion Model (SGDM) built on Stable Diffusion V1.5, where a ControlNet-style branch embeds per-frame 3D mesh conditions into the U-Net encoder, and appearance identity is injected by replacing text CLIP with image CLIP features in cross-attention. A two-stage training strategy first pre-trains on 27 hours of multi-identity anchor video (foreground-segmented) to learn general motion-to-appearance mapping, then fine-tunes the full network on a single identity's one-minute video (foreground + background) to bind specific appearance to motion. For temporal consistency without additional training, the 2D U-Net is extended to a 3D U-Net with an all-frame cross-attention module, and a batch-overlapped temporal denoising algorithm partitions the latent sequence into sliding windows (ws=16, os=4), processes each through the video model, and normalizes overlapping noise predictions to produce arbitrarily long coherent video. A separate Face SGDM module performs inpainting-style enhancement on cropped facial regions using a facial mask, masked latent, and original latent as concatenated inputs, improving facial detail without requiring paired data.

## Results

On the 10-identity anchor video benchmark (30 test clips, 300 frames each), Make-Your-Anchor achieves FID 40.33 and FVD 139.82, substantially outperforming the best GAN-based baseline Pose2Img (FID 51.92, FVD 328.18) and the best diffusion-based baseline DisCo (FID 60.95, FVD 390.77). For structural fidelity, the method attains LMD(Face) 1.44 and LMD(Hand) 5.41, roughly 61% and 22% lower than Pose2Img respectively, while LMD(Body) 4.88 is comparable to Pose2Img's 4.78. All experiments run on a single 40G A100 GPU with 20 denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Make-Your-Anchor outperforms all four baselines on FID and FVD, the two primary quality and temporal-consistency metrics. Against the closest GAN-based competitor Pose2Img, the gap is 22% on FID (40.33 vs 51.92) and 57% on FVD (139.82 vs 328.18), with a 61% reduction in facial landmark error (1.44 vs 3.73). Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline DisCo, improvements are even larger: 34% lower FID and 64% lower FVD. The warping-based TPS baseline is far behind on all metrics (FID 136.02, FVD 884.97), confirming its limitation in unfamiliar domains. DreamPose, designed for fashion-style simple scenes, struggles with complex anchor motions and backgrounds, yielding FVD 868.20. The only metric where the proposed method is marginally behind a baseline is LMD(Body) (4.88 vs Pose2Img's 4.78), which the authors attribute to the inherent inaccuracy of OpenPose hand landmarks in complex gestures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion paradigm (Stable Diffusion) and adapts ControlNet-style conditioning to bind 3D SMPL-X mesh motion to human appearance, departing from prior diffusion-based human video work (DreamPose, DisCo) that relies on single-image inputs and 2D pose. It extends GAN-based talking-head and motion-transfer methods by replacing warping or regional editing with a full-body generative approach, and introduces a training-free temporal extension (batch-overlapped denoising with all-frame cross-attention) to overcome the frame-wise limitation of image diffusion models. The two-stage training strategy—pre-training on multi-identity anchor data then fine-tuning on one minute of a target person—draws on the DreamBooth/Textual Inversion personalization lineage but applies it to a motion-conditioned video generation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The system offers a practical pipeline for generating full-body, anchor-style digital human videos from just one minute of footage, with direct applicability to e-commerce, education, and virtual production. Key open questions include handling body orientations significantly different from training data (a limitation of the binding-style fine-tuning), modeling foreground occlusions, and whether the simple batch-overlapped denoising generalizes to more complex multi-person or scene-changing scenarios. The training-free temporal extension is notable for its simplicity, but its robustness beyond the tested 300-frame clips and single-identity setting remains unverified.
