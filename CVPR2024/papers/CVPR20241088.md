## Problem

Real-world image restoration requires paired clean-degraded training data, but collecting such pairs is expensive and suffers from misalignment. Existing noise modeling methods either rely solely on paired data (limited by scarcity) or unpaired data (less accurate), leaving the semi-supervised regime—where a small number of paired samples coexist with abundant unpaired clean and degraded images—underexplored for learning an accurate degradation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241088/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a semi-supervised dataset consisting of a small paired set (e.g., 10–960 clean-degraded pairs), a large unpaired clean-image set (source domain), and a large unpaired degraded-image set (target domain), all as RGB images processed in 64×64 patches during training. It outputs a trained degradation model that, given a clean image, produces a synthetic degraded image via ancestral sampling, yielding augmented paired training samples for downstream restoration networks. A degradation-level prediction network further enables generating images at controllable degradation strengths from a single clean input.

## Method

SeNM-VAE introduces a hierarchical VAE with a graphical model containing two latent variables—z (image content) and z_n (degradation)—where z_n is conditionally generated from z to capture signal-dependent noise. The key technical contribution is a mixture inference model q(z|x,y) = 0.5·q(z|x) + 0.5·q(z|y), combined with a proposition bounding the KL divergence, which decomposes the cELBO into three separate losses (Loss_p, Loss_s, Loss_t) computable on paired, source, and target domains respectively. The architecture uses L=7 hierarchical layers with Residual Dense Blocks for both encoding and decoding, and incorporates LPIPS and GAN losses alongside L2 for improved generation quality. Degradation generation proceeds via ancestral sampling: z ← q(z|x), z_n ← p(z_n|z), y ← p(y|z,z_n), with a degradation-level conditioning vector appended to z_n for controllable synthesis.

## Results

On the SIDD validation set, SeNM-VAE with only 10 paired samples (0.01%) achieves 37.96 dB PSNR for denoising, surpassing the best self-supervised method SDAP (37.55 dB) and the DRUNet baseline trained on the same 10 pairs (34.48 dB). With 1% paired data (960 samples), SeNM-VAE reaches 39.39 dB, approaching the fully supervised DRUNet (39.55 dB). For noise modeling quality (Table 1), SeNM-VAE with 10 paired samples achieves FID 17.25 and KLD 0.036, dramatically outperforming the best unpaired method LUD-VAE (FID 35.31, KLD 0.108) and even the best fully paired method NeCA-W (FID 19.96, KLD 0.030). On the SIDD benchmark with full paired data, finetuning NAFNet with SeNM-VAE-generated samples yields 40.38 dB PSNR, the best among all compared restoration networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unpaired noise modeling baselines (C2N, DeFlow, LUD-VAE), SeNM-VAE with just 10 paired samples achieves FID 17.25 vs. 33.97–39.45 for unpaired methods, and PSNR 36.73 vs. 33.82–34.91, a gap of over 2 dB. Against fully paired noise modeling methods (DANet, Flow-sRGB, NeCA-W), SeNM-VAE with only 1% paired data (FID 15.10, KLD 0.020) outperforms the SOTA NeCA-W (FID 19.96, KLD 0.030) trained on 100% paired data. For downstream denoising, SeNM-VAE with 10 pairs (37.96 dB) beats all self-supervised methods including SDAP (37.55 dB) and SCPGabNet (36.53 dB) on SIDD validation, and with 1% pairs (39.39 dB) nearly matches fully supervised DRUNet (39.55 dB). On the DND benchmark, SeNM-VAE with 1% pairs (39.47 dB) surpasses fully supervised VDN (39.38 dB) and approaches DeamNet (39.63 dB). For SR on AIM19 and NTIRE20, SeNM-VAE (22.48 and 25.91 dB) outperforms both the supervised ESRGAN (21.47 and 25.05 dB) and the best unpaired method LUD-VAE (22.25 and 25.78 dB) using only 10 paired samples. In the finetuning experiment (Table 3), SeNM-VAE-augmented NAFNet achieves 40.49 dB on SIDD validation, surpassing NAFNet alone (40.30 dB), Restormer (40.02 dB), and MAXIM (39.96 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

SeNM-VAE builds on the hierarchical VAE architecture (Vahdat & Kautz 2020; Child 2020) and the mixture-of-experts variational decomposition (Shi et al. 2019) to construct a principled semi-supervised degradation model. It departs from prior deep degradation modeling work (DeFlow, LUD-VAE, DANet, NeCA-W) which operates exclusively on either paired or unpaired data, by introducing a graphical model with two latent variables (content z and degradation z_n) whose variational inference objective naturally decomposes into losses for paired, source, and target domains. Unlike heuristic semi-supervised restoration methods (CycleGAN losses, TV priors), the loss function here is derived from the cELBO, providing theoretical grounding for how unpaired data contributes to learning the conditional distribution p(y|x).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a principled variational framework can bridge the gap between self-supervised and fully supervised denoising with as few as 10 paired samples, which is practically significant given the cost and misalignment issues of collecting real paired data. The semi-supervised formulation is general enough to apply to any degradation type (noise, blur, compression), as shown by the SR experiments. Limitations include evaluation primarily on denoising and SR tasks, reliance on a fixed hierarchical depth (L=7), and the assumption that the degradation process is stationary across the training domain. Open questions include whether the mixture decomposition is truly necessary versus a simpler amortized inference, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame restoration where temporal consistency matters.
