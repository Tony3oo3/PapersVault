## Problem

Existing 6D pose estimation and tracking methods are fragmented: they are either instance-level (requiring per-object training), category-level (limited to predefined categories), or handle only one of two setups (model-based with CAD vs. model-free with reference images) and only one of two tasks (single-frame estimation vs. video tracking). No prior work provides a single unified framework that instantly generalizes to arbitrary novel objects across all four task-setup combinations without fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGBD image plus either a textured CAD model (model-based) or approximately 16 reference images (model-free), along with a 2D detection from an off-the-shelf detector (Mask R-CNN or CNOS). In the model-free setup, a per-object neural field is trained once in seconds from the reference images to enable RGBD rendering. The output is a 6D pose (rotation in SO(3) + translation in R³) for estimation, or a continuous pose sequence at ~32 Hz for tracking, with pose initialization via icosphere sampling (N_s viewpoints × N_i in-plane rotations) feeding into iterative refinement.

## Method

FoundationPose unifies both setups through an object-centric neural field built on SDF representation with multi-resolution hash encoding, which serves as a drop-in rendering replacement for CAD models in the model-free case. Training data is generated at scale via an LLM-aided pipeline: ChatGPT produces appearance prompts for Objaverse/GSO assets, which are textured with TexFusion and rendered photorealistically in Isaac Sim with physics simulation. The pose estimation pipeline consists of (1) icosphere-based global initialization, (2) a transformer-based refinement network that takes a single rendered view and a pose-conditioned crop to predict disentangled translation and rotation updates, and (3) a hierarchical two-level pose ranking module that first scores each hypothesis independently then performs cross-hypothesis multi-head self-attention to select the best pose. The ranking is trained with a pose-conditioned contrastive triplet loss that only compares pairs where the positive sample is within a geodesic distance threshold of ground truth, avoiding ambiguous comparisons among far-off poses.

## Results

On LINEMOD (model-free, ADD-0.1d), FoundationPose achieves 99.9% average across 13 objects, dramatically outperforming the best prior method FS6D+ICP (91.5%) and OnePose++ (76.9%). On YCB-Video (model-free, ADD AUC), it reaches 91.5% versus 42.1% for FS6D-DPM and 26.2% for LoFTR. For model-based pose estimation on BOP datasets (AR), it scores 83.3% mean, surpassing MegaPose-RGBD (58.6%) and even the instance-level SurfEmb+ICP (79.7%). In pose tracking on YCBInEOAT (ADD-S AUC), it achieves 96.42%, beating the instance-level se(3)-TrackNet (95.53%) and Bundle-SDF (93.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In model-free pose estimation, FoundationPose (99.9% avg ADD-0.1d on LINEMOD) outperforms all baselines by wide margins: FS6D+ICP (91.5%, requires fine-tuning and ICP), LatentFusion (87.1%), OnePose++ (76.9%), and OnePose (63.6%). On YCB-Video (ADD AUC), the gap is even more extreme: 91.5% vs. FS6D-DPM 42.1%, LoFTR 26.2%, and PREDATOR 24.3%. In model-based estimation (AR on BOP), it beats MegaPose-RGBD (58.6%), GCPose (67.9% on T-LESS only), OVE6D (49.6%), OSOP (48.2%), and Zephyr (59.8%), and even surpasses the instance-level SurfEmb+ICP (79.7%). For pose tracking on YCBInEOAT, it outperforms se(3)-TrackNet (95.53% ADD-S, requires instance-level training and GT init), Bundle-SDF (93.77%), Bundle-Track (92.53%), Wüthrich (89.18%), and RGF (39.90%). On YCB-Video tracking, it beats ICG (86.4% ADD), RGF (59.2%), Wüthrich (78.0%), DeepIM (82.3%), and PoseRBPF+SDF (87.5%), with the only method matching or exceeding it being se(3)-TrackNet (93.0%) which requires instance-level training and re-initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoundationPose unifies four previously separate problem settings—model-based and model-free 6D pose estimation and tracking—into a single framework, departing from prior work (MegaPose, FS6D, OnePose, BundleSDF) that each addresses only one or two of these combinations. The neural object field for model-free rendering directly builds on BundleSDF's hybrid SDF and NeuS's truncated volumetric rendering, while the disentangled geometry-appearance representation follows Yariv et al. The key architectural departure is the transformer-based pose refinement and hierarchical contrastive ranking network, replacing the CNN-only or correspondence-based pipelines in prior render-and-compare methods. The LLM-aided synthetic data generation pipeline (ChatGPT prompts → TexFusion) is a novel contribution that scales training diversity beyond the random texture pasting used by FS6D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single trained model handles arbitrary novel objects in both CAD-based and reference-image-based settings, eliminating the need for per-object or per-category training that plagues instance-level and category-level methods. The LLM-aided data generation pipeline is a transferable technique for scaling synthetic training in any vision task requiring diverse 3D assets. Limitations include the requirement for RGBD input (not pure RGB), per-object neural field training (though fast, ~seconds), and restriction to single rigid objects. The authors note that extending to state estimation beyond single rigid objects (e.g., articulated or deformable) is a natural next step, and the gap between model-free and model-based performance, while small, remains an open question for textureless or highly symmetric objects.
