## Problem

Existing 3D pose transfer methods require clean, pre-processed pose sources (e.g., meshes registered to SMPL), involving cumbersome pipelines that take 1–2 minutes per frame and prevent real-time use. These methods are also fragile to noisy inputs, raw scans, and unseen domains, and no prior work has studied the robustness of 3D pose transfer or applied adversarial learning to this generative task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240957/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a source pose as either a clean mesh, a noisy point cloud, or a raw 3D scan, paired with a target identity mesh (up to ~27,000 vertices), and produces a pose-transferred 3D mesh preserving the identity's intrinsic shape. Unlike prior methods that require the pose source to be pre-registered to a parametric model, 3D-PoseMAE can consume raw scans directly, and the target mesh vertex count is flexible across identities.

## Method

The architecture, 3D-PoseMAE, combines a multi-scale masked encoder (downsampling the pose point cloud by 2^(i-1) for three scales, masking at ratio φ, and aggregating latent codes via Mini-pointNet) with a decoder that replaces spatial attention with a channel-wise cross-attention whose map is [C′,C′] rather than [N,N], reducing model size and avoiding redundant geometric detail. For adversarial training, the paper introduces a novel pose-transfer adversarial function f_adv = ‖F(M_pose, M_id; θ) − M_GT‖^(−1) that pushes generated output away from ground truth, and computes adversarial perturbations on-the-fly via FGM/PGD gradients during backpropagation rather than using pre-computed samples. Training is two-stage: 200 epochs on clean data to stabilize the model, followed by adversarial training where the model is set to eval mode to compute the perturbation, then switched to train mode to learn from the adversarial input. This is the first 3D generative pipeline to jointly generate and defend against adversarial samples within a single training loop.

## Results

On the SMPL-NPT dataset with clean samples, 3D-PoseMAE achieves a PMD of 0.6 (×10⁻⁴) on the seen protocol and 3.4 on the unseen protocol, matching GC-Transformer on seen and outperforming it on unseen. Under adversarial FGM attacks (budget ε=0.08), 3D-PoseMAE with adversarial training achieves a PMD of 16.9, substantially better than GC-Transformer (19.3) and NPT (59.0). On cross-dataset evaluation, 3D-PoseMAE reduces PMD from 25.21 to 13.70 on raw DFAUST scans and from 12.66 to 6.13 on Gaussian-noised SMPL-NPT meshes compared to NPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DT (3D-Coded) [19], 3D-PoseMAE achieves a large margin of improvement, reducing PMD from 7.3 to 0.6 (seen) and from 7.2 to 3.4 (unseen). Compared to NPT [41], 3D-PoseMAE halves the unseen PMD (3.4 vs. 9.3) and slightly improves the seen score (0.6 vs. 1.1); under adversarial evaluation, the gap widens dramatically (16.9 vs. 59.0 with adversarial training). Against 3D-CoreNet [37], 3D-PoseMAE matches the seen performance (0.6 vs. 0.8) and is the only method that reports an unseen score, where 3D-CoreNet is not evaluated. Versus GC-Transformer [10], the closest competitor, 3D-PoseMAE ties on the seen protocol (both 0.6) and wins on unseen (3.4 vs. 4.0); under adversarial attacks with training, 3D-PoseMAE also leads (16.9 vs. 19.3). On raw DFAUST scans, 3D-PoseMAE nearly halves the PMD of NPT (13.70 vs. 25.21), demonstrating clear generalizability to real-world noisy inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GC-Transformer [10] for the 3D pose transfer decoder architecture and on the MAE paradigm [20] for the encoder, but departs from both in key ways: it replaces spatial attention with a channel-wise cross-attention mechanism and introduces a multi-scale masking strategy tailored to extrinsic (pose) attribute learning. More fundamentally, it is the first to apply adversarial training to 3D pose transfer, departing from prior 3D adversarial work [46, 50] that relies on pre-computed adversarial samples and discriminative loss functions. The proposed PT adversarial function (inverse reconstruction error) and on-the-fly perturbation computation via backpropagation represent a new formulation for attacking generative 3D models, as opposed to the targeted/misclassification-based attacks used in 3D classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling end-to-end 3D pose transfer directly from raw scans without the 1–2 minute SMPL registration pipeline, which is a meaningful step toward real-time applications in AR/VR and animation. The adversarial function is presented as pluggable into other attack methods, potentially generalizing to other 3D generative tasks. However, limitations include evaluation only on human body meshes (SMPL-NPT, FAUST, DFAUST), a 2.7× training time overhead from adversarial training, and the fact that robustness gains, while large in absolute PMD, are demonstrated on relatively small-scale datasets. Open questions include whether the channel-wise attention and multi-scale masking transfer to other 3D generation tasks, and whether the adversarial function generalizes beyond pose transfer to broader 3D generative problems.
