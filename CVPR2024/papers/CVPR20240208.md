## Problem

Existing protein representation learning methods treat all amino acids equally, ignoring the well-established biological fact that only a small subset of critical amino acids determine a protein's structure and function. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a neural clustering framework that automatically discovers these critical components by jointly considering both primary (1D sequence) and tertiary (3D spatial) structure information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240208/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein as a graph where each node is an amino acid with a 256-dim feature vector (combining one-hot type encoding, 3D coordinates, orientation vectors, and sequential position) and edges encode spatial or sequential proximity. Through T=4 iterative clustering rounds, the number of amino acids is progressively reduced by a nomination fraction ω (default 40%), ultimately producing a compact set of critical amino acids whose features are projected to a class score vector. The framework handles both single-label (fold, reaction) and multi-label (EC, GO) classification, and is robust to up to 40% missing 3D coordinates.

## Method

The core contribution is an iterative neural clustering pipeline that stacks three steps per round: (1) Spherical Cluster Initialization groups amino acids within an expanding radius (r, 2r, 3r, 4r across iterations) to form local clusters; (2) Cluster Representation Extraction uses cross-attention between the medoid and constituent nodes to produce a weighted cluster representation, incorporating relative 3D geometry, orientations, and 1D sequence distance; (3) Cluster Nomination applies a GCN to score each cluster and retains only the top-ω fraction whose medoids seed the next iteration. The entire process is trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy loss, so the attention weights and nomination scores are implicitly learned from classification supervision. This hierarchical downsampling yields a compact, interpretable representation where the surviving amino acids correspond to functionally critical motifs, as validated by UMAP/DBSCAN visualization showing that same-cluster proteins share structural motifs relevant to their enzyme reaction class.

## Results

The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four protein classification tasks. On EC number prediction, it reaches F_max = 0.866, surpassing the previous best CDConv (0.820) by 5.6%. On protein fold classification (Fold scenario), it achieves 63.1% accuracy, outperforming CDConv (56.7%) by 6.4% and ProNet (52.7%) by 10.4%. On enzyme reaction classification, it attains 89.6% accuracy, exceeding CDConv (88.5%) by 1.1%. On GO term prediction, it sets new records on all three sub-tasks (BP: 0.474, MF: 0.675, CC: 0.483), with the largest margin over GearNet (0.400 BP, 0.581 MF, 0.430 CC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 15 baselines across every task and metric. The closest competitor is CDConv [22] (ICLR 2023), which the method surpasses by 5.6% F_max on EC (0.866 vs 0.820), 2.1% on GO-BP (0.474 vs 0.453), 2.1% on GO-MF (0.675 vs 0.654), 6.4% on Fold accuracy (63.1 vs 56.7), and 1.1% on Reaction accuracy (89.6 vs 88.5). GearNet [94] (ICLR 2023) is the second-strongest baseline, with the method leading by 6.9% on EC, 7.4% on GO-BP, 9.4% on GO-MF, 14.8% on Fold, and 4.3% on Reaction. ProNet [81] (ICLR 2023) is beaten by 10.4% on Fold and 3.2% on Reaction. Among protein language models (Table 6), the method matches or exceeds ESM-1b [68] on EC (0.866 vs 0.864) and GO-CC (0.483 vs 0.488, slightly behind), while substantially outperforming it on Fold (63.1 vs 26.8) and Reaction (89.6 vs 83.1) without any pre-training. Earlier baselines such as GCN [46], GAT [79], GVP [44], 3DCNN [15], GraphQA [7], and New IEConv [36] are all surpassed by large margins (e.g., +22.3% over GCN and +34% over GAT on Reaction accuracy). Notably, the method also matches CDConv on Family classification (99.6%) and slightly exceeds it on Superfamily (81.2 vs 77.7), while maintaining comparable inference speed (10.9 ms vs 10.5 ms per prediction).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in protein representation learning—where GNNs (CDConv, GearNet, ProNet, GVP) treat all amino acids as equally important nodes—by introducing an iterative neural clustering framework that progressively identifies and focuses on the most critical amino acids. Conceptually, it repurposes the spherical cluster initialization idea from PointNet++ (3D geometry) and combines it with cross-attention-based cluster representation and GCN-based cluster nomination, creating a hierarchical downsampling pipeline unique to the protein domain. The biological motivation (that a small subset of amino acids determines protein function) is well-established in the literature, but no prior deep learning method had operationalized this insight through a learnable, end-to-end clustering procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework achieves state-of-the-art results across four standard protein classification benchmarks while simultaneously providing interpretability through the identified critical amino acids, which visualizations confirm align with known functional motifs. A notable open question raised by the authors is whether this clustering paradigm can be combined with large-scale protein language models (ESM, ProtBERT), since their method matches or exceeds models pretrained on billions of sequences for fold classification without any pretraining. Limitations include the dependence on 3D structural coordinates (though robustness to up to 40% missing coordinates is demonstrated) and the fixed number of iterations (T=4), which may not generalize to all protein families or tasks.
