## Problem

Active recognition agents rely on recognition modules trained under the closed-world assumption, where softmax outputs are used as confidence measures. When the agent encounters unexpected inputs—such as the target being absent from view or heavily occluded—these models produce overconfident but incorrect predictions, leading to misleading rewards that degrade policy learning. This paper addresses the gap by introducing per-step uncertainty quantification and principled evidence fusion into the active recognition loop.

## Input / Output

At each of T=10 timesteps, the agent receives a 640×800 RGB frame from a camera mounted at 1 m height, along with a ground-truth bounding box query identifying the target object. The per-step output is a belief function over K=27 object classes plus an overall uncertainty mass (summing to 1), and the final output is a class label obtained by Dempster-Shafer combination of all T per-step opinions. The agent selects from a 5-action space (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, look_up, look_down) with 0.25 m steps and 10° rotations.

## Method

The method formulates active recognition as sequential evidence gathering under Dempster-Shafer theory. A Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50 backbone) is trained with an evidential deep learning loss (Dirichlet prior + KL regularization) to output per-step class evidence and uncertainty, replacing the standard softmax head. Across T observations, per-step beliefs are fused via the Dempster-Shafer combination rule (a commutative, uncertainty-weighted late fusion), so that high-uncertainty frames contribute less to the final prediction. The policy is trained with PPO using an uncertainty-aware reward r^t = b_y^t (the belief mass for the correct class), which naturally suppresses rewards for actions leading to ambiguous or target-absent views. Training is staged: the recognition model is first trained with a heuristic fixation policy, then the policy is optimized with PPO while the recognition model is frozen.

## Results

On the proposed MP3D-based active recognition dataset (13,200 test instances, 27 categories), the full method achieves 64.4% top-1 and 84.3% top-3 success rate overall, with the largest gains on hard instances (58.0% top-1, +8.3% over single-view). The uncertainty-aware policy learning yields a +3.5% overall improvement over the single-view baseline, compared to Amodal-Rec's -0.2% degradation. The method also outperforms the Fixation heuristic by +2.6% overall and the Random policy by +11.0% overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary learned baseline is Amodal-Rec [55], which uses a convolutional GRU for early feature-level fusion and softmax-based predictions. The proposed method outperforms Amodal-Rec by +7.2% top-1 overall (64.4% vs. 57.2%) and +6.6% top-3 (84.3% vs. 77.7%), with the gap widening dramatically on hard instances (+15.1% top-1: 58.0% vs. 42.9%). Notably, Amodal-Rec's success rate actually *decreases* as more steps are taken (−0.2% overall), whereas the proposed method improves by +3.5%, indicating that early feature fusion degrades under open-world conditions. Against heuristic policies, the proposed method beats Fixation by +2.6% overall and Random by +11.0%, confirming that the learned uncertainty-aware policy is essential. The single-view variant of the proposed method (60.9%) already surpasses Amodal-Rec's full pipeline (57.2%), suggesting that the evidential recognition module itself provides a substantial advantage independent of the policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., ICLR 2018), which replaces softmax with Dirichlet-parameterized beliefs to quantify per-class uncertainty, and active recognition via reinforcement learning (Yang et al., ICCV 2019; Jayaraman & Grauman, ECCV 2016), where an agent learns movement policies to improve object classification. The key departure is that prior active recognition methods treat the recognition module as a closed-world classifier whose softmax output serves directly as the RL reward, making them vulnerable to unexpected inputs (absent targets, heavy occlusion). Here, the authors reframe the recognition episode as a sequential evidence-gathering process under Dempster-Shafer theory, using per-step uncertainty to both gate the reward signal and weight the final fused prediction via subjective-logic combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uncertainty-aware reward and evidence-fusion mechanism offer a principled way to make embodied recognition agents robust to the open-world conditions (out-of-view targets, partial occlusion) that are inevitable in real deployment but poorly handled by closed-world classifiers. The new 13,200-instance MP3D-based dataset with explicit difficulty annotations (visibility, distance, observed pixels) fills a gap in evaluation methodology for active recognition. Open questions include adaptive stopping (the agent currently takes a fixed T=10 steps), generalization beyond a single simulator, and whether the evidential fusion framework scales to larger category spaces or multi-object settings. The paper also notes that the fixed-step evaluation protocol limits the practical utility of the learned policy.
