## Problem

Autoregressive sequence-to-sequence vision-language models must be executed T times to generate an output sequence of length T, creating a linear inference cost that limits their practical deployment. Existing non-autoregressive approaches for vision-language tasks (e.g., set-to-set matching) handle orderless visual outputs well but struggle with language-focused tasks where inter-word dependencies are strong. The paper asks whether a transformer architecture can perform joint decoding of an entire output sequence in a single forward pass while matching autoregressive accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (embedded via a ResNet CNN backbone into W×H spatial tokens of dimension D with positional encoding) concatenated with a BPE-tokenized text prompt, forming a unified input sequence for the shared OFA encoder. The decoder produces a variable-length output sequence whose content is task-dependent: natural language tokens for VQA, captioning, and entailment, or fixed-length bounding box coordinate tokens for grounding. The number of decoder input tokens is a fixed hyperparameter (e.g., 5 for grounding, 20 for captioning) set larger than the maximum target sequence length, and a blank token is included in the vocabulary for the collapse operation.

## Method

NARVL repurposes the OFA autoregressive decoder by replacing its sequential input with a fixed-length layer of Learnable Query Tokens (LQTs), enabling all output tokens to be generated in parallel via bidirectional attention in a single forward pass. Training uses a novel Query-CTC (Q-CTC) loss that marginalizes the cross-entropy over all valid monotonic alignments from the N redundant LQT positions to the T target tokens (N > T), where validity is defined by a collapse operation that removes blanks and merges consecutive repeats. Unlike standard CTC which marginalizes over encoder-derived paths, Q-CTC marginalizes over paths from the learnable query parameters, making the loss a function of both encoder/decoder weights θ and the LQT parameters q. Knowledge distillation from a same-size autoregressive teacher model is applied for long-sequence tasks (captioning, VQA) to reduce the solution space and make training targets more deterministic. At inference, the model runs exactly once with greedy decoding followed by the same collapse rule, reducing complexity from O(T) sequential steps to O(1) parallel generation.

## Results

NARVL achieves on-par or superior accuracy to its autoregressive OFA counterpart across four vision-language tasks while reducing inference time by 1.4×–12.7×. On visual grounding (RefCOCO), NARVL-base outperforms AR by +0.83 average Acc@0.5 (88.78/90.63/84.67 vs 88.15/90.08/83.45) at 2.4× speed (54.8 ms vs 133.6 ms). On VQA, NARVL-KD-base achieves 75.69/75.75 (test-dev/test-std) at 50.7 ms, a 12.7× speedup over AR's 645.1 ms, with a ~1.8 point accuracy drop. On image captioning, NARVL-KD-base reaches BLEU@4 36.4 / CIDEr 123.1 at 51.2 ms (7.2× faster than AR's 366.0 ms), while NARVL-KD-huge narrows the gap to BLEU@4 40.1 / CIDEr 136.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On visual grounding, NARVL-huge achieves state-of-the-art Acc@0.5 on all RefCOCO subsets (91.8/94.24/88.01), surpassing OFA* (91.24/93.41/87.16), UNICORN (88.29/90.42/83.06), MDETR (86.75/89.58/81.41), VILLA, and UNITER, while running 5.2× faster than OFA* (150.8 ms vs 284.9 ms). On SNLI-VE visual entailment, NARVL-huge matches OFA (91.1 vs 91.2 test accuracy) and substantially outperforms all prior methods (Florence 80.4, SimVLM 80.3, VLMo 80.0). On VQA, NARVL-KD-huge (79.59/79.39 test-dev/test-std) exceeds OFA-base beam search (77.48/77.58) and all prior baselines (SimVLM 80.3/80.0, Florence 80.4/80.4 are close but NARVL is 8× faster), while NARVL-KD-base (75.59/75.75) trails OFA-base beam search by ~1.9 points. On MSCOCO captioning, NARVL-KD-huge (BLEU@4 40.1, CIDEr 136.7) remains below OFA (43.9, 145.3) and LEMON (41.5, 139.1), but is 4.2× faster than OFA (130.6 ms vs 545.1 ms); NARVL-KD-base (36.4, 123.1) is competitive with OSCAR (37.4, 127.8) at 10.7× lower latency. Overall, NARVL wins or ties on grounding and entailment, trades a small accuracy drop for large speed gains on VQA and captioning, and consistently outperforms all non-OFA baselines on every task.

## Relation to Prior Work

NARVL repurposes the OFA autoregressive vision-language model by replacing its sequential decoder with a parallel decoder driven by a fixed set of learnable query tokens (LQTs), rather than the encoder outputs used in prior NAR NLP approaches (Gu et al., 2018; Gu & Kong, 2021). The training signal is a novel Query-CTC loss that adapts the CTC framework from speech recognition (Graves et al., 2006) to marginalize over alignment paths from LQTs to output tokens, a formulation not previously applied to the visual domain. This design deliberately departs from set-to-set decoders (DETR, MDETR) that excel on orderless visual tasks but struggle with ordered text generation, positioning NARVL as a middle ground between autoregressive and set-based non-autoregressive paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.4–12.7× inference speedup (constant-time single forward pass vs. T sequential steps) makes unified vision-language models far more deployable in latency-sensitive settings, and the fact that NARVL matches or even exceeds AR accuracy on visual grounding suggests bidirectional attention in the decoder is genuinely beneficial for coordinate prediction. However, the best results on VQA and captioning still require knowledge distillation from a separately trained AR teacher, which the authors acknowledge is inconvenient in practice, and the conditional-independence assumption can produce merged or incoherent sequences. An open question is whether the Q-CTC formulation and LQT design can be made self-sufficient (without a teacher) as model scale or data volume increases.
