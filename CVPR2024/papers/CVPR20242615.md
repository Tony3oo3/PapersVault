## Problem

End-to-end temporal action detection (TAD) has shown promise over feature-based methods, but GPU memory constraints have restricted it to small backbones and short video inputs, preventing it from matching the performance of feature-based approaches that can leverage 1B-parameter models and long videos. No prior end-to-end TAD method had surpassed the best feature-based results, and full fine-tuning of large pretrained backbones risks catastrophic forgetting and overfitting on the relatively small TAD datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw untrimmed RGB video as a single frame sequence (not snippet-based clips), with inputs ranging from 768 to 1536 frames at 160² or 224² resolution, processed through a frozen video backbone (e.g., VideoMAEv2-giant). After the backbone, features are resized to fixed temporal lengths (192, 768, or 768 depending on dataset) and fed into an ActionFormer detection head that outputs action proposals with predicted start/end times, category labels, and confidence scores evaluated at multiple tIoU thresholds.

## Method

AdaTAD introduces a Temporal-Informative Adapter (TIA), a lightweight bottleneck module inserted between backbone layers that combines a standard down-project → up-project adapter with a temporal depth-wise convolution (kernel size 3) to aggregate context from adjacent frames, plus a learnable scalar α to control output amplitude. Only TIA parameters (≈4.7% of the backbone) are updated during fine-tuning, with the backbone kept frozen, which simultaneously reduces trainable parameters and training memory. A second variant, AdaTAD†, places TIAs externally alongside the backbone rather than inside it, so backpropagation never traverses the original network, cutting memory further and enabling the combination of a 1B-parameter VideoMAEv2-giant backbone with 1536 frames at 224² resolution. The method also adopts frame-level representation (treating the whole video as one clip) over the conventional snippet representation, which achieves comparable or better mAP at roughly 8× lower memory cost.

## Results

AdaTAD with VideoMAEv2-giant (1B params) and 1536 frames at 224² resolution achieves 76.9% avg mAP on THUMOS14 and 41.9% avg mAP on ActivityNet-1.3, setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. The lighter AdaTAD† variant (adapters outside the backbone) reaches 75.4% on THUMOS14 at 43.6 GB memory, surpassing the previous best feature-based result (InternVideo, 71.5%) by 3.9 points. On EPIC-Kitchens 100, AdaTAD with VideoMAE-L achieves 29.3% avg mAP on both Verb and Noun tasks, outperforming the best feature-based method TriDet (25.4% Verb, 23.8% Noun).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaTAD with VideoMAEv2-giant (1536×224²) achieves 76.9% avg mAP on THUMOS14, outperforming the previous best feature-based method InternVideo [57] (71.5%) by 5.4 points and the best prior end-to-end method Re²TAL [69] (59.4%) by 17.5 points. On ActivityNet-1.3, AdaTAD reaches 41.9% avg mAP versus InternVideo's 39.0%, a 2.9-point gain. With the same SlowFast-R50 backbone, AdaTAD (66.0% THUMOS14, 4.3 GB) substantially outperforms E2E-TAD [33] (54.2%, 12 GB) by 11.8 points while using less than half the memory. On EPIC-Kitchens 100, AdaTAD with VideoMAE-L (29.3% Verb, 29.3% Noun) beats TriDet [47] (25.4% Verb, 23.8% Noun) and ActionFormer [66] with the same backbone (23.5% Verb, 21.9% Noun), establishing the first end-to-end result on this dataset. The scaling trend is consistent: from VideoMAE-S (68.8%) to VideoMAEv2-g (74.8%) on THUMOS14, each size increase yields 1–3 points of improvement, confirming the scaling law holds for TAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established trends in temporal action detection: end-to-end training (E2E-TAD, TALLFormer, Re2TAL) and scaling up backbones (VideoMAEv2, InternVideo). It departs from prior end-to-end methods by replacing full fine-tuning with parameter-efficient adapter tuning, specifically extending the standard bottleneck adapter [Houlsby et al., ICML 2019] with temporal depth-wise convolutions to form the Temporal-Informative Adapter (TIA). The detection head is ActionFormer [Zhang et al., ECCV 2022], and the frame-level representation scheme (as opposed to snippet-based) is adopted from TALLFormer and Re2TAL, though the paper provides the first systematic comparison of the two under identical conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 1B-parameter end-to-end model with 1536 frames can outperform the best feature-based methods by a large margin (75.4% vs 71.5% mAP on THUMOS14), this work suggests a paradigm shift in TAD from offline feature extraction plus detectors to unified end-to-end training. The TIA module and the AdaTAD† external-adapter placement offer a practical recipe for scaling video models under memory constraints, likely applicable beyond TAD to other dense video prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the scaling trend continues beyond 1B parameters, whether the approach transfers to other video understanding tasks (e.g., video segmentation, grounding), and whether the modest performance gap between AdaTAD and AdaTAD† can be closed without sacrificing the memory savings.
