## Problem

Enforcing strict structural constraints on neural network components—such as requiring convolutional filters to be valid wavelets—is infeasible with standard soft-constraint loss terms, which only satisfy constraints approximately on average. Since a wavelet is only a wavelet when its defining equations hold exactly, a rigorous mechanism is needed to keep network parameters on the constraint manifold throughout training.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single 2D medical image (CT spleen at 224×224 or T2-weighted MRI prostate at 192×192) and outputs an organ boundary contour parameterized by arc length, represented not as a pixel mask but as a multi-resolution wavelet decomposition: approximation coefficients at levels j₀ through j₂ and detail coefficients at levels j₀ through j₁−1, one set per spatial component. Only the approximation coefficients at the highest resolution level j₂ are supervised during training; the contour is reconstructed via the inverse discrete wavelet transform (Mallat's Pyramid Algorithm).

## Method

The core contribution is the Constrained Empirical Risk Minimization (CERM) framework, which formulates wavelet validity as a finite system of equations F_M(h)=0 (M+1 equations in 2M−1 unknowns encoding orthonormality and non-vanishing Fourier transform of the scaling map). Under a non-degeneracy condition, the solution set is an (M−2)-dimensional Riemannian submanifold, and SGD is performed intrinsically on this manifold using graph coordinates constructed via the Implicit Function Theorem, so constraints are satisfied exactly at every step. The architecture is a U-Net hybrid: a 2D residual convolutional encoder feeds a fully connected bottleneck that predicts the lowest-resolution wavelet coefficients, and a 1D decoder applies the Mallat Pyramid Algorithm with learnable wavelet filters (constrained by F_M) to reconstruct the contour at higher resolutions, with skip connections supplying intermediate detail coefficients. Two separate optimizers handle the unconstrained encoder/MLP parameters (flat-space SGD/Adam) and the constrained wavelet filter parameters (Riemannian SGD on the submanifold), with distinct learning-rate schedules for each.

## Results

On the Medical Segmentation Decathlon test sets, the best wavelet network (Order 7) achieves a Dice score of 0.921 on spleen CT and 0.935 on prostate MRI (Order 4/5), outperforming the nnUNet baseline (0.914 and 0.896, respectively). The wavelet networks use only 5–10M parameters compared to nnUNet's 40M (spleen) and 18M (prostate), while also being faster at inference (12.5–14 ms vs. 17.8–23.4 ms per batch of 32). The prostate task shows the largest gain (+0.039 Dice), while the spleen improvement is more modest (+0.007 Dice).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the 2D nnUNet [Isensee et al., Nature Methods 2021], a self-configuring segmentation architecture. The wavelet networks outperform nnUNet on both organs: on prostate MRI, the best wavelet orders (4 and 5) reach 0.935 Dice versus nnUNet's 0.896, a substantial +0.039 improvement; on spleen CT, Order 7 achieves 0.921 versus nnUNet's 0.914, a smaller +0.007 gain. Notably, the wavelet networks achieve these results with far fewer parameters (5–10M vs. 40M/18M for nnUNet) and lower inference latency (12.5–14 ms vs. 17.8–23.4 ms per batch of 32). The nnUNet also exhibits much higher variance on the spleen test set (std 1.74·10⁻¹ vs. ~7·10⁻² for wavelet models), suggesting less stable generalization. Overall, the constrained wavelet formulation provides a clear performance advantage, particularly on the prostate task, while being more parameter-efficient and faster at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the CERM framework, which formulates hard parameter constraints as a Riemannian manifold problem and performs SGD intrinsically on that manifold using graph coordinates derived from the Implicit Function Theorem. It explicitly departs from the dominant 'soft constraint' paradigm (penalty terms in the loss) and from Geometric Deep Learning, which handles only equivariance-type constraints, by addressing general nonlinear constraints of the form F(W)=0. The wavelet application repurposes classical MRA theory (Mallat, Daubechies) by treating the low-pass filter coefficients as learnable parameters constrained to the wavelet manifold, while the network architecture is a hybrid U-Net with a 1D wavelet decoder. The Riemannian optimization machinery builds on prior work (Bonnabel, Boumal) but claims a fully intrinsic, chart-based approach that avoids the extrinsic projection problems of earlier methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CERM framework is a clean, general-purpose tool for enforcing exact structural constraints during training, with potential applications beyond wavelets (e.g., divergence-free fields, closed curves, surfaces). However, the empirical validation is narrow: two 2D organs, modest Dice improvements over nnUNet (up to ~4 points on prostate), and the computational overhead of solving linear systems for the Riemannian metric at each step limits scalability. Open questions include whether the framework extends to higher-dimensional wavelets (tensor products), larger networks, and more complex constraint geometries, and whether the parameter efficiency advantage (5–10M vs. 18–40M parameters) persists at scale. The disconnected-manifold topology issue also raises practical concerns about initialization sensitivity.
