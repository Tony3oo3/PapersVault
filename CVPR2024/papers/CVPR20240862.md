## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion models operate in an open-loop fashion, generating images in a single pass without verifying whether the output actually matches the prompt. This leads to persistent errors in object counting, spatial relationships, and attribute binding, even in state-of-the-art systems like DALL-E 3. Existing remedies either require expensive retraining or use LLMs only for prompt pre-processing, leaving downstream generation errors unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and an initial image produced by any diffusion model (including API-only systems like DALL-E 3) as input, and outputs a corrected image whose objects, counts, attributes, and spatial layout align with the prompt. For the editing variant, the prompt instead specifies both the current scene and the desired modification (e.g., 'replace the banana with an orange while keeping the two apples unchanged'). The pipeline is entirely training-free and operates on standard RGB images at the resolution of the underlying diffusion model (e.g., 512×512 or 1024×1024 for SDXL).

## Method

SLD is a closed-loop, training-free framework with two components: (1) LLM-driven object detection, where an LLM parser extracts key object phrases (names, attributes, quantities) from the prompt and an open-vocabulary detector (OWL-ViT v2) localizes them into bounding boxes; and (2) LLM-controlled assessment and correction, where a GPT-4 controller compares detected boxes against the prompt and outputs corrected bounding boxes with object IDs to track correspondence. The difference between original and corrected boxes is translated into four latent-space operations—addition, deletion, repositioning, and attribute modification—executed via SAM segmentation, Gaussian-noise reinitialization, and masked latent compositing on a base diffusion model (Stable Diffusion / LMD+). A final forward diffusion pass uses frozen steps for unmodified regions followed by unfrozen steps to blend edits seamlessly. The process iterates up to K rounds, terminating when the LLM outputs an unchanged layout, guaranteeing alignment up to detector and LLM accuracy.

## Results

On the LMD benchmark, SLD applied to DALL-E 3 achieves 78.5% average accuracy across four tasks (Negation, Numeracy, Attribute, Spatial), a +26.5% improvement over DALL-E 3 alone (52.0%). When applied to LMD+, SLD reaches 88.3% average accuracy (+9.0% over LMD+ at 79.3%), surpassing all other baselines. The largest per-task gains are in Numeracy (DALL-E 3: 38%→61%; LMD+: 82%→98%) and Spatial reasoning (DALL-E 3: 71%→83%; LMD+: 86%→92%). A second round of self-correction on Stable Diffusion further lifts average accuracy from 54.5% to 60.0% (Table 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLD applied to LMD+ (88.3% avg) is the strongest result in the paper, beating LMD+ alone (79.3%) by 9.0 points and DALL-E 3 + SLD (78.5%) by 9.8 points. SLD on DALL-E 3 (78.5%) substantially outperforms all other standalone baselines: LayoutGPT+GLIGEN (51.3%), BoxDiff (40.0%), Backward Guidance (38.0%), and MultiDiffusion (30.5%). Notably, LMD+ without SLD (79.3%) slightly edges out DALL-E 3 + SLD (78.5%) on average, but SLD on LMD+ dominates every individual task. In the image editing comparison (Fig. 6), SLD succeeds at object-level edits (e.g., replacing an apple with a pumpkin) where InstructPix2Pix completely fails and DiffEdit produces inaccurate localizations. The multi-round ablation (Table 2) shows that one round of SLD on Stable Diffusion already lifts accuracy from 28.5% to 54.5%, with a second round adding a further 5.5 points to 60.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLD builds directly on LMD+ (Lian et al., 2023) as its base model for latent space operations, extending the LLM-grounded diffusion paradigm from a single-pass open-loop generation into an iterative closed-loop self-correction process. It departs from prior LLM-controlled diffusion methods (LayoutGPT, LMD+, BoxDiff) that produce images in one iteration without verifying alignment, and from diffusion-based editing methods (InstructPix2Pix, DiffEdit, SDEdit) that handle only global style changes or limited local modifications. The key conceptual departure is the integration of an open-vocabulary detector (OWL-ViT) and an LLM controller in a feedback loop that programmatically diffs bounding boxes to derive latent-space editing operations, a mechanism absent from both the generation and editing literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLD demonstrates that a training-free, model-agnostic post-hoc correction loop can substantially close the gap between state-of-the-art generators and their prompt specifications, with the practical advantage of working through APIs like DALL-E 3 without access to model weights. The unification of text-to-image generation and object-level editing under a single pipeline (differing only in LLM instructions) simplifies what were previously separate research threads. However, the method's reliance on SAM for region segmentation introduces a clear limitation for objects with complex or ambiguous boundaries (e.g., hair), and the evaluation is confined to the LMD benchmark with 100 prompts per category, leaving open questions about robustness on more diverse or adversarial prompts and the cost of multi-round inference in practice.
