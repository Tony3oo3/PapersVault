## Problem

Existing visibility reconstruction methods for radio interferometry suffer from two coupled deficiencies: they fail to recover high-frequency components in the uv-plane (causing blurred edges and persistent artifacts in images), and their point-level Transformer encoding incurs quadratic computation cost that scales poorly with the number of sparse samples. No prior work has simultaneously addressed both the completeness of frequency reconstruction and the efficiency of encoding irregular visibility point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240469/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse visibility samples {u_s, v_s, V(u_s, v_s)} where V is a complex value at each sampled (u,v) coordinate, generated by simulating an 8-telescope EHT observation over 128×128 galaxy images. It produces a dense 128×128 complex visibility map on a regular grid, which is then inverse-Fourier-transformed into a clean radio interferometric image. The real and imaginary parts of each visibility sample are embedded separately before encoding.

## Method

PolarRec converts visibility samples from Cartesian (u,v) to polar (r, θ) coordinates, exploiting the fact that Earth-rotation-based sampling distributes points more uniformly in polar space. Two key mechanisms follow: (1) a Radial Visibility Loss (RVL) that multiplies the per-point complex visibility distance by a radial weight w₂ = (r/max(r)+1)^β, forcing the model to attend to high-frequency components far from the uv-plane center, combined with a Focal Frequency Loss weight w₁ that down-weights already-well-predicted points; (2) a group-based encoding scheme that sorts samples by angular coordinate θ, applies an MLP and adaptive average pooling within each of m angular groups to produce m group tokens, and feeds only these m tokens (rather than all n points) into a Transformer encoder. The encoder output tokens condition a multi-layer neural field via FiLM modulation, which generates the dense visibility map at arbitrary (u,v) coordinates. The neural field and encoder are jointly optimized end-to-end under RVL.

## Results

PolarRec achieves the best LFD, PSNR, and SSIM across all four galaxy morphology datasets (MG, IRSG, UTSG, EGB). On the IRSG dataset, it reaches PSNR 26.54 dB and SSIM 0.911, while on MG it achieves PSNR 24.21 dB and SSIM 0.889. In terms of computational efficiency, PolarRec with group size 32 requires only 24.40 GFLOPs at batch size 32, compared to 352.16 GFLOPs for Neural Interferometry — a 14.4× reduction in FLOPs and a 43× reduction in inference latency (5.37 ms vs. 232.04 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolarRec outperforms all four baselines on every metric (LFD, PSNR, SSIM) across all four datasets. Against the closest competitor, Neural Interferometry [31], PolarRec improves PSNR by 3.6–5.4 dB (e.g., 26.54 vs. 24.10 on IRSG) and reduces LFD by 0.25–0.52, while also cutting inference latency from 232 ms to 5.4 ms at batch size 32. Radionets [23] produce discontinuous visibility reconstructions and miss faint sources, yielding PSNR 4.5–5.1 dB lower than PolarRec. U-Net [34] (adapted from MRI reconstruction) is dominated by artifacts and achieves the lowest PSNR among deep-learning baselines (14.9–19.8 dB). The classic CLEAN algorithm [14] assumes point sources and cannot produce visibility-domain metrics (LFD = N/A), but still trails PolarRec by 5.5–6.2 dB in PSNR. All differences are statistically significant (p < 0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

PolarRec extends the transformer-conditioned neural field pipeline of Neural Interferometry (Wu et al., 2022) by replacing point-level token encoding with angular-coordinate group encoding, reducing the quadratic attention cost to a near-linear one. It also builds on Focal Frequency Loss (Jiang et al., 2021) but augments it with a radial-coordinate weighting term that explicitly up-weights high-frequency visibility components. Conceptually, the paper repurposes polar coordinate representations already validated in vision tasks (PolarMask, PARTNER) to the radio interferometry domain, arguing that the polar system naturally mirrors the Earth-rotation-based sampling geometry of VLBI observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~14× reduction in encoder FLOPs (352→24 GFLOPs) while simultaneously improving PSNR by 3–4 dB over the strongest baseline makes transformer-based visibility reconstruction practical for large telescope arrays and long observation campaigns, where point-level encoding was previously prohibitive. However, the method is validated primarily on synthetic Galaxy10 data with a single real-data example that still shows residual artifacts, leaving open questions about generalizability to diverse source morphologies and longer baselines. The authors note potential transfer to MRI, seismic imaging, and point-cloud analysis, but no experiments support these claims yet.
