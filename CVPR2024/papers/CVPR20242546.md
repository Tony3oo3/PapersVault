## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models require dozens of iterative denoising steps for inference, creating a persistent bottleneck for deployment. Existing acceleration strategies—improved ODE solvers and trajectory distillation—can reduce sampling to roughly 4–10 steps but struggle to reach a single step without severe quality degradation, because the highly non-linear PF-ODE trajectory is intrinsically difficult to compress. The paper asks whether a fundamental reformulation of the diffusion training objective, rather than a better sampler, can enable truly one-step generation at web scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242546/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary input is a free-form text prompt, which is encoded by the CLIP text encoder and fed into a UNet operating in the 4× downsampled latent space of Stable Diffusion 1.5, producing a 512×512 RGB image in a single forward pass (~0.09 s on a single GPU). For image-to-image editing, an input image is noised via SDEdit and then denoised in one step conditioned on a new prompt. For controllable generation, an additional lightweight adapter network consumes depth maps or Canny edge maps alongside the text prompt, and the same one-step UNet produces the controlled output.

## Method

UFOGen is a diffusion-GAN hybrid that modifies the SIDDM objective in two key ways to enable one-step sampling. First, the generator is re-parameterized so that x_{t-1} is sampled from the forward diffusion kernel q(x_{t-1}|x_0=G_θ(x_t,t)) rather than the Gaussian posterior q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0), which makes the adversarial matching implicitly operate on the clean-sample distribution. Second, the reconstruction term is changed from the noisy-space loss at x_{t-1} to an explicit L2 loss ‖x_0 − x_0′‖² at the clean sample, removing the variance introduced by additive noise and making the x_0 distribution matching direct. Both the generator and discriminator share the same UNet architecture and are initialized from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.5 checkpoint, so training is a single fine-tuning stage with an adversarial loss on noisy samples at t−1 plus the clean-sample reconstruction loss, requiring only a minimal number of iterations. At inference, one simply samples x_T ~ N(0,I) and runs a single forward pass G_θ(x_T) to obtain the final image.

## Results

On MS-COCO-2017 5k, UFOGen achieves FID-5k of 22.5 and CLIP score of 0.311 in a single sampling step (0.09s), outperforming Progressive Distillation at 1 step (FID 37.2, CLIP 0.275) and CFG-Aware Distillation at 8 steps (FID 24.2, CLIP 0.30). UFOGen also surpasses the same-size InstaFlow-0.9B (FID 23.4, CLIP 0.304) and is competitive with the larger InstaFlow-1.7B (FID 22.4, CLIP 0.309) despite having half the parameters. On the broader MS-COCO-2014 30k benchmark, UFOGen achieves FID-30k of 12.78, slightly ahead of InstaFlow (13.10) at the same 0.9B parameter scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFOGen clearly outperforms all distillation-based baselines: Progressive Distillation at 1 step (FID 37.2 vs 22.5), 2 steps (26.0), and 4 steps (26.4), as well as CFG-Aware Distillation at 8 steps (FID 24.2 vs 22.5, CLIP 0.300 vs 0.311). Against the closest one-step competitor InstaFlow-0.9B, UFOGen wins on both FID (22.5 vs 23.4) and CLIP (0.311 vs 0.304). Against the larger InstaFlow-1.7B (stacked UNet, ~2x parameters), UFOGen is essentially tied on FID (22.5 vs 22.4) and slightly ahead on CLIP (0.311 vs 0.309). The only method that beats UFOGen on FID is DPM-Solver at 25 steps (20.1), but at 25x the inference time. In the broader Table 3 comparison on MS-COCO-2014 30k, UFOGen (FID 12.78) edges out InstaFlow (13.10) at the same 0.9B scale, while GAN-based GigaGAN (FID 9.09) and the full multi-step SD (FID 9.62) remain stronger on raw quality but at 20-30x the inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFOGen extends the diffusion-GAN hybrid lineage initiated by DDGAN (Xiao et al., 2022) and refined by SIDDM (Xu et al., 2023), which replaced the Gaussian denoising assumption with adversarial distribution matching to enable larger denoising steps. The key departure is a modified generator parameterization—sampling x_{t-1} from the forward diffusion q(x_{t-1}|x_0) rather than the posterior q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0)—combined with an explicit x_0 reconstruction loss, which together unlock one-step sampling that prior diffusion-GAN hybrids could not achieve. The paper also positions itself against ODE-solver acceleration (DPM-Solver) and distillation methods (Progressive Distillation, Consistency Models), arguing these are intrinsically limited at very few steps, and against concurrent one-step work InstaFlow, which takes a different route via rectified flow trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UFOGen demonstrates that a single forward pass through a 0.9B UNet can produce text-conditioned images competitive with 8–25 step diffusion samplers, a ~20–50× inference speedup with direct practical implications for deployment. The ability to initialize both generator and discriminator from a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model makes the training pipeline remarkably simple (one fine-tuning stage), lowering the barrier to adopting diffusion-GAN hybrids at scale. However, the FID-30k of 12.78 still trails the best multi-step models (SD: 9.62, GigaGAN: 9.09), and all experiments are confined to SD 1.5; whether the approach generalizes to larger backbones (SDXL, Flux) or higher resolutions remains an open question. The extension to image-to-image and controllable generation in one step is a notable practical advantage over GAN-based alternatives, but the quality ceiling for these downstream tasks at one step has not been thoroughly stress-tested.
