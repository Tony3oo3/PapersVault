## Problem

While text-to-image models can generate images at scale, it remains unclear whether synthetic images follow the same power-law scaling behavior as real images when used to train supervised vision models. This paper asks: how effectively do synthetic images scale for training image classifiers and CLIP, and which generation factors (prompt design, guidance scale, model choice) govern that scaling behavior?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study takes text prompts (class names, WordNet hypernyms, CLIP templates, BLIP-2-generated IN-Captions) as conditioning for three T2I models (Stable Diffusion, Imagen, Muse) at various classifier-free guidance scales, producing 256×256 synthetic images. For supervised training, datasets range from 0.125M to 64M images; for CLIP training, image-text pairs scale from 1M to 371M from LAION-400M. The primary outputs are power-law scaling parameters (slope k and y-intercept at 1.3M) fitted in log space, alongside zero-shot classification accuracy on ImageNet and 15 fine-grained benchmarks.

## Method

The paper formulates an empirical scaling-law analysis by generating synthetic ImageNet datasets under 54 distinct configurations (model × prompt × CFG scale) and fitting the power-law L_D ∝ (1/D)^k to validation loss in log space, defining two quantitative metrics: scaling ability (k) and performance at 1.3M (the y-intercept). They identify a recognizability–diversity trade-off governed by CFG scale and prompt diversity, showing that lower CFG and more diverse prompts (e.g., IN-Captions over bare class names) improve scaling. For CLIP training, they compare synthetic-only, real-only, and mixed synthetic+real data at matched text scales. A per-class decomposition reveals three categories—'Easy,' 'Scaling,' and 'Poor'—where the poor classes correspond to concepts the T2I models cannot faithfully render, explaining the overall scaling gap versus real data. Notably, synthetic data outperforms real data on OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch) and in low-data CLIP regimes, suggesting targeted use cases for synthetic scaling.

## Results

On supervised ImageNet training, synthetic images from all three T2I models (Stable Diffusion, Imagen, Muse) follow power-law scaling from 0.125M to 4M images, but with a smaller scaling ability (k) than real ImageNet images. For CLIP training on LAION-400M, synthetic-only data achieves 48.8% average zero-shot accuracy at 371M scale versus 65.7% for real data, while combining synthetic+real reaches 66.1%, surpassing real-only by 0.4 points. At smaller scales (≤10M pairs), adding synthetic images boosts zero-shot performance by up to 5% over real-only training. On OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch), synthetic data from Muse can outperform real ImageNet training data at sufficient scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three off-the-shelf T2I models—Stable Diffusion (latent diffusion), Imagen (pixel-space diffusion), and Muse (masked generative transformer)—as synthetic data generators for training supervised classifiers and CLIP. For supervised ImageNet training, all three models exhibit power-law scaling but with consistently smaller scaling ability (k) than real ImageNet images; the best synthetic configuration still underperforms real data in in-domain validation. However, on OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch), synthetic data from Muse can surpass real ImageNet training data at scale, and Stable Diffusion with 80 CLIP template prompts shows even larger OOD gains. For CLIP training, the scaling gap between synthetic and real is less pronounced than in the supervised setting, and combining synthetic+real images yields the best zero-shot accuracy at every scale tested (e.g., 66.1% vs. 65.7% at 371M; 51.9% vs. 49.8% at 16M). Among the three T2I models, Stable Diffusion, Imagen, and Muse show similar recognizability-diversity trade-offs when using IN-Caption prompts, with no single model dominating across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the power-law scaling framework from Kaplan et al. [32]—originally developed for language models—to the setting of training vision models on synthetic images from off-the-shelf text-to-image generators (Stable Diffusion, Imagen, Muse). While prior work has used synthetic images for specific CV tasks (detection, segmentation, driving) or studied learning from T2I-generated data at fixed scale [18, 4, 75], this is the first systematic study of how synthetic image quality and diversity interact with dataset size to determine scaling behavior. The paper also extends CLIP training analysis [45] by isolating the contribution of synthetic versus real image-text pairs across the full LAION-400M scale range.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear decision framework: synthetic data from current T2I models is not a drop-in replacement for real data in supervised classification, but it becomes competitive or even superior in OOD evaluation, data-scarce regimes, and mixed synthetic+real CLIP training. The per-class analysis identifying 'poor' classes that T2I models fundamentally cannot generate points to a concrete bottleneck—concept coverage—that future T2I improvements must address before synthetic data can fully substitute for curated real data. Open questions include whether fine-tuning T2I models on target domains (as in [4, 75]) closes the gap, and whether the OOD advantage persists as T2I models improve in fidelity.
