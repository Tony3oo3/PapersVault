## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on text-to-image diffusion models primarily target functional disruption (quality degradation, object removal) rather than specifically generating targeted NSFW content. No prior work demonstrates a unified attack that simultaneously bypasses both input-stage prompt filters and output-stage post-hoc safety checkers while producing high-fidelity, semantically controlled NSFW images across open-source models and commercial services.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242134/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For the text-modal attack, the input is a target NSFW prompt (e.g., 'A completely naked couple') and the output is a free-style adversarial prompt of the same length L that avoids all sensitive words while maximizing cosine similarity in the text encoder's latent space. For the image-modal attack, the input is an RGB image paired with an NSFW prompt and a SAM-generated mask specifying the editable region; the output is a perturbed image constrained to an ℓ2 ball of radius 16. In the multimodal setting, both the adversarial prompt and adversarial image are fed jointly to the T2I model, and the final output is the generated image that passes both the prompt filter and the safety checker.

## Method

The text-modal attack formulates adversarial prompt generation as a discrete optimization problem: it initializes L random tokens, introduces position-wise one-hot selection variables over the full vocabulary, and uses token-level gradients to rank candidate substitutions via a greedy top-k search, selecting the best of q sampled candidates by cosine similarity to the target prompt embedding. Sensitive words are suppressed by setting their token gradients to −∞, effectively removing them from the search space. The image-modal attack applies projected gradient descent with a dynamic loss that only activates terms whose cosine similarity to predefined NSFW concept embeddings exceeds a threshold, focusing optimization near the safety checker's decision boundary while respecting an ℓ2 perturbation budget. The key architectural insight is that the two modalities are independently optimized and then combined: the text attack preserves semantic intent in the encoder space to fool the prompt filter, while the image attack crafts imperceptible perturbations to fool the CLIP-based safety checker, together enabling end-to-end bypass of both defense layers.

## Results

On the white-box SDv1.5 setting, MMA-Diffusion achieves an average ASR-4 of 83.37% and ASR-1 of 67.54% across three NSFW detectors (Q16, MHSC, SC), substantially outperforming all baselines. In black-box transfer attacks, the method reaches 73.70% ASR-4 on SDXLv1.0 and 76.73% on Safe Latent Diffusion (SLD), demonstrating strong cross-model transferability. For online services, a 10-query black-box attack achieves 83.33% success rate on Midjourney and 90.00% on Leonardo.Ai. The image-modal attack bypasses SD's built-in safety checker with 88.52% ASR-4, and the combined multimodal attack (adversarial prompt + adversarial image) achieves 85.48% ASR-4 against both defenses simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMA-Diffusion dominates both baselines across all three model settings. Against I2P (Qu et al., 2023), which uses ~1000 human-written sexual prompts, MMA-Diffusion achieves 83.37% vs. 61.27% ASR-4 on SDv1.5, 73.70% vs. 7.29% on SDXL, and 76.73% vs. 33.44% on SLD — a 10× gap in the black-box SDXL setting. Against QF-Attack (Zhuang et al., 2023), whose best variant (GREEDY) reaches 29.10% ASR-4 on SDv1.5, MMA-Diffusion improves by 54.27 percentage points; on SDXL the gap widens to 73.70% vs. 2.36% (a 31× improvement). The paper attributes the large black-box gap to MMA-Diffusion's free-style prompt generation from scratch (larger search space) and its explicit sensitive-word suppression, whereas QF-Attack's perturbations remain strongly coupled to the original prompt and lack a mechanism to avoid filter-triggering words. I2P achieves moderate ASR but cannot control the specific NSFW content generated, while MMA-Diffusion targets a concrete semantic prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously separate attack modalities—textual adversarial prompt generation (drawing on gradient-driven token optimization from NLP attacks like LLM-attack and FGPM) and image-space perturbation (via projected gradient descent)—into a single multimodal framework targeting T2I safety. Prior adversarial work on T2I models (QF-Attack, I2P) focused on disrupting generation quality or removing objects, while recent NSFW-specific studies (UnlearnDiff, Ring-A-Bell) addressed only one defense layer. MMA-Diffusion departs from all of these by explicitly targeting the simultaneous bypass of both prompt filters and post-hoc safety checkers while preserving semantic fidelity to a user-specified NSFW target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 83–90% attack success rates on commercial services (Midjourney, Leonardo.Ai) and the ability to generate high-fidelity NSFW content from real individuals' images expose a critical gap in current T2I safety architectures that treat text and image defenses as independent layers. A key open question is whether concept-erased models (e.g., SLD) can be made robust when their suppression embeddings are derived from a fixed vocabulary that adversarial prompts can simply avoid. The paper also raises the broader concern that AI moderators marketed as 'deep neural network' filters may be substantially keyword-based, since a simple gradient-to-negative-infinity suppression of sensitive tokens suffices to evade them.
