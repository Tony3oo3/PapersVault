## Problem

Neural fields lack a comprehensive theoretical framework connecting activation functions, weight initialization, and the degree of overparameterization required for gradient descent to converge to a global minimum. While prior work established scaling laws for ReLU networks (quadratic/cubic in dataset size), no comparable theory existed for the periodic and localized activations (sine, sinc, Gaussian, wavelet) that practitioners actually use in neural fields, leaving the field to scale architectures ad hoc.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The neural fields take low-dimensional coordinate inputs (1D scalars, 2D pixel coordinates, 3D points with viewing directions, or spatial-temporal coordinates) and regress continuous field values (RGB colors, binary occupancy, volume density plus color, or velocity/pressure fields). Training uses full-batch gradient descent on the MSE loss, and the central quantity of interest is the hidden-layer width as a function of the number of training samples N, rather than a fixed architecture. Positional embedding layers (Random Fourier Features) may optionally precede the MLP to lift inputs into higher dimensions.

## Method

The paper proves that for sine, sinc, Gaussian, and wavelet activations with standard LeCun/Xavier/Kaiming initializations, shallow networks require Ω(N^{3/2}) hidden units and deep networks require Ω(N^{5/2}) in the penultimate layer for GD convergence to a global minimum—strictly better than the Ω(N²)/Ω(N³) bounds known for ReLU. The key analytical step is bounding the smallest singular value of the hidden layer and the spectral norm of the final-layer weight matrix, revealing that the product n_{L-1}^{3/4}‖W_L⁰‖₂ governs the overparameterization threshold. Exploiting this, the authors propose Initialization 1 (normal) and Initialization 2 (uniform), which sample the final output layer from a Gaussian/Uniform with variance reduced by a factor of 1/√n_{L-1} (or 1/n_{L-1}^{3/4} for uniform), thereby shrinking ‖W_L⁰‖₂ and lowering the required width to Ω(N) for shallow and Ω(N²) for deep networks. The theory is validated across six neural-field applications (curve fitting, image regression, super-resolution, occupancy fields, NeRF, and PINNs), where the proposed initializations consistently outperform LeCun, Xavier, and Kaiming baselines in PSNR, SSIM, and IOU.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves that shallow neural fields with sine/sinc/Gaussian/wavelet activations and standard initializations (LeCun, Xavier, Kaiming) require Ω(N^{3/2}) hidden-layer width for gradient descent convergence, and deep networks require Ω(N^{5/2}) — both strictly better than the Ω(N²) and Ω(N³) bounds for ReLU-PE networks [20]. With the proposed Initialization 1, these bounds improve to linear Ω(N) (shallow) and quadratic Ω(N²) (deep). Empirically, on the occupancy field task, Initialization 2 (uniform) achieves 24.5 dB PSNR and 0.91 IOU, outperforming all standard initializations by 2–4 dB in PSNR and 0.04–0.11 in IOU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ReLU-PE scaling results of Nguyen [20], the paper demonstrates that sinc/sine/Gaussian/wavelet activations with standard initializations require only O(N^{3/2}) parameters in the shallow case versus O(N²) for ReLU-PE, and O(N^{5/2}) versus O(N³) in the deep case — confirmed empirically in Figure 3 where sinc networks converge with substantially fewer parameters across all dataset sizes. Against LeCun normal [16], the proposed Initialization 1 improves PSNR by 2.4 dB (22.7 vs 20.3) and IOU by 0.08 (0.89 vs 0.81) on the occupancy task; against Xavier normal [11], the gain is 1.5 dB and 0.05 IOU; against Kaiming normal [13], the gain is 2.8 dB and 0.09 IOU. In the uniform variant, Initialization 2 outperforms Kaiming uniform [13] by 4.3 dB (24.5 vs 20.2) and 0.09 IOU on occupancy, and by 1.1 dB in training PSNR on NeRF (Lego scene). Across all tested applications (super-resolution, occupancy, NeRF, PINNs), both proposed initializations consistently achieve the highest PSNR and SSIM/IOU, with the uniform variant (Initialization 2) generally outperforming the normal variant (Initialization 1) by 1–2 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the overparameterization and gradient-descent convergence literature (NTK [15], Neural Tangent Hierarchy [14], ReLU convergence results [4, 10, 20, 21]) to non-ReLU activations—sine, sinc, Gaussian, and wavelet—under the standard initializations practitioners actually use (LeCun, Xavier, Kaiming), rather than the N(0,1) or NTK parameterizations common in prior theory. It departs from Nguyen [20], which established quadratic (shallow) and cubic (deep) scaling for ReLU networks, by showing that periodic/decaying activations require only superlinear (N^{3/2}) and super-quadratic (N^{5/2}) scaling, and then further reduces these to linear and quadratic via a novel last-layer initialization that shrinks the output weight variance by a factor of 1/√n_{L-1}. The work thus carves new ground at the intersection of activation-dependent convergence theory and practical initialization design for coordinate-based neural fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical scaling framework that directly connects activation choice, initialization scheme, and required overparameterization for neural fields, giving practitioners a principled basis for architecture design rather than ad hoc scaling. The proposed initialization (Initialization 1/2) is trivially simple—just a smaller variance on the final layer—and empirically outperforms standard schemes across six diverse applications (image regression, super-resolution, occupancy fields, NeRF, PINNs, curve fitting). A key limitation is that all theoretical guarantees and experiments assume full-batch gradient descent; extending the analysis to mini-batch training, which is the standard in practice, remains an open problem that the authors explicitly flag. The broader implication is that the 'one-size-fits-all' initialization (e.g., Kaiming for everything) may be suboptimal, and activation-aware initialization could become a standard design consideration for neural field architectures.
