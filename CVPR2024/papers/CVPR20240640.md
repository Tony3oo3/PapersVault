## Problem

Physics-based, nonlinear, time-coherent elastodynamic motion synthesis for NeRF scenes remains largely unexplored because standard simulation methods (FEM, MPM) require explicit meshing or grid discretization that is inherently incompatible with implicit neural representations. Existing NeRF editing systems rely on geometry-based heuristic energies (e.g., ARAP) or coarse cage deformations, lacking the ability to faithfully model real-world hyperelastic materials at interactive rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 100 multi-view RGB images of a static scene to train an NGP-NeRF, then accepts user-applied external forces and position constraints as interactive inputs. It produces real-time novel view renderings of the deformed model at interactive rates, with the simulation cost depending only on the number of Q-GMLS kernels (typically a few dozen, e.g., 78 for a plant) rather than the sampling resolution. The rendering step warps deformed ray positions back to rest-pose coordinates via a quadratic Taylor expansion to retrieve accurate color and texture from the original NeRF.

## Method

PIE-NeRF discretizes nonlinear hyperelasticity in a fully meshless way using quadratic generalized moving least squares (Q-GMLS) for spatial model reduction. An augmented Poisson disk sampling strategy adaptively places particles based on the NeRF density gradient (via AutoDiff), concentrating samples at shape boundaries; a Voronoi partition then groups particles into Q-GMLS kernels whose centers serve as generalized coordinates. Energy integration is performed at a separate set of integrator points (IPs) modeled as small elastic cuboids, supporting arbitrary hyperelastic material models (Neo-Hookean, ARAP, etc.) with exact first-order approximation of the deformation gradient within each cuboid. The resulting dense 30n×30n nonlinear system is solved with Newton's method on GPU, and a quadratic warping scheme—exploiting the quadratic displacement prior—maps deformed positions back to rest-pose for accurate NeRF rendering, avoiding the blurring artifacts of linear warping under large deformation.

## Results

PIE-NeRF achieves interactive (real-time) simulation using as few as 78 Q-GMLS kernels for complex models like the swaying plant, with the full pipeline (matrix assembly, nonlinear solve, and quadratic warping) running at interactive rates on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU. In the volume preservation test, the Neo-Hookean material model retains volume far better than ARAP, which loses nearly 40% of the original volume under compression. PIE-NeRF runs approximately two orders of magnitude faster than PAC-NeRF while producing sharper local detail under large deformation. Comparison against FEM ground truth (Fig. 8) shows that PIE-NeRF's meshless Q-GMLS results are visually nearly identical to the tetrahedral FEM solution despite using far fewer degrees of freedom.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PAC-NeRF [37], PIE-NeRF wins decisively on both rendering quality and speed: under large deformation, PAC-NeRF's MPM-based particle-to-grid interpolation causes over-blurring at fine local shapes, while PIE-NeRF's quadratic warping preserves texture fidelity; PIE-NeRF is also roughly two orders of magnitude faster due to its implicit time integration and reduced kernel count. Against FEM ground truth [92], PIE-NeRF matches the reference solution closely (Fig. 8), with only minor differences highlighted, while using significantly fewer kernels than FEM vertices and fewer integrator points than FEM tetrahedra. Against ARAP [67] as a material model, PIE-NeRF with Neo-Hookean energy clearly outperforms: the ARAP jelly collapses unnaturally and loses ~40% volume under compression, whereas the Neo-Hookean formulation preserves volume via its log-barrier term, producing physically realistic incompressible behavior. Overall, PIE-NeRF wins or ties on all three comparisons, with the FEM comparison serving as validation rather than a competitive baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIE-NeRF sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: implicit 3D representation (NeRF/NGP) and meshless computational mechanics (MLS, GMLS). It departs from prior NeRF editing methods (CageNeRF, Deforming Radiance Fields with Cages, NeRF-Editing) that require converting the implicit representation to an explicit mesh or voxel grid before applying physics, and from PAC-NeRF which couples NeRF with the material point method. The key conceptual departure is using a quadratic generalized moving least squares (Q-GMLS) discretization directly on adaptively sampled particles from the NeRF density field, eliminating the need for any intermediate shape proxy while retaining the expressivity needed for nonlinear hyperelasticity and codimensional geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that interactive, physics-grounded manipulation of NeRF scenes is feasible without the meshing bottleneck that has limited prior approaches, opening the door to real-time material-aware editing of photorealistic 3D content. The meshless formulation naturally accommodates topology changes (e.g., cutting) and thin/codimensional shapes that are problematic for FEM-based pipelines. Open questions include scalability to very large or multi-object scenes, automatic estimation of material parameters from video, and whether the approach transfers to emerging representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting, which the authors explicitly flag as future work.
