## Problem

Existing deep-learning-based SLAM systems (DROID-SLAM, DPVO) assume a single continuous video stream and cannot perform the wide-baseline matching and non-local optimization required to connect disjoint sequences. The only monocular methods supporting Multi-Session SLAM (ORB-SLAM3, CCM-SLAM) rely on classical feature descriptors, making them less accurate and prone to catastrophic tracking failures in low-texture or high-motion settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242558/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes multiple disjoint RGB video sequences (e.g., 20 FPS EuRoC-MAV, or reversed sub-clips from ETH3D) along with camera intrinsics, selecting 96 anchor points per frame from a mix of Superpoint detections and random samples. It produces camera poses under a single global reference frame, per-anchor 3D depth estimates, bi-directional sparse optical flow, and relative Sim(3) transformations that align disjoint trajectories. The full pipeline runs in real-time and operates on a sliding window of 20 keyframes for the VO frontend.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified recurrent backbone (inspired by RAFT/DPVO) that jointly refines bi-directional optical flow and camera poses through iterative update operators. For two-view pose, a differentiable solver layer minimizes the symmetric epipolar distance (SED) via Levenberg-Marquardt, pre-conditioned by a weighted 8-point algorithm to ensure convergence; for visual odometry, the solver is swapped for bundle adjustment over a multi-view window. After each solver step, matches are clamped to be physically consistent with the current pose and depth estimates (epipolar-line clamping for two-view, reprojection for multi-view), creating a tight coupling between matching and geometry. Trajectory joining uses NetVLAD retrieval to find co-visible image pairs, applies the two-view solver for rotation and translation direction, and recovers translation magnitude and relative scale by aligning VO depth with triangulated two-view depth via a RANSAC-like inlier maximization.

## Results

On the Scannet two-view pose benchmark, the method achieves 30.9% / 51.1% / 67.6% pose-error AUC at 5°/10°/20°, outperforming all baselines including ASpanFormer (25.6/46.0/63.3) and Roessle & Nießner with bundle adjustment (25.7/47.2/66.4). On Megadepth, it reaches 60.4/72.7/81.3% AUC, competitive with but slightly below the best LO-RANSAC-based methods. For Multi-Session SLAM on EuRoC-MAV, the system achieves 0.022–0.036 m RMSE ATE across all trajectory groups, beating ORB-SLAM3 by up to 12× (0.022 vs 0.284 m on Vicon 2). On the ETH3D benchmark, it completes all 5 scenes without failure (0.005–0.035 m ATE) while ORB-SLAM3 fails on 3 of 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Scannet, the method decisively outperforms all eight baselines, gaining +5.2 AUC@5° over the strongest prior method (ASpanFormer, 25.6%) and +5.2 over Roessle & Nießner with bundle adjustment (25.7%), confirming that the iterative optical-flow-plus-solver design is superior to match-then-solve pipelines in texture-poor indoor scenes. On Megadepth (photo-tourism), the advantage narrows: the method (60.4% AUC@5°) is slightly below Roessle & Nießner with BA (61.2%) and well below LO-RANSAC variants of ASpanFormer (69.4%), indicating that in high-texture settings where RANSAC-based solvers excel, the differentiable SED solver offers less benefit. For Multi-Session SLAM on EuRoC-MAV, the system beats ORB-SLAM3 on all four trajectory groups (e.g., 0.022 vs 0.284 m on Vicon 2) and CCM-SLAM (0.022 vs 0.077 m on MH01-03), while also outperforming mono-inertial VINS and ORB-SLAM3. On ETH3D, the method's key advantage is robustness: it completes all 5 scenes (0.005–0.035 m ATE) whereas ORB-SLAM3 catastrophically fails on 3 of 5 (Sofa, Plant Scene, Einstein) due to initialization or tracking loss, highlighting the method's resilience to the wide-baseline and low-texture challenges inherent to multi-session settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAFT/DPVO/DROID-SLAM lineage of recurrent optical-flow-based SLAM to the multi-session setting, where prior deep methods (DROID-SLAM, DPVO) assumed a single continuous video and could not handle wide-baseline matching or cross-trajectory alignment. The key architectural departure is a differentiable symmetric-epipolar-distance (SED) solver layer that replaces bundle adjustment for two-view pose, pre-conditioned by a weighted 8-point algorithm from Ranftl & Koltun, enabling the same backbone to perform both wide-baseline matching and visual odometry by swapping solvers. Unlike classical multi-session systems (ORB-SLAM3, CCM-SLAM) that rely on hand-crafted descriptors and Umeyama alignment, this approach learns matching and pose jointly end-to-end from RGB images alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first monocular RGB-only deep-learning system for multi-session SLAM that outperforms the classical ORB-SLAM3 baseline, and it demonstrates meaningful robustness: ORB-SLAM3 catastrophically fails on 3 of 5 ETH3D scenes while this method succeeds on all, suggesting deep semi-indirect methods can overcome the texture/initialization fragilities of feature-descriptor pipelines. The differentiable SED solver is a reusable component for any setting requiring wide-baseline two-view pose without pre-computed matches. Open questions include whether the approach extends to stereo/RGB-D, scales to very large multi-robot deployments, and how it performs under significant illumination change or dynamic scenes; the paper also notes reduced competitiveness on photo-tourism-style data (Megadepth) where RANSAC-based matching remains strong.
