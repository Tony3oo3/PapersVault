## Problem

Full fine-tuning of pre-trained point cloud transformers is computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and risks catastrophic forgetting of pre-training knowledge. Existing parameter-efficient transfer learning methods from NLP (Adapter tuning with a static manual scale, Prompt tuning with random-initialized external prompts) fail to adapt well to the complex geometry and non-uniform distribution of 3D point clouds, leaving a significant accuracy gap versus full fine-tuning on challenging benchmarks like ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (typically 1024 points) as input, processed by a frozen pre-trained ViT backbone (Point-BERT, Point-MAE, or RECON). It outputs a single class label for 3D classification or per-point part labels for segmentation. The key constraint is that only ~5% of total parameters (the inserted Dynamic Adapter modules, TFTS factors, and classification head) are updated, while the entire backbone remains frozen, reducing trainable parameters by 95% and training GPU memory by up to 35% compared to full fine-tuning.

## Method

DAPT introduces three components inserted into a frozen ViT backbone: (1) a Task-agnostic Feature Transform Strategy (TFTS) that applies learnable per-channel scaling and shifting (γ, β) to bridge the gap between pre-training and downstream representations; (2) a Dynamic Adapter that replaces the static manual scale of standard Adapter tuning with a per-token dynamic scale S_d = ReLU(x W_s^T), so each token is adjusted according to its task-specific significance, with the adapter output zero-initialized to stabilize early training; and (3) an Internal Prompt tuning mechanism that generates instance-specific prompts by GELU-activating and mean-pooling the Dynamic Adapter's output, then concatenating them between the classification token and patch tokens at selected layers. This design ensures prompts capture instance-specific features rather than relying on random initialization, and the bi-directional self-attention interaction between prompts and point tokens provides a global perspective complementary to the local per-token adjustment of the adapter.

## Results

On the hardest ScanObjectNN variant (PB_T50_RS), DAPT with Point-BERT achieves 85.43% overall accuracy, surpassing full fine-tuning (83.07%) by 2.36% while tuning only 1.1M parameters (4.97% of the 22.1M total). With Point-MAE, DAPT reaches 85.08% (vs. 85.18% full fine-tuning), and with RECON it achieves 89.38% (vs. 90.01%). DAPT reduces trainable parameters by 95% and training GPU memory by up to 35% compared to full fine-tuning, while outperforming the prior SOTA PETL method IDPT by 0.14% on PB_T50_RS with 35% fewer tunable parameters (1.1M vs. 1.7M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPT consistently outperforms IDPT (the prior SOTA PETL method for point clouds) across nearly all ScanObjectNN variants while using 35% fewer tunable parameters (1.1M vs. 1.7M); the most notable gap is on Point-BERT PB_T50_RS where DAPT gains +0.74% over IDPT (85.43% vs. 83.69%). Against full fine-tuning, DAPT with Point-BERT surpasses the full model on all three ScanObjectNN variants (+3.62%, +1.55%, +2.36%), while with Point-MAE it matches or slightly underperforms on PB_T50_RS (−0.10%) but wins on OBJ_ONLY (+1.90%). On RECON, DAPT matches full fine-tuning on OBJ_BG and stays within 0.63% on PB_T50_RS, whereas IDPT drops by 2.74%. Compared to other PETL methods from NLP/2D vision (Adapter, VPT, LoRA, Prefix tuning, BitFit, AdaptFormer, SSF), DAPT achieves the best PB_T50_RS accuracy (85.08%) with only 1.1M parameters, outperforming the next-best IDPT (84.94%) and significantly beating Adapter (83.93%) and VPT (81.09%). On ModelNet40, DAPT with RECON achieves 94.1% with voting, a +1.1% gain over full fine-tuning, and remains the top method even after test-time voting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) from NLP and 2D vision to 3D point cloud analysis, building directly on Adapter tuning (Houlsby et al., ICML 2019) by replacing its static manual scale with a per-token dynamic scale, and on Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al., ECCV 2022) by substituting external random-initialized prompts with prompts derived from the adapter's own internal output. The Task-agnostic Feature Transform Strategy (TFTS) adapts the scaling-and-shifting idea from SSF (Lian et al., NeurIPS 2022). The key conceptual departure from prior PETL work is the argument that 3D point clouds' irregular geometry and non-uniform distribution demand instance-adaptive, token-level modulation rather than the global static scales or task-agnostic random prompts that suffice for NLP and 2D images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAPT offers a practical, plug-and-play recipe for adapting large pre-trained point cloud transformers (Point-BERT, Point-MAE, RECON) with under 5% tunable parameters and 35% less GPU memory, making fine-tuning accessible on consumer hardware and reducing storage from M full model copies to one shared backbone plus small task-specific adapters. The finding that a trivially simple affine transform (TFTS) recovers most of the linear-probing-to-full-fine-tuning gap suggests that much of the 'adaptation' in 3D pre-trained models is a feature-space alignment problem rather than a deep representational one. The authors acknowledge that applicability to more complex tasks (3D detection, generation) remains untested, and the modest gains over IDPT (the prior SOTA PETL method for point clouds) leave room for further improvement.
