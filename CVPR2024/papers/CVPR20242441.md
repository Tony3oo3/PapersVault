## Problem

Existing learnable 3D mesh representations face a fundamental trade-off: implicit methods (Poisson, NMC, NDC) guarantee topological validity but smooth out sharp features, while explicit methods that preserve edges (VoroMesh) produce meshes littered with spurious facets and wrong normals. No prior representation simultaneously captures ridges and corners, guarantees watertight and self-intersection-free output, and handles open surfaces with boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242441/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the learning task, the input is a (N+1)³ SDF grid; the network predicts P=4 points per voxel cell along with their normals and QEM matrices, plus a binary occupancy flag per cell. For the optimization task, the input is a dense point sample with normals. The output is a PoNQ representation (points, normals, 4×4 QEM matrices) from which a mesh is extracted whose vertices are the QEM-optimal positions v* = A⁻¹b rather than the predicted points themselves, yielding a watertight, non-self-intersecting triangle mesh. The representation is grid-free in practice and can optionally produce open surfaces with boundaries.

## Method

PoNQ encodes local geometry at each point via a Quadric Error Metric matrix (4×4), whose eigenstructure naturally captures surface anisotropy (pancake for flat regions, cigar for edges, sphere for corners). The network is a CNN with a shared 5-layer encoder and five separate 6-layer decoders predicting points, normals, QEM quadratic forms (stored as a Cholesky factor U to guarantee positive-definiteness without inversion), QEM-optimal vertices v*, and occupancy. Mesh extraction proceeds by Delaunay tetrahedralizing the v* vertices, tagging tetrahedra as inside/outside via the Crust circumcenter criterion, resolving ambiguous tetrahedra with a minimum-cut on the Voronoi dual graph weighted by triangle fitness scores, and finally extracting the inside/outside boundary. Training is supervised by a combination of Chamfer distance, normal consistency, QEM quadratic-form fitting, v* plane-distance, regularization, and occupancy losses, all restricted to ground-truth surface voxels.

## Results

On the ABC dataset (trained and tested on ABC), PoNQ achieves the best normal consistency (NC=0.984 at 128³), best edge Chamfer distance (ECD=0.109 at 64³), and best edge F-score (EF1=0.878 at 128³) while guaranteeing 100% watertight, self-intersection-free meshes at all resolutions. On the generalization test (trained on ABC, evaluated on Thingi30), PoNQ-lite with only 1 point per cell already outperforms NDC on ECD and EF1 at 128³ (0.055 vs. 0.065 and 0.699 vs. 0.644) with 100% watertightness, while full PoNQ achieves the best CD (0.641×10⁻⁵) and F1 (0.939). In optimization-based reconstruction on Thingi30, PoNQ achieves the best NC (0.988 at 128³) and ECD (0.039×10³ at 128³) with roughly half the face count of competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VoroMesh [35], the closest competitor that also guarantees watertightness, PoNQ dramatically outperforms on sharp-edge metrics: ECD is 0.184 vs. 0.802 (32³) and EF1 is 0.713 vs. 0.232 (32³) on ABC, with VoroMesh's spurious Voronoi facets creating false sharp edges. PoNQ also produces roughly half the face count (10.2k vs. 20.0k at 32³). Against NMC [8] and NDC [10], PoNQ wins on every metric at every resolution while being 100% watertight, whereas NMC achieves only 12–26% watertightness and NDC only 14–44%; NMC additionally suffers from aliasing artifacts due to its regular-grid meshing. In the optimization-based setting (Table 2), PoNQ outperforms SAP [42] and DPF [43] on NC and ECD at all resolutions (e.g., NC 0.988 vs. 0.986 for DPF, ECD 0.039 vs. 0.086×10³ at 128³), while matching their surface CD within 0.5%. The PoNQ-lite variant (1 point/cell) already beats NDC on ECD and EF1 at 128³ on Thingi30 (0.055 vs. 0.065, 0.699 vs. 0.644) with 100% watertightness, demonstrating the inherent advantage of grid-free QEM-based representations over grid-locked isosurfacing.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoNQ repurposes the Quadric Error Metric (QEM), a classical tool from mesh decimation (Garland & Heckbert, 1997), as the core geometric descriptor in a neural shape representation — the first such use in a learning context. For mesh extraction, it combines the Crust algorithm's circumcenter criterion with a graph-cut over the Delaunay tetrahedralization, drawing on combinatorial geometry (Amenta et al., 1998; Kolluri et al., 2004) to guarantee watertight, intersection-free outputs. This positions PoNQ between implicit methods (NMC, NDC) that smooth sharp features and explicit methods (VoroMesh, SAP, DPF) that either produce spurious artifacts or rely on Poisson reconstruction, by encoding second-order local geometry (via QEM matrices) rather than just points and normals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PoNQ addresses a persistent gap in learnable 3D representations: simultaneously capturing sharp features, handling open surfaces with boundaries, and guaranteeing topological validity (watertight, no self-intersections) — properties no prior method achieved together. The QEM-based representation also enables free multi-scale simplification via matrix summation (PoNQ-lite), which outperforms grid-based NDC at equal element counts, suggesting grid-free explicit representations are fundamentally more efficient. Open questions include integrating PoNQ into differentiable rendering pipelines, refining boundary extraction beyond score-based filtering, and exploring adaptive per-cell simplification. A noted limitation is that the Delaunay-based extraction can produce non-manifold vertices, requiring a post-hoc fix.
