## Problem

Existing image editing methods lack fine-grained, independent control over individual objects in a scene. Text-prompt-based approaches cannot precisely describe the shape and appearance of multiple objects simultaneously, while low-level conditioning methods (masks, sketches) typically fail to manipulate multiple objects independently or still rely on text for appearance. No prior framework unifies structure and appearance control at the object level within a single diffusion model without requiring inversion of real images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image (whose objects are to be edited), a reference image (supplying appearance via pooled VGG and DINOv2 features), an optional text prompt, and optionally a user-modified shape mask for structure editing. Structure is extracted as a panoptic segmentation map (category + binary mask per object), and appearance is encoded as a tuple of L2-normalized feature vectors from VGG (low-level) and DINOv2 (high-level) layers, spatially splatted back using the object masks. The output is a full-resolution edited image where specified objects have altered structure, appearance, or both, with no inversion step required for in-the-wild editing.

## Method

PAIR Diffusion decomposes each object into structure (category + shape from an off-the-shelf panoptic segmentation network) and appearance (pooled features from VGG initial layers for texture/color and DINOv2 middle layers for semantic content), then conditions a diffusion model on these per-object representations. For Stable Diffusion, a ControlNet module receives VGG features as input and injects DINOv2 features after cross-attention in the first two encoder blocks; for unconditional LDM, features are simply concatenated to the input channels. The key inference contribution is a multimodal classifier-free guidance formulation (Eq. 4) that factorizes the conditional score into independent terms for structure, appearance, and text, each with its own guidance strength (s_S, s_F, s_y), enabling users to dial the relative influence of reference-image appearance versus text prompt. Training uses the standard denoising objective with random dropout of conditioning signals, and the framework supports appearance editing, shape editing, object addition, and object-level variation from a single trained model.

## Results

On the LSUN Bedroom appearance-control benchmark, PAIR Diffusion achieves FID 12.81, L1 0.02, and SSIM 0.51, outperforming E2EVE (FID 13.59, L1 0.05, SSIM 0.34) on all three metrics simultaneously. For structure control on CelebA-HQ, PAIR Diffusion reaches mIoU 0.67 and SSIM 0.52, surpassing SEAN (mIoU 0.64, SSIM 0.32). The method is the only one to balance naturalness (FID), locality (L1), and faithfulness (SSIM) at the same time, whereas baselines trade off one criterion for another.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against E2EVE [3], PAIR Diffusion wins on all three metrics (FID 12.81 vs. 13.59, L1 0.02 vs. 0.05, SSIM 0.51 vs. 0.34), confirming superior simultaneous control of naturalness, locality, and faithfulness. Compared to LDM Inpainting [38], PAIR Diffusion sacrifices some naturalness (FID 12.81 vs. 8.25) but dramatically improves faithfulness (SSIM 0.51 vs. 0.17), showing that pure inpainting cannot transfer appearance from a reference. For structure control, PAIR Diffusion outperforms SEAN [62] on both mIoU (0.67 vs. 0.64) and SSIM (0.52 vs. 0.32), indicating more precise shape adherence while retaining appearance. Qualitatively, PAIR Diffusion also surpasses Prompt-Free Diffusion [53] on complex-object appearance editing and uniquely supports blending text and reference-image cues via multimodal CFG, whereas PFD lacks this ability. Paint-By-Example [54] fails to follow user-specified shapes and struggles with large objects, while PAIR Diffusion handles both shape editing and object addition with smooth boundary blending.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAIR Diffusion builds directly on the latent diffusion model (LDM/Stable Diffusion) as its generative backbone and on ControlNet as an efficient conditioning mechanism, extending the classifier-free guidance framework from Ho & Salimans to a multimodal setting with separate guidance strengths for structure, appearance, and text. It departs from text-prompt-based editing methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, InstructPix2Pix) that lack fine-grained per-object control, and from low-level conditioning approaches (masks, sketches) that cannot independently manipulate multiple objects. Unlike recent object-level editing works (Epstein et al., Mou et al.) that rely on classifier guidance at inference time and require inversion of real images, PAIR Diffusion trains a conditional model with explicit per-object structure (panoptic segmentation) and appearance (VGG + DINOv2 pooled features) representations, eliminating the inversion step and enabling a unified framework for appearance editing, shape editing, object addition, and variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, unified framework that consolidates multiple object-level editing operations into a single trained model without requiring inversion, which is a significant practical simplification over prior methods. The multimodal classifier-free guidance mechanism offers an intuitive knob-based control over how much text vs. reference image influences the output, which is useful for real-world editing workflows. However, the architecture modifications are deliberately simple (channel concatenation or ControlNet injection), and the authors acknowledge limitations in identity preservation, lack of control over illumination/pose, and the absence of standardized evaluation metrics for image editing. Open questions include whether more sophisticated conditioning designs can improve fidelity while maintaining the object-level formulation, and whether the framework can be extended to video or 3D editing.
