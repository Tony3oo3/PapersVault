## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detection (OvOD) detectors exhibit significant and inconsistent performance when the user-specified vocabulary spans different semantic granularities (e.g., 'Labrador' vs. 'Dog' vs. 'Animal' for the same visual region). This variability undermines reliable real-world deployment, particularly in safety-critical applications, because the same physical object should be detectable regardless of the abstraction level at which the user names it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240819/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image and a user-defined class vocabulary (text prompts of the form 'a {Class Name}') and produces bounding boxes with class labels for detection, or class labels for classification. The semantic hierarchy (either a dataset-provided taxonomy or an LLM-generated three-level hierarchy) is consumed exclusively offline to pre-compute one nexus vector per class; at test time, the detector simply performs cosine similarity between region embeddings and these pre-computed vectors, incurring zero additional computational overhead relative to the vanilla name-based classifier.

## Method

SHiNe is a training-free classifier that replaces the standard text-embedding-based classifier in any VLM-based OvOD detector with a hierarchy-aware 'nexus' vector per class. For each target class, it (1) retrieves relevant super- and sub-categories from a semantic hierarchy, (2) integrates them into a set of hierarchy-aware sentences using a proposed Is-A connector that explicitly models the 'is-a' relationships from specific to abstract (e.g., 'A wooden baseball bat, which is a baseball bat, which is a bat, which is a sports equipment'), and (3) encodes these sentences with a frozen CLIP text encoder and fuses the resulting embeddings via a mean aggregator (or optionally a principal-eigenvector aggregator) to form the final nexus vector. The key insight is that natural-language sentences with explicit Is-A connectors capture inter-level semantic structure that simple concatenation or ensembling of category names misses, yielding more robust and granularity-invariant representations. The method is plug-and-play: once nexus vectors are constructed offline, they drop into any off-the-shelf OvOD detector (Detic, CoDet, VLDet, CORA) without retraining, and it works with both ground-truth and noisy LLM-generated hierarchies.

## Results

On iNatLoc with a Swin-B backbone and the strongest supervision (LVIS&COCO + IN-21k), SHiNe with ground-truth hierarchies boosts the Detic baseline mAP50 from 57.5% to 86.3% at L5 (+28.8) and from 67.2% to 84.3% at L3 (+17.1), with the maximum gain reaching +31.9 points. On FSOD, gains of up to +17.5 mAP50 are observed at the coarsest level (L1: 25.0→42.5). For open-vocabulary classification on ImageNet-1k, SHiNe improves the CLIP ViT-B/16 zero-shot baseline by +2.8% top-1 accuracy (63.9→66.7) using LLM-generated hierarchies, while maintaining the same inference speed (149 FPS vs. 152 FPS for CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHiNe is applied as a drop-in classifier replacement on top of three off-the-shelf OvOD detectors. On Detic (Zhou et al., ECCV 2022), SHiNe consistently improves mAP50 across all granularity levels, with average gains of +16.8 (GT-H) and +13.4 (LLM-H) on iNatLoc. On CoDet (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2023), SHiNe yields even larger absolute gains (e.g., +37.7 at L5 on iNatLoc), while on VLDet (Lin et al., ICLR 2023) improvements are more modest (+1.0 to +6.2) since VLDet already achieves high baseline scores. For open-vocabulary classification on ImageNet-1k, SHiNe outperforms both H-CLIP (Ge et al., CVPR 2023) and CHiLS (Novack et al., ICML 2023) in top-1 accuracy (66.7% vs. 60.1% and 66.1% with LLM hierarchies on ViT-B/16) while running at 149 FPS compared to 2 and 27 FPS respectively, because SHiNe operates offline with no search-on-the-fly overhead. The only setting where SHiNe underperforms is the BREEDS L1 level (10 classes), where the extremely coarse vocabulary makes hierarchy information less discriminative.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHiNe builds on the VLM-based open-vocabulary detection pipeline (Detic, CoDet, VLDet) by replacing the frozen text classifier with a hierarchy-aware nexus vector, drawing conceptual inspiration from open-vocabulary classification methods that exploit semantic hierarchies (CHiLS, H-CLIP). The key departure is that CHiLS and H-CLIP perform combinatorial search over super-/sub-category prompt combinations at inference time, incurring significant computational overhead that limits them to classification; SHiNe instead pre-computes a single fused vector offline via an Is-A connector that explicitly encodes hierarchical 'is-a' relationships into natural-language sentences, then aggregates their embeddings. This makes SHiNe training-free, inference-overhead-free, and directly applicable to detection, where per-region search would be prohibitive. The method also extends beyond ground-truth taxonomies by showing that noisy LLM-generated (ChatGPT) three-level hierarchies retain most of the benefit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically critical but underexplored failure mode: off-the-shelf OvOD detectors exhibit large, uncontrolled performance swings when users specify classes at different semantic granularities (e.g., 'Labrador' vs. 'Dog' vs. 'Animal'), which is a real deployment concern in safety-critical settings. SHiNe's plug-and-play, training-free design means it can be adopted by any existing VLM-based detector without retraining, and the fact that it works with LLM-generated hierarchies lowers the barrier to entry for domains lacking curated taxonomies. Limitations include underperformance relative to CHiLS/H-CLIP at very coarse granularities on BREEDS (where the hierarchy's sub-classes precisely match test objects, favoring search-based methods), and the method's effectiveness is bounded by hierarchy quality. Open questions include how SHiNe scales to very large or non-tree (DAG) hierarchies, and whether the Is-A connector generalizes to non-English or domain-specific ontologies.
