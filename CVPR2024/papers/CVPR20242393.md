## Problem

Existing 3D human reconstruction methods from monocular video assume the subject is fully visible and centered in the frame, failing in in-the-wild scenarios where humans are partially occluded, cropped, or observed from only a few angles. Additionally, compositing multiple dynamic humans with a static background in NeRF-based pipelines is non-trivial and computationally expensive, limiting practical 4D scene reconstruction from casual monocular video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of T frames with corresponding camera intrinsics/extrinsics as input, with no requirement for foreground masks or multi-view coverage. It outputs a unified 3D Gaussian Splatting scene comprising a static background and N individual humans, each stored in a canonical (A-pose) SMPL space. Because humans are parameterized by SMPL pose vectors, the output supports arbitrary re-posing, per-person removal, and real-time novel view rendering at ~300 FPS on 1K×1K resolution with ~1.5 GB VRAM.

## Method

The method represents both the static background and all dynamic humans in a single 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, enabling trivial α-blended compositing without the multi-NeRF integration overhead of prior work. Each human is canonicalized into SMPL rest-pose space and deformed to posed space via Linear Blend Skinning applied to Gaussian centers and covariance matrices, with skinning weights precomputed in a voxel grid for speed. The key innovation is a diffusion-guided reconstruction loss: at each iteration, a virtual camera and random pose are sampled, the human's 3D-GS is rendered, and an SDS loss computed against a Stable Diffusion model synthesizes plausible appearances for unseen body parts. To preserve the target individual's identity rather than generating a generic person, CustomDiffusion (Texture Inversion) fine-tunes the diffusion model per person using cropped observations, and an OpenPose ControlNet aligns the generated pose with the SMPL skeleton. Training combines standard reconstruction losses (MSE, SSIM, LPIPS) on observed frames with the per-person SDS loss and a hardsurface opacity regularization to prevent transparent artifacts.

## Results

On the Panoptic dataset (sparse single-view input, 6 people), the method achieves 23.60 PSNR and 25.41 LPIPS×100, outperforming HumanNeRF (19.59 PSNR, 38.69 LPIPS×100) and InstantAvatar (15.03 PSNR, 65.95 LPIPS×100) by large margins. On Hi4D (close human interactions), it reaches 23.76/25.14 PSNR on pair00-dance/pair01-hug, surpassing all baselines by 2–6 dB. On ZJU-Mocap with sufficient observations, the method matches HumanNeRF (30.13 vs. 30.23 PSNR) while rendering at 361 FPS vs. 18 FPS, demonstrating no quality penalty from the diffusion-guided pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HumanNeRF [51], the proposed method wins decisively on PSNR (+4.0 dB on Panoptic, +5.0 dB on Hi4D dance) and LPIPS, while HumanNeRF retains a slight SSIM edge on Panoptic (0.8370 vs. 0.8323); HumanNeRF also marginally leads on ZJU-Mocap PSNR (30.23 vs. 30.13) when observations are sufficient. Against InstantAvatar [16], the gap is even larger (PSNR +8.6 dB on Panoptic, +5.2 dB on Hi4D), and the proposed method additionally renders 20× faster (361 vs. 18 FPS at 512×512) with 3.3× less VRAM. Against Shuai et al. [46], the method wins on PSNR (+7.8 dB on Panoptic, +3.0 dB on Hi4D) and LPIPS, while Shuai et al. holds a small SSIM advantage on Panoptic (0.8370 vs. 0.8323) but loses on Hi4D SSIM (0.9165 vs. 0.9328). Overall, the diffusion-guided 3D-GS approach dominates in the sparse-observation regime that is the paper's focus, while remaining competitive (within 0.1 dB) when full observations are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) as a single representation for both static backgrounds and multiple dynamic humans, departing from prior compositional approaches (ST-NeRF, NeuralBody) that require separate implicit functions per person and complex multi-NeRF compositing. For completing unseen body parts under sparse observations, it repurposes Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from text-to-3D generation (DreamFusion) and augments it with CustomDiffusion-based Texture Inversion to preserve target identity — a combination not previously applied to human reconstruction. The canonical-space optimization via SMPL-based Linear Blend Skinning follows the lineage of HumanNeRF and SelfRecon, but the key departure is treating diffusion as a virtual camera prior to hallucinate occluded or cropped regions rather than relying solely on observed multi-frame correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap: in-the-wild monocular videos routinely contain occluded, cropped, and sparsely observed humans, yet prior reconstruction methods assume near-complete visibility. The 40× rendering speedup over NeRF-based baselines (300+ FPS at 1K resolution) makes real-time novel-view and re-posing applications feasible. However, the approach still requires external SMPL fitting, handles only humans (not other dynamic objects), and the diffusion-synthesized regions retain visible artifacts, leaving open the question of whether higher-fidelity completion is achievable without per-person diffusion fine-tuning.
