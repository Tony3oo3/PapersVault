## Problem

Soft prompt tuning for Vision-Language models (e.g., CoOp) achieves strong few-shot accuracy but relies on spurious correlations such as background rather than class-specific semantics, causing significant performance degradation on novel classes and out-of-distribution data. While visual attributes have been explored for zero-shot recognition, their role in guiding few-shot transfer learning to correct model rationales remains under-explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242212/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by a ViT-B/16 vision encoder) and produces class labels via cosine-similarity scoring in CLIP feature space. Training uses 16 shots per class, and the text-side input is not a fixed template but a structured prompt concatenating learnable soft tokens, the class-name embedding, and LLM-generated attribute embeddings (GPT-3, 15 candidates per class reduced to 3 via sampling). The output is a softmax probability over all classes, with the final prediction being the argmax class.

## Method

ArGue replaces the conventional soft-prompt-plus-class-name structure with an attribute-guided prompt {p₁,…,p_M, e_c, v_c^j} that appends LLM-generated primitive visual attributes (e.g., 'long tail', 'black paw') after the class embedding, forcing the model to express high confidence in class-specific semantics rather than background shortcuts. An attribute sampling step clusters the 15 LLM-generated attributes into 3 groups by CLIP-space similarity and selects the most image-relevant attribute per cluster, filtering out non-visual descriptors and semantically mismatched features (e.g., 'red' for a black-and-white sketch). A prompt regularization loss (contrastive, aligning each soft prompt with its natural-language counterpart) prevents overfitting to the few training samples. The ArGue-N variant additionally introduces a negative prompt ('the background of a {class}') and enforces a uniform predictive distribution via an entropy-maximization loss, explicitly teaching the model to ignore spurious correlations. The total objective combines cross-entropy, regularization (β=20), and negative entropy (γ=3) losses, optimized with SGD on the soft tokens only.

## Results

On the novel class prediction task (16-shot, 11 datasets), ArGue-N achieves an average harmonic mean of 81.18%, outperforming the previous SOTA LASP (79.48%) by 1.70% and CLIP zero-shot (71.70%) by 9.48%. The largest gains appear on fine-grained and challenging datasets: FGVCAircraft (+4.55% over LASP) and EuroSAT (+3.98%). For out-of-distribution generalization (ImageNet → 4 variants), ArGue-N leads on all four OOD sets, e.g., 51.47% on ImageNet-A vs. 50.63% (CoCoOp) and 49.11% (LASP), and 49.25% on ImageNet-Sketch vs. 48.75% (CoCoOp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArGue-N outperforms all four baselines on the average harmonic mean across 11 novel-class datasets: 81.18% vs. LASP 79.48% (+1.70%), CoCoOp 75.83% (+5.35%), CoOp 71.66% (+9.52%), and CLIP 71.70% (+9.48%). Notably, ArGue-N surpasses CLIP's zero-shot novel-class accuracy on 10 out of 11 datasets, a first for a prompt-tuning method. The largest margins over LASP appear on fine-grained datasets (FGVCAircraft +4.55%, EuroSAT +3.98%, Flowers102 +3.37%), while the gap narrows on DTD (−0.23% vs. ArGue) where background/foreground distinction is ambiguous. On OOD generalization, ArGue-N leads every variant: ImageNet-A 51.47% (vs. CoCoOp 50.63%, LASP 49.11%), ImageNet-Sketch 49.25% (vs. CoCoOp 48.75%), ImageNetV2 65.02% (vs. CoOp 64.20%), and ImageNet-R 76.96% (vs. CoCoOp 76.18%). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that simply adding LLM-generated attributes to CoOp yields a 7.64% average H gain, with attribute sampling and negative prompting contributing a further +1.88%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the soft prompt tuning lineage for V&L models (CoOp → CoCoOp → LASP) by replacing the direct alignment with class names with alignment to LLM-generated primitive visual attributes, arguing that high confidence in attributes signals correct rationales rather than spurious correlations. It adopts LASP's prompt regularization loss but reinterprets it through the lens of shortcut learning, and introduces two novel components—attribute sampling (clustering + CLIP-space similarity ranking) and negative prompting (enforcing uniform distribution on class-agnostic attributes)—that have no direct precedent in prior prompt tuning work. The approach is conceptually closest to Mao et al.'s 'why prompt' for visual rationales but differs in using LLM-generated attributes instead of manually curated ones and in operating within the few-shot prompt tuning framework rather than full fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic demonstration that LLM-generated visual attributes, when properly filtered and combined with negative prompting, can push few-shot prompt tuning beyond zero-shot CLIP on novel class accuracy (10/11 datasets), a milestone for the prompt tuning community. The attribute sampling finding—that removing 80% of attributes improves accuracy while reducing computation—suggests that quality over quantity matters for attribute-based methods, a principle likely transferable to zero-shot attribute recognition. Limitations include reliance on a single general negative prompt ('the background of a'), which underperforms on texture datasets like DTD where foreground/background distinction is meaningless, and the modest 1.70% average gain over LASP raises questions about whether more class-specific negative attributes or richer attribute generation could yield larger improvements.
