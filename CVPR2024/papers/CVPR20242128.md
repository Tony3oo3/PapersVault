## Problem

3D captures often contain missing regions due to reconstruction failures, unobserved contact areas, or unwanted occluders, yet 2D generative inpainting models do not produce multi-view consistent completions by default. Prior 3D inpainting work focused narrowly on removing entire foreground objects with tight masks, leaving the more general problem of completing arbitrary missing scene regions—without text prompts or precise object boundaries—largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242128/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated multi-view images of a 3D scene (rendered at ~512×512 in experiments) along with binary inpainting masks specifying which pixels are unknown, and optionally a single reference inpaint image to guide content. It produces a completed NeRF representing the full 3D scene with geometry and radiance in the previously missing regions. No text prompts or tight object segmentation masks are required; the scene context alone drives the completion.

## Method

NeRFiller exploits a discovered 'Grid Prior' in Stable Diffusion: tiling four masked images into a 2×2 grid and inpainting them as a single image yields more multi-view consistent results than independent inpainting, likely due to grid-structured examples in the training data. This is generalized to arbitrary image counts via Joint Multi-View Inpainting, which shuffles N images into N/4 grids, obtains noise predictions from the diffusion U-Net for M random permutations, and averages the predictions before each denoising step, effectively sharing context across the full set without reducing per-image resolution. These jointly inpainted images are then distilled into a globally consistent NeRF through Inpaint DU: every S training iterations, N rendered views are encoded to latents, partially noised, jointly inpainted, decoded, and swapped into the training dataset, with the noise level annealed from t=1 to t=0.4 to encourage convergence. An optional relative depth ranking loss from ZoeDepth regularizes geometry in inpainted regions for indoor scenes.

## Results

On the 10-scene completion benchmark (Table 2), NeRFiller achieves 28.28 PSNR, 0.91 SSIM, 0.06 LPIPS, 3.73 MUSIQ, and 696 high-quality LoFTR correspondences, outperforming all baselines on the geometry metric (Corrs) and matching or exceeding them on photometric metrics. The ablation without depth supervision (Ours w/o depth) scores slightly higher on PSNR (28.41) and SSIM (0.92) but lower on Corrs (682 vs. 696), confirming depth ranking loss improves 3D geometry. On the NeRF Synthetic multi-view inpainting evaluation (Table 1), Joint Multi-View Inpainting achieves 15.89 PSNR and 0.82 SSIM, surpassing Extended Attention (14.57 PSNR) and Grid Prior (14.43 PSNR) in multi-view consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRFiller outperforms all three baselines on the geometry metric (Corrs): 696 vs. 643 for LaMask, 665 for SD Image Cond, and 660 for Inpaint+DU. LaMask (LaMa) achieves a deceptively high PSNR of 27.39 but the lowest Corrs (643) because its deterministic inpainter tends to copy the white background into masked regions, producing a flat, geometry-poor NeRF. Inpaint+DU (an adaptation of IN2N) reaches 26.60 PSNR and 660 Corrs, trailing NeRFiller by 1.68 dB in PSNR and 36 correspondences, with visible geometric inconsistencies and view-dependent blur. Extended Attention (from Tune-A-Video) is evaluated in the multi-view inpainting study (Table 1) where it scores 14.57 PSNR and 0.77 SSIM, below Joint Multi-View Inpainting's 15.89 PSNR and 0.82 SSIM, indicating the Grid Prior mechanism provides stronger cross-view consistency than shared attention keys/values. Overall, NeRFiller wins on 3D consistency and geometry while remaining competitive on photometric quality, with the depth-supervised variant trading a negligible 0.13 dB PSNR for a 14-correspondence gain in geometric coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRFiller builds directly on the iterative Dataset Update (DU) mechanism from Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (IN2N) [18], repurposing it for scene completion rather than instruction-based editing. It departs from the object-removal line of work (SPIn-NeRF, InpaintNeRF360) by targeting arbitrary missing regions without requiring tight object masks or text prompts, and from text-to-3D generation methods (DreamFusion, SceneScape) by grounding completions in an existing 3D capture rather than generating from scratch. The key conceptual departure is the Grid Prior observation—that tiling four images into a 2×2 grid before passing them through a 2D inpainting diffusion model yields more multi-view consistent outputs—combined with Joint Multi-View Inpainting, which generalizes this to arbitrarily many images via averaged noise predictions inspired by MultiDiffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: many real 3D captures (photogrammetry, NeRFs from casual video) have missing regions due to contact surfaces, occlusions, or reconstruction failures, and no prior method handles this general scene-completion setting with off-the-shelf 2D models. The Grid Prior is a simple, reusable observation about diffusion model behavior that could benefit other multi-view generation tasks. However, the method struggles with high-frequency detail in regions far from training cameras, cannot handle the large mask patterns typical of casual-capture artifacts (e.g., Nerfbusters), and the quantitative gains over the IN2N-based baseline are moderate (~1 dB PSNR), leaving open whether more principled 3D-aware diffusion priors would be needed for truly robust scene completion.
