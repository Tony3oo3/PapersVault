## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently hallucinate, generating descriptions of non-existent objects, wrong attributes, positions, or numbers that are not grounded in the input image, rendering them untrustworthy for real-world applications. Existing RLHF approaches rely on coarse-grained overall ranking labels, which suffer from annotation ambiguity (annotators struggle to rank complex responses with mixed strengths and flaws) and inefficient credit allocation through linguistic variance, making it difficult to learn precise behavior boundaries against hallucination.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image at 448×448 resolution and a text prompt as input, producing a free-form natural language response. Training data consists of 1.4k preference pairs where human annotators directly correct hallucinated segments within MLLM outputs (averaging 2.65 corrected segments per 64.4-word response), yielding dense segment-level (y_w, y_l) pairs rather than whole-response rankings. The base model is Muffin, combining a BEiT3 visual encoder with a 13B Vicuna v1.0 LLM backbone.

## Method

RLHF-V introduces Dense Direct Preference Optimization (DDPO), a variant of DPO that scores a response as a weighted aggregation of fine-grained segments: corrected (hallucinated) segments receive γ=5× higher weight than unchanged segments in the log-probability computation, with a normalizing factor N=|y_u|+γ|y_c| to prevent length bias. Human feedback is collected as segment-level corrections on hallucinations rather than overall rankings, providing unambiguous, dense preference signals that directly target hallucinated content while excluding linguistic variance and non-robust bias. The framework additionally mitigates hallucination from vision-language mismatch by post-training on human-labeled VQAv2 data and excluding image cropping from data augmentation. Training is computationally efficient, requiring less than 1 hour on 8 A100 GPUs with β=0.5, learning rate 5e-7, and batch size 32 over 7 epochs.

## Results

RLHF-V achieves state-of-the-art trustworthiness among open-source MLLMs, reducing the Object HalBench response-level hallucination rate to 12.2% (vs. 50.5% for the base Muffin model, a 75.8% relative reduction) and the MHumanEval overall hallucination rate to 55.5% (a 34.8% absolute reduction over Muffin's 74.7%). On helpfulness benchmarks, RLHF-V maintains competitive performance: 93.1 on LLaVA Bench conversation, 75.3 on detailed description, and 80.0 on VQAv2 test-dev, comparable to the base model. Notably, RLHF-V is trained on only 1.4k preference samples, an order of magnitude less than LLaVA-RLHF's 10k, yet outperforms it on all hallucination metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLHF-V substantially outperforms all open-source baselines on hallucination metrics: it reduces the Object HalBench response-level rate from 50.5% (Muffin) to 12.2%, beating LLaVA-RLHF (38.1%), InstructBLIP (25.9%), LLaVA 1.5 (46.3%), and LRV (32.3%). Against the closest concurrent method LLaVA-RLHF, RLHF-V achieves a 25.9-point lower Object HalBench rate and a 17.1-point lower MHumanEval overall rate, while using roughly 7× less preference data (1.4k vs. 10k). On the MHumanEval benchmark, RLHF-V (55.5%) also beats InstructBLIP (63.7%) and LLaVA 1.5 (61.0%), though GPT-4V remains lower at 45.9%. On helpfulness, RLHF-V matches or exceeds the base Muffin model on LLaVA Bench conversation (93.1 vs. 89.3) and VQAv2 (80.0), confirming that hallucination reduction does not come at the cost of general capability. In the over-generalization robustness analysis (Table 2), RLHF-V shows the smallest scene-conditioned hallucination increase (avg. Δ = +1.7) compared to GPT-4V (Δ = +5.0) and LLaVA-RLHF (Δ = +5.2), indicating superior resistance to spurious correlation-driven hallucinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) into the multimodal setting by introducing DDPO, which reweights segment-level log-probabilities to exploit fine-grained correctional feedback rather than whole-response rankings. It departs from the traditional RLHF pipeline (reward model + PPO) used by concurrent work like LLaVA-RLHF, replacing it with a simpler supervised objective. The base architecture is Muffin, and the paper additionally identifies training-data sources of hallucination (noisy web text, untrustworthy image cropping) that prior MLLM work largely overlooked, positioning hallucination mitigation as both a preference-learning and a data-hygiene problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that 1.4k segment-level corrections outperform 10k overall-ranking labels, suggesting that feedback granularity and quality can substitute for data volume in MLLM alignment—a finding that could reshape how teams budget annotation effort. The framework's demonstrated transferability to LLaVA and OmniLMM-12B indicates broad applicability. However, the evaluation relies heavily on a small 146-sample human benchmark (MHumanEval), the primary base model is a single 13B architecture, and the over-generalization robustness claim against GPT-4V rests on a narrow scene-based analysis, leaving open whether the gains hold at scale or across diverse model families. The authors note future directions including collecting preferences from MLLMs themselves and extending the approach to text-only LLMs.
