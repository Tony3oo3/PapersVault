## Problem

Existing automated wildlife detection methods struggle to generalize across species and landscapes because they learn salient features of homogeneous backgrounds (grassland, woodland) rather than the objects of interest, causing performance to degrade when terrain changes even slightly. This limits the scalability of aerial wildlife surveys, which remain central to conservation biology but are currently bottlenecked by labor-intensive manual counting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 1024×1024×3 RGB tiles extracted from 8256×5504 nadir aerial photographs with 25% spatial overlap, and produces bounding boxes with one of 21 species class labels in a single forward pass. At inference, detections are filtered at a 50% confidence threshold followed by non-maximum suppression to remove overlapping boxes. The architecture is single-stage and end-to-end, requiring no separate region proposal or multi-scale feature pyramid.

## Method

WildlifeMapper adapts the SAM architecture with three novel modules that jointly address background dominance and small-object localization. The High-Frequency Feature Generator (HFG) applies a Discrete Fourier Transform to suppress low-frequency components (the homogeneous background), producing a feature map that highlights potential animal locations; this is merged with long-range contextual features from a large-kernel (16×16) dilated patch embedding layer via cross-attention in the Feature Refiner. The Query Refiner then uses the HFG-derived location cues to refine 100 learnable queries before they are decoded by a lightweight two-way attention box decoder against frozen ViT encoder features (pre-trained from SAM). Training follows the DETR paradigm with Hungarian bipartite matching and L1/GIoU/cross-entropy losses, supplemented by mosaic augmentation (+0.07 mAP) and a hard-negative mining stage that relabels false-positive regions as background to reduce confusion with rocks and dead trees.

## Results

On the Mara-Wildlife dataset (21 species, 77,966 tiles), WildlifeMapper achieves 0.56 mAP and 0.79 mAP@50, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline (YOLOv5 at 0.30 mAP) by 0.26 mAP. On the combined Virunga-Garamba-AED dataset (6 species, 4 countries), WM reaches 0.68 mAP and 0.85 mAP@50, improving over the best baseline (YOLOv5/v8 at 0.48 mAP) by 0.20 mAP (~42% relative gain). WM also achieves the lowest counting error (1.9 animals/image on Mara-Wildlife; 0.11 on Virunga-Garamba-AED), indicating superior per-species count accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildlifeMapper outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets by a wide margin. On Mara-Wildlife (21 species), WM's 0.56 mAP exceeds YOLOv5 (0.30), LSKNet (0.29), Co-DETR-SwinL (0.28), YOLOv8 (0.27), Co-DETR-R50 (0.27), Faster R-CNN (0.24), DETR (0.22), and DroneDetect (0.18) — a 0.26 mAP gap over the strongest competitor. On the simpler Virunga-Garamba-AED benchmark (6 species), WM (0.68 mAP) beats YOLOv5 and YOLOv8 (both 0.48), Faster R-CNN (0.34), and DETR (0.30) by 0.20 mAP. The authors attribute the baselines' underperformance to their reliance on dominant homogeneous background features rather than object-specific local structure, a limitation that WM's High-Frequency Feature Generator explicitly addresses. WM also achieves the lowest counting error in both settings (1.9 vs. 2.12 for YOLOv5 on Mara-Wildlife; 0.11 vs. 0.12 for YOLOv5 on Virunga-Garamba-AED), confirming that the mAP advantage translates into more accurate per-species population estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

WildlifeMapper adapts the DETR end-to-end detection framework (Hungarian matching, transformer decoder) and the SAM ViT image encoder to the aerial wildlife detection problem, but its core departure is the High-Frequency Feature Generator, which applies a DFT-based high-pass filter to suppress the dominant low-frequency background (grassland, woodland) that causes prior CNN and Transformer detectors to overfit to landscape rather than object properties. The Feature Refiner and Query Refiner modules, which use cross-attention to inject location-candidate features into both the image encoder path and the learnable queries, extend the guided-query idea from GTNet and MethaneMapper to the aerial wildlife setting. The large-kernel patch embedding for long-range context draws on recent large-kernel CNN work, addressing the challenge that small animal footprints in aerial tiles require surrounding scene context for species discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 42% mAP improvement over the strongest baselines and the release of a 21-species, 28k-annotation dataset verified by ecologists provide a much-needed benchmark for multi-species aerial wildlife detection, a task where prior datasets covered at most 6 species. The HFG's frequency-domain background suppression is a conceptually simple but effective mechanism that could transfer to other aerial detection tasks (e.g., livestock, vehicles in homogeneous terrain). Open questions include whether the approach scales to even denser herds or smaller species, how it performs under varying seasonal lighting and canopy conditions, and whether the frozen SAM encoder limits adaptability when fine-tuning for new taxonomic groups. Failure cases involving shadow occlusion and rock/animal confusion remain unresolved.
