## Problem

Animating personalized images (e.g., from DreamBooth or LoRA models) with realistic, text-controllable motions while preserving their distinct styles and high-fidelity details remains unsolved. Existing temporal alignment layers for T2V generation conflate appearance and motion alignment, making it difficult to follow motion-related text prompts, and no dedicated benchmark exists for evaluating personalized image animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240149/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

PIA takes a single personalized image (encoded to latent z^I), a text prompt describing the desired motion, and a user-settable inter-frame affinity map (a single-channel [1×h×w] feature controlling motion magnitude) as inputs, and produces a 16-frame animated video clip whose first frame resembles the condition image. The inter-frame affinity is computed during training as normalized L1 distance in HSV space between frames, but at inference users can manually set affinity values to three preset levels (light, middle, large) for motion magnitude control. The method is plug-and-play: any personalized T2I model can be swapped in at inference without retraining.

## Method

PIA builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net with AnimateDiff-style temporal alignment layers and introduces a lightweight condition module—a single learnable convolution with zero-initialized weights—that concatenates the condition frame latent z^I with the inter-frame affinity s^i and adds the result to the first convolution layer's output. This design offloads appearance alignment to the condition module, freeing the temporal alignment layers to focus on motion-related guidance. Training jointly fine-tunes the condition module and temporal alignment layers on WebVid video clips (16 frames) with 20% random dropping of condition inputs to retain T2V capability, while the base T2I model remains frozen. The key architectural insight is that by injecting appearance information at the input layer via the affinity-guided condition module, the temporal attention layers no longer need to simultaneously enforce both appearance consistency and motion generation, resolving the trade-off between image fidelity and text-controllable motion.

## Results

On AnimateBench, PIA achieves the highest CLIP image alignment score of 93.44 (vs. 90.10 for VideoComposer and 89.72 for AnimateDiff) and the highest text alignment score of 68.74. On WebVid, PIA scores 85.11 (image) and 76.59 (text), outperforming AnimateDiff by 12.29 and 3.73 points respectively. In the user study, PIA receives a strong preference rate of 0.525 for image alignment and 0.670 for text alignment, substantially exceeding VideoComposer (0.180/0.110) and AnimateDiff (0.295/0.220).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIA outperforms all quantitative baselines across every metric. Against AnimateDiff (the closest open-source baseline), PIA gains +3.72 in image CLIP and +0.33 in text CLIP on AnimateBench, and a much larger +12.29 in image CLIP on WebVid, indicating AnimateDiff struggles with image fidelity in open-domain settings. Against VideoComposer, PIA leads by +3.34 in image CLIP and +2.57 in text CLIP on AnimateBench, while matching its WebVid image score (85.11 vs. 85.03) and exceeding its text score by +1.07. In the user study, PIA's preference rates (0.525 image, 0.670 text) are roughly 1.8×–6× higher than the baselines, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality. Qualitatively, the paper notes that Pika Labs and Gen-2 produce high-fidelity videos but respond poorly to motion-related prompts, whereas PIA generates more text-aligned motion (e.g., adding fire or walking) while preserving input image details.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIA builds directly on AnimateDiff's plug-and-play temporal alignment layers, which transform any personalized T2I model (e.g., DreamBooth, LoRA) into a video generator, and on Stable Diffusion as the base architecture. The key departure is the introduction of a lightweight condition module at the U-Net input layer that explicitly injects appearance information from a condition frame guided by inter-frame affinity scores, thereby offloading appearance alignment from the temporal layers so they can focus on motion. This design philosophy—separating appearance and motion alignment into different architectural components—contrasts with prior T2V approaches (AnimateDiff, VideoComposer, Tune-A-Video) where temporal attention must simultaneously handle both, and the zero-initialization strategy for the new module echoes ControlNet's approach to conditionally extending pre-trained diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIA provides a practical, plug-and-play solution for the growing personalized T2I community (Civitai, Hugging Face) to animate their unique images with text-controlled motion without retraining, and the AnimateBench benchmark fills a notable evaluation gap for this specific task. However, the paper acknowledges significant limitations: color discrepancies arise when input images deviate from the WebVid training domain, and performance depends on including personalized trigger words in animation prompts. Open questions include whether training on more diverse, higher-quality video data would resolve the style-domain gap, whether the 16-frame training length limits scalability to longer clips, and whether the inter-frame affinity mechanism generalizes to more complex multi-object motion scenarios.
