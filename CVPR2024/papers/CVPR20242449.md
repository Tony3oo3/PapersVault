## Problem

Transductive inference—making joint predictions across a batch of query samples—has been shown to significantly boost few-shot classification accuracy for vision-only models, yet it has been entirely overlooked for vision-language models like CLIP. The paper identifies two key obstacles: standard clustering and transductive methods ignore CLIP's textual knowledge, and their Gaussian distributional assumptions are ill-suited to the probability-simplex structure of CLIP's outputs, causing them to underperform even inductive baselines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a mini-batch of 75 query images, K class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [class name]'), and optionally a support set of s labeled images per class. Features are computed as CLIP's zero-shot probability vectors—softmax of temperature-scaled cosine similarities between image and text embeddings—yielding K-dimensional vectors on the unit simplex Δ_K. The output is a class label per query image, with the entire batch processed jointly rather than independently; in the zero-shot case (empty support set), the problem reduces to simplex clustering followed by bipartite graph matching to assign cluster identities to class labels.

## Method

The paper formulates transductive CLIP classification as a maximum-likelihood problem on the unit simplex, modeling each class-conditional distribution with a Dirichlet law whose parameters are estimated jointly with soft class assignments. The objective combines a Dirichlet log-likelihood data-fitting term, an entropic barrier enforcing simplex constraints, and an MDL partition-complexity penalty that discourages overly balanced (and thus unrealistic) cluster sizes. A novel block Majorization-Minimization algorithm solves the problem by alternating closed-form updates: Dirichlet parameters are updated via a tight quadratic majorant (Lemma 1) that avoids the Newton iterations required by prior Minka-style approaches, while assignments are updated via a softmax of log-likelihoods plus a linearized MDL correction. When the support set is empty and λ equals the query set size, the algorithm provably reduces to standard EM for a Dirichlet mixture model, establishing a clean theoretical link between the proposed transductive objective and classical mixture-model inference.

## Results

On the 4-shot few-shot classification benchmark (averaged over 1,000 tasks across 11 datasets), EM-Dirichlet achieves 73.6% average accuracy, outperforming the best inductive CLIP method TIP-Adapter (68.3%) by 5.3 points and the best transductive baseline Laplacian Shot (64.8%) by 8.8 points. In the zero-shot setting with 75-sample query batches, Hard EM-Dirichlet reaches 67.6% average accuracy versus 58.5% for inductive zero-shot CLIP, with a near-20-point gain on ImageNet (77.6% vs. 58.3%). The method also runs in ~1.04 s per task on ImageNet, comparable to or faster than most transductive baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EM-Dirichlet (73.6% avg) decisively outperforms all transductive few-shot baselines: PADDLE (60.9%), Laplacian Shot (64.8%), α-TIM (57.8%), and BDSCPN (57.5%), with the largest margins on fine-grained datasets like DTD (+12.6 over PADDLE) and ImageNet (+26.3 over PADDLE). Against inductive CLIP-specific methods, EM-Dirichlet beats TIP-Adapter (68.3%) by 5.3 points and CoOp (65.5%) by 8.1 points on average, though TIP-Adapter retains an edge on EuroSAT (72.5 vs. 50.8), likely due to text-prompt confounding in that dataset. In the zero-shot setting (Table 1), Hard EM-Dirichlet (67.6% avg) surpasses inductive zero-shot CLIP (58.5%) by 9.1 points and the best Gaussian-based clustering baseline EM-Gaussian diag-cov (49.7%) by 17.9 points, confirming that the Dirichlet model on simplex probability features is critical. The paper notes that on Food101, zero-shot performance (90.2%) actually exceeds few-shot (87.9%), consistent with CLIP's observation that few labeled examples can introduce noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transductive few-shot classification framework (pioneered for vision-only models by PADDLE [34] and others) to vision-language models like CLIP, which had been entirely overlooked in the transductive literature. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that CLIP's zero-shot probability outputs live on the unit simplex, making Dirichlet distributions the natural data model rather than the Gaussians assumed by standard clustering and transductive methods. The algorithmic contribution builds on Minka's [35] MM approach for Dirichlet parameter estimation but introduces a novel tight majorant that yields closed-form quadratic updates, eliminating the costly digamma-function inversion required by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that transductive inference can substantially boost CLIP's classification accuracy (~20% on ImageNet zero-shot), opening a practical avenue for batch-based inference in streaming and on-device settings. A notable negative result—that standard clustering methods (K-means, EM-Gaussian) actually score below inductive CLIP when applied to probability features—explains why transductive CLIP had not been explored and highlights the importance of distributional assumptions for simplex data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to pixel-level tasks (segmentation), out-of-distribution detection, and whether the gains persist with larger batch sizes or different CLIP backbones beyond ResNet-50.
