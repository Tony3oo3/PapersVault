## Problem

General-purpose vision-language models (e.g., LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) hallucinate or produce inaccurate answers when applied to remote sensing imagery due to the domain's unique challenges: high-resolution scenes with diverse object scales, many small objects requiring region-level reasoning, and a complete absence of domain-specific multimodal instruction-following data. No existing RS VLM supports unified multitask conversation with visual grounding (spatial coordinates in outputs) and region-level dialogue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242163/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GeoChat accepts a 504×504 remote sensing image, a natural language query, an optional region bounding box (encoded textually as {x_left, y_top, x_right, y_bottom|θ} with coordinates normalized to [0,100]), and a task-specific token ([grounding], [identify], or [refer]) that switches the model's response mode. Outputs are natural language responses that can be interleaved with object bounding boxes in the same textual coordinate format, enabling visually grounded captions, referring expression detection, and region-specific VQA within a single conversational turn.

## Method

GeoChat builds on LLaVA-v1.5's architecture (CLIP-ViT-L/14 backbone, MLP adaptor, Vicuna-v1.5 7B LLM) but introduces three key modifications: (1) positional encodings are interpolated from 336×336 to 504×504, doubling the patch count to 1296 for finer spatial resolution on small RS objects; (2) task-specific tokens and a textual spatial coordinate representation are injected into both inputs and outputs, enabling the model to accept region prompts and generate grounded object locations; (3) only the LLM is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 64) on a novel 318k-instruction RS dataset generated from SAMRS object detection, LRBEN VQA, and NWPU-RESISC-45 scene classification data, while the CLIP encoder and MLP adaptor remain frozen. Training proceeds in two stages: one epoch over all instruction types followed by 1600 additional steps on grounding data alone, preserving the base model's general conversational abilities while injecting RS domain knowledge.

## Results

On zero-shot scene classification, GeoChat achieves 84.43% accuracy on UCMerced and 72.03% on AID, substantially outperforming LLaVA-1.5 (68.00%/51.00%) and Qwen-VL (62.90%/52.60%). On the RSVQA-LRBEN VQA benchmark, GeoChat reaches 90.70% average accuracy in a zero-shot setting, approaching supervised specialists like RSGPT (92.29%) and SHRNet (91.84%) while far exceeding other zero-shot VLMs (LLaVA-1.5: 62.77%, Qwen-VL: 55.35%). On RSVQA-HRBEN, GeoChat attains 72.30% average accuracy, beating LLaVA-1.5 by 3.9% and MiniGPTv2 by 25.8%. For visual grounding, GeoChat achieves 10.6% overall acc@0.5% versus 7.6% for MiniGPTv2, and dominates region captioning with 83.9 METEOR vs. 10.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoChat decisively outperforms all three general-purpose zero-shot VLMs: it beats LLaVA-1.5 by 27.93 points (90.70% vs. 62.77%) on RSVQA-LRBEN average accuracy, surpasses Qwen-VL-Chat by 35.35 points, and exceeds MiniGPTv2 by 35.74 points. Against supervised RS-VQA specialists, GeoChat (trained only via LoRA on a generic instruction set, not fine-tuned on the target VQA dataset) trails RSGPT by 1.59 points and SHRNet by 1.14 points, but remains within ~1.6% of the best supervised method while retaining conversational and grounding capabilities those models lack. On RSVQA-HRBEN, GeoChat leads all zero-shot baselines by a wide margin (72.30% vs. LLaVA-1.5's 68.40% and MiniGPTv2's 46.46%), with a particularly strong 15.9-point advantage over LLaVA-1.5 on the Comparison subset. For visual grounding, GeoChat improves overall acc@0.5% from 7.6% (MiniGPTv2) to 10.6%, with the largest gains on single-object grounding (16.0% vs. 9.1%) and [grounding] tasks (11.8% vs. 2.6%). On region captioning, GeoChat achieves 83.9 METEOR versus MiniGPTv2's 10.0, a roughly 8× improvement, indicating far more coherent and accurate region-level descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoChat directly extends the LLaVA-v1.5 architecture (CLIP-ViT encoder + MLP adaptor + Vicuna-v1.5 LLM) by adding spatial location representations in both inputs and outputs, task-specific tokens for grounding/identify/refer, and LoRA fine-tuning on a purpose-built remote sensing instruction dataset. It departs from prior RS VLMs like RSGPT, which required separate finetuning per task and lacked region-level reasoning or visual grounding, by unifying image-level, region-level, and grounded conversation tasks in a single model. The dataset construction pipeline—leveraging Vicuna to generate multi-round conversations from object detection annotations (SAMRS) and existing VQA/classification datasets—is a novel data-generation strategy that addresses the absence of RS-specific instruction-following corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoChat establishes the first unified RS VLM with visual grounding capability and provides a 318k instruction dataset plus a multitask conversation benchmark that the field lacked, making it a practical foundation for future RS conversational AI. However, grounding accuracy remains low (10.6% overall, 2.9% on small objects), indicating that spatial reasoning in high-resolution RS imagery is far from solved. Open questions include whether scaling the grounding training data or using higher-resolution encoders can close this gap, and whether the LoRA-based domain adaptation strategy generalizes to other specialized imaging domains beyond remote sensing.
