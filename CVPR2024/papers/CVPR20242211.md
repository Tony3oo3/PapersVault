## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods either require a separate model per image class, rely on dataset class names that may be ambiguous or unknown to foundation models, or assume balanced class distributions. No prior method simultaneously handles multi-class detection with a single model, operates without informative class names, and remains robust to the long-tailed sample distributions natural in manufacturing settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242211/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images from multiple classes as input, along with a class identity index (but critically, not a class name string). The ALIGN image encoder extracts multi-scale features from layers {3, 10, 17, 37}, which are split into patch-level feature vectors. Output is a per-patch anomaly score combining reconstruction error and a semantic posterior probability, which can be aggregated for image-level AUROC or spatially arranged for pixel-level segmentation masks.

## Method

LTAD combines a transformer-based reconstruction module (RM) that projects patch features onto the normal manifold with a semantic AD (SAD) module that classifies patches in ALIGN text space using learned pseudo-class-name-conditioned prompts. The key insight is learning 2-token pseudo-class names s_c (initialized as 'object object') via backpropagation during a VAE-based Phase 1, which both conditions feature synthesis for minority-class augmentation and provides class-sensitive text prompts for the SAD binary classifier. Phase 2 then trains the RM transformer and linear projections Φ_l that map multi-layer patch features into ALIGN text embedding space, using noise-perturbed patches as pseudo-anomalies. The final anomaly score is a weighted sum S = S_rec + λ·S_sem, where S_rec is the squared reconstruction error and S_sem is the softmax posterior from dot products with text-encoded 'a' and 'a broken' prompts.

## Results

On MVTec, LTAD achieves 88.86% detection AUROC and 94.46% segmentation AUROC under the exp100 configuration, outperforming the strongest baseline UniAD (87.70% / 93.95%). Across all three datasets (MVTec, VisA, DAGM) and 9 imbalance configurations (32 total detection/segmentation tasks), LTAD wins 29 out of 32 tasks (90.6%). On VisA and DAGM, LTAD achieves the best performance in all 24 tasks, with detection AUROC reaching 84.80% (VisA step100) and 94.40% (DAGM exp100). Adding the semantic AD module improves performance on 31 out of 32 tasks (96.9%) over the reconstruction-only variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTAD outperforms all six baselines on 29 of 32 tasks across MVTec, VisA, and DAGM. The older single-model-per-class methods (Cut&Paste, MKD, DRAEM) are consistently the weakest, scoring 69–80% detection AUROC on MVTec versus LTAD's 85–89%. Among the multi-class baselines, RegAD is the closest competitor on MVTec, beating LTAD on 2 of 8 segmentation tasks (95.20 vs. 94.46 on exp100; 95.10 vs. 93.83 on step100), but it is unavailable for several long-tailed configurations and trails LTAD by 8–13 points on VisA and DAGM. UniAD wins 1 detection task on MVTec (86.21 vs. 86.05 on exp200) but is outperformed by 4–10 points on VisA and 8–13 points on DAGM. AnomalyGPT, which relies on a large vision-language model, is consistently outperformed by LTAD across all three datasets, with gaps of 2–12 points on detection AUROC. The gains are largest on VisA and DAGM, where LTAD achieves the best result in every task, suggesting the learnable pseudo-class names and VAE-based augmentation are most beneficial when class names are ambiguous or data is severely imbalanced.

## Relation to Prior Work

LTAD sits at the intersection of two lines of multi-class anomaly detection: reconstruction-based methods (UniAD, DRAEM) that project images onto a normal manifold, and semantic AD methods (WinCLIP, AnomalyGPT) that leverage vision-language foundation models with explicit class-name prompts. The key departure is the combination of both scoring mechanisms in a single architecture, coupled with learnable pseudo-class names that replace dataset-provided labels—addressing the ambiguity and unknown-class problems that limit prior semantic AD. The VAE-based feature synthesis conditioned on learned class prompts draws from long-tailed recognition literature (generative augmentation for minority classes) but is the first application of this idea to anomaly detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-tailed class distributions are natural in manufacturing (different products have different production volumes), yet all prior AD benchmarks assume balanced classes; this paper provides the first systematic formulation with 9 imbalance configurations across 3 datasets, filling a practical gap. The learnable pseudo-class-name mechanism is a lightweight but effective solution to the class-name ambiguity problem that plagues semantic AD, and the two-phase training strategy (VAE augmentation → joint RM+SAD training) is a reusable recipe for data-scarce AD settings. Limitations include reliance on the older ALIGN foundation model (rather than CLIP or newer VLMs), evaluation on only 3 datasets with at most 15 classes, and the somewhat ad hoc initialization of pseudo-class names with 'object object.' Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of classes, whether newer foundation models change the optimal prompt design, and whether the VAE augmentation generalizes to video or 3D industrial inspection.
