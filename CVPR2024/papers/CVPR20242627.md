## Problem

Existing anchor-based large-scale multi-view clustering methods focus on exploring global correlations between anchor graphs or projection matrices, rather than directly learning the correlations of embedding features within and across views. The paper asks whether directly modeling inter- and intra-view embedding feature correlations would be more effective for obtaining the consensus representation that drives clustering performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-computed nonlinear RBF anchor graphs φ(X_v) ∈ R^{M×N} for each of V views (with M anchors, M ≪ N), a low-frequency parameter L controlling how many Fourier components to retain, and regularization parameters β and λ. It produces a consensus embedding feature B̃ ∈ R^{K×N} (K ≪ M) from which a binary clustering indicator matrix G ∈ {0,1}^{C×N} is obtained via k-means. All embedding features are constrained to z-score normalization (zero mean, unit variance per sample column), and the anchor graphs are constructed offline before the main optimization loop.

## Method

S²MVTC stacks the per-view embedding features B_v ∈ R^{K×N} into a rotated tensor B ∈ R^{K×V×N} and introduces a novel Tensor Low-Frequency Approximation (TLFA) operator that retains only the first L low-frequency frontal slices in the FFT domain along the sample dimension, thereby enforcing a smooth (graph-similarity-aware) representation of samples within each view. A consensus constraint ‖B̃ − B_v‖_F² ensures inter-view semantic consistency, where B̃ is the z-score-normalized average of all view features. The joint optimization over projection matrices U_v, embedding features B_v, the TLFA auxiliary variable Y, and the consensus B̃ is solved by alternating closed-form updates (ridge regression for U_v, weighted averaging for B_v, FFT-domain truncation for Y, and normalization for B̃), with τ scaled by 1.1× per iteration over a fixed 7-iteration schedule. This design avoids the O(VN³) cost of full membership graphs, achieving O(max(KMN·T, M³·T)) computation and O(max(KVN, MN)) storage.

## Results

S²MVTC achieves the best or near-best clustering performance across all six large-scale multi-view datasets. On CCV, it reaches 54.79% ACC and 43.86% ARI, surpassing the strongest baseline sMERA-MVC (45.54% ACC, 30.15% ARI) by 9.25% and 13.71% respectively. On Caltech102, S²MVTC attains 55.47% ACC and 64.81% ARI versus sMERA-MVC's 45.23% and 34.72%. In terms of efficiency, S²MVTC achieves up to 77.58× speedup over k-means on AwA and 29.13× on YouTubeFace_sel, while running ~39× faster than sMERA-MVC on AwA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²MVTC consistently outperforms all eight baselines on ACC across five of six datasets, with the most dramatic gains over sMERA-MVC (the strongest tensor-based competitor): +9.25% ACC on CCV, +10.24% on Caltech102, +2.60% on NUS-WIDE-OBJ, +0.87% on AwA, and +3.51% on YouTubeFace_sel. On CIFAR-10, sMERA-MVC achieves a perfect 100% ACC while S²MVTC reaches 99.94%, a negligible gap. In terms of speed, S²MVTC is dramatically faster than sMERA-MVC on large datasets (10.35 s vs. 390.59 s on AwA, ~39× speedup; 11.13 s vs. 214.23 s on CIFAR-10, ~20× speedup). Compared to the other baselines (BMVC, SFMC, OPMC, FPMVS-CAG, FMVACC, FastMICE, FSMSC), S²MVTC achieves substantially higher clustering quality (e.g., 54.79% vs. ≤22.84% ACC on CCV) while also being among the fastest, with CPU times of 1.61 s (CCV), 3.56 s (Caltech102), and 10.35 s (AwA). The only metric where S²MVTC trails sMERA-MVC is NMI on NUS-WIDE-OBJ (74.40% vs. 82.12%), but it still leads on ACC and ARI for that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²MVTC departs from the dominant paradigm in anchor-based large-scale multi-view clustering, where prior methods (e.g., sMERA-MVC, FMVACC, FPMVS-CAG) explore global correlations at the level of anchor graphs or projection matrices before deriving embedding features. Instead, this paper shifts the tensor-level reasoning directly onto the embedding feature tensor, introducing a novel Tensor Low-Frequency Approximation (TLFA) operator built on t-SVD/FFT to enforce intra-view smoothness, complemented by a consensus constraint for inter-view semantic consistency. Conceptually, it repurposes the smooth-representation clustering idea (Hu et al., 2014) and the t-SVD machinery (Kilmer et al., 2013) in a new location within the MVC pipeline, while the closest competitor sMERA-MVC applies tensor network decomposition to anchor graphs rather than to the resulting embedding features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: S²MVTC achieves 20–77× speedups over the strongest tensor-based baseline (sMERA-MVC) on large datasets while matching or exceeding its clustering accuracy, making high-quality multi-view clustering feasible on datasets with 50k–100k samples on a single CPU. The ablation revealing that the TLFA component is critical (removing it drops ACC from ~55% to ~15% on CCV) underscores that intra-view smoothness, not just inter-view consensus, is the dominant driver of performance. Open questions include how the method handles incomplete or dynamically changing view sets, whether the fixed RBF anchor graph construction remains optimal for very high-dimensional or non-Euclidean data, and whether the FFT-based TLFA generalizes to higher-order tensors beyond the 3D case studied here.
