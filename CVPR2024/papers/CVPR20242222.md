## Problem

Existing self-supervised 3D correspondence methods, such as CPAE, assume that input point cloud pairs are precisely aligned, and their accuracy degrades sharply when shapes are arbitrarily rotated. This assumption is rarely satisfied in practice, where object scans and shape instances can appear in any orientation, severely limiting real-world applicability of dense correspondence estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242222/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two point clouds P₁, P₂ ∈ ℝ^{N×3} of the same semantic category, each potentially in an arbitrary SO(3) rotation, and outputs dense point-wise correspondences {pᵢ, qᵢ} between them. Internally, the encoder produces an SO(3)-equivariant global shape descriptor Z ∈ ℝ^{C×3} (C=170) and N per-point dynamic local shape transforms f_θᵢ: ℝ^{C×3} → ℝ^{C′×3}. At inference, cross-reconstruction via the decoder yields a pose-aligned point cloud, from which correspondences are extracted by nearest-neighbor matching.

## Method

RIST uses a VN-DGCNN encoder to simultaneously extract an SO(3)-equivariant global shape descriptor Z and, for each point, a dynamic SO(3)-invariant local shape transform f_θᵢ whose parameters θᵢ ∈ ℝ^{C′×C} are predicted from SO(3)-invariant point-wise features via an MLP. Each local shape transform maps the global descriptor Z to a point-specific local shape descriptor that encodes local semantics and geometry. Training is fully self-supervised: a self-reconstruction loss (MSE + EMD) forces the decoder to recover the input from its own local descriptors, while a cross-reconstruction loss (Chamfer distance) forces local transforms from one shape to successfully reconstruct another shape of the same category, thereby encouraging semantically corresponding points to produce similar local descriptors. Because both encoder and decoder are SO(3)-equivariant (built from Vector Neurons), the cross-reconstructed point cloud is automatically aligned to the target's pose, enabling rotation-invariant correspondence extraction without any alignment step.

## Results

On ShapeNetPart (8 categories), RIST achieves 57.6% average IoU for part label transfer without rotation augmentation and 57.8% with rotation augmentation, outperforming the best baseline (DPC at 34.0%) by over 23 points. On ScanObjectNN (real-world chair data), RIST reaches 39.6% IoU (w/o rotations) and 37.9% (w/ rotations), surpassing the next-best method (CPAE at 24.4%) by ~15 points. On KeypointNet, RIST consistently outperforms all baselines across all 12 categories and distance thresholds, with gains of up to 10× on certain classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIST decisively outperforms all four baselines on both ShapeNetPart and ScanObjectNN. Against CPAE [3], the primary SOTA self-supervised correspondence method, RIST gains approximately 25–27 points in average IoU (57.6% vs. 30.5% w/o rotations; 57.8% vs. 32.4% w/ rotations), and the gap widens on ScanObjectNN real-world data (39.6% vs. 24.4%). Against DPC [17], the strongest baseline, RIST still leads by ~23–25 points (57.6% vs. 32.2%). FoldingNet [38] and AtlasNetV2 [8] trail RIST by roughly 29 points on ShapeNetPart (28.8% and 28.9% respectively). Notably, RIST's advantage is consistent regardless of whether rotation augmentation is used during training, whereas baselines show only marginal or no improvement from augmentation, confirming that RIST's SO(3)-invariant design addresses rotation robustness by architecture rather than data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised 3D correspondence line of work (CPAE, DPC, Liu & Liu) by removing their critical assumption of aligned input shapes. Rather than relying on rotation augmentation or canonical-space alignment, RIST repurposes SO(3)-equivariant Vector Neural Networks (VNNs) as the backbone and introduces a novel dynamic per-point local shape transform that maps a global equivariant descriptor to a local invariant descriptor. The conceptual departure is that correspondence is established through cross-reconstruction using another shape's local transforms, rather than through a shared canonical representation, making the approach inherently rotation-robust by design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing the rotation assumption that has limited real-world deployment of self-supervised 3D correspondence, RIST opens the door to practical applications in AR/VR, texture mapping, and robotics where object orientation is uncontrolled. The method's limitation to same-category shapes and its degraded performance on rotationally symmetric objects (bathtub, table) highlight open questions about generalizing to cross-category or highly symmetric shapes. The authors note future work on robustness under common corruptions (noise, partial scans), and the dynamic local transform paradigm could potentially be extended to other equivariant tasks beyond correspondence.
