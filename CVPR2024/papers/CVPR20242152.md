## Problem

Post-training quantization methods designed for CNNs degrade severely on vision transformers because ViT activations exhibit drastic per-instance scale variations across channels (lacking BatchNorm), and conventional group quantization fixes channel-to-group assignments during calibration, making them unable to adapt to the diverse distributions observed at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242152/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained ViT (ViT, DeiT, or Swin Transformer) and a tiny calibration set (32 ImageNet images for classification, 1 COCO image for detection/segmentation) to produce a quantized model at 4-bit or 6-bit. At inference, the quantized model consumes standard RGB images and outputs class labels, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks, but internally re-partitions activation channels and attention rows into groups dynamically for each input instance before performing the quantized matrix multiplications.

## Method

IGQ-ViT splits activation channels (for FC-layer inputs) and softmax attention rows (across tokens) into G groups at runtime for each input instance, assigning each channel/row to the quantizer whose bounds minimize a squared distance to its min/max, then optimizing the quantizer bounds via an EM-style alternating procedure. The same instance-aware grouping is applied to both linear operations and softmax attentions, addressing scale variation in both. A group size allocation step formulates per-layer group counts as an integer linear program that minimizes total KL-divergence perturbation under a bit-operation budget, solved with the PULP library. The approach requires only ~3.3% additional BOPs over layer-wise quantization even with 16 groups, since the extra cost is limited to per-channel min/max computation, group assignment, and a small number of floating-point group summations.

## Results

On ImageNet under 4/4-bit quantization, IGQ-ViT with 12 groups achieves 73.61% top-1 accuracy on ViT-S, 79.23% on DeiT-B, and 83.14% on Swin-B, outperforming the previous best (RepQ-ViT) by 8.56, 3.62, and 4.82 points respectively. Under 6/6-bit, IGQ-ViT reaches 80.76% on ViT-S and 81.71% on DeiT-B, narrowing the gap to full-precision to under 0.7%. On COCO object detection with Swin-T Mask R-CNN at 4/4-bit, IGQ-ViT achieves 40.5 AP^box and 38.5 AP^mask, surpassing RepQ-ViT (36.1/36.0) and APQ-ViT (23.7/22.6) by large margins, while at 6/6-bit it nearly matches full-precision (48.2 vs. 48.5 AP^box on Swin-S).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGQ-ViT consistently outperforms all three PTQ baselines (PTQ4ViT, APQ-ViT, RepQ-ViT) across every architecture and bit-width setting. At 4/4-bit, the gap over RepQ-ViT (the strongest prior method) is largest on ViT-S (73.61% vs. 65.05%, +8.56 pts) and Swin-B (83.14% vs. 78.32%, +4.82 pts), while PTQ4ViT and APQ-ViT suffer catastrophic degradation (e.g., 42.57% and 47.95% on ViT-S). At 6/6-bit, IGQ-ViT still leads RepQ-ViT by 0.33 pts on ViT-S and 0.44 pts on DeiT-B, and the group size allocation technique adds a further 0.1–0.5 pts over the fixed-group variant. On COCO detection/segmentation at 4/4-bit, IGQ-ViT beats RepQ-ViT by 4.4 AP^box on Swin-T Mask R-CNN (40.5 vs. 36.1) and by 2.8 AP^box on Swin-S Cascade Mask R-CNN (50.5 vs. 47.7), while PTQ4ViT and APQ-ViT collapse to near-random performance. In the ablation comparing grouping strategies (Table 5), IGQ-ViT's instance-aware dynamic grouping outperforms both uniform consecutive-channel grouping (VS-Quant, Q-BERT) and calibration-time sorting (PEG) by 16–15 points on ViT-S and DeiT-B, confirming that fixed channel-to-group assignments are fundamentally inadequate for ViTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends group quantization techniques (VS-Quant, Q-BERT) that partition channels into fixed groups calibrated offline, by making the group assignment dynamic per input instance at runtime. It departs from prior PTQ methods for ViTs (PTQ4ViT, APQ-ViT, RepQ-ViT) that rely on layer-wise quantizers, which fail to handle the significant inter-channel scale variations in ViT activations. The key conceptual shift is recognizing that, unlike CNNs with BatchNorm, ViT activation distributions shift drastically across input instances, rendering any fixed channel-to-group mapping suboptimal. The paper also extends grouping to softmax attention rows across tokens and introduces an ILP-based group size allocation under a BOP budget, neither of which appears in prior group quantization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IGQ-ViT provides a practical, retraining-free path to deploying ViTs at 4-bit on resource-constrained devices, closing much of the accuracy gap to full-precision with only ~3% additional BOPs. The finding that dynamic per-instance grouping is essential (rather than merely beneficial) for ViTs reframes how practitioners should think about activation quantization in transformer architectures lacking BatchNorm. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-uniform or mixed-precision quantizers, how it behaves under hardware constraints that limit runtime min/max computation, and whether the ILP-based group allocation generalizes to much deeper models where the search space becomes even more intractable.
