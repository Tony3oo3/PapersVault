## Problem

Category-level 3D pose estimation is critical for robotics and 3D generative models, but existing methods require large annotated datasets, CAD models, or RGB-D sensors at inference. This paper tackles the more challenging problem of learning a single-image category-level pose estimator entirely from casually captured object-centric videos, with no human labels, CAD models, or depth input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240313/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image (448×448) and outputs a 3D object pose (rotation and translation) along with dense 2D-to-3D correspondences mapping each image pixel to a vertex in a category-level template mesh. During training, it consumes ~50 object-centric videos per category (each ~100–200 frames with 360° viewpoint sweeps) from CO3D, from which coarse meshes are reconstructed via structure-from-motion and DINOv2 features (384-dim, 32×32) are projected onto mesh vertices to form neural meshes.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a multi-view alignment procedure brings all object instances into a canonical frame: coarse SFM meshes are annotated with DINOv2 surface features, and a novel 3D cyclical distance (nested nearest-neighbor in feature space) weights correspondences by reliability, enabling robust SE(3) estimation via RANSAC + Umeyama under a combined Chamfer-geometry and feature-appearance objective. Second, a frozen DINOv2 backbone augmented with three upsampling ResNet blocks is trained to predict, per pixel, a 128-dim feature vector matching the nearest visible vertex in a prototypical template mesh, using a cross-entropy loss over Von-Mises-Fisher distributions that also model background. At inference, pose is recovered by render-and-compare: a differentiable renderer projects the template mesh's vertex features under candidate poses, and the pose maximizing the joint vMF likelihood against the image feature map is selected via coarse template matching followed by iterative refinement.

## Results

On the CO3D alignment task across 20 categories, the method achieves 77.0% 30° accuracy and 61.6% 15° accuracy, outperforming UCD+ (69.8% / 54.6%) and ZSP (49.4% / 28.4%) by large margins. For in-the-wild 3D pose estimation on PASCAL3D+ (7 categories), the method reaches 69.2% mean 30° accuracy, surpassing the only other unsupervised baseline ZSP (46.0%) by 23.2 points, while supervised methods StarMap and VoGE achieve 82.49% and 90.93% respectively. On ObjectNet3D (20 categories), the method attains 52.4% 30° accuracy versus ZSP's 42.2%. Inference is also dramatically faster: 0.22 s per sample versus ZSP's 10.92 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TEASER++ (a point-cloud registration method), the proposed method wins by an overwhelming margin: 77.0% vs. 3.8% 30° accuracy on CO3D alignment, confirming that geometric-only registration is insufficient for noisy, topology-varying object reconstructions. Against ZSP (the closest prior unsupervised method), the paper wins on all three evaluation tasks: alignment (77.0% vs. 49.4%), PASCAL3D+ pose estimation (69.2% vs. 46.0%), and ObjectNet3D pose estimation (52.4% vs. 42.2%), with the largest gap on PASCAL3D+ where ZSP also requires depth annotations from CAD models. Against UCD+ (an extension of ZSP using many-to-many correspondences), the method improves alignment accuracy by 7.2 points (77.0% vs. 69.8%), attributed to the explicit 3D geometric distance and weighted correspondences. Against the two supervised baselines StarMap (82.49%) and VoGE (90.93%) on PASCAL3D+, the unsupervised method trails by 13–22 points, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; however, it is the first method to approach supervised performance without any pose labels, CAD models, or depth input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised video alignment line of work initiated by ZSP (Goodwin et al., ECCV 2022) and UCD+ (Goodwin et al., 2023), which used DINO features and 2D cyclical distances to align object-centric videos. The key departures are: (1) lifting the cyclical distance into 3D by combining it with Chamfer geometric distance on reconstructed meshes, (2) introducing weighted correspondences via a 3D cyclical validity criterion for robust SE(3) regression under noisy reconstructions, and (3) using the aligned videos to train a neural mesh with viewpoint-invariant vertex features via a vMF-based render-and-compare objective inherited from Kortylewski et al. (CVPR 2020). Unlike prior methods that require RGB-D input, CAD models, or pose annotations, this pipeline operates purely on RGB video with off-the-shelf SfM and DINOv2 features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully unsupervised pipeline that produces a single-image category-level 3D pose estimator from casually captured object-centric videos, removing the need for pose labels, CAD models, or depth sensors that all prior methods require. However, the gap to supervised methods remains substantial (69.2% vs. 90.93% mean 30° accuracy on PASCAL3D+), and performance degrades significantly for categories with high intra-class variability (e.g., chairs, suitcases). Open questions include whether relaxing the rigidity assumption with deformable shape models, enabling continuous learning from streaming data, and bridging the toy-to-real domain gap (e.g., toybus → real bus) can close this gap. The practical relevance is high for embodied agents and 3D generative model training where annotation is infeasible.
