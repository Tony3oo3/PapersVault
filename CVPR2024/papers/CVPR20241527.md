## Problem

Audiovisual segmentation (AVS) must segment sounding objects in video using raw, non-separated audio, but existing methods directly fuse mixed multi-source audio with visual features, failing when multiple sound events entangle at a single timestamp or when background noise corrupts acoustic cues. The core gap is the lack of a mechanism to disentangle multi-source audio semantics before audiovisual interaction, and the instability of frame-level audio representations when sound events change frequently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241527/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (frames resized to longest side 224) and its associated non-separated audio waveform (encoded by VGGish) as input, producing per-frame binary segmentation masks for each active sound source along with predicted class labels and bounding boxes. The audio is never pre-separated; instead, N=5 learnable semantic tokens are extracted from the mixed signal, and a shared codebook of K=128 codewords constrains each token to a single-source semantic subspace. The output is structured as N parallel prediction streams (one per decomposed source), matched to ground-truth objects via Hungarian assignment.

## Method

QDFormer applies Product Quantization as an information bottleneck to decompose multi-source audio into N disentangled single-source semantic tokens, assuming the multi-source semantic space is a Cartesian product of identical single-source subspaces. A global decomposition module fuses clip-level audio with video via cross-attention, decodes N tokens with a transformer decoder and learnable prototypes, and quantizes them against a shared codebook; the quantized tokens then generate dynamic channel-wise filters that modulate visual features (audiovisual semantic recombination). A local calibration module distills knowledge from the stable global codebook into frame-level audio by decoding each frame's audio with the same prototypes but freezing the codebook, so local tokens are pulled toward globally-learned semantics. Finally, a semantic-guided mask decoder uses dynamic convolution between pyramid visual features and per-frame, per-source filters to produce masks, trained end-to-end with a quantization commitment loss plus a Hungarian-matched segmentation loss (Dice+BCE, focal, GIoU+L1).

## Results

On AVS-Semantic with ResNet-50, QDFormer achieves 46.6 mIoU, surpassing the previous SOTA AOT (25.4 mIoU) by +21.2 mIoU and AVSegFormer (24.9 mIoU) by +21.7 mIoU. On AVS-Object-Multi with ResNet-50, QDFormer reaches 61.6 J&F, outperforming CATR (59.4) by +2.2 and AVSegFormer (56.2) by +5.4. With the Swin-Tiny backbone, QDFormer further improves to 64.0 J&F on AVS-Object-Multi and 53.4 mIoU on AVS-Semantic, demonstrating consistent gains across backbone architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QDFormer consistently outperforms all baselines on the multi-source AVS-Object-Multi setting, which is the most challenging scenario: +2.2 J&F over CATR (59.4→61.6) and +5.4 J&F over AVSegFormer (56.2→61.6) with ResNet-50, and +14.0 J&F over LGVT (50.0→64.0) with Swin-Tiny. On AVS-Semantic, the gains are most dramatic: QDFormer achieves 46.6 mIoU with ResNet-50 versus 25.4 for AOT (+21.2), 24.9 for AVSegFormer (+21.7), and 20.2 for the original AVS method (+26.4). On the single-source AVS-Object-Single setting, improvements are more modest (+0.6 J&F over AVSegFormer), which the authors attribute to single-source audio already having disentangled semantics. With the Swin-Tiny backbone, QDFormer also surpasses the larger PVT-v2-Base backbone used by AVS [82] on AVS-Semantic (53.4 vs. 29.8 mIoU), indicating the method's efficiency. The paper notes that CATR's higher F-score (65.6 vs. 63.5) on AVS-Object-Multi is attributable to its use of atrous spatial pyramid pooling, a component that can be integrated into QDFormer as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Product Quantization (PQ), originally developed for nearest-neighbor search in information retrieval, as an information bottleneck for decomposing multi-source audio semantics in the audiovisual domain. It departs from prior AVS/AVSS methods (AVS [82], AVSegFormer [21], CATR [34]) that directly fuse mixed audio features with visual content via cross-attention, instead first disentangling audio into single-source semantic tokens through a shared codebook before visual interaction. The global-to-local calibration mechanism draws on knowledge distillation principles, using clip-level quantized features to stabilize frame-level representations. The mask generation stage builds on dynamic convolution [13] and the Hungarian-algorithm-based assignment framework from Mask2Former-style detectors [14].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantization-based decomposition offers a general principle for handling multi-source entanglement in any multi-modal setting where a single input contains superimposed semantic factors, potentially extending beyond AVS to other audio-visual or multi-signal tasks. The substantial improvement (+21.2% mIoU on AVS-Semantic) and consistent noise-robustness gains suggest that discrete, bottlenecked representations are more resilient than continuous features in complex acoustic environments. Open questions include whether the independence assumption for sound events holds in highly overlapping real-world scenes, how the method scales to more than 5 sources or larger codebooks, and whether the PQ-based decomposition transfers to other modalities (e.g., multi-speaker text or multi-object point clouds).
