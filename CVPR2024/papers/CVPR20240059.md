## Problem

Existing arbitrary-scale super-resolution methods are trained on simulated degradations (e.g., bicubic downsampling) and produce blurry, artifact-ridden results on real-world images. Moreover, prior real-world SR datasets (RealSR, City100, SR-RAW, DRealSR) only capture fixed magnification scales, lacking the continuous scale variation needed to train arbitrary-scale models on genuine optical degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240059/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (48×48 patches during training) and a continuous scale factor s ∈ [1.0, 6.0] as input, and produces a high-resolution RGB image at that arbitrary scale. The COZ dataset provides aligned LR-HR pairs at fine-grained focal-length steps (e.g., 60 images per scene spanning 35–140 mm), enabling training at non-integer scales like ×2.3 or ×3.7. Testing covers both in-scale (×2–×4) and out-of-scale (×5–×6) magnifications.

## Method

The Local Mix Implicit Network (LMI) builds on the MLP-mixer architecture with a meta-learning component to handle complex real-world degradation. For each query point, it extracts a 4×4 grid of latent codes from the encoder (EDSR or RDN), treating each as an independent token with its relative coordinate. The Meta Spatial Mix Module uses a separate meta-learning network to generate spatial mix weights from the relative coordinates, which are then concatenated with the transposed tokens and passed through an MLP to perform coordinate-aware feature mixing across all 16 local points simultaneously. A Query Mix Module further embeds the original LR RGB values and coordinates into each token, and a final ensemble step aggregates all 16 token predictions weighted by geometric distance to the query point. This multi-point mixing strategy, contrasted with prior methods that decode from a single nearest coordinate, provides robustness against the complex, non-linear degradations present in real optical zoom.

## Results

On the COZ testing set, LMI achieves 28.86 dB PSNR at ×2 and 25.22 dB at ×4 (in-scale) with the EDSR-baseline encoder, and 28.86 dB at ×2 and 25.30 dB at ×4 with RDN, consistently outperforming all six baselines across every tested scale (×2 through ×6). LMI uses only 87.9K parameters, roughly 4–60× fewer than competing methods (346.9K–5.3M). On out-of-scale settings (×5, ×5.5, ×6), LMI also leads, e.g., 24.39 dB at ×5 and 23.29 dB at ×6 with EDSR-baseline, versus 24.31–24.35 dB and 23.19–23.26 dB for the best baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMI outperforms all six baselines (MetaSR, LIIF, LTE, LINF, SRNO, LIT) at every tested scale from ×2 to ×6 with both EDSR-baseline and RDN encoders, with margins of 0.06–0.14 dB in PSNR. The advantage is most pronounced at higher scales: at ×6 with RDN, LMI reaches 23.37 dB versus 23.25–23.31 dB for the best baselines (LIT, MetaSR). LMI also uses dramatically fewer parameters (87.9K) compared to LIT (5.3M, ~60× more), LINF (794.9K), and SRNO (705.2K), suggesting the meta-learning-guided multi-coordinate mixing is more parameter-efficient than the single-coordinate implicit function approaches used by LIIF, MetaSR, and LTE. The paper further shows that training on the real COZ dataset (vs. bicubic-downsampled simulated data) yields consistent PSNR/SSIM gains of ~0.5 dB across all methods, confirming the dataset's value independent of the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit neural representation framework for arbitrary-scale SR (MetaSR, LIIF, LTE, LINF, SRNO, LIT) by departing from the single-coordinate query paradigm: instead of predicting RGB from one nearest latent code and its relative offset, LMI simultaneously mixes a 4×4 neighborhood of local tokens using an MLP-mixer architecture guided by meta-learned spatial weights. The meta-learning component draws on MAML-style weight generation (Finn et al., 2017) to produce coordinate-dependent mixing weights, while the ensemble decoding step follows LIIF's local ensemble strategy. On the data side, the paper addresses a gap left by prior real-world SR datasets (RealSR, City100, SR-RAW, DRealSR), which only provide fixed-scale pairs, by introducing the first continuous-scale real-world benchmark via an automatic optical zooming system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The COZ dataset fills a critical gap: all prior arbitrary-scale SR methods were trained on bicubic-downsampled synthetic pairs, and this paper demonstrates that this domain gap produces visible artifacts and measurable PSNR/SSIM losses on real images. The finding that a compact 87.9K-parameter decoder outperforms 445K–5.3M-parameter alternatives suggests that multi-coordinate mixing is a more sample-efficient inductive bias than deeper single-coordinate decoders. Limitations include the small dataset (153 training scenes, single camera, 35–140 mm focal range), the absence of video or multi-device training, and the fact that PSNR gains over SOTA are modest (~0.1–0.2 dB), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse real-world collections or to video super-resolution.
