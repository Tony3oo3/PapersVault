## Problem

SimGCD, the current state-of-the-art for Generalized Category Discovery, uses class mean entropy to shift model focus toward novel classes, but this causes catastrophic forgetting of known classes—accuracy on known categories drops from ~74% to 64% on CUB during training. No existing GCD method explicitly preserves known-class knowledge while simultaneously learning novel categories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled set of 50% known-class images and an unlabeled set containing both known and novel class images, processed through a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone (only the last attention block is fine-tuned). The classifier produces a soft probability distribution over K total categories via cosine similarity between the [CLS] token feature and K learned prototypes, scaled by a temperature parameter. Evaluation uses clustering accuracy computed with the Hungarian assignment algorithm over the unlabeled set.

## Method

LegoGCD augments SimGCD with two plug-and-play loss terms that add no new parameters. Local Entropy Regularization (LER) selects high-confidence potential known samples from unlabeled data using a confidence threshold δ, then applies a Margin-aware Pattern (MAP) entropy regularizer to stabilize their predictions and prevent drift toward novel classes. Dual-views KL divergence constraint (DKL) enforces prediction consistency between two augmented views of the same image via KL divergence, ensuring that the threshold-based sample selection in LER is reliable and not corrupted by view-level noise. The final training objective is L = α·(L_rep + L_cls) + β·L_LER, where DKL is embedded within L_cls, and β controls the trade-off between novel-class learning and known-class retention.

## Results

LegoGCD achieves clustering accuracy (ACC) of 63.8% (All), 71.9% (Old), and 59.8% (New) on CUB, surpassing the SimGCD baseline by +1.9/+5.4/+0.2 points respectively. On Stanford Cars, it reaches 57.3% All / 75.7% Old / 48.4% New (+3.9/+5.1/+3.4 over SimGCD*), and on CIFAR-100 it achieves 81.8% All / 81.4% Old / 82.5% New (+2.8/+3.5/+1.0). On the challenging ImageNet-1k, LegoGCD attains 62.4% All / 79.5% Old / 53.8% New, marginally exceeding SimGCD* (+0.1/+0.4/+0.0). The largest gains are consistently on the 'Old' (known) categories, confirming the method's effectiveness against catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LegoGCD outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the direct predecessor SimGCD (and its re-run SimGCD*), the gains are most pronounced on known ('Old') classes: +5.4% on CUB, +5.1% on Stanford Cars, +3.5% on CIFAR-100, and +2.1% on ImageNet-100, while novel ('New') class accuracy is maintained or slightly improved (e.g., +3.4% on Stanford Cars, +3.5% on ImageNet-100). The earlier GCD method [36] is surpassed by a large margin (e.g., 63.8 vs. 51.3 All on CUB; 57.3 vs. 39.0 All on Stanford Cars). NCD-derived baselines (RS+ [9], UNO+ [7]) and ORCA [19] are significantly outperformed, with UNO+ achieving only 35.1% All on CUB versus LegoGCD's 63.8%. The k-means baseline [20] is the weakest, scoring below 35% on all fine-grained datasets. On the large-scale ImageNet-1k, LegoGCD edges out SimGCD* by 0.1% All and 0.4% Old while matching on New (53.8%), confirming that the forgetting mitigation generalizes to large-scale settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

LegoGCD is a direct extension of SimGCD (Wen et al., ICCV 2023), which itself replaced the clustering-based inference of the original GCD (Vaze et al., CVPR 2022) with a parametric classifier trained via pseudo-labeling and self-distillation. The paper borrows the confidence-threshold mechanism from FixMatch (Sohn et al., NeurIPS 2020) to identify high-confidence known samples, and introduces two new loss terms—Local Entropy Regularization (LER) and Dual-views KL divergence (DKL)—that are plugged into SimGCD's existing training loop without altering its architecture or adding parameters. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of semi-supervised consistency regularization and continual-learning-style knowledge preservation, but is framed entirely within the GCD setting rather than as a general anti-forgetting technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode of SimGCD—catastrophic forgetting of known classes during novel-class learning—and shows that a lightweight, parameter-free plug-in can recover a substantial portion of the lost accuracy (up to 7.74% on CUB known classes) while maintaining or slightly improving novel-class performance. This is practically useful for anyone building on SimGCD as a GCD baseline. However, the method is tightly coupled to SimGCD's specific pseudo-labeling and self-distillation pipeline, and it remains unclear whether the LER/DKL combination transfers to other GCD formulations (e.g., contrastive or clustering-based). An open question is whether the forgetting problem is fundamentally a capacity issue in the shared backbone or a loss-design issue, which would determine whether entropy-based regularization is the right class of fix.
