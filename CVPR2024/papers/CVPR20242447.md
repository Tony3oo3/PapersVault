## Problem

Existing filtering-based bokeh rendering methods simulate light propagation using only the circle-of-confusion area, ignoring occlusion by other scene geometries. This inaccuracy produces two persistent artifacts—color bleeding at depth boundaries and unnatural semi-transparent partial occlusion effects—that require post-processing or learning-based fixes. The paper aims to correct the underlying lens model so that occlusion is handled physically within the filtering framework itself, without training.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an all-in-focus RGBD image, extracts a salient object mask (via an off-the-shelf detector) to form a two-layer RGBA scene representation, and inpaints the occluded background with LaMa. User-defined lens parameters (focal plane distance, blur radius, lens shape) control the rendering. The forward pass outputs a single photorealistic bokeh image; in differentiable mode, the same module backpropagates through the rendering to produce a continuous depth map for depth-from-defocus. Computation scales as O(W×H×L×R²), matching classical filtering-based methods, and is implemented in CUDA as a custom PyTorch layer.

## Method

Dr.Bokeh extends the standard thin-lens scattering equation with two geometric occlusion terms computed per layer: an on-focal occlusion term O_l(y,x) that blocks radiance from pixels behind objects on the focal plane (preventing color bleeding), and a non-focal occlusion term V_l(x) that computes visibility as the mean alpha over the CoC region to blend front-to-back layers with energy conservation (producing natural partial occlusion). Both terms are softened into continuous, differentiable values rather than binary masks. The full rendering is made differentiable by replacing step functions and Dirac deltas with smooth approximations, and custom CUDA backward passes are derived for all terms. For depth-from-defocus, a hierarchy SSIM loss is introduced alongside L1 and gradient losses to supervise the patch-level scattering operation more effectively than per-pixel losses alone.

## Results

On the synthetic bokeh benchmark (100 scenes rendered via physically-based ray tracing), Dr.Bokeh achieves the best scores across all metrics: RMSE 0.0133, PSNR 38.73 dB, SSIM 0.9757, and ZNCC 0.9979, outperforming the closest competitor BokehMe (RMSE 0.0144, PSNR 37.77). On the Light Field Dataset for depth-from-defocus, Dr.Bokeh attains RMSE 0.0123, SSIM 0.9807, and PSNR 38.45, surpassing Aperture (RMSE 0.0133) and GaussPSF (RMSE 0.0132). A 41-participant user study confirms Dr.Bokeh is perceived as more realistic than SteReFo (81%), DeepLens (74%), BokehMe (68%), and MPIB (61%), with T-test significance at the 0.001 level for all comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr.Bokeh outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the gathering-based SteReFo [2], Dr.Bokeh reduces RMSE from 0.0179 to 0.0133 (−26%) and gains 2.81 dB in PSNR, primarily by eliminating color-bleeding and unnatural partial-occlusion artifacts. The learning-based DeepLens [61] performs worst (RMSE 0.0461, PSNR 27.35), likely because its synthetic training data lacks realistic foreground blur. BokehMe [40] is the closest competitor (RMSE 0.0144, PSNR 37.77), but Dr.Bokeh still leads by 0.96 dB in PSNR and 0.0049 in ZNCC. MPIB [41], designed specifically for partial occlusion, scores slightly worse than BokehMe quantitatively (RMSE 0.0152 vs. 0.0144), suggesting generalization issues on unseen data, though it renders more natural partial occlusion qualitatively. For depth-from-defocus, Dr.Bokeh beats both Aperture [53] (RMSE 0.0133 → 0.0123) and GaussPSF [8] (RMSE 0.0132 → 0.0123), with the occlusion-aware rendering and hierarchy-SSIM loss each contributing measurable gains in the ablation study.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dr.Bokeh extends the filtering-based bokeh rendering lineage (SteReFo's gathering framework, MPIB's layered RGBA representation) by identifying and correcting a fundamental physics violation: existing scattering/gathering methods ignore occlusion during light propagation, causing color bleeding and unnatural partial occlusion artifacts. Rather than patching these artifacts with post-processing or training a network on artifact-free data (as BokehMe and MPIB do), the paper introduces explicit on-focal and non-focal occlusion terms directly into the rendering equation, making the filtering-based simulation physically grounded. The differentiable implementation follows SteReFo's softening strategy but requires custom CUDA backward passes due to the added occlusion geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a non-learning, physics-based renderer outperforms learning-based methods (BokehMe, MPIB) on both quantitative metrics and a 40-question user study is notable in a field trending toward data-driven approaches, suggesting that correct physical modeling can substitute for learned artifact correction. The plug-and-play differentiable module also advances depth-from-defocus by showing that a more accurate blur model improves learned depth quality. However, the method remains dependent on the quality of upstream depth estimation and background inpainting, and the authors acknowledge that noisy inputs still produce artifacts—a gap they suggest could be addressed by leveraging differentiability to learn input robustness.
