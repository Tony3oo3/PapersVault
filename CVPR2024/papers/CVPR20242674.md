## Problem

Instance segmentation of 3D point clouds typically requires expensive per-point and bounding-box annotations, with a single ScanNet scene taking ~22 minutes to label. Unsupervised class-agnostic instance segmentation on point clouds remains largely unexplored, and the only concurrent effort (UnScene3D) still leaves substantial room for accuracy improvement. FreePoint tackles this gap by producing instance masks from raw point clouds without any manual labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242674/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw indoor point cloud where each point carries 6D attributes (XYZ coordinates + RGB color) and requires no annotations. It outputs per-point class-agnostic instance masks for foreground objects, with background surfaces (floors, walls, ceilings) identified via plane segmentation and labeled separately. The foreground is downsampled by farthest point sampling to half its original size before graph partitioning, then upsampled back via k-NN majority voting. The trained Mask3D backbone can additionally be fine-tuned for supervised semantic instance segmentation on S3DIS with as little as 10% labeled masks.

## Method

FreePoint generates pseudo labels by combining self-supervised deep features (from a CSC pre-trained backbone) with traditional coordinate and color features to compute point affinities, then solving a minimum-cost multicut problem on a k-NN graph using the GPU-accelerated RAMA algorithm. An 'id-as-feature' strategy runs RAMA T times, concatenates the resulting instance IDs as a new feature vector, and re-runs RAMA to reduce partitioning randomness. Two mask levels are produced by adjusting the affinity offset σ: over-segmented base masks that localize most objects but split them into parts, and under-segmented masks that merge nearby objects. A two-step training strategy first trains Mask3D on base masks to learn point-level feature representations, then fine-tunes on under-segmented masks for a few epochs to merge object parts into complete instances. A weakly-supervised loss combining Dice, BCE, center-mean, and bounding-box terms, together with an undersegmentation-ignore mechanism during bipartite matching, prevents the noisy pseudo labels from degrading training.

## Results

On ScanNet (train split), FreePoint achieves 21.4 AP and 38.7 AP50 for unsupervised class-agnostic instance segmentation, surpassing the closest concurrent work UnScene3D (3D-only features) by 5.5% AP and traditional clustering methods (DBSCAN, HDBSCAN, Nunes et al.) by over 18.2% AP. On the validation split, FreePoint scores 18.9 AP and 36.4 AP50. When used as an unsupervised pre-training pretext task and fine-tuned on S3DIS with 10% labeled masks, FreePoint reaches 42.9 AP, outperforming the best prior self-supervised method CSC by 6.0% AP and even exceeding fully supervised pre-training by 6.0% AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreePoint decisively outperforms all traditional clustering baselines: DBSCAN (3.2 AP), HDBSCAN (1.6 AP), and Nunes et al. (2.3 AP) on the ScanNet train split, with margins exceeding 18 AP points. Against the closest concurrent deep-learning method UnScene3D, FreePoint wins by 5.5% AP (21.4 vs. 15.9) in the fair 3D-only-feature comparison, and by 8.1% AP (21.4 vs. 13.3) when UnScene3D uses both 2D and 3D pre-trained features. In the pre-training setting on S3DIS with 10% masks, FreePoint (42.9 AP) surpasses CSC (37.1 AP) by 6.0%, PointContrast (36.1 AP) by 6.8%, and DepthContrast (36.8 AP) by 6.1%, and even exceeds the fully supervised pre-training baseline (36.9 AP) by 6.0%. With 20% masks, FreePoint (47.4 AP) still leads CSC (46.3 AP) by 1.1% and supervised pre-training (45.7 AP) by 1.7%. The paper also shows that top-down 2D segmentation methods (FreeMasks, Spectral) fail to transfer to cluttered 3D scenes, achieving only 2.3–2.9 AP for base masks versus RAMA's 5.4 AP, validating the choice of a bottom-up multicut approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreePoint sits at the intersection of graph-partitioning-based segmentation and Transformer-based instance segmentation. It repurposes the GPU-accelerated multicut solver RAMA for point cloud pseudo-label generation and builds its step-training pipeline around Mask3D, a Transformer-based instance segmenter. Conceptually, it departs from the concurrent UnScene3D by relying solely on 3D self-supervised features (rather than fusing 2D and 3D pre-trained backbones) and replacing multi-round self-training with a single two-step weakly-supervised training protocol. The hybrid feature representation (coordinates + colors + deep embeddings) draws on classical clustering heuristics like Supervoxel, while the id-as-feature ensemble strategy is a novel way to stabilize the inherently stochastic multicut output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first deep-learning-based approaches to unsupervised class-agnostic instance segmentation on point clouds, demonstrating that the task is tractable without any manual annotations and that the learned representations transfer well as a pretext task (outperforming supervised pre-training by 6% AP with only 10% labeled masks on S3DIS). A notable empirical finding is that top-down spectral and FreeMasks-style methods, successful in 2D unsupervised segmentation, fail on cluttered 3D indoor scenes, suggesting that bottom-up graph partitioning is better suited to multi-object 3D settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the reliance on a pre-trained self-supervised backbone limits applicability, and whether the two-step training strategy can be extended to iterative self-training for further gains.
