## Problem

Existing N:M sparsity methods treat all weight blocks equally, failing to differentiate the relative importance of weights among blocks and leaving critical weights underappreciated. Additionally, they apply the N:M sparse pattern to the entire network from the very beginning of training, causing severe information loss and hindering convergence. These two limitations make current N:M sparsity approaches suboptimal in both accuracy and training stability.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard CNN backbones (ResNet34, ResNet50, MobileNetV1) and detection/segmentation models (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN) consuming 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet or COCO. At inference, the trained sparse network produces task-specific outputs (class labels, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks) while the soft N:M masks are precomputed constants folded into the weight matrices, preserving the N:M sparse pattern and enabling hardware acceleration on NVIDIA Ampere Tensor Cores without any additional computational overhead.

## Method

MaxQ introduces a Multi-Axis Query mechanism that measures weight importance along both the filter (output channel) axis and the kernel (spatial) axis using a parameter-free sigmoid function G(V, p, τ), where the threshold σ is dynamically derived from the weight magnitude distribution itself rather than learned. The resulting soft masks (satisfying the N:M constraint) are combined with the hard binary mask via element-wise multiplication, amplifying gradients for more important weights during training while remaining foldable into weights at inference. A second key component is an incremental sparsity schedule that gradually increases the fraction of N:M sparse blocks δ_t from 0 to 1 over training epochs (following a cubic ramp), prioritizing blocks with larger ℓ₁-norm for early sparsification. This progressive approach allows the network to heal from pruning-induced damage, yielding more stable convergence and notably higher final accuracy compared to applying full N:M sparsity from epoch zero.

## Results

On ImageNet, MaxQ achieves 74.6% top-1 accuracy for ResNet50 with 1:16 N:M sparsity, surpassing the previous best (LBC, 71.8%) by 2.8%. At the 2:4 pattern, MaxQ reaches 77.6% top-1 for ResNet50, exceeding the dense baseline (77.3%) and outperforming SR-STE (77.0%) and LBC (77.2%). For object detection on COCO, MaxQ with Faster R-CNN at 2:4 sparsity achieves 38.7 mAP, beating LBC (38.5) and the dense baseline (37.4) by 0.2 and 1.3 mAP respectively. MaxQ also demonstrates quantization-friendliness, with only 0.5% top-1 drop (77.6%→77.1%) under INT8 PTQ for ResNet50 at 2:4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaxQ consistently outperforms all N:M sparsity baselines (ASP, SR-STE, LBC) across every sparse pattern and architecture tested. The gap is most dramatic at high sparsity: at 1:16, MaxQ (74.6%) beats LBC (71.8%) by 2.8% and SR-STE (71.5%) by 3.1% on ResNet50, while at 2:4 the margin narrows to 0.4–0.6% over SR-STE and LBC. Against unstructured sparsity methods (RigL, GMP, STR, OptG, DNW) at comparable FLOPs, MaxQ at 1:4 matches the dense baseline (77.3%) and exceeds STR (76.2%) and OptG (72.5% at 95% sparsity) by 1.1% and 4.2% respectively. On COCO, MaxQ with Faster R-CNN at 2:4 achieves 38.7 mAP, surpassing LBC (38.5) and the dense baseline (37.4); for Mask R-CNN at 1:4, MaxQ (38.3 Box mAP, 34.4 Mask mAP) approaches the dense baseline (38.2, 34.7) while using only 7.93M parameters vs. 25.6M. ASP, which uses a 200-epoch PPF pipeline, is outperformed by MaxQ in every setting despite MaxQ using only 120 epochs, highlighting the efficiency of the from-scratch soft-mask approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaxQ extends the N:M sparsity line initiated by ASP (Mishra et al.) and refined by SR-STE (Zhou et al., ICLR 2021) and LBC (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022), which established learning N:M sparse networks from scratch via straight-through estimators or combinatorial optimization. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) instead of treating each N:M block independently and applying a uniform binary mask, MaxQ queries weight importance along both the filter and kernel axes to generate soft, parameter-free masks that amplify more critical weights; and (2) rather than imposing the full N:M constraint on the entire network from epoch zero, it introduces an incremental schedule that gradually increases the fraction of blocks subject to N:M sparsity. This positions MaxQ as a refinement of the from-scratch N:M training paradigm rather than a new sparsity formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because N:M sparsity is natively supported by NVIDIA Ampere Tensor Cores, methods that improve accuracy at a given N:M pattern have direct deployment value on current hardware. The 2.8% top-1 gain at 1:16 on ResNet50 and the near-lossless 2:4 results (slightly exceeding dense baselines) make MaxQ a practical default for teams targeting Ampere-accelerated inference. The finding that the resulting reparameterized network remains quantization-friendly (only 0.5% drop under INT8 PTQ) is notable, as structured reparameterized networks typically suffer severe quantization degradation. Open questions include whether the multi-axis query generalizes to non-convolutional architectures (e.g., transformers) and whether the incremental schedule transfers to other semi-structured patterns like 1×N.
