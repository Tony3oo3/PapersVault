## Problem

Existing defenses against face editing abuse are either post-event (passive detection of forgeries) or pre-event but identity-unrelated (adversarial perturbations applied to specific images). No method exists that proactively prevents a face editing model from editing specific protected identities (POI) at the model level, without requiring any preprocessing of input images. This paper pioneers the pre-event, identity-related defense category by enabling developers to embed identity-aware rejection directly into their models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242580/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a face image and an editing condition (attribute label for attribute editing, or target identity for face swapping) as input. For non-POI identities, it produces a normally edited face image indistinguishable from the original model's output (FID increase ~1). For any image containing a protected POI identity—regardless of whether it appeared in training or underwent preprocessing—the model outputs a black image, effectively rejecting the edit. The identity perception operates on intermediate features of the face editing model rather than the raw input, and the ID Extractor is trained exclusively on non-POI data to learn general identity-discriminative features.

## Method

IDGuard inserts a single ID Normalization Layer (F' = αF + β) into the later stage of a face editing model G, making it the only architectural addition. An ID Extractor, trained with triplet loss on non-POI data, extracts identity features from G's intermediate representations; a transformer-based ID Encoder then maps a variable-length sequence of POI identity features into the constant-length parameters α and β. Training combines three losses: triplet loss for the ID Extractor, standard adversarial loss to preserve editing quality on non-POI, and an L2-to-black-image loss to force rejection on POI. A key design choice is that new POI identities can be added at inference time by simply forward-passing their images through the trained ID Extractor and ID Encoder to regenerate α and β, requiring no backpropagation or retraining. The method is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated across six different face editing models (StarGAN, AttGAN, HiSD, AGGAN, SimSwap, FaceShifter).

## Results

IDGuard achieves a perfect SR_mask of 1.0000 across all four face editing models (StarGAN, AGGAN, AttGAN, HiSD) on unseen POI images, indicating 100% protection accuracy. In terms of output disruption quality (log₁₀ FID), IDGuard scores 2.50–2.54, substantially exceeding the best adversarial-attack baseline CMUA (1.71–2.30). On fidelity, incorporating IDGuard increases FID by only ~1 point on average (e.g., StarGAN: 5.71→7.24; SimSwap: 12.66→13.07), which is visually negligible for regular non-POI editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDGuard dominates all seven adversarial-attack baselines on both SR_mask and log₁₀ FID across all four models. The strongest baseline, CMUA [25], achieves near-perfect SR_mask on StarGAN and AGGAN (1.0000, 0.9988) but drops to 0.8708 on AttGAN and 0.9987 on HiSD, while IDGuard maintains a perfect 1.0000 everywhere. On log₁₀ FID, CMUA trails IDGuard by 0.23–0.73 points (e.g., 1.8133 vs. 2.5025 on AttGAN), indicating IDGuard produces more thoroughly disrupted outputs. Single-step attacks (BIM, MIM, PGD) and multi-step transferable attacks (DI²-FGSM, M-DI²-FGSM, AutoPGD) are particularly weak on AttGAN and HiSD, with SR_mask values often below 0.05, whereas IDGuard is unaffected by model architecture. Crucially, all baselines require per-image adversarial preprocessing, meaning they fail entirely on unprocessed images, while IDGuard protects any image containing the POI identity without any input modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new quadrant in the face-editing defense landscape: identity-centric, pre-event, developer-side protection. Prior identity-related work (Agarwal, Boháček, Cozzolino) operates post-event by detecting anomalies in behavioral references, while prior proactive defenses (Dong, Sun, Huang/CMUA) are identity-unrelated and require adversarial preprocessing of every protected image. IDGuard repurposes contrastive triplet loss for identity feature extraction, a transformer encoder for variable-length identity sequences, and a parameterized normalization layer (analogous to LayerNorm but identity-conditioned) to create a hard gate inside the editing model itself, eliminating both the post-event detection delay and the per-image preprocessing requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The developer-side framing shifts the burden of POI protection from users or downstream detectors to the model provider, aligning with growing regulatory expectations for responsible AIGC release. The ability to add new POI identities at inference time via a single forward pass (no retraining) is practically significant for organizations that must update protection lists frequently. However, the evaluation is confined to GAN-based editing models (StarGAN, AttGAN, HiSD, AGGAN, SimSwap, FaceShifter); the authors themselves flag diffusion and text-driven models as future work, and the binary 'black image' rejection, while effective, offers no graceful degradation or user feedback. Open questions include whether the identity-conditioned normalization approach transfers to diffusion architectures and how the method behaves under more sophisticated model-extraction or fine-tuning attacks beyond the simple removal/noise perturbations tested.
