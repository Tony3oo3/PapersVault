## Problem

Existing spectro-polarimetric datasets suffer from limited scene and illumination diversity, contain primarily linear polarization information, and offer too few images to support data-driven research. This paper addresses that gap by introducing two comprehensive real-world datasets with full-Stokes polarization (both linear and circular) across multiple spectral channels, enabling systematic statistical analysis of natural spectro-polarimetric images that was previously infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240464/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The acquisition pipeline takes raw intensity images captured under multiple polarization-filter configurations (four for the hyperspectral camera via rotating QWP; four or eight for the trichromatic camera via fixed micro-filters) and reconstructs per-pixel Stokes vectors via least-squares inversion of the Mueller-matrix image formation model. The trichromatic dataset yields 2,022 images at 1900×2100×4×3 resolution, while the hyperspectral dataset yields 311 images at 512×612×4×21 resolution (450–650 nm in 10 nm steps). Each image is annotated with environment, illumination type, timestamp, and scene-type labels; the analysis further produces PCA/INR compressed representations (reducing a 100 MB hyperspectral Stokes image to ~2.22 MB) and polarization statistics including DoP, AoLP, DoLP, DoCP, and CoP distributions.

## Method

The paper's technical core combines dataset construction with two efficient representation strategies for the high-dimensional spectro-polarimetric data. For PCA, they apply it to non-overlapping hyperspectral Stokes patches, revealing that 2.22 MB of coefficients (out of 100 MB) suffices for reconstruction with MSE of 2.69×10⁻⁵, and that spectral features dominate over spatial features in the polarization components s₁, s₂, s₃. For the INR, they adapt the NeSpoF architecture into an MLP F_γ(p_x, p_y, c) that maps pixel position and spectral channel index to a Stokes vector, trained by minimizing reconstruction loss; with 8 layers (BPP=60) it achieves a converged error of 1.90×10⁻⁵. Both representations also serve as denoisers, outperforming the pretrained KBNet intensity denoiser on single-shot captures. The empirical analysis further examines Stokes-vector distributions, gradient statistics (showing Hyper-Laplacian-like priors), polarized/unpolarized intensity separation, spectral dependency of shape-from-polarization normals, and environment-dependent polarization statistics on the Poincaré sphere.

## Results

The trichromatic Stokes dataset contains 2,022 images at 1900×2100×4×3 resolution, while the hyperspectral Stokes dataset contains 311 images at 512×612×4×21 resolution, with 99% of reconstructed Stokes vectors satisfying the physical DoP constraint (0 ≤ ρ ≤ 1). For efficient representation, PCA with 2.22 MB of coefficients compresses a 100 MB hyperspectral Stokes image to a reconstruction error of 2.69×10⁻⁵, while an 8-layer INR (BPP=60) achieves a lower error of 1.90×10⁻⁵ using the same 2.22 MB parameter budget. For denoising, averaging 4/25 shots is needed to exceed 35 dB PSNR for the trichromatic/hyperspectral cameras respectively, and applying KBNet intensity denoising to a single shot yields 34.5 dB PSNR, with significant PSNR gains for s₁, s₂, and s₃ components.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary method comparison is against KBNet [66], a learned intensity denoiser, for reducing noise in spectro-polarimetric images. The paper demonstrates that both PCA and INR representations outperform KBNet single-shot denoising for reconstructing clean Stokes images (Figure 4b), and that applying KBNet to individual polarization configurations before Stokes reconstruction significantly improves PSNR for the polarized components s₁, s₂, and s₃ (Figure 5c). In terms of dataset coverage, the proposed datasets are the only existing spectro-polarimetric datasets that simultaneously provide full-Stokes (linear + circular) polarization, multiple spectral bands, and diverse real-world indoor/outdoor scenes; prior full-Stokes datasets [19, 34] are limited to 67 flat lab objects or 4 multiview scenes, while large-scale datasets [38, 40, 46, 48] capture only linear polarization. The shape-from-polarization analysis using Lei et al. [41] reveals that estimated normals vary significantly across spectral channels, with x and y components showing larger standard deviations than z, highlighting an overlooked spectral dependency in SfP methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of spectro-polarimetric dataset construction by addressing four simultaneous gaps in prior work: limited scene diversity, linear-only polarization, restricted illumination, and small image counts. It builds directly on the NeSpoF architecture [34] for its implicit neural representation, repurposing a volumetric field model to represent a single hyperspectral Stokes image. The empirical analysis draws on established polarization theory (Stokes vectors, Mueller matrices, Poincaré sphere) and applies standard tools (PCA, gradient priors, KBNet denoising) to a data regime—real-world full-Stokes multi-spectral scenes—that had not been systematically characterized before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dataset to combine full-Stokes (linear + circular) polarization with multiple spectral channels across diverse real-world indoor/outdoor scenes, providing a foundation for data-driven spectro-polarimetric research that prior lab-only or linear-only datasets could not support. The finding that shape-from-polarization normals exhibit significant spectral variation—particularly in the x,y components governed by AoLP—reveals an overlooked practical limitation that could affect any SfP pipeline operating on multi-channel data. Open questions include whether the observed compressibility and polarization statistics hold at higher resolutions and broader spectral ranges, and how learning-based methods can exploit the environment-dependent priors (e.g., depolarization under cloudy skies) for improved reconstruction.
