## Problem

Existing photorealistic relightable hand models require extensive identity-specific captures across views, poses, and illuminations, and rely on expensive teacher-student distillation to generalize from discrete point lights to continuous environment maps. Cross-identity generalization remains an open problem: no prior method can produce a single universal model that relights unseen hands under arbitrary natural illuminations in real time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242228/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a per-identity mean texture (1024×1024×3 unwrapped RGB), a 60-joint hand pose in Euler angles, a coarse UHM mesh (15,930 vertices / 32,340 faces), and an illumination signal (either L=5 grouped point lights or a continuous environment map). It outputs a photorealistic relit hand image, a refined displacement map (1024×1024×3, ±3 mm range) and corresponding normal map, and a roughness map (1024×1024). The neural renderer parameterizes the final appearance as a texel-aligned gain map and bias map over the mean texture (C = g ⊙ T + b·σ_T), rather than predicting the full texture directly, which keeps the output linear in the lighting input.

## Method

The core contribution is a spatially varying linear lighting model: by removing all non-linear activations and biases from the convolutional encoder-decoder that processes physically based shading features (diffuse and specular components from a Disney BRDF render), the network provably preserves linearity of light transport, enabling single-stage training on grouped point lights that generalizes to arbitrary environment maps without distillation. A parallel physical branch (a 2D U-Net predicting displacement and roughness in UV space, followed by first-bounce Disney BRDF rendering) refines geometry and supplies the spatially varying shading features that encode pose-dependent visibility and shadows. Pose- and identity-dependent non-linearity is injected through a separate non-linear branch whose features are fused with the linear branch via element-wise multiplication in the decoder, so the output remains linear in illumination but non-linear in identity and pose. Training is joint and end-to-end, combining L1 + EfficientNet perceptual reconstruction loss, a multi-scale lighting-aware GAN loss (hinge loss with a discriminator conditioned on Phong diffuse/specular cues), and an L1 regularization on all intermediate features of the linear branch to suppress flickering.

## Results

In per-identity evaluation, URHand (Full model) achieves 27.77 / 26.36 / 27.75 dB PSNR across three subjects, outperforming RelightableHands [14] by 1.8–2.2 dB PSNR and reducing LPIPS from ~0.13–0.14 to ~0.12–0.13. In the more challenging cross-identity generalization setting, URHand reaches 26.94 / 26.24 / 27.58 dB PSNR, surpassing Handy+Phong by over 10 dB PSNR (e.g., 26.94 vs. 16.65 on Subject 1) and reducing LPIPS from ~0.30 to ~0.13. The physical-only branch (23.44–24.90 dB) confirms that the neural branch contributes a substantial ~3–4 dB gain over pure physically-based rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RelightableHands [14] (per-identity, †), URHand wins on all three subjects across every metric: +1.8 dB PSNR on Subject 1 (27.77 vs. 25.97), +0.4 dB on Subject 2 (26.36 vs. 25.92), and +0.6 dB on Subject 3 (27.75 vs. 27.16), with LPIPS consistently lower (0.1204 vs. 0.1425 on Subject 1). Against Handy [37] (cross-identity, *), URHand dominates by a wide margin—approximately 10 dB PSNR higher (26.94 vs. 16.65 on Subject 1) and LPIPS roughly halved (0.1335 vs. 0.3026)—because Handy's data-driven latent texture space fails to reproduce the target hand's base albedo, and its simple Phong/GGX relighting produces visible artifacts. The paper also ablates against the MLP-based linear model of Yang et al. [57] (Table 2), showing that the spatially varying design outperforms the holistic environment-map representation by 3.8 dB PSNR on fully-lit frames (20.61 vs. 11.56), confirming that per-texel visibility is critical for articulated hands. URHand's physical-only branch (23.44–24.90 dB) serves as an internal baseline, demonstrating that the neural branch adds ~3–4 dB over pure Disney-BRDF rendering, validating the hybrid neural-physical design.

## Relation to Prior Work

URHand extends the neural relighting line of work (RelightableHands, DRAMA) from per-identity to cross-identity modeling, while incorporating the geometry backbone from UHM for hand representation. The key architectural departure is the spatially varying linear lighting model, which removes non-linear activations and bias from the neural renderer to explicitly preserve the linearity of light transport, eliminating the expensive teacher-student distillation that prior neural relighting methods require for environment-map generalization. The hybrid neural-physical framework—jointly learning a Disney BRDF branch and a neural relighting branch—repurposes inverse rendering not as the final appearance model but as a feature provider that constrains the neural branch against hallucination, a role distinct from prior physically-based hand models (NIMBLE, DART) that rely solely on parametric BRDFs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single model can produce photorealistic, relightable hand renderings for arbitrary identities, poses, and illuminations in real time, and that a casual phone scan suffices for personalization without fine-tuning—removing the need for expensive light-stage capture in production pipelines. The spatially varying linear architecture is a clean, generalizable principle that could transfer to other articulated objects where holistic illumination representations fail due to articulation-induced shadow variation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hands with accessories (rings, watches), whether the 93-identity training set is sufficient for truly diverse populations, and how the method handles extreme out-of-distribution appearances beyond the demonstrated tattoo case.
