## Problem

Existing Learning-to-Optimize (L2O) models lack theoretical guarantees for their performance in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where the optimization problem differs from the training domain. It is unclear how and why convergence degrades under OOD, and no principled criterion exists for designing L2O models that remain robust when deployed on unseen problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242295/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes only gradient vectors ∇f(x), implicit subgradient vectors g_k, and a fixed-length (T=2) historical sequence of combined gradient-subgradient values as its input, deliberately excluding the optimization variable x itself. This gradient-only design reduces the input feature dimensionality by one-third compared to prior Math-L2O formulations, which directly shrinks the virtual feature magnitude that governs OOD convergence deterioration. At each iteration the model outputs a single update vector d(z) that is subtracted from the current variable to produce the next iterate, ultimately yielding a solution to the convex composite objective min F(x) = f(x) + r(x).

## Method

The paper introduces a virtual-feature framework that aligns an OOD trajectory with a corresponding InD trajectory by defining s_k = x'_k − x_k and s'_k = z'_k − z_k, then proves (via Theorems 1–3 and Corollaries 1–3) that per-iteration and multi-iteration convergence loss in OOD is upper-bounded by the norm of the virtual feature s'. This theoretical result motivates GO-Math-L2O, which eliminates all variable-related input features and models history exclusively through gradient and subgradient values. The update operator d_k is parameterized by matrices R_k, Q_k, B_k and bias vectors b_{1,k}, b_{2,k} following the necessary convergence conditions (asymptotic fixed point and global convergence) derived in Theorem 3; R_k is generated by a one-layer linear network with Sigmoid activation to ensure boundedness, while a two-layer LSTM cell produces the historical state v_k. An implicit subgradient g_k is recovered at each step via the gradient-map method applied to the invertible L2O operator, avoiding arbitrary subgradient selection. The model is trained with the Adam optimizer on 32,000 synthetic LASSO and logistic regression problems, and achieves up to 10× convergence speedup over the SOTA L2O-PA baseline in OOD settings.

## Results

On LASSO Regression (InD), GO-Math-L2O achieves the fastest convergence and best final objective value among all 7 baselines. In the real-world OOD scenario (Figure 2), GO-Math-L2O converges at approximately 400 iterations while all other baselines require 1,000 iterations, yielding a ~2.5× speedup. In the synthetic OOD Trigger 2 scenario with t=±10, GO-Math-L2O converges in roughly 20 steps whereas L2O-PA fails to converge entirely. The paper reports up to 10× convergence speedup over SOTA L2O models in OOD scenarios across multiple test configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GO-Math-L2O outperforms the primary SOTA competitor L2O-PA [14] in every reported scenario: InD (Figure 1), real-world OOD (Figure 2, 400 vs. 1000 iterations), OOD Trigger 1 with shifted initial points (Figure 3, all 16 sub-cases), and OOD Trigger 2 with function translation (Figure 4, where L2O-PA fails to converge at t=±10 while GO-Math-L2O converges in ~20 steps). Against hand-crafted algorithms (FISTA, ISTA, Adam, AdamHD), GO-Math-L2O achieves faster convergence in both InD and OOD settings, with the gap widening substantially under OOD conditions. Among other learned baselines (L2O-DM, L2O-RNNprop, Ada-LISTA), GO-Math-L2O consistently reaches lower objective values at every iteration count, with the advantage most pronounced in OOD scenarios where these baselines degrade sharply. The paper does not report a single tabular metric (e.g., final objective gap or AUC); instead, all comparisons are made via convergence curves of normed objective value versus iteration count, making direct numerical deltas harder to extract but the qualitative dominance of GO-Math-L2O unambiguous across all 7 baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Math-L2O framework of Liu et al. (ICML 2023), which introduced a mathematics-driven L2O model for convex optimization but lacked theoretical analysis of OOD robustness. The authors introduce a 'virtual feature' methodology that algebraically connects OOD trajectories to InD trajectories via a virtual Jacobian, enabling the first rigorous convergence bounds for L2O under distribution shift. Departing from Math-L2O's use of both variable and gradient features, the proposed GO-Math-L2O restricts inputs to gradients and subgradients only, motivated by the theoretical finding that smaller input feature magnitude reduces OOD convergence degradation. The gradient-based history modeling (replacing variable-based history) and implicit subgradient recovery via the gradient map method are novel architectural choices within the algorithm-unrolling L2O family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how OOD shifts degrade L2O convergence, establishing that the magnitude of the model's input features is the fundamental limiting factor—a clean design principle for future L2O architectures. However, the analysis is confined to convex optimization (smooth and non-smooth), and the OOD scenarios tested are relatively mild (initial-point shifts and objective translations), leaving open how the framework extends to non-convex problems, adversarial perturbations, or structurally different objectives. The 10× speedup claim, while impressive, is demonstrated on two regression tasks with specific perturbation settings, so broader generalizability remains to be established.
