## Problem

Standard Hungarian matching for Transformer-based VRD models trains 'unspecialized' queries that must detect every relation, leaving model capacity underutilized, while simultaneously suppressing near-correct predictions by assigning them a 'no relation' label. Approximately 45% of high-quality predictions on Visual Genome receive this negative signal, and the long-tailed predicate distribution further aggravates the ambiguity of training a single query on all relations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images and produces a fixed set of N_q scene graph triplets, each containing subject and object bounding boxes with class labels and a predicate label. Crucially, SpeaQ modifies only the training-time label assignment procedure; the inference pipeline, model architecture, and number of output queries remain identical to the baseline, incurring zero additional inference cost or parameters.

## Method

SpeaQ introduces two complementary training-time modifications to the standard Hungarian matching. First, Groupwise Query Specialization partitions predicate classes into N_g disjoint frequency-based groups and proportionally assigns queries to matching query groups, then enforces specialization by adding an infinite grouping cost to the matching cost whenever a GT and prediction belong to different groups, so each query only receives supervision for its designated predicate subset. Second, Quality-Aware Multi-Assignment replaces the one-to-one GT-to-prediction constraint by duplicating each GT d_i times in the augmented GT set, where d_i is adaptively determined from a triplet-level quality vector combining subject/object IoU (via a max relation function) and the predicted predicate probability, followed by a top-k summation. The final assignment minimizes the sum of matching and grouping costs over the augmented GT set, and the resulting permutation drives standard classification and box regression losses for subject, predicate, and object heads.

## Results

On Visual Genome (VG150), ISG*+SpeaQ achieves R@100 of 35.5 (+5.4) and mR@100 of 17.6 (+1.0) over the ISG baseline, and without loss re-weighting reaches R@100 of 36.0 and mR@100 of 14.1 — the first method to simultaneously achieve the best on both R@100 and mR@100. On HICO-DET, GEN*+SpeaQ improves full mAP to 34.00 (+0.88) and rare mAP to 30.20 (+3.08). All gains are achieved with zero additional inference cost, model parameters, or post-processing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeaQ is applied as a plug-in label assignment strategy on top of existing Transformer-based VRD models (ISG, HOTR, GEN) and consistently outperforms their baselines across all metrics. On Visual Genome, ISG*+SpeaQ surpasses the strongest prior Transformer-based baselines: it beats SGTR [22] by +9.4 on R@100 and +2.5 on mR@100, and beats ISG* [14] by +5.4 on R@100 and +1.0 on mR@100 (with loss re-weighting) or +3.9/+5.7 (without). On HICO-DET, GEN*+SpeaQ outperforms CDN [42] by +2.56 full mAP and +2.81 rare mAP, and UPT [44] by +2.34 full mAP and +4.26 rare mAP. The method also outperforms frequency-based grouping strategies from BGNN [21] and SHA [7] in the predicate grouping ablation (Table 8), achieving 36.0 R@100 vs. 32.7 and 34.1 respectively. Notably, SpeaQ is the first method to simultaneously achieve the best R@100 and mR@100 on Visual Genome, a feat no prior baseline (including VCTree-BPLSA [8], DT2-ACBS [5], or RelDN [45]) has accomplished.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard one-to-one Hungarian matching label assignment inherited from DETR for Transformer-based VRD models (ISG, HOTR, GEN), identifying it as the root cause of 'unspecialized' queries and suppressed near-correct predictions. It draws conceptual inspiration from object detection works that modified assignment strategies (Group DETR, Dynamic Sparse R-CNN, DETRs with Hybrid Matching) but extends the idea to the triplet-level VRD setting with a novel groupwise specialization mechanism. Unlike prior long-tail mitigation strategies (data resampling, loss re-weighting, class-specific classifiers) that trade off common vs. rare class performance, SpeaQ enhances the model's effective capacity by training each query as a specialist, improving performance across all frequency groups simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~45% of high-quality predictions are suppressed under standard Hungarian matching reveals a significant and previously unquantified inefficiency in training Transformer-based VRD models, and the proposed fix requires zero additional inference cost or model parameters. Achieving the best performance on both R@100 and mR@100 simultaneously on Visual Genome—a feat no prior method accomplished—demonstrates that the common-vs-rare trade-off can be partially overcome through better training rather than architectural changes. Open questions include whether the frequency-based grouping strategy can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach generalizes to other set-prediction tasks beyond VRD, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales or with more decoder layers.
