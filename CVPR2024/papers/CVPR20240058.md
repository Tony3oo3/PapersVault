## Problem

Existing video summarization datasets are extremely small (TVSum: 50 videos, SumMe: 25), causing state-of-the-art methods to overfit to narrow domains and generalize poorly. Additionally, prevailing formulations frame summarization as per-frame binary classification or importance scoring, which suffers from severe class imbalance and ignores sequential dependencies between summary moments, leading to repetitive selections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240058/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a long video sampled at 1 fps (8–33 minutes) and an optional transcribed speech transcript (sentences with timestamps) as input; when text is unavailable, a special MASK token substitutes for the text stream. The output is a condensed summary video (m ≪ n frames) produced by autoregressively decoding continuous CLIP-space feature vectors for summary moments, which are then matched back to original video frames via nearest-neighbor retrieval and temporally aggregated into the final summary clip sequence.

## Method

The architecture is a Transformer encoder-decoder with four components: a 6-layer video encoder over CLIP-ViT-L/14 frame embeddings, a 3-layer text encoder over SRoBERTa sentence embeddings, a cross-modal attention layer (visual features as queries, text features as keys/values), and a 6-layer autoregressive summary decoder. The key departure from prior work is decoding continuous feature representations of summary moments sequentially (next-summary-moment prediction) rather than predicting discrete per-frame importance scores in parallel, which mitigates class imbalance and captures inter-moment context. Training uses an L2 reconstruction loss between predicted and pseudo-ground-truth CLIP features, with random text masking (0–100%) so the model remains functional on non-narrated videos. The model is pretrained on the LfVS-P dataset (250K video-summary pairs) generated by prompting GPT-3.5/4 to perform extractive summarization over Whisper transcripts of HowTo100M videos, with CLIP-based nearest-neighbor search mapping selected sentences back to video segments.

## Results

On the newly introduced LfVS-T benchmark (1,200 long videos, 8–33 min), the proposed autoregressive model achieves F1=68.11, Kendall's τ=0.158, and Spearman's ρ=0.277, outperforming the strongest baseline (TL:DW?) by 1.86 F1 points, 0.020 τ, and 0.044 ρ. On the established SumMe and TVSum benchmarks, the model is competitive on F1 (56.94 and 66.04) and leads all prior methods on τ and ρ, confirming the benefit of autoregressive decoding for capturing sequential dependencies. Cross-dataset fine-tuning from LfVS-P pretraining yields F1=60.42 on SumMe and F1=72.38 on TVSum, surpassing from-scratch training by 6.1% and 9.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four video summarization baselines on every metric of the LfVS-T benchmark. Against the closest competitor A2Summ [8] (F1=66.04, τ=0.143, ρ=0.246), the gain is +2.07 F1, +0.015 τ, and +0.031 ρ; against TL:DW? [26] (F1=66.25, τ=0.138, ρ=0.233), the margin is +1.86 F1, +0.020 τ, and +0.044 ρ. The video-only method iPTNet [10] (F1=65.80) and the language-guided CLIP-It [25] (F1=62.87) trail by 2.31 and 5.24 F1 points respectively, underscoring the advantage of multi-modal input and autoregressive decoding. On SumMe and TVSum (Table 3), the method leads all baselines on τ and ρ but is marginally below A2Summ on F1 (56.94 vs 57.09 on SumMe; 66.04 vs 66.10 on TVSum), while the human reference [28] remains substantially ahead on τ and ρ (e.g., τ=0.205 vs 0.130 on SumMe), indicating a remaining gap to human-level temporal ordering. Cross-dataset fine-tuning from LfVS-P pretraining closes much of this gap, reaching F1=72.38 on TVSum (vs 66.10 for A2Summ trained from scratch).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the extractive text summarization capability of LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4) as an oracle to generate pseudo-ground truth video summaries at scale, bridging the gap between the rapid progress in LLM-based text summarization and the stagnation in video summarization due to dataset scarcity. Methodologically, it departs from the dominant binary-classification or frame-importance-prediction formulation used by prior works (CLIP-It, TL:DW?, iPTNet, A2Summ) by proposing an autoregressive decoder that sequentially generates continuous feature representations of summary moments, explicitly modeling temporal dependencies between selected segments. The multi-modal encoder-decoder architecture (CLIP visual tokens + SRoBERTa sentence embeddings + cross-modal attention) extends the multi-modal video summarization line initiated by TL:DW? and A2Summ, but with a fundamentally different decoding paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LfVS-P pretraining dataset (250K pairs, 20× larger than TVSum) and the LfVS-T benchmark (1,200 long-form videos with human-annotated summaries) address the core bottleneck in video summarization research: the absence of large-scale, diverse training data and a realistic long-form evaluation setting. The strong cross-dataset generalization (competitive zero-shot and SOTA fine-tuned results on SumMe/TVSum from LfVS-P pretraining) suggests that LLM-generated pseudo-ground truth is sufficiently faithful to transfer across domains, opening the door to even larger-scale pretraining. Open questions include whether the pseudo-ground truth quality plateaus or degrades for videos with sparse or noisy narration, whether the autoregressive decoding advantage persists at even longer video durations, and how the approach handles non-extractive or abstractive summary preferences that differ from LLM extractive selections.
