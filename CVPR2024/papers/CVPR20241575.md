## Problem

Existing 3D GANs for human generation face a fundamental tradeoff: volumetric representations are expressive but too slow for the tens of millions of rendering passes GAN training requires, while mesh-based rasterization is fast but cannot model features like hair and loose clothing that deviate from the template. 2D convolutional upsamplers partially compensate for low-resolution rendering but introduce multi-view-inconsistent aliasing artifacts. 3D Gaussians offer both speed and expressivity, but combining their irregular, variable-count primitives with grid-based CNN generators remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a StyleGAN2 latent code and produces a 3D texture stack of shape N×H×W×11 (8 shells at 512×512, encoding color, opacity, scaling, and rotation per shell). At inference, SMPL pose θ and shape β parameters deform the shell scaffold to articulate the generated human. The output is a set of ~100k 3D Gaussians rendered natively at 512×512 via Gaussian splatting, producing an RGB image and alpha mask without any post-rendering upsampler.

## Method

GSMs anchor 3D Gaussians at fixed quasi-uniform locations on N shell meshes (inflated/deflated SMPL layers via Laplacian smoothing), with each Gaussian's position encoded as barycentric coordinates on its host triangle. A StyleGAN2-based CNN generates per-shell texture maps that store all Gaussian parameters except position (color, opacity, scale, rotation), so geometry is learned implicitly through opacity and covariance rather than explicit vertex displacement. Rendering uses differentiable EWA Gaussian splatting with α-blending, and articulation is achieved by deforming the shells through the SMPL model with linear blend skinning for rotation. Training is adversarial with a main mask discriminator (RGB + alpha input) plus dedicated face and feet/hands discriminators, plus a scaling regularization loss that penalizes deviation from a reference nearest-neighbor distance to prevent divergence.

## Results

On DeepFashion, GSM achieves FID 15.78 and PCK 99.48; on SHHQ, FID 13.30 and PCK 99.27. Inference speed is 28 ms/image at 512² resolution on an A6000 GPU, tying with StylePeople as the fastest method and substantially outpacing volume-rendering baselines (e.g., EVA3D at 200 ms, AG3D at 105 ms). GSM ranks top on PCK (pose consistency) across both datasets and is competitive on FID, though AG3D (10.93) and EVA3D (11.99) report lower FID on DeepFashion and SHHQ respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSM achieves the best PCK on both datasets (99.48 on DeepFashion, 99.27 on SHHQ), outperforming StylePeople (98.31/98.58) and GNARF (97.83/98.96), confirming superior pose-deformation consistency. On FID, AG3D (10.93) and EVA3D (11.99) report lower scores on DeepFashion and SHHQ respectively, but the paper notes AG3D uses normal-map supervision and GetAvatar is trained on multi-view data, making the comparison less direct. GSM ties StylePeople at 28 ms inference and is 3–7× faster than volume-rendering methods (EVA3D 200 ms, AG3D 105 ms, ENARF 104 ms). Qualitatively, GSM avoids the multi-view aliasing/flickering artifacts that plague baselines relying on 2D convolutional upsamplers, as demonstrated in Figure 6 where AG3D's garment pattern shifts between views while GSM's texture-map-based rendering remains consistent. Overall, GSM trades a small FID gap for substantially better articulation fidelity, rendering speed, and view consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: 3D GANs for human generation (GNARF, EVA3D, StylePeople, AG3D) that rely on volumetric or mesh-based representations, and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) that has been used only for single-scene reconstruction. The key conceptual contribution is using shell maps as an intermediary that anchors sparse, 'Lagrangian' 3D Gaussians onto a regular 'Eulerian' texture grid, enabling the use of standard StyleGAN2 CNN backbones. A concurrent work, LSV-GAN, also uses layered surfaces offset from SMPL but composites per-layer rasterization; GSM departs by placing learnable 3D Gaussians on those layers, allowing larger geometric deviation from the template.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: GSM achieves 125 FPS rendering at 512×512 without convolutional upsamplers, eliminating the multi-view aliasing artifacts that plague all competing methods that rely on post-rendering 2D upsampling. This makes it the fastest 3D human GAN to date while matching SOTA FID and PCK scores. Open questions remain around articulation of hair and loose clothing (limited by SMPL's rigid skinning), geometry/normal extraction from the Gaussian representation, and whether combining multi-view studio captures with in-the-wild data can push toward photorealism.
