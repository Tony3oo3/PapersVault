## Problem

Existing vision-language models produce text-only outputs and lack the ability to generate or consume spatial locations (pixel coordinates, bounding boxes) as first-class inputs or outputs. It remains unclear how large language models can achieve fine-grained spatial understanding—localizing specific words to pixels or grounding a query to a region—without resorting to discrete token encodings that bloat the vocabulary or interfere with text generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241476/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed at 384×384) together with an optional location prompt (a bounding box or point sequence encoded via sine-cosine positional embeddings) or a text prompt (query sentence). Beyond the standard caption output, it produces a per-word pixel trajectory of the same length as the sentence, where each coordinate is a continuous 2D regression from the language model's hidden state. For referring localization, the model outputs a single bounding box at the <EOS> token; for segmentation, a SAM mask decoder is plugged in on top of the predicted box.

## Method

PixelLLM adds a small MLP regression head in parallel with the vocabulary mapping layer of a T5-XL language model, predicting 2D pixel coordinates for every output word without feeding the location back into the autoregressive loop. The visual front-end combines a frozen SAM ViT-H backbone (for localization features and segmentation) with a tunable EVA02 ViT-L backbone (for semantics), whose concatenated features are processed by a QFormer-style two-way transformer prompt feature extractor that extracts location-specific visual features conditioned on the input box. Training uses the Localized Narratives dataset, where human annotators narrate images while their mouse traces provide synchronized per-word pixel supervision; the loss combines label-smoothed cross-entropy for captioning with L1 regression for localization (weight λ=0.1). LoRA (rank 32) fine-tunes the T5-XL self-attention projections while keeping the rest frozen. The key design choice—continuous per-token regression rather than discrete spatial bin tokens—enables dense supervision from all words (not just nouns) and avoids the 5× sequence-length blowup that token-based approaches would require.

## Results

On RefCOCO referring localization, PixelLLM achieves 89.8 P@0.5 (val), 92.2 (testA), and 86.4 (testB), outperforming the previous best Ferret-7B by 2.3 points on val. For referring segmentation, it reaches 76.9 cIoU on RefCOCO val, surpassing LISA by 2.0 points. On dense object captioning (Visual Genome), PixelLLM achieves 17.02 mAP, exceeding GRiT by 1.5 points. For location-conditioned captioning, it scores 19.9 METEOR and 148.9 CIDEr on Visual Genome, and 14.3 METEOR and 82.3 CIDEr on RefCOCOg, both state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelLLM achieves state-of-the-art on all evaluated tasks. On referring localization, it outperforms Ferret-7B [52] by 2.3 P@0.5 on RefCOCO val (89.8 vs 87.5) and by 2.4 on RefCOCO+ val (83.2 vs 80.8), and beats Shikra-7B [4] by 2.8 on RefCOCO val. On referring segmentation, it surpasses LISA [24] by 2.0 cIoU on RefCOCO val (76.9 vs 74.9) and by 2.8 on RefCOCOg val (70.7 vs 67.9). For dense object captioning on Visual Genome, PixelLLM exceeds GRiT [48] by 1.5 mAP (17.02 vs 15.48), attributed to its learnable prompt feature extractor replacing ROIAlign. On location-conditioned captioning, it beats GPT4RoI [55] by 2.5 METEOR and 3.7 CIDEr on Visual Genome, and outperforms Kosmos-2 [36] by 0.2 METEOR and 20.0 CIDEr on RefCOCOg. For trace generation on the COCO subset of Localized Narratives, PixelLLM achieves 0.153 LBM, improving over MITR [34] (0.163), while producing traces from language model features rather than visual features.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelLLM builds directly on the BLIP-2/GIT vision-language architecture (QFormer-style prompt feature extractor, T5-XL language model) and inherits SAM's location prompt encoder and mask decoder. Its key departure from prior localization-in-LLM work (Pix2Seq, UniTab, Kosmos-2, Shikra, Ferret) is the representation: rather than encoding coordinates as vocabulary tokens or raw strings, it adds a lightweight MLP regression head in parallel with the vocabulary mapping layer, enabling dense per-word pixel outputs. This regression formulation uniquely allows the model to exploit the Localized Narratives dataset's human attention traces, which prior discrete-token methods could not leverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple regression head on frozen LLM features, trained with dense per-word pixel alignment, achieves SOTA across referring localization, segmentation, and dense captioning without task-specific architectural changes. The ablation showing a 3.7-point gain from dense supervision over sparse noun-only grounding is a practical insight for future grounding work. However, the model still requires separate fine-tuning per task, the Localized Narratives dataset is relatively small, and the broader question of whether LLMs develop genuine spatial reasoning (versus pattern-matching on coordinates) remains open. The reliance on a 4B T5-XL backbone rather than a decoder-only LLM also limits direct comparison with the rapidly evolving LLM-based VLM landscape.
