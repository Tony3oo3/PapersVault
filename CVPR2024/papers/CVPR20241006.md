## Problem

Existing hand mesh recovery methods are limited to single-task regression (e.g., monocular reconstruction) and cannot simultaneously support generation, inpainting, fitting, and reconstruction. Regression-based approaches fail to model the underlying distribution of natural hand poses, while existing implicit hand priors (VAE/GAN-based) operate in parameter or skeleton space and require additional inversion or fitting steps for mesh-level tasks. The paper asks whether a single generative model with strong multimodal controllability can unify all these tasks end-to-end without task-specific retraining.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any combination of an RGB image (encoded via ResNet50 into an 8×8 patch grid, then processed by a ViT with a global token), a 2D skeleton, a 3D skeleton, or no condition at all, all mapped into a shared feature space of dimension (P+3)×D. The output is a complete 3D hand mesh represented as vertex coordinates (not MANO parameters), from which joint positions are regressed via a pre-trained joint regression matrix. Task selection is purely determined by which condition channels are populated versus set to empty, requiring no architectural change or retraining.

## Method

The architecture is a U-shaped graph diffusion network where each block stacks a GCN layer (local mesh neighborhood aggregation), a self-attention layer (global vertex-to-vertex relationships), and a cross-attention layer (condition injection), with optional up/downsampling. All condition modalities are mapped to a shared feature space and concatenated, then subjected to a two-level random masking strategy: at the modality level, each condition is independently dropped with probability p_m (and all conditions jointly dropped with p_all for unconditional generation); at the feature level, image patches are randomly masked and skeleton joints are noise-perturbed or finger-masked, forcing the model to learn cross-modal correlations. During inference, a condition-aligned gradient guidance adds a bias term s·Σ_t·∇_{x_t}‖Pf_θ(x_t,c,t)−Px_0‖ to the reverse-process means, where P is a task-specific operator (e.g., joint projection matrix or per-vertex binary mask), enabling precise alignment with observed data without training an extra classifier. The model is trained with a combination of diffusion L1 data loss, vertex/joint L1 losses, and mesh smoothness losses (normal consistency and edge-length matching), predicting x_0 directly rather than noise.

## Results

On the FreiHand dataset, HHMR achieves MPJPE=6.0 mm and MPVPE=6.0 mm with a single hypothesis (n=1, T=10 DDIM steps), matching the SOTA MeshGraphormer (5.9/6.0). With multi-hypothesis sampling (n=8), it improves to MPJPE=5.9 mm and MPVPE=5.9 mm, surpassing all single-hypothesis baselines. For hand mesh generation, the method produces more diverse poses (APD=17.30 mm vs. 15.00 mm for PCA) with far fewer self-intersecting faces (SI=0.05% vs. 0.39%). For 2D skeleton fitting with combined image+skeleton conditions and gradient guidance, it reaches MPJPE=4.94 mm and MPVPE=5.05 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HHMR is compared against 11 baselines spanning regression-based reconstruction, parametric fitting, and prior-based methods. On single-hypothesis reconstruction (n=1), it ties MeshGraphormer [30] on MPVPE (6.0 mm) and is within 0.1 mm on MPJPE (6.0 vs. 5.9), while clearly outperforming MobRecon [4] (6.1/6.2) and all earlier methods (≥6.7 MPJPE). With multi-hypothesis sampling (n=8), it surpasses MeshGraphormer on MPVPE (5.9 vs. 6.0) and matches it on MPJPE, establishing a new SOTA. For hand mesh generation and inpainting (Table 1), the diffusion prior produces significantly more diverse and realistic meshes than MANO PCA sampling [38] (APD 17.30 vs. 15.00; SI 0.05% vs. 0.39%). For 2D skeleton fitting (Table 5), the combined image+skeleton+gradient-guidance configuration (MPJPE=4.94) outperforms the PCA-prior fitting baseline [38] (MPJPE=7.27) by over 2 mm. The method also outperforms the original FreiHand baseline [63] (11.0/10.9), YotubeHand [26] (8.4/8.6), I2L-MeshNet [33] (7.4/7.6), HIU-DMTL [57] (7.1/7.3), I2UV-HandNet [2] (6.7/6.9), METRO [31] (6.7/6.8), and Tang et al. [45] (6.7/6.7) by large margins, while CMR [3] shows an anomalous MPJPE of 16.9 due to its camera-space formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) and DDIM sampling (Song et al., 2020), but departs from prior hand mesh recovery work in two key ways. First, it moves beyond regression-based architectures (MeshGraphormer, MobRecon) that produce a single hypothesis by leveraging diffusion's generative nature for multi-hypothesis output. Second, it replaces VAE-based hand priors that operate in parameter or skeleton space with a graph diffusion model that works directly on 3D mesh vertices, using GCN + attention blocks in a U-shaped architecture. The condition-aligned gradient guidance is inspired by classifier guidance in image diffusion but eliminates the need for a separate classifier network, making it a lightweight, training-free alignment mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of generation, inpainting, reconstruction, and fitting in a single model without task-specific finetuning or inversion steps offers a practical simplification for downstream applications like gesture recognition and mesh editing. The plug-and-use gradient guidance (requiring no extra training) is a useful technique that could transfer to other 3D generation tasks. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: performance degrades with extremely incomplete or noisy inputs, and increasing denoising steps for higher precision remains time-consuming. Open questions include whether this framework scales to multi-hand or hand-object interaction scenarios, and whether the 10-step DDIM inference is fast enough for real-time AR/VR applications.
