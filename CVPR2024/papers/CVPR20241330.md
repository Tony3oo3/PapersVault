## Problem

Accurate 3D human-object interaction (HOI) capture typically requires dense multi-view camera rigs (3–8 views) or suffers from depth ambiguity and occlusion under monocular RGB. While IMU-based motion capture is well-studied for human-only scenarios, the natural and convenient setting of attaching a single IMU to the interacted object—combined with a minimal RGB camera—remains largely unexplored, and no prior method jointly tracks both human and object in this hybrid modality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 64-frame monocular RGB sequence (segmented via SAM, encoded by a pre-trained ResNet-34) together with object-mounted IMU data providing 6D rotation and 3D free acceleration at 60 Hz. It outputs SMPL-H body-and-hand parameters (10-dim shape, 66-dim pose) and object pose (6D rotation + 3D translation) per frame. Notably, the diffusion refinement stage treats the 486-dimensional over-parameterized interaction vector (body joints, hand joints, object pose, and raw IMU signals) as the denoising target, so hand motions are explicitly generated rather than merely regressed.

## Method

I'm-HOI adopts a two-stage paradigm. Stage 1 (general interaction motion inference) uses a multi-scale CNN to predict 3D body keypoints, an IK optimization layer to recover SMPL pose/shape, and a progressive mesh-alignment-feedback (MAF) loop with per-iteration MLP regressors that fuses IMU rotation/translation with visual cues to track the object, optionally followed by a lightweight optimization that enforces IMU acceleration and rotation constraints. Stage 2 (category-specific interaction diffusion filter) is a conditional DDPM trained per object category, where the condition tuple (body joints, object pose, IMU rotation, IMU acceleration) is concatenated with masked hand motion, and four regularization terms (joint-offset consistency, velocity smoothness, SMPL-H skinning consistency, and IMU rotation/acceleration alignment) guide the denoiser to project results onto the category-specific interaction manifold while infilling vivid hand motions.

## Results

On the fast-motion IMHD² dataset, I'm-HOI achieves 6.50 cm per-frame Chamfer Distance for human (SMPL) and 6.93 cm for object tracking, outperforming the best baseline (CHORE) by ~7.7 cm on human and ~9.9 cm on object. On the slower BEHAVE and InterCap datasets, I'm-HOI matches or slightly exceeds VisTracker on human tracking (5.26 vs 5.25 cm on BEHAVE; 5.66 vs 6.76 cm on InterCap) while consistently improving object tracking (7.43 vs 8.04 cm on BEHAVE; 8.92 vs 10.32 cm on InterCap). The 10-second sliding-window CD further confirms temporal consistency, with I'm-HOI reaching 5.36 cm (human) and 8.53 cm (object) on IMHD² versus 17.02/18.10 cm for VisTracker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I'm-HOI decisively outperforms all three baselines on the fast-motion IMHD² dataset, where the gap is largest: object tracking improves from 16.81 cm (CHORE) and 23.28 cm (VisTracker) to 6.93 cm, a margin of roughly 10–16 cm. On the slower BEHAVE dataset, I'm-HOI matches VisTracker on human tracking (5.26 vs 5.25 cm per-frame) but leads on object tracking (7.43 vs 8.04 cm) and substantially on the 10-second temporal metric (4.82 vs 8.49 cm for object). On InterCap, I'm-HOI beats VisTracker on both human (5.66 vs 6.76 cm) and object (8.92 vs 10.32 cm) per-frame CD, and again dominates on the 10-second window (5.81/7.14 vs 9.35/11.38 cm). PHOSA, being purely optimization-based, is consistently the weakest baseline across all datasets and metrics, with per-frame object CD exceeding 20 cm on every dataset. The paper also reports that I'm-HOI runs at ~0.5 s/frame versus 20 s/frame for VisTracker and ~2 min/frame for PHOSA, making it the fastest method by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the monocular HOI tracking line (PHOSA, CHORE, VisTracker) by replacing their fitting-learning-optimization paradigm with an end-to-end holistic CNN tracker that treats the object as an additional body joint, and by fusing object-mounted IMU signals via a mesh alignment feedback loop adapted from PyMAF. It departs from prior multi-modal RGB+IMU work (HybridCap, Fusing monocular images and sparse IMUs) which targets human-only capture, by being the first to attach the IMU to the interacted object rather than the body. The second stage repurposes conditional diffusion models (DDPM, x0-prediction formulation) as a category-specific motion refinement filter, a use not previously explored in the HOI tracking context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IMHD² dataset (295 sequences, 892k frames, 10 objects with dense RGB and object-mounted IMU) fills a notable gap in multi-modal HOI data and will likely become a standard benchmark. The practical relevance is growing as IMUs become ubiquitous in consumer devices (phones, smartwatches), making the single-camera-plus-object-IMU setting increasingly accessible. Key limitations remain: the method requires pre-scanned rigid object templates and manual sensor-to-template alignment, and extending to articulated or deformable objects in a template-free framework is left as future work. The 0.5 s/frame runtime (vs. 20–120 s for baselines) makes the approach viable for near-real-time applications, but the dependency on SAM for segmentation and a pre-trained ResNet-34 encoder adds practical overhead.
