## Problem

Existing video large multimodal models treat videos as predetermined offline clips, making them unsuitable for real-time streaming video where the model must (1) respond at temporally aligned moments, (2) retain long-context history without exceeding LLM context windows, and (3) generate answers fast enough to keep pace with the video stream. Per-frame dialogue approaches, while conceptually simple, are too slow and token-expensive for real-time use, and even GPT-4V with prompt engineering fails to meet these requirements.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous video stream at 2 FPS, encoding each frame into a single token via CLIP ViT-L, interleaved with user text queries inserted at arbitrary timestamps. Output is a temporally aligned language response (narration, answer, or forecast) that appears only at critical moments; for all other frames the model predicts an EOS token to remain silent, with the EOS token excluded from the context to avoid corrupting subsequent language modeling. This design keeps the key-value cache small and enables >10 FPS throughput on a single A100 GPU for 5-minute clips.

## Method

VideoLLM-online builds on a LLaVA-style architecture (CLIP ViT-L encoder → 2-layer MLP projector → Llama-2-7B-Chat with LoRA rank 128) but introduces a novel Streaming EOS prediction training objective: at timestamps where no response is needed, the model is trained to predict an EOS token directly on the frame embedding (max P(EOS | Ctx, Frame)), while at response timestamps it performs standard autoregressive language modeling. The combined loss uses condition indicators to apply LM loss only to response tokens and streaming loss only to the last token of frames preceding a response. For data, offline temporal annotations are converted into streaming dialogues by prompting Llama-2-13B-Chat to answer templated queries at critical timestamps. At inference, a continuous key-value cache avoids re-encoding, a probability threshold (θ=0.5) suppresses spurious EOS predictions, and the fast CLIP encoder is parallelized with the slower LLM decoder via a frame-token buffer to prevent bottlenecks.

## Results

On the COIN benchmark, VideoLLM-online achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across all five sub-tasks: 59.8% step recognition, 92.1% task recognition, 48.1% next-step forecasting, 47.9% procedure forecasting, and 52.9% procedure forecasting with goal. On the Ego4D LTA benchmark (ED@Z=20), it obtains 0.697 (verb), 0.698 (noun), and 0.897 (action), the best results among end-to-end models. For streaming dialogue on Ego4D Narration Stream, the model achieves LM-PPL of 2.43, TimeDiff of 2.32s, and Fluency of 42.6%, while running at 13.5 FPS with only 18.2 GB memory on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the COIN benchmark, VideoLLM-online outperforms all seven baselines across every sub-task, with the largest margins over ClipBERT (+29.0 on step, +26.7 on task) and TimeSformer (+13.3 on step, +6.8 on task, +14.1 on next forecasting). Among the strongest COIN baselines, it surpasses VideoTF by +3.3 (step), +1.1 (task), +5.7 (next), +7.7 (procedure), and +6.5 (procedure+goal), and ProcedureVRL by +2.9 (step), +1.3 (task), +1.3 (next). On Ego4D LTA, VideoLLM-online achieves the best ED@Z=20 among end-to-end models, beating VideoLLM [10] by 0.024 (verb), 0.027 (noun), and 0.024 (action). However, it trails Palm [32] (0.696/0.651/0.886) and AntGPT [88] (0.650/0.650/0.877) on verb and action metrics; the authors note these methods leverage egocentric pre-trained visual features (EgoVLP) and complex cascading pipelines, whereas VideoLLM-online directly outputs language in a single end-to-end pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA/LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP ViT-L encoder + MLP projector + Llama-2-7B-Chat with LoRA) but departs fundamentally from the offline video understanding paradigm where models receive predetermined clips. It extends the line of interleaved vision-language dialogue models (GPT-4V, Gemini, Otter) by replacing per-frame or per-clip language modeling with a novel streaming EOS prediction objective that teaches the model when to remain silent in a continuous frame stream. The inference pipeline draws on key-value caching and encoder-decoder parallelization from efficient LLM serving literature, but applies them specifically to the streaming video setting where frame skipping is unacceptable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formulate and solve free-form, temporally aligned dialogue over a continuous video stream with a general-purpose LMM, opening a path toward always-on contextual assistants (e.g., smart AR glasses). The practical efficiency (>10 FPS, <20 GB on a single A100 for 5-minute clips) makes the approach deployable rather than purely academic. Key limitations include the use of only 1 token per frame at 2 FPS (severely limiting spatial reasoning), reliance on egocentric/instructional video domains, and the fact that the streaming EOS objective, while effective, has not been validated on more complex multi-object or long-horizon scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher frame rates, richer spatial token budgets, and truly open-world streaming without curated annotation timelines.
