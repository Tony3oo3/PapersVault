## Problem

Detecting screen-recaptured document images is critical for protecting confidential information, but common forensic cues such as moiré patterns are weakened or removed when images are transmitted through online social networks (compression, resizing, blurring). Existing methods relying on hand-crafted texture/spectral features or multi-modal FAS cues (depth, physiological signals) either lack robustness under OSN distortions or are inapplicable to document images, leaving a gap in robust, generalizable recaptured document detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB document images, splits them into 224×224 patches, and derives a per-patch chromaticity map via normalized RGB computation with Z-score normalization. Both the RGB patch and the chromaticity patch are consumed by the network; the chromaticity patch is projected into latent prompt tokens while the RGB patch follows the standard ViT tokenization path. The output is a binary genuine-vs-recaptured decision per patch, aggregated to image level via majority voting.

## Method

The paper first derives a 1D pixel-level distortion model showing that misaligned resampling of display sub-pixels by camera pixels produces unbalanced RGB intensities along character edges, manifesting as color artifacts. These artifacts are aggregated into a chromaticity map (Eq. 5: per-channel intensity divided by the sum of all channels, then Z-score normalized), which amplifies the forensic signal independent of document content. The Chromaticity Map Adapter (CMA) then projects each chromaticity patch through a trainable linear layer into m-dimensional prompt tokens (m = ViT-B16 embedding size × 10 prompts) and injects them into a frozen ViT-B16 backbone pretrained via Visual Prompt Tuning on ImageNet. Only the adapter's linear projection and the VPT prompt parameters are trained, keeping the backbone frozen for parameter-efficient adaptation. This adapter-based injection of chromaticity as explicit prompt tokens outperforms direct multi-modal concatenation (which suffers from limited cross-modal fusion in ViT) and single-modality RGB baselines, achieving 0.8991 AUC / 20.79% EER on the cross-dataset protocol and 0.9475 AUC / 12.77% EER in the in-the-wild setting.

## Results

Under the in-the-wild protocol ROD_HQ→ROD_M&F, CMA achieves an AUC of 0.9475 and EER of 12.77%, outperforming the best single-modality RGB baseline (MobileNetV2 with moiré augmentation, AUC 0.8094, EER 26.27%) by 13.50 p.p. in EER. Under the more challenging ROD_HQ→DLC2021 protocol, CMA attains AUC 0.8489 and EER 25.82%, still leading the best non-CMA multi-modal ViT (AUC 0.7651, EER 33.22%) by 7.40 p.p. in EER. In the cross-dataset robustness evaluation (ROD_HQ→ROD_LQ), CMA maintains AUC 0.8688 under JPEG compression at QF=70 and AUC 0.7554 at 0.7× resolution, where all RGB-only baselines degrade to AUCs below 0.75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMA outperforms all seven baselines on both in-the-wild protocols. Against the strongest single-modality RGB baselines, CMA reduces EER by 13.50 p.p. over MobileNetV2 with moiré augmentation [4] (26.27%→12.77%) and by 15.68 p.p. over VPT [30] (28.45%→12.77%) on ROD_M&F; on DLC2021, the margin narrows to 5.07 p.p. over VPT (30.89%→25.82%) due to severe video compression and blur. Among texture-based recapture detectors, LTC-PE [58] and CNN+ViT [19] suffer the largest gaps (33.37 p.p. and 20.83 p.p. EER on ROD_M&F), confirming that moiré and LBP-style cues are unreliable under OSN distortions. The multi-modal ViT backbone with direct RGB+chromaticity token concatenation (AUC 0.8094, EER 28.22% on ROD_M&F) is outperformed by CMA by 15.45 p.p. in EER, demonstrating that the adapter-based prompt injection is substantially more effective than naive multi-modal token concatenation. ResNet50 [26] and ResNeXt101 [50] with moiré augmentation trail CMA by 25.41 p.p. and 23.55 p.p. in EER on ROD_M&F respectively, and by 13.59 p.p. and 13.91 p.p. on DLC2021, indicating that CNN backbones with synthetic moiré data lack the generalization of the chromaticity-map-driven adapter approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant moiré-pattern-based approach to screen-recapture detection, which the authors show fails under OSN compression and down-sampling. Instead, it grounds a new forensic cue—sub-pixel color artifacts arising from display-camera misalignment—in a pixel-level distortion model, and draws the chromaticity map formulation from the IIC color space used in illumination-based manipulation detection [43]. Architecturally, it adapts the Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) paradigm [30] and the adapter design lineage from NLP [27] and vision [13, 38] to inject a task-specific forensic modality into a frozen ViT backbone, rather than the multi-modal fusion strategies (depth, rPPG) borrowed from face anti-spoofing that are inapplicable to documents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical urgency is clear: confidential office documents displayed on screens can be silently recaptured and redistributed via OSNs, and existing moiré-based detectors lose discriminative power exactly under the compression that OSNs impose. The chromaticity map offers a content-independent, compression-robust forensic signal, and the ROD dataset (10.9K images across 24 device pairs) provides a much-needed benchmark for cross-domain document recapture detection. However, performance still degrades substantially under heavy Gaussian blur (AUC 0.6282 at σ=3) and on the more degraded DLC2021 video frames, leaving open the question of whether the approach generalizes to arbitrary capture conditions. The authors also suggest the chromaticity map could extend to splicing localization and deepfake document detection, but these remain unexplored.
