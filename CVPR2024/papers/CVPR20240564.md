## Problem

Temporal action detection (TAD) models have not been systematically evaluated for robustness against temporal corruptions such as missing or blurred frames, despite their critical role in real-world deployment. Existing video robustness benchmarks apply corruptions to all frames, which conflates recognition and localization failures and does not specifically probe the temporal continuity that TAD relies on. This paper identifies that even a single corrupted frame within an action instance causes significant mAP drops across leading TAD methods, revealing a previously unexplored vulnerability.

## Input / Output

The TAD models consume untrimmed video as a sequence of frames (or pre-extracted feature sequences from I3D, VideoMAEv2, or TSP backbones) and output a set of temporal bounding boxes, each annotated with a start time, end time, and action category label. For robustness evaluation, the corrupted test videos replace the central l% (l ∈ {1, 5, 10}) of frames within each ground-truth action instance with one of five corruption types (black frame, motion blur, overexposure, packet loss, occlusion), while the remaining frames stay clean. The proposed training method additionally produces two prediction sets per video (from clean and FrameDrop-augmented inputs) whose tIoU distributions are aligned during training.

## Method

The paper introduces two benchmark datasets (THUMOS14-C and ActivityNet-v1.3-C) containing 15 corruption types (5 types × 3 severity levels) applied to central action frames, and a new relative robustness metric γ^r that normalizes the mAP drop by clean performance. For defense, the authors propose a training strategy with two components: (1) FrameDrop augmentation, which groups the video into adjacent action-background pairs and randomly replaces one frame per pair with a black frame, forcing the model to rely on uncorrupted temporal context; and (2) a Temporal-Robust Consistency (TRC) loss that performs action-centric sampling—selecting the top-K predictions whose center timestamps are closest to the ground-truth action center—computes tIoU distributions for both clean and corrupted predictions, and minimizes the KL divergence of each distribution from their element-wise average. This alignment encourages the model to produce temporally consistent localization regardless of corruption, and experiments show it improves both robustness and clean-data mAP simultaneously.

## Results

On THUMOS14-C, existing TAD models suffer mAP drops ranging from 9.62% (TriDet with I3D) to 25.86% (E2E-TAD) under temporal corruptions, with relative robustness scores between 54.16% and 84.31%. The proposed FrameDrop + TRC training strategy consistently improves corrupted mAP by 2.36%–9.96% and relative robustness by 2.01%–13.22% across five TAD models, while also boosting clean mAP in most cases (e.g., ActionFormer+VideoMAEv2: 73.84→74.06 clean, 58.33→68.29 corrupted). On ActivityNet-v1.3-C, the same strategy yields consistent robustness gains (e.g., TriDet+TSP: relative robustness 41.42→47.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven TAD models (BasicTAD, E2E-TAD, TemporalMaxer, ActionFormer, AFSD, TriDet, VSGN) against temporal corruptions. End-to-end methods (BasicTAD, E2E-TAD, AFSD) are notably more vulnerable than models with fixed feature extractors: E2E-TAD drops 25.86% mAP (relative robustness 54.16%) versus TriDet's 9.62% drop (84.31%). The proposed FrameDrop+TRC training strategy outperforms all baselines on corrupted data across every model tested, with the largest gains for ActionFormer+VideoMAEv2 (+9.96% corrupted mAP, +13.22% relative robustness) and TriDet+VideoMAEv2 (+7.18%, +9.03%). The method also improves clean mAP in 4 out of 5 THUMOS14-C configurations, suggesting the robustness training acts as a regularizer. On ActivityNet-v1.3-C, the strategy still yields consistent improvements (e.g., TriDet+TSP: +2.10% corrupted mAP, +6.03% relative robustness), confirming generality across datasets and corruption types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of temporal action detection (TAD) and robustness evaluation. While prior video robustness benchmarks (Schiappa et al. [57], Yi et al. [79]) apply corruptions uniformly across all frames to test action recognition, this work departs by corrupting only a subset of frames to specifically disrupt temporal continuity and isolate localization vulnerability. The training strategy repurposes ideas from image-domain corruption augmentation (AugMix, DeepAugment) and consistency regularization, adapting them to the temporal setting via FrameDrop and a KL-divergence-based alignment loss. The paper does not build on a single prior method but rather benchmarks seven leading TAD architectures (CNN, Transformer, Graph-based) under a unified corruption protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark for temporal robustness of TAD models, and the authors strongly recommend that future TAD work include corruption-robustness evaluation alongside standard clean-data metrics. The finding that vulnerability stems primarily from localization error (not classification) redirects future research toward boundary-refinement mechanisms rather than classification heads. Open questions include whether the five corruption types and three severity levels adequately capture real-world deployment scenarios, whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or streaming settings, and whether the clean-data improvement from robust training is an artifact of the specific augmentation or a generalizable regularization effect.
