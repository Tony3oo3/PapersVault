## Problem

Existing NeRF watermarking methods (StegaNeRF, CopyRNeRF) are restricted to either implicit or explicit NeRF representations and lack robustness against common image distortions such as cropping and JPEG compression. This paper addresses the question of how to embed watermarks into both NeRF representation types simultaneously while maintaining robustness under diverse attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240218/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained NeRF model (implicit MLP-based or explicit tensor-based), a fixed binary message of 4–48 bits, and camera parameters for rendering. It produces a fine-tuned NeRF model whose every rendered view carries the embedded watermark, plus a single pre-trained HiDDeN decoder (trained once, reused across models) that extracts the message from the 2-level DWT LL subband of any rendered image. The decoder operates on full-resolution rendered images, and PCA whitening is applied to its final linear layer to remove extraction bias on vanilla-rendered inputs.

## Method

WateRF uses a two-phase pipeline: (1) pre-train a HiDDeN decoder with a noise layer for distortion robustness, then discard the encoder; (2) fine-tune the NeRF model by minimizing a combined loss in the frequency domain. The key insight is embedding the watermark in the 2-level DWT LL subband of rendered images rather than the spatial domain, which empirically yields higher bit accuracy and robustness to cropping and JPEG compression. To handle memory constraints of full-resolution rendering, the method employs deferred back-propagation: it first renders a full-resolution image without auto-differentiation to compute message loss (BCE) and Watson-VGG perceptual loss, caches the gradients, then re-renders image patches with auto-differentiation and applies an additional patch-wise loss (MAE + TV + SSIM) for a second back-propagation pass. This dual back-propagation strategy simultaneously improves reconstruction quality and bit accuracy without modifying the NeRF architecture, making the method applicable to both implicit and explicit representations.

## Results

At 16-bit message length, WateRF with TensoRF achieves 95.67% bit accuracy, 32.79 PSNR, 0.948 SSIM, and 0.033 LPIPS, while WateRF with Vanilla NeRF achieves 94.24% bit accuracy, 28.81 PSNR, 0.954 SSIM, and 0.025 LPIPS. Under robustness attacks (Table 3), WateRF with TensoRF maintains 95.36% bit accuracy under Gaussian noise, 93.13% under rotation, and 84.12% under combined distortions (crop + brightness + JPEG), substantially outperforming CopyRNeRF which does not report results for crop, brightness, JPEG, or combined attacks. Training time is approximately 6× faster than CopyRNeRF for both implicit and explicit NeRF representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WateRF outperforms CopyRNeRF [30] on every reported metric at 16 bits: bit accuracy improves from 91.16% to 94.24% (NeRF) and 95.67% (TensoRF), PSNR improves from 26.29 to 28.81/32.79, SSIM from 0.910 to 0.954/0.948, and LPIPS decreases from 0.038 to 0.025/0.033. On robustness, CopyRNeRF only reports results for Gaussian noise, rotation, scaling, and Gaussian blur, while WateRF additionally survives crop (83.48–95.40%), brightness (84.14–90.91%), JPEG (86.88–86.99%), and combined attacks (73.64–84.12%), demonstrating substantially broader attack coverage. Against the HiDDeN+NeRF baseline, WateRF improves bit accuracy from ~50% (chance level) to 94–96%, confirming that post-hoc 2D watermarking before NeRF training is ineffective. WateRF also trains approximately 6× faster than CopyRNeRF while simultaneously supporting both implicit (Vanilla NeRF) and explicit (TensoRF) representations, a capability neither baseline offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRF watermarking line initiated by CopyRNeRF (implicit-only) and StegaNeRF (explicit-only) by unifying both representations under a single fine-tuning framework. It repurposes the HiDDeN deep watermarking decoder as a fixed extraction network and introduces a frequency-domain (DWT) embedding strategy, departing from the spatial-domain fine-tuning used by StegaNeRF. The deferred back-propagation technique borrowed from ARF is combined with a novel patch-wise loss to address the memory bottleneck of full-resolution rendering during fine-tuning, a practical constraint not addressed by prior NeRF watermarking methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As NeRF becomes the dominant 3D representation for Metaverse, VR, and AR content, copyright protection of both the model and its rendered outputs is increasingly critical. The ability to watermark both implicit and explicit NeRF with a single pipeline broadens applicability significantly. However, the method still encodes only one fixed message per model (requiring re-fine-tuning for a new message), decoder training takes ~12 hours, and robustness to model-level attacks (e.g., adversarial perturbation of NeRF weights) remains unaddressed — an open question the authors explicitly flag for future work.
