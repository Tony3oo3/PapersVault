## Problem

Existing visual scanpath prediction models produce observer-agnostic scanpaths, treating all individuals' attention as uniform and ignoring well-documented inter-observer variations driven by factors like gender, age, and neurodevelopmental conditions. No standardized method exists for quantifying individual attention traits or integrating them into scanpath prediction frameworks, leaving a gap between the personalized nature of human attention and the collective nature of current models.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a one-hot observer identifier, and task-specific guidance (a search target, a natural-language question, or none for free-viewing) as input. It outputs a sequence of T fixation probability maps along with Gaussian distribution parameters (mean and variance) for sampling fixation positions and durations. The observer identifier is consumed as a low-dimensional one-hot vector that is linearly embedded, making the approach applicable to any number of observers without architectural changes, and the method is designed as a plug-in module that can be grafted onto existing LSTM- or Transformer-based scanpath models.

## Method

The ISP method introduces three components that individualize a general scanpath model: (1) an Observer Encoder that linearly embeds a one-hot observer identifier into a feature vector u and computes an observer guidance map m_u by combining u with visual features via a tanh-softmax operation; (2) an Observer-Centric Feature Integration module that concatenates the previously fixated visual features (gated by the prior step's prediction) with observer-centric visual features (gated by m_u), applies spatial and channel-wise average pooling with observer-feature addition, and combines the two pooled vectors via an outer product to yield observer-centric feature maps R_t; (3) an Adaptive Fixation Prioritization module that extracts multi-channel semantic feature maps A_t from the decoder, computes observer-conditioned attention weights β_t over the channels, and produces the final fixation map as a weighted combination of these channels. Training uses 15 epochs of supervised learning followed by 10 epochs of self-critical sequence training, with each batch containing different observers' scanpaths for the same image to encourage learning of discriminative inter-observer features.

## Results

On the OSIE-ASD dataset (most diverse observer group), Gazeformer-ISP achieves the highest ScanMatch (SM=0.406) and lowest String-Edit Distance (SED=6.823), outperforming the general Gazeformer (SM=0.388, SED=7.081) and ChenLSTM (SM=0.341, SED=7.602). On COCO-Search18, ChenLSTM-ISP reaches SM=0.480, exceeding the human-to-human upper bound of 0.458. In ranking-based evaluation, ChenLSTM-ISP more than doubles R@1 on OSIE-ASD (4.8% vs. 2.4% for ChenLSTM), confirming the models' ability to distinguish individual observers' scanpaths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both ISP models (ChenLSTM-ISP and Gazeformer-ISP) consistently outperform all five baselines across all four datasets on every value-based metric. Against the strongest general baselines, Gazeformer-ISP improves SM by 0.018 on OSIE-ASD (0.406 vs. 0.388) and by 0.023 on COCO-Search18 (0.455 vs. 0.432), while ChenLSTM-ISP gains 0.060 SM on OSIE-ASD (0.401 vs. 0.341) and 0.026 on COCO-Search18 (0.480 vs. 0.454). The older baselines (SaltiNet, PathGAN, IOR-ROI) are substantially outperformed, with SM gaps exceeding 0.2 on most datasets. Fine-tuning the general models (ChenLSTM-FT, Gazeformer-FT) yields only marginal or inconsistent gains (e.g., Gazeformer-FT actually drops SM on COCO-Search18 from 0.432 to 0.432 and AiR-D from 0.349 to 0.350), whereas the ISP models show uniform improvements, demonstrating that explicit observer encoding is more effective than per-observer parameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scanpath prediction line of work (ChenLSTM, Gazeformer) by introducing an individualization layer on top of existing observer-agnostic architectures. It departs from prior individualized saliency models, which predict static fixation maps, by targeting the dynamic, sequential nature of scanpaths. The three proposed components (observer encoder, observer-centric feature integration, adaptive fixation prioritization) are designed as plug-and-play modules that can be grafted onto either LSTM- or Transformer-based decoders, positioning the work as a general framework rather than a single new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes individualized scanpath prediction as a distinct task and provides the first systematic evaluation protocol combining value-based (ScanMatch, MultiMatch, SED) and ranking-based (MRR, R@K) metrics for this purpose. The demonstration that observer-specific fine-tuning fails to produce distinguishable scanpaths while the ISP framework succeeds (even modestly) suggests that explicit observer encoding is necessary rather than optional. Open questions include scalability beyond the small observer pools (15–20 per dataset), whether the one-hot observer identifier generalizes to unseen observers, and whether the learned observer features can be transferred across tasks or populations without retraining.
