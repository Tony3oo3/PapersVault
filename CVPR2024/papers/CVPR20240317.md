## Problem

Existing automatic audio description (AD) systems rely on downstream fine-tuning over short video clips and struggle to generate story-coherent, character-centric narrations for long-form videos spanning hours. Prior methods also underutilize spoken dialogues/subtitles for character identification and lack mechanisms to track evolving storylines across extended durations, making them unsuitable for the full complexity of real-world AD generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240317/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

MM-Narrator processes a long-form video scene-by-scene in an autoregressive loop. For each scene (a clip of N frames with timestamp t), it consumes per-frame CLIP-ViT features, GRiT image captions, YOLOv7 person detections, and concatenated subtitles within a time window T_sub, alongside a short-term queue of K most recent predicted ADs and a long-term visual memory bank of CLIP features from all prior frames. The output is a single AD sentence per scene that names characters, describes actions, and complements (rather than overlaps) the original audio dialogue.

## Method

MM-Narrator is a training-free, GPT-4-prompted system that combines three key mechanisms: (1) a short-term textual memory queue holding the K most recent ADs to ensure narrative coherence, (2) a long-term visual memory using a register-and-recall mechanism where CLIP-ViT frame features are stored globally and retrieved via cosine similarity to re-identify characters in close-up shots (activated only when a single person is detected), and (3) a complexity-based multimodal in-context learning (MM-ICL) strategy that selects few-shot demonstrations by shortest chain-of-thought reasoning steps rather than random or similarity-based sampling, yielding more intuitive examples for multimodal reasoning. The entire pipeline—multimodal perception via off-the-shelf experts, memory retrieval, and LLM prompting—requires no parameter updates. Additionally, the paper introduces SegEval, a GPT-4-based segment evaluator that scores recurrent AD generation across text-level (originality, consistency) and sequence-level (coherence, diversity, specificity) dimensions using oracle context windows of variable length.

## Results

On MAD-eval-Named, MM-Narrator with complexity-based MM-ICL achieves ROUGE-L 12.1, CIDEr 11.6, SPICE 4.5, and R@5/16 48.0, outperforming the fine-tuning-based AutoAD-I (R-L 11.9, SPICE 4.4, R@5/16 42.1) and the GPT-4V-based MM-Vid (R-L 9.8, SPICE 3.8, R@5/16 46.1) in most metrics. The training-free design matches or exceeds fine-tuning-based SOTAs without any parameter updates, with the only notable loss being CIDEr against AutoAD-I (11.6 vs. 14.3). With an external character bank, MM-Narrator reaches R-L 13.4 and SPICE 5.2, matching the fine-tuning-based AutoAD-II on ROUGE-L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-Narrator (w/ MM-ICL) decisively outperforms both LLM baselines VLog (R-L 7.5) and VideoChat (R-L 7.9) across all four metrics, with the largest gap on CIDEr (11.6 vs. 1.3/2.4), attributed to the short-term memory queue and long-term visual memory. Against the GPT-4V-based MM-Vid, MM-Narrator wins on every metric (R-L 12.1 vs. 9.8; CIDEr 11.6 vs. 6.1; SPICE 4.5 vs. 3.8; R@5/16 48.0 vs. 46.1), demonstrating that memory-augmented text-only GPT-4 can surpass vision-enhanced GPT-4V for AD generation. Compared to fine-tuning-based AutoAD-I, MM-Narrator wins on R-L (12.1 vs. 11.9), SPICE (4.5 vs. 4.4), and R@5/16 (48.0 vs. 42.1) but trails on CIDEr (11.6 vs. 14.3). Against the strongest fine-tuning baseline AutoAD-II with an external character bank, MM-Narrator ties on R-L (13.4) and wins on SPICE (5.2 vs. not reported) but loses on CIDEr (13.9 vs. 19.5) and R@5/16 (49.0 vs. 50.8). Overall, the training-free MM-Narrator matches or exceeds all fine-tuning-based SOTAs in most scenarios without any gradient updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the fine-tuning-based AD generation line (AutoAD, AutoAD-II, ClipCap, ClipDec) by proposing a fully training-free GPT-4 prompting framework with a memory-augmented recurrent generation loop. It also extends the in-context learning literature by replacing the standard random or similarity-based demonstration selection with a complexity-based strategy grounded in chain-of-thought reasoning steps, arguing that for complex multimodal text generation, intuitive simplicity of examples matters more than surface-level similarity. The register-and-recall visual memory mechanism repurposes CLIP frame-level features for long-term character re-identification, a use case not explored in prior video captioning or AD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design lowers the barrier to AD generation and suggests that memory-augmented LLM prompting can match or exceed expensive fine-tuning pipelines for long-form video narration. The complexity-based ICL finding challenges the prevailing assumption that similarity-based retrieval is optimal for demonstration selection, opening a question about what makes good ICL examples for complex generation tasks more broadly. Limitations include dependence on proprietary GPT-4/GPT-4V, evaluation on a single benchmark (MAD-eval), and the observation that GPT-4V's richer outputs actually hurt reference-based scores, highlighting a mismatch between current metrics and advanced generation quality. Open questions include whether the complexity-based selection generalizes beyond AD and whether the memory mechanism scales to multi-hour content without degradation.
