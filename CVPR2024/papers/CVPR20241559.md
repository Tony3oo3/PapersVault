## Problem

Egocentric video understanding requires processing sequences spanning minutes to hours, yet existing high-level representations (verb-noun labels, narrations, bounding boxes, hand-object states) are all short-range, describing only a few seconds of activity. There is no comprehensive, temporally evolving representation that captures the full structure of actions—including interacted objects, their relationships, and how actions unfold over time—that algorithms can rely on for long-form tasks like action anticipation and activity summarization.

## Input / Output

For EASG generation, the model consumes pre-extracted SlowFast clip-level features (averaged over PRE-to-POST frames) and Faster R-CNN ROIAlign features for each object bounding box, producing a directed graph with a fixed camera-wearer node, a verb node, and object nodes connected by relationship edges (direct object, prepositions, action). Each object node carries three grounding bounding boxes (PRE, PNR, POST frames), and object indices are maintained consistently across timesteps to form a dynamic graph. For downstream tasks, EASG sequences are serialized as triplet strings (e.g., 'CW - verb - wash; wash - direct object - car') or natural-language sentences and fed to LLMs, which output a predicted future graph or a 1-2 sentence activity summary.

## Method

The paper introduces Egocentric Action Scene Graphs (EASGs), a time-varying directed graph G(t)=(V(t),E(t)) where each temporal realization corresponds to three salient frames (PRE, PNR, POST) and encodes the camera wearer, the action verb, involved objects, and their pairwise relationships. For the EASG generation task, a baseline model uses task-specific fully-connected layers on top of pre-extracted visual features, with three progressive difficulty levels: Edge Classification (predict relationships given verb and objects), Scene Graph Classification (predict objects and edges given verb), and full EASG Classification (predict all three components). The dataset is annotated through a multi-stage pipeline: graph initialization from SCOD annotations, refinement by three independent AMT annotators who verify verb-noun pairs and add indirect objects with groundings, a validation stage resolving inter-annotator disagreements via targeted questions, and a temporal recollection step that re-indexes object nodes for cross-timestep consistency. For downstream evaluation, GPT-3 and GPT-3.5 are prompted with EASG sequences (as triplets or sentences) to perform action anticipation and activity summarization, demonstrating that the structured graph representation outperforms flat verb-noun sequences by up to +7.56 in Top-5 verb accuracy.

## Results

For EASG generation (the primary task), the baseline achieves R@10 of 60.4% on Edge Classification (with constraint), 41.4% on Scene Graph Classification, and 14.3% on full EASG Classification, compared to 8.0%, 0.2%, and 0.0% for random guess. On the downstream action anticipation task, EASG representations outperform verb-noun (V-N) sequences across all metrics: at T=20, EASG achieves Top-5 verb accuracy of 15.97% vs. 8.41% for V-N, and Top-5 action accuracy of 9.24% vs. 4.98%. Notably, even short EASG sequences (T=5) outperform longer V-N sequences (T=20), demonstrating higher representational power. For activity summarization, EASG inputs yield a CIDEr score of 13.79 vs. 9.42 for V-N, though narrations still lead at 19.99.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares EASG against the standard verb-noun (V-N) action representation, which is the dominant format in egocentric vision datasets like Ego4D and Epic-Kitchens. On action anticipation, EASG consistently outperforms V-N across all sequence lengths and metrics, with the largest gains on verb prediction (Top-5: 15.97% vs. 8.41% at T=20, a +7.56 absolute improvement) and action prediction (Top-5: 9.24% vs. 4.98%). A particularly striking finding is that short EASG sequences (T=5, ~19s) outperform longer V-N sequences (T=20, ~82s), indicating that the structured graph encoding captures richer relational information per timestep. On activity summarization, EASG inputs improve CIDEr from 9.42 (V-N) to 13.79, but still trail raw narrations (19.99), which is expected given LLMs' natural bias toward free-form text. For the EASG generation task itself, the baseline substantially exceeds random guess (e.g., 60.4% vs. 8.0% R@10 on Edge Cls), but full EASG Classification remains challenging at only 14.3% R@10, suggesting that jointly predicting verbs, objects, and relationships is significantly harder than predicting edges alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scene graph generation literature (Action Genome, Visual Genome, Panoptic Scene Graph) to the egocentric video domain, where prior graph-based work has focused on autonomous driving, multi-view fusion, or scene topology (Ego-Topo) rather than explicit action encoding. It builds directly on the Ego4D dataset's annotation framework (PRE/PNR/POST frames, SCOD benchmark) to introduce Egocentric Action Scene Graphs, a temporally evolving directed graph that explicitly encodes action verbs as nodes alongside objects and their relationships. The key departure from prior egocentric representations (verb-noun labels, temporal segments, hand-object interaction states) is the shift from short-range, flat annotations to a structured, long-form graph that captures how actions unfold over minutes of video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ego4D-EASG is the first dataset explicitly designed for long-form egocentric video understanding with graph-based annotations, filling a gap where existing representations only cover temporal spans of a few seconds. The EASG generation baseline is deliberately simple (fully-connected layers on pre-extracted features), and the very low EASG Cls scores (R@50 ≈ 18–21) indicate the task is far from solved, leaving substantial room for future work. The LLM-based downstream experiments (action anticipation, summarization) are preliminary and rely on GPT-3/3.5 as a proxy for symbolic reasoning, so the practical integration of EASGs into end-to-end vision systems remains open. The dataset's modest scale (221 clips, 11.4 hours) and the 16% of annotations requiring validation also limit immediate generalizability.
