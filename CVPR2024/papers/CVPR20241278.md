## Problem

All existing video anomaly detection (VAD) methods require a training procedure with varying levels of supervision, making them domain-specific and costly to deploy in new settings where data collection may be infeasible due to privacy or rarity of anomalies. This paper asks whether VAD can be performed entirely without training or data collection, leveraging the generalization capabilities of pre-trained foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed surveillance video as input, sampling every 16 frames for computational efficiency. It produces a per-frame anomaly score in [0,1] for each sampled frame, which is then thresholded to identify anomalous temporal windows. No training data, labels, or fine-tuning are required at any stage; all components operate purely at inference time using frozen off-the-shelf models (BLIP-2 for captioning, Llama-2-13b-chat for language reasoning, and ImageBind for cross-modal encoders).

## Method

LAVAD decomposes VAD into three training-free components operating on a language-driven pathway. First, Image-Text Caption Cleaning replaces noisy per-frame BLIP-2 captions with the semantically closest caption from the entire video using cosine similarity between image and text embeddings from a VLM. Second, LLM-based Anomaly Scoring prompts Llama-2 to summarize cleaned captions within a 10-second temporal window into a concise scene description, then assigns an anomaly score via a context prompt that impersonates a law enforcement end-user. Third, Video-Text Score Refinement aggregates the initial scores of the K=10 most semantically similar frames (measured by video-text cosine similarity via ImageBind) using a softmax-weighted average to produce the final per-frame score. The key insight is that LLMs possess inherent anomaly-judgment capability from textual scene descriptions, but require temporal context and noise mitigation to approach the performance of trained detectors.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, LAVAD achieves 80.28% AUC ROC without any training, outperforming the best unsupervised method DyAnNet (79.76%) by +0.52% and the best one-class method GODS (70.46%) by +9.82%. On XD-Violence, LAVAD reaches 85.36% AUC and 62.01% AP, surpassing the best unsupervised method RareAnom (68.33% AUC) by a substantial +17.03% margin. Among training-free baselines, LAVAD significantly outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (72.84% AUC on UCF-Crime, 79.62% on XD-Violence) and all zero-shot VLM baselines (ZS CLIP, ZS ImageBind), which score below 56% AUC on UCF-Crime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAVAD outperforms all unsupervised and one-class baselines on both datasets without any training. On UCF-Crime, it edges out DyAnNet (79.76%) by +0.52% AUC and surpasses GCL (74.20%) by +6.08%; on XD-Violence, it beats RareAnom (68.33%) by a large +17.03% AUC margin. Among training-free baselines, LAVAD substantially outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (72.84% on UCF-Crime, 79.62% on XD-Violence) by leveraging temporal summarization and cross-modal score refinement rather than single-frame prompting. Zero-shot VLM baselines (ZS CLIP, ZS ImageBind) perform poorly (53–56% AUC on UCF-Crime), confirming that naive VLM similarity is insufficient for anomaly detection. LAVAD still trails the best weakly-supervised methods (e.g., CLIP-TSA at 87.58% on UCF-Crime, MSL at 78.58% AP on XD-Violence), but does so without any training data or supervision, highlighting the gap that training-free approaches can close.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper fundamentally departs from all prior VAD work, which requires some form of training (supervised, weakly-supervised, one-class, or unsupervised) to learn the normality distribution. Rather than building on a specific VAD architecture, LAVAD repurposes off-the-shelf foundation models—BLIP-2 for captioning, Llama-2 for temporal summarization and anomaly scoring, and ImageBind for cross-modal similarity—into a novel inference-only pipeline. The closest prior effort is Kim et al. [12], which uses VLMs for anomaly detection but still requires human-in-the-loop refinement and further model adaptation; LAVAD eliminates both requirements. The cross-modal similarity-based cleaning and score refinement techniques draw on the general principle of aligning modalities in a shared embedding space, but their application to iterative VAD score correction is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAVAD demonstrates that VAD can be performed without any training data or model fine-tuning, which is practically significant for privacy-sensitive deployment (e.g., surveillance) where data collection is restricted or where domain shifts make retraining costly. However, the method still trails weakly-supervised SOTA by 7–17% in AUC, and its reliance on static-camera surveillance-style videos and specific prompting strategies limits generalizability to more diverse anomaly types or dynamic camera settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos, whether more capable LLMs close the remaining gap, and whether the cross-modal refinement can be extended to handle domain-specific anomalies without manual prompt engineering.
