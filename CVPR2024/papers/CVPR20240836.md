## Problem

Existing cross-modal generative models are limited to producing a single output modality from a single input modality, and the only prior work capable of concurrent multi-modality generation (CoDi) relies on linear interpolation in a shared latent space, which degrades synthesis quality. There is no flexible architecture that can accept arbitrary combinations of modalities as compound conditions while simultaneously generating multiple output modalities with high fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240836/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

C3Net accepts any non-empty subset of {audio clip, text prompt, image} as compound conditions and simultaneously generates any subset of {image, audio, text caption} as outputs. Each modality's condition is independently encoded into the shared CLIP latent space, and the C3-UNet receives a weighted linear interpolation of all provided condition latents, while the Control C3-UNet receives each modality's latent separately. When only a single condition is provided, the Control C3-UNet's contribution is zeroed out since no additional cross-modal information is available.

## Method

C3Net aligns modality-specific conditions (audio, image, text) to a shared CLIP latent space ξ using modality-specific encoders that are first pre-trained unimodally (e.g., SSAST masked autoencoder for audio) and then fine-tuned via contrastive learning against a frozen text encoder. Generation is performed by a pair of UNets per modality: a frozen C3-UNet (initialized from CoDi's trained UNet) that takes the linear interpolation of aligned latents as its condition, and a trainable Control C3-UNet (a ControlNet-style copy) that takes each modality's aligned latent separately and injects additional control at every skip-connection level via zero-convolution layers scaled by a constant (empirically 0.1). Because all conditions share the same latent space ξ, a single set of Control C3-UNet parameters suffices for all modalities, substantially reducing training complexity. The unimodal pre-training stage addresses the scarcity of high-quality paired multimodal data, and the Control C3-UNet recovers semantic information lost during latent interpolation, enabling synthesis quality beyond what CoDi's linear interpolation achieves.

## Results

On the Tri-modality Test Set, C3Net outperforms CoDi on compound-conditioned image synthesis (FID 10.97 vs. 11.39; CLIP score 25.29 vs. 25.17) and text synthesis (CIDEr-D 0.0713 vs. 0.0651; SPIDEr 0.0665 vs. 0.0631). For audio synthesis, C3Net achieves higher OVL (63.25 vs. 62.91) and REL (59.83 vs. 59.01) but a slightly worse FAD (11.7 vs. 11.4). On the ESC-50 audio classification task, C3Net reaches 23.25% accuracy versus CoDi's 21.05%, confirming better preservation of shared multimodal features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C3Net is compared exclusively against CoDi (Composable Diffusion, NeurIPS 2024), the only prior work capable of concurrent any-to-any multimodal generation. C3Net wins on image synthesis (FID 10.97 vs. 11.39, CLIP 25.29 vs. 25.17), text synthesis across all four metrics (e.g., CIDEr-D 0.0713 vs. 0.0651, BLEU-1 0.1104 vs. 0.1059), and audio perceptual quality (OVL 63.25 vs. 62.91, REL 59.83 vs. 59.01). The sole metric where C3Net trails is FAD for audio (11.7 vs. 11.4), a marginal difference suggesting comparable spectral fidelity. The unimodal pre-training ablation (Tables 1–2) further isolates the encoder contribution: with audio as the sole condition, C3Net's pre-trained audio encoder improves CIDEr-D from 0.0654 to 0.0704 and CLIP from 23.192 to 23.325 over CoDi's from-scratch encoder. Overall, C3Net matches or exceeds CoDi on every headline metric, with the Control C3-UNet architecture providing the largest gains on compound (multi-condition) settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

C3Net directly extends Composable Diffusion (CoDi) by replacing its linear interpolation of aligned latent conditions with a learnable Control C3-UNet, which is a multimodal adaptation of ControlNet's trainable-copy-plus-zero-convolution design. The shared latent space is inherited from CLIP, and the audio encoder initialization draws on SSAST's self-supervised masked autoencoder pre-training. The key conceptual departure from CoDi is that instead of treating compound conditions as a simple weighted sum in latent space, C3Net trains a dedicated network to coordinate them, while also introducing unimodal pre-training to mitigate the scarcity of high-quality paired multimodal data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper contributes the first high-quality tri-modal (image, text, audio) validation set, which is practically useful for benchmarking future multimodal generators. However, the empirical gains over the sole baseline (CoDi) are modest across all metrics (e.g., FID 11.39→10.97, CLIP 25.17→25.29), and the FAD for audio synthesis is actually slightly worse. Open questions include whether the CLIP latent space is truly optimal for audio, whether contrastive alignment sacrifices modality-specific information, and whether the Control C3-UNet's advantage over linear interpolation persists at larger scale or with more modalities.
