## Problem

Pretrained text-to-image diffusion models can generate realistic details for real-world image super-resolution, but heavy degradation in LR inputs destroys local structures and leads to ambiguous semantics, causing semantic errors in reconstructed HR images. Existing diffusion-based Real-ISR methods either ignore semantic text cues entirely or rely on off-the-shelf tagging/captioning models that produce erroneous prompts under strong degradation, failing to preserve correct object identity in the output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241422/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (typically 128×128 for ×4 upscaling, or 512×512 patches) and produces a high-resolution RGB image at 512×512. Beyond the raw LR image, the pipeline internally extracts two types of semantic prompts from the input: hard prompts (discrete tag texts from a tagging head) and soft prompts (continuous representation embeddings from an image encoder). During inference, the LR latent is additionally embedded into the initial Gaussian noise of the diffusion sampler (LRE strategy) to reduce training-inference discrepancy.

## Method

SeeSR is a two-stage diffusion-based Real-ISR framework. In stage 1, a Degradation-Aware Prompt Extractor (DAPE) is fine-tuned from the RAM tagging model via LoRA, trained with MSE and cross-entropy losses to align the LR image's representation and logits embeddings to those of the corresponding HR image, making it robust to complex degradation. In stage 2, the trained DAPE provides both hard prompts (tag texts fed to the frozen text encoder) and soft prompts (representation embeddings injected via newly added Representation Cross-Attention modules after the text cross-attention layers in the UNet), while the LR image itself is encoded and passed through a ControlNet branch. The SD 2-base backbone is frozen; only the ControlNet, image encoder, and RCA modules are trained with the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective. A key design choice is the use of tag-style prompts (covering all objects) rather than caption-style prompts, combined with soft embeddings to compensate for the recall limitations of threshold-based hard tags.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val, SeeSR achieves the best FID (31.93 vs. 36.57 for StableSR), LPIPS (0.3843 vs. 0.3868 for Real-ESRGAN), DISTS (0.2257 vs. 0.2428 for FeMaSR), MUSIQ (68.33 vs. 66.85 for PASD), and CLIPIQA (0.6946 vs. 0.6799 for PASD). On the real-world RealSR benchmark, SeeSR leads in FID (125.55 vs. 128.51 for StableSR), MUSIQ (69.77 vs. 68.69 for PASD), and CLIPIQA (0.6612 vs. 0.6590 for PASD). On the newly introduced RealLR200 dataset, SeeSR attains the best no-reference scores: MUSIQ 69.71, CLIPIQA 0.6813, and MANIQA 0.6254. In the semantics preservation test on COCO, SeeSR achieves 30.0 PQ (panoptic segmentation) and 41.3 mIOU (semantic segmentation), surpassing the next-best DiffBIR (27.2 PQ, 37.7 mIOU) by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeeSR consistently outperforms all 10 baselines on FID, MUSIQ, and CLIPIQA across all four evaluation datasets. Among GAN-based methods (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, LDL, DASR, FeMaSR), Real-ESRGAN achieves the highest PSNR/SSIM but trails SeeSR by 4–10 points on MUSIQ and 0.10–0.15 on CLIPIQA, reflecting the fidelity–perception trade-off. Among diffusion-based methods, PASD is the closest competitor (also using semantic prompts), yet SeeSR surpasses it by 1.5–2.5 points on MUSIQ and 0.01–0.02 on CLIPIQA on every dataset, attributed to the degradation-aware tag-style prompts versus PASD's caption-style prompts. DiffBIR, which uses a two-stage restoration-then-enhancement pipeline, is outperformed by SeeSR on FID (e.g., 31.93 vs. 40.42 on DIV2K-Val) and all no-reference metrics. StableSR, which relies solely on the LR image as a control signal without text prompts, is beaten by SeeSR on FID by 4.6 points on DIV2K-Val and 3.0 on RealSR. In the semantics preservation evaluation on COCO, SeeSR leads all methods by 2.8 PQ over DiffBIR and 7.6 mIOU over DiffBIR, confirming that semantic prompts yield more structurally faithful reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeeSR extends the diffusion-prior-based Real-ISR line (StableSR, PASD, DiffBIR) by introducing a degradation-aware prompt extractor (DAPE) fine-tuned from the RAM tagging model, combined with ControlNet and cross-attention modules to inject both hard (tag text) and soft (representation embedding) semantic prompts into a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone. Unlike PASD, which relies on off-the-shelf ResNet/YOLO/BLIP models that degrade under strong corruption, DAPE is explicitly trained to align LR-image encodings with HR-image encodings, making tag extraction robust to real-world degradation. The paper also departs from prior SD-based Real-ISR methods by proposing the LR Embedding (LRE) inference strategy, which embeds the LR latent into the initial sampling noise to close a training-inference gap that causes artifacts in smooth regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic-preservation angle addresses a practical failure mode of current diffusion-based SR methods—erroneous object reconstruction (e.g., a ship becoming a bird or fish)—that fidelity metrics alone do not capture. The LRE strategy is a simple, drop-in fix applicable to any SD-based Real-ISR pipeline, making it broadly useful beyond SeeSR itself. However, the authors acknowledge that DAPE can still mislabel heavily degraded images, tag-to-region alignment remains imprecise under severe corruption, and small-scale scene text reconstruction is still challenging, leaving room for mask-guided or multi-scale prompting approaches.
