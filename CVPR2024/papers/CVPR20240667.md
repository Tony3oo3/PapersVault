## Problem

In Heterogeneous Federated Learning (HtFL), clients with different model architectures struggle to share useful global knowledge: existing approaches rely on global datasets (availability and relevance issues), auxiliary global models (high per-iteration communication cost), or class-wise prototypes (limited information and biased under model heterogeneity). The paper asks how to transfer pre-existing knowledge from a server-side pre-trained generator to heterogeneous clients in an upload-efficient manner without fine-tuning the generator or sharing full model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240667/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client uploads only its class-wise prototypes in a fixed-dimensional ETF space (|C_i| × K elements, where K = C equals the number of classes), making the upload cost the lowest among all compared methods. The server downloads C generated images (64×64 from StyleGAN-XL, rescaled to match client data resolution) paired with C latent centroid vectors in the generator's W-space (dimension H). Clients produce class labels via cosine similarity against the shared ETF classifier vectors, and the additional projection layer h_i' maps extracted features to the H-dimensional latent space for the knowledge-transfer loss.

## Method

FedKTL operates a six-step knowledge-transfer loop per iteration: (1) clients train with an ArcFace loss against a shared simplex ETF classifier, (2) clients upload unbiased class prototypes, (3) a lightweight server-side feature transformer F (two FC layers + BN) is trained to map prototypes into the generator's valid W-space using a combined MSE loss (preserving class-wise discrimination) and MMD loss (aligning the prototype domain with the generator's valid latent domain), (4) the server generates one image per class from the averaged latent centroids via the frozen synthesis network G_s, (5) clients download the C image-vector pairs, and (6) clients perform an additional supervised local task where a projection layer h_i'∘f_i is trained to reconstruct the latent centroids from the generated images, thereby injecting the generator's feature-extraction knowledge into the client's feature extractor. The key insight is that semantic relevance between generated images and local data is unnecessary—only class-discriminative structure matters—so the scheme works even when the generator's pre-training domain differs from the clients' task domain.

## Results

FedKTL achieves the highest test accuracy across all four datasets in both pathological and practical data heterogeneity settings using HtFE_8 (8 model architectures). In the practical setting, FedKTL reaches 87.63% on CIFAR-10, 46.94% on CIFAR-100, 53.16% on Flowers102, and 28.17% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming the best baseline by up to 5.40% (CIFAR-100). In the pathological setting, the gains are 1.11% (CIFAR-10), 3.75% (CIFAR-100), 4.82% (Flowers102), and 2.10% (Tiny-ImageNet) over the strongest competitor. The upload cost is only 0.09M parameters per iteration, the lowest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedKTL outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset in both heterogeneity settings. The largest margins appear in the practical setting: on CIFAR-100, FedKTL (46.94%) beats the closest competitor FedDistill (41.54%) by 5.40%, and on Flowers102 it leads FedDistill (49.13%) by 4.03%. In the pathological setting, FedKTL leads FedGen (58.26%) by 3.75% on CIFAR-100 and FedKD (32.64%) by 2.10% on Tiny-ImageNet. FedProto consistently underperforms all other methods, dropping to 36.34% on practical CIFAR-100 and 19.01% on practical Tiny-ImageNet, likely due to biased prototype aggregation under model heterogeneity. LG-FedAvg, FedGen, and FedGH assume a homogeneous classifier and are not applicable to the fully heterogeneous HtM_10 scenario (which includes ViTs), whereas FedKTL handles all model heterogeneity levels and even improves with more diverse architectures (reaching 50.33% on HtFE_9 vs. 48.06% on HtFE_2). The upload cost of FedKTL (0.09M) matches FedDistill but is 16–200× lower than FML (18.50M) and FedKD (16.52M), while achieving substantially higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedKTL builds on the ETF classifier idea from FedETF (which assumes homogeneous models) and extends it to full model heterogeneity by using a fixed simplex ETF to produce unbiased prototypes from architecturally diverse clients. It departs from prior HtFL knowledge-sharing approaches—global-dataset distillation (FedMD), mutual distillation with auxiliary models (FML, FedKD), and prototype sharing (FedProto, FedDistill)—by repurposing a server-side pre-trained generator (StyleGAN-XL or Stable Diffusion) as a compact knowledge carrier. The key conceptual departure is the domain-alignment step (MMD + MSE) that maps client prototypes into the generator's valid latent space, enabling the generator to produce task-relevant image-vector pairs without fine-tuning, which no prior HtFL method attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The upload-efficient design (0.09M per iteration, the lowest among all baselines) directly addresses the practical asymmetry between uplink and downlink bandwidth in real-world edge deployments, making HtFL more deployable. The finding that generated images need not be semantically related to client data—only class-discriminative—broadens the applicability of pre-trained generators as knowledge sources beyond their original training domain. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-image modalities, how sensitive it is to the quality and domain of the pre-trained generator, and whether the iterative domain-alignment process converges reliably under extreme data heterogeneity or very few clients.
