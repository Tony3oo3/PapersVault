## Problem

Few-shot fine-tuning of diffusion models (e.g., Dreambooth, LoRA) enables high-quality style and object replication, raising serious copyright infringement concerns. Existing post-caution authentication pipelines (text-to-image, inpainting, img2img) fail to reliably recover training samples due to the inherent diversity of generative models, making it difficult to produce the vivid, human-interpretable visual evidence required for legal proof of unauthorized usage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial representation of a target image (created by block-wise masking with block size 4, removing half the pixels) along with the pre-trained model θ and the fine-tuned model θ'. It produces a fully recovered image after N=1000 PGD steps under an L2 budget of 70, and a similarity score computed in CLIP and DINO feature spaces between the recovered and original images. The recovered image serves as the visual evidence, while the similarity score provides the quantitative discrimination between membership and holdout samples.

## Method

CGI-DM formulates copyright authentication as a contrasting gradient inversion problem: it maximizes the KL divergence between the latent variable distributions of the fine-tuned and pre-trained models conditioned on a partial image. The key theoretical contribution proves this KL divergence reduces to the difference in MSE noise-prediction losses between the two models (Eq. 7), enabling optimization via Monte Carlo sampling over the diffusion time step t and noise ε_t. Using PGD with L2-norm-constrained updates (step size α=2, total budget ε=70), the algorithm iteratively modifies the partial image so the fine-tuned model's noise prediction becomes far more accurate than the pre-trained model's. If the image was memorized during fine-tuning, the recovered image closely matches the original; otherwise, the reconstruction is unrecognizable, providing both a quantitative similarity metric and a visually interpretable artifact for legal authentication.

## Results

On the WikiArt dataset (style-driven generation), CGI-DM achieves 90% Acc.(C) and 98% AUC(D) under Dreambooth with prior loss, outperforming the best baseline (Img2img) by 8 and 6 percentage points respectively. On the Dreambooth dataset (subject-driven generation), CGI-DM reaches 89% Acc.(C) and 94% AUC(D) under Dreambooth without prior loss, surpassing Img2img by 14 and 7 points. Under LoRA fine-tuning, CGI-DM still leads with 80% Acc.(D) and 87% AUC(D) on WikiArt, maintaining a consistent 6–11 point advantage over all generation-based baselines across every fine-tuning scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGI-DM is compared against three generation-based pipelines that could serve as post-hoc copyright authentication: Text-to-Image generation, Inpainting via DDNM [33], and Image-to-Image generation. CGI-DM consistently outperforms all three baselines across every combination of dataset (WikiArt, Dreambooth) and fine-tuning method (Dreambooth with/without prior loss, LoRA). The strongest baseline is Img2img, which achieves up to 94% AUC(D) on WikiArt under Dreambooth without prior loss, but CGI-DM still leads by 5 points (99% AUC(D)). The gap is most pronounced on the Dreambooth subject-driven dataset, where CGI-DM achieves 94% AUC(D) versus 87% for Img2img under Dreambooth without prior loss. Inpainting (DDNM) performs competitively on WikiArt but degrades sharply under LoRA (59% AUC(D)), while Text2img is the weakest baseline overall, never exceeding 79% AUC(D). The consistent margin (5–14 points in AUC(D)) across all 18 evaluation settings demonstrates that exploiting the KL-divergence-based conceptual difference between pre-trained and fine-tuned models is substantially more effective than relying on generation diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends gradient inversion (model inversion) from classification networks to generative diffusion models, a domain the authors note has no systematic prior work. The optimization procedure directly adapts PGD [18] with Monte Carlo sampling over diffusion timesteps and noise, while the core loss is derived by showing that the KL divergence between pre-trained and fine-tuned model posteriors reduces to the difference in noise-prediction MSE (Eq. 7). Conceptually, it positions itself as a 'post-caution' complement to adversarial-protection methods (AdvDM, Glaze, Anti-Dreambooth) and as a visualizable alternative to membership inference attacks that rely on opaque loss-based metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides the first visually interpretable, end-to-end pipeline for demonstrating that a specific image was used to fine-tune a diffusion model, which is the type of evidence needed in ongoing AI-copyright litigation. Key limitations include the strict white-box requirement (access to both pre-trained and fine-tuned parameters), the high computational cost of 1000 Monte Carlo steps, and the fact that baselines (text2img, inpainting, img2img) are not purpose-built for authentication, making the large performance gap partly a comparison artifact. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to black-box or API-only settings, how it scales to next-generation models (SDXL, Flux), and whether stronger defenses beyond RandAugment can meaningfully degrade authentication accuracy.
