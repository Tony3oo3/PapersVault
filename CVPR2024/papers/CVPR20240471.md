## Problem

In text-to-image personalization, non-subject elements from reference images (backgrounds, nearby objects, tied objects, style substances, poses) become entangled into the subject embedding, causing generated images to reflect reference-image biases and reducing prompt alignment. This undesired embedding entanglement was only superficially understood, and existing mitigations like segmentation masks or negative prompts fail to address all bias types, particularly substance and pose biases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (typically 3–7, or a single image) of a target subject plus a generation prompt, and produces personalized images of that subject in novel contexts. The key input modification is the training text description: instead of the standard 'a [v] [class name]' format, each reference image is paired with a Selectively Informative Description (SID) that includes class identifications and detailed specifications of all undesired objects in the scene. The SID is generated automatically by multimodal GPT-4 given a structured instruction, requiring no manual annotation.

## Method

SID modifies only the training text descriptions in optimization-based personalization models (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, SVDiff, Textual Inversion) without altering model architecture or loss functions. The core mechanism is to include class identifications and informative specifications of undesired objects in the training descriptions while deliberately excluding informative specifications of the subject itself. This works because the diffusion model's alignment objective causes non-subject image regions to match their corresponding text tokens rather than accidentally co-aligning with the subject identifier [v]. The subject retains only its class identification in the description, preserving identity encoding in [v]. Multimodal GPT-4 is used as the VLM to generate SIDs automatically, outperforming BLIP-2 and LLaVA in instruction-following fidelity for this task.

## Results

The paper evaluates SID-integrated models against their baselines using three custom metrics: subject-alignment (SA), non-subject-disentanglement (NSD), and text-alignment (TA), computed over 7,500 generated images across commonly used personalization datasets. All SID-integrated models (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, SVDiff) form the Pareto boundary in the multi-reference-image setting (Fig. 8), achieving the best or near-best values on all three metrics simultaneously. In the single-reference-image setting (Fig. 9), SID-integrated optimization-based models also dominate the Pareto frontier and outperform the two encoder-based models (ELITE and BLIP-Diffusion). The paper reports that average improvements across the three metrics are positive with a substantial margin in all cases, except for subject-alignment in the single-reference setting, where SID slightly reduces SA due to mitigating subject overfitting (e.g., pose bias).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, SVDiff, and Textual Inversion (all optimization-based baselines), SID-integrated versions consistently win on non-subject-disentanglement and text-alignment while maintaining or marginally improving subject-alignment, forming the Pareto boundary in both multi- and single-reference settings. Against the encoder-based baselines ELITE and BLIP-Diffusion (compared only in the single-reference-image setting), SID-integrated DreamBooth outperforms both on all three metrics, with the advantage being particularly pronounced for pose bias scenarios where encoder-based models struggle. The paper also compares SID against two alternative disentanglement strategies—negative prompts and segmentation masks—and finds that SID achieves higher non-subject-disentanglement and text-alignment than either alternative, as negative prompts cannot unravel already-entangled representations at inference time and segmentation masks fail on substance, tied-object, and pose biases. Human evaluation (130 participants, 5,200 responses) confirms the metric-based findings, with SID-integrated models preferred on subject-alignment, non-subject-disentanglement, and text-alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on optimization-based text-to-image personalization methods (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, SVDiff, Textual Inversion) that fine-tune a pretrained diffusion model with a small set of reference images. It departs from the standard practice of using minimal training descriptions ('a [v] [class name]') by proposing Selectively Informative Description (SID), which adds informative specifications of non-subject objects to the training text, leveraging the model's inherent text-image alignment objective to prevent those objects from entangling with the subject identifier. The paper also positions itself against segmentation-mask-based approaches (used in Break-a-Scene, Taming Encoder, BLIP-Diffusion, InstantBooth, ELITE), demonstrating that masks fail for substance, tied-object, and pose biases where the 'undesired' information is not spatially separable from the subject.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners: a text-only modification, generated automatically by multimodal GPT-4, can be plugged into any existing optimization-based personalization pipeline without architectural changes or additional loss functions, yet substantially reduces five identified bias types. Limitations include dependence on VLM captioning quality (occasional failures), inability to handle strong facial-expression entanglement when the VLM omits such details, and restriction to optimization-based models—integration into encoder-based models during pre-training remains an open question. The formalization of 'undesired embedding entanglement' as a distinct phenomenon (rather than generic overfitting) and the five-bias taxonomy provide a useful diagnostic framework for the field.
