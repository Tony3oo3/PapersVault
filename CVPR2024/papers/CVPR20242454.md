## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) is highly sensitive to the classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale, producing oversmoothed results at small scales and oversaturated results at large ones. The paper reveals that the practical SDS gradient (which uses CFG noise) does not actually derive from the L2 diffusion loss as originally claimed, but instead originates from a WGAN generator loss with a fixed, sub-optimal discriminator. Variational Score Distillation (VSD) partially addresses this but still uses an incomplete discriminator optimization objective, leading to instability during distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242454/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For 2D distillation and text-to-3D, the method takes a text prompt and optimizes either image pixels or a NeRF representation (rendered at random views via differentiable rendering) using a frozen Stable Diffusion v2-1-base as the diffusion backbone. For image editing, it additionally takes a source image and a modified caption, feeding the source image into a second discriminator to guide edits toward the target prompt while away from the source. The 3D output is a NeRF requiring over 5 hours to distill on a single A800 GPU; 2D outputs are standard-resolution images.

## Method

ASD reinterprets score distillation through the WGAN lens by defining a discriminator D(x_t; y) = log p(y|x_t)/p(φ|x_t) implemented via the diffusion model, where φ is an optimizable conditional embedding (textual inversion) or LoRA branch. The generator loss is the WGAN generator loss with a CFG-like weighting λ, yielding a gradient that matches the practical SDS gradient but with the key difference that φ is optimizable rather than approximated by raw noise. The core contribution is updating this discriminator with the complete WGAN discriminator loss (including a penalty term controlled by hyperparameter γ), rather than the partial loss used by VSD (which corresponds to γ=0). For text-conditioned tasks without real samples, an upper-bound discriminator loss L_D' is used; for image-conditioned tasks like editing, the full loss with real-sample terms is applied. For image editing, ASD extends to two discriminators (source prompt z and target prompt y), and the paper shows Delta Denoising Score (DDS) is a special case where both discriminators are fixed.

## Results

On the text-to-3D task, ASD achieves the highest CLIP scores across all three CLIP variants: 0.3314 (B/32), 0.3376 (B/16), and 0.2854 (L/14), outperforming SDS (0.3282, 0.3290, 0.2889) and VSD (0.3203, 0.3258, 0.2789). In a user study comparing ASD and VSD, ASD is preferred 64.7% vs. 35.3% for details, 54.3% vs. 45.7% for semantic alignment, and 65.5% vs. 34.5% for overall quality. Qualitatively, ASD produces more stable distillation progress (less structural drift) and recovers from over-saturated initializations, while VSD exhibits content fluctuation at small CFG scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASD outperforms SDS (DreamFusion) on all three CLIP score variants (e.g., 0.3314 vs. 0.3282 on B/32) and visually produces less over-saturation and over-smoothing across 2D and 3D distillation. Against VSD (ProlificDreamer), ASD achieves higher CLIP scores (0.3314 vs. 0.3203 on B/32; 0.3376 vs. 0.3258 on B/16) and wins the user study decisively on all three criteria (64.7% vs. 35.3% for details, 65.5% vs. 34.5% for overall quality). The paper also shows that VSD's instability at small CFG scales (content fluctuation during distillation) is absent in ASD, which maintains stable object identity and structure throughout optimization. In the image editing task, ASD surpasses DDS (Delta Denoising Score) by producing more realistic and semantically coherent edits—e.g., adding photorealistic details to cartoon images and performing object replacement without residual artifacts (such as the leftover squirrel tail in DDS results). The paper further demonstrates that ASD can recover from over-saturated initializations where SDS with high CFG fails, highlighting the advantage of the complete adversarial discriminator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-derives the SDS gradient (DreamFusion) and shows it actually originates from a WGAN generator loss rather than the L2 diffusion loss originally claimed, revealing that SDS implicitly uses a fixed sub-optimal discriminator. VSD (ProlificDreamer) is reinterpreted as using an optimizable but incompletely-trained discriminator under the same WGAN lens. ASD completes the picture by optimizing the full WGAN discriminator loss via LoRA or textual-inversion embeddings, and subsumes DDS (Delta Denoising Score) as a special case for image editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WGAN unification provides a principled explanation for the long-standing CFG-sensitivity problem in score distillation (oversmoothing at low scales, oversaturation at high scales) and offers a concrete fix through complete discriminator optimization. More broadly, the framework suggests that any downstream task (text-to-3D, image editing, diffusion distillation) can be addressed by designing an appropriate GAN paradigm around a frozen diffusion model, eliminating the need for task-specific diffusion architectures or large fine-tuning datasets. Key limitations include comparable training speed to VSD (5+ hours for a 3D NeRF on A800) and the heuristic nature of the γ hyperparameter, with open questions around dynamic scheduling and forward-only approximations of the discriminator noise.
