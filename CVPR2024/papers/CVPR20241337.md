## Problem

3D diffusion models are constrained by the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale 3D datasets, resulting in poor generalization on complex text prompts compared to their 2D image counterparts. Existing lifting approaches (e.g., SDS-based methods) require hours of per-object optimization and still suffer from 3D inconsistency, while 3D-native diffusion models trained on limited 3D data fail to capture intricate multi-concept prompts. The paper asks whether a 3D diffusion model can achieve robust generalization by directly inheriting knowledge from pre-trained 2D image diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 3D shape represented as a triplane NeRF: three axis-aligned feature maps (F_xy, F_xz, F_yz) each of size 256×256×6, decoded by a shared 4-layer MLP into per-point color and density for volume rendering. The triplane is initially sampled in ~3 seconds via DDPM (50 steps, CFG scale 5.0), then refined for 2000 SDS steps (~3 minutes on A100) with noise levels restricted to U(0.1, 0.5) to prevent over-optimization. Triplanes are scaled to [-1,1] to match the variance of natural images, enabling joint training with real 2D data.

## Method

PI3D represents each 3D object as 6 pseudo-images (2 channels per triplane plane) and fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v2.1 by stacking them along the batch dimension, replacing self-attention with a global attention block that aggregates tokens across all 6 pseudo-images, and adding per-pseudo-image learnable embeddings to the timestep conditioning. The triplane fitting stage uses depth-aware volume rendering supervision (color, mask, depth losses) plus L2/TV regularization and a 3-view visual hull constraint to encourage correct geometry. Training mixes 20k Objaverse/Cap3D triplane pairs with 50% real LAION-5B images (with global attention disabled for the 2D portion), which is critical for preserving 2D semantic generalization. The lightweight refinement stage continues optimizing the sampled triplane with 2D SDS (DeepFloyd-IF guidance, CFG 20) for only 2000 steps, converging rapidly thanks to the strong 3D initialization from the diffusion model.

## Results

On 415 test prompts from the DreamFusion gallery, PI3D achieves a CLIP Score of 65.9 (ViT-B-32) and 53.8 (ViT-L-14), with R-Precision@1 of 25.2 and 29.3 respectively, substantially outperforming both Point-E and Shap-E across all metrics. The full pipeline (3-second diffusion sampling + 3-minute SDS refinement) produces 3D-consistent shapes in under 3 minutes on a single A100 GPU. Qualitative comparisons show PI3D handles complex compositional prompts (e.g., 'a humanoid banana sitting at a desk doing homework') where baselines fail to capture key semantic elements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Point-E, PI3D achieves a 3.7× improvement in CLIP Score (65.9 vs. 22.3, ViT-B-32) and a 5.5× improvement in R-Precision@1 (25.2 vs. 4.6), reflecting Point-E's inability to handle complex compositional prompts. Against Shap-E, PI3D leads by 3.9 points in CLIP Score and 9.2 points in R-Precision@1 (ViT-B-32), with the gap widening on ViT-L-14 (R-Precision@1: 29.3 vs. 17.3). Against DreamFusion (SDS-based), PI3D converges in ~3 minutes versus hours of optimization while producing more 3D-consistent results, thanks to the strong triplane initialization from the diffusion model. Against ATT3D, PI3D achieves superior 3D consistency and output diversity despite a slightly longer inference time (3 s sampling + 3 min refinement vs. 1 s), and supports arbitrary complex prompts rather than being limited to combinations of seen words.

## Relation to Prior Work

PI3D bridges two previously separate lines of text-to-3D research: 3D diffusion models trained directly on 3D data (Point-E, Shap-E, NFD, 3DGen) and 2D-lifting methods that optimize 3D representations via Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer, MVDream). Its core departure is the pseudo-image representation, which reinterprets triplane feature maps as a set of 6 images and fine-tunes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model to generate them directly, inheriting 2D generalization while producing 3D-consistent outputs. The global cross-pseudo-image attention is inspired by video and multi-view diffusion models, and the lightweight SDS refinement stage builds on the DreamFusion/three-studio pipeline but benefits from a strong diffusion-sampled initialization that eliminates the need for hours of optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PI3D's 3-minute end-to-end generation (3 s sampling + 3 min refinement) is a substantial practical speedup over SDS-based methods that require hours, while matching or exceeding their quality on text alignment and 3D consistency. The pseudo-image trick is a generalizable architectural insight: it shows that 2D diffusion architectures can be repurposed for 3D generation with minimal modification and only 20k 3D training pairs, reducing the data bottleneck that has constrained 3D generative models. Key limitations remain: per-object triplane fitting scales linearly with dataset size, and the fixed 6-channel triplane caps geometric detail. Open questions include whether autoencoder-based triplane encoding can remove the fitting bottleneck and whether higher-dimensional pseudo-image sets can be modeled effectively for finer geometry.
