## Problem

Existing audio-video synchronization models rely on CNN feature extractors that require fixed-size inputs, forcing conversion to 25 fps and 16 kHz — an unacceptable modification for professional film production and live broadcasting where original media specifications must be preserved. These methods also operate exclusively on single-face clips, do not generalize to complex real-world scenarios (multiple speakers, makeup, animation, background noise), and have never been evaluated against actual synchronization offsets or at the title level.

## Input / Output

DiVAS ingests raw video frames (face crops at 96×96×3 or full frames at 240×240×3) and a raw mono audio signal over a fixed 0.2-second window, where the number of frames (F = t × fps) and audio samples (S = t × sr) vary dynamically with the original media specifications rather than being fixed. The model outputs a synchronization offset in milliseconds, a confidence score that doubles as a speaker-activity indicator, and — when aggregated across dialog scenes via RANSAC — a title-level assessment classifying the issue as constant offset, drift early, drift late, or intermittent.

## Method

DiVAS replaces all CNN encoders with a 3D Vision Transformer for video (patch size 16×16×1) and a 1D Transformer for audio (patch size 128 samples), each with 3 layers, 3 heads, and 1024-dim MLP, totaling only 4.8M parameters. The key architectural innovation is a time-aware positional encoding that modulates the standard sinusoidal encoding with a temporal factor (100·f/fps for video, 100·s/sr for audio), encoding actual timestamp information rather than mere sequence order, which makes the model invariant to frame rate and sample rate. Training uses a contrastive margin loss that minimizes distance for in-sync pairs and maximizes it for artificially offset pairs (50% negative rate, offsets from 1 frame to clip length). At inference, a sliding-window protocol computes a distance matrix over candidate offsets, and a RANSAC-based regression across all high-confidence dialog-scene predictions yields a title-level synchronization timeline that distinguishes constant offsets from progressive drifts.

## Results

On the LRS2 benchmark, DiVAS achieves 99.4% accuracy across all three tolerance levels (±40 ms, [-125, 45] ms, [-185, 90] ms), matching SyncNet while using only 4.8M parameters (vs. 13.6M for SyncNet and 80.1M for VocaLiST) and running at 4500 FPS (vs. 265 and 13 FPS respectively). On the new challenging dataset of 218k dialog scenes from 170 titles, DiVAS trained on that data reaches 89.4% / 93.7% / 97.0% accuracy at clip level, and 93.5% / 96.4% / 99.4% at title level. On LRS3-multfps, DiVAS generalizes to unseen 60 fps videos with 96.8% accuracy at ±40 ms, while the base model without timestamp encoding drops to 82.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SyncNet [9], DiVAS matches the 99.4% accuracy on LRS2 while being ~3× smaller (4.8M vs. 13.6M params) and ~17× faster (4500 vs. 265 FPS). Against VocaLiST [15], DiVAS improves accuracy by 0.7 points (99.4% vs. 98.7%), reduces parameters by ~16× (4.8M vs. 80.1M), and is ~350× faster (4500 vs. 13 FPS), partly because VocaLiST's classification design requires running the model W×O times per clip versus W times for DiVAS. On the challenging multi-title dataset, DiVAS trained on LRS2 (54.5% at ±40 ms) outperforms VocaLiST trained on LRS2 (59.2%) only at the loosest tolerance (82.1% vs. 80.5%), but DiVAS trained on LRS3-multfps (61.9%) clearly surpasses VocaLiST at all tolerances. Against Iashin et al. [14] on the full-frame setting, DiVAS-frame achieves 92.2% Acc21 versus 80.7% (a 10.5-point gain) with fewer parameters (17M vs. 55.3M), though Iashin et al. report a higher Acc21tol (96.9% vs. 94.0%) due to their use of a 200 ms tolerance that the authors argue is too permissive for real applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiVAS departs from the dominant SyncNet-to-VocaLiST lineage, where two-stream CNNs extract fixed-size features (MFCCs, grayscale mouth crops) before a Transformer or classifier aligns them. By replacing all CNN encoders with a 3D Vision Transformer on raw video patches and a 1D Transformer on raw audio samples, DiVAS inherits the variable-length flexibility of pure Transformers and introduces a time-aware sinusoidal positional encoding that encodes actual timestamps rather than sequence indices. The RANSAC-based title-level regression for drift detection has no direct predecessor in the AV-sync literature, which has been confined to per-clip offset estimation. For the full-frame setting, DiVAS-frame extends the sparse-selector approach of Iashin et al. [14] but removes the 21-class discretization and the 25 fps conversion requirement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model's frame-rate and sample-rate invariance removes a critical preprocessing bottleneck for professional pipelines (film mastering, live broadcasting) where converting to 25 fps can itself introduce sync artifacts, and the 350× speedup over VocaLiST makes per-title quality checking feasible at scale. The title-level drift detection (constant offset vs. early/late drift vs. intermittent) addresses a real production failure mode that clip-level methods cannot diagnose. Open questions include whether the full-frame variant can generalize beyond speech to transient or ambient sound sources, whether the RANSAC aggregation degrades on titles with very sparse dialog coverage, and how the model behaves on content with extreme frame rates (e.g., 120 fps) or heavily compressed codecs beyond H.264/AAC.
