## Problem

Camera-parameter-free multi-view 3D HPE methods hard-code camera parameters during training because existing datasets offer at most one or two distinct camera settings, causing severe performance degradation on unseen camera configurations. The straightforward remedy—synthesizing comparable sample counts under many new camera settings—is computationally prohibitive, as it multiplies training scale by the number of settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241235/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2D keypoint sequences from four synchronized camera viewpoints (17 joints per frame, 27-frame temporal windows) and jointly regresses a 3D pose (17 joint positions) and full camera parameters: rotation as a rotation vector [axis x angle], intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy), and five distortion coefficients (k1-k3, p1-p2). The 2D inputs are not raw images but synthesized 2D poses generated by projecting 3D ground-truth poses through randomly sampled camera settings with realistic detection noise.

## Method

PoseIRM combines a principled camera-setting sampler (constraining roll, pitch, inter-view azimuth separation, and uniform sampling) with two pose synthesis algorithms—DAPSA, which injects per-joint Student's t-distributed noise into projected 2D poses, and MVCPSA, which triangulates noisy 2D detections into 3D and re-projects to a new viewpoint—then trains under a relaxed Invariant Risk Minimization objective. The IRM loss adds a variance penalty over per-environment MPJPE and camera-parameter losses, forcing a single shared regression head to be simultaneously optimal across all camera settings rather than memorizing one. The backbone uses PoseFormer for per-view temporal feature extraction, a two-layer transformer decoder for cross-view fusion, and two three-layer FCN heads for 3D pose and camera parameter regression. This design requires only a handful of samples per synthesized setting, avoiding the combinatorial training cost while improving both unseen-setting generalization and seen-setting accuracy.

## Results

On Human3.6M, PoseIRM achieves 25.1 MPJPE (CPN, T=27), the best among all camera-parameter-free multi-view methods, outperforming MTF-Transformer (26.2) and FLEX (31.7). On TotalCapture, PoseIRM achieves 14.7 MPJPE on seen cameras and 16.6 MPJPE on unseen cameras, with only a 1.9-point drop, compared to MTF-Transformer's 24.7→36.6 (11.9-point drop) and FLEX's 49.4→125.4 (76-point drop). The camera-parameter-required methods (AdaFuse 19.5, MvP 18.6) still outperform PoseIRM on Human3.6M, but PoseIRM narrows the gap to unseen-camera performance from 11.9 to 1.9 points on TotalCapture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseIRM (25.1 MPJPE) outperforms all camera-parameter-free multi-view baselines on Human3.6M: MTF-Transformer (26.2, +1.1), Probabilistic Triangulation (27.8, +2.7), and FLEX (31.7, +6.6). It also beats all monocular methods (best: Occlusion-aware Network at 42.9) by a large margin (~18 points). Among camera-parameter-required methods, PoseIRM surpasses MTF-Transformer+ (25.8, +0.7), Crossview Fusion (26.2, +1.1), Epipolar Transformers (26.9, +1.8), Canonical Fusion (30.2, +5.1), and DeepFuse (37.5, +12.4), but trails AdaFuse (19.5, −5.6) and Learnable Triangulation (20.8, −4.3). On TotalCapture, the generalization advantage is most striking: PoseIRM drops only 1.9 MPJPE from seen to unseen cameras (14.7→16.6), while MTF-Transformer drops 11.9 (24.7→36.6), MTF-Transformer+ drops 1.9 (25.1→27.0), and FLEX drops 76.0 (49.4→125.4). The camera-parameter-required methods (Canonical Fusion, MTF-Transformer+) show smaller unseen-camera drops (10.7 and 1.9 respectively) because they receive explicit camera parameters at test time, but PoseIRM approaches their robustness without any camera input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) paradigm, originally proposed by Arjovsky et al. (2019) for distributional shift robustness, to the domain of multi-view 3D human pose estimation for the first time. It departs from prior camera-parameter-free methods (HPCP, FLEX, MTF-Transformer, Probabilistic Triangulation) that implicitly or explicitly regress camera parameters but hardcode them during training, making them unable to adapt at inference. The key conceptual contribution is combining a lightweight 2D pose synthesis algorithm (projecting 3D GT poses through novel camera settings with realistic noise) with IRM's variance-penalized objective, which enforces a common optimal regression head across all camera environments and thus eliminates the need for large per-setting data volumes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: camera-parameter-free multi-view HPE methods suffer significant performance drops on unseen camera configurations, limiting their deployment in uncontrolled or dynamic-camera scenarios. The IRM-based training allows accurate inference on novel camera settings using only a handful of synthesized samples per setting, avoiding the computationally prohibitive cost of balanced multi-setting training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than four viewpoints, whether it transfers to other sensor modalities (e.g., IMU-fused), and whether the variance-penalized IRM relaxation is truly sufficient compared to the full bi-level formulation. The finding that IRM also improves seen-setting performance suggests broader utility as a regularizer for multi-environment training in pose estimation.
