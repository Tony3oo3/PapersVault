## Problem

While text-to-3D generation methods like DreamFusion and ProlificDreamer produce impressive results for common objects, they lack the structural constraints needed to generate consistent, high-quality 3D human avatars. The articulated complexity of the human body—multiple joints, self-occlusion in extreme poses, and the need for detailed facial features—remains an open challenge that existing diffusion-based 3D generation methods cannot adequately address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241404/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing the desired avatar appearance, along with SMPL shape parameters (β) and pose parameters (ξ) that define the target body shape and articulation. It produces a trainable NeRF encoding density, RGB color, and normal direction for the full 3D avatar, which can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints. Training requires only 10,000 iterations on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU, and the canonical pose is set to A-pose rather than the standard T-pose to better align with diffusion model priors.

## Method

DreamAvatar jointly optimizes a single trainable NeRF across two observation spaces—a canonical (A-pose) space and a posed space—connected by a learnable deformation field composed of inverse SMPL linear blend skinning plus an MLP corrective offset. The NeRF's density is initialized from SMPL-derived signed distances to the body surface in both spaces, providing a strong geometric prior that prevents the unstructured geometry artifacts seen in prior methods. Training uses Variational Score Distillation with Stable Diffusion v2.1, where LoRA-parameterized denoising networks receive camera parameters as additional conditioning. To address the multi-face 'Janus' problem and improve facial detail, the method simultaneously optimizes a zoomed-in head VSD loss using a landmark-based ControlNet and a learned <back-view> token, in addition to the full-body loss.

## Results

The paper does not report standard quantitative metrics (e.g., FID, PSNR, or CLIP scores) in a tabular format. Evaluation is primarily qualitative, supplemented by a user study in which 25 volunteers rated 20 examples across seven competing methods on geometry quality, texture quality, and text consistency on a 1–8 scale. The authors report that DreamAvatar achieves the best ranking in all three user-study dimensions (Fig. 9). Qualitative comparisons (Figs. 4–5) show DreamAvatar producing topologically correct geometry and higher-resolution texture than all baselines, particularly for extreme poses and detailed facial regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamAvatar is compared against eight baselines: DreamFusion, Latent-NeRF, Fantasia3D, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer, DreamWaltz, AvatarCLIP, and TADA!. The paper reports that DreamAvatar consistently outperforms all general-purpose text-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, Latent-NeRF, Fantasia3D, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer) in producing structurally correct human geometry, as those methods lack shape priors and often generate distorted or incomplete body structures. Against the avatar-specific baselines (AvatarCLIP, DreamWaltz, TADA!), DreamAvatar achieves substantially higher-resolution texture and geometry, with the user study confirming superior rankings in geometry quality, texture quality, and text consistency. The dual-observation-space design is specifically credited for maintaining quality under extreme poses where other methods degrade due to self-occlusion. No quantitative per-method scores are provided; the superiority claims rest on visual inspection and the user-study ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamAvatar extends the text-to-3D generation pipeline established by DreamFusion and ProlificDreamer (which use score distillation from pretrained diffusion models to optimize NeRFs) by introducing SMPL parametric shape priors and a dual-observation-space (DOS) design that jointly optimizes canonical and posed spaces via a learnable deformation field. It departs from prior avatar-specific methods (AvatarCLIP, TADA!, DreamWaltz) that either lack explicit shape/pose control or only optimize in the posed space, by leveraging the SMPL model to derive initial density fields and by exploiting the canonical space to reduce self-occlusion during texture synthesis. The zoomed-in head optimization with ControlNet and a learned back-view token builds on the authors' prior HeadSculpt work, extending it from reconstruction to generative avatar creation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DreamAvatar addresses a practical gap in 3D content creation: generating high-quality, pose-controllable human avatars from text without requiring multi-view captures or large 3D datasets, which is directly relevant to film, gaming, and AR/VR pipelines. The DOS design offers a generalizable principle for any articulated 3D generation task where posed observations suffer from self-occlusion. Key limitations include the absence of animation support and inherited biases from the pretrained diffusion model's training distribution, leaving open questions about temporal consistency, generalization to underrepresented subjects, and whether the per-prompt optimization (10k iterations on a single GPU) can be made more efficient.
