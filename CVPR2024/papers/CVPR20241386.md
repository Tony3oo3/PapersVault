## Problem

Existing generalized category discovery (GCD) methods require centralized access to all training data, which is infeasible in privacy-sensitive real-world scenarios where data is distributed across clients with non-overlapping label spaces. This paper introduces Federated GCD (Fed-GCD), a new task that combines the open-set challenge of discovering unknown categories with the severe data heterogeneity of federated learning, where clients may share no common labeled categories at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241386/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of 5 local clients holds a partially-labeled image subset (50% of instances from known classes are labeled; the rest are unlabeled and may include unknown categories), split via a Dirichlet distribution (β=0.2 or 0.05) to control heterogeneity. The server receives only model parameters and GMM statistics (means/covariances), never raw images. At test time, the trained global model assigns cluster labels to unlabeled server-side images, evaluated by clustering accuracy on All, Old, and New categories after estimating the number of clusters from labeled server data.

## Method

The AGCL framework couples a server-side Client Semantic Association (CSA) with a client-side Global-Local GMM Contrastive Learning (GCL). CSA samples feature-level instances from each component of uploaded local GMMs and re-clusters them via parameter-free FINCH to produce a global GMM that implicitly aligns shared categories and aggregates client-specific knowledge without accessing raw data. On each client, GCL performs class-level contrastive learning using a squared Mahalanobis distance derived from learnable GMMs (with diagonal covariance), replacing PCL's scalar temperature with dimension-wise variance control that makes the loss robust to mislabeled pseudo-samples. A maximum-likelihood regularization term prevents trivial solutions where variance grows unboundedly. The final client loss is a convex combination of instance-level contrastive loss, local GCL, and global GCL (weighted by γ=0.9), with local GMMs initialized by a semi-supervised FINCH extension that leverages labeled data to estimate cluster count and assign pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the generic datasets (NH setting, β=0.2), FedAvg+AGCL achieves 84.7% All / 85.5% Old / 84.6% New clustering accuracy on CIFAR-10, 56.1% All on CIFAR-100, and 74.8% All on ImageNet-100, consistently outperforming all FedAvg-based baselines. On the fine-grained CUB-200 under the extreme heterogeneity (EH, β=0.05) setting, FedAvg+AGCL reaches 53.1% All / 54.2% New, a 9.8-point gain over FedAvg+GCD (43.3% All) and 5.6 points over FedAvg+PCL (47.5% All). The centralized upper bound (Centralized-GCD) remains 1.9–8.2 points above the best federated method, confirming the inherent difficulty of the Fed-GCD task. FedProx+AGCL yields marginal additional gains (e.g., 83.0% vs 82.5% All on CIFAR-10 EH), showing that the AGCL framework is compatible with advanced FL aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAvg+AGCL consistently outperforms all three adapted GCD baselines (FedAvg+GCD, FedAvg+PCL, FedAvg+GPC) across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. On CIFAR-10 (NH), AGCL gains +4.0 points over GCD (84.7 vs 80.7 All) and +3.1 over PCL (84.7 vs 81.6). The advantage is most pronounced on fine-grained CUB-200 under EH: AGCL achieves 53.1% All vs 43.3% (GCD), 47.5% (PCL), and 45.3% (GPC), a 9.8-point margin over the weakest baseline. The partial variant FedAvg+GCL (local GCL only, no global GMM) already recovers much of the gap (e.g., 52.2% on CUB-200 EH), confirming that class-level GMM supervision is the primary driver, while the CSA global aggregation adds a further +0.9 points. FedProx+AGCL marginally surpasses FedAvg+AGCL in most settings (e.g., 83.0 vs 82.5 on CIFAR-10 EH), indicating the framework benefits from proximal regularization under high heterogeneity. All federated methods remain below the centralized upper bounds by 1–10 points, underscoring the residual cost of privacy-preserving decentralized training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends generalized category discovery (GCD) into a federated learning setting, building on FedAvg as the communication backbone and GCD's instance-level contrastive loss for local training. The key methodological departure is replacing PCL's scalar-temperature prototypical similarity with a full GMM-based Mahalanobis distance, enabling class-level contrastive learning that is robust to mislabeled pseudo-labels and avoids the memory-intensive instance buffer of PCL. Unlike GPC, which uses frozen semi-supervised GMMs only for estimating the number of unknown categories, AGCL learns GMMs end-to-end and additionally introduces a server-side Client Semantic Association (CSA) that aggregates heterogeneous local GMMs via category-agnostic sampling and re-clustering, a mechanism absent from prior federated or centralized GCD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fed-GCD is the first formalization of GCD under privacy-preserving federated constraints with heterogeneous, non-overlapping label spaces, opening a practically important research direction for decentralized category discovery (e.g., species monitoring across institutions). The 6-dataset benchmark with two Dirichlet-controlled heterogeneity levels provides a reusable evaluation protocol. However, the method still requires labeled data on the central server for class-number estimation, and the ablation reveals that the global GMM contributes only a modest boost over local GCL alone (γ=0.9 optimal), suggesting that the federated aggregation mechanism is currently more of a complementary signal than a primary driver—raising open questions about how to make server-side knowledge transfer more impactful as client count or heterogeneity grows.
