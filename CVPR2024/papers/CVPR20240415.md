## Problem

Relative pose estimation for unseen objects between a single reference and a query image remains challenging because existing hypothesis-based methods require scoring hundreds of thousands of discrete pose samples (computationally expensive and prone to unnaturally large errors), while image-matching methods fail under the large viewpoint gaps typical of single-reference scenarios. No prior approach computes the relative pose in a hypothesis-free, end-to-end manner that is both accurate and efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of cropped RGB images (reference and query) depicting the same previously unseen object from different viewpoints, with objects extracted via ground-truth bounding boxes. It outputs the relative object rotation ΔR ∈ SO(3) parameterized as a 6D continuous representation; translation is delegated to zero-shot object detectors. The network is trained on 41 CO3D categories and tested on 10 held-out categories, or on the GROP benchmark with 128 Objaverse and 5 LINEMOD objects excluded from training.

## Method

DVMNet lifts both input images to 3D voxel representations via an autoencoder: a 2D-3D encoder uses a pretrained ViT backbone with cross-attention modules to capture cross-view 3D information, and a 3D-2D decoder reconstructs masked object images to encourage object-aware voxel features. A score matrix of cosine similarities between query and reference voxels drives a differentiable soft assignment that aligns the two voxel sets. The aligned correspondences are fed into a Weighted Closest Voxel (WCV) algorithm that computes per-voxel-pair weights from replicated 2D object masks and learned 3D objectness maps, then solves a weighted least-squares rotation problem via SVD. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with an image reconstruction loss plus a 6D rotation loss, eliminating the need for pose hypotheses or voxel-wise annotations.

## Results

On CO3D (10 unseen categories), DVMNet achieves a mean angular error of 19.95°, outperforming the best hypothesis-based baseline 3DAHV (28.44°) by 8.49° and the best image-matching baseline SuperGlue (71.47°) by 51.52°. On the GROP benchmark, DVMNet reduces the mean angular error to 36.82° on LINEMOD (vs. 41.55° for 3DAHV) and 20.19° on Objaverse (vs. 28.11° for 3DAHV). In terms of efficiency, DVMNet processes a reference-query pair in 23 ms on an A100 GPU, faster than RelPose++ (29 ms) and 3DAHV (35 ms) despite the latter benefiting from parallel hypothesis scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVMNet outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated dataset and category. Against image-matching methods (SuperGlue, LoFTR, ZSP), the gains are dramatic: DVMNet's 19.95° mean angular error on CO3D is roughly 3–4× lower than these methods (71–87°), confirming that 2D-2D correspondences are unreliable under large viewpoint gaps. Among hypothesis-based methods, DVMNet beats 3DAHV (28.44°) by 8.49° and RelPose++ (32.82°) by 12.87° on CO3D, while also requiring fewer MACs since it avoids scoring hundreds of thousands of discrete pose samples. On the GROP benchmark, DVMNet leads 3DAHV by 7.92° on Objaverse (20.19° vs. 28.11°) and by 4.73° on LINEMOD (36.82° vs. 41.55°), though it is slightly worse than 3DAHV on the single LINEMOD object 'Dri.' (46.29° vs. 32.65°). The direct-regression baseline from RelPose++ (47.08°) is also clearly surpassed, validating the WCV-based least-squares formulation over end-to-end regression. In inference speed, DVMNet (23 ms) is faster than both RelPose++ (29 ms) and 3DAHV (35 ms) on an A100 GPU, despite the latter processing all hypotheses in parallel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant hypothesis-based paradigm for relative pose estimation (RelPose, RelPose++, 3DAHV), which approximates the continuous rotation space with up to 500,000 discrete samples and scores each one, by instead computing the pose in a single differentiable pass. It also moves beyond 2D image-matching methods (SuperGlue, LoFTR, ZSP) that the authors show are unreliable under large viewpoint gaps, by lifting both images into 3D voxel space via a cross-attention autoencoder and performing matching there. The Weighted Closest Voxel (WCV) algorithm draws on the point-cloud registration literature (Deep Closest Point, PointNetLK) but is adapted to handle noisy, image-derived voxels through learned objectness maps and 2D masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for pose hypotheses, DVMNet removes the primary computational bottleneck of prior relative-pose methods while simultaneously improving accuracy and robustness (fewer catastrophic failure cases), making single-reference pose estimation more practical for real-world deployment. The differentiable voxel-matching formulation also enables direct end-to-end supervision on the rotation target, a property unavailable to 2D matching pipelines that detach pose recovery as a post-processing step. Limitations include the restriction to rotation-only estimation (translation is delegated to external detectors), reliance on ground-truth bounding boxes for cropping, and evaluation limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks; the authors note future integration with zero-shot detectors for full 6D pose as an open direction.
