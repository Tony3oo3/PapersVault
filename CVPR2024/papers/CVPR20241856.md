## Problem

Transfer-based black-box adversarial attacks produce perturbations that overfit the source model and are sensitive to minor transformations, limiting their effectiveness against unknown architectures. The root cause is that deterministic gradient ascent from a single input cannot thoroughly explore the high-dimensional perturbation space, and existing random-restart strategies (e.g., PGD) yield homogeneous deviations that lack the diversity needed for robust transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241856/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (224×224 from ImageNet), its ground-truth label, and white-box access to one pre-trained source model, with perturbations constrained to an L∞ ball of radius ε. Rather than outputting a single adversarial example, ANDA/MultiANDA learns a Gaussian (or Gaussian mixture) perturbation distribution N(δ̄, σ) centered at the local optimum, from which an unlimited number of adversarial examples can be drawn for the same input. Each sampled perturbation is projected back into the ε-ball via clipping.

## Method

ANDA exploits the asymptotic normality of stochastic gradient ascent: by injecting random data augmentation (e.g., image translation) at each BIM iteration, the optimization becomes stochastic, and the gradient noise near the local optimum converges to a Gaussian stationary distribution. The algorithm tracks the running mean and covariance of all stochastic gradients along the trajectory to estimate this posterior over perturbations. MultiANDA extends this with a deep ensemble strategy—K independent ANDA runs from random initializations—producing a mixture of Gaussians that approximates Bayesian marginalization and captures multiple basins of attraction. This distributional formulation replaces the single-point search with a learned posterior, enabling diverse adversarial sampling and substantially improved transferability, particularly against defense-trained models.

## Results

On normally trained models (Table 1), MultiANDA achieves average transfer success rates of 79.6% (Inc-v3 source), 95.2% (ResNet-50 source), 87.7% (IncRes-v2 source), and 65.9% (VGG-19 source), consistently outperforming all 10 baselines. On defense models (Table 2), the advantage is even more pronounced: with ResNet-50 as source, MultiANDA reaches 50.1% average success rate versus 36.4% for the best baseline (VMI-FGSM), and with IncRes-v2 as source it reaches 41.8% versus 38.4%. When ResNet-50 is the source model, MultiANDA fools all six black-box target models on 863/1000 images, compared to 517/1000 for VMI-FGSM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiANDA outperforms all 10 baselines (BIM, TIM, SIM, DIM, FIA, TAIG, MI-FGSM, NI-FGSM, VMI-FGSM, VNI-FGSM) on every source model in both normally trained and defense model settings. The largest margins appear on defense models: with ResNet-50 as source, MultiANDA (50.1% avg) beats VMI-FGSM (36.4%) by 13.7 points and FIA (32.6%) by 17.5 points. On normally trained models with ResNet-50 as source, MultiANDA (95.2%) exceeds the next-best VMI-FGSM (78.4%) by 16.8 points. The closest competitor is FIA, which uses mid-layer feature importance; however, FIA degrades sharply when VGG-19 is the source (47.9% vs. MultiANDA's 65.9%), while MultiANDA maintains a consistent lead. The paper also notes that ResNet-50 as source consistently yields the best transferability across all methods, aligning with prior findings on skip connections enhancing black-box attack transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the SWAG framework (Maddox et al., 2019) and the asymptotic normality theory of SGD (Mandt et al., 2017) from model-parameter uncertainty estimation to adversarial perturbation modeling, treating the stochastic gradient ascent trajectory as samples from a posterior over perturbations rather than a path to a single optimum. The deep ensemble strategy (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) is then applied across multiple ANDA runs to approximate a Bayesian mixture-of-Gaussians posterior (MultiANDA), extending the unimodal SWAG approximation to a multimodal one. Conceptually, this departs from prior transfer-based attacks (BIM, MI-FGSM, VMI-FGSM, etc.) that seek a single deterministic perturbation, and from PGD's uniform random restarts or ADT's training-data-centric distribution learning, by explicitly learning a perturbation distribution centered at the local optimum for each input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to draw an unlimited number of adversarial examples from a learned per-input distribution opens a practical pathway for adversarial training that does not require re-optimizing from scratch for each new sample, potentially reducing the cost of robust model construction. The consistent and substantial gains over ten SOTA baselines—particularly on defense models where the gap widens (e.g., 50.1% vs. 36.4% average with ResNet-50 source)—suggest that distribution-based exploration of the perturbation space is a more effective strategy than point-estimation for transferability. Open questions include whether the local Gaussian approximation around a single basin of attraction can be made more global, whether the method extends to targeted attacks or non-image modalities, and whether the source-model dependence (ResNet-50 consistently outperforming others) can be mitigated.
