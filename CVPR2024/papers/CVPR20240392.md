## Problem

Existing 3D cloth generative models are either garment-specific (PCA on fixed templates) or trained entirely on synthetic data that produces overly smooth surfaces lacking realistic wrinkles and creases. Additionally, no large-scale real-world 3D cloth dataset existed to train garment-agnostic generative models, and prior conditioning mechanisms (UV maps, point clouds) were impractical for end users.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240392/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-drawn 2D binary visibility mask (constrained to the frontal hemisphere by rasterizing only vertices in front of the Z-axis plane) and SMPL shape parameters β as input, and produces a detailed 3D cloth mesh as output. The intermediate representation is an unsigned distance field (UDF) over a triplane grid, which is decoded to a discrete mesh via the differentiable MeshUDF algorithm. The mask is intentionally simple—a binary image—requiring no 3D expertise, while the shape conditioning allows the same style to be generated across different body morphologies.

## Method

Design2Cloth is a GAN built on a triplane implicit representation (inspired by EG3D) where a 2D CNN backbone outputs three plane feature images, and a single MLP decoder predicts the UDF at query points. The encoder concatenates a MobileNetV2-based mask encoder and a shape encoder to form the latent code z. The key architectural novelty is a dual-resolution discriminator: a low-frequency branch (PointNet++ on uniformly sampled surface points) captures overall garment structure, while a high-frequency branch (PointNet++ on points sampled from regions of maximum Gaussian mean curvature) explicitly enforces wrinkle and crease generation. Training combines UDF regression loss, adversarial loss from both discriminator branches, and a gradient-matching loss to regularize MeshUDF triangulation. The model is trained on the newly curated DigitalMe dataset of 2,010 real-world scans spanning 31 garment categories, and its full differentiability enables plug-and-play inverse problems such as single-image reconstruction via latent code optimization with IoU and prior losses.

## Results

On the CustomHumans dataset for single-image 3D garment reconstruction, Design2Cloth achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.12×10⁻², substantially outperforming SMPLicit (0.40×10⁻²), ClothWild (0.62×10⁻²), and DrapeNet (1.14×10⁻²). For generation quality on the DigitalMe dataset, the method reaches CD=0.12×10⁻² and Normal Consistency of 0.98, compared to DrapeNet's 0.56×10⁻² and 0.96. In a 50-participant user study, generated garments from Design2Cloth scored 7.2/10 in realism, surpassing DrapeNet (4.6/10) and even the Cloth3D ground truth (7.0/10), while over 70% of participants preferred Design2Cloth's in-the-wild reconstructions over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Design2Cloth outperforms all three baselines across every reported metric. Against DrapeNet (the closest competitor), it reduces CD by 4.7× on CustomHumans (0.12 vs 1.14×10⁻²) and by 4.7× on DigitalMe (0.12 vs 0.56×10⁻²), while also improving Normal Consistency from 0.96 to 0.98. Against SMPLicit, the CD gap is 3.3× (0.12 vs 0.40×10⁻²), and against ClothWild it is 5.2× (0.12 vs 0.62×10⁻²). In the human evaluation for in-the-wild reconstruction, over 70% of participants selected Design2Cloth as most similar to the input image, compared to fewer than 15% for ClothWild. The qualitative advantage is particularly pronounced in high-frequency detail: Design2Cloth captures wrinkles and creases that all three baselines render as overly smooth surfaces, a gap attributed to the dual-resolution discriminator and training on real-world scans rather than synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Design2Cloth extends the implicit-function-based garment generation line (SMPLicit, DrapeNet) by replacing their complex conditioning inputs (UV occupancy maps, 3D point clouds) with a simple 2D visibility mask, making the generation process accessible to non-experts. The triplane GAN backbone follows the efficient geometry-aware GAN paradigm, while the dual-resolution discriminator elevates the low/high-frequency separation concept from TailorNet into an adversarial setting. Crucially, the method departs from prior work's reliance on synthetic data by training on the newly curated DigitalMe dataset of 2,010 real-world scans, addressing the domain gap that causes overly smooth outputs in simulation-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DigitalMe dataset (2,010 subjects, 2,000+ garments, 31 categories) is the largest real-world 3D cloth collection to date and fills a critical gap that has constrained the field to synthetic data with unrealistic smoothness. The fully differentiable pipeline enables plug-and-play reconstruction from in-the-wild images and corrupted scans, opening practical applications in virtual try-on and digital avatar creation. Open questions include whether the single-layer, texture-free formulation can extend to multi-layer garments or appearance modeling, and whether the mask-based conditioning generalizes to garments with complex occlusions or accessories beyond the 31 categories seen in training.
