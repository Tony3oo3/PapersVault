## Problem

Existing LLM-based multimodal models (e.g., Video-LLaMA, VideoChat) process all video frames simultaneously, making them infeasible for long videos due to LLM context length limits (e.g., 2048 tokens for LLaMA) and GPU memory constraints. Naive workarounds like temporal average pooling lose explicit temporal modeling, while adding a separate video Q-Former increases complexity and still cannot scale to movie-length inputs. The paper addresses this gap by enabling sequential, online video processing with persistent memory of past content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241761/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Video frames are fed one at a time into a frozen ViT-G/14 encoder (EVA-CLIP), producing per-frame patch features of shape P×C with temporal position embeddings. A text question or prompt is appended to the LLM input. Crucially, only the Q-Former output at the final timestep (32 tokens) is passed to the frozen Vicuna-7B LLM, reducing the text token count from N×T to N regardless of video length. The model outputs free-form text: class labels for long-video understanding, answers for QA, captions, or action predictions for online anticipation.

## Method

MA-LMM augments the BLIP-2-style Q-Former with two long-term memory banks that accumulate information autoregressively as frames are processed sequentially. The visual memory bank stores raw frame features and serves as key/value in cross-attention, while the query memory bank stores the evolving learned queries and serves as key/value in self-attention, giving each Q-Former block access to the full temporal history. To prevent unbounded memory growth, a Memory Bank Compression (MBC) step triggers when the bank exceeds a fixed threshold M: at each spatial location, the pair of temporally adjacent tokens with the highest cosine similarity is identified and averaged into one token, consolidating redundant content while preserving discriminative features and temporal ordering. Only the Q-Former parameters are trained (initialized from InstructBLIP); the visual encoder and LLM remain frozen, and the memory bank can be plugged into existing multimodal LLMs off-the-shelf without retraining.

## Results

On the LVU long-term video understanding benchmark, MA-LMM achieves 63.0% average top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best S5 (59.2%) by 3.8%. On Breakfast and COIN, it reaches 93.0% and 93.2% top-1 accuracy respectively, improving over S5 by 2.3% and 2.4%. For video question answering, MA-LMM attains 48.5% on MSRVTT-QA and 60.6% on MSVD-QA (both SOTA), though it trails VideoCoCa on ActivityNet-QA (49.8% vs. 56.1%). On video captioning, it achieves CIDEr scores of 74.6 (MSRVTT), 179.1 (MSVD), and 131.2 (YouCook2), outperforming Video-LLaMA across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary LVU benchmark, MA-LMM outperforms all long-term video baselines: S5 (59.2% → 63.0%, +3.8%), ViS4mer (53.7%), Orthoformer (50.8%), and VideoBERT (45.6%), with the largest gains on Writer (+19.1% over S5) and Scene (+6.8% over S5). On Breakfast and COIN, it beats S5 by 2.3% and 2.4% respectively, and surpasses D-Sprv. (89.9%/90.0%) by over 3 points. For video QA, MA-LMM achieves new SOTA on MSRVTT-QA (48.5%) and MSVD-QA (60.6%), outperforming Video-LLaMA (46.5%/58.3%) and mPLUG-2 (48.0%/58.1%), but loses to VideoCoCa on ActivityNet-QA (49.8% vs. 56.1%), which the authors attribute to VideoCoCa's large-scale video-text pre-training. On video captioning, MA-LMM edges out Video-LLaMA on all three datasets (e.g., CIDEr 179.1 vs. 175.3 on MSVD) and matches or exceeds mPLUG-2 and GIT. On EpicKitchens-100 action anticipation, it surpasses Video-LLaMA in both accuracy (32.7% vs. 29.7% action top-5) and recall (12.2% vs. 11.7%), confirming its advantage in online sequential processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

MA-LMM builds directly on the BLIP-2/InstructBLIP Q-Former architecture, repurposing its cross-attention and self-attention layers by augmenting them with long-term memory banks that store past visual features and learned queries. It departs from prior LLM-based video models (Video-LLaMA, VideoChat, VideoChatGPT) that process all frames simultaneously and are constrained by LLM context length, instead adopting an autoregressive online processing strategy inspired by MemViT's memory-augmented design. The memory bank compression technique, which merges the most temporally redundant adjacent features, is a novel contribution not present in prior memory-augmented video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the memory bank—insertable into existing multimodal LLMs without architectural redesign—makes this a practically accessible solution to the context-length bottleneck that currently limits video understanding to short clips. The constant GPU memory footprint regardless of video length is a significant efficiency win for deploying video LMMs on real hardware. However, the paper only evaluates videos up to ~3 minutes, leaving open whether the memory bank compression heuristic scales gracefully to truly long-form content (movies, hours-long streams), and whether the frozen visual encoder becomes a bottleneck for fine-grained temporal reasoning over extended sequences.
