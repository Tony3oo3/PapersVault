## Problem

Existing video-language pre-training frameworks rely on patch-wise cross-attention or query-driven object tracking that fail to capture structured spatio-temporal action contexts, while language encoders treat sentences as flat token sequences without exploiting the verb-argument structure that directly corresponds to object trajectories in video. No prior method holistically integrates action-related knowledge from both modalities—tube-level temporal features in video and semantic role labeling in text—into a unified cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 uniformly sampled frames at 224×224 resolution from a video clip and a natural language sentence. The video encoder outputs global visual features V (196×768) and N tube features U (N×K×256) representing object trajectories; the text encoder outputs global text features T (196×768) and semantic phrase features S derived from SRL (VERB, ARG0, ARG1 groupings). The Cross Fusion Transformer produces two fused embedding sequences—M_G for global context and M_L for action-centric semantics—which are then used for retrieval ranking, VQA classification, or autoregressive caption generation depending on the downstream task.

## Method

SRTube introduces a DETR-inspired tube builder (pre-trained on AVA v2 for action recognition with bounding-box regression) that generates N tube features tracking object trajectories across K frames via spatial-temporal self-attention and cross-attention with global visual features. In parallel, an SRL builder (BERT-based) extracts semantic phrases by grouping VERB tokens with their ARG0/ARG1 noun phrases, providing structured action semantics from text. A Cross Fusion Transformer fuses these features through two parallel attention paths: Visual-to-Text Attention (VTA) aligns global features, while the novel Tube-to-SRL Attention (TSA) aligns tube features with semantic phrases for action-oriented cross-modal matching. Training uses five proxy tasks—standard MLM, VTM, VTC plus two novel objectives: Masked Action Modeling (MAM), which masks VERB tokens and predicts them from tube+SRL features, and Action Numbering Modeling (ANM), a self-supervised regression predicting the verb count to regularize temporal discrimination and avoid motion bias.

## Results

On text-to-video retrieval (fine-tuned), SRTube achieves R@1 of 46.3 on MSRVTT, 27.7 on LSMDC, and 54.9 on DiDeMo, setting new state-of-the-art on MSRVTT and LSMDC. In zero-shot retrieval, SRTube leads all baselines across all three datasets, reaching R@1 of 29.1 (MSRVTT), 16.4 (LSMDC), and 34.4 (DiDeMo). On VQA, SRTube attains 44.1% (MSRVTT), 46.0% (MSVD), and 79.6% (TVQA) accuracy. For video captioning, SRTube achieves a CIDEr score of 61.8 on MSRVTT and 142.5 on MSVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRTube outperforms the closest baseline VindLU [12] by 2.5 points on MSRVTT R@1 (46.3 vs. 43.8) and by a large margin on LSMDC R@1 (27.7 vs. not reported, but 27.7 vs. 21.0 for TW-BERT). On DiDeMo, SRTube edges out VindLU on R@1 (54.9 vs. 54.6) but trails slightly on R@5 (82.1 vs. 82.3) and R@10 (87.2 vs. 88.2), which the authors attribute to DiDeMo's long, multi-scene videos. Compared to object-centric methods OA-Trans [44] and TW-BERT [49], SRTube improves MSRVTT R@1 by approximately 10.5% and 7.9% respectively, while using the same 5.5M pre-training pairs. On ActivityNet Caption, SRTube surpasses HiTeA [50] (46.5 vs. 45.1 R@1) and X-CLIP [36] (46.5 vs. 44.3 R@1) despite using far fewer pre-training pairs (5.5M vs. 400M for X-CLIP). In VQA, SRTube leads all baselines on all three datasets (MSRVTT 44.1, MSVD 46.0, TVQA 79.6), and in video captioning it achieves the best CIDEr on MSRVTT (61.8) but is marginally behind LAVENDER [30] on MSVD (142.5 vs. 142.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

SRTube builds directly on VindLU [12] as its architectural baseline, reusing the same BEiT-based video encoder, BERT text encoder, and cross-fusion transformer, while adding a tube builder (inspired by DETR-style detection in TubeR [56]) and an SRL-based semantic phrase builder. It departs from prior object-centric video transformers (OA-Trans [44], STOA-VLP [57]) that relied on pre-extracted object trajectories or anchor-frame sampling, instead learning tube features end-to-end with spatial-temporal self-attention. The key conceptual departure is the holistic pairing of video tube features with SRL-derived semantic phrases for action-centric cross-modal alignment, whereas prior work used scene graphs [13, 51], PoS tagging [17], or trajectory-word alignment [49] in isolation without explicitly linking verb-argument structure to object trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The action-centric framing—explicitly aligning object trajectories with verb-argument semantic roles—offers a principled alternative to the prevailing patch-wise cross-attention paradigm in VidLP, and the zero-shot retrieval gains (up to 5.2% R@1 over BridgeFormer) suggest the learned alignment generalizes well without task-specific fine-tuning. However, the paper acknowledges that tube features assume continuous scenes, which limits performance on datasets with diverse scene changes (DiDeMo), and future work on adaptive frame selection or handling non-continuous clips remains open. The moderate but consistent improvements across retrieval, VQA, and captioning tasks position SRTube as a useful reference point for action-aware VidLP, though the gains over VindLU are incremental rather than transformative.
