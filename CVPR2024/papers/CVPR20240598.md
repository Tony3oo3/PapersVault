## Problem

Existing grounded MLLMs capture language-to-object grounding via bounding boxes as sequences of location tokens, which cannot represent amorphous stuff, semantic parts, irregular shapes, or groups of instances simultaneously. This box-based paradigm also lacks interpretability: when the model hallucinates, it is impossible to diagnose whether the failure stems from missed detection or incorrect language-object alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image, a text prompt that may contain spatial pointer tokens (<PTR>) resolved via SAM into binary masks, and a set of class-agnostic entity mask proposals from a plug-in mask proposal network (default: Mask2Former+). Output is a grounded text response where groundable phrases are delimited by <GRD>...</GRD> tokens; each phrase's grounding query is scored against all entity masks, and the top-scoring masks are merged via pixel-wise maximization into a single segmentation mask. The decoupled design allows the mask proposal network to be swapped independently (SAM, domain-specific segmenters, user-provided masks) without retraining the MLLM.

## Method

GROUNDHOG decouples grounding into entity mask proposal and language-guided mask selection. A masked feature extractor pools CLIP and DINOv2 patch features through a convolutional mask pooling layer conditioned solely on binary masks (no embeddings from the proposal model), producing visual entity tokens aligned to the MLLM's input space via an MLP. The MLLM backbone (LLaMA2-7B initialized from LLaVA-1.5) processes these tokens alongside text; grounding queries are formed by summing the last-layer output embeddings of <GRD> and </GRD> tokens, then scored against each entity token via an MLP to retrieve relevant masks. The default mask proposal network, Mask2Former+, extends Mask2Former with 50 expert queries each for semantic parts and visual text (on top of 200 entity queries) and is trained on merged annotations from COCO, LVIS, Entity-v2, Pascal, PACO, MHP-v2, and TextOCR. Training uses the curated M3G2 dataset (2.5M text-image pairs, 36 sub-problems from 27 datasets) spanning four task types—grounded captioning, referring expression segmentation, grounded VQA, and referential dialogue—enabling a single set of weights to handle all grounding tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On the seven Referring Expression Segmentation benchmarks, GROUNDHOG (7B) achieves 78.5/79.9/75.7 cIoU on RefCOCO val/test-A/test-B, 70.5/75.0/64.9 on RefCOCO+, and 66.7 on gRefCOCO, outperforming the generalist LISA 7B across all splits and specialist models on multi-instance and reasoning tasks (54.5 PhraseCut, 56.2 ReasonSeg). On grounded captioning (Flickr30K-Entity), it reaches 91.3 CIDEr and 32.1 F1-all, surpassing Ferret-13B (76.1 CIDEr, 15.1 F1-all). On the POPE hallucination benchmark, GROUNDHOG achieves 91.03/90.13/86.33 accuracy on Random/Popular/Adversarial splits, a 5.2-point improvement over Ferret-13B on Popular. On TextVQA-X, it reaches 39.8 mIoU versus 29.0 for the specialist SAB model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROUNDHOG outperforms the generalist LISA 7B (and its fine-tuned variant) across all seven RES benchmarks, with the largest gains on multi-instance (gRefCOCO: 66.7 vs. –) and reasoning tasks (ReasonSeg: 56.2 vs. 52.9 for LISA-FT; RIO test-c: 57.9 vs. –). Against specialist models, GROUNDHOG is competitive on single-instance RefCOCO (78.5 val vs. UNINEXT-H's 82.2) but substantially surpasses them on complex queries: PhraseCut 54.5 vs. MDETR's 53.7, and ReasonSeg 56.2 vs. PolyFormer's 48.8. On phrase grounding (Flickr30K-Entity), GROUNDHOG (79.2 R@1 val) beats Shikra-7B (75.8) and Shikra-13B (77.4) but trails Ferret-7B (80.4) by ~1 point. On grounded captioning, GROUNDHOG's 91.3 CIDEr and 32.1 F1-all dramatically exceed Ferret-13B (76.1/15.1) and Shikra-13B (73.9/–). On object hallucination (POPE), GROUNDHOG leads all baselines on all three splits, with a 5.2-point accuracy gap over Ferret-13B on Popular (90.13 vs. 84.90) and 4.0 points on Adversarial (86.33 vs. 82.36). On spatial prompt QA, GROUNDHOG (72.4 PointQA, 85.5 V7W) edges out Shikra-13B (70.3/85.3) and GPT4RoI-13B (–/84.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

GROUNDHOG departs from the dominant paradigm in grounded MLLMs (Shikra, Ferret, Kosmos-2, GPT4RoI) that represent grounded objects as bounding-box coordinate tokens in a causal language modeling sequence, instead grounding phrases to pixel-level segmentation masks via a decoupled mask-proposal-and-retrieval mechanism. Architecturally, it builds on Mask2Former (Cheng et al., CVPR 2022) as the mask proposal backbone, upgraded to Mask2Former+ with additional expert queries for semantic parts and visual text, and on LLaVA-1.5 (Liu et al., 2023) as the MLLM backbone. Conceptually, it unifies the referring expression segmentation literature (RefCOCO series, LISA) with grounded captioning, VQA, and referential dialogue under a single instruction-tuning framework, whereas prior work typically addressed these as separate tasks requiring task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from box-level to pixel-level grounding in MLLMs addresses a fundamental limitation: bounding boxes cannot represent amorphous stuff, semantic parts, irregular shapes, or multi-instance groups without including irrelevant semantics. The decoupled design (mask proposal model + MLLM retrieval) provides genuine diagnosability—users can inspect which mask proposals were scored and why a grounding failed—something box-coordinate models cannot offer. The M3G2 dataset (2.5M pairs, 36 sub-problems) is a substantial resource for grounded instruction tuning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LLM backbones, how inference latency compares to box-based methods given the mask retrieval step, and whether the plug-and-play mask proposal design holds up in truly open-world, out-of-distribution settings beyond the evaluated benchmarks.
