## Problem

Prior amodal segmentation and completion methods are restricted to closed-world settings, operating only on the specific datasets and object classes they were trained on, and cannot generalize to novel or physically implausible occlusions (e.g., art, illusions). This paper asks whether the whole-object priors implicitly learned by internet-scale diffusion models can be reconfigured to perform zero-shot amodal completion that generalizes beyond any training distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241163/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a point or mask prompt identifying the visible (modal) region of an occluded object, and produces a synthesized RGB image of the complete object with the occluder removed. From this amodal completion image, downstream tasks are derived: segmentation masks via thresholding with majority vote over multiple diffusion samples, object class labels via CLIP zero-shot classification, and textured 3D meshes via SyncDreamer with score distillation sampling. The conditioning is dual-stream: a CLIP image embedding provides high-level semantic context while VAE embeddings of the input image and prompt provide low-level pixel details (color, texture, shade).

## Method

The core approach fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion latent diffusion model on a synthetically curated dataset of 837K occluded/whole object pairs, constructed by using SAM to segment objects in SA-1B, MiDaS monocular depth to select whole objects (those closer to camera than neighbors), and then superimposing occluders to create paired training data. The diffusion model is conditioned in two separate streams: CLIP cross-attention on the semantic features of the prompted region for high-level perception, and VAE channel concatenation of the input image embedding and prompt embedding for low-level visual fidelity, following the design of InstructPix2Pix and Zero-1-to-3. Classifier-free guidance is applied by randomly dropping conditioning to a null vector during training. The key insight is that large-scale pretrained diffusion models already encode rich whole-object representations from their internet-scale training, and a relatively small fine-tuning on synthetic occlusion pairs is sufficient to reconfigure these priors for explicit amodal grouping and completion, achieving zero-shot generalization that surpasses supervised baselines on closed-world benchmarks.

## Results

On Amodal COCO, pix2gestalt achieves 82.87 mIoU in a zero-shot setting, outperforming the supervised PCNet baseline (81.35) and even PCNet-Sup (82.53), which is trained with ground-truth amodal masks. On Amodal BSDS, it reaches 80.76 mIoU, surpassing SAM (65.25) and SD-XL Inpainting (74.19) by 15.5 and 6.6 points respectively. Sampling three completions and taking the best ('Best of 3') further improves results to 87.10 mIoU on COCO-A and 85.68 on BSDS-A. For occluded object recognition, Ours+CLIP achieves 43.39% Top-1 on Occluded COCO and 31.15% on Separated COCO, versus 23.33% and 26.04% for CLIP alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pix2gestalt outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated task. On amodal segmentation, it beats the supervised PCNet [51] (81.35 mIoU) by 1.5 points despite being zero-shot, and surpasses the stronger PCNet-Sup variant (82.53) by 0.34 points. Among zero-shot methods, it improves over SAM [20] by 15.7 points on COCO-A and 15.5 on BSDS-A, and over SD-XL Inpainting [31] by 6.4 and 6.6 points respectively, demonstrating that fine-tuning on synthetic occlusion pairs is critical beyond internet-scale pre-training alone. For occluded recognition, Ours+CLIP [34] lifts Top-1 accuracy from 23.33% to 43.39% on Occluded COCO and from 26.04% to 31.15% on the harder Separated COCO, while the visual-prompting baseline CLIP+RC [40] actually degrades performance on Separated COCO (25.64%). For 3D reconstruction, using pix2gestalt as a drop-in module to SyncDreamer [26] reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.109 to 0.076 and increases Volumetric IoU from 0.192 to 0.321, outperforming even the ground-truth-mask variant of SyncDreamer (CD 0.1084, IoU 0.1027).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the closed-world amodal segmentation line (PCNet, KINS, WALT) by repurposing internet-scale latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) as a generative prior for whole-object synthesis, fine-tuned on a synthetic occlusion dataset rather than real amodal annotations. Conceptually, it follows the analysis-by-synthesis paradigm (Tu et al., 2005) but instantiates it with modern diffusion architectures and classifier-free guidance, conditioning on both CLIP semantic features and VAE pixel-level details in separate streams. The training data construction—overlaying objects from SA-1B onto natural images using MiDaS depth heuristics—repurposes off-the-shelf segmentation and depth tools to create 837K paired examples without any manual amodal annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a drop-in module that improves occluded object recognition, amodal segmentation, and 3D reconstruction without modifying downstream models, making it broadly applicable. The paper acknowledges limitations in commonsense and physical reasoning (e.g., a car facing the wrong direction, a hand not holding a box), suggesting that diffusion priors capture appearance but not causal structure. Open questions include whether the synthetic training distribution covers sufficiently diverse occlusion geometries for video or multi-object scenes, and whether the inherent ambiguity (multiple plausible completions) can be resolved with additional cues like depth or motion.
