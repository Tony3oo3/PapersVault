## Problem

Existing spike- and event-guided motion deblurring methods assume perfect spatial co-alignment and temporal synchronization between the neuromorphic sensor and the RGB camera, which is unrealistic in practice due to calibration errors, vibration-induced displacements, and the inherent difficulty of synchronizing a continuous 20 kHz spike stream with discrete image frames. This paper is the first to tackle spike-guided deblurring under unknown spatiotemporal alignment, where both spatial offsets (up to 10%) and temporal misalignment (up to 25% of the exposure window) are present and uncalibrated.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurred RGB frame and K randomly sampled segments of a 20 kHz spike stream (each segment covering a short window around the image's exposure time) as input. The spike segments are intentionally offset in both space and time relative to the image, and the spike data is a binary event tensor of shape H_S × W_S × C (C = T/50μs readout cycles) at a lower spatial resolution than the RGB frame. The output is a single deblurred RGB image at the original resolution, trained and evaluated on X4K1000FPS (1/120 s exposure) and REDS (1/24 s exposure) datasets.

## Method

UaSDN is a three-stage progressive network. Stage 1 applies a halved-channel NAFNet to perform basic deblurring, enhancing the blurred image's texture so that subsequent cross-modal alignment becomes feasible. Stage 2 introduces a Bi-directional Deformable Modal Aligning (BiDMA) module that learns learnable offsets via deformable convolutions in both directions (image→spike and spike→image) across multiple scales, while a 4-layer shallow network (M_S2I) converts each spike segment into a rough grayscale intensity estimate; K independently aligned spike segments are fused to produce an enhanced intermediate image. Stage 3 computes optical flow (via a pretrained RAFT network) between each spike-estimated grayscale image and the Stage-2 output, warps the spike features accordingly, and fuses them through a U-shaped decoder built from Attention-based Dense Blocks (channel attention + residual dense blocks) to produce the final deblurred image. The three stages are trained sequentially with L1 loss, enabling the model to progressively incorporate spike texture information despite unknown alignment.

## Results

On X4K1000FPS, UaSDN achieves 35.78 dB PSNR and 0.964 SSIM under unknown spatiotemporal alignment, outperforming all five baselines by 1.31–2.50 dB in PSNR. On REDS, the method reaches 33.61 dB PSNR and 0.942 SSIM, surpassing the strongest baseline (REFID) by 2.38 dB. Under precise alignment (ablation), UaSDN further improves to 36.92 dB on X4K1000FPS, indicating substantial headroom when alignment is known.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UaSDN outperforms all five baselines on both datasets. Against the two image-only methods, it gains +2.04 dB over HINet and +1.65 dB over NAFNet on X4K1000FPS, confirming that spike guidance adds substantial value beyond pure image restoration. Among the three spike/event-guided baselines, the margin over EFNet is the largest (+2.50 dB), while the gap over SpkDeblurNet (the closest competitor at +1.31 dB) and REFID (+1.56 dB) is still significant. On REDS, the advantages widen: UaSDN beats NAFNet by 2.88 dB, REFID by 2.38 dB, and SpkDeblurNet by 2.45 dB, suggesting the method generalizes well across datasets with different blur characteristics. Notably, EFNet performs worse than the image-only baselines on X4K1000FPS (33.28 vs. 34.13 dB), indicating that its cross-modal attention design is particularly sensitive to the unknown alignment condition that UaSDN is explicitly designed to handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of spike-camera research (spike-to-image reconstruction, spike-guided video interpolation) and event-based motion deblurring (EFNet, REFID), but departs from both in a key way: prior event-based deblurring methods assume perfect spatiotemporal co-registration between the two sensors, while this work explicitly tackles unknown spatial offsets and temporal misalignment. Architecturally, it repurposes NAFNet as a basic deblurring backbone, introduces a bi-directional deformable convolution module (BiDMA) for learned cross-modal alignment, and leverages a pretrained RAFT optical-flow estimator for precise feature warping in a multi-scale fusion stage. The three-stage progressive design—basic deblur, deformable alignment, flow-guided fusion—is a novel composition of these existing components rather than a departure from a single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real hybrid spike-RGB camera rigs will never achieve the pixel-perfect, time-synchronized alignment assumed by prior work, so a method that tolerates unknown misalignment is essential for deployment. However, a significant limitation is that all training and most evaluation use *simulated* spike streams generated from high-frame-rate video via EMA-VFI, with only a small set of real-world qualitative examples; the generalization to genuine spike-camera noise characteristics and hardware-specific artifacts remains untested. Open questions include how performance degrades under more extreme misalignment (beyond the 10% spatial / 25% temporal offsets tested), whether the approach extends to event cameras or other neuromorphic sensors, and whether the simulated-spike training gap affects real-system performance.
