## Problem

Vanilla text-to-image diffusion models frequently produce human images with anatomical inaccuracies such as unnatural postures and disproportionate limbs. Existing remedies either require additional conditions (e.g., pose maps via ControlNet) at inference, limiting end-user convenience, or fine-tune the full model (e.g., LoRA), risking catastrophic forgetting of the model's original generative expressiveness. The paper asks whether human-centric prior knowledge can be absorbed entirely during fine-tuning so that inference requires only a text prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method accepts only a free-form text prompt and outputs a human image, with no pose, depth, or other auxiliary conditions. During training, key pose images (or depth images) at 256×256 resolution serve as the human-centric prior, and a pre-trained entity-relation network extracts human-centric tokens from the prompt to restrict the alignment loss to relevant attention indices. The HcP layer is a plug-and-play module (three 1024-dim MLP blocks) that can be attached to any cross-attention layer in a frozen U-Net, demonstrated on both Stable Diffusion v1-5 and SDXL-base.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Human-centric Prior (HcP) layer that injects an additional key K_h into the cross-attention computation, derived from text embeddings via MLPs, producing a human-centric attention map M_h that is blended with the original attention map via a fixed weight γ (0.9). A Human-centric Alignment Loss minimizes cosine distance between M_h (at human-centric token indices) and ResNet-50 features extracted from key pose images, thereby encoding pose structure into the attention maps without altering the frozen U-Net. Guided by an empirical analysis showing that early timesteps and specific U-Net resolution scales (down, mid, up) govern structural formation while later steps refine details, the training applies cosine-shaped loss weights λ^l(t) that shift emphasis across timesteps per scale stage. The overall objective combines this scale- and step-aware alignment loss with the standard denoising loss, and only the HcP layer parameters are updated, preserving the base model's generative diversity.

## Results

On the Human-Art validation set, HcP achieves FID 28.71, KID 5.62, and CLIP-Score 32.72, outperforming both vanilla SD (FID 33.31, KID 9.38, CLIP-Score 31.85) and LoRA fine-tuning (FID 29.22, KID 5.83, CLIP-Score 31.91). In human evaluation (5-point Likert scale), HcP shows the largest gains on complex action categories: Acrobatics AQ improves from 1.6 (SD) to 2.7, Dance AQ from 2.0 to 3.5, and Drama AQ from 2.0 to 3.2, while maintaining competitive scores on simpler categories like Cosplay and Movie.

| SD | 33.31 | 9.38 | 31.85 |
| + LoRA | 29.22 | 5.83 | 31.91 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

HcP outperforms vanilla SD on all three quantitative metrics, with the most notable improvement in KID (5.62 vs. 9.38, a 40% reduction) and CLIP-Score (32.72 vs. 31.85). Against LoRA fine-tuning, HcP achieves better FID (28.71 vs. 29.22), KID (5.62 vs. 5.83), and a substantially higher CLIP-Score (32.72 vs. 31.91), indicating superior text-image alignment without the catastrophic forgetting that LoRA introduces. In qualitative comparison with ControlNet-OpenPose, simply plugging the HcP layer (trained only on SD) into ControlNet further improves both structural accuracy and consistency beyond what ControlNet alone achieves. On SDXL-base, HcP addresses residual structural issues (e.g., disproportionate legs, extra limbs) that the larger model still exhibits, confirming the method's scalability. In human evaluation, HcP's advantage is most pronounced on complex action domains (Acrobatics, Dance, Drama) where SD and LoRA scores are lowest, while on simpler domains (Cosplay, Movie) all methods perform comparably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on improving human image generation in diffusion models: condition-based control (ControlNet, HumanSD) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA). It departs from both by proposing to inject human-centric priors (pose images) exclusively during training via a plug-and-play HcP layer that modifies cross-attention maps, thereby eliminating the need for auxiliary inputs at inference. The cross-attention analysis builds directly on Hertz et al.'s Prompt-to-Prompt framework [14], extending the observation that text-token attention maps govern structural content to a systematic study of how different U-Net scales and diffusion timesteps contribute to human anatomy formation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a text-only pipeline for human image generation without pose/depth inputs at inference lowers the barrier for end users and preserves the generative diversity of the base model, which LoRA-style fine-tuning tends to erode. The plug-and-play compatibility with ControlNet and the demonstrated transfer to SDXL-base suggest broad applicability. However, the method is validated primarily on SD v1.5 and SDXL-base with a single prior type (pose) and a fixed ResNet-50 extractor; open questions include whether multiple prior types (pose + depth + segmentation) can be jointly integrated, whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion, and whether the scale-aware cosine weighting strategy transfers to architectures with different U-Net depth or resolution schedules.
