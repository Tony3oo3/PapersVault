## Problem

Existing portrait relighting methods face a trilemma: they are either restricted to the input viewpoint, produce flickering artifacts when naively applied frame-by-frame to video, or require prohibitive training/inference times (e.g., ReliTalk needs 3 days of training and 0.2 s per frame). No prior method achieves real-time, 3D-aware relighting of in-the-wild talking-face videos with both novel-view and novel-lighting control simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241220/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a portrait video (RGB frames with an overlaid coordinate map forming a 5-channel input), a target lighting condition encoded as second-order spherical-harmonic coefficients, and a desired camera pose. It outputs 512×512 relighted RGB frames, along with disentangled albedo, shading, and depth maps, at 32.98 fps on a single RTX 4090 GPU. The intermediate representation is a pair of tri-planes (albedo and shading) per frame, which are volumetrically rendered from any viewing angle.

## Method

The core architecture distills the NeRFFaceLighting generator into two feed-forward encoders: a ViT-based Albedo Encoder (using DeepLabV3 for global context and a CNN for high-frequency details) that predicts an albedo tri-plane, and a CNN+StyleGAN Shading Encoder conditioned on the albedo tri-plane and SH lighting coefficients to predict a spatially aligned shading tri-plane. A Temporal Consistency Network composed of two transformers (C_A, C_S) with cross-attention between the albedo and shading branches ingests tri-planes from n consecutive frames and outputs residual tri-planes that suppress flickering. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (albedo-only → independent branches → joint) followed by separate training of the temporal network on synthetic data with interpolated camera paths and injected noise. The final loss combines L1, LPIPS, identity (ArcFace), and adversarial terms for the encoders, plus short-term and long-term optical-flow-warped temporal losses for the consistency network.

## Results

On the INSTA video dataset, the method achieves 32.98 fps (0.03 s/frame) on a single RTX 4090, the best identity preservation (ID = 0.5396), the lowest warping error (WE = 0.0003), and the lowest inter-frame LPIPS (0.0159) among all compared baselines. It attains the second-lowest lighting error (LE = 0.7710) and lighting instability (LI = 0.2533), trailing only the single-frame B-E4E encoder. On the FFHQ input-view relighting benchmark, the method achieves the best FID (45.08) and highest identity score (0.7711) across six competing relighting methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against B-DPR (PTI + DPR) and B-SMFR (PTI + SMFR), the proposed method wins on every metric, reducing per-frame time from 200 s to 0.03 s (~6,700× speedup) while improving identity preservation (0.5396 vs. 0.5222 / 0.4479) and temporal consistency (LPIPS 0.0159 vs. 0.1015 / 0.0626). Compared to B-E4E (the single-frame NeRFFaceLighting encoder), the method trades a small increase in lighting error (0.7710 vs. 0.6384) and instability (0.2533 vs. 0.1963) for substantially better identity preservation (0.5396 vs. 0.2892), warping error (0.0003 vs. 0.0007), and a 6.7× speedup (0.03 s vs. 0.2 s). Versus B-PTI (PTI-finetuned NeRFFaceLighting), the method dominates on all six metrics, cutting inference time from 30 s to 0.03 s while improving LE, LI, ID, WE, and LPIPS. On the input-view video relighting benchmark (Table 4), the method achieves the lowest lighting instability (0.2841) and fastest inference (0.03 s), with lighting error (0.7816) only marginally above DPR (0.7600) and well below SMFR (1.1381) and ReliTalk (1.2012). On the FFHQ single-image relighting benchmark (Table 3), the method attains the best FID (45.08) and ID (0.7711), outperforming Lumos (FID 55.18, ID 0.7374), SMFR (FID 51.16, ID 0.6285), TR (FID 55.30), NVPR (FID 65.23), and SIPR-W (FID 87.39).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper distills the disentanglement framework of NeRFFaceLighting [20] (albedo + shading tri-planes conditioned on spherical-harmonic lighting) into a feedforward dual-encoder architecture, extending the LP3D [44] tri-plane encoder to produce two separate tri-planes rather than one. It departs from prior 3D-aware relighting methods (NeRFFaceLighting, FaceLit) that operate on single images by introducing a temporal consistency network with cross-attention between albedo and shading branches, and from prior video relighting methods (ReliTalk, NVPR) that are either slow or not 3D-aware. The EG3D tri-plane representation [5] provides the underlying 3D structure, while the GAN inversion literature (PTI, StyleGAN encoders) informs the encoder design but is explicitly avoided in favor of pure feedforward inference for real-time speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve real-time (~33 fps on a single RTX 4090) 3D-aware portrait video relighting with novel view synthesis, which is a prerequisite for interactive AR/VR applications where users need to adjust lighting and viewpoint on-the-fly. The paper acknowledges two key limitations: inability to model specular reflections (e.g., eyeglasses glare) and the entanglement of motion and identity, which prevents video-driven animation. Open questions include whether the temporal consistency network generalizes to longer sequences or more dynamic expressions, and whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes, occlusions, or full-body relighting.
