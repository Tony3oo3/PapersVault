## Problem

Existing VINS algorithms face a fundamental trade-off: optimization-based methods (e.g., BASALT, DM-VIO) achieve high accuracy by jointly estimating poses and landmarks but incur high computational cost, while filter-based methods (e.g., MSCKF, OpenVINS) are efficient but sacrifice accuracy by not jointly optimizing landmarks with camera poses. No prior method simultaneously delivers high-precision 6-DOF localization and low computational complexity suitable for resource-constrained devices.

## Input / Output

The system consumes stereo camera images and IMU measurements, using FAST corner features and a depth-filter for landmark initialization. It maintains a local map of up to 10 keyframes (FIFO) and a sliding window of 2 old keyframes plus 2 latest temporal frames for the EKF back-end. The output is a 6-DOF pose estimate (quaternion + position) for each frame in the sliding window, along with 3D landmark positions estimated via a per-landmark EKF update, all produced in real time with ~18 ms average CPU overhead.

## Method

SchurVINS formulates a full residual model over all triangulated landmarks in the sliding window, explicitly constructing the gradient, Hessian, and observation covariance (Eqs. 20–23). It then applies the Schur complement to decompose this high-dimensional system into two independent sub-problems: an ego-motion residual model (Eq. 30) for the sliding-window poses and a landmark residual model (Eq. 32) for 3D landmark positions. Because the landmark Hessian is block-diagonal, the landmark model further splits into m independent per-landmark EKF updates (Eq. 40), each operating on a small 3×3 system. The ego-motion update uses the standard EKF gain with the Schur-complemented Hessian as the effective observation model, while each landmark is updated sequentially with its own small gain. Built on the SVO2.0 frontend (feature alignment, depth filter), this replaces both the original GN-based landmark optimizer and the sparse image alignment module, yielding near-optimization-level accuracy at filter-level computational cost.

## Results

On the EuRoC dataset, SchurVINS achieves an average APE RMSE of 0.075 m, the best among all filter-based methods and competitive with top optimization-based methods (BASALT: 0.072 m, DM-VIO: 0.064 m). On TUM-VI, SchurVINS obtains 0.182 m average RMSE, outperforming OpenVINS (0.314 m) and ROVIO (0.471 m) while remaining close to DM-VIO (0.141 m). In terms of efficiency, SchurVINS uses only 18% average CPU compared to 89% for SVO2.0, 98% for DM-VIO, and 46% for BASALT, while its optimizeStructure module runs ~3× faster than the GN-based alternative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SchurVINS (0.075 m avg APE RMSE) outperforms all filter-based baselines on EuRoC: OpenVINS (0.096 m, +28% worse), OpenVINS-4 (0.114 m, +52% worse), ROVIO (0.232 m, +209% worse), and MSCKF (0.342 m, +356% worse). Among optimization-based methods, SchurVINS beats OKVIS (0.225 m), VINS-Mono (0.174 m), Kimera (0.134 m), ICE-BA (0.112 m), and SVO2.0 (0.109 m), but is slightly outperformed by BASALT (0.072 m, −4%) and DM-VIO (0.064 m, −15%). On TUM-VI, SchurVINS (0.182 m) beats OpenVINS (0.314 m) and ROVIO (0.471 m) but trails DM-VIO (0.141 m) and BASALT (0.198 m is close). In efficiency, SchurVINS uses 18% avg CPU vs. 37% for OpenVINS, 45% for VINS-Mono, 46% for BASALT, and 98% for DM-VIO, while its total per-frame runtime (3.83 ms) is lower than SVO2.0 (9.28 ms), OpenVINS (10.91 ms), and SMSCKF (8.53 ms). The ablation confirms that removing the EKF-based landmark solver (SV-OFF) degrades accuracy to 0.107 m avg, validating the joint pose-landmark estimation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SchurVINS is built directly on the SVO2.0 frontend (Forster et al., 2017) and the error-state EKF propagation framework of Sola (2017), replacing SVO2.0's optimization-based backend with a sliding-window EKF. Its key conceptual departure is from the MSCKF family (Geneva et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2018), which uses left-nullspace projection to simplify residuals at the cost of discarding landmark information, and from optimization-based VIO (BASALT, DM-VIO), which achieves high accuracy through full bundle adjustment but at high computational cost. The paper's central idea—applying Schur complement (borrowed from bundle adjustment, Sibley et al., 2010) inside the EKF update to decompose the full pose-landmark residual into independent pose and per-landmark sub-problems—bridges the accuracy-efficiency gap that has long separated these two method families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For resource-constrained platforms (smartphones, micro aerial vehicles) where both sub-100 ms latency and centimeter-level accuracy are required, SchurVINS offers a practical path that avoids the heavy LBA/GBA machinery of optimization-based VIO while retaining joint pose-landmark estimation. The ablation confirms that removing the EKF-based landmark solver causes divergence, underscoring that the full residual model is essential for long-term consistency. However, the method still trails the best optimization-based competitors (DM-VIO, BASALT) by a small margin in APE RMSE, and the authors acknowledge that local map refinement remains an open direction. The technique of Schur-complement decomposition within EKF is stated to be generalizable to other high-dimensional EKF-SLAM systems, which could have broader applicability beyond VINS.
