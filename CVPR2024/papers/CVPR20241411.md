## Problem

Existing zero-shot video translation methods rely primarily on inter-frame optical flow to enforce temporal consistency, but this suffers from three unresolved issues: inconsistent guidance when flow changes during manipulation, undercoverage in occluded or rapidly moving regions, and error accumulation from sequential frame-by-frame generation. The paper argues that intra-frame spatial correspondence—ensuring semantically similar content is transformed cohesively within a single frame—is equally critical but largely unexplored in prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241411/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of video frames, a target text prompt, and optional ControlNet structural conditions (edges, poses, or depth maps) as input, and produces a re-rendered video preserving the original motion and semantics. The SDEdit noise level T controls the degree of appearance transformation, and the framework is compatible with LoRA adapters for style customization. For long videos, a heuristic keyframe selection algorithm picks frames based on inter-frame L2 distance, and non-keyframes are interpolated via Ebsynth; batch size is typically 6–8 frames constrained by GPU memory.

## Method

FRESCO introduces two complementary adaptations to the U-Net decoder. First, FRESCO-aware feature optimization performs gradient descent (K=20 steps, Adam, lr=0.4) on decoder-layer features to minimize a temporal consistency loss (L1 distance between optically-warped adjacent-frame features) and a spatial consistency loss (Frobenius norm between Gram matrices of normalized features and their single-step DDPM reference). Second, FRESCO-guided attention replaces self-attention with three consecutive modules: spatial-guided attention that aggregates patches based on the input frame's self-similarity (computed via a single-step DDPM forward-backward pass), efficient cross-frame attention that references only newly-revealed (occlusion) regions across all batched frames to avoid redundancy while handling newly appearing objects, and temporal-guided attention that aggregates features along optical flow trajectories without FLATTEN's per-patch restriction. Multi-frame batch processing with shared anchor frames ensures inter-batch consistency for long video translation.

## Results

On 23 test videos, FRESCO achieves the best scores across all four quantitative metrics: Fram-Acc 0.978, Tem-Con 0.975, Pixel-MSE 0.012, and Spat-Con 0.0805. In a user study with 57 participants over 11 test videos, FRESCO is preferred 65.0% of the time, compared to 23.3% for Rerender-A-Video, 9.1% for Text2Video-Zero, and 2.6% for ControlVideo. The method also demonstrates long-video capability, translating a 16-second (400-frame) video using only 32 keyframes with Ebsynth interpolation for the remaining 368 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRESCO outperforms all three inversion-free zero-shot baselines on every reported metric. Against Rerender-A-Video (the strongest baseline), FRESCO improves Fram-Acc from 0.955 to 0.978, Tem-Con from 0.969 to 0.975, and reduces Pixel-MSE from 0.016 to 0.012, while also achieving the lowest Spat-Con (0.0805 vs. 0.0836). The gap is most pronounced against ControlVideo, which struggles with low-quality ControlNet conditions (e.g., motion blur) and yields the worst Pixel-MSE (0.066) and Spat-Con (0.0957). Text2Video-Zero, which relies on noise-based motion simulation, shows the lowest editing accuracy (Fram-Acc 0.918) and highest Pixel-MSE (0.038). In the user study, FRESCO's 65.0% preference rate is nearly triple that of Rerender-A-Video (23.3%), confirming that the spatial-temporal correspondence guidance produces visibly more coherent translations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inversion-free zero-shot video translation pipeline built on SDEdit and ControlNet, which prior methods (Text2Video-Zero, ControlVideo, Rerender-A-Video) use for structure-preserving video editing. The key departure from FLATTEN and Rerender-A-Video, which rely solely on inter-frame optical flow for temporal consistency, is the introduction of intra-frame spatial correspondence (FRESCO) as an equally important constraint, applied at both the attention level (spatial-guided and temporal-guided attention) and the feature level (explicit gradient-based optimization of U-Net decoder features). The efficient cross-frame attention design also departs from prior approaches that either reference only the previous frame or all frames, instead attending only to newly revealed (occluded) regions across the batch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that preserving intra-frame spatial self-similarity is as critical as inter-frame temporal alignment for coherent video translation, a dimension largely unexplored in prior zero-shot methods. Its high compatibility with ControlNet, SDEdit, and LoRA makes it a practical drop-in enhancement for existing image diffusion pipelines. However, the method inherently cannot handle large shape deformations or significant appearance changes since it anchors to the input video's spatial structure, and the authors acknowledge that pixel-level alignment (as in Rerender-A-Video) can outperform it when optical flow is high quality. An open direction is adaptively combining flow-based pixel alignment with the proposed spatial-temporal correspondence constraints.
