## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) has been extensively studied in the fully supervised setting, but zero-shot adversarial robustness—maintaining robustness on categories never seen during training—remains largely unexplored. Earlier attempts combined attribute-based zero-shot learning with AT but lacked scalability, and the recent TeCoA method only achieved robustness under extremely tiny perturbations (ε=1/255). This paper identifies a previously unnoticed obstacle: the high cosine similarity among CLIP text anchors, which prevents them from being directly used in anchor-based adversarial training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 84×84 for MiniImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and l2-normalized text anchors extracted from a frozen CLIP text encoder using the prompt 'This is a photo of a {category_name}'. At zero-shot inference, a novel image is encoded by the trained image encoder and classified by computing cosine similarity against expanded text anchors of the target categories; the category with the highest similarity is predicted. In the few-shot extension, image-text blended anchors combine the text anchor (weighted by β=2) with the mean of support-set image features.

## Method

LAAT (Language-driven, Anchor-based Adversarial Training) uses CLIP text encoder outputs as fixed anchors to supervise adversarial training of an image classifier, transferring semantic consistency to novel categories. The core technical contribution is an expansion algorithm that remaps clustered CLIP anchors onto the unit hypersphere by rotating the cluster center to the polar axis and scaling polar angles to fill the hemisphere, reducing average inter-anchor cosine similarity from ~0.7 to ~0.2 while preserving relative semantic distances. The supervision objective combines an Alignment Cross-Entropy (A-CE) loss—which embeds the cos θ anchor-alignment term inside a softmax to relax strict proximity to the GT anchor—with a label-independent smoothness loss that penalizes divergence between adversarial and benign features. Adversarial examples are generated via PGD (ε=8/255, l∞) during training, and the full loss is L = L₁ + α·L₂ with α=3.

## Results

On CIFAR-FS (5-way, ε=8/255), LAAT in the zero-shot setting achieves 55.60% clean accuracy and 40.12% PGD-20 robust accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA few-shot method GR (1-shot: 45.27% clean, 38.03% PGD) by over 10 points in clean accuracy and 2 points in robust accuracy. On MiniImageNet, LAAT zero-shot reaches 46.97% clean and 29.27% PGD robust accuracy, again outperforming GR 1-shot (36.14% clean, 27.57% PGD). In the GZSL setting on ImageNet-1K-trained ViT-B/16, LAAT achieves 34.97% adversarial accuracy on aPY and 49.41% on STL-10, while TeCoA (the prior zero-shot adversarial method) scores only 6.45% and 1.66% respectively under the same PGD-20, ε=8/255 evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAAT (zero-shot) decisively outperforms all three few-shot baselines on both CIFAR-FS and MiniImageNet across every attack type. Against GR (the prior SOTA few-shot method, [13]), LAAT zero-shot gains +10.33 points clean and +2.09 points PGD on CIFAR-FS, and +10.83 clean and +1.70 PGD on MiniImageNet, despite having no access to any support images. Against AQ [19], LAAT improves PGD robustness by +9.71 points on CIFAR-FS (40.12 vs 30.41) and +8.32 on MiniImageNet (29.27 vs 20.95). Against R-MAML [46], the margin is even larger: +13.91 PGD on CIFAR-FS and +7.53 on MiniImageNet. In the GZSL setting, LAAT (ViT-B/16, ε=8/255 training) dramatically outperforms TeCoA [34] on nearly all downstream datasets — e.g., 34.97% vs 6.45% on aPY, 49.41% vs 1.66% on STL-10, and 37.89% vs 7.03% on Caltech-101 — while TeCoA's originally released checkpoint (ε=1/255) scores near 0% on most datasets. The only datasets where LAAT shows weaker performance are Cifar100, DTD, and SUN397, attributed to resolution mismatch and large inter-class differences from ImageNet-1K.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAAT extends the anchor-based adversarial training paradigm (MMC, Hypersphere Embedding) to the zero-shot setting by replacing learned or random anchors with CLIP text-encoder embeddings, leveraging their semantic consistency for cross-category transfer. It departs from TeCoA (Mao et al., ICLR 2023), the closest prior work, which also uses CLIP text anchors for zero-shot adversarial robustness but only achieves meaningful results at ε=1/255; LAAT identifies the previously unnoticed high cosine similarity among CLIP anchors as the root cause of this failure and addresses it with an expansion algorithm and an Alignment Cross-Entropy loss. The A-CE loss is framed as a special case of InfoNCE with l2-normalized features, and the expansion algorithm is explicitly inspired by the anchor-spreading principle in MMC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot adversarial robustness addresses a practical scalability bottleneck: retraining from scratch for every new category is infeasible, and the paper shows that language-driven anchors can transfer robustness to unseen categories without any labeled examples. The identification of the high CoS problem is a useful diagnostic for anyone repurposing CLIP embeddings in adversarial settings, and the expansion algorithm provides a general, lightweight fix. Limitations include degraded performance on datasets with large inter-class differences from ImageNet (DTD, SUN) and the inability to perform adversarial training directly on large-scale image-text pairs, which the authors flag as a promising but unexplored direction.
