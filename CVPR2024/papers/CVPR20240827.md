## Problem

4D medical image interpolation requires ground truth intermediate frames for supervised training, but these are extremely scarce due to radiation exposure (CT), long scan times (MRI), and motion artifacts. Existing unsupervised approaches either need multiple intermediate frames in a sequence or rely on post-hoc linear flow scaling, which causes spatial distortion and artifacts. The paper asks whether a VFI model can be trained entirely without any ground truth intermediate frames, using only the two endpoint volumes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240827/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes exactly two 3D volumetric medical images (e.g., end-diastolic and end-systolic cardiac MRI, or end-inspiratory and end-expiratory chest CT) and produces an interpolated 3D volume at any continuous temporal position t ∈ [0,1]. No intermediate frames are required at either training or inference time. The flow calculation model uses the VoxelMorph architecture, and a small 3D-UNet serves as the reconstruction module. Evaluation is performed on the ACDC cardiac dataset (100 4D MRI volumes) and the 4D-Lung dataset (82 chest CT scans), with metrics computed voxel-wise across all slices.

## Method

UVI-Net introduces a time-domain cycle consistency constraint that provides pseudo-supervision without ground truth intermediate frames. During training, virtual intermediate volumes are generated by warping the two inputs with temporally scaled optical flow (t·φ), then candidate reconstructions of the originals are produced by warping these virtual frames back using the same flow model. A 3D-UNet reconstruction module fuses distance-weighted candidate images with multi-scale warped feature maps (at 1×, 0.5×, and 0.25× resolution) to predict the original volumes, and a cycle consistency loss enforces that these reconstructions match the true inputs. At inference, both forward-warped and backward-warped candidates are combined symmetrically through the reconstruction network, avoiding the one-sided warping artifacts of prior methods. An optional instance-specific optimization step fine-tunes the pre-trained model on each test pair for further gains.

## Results

On the ACDC cardiac dataset, UVI-Net achieves PSNR 33.57, NCC 0.565, SSIM 0.977, NMSE 2.409×10⁻², and LPIPS 1.134×10⁻² (without instance-specific optimization), outperforming the best unsupervised baseline IDIR (PSNR 31.56, LPIPS 1.675) and the best supervised baseline MPVF (PSNR 33.15, LPIPS 1.941). On the 4D-Lung dataset, UVI-Net reaches PSNR 33.90, NCC 0.319, SSIM 0.980, NMSE 0.558×10⁻², and LPIPS 1.512×10⁻², surpassing IDIR (PSNR 32.91, LPIPS 2.035) and MPVF (PSNR 31.18, LPIPS 2.554). With instance-specific optimization, gains are modest but consistent (e.g., LPIPS drops to 1.066 on cardiac and 1.489 on lung). Notably, UVI-Net trained on a single sample still outperforms baselines trained on the full dataset (90 cardiac / 68 lung samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UVI-Net outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest unsupervised baseline IDIR (which re-trains per test pair), UVI-Net gains +2.01 dB PSNR and −0.54 LPIPS on cardiac, and +0.99 dB PSNR and −0.52 LPIPS on lung, while requiring only a single shared training run. Against the best supervised method MPVF, UVI-Net leads by +0.42 dB PSNR and −0.81 LPIPS on cardiac, and by +2.72 dB PSNR and −1.04 LPIPS on lung — the larger lung gap reflecting MPVF's sensitivity to the 4D-Lung dataset's limited intermediate-frame supervision (648 total frames vs. 2,556 for ACDC). Among the flow-scaling unsupervised baselines (VM, TM, Fourier-Net+, R2Net, DDM), UVI-Net's advantage is most pronounced on perceptual quality: LPIPS of 1.134 vs. 1.772 (VM) and 3.482 (R2Net) on cardiac, and 1.512 vs. 2.063 (VM) and 3.277 (R2Net) on lung. The cycle-consistency reconstruction with multi-scale features is the key differentiator, as all flow-scaling baselines rely solely on post-hoc linear interpolation of the flow field without any image synthesis step.

## Relation to Prior Work

UVI-Net builds directly on VoxelMorph's flow-based registration architecture for computing 3D coordinate transformations and adapts the temporal cycle-consistency framework from Reda et al. (ICCV 2019) to the volumetric medical domain. It departs from prior unsupervised VFI methods (which require multiple intermediate frames in a sequence) and from medical-specific approaches like DDM (Kim & Ye, MICCAI 2022) that perform post-hoc linear flow scaling without image synthesis, by introducing a two-stage virtual-sample generation and reconstruction loop that enforces spatial continuity during training. The key conceptual departure is operating on exactly two endpoint frames with zero intermediate supervision, using the real endpoints as pseudo-supervision through the cyclic reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a genuine clinical bottleneck: 4D medical image acquisition is limited by radiation dose (CT) and scan duration (MRI), making high-frame-rate data scarce and intermediate ground truth unreliable. The demonstrated data efficiency—outperforming supervised baselines trained on 60+ samples while using only one training pair—suggests practical deployment in settings where only a handful of patients are available. Open questions include generalization to other modalities (ultrasound, PET), longer temporal sequences beyond two endpoints, and whether the linear flow interpolation assumption degrades for highly non-linear motions (e.g., cardiac systole). The instance-specific optimization, while effective, adds per-sample inference cost that may limit real-time clinical use.
