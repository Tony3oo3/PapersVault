## Problem

DNN-based steganographic networks are large (>100MB) and must be transmitted to senders and receivers for covert communication, raising the question of how to imperceptibly and securely deliver these tools. Existing DNN model steganography either cannot prevent unauthorized model extraction (multi-task learning) or only conceals a decoding network while requiring side information for recovery. This paper addresses the gap of hiding both a secret encoding and a secret decoding network within a single benign network that can be publicly distributed, with no side information needed.

## Input / Output

The PUSNet operates in three modes sharing one 19-layer CNN architecture: (1) purified mode takes a noisy RGB image and outputs a denoised image; (2) encoding mode takes a cover image and a secret image (both 512×512 at inference, 256×256 patches at training) and outputs a stego-image; (3) decoding mode takes a stego-image and outputs the recovered secret image. The encoding mode is unusual in that it requires two image inputs, handled by duplicating the first half of the network to extract separate feature maps from the cover and secret before concatenation.

## Method

The PUSNet is constructed as a sparse network (default 90% sparsity via magnitude-based pruning at initialization) whose dense versions serve as the steganographic networks. A key-based sparse weight filling mechanism triggers mode switching: a key seeds Xavier initialization to generate random weights that fill the sparse positions, with different keys producing the encoding or decoding network. Training is multi-task supervised learning that jointly optimizes three losses (embedding L2, recovery L2, denoising L2) while updating only the shared sparse weights, with hyperparameters λ_e=1.0, λ_r=0.75, λ_d=0.25 balancing the three gradient contributions. The architecture uses 19 Conv layers with Group Normalization, LReLU activations, and skip connections from the 4th to 16th layers; for the encoding task, the first 9 Conv layers are duplicated to process cover and secret images in parallel, and the 10th layer's filters are split in half to convolve each feature stream before concatenation feeds the remaining layers.

## Results

On the cover/stego-image pair (embedding quality), PUSNet-E achieves PSNR of 38.15 dB (DIV2K), 39.09 dB (COCO), and 38.94 dB (ImageNet), outperforming HiDDeN by over 9.7 dB and Baluja by over 9.7 dB across all three datasets. On the secret/recovered image pair (recovery quality), PUSNet-D achieves PSNR of 26.88 dB (DIV2K), 26.96 dB (COCO), and 26.28 dB (ImageNet), which is lower than all compared baselines. For the purified denoising task, PUSNet-P achieves 32.25 dB PSNR on DIV2K with less than 0.8 dB degradation compared to a dedicated denoising network (PUSNet-C at 33.03 dB). Against the SOTA DNN model steganography method (Li et al. 2023), PUSNet additionally supports secret embedding (39.09 dB) while incurring lower denoising performance degradation (0.73 dB vs. 1.24 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUSNet-E clearly outperforms HiDDeN and Baluja on stego-image quality by approximately 10 dB in PSNR across all three datasets, confirming that the sparse weight filling approach does not significantly degrade embedding performance. However, PUSNet-E trails HiNet by roughly 6–8 dB in stego PSNR, as HiNet leverages invertible neural networks in the wavelet domain for near-lossless embedding. On recovery quality, PUSNet-D underperforms all four steganographic baselines (HiDDeN, Baluja, UDH, HiNet) by 1.5–3.6 dB in PSNR, indicating that the shared sparse architecture imposes a cost on the decoding network. Against the SOTA DNN model steganography method (Li et al. 2023), PUSNet uniquely supports both secret embedding and recovery from a single purified model, while Li et al. only supports recovery; PUSNet also incurs less denoising degradation (0.73 dB vs. 1.24 dB) but achieves slightly lower recovery PSNR (26.96 vs. 28.52 dB on COCO). Overall, PUSNet trades some recovery fidelity for the critical capability of covertly transmitting both encoding and decoding networks within a single publicly available model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of DNN-based image steganography (HiDDeN, Baluja, UDH, HiNet) and the nascent field of DNN model steganography. It departs from Salem et al.'s multi-task learning approach, which cannot prevent unauthorized model extraction, and from Li et al.'s neuron-replacement scheme, which requires side information and only conceals a decoding network. PUSNet repurposes sparse network initialization (magnitude-based pruning) and key-seeded weight generation (Xavier) into a 'sparse weight filling' mechanism, allowing a single purified denoising network to be triggered into either a secret encoder or decoder without any side information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in DNN-based steganography: the covert transmission of large (>100MB) encoding/decoding networks to sender and receiver. By hiding both networks in a publicly shareable denoising model, it removes the need for secure out-of-band channels for tool distribution. However, the steganographic quality (PSNR ~26dB for recovery) lags behind dedicated SOTA methods (28–30dB), and the approach has only been validated on a single 19-layer CNN architecture for image tasks. Open questions include scalability to larger/deeper networks, robustness to model compression or quantization, and whether the sparse-filling paradigm generalizes to video or text steganography.
