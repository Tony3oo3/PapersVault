## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering and high image quality but requires a very large number of Gaussians (often exceeding 1 GB for realistic scenes) with per-Gaussian attributes like high-degree spherical harmonics and covariance matrices, creating a severe memory and storage bottleneck. The paper addresses this by simultaneously reducing the number of Gaussians and compressing their attributes without sacrificing reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242275/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view 2D images and an SfM point cloud (from COLMAP) as input, producing a compact 3D Gaussian scene representation where positions and opacities are stored as 16-bit half-tensors, scale and rotation are encoded as R-VQ codebook indices, and view-dependent color is stored in a shared hash grid plus a tiny MLP rather than per-Gaussian spherical harmonics. After optional post-processing (8-bit quantization, pruning, Huffman encoding), the total storage drops to roughly 20–50 MB per scene. The rendered output is novel-view images at real-time frame rates (128–185 FPS on real scenes).

## Method

The framework builds on 3DGS and introduces three complementary compression mechanisms. First, a learnable volume-based masking strategy applies binary masks (via straight-through estimator) to both opacity and scale attributes, with a sparsity loss L_m encouraging removal of small, transparent Gaussians throughout the entire training process rather than only at the end. Second, a residual vector quantization (R-VQ) codebook with L stages and codebook size C compactly represents scale and rotation, initialized via K-means and activated only in the final 1K iterations to avoid destabilizing early training. Third, a hash-grid neural field (Instant NGP) followed by a tiny MLP replaces the 48-parameter per-Gaussian spherical harmonics for view-dependent color, with positions contracted into a bounded range following mip-NeRF 360. The full model is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of rendering (L1+SSIM), masking, and codebook objectives, and further benefits from post-processing (8-bit quantization, parameter pruning, Huffman encoding) to achieve over 25× storage reduction versus 3DGS.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, the proposed method achieves PSNR 27.08 / SSIM 0.798 / LPIPS 0.247 with only 48.8 MB storage (vs. 734 MB for 3DGS), a ~15× reduction, while maintaining 128 FPS rendering. On Deep Blending, the method actually surpasses 3DGS in reconstruction quality (PSNR 29.79 vs. 29.41) while using 43.2 MB storage (vs. 676 MB) and rendering at 181 FPS (vs. 137 FPS, ~32% faster). With post-processing (8-bit quantization, pruning, Huffman coding), storage drops further to 29.1 MB on Mip-NeRF 360 and 23.8 MB on Deep Blending, yielding over 25× total compression relative to 3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (the primary baseline), the proposed method trades a small quality drop on Mip-NeRF 360 (PSNR 27.08 vs. 27.21, SSIM 0.798 vs. 0.815) for a dramatic ~15× storage reduction (48.8 MB vs. 734 MB) and comparable rendering speed (128 vs. 134 FPS). On Tanks&Temples, the quality gap narrows (PSNR 23.32 vs. 23.14) while rendering is actually faster (185 vs. 154 FPS) with 10× less storage. On Deep Blending, the method wins on all quality metrics (PSNR 29.79 vs. 29.41, SSIM 0.901 vs. 0.903) while being ~16× smaller and ~32% faster in rendering. Compared to grid-based NeRF methods (Plenoxels, INGP, Mip-NeRF 360), the proposed approach achieves substantially higher PSNR (27.08 vs. 23.08–27.69) and real-time rendering (128 FPS vs. 0.06–11.7 FPS), though Mip-NeRF 360 uses less storage at the cost of 48-hour training and near-zero FPS. The INGP hash-grid baseline is the closest in storage footprint (13–48 MB) but lags significantly in image quality (PSNR 25.30–25.59 vs. 27.08) and rendering speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by addressing its primary practical limitation—excessive storage and memory—through three complementary compression mechanisms: a learnable volume-based masking strategy (using the straight-through estimator) to prune redundant Gaussians, a hash-grid neural field (borrowed from Instant NGP) to replace per-Gaussian spherical harmonics for view-dependent color, and residual vector quantization (adapted from SoundStream) to compactly encode scale and rotation via learned codebooks. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of 3DGS-based rendering and classical signal compression (VQ, entropy coding), repurposing audio codec techniques for geometric attribute compression. The paper departs from prior 3DGS compression efforts (which typically focus on pruning or quantization alone) by jointly optimizing all three axes—Gaussian count, color representation, and geometry encoding—within a single end-to-end training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 25×+ storage reduction with maintained (and in one case improved) reconstruction quality makes 3DGS practically deployable on memory-constrained devices, addressing a key barrier to real-world adoption of real-time radiance field rendering. The modular design—where masking, hash-grid color, and R-VQ geometry can be applied independently—suggests the techniques are transferable to other 3DGS variants and future extensions. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, whether the codebook-based geometry can capture more diverse shapes in complex environments, and whether the hash-grid color representation generalizes well to scenes with extreme view-dependent effects (e.g., specular highlights). The reliance on SfM initialization and the modest training-time overhead of the hash grid remain practical considerations.
