## Problem

While extensive research has focused on making attention computation more efficient, the transformer's feature transformation process (MHA plus residual connection) has not been analyzed from a probabilistic distributional viewpoint. The paper identifies that the standard inner-product attention corresponds to an ill-posed kernel that introduces a bias toward increasing feature norms during training, and asks whether a well-defined probabilistic update rule can replace it while improving representational quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240943/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images that are split into fixed-size patch tokens (e.g., 16×16 patches for ViT), producing a sequence of d-dimensional feature vectors per image. The MSF-transformer module replaces every standard transformer block in the network, operating on these token sequences to produce updated feature representations that are ultimately pooled and classified. For downstream transfer, the same backbone produces per-patch feature maps consumed by a segmentation decoder (SETR) or a fine-grained classifier head.

## Method

The paper reformulates the transformer's MHA-plus-residual update as a gradient ascent on a kernel density estimate over input token features, revealing that the standard inner-product attention is equivalent to an ill-posed exponential kernel that inflates feature norms. Replacing it with a Gaussian kernel yields a mean-shift update that moves each token toward a local mode of the token distribution without norm bias. Concretely, the MSF-transformer (Eq. 12) computes attention weights from squared L2 distances ‖K_h^T x_i − Q_h^T x‖², aggregates VALUE features with those weights, subtracts a new PROBE projection P_h^T x, and back-projects the result through WEIGHT W_h before the residual connection. An interleaved grouped projection further reduces the parameter count of Q, K, V, P, and W by a factor of G while ensuring each head receives gradient signals from all feature components during back-propagation, unlike block-wise grouping.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (100-epoch training), the MSF-transformer consistently improves over the original transformer across all tested architectures: ViT-S reaches 79.79% top-1 accuracy (vs. 78.98% for the original), ViT-B reaches 81.79% (vs. 81.47%), and Swin-S reaches 81.33% (vs. 81.25%). The 2-group interleaved projection variant reduces parameters by ~15% while retaining most of the gain (e.g., ViT-S: 79.55% with 20.14M params vs. 78.98% with 21.91M for the original). On transfer tasks, MSF ViT-S backbone improves iNaturalist 2018 accuracy by +1.35% (68.19% vs. 66.84%) and ADE20K mIoU by +2.20 points (44.01 vs. 41.81) over the original ViT-S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original ViT [Beyer et al., 2022], the MSF-transformer wins on every architecture tested: +0.81% on ViT-S (79.79% vs. 78.98%), +0.32% on ViT-B (81.79% vs. 81.47%), and +0.79% on ViT-Ti (73.50% vs. 72.25%) at 100 epochs. Against Swin Transformer [Liu et al., ICCV 2021], MSF improves Swin-T by +0.69% (79.61% vs. 78.92%) and Swin-S by +0.08% (81.33% vs. 81.25%), with the 2-group variant matching or exceeding the original at fewer parameters (Swin-T: 79.25% with 26.04M vs. 78.92% with 28.20M). Against CNN baselines, MSF ViT-S (79.79%, 23.68M) outperforms ResNet-50 (75.97%, 25.50M) [He et al., CVPR 2016] by +3.82% and ResNeXt-50 (77.67%, 24.96M) [Xie et al., CVPR 2017] by +2.12% at 100 epochs, while MSF ViT-Ti (73.50%, 6.09M) edges out EfficientNet-B0 (73.47%, 5.24M) [Tan et al., ICML 2019] with comparable parameters. The 2-group MSF ViT-S (79.55%, 20.14M) surpasses ResNet-50 by +3.58% while using fewer parameters, demonstrating that the grouped projection preserves the advantage over CNNs even under tighter parameter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the transformer's multi-head attention through the lens of kernel density estimation (KDE) and mean-shift mode seeking, showing that the original inner-product attention is a 'relaxation' of a gradient ascent on a pseudo-probability density over input feature tokens. It departs from the large body of work that improves attention computation (low-rank approximation, hashing, clustering) by instead targeting the full feature-transformation process—projection into subspace, aggregation, back-projection, and residual connection—and replacing the ill-posed inner-product kernel with a well-defined Gaussian-kernel mean-shift update that introduces an additional PROBE projection. The grouped (interleaved) linear projection for parameter efficiency draws on grouped convolution and interleaved group convolution literature, but is analyzed specifically for its effect on back-propagation in a multi-head setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled probabilistic justification for why transformer attention works (mode seeking on the token distribution) and identifies a concrete pathology of the original inner-product kernel—bias toward inflating feature norms—that the Gaussian-kernel mean-shift formulation avoids, as empirically confirmed by stable feature norms across layers. As a drop-in replacement for the transformer module in ViT and Swin architectures, the method offers a modest but consistent accuracy gain (~0.5–1%) with the added benefit of parameter reduction via interleaved grouped projection. Open questions include whether the mean-shift interpretation and PROBE component generalize to other attention variants (cross-attention, sparse attention), whether the gains persist at larger scales (ViT-L, ViT-H), and whether the theoretical connection to KDE can be exploited for further architectural design beyond the current formulation.
