## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion models excel at single-instance generation but fail at Multi-Instance Generation (MIG), where multiple objects must simultaneously appear at specified positions with distinct attributes. Two core difficulties arise: textual leakage (CLIP's causal attention causes semantic confusion across multiple instance descriptions) and spatial leakage (Cross-Attention lacks precise position control, causing instances to bleed into each other's regions). No prior method achieves joint control over quantity, position, attribute, and interaction in a single generation pass.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a global text prompt, a set of N instance bounding boxes (x1, y1, x2, y2 coordinates), and N corresponding instance descriptions. Bounding boxes are converted to Fourier embeddings and projected through an MLP to produce position tokens that are concatenated with CLIP text embeddings to form 'grounded phrase tokens.' The output is a single RGB image where each instance appears within its designated box with the correct color/shape/material attributes, generated at 512×512 resolution using 50 denoising steps (MIGC active in the first 25 steps).

## Method

MIGC applies a divide-and-conquer strategy entirely within Stable Diffusion's Cross-Attention layers. (1) Divide: MIG is decomposed into N single-instance 'shading' subtasks, each producing a masked residual R^i that encodes one instance's semantics within its bounding box region. (2) Conquer: A trainable Enhancement Attention (EA) layer takes grounded phrase tokens (CLIP text embedding concatenated with MLP-projected Fourier position tokens) to produce an enhanced shading result that solves instance merging (via position tokens) and instance missing (via learned attention that forces correct semantics regardless of initial noise). (3) Combine: A Layout Attention layer (self-attention with instance-region masks preventing cross-instance leakage) produces a shading template, and a Shading Aggregation Controller (SAC) dynamically fuses all instance residuals, the background residual, and the template via softmax-weighted intra- and inter-attention at each spatial pixel. Only the EA, LA, and SAC modules are trained (on 8×8 and 16×16 UNet layers) with a denoising loss plus an inhibition loss that suppresses attention in background regions, while the base SD v1.4 remains frozen.

## Results

On the proposed COCO-MIG benchmark, MIGC achieves an Instance Success Rate of 58.43% and mIoU of 51.48, substantially outperforming the previous best method GLIGEN (32.39% / 32.25). On COCO-Position, MIGC raises the AP score from 40.68/68.26/42.85 (GLIGEN) to 54.69/84.17/61.71, with a Success Rate of 80.29% and mIoU of 77.38. On DrawBench, MIGC achieves 97.50% attribute success rate (vs. 48.20% for GLIGEN) and 67.76% count success rate (vs. 44.08% for GLIGEN). Inference time is 15.61 s, close to the original Stable Diffusion's 9.18 s, confirming near-original speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIGC outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against the strongest layout-to-image competitor GLIGEN, MIGC improves Instance Success Rate by 26.04 points (58.43% vs. 32.39%) on COCO-MIG, AP by 14.01/15.91/18.86 points on COCO-Position, and attribute success rate by 49.30 points (97.50% vs. 48.20%) on DrawBench. Compared to Multi Diffusion, MIGC gains 38.31 points in ISR on COCO-MIG and 56.43 points in AP on COCO-Position. BOX-Diffusion and TFLCG trail significantly, with ISR of only 13.96% and 8.62% respectively on COCO-MIG. On COCO-Position, Layout Diffusion (50.53% Success Rate, 23.45 AP) is also clearly surpassed by MIGC (80.29%, 54.69 AP). Notably, MIGC achieves these gains while maintaining an inference time (15.61 s) closer to vanilla Stable Diffusion (9.18 s) than any other layout method, which range from 19.92 s (TFLCG) to 44.17 s (BOX-Diffusion).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layout-to-image generation line (GLIGEN, Layout Diffusion, Box-Diffusion, Multi-Diffusion, TFLCG) by reframing multi-instance generation as a 'shading' problem within Stable Diffusion's Cross-Attention layers. Rather than modifying the full UNet or operating at the noise level, MIGC applies a divide-and-conquer strategy exclusively in the attention space: it decomposes the multi-instance task into per-instance shading subtasks, enhances each with a trainable Enhancement Attention layer, and fuses them via a Layout Attention layer and Shading Aggregation Controller. This departs from prior methods that inject layout as positional embeddings or initial-noise edits, instead treating Cross-Attention outputs as explicit per-region 'shading' results that can be independently controlled and recombined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces both a practical task formulation (simultaneous control of position, attribute, and quantity for multiple instances) and a dedicated benchmark (COCO-MIG), filling a gap where prior layout-to-image methods were evaluated primarily on position accuracy without per-instance attribute verification. The near-original-SD inference speed is practically important for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond COCO-scale object categories, how it handles complex inter-instance interactions (acknowledged as future work), and whether the divide-and-conquer-in-attention-space principle transfers to other diffusion backbones or higher-resolution generation.
