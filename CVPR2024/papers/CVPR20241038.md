## Problem

In Federated Class-Incremental Learning, Data-Free Knowledge Transfer (DFKT) is critical for mitigating catastrophic forgetting without accessing private client data, yet prior methods treat DFKT as a standalone module decoupled from model training. This lack of synergy leaves the teacher model's high-confidence regions unanchored and disorganized, forcing the generator to produce large volumes of low-quality synthetic data to cover the full knowledge space, which limits forgetting mitigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241038/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) alongside precomputed label text embeddings (LTE) from a frozen pretrained language model (CLIP by default), the previous server model, and synthetic data from the prior task. The LTE is queried once per class via a prompt template (e.g., 'A photo of a {class_name}') and stored in a fixed pool; it is never fine-tuned. Outputs are class predictions from the ResNet-18 backbone and synthetic images produced by a generator that takes the LTE (plus a randomly reinitialized noisy layer) as its conditioning input rather than raw Gaussian noise.

## Method

LANDER introduces label text embeddings as shared anchor points that organize the latent space during both client training and server-side data generation. During client training, a novel Bounding Loss constrains each sample's penultimate-layer feature to lie within a fixed radius r of its class LTE (rather than collapsing onto it), preserving natural inter-sample variance and mitigating embedding overlap under heterogeneous non-IID federated partitions. During DFKT, the LTE is fed directly into the generator through a noisy layer (BatchNorm + Linear) that is reinitialized each iteration for diversity, so synthetic samples are generated in the semantically meaningful anchored region. A Learnable Data Stats module replaces fixed training-set normalization with jointly optimized mean and standard deviation, eliminating the privacy cost of disclosing dataset statistics. The generator is trained with a combined objective of adversarial diversity (negative KL against a student discriminator), batch-norm alignment, one-hot cross-entropy against the teacher, and the Bounding Loss, while the client loss adaptively scales current-task CE+Bounding and previous-task KL+MSE distillation terms by the ratio of old to new classes.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (5 tasks, IID), LANDER achieves 52.60% average accuracy and 18.03 forgetting score, outperforming the previous SOTA TARGET (36.31% / 32.23) by 16.3 points in accuracy and 14.2 points in forgetting. Under the most challenging non-IID setting (Dirichlet β=0.1, 10 tasks), LANDER still reaches 29.29% accuracy versus TARGET's 19.25%. On ImageNet (5 tasks, IID), LANDER attains 43.24% final-task accuracy compared to TARGET's 31.83%, and on Tiny-ImageNet it achieves 30.29% (IID) versus TARGET's 26.25%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LANDER outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and partition setting. Against the strongest baseline TARGET [63], LANDER gains +16.3 points in accuracy (52.60 vs 36.31) and reduces forgetting by 14.2 points (18.03 vs 32.23) on CIFAR-100 IID 5-task; the gap widens under non-IID (e.g., +15.1 points at β=0.1, T=5). Against FedLwF [32], the improvement is +22.0 points in accuracy and −27.3 in forgetting on the same setting. FedWeIT [60] and FedEWC [25] are regularization-based methods that lag further behind, with LANDER exceeding them by 24+ points in accuracy. On ImageNet, where only TARGET reports results, LANDER improves final-task accuracy from 31.83% to 43.24% (IID) and from 29.83% to 41.75% (NIID(1)), confirming the advantage scales to large-scale datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

LANDER extends the data-free FCIL pipeline established by MFCL [5] and TARGET [63] by introducing a critical missing link: shared anchors between the training and DFKT phases. The generator design (LTE-as-input with a noisy layer) is directly inherited from Nayer [53], and the adaptive loss scaling follows R-DFCIL [19]. The key departure is that prior methods treat DFKT as a standalone module operating on an unorganized latent space, whereas LANDER constrains feature embeddings around label-text embeddings during client training, so that the server model's high-confidence regions are pre-organized for more effective synthetic data generation. The Bounding Loss and Learnable Data Stats are novel contributions that address federated-specific issues (data imbalance and privacy) absent from the centralized DFKT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FCIL is a practically important but underexplored setting (healthcare, IoT, autonomous driving) where both privacy and continual adaptation are required, and LANDER's two-digit accuracy gains over the prior SOTA (TARGET) across CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet make it a strong reference point. The finding that allowing embedding flexibility (Bounding Loss) outperforms tight MSE anchoring is a useful counterintuitive insight for federated settings with heterogeneous data distributions. Open questions include scalability to very long task sequences (beyond 10 tasks), whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities, and the computational overhead of maintaining the LTE pool and running the generator on the server side at scale.
