## Problem

Rendering a 3D human from a single image in a free-view manner remains challenging because generalizable implicit fields produce over-smooth textures, while Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) with 2D diffusion priors leads to texture-inconsistent and over-saturated novel views, particularly in back and side regions. The core gap is generating invisible-region content that preserves the texture style and fine details of the input reference image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240017/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a person (resized to 648×648 with the human occupying ~70% of image height), a foreground mask from an off-the-shelf background remover, and a text prompt describing the person's appearance (gender, hair, clothing). It produces a person-specific DMTet mesh with an Instant-NGP texture field that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints. The pipeline is per-subject and requires over one hour of optimization, distinguishing it from feed-forward approaches like PIFu or PaMIR.

## Method

ConTex-Human operates in three stages. First, a coarse Instant-NGP NeRF is optimized using Zero-1-to-3 SDS loss plus mask, RGB, and normal supervision to obtain rough geometry. Second, a texture-consistent back view is synthesized by performing DDIM inversion on the front image (concatenated with its depth map), then running DDIM sampling for the back view (concatenated with back-view depth) while injecting key and value attention features from the front-view branch into the back-view branch at each timestep, guided by a 'back view' text prompt. Third, the NeRF is converted to a DMTet mesh for geometry refinement with front/back normal supervision, and an Instant-NGP texture field is optimized by mapping the front and synthesized back images onto the mesh, combined with a joint Stable-Diffusion/Zero-1-to-3 SDS loss for side-region filling and a novel visibility-aware patch consistency loss (L_vpc) that enforces color coherence between invisible and visible pixels within randomly sampled patches, mitigating the color distortion and style inconsistency typical of SDS-only optimization.

## Results

On THuman2.0, ConTex-Human achieves the best PSNR (21.4 dB), LPIPS (0.063), and CLIP similarity (0.932) among all methods, while ranking second on SSIM (0.923 vs. PaMIR's 0.925). On the real-image SSHQ dataset, the method attains the highest CLIP score (0.903) for novel-view consistency, with a marginally higher LPIPS (0.059) than Magic123 (0.056) in the front view. Overall, the method leads on 5 out of 6 reported metrics, demonstrating superior texture consistency and fidelity in free-view human rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PIFu and PaMIR (feed-forward implicit-field methods), ConTex-Human clearly outperforms on PSNR (+0.4 dB over PaMIR), LPIPS (0.063 vs. 0.072/0.079), and CLIP (0.932 vs. 0.913/0.889) on THuman2.0, confirming that per-subject optimization with diffusion priors yields sharper, more texture-consistent results than generalizable pixel-aligned fields. Against Magic123 (the closest per-subject SDS-based competitor), ConTex-Human wins on PSNR (21.4 vs. 18.8), LPIPS (0.063 vs. 0.099), and CLIP (0.932 vs. 0.910) on THuman2.0, and on CLIP (0.903 vs. 0.882) on SSHQ, though Magic123 edges ahead on SSHQ front-view LPIPS (0.056 vs. 0.059). The only metric where ConTex-Human does not lead is THuman2.0 SSIM, where PaMIR is marginally higher (0.925 vs. 0.923), and SSHQ LPIPS, where Magic123 is slightly better. Qualitatively, the paper highlights that Magic123 produces over-saturated and texture-inconsistent back views due to unguided SDS, while PIFu/PaMIR yield blurry results; ConTex-Human's depth-and-text-guided back view synthesis and visibility-aware patch consistency loss specifically address these failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of single-image human reconstruction (PIFu, PaMIR, ECON) and image-to-3D generation via Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion, Magic123, RealFusion). It departs from the SDS-only paradigm for handling invisible regions by introducing a texture-consistent back view synthesis module that borrows attention injection techniques from 2D image editing (MAS-Actrl, FateZero) and uses DDIM inversion to transfer front-view texture details to the back view. The coarse stage specifically adopts Zero-1-to-3 rather than raw Stable Diffusion as the diffusion prior to avoid multi-head/multi-limb artifacts, and the fine stage introduces a visibility-aware patch consistency loss to regularize side-region texture that SDS alone cannot handle coherently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent problem in single-image human rendering: SDS-based methods produce over-saturated, style-inconsistent textures in invisible regions, which is particularly noticeable for back views. The attention-injection back view synthesis and VPC loss offer concrete, modular techniques that could be adopted in other image-to-3D pipelines. However, the method remains person-specific (over one hour of optimization per subject), produces coarse geometry in hands and feet, and side regions still exhibit lower quality than front/back views. Open questions include whether the back view synthesis can be made feed-forward or generalizable, and whether the geometry limitations can be addressed without sacrificing texture consistency.
