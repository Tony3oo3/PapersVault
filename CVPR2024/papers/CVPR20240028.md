## Problem

Event cameras suffer from background activity (BA) noise that degrades downstream tasks, yet existing paired denoising datasets are limited in scale, resolution, and diversity of noise conditions (mostly daytime, low-resolution, or simulation-based). This gap hinders the development of robust denoising methods for realistic scenarios with varying illumination, camera thresholds, and complex scene content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DED framework takes two spatiotemporally synchronized noisy event streams (from a co-axial dual-camera setup) at 1200×680 resolution and produces a high-quality denoised ground truth via spatial similarity and spatiotemporal correlation filtering. The DTSNN model consumes binary event frames within a fixed temporal window Δt as input and outputs a denoised event matrix of the same spatial dimensions, trained on 600 sequences (10 segments each) from the LED dataset with 60 held-out test sequences.

## Method

The DED framework exploits the key insight that BA noise is inconsistent across dual samplings while signal events remain consistent: it first applies a Hadamard product on temporally binned binary frames to retain only spatially overlapping events, then removes residual isolated noise via a spatiotemporal correlation minimization that enforces motion-model continuity. For denoising inference, DTSNN is a fully spiking network with two branches: a Dynamic Threshold Branch (DTB) that predicts a spatial threshold map from successive event inputs, and an Event Denoising Branch (EDB) whose final LIF layer uses this map as a learnable, spatially varying firing threshold to selectively suppress noise while preserving signal. The network is trained end-to-end with L1 + BCE loss in SpikingJelly, where the DTB is supervised by signal events over a longer temporal window than the EDB, enabling it to learn anisotropic thresholding that adapts to local noise density.

## Results

On the LED dataset, DTSNN achieves a denoising accuracy (DA) of 86.2%, with signal retention (SR) of 86.0% and noise removal (NR) of 86.5%, outperforming the next-best method AEDNet (DA 82.4%, SR 81.2%, NR 83.6%). On DVSCLEAN, DTSNN attains the highest SNR of 29.26 dB (vs. 25.58 for AEDNet), and on DVSNOISE20 it achieves the lowest RPMD of 16.13 (vs. 18.51 for AEDNet). Inference speed is 8.20 ms per 120×120 event grid, roughly 86× faster than AEDNet (708.1 ms) and 31× faster than EDnCNN (251.4 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTSNN outperforms all seven baselines on the primary denoising accuracy metric (DA) on LED, leading AEDNet by 3.8 points (86.2 vs. 82.4) and EDnCNN by 5.2 points (86.2 vs. 81.0). On DVSCLEAN, DTSNN achieves the best SNR (29.26 dB), surpassing AEDNet (25.58 dB) by 3.7 dB and DWF (26.96 dB) by 2.3 dB. On DVSNOISE20, DTSNN records the lowest RPMD (16.13), beating AEDNet (18.51) and Knoise (17.64). The runtime advantage is dramatic: DTSNN runs in 8.20 ms, making it 86× faster than AEDNet (708.1 ms) and 31× faster than EDnCNN (251.4 ms), while still exceeding both in accuracy. Filtering-based methods (Knoise, DWF, STDF, TimeSurface) achieve high noise removal rates (96–99%) but sacrifice signal retention severely (SR 16–43%), resulting in substantially lower overall DA. EvFlow, a motion-estimation-based method, balances SR and NR better (52.3/96.4) but still trails DTSNN by 11.8 DA points and is 18× slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multi-sampling denoising principle from frame-camera image processing (burst photography, Noise2Noise) to the event-camera domain, where prior paired datasets relied on heterogeneous multi-modal cues (APS image gradients, IMU) to synthesize ground truth. The key conceptual departure is using two co-axial event cameras to generate GT via spatial similarity and spatiotemporal correlation, exploiting the fact that BA noise is random across samples while signal events are consistent. On the method side, DTSNN extends standard LIF-based SNNs with a learnable dynamic threshold branch, drawing on biologically-inspired threshold modulation literature, and positions itself against both filtering-based (DWF, STDF) and deep-learning-based (EDnCNN, AEDNet) event denoising approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LED dataset (3000 sequences, 1200×680, four noise levels, day-to-night illumination) fills a significant gap: prior real paired datasets were small (≤144 sequences), low-resolution (≤346×260), or synthetic, limiting generalization to realistic BA noise distributions. The dual-event GT generation approach avoids the domain gap inherent in image-assisted methods and could be adopted by other event-camera tasks needing clean references. Limitations include potential failure when noise dominates the spatially-similar region, difficulty preserving weak signals at structural peripheries, and the practical cost of a dual-camera rig. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-resolution sensors (e.g., 4MP+) and whether single-camera temporal multi-sampling could approximate the dual-sampling benefit.
