## Problem

Conventional event cameras transmit only 1-bit polarity changes, which is too lossy to support general-purpose vision tasks without dedicated event-specific algorithms or supplementary frame cameras. The paper asks whether it is possible to design event cameras that inherently preserve rich scene intensity information while retaining the bandwidth and temporal-resolution advantages of event-based sensing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242037/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary photon-detection frames from a SPAD array (e.g., SwissSPAD2 at 512×256, 96.8 kHz) and produces an asynchronous, multi-bit generalized event stream whose readout rate is decoupled from the SPAD frame rate and instead driven by scene dynamics (e.g., 431 bps/pixel vs. 96,800 bps/pixel raw). A lightweight refinement CNN then converts periodic samplings of the integrator values into a high-speed video reconstruction (e.g., 3025 FPS), enabling plug-and-play inference with standard vision models (HRNet, DETR, SAM, RAFT) without retraining or dedicated event datasets.

## Method

The paper formalizes a two-axis design space for event cameras: an integrator Σ defining what to transmit (e.g., cumulative flux since the last event, coded-exposure sums, or patch-level means) and a change detector Δ defining when to transmit. Three concrete SPAD-based designs are proposed: (1) an adaptive-exposure camera with a Bayesian online change-point detector (BOCPD) that tracks Beta-prior forecasters over Bernoulli photon statistics; (2) a spatiotemporal chunk camera operating on 4×4 patches with 32-frame temporal averaging and a learned linear-feature matrix P for variance-aware, spatially-structured change detection; and (3) a coded-exposure camera that multiplexes photon detections with orthogonal binary codes and triggers events via binomial confidence intervals. All three are validated as on-chip-feasible on the UltraPhase SPAD compute platform, reducing bandwidth and power by over two orders of magnitude relative to raw photon readout. A densely-connected residual refinement network, trained on simulated SPAD data from XVFI high-speed video, converts the asynchronous event stream into temporally aligned video frames.

## Results

In the rate-distortion analysis (Fig. 8), generalized event cameras achieve a 4–8 dB PSNR improvement over all baselines (EDI++, long exposure, compressive sensing, burst denoising) at matched readout rates, and can compress raw SPAD data by ~80× before a noticeable PSNR drop-off. For high-speed videography, the spatiotemporal chunk method reconstructs at 3025 FPS with only 431 bps/pixel (227× compression of the 96.8 kHz SPAD stream). In low-light (7 lux) urban scenes, the method achieves 307× compression while preserving fine detail (e.g., a motorcyclist) that E2VID+ and EDI++ fail to recover. On-chip validation on UltraPhase confirms >100× reductions in both bandwidth and power versus raw photon readout.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against E2VID+ [57] (intensity-from-events on Prophesee DVS events), generalized events clearly win: E2VID+ fails to recover intensities in static regions and produces severe artifacts in low light, while generalized events preserve scene intensity inherently. Against EDI++ [42] (an idealized hybrid event-frame method with perfect SPAD alignment), generalized events still outperform, with EDI++ showing considerable reconstruction artifacts especially on fast-moving objects. For plug-and-play inference (Fig. 7), generalized events at 520 bps/pixel support accurate pose estimation, corner detection, optical flow, object detection, and segmentation using off-the-shelf models (HRNet, Harris, RAFT, DETR, SAM), whereas conventional events at 331 bps/pixel are too lossy for reliable inference; event-specific methods like Arc* [3] and E-RAFT [16] are outperformed by simply running standard models on generalized-event reconstructions. Burst photography [19] achieves comparable or slightly better image quality for purely rigid-motion scenes but at 15100 bps/pixel—roughly 29× the readout of generalized events—making generalized events the clear winner on bandwidth efficiency. In the rate-distortion sweep, all three proposed designs (Bayesian, chunk, coded) outperform EDI++, long exposure, 8-bucket compressive sensing, and 32-exposure burst denoising by 4–8 dB PSNR at the same readout rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the event camera design space by decomposing existing designs (DVS, DAVIS, ATIS) into two orthogonal axes—an integrator Σ (what to transmit) and a change detector Δ (when to transmit)—and then populating new points in this (Σ, Δ) space. It draws on Bayesian online changepoint detection (BOCPD) for noise-aware temporal change detection, on coded-exposure multiplexing (from SODAcam and Raskar et al.) for a lower-latency design, and on single-photon avalanche diode (SPAD) arrays as the hardware substrate that enables arbitrary software-level signal transformations at the sensor. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant 'intensity-from-events' reconstruction literature (E2VID, EDI, manifold regularization) by arguing the problem should be solved at the sensor level—designing events that inherently preserve intensity—rather than at the reconstruction level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play capability—running standard vision models (SAM, DETR, RAFT, HRNet) directly on generalized event streams without retraining or dedicated event-vision architectures—addresses a major adoption barrier for event cameras. The 227× bandwidth compression at 3025 FPS, validated on real SwissSPAD2 hardware and on-chip UltraPhase compute, makes high-speed single-photon imaging practically deployable without exorbitant data-transfer costs. Open questions include ego-motion compensation, handling modulated light sources, scaling to megapixel SPAD arrays, and building an integrated sensor-to-inference pipeline; the authors explicitly frame this work as 'the first steps' toward that goal.
