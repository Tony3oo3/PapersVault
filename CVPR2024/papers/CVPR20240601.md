## Problem

Generalist large multimodal models (LMMs) suffer non-negligible performance degradation when fine-tuned on a broad collection of tasks, because different tasks demand diverse capabilities (fine-grained visual recognition, world knowledge, OCR reading) that conflict during joint optimization. While Mixture-of-Experts architectures mitigate this by allowing experts to specialize, conventional MoE for 50–100B models replicates high-rank expert parameters, making it prohibitively expensive to scale to the hundreds of experts needed for strong generalist performance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (processed at 672, 812, or 1064 resolution depending on model size and task mixture) concatenated with text tokens, and optionally OCR-extracted text tokens prepended to the text input for document-heavy tasks. A single unified prompt format is used across all tasks ('Generate the alt_text in {lang} at 0:' for captioning, 'Answer in en: {question}' for VQA) rather than task-specific prompts, and the same set of model parameters handles both in-domain and out-domain benchmarks. Output is free-form text: image captions or short VQA answers.

## Method

Omni-SMoLA augments a frozen or fine-tuned base model (PaLI-3 or PaLI-X) with Soft MoE blocks whose experts are zero-initialized low-rank matrix pairs (rank 4) rather than full-rank MLPs, so the backbone retains the majority of parameters and the total parameter count scales linearly with expert count rather than with expert size. A Soft MoE dispatcher and combiner (learned routing matrix Φ with softmax gating) softly route all tokens to all experts without token dropping, and each expert's low-rank output is combined additively with the base model's linear-layer output. The 'Omni' design instantiates three separate SMoLA blocks per layer—one operating only on visual tokens, one only on text tokens, and one on all multimodal tokens—allowing modality-specific specialization. This enables scaling to 48–144 experts per modality with only ~1.4% inference slowdown, and the architecture is compatible with any dense or MoE backbone and can be applied on top of raw, LoRA-tuned, or fully fine-tuned checkpoints.

## Results

On natural image tasks (Table 1), SMoLA_O^48-PaLI-XFT achieves a new SOTA CIDEr of 149.8 on COCO captioning (vs. 149.2 for PaLI-X specialist) and 85.0 on VQAv2, outperforming the PaLI-XFT base by +1.1 and +0.6 respectively. On text-heavy tasks with OCR input (Table 2), the same model surpasses specialist SOTA on 6 of 10 benchmarks, including DocVQA (90.6 vs. 88.6), InfoVQA (65.6 vs. 62.4), and ChartQA (73.8 vs. 72.3). The 5B PaLI-3 variant also benefits consistently, with SMoLA_O^48-PaLI-3FT improving over PaLI-3FT by +1.07 average points across all tasks. Inference speed is only 1.4% slower than the base model despite 48 experts per modality block.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PaLI-X specialist (per-task fine-tuned), SMoLA_O^48-PaLI-XFT wins on COCO (149.8 vs. 149.2) and A-OKVQA (65.3 DA vs. 62.1 InstructBLIP SOTA), but trails on VQAv2 (85.0 vs. 86.0), OKVQA (62.4 vs. 66.1), and TallyQA (83.3 vs. 86.0). Against the PaLI-XFT generalist base, SMoLA improves on every reported benchmark, with the largest gains on OKVQA (+1.7) and A-OKVQA (+1.4 DA). On text-heavy tasks with OCR (Table 2), SMoLA_O^48-PaLI-XFT beats specialist SOTA on 6/10 datasets (TextVQA, ST-VQA, InfoVQA, DocVQA, AI2D, ChartQA), while CogVLM and Qwen-VL generalist baselines are substantially weaker (e.g., Qwen-VL scores 65.7 on ChartQA vs. 73.8 for SMoLA). The 5B PaLI-3FT base underperforms PaLI-XFT across the board, but SMoLA narrows the gap and even surpasses PaLI-3 specialists on 4 benchmarks (VQAv2, ST-VQA, OCRVQA, AI2D). Inference cost is negligible: only 1.4% slowdown (30.85 vs. 31.29 examples/sec) with 48 experts per modality block on all linear layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established parameter-efficient techniques—Soft MoE routing (Puigcerver et al., 2023) and LoRA low-rank adaptation (Hu et al., 2022)—into a unified architecture applied to multimodal transformer linear layers. It departs from conventional MoE designs (GShard, Switch Transformer) that replace entire MLP blocks with high-rank experts, instead keeping the pretrained backbone intact and residually adding many small-rank experts dispatched by a soft router. The motivation draws on Shen et al. (2023), who showed MoE architectures benefit more from instruction tuning than dense models, but addresses the parameter-replication bottleneck that limits expert count in large LMMs. Compared to concurrent work like Mixture-of-LoRA (Zadouri et al., 2023) and AdaMix (Wang et al., 2022), SMoLA achieves significantly lower computational overhead and scales to hundreds of experts with negligible inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single generalist LMM can match or exceed per-task specialist performance across a broad range of vision-language benchmarks, challenging the assumption that multitask fine-tuning necessarily degrades performance relative to specialists. The architecture's compatibility with any base model (dense or MoE) and its ability to scale expert count without proportional parameter growth make it a practical recipe for building versatile multimodal systems. Open questions include whether the modality-specific expert split (visual/text/multimodal) is essential or merely a convenient inductive bias, whether the approach extends to additional modalities (video, audio), and whether the gains persist as base models grow beyond 55B parameters. The modest average improvements (~1-2 points) suggest diminishing returns may set in, and the reliance on PaLI-family models limits generalizability claims.
