## Problem

Reference-guided image synthesis tasks—such as inpainting a masked target conditioned on a reference view or generating novel views from a single image—require capturing fine-grained spatial correspondence between views. Existing approaches either attach heavy image encoders/adapters to T2I models, perform expensive full fine-tuning with large batch sizes, or rely on fragile multi-stage pipelines (depth estimation, pose estimation, homography warping), making them computationally intensive and difficult to generalize across tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single horizontally stitched image of shape H×2W, where the left half is the reference view and the right half is the masked target (partially masked for inpainting, fully masked for NVS). For multi-view scenarios, v such stitched pairs are processed jointly. Task-specific and view-specific prompt embeddings (50 tokens total) are injected via cross-attention, and for NVS a 4-channel relative pose in polar coordinates is appended to the prompt. The output is simply the right-side generation from the stitched canvas; the left-side output is discarded.

## Method

LeftRefill reformulates both reference-guided inpainting and novel view synthesis as a contextual inpainting problem by horizontally stitching the reference and masked target into one image, eliminating the need for separate image encoders or architectural adapters. Generation is guided by lightweight task-and-view prompt tuning (only 0.05–0.065M trainable tokens) injected into the cross-attention modules of a frozen or lightly fine-tuned Stable Diffusion U-Net. For multi-view correlation, features are reshaped before self-attention (b×vhw×c) so that all views attend to each other, with incremental positional encoding (learnable view embedding + Fourier absolute position) added per self-attention block. For autoregressive multi-view NVS, a block causal masking strategy is introduced that masks attention at the granularity of an hw×hw block (one stitched reference-target pair), enabling parallel training of sequential view generation while preserving geometric consistency at inference.

## Results

On MegaDepth for 1-view reference-guided inpainting, LeftRefill achieves PSNR 20.93, SSIM 0.836, FID 18.68, and LPIPS 0.096 with only 0.05M extra trainable parameters, substantially outperforming SD-inpainting (FID 30.26) and ControlNet (FID 33.66) while using 7000× fewer parameters than ControlNet. For 1-view NVS on Objaverse, LeftRefill reaches PSNR 21.40, SSIM 0.882, LPIPS 0.115, and CLIP 0.797, surpassing Zero123 (PSNR 19.40, CLIP 0.782) across all metrics. Scaling to 4 reference views further improves NVS to PSNR 24.69 and LPIPS 0.063.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeftRefill decisively outperforms all SD-based baselines (SD-inpainting, ControlNet, Perceiver+ImageCLIP, Paint-by-Example) on every metric for Ref-inpainting, with the largest gap in FID (18.68 vs. 29.56–34.71) and LPIPS (0.096 vs. 0.135–0.182), while using 0.05M trainable parameters versus 52M–866M for the others. Against the dedicated inpainting method TransFill, LeftRefill trades a small PSNR/SSIM deficit (20.93 vs. 22.74) for substantially better perceptual quality (FID 18.68 vs. 26.29; LPIPS 0.096 vs. 0.110), which the authors attribute to TransFill's blur and color artifacts under challenging viewpoints. For NVS, LeftRefill surpasses Zero123 on all four metrics (PSNR 21.40 vs. 19.40, SSIM 0.882 vs. 0.858, LPIPS 0.115 vs. 0.131, CLIP 0.797 vs. 0.782) despite Zero123 potentially having seen the Objaverse validation set during training. A LoRA-based variant of LeftRefill trained with only batch size 48 on 2 A6000 GPUs remains competitive (PSNR 19.51), breaking Zero123's requirement for large batch sizes. In the multi-view NVS setting (Table 3), LeftRefill's autoregressive mode achieves the best pairwise CLIP consistency (0.796) while maintaining PSNR 21.27, outperforming Zero123's last-generated-view conditioning (PSNR 14.62, P-CLIP 0.767).

## Relation to Prior Work

LeftRefill departs from the dominant paradigm of reference-guided synthesis that relies on additional image encoders (Image CLIP in Zero123, Perceiver in Paint-by-Example) or trainable adapters (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) to inject visual conditioning into diffusion models. Instead, it reformulates both reference-guided inpainting and novel view synthesis as a single contextual inpainting problem by horizontally stitching reference and target views into one canvas, leveraging the self-attention of the off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion U-Net to capture spatial correspondence without any new encoder. The prompt tuning mechanism draws from the NLP prompt-tuning literature but is applied to cross-attention text embeddings in a diffusion model, while the block causal masking technique is a novel contribution that adapts autoregressive generation to self-attention-based diffusion models for multi-view consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LeftRefill demonstrates that a unified, extremely lightweight (0.05M–0.065M trainable parameters) formulation can handle both reference-guided inpainting and novel view synthesis end-to-end, eliminating the need for complex 3D pipelines (depth/pose estimation, homography warping) or large adapter networks. The block causal masking technique opens a path toward autoregressive multi-view generation within diffusion models, which could generalize beyond the specific tasks studied. Limitations include reliance on SD 2.0 at 256–512 resolution, moderate (though consistent) gains over Zero123 on NVS, and the fact that multi-view NVS still suffers from residual geometric ambiguity at large viewpoint changes. Open questions include whether the stitching formulation scales to higher-resolution models (SDXL, video diffusion) and whether block causal masking can be extended to longer view sequences or 3D-consistent video generation.
