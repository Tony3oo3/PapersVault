## Problem

Estimating metric relative pose from two images requires resolving the scale ambiguity inherent in 2D feature matching. Prior approaches combine 2D matchers with separate depth estimators, but these components operate independently—feature detectors fire on corners and depth discontinuities where depth networks struggle—and depth supervision is often unavailable in real-world domains. The paper asks whether metric correspondences can be learned end-to-end using only image pairs and their relative poses, without depth measurements, scene reconstructions, or overlap information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242083/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images and their camera intrinsics (K, K') as input, with no depth maps, overlap scores, or SfM reconstructions required. It outputs a metric relative pose (rotation and translation in meters), per-keypoint 3D coordinates in camera space (derived from sub-pixel 2D offsets on a 14×14 patch grid plus a predicted depth), descriptors, keypoint confidences, match probabilities, and a soft-inlier pose confidence score. The DINOv2 encoder is frozen, so the network operates at a coarse 14×14 spatial resolution while still achieving sub-pixel keypoint localization via learned offsets.

## Method

MicKey is a multi-head network with a frozen DINOv2 encoder that predicts, per 14×14 patch, a 2D sub-pixel offset, a depth value, a confidence score, and a descriptor vector; the 3D keypoint coordinate is computed by back-projecting the offset with the predicted depth. Training is fully differentiable: a probabilistic correspondence matrix (combining dual-softmax descriptor matching and spatial-softmax keypoint selection) feeds a differentiable RANSAC loop that uses the Kabsch solver to generate pose hypotheses, scored by soft-inlier counting and refined iteratively. The loss is the Virtual Correspondence Reprojection Error (VCRE), and gradients flow through two paths—REINFORCE-style policy gradients update the correspondence probabilities (descriptors and confidences), while direct backpropagation through the differentiable Kabsch solver optimizes the 3D keypoint coordinates (offsets and depths). A curriculum learning strategy selects only the lowest-loss image pairs from each batch for optimization, gradually increasing the fraction of examples used, and a null hypothesis anchors the loss to prevent noise from all-incorrect RANSAC samples.

## Results

On the Map-free Relocalization benchmark, MicKey achieves the best VCRE AUC of 0.74 (0.75 with overlap supervision) and 49.2% precision at the 90-pixel threshold, outperforming the previous best method RoMa (0.67 AUC, 45.6% precision) by 7 and 3.6 points respectively. MicKey also attains the lowest median reprojection error (126.9 px) and runs 85% faster than RoMa (119.8 ms vs. 820.2 ms). On ScanNet, MicKey trained with only pose supervision reaches 0.99 VCRE AUC and 92.8% precision, matching methods trained with full depth+overlap+pose supervision (e.g., RoMa at 0.99/94.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MicKey outperforms all feature-matching baselines (SIFT, DISK, SuperPoint+SuperGlue, LoFTR, ASpanFormer) and the dense matcher RoMa on the Map-free VCRE metric, achieving 0.74 AUC vs. RoMa's 0.67 and ASpanFormer's 0.64. Against the closest competitor RoMa, MicKey gains +7 AUC points and +3.6 precision points while running 6.8× faster (119.8 ms vs. 820.2 ms); however, RoMa achieves lower absolute pose errors (1.23 m / 11.1° vs. 1.59 m / 25.9°), indicating MicKey trades some fine-grained pose accuracy for better confidence calibration and VCRE. On ScanNet, MicKey with pose-only supervision (0.99 AUC, 92.8% precision) matches or exceeds fully supervised matchers like SuperGlue (0.98/90.6%) and LoFTR (0.99/91.3%), though RoMa retains a slight precision edge (94.4%). The RPR baselines from the Map-free paper degrade severely without overlap supervision (AUC drops from 0.40 to 0.18), whereas MicKey's curriculum learning strategy maintains strong performance with pose-only signals. The depth ablation (Table 3) further shows that MicKey's learned depth maps boost SuperGlue from 0.60 to 0.71 AUC and LoFTR from 0.61 to 0.71 AUC, surpassing DPT and KBR depth estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

MicKey builds on the differentiable RANSAC lineage (DSAC, NG-DSAC) by replacing the PnP or essential-matrix solver with the differentiable Kabsch algorithm, enabling end-to-end optimization of 3D keypoint coordinates from pose supervision alone. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (2D matching + separate depth estimation, e.g., SuperGlue + DPT) by jointly learning keypoint detection, descriptors, and metric depth within a single network, and from RPR methods by producing explicit correspondences with confidence scores rather than a single pose regression. The use of a frozen DINOv2 backbone and a REINFORCE-based correspondence sampling objective (inherited from NG-DSAC) positions the work at the intersection of learned feature matching and differentiable geometric optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that state-of-the-art metric relative pose estimation can be trained with only image pairs and ground-truth poses—no depth maps, no overlap annotations, no SfM reconstructions—making the pipeline far more accessible for new domains where such supervision is unavailable. A notable side finding is that MicKey's learned depth maps, tailored to where keypoints are detected, actually improve the VCRE of other matchers (SuperGlue, LoFTR) when substituted in, suggesting the depth is optimized for matching rather than general scene understanding. Open questions include whether higher-resolution backbones (beyond DINOv2's 14×14 patches) can close the remaining gap to RoMa on fine-grained pose accuracy, and whether the approach extends to multi-view or sequential settings beyond pairwise estimation.
