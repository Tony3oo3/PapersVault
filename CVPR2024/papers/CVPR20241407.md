## Problem

Source-free UDA for semantic segmentation has been studied only for pinhole-to-pinhole transfer, leaving the pinhole-to-panoramic setting unexplored. This setting is uniquely challenging because panoramic images introduce three compounding domain shifts absent in prior work: semantic mismatch from the 70° vs. 360° FoV gap, inevitable geometric distortion in equirectangular projection, and style discrepancies from different camera sensors and scenes. Naively adapting existing SFUDA methods to panoramic images yields only marginal gains, confirming that the projection and semantic structure of 360° data requires dedicated treatment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained pinhole source model (e.g., SegFormer-B1/B2 trained on Cityscapes or SynPASS) and a batch of unlabeled panoramic ERP images (H×W×3). Each ERP image is simultaneously projected into 18 tangent-projection patches and 4 fixed-FoV (90°) patches, which are fed through the source model to extract features and predictions; the original ERP image is fed through the target model. The output is a full-resolution per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over the entire 360°×180° panoramic image, evaluated on 13 (outdoor) or 8 (indoor) overlapping classes.

## Method

The framework extracts knowledge from the source model via two complementary projections of the target ERP images: Tangent Projection (TP) reduces geometric distortion, while Fixed FoV Projection (FFP) slices the panorama into 90° patches that mimic pinhole FoV. A Panoramic Prototype Adaptation Module (PPAM) computes class-wise prototypes from TP and FFP features via masked average pooling, integrates them into iteratively updated global panoramic prototypes, and additionally fine-tunes the source model by enforcing MSE alignment among prototypes extracted from the four FFP patches of the same panorama. Knowledge transfer to the target model operates at three levels: (1) a CE loss supervises target ERP predictions with pseudo-labels rebuilt from stitched FFP predictions, (2) an MSE loss aligns target prototypes with the global panoramic prototypes, and (3) a Cross-Dual Attention Module (CDAM) aligns spatial and channel attention distributions between ERP target features and rebuilt FFP source features via KL divergence, preceded by a Batch Normalization Statistics constraint that anchors both feature maps to the source model's BN parameters. All losses are jointly optimized with trade-off weights λ and γ, and the source model is updated synchronously through the FFP prototype-alignment loss.

## Results

On the Cityscapes-to-DensePASS (C-to-D) benchmark, the proposed method with SegFormer-B2 backbone achieves 50.12% mIoU, outperforming the best source-free baseline DTAC (43.06%) by +7.06% and the source-only model (38.65%) by +11.47%. On the synthetic-to-real SynPASS-to-DensePASS (S-to-D) scenario, it reaches 42.18% mIoU (+6.37% over source). On the indoor Stanford2D3D benchmark, it achieves 65.75% mIoU (+10.99% over source), approaching the UDA method Trans4PASS+ w/ MPA (67.16%) that requires source data access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all source-free UDA baselines across all three benchmarks. On C-to-D, it surpasses SFDA [25] by +6.08% mIoU (50.12 vs. 42.70) and DTAC [41] by +7.06% (50.12 vs. 43.06), while remaining competitive with UDA methods that require source data access (e.g., DATR at 56.81%, DPPASS at 55.30%). On S-to-D, it leads SFDA by +3.97% and DATC by +3.64% mIoU. On Stanford2D3D, it outperforms SFDA by +10.99% (65.75 vs. 54.76) and nearly matches Trans4PASS+ w/ MPA [50] (67.16%), which uses source data, even surpassing it on specific categories like Door (65.81% vs. 42.89%) and Window (69.88% vs. 57.54%). The ablation in Table 4 confirms that PPAM contributes +6.77% and CDAM contributes +5.59% over the source baseline, with the full combination yielding the largest gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: source-free UDA for pinhole-to-pinhole semantic segmentation (SFDA, DATC, GTA, HCL) and UDA for panoramic semantic segmentation (Trans4PASS, DPPASS, DATR). Rather than building directly on any single prior method, it repurposes prototype-based adaptation and pseudo-labeling ideas from the SFUDA literature and distortion-aware projection strategies from the panoramic segmentation literature, combining them into a new pinhole-to-panoramic source-free setting. The key conceptual departure is the use of multi-projection (Tangent Projection + Fixed FoV Projection) as a bridge between the source model's pinhole training distribution and the target's ERP representation, a strategy absent from both prior SFUDA and prior panoramic UDA methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to address the practically important scenario where a pinhole-trained segmentation model must be adapted to 360° panoramic images without access to the original source data (e.g., due to privacy or storage constraints in autonomous driving). The multi-projection knowledge extraction strategy and the panoramic prototype adaptation module offer reusable components for other 360° vision tasks. Limitations include the computational overhead of tangent projection during training and the fact that baselines are naively adapted pinhole-to-pinhole methods rather than purpose-built panoramic SFUDA approaches, leaving the true competitive landscape somewhat unclear. The authors suggest LLMs/MLLMs as a promising direction for further closing the semantic gap between pinhole and panoramic domains.
