## Problem

Diffusion model sampling requires hundreds to thousands of network evaluations, making generation slow. Learning-free ODE solvers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, DEIS, EDM) degrade in quality at small NFE because their sampling paths deviate from the true ODE trajectory, while learning-based distillation methods (progressive distillation, consistency models) demand extensive retraining of the denoising network for each dataset-sampler combination. The paper seeks a distillation approach that captures the trajectory-faithfulness of learning-based methods with the minimal overhead of learning-free solvers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained denoising network (either noise-prediction or data-prediction parameterization) and a Gaussian noise sample, producing a generated RGB image at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (LSUN Bedroom). A key operational detail is that the distillation phase requires only a single batch of 100 samples (25 for LSUN Bedroom) to solve for the per-timestep parameter λ*, a process taking a few CPU minutes; the optimized λ* is then reused across all subsequent sampling batches without any further network updates.

## Method

D-ODE solvers exploit the high cosine similarity between denoising outputs at neighboring timesteps by introducing a single scalar parameter λ_t that forms a constrained linear combination of the current and previous denoising outputs: O_t ≈ d_t + λ_t(d_t − d_{t+1}). This formulation is deliberately constrained so that the mean of O_t matches that of d_t, keeping the sampling trajectory on the data manifold rather than drifting off it. For high-order solvers (PNDM, DPM-Solver3, DEIS3, EDM), d_t is replaced by the solver's existing high-order approximation, and λ_t is applied on top to further reduce score-function fitting error. The parameter λ_t is optimized per timestep via knowledge distillation: a teacher run with C×T steps (C=10) provides target intermediate samples, and a student run with T steps minimizes the L2 distance to those targets, yielding a closed-form solution for λ_t. Once computed, the denoising network remains entirely frozen and the D-ODE solver with λ* can generate samples in T steps at quality comparable to the teacher's C×T steps.

## Results

D-ODE solvers consistently achieve lower FID than their corresponding ODE solver baselines across CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet (64×64 and 128×128), FFHQ, and LSUN Bedroom at most NFE values. A headline result is that D-DDIM with 25 NFE produces samples comparable to DDIM with 250 NFE in FID, yielding roughly a 10× speedup. D-DPM-Solver3 notably alleviates the instability of multi-step solvers at extremely small NFE (2–3), surpassing DPM-Solver3 by a significant margin. In terms of distillation cost, D-EDM requires only 2.55 units of time (measured in 10-step DDIM sampling time for 50k samples) to reach 3 FID on CIFAR-10, versus 187.25 for Consistency Distillation and 106.16 for Progressive Distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDIM, D-DDIM achieves lower FID whenever NFE exceeds 5, with the gap narrowing as NFE grows (both converge at high NFE); at 2 NFE, DDIM occasionally edges out D-DDIM slightly because the 2-step teacher lacks sufficient capacity. Against high-order solvers iPNDM, DPM-Solver3, and DEIS3, the D-ODE formulation consistently improves FID (except iPNDM at 2 NFE), with the largest gains for DPM-Solver3 at very small NFE where multi-step instability is most severe. Against EDM (Heun's second-order method), D-EDM produces substantially clearer images at NFE ≤ 5, where EDM's FID exceeds 250 while D-EDM remains competitive. Against prior distillation methods (Consistency Distillation and Progressive Distillation), D-ODE solvers achieve comparable or better FID at a fraction of the computational cost—2.55 vs. 187.25 and 106.16 time units respectively—because only a single scalar λ_t per timestep is optimized rather than the full denoising network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work on accelerating diffusion model sampling: learning-free ODE solvers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, DEIS, EDM) that use numerically determined weights to approximate the score function, and learning-based distillation methods (progressive distillation, consistency models) that retrain the denoising network to reduce steps. D-ODE solvers depart from both by introducing a single scalar parameter λ_t into existing ODE solvers, optimized via a lightweight one-batch distillation from a larger-step teacher to a smaller-step student, while keeping the denoising network entirely frozen. The formulation is grounded in the empirical observation that denoising outputs from neighboring timesteps are highly correlated, making a simple linear combination of current and previous outputs a meaningful correction to the solver's score estimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: distilling D-ODE solvers takes minutes on CPU versus hours or days for network-level distillation (Table 1 shows 2.55 vs. 187.25/106.16 time units), making fast-sampler adaptation nearly free and democratizing access to efficient sampling across datasets and architectures. However, the single-parameter design is acknowledged as potentially insufficient for high-resolution generation, and the dataset-specific λ* must be re-optimized whenever the data distribution changes. Open questions include whether local or spatially-varying parameters (e.g., via image grid divisions or latent-space manipulations) could extend the approach to higher resolutions, and whether the method generalizes to conditional or guided sampling settings beyond the unconditional benchmarks tested here.
