## Problem

Existing compressed video quality enhancement (VQE) methods rely on optical flow or learned deformable offsets to exploit temporal information, ignoring the coding priors (motion vectors, predictive frames, residual frames) that are already embedded in the compressed bitstream and carry explicit temporal and spatial cues. Moreover, prior VQE datasets provide only raw and compressed videos without these coding priors, making it impossible to train or evaluate models that leverage them.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a window of 2T+1 = 7 consecutive LQ HEVC-compressed frames together with 2T motion vectors, 2T+1 predictive frames, and the current residual frame, all extracted from the bitstream. Training uses 128×128 spatial crops at resolutions ranging from 272×480 to 4K under LDB and RA configurations at QPs 22–37. The output is a single enhanced high-quality frame in the Y channel of YCbCr space, evaluated by ΔPSNR and ΔSSIM relative to the compressed input.

## Method

CPGA comprises three modules. The Inter-frame Temporal Aggregation (ITA) module aligns LQ frame features via MV-based warping and separately uses predictive frames as a second alignment source; element-wise products with the current frame feature, softmax weighting, a FusionNet (Unet-style, borrowed from STDF), and a final deformable convolution produce the temporally-aggregated feature. The Multi-scale Non-local Aggregation (MNA) module then uses the current residual frame to guide non-local aggregation units (NLAU) at three progressively finer scales, fusing coarse-to-fine spatially-aggregated features. The Quality Enhancement (QE) module applies two 3×3 convolutions followed by G=2 shift channel attention blocks (SCAB), each embedding partial horizontal and vertical channel shifting before a channel attention block to enlarge the receptive field without extra parameters. Training is supervised with Charbonnier loss, Adam optimizer (lr=1e-4), batch size 32, and 7-frame clips on the newly constructed VCP dataset of 300 videos.

## Results

Under LDB configuration at QP=37, CPGA achieves an average ΔPSNR of 0.82 dB and ΔSSIM of 1.56 (×10⁻²) across 18 JCT-VC test sequences, outperforming the previous SOTA STDR (0.79 dB / 1.52) by 0.03 dB. Under RA configuration at QP=37, CPGA reaches 0.57 dB ΔPSNR and 1.07 ΔSSIM, again surpassing STDR (0.55 / 1.04). The gains are consistent across all four QPs (22, 27, 32, 37) and both coding configurations, with improvements of 0.02–0.06 dB over STDR. In terms of efficiency, CPGA (1386K params, 10.42 FPS at 832×480) achieves a 10% higher inference speed than STDR (1324K params, 8.77 FPS) while using comparable parameter counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPGA consistently outperforms all five compared multi-frame VQE methods (MFQE, STDF-R3L, RFDA, CF-STIF, STDR) on both LDB and RA configurations across all QPs. Against the strongest baseline STDR, CPGA gains 0.03 dB ΔPSNR under LDB at QP=37 and 0.02 dB under RA at QP=37, with the margin widening at lower QPs (e.g., +0.04 dB at QP=27 under RA). The gap over earlier methods is larger: CPGA surpasses STDF-R3L by 0.13 dB and RFDA by 0.08 dB under LDB at QP=37. In terms of efficiency, CPGA (10.42 FPS at 832×480) is faster than STDR (8.77 FPS) and substantially faster than CF-STIF (4.17 FPS), while using a comparable parameter budget (~1386K vs. 1324K for STDR). The ablation study further confirms that each coding prior contributes independently: MVs add 0.03 dB, predictive frames add 0.05 dB, and residual frames add 0.05 dB over the no-prior baseline, with the full combination yielding a cumulative 0.13 dB gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-frame compressed video quality enhancement line (MFQE, STDF, RFDA, STDR) by replacing learned optical-flow or deformable-convolution alignment with explicit coding priors—motion vectors and predictive frames—extracted directly from the HEVC bitstream. Architecturally, it borrows the FusionNet/Unet structure from STDF [6] for temporal feature fusion and introduces a multi-scale non-local aggregation module guided by residual frames, a design not present in prior VQE methods. The approach is conceptually inspired by compressed-domain video super-resolution work (CDVSR [3], codec-assisted VSR [44]) that already demonstrated the value of coding priors, but this is the first to systematically apply all three priors (MVs, predictive frames, residual frames) to the VQE task and to provide a dedicated dataset (VCP) containing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VCP dataset addresses a concrete gap: prior VQE benchmarks (LDV, LDV2.0, MFQE2.0) contained only raw and compressed videos without coding priors, making it impossible to train or evaluate methods that exploit bitstream information. The practical advantage of using pre-computed MVs and predictive frames instead of estimating optical flow at inference time reduces computational overhead, as evidenced by the 10% speed gain over STDR. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to next-generation codecs (VVC, AV1) with different partitioning and prediction structures, whether it remains effective at very high QPs where residual frames become sparse, and whether the non-local aggregation module scales to longer temporal windows without prohibitive memory cost.
