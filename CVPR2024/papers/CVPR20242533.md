## Problem

Outlier-robust 3D registration methods that achieve high accuracy (consistency-graph-based, deep learning, semidefinite programming) are fundamentally unscalable: they require quadratic memory or time, overflowing 16 GB of RAM for roughly 30,000 point pairs. No existing method can handle 10^7 point pairs with over 99% random outliers, leaving a critical gap between accuracy and scalability in large-scale registration.

## Input / Output

The method takes N 3D point pairs {(y_i, x_i)} with known correspondences, where up to 99.8% are random outliers drawn from a wider Gaussian distribution. It outputs a single SE(3) transformation (rotation R ∈ SO(3) and translation t ∈ R^3) that aligns the inlier pairs. Per-point threshold hyperparameters ξ_i (set to 5.54σ for synthetic data) control the inlier/outlier decision boundary, and the method operates directly on the full input without downsampling.

## Method

TEAR introduces a robust loss based on truncated entry-wise absolute residuals (ℓ1 norm with per-point truncation), which is separable across coordinates and enables tighter, more efficiently computable branch-and-bound bounds than consensus maximization (which only counts inliers) or truncated least-squares (whose lower bounds cost O(N²)). The 6-dimensional SE(3) problem is decomposed into two successive subproblems: TEAR-1 (3 DoF: find r₁ ∈ S² and t₁ ∈ R) and TEAR-2 (2 DoF: find r₂ ⊥ r₁ and t₂), followed by a final SVD on the surviving inlier set. Each subproblem is solved to global optimality via a customized branch-and-bound that searches a 2D spherical-coordinate space rather than the full 3D sphere, with upper and lower bounds computable in O(N log N) time through novel median-based algorithms. This design yields near-linear scaling in N, allowing TEAR to process 10^7 point pairs with 99.8% outliers in under 2000 seconds on a single laptop.

## Results

On 3DMatch, TEAR achieves 94.52% Registration Recall and 89.65% F1-score with 2.06° rotation error and 6.55 cm translation error in 1.26 s, outperforming all baselines on accuracy and speed. On KITTI, TEAR reaches 99.10% RR and 0.39° RE in 0.25 s; on ETH, it achieves 96.43% RR and 93.14% F1 in 0.38 s, while MAC, PointDSC, and VBReg all run out of memory. On the huge-scale Stanford 3D Scanning dataset (10^7 point pairs, 99.8% outliers), TEAR is the only method that completes registration, achieving 0.07° rotation error and 0.06 cm translation error in 1972 s, whereas RANSAC takes ≥8 hours and FGR yields 88.9° rotation error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEAR outperforms all nine baselines on the 3DMatch dataset, achieving the best RR (94.52%), F1 (89.65%), RE (2.06°), and running time (1.26 s), beating the closest competitor SC²-PCR by 0.07% RR and 3.6× faster. Against the branch-and-bound method TR-DE, TEAR is up to 70× faster on real data while also improving accuracy (e.g., 94.52% vs. 91.37% RR on 3DMatch). Consistency-graph methods (TEASER++, SC²-PCR, MAC) and deep learning methods (PointDSC, VBReg) are competitive in accuracy when memory permits, but MAC frequently exhausts 16 GB RAM and PointDSC/VBReg fail entirely on the ETH dataset. On the extreme-scale Stanford 3D Scanning experiments (10^7 pairs, 99.8% outliers), TEAR is the sole method that completes registration accurately (0.07° RE), while RANSAC takes ≥8 hours with 37° error, FGR yields 88.9° error, and GORE/TR-DE exceed 9–12 hours. The paper also shows that downsampling Lucy to 10^4 points before applying other methods still produces larger rotation errors than TEAR operating on the full 10^7 pairs, confirming that scalability cannot be substituted by discarding data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant consistency-graph paradigm (TEASER++, SC²-PCR, MAC) and deep-learning approaches (PointDSC, VBReg) that are limited by quadratic memory, and from prior branch-and-bound methods (TR-DE) that are too slow to scale. It repurposes the branch-and-bound framework by introducing a new robust loss (Truncated Entry-wise Absolute Residuals) that is neither consensus maximization nor truncated least-squares, and by decomposing the 6-dimensional SE(3) search into successive 3D and 2D subproblems with novel O(N log N) bounding functions. The dimensional decomposition and the specific ℓ₁-based truncation are the key departures that make the bounds both tighter than CM and cheaper to compute than TLS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TEAR is the first method demonstrated to register 10⁷ point pairs at 99.8% outlier ratio in under 35 minutes, a regime where all prior methods either run out of memory or produce grossly inaccurate results. This removes the practical need for downsampling, which the paper shows degrades accuracy by discarding inliers and even increasing the effective outlier ratio. The authors note the approach could extend to absolute pose estimation (2D-3D registration) and other geometric vision problems, but the current formulation is specific to 3D-3D registration with known correspondences. An open question is whether the O(N log N) bound computation and the two-stage decomposition generalize cleanly to higher-dimensional or non-rigid registration settings.
