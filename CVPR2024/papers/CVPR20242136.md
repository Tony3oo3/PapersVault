## Problem

Existing attention-based decoders for medical image segmentation, such as CASCADE, rely on computationally expensive convolutional blocks (e.g., triple 3×3 convolutions at each stage) and single-scale convolutions, limiting their deployment in resource-constrained clinical settings. The paper addresses the gap between high segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency by proposing a decoder that achieves state-of-the-art performance while drastically reducing parameters and FLOPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242136/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D medical images (polyp, skin lesion, cell, breast ultrasound, abdominal organ, and cardiac MRI) resized to 224×224, 256×256, or 352×352 depending on the dataset, and produces pixel-wise binary or multi-class segmentation masks. The decoder consumes four multi-stage feature maps from a hierarchical vision encoder (PVTv2-B0 or B2) and generates four intermediate segmentation maps at progressively higher resolutions; the final prediction is taken from the last (highest-resolution) stage after a Sigmoid (binary) or Softmax (multi-class) activation. Training employs a multi-scale strategy with input scales {0.75, 1.0, 1.25} and a combinatorial multi-stage loss (MUTATION) that aggregates losses over all 15 non-empty subsets of the four prediction heads.

## Method

EMCAD is a cascaded fully-convolutional decoder built from four key modules: Multi-Scale Convolutional Attention Modules (MSCAMs) that refine encoder features via channel attention, spatial attention, and a multi-scale convolution block (MSCB) performing parallel depth-wise convolutions at kernels [1, 3, 5] with channel shuffle (adapted from MobileNetV2's inverted residual design); Large-Kernel Grouped Attention Gates (LGAGs) that fuse skip-connection features using 3×3 group convolutions instead of 1×1 convolutions to capture larger spatial context at lower cost; Efficient Up-Convolution Blocks (EUCBs) for upsampling with depth-wise convolutions; and 1×1 segmentation heads at each stage. The key insight is replacing standard convolutions throughout the decoder with depth-wise and group convolutions, reducing the decoder to only 1.91M parameters and 0.381G FLOPs (standard encoder) while outperforming CASCADE by 0.85% DICE with 79.4% fewer parameters and 80.3% fewer FLOPs. The architecture is encoder-agnostic and compatible with any hierarchical vision backbone.

## Results

On 10 binary medical image segmentation datasets, PVT-EMCAD-B2 achieves the highest average DICE of 91.10% with 26.76M parameters and 5.6G FLOPs, while the smaller PVT-EMCAD-B0 reaches 90.52% DICE with only 3.92M parameters and 0.84G FLOPs. On the Synapse multi-organ dataset, PVT-EMCAD-B2 attains 83.63% DICE, 15.68 HD95, and 74.65 mIoU. On ACDC cardiac segmentation, PVT-EMCAD-B2 achieves 92.12% average DICE. Compared to the closest baseline PVT-CASCADE, EMCAD reduces parameters by 79.4% and FLOPs by 80.3% while improving DICE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVT-EMCAD-B2 (91.10% avg DICE) outperforms the strongest transformer-based baselines PVT-CASCADE (90.42%), SSFormer-L (90.03%), and PolypPVT (89.87%) across the 10 binary segmentation datasets, while using 79.4% fewer parameters and 80.3% fewer FLOPs than PVT-CASCADE. The tiny PVT-EMCAD-B0 (3.92M params, 0.84G FLOPs) also surpasses PVT-CASCADE (34.12M params, 7.62G FLOPs) by 0.10% DICE, and beats the lightweight UNeXt (1.47M params) by 7.63% DICE. On Synapse multi-organ, PVT-EMCAD-B2 (83.63% DICE, 15.68 HD95) exceeds TransCASCADE (82.68%, 17.34) and MISSFormer (81.96%, 18.20). On ACDC cardiac segmentation, PVT-EMCAD-B2 (92.12% DICE) edges out Cascaded MERIT (91.85%) and TransCASCADE (91.63%) with substantially lower computational cost. The only dataset where PVT-EMCAD-B0 does not achieve the top score is ColonDB, where SSFormer-L (92.11%) slightly leads (91.71%), and Kvasir/BKAI where PVT-CASCADE marginally wins.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMCAD is a direct successor to the authors' own CASCADE decoder (WACV 2023), replacing its computationally expensive triple 3×3 convolution layers with multi-scale depth-wise convolutions and a large-kernel grouped attention gate. The multi-scale convolution block adapts MobileNetV2's inverted residual design by performing depth-wise convolutions at multiple kernel sizes in parallel, while the channel and spatial attention components draw on CBAM and SCA-CNN respectively. The loss aggregation strategy (MUTATION) is inherited from MERIT, and the overall encoder-decoder framework follows the PVTv2 hierarchical vision transformer paradigm. Conceptually, the paper's departure from prior work is the systematic substitution of standard convolutions with depth-wise and grouped variants throughout the decoder, achieving large efficiency gains without sacrificing segmentation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 79.4% parameter and 80.3% FLOP reduction relative to CASCADE while simultaneously improving DICE scores makes EMCAD particularly relevant for point-of-care and edge deployment in medical imaging, where computational budgets are tight. The broad evaluation across 12 datasets spanning six segmentation tasks (polyp, skin lesion, cell, breast cancer, abdominal organ, cardiac organ) provides strong evidence of generalizability. Open questions include whether the depth-wise convolution strategy transfers to 3D volumetric segmentation, whether other encoder families (Swin, MaxViT) benefit equally, and whether the multi-scale kernel selection [1,3,5] generalizes beyond the tested architectures. The paper is limited to 2D segmentation and PVTv2 encoders, leaving the full design space unexplored.
