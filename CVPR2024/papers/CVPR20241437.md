## Problem

Existing GAN-based super-resolution discriminators measure real-fake distribution distance in a coarse-grained, semantics-agnostic manner, making them susceptible to generating virtual or counter-intuitive textures that do not match the actual content of the image. No prior work conditions the discriminator on image semantics to enforce fine-grained, content-aware texture generation during adversarial training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241437/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (×4 bicubic-downsampled, 256×256 training patches) and produces a high-resolution RGB image. During training, the HR reference image is additionally fed through a frozen CLIP RN50 semantic extractor to obtain pixel-wise semantic features that condition the discriminator; at inference, only the SR generator is needed with no extra semantic computation or parameters.

## Method

SeD introduces a Semantic-aware Fusion Block (SeFB) that incorporates pixel-wise semantics from a frozen pretrained CLIP RN50 (3rd-layer features) into the discriminator via cross-attention: semantics are processed through group normalization, layer normalization, and self-attention to form the query, while the discriminator's image features (from both the SR output and HR reference) serve as keys and values. The cross-attention output is concatenated with the original features to produce semantic-aware representations that the discriminator uses to measure distribution distance conditioned on local content. SeD is plug-and-play, replacing convolution layers in either a PatchGAN (patch-wise) or U-Net (pixel-wise) discriminator backbone, and is trained with standard GAN objectives (L1 + VGG perceptual + adversarial loss) alongside an RRDB or SwinIR generator.

## Results

On classical image SR (×4), RRDB+P+SeD achieves the best LPIPS across all five benchmarks: 0.064 (Set5), 0.117 (Set14), 0.094 (DIV2K), 0.106 (Urban100), and 0.048 (Manga109), while RRDB+U+SeD attains the highest PSNR/SSIM (e.g., 31.73 dB / 0.880 on Set5, 30.46 dB / 0.897 on Manga109). With the SwinIR generator, SwinIR+P+SeD further improves LPIPS to 0.061 (Set5) and 0.044 (Manga109). On real-world SR, SeD reduces NIQE to 4.49 on DPED and 5.34 on RealSRSet with RealESRGAN, outperforming both the vanilla discriminator (5.27/5.80) and LDL (5.36/6.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeD consistently outperforms all six baselines on LPIPS (perceptual quality) across every classical SR dataset. Compared to the strongest prior methods RRDB+DualFormer (LPIPS 0.068/0.121/0.103/0.115/0.053) and RRDB+LDL (0.069/0.121/0.101/0.110/0.055), RRDB+P+SeD improves LPIPS by 0.004–0.011 per dataset, with the largest gain on Manga109 (0.048 vs. 0.053). Against the vanilla discriminators (RRDB+P and RRDB+U), SeD yields up to 1.0 dB PSNR improvement on Manga109 while also reducing LPIPS, confirming that semantic conditioning helps both objective and perceptual metrics. On real-world SR (Table 2), SeD with RealESRGAN achieves the lowest NIQE on all three benchmarks (4.49/2.73/5.34 vs. vanilla 5.27/2.82/5.80 and LDL 5.36/2.83/6.07), though with SwinIR the gains are more modest (5.16 vs. 4.95 vanilla on DPED). The method also generalizes across generator architectures, with SwinIR+P+SeD achieving the best LPIPS on Set5 (0.061) and Manga109 (0.044) among all configurations tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAN-based SR lineage (SRGAN, ESRGAN, Real-ESRGAN) by rethinking the discriminator rather than the generator. Prior work has explored image-wise, patch-wise (PatchGAN), and pixel-wise (U-Net) discriminators to capture finer distributional structure, but all treat the real/fake decision as semantically agnostic. SeD departs from the common strategy of injecting semantics into the generator (which suffers from error propagation on degraded inputs and inference overhead) by instead conditioning the discriminator on pixel-wise semantics extracted from a frozen pretrained vision model (CLIP RN50), fused via a cross-attention-based Semantic-aware Fusion Block. The design is conceptually inspired by panoptic semantic discriminators for inpainting [74], but is the first to apply this idea to SR and to leverage vision-language pretrained features for the conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SeD (no added inference cost, compatible with RRDB, SwinIR, and multiple discriminator architectures) makes it a practical drop-in improvement for existing GAN-based SR pipelines. However, the approach is limited to two SR settings (classical bicubic and real-world), relies on a relatively small CLIP variant (RN50), and the semantic extractor is fixed at training time, raising open questions about scalability to larger PVMs, video SR, or other restoration tasks. Whether the semantic conditioning generalizes beyond the tested architectures and degradation models, and whether the gains persist under stronger perceptual metrics or human evaluation at scale, remain to be established.
