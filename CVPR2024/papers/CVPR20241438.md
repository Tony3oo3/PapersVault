## Problem

Prior studies probing social biases in vision-language models have focused exclusively on single attributes (e.g., gender or race in isolation), ignoring intersectional biases where combinations of attributes (e.g., race × gender) produce distinct and potentially stronger biases. This limitation stems from the difficulty of collecting exhaustive real image-text pairs covering all combinations of social attributes and subject types, making it impossible to isolate the effect of one attribute while controlling for others.

## Input / Output

The method takes templated text captions (e.g., 'A photo of an Asian female doctor') constructed from 260 occupations × 4 prefixes × 6 races × 5 physical characteristics × 2 gender terms, and produces 171k counterfactual image-text pairs organized into 13,824 sets where images within each set depict the same subject but differ only in the targeted social attributes. For bias probing, attribute-neutral prompts (e.g., 'A construction worker') are used to retrieve top-K images from each counterfactual set, and MaxSkew@K is computed over the retrieved proportions. For debiasing, the dataset is split 80/20 by occupation into training and held-out test segments for fine-tuning VLMs.

## Method

The core pipeline uses Stable Diffusion with Prompt-to-Prompt cross-attention control to generate counterfactual image sets where differences are isolated to the social attribute change while preserving all other visual details (background, pose, clothing). Because minor prompt changes can cause large unintended image modifications, the authors over-generate 100 candidate images per caption by sampling the shared-attention parameter p ~ U(0.1, 0.9), then apply three sequential filters: (1) CLIP cosine similarity ≥ 0.2 between caption and image and between images within a set, (2) NSFW removal via a fine-tuned ViT classifier, and (3) a learned threshold-based heuristic ensuring CLIP can detect the targeted social attribute in a sufficient number of images per set. This over-generate-then-filter strategy yields 171k high-quality pairs (97.5% caption accuracy post-filtering) across three intersectional segments. For debiasing, the authors fine-tune CLIP, ALIP, and FLAVA on 80% of occupations and evaluate on the held-out 20%, demonstrating that debiasing generalizes to unseen subjects and transfers to real-image benchmarks (VisoGender, PATA) with 12–15% relative skewness reductions.

## Results

Across six VLMs, all exhibit significant intersectional social bias, with CLIP and FLAVA showing the lowest pre-trained MaxSkew@K (e.g., 1.02 for Race-Gender) and ALIP the highest (1.40). Training on SocialCounterfactuals yields up to a 0.426 absolute reduction in MaxSkew@K for ALIP and 0.327 for CLIP on (Physical Char., Race) bias. On real image-text datasets, the debiased CLIP achieves 15% and 12% relative reductions in skewness on VisoGender and PATA respectively. Debiasing improves text retrieval R@1 on Flickr30K from 67.1 to 69.2 while causing only minor degradation in image retrieval (69.4→67.3 R@1) and zero-shot classification (e.g., ImageNet 59.25→55.38).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper probes six VLMs (CLIP, ALIP, FLAVA, LaCLIP, OpenCLIP, SLIP) on SocialCounterfactuals, finding that all exhibit significant intersectional bias, with CLIP and FLAVA having the lowest overall MaxSkew@K and SLIP showing no zero-skewness occupations across any segment. For debiasing, the paper finetunes CLIP, ALIP, and FLAVA on SocialCounterfactuals and evaluates on held-out occupations. ALIP benefits most from debiasing (up to 0.426 absolute MaxSkew@K reduction), while CLIP and FLAVA, already having lower pre-trained bias, show smaller but still meaningful reductions (0.25 and 0.19 for Race-Gender respectively). On real image-text benchmarks, debiased CLIP outperforms pre-trained CLIP on both VisoGender (0.219 vs. 0.269) and PATA (0.283 vs. 0.323), confirming that synthetic counterfactual debiasing generalizes to real data. The strongest pre-trained biases (Physical Char.-Race) also receive the largest debiasing gains, and these effects generalize to unseen occupations, suggesting the dataset captures transferable bias patterns rather than memorizing specific subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Prompt-to-Prompt cross-attention control mechanism (Hertz et al., 2022) and the over-generation strategy from InstructPix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023) from single-image editing to batched generation of entire counterfactual sets that differ only in social attributes. It departs from prior VLM bias-probing work (VLStereoSet, VisoGender, MultiModal Bias) which relied on small collections of real image-text pairs and examined only one social attribute at a time, by using Stable Diffusion to synthesize large-scale counterfactual sets enabling intersectional (multi-attribute) bias measurement. The over-generate-then-filter pipeline—combining CLIP similarity, NSFW detection, and attribute detectability thresholds—is a novel quality-control methodology specific to the bias-probing setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SocialCounterfactuals (171k pairs) is the largest and first intersectional bias benchmark for VLMs, revealing that gender bias manifests along starkly different racial dimensions depending on the model (e.g., OpenCLIP favors Asian male doctors while others favor Indian male doctors), a compositional effect invisible to single-attribute evaluation. The finding that debiasing with synthetic counterfactuals generalizes to unseen occupations and transfers to real-image datasets (VisoGender, PATA) suggests a practical path to reducing intersectional bias without exhaustive real-data collection. Key limitations include binary gender framing, a fixed set of six races, and the gap between synthetic and real image distributions; open questions include extending to more attribute combinations, balancing debiasing with task performance, and applying the methodology to generative text-to-image models.
