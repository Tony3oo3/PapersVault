## Problem

Back-projection (BP) guidance is a powerful strategy for guiding diffusion-model-based image restoration toward data consistency, but it amplifies observation noise when the measurement operator A has small singular values (as in deblurring and super-resolution), requiring strong regularization that degrades performance. Existing alternatives either demand full SVD of A (DDRM) or many neural function evaluations (DPS), limiting practical applicability. The paper asks whether one can retain BP's early-iteration convergence benefits while gaining LS's noise robustness in later iterations, without SVD or excessive NFEs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded linear observation y = Ax* + e (where A is any m×n measurement operator such as a Gaussian/motion blur kernel or bicubic downsampling) and a pretrained diffusion-model denoiser ε_θ(·,t), and outputs a restored n-dimensional image x*. It operates zero-shot: no task-specific training is needed, and A need only support forward and adjoint operations (no SVD required). The method handles both noiseless and noisy observations, with the noise level σ_e determining the guidance trajectory parameters.

## Method

The key insight is recognizing the BP step x_{t-1} = x_{0|t} − A†(Ax_{0|t} − y) as a preconditioned LS gradient step with W = (AA^T)^{-1}. The paper proposes an iteration-dependent preconditioner W_t = (1−δ_t)(AA^T + ηI)^{-1} + δ_t·cI, a convex combination that smoothly traverses from regularized BP (δ_t≈0, early iterations) to LS (δ_t≈1, late iterations), yielding a guidance g_{δ_t} = (1−δ_t)g_BP + δ_t g_LS. This is deployed in two forms: IDPG, a deterministic PnP scheme alternating denoising and preconditioned guidance, and DDPG, a sampling-based DDM scheme built on modified DDIM where the effective predicted noise ε̂_t accounts for the guidance-induced signal change and is scaled by δ_t to suppress noise amplification during BP-dominated iterations. The trajectory δ_t = ᾱ_t^γ is set by a single hyperparameter γ tied to the DDPM noise schedule, requiring only η, γ, and ζ for tuning.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ 1K, DDPG achieves 29.02 dB PSNR / 0.082 LPIPS on motion deblurring (σe=0.05), outperforming DiffPIR (27.96/0.102) and DPS (19.63/0.227) by large margins. For Gaussian deblurring at σe=0.1, DDPG reaches 29.18 dB PSNR / 0.080 LPIPS, beating DDRM (28.79/0.088) and DiffPIR (27.59/0.083). In noiseless Gaussian deblurring, DDPG achieves 45.46 dB PSNR, on par with DDNM (45.56) and far above DDRM (42.49) and DiffPIR (32.97). The deterministic IDPG variant achieves the highest PSNR in most noisy settings (e.g., 31.08 dB on Gaussian deblurring σe=0.05) but with worse perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.150 vs. DDPG's 0.068).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDPG consistently outperforms DPS by a wide margin in all noisy settings, most dramatically on motion deblurring where DDPG achieves 29.02 dB vs. DPS's 19.63 dB (σe=0.05), a gap of nearly 10 dB, while also using 10× fewer NFEs (100 vs. 1000). Against DiffPIR, DDPG wins on PSNR in every noisy task (e.g., 29.02 vs. 27.96 on motion deblurring σe=0.05; 29.18 vs. 27.59 on Gaussian deblurring σe=0.1) and also achieves better LPIPS. Compared to DDRM, DDPG is competitive or slightly ahead in PSNR for noisy deblurring (29.18 vs. 28.79 at σe=0.1) and substantially better in LPIPS (0.080 vs. 0.088), while DDRM is inapplicable to motion deblurring due to its SVD requirement. DDNM matches DDPG in noiseless Gaussian deblurring (45.56 vs. 45.46 dB) but is entirely inapplicable to noisy observations, leaving DDPG as the only method that handles both noiseless and noisy settings with strong performance. The deterministic IDPG variant achieves the highest PSNR in most noisy settings (e.g., 31.08 dB on Gaussian deblurring σe=0.05) but at the cost of noticeably worse perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.150 vs. DDPG's 0.068), confirming the benefit of the sampling-based formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate guidance strategies in diffusion-based image restoration—back-projection (BP) guidance (IDBP, DDNM, pseudoinverse/range-null-space methods) and least-squares (LS) gradient guidance (DPS)—by identifying the BP step as a preconditioned LS step with W=(AA^T)^{-1}. Building on the PnP/RED iterative denoising framework and the DDIM sampling scheme, it introduces an iteration-dependent preconditioner W_t that smoothly traverses from the (regularized) BP step to the LS step, combining BP's fast convergence and strong data consistency in early iterations with LS's noise robustness in later iterations. The sampling scheme (DDPG) further modifies the DDIM noise injection by re-estimating the effective predicted noise after guidance and scaling it by the LS weight δ_t, drawing on the DiffPIR idea of adapting noise to guided estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: BP-based diffusion restoration (DDNM) fails under observation noise, and SVD-based methods (DDRM) cannot handle non-separable or high-dimensional operators (e.g., random motion deblurring). DDPG requires only A and A^T applications, making it broadly applicable to any linear observation model without SVD computation or thousands of NFEs. The bias-variance and condition-number analysis (Theorem 3.4, Claim 3.5) provides a principled justification for the BP-to-LS trajectory, though the analysis is restricted to Tikhonov priors and fixed δ. Open questions include learning the preconditioner sequence {W_t} in a data-driven way, extending to non-linear or unknown observation models, and whether the simple convex-combination schedule (20) is near-optimal or can be substantially improved.
