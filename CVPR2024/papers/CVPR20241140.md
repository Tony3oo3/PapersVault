## Problem

Previous motion retargeting methods focus on trajectory-level joint positions and rely on human-designed representations (joint distance matrices, self-contacts) that fail to capture high-level motion semantics, leading to semantic loss and costly manual artist intervention. The fundamental challenge is that the most comprehensive form of motion semantics—human-level natural language—is extremely difficult to label at scale for motion data, leaving a gap between what artists need and what existing methods preserve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source motion sequence represented as per-frame node features (6D joint rotations + 3D joint positions, Q ∈ R^{T×N×9}) with skeleton hierarchy edge features (3D parent offsets, E ∈ R^{M×3}), along with a target character in T-pose, and produces retargeted joint angles for the target character. During fine-tuning, the 3D joint angles are converted to skinned mesh vertices via differentiable linear blend skinning and then rendered to 2D images from three fixed perspectives (front, left, right) to feed the vision-language model, making the entire semantic extraction pipeline differentiable with respect to the joint angles.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline that uses a frozen vision-language model (BLIP-2 with FlanT5-XXL) as an unsupervised semantic supervisor. In stage one, a graph motion encoder-decoder (spatial-temporal graph convolutions) is pre-trained at the skeletal level with reconstruction, cycle consistency, adversarial, and joint distance matrix losses to establish a robust initialization. In stage two, the network is fine-tuned per source-target pair by rendering the retargeted motion through differentiable skinning and projection, then extracting latent semantic embeddings from the LLM encoder of BLIP-2 via a two-step guiding visual question answering prompt ('Where are the hands?' → 'What is the character doing?'). A semantics consistency loss (MSE between source and target latent embeddings) and a signed distance field interpenetration loss jointly optimize the fine-tuning, with the two-stage design critical because the semantics loss is highly non-linear and requires good skeletal initialization to converge.

## Results

On the Mixamo dataset, SMT achieves the best performance across all six evaluation metrics: global MSE 0.284, local MSE 0.229, interpenetration rate 3.50%, ITM score 0.680, FID 0.436, and semantics consistency loss 0.143. The interpenetration rate is reduced from 7.62% (R2ET) to 3.50%, and the FID drops from 5.469 (R2ET) to 0.436, indicating substantially better semantic preservation. In the user study, over 92% of subjects prefer SMT's retargeting results, with the highest scores in overall quality (0.89) and semantics preservation (0.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMT outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against NKN [22] (Villegas, CVPR 2018) and SAN [2] (Aberman, ACM TOG 2020), which focus on skeleton-level features, SMT reduces interpenetration from 8.71%/9.74% to 3.50% and improves FID from 27.79/28.33 to 0.436, showing that skeleton-only methods suffer severe semantic loss. The closest competitor is R2ET [25] (Zhang, CVPR 2023), which uses a human-designed joint distance matrix as a semantic proxy; SMT still achieves a 12.5× lower FID (0.436 vs. 5.469) and 2.8× lower SCL (0.143 vs. 0.405), demonstrating that vision-language model–derived semantics capture higher-level motion meaning that joint-level representations miss. In the user study, SMT scores 0.89 in quality and 0.92 in semantics, versus R2ET's 0.80 and 0.85, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceptual terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-based motion retargeting line (NKN, SAN) by introducing a frozen vision-language model (BLIP-2) as an unsupervised semantic supervisor, a role no prior retargeting method has assigned to VLMs. It builds on the differentiable rendering pipeline from Soft Rasterizer to bridge the 3D motion domain into the 2D image domain where VLMs operate, and adopts the unsupervised training recipe (reconstruction, cycle consistency, adversarial loss) from NKN for the skeletal pre-training stage. The key departure from R2ET, the closest prior work, is replacing human-designed joint distance matrices with VLM-extracted latent embeddings, shifting semantics representation from hand-crafted geometric features to learned natural-language-aligned features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen, off-the-shelf VLM can serve as a practical semantic constraint for motion retargeting without any labeled motion-semantic data, which is a meaningful step toward reducing the manual artist intervention that plagues current pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to a small Mixamo subset (3 source and 3 target characters, 30 test motions), and the 2D projection inherently discards spatial depth information, limiting the fidelity of the semantic signal. The authors themselves flag that 3D VLMs fine-tuned for motion semantics are the natural next step, and the heavy dependency on a large frozen model (FlanT5-XXL) raises practical deployment questions for real-time or resource-constrained animation workflows.
