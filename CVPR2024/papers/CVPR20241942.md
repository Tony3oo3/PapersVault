## Problem

Existing text-to-image GANs represent images as discrete 2D pixel arrays, tying generation to a fixed spatial resolution and preventing natural extrapolation beyond training boundaries. While Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) offer a continuous parameterization that enables arbitrary-resolution generation, simply conditioning an unconditional INR-GAN on text embeddings yields unstable training and poor quality. The paper asks how to effectively condition INR-based generators on text while preserving the continuous-signal properties (extrapolation, super-resolution, resolution independence).

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt tokenized and padded to 77 tokens, from which it extracts both sentence-level embeddings (CLIP projection and EOT token) and word-level contextual features (512-dim per token) from a frozen CLIP text encoder. The output is a continuous RGB function: an MLP decoder predicts (R,G,B) at any (x,y) coordinate, trained on the grid [-1,1]² at 256² resolution but capable of sampling denser grids for super-resolution (up to 1024²) or extending beyond [-1,1]² for extrapolation (up to 512² and beyond) without retraining.

## Method

HyperCGAN builds on the INR-GAN backbone where a hypernetwork H(z) generates low-rank modulating matrices for an MLP decoder F_θ(z)(x,y). The key contribution is a two-stage weight modulation: first, sentence-level conditioning concatenates the noise vector z with a global text embedding to produce a base modulating tensor per layer; second, a Word-level Hyper Attention (WHAtt) mechanism applies scaled dot-product attention over C word-level modulating tensors (one per token) to select the most relevant word weights for each decoder layer. To make per-word full-rank tensors tractable, an Extreme Modulating Tensor Factorization (X-factorization) uses CP decomposition to represent each word's modulation as an outer product of two vectors, reducing parameter count by ~99.6%. Training uses standard conditional GAN objectives augmented with CLIP-based contrastive regularizers for both the discriminator and generator, and a LAFITE-style discriminator for text-image alignment.

## Results

On CUB 256², HyperCGAN achieves FID 14.12 and CLIP-R 51.87%, outperforming most discrete baselines including XMC-GAN (FID 15.56), DM-GAN (FID 16.09), and AttnGAN (FID 23.98). On COCO 256², it reaches FID 13.54 and CLIP-R 85.12%, competitive with VQ-Diffusion-F (FID 13.86) but trailing LAFITE (FID 8.12) and GALIP (FID 5.85). On the newly introduced ArtEmis 256² benchmark, HyperCGAN attains FID 15.89 and CLIP-R 55.23%, substantially outperforming XMC-GAN (CLIP-R 36.68%) and DF-GAN (CLIP-R 9.81%). The model uses only 57M parameters and is the sole method offering continuous image properties (extrapolation and super-resolution) alongside text conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperCGAN outperforms all pre-2022 discrete GAN baselines (AttnGAN, ControlGAN, DM-GAN, DAE-GAN, TIME, DF-GAN, SSA-GAN) on FID across all three datasets, with the largest margins on CUB (e.g., FID 14.12 vs. AttnGAN's 23.98). Against the strongest discrete competitors, HyperCGAN trails LAFITE (FID 8.12 vs. 13.54 on COCO; 10.48 vs. 14.12 on CUB) and GALIP (FID 5.85 on COCO), but uses fewer parameters (57M vs. 75M/82M) and uniquely provides continuous image properties. On the ArtEmis benchmark, HyperCGAN achieves the best CLIP-R (55.23%) among all compared methods, surpassing XMC-GAN (36.68%) by nearly 19 points, though LAFITE still leads on FID (12.04 vs. 15.89). Compared to the diffusion-based VQ-Diffusion-F (370M params, pre-trained on 7M Conceptual Captions), HyperCGAN achieves a slightly better COCO FID (13.54 vs. 13.86) with 6.5× fewer parameters and no pre-training. For extrapolation (Table 2), HyperCGAN achieves ScaleInv-FID of 62.01 at 2× and 85.4 at 4×, outperforming InfinityGAN's unconditional (103.13/153.64) and pseudo-conditional (132.24/120.32) variants. For super-resolution (Table 3), HyperCGAN matches AnyResGAN (pFID 34.64 vs. 34.68) in zero-shot fashion with fewer parameters (57M vs. 61M) and comparable inference speed (0.019s vs. 0.006s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconditional INR-GAN framework of Skorokhodov et al. (CVPR 2021) by introducing text conditioning through hypernetwork-based weight modulation, departing from the embedding-based conditioning paradigm used by discrete T2I GANs (AttnGAN, LAFITE, GALIP). The key conceptual shift is replacing hidden-state conditioning with direct weight modulation via a hypernetwork, where a novel word-level attention mechanism (WHAtt) selects relevant word-specific weight tensors, and an extreme CP-decomposition (X-factorization) makes the modulation computationally tractable. The discriminator and contrastive losses are borrowed from LAFITE, while the continuous representation inherits properties (extrapolation, arbitrary resolution) from the INR-GAN lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HyperCGAN establishes the first text-to-continuous-image generation pipeline, demonstrating that INR-based generators can be meaningfully conditioned on text while retaining zero-shot extrapolation and arbitrary-resolution sampling — properties fundamentally unavailable to discrete pixel-array GANs and diffusion models. However, the quality gap to SOTA discrete methods (FID 13.54 vs. 5.85 for GALIP on COCO) and persistent artifacts (blob patches, wavy textures, counting failures) indicate the paradigm is not yet competitive for production use. Open questions include whether the quality gap can be closed with better INR architectures or training strategies, whether the hypernetwork conditioning approach generalizes to higher resolutions or more complex scenes, and whether the ArtEmis affective benchmark will catalyze emotion-aware generation research.
