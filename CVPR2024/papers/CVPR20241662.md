## Problem

Existing SDS-based 3D human generation methods rely on text prompts extracted from the reference image, which capture only global semantics and cannot preserve fine texture details, leading to view-inconsistent appearances, over-saturation, and over-smoothing. The paper addresses this by introducing direct image-level guidance into the diffusion denoising process, enabling the generated 3D human to maintain photorealistic, view-consistent textures that match the input image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241662/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a clothed human and additionally extracts auxiliary signals (silhouette via Mask R-CNN, four body-region masks via human parsing, a text caption via BLIP-2, an SMPL-X body mesh, and front/back normal maps via ECON) to serve as optimization constraints. The output is a hash-encoded SDF network with two sub-networks (SDF value and color) rendered via orthographic volume rendering, optimized coarsely at 64×64 and refined up to 512×512 resolution, producing a complete 3D clothed human with geometry and view-consistent texture.

## Method

The core contribution is a Reference-Guided Score Distillation Sampling (Ref-SDS) loss that injects the reference image directly into the diffusion U-Net's attention mechanism: the reference image is first passed through the U-Net to cache latent features before each self-attention block, then these features are concatenated with the target's features and a 'ref-self-attention' operation transfers reference image information into the denoising of the rendered image. To ensure precise local correspondence, a region-aware attention mechanism uses human parsing masks to identify semantically matching body regions (e.g., head-to-head) and suppresses attention scores outside those regions by a factor γ=0.3. The SDF is initialized from the estimated SMPL-X body and optimized coarsely-to-finely using a combined loss of reconstruction (RGB + IoU), normal matching, Ref-SDS, perceptual, SDF pose constraint, and smoothness terms, with Stable Diffusion 1.5 and the UniPC scheduler providing the diffusion prior.

## Results

On 50 diverse in-the-wild human images, HumanRef achieves LPIPS 0.032, Contextual distance 1.969, and CLIP score 90.0%, outperforming the closest baseline TeCH (0.044, 2.882, 85.3%) by a wide margin. On the CAPE dataset, HumanRef attains PSNR 28.15, SSIM 0.949, LPIPS 0.061, and Contextual 2.16, while on THuman2.0 it reaches PSNR 25.70, SSIM 0.927, LPIPS 0.077, and Contextual 2.22. For geometry (Normal error), HumanRef achieves 0.0423 on CAPE and 0.0648 on THuman2.0, comparable to dedicated reconstruction methods like ECON (0.0422 / 0.0629).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanRef outperforms all six baselines on the primary generation metrics (LPIPS, Contextual, CLIP score). Against the closest competitor TeCH (a concurrent SDS-based method), HumanRef reduces LPIPS from 0.044 to 0.032 and Contextual distance from 2.882 to 1.969, while improving CLIP score from 85.3% to 90.0%, demonstrating substantially better view-consistent texture. Learning-based baselines PIFu and PaMIR trail further behind (LPIPS 0.054/0.050, Contextual 3.18/2.96), as their textures blur in invisible regions due to limited 3D training data. On geometry (Normal error on CAPE/THuman2.0), HumanRef (0.0423/0.0648) is essentially tied with ECON (0.0422/0.0629) and slightly behind on THuman2.0, while clearly beating FOF (0.0582/0.0742) and D-IF (0.0483/0.0682). Notably, HumanRef matches or beats dedicated reconstruction methods on geometry while simultaneously producing far superior textures (PSNR 28.15 vs. TeCH's 27.49 on CAPE), a combination none of the baselines achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

HumanRef extends the DreamFusion lineage of score distillation sampling (SDS) for 3D generation by replacing text-only guidance with direct image-level guidance injected into the diffusion U-Net's attention layers, drawing on the mutual self-attention mechanism from MASACTRL. It departs from concurrent work TeCH, which also applies SDS to 3D human generation but relies on text prompts and textual inversion, by arguing that text fundamentally cannot capture lower-level texture features needed for view-consistent appearance. The region-aware attention module further differentiates the method by leveraging human parsing to enforce body-part correspondence (head-to-head, torso-to-torso) during cross-image attention, a constraint absent from general-purpose SDS or VSD variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that image-level guidance in the denoising process is a more effective mechanism than text guidance for preserving fine texture details in 3D human generation, offering a practical recipe (Ref-SDS + region-aware attention) that other SDS-based pipelines could adopt. However, the method remains per-instance optimization-based, requiring SMPL-X fitting and multi-stage coarse-to-fine rendering, which limits throughput and scalability compared to feed-forward approaches. Open questions include whether the attention-injection strategy generalizes to other 3D generation tasks beyond humans, how it behaves under extreme poses or occlusions, and whether the orthographic rendering assumption holds for wider applications.
