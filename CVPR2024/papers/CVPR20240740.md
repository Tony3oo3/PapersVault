## Problem

Standard super-resolution methods optimize pixel-wise or generic perceptual losses, which do not guarantee reconstruction of the task-relevant high-frequency features needed for downstream recognition tasks like segmentation, detection, or classification. Because SR is ill-posed, these methods may restore visually plausible but task-irrelevant details, yielding only marginal improvements in recognition accuracy when processing low-resolution inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240740/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a low-resolution RGB image (bicubic-downsampled at ×4 or ×8) and produces a super-resolved image that is specifically optimized to benefit a chosen downstream task. The SR network (EDSR or SwinIR) is paired with a task-specific head (DeepLabV3 for segmentation, Faster-RCNN for detection, or ResNet-18 for classification), so the final outputs are task predictions—segmentation maps, bounding boxes with class labels, or a single class label—rather than just a standalone SR image. The SR images are evaluated both on task metrics (mIoU, mAP, Top-1 accuracy) and perceptual quality (LPIPS, PSNR).

## Method

SR4IR introduces a Task-Driven Perceptual (TDP) loss that computes feature-space distance between SR and HR images using the feature extractor of the downstream task network rather than a fixed VGG, thereby steering the SR network to restore task-relevant high-frequency content. To prevent the task network from learning shortcut features that would degrade TDP loss signals, a Cross-Quality patch Mix (CQMix) augmentation randomly replaces grid patches with either HR or SR versions during task-network training. The two networks are trained in an alternate framework: in phase 1 the SR network is updated with pixel loss plus TDP loss while the task network is frozen, and in phase 2 the task network is updated with task-specific loss on a mixture of HR, SR, and CQMix-augmented images while the SR network is frozen. This alternation avoids the domain gap of using a fixed feature extractor and prevents the joint-optimization collapse observed when both networks are trained simultaneously.

## Results

On semantic segmentation (PASCAL VOC2012, x4, SwinIR), SR4IR achieves 62.2 mIoU, approaching the 63.3 oracle (HR-trained) and surpassing the best SR baseline S→T (61.4) by +0.8. At x8 scale, the gap widens: SR4IR reaches 56.5 mIoU vs. 53.9 for S→T and 49.3 for bilinear-up, a +7.2 gain over bilinear. For object detection (x8, SwinIR), SR4IR attains 27.8 mAP, outperforming S→T (23.7) by +4.1 and bilinear-up (18.9) by +8.9. On image classification (x8, SwinIR), SR4IR improves Top-1 accuracy by +12.9% on StanfordCars (73.9% vs. 61.0% bilinear) and +7.1% on CUB-200-2011 (65.3% vs. 58.2% bilinear).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two external task-driven SR baselines, SR4IR clearly dominates: on x8-scale semantic segmentation it achieves 55.0 mIoU vs. 49.8 for TDSR [19] (+5.2) and 51.6 for SOD-MTGAN [3] (+3.4), and on x8-scale image classification it reaches 71.4% Top-1 vs. 56.4% (TDSR) and 68.1% (SOD-MTGAN). Internally, SR4IR outperforms all four training-protocol baselines (S→T, T→S, S+T, bilinear) across every task, scale, and SR architecture tested. The largest gains appear at x8 scale where high-frequency loss is most severe: +7.2 mIoU over bilinear for segmentation and +8.9 mAP over bilinear for detection with SwinIR. Notably, joint training (S+T) performs worse than sequential S→T, confirming that the alternate training framework is essential to avoid the TDP loss collapsing into a degenerate local optimum. SR4IR also achieves the best LPIPS in all settings, indicating that task-driven restoration does not sacrifice perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the perceptual loss concept from Johnson et al. (ECCV 2016) by replacing the fixed VGG feature space with the feature space of a task-specific network, creating the Task-Driven Perceptual (TDP) loss. It departs from prior task-driven SR methods (TDSR, SOD-MTGAN) that rely on joint reconstruction+detection losses or adversarial training, instead using a perceptual-loss formulation that is task-agnostic. The alternate training framework and CQMix augmentation represent a departure from both sequential (S→T, T→S) and joint (S+T) training paradigms, addressing a domain-gap problem that arises when the perceptual loss feature extractor is itself being trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, plug-and-play recipe for improving any downstream recognition task when only LR inputs are available, validated across three tasks and two SR architectures at two scale factors. A key open question is whether the approach extends to tasks where paired HR-LR training data is unavailable or to video/multi-frame settings. The requirement for a pre-trained task network and the sensitivity to the number of alternate training iterations (not fully ablated) are practical limitations. The finding that joint training with TDP loss catastrophically fails suggests broader implications for how perceptual losses should be integrated into multi-network training pipelines.
