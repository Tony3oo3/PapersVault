## Problem

Existing diffusion-based fine-grained image editing methods (e.g., DragDiff, DragonDiff) suffer from two key weaknesses: (1) editing results lack accuracy and exhibit unexpected artifacts in complex scenarios, and (2) deterministic ODE sampling limits flexibility, preventing the model from imagining new content needed to harmonize editing operations (e.g., generating an open mouth). Additionally, these methods underutilize the descriptive power of prompts, treating text as a simple condition rather than a detailed content specification.

## Input / Output

The method operates at 512×512 resolution in the latent space of Stable Diffusion V1.5. Given a source RGB image, an editing region mask, a text prompt, optional point-dragging instructions, and (for cross-image tasks) a reference image, it produces a single edited RGB image. The source image is additionally encoded by a trainable image prompt encoder (CLIP + QFormer) into 64 embedding tokens that are concatenated with text tokens in the cross-attention layers; for tasks with a reference image, tokens from both images are jointly fed into the cross-attention.

## Method

DiffEditor introduces three key mechanisms on top of the DragonDiff framework. First, a trainable image prompt encoder (CLIP image encoder → linear projection → 8-layer QFormer without self-attention, producing 64 tokens) is integrated into SD's cross-attention alongside text tokens, providing richer content description; only the linear layer and QFormer are trained via L2 loss on LAION data while SD and CLIP remain frozen. Second, regional SDE sampling replaces the deterministic ODE by injecting controlled stochasticity (η₁=0.4 inside the editing mask, η₂=0.2 outside) only during a designated time interval τ_SDE, balancing editing flexibility with content consistency. Third, a time travel strategy performs U=3 deterministic DDIM-inversion rollbacks (z_t ← z_{t-1}) per guidance step within interval τ_TT, enabling recurrent gradient refinement without the random noise that would break consistency. Regional score-based gradient guidance further prevents interference by masking E_edit to the editing region and E_content to the complement.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ face manipulation, DiffEditor achieves 17.05 MSE distance (17-point) and 11.52 (68-point), outperforming DragonDiff (18.51 / 13.94) and DragDiff (22.95 / 17.32), while attaining the best FID of 33.10/33.02 among all diffusion-based methods. On object pasting, moving, and appearance replacing, DiffEditor achieves CLIP distances of 0.274, 0.288, and 0.281 respectively, surpassing DragonDiff (0.260, 0.282, 0.263) and Self-Guidance (0.246, 0.243). Inference time is 13.88s per edit, lower than DragDiff (19.52s) and DragonDiff (15.00s), with a preparing cost of only 3.53s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffEditor outperforms DragonDiff on all face-manipulation metrics (17.05 vs 18.51 MSE at 17 points; 11.52 vs 13.94 at 68 points; FID 33.10 vs 35.75) and on all three cross-image tasks in Table 2 (CLIP 0.274/0.288/0.281 vs 0.260/0.282/0.263). Against DragDiff, the gains are even larger (17.05 vs 22.95 MSE at 17 points; FID 33.10 vs 38.06). DragGAN achieves slightly lower MSE on aligned faces (15.96 vs 17.05 at 17 points) but has substantially worse FID (39.27 vs 33.10) and cannot handle unaligned or general faces. DiffEditor decisively beats UserControllableLT (17.05 vs 32.32 MSE) and Self-Guidance on moving/replacing (0.288/0.281 vs 0.246/0.243 CLIP). On object pasting, DiffEditor edges out Paint-by-example (0.274 vs 0.265 CLIP) while better preserving object identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct extension of DragonDiff [28] (same first author), inheriting its score-based gradient guidance, visual cross-attention, and memory bank design while replacing the deterministic ODE sampling with a regional SDE strategy and adding a deterministic time-travel rollback mechanism inspired by DragDiff's iterative latent optimization. The image prompt encoder repurposes the QFormer-based architecture from IP-Adapter [51] and DALL-E2 [32]—previously applied to object customization—into the fine-grained editing pipeline, where prior methods (DragDiff, DragonDiff) had entirely ignored the conditioning prompt's role. The regional gradient guidance formalizes a practical fix for the known interference between editing and consistency energy terms in the DragonDiff framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, plug-and-play recipe (only the image prompt encoder is trained; the base SD and all editing logic are frozen) that simultaneously improves editing accuracy and flexibility, two properties that prior methods treated as a trade-off. The regional SDE and time-travel strategies are general enough to be adopted by other diffusion-based editing pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge a fundamental limitation: the base Stable Diffusion model lacks 3D object perception, so operations requiring substantial content imagination (e.g., rotating a car) still fail, pointing to a broader open question about whether 2D diffusion priors can support truly geometric editing without explicit 3D understanding.
