## Problem

Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation requires transferring image-text alignment from global captions to pixel-level predictions. Existing approaches either rely on expensive dense annotations or computationally intensive contrastive training on large image-caption pairs, and captions inherently lack fine-grained localization of individual concepts. This paper asks whether the localization knowledge embedded in diffusion models' cross-attention can replace contrastive training entirely, enabling a fully training-free segmentation pipeline.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image (resized to shorter side 448, processed with a 224-pixel sliding window) and an arbitrary set of free-form textual category names, and produces a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask. The offline stage consumes only the text of COCO Captions (not the associated images) to generate synthetic scenes and build a pre-indexed collection of textual-visual reference embeddings stored in a FAISS index. An optional threshold on the final combined similarity can flag pixels as belonging to an 'unknown' background class.

## Method

FreeDA operates in two phases. Offline, it conditions Stable Diffusion on COCO captions to generate synthetic images, extracts per-word attribution maps from the U-Net's cross-attention layers (averaged across heads, layers, and timesteps), binarizes them into weak localization masks, and pools DINOv2 dense features over each mask to produce visual prototypes; textual keys are formed by linearly interpolating template-averaged noun embeddings with full-caption CLIP embeddings. At inference, each query category retrieves K=350 nearest prototypes via cosine similarity in the FAISS index, and their mean forms a representative visual prototype. The input image is partitioned into class-agnostic regions using Felzenszwalb superpixels, each region is pooled in DINOv2 space to yield a local similarity against the category prototype, and this is combined with a global CLIP image-text similarity via a weighted sum (β=0.8). The pixel assignment is the argmax over categories of this combined score, requiring no learned parameters at any stage.

## Results

FreeDA achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on all five main benchmarks without any training. The ViT-L variant reaches 87.9, 43.5, 28.8, 36.7, and 23.2 mIoU on Pascal VOC, Pascal Context, COCO Stuff, Cityscapes, and ADE20K respectively, surpassing the best trained competitor (TCL with PAMR) by an average of 7.9 mIoU points. The ViT-B variant achieves 85.6, 43.1, 27.8, 36.7, and 22.4 mIoU with a 7.0-point average improvement. When the unknown/background category is included, FreeDA (ViT-L) scores 55.4, 38.3, and 37.4 mIoU on VOC, Context, and COCO Object, outperforming TCL+PAMR by 0.4, 7.9, and 5.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeDA outperforms all eight compared baselines across every benchmark. Against TCL [6] (the strongest trained competitor, using 21.7M trainable parameters on CC3M+CC12M), FreeDA (ViT-L) gains +10.9 mIoU on average without PAMR and +7.9 mIoU even when TCL is augmented with PAMR. OVDiff [18], the only other training-free diffusion-based method, trails FreeDA by 6.2 mIoU on VOC and 8.3 on ADE20K, and does not report results on Cityscapes or COCO Stuff. GroupViT [43] and MaskCLIP [52], both relying on contrastive training, are surpassed by 8–12 mIoU points on most datasets. ReCo [38] (retrieval-based, no training) lags FreeDA by 28–30 mIoU on VOC and 11–12 on ADE20K. In the unknown-category setting (Table 2), FreeDA beats TCL+PAMR by 0.4/7.9/5.8 mIoU on VOC/Context/COCO Object, and outperforms ViewCo [35], SegCLIP [27], and OVSegmentor [44] by 3–18 mIoU points depending on the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeDA departs from the dominant paradigm in open-vocabulary segmentation—contrastive training on large-scale image-caption pairs (TCL, GroupViT, MaskCLIP, OVSegmentor)—by replacing learned text-pixel alignment with a non-parametric retrieval-and-matching pipeline. It repurposes the cross-attention localization capability of Stable Diffusion (established by DAAM and DiffuMask) not for direct mask generation but for offline construction of context-aware visual prototypes, and combines DINOv2's semantic correspondence with CLIP's global classification in a way that no prior training-free method (ReCo, OVDiff) had attempted. The use of superpixels as class-agnostic region proposals bridges the gap between DINOv2's strong cross-image matching and its known weakness in boundary adherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a fully non-parametric, retrieval-based pipeline can outperform contrastively-trained methods by 7–10 mIoU points across five benchmarks challenges the assumption that gradient-based text-pixel alignment is necessary for competitive open-vocabulary segmentation. Practically, this shifts the computational burden from training to a one-time offline generation stage, making the approach accessible without large-scale training infrastructure. Open questions include whether the offline prototype collection scales gracefully to very large or domain-specific category sets, how sensitive performance is to the quality and diversity of the diffusion-generated scenes, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D segmentation where superpixel-based region proposals are less natural.
