## Problem

Existing ego-exo video datasets are small, lack time synchronization across cameras, are staged in lab or home settings, or are restricted to a single viewpoint, making it impossible to study fluid translation between first- and third-person perspectives for skilled human activity. No prior resource combines large-scale, time-synchronized multiview video with the rich multimodal signals (audio, gaze, IMU, 3D geometry) and domain-expert language needed to benchmark perception of skill execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241773/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sequence provides synchronized ego video from Aria glasses (8 MP RGB, two grayscale SLAM cameras, 7-channel audio, IMU, eye gaze) alongside 4–5 stationary GoPro exo streams, all localized in a gravity-aligned metric frame, with takes spanning 8 seconds to 42 minutes. The four benchmark families consume different subsets: correspondence takes query object masks in one view and predicts per-frame masks in the other; keystep recognition takes a trimmed ego clip and outputs one of 689 keystep labels; proficiency estimation takes ego+exo video and outputs either a 4-level skill class or temporally localized (timestamp, good/needs-improvement, probability) tuples; ego pose takes ego video and outputs 17 body and 21 per-hand 3D joint positions per frame. The energy-efficient variant additionally constrains total power (sensor + inference + memory) to 20 mW, forcing the model to select which modality to activate at each timestep.

## Method

The primary contribution is the dataset and benchmark suite rather than a single architecture. A low-cost (<$3,000) rig pairs Aria glasses with 4–5 GoPros, auto-synchronized and auto-segmented into takes via a QR-code procedure, enabling continuous captures up to ~60 minutes across 740 participants in 123 real-world scenes spanning 8 domains and 43 activities. Three time-indexed language corpora are annotated: 117,812 expert commentary pieces from 52 domain coaches/teachers, first-person narrate-and-act descriptions (~10% of takes), and 432K third-party atomic action sentences, totaling over 200,000 annotator-hours. Baselines employ CNN and Vision Transformer backbones trained under supervised and weakly-supervised paradigms; the procedure-understanding task in particular offers two supervision levels (instance-level with labeled segments vs. procedure-level with only keystep names), and the energy-efficient task introduces a power-budgeted online detection formulation that measures total device power rather than FLOPs or FPS alone.

## Results

Ego-Exo4D is a dataset paper whose primary quantitative contribution is scale: 1,286 hours of time-synchronized ego-exo video from 740 participants across 123 scenes in 13 cities, comprising 5,035 instances (8 sec to 42 min each) spanning 8 domains, 43 activities, and 689 keysteps. The annotation effort exceeds 200,000 hours, yielding 117,812 time-stamped expert commentary pieces, 432K atomic action descriptions, and ~14M frames of 3D body/hand pose ground truth. Baseline models for all four benchmark task families (relation, recognition, proficiency, ego-pose) are provided in the appendices, with the first public challenges scheduled for 2024. No single headline accuracy number dominates the paper, as the contribution is the benchmark suite itself rather than a state-of-the-art method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare a proposed method against existing baselines in the traditional sense; it is a dataset and benchmark introduction. Instead, it develops baseline models for each of the four new task families (ego-exo correspondence/translation, keystep recognition, proficiency estimation, and ego-pose) to provide starting points for the research community. The baselines are detailed in the appendices (Appendices 13.A–13.D) and are intended to establish initial performance levels rather than to claim state-of-the-art results. The paper positions itself against prior ego-exo datasets (CMU-MMAC, CharadesEgo, Assembly101, H2O, Homage) on scale and diversity—offering an order of magnitude more participants (740 vs. 27–71), hours (1,286 vs. 5–513), and scenes (123 vs. 1–2)—but does not run head-to-head method comparisons on a shared benchmark. The energy-efficient keystep recognition task introduces a novel 20 mW power budget constraint that prior efficiency benchmarks (which optimize FLOPs or FPS) do not address.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ego-Exo4D extends the ego-exo video line initiated by small-scale efforts like CMU-MMAC, CharadesEgo, Assembly101, and H2O, which were limited to lab settings, few participants, or unsynchronized captures. It also builds on the egocentric video tradition (Ego4D, EPIC-Kitchens) and instructional video datasets (HowTo100M, COIN), but departs by focusing specifically on skilled activities captured in authentic real-world settings with time-synchronized multi-view video. The use of Project Aria glasses for rich multimodal ego capture (gaze, IMU, 7-channel audio, SLAM cameras, point clouds) and the introduction of expert commentary as a language modality represent novel contributions not present in any prior dataset. The four benchmark task families—particularly ego-exo correspondence and translation across extreme viewpoint changes—are new formulations that go beyond prior cross-view matching or synthesis work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 1,286 hours across 740 participants in 13 cities, Ego-Exo4D is an order of magnitude larger than any prior ego-exo resource and provides the first large-scale testbed for cross-view learning between first- and third-person perspectives. The expert commentary (117,812 time-stamped critiques from 52 domain experts) is a genuinely novel language resource that surfaces subtle execution differences invisible to non-experts, opening avenues for AI coaching and proficiency estimation that prior datasets cannot support. Key open questions include whether models can truly translate fluently between ego and exo views under extreme viewpoint and occlusion changes, whether proficiency can be reliably estimated from video alone across diverse skill levels, and whether the energy-efficient formulation (20 mW budget) is achievable with current architectures. The dataset's gated licensing and the 2024 challenge launch will shape the research agenda for ego-exo perception for years to come.
