## Problem

Existing 3D human motion prediction methods focus exclusively on major body joints (17 or 25) and neglect fine-grained hand gestures and their interaction with objects. This paper introduces the novel task of forecasting 3D whole-body human poses (all 55 joints including both hands) during object grasping, which requires handling two distinct challenges: internal heterogeneity across body parts with different motion patterns, and external interactivity between human and object modalities that varies dynamically over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241166/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical 3D whole-body pose sequence of 55 joints (25 body + 15 left hand + 15 right hand) over T=30 frames at 30 fps, partitioned into three separate components, along with an object mesh downsampled to 1024 vertices as a point cloud. It produces a future whole-body pose sequence of the same 55 joints over ΔT=30 frames. The observation and prediction windows are equal in length (1 second each), and the object mesh is treated as a static shape feature across the entire sequence.

## Method

C3HOST (cross-context cross-modal consolidation) processes the three body parts independently through DCT (temporal) and fully-connected GCN (spatial) encoders, then applies maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) to align the heterogeneous feature distributions across body, left hand, and right hand. A circular cross-attention mechanism captures internal interactions between body parts. For external human-object interaction, PointNet++ encodes the object mesh, a gated sharing unit modulates per-part human-object distance information over time, and a learnable cross-modal alignment (CMA) with a mixing factor α harmonizes the marginal distributions of human and object features. A graph attention network (GAT) then fuses all four modalities (object, body, left hand, right hand) as graph nodes with learned attention weights, avoiding the redundancy of direct feature concatenation. Training uses a composite loss combining joint L2 error, bone-length L1 constraint, hand-object distance L1 loss, and the MMD alignment term.

## Results

On the GRAB dataset (w/ object), C3HOST achieves a whole-body MPJPE of 106.8 mm at 1.0 s, outperforming the best baseline SPGSN (120.0 mm) by 13.2 mm and LTD (119.7 mm) by 12.9 mm. The advantage grows with prediction horizon: at 0.8 s the gap is 11.4 mm over SPGSN (84.3 vs 95.7 mm), while at 0.2 s the gap narrows to 0.8 mm (19.2 vs 20.0 mm). On the BEHAVE dataset, C3HOST achieves an average whole-body MPJPE of 69.7 mm, beating SPGSN (78.4 mm) and LTD (77.1 mm) by 8.7 and 7.4 mm respectively. For hand-specific prediction at 1.0 s, C3HOST reduces right-hand error to 161.5 mm (vs. 187.0 mm for LTD) and left-hand error to 153.3 mm (vs. 177.4 mm for LTD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C3HOST outperforms all four baselines (LTD, DMGNN, PGBIG, SPGSN) on every body part and prediction horizon in the w/object setting on GRAB. The largest gains appear at longer horizons: at 1.0 s whole-body, C3HOST (106.8 mm) beats SPGSN (120.0 mm) by 13.2 mm and DMGNN (126.4 mm) by 19.6 mm. For hand prediction at 1.0 s, C3HOST reduces right-hand MPJPE to 161.5 mm versus 187.0 mm (LTD) and 169.3 mm (SPGSN), and left-hand MPJPE to 153.3 mm versus 177.4 mm (LTD) and 157.5 mm (SPGSN). On the BEHAVE dataset, C3HOST achieves 69.7 mm average whole-body error, a 7.4–13.3 mm improvement over LTD (77.1), SPGSN (78.4), and PGBIG (83.0). Notably, SPGSN is the strongest baseline overall, but C3HOST's cross-context alignment and cross-modal interaction learning yield consistent 10–20 mm reductions in the object-grasping setting where baselines neglect heterogeneity between body parts and human-object modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human motion prediction line (LTD, DMGNN, PGBIG, SPGSN) from major body joints (17–25) to a 55-joint whole-body formulation that explicitly includes both hands, and further incorporates object mesh geometry via PointNet++ as an additional input modality. The architectural backbone reuses the fully-connected GCN from Mao et al. (LTD) for spatial encoding and DCT for temporal encoding, but the key departure is the introduction of MMD-based cross-context alignment to harmonize heterogeneous body-part features and a GAT-based cross-modal fusion to integrate object features without direct concatenation. The gated distance-sharing unit and circular cross-attention are novel components not present in prior motion prediction or human-object interaction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in human-robot interaction where hand-level precision during grasping is critical but has been systematically neglected by body-only predictors. The ablation revealing that naive object-feature fusion degrades performance (64.8→65.6 mm) and that alignment is necessary before fusion is a useful cautionary finding for multimodal motion prediction. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets, reliance on retrained (not original) baselines, and a fixed 1-second prediction horizon; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-object scenes, longer horizons, or real-time deployment in robotics.
