## Problem

Existing federated dynamic pruning frameworks for cross-device FL suffer from two critical issues: significant post-pruning accuracy degradation caused by hastily eliminating low-magnitude parameters that still carry useful information, and high activation memory usage that is overlooked because these methods focus only on reducing model size. No prior method simultaneously addresses both accuracy preservation and activation memory reduction under the strict resource constraints of edge devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240157/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/CINIC-10, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) distributed non-IID across K=100 devices (Dirichlet α=0.5), and produces class labels via ResNet18 or MobileNetV2. Unusually, the optimization is jointly constrained by both a target parameter sparsity (s_tm) and a target activation sparsity (s_ta), both set to 0.9 by default, reflecting the dual memory budget of weights and intermediate caches on the device. The output is a specialized sparse model per device whose mask structure is periodically adjusted by the server through magnitude-based pruning and gradient-magnitude-based growing.

## Method

FedMef introduces two components. First, budget-aware extrusion (BaE) salvages information from parameters marked for pruning by training with a surrogate loss that adds L2 regularization on the lowest-magnitude unpruned parameters, transferring their signal to surviving weights within a limited training budget; a modified REX schedule with a sigmoid-scaled term (2σ(‖θ_low‖)−1) dynamically boosts the learning rate when extrusion is incomplete. Second, scaled activation pruning (SAP) prunes activation caches during the forward pass and uses the sparse caches directly for backward gradient computation, replacing batch normalization with Normalized Sparse Convolution (NSConv) that applies filter-wise weight standardization (Eq. 7) to guarantee zero-mean outputs regardless of batch size, thereby making activation sparsification effective even under the small-batch, non-IID conditions typical of edge FL.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18 at 90% parameter sparsity, FedMef achieves 78.1% accuracy, outperforming FedDST (76.2%) and FedTiny (76.1%) by ~2%, while incurring only 0.003× additional FLOPs and 0.005× additional communication over baselines. At 80% sparsity, FedMef reaches 81.7%, surpassing even the unpruned FedAvg baseline (81.2%). On MobileNetV2/CIFAR-10, FedMef reduces memory footprint by 28.5% relative to FedTiny while improving accuracy by 2.13%. Across all three datasets (CIFAR-10, CINIC-10, TinyImageNet) and both architectures (ResNet18, MobileNetV2), FedMef consistently achieves the best accuracy-memory trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMef outperforms all baselines across every sparsity level and dataset tested. Against FedDST [3], FedMef gains +1.9% accuracy at 90% sparsity (78.1% vs 76.2%) and +2.0% at 80% sparsity (81.7% vs 79.7%) on CIFAR-10/ResNet18, with negligible additional compute (0.116× vs 0.113× FLOPs). Against FedTiny [17], the primary SOTA baseline, FedMef improves accuracy by +2.0% at 90% sparsity and +1.4% at 80% sparsity, while also reducing memory footprint by 28.5% on MobileNetV2. Against FL-PQSU [45] (a static pruning lower bound), FedMef achieves substantially higher accuracy at equivalent sparsity. Notably, at 80% sparsity FedMef (81.7%) even exceeds the unpruned FedAvg baseline (81.2%), demonstrating that the budget-aware extrusion mechanism effectively preserves model capacity despite aggressive pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedMef extends the federated dynamic pruning pipeline established by FedTiny and FedDST (which themselves build on RigL-style dynamic sparse training) by addressing two gaps those methods leave open: post-pruning accuracy degradation and activation memory overhead. For the accuracy issue, it draws on the Dual Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and the REX budgeted schedule to introduce budget-aware extrusion, a surrogate-loss mechanism that transfers information from low-magnitude parameters before they are pruned. For the memory issue, it adapts DropIT's forward-pass activation pruning but replaces the batch-normalization dependency with Normalized Sparse Convolution (NSConv), a filter-wise weight standardization technique borrowed from unnormalized ResNet work, enabling effective activation sparsification under the small-batch, non-i.i.d. conditions typical of cross-device FL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical blind spot in federated pruning research: prior methods optimize parameter sparsity while ignoring activation caches, which dominate memory on edge models like MobileNetV2. By jointly addressing both, FedMef offers a more realistic path to deploying FL on genuinely resource-constrained devices. However, evaluation is limited to three small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10, CINIC-10, TinyImageNet) and two architectures (ResNet18, MobileNetV2), leaving open whether the 28.5% memory reduction and accuracy gains hold for larger models, higher-resolution inputs, or more heterogeneous device fleets. The NSConv replacement for BN also raises questions about applicability to architectures where filter-wise standardization interacts poorly with depthwise or inverted residual blocks.
