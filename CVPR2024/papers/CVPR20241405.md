## Problem

Existing WSI analysis methods either treat slides as instance-bags (MIL) that ignore inter-patch interactions, or construct graphs using fixed spatial topologies that restrict flexible information exchange between distant patches. Additionally, conventional GNN-based approaches model WSIs as undirected graphs, overlooking the directed contributions between neighboring entities, and suffer from over-parameterization that reduces generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes histopathology WSIs at 20× magnification, segments foreground tissue via Otsu thresholding, and divides it into 256×256 non-overlapping patches. A ViT-Small encoder pretrained on ImageNet extracts 384-dimensional patch features, which are expanded to 512 dimensions. The output is a WSI-level probability vector for cancer typing (2–3 histological subtypes) or staging (4 TNM stages), trained with cross-entropy loss under a weakly supervised paradigm using only slide-level labels.

## Method

WiKG constructs a dynamic directed graph where each patch is assigned separate head and tail embeddings via linear projections; directed edges are formed by computing head-tail dot-product similarity and selecting top-k neighbors, with edge embeddings computed as a weighted interpolation of the source tail and target head. A knowledge-aware attention mechanism then aggregates neighbor information by scoring each (head, edge, tail) triplet through a tanh-based nonlinearity followed by softmax, allowing the head node to selectively capture signals from its neighbors. Node updates employ a dual-interaction mechanism combining additive and element-wise multiplicative fusion of the original head with the aggregated neighbor representation. Finally, a global pooling readout produces the graph-level embedding for classification, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a single WSI per batch (batch size 1) using Adam at lr=1e-4.

## Results

On the three TCGA typing benchmarks, WiKG achieves 90.37% accuracy / 95.23% AUC on ESCA, 97.08% / 99.65% on KIDNEY, and 84.02% / 90.78% on LUNG, outperforming all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. For the more challenging staging task, WiKG reaches 69.96% AUC (ESCA), 69.71% (KIDNEY), and 60.34% (LUNG), again leading all competitors. On the in-house FROZEN-LUNG generalization test, WiKG attains 87.06% accuracy and 87.05% F1, surpassing the best MIL baseline (DSMIL, 86.18% acc) while exhibiting the lowest per-class standard deviation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WiKG outperforms all eight baseline families across every dataset and metric. Among MIL methods, the closest competitor is DTFD-MIL (88.81% acc on ESCA, 96.27% on KIDNEY, 82.41% on LUNG), which WiKG surpasses by 1.6, 0.8, and 1.6 percentage points in accuracy respectively; CLAM-SB and DSMIL trail by 1–3 points. Among graph-based methods, Patch-GCN (88.30/96.68/79.72 acc) and GTP (78.03/83.54/78.07 acc) are the most relevant spatial-topology baselines; WiKG leads Patch-GCN by 2.1, 0.4, and 4.3 points and GTP by a wide margin (up to 12 points on ESCA). HIPT, the strongest hierarchical ViT baseline, is beaten by 1.1, 0.2, and 3.6 points on the three datasets. On the FROZEN-LUNG generalization test, WiKG (87.06% acc) edges out DSMIL (86.18%) and CLAM-SB (84.71%), while TransMIL and ABMIL degrade severely (70.29% and 70.15% acc), highlighting WiKG's superior cross-domain robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

WiKG carves new ground at the intersection of knowledge graph representation and histopathology WSI analysis. While prior graph-based WSI methods (Patch-GCN, GTP, H2-MIL) construct edges from explicit spatial proximity, and MIL methods (ABMIL, CLAM, TransMIL) aggregate patches without modeling inter-patch topology, WiKG repurposes the knowledge graph triplet structure (head, relation, tail) from recommendation systems to dynamically construct directed edges between patches via learnable embeddings. The knowledge-aware attention mechanism draws on KGAT-style scoring over triplets, replacing standard GNN message passing (GCN, GIN, GAT) with a mechanism that jointly weighs neighbor and edge information. The ViT encoder and MIL training paradigm (slide-level labels only) are inherited from the broader WSI literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic directed graph formulation addresses a concrete limitation of spatial-topology GNNs: patches that are morphologically related but spatially distant cannot interact, and the directionality of contextual influence between patches is ignored. The in-house FROZEN-LUNG generalization experiment is practically valuable, showing WiKG maintains balanced per-class accuracy where other methods exhibit large inter-class gaps. However, staging performance remains modest across all methods (AUC ~60-70%), suggesting that morphology alone is insufficient for staging and that integrating clinical metadata or multi-modal signals is an open challenge. The authors also flag interpretability and graph pooling as unresolved directions.
