## Problem

Modeling high-frequency view-dependent appearance (specular highlights, glossy interreflections) in NeRF remains difficult because existing analytical directional encodings are angular-only and require large MLPs to fit spatio-angular details, leading to slow convergence and inference. Near-field interreflections are especially problematic since different surface points query the same angular encoding, causing spatially varying reflection components to be averaged out during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240176/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces rendered novel-view images at arbitrary viewpoints. The per-pixel color is decomposed into a diffuse component, a specular tint, and a specular color decoded from a small MLP conditioned on spatial features, a directional encoding, and the cosine of the viewing angle. A notable output property is the natural separation of far-field (environment) and near-field (interreflection) reflections, enabling selective rendering or removal of each component for editability.

## Method

NDE transfers the feature-grid encoding paradigm from the spatial to the angular domain using two complementary representations: a global cubemap feature grid (mip-mapped with GGX kernels) for far-field reflections, and a mip-mapped tri-plane spatial volume cone-traced along the reflected ray for near-field interreflections. The key insight is a spatio-spatial parameterization—cone tracing accumulates spatial features along the reflected direction, making the encoding spatially varying and capable of modeling multi-bounce interreflections that angular-only encodings cannot capture. Far- and near-field features are composited using cone-traced opacity in a volume-rendering-style blend, then fed into a tiny 2-layer MLP (64 width) to produce the specular color. Training uses a Charbonnier loss in tone-mapped space plus Eikonal and density-matching regularization, with an occupancy grid to skip empty space during both primary ray marching and cone tracing. A real-time variant (NDE-RT) bakes spatial features onto a marching-cubes mesh and evaluates only the cubemap lookup and cone tracing per pixel, achieving 66 FPS in WebGL.

## Results

On synthetic specular scenes (Shinny Blender + NeRF Synthetic Materials), NDE achieves a mean PSNR of 37.19, SSIM of 0.982, and LPIPS of 0.022, outperforming all baselines on PSNR and LPIPS while tying ENVIDR on SSIM. The method uses only 75k color-MLP parameters (vs. 521k for Ref-NeRF and 454k for NeRO), enabling 3.03 FPS offline color evaluation and 66 FPS real-time rendering (NDE-RT) on an NVIDIA 3090, compared to 0.08–0.55 FPS for baselines. On the Materials scene specifically, NDE (31.53 PSNR) trails Ref-NeRF (35.41) due to SDF limitations on concave geometry, but still produces perceptually better appearance with a much smaller network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDE outperforms Ref-NeRF [48] by +1.31 dB mean PSNR (37.19 vs. 35.88) and halves the LPIPS (0.022 vs. 0.053), while using 7× fewer color-MLP parameters (75k vs. 521k) and running 38× faster (3.03 vs. 0.08 FPS). Against ENVIDR [26], NDE gains +2.24 dB PSNR and matches SSIM (0.982) with a 5.5× speed advantage (3.03 vs. 0.55 FPS). Compared to NeRO [31], NDE improves PSNR by +6.58 dB and LPIPS by 5× (0.022 vs. 0.109), with a 27× speedup. The sole exception is the Materials scene where Ref-NeRF's non-SDF geometry handles concave shapes better (35.41 vs. 31.53 PSNR). For real-time rendering, NDE-RT (66 FPS) substantially outperforms 3DGS (235 FPS but only 30.30 PSNR) in visual quality while remaining above 60 FPS, and the PSNR gap to the offline model (35.48 vs. 37.19) is attributed to marching-cube mesh extraction artifacts at object boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

NDE extends the feature-grid paradigm that accelerated spatial encoding in NeRF variants (Instant-NGP, DVGO, TensoRF) into the angular domain, replacing the analytical directional encodings used by Ref-NeRF's IDE and ENVIDR with a learnable cubemap of feature vectors. The key conceptual departure is a spatio-spatial reparameterization of view-dependent appearance: instead of the standard spatio-angular formulation where direction is encoded independently of position, NDE cone-traces a spatial feature volume along the reflected ray, making the directional encoding spatially varying and naturally capturing multi-bounce interreflections. The geometry backbone follows VolSDF's SDF-to-density conversion, and the near-field representation uses a mip-mapped tri-plane (à la Tri-MipRF) rather than a hash grid, motivated by the need for consistent multi-resolution filtering of the indirect density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practical impact is enabling real-time (66–75 FPS) rendering of specular objects with quality matching or exceeding offline baselines, a regime previously infeasible (Ref-NeRF runs at 0.02 FPS). The small MLP requirement (75k vs. 521k parameters) suggests the cubemap-plus-cone-tracing decomposition is a more efficient inductive bias for high-frequency angular signals than large decoder networks. Limitations remain: sensitivity to surface normal quality prevents use of faster hash-grid SDFs, training still takes 10–18 hours per scene, and multi-bounce editability is restricted. Open questions include whether the spatio-spatial parameterization extends to transparent or subsurface-scattering materials, and whether the cubemap encoding can be made resolution-adaptive for unbounded scenes.
