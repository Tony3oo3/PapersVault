## Problem

Monocular depth estimation is ill-posed due to scale ambiguity, and existing approaches rely solely on visual cues or generic regularizers that lack scene-specific information. The paper asks whether two inherently ambiguous modalities—an image (ambiguous in scale) and a text caption (ambiguous in spatial layout)—can be combined to resolve each other's deficits and produce metric-scaled depth maps. To the best of the authors' knowledge, no prior work has modeled language as an explicit variational prior for depth estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (480×640 for NYU Depth V2, 352×1216 for KITTI) and a short text caption (generated by ExpansionNet v2) as joint input, and produces a dense per-pixel depth map of matching spatial resolution. The text caption is encoded by a frozen CLIP ResNet-50 text encoder into a 1024-d feature, while the image is processed by a Swin-L transformer backbone. At inference, no stochastic sampling is required: the conditional sampler deterministically selects the latent from the text-learned distribution, yielding a single depth map. A secondary mode allows text-only input to generate plausible depth maps by sampling from the learned latent distribution.

## Method

WorDepth encodes the text caption through a frozen CLIP text encoder followed by a 3-layer MLP that predicts the mean μ and standard deviation σ of a 128-d latent distribution over plausible metric-scaled depth maps (the text-VAE). An image-based conditional sampler (Swin-L backbone) predicts patch-wise noise vectors ε̃ of shape d×h×w from the input image, which replace the standard Gaussian ε in the reparameterization trick to select a specific latent z = μ + ε̃·σ, thereby conditioning the prior on observed layout and object shapes. The latent is spatially duplicated to d×h×w and decoded by a 3-layer convolutional up-sampling network into the final depth map, with skip connections from the conditional sampler injected into the decoder. Training uses an alternating optimization scheme: with probability p=1%, the text-VAE branch is updated (sampling ε~N(0,1)); with probability 1−p, the conditional sampler is updated (predicting ε̃ from the image), both minimizing a scale-invariant depth loss plus a KL-divergence regularizer that keeps the predicted noise near a standard Gaussian. The CLIP text encoder remains frozen throughout, and the depth decoder is shared across both branches.

## Results

On NYU Depth V2, WorDepth achieves δ<1.25 = 0.932, Abs Rel = 0.088, log₁₀ = 0.038, and RMSE = 0.317, outperforming the Swin-L baseline (0.910 / 0.098 / 0.043 / 0.351) by +2.42%, −10.20%, −11.63%, and −9.69% respectively. On KITTI (Eigen Split), WorDepth reaches δ<1.25 = 0.979, Abs Rel = 0.049, RMSE_log = 0.074, and RMSE = 2.039, improving over the baseline by +1.03%, −9.26%, −12.94%, and −12.97%. In zero-shot transfer from NYU to SUN-RGBD, WorDepth achieves δ<1.25 = 0.833 and Abs Rel = 0.123, substantially outperforming the baseline (0.803 / 0.141) and prior methods like AdaBins (0.771 / 0.159).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorDepth outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every metric for both NYU Depth V2 and KITTI. On NYU, the closest competitors are DepthFormer (Swin-L, δ<1.25 = 0.923, Abs Rel = 0.094) and NeWCRFs (Swin-L, δ<1.25 = 0.922, Abs Rel = 0.095); WorDepth surpasses them by +0.9 and +1.0 points on δ<1.25 and by 0.006–0.007 in Abs Rel. The language-based baselines DepthCLIP (δ<1.25 = 0.394) and CLIPMDE (0.465) are far behind, confirming that implicit CLIP-based approaches cannot match an explicit variational language prior. On KITTI, WorDepth leads DepthFormer (RMSE 2.039 vs. 2.143) and NeWCRFs (2.039 vs. 2.129) by roughly 5% in RMSE. In the zero-shot SUN-RGBD transfer, WorDepth (δ<1.25 = 0.833, Abs Rel = 0.123) beats DepthFormer (0.815 / 0.137) and AdaBins (0.771 / 0.159) by a wide margin, suggesting language priors generalize better across domains than purely visual features.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorDepth carves new ground by repurposing the variational autoencoder framework for depth estimation in a fundamentally different way: prior VAE-based depth methods (VDN, CodeSLAM, Xia et al.) use the image to parameterize the latent distribution's mean, whereas here the text caption provides the mean and standard deviation, and the image serves only to select a specific sample via a conditional sampler. This departs from vision-language depth approaches (DepthCLIP, CLIPMDE, VPD) that treat language as an implicit feature or cross-attention signal; instead, WorDepth explicitly models language as a probabilistic prior over scene layouts. The alternating optimization scheme between the text-VAE and the image-based conditional sampler is a novel training procedure not seen in prior depth or VAE literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that two inherently ambiguous modalities—image (ambiguous in scale) and text (ambiguous in layout)—can be combined to resolve each other's deficits, directly addressing the long-standing scale ambiguity problem in monocular depth estimation. The zero-shot transfer to SUN-RGBD suggests language priors generalize more robustly across domains than visual signals, opening a path toward more transferable depth models. However, the method's effectiveness is bounded by caption quality: vague or incorrect descriptions provide little benefit or can actively mislead, and the reliance on an external captioner (ExpansionNet v2) introduces a practical dependency that limits deployment flexibility. An open question is whether this variational language-prior framework extends naturally to other 3D reconstruction tasks (e.g., structure-from-motion, novel view synthesis) where scale ambiguity is equally problematic.
