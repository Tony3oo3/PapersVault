## Problem

Existing segmentation models are specialists tied to specific tasks and data distributions, requiring expensive retraining for new scenarios. Unified models like SAM and SEEM rely on point-based prompts that lack semantic concept awareness, while UniNext and SegGPT focus on instance-level or visual in-context paradigms that cannot handle arbitrary granularity from fine-grained parts to whole scenes. The paper asks whether language, as a flexible and informative prompt, can serve as a universal interface for segmentation at any semantic level.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 480×480) and a free-form language prompt as input, producing a binary segmentation mask at the original image resolution. The language prompt is task-adaptive: natural captions for referring image/video segmentation, 'all {category}' for semantic and part segmentation, and 'the most salient object' for salient object detection. For video tasks, the model processes frames independently without explicit temporal modeling, and no data augmentation or post-processing is applied during inference.

## Method

UniLSeg employs a two-stream visual-linguistic decoding architecture built on a Swin Transformer vision encoder and CLIP text encoder. A Pre-Fusion module uses multi-head cross-attention (visual features as Query, word embeddings as Key/Value) to roughly activate target regions before full decoding. The Vision Path applies a Multi-Modal Transformer (self-attention over concatenated visual and word tokens, followed by cross-attention) plus an FPN to integrate multi-scale features, while the symmetric Language Path updates sentence-level embeddings with visual context via cross-attention to produce content-aware prompts. The final mask is generated by computing similarity between activated visual features and content-aware linguistic embeddings, followed by bilinear upsampling and binarization. Training uses a two-stage strategy: pre-training on 20% or 100% of SA-1B with automatically generated pseudo caption-mask pairs (via SAM, BLIP, RAM, and Grounding DINO), followed by fine-tuning on supervised multi-task data, with a hide-and-seek patch-hiding strategy to mitigate pseudo-label noise.

## Results

On the primary referring image segmentation benchmarks, UniLSeg-100 achieves 81.74 oIoU on RefCOCO val, 73.18 on RefCOCO+ val, and 79.27 on G-Ref val, surpassing the best proprietary method (PolyFormer: 74.82/67.64/67.76) by 6.9/5.5/11.5 points and the best unified model (UniNext: 80.32/70.04/73.41) by 1.4/3.1/5.9 points. On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (ADE20K-150), UniLSeg-100 reaches 29.5 mIoU without any additional large multi-modal model, outperforming the best prior method OVSeg (24.8) by 4.7 points. On salient object detection, UniLSeg-100 attains 0.961 F_mean on ECSSD and 0.889 on PascalS, leading all baselines. On part segmentation (PartImageNet), it achieves 63.87 Val IoU, exceeding SegFormer (61.97) by 1.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniLSeg-100 outperforms all proprietary RIS methods by a wide margin: it exceeds PolyFormer (the strongest specialist) by 6.9 oIoU on RefCOCO val and 11.5 on G-Ref val, and surpasses GRES and LAVT by 7.9 and 9.0 points respectively on G-Ref val. Against unified segmentation models, UniLSeg-100 beats UniNext (the strongest unified competitor) by 1.4 oIoU on RefCOCO val, 3.1 on RefCOCO+ val, and 5.9 on G-Ref val, while SEEM reports only a G-Ref val score of 65.60, trailing UniLSeg-100 by 13.7 points. On referring video object segmentation (Ref-YouTubeVOS), UniLSeg-100 achieves 64.9 J&F, edging out VLT (63.8) and SOC (62.4). For open-vocabulary segmentation, UniLSeg-100 (29.5 mIoU on ADE20K-150) surpasses all prior methods including those leveraging CLIP or ALIGN, with a 4.7-point lead over OVSeg. On salient object detection, UniLSeg-100 leads ICON (0.936) by 2.5 points on ECSSD F_mean, and on part segmentation it exceeds SegFormer by 1.9 IoU on PartImageNet validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniLSeg departs from the dominant point-based interaction paradigm of SAM and SEEM, and from the visual in-context learning approach of Painter and SegGPT, by proposing language as the universal prompt for segmentation. It also moves beyond the object-centric focus of UniNext, which limits granularity to instance-level, by supporting arbitrary semantic levels from fine-grained parts to whole scenes. The architecture draws on established components—Swin Transformer backbone, CLIP text encoder, multi-modal cross-attention, and FPN—combined with the hide-and-seek strategy [61] for robust pseudo-label training, while the automatic annotation engine repurposes SAM, BLIP, RAM, and Grounding DINO as a pipeline for generating caption-mask pairs from unlabeled data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The language-instruction paradigm offers a natural, scalable interface for segmentation that avoids the ambiguity of point prompts and the rigidity of visual exemplars, potentially simplifying the design of future unified models. A key open question is whether this approach generalizes to truly novel tasks or semantic levels beyond the six evaluated, and whether the frame-by-frame video processing (acknowledged limitation) can be extended with temporal modeling. The finding that multi-task joint training without large-scale pre-training actually degrades short-text task performance highlights a practical tension in multi-task learning that remains unresolved.
