## Problem

Existing social interaction modeling for trajectory prediction is split between model-based approaches (relying on rigid mathematical rules that struggle to generalize to complex scenes) and model-free approaches (data-driven but with limited explainability and heavy dependence on network structure). The paper seeks a middle ground: introducing 'lite-rules'—simple, interpretable constraints inspired by echolocation—that guide data-driven backbones without imposing complex equations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes t_h=8 frames of 2D historical trajectories for the target agent, trajectories of its 50 nearest neighbors, and a scene image at the final observation step. It produces 20 candidate future trajectories over t_f=12 frames (4.8 s at 0.4 s intervals). Unusually, the SocialCircle representation is computed purely from relative geometry (angles, distances, velocities) in a discretized 360° angle space with N_θ=t_h² partitions, and is then zero-padded to match the trajectory sequence length before fusion.

## Method

SocialCircle discretizes the full 360° around the target agent into N_θ angular partitions, each encoding three meta components—average velocity, average distance, and average direction—of all neighbors falling in that sector. These meta vectors are embedded via a two-layer MLP and concatenated into a cyclic head-to-tail representation f^i ∈ R^{N_θ×d_sc}. The key architectural insight is treating this spatial representation as a 'virtual temporal sequence': it is zero-padded to length t_h and fused with the trajectory embedding via a learned linear projection followed by tanh, replacing the backbone's original social representation entirely. This allows the backbone (Transformer, MSN, V²-Net, E-V²-Net) to attend jointly over temporal trajectory patterns and angular interaction context in a single sequence, with no additional loss terms required.

## Results

On ETH-UCY (t_h=8, t_f=12), E-V2-Net-SC and V2-Net-SC both achieve 0.17/0.27 minADE/minFDE, outperforming Agentformer (0.18/0.29) by 5.6% ADE and 6.9% FDE. On SDD, E-V2-Net-SC reaches 6.54/10.36, surpassing the prior SOTA NSP-SFM (6.52/10.61) by 2.36% FDE, while V2-Net-SC (6.71/10.66) beats Y-net (7.85/11.85) by 14.52% ADE. On NBA SportVU (t_h=5, t_f=10), E-V2-Net-SC achieves 1.18/1.46 ADE/FDE at 4.0s, outperforming MemoNet (1.25/1.47) by 5.60% ADE and GroupNet+CVAE (1.13/1.69) by 13.60% FDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ETH-UCY, E-V2-Net-SC and V2-Net-SC tie for best ADE (0.17) and FDE (0.27), beating Agentformer by 5.6%/6.9% and Y-net by 5.6%/0% respectively; MSN-SC (0.19/0.33) trails the top methods but still outperforms EqMotion by 9.5% ADE. On SDD, E-V2-Net-SC (6.54/10.36) edges out NSP-SFM (6.52/10.61) on FDE by 2.36% while matching its ADE, and V2-Net-SC (6.71/10.66) substantially outperforms Y-net (7.85/11.85) by 14.52% ADE and 10.04% FDE, as well as LED (8.48/11.36) by 20.87% ADE. On NBA, E-V2-Net-SC (1.18/1.46 at 4.0s) loses to GroupNet+CVAE (1.13/1.69) on ADE by 4.42% but wins on FDE by 13.60%, and beats MemoNet (1.25/1.47) on ADE by 5.60% while matching its FDE. Overall, SocialCircle consistently improves its backbone models (e.g., E-V2-Net gains 2.91% ADE and 3.76% FDE from adding SocialCircle), and the method is most competitive on SDD where it achieves or approaches SOTA across all three backbone variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a bridge between model-based social interaction methods (Social Force, Dark Matter, neural ODEs) and model-free data-driven approaches (Social Pooling, GATs, GCNs), introducing 'lite-rules'—weak angle-based constraints inspired by echolocation—to guide data-driven backbones without imposing rigid mathematical physics. It builds directly on Transformer, MSN, V2-Net, and E-V2-Net as backbone predictors, replacing their original social interaction representations (e.g., social pooling or graph edges) with the angle-discretized SocialCircle vector. The serialized modeling strategy, which treats the spatial angle partitions as a virtual temporal sequence fused with trajectory embeddings, departs from the graph-structured or pooling-based interaction encodings that dominate prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, plug-and-play design of SocialCircle (compatible with multiple backbones, no additional loss functions) makes it a practical tool for improving social interaction modeling in trajectory prediction without architectural overhauls. The echolocation-inspired angle-based framing offers a more interpretable alternative to graph-based interaction modeling, as demonstrated by the attention-score visualizations and toy experiments. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: SocialCircle lacks direction-of-arrival information and does not model high-order interactions among neighbors, leaving open questions about whether richer angular encodings or multi-agent interaction graphs could further improve performance. The modest but consistent gains across three datasets and four backbones suggest the approach is robust but not transformative.
