## Problem

Existing video domain adaptation methods require continuous access to source data during adaptation, which is impractical due to privacy and transmission constraints. While image-based test-time adaptation (TTA) methods exist, they cannot be directly applied to multimodal video because videos exhibit diverse domain shifts across both modalities (RGB and optical flow) and temporal dimensions, making it difficult to maintain class discriminability during source-free adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241871/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB video clips (8 frames at 224×224, 3 channels) and optical flow clips (8 frames at 224×224, 2 channels) segmented into T=10 clips per video, along with a pre-trained I3D-based source model (feature extractors + single-modality and multimodal classifiers). During adaptation, only unlabeled target videos are consumed in an online, single-epoch fashion, and the last classification layer parameters are frozen to anchor the representation to the source distribution. The output is a single action class label per video instance.

## Method

MC-TTA employs a teacher-student architecture where the teacher maintains a memory bank of source-friendly features (selected via the SSFR module, which filters clips by modality correlation cosine distance and prediction confidence) to generate pseudo-source prototypes, while the student maintains a memory bank of all target features to generate target prototypes. Three losses drive adaptation: (1) Multimodal Prototype Alignment minimizes L2 distance between pseudo- and target-prototypes per class to reduce domain shift; (2) Cross-Modal Relative Consistency applies symmetric KL divergence between class-prototype relationship matrices of RGB and optical flow in the student bank, letting the more stable modality guide the more shift-susceptible one; (3) Cross-entropy with teacher-generated pseudo-labels enforces prediction consistency. The teacher is updated via EMA from the student, and the entire pipeline operates without any source data access.

## Results

MC-TTA achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all four benchmark datasets. On UCF-HMDB full (the largest cross-domain setting), it reaches 88.61% (U→H) and 91.07% (H→U), improving over the previous best ViTTA by 2.22% and 1.93% respectively. On UCF-HMDB small, it attains 97.88% and 98.21%, and on the challenging Epic-Kitchens fine-grained dataset it achieves a mean accuracy of 45.19%, outperforming the second-best ViTTA (43.96%) by 1.23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-TTA outperforms all six baselines on every task in Table 1 (UCF-Olympic, UCF-HMDB small, UCF-HMDB full). The closest competitor is ViTTA [25], which is the only other method designed for video TTA; MC-TTA surpasses it by up to 2.22% on UCF-HMDB full (U→H: 88.61 vs. 86.39) and by 0.53–0.59% on UCF-HMDB small. MM-TTA [34], though designed for multimodal TTA, underperforms even Source-only on UCF-Olympic (92.73/92.86 vs. 92.73/90.48), confirming that image-based multimodal TTA strategies do not transfer well to temporal video data. T3A [18] and TSD [41] are competitive on smaller datasets but lag by 2–4% on UCF-HMDB full where domain shift is most severe. On Epic-Kitchens (Table 2), MC-TTA wins 5 of 6 domain-transfer tasks, with the largest margin of 1.93% over ViTTA on D₁→D₃ (50.73 vs. 48.80), and achieves the best mean accuracy of 45.19% versus 43.96% for ViTTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student memory bank framework from T3A [18] and TSD [41] to the multimodal video setting, where the key challenge is that different modalities (RGB vs. optical flow) experience different magnitudes of domain shift. It departs from prior video TTA work (ViTTA [25]) which handles only single-modality temporal alignment, and from multimodal TTA (MM-TTA [34]) which targets image pairs rather than temporal video sequences. The SSFR module introduces a novel mechanism for constructing pseudo-source features by exploiting cross-modal prediction agreement and confidence as proxies for source-domain proximity, while the cross-modal relative consistency loss is a new regularization that preserves discriminability through inter-modal structural correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important deployment scenario where source videos are unavailable due to privacy or bandwidth constraints, making source-free adaptation essential for real-world action recognition systems. The consistent 1–2% gains over strong baselines (ViTTA, TSD) across four datasets demonstrate that explicitly modeling multimodal domain shift is more effective than treating modalities independently. Limitations include reliance on a single CNN backbone (I3D), only two modalities (RGB + optical flow), and manual hyperparameter tuning where the adaptive threshold strategy underperformed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more modalities (audio, depth), transformer-based backbones, and streaming/continual settings.
