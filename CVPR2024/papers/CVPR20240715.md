## Problem

Human image editing tasks—reposing, virtual try-on, and text-guided manipulation—are typically addressed in isolation, overlooking the mutual reinforcement that joint learning can provide. Additionally, existing datasets are constrained in scale and diversity (studio photography, limited age groups, simple backgrounds), and few prior works explore effective adaptation to unseen in-the-wild human images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240715/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source person image, a target pose, and optionally a garment image or text prompt, producing a single edited human image at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The task is determined by which optional inputs are present: no prompt yields reposing, a garment image triggers virtual try-on, and a text description triggers text manipulation. The pose-warping module consumes both dense (UV-map) and sparse (keypoint) pose representations, making it agnostic to the specific pose format used by the off-the-shelf detector.

## Method

UniHuman is built on Stable Diffusion and unifies three editing tasks through two key components: a part encoder that extracts DINOv2 features at the feature-map level (segmented by human parsing into face, hair, clothing, etc., and concatenated with CLIP embeddings) to supply style/texture context, and a training-free pose-warping module that explicitly maps source RGB pixels to the target pose via UV-map correspondence (reposing) or sparse-keypoint perspective warping (try-on), producing a pose-aligned visible texture. A ControlNet-style conditioning encoder injects the target pose, pose-warped texture, and partial background into the UNet decoder, while the part features and encoded pose-warped texture serve as keys/values in a dedicated cross-attention layer. Two auxiliary attention-constraint losses (L_B for part regions, L_E for visible pose-warped pixels) prevent texture blending between source and target regions. Training combines the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with these constraints, and the model is trained jointly on 400K curated in-the-wild images (LH-400K) alongside existing paired datasets to improve out-of-domain generalization.

## Results

On in-domain DeepFashion reposing (256×256), UniHuman achieves FID 5.089, SSIM 0.815, and LPIPS 0.123, outperforming the best baseline DisCo (FID 9.842) by nearly 2×. On out-of-domain WPose (512×512), UniHuman reduces FID from 58.3 (DisCo) to 27.7, demonstrating substantially better generalization to unseen poses and backgrounds. For virtual try-on on out-of-domain WVTON, UniHuman achieves FID 127.9 and KID 1.67, beating the best try-on-specific method LaDI-VTON (FID 147.4, KID 2.42). In user studies, UniHuman is preferred in 72–88% of comparisons across reposing and try-on tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniHuman dominates all reposing baselines on both in-domain and out-of-domain metrics. Against DisCo [34] (the strongest reposing baseline, trained on 700K images including TikTok dance videos), UniHuman halves the out-of-domain FID from 50.9 to 27.6 at 256×256 and from 58.3 to 27.7 at 512×512, while also improving M-SSIM by 0.015–0.019. Against PIDM [2] (a diffusion-based reposing method), UniHuman achieves FID 5.089 vs. 6.401 in-domain and 27.6 vs. 88.4 out-of-domain, a 3.2× gap in FID on WPose. UPGPT [7], the only prior unified multi-task model, is outperformed by UniHuman on every metric in both settings (e.g., FID 5.089 vs. 9.611 in-domain). For virtual try-on, UniHuman beats LaDI-VTON [26] (FID 127.9 vs. 147.4 on WVTON) and HR-VITON [20] (FID 127.9 vs. 159.6), with the gap widening on out-of-domain data. In user studies, UniHuman is preferred over DisCo in 72.1% of pose-accuracy comparisons and over PIDM in 84.3%, confirming the quantitative advantage is perceptually meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniHuman builds directly on Stable Diffusion as its generative backbone and adapts the ControlNet-style conditioning encoder to inject pose-warped textures and partial backgrounds into the UNet decoder. It extends the unified multi-task paradigm initiated by UPGPT, which bridged visual and text domains via frozen CLIP embeddings, by additionally exploiting the visual-visual synergy between reposing and virtual try-on (both require maintaining visible texture consistency under geometric change). A key departure from prior task-specific methods (PIDM, DisCo, HR-VITON, GP-VTON) is the introduction of a training-free pose-warping module that uses explicit UV-map or sparse-keypoint correspondence rather than a learned warping network, and the use of DINOv2 part-level features as a texture/style reference in cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that jointly learning reposing, virtual try-on, and text-guided editing in a single diffusion model yields mutual reinforcement, with the most striking gains on out-of-domain data (FID dropping from ~58 to ~27 at 512×512 for reposing). The curated LH-400K dataset and the WPose/WVTON out-of-domain benchmarks address a real gap: existing human-editing datasets are dominated by studio-shot fashion models with limited pose, background, and age diversity. Open questions include whether the unified formulation scales to video editing (acknowledged as future work), whether the cross-task transfer benefits persist at higher resolutions or with more diverse garment categories, and whether the training-free pose-warping module can be extended to handle severe occlusions or multi-person scenes.
