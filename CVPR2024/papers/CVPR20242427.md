## Problem

Existing face de-identification methods either destroy facial structure so severely that humans cannot recognize the person (e.g., blurring, pixelation) or fail to sufficiently obfuscate identity from automated recognition systems. No prior work has explored cartoon/toon avatars as a de-identification medium that simultaneously evades face recognition algorithms while preserving human recognizability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242427/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single real face image (512×512 RGB, resized to 256×256 during training) and produces a high-resolution 1024×1024 de-identified toon avatar. Training requires paired face-toon images from the newly introduced ToonSet dataset (23,000 pairs derived from FFHQ faces stylized via toonme.com). The output retains overall facial geometry and attributes (gender, race, age, accessories) recognizable to humans while shifting the image distribution away from the real-face manifold to defeat automated indexing.

## Method

ToonerGAN employs a three-step progressive training process on an encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections. Step 1 (Style Learning) trains the identity encoder and a lightweight style decoder via image-to-image translation with adversarial and L2 losses to learn the toon domain. Step 2 (Style Distillation) replaces the style decoder with a pre-trained StyleGAN2, conditioning each of its upsampling blocks on PSP-encoded features of the Step-1 toon output in W+ space, with noise inputs removed for temporal consistency. Step 3 (De-Identification) jointly trains the full pipeline with a composite loss combining L2 reconstruction, perceptual loss, and an identity loss that minimizes cosine similarity between AdaFace embeddings of the original and generated images, alongside adversarial training. The key architectural insight is using the toon-style output as an intermediate representation to guide the StyleGAN2 decoder, ensuring the final high-resolution output inherits cartoon characteristics that naturally diverge from the real-face distribution while preserving structural recognizability.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, ToonerGAN achieves a Recall@1 of 0.015% and Recall@10 of 0.060% for cartoon re-identification, substantially outperforming all baselines including DeIDGAN (0.02%/0.52%) and CIAGAN (0.26%/3.74%), while maintaining a 99.56% face detection rate. On LFW, ToonerGAN achieves a TPR@FPR=0.001 of 0.009, the lowest among all compared methods (next best: RiDDLE at 0.016). The mean identity similarity between original and de-identified images is 0.104, comparable to RiDDLE (0.056) and CIAGAN (0.068).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToonerGAN outperforms all 10 baselines on both re-identification metrics: its Recall@1 of 0.015% is 4× lower than DeIDGAN (0.02%), 17× lower than CIAGAN (0.26%), and 5,000× lower than LIVE (76.38%), which essentially fails to de-identify. On LFW verification, ToonerGAN's TPR of 0.009 beats RiDDLE (0.016), IdentityMask (0.017), CIAGAN (0.019), IVFG (0.019), IdentityTransformer (0.027), and LIVE (0.035). The only metric where ToonerGAN is not the best is ID similarity (0.104 vs. RiDDLE's 0.056 and CIAGAN's 0.068), though the authors argue the difference is modest and the method still effectively obscures identity. Critically, ToonerGAN maintains a 99.56% face detection rate, far exceeding Blurring (6.58%) and Pixelation (48.54%), confirming that the toon avatars remain usable for downstream tasks while defeating recognition systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToonerGAN sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: face de-identification (CIAGAN, DeIDGAN, LIVE, RiDDLE) and portrait style transfer (Toonify, AnimeGAN2, DualStyleGAN). It builds directly on StyleGAN2 as a high-resolution decoder and on the PSP encoder for latent-space style distillation, while adopting the Pix2Pix-style adversarial training regime for the initial style-learning stage. The key departure is that prior de-identification methods either replace the face with a synthetic identity or apply adversarial perturbations that degrade human recognizability, whereas ToonerGAN reframes de-identification as a distributional shift toward a cartoon manifold, arguing that the stylistic gap between real and toon faces is sufficient to break biometric matching while preserving human-readable structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important tension in biometric privacy: existing anonymization methods that achieve strong de-identification (e.g., blurring, pixelation) destroy data utility, while methods that preserve utility (e.g., LIVE) fail to obfuscate identity. ToonerGAN's human study (100% closed-set identification, 92.35% verification accuracy) alongside near-zero machine recall (0.015% at k=1) demonstrates that this tension can be resolved, which has direct implications for social media, metaverse avatars, and privacy-preserving data sharing. Open questions include robustness against future, more powerful face recognition models, whether the toon style is the optimal obfuscation target, and the dataset's dependence on a single commercial toonification service (toonme.com), which limits diversity and reproducibility of the training pairs.
