## Problem

Despite rapid growth in NeRF-based RGB-D SLAM methods, there is no unified protocol to isolate and compare the individual contributions of implicit neural representation (INR) architectures and geometric rendering functions to mapping and localization performance. Existing SOTA systems bundle many design choices (keyframe selection, sampling, training strategy) with their INR, making it impossible to attribute performance gains to specific representation or rendering decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241624/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous stream of synchronized RGB-D frames, where each pixel's color and depth define a ray along which N samples are drawn (stratified around ground-truth depth for surface samples, uniform for free space). The benchmark evaluates two scenarios: a Lab scenario on eight full-coverage Replica sequences with ground-truth trajectories, and a Practical scenario on seven NeuralRGBD sequences with sensor noise and intentionally partial scene coverage. Outputs include per-frame 6-DOF camera poses, an implicit SDF scene map, rendered depth and color images, with metrics spanning reconstruction quality (Acc., Comp.), trajectory accuracy (ATE), and procedural quality (PSNR, Depth L1).

## Method

The paper establishes a unified SLAM framework that fixes sampling, keyframe selection, and training protocols, then systematically varies the INR function F (MLP, dense grid, hash grid, tri-plane, factorization) and geometric rendering function G (SDF direct, SDF density, SDF surface), along with coupled vs. decoupled color-geometry encoding structures. Key findings reveal that dense grid with coupled encoding dominates in accuracy and speed, while decomposition-based INRs (tri-plane, factorization) benefit from decoupled structures and excel in completeness under partial observations. Building on these insights, the authors propose a hybrid encoding that combines tri-plane at a coarse resolution level with dense grid at a fine level, and an explicit hybrid encoding that replaces the coarse grid with an octree and uses a single-level dense grid with SDF residual optimization for memory-efficient real-time mapping at ~1 Hz update rate.

## Results

In the Lab scenario (Replica dataset), the dense grid with SDF(density) rendering achieves the best overall performance: 1.60 cm accuracy, 1.31 cm ATE, 27.77 dB PSNR, and 4.42 cm Depth L1, while using only 2×2 feature dimensions. In the Practical scenario (NeuralRGBD), the proposed hybrid encoding (dense grid + tri-plane) with SDF(direct) rendering achieves 2.40 cm accuracy, 1.86 cm ATE, 25.25 dB PSNR, and 1.68 cm Depth L1. Against SOTA baselines, the method reduces Depth L1 by over 50% (1.50 vs 3.53 cm vs NICE-SLAM; 1.68 vs 3.02 cm vs CO-SLAM) and improves ATE by 30% (1.37 vs 1.95 cm) in the Lab scenario, while using substantially fewer feature dimensions (2×2 vs 3×32 or 16×2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NICE-SLAM (Lab scenario, both using dense grid), the proposed method achieves substantially better depth estimation (1.50 vs 3.53 cm Depth L1, a 57% reduction), reconstruction accuracy (1.65 vs 2.85 cm), and trajectory accuracy (1.37 vs 1.95 cm ATE), while using only 2×2 feature dimensions versus NICE-SLAM's 3×32. The trade-off is lower completeness (5.62 vs 3.00 cm Comp., 83.93% vs 89.33% Comp.%), reflecting the smaller feature budget. Against CO-SLAM (Practical scenario, both using hybrid encoding), the method again wins on Depth L1 (1.68 vs 3.02 cm, 44% reduction), accuracy (2.40 vs 2.95 cm), and ATE (1.86 cm, where CO-SLAM does not report), while using 2×2 features versus CO-SLAM's 16×2. The completeness gap persists (4.64 vs 2.96 cm Comp.), but the paper argues that in partial-observation settings, the targeted SLAM metrics (ATE, Depth L1, PSNR) are more meaningful than full-scene completeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first unified benchmark for evaluating implicit neural representations (INRs) and geometric rendering functions within real-time RGB-D SLAM, addressing the absence of a common evaluation protocol that has hampered comparison across NeRF-SLAM methods like NICE-SLAM, CO-SLAM, and ESLAM. It builds directly on the dense grid encoding from NICE-SLAM, the hash grid and SDF(direct) rendering from CO-SLAM, the tri-plane and SDF(density) rendering from ESLAM, and the SDF residual strategy from H2-Mapping, but isolates these components from the idiosyncratic training pipelines, keyframe selection, and sampling strategies that each prior system bundles with its representation. The paper departs from prior work by treating the INR and rendering function as the sole variables under a controlled SLAM framework, and by introducing two new encoding designs (hybrid and explicit hybrid) that synthesize insights from the benchmark results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark provides the field with the first systematic, apples-to-apples comparison of INR and rendering choices for RGB-D SLAM, giving practitioners concrete guidance (e.g., dense grid over hash grid for accuracy, decomposition methods for noisy/partial observations) that was previously obscured by confounded system-level comparisons. The finding that the sophisticated SDF(surface) rendering from offline 3D reconstruction degrades significantly in online SLAM highlights a gap between offline rendering theory and online system requirements that remains open. Limitations include the focus on indoor scenes, controlled low feature dimensions (2×2), and the exclusion of occupancy-based representations, leaving open how these findings generalize to large-scale outdoor environments, higher-dimensional features, or dynamic scenes.
