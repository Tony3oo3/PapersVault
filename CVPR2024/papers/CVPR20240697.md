## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction synthesis methods rely on occupancy-based or distance-based virtual sensors that are limited in expressiveness and global with respect to the hand, hindering generalization across object categories, shapes, and sizes. This is compounded by the scarcity of dynamic hand-object interaction training data, making it difficult for models to learn the intricate correlations between individual finger movements during interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240697/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hand trajectory (wrist 3D position and SO(3) orientation per frame), an object trajectory (position and orientation per frame), and a static object template mesh. The object mesh is first cropped by a cube-shaped sensor (18 cm side) rigidly attached to the wrist for coarse initialization, then queried by sphere-shaped sensors (2.5 cm radius, up to 300 points) at each predicted joint for fine-grained geometry. The output is a sequence of MANO shape and pose parameters, from which hand meshes are recovered via optimization with temporal smoothness regularization.

## Method

GEARS introduces a joint-centered point-based local geometry sensor: object surface points within a small radius of each hand joint are transformed into a joint-local canonical frame (derived via inverse kinematics from the MANO template), and a shared three-layer PointNet processes them to produce joint-agnostic local features. A two-stage pipeline first uses a Joint Initialization Network (PointNet on wrist-cropped object points + FC layers on trajectory) to predict coarse joints, then a Joint Displacement Network with interleaved spatial and temporal self-attention modules refines the joints by predicting displacement vectors in the template frame. The spatial attention captures inter-finger correlations within a frame while temporal attention enforces smooth joint trajectories across the sequence. To mitigate data scarcity, the authors synthesize dynamic grasping sequences from static ObMan grasps via linear interpolation of translations and SLERP of joint rotations, filtering out sequences with excessive hand-object intersection.

## Results

On the GRAB dataset, GEARS achieves the best performance across all four metrics: 7.24 mm MPJPE, 4.36 mm penetration depth, 2.24 cm³ intersection volume, and 22.7% contact IoU. On the InterCap dataset (larger objects), GEARS also leads with 7.44 mm PD and 5.21 cm³ IV, outperforming both ManipNet and GRIP. The method generalizes to objects of varying sizes despite being trained primarily on small-to-medium household objects from GRAB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEARS outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against TOCH (ECCV 2022), GEARS reduces MPJPE by 0.94 mm (7.24 vs 8.18) and improves C-IoU by 2.6 points (22.7% vs 20.1%), while also achieving lower penetration depth and intersection volume. Against ManipNet (ACM TOG 2021), the gains are most pronounced: GEARS cuts MPJPE by 2.08 mm (7.24 vs 9.32) and C-IoU improves by 4.4 points (22.7% vs 18.3%), reflecting the advantage of joint-local geometry sensing over global occupancy/distance sensors. Against GRIP (2024), which uses a similar two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline, GEARS still leads on all metrics with a 0.47 mm MPJPE advantage and a 2.8-point C-IoU gain. On the InterCap dataset (larger objects), GEARS achieves 7.44 mm PD and 5.21 cm³ IV, beating GRIP (7.92 mm, 5.68 cm³) and ManipNet (8.22 mm, 6.15 cm³), confirming better generalization to unseen object sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEARS departs from the dominant virtual-sensor paradigm in dynamic hand-object interaction synthesis (ManipNet's occupancy grids, GRIP's distance-based sensors) by introducing a joint-centered point-based sensor that queries local object geometry in a canonical frame per joint. This is paired with a spatio-temporal transformer that explicitly models inter-joint correlations, addressing what the authors identify as the global-with-respect-to-hand limitation of prior sensors. The data augmentation strategy of synthesizing dynamic sequences from static grasps (ObMan) via linear/slerp interpolation is a practical repurposing of existing static grasp datasets, complementing the small pool of dynamic MoCap data (GRAB).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint-centered sensor provides a principled mechanism for generalizing across object categories and sizes without category-specific tuning, which is a persistent bottleneck in hand-object interaction research. The data augmentation from static grasps is particularly relevant given the extreme scarcity of dynamic hand-object MoCap data, and the modest but consistent gains across four metrics on two datasets validate the approach. Open questions include whether the method extends to articulated objects, multi-hand interactions, or real-time settings, and whether the reliance on a known object mesh limits practical deployment. The paper does not address physical plausibility (force closure, friction), leaving a gap between visual realism and physically valid manipulation.
