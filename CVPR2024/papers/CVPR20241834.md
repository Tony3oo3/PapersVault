## Problem

Existing category-level 6D pose datasets face a fundamental trade-off: they either provide large quantities of data with low annotation quality (e.g., NOCS) or high-quality annotations with limited viewpoint coverage and scene diversity (e.g., PhoCaL). None simultaneously offer realistic marker-free scenes, photometrically challenging objects (glass, cutlery), multi-modal sensing, and mm-accurate pose annotations at scale, blocking progress toward practical category-level pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241834/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized RGB, active stereo depth, and polarimetric RGB (4 filter angles) images across 23.5k frames in 41 scenes, with ~160k mm-accurate 6D object pose annotations (1.35–1.74 mm RMSE) and 10M parallel-jaw grasp pose labels for 16 scenes. For pose estimation benchmarks, methods consume RGB-D input and predict 6D object poses evaluated via 3D IoU at 25%/50% thresholds; for grasping, methods produce 6D gripper poses evaluated by coverage rate, object success rate, and real-world success rate on a 7-DoF Franka robot over 15 trials per object.

## Method

The annotation pipeline replaces the robotic end-effector used in PhoCaL with a handheld multi-camera rig tracked by an external 4-camera IR system (ARTTRACK), enabling wider viewpoint coverage while maintaining mm-level accuracy. A novel hand-eye calibration aggregates multiple ChArUcO board captures and applies Horn's alignment method to extract the camera-to-tracker transformation (RMSE 0.27 mm / 0.42°), improving over the single-image closed-form solution in prior work. Timestamp synchronization between the tracker and cameras uses ICP-based trajectory alignment rather than similarity maximization, making it robust to arbitrary sampling frequencies without interpolation. A final COLMAP bundle adjustment step with manually selected fixed frames further reduces residual motion-induced pose offsets. Grasp annotations are generated via antipodal sampling validated in Isaac Gym simulation, followed by environment-level collision checking and reprojection into each camera frame.

## Results

On the HouseCat6D test split, VI-Net achieves the best overall 3D IoU of 80.7% (25% threshold) and 56.4% (50% threshold), outperforming GPV-Pose (74.9/50.7), FS-Net (74.9/48.0), and NOCS (50.0/21.2). The dataset's annotation quality is validated with a point-wise RMSE of 1.35–1.73 mm, surpassing checkerboard-based datasets (≥17 mm) and approaching robotic-acquisition accuracy (0.80 mm for PhoCaL). For grasping, KGN with keypoint refinement improves grasp coverage rate (GCR) substantially over the no-refinement variant (e.g., 58.3% vs 35.4% for Can, 40.8% vs 16.3% for Glass), with real-world grasp success rates of 53–80% across tested categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VI-Net [38] clearly leads all baselines on overall 3D IoU (80.7/56.4), with particularly large margins on Bottle (90.6 vs 66.8 for GPV-Pose) and Cutlery (89.2 vs 70.9). GPV-Pose [14] and FS-Net [8] are nearly tied on the 25% threshold (both 74.9) but GPV-Pose edges ahead on the 50% threshold (50.7 vs 48.0), with GPV-Pose stronger on Glass (62.7 vs 32.4) and FS-Net stronger on Shoe (55.4 vs 50.2). NOCS [59] lags significantly behind all geometry-guided methods (50.0/21.2), especially on Cup (68.8/2.0) and Cutlery (14.7/6.0), confirming the advantage of 3D geometry over 2D lifting. For grasping, KGN [10] with keypoint refinement improves GCR by 10–25 points over the no-refinement variant across most categories, and achieves 100% OSR on Cup, Glass, and Tube. A critical finding is that sensor depth severely degrades 2D-to-3D lifting: NOCS with ground-truth maps but real sensor depth drops to 22.6% mean IoU at 75%, and VI-Net trained on a PhoCaL-like limited pose subset drops from 80.7/56.4 to 68.4/31.3, underscoring the dataset's value in pose diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the category-level 6D pose estimation dataset lineage initiated by NOCS [59] and advanced by PhoCaL [61], kPAM [45], and TOD [39]. The annotation pipeline is directly adapted from PhoCaL's robotic tip-based approach, but replaces the constrained robotic end-effector with an external IR tracking system to achieve wider viewpoint coverage while maintaining sub-2mm accuracy. For grasp annotations, it follows the antipodal sampling and collision-checking pipeline from GraspNet-1Billion [21] and ACRONYM [19]. The key conceptual departure is combining three modalities (RGB, active stereo depth, and polarimetric RGB+P) in a single dataset with marker-free, cluttered household scenes, addressing the trade-off between annotation quality (PhoCaL) and scale/variety (NOCS) that prior datasets could not simultaneously satisfy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HouseCat6D provides the first category-level 6D pose benchmark that simultaneously offers mm-accurate ground truth, photometrically challenging objects (glass, cutlery), dense occlusions, and multi-modal data, filling a critical gap that has limited progress in this area. The benchmark reveals that even the best current method (VI-Net) achieves only 80.7/56.4 3D IoU, and that sensor depth noise can degrade even perfect 2D predictions to 22.6% at 75% IoU—highlighting that the field's reliance on RGB-D pipelines is a fundamental bottleneck. Open questions include how to effectively exploit the polarimetric modality for pose estimation, how to handle the significant occlusion cases that current methods largely ignore, and whether the dense grasp annotations can enable robust category-level robotic manipulation in unstructured environments.
