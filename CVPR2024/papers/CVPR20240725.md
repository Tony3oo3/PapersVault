## Problem

Existing adversarial distillation methods suffer from two critical limitations: (1) KL-divergence-based exact matching causes training interference when teacher and student predictions differ substantially, leading to the counterintuitive result that stronger teachers can produce less robust students; and (2) matching solely on output values fails to transfer the teacher's behavioral decision-making patterns, leaving the student unable to fully replicate the teacher's focus on relevant input features.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 (10 classes) and CIFAR-100 (100 classes). The student model (ResNet-18 or MobileNet-V2) produces a class label prediction, while the teacher model (WideResNet-28-10 or 34-10) provides both soft probability distributions and CAM-based attribution heatmaps. Adversarial examples are generated under L∞ perturbation with ε=8/255, and the student is evaluated under FGSM, PGD-20, C&W∞, and AutoAttack at the same perturbation budget.

## Method

SmaraAD replaces the standard KL-divergence matching loss with a Spearman correlation coefficient computed over soft rankings (using a sigmoid-based differentiable approximation of the rank function), which matches the relative ordering of class predictions rather than their absolute values, thereby reducing training interference from prediction-scale mismatches. In parallel, an attribution region alignment loss minimizes the MSE between CAM heatmaps of the teacher and student (resized to a common spatial dimension via bilinear interpolation), forcing the student to attend to the same input regions as the teacher. The overall training follows a min-max adversarial distillation framework: the inner maximization generates adversarial examples by maximizing KL divergence plus alignment loss, while the outer minimization optimizes the student using slack matching and alignment losses on both clean and adversarial inputs. The enhanced variant SmaraAD++ further modulates the teacher-guidance weight by the teacher's confidence on adversarial examples and applies entropy-based sample weighting to down-weight uncertain predictions, encouraging self-learning when the teacher is unreliable.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a ResNet-18 student and WideResNet-34-10 teacher (ε=8/255), SmaraAD++ achieves 58.58% PGD-20 robust accuracy and 53.40% AutoAttack robust accuracy, outperforming the strongest AD baseline AdaAD (55.40% PGD-20, 51.41% AA) by 3.18% and 1.99% respectively. On CIFAR-100 under the same setup, SmaraAD++ reaches 35.79% PGD-20 and 30.63% AA, surpassing AdaAD (32.89% PGD-20, 27.18% AA) by 2.90% and 3.45%. The method also outperforms all AT baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES, LAS-AT) on adversarial metrics while maintaining competitive clean accuracy (86.90% on CIFAR-10, 62.64% on CIFAR-100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmaraAD and SmaraAD++ consistently outperform all seven AD baselines (ARD, IAD, RSLAD, MTARD, AdaAD) and three AT baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES, LAS-AT) on adversarial robustness metrics across both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The largest margins are against MTARD, which achieves high clean accuracy (86.84% on CIFAR-10) but suffers severe robustness degradation (50.87% PGD-20, 46.12% AA), trailing SmaraAD++ by 7.71% on PGD-20. Against the strongest AD baseline AdaAD, SmaraAD++ gains 3.18% on PGD-20 and 1.99% on AA (CIFAR-10, RN-18/WRN-34-10), and 2.90% on PGD-20 and 3.45% on AA (CIFAR-100, RN-18/WRN-34-10). On the MobileNet-V2 student, SmaraAD++ similarly leads AdaAD by 1.15% on PGD-20 and 1.97% on AA (CIFAR-10, WRN-28-10 teacher). A key differentiator highlighted in the paper is that SmaraAD's student robustness increases monotonically with teacher quality, whereas KL-based methods (ARD, RSLAD, AdaAD) show decreasing student robustness as the teacher improves, confirming that the Spearman-correlation slack matching and attribution region alignment address a fundamental limitation of exact-matching distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SmaraAD extends the adversarial distillation lineage initiated by ARD (Goldblum et al., 2020) and refined by RSLAD, IAD, MTARD, and AdaAD, all of which rely on KL divergence to match teacher and student predictions. The paper departs from this lineage in two ways: it replaces KL divergence with a Spearman correlation-based slack matching criterion (inspired by Huang et al.'s work on distilling from stronger teachers), and it introduces attribution region alignment via CAM as a behavioral learning signal absent from all prior AD methods. The min-max optimization framework and the teacher-reliability weighting follow IAD's approach, but the combination of rank-based matching with spatial attention alignment is novel within the AD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that stronger teachers can degrade student robustness under KL-based distillation is practically important for practitioners selecting teacher-student pairs for edge deployment, and the Spearman correlation remedy offers a drop-in replacement that restores the expected monotonic relationship. However, the evaluation is confined to CIFAR-10/100 with two CNN student architectures, leaving open whether the attribution region alignment generalizes to higher-resolution datasets, transformer backbones, or non-visual tasks where CAM is not directly applicable. The moderate 1–3% gains over strong baselines like AdaAD, while consistent, suggest the technique is a useful refinement rather than a paradigm shift in adversarial robustness.
