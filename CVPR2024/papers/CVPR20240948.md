## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language models either rely on multi-view image projections that incur heavy computational cost and discard essential geometry/depth information, or build task-specific 3D specialists that cannot scale or generalize. Additionally, plain textual instructions are inherently ambiguous in cluttered 3D scenes, limiting instruction-following performance. The paper asks whether a single LLM-driven assistant can directly consume point clouds and handle both text and visual interactions to resolve these ambiguities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240948/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud (40k points with coordinates, color, normal, and height), a textual instruction formatted with '### human:' / '### assistant:' chat identifiers, and optional visual prompts (user clicks normalized to [0,1] or 3D bounding-box ROI features from Vote2Cap-DETR). Output is free-form natural language in which 3D coordinates are discretized into unsigned integers in [0,255] and rendered as special tokens (<loc>x,y,z</loc> for points, <obj>cx,cy,cz,w,h,l</obj> for boxes) that fit directly into the frozen OPT-1.3B vocabulary without any additional learnable tokens.

## Method

LL3DA introduces an Interactor3D module built around a multi-modal transformer (MMT) inspired by Q-Former: a frozen ScanNet-pretrained masked transformer encodes the point cloud into M×d scene features via FPS, while a visual prompt encoder maps clicks through 3D Fourier positional embeddings and boxes through ROI features (each as 8 tokens). The MMT performs self-attention among 32 learnable queries, visual prompts, and text instructions, followed by cross-attention between queries+prompts and the scene embeddings, producing 32 interaction-aware tokens projected into the LLM's word-embedding space as a prefix. This early-fusion design lets the queries attend directly to 3D geometry conditioned on both text and visual cues, resolving the permutation-invariance-to-causal-LLM mismatch. Training uses token-wise cross-entropy on the frozen OPT-1.3B backbone (float16) with AdamW and cosine-annealed LR from 1e-4 to 1e-6 over ~100k iterations on ≤8 RTX 3090 GPUs, with auxiliary 'describe and localize' / 'answer and localize' instructions and random click/box prompts to diversify the training signal.

## Results

On 3D Dense Captioning, LL3DA achieves 65.19% C@0.5 on ScanRefer and 51.18% C@0.5 on Nr3D, surpassing the previous best (Vote2Cap-DETR) by +3.38% and +7.34% respectively. On 3D Question Answering, LL3DA achieves 76.79 CiDEr on the ScanQA validation set, outperforming the generation-based 3D-LLM by +7.39 CiDEr and all classification-based methods by a wide margin. The model also demonstrates strong generalist capability, achieving 62.98% C@0.5 on ScanRefer and 75.67 CiDEr on ScanQA without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LL3DA consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across both evaluation tasks. On 3D Dense Captioning, it surpasses the strongest prior method Vote2Cap-DETR [10] by +3.38% C@0.5 on ScanRefer (65.19 vs 61.81) and +7.34% C@0.5 on Nr3D (51.18 vs 43.84), while also beating multi-task pre-trained models like 3D-VLP [34] (+10.25% C@0.5 on ScanRefer) and 3DJCG [4] (+15.71% C@0.5 on ScanRefer). On 3D Question Answering, LL3DA (76.79 CiDEr validation) outperforms the generation-based 3D-LLM [29] by +7.39 CiDEr and the best classification-based method 3D-VLP [34] by +9.82 CiDEr. Notably, LL3DA also beats 3D-VisTA [70] (which was not trained on additional 3D scenes) by +3.59% C@0.5 on ScanRefer, confirming the advantage of direct point-cloud encoding over multi-view image reconstruction. The generalist model (without task-specific fine-tuning) still achieves 62.98% C@0.5 on ScanRefer and 75.67 CiDEr on ScanQA, approaching the fine-tuned expert performance and demonstrating strong instruction-following across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LL3DA adapts the Q-Former architecture from BLIP-2 and the instruction-aware feature extraction paradigm from InstructBLIP to the 3D point cloud domain, replacing 2D image encoders with a frozen ScanNet-pretrained masked transformer encoder. It departs from 3D-LLM, which reconstructs 3D features from multi-view images (incurring high computational cost and discarding native geometry), by taking point clouds as direct input. Unlike prior 3D specialists (Vote2Cap-DETR, 3DJCG, 3D-VLP) that require separate heads per task, LL3DA unifies 3D Dense Captioning, 3D Question Answering, and scene description under a single frozen LLM with task disambiguation via textual instructions and visual prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that visual interactions (clicks, 3D boxes) as explicit inputs to a 3D language model can resolve ambiguities inherent in natural language descriptions of cluttered scenes, yielding a +6.12% CiDEr gain on ScanQA at test time. However, the evaluation is confined to indoor ScanNet scenes with a small frozen OPT-1.3B backbone, and the generalist model degrades sharply on Nr3D (23.94% vs. 51.18% fine-tuned), suggesting limited cross-dataset generalization. Open questions include whether the visual-interaction paradigm scales to outdoor or large-scale 3D environments, whether larger LLMs further amplify the gains, and how to handle more diverse interaction types beyond clicks and boxes.
