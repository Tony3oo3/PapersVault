## Problem

Low-resource vision—where training data is truly scarce (a few hundred images), differences between images are extremely fine-grained, and the domain is highly specialized (circuit diagrams, historic maps, mechanical drawings)—remains under-explored compared to its NLP counterpart. The paper demonstrates that current vision foundation models (CLIP, ImageBind, DINOv2, SAM, BLIP, AIM) fail to generalize to these tasks even zero-shot, and that standard transfer learning and data augmentation techniques are insufficient when all three challenges compound simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are specialized RGB images with severely limited training sets (102–300 samples): circuit diagrams to be classified into 32 function classes, historic city maps to be matched against modern satellite images, and mechanical drawings to be matched against 3D-rendered component photos (bidirectional retrieval). Outputs are either a discrete function label (Top-1/Top-5 accuracy) or a ranked list of candidate images (Recall@1, Recall@5, mean rank). A key constraint is that the retrieval pairs span visually dissimilar modalities (e.g., 18th-century cartographic style vs. modern satellite imagery), making the task fundamentally cross-domain rather than same-domain matching.

## Method

The paper proposes three lightweight baselines, each targeting one low-resource challenge, all applied on top of a frozen foundation model (primarily ImageBind) with AdaptFormer as the transfer-learning backbone. (1) For data scarcity, Stable Diffusion generates both label-preserving augmentations (small timestep, γ=0.3) fed into the task loss and label-breaking augmentations (larger timestep, τ=0.6) used in a contrastive loss with a memory bank, enlarging the effective training space without requiring known labels for diverse samples. (2) For fine-grained details, the original linear projection kernel is decomposed into T smaller sub-kernels (u×u with u=7, q=14, giving T=49) that attend to local regions within each patch; a learned softmax weighting combines sub-kernel outputs into patch-level features, adding only T scalar parameters. (3) For domain shift, C=10 global attention maps are learned and shared across all tokens and samples; each token crops its spatially corresponding h×h sub-map, applies softmax, and multiplies with attention values, with a learned scalar α blending this domain-specific attention into the original multi-head self-attention. The three baselines are independent and composable, and the combined approach improves over zero-shot transfer, standard data augmentation, fine-grained methods, and existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning across all six foundation models tested.

## Results

On the LITE benchmark, the best zero-shot foundation model (ImageBind) achieves only 19.3% Top-1 on circuit diagram classification, 28.1% R@1 on historic map retrieval, and 13.2% R@1 on mechanical drawing retrieval. Adding the proposed baselines (generated data + selective tokenization + domain attention) on top of AdaptFormer improves ImageBind to 24.1% Top-1, 36.4% R@1, and 60.0% R@1 respectively. Across all six foundation models tested, the combined baselines consistently outperform both zero-shot transfer and AdaptFormer alone, with gains of +4.7 to +5.7% R@1 on mechanical drawing retrieval over AdaptFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed baselines consistently outperform all 20 compared methods across the three LITE tasks. On circuit diagram classification (ImageBind), the combined baselines reach 24.1% Top-1, beating AdaptFormer (19.8%), LoRA (15.5%), VPT (19.4%), IA3 (18.2%), CLIP-Adapter (16.3%), TOAST (16.4%), and full-parameter finetuning (13.2%). For data augmentation, the generated-data baseline (21.3% Top-1) surpasses Mixup (20.8%), CutMix (20.0%), Random Erasing (20.8%), DA-Fusion (19.6%), and SyntheticData (20.8%). For fine-grained methods, the tokenization baseline (20.9% Top-1) outperforms Adaptive-FGSBIR (16.7%), PLEor (17.1%), and PDiscoNet (16.2%), which all degrade below zero-shot due to overfitting on scarce data. On retrieval tasks, the combined baselines improve R@1 by +4.7 to +5.7% over AdaptFormer for CLIP, DINOv2, and ImageBind on mechanical drawing retrieval, and by +6.1% for ImageBind on historic map retrieval (36.4 vs. 30.3). The paper notes that despite these gains, absolute performance remains low (24.1% Top-1, 36.4% R@1), indicating the tasks are far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established concept of low-resource NLP to computer vision, arguing that the combination of truly scarce data (hundreds of images total), fine-grained differences, and specialized domain shift constitutes a problem distinct from few-shot learning (which assumes thousands of images per domain) or domain generalization (which assumes abundant in-domain data). It builds on AdaptFormer as the parameter-efficient transfer learning backbone but departs from prior adaptation work by showing that standard PEFT methods (LoRA, VPT, IA3, TOAST) fail on these tasks, and by introducing three lightweight baselines targeting each challenge: generative data augmentation via Stable Diffusion, sub-kernel selective tokenization, and learned global attention maps for domain-specific patterns. The paper positions itself as a diagnostic study and initial baseline rather than a state-of-the-art method, explicitly framing its contribution as identifying a gap and providing starting points for future work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LITE benchmark is the first to systematically collect and evaluate truly low-resource vision tasks, filling a gap where prior work either assumed high-resource domains or studied individual challenges in isolation. The finding that all six major vision foundation models (CLIP, ImageBind, DINOv2, SAM, BLIP, AIM) perform near-chance on these tasks, and that standard transfer learning methods fail to improve over zero-shot, challenges the assumption that foundation model generalizability extends to specialized non-natural-image domains. However, the benchmark is small (154–300 training images), the proposed baselines achieve only modest performance (24.1% Top-1 on circuit classification, 37.1% R@1 on map retrieval), and the paper explicitly states the tasks are 'far from solved,' leaving substantial open questions about how to handle intra-class variation, sub-domain shifts, and the need for multi-region reasoning in low-resource settings.
