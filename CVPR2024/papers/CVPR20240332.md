## Problem

Diffusion models have shown strong potential for image super-resolution, but extending them to video super-resolution faces two critical challenges: the inherent stochasticity of the diffusion process can distort visual appearance and hallucinate content, and independent frame-wise processing ignores temporal relationships, producing inconsistent high-resolution videos. No prior method effectively addresses both spatial fidelity preservation and temporal coherence simultaneously within a diffusion-based video SR framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240332/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 6-frame low-resolution video clip (at 1/4 the target resolution) and produces a high-resolution video at the target resolution. The LR video is first upsampled by a transformer-based video upscaler before entering the diffusion pipeline. Evaluation is conducted on REDS4 (1280×720) and Vid4 (720×480) benchmarks, with training performed on Vimeo-90K clips (448×256, 7 frames each). The final output is refined by a trainable video refiner that blends the decoded HR video with the upsampled LR video to balance synthesized quality and color fidelity.

## Method

SATeCo freezes all pre-trained Stable Diffusion UNet and VAE parameters and inserts two lightweight modules—Spatial Feature Adaptation (SFA) and Temporal Feature Alignment (TFA)—into every decoder block of both the UNet and VAE. SFA uses two 2D convolutions on LR latent features to predict pixel-wise affine parameters (scale and bias) that modulate normalized HR intermediate features, injecting precise spatial guidance for appearance preservation. TFA partitions HR features into 3D tubelets (8×8 spatial windows across 6 frames), applies self-attention within each tubelet for cross-frame feature interaction, then performs cross-attention between the HR tubelet and its LR counterpart for temporal feature calibration. A four-stage training procedure sequentially optimizes the video upscaler (Charbonnier loss), UNet SFA/TFA modules, VAE SFA/TFA modules, and finally the video refiner, all under a linear noise scheduler with T=1000 steps.

## Results

On REDS4, SATeCo achieves the best perception-based scores across all four metrics: LPIPS 0.1735, DISTS 0.0607, NIQE 4.104, and CLIP-IQA 0.6622, while matching the SOTA regression model IconVSR on PSNR (31.62 dB). On Vid4, SATeCo again leads all perception metrics (LPIPS 0.2291, DISTS 0.1015, NIQE 5.212, CLIP-IQA 0.7451), with a 26% relative DISTS reduction over the best competitor VRT. On pixel-based metrics, SATeCo is competitive but slightly trails VRT on Vid4 PSNR (27.44 vs. 27.93 dB) and SSIM (0.8420 vs. 0.8425).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the diffusion-based baseline StableSR, SATeCo substantially outperforms on all metrics on both datasets (e.g., PSNR 31.62 vs. 24.79 on REDS4; LPIPS 0.1735 vs. 0.2412), confirming that the SFA/TFA modules effectively mitigate the stochasticity and frame inconsistency inherent to frame-wise diffusion super-resolution. Versus the regression-based SOTA VRT, SATeCo wins decisively on all four perception metrics (DISTS 0.0607 vs. 0.0823 on REDS4; 0.1015 vs. 0.1372 on Vid4) but trails slightly on pixel-based metrics (PSNR 31.62 vs. 31.60 on REDS4; 27.44 vs. 27.93 on Vid4). Against IconVSR, SATeCo ties on REDS4 PSNR (31.62) but edges out on SSIM (0.8932 vs. 0.8948 is a near-tie) and dominates on all perception scores. Compared to BasicVSR and EDVR-M, SATeCo leads on every metric across both datasets, with the largest gaps on DISTS (0.0607 vs. 0.0808 on REDS4). TOFlow and Bicubic interpolation are clearly inferior across the board, serving as lower-bound references.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model-based image super-resolution line (StableSR, Pixel-Aware Stable Diffusion) to the video domain, where the core challenge shifts from spatial fidelity alone to joint spatial-temporal consistency. It builds on the latent diffusion architecture of Stable Diffusion (freezing all UNet/VAE parameters) and borrows VRT's temporal mutual self-attention blocks for its video upscaler, but departs from prior diffusion-based ISR by introducing two lightweight modules—Spatial Feature Adaptation (pixel-wise affine modulation from LR latent features) and Temporal Feature Alignment (tubelet-based self- and cross-attention)—inserted into every decoder block of both UNet and VAE. Unlike regression-based VSR methods (BasicVSR, VRT, EDVR) that propagate information through recurrent or sliding-window architectures, SATeCo leverages the generative prior of diffusion while constraining stochasticity through explicit pixel-level guidance from the LR input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a diffusion model can match the pixel fidelity (PSNR) of the best regression VSR baseline while substantially outperforming it on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, CLIP-IQA), suggesting a viable path to combining the detail-richness of generative models with the fidelity of regression. However, the evaluation is limited to 4× upscaling on very small test sets (4 clips each) with only 6-frame clips, leaving open questions about scalability to longer sequences, higher upscaling factors, and real-world degradation. The fixed trade-off parameter in the video refiner and the reliance on a pre-trained VAE for both encoding and decoding also constrain flexibility. Future work could explore whether the SFA/TFA design generalizes to other diffusion-based restoration tasks or to longer video horizons without the tubelet window becoming a bottleneck.
