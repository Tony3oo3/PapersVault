## Problem

Self-supervised pretraining for face recognition has not been shown to scale effectively from limited to large-scale identity data, and few-shot (especially 1-shot) face recognition lacks a principled way to leverage the vast pool of unlabeled facial images available in the wild. Additionally, standard self-supervised methods rely on random image crops that ignore the structured, part-based geometry of faces, which is known to be critical for recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240854/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB facial images and first passes them through a fixed, pre-trained landmark CNN to extract 196 dense facial landmark coordinates, which then define the patch positions for a Part fViT backbone. During self-supervised pretraining, the teacher branch processes all 196 landmarks while the student branch processes a random subset of 36 landmarks, replacing DINO's global/local image crop views. The final output is a discriminative face recognition embedding, fine-tuned with CosFace loss plus a landmark-position regularization term.

## Method

LAFS replaces DINO's random image crop views with landmark-localized patches: a fixed landmark CNN (from Part fViT) generates 196 facial landmarks, and the self-supervised objective minimizes the representation gap between the full landmark set (teacher) and a randomly sampled 36-landmark subset (student), using DINO's teacher-student EMA and cross-entropy framework. Two landmark-specific augmentations further regularize learning: Landmark Shuffle randomizes the order in which landmarks are fed to the ViT (exploiting the fact that part-based ViT does not require sequential patch ordering), and Landmark Coordinate Perturbation adds Gaussian noise (α=2 optimal) to landmark positions to encourage exploration of surrounding regions. For fine-tuning, the model is trained with CosFace identity loss plus an L2 regularization term that penalizes the trainable landmark CNN from deviating too far from a fixed reference landmark CNN, preventing the landmarks from collapsing to standard ViT grid positions.

## Results

On MS1MV3, LAFS (Part fViT-B) achieves 97.48% on IJB-C and 96.24% on IJB-B, outperforming the previous SOTA AdaFace (97.09% IJB-C, 95.84% IJB-B) by 0.39 and 0.40 points respectively. On Webface4M, LAFS reaches 97.55% IJB-C and 96.28% IJB-B, surpassing AdaFace by 0.16 and 0.25 points. In few-shot settings (1% labels, 1-shot), LAFS achieves 33.97% IJB-B and 37.89% IJB-C, substantially outperforming DINO pretraining with Part fViT (18.51% and 21.40%) and even matching DINO+fViT (33.48% and 37.49%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAFS (Part fViT) decisively outperforms AdaFace [36], the prior SOTA, by +0.40 on IJB-B (96.24 vs 95.84) and +0.39 on IJB-C (97.48 vs 97.09) on MS1MV3, and by +0.25 and +0.16 on the same benchmarks on Webface4M. Against the supervised Part fViT baseline [55], LAFS adds +0.13 (IJB-B) and +0.19 (IJB-C) on MS1MV3, confirming the benefit of landmark-based self-supervised pretraining. LAFS also surpasses DINO pretraining with fViT by +0.11 on IJB-B and +0.21 on IJB-C, demonstrating that landmark-based patches are more effective than standard grid patches for self-supervised face representation. Notably, LAFS without data augmentation still beats AdaFace by +0.14 (IJB-B) and +0.11 (IJB-C), indicating the representation quality is intrinsic to the pretraining strategy rather than relying on strong augmentation. On Webface4M, LAFS also outperforms QAFace [49] on IJB-B (96.28 vs 95.67) and IJB-C (97.55 vs 97.20), while QAFace retains a slight edge on CFP-FP (99.21 vs 99.18). In few-shot (1%, 1-shot) evaluation, LAFS achieves 33.97% IJB-B, far exceeding DINO+Part fViT (18.51%) and matching DINO+fViT (33.48%), highlighting its particular strength in data-scarce regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINO self-supervised framework (teacher-student EMA with global-local view consistency) by replacing random image patches with facial landmark-localized patches, building directly on Part fViT's landmark CNN for part-based Vision Transformer face recognition. It departs from prior SSL-for-faces work (e.g., Face-MAE, StyleGAN-based reconstruction) by operating entirely in landmark space rather than pixel space, and from standard DINO by introducing two landmark-specific augmentations (shuffle and coordinate perturbation). The paper also positions itself against the empirical finding that SSL with ResNet fails to scale to large datasets, demonstrating that ViT-based SSL can overcome this limitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: leveraging the vast pool of unlabeled facial images in real-world deployments for face recognition, particularly in few-shot settings where labeled data per identity is scarce. The finding that landmark-based SSL (LAFS) consistently outperforms standard DINO pretraining for Part fViT, and that SSL with ViT can exceed supervised training margins that ResNet-based SSL cannot achieve, suggests a promising direction for data-efficient face recognition. Key limitations include the fixed landmark CNN during self-supervised training (landmarks do not adapt), and the modest absolute improvements on large-scale benchmarks (0.1–0.4 points), with the most compelling gains appearing in few-shot scenarios. Open questions include whether landmarks can be jointly refined during SSL and whether the approach generalizes to other facial analysis tasks.
