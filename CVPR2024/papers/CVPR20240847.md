## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal alignment methods (CLIP, ALIGN, ImageBind) require internet-scale paired datasets (400M–5B pairs) and large GPU clusters to jointly train encoders from scratch, making them prohibitively expensive for most practitioners. The paper asks whether frozen pre-trained unimodal encoders, which already encode rich modality-specific semantics, can be bootstrapped into a shared multimodal space using minimal paired data and a single GPU.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240847/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed latent vectors from frozen unimodal encoders (penultimate-layer [CLS] or mean-pooled tokens from DINOv2/UNICOM for images, BGE/E5 for text, Whisper/HTS-AT for audio) rather than raw inputs, so the large encoders are discarded after a one-time extraction pass. The trainable fusion adapters (lightweight inverted-bottleneck MLPs) map these latents into a shared 512-dimensional space, producing cosine-similarity-compatible embeddings for cross-modal retrieval. In the audio-to-image generation application, the aligned audio embedding is fed as a conditioning signal into the CLIP text encoder of GLIDE to produce generated images.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-and-play fusion framework that freezes arbitrary pre-trained unimodal encoders and trains only lightweight MLP adapters to align their latent spaces via a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive objective, requiring no backpropagation through the large encoders. To compensate for the small amount of paired data (5M image-text pairs, 65K audio-text pairs), the paper introduces FuseMix, a latent-space augmentation that takes convex combinations of two positive pairs in each modality while sharing the same Beta-distributed mixing coefficient λ across modalities, preserving semantic consistency of the augmented positive pair. Because the encoders are frozen and their outputs are pre-computed, the entire training loop operates on low-dimensional latents with batch sizes up to 20K on a single 32GB V100 GPU. The framework is modality-agnostic and encoder-agnostic: swapping in a new unimodal encoder only requires re-extracting latents and retraining the small adapter, enabling rapid incorporation of state-of-the-art unimodal models without retraining the full pipeline.

## Results

On Flickr30K text-to-image retrieval, FuseMix(D,B) trained on only 5M image-text pairs achieves R@1 = 71.2, R@5 = 91.4, R@10 = 94.7, outperforming CLIP (400M pairs) on R@1 (71.2 vs. 68.7) and R@5 (91.4 vs. 90.6) while using ~80× fewer pairs and ~600× fewer GPU days. On COCO text-to-image, FuseMix(D,B) reaches R@1 = 46.3, R@5 = 74.6, R@10 = 83.4. For audio-text retrieval, FuseMix(W&H,B) trained on 65K pairs achieves AudioCaps text-to-audio R@1 = 43.1, R@5 = 77.4, R@10 = 87.4, surpassing all low-data baselines and approaching methods trained on 2.4M–27M pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Flickr30K text-to-image retrieval, FuseMix(D,B) with 5M pairs outperforms CLIP (400M pairs) on R@1 (71.2 vs. 68.7) and R@5 (91.4 vs. 90.6), though it trails slightly on R@10 (94.7 vs. 95.2). Against the low-data CLIP baseline (3M pairs), FuseMix(D,B) achieves a dramatic +16.9 R@1 improvement (71.2 vs. 54.3). However, FuseMix still lags behind the strongest internet-scale methods: ALIGN (1B pairs) reaches 75.7 R@1 and FILIP (300M pairs) reaches 75.0 R@1 on Flickr30K text-to-image. For audio-text retrieval, FuseMix(W&H,B) with 65K pairs beats LAION-CLAP (700K pairs) by +6.9 R@1 on AudioCaps text-to-audio (43.1 vs. 36.2) and outperforms ONE-PEACE (2.4M pairs) by +0.6 R@1 (43.1 vs. 42.5), while remaining below VAST (27M pairs) which achieves 52.0 R@1. Among low-data audio-text baselines, FuseMix(W&H,B) leads all competitors: CLAP-HTSAT (70K) at 34.7 R@1, ML-ACT (50K/15K) at 33.9, and MMT (50K/15K) at 36.1 on AudioCaps text-to-audio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of multimodal alignment (CLIP, ALIGN, ImageBind) that trains one or both encoders end-to-end with contrastive objectives on internet-scale paired data. Instead, it freezes arbitrary pre-trained unimodal encoders and trains only lightweight MLP fusion adapters, drawing its augmentation strategy from mixup (Zhang et al., ICLR 2018) but operating in latent space with a shared interpolation coefficient across modalities. The closest prior work is LiT, which also freezes a pre-trained image encoder, but still trains the text encoder from scratch; FuseMix eliminates all encoder training entirely. The plug-and-play modular design also contrasts with the tightly coupled end-to-end fusion that characterizes most prior multimodal alignment methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: multimodal fusion on a single GPU with ~5M pairs (vs. 400M+) dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for research and deployment in compute- and data-constrained settings. The finding that dataset quality and diversity (via DPPs) matter as much or more than raw quantity in low-data regimes provides actionable guidance for practitioners sourcing paired data. A key limitation is that performance is bounded by the semantic information already encoded in the frozen unimodal encoders, and the paper acknowledges that efficient fine-tuning of those encoders during fusion remains an open direction. The framework also opens the door to multimodal fusion with encoders accessible only via API, which could enable new deployment scenarios.
