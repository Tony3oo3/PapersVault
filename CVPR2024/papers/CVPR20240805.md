## Problem

Existing multi-agent human pose forecasting methods are limited to short horizons (~3s) and few agents (~2–3), and they model inter-agent interaction by attending over all joints of all agents simultaneously, yielding O(T²·N²·J²) complexity that becomes intractable in long-term, multi-agent settings. Compounding this, no dataset with authentic long-term (5s+) multi-agent (6+) interactions existed, forcing prior work to naively merge unrelated scenes that lack genuine agent-to-agent influence.

## Input / Output

The model takes a past 3D pose sequence of N_A agents (each with J joints over T_p timesteps) in global coordinates and produces F multi-modal future pose sequences of T_f timesteps in global coordinates. Internally, the input is decomposed into a global hip-joint trajectory and a local pose (all joints relative to the hip), and the output is composed by joint-wise addition of the predicted trajectory and local pose. The new JRDB-GMP dataset supports up to 24 agents and 5-second prediction horizons, with 27 real-world locations captured via omnidirectional cameras.

## Method

T2P (Trajectory2Pose) follows a coarse-to-fine strategy: a pose encoder (MPBP + transformer) encodes intra-agent local pose dynamics, a trajectory module (HiVT-style graph attention) encodes inter-agent global hip trajectories, and a Traj-Pose module fuses both via agent-wise graph attention to capture reciprocal global–local cues. A trajectory decoder then predicts F multi-modal global hip trajectories, and a transformer-based pose decoder generates local poses conditioned on each trajectory mode using concatenated trajectory+pose queries, with IDCT converting frequency-domain outputs back to local coordinates. This decoupling reduces interaction complexity from O(T²·N²·J²) to O(T·N²) by modeling inter-agent interaction only at the trajectory (hip) level rather than across all joints. Training uses a joint L2 loss on the closest-to-GT mode for both trajectory and pose, supervised end-to-end on a single A6000 GPU.

## Results

On the novel JRDB-GMP long-term setting (2s input / 5s prediction), T2P achieves JPE@5s of 390.4 and FDE@5s of 361.0, outperforming the best baseline TBIFormer by 18.9% and 21.3% respectively. On the standard CMU-Mocap (UMPM) benchmark (1s/2s), T2P reaches JPE@2s of 262.7 and FDE@2s of 188.9, a 17.3% FDE improvement over TBIFormer. On 3DPW (0.8s/1.6s), the FDE gain is even larger at 26.7% (158.2 vs 215.9). The paper reports over 10% FDE improvement on all three datasets, with the largest gains concentrated in the long-term multi-agent JRDB-GMP setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2P outperforms all three baselines on JPE and FDE across every dataset and prediction horizon. Against TBIFormer (the closest competitor and the paper's architectural baseline), T2P achieves 9.7–18.9% lower JPE and 17.3–26.7% lower FDE, with the largest margins on the long-term JRDB-GMP 2s/5s setting (JPE@5s: 390.4 vs 481.3; FDE@5s: 361.0 vs 458.8). Against MRT, gains are comparable in magnitude (e.g., FDE@5s: 361.0 vs 454.8 on JRDB-GMP). JRT is the weakest baseline, particularly on JRDB-GMP where its joint-relation attention mechanism fails to model global locomotion, yielding JPE@5s of 538.8 (37.7% worse than T2P). On APE (local pose accuracy), T2P wins in most settings but TBIFormer edges out by 0.2 mm on JRDB 1s/2s APE@1s (70.6 vs 70.8) and ties at 83.3 on APE@2s, indicating that the local-pose advantage is most pronounced in the long-term multi-agent regime where trajectory conditioning provides stronger global-intent cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TBIFormer [47] for its pose encoder (MPBP module + transformer) and on HiVT [88] for its trajectory encoder and graph-attention interaction mechanism. The key conceptual departure is a coarse-to-fine decomposition inspired by goal-conditioned trajectory prediction: rather than holistically predicting all joints in global coordinates as prior multi-agent pose forecasters (MRT, JRT, TBIFormer) do, T2P first forecasts multi-modal global hip trajectories and then conditions local pose generation on each trajectory mode. This decoupling reduces the interaction modeling complexity from O(T²·N²·J²) to O(T·N²), enabling practical long-term multi-agent forecasting that prior joint-wise methods could not scale to.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JRDB-GMP dataset fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark supported long-term (5s+) multi-agent (6+) pose forecasting with authentic interactions, forcing previous works to naively merge disparate scenes. The consistent 10%+ FDE improvement across all three datasets (including simpler ones) suggests the trajectory-conditioned decomposition generalizes well beyond the target regime. Open questions include whether the BEV-based 3D pose extraction pipeline introduces systematic biases that limit dataset fidelity, whether the approach scales to even longer horizons or denser crowds, and whether the coarse-to-fine strategy transfers to other articulated-body forecasting tasks (e.g., animals, robots).
