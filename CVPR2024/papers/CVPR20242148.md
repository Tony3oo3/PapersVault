## Problem

Existing diffusion-based adversarial purification methods (e.g., DiffPure, GDPM) use the adversarial sample as input or guidance, so the imperceptible perturbation still contaminates the diffusion model's generative trajectory, causing the output to deviate from what a clean input would produce. The paper asks whether one can mimic the clean-input diffusion trajectory without actually knowing the clean image, thereby eliminating the perturbation's influence on the reverse process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242148/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single adversarial RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 256×256 for ImageNet) and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model (EDM checkpoint for CIFAR-10/100, Nie et al.'s model for ImageNet). Unlike prior purification methods that start the reverse process from a noisy version of the adversarial sample, MimicDiffusion starts from pure Gaussian noise and uses the adversarial image solely as a guidance signal applied only during a middle time interval [50%T, 20%T], producing a purified clean image as output.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the standard L2 guidance distance with Manhattan (L1) distance, exploiting the fact that L1 gradients are binary (±1), so when the minimum per-pixel distance between the generated image and the clean image exceeds the maximum perturbation magnitude (long-range regime), the guidance gradient is provably identical whether computed against the adversarial or clean sample (Lemma 1). For the short-range regime where this equivalence breaks down, a 4× super-resolution (Bicubic interpolation) non-linear transform is applied to both the estimated image and the adversarial sample, inflating the Manhattan distance back into the long-range regime. Two independent guidance terms—long-range (direct L1 to adversarial sample) and short-range (L1 after super-resolution)—are summed and applied only in the middle reverse-process interval [50%T, 20%T], avoiding the late steps where the short-range condition dominates and reducing gradient computation cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WideResNet-28-10 against AutoAttack (ℓ∞, ε=8/255), MimicDiffusion achieves 92.67% robust accuracy and 93.32% standard accuracy, outperforming the best diffusion-based baseline (GDPM at 71.18%) by 21.49%. On CIFAR-100 with WideResNet-28-10, it reaches 61.35% robust accuracy versus 38.83% for the best adversarial training baseline. On ImageNet with ResNet-50, the method achieves 61.53% average robust accuracy, a 17.64% absolute improvement over the prior best. The gap between standard and robust accuracy is narrowed to under 1.1% across all settings, indicating near-complete purification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MimicDiffusion outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and threat model tested. On CIFAR-10 (ℓ∞, ε=8/255), it surpasses the strongest diffusion-based purification baselines—GDPM (71.18%) and DiffPure/Nie et al. (71.03%)—by over 21% in robust accuracy on WRN-28-10, and beats the best adversarial training method (Rebuffi et al., 65.24%) by 27.43%. On CIFAR-100, it improves over the best adversarial training baseline (Wang et al., 38.83%) by 22.52% on WRN-28-10. Against the adaptive BPDA+EOT attack specifically designed to break purification methods, MimicDiffusion achieves 92.00% robust accuracy versus 81.56% for the best prior method (Nie et al. with t*=0.1), a 10.44% margin. The method also maintains the highest standard accuracy (93.32–93.63%) among all approaches, confirming that purification does not degrade clean-image performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the guided diffusion purification framework of GDPM (Wang et al., 2022), which uses an adversarial sample as a guidance signal during the reverse diffusion process, and on the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) formulation (Chung et al., ICLR 2023) for the guided factor and super-resolution operation. The key departure is a re-examination of the distance metric in the guidance term: prior methods (DiffPure, GDPM) rely on L2 distance and careful hyperparameter tuning (optimal time step, iteration count), whereas this paper argues that the adversarial perturbation contaminates the L2 gradient and proposes Manhattan (L1) distance, whose ±1-valued gradients are provably invariant to small perturbations in the 'long-range' regime. The super-resolution trick for the 'short-range' regime is borrowed from DPS's measurement framework but repurposed to convert a short-range problem into a long-range one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is training-free (uses a pre-trained diffusion model) and achieves robust accuracy within ~1% of standard accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, a gap that prior purification methods could not close. The core insight—that L1 distance's discrete gradient structure naturally neutralizes small adversarial perturbations in the guidance term—is simple, theoretically grounded (Lemma 1), and potentially transferable to other guided-diffusion or score-based inverse problems. The main limitation acknowledged is the gradient computation cost of the two guidance terms; the authors flag gradient-free alternatives as future work, and the method's dependence on a high-quality pre-trained diffusion model for the target domain remains a practical constraint.
