## Problem

Reconstructing large-scale 360° dynamic driving scenes from sparse, outward-facing multi-camera views at high ego-vehicle speeds remains unsolved: NeRF-based methods are computationally heavy and fail with minimal view overlap, while existing 3DGS extensions handle only single dynamic objects or require dense multi-view inputs. No prior framework simultaneously models the extensive static background and multiple fast-moving objects with accurate occlusion relationships in a unified, efficient representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241427/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential keyframes from 6 surrounding cameras (nuScenes) or a single front camera (KITTI-360), optional multi-frame LiDAR sweeps, and 3D bounding boxes (from annotations or a detector/tracker) for dynamic objects. Inputs are organized into depth-ordered bins as the ego vehicle moves, and LiDAR points are projected onto each camera image to serve as geometric initialization. The output is a composite 3D Gaussian scene (static background + per-object dynamic Gaussians) that supports photorealistic rendering from arbitrary surrounding viewpoints, long-term dynamic reconstruction, and controllable insertion of new objects for corner-case simulation.

## Method

DrivingGaussian introduces Composite Gaussian Splatting, which hierarchically decomposes the scene into an Incremental Static 3D Gaussian background and a Composite Dynamic Gaussian Graph. The static background is built progressively: the scene is divided into depth-ordered bins, each initialized from LiDAR-projected points, and successive bins are aligned to the previous bin's Gaussians via overlapping multi-camera regions, avoiding scale confusion from distant views. Each dynamic object receives its own ensemble of Gaussians parameterized by a transform matrix, bounding-box center, and orientation; a SAM-based model extracts pixel-wise masks within the bounding boxes for precise object isolation. During global rendering, dynamic objects are composited into the static field in chronological order, with opacity adjusted by camera distance to resolve inter-object occlusion. Training minimizes a combined loss of Tile SSIM, a robust L2 term, and a LiDAR position supervision term, with the LiDAR prior also used for dense bundle adjustment to improve multi-camera registration.

## Results

On nuScenes (6-camera surrounding views), DrivingGaussian with LiDAR prior (Ours-L) achieves PSNR 28.74, SSIM 0.865, and LPIPS 0.237, outperforming the strongest NeRF-based competitor EmerNeRF (PSNR 26.75, SSIM 0.760, LPIPS 0.311) by ~2 dB in PSNR and 0.105 in SSIM. The SfM-initialized variant (Ours-S) still reaches PSNR 28.36, SSIM 0.851, LPIPS 0.256, surpassing 3DGS (PSNR 26.08) and 4DGS (PSNR 19.79). On KITTI-360 (monocular), Ours-L achieves PSNR 25.62 and SSIM 0.868, leading the best baseline DNMP (PSNR 23.41, SSIM 0.846) by over 2 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrivingGaussian wins across all metrics on both datasets against every compared baseline. On nuScenes, the largest margin is over 4DGS (PSNR 19.79 → 28.74, +8.95 dB), reflecting 4DGS's inability to handle multi-camera driving scenes. Against the closest NeRF competitor EmerNeRF (PSNR 26.75), DrivingGaussian gains +1.99 dB PSNR and +0.105 SSIM while reducing LPIPS from 0.311 to 0.237. Over 3DGS (PSNR 26.08), the gain is +2.66 dB PSNR and +0.148 SSIM, attributed to the incremental static and dynamic Gaussian graph modules. Among LiDAR-assisted NeRF methods, S-NeRF (PSNR 25.43) and SUDS (PSNR 21.26) are outperformed by 3.31 and 7.48 dB respectively. On KITTI-360 (monocular), Ours-L (PSNR 25.62) leads DNMP (23.41) by 2.21 dB and 3DGS (22.93) by 2.69 dB, confirming the method's robustness even without multi-camera input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and 4DGS to the challenging domain of large-scale, surrounding (360°) dynamic driving scenes by introducing a Composite Gaussian Splatting framework that decomposes the scene into an incrementally-built static background and a graph of individually-modeled dynamic objects. It departs from NeRF-based driving scene methods (EmerNeRF, S-NeRF, SUDS) that rely on ray sampling and struggle with sparse multi-camera views and high-speed motion, and from prior dynamic 3DGS work (4DGS, Deformable 3DGS) that targets single-camera or bounded scenes. A key conceptual departure is using LiDAR as a geometric initialization prior for Gaussians rather than merely as depth supervision, and the incremental bin-based construction of static Gaussians that exploits ego-vehicle motion to avoid scale confusion across distant time steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DrivingGaussian provides the first end-to-end Gaussian Splatting framework for full-surround dynamic driving scenes, enabling photorealistic multi-camera view synthesis and controllable corner-case simulation that directly supports autonomous driving safety validation. The practical impact is significant: the ability to insert arbitrary dynamic objects into reconstructed scenes (Figure 6) opens a path for generating safety-critical training data without real-world collection. Limitations include the heavy compute requirement (8× RTX 8000, 60K iterations), dependence on accurate 3D bounding boxes and SAM-based segmentation for dynamic object extraction, and evaluation limited to two datasets. Open questions include scalability to much longer driving sequences, real-time inference for closed-loop simulation, and robustness to adverse weather or sensor degradation.
