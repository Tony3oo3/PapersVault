## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods assume complete, noise-free visual observations, yet in real-world autonomous driving, pedestrians and vehicles frequently fall entirely outside the camera's field of view due to blind spots, occlusions, or high vehicle speed. No prior work addresses predicting trajectories solely from out-of-sight observations, and the noisy sensor data (GPS, odometers) available for such objects lacks ground truth for supervised denoising, creating a critical safety gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the noisy 3D sensor trajectory (GPS/odometer) of an out-of-sight agent over 100 timestamps, plus 100-timestamp pairs of in-sight visual (2D camera-frame) and sensor trajectories from other visible agents. It produces a denoised 2D visual trajectory for the out-of-sight agent and a predicted future visual trajectory for the next 100 timestamps. The in-sight pairs are consumed solely to estimate camera matrix embeddings, not as direct trajectory context for the target agent.

## Method

The core insight is to construct unsupervised denoising supervision by projecting denoised sensor trajectories into the visual domain via a learned world-to-camera transformation, then comparing against ground-truth visual trajectories available only during training. The architecture comprises four modules: a Mobile Denoising Encoder (Transformer) that refines noisy 3D sensor trajectories; a Camera Parameters Estimator (Transformer) that predicts per-timestamp camera matrix embeddings M = w·K·R_t from in-sight visual-sensor trajectory pairs; a Visual Positioning Projection that applies the estimated M to map denoised 3D coordinates into 2D camera-frame visual trajectories; and an Out-of-Sight Prediction Decoder (Transformer) that predicts future visual trajectories from the denoised visual sequence. The denoising loss (L2 between projected and ground-truth visual trajectories) jointly trains the MDE and CPE without requiring ground-truth denoised sensor data, while a separate prediction loss supervises the OPD.

## Results

On the Vi-Fi dataset, OOSTraj achieves a SUM score of 27.24 (MSE-D: 13.42, MSE-P: 13.83), outperforming the best baseline Vanilla Transformer (SUM: 28.33) and dramatically surpassing ViTag (SUM: 200.90). On the JRDB dataset, OOSTraj achieves SUM 25.51 (MSE-D: 10.52, MSE-P: 14.99), beating Vanilla Transformer (SUM: 33.37) by a margin of 7.86. The method's advantage is most pronounced in the denoising metric (MSE-D), where it reduces error by 5.5% on Vi-Fi and 37% on JRDB relative to the strongest baseline, confirming that the vision-positioning denoising module is the primary driver of performance gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OOSTraj outperforms all five baselines on the SUM metric across both datasets. Against the closest competitor, Vanilla Transformer, it improves SUM by 1.09 on Vi-Fi (27.24 vs. 28.33) and by 7.86 on JRDB (25.51 vs. 33.37), with the JRDB gap driven primarily by a 37% reduction in denoising error (MSE-D: 10.52 vs. 16.71). ViTag, the state-of-the-art method for vision-wireless identity association on Vi-Fi, performs worst on both datasets (SUM 200.90 on Vi-Fi, 143.08 on JRDB), indicating that identity-association architectures are poorly suited to trajectory denoising and prediction. Vanilla RNN shows the most dataset-dependent behavior: it is the second-best baseline on Vi-Fi (SUM 31.61) but the worst on JRDB (SUM 112.40), suggesting its inability to handle JRDB's different sensor noise characteristics. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 2) further confirm that adding the VPD module to any baseline consistently improves both denoising and prediction, with the largest gains observed for Vanilla RNN on JRDB (SUM drops from 138.41 to 31.32).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem space at the intersection of vision-wireless fusion and obstructed trajectory prediction. Prior work on partial/obstructed trajectory prediction (Xu et al. 2023, Fujii et al. 2021, Zhang et al. 2023) still requires at least some visual observations of the target agent, while vision-wireless fusion studies (Alahi et al. 2015, Liu et al. 2020) focus on identity association or localization rather than trajectory denoising and prediction. The key conceptual departure is using visual trajectories of *other* in-view agents to estimate camera parameters, which then enables unsupervised denoising of a completely unseen agent's noisy sensor trajectory via world-to-camera projection — repurposing geometric camera calibration as a denoising supervision signal rather than a one-time calibration step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine safety gap in autonomous driving where pedestrians in blind spots or behind occlusions have no visual history for prediction models. However, the experimental validation is limited: out-of-sight scenarios are simulated by artificially obscuring pedestrians in existing datasets rather than arising from true occlusion geometry, and baselines are relatively simple vanilla sequence models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes when no in-view agents are available for camera parameter estimation, how it performs under real-time constraints, and whether the simulated occlusion faithfully captures the distribution shift of genuinely out-of-sight trajectories. The plug-and-play framing suggests the VPD module could be adopted by other trajectory prediction pipelines, but this remains to be validated on more diverse architectures and datasets.
