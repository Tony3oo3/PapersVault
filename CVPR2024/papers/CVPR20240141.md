## Problem

Many relationships between tasks and datasets—such as hierarchical containment and transferability—are inherently asymmetric, yet standard representation and visualization tools (t-SNE, UMAP) rely on symmetric Euclidean distances that cannot capture directionality. No prior work has explored using geometric embedding spaces (box embeddings, hyperbolic spaces) to represent entire datasets and model these asymmetric task relationships in a learnable, interpretable way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240141/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base task representation vector per dataset—either a CLIP mean/variance concatenation (2048 or 4096-d), a diagonal Fisher Information Matrix (17024-d), or a binary attribute vector (15-d)—and produces a k-dimensional axis-aligned hyperrectangle defined by lower-left and upper-right coordinates in R^{2×k}. From these boxes, an asymmetric relationship score is computed as the intersection volume normalized by the source box volume, and the embeddings can be projected to 2D for interpretable visualization where box size encodes dataset scale and overlap encodes relationship strength and direction.

## Method

TASK2BOX learns a 3-layer MLP mapping f_θ from a Euclidean task representation to a box embedding, with two linear heads predicting the k-dimensional lower-left corner and the k-dimensional side lengths. The training objective combines three terms: (1) MSE between ground-truth asymmetric relationship values and the box volumetric overlap d_box(z_i, z_j) = vol(z_i ∩ z_j) / vol(z_i), which naturally encodes containment (d_box = 1) and partial overlap (0 < d_box < 1); (2) a Euclidean distance term that pulls non-overlapping box centers closer to bootstrap learning from sparse supervision; and (3) a regularization term penalizing irregular box shapes while preventing degenerate zero-volume solutions. The key insight is that the geometric inductive bias of volumetric overlap enforces transitivity and directionality automatically (if z_i ⊂ z_j and z_j ∩ z_k = ∅, then z_i ∩ z_k = ∅), yielding substantially better generalization to unseen relationships and novel datasets than MLP or linear classifiers trained on the same base representations.

## Results

On iNaturalist+CUB, TASK2BOX(5D) with CLIP mean embeddings achieves 84.67% F1 on existing dataset relationships and 73.79% on novel datasets, outperforming the MLP classifier (45.25% / 39.06%) by over 39 and 34 percentage points respectively. On ImageNet instruments, TASK2BOX(5D) reaches 90.58% F1 on existing and 76.95% on novel datasets. On Taskonomy, TASK2BOX(3D) achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.93 on existing tasks and 0.48 on novel tasks, compared to 0.88 and 0.31 for the MLP baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TASK2BOX consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset and representation type. Against the Task2Vec-derived asymmetric cosine similarity [4], TASK2BOX(5D) achieves 84.67% F1 versus 9.29% (μ_CLIP) and 11.54% ([μ,σ²]_CLIP) on existing iNaturalist+CUB datasets—a gap of over 70 percentage points. The MLP classifier, which directly predicts relationships from the same base representations without the box-embedding inductive bias, is the strongest baseline but still trails TASK2BOX by 39.42 points (84.67% vs 45.25%) on existing iNaturalist+CUB relationships. On ImageNet, the gap narrows slightly for novel datasets (76.95% vs 62.24% for MLP), suggesting the box prior is most beneficial when generalizing to unseen relationships. KL divergence and asymmetric Euclidean baselines perform near-random, confirming that simple asymmetric distances are insufficient for capturing hierarchical task structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes box embeddings—originally developed for WordNet hierarchies and knowledge graph reasoning (Query2box, Chheda et al.)—to represent entire datasets and model asymmetric relationships between them, a setting not previously explored. It builds on established task representation methods (CLIP features, Task2Vec's Fisher Information Matrix, attribute vectors) but introduces a learnable MLP mapping from these representations into box space, rather than using the representations directly for pairwise comparison. The work departs from symmetric visualization approaches (t-SNE, UMAP) and tree-based layouts that cannot capture multi-parent or cyclic relationships, and from simpler asymmetric distances (KL divergence, asymmetric cosine) that lack the geometric structure to enforce transitivity of containment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is an interpretable, low-dimensional visualization of dataset relationships that supports constant-time embedding of new datasets without retraining, demonstrated on 131 Hugging Face classification datasets. However, performance on truly novel datasets drops substantially (e.g., FIM-based 5D drops from 79.72% to 34.88% F1 on iNaturalist+CUB), and the method does not incorporate information about training procedures or model architecture, limiting its applicability to broader transfer learning scenarios. Open questions include extending beyond image-text modalities (where CLIP is confined), scaling to larger and more heterogeneous task collections, and whether the box inductive bias generalizes to non-hierarchical asymmetric relationships.
