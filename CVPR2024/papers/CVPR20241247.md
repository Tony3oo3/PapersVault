## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud backbones face a fundamental trade-off: point-based methods excel on individual objects and small indoor scenes but fail to scale to large outdoor scenes, while sparse-voxel methods scale well but introduce discretization errors on small or irregularly-distributed point sets. Laser-scanned sensors (e.g., LiDAR) produce point clouds with inherent 1D curve-like traversal structure that all existing backbones discard, treating points as unstructured sets or forcing them into global coordinate grids.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires laser-scanned point clouds where each point carries not only 3D coordinates but also an acquisition timestamp and an integer laser beam ID (readily available from LiDAR sensors). These metadata fields are used to order points along each beam's sweep and split them into polylines at surface discontinuities (where consecutive points exceed a distance threshold δ). The output is a per-point semantic class label for segmentation, or a class prediction for object classification, with the network operating at the original point resolution without any voxelization or projection.

## Method

CurveCloudNet first converts the input point cloud into a 'curve cloud'—an unordered set of polylines formed by ordering each laser beam's points by timestamp and splitting at spatial discontinuities. It then applies a U-Net-style backbone that strategically mixes curve layers at high resolution (where 1D structure is dense) with standard point-based layers at low resolution (for cross-curve communication). The curve-specific operations include O(N) 1D farthest-point sampling for downsampling, 1D ball-query grouping that keeps neighborhoods on continuous surface sections, distance-weighted feature interpolation for upsampling, and symmetric 1D convolutions that concatenate L1-norm feature gradients along each curve and apply bi-directionally symmetric kernels. This design injects a lightweight surface-aware prior from the curve ordering while maintaining the flexibility of point-based representations, achieving state-of-the-art segmentation across object-level (ShapeNet, KortX) and scene-level (A2D2, nuScenes, KITTI) benchmarks.

## Results

CurveCloudNet achieves the best average mIOU of 72.7% across five diverse datasets (ShapeNet, KortX, A2D2, nuScenes, Semantic KITTI), outperforming the best point-based baseline (PointNext, 66.6%) and the best voxel-based baseline (SphereFormer, 70.6%). On object-level tasks, CurveCloudNet leads with 83.1% mIOU on ShapeNet and 78.9% on KortX. On large outdoor scenes, it is competitive with SphereFormer (78.0 vs. 79.5 on nuScenes; 54.1 vs. 55.1 on A2D2) while using significantly less GPU memory (1.14 GB vs. 0.81 GB on nuScenes) and fewer parameters (28.8M vs. 32.3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CurveCloudNet outperforms all point-based baselines (PointNet++, DGCNN, PointMLP, PointNext, RandLANet) on every dataset, with the largest gaps on large outdoor scenes: on nuScenes it beats PointMLP by 10.1 mIOU (78.0 vs. 67.9) and PointNext by 13.0 mIOU (78.0 vs. 65.0), while also reducing latency by ~2×. Against the closest point-based competitor PointNext, CurveCloudNet gains 3.3 mIOU on ShapeNet (83.1 vs. 82.8) and 5.2 mIOU on KortX (78.9 vs. 73.7). CurveNet, which also uses curve-like structures but via guided random walks rather than physical laser traversals, is competitive on ShapeNet (82.8 vs. 83.1) but fails catastrophically on large scenes (4.4 mIOU on A2D2, >48 GB GPU on nuScenes). Among voxel-based methods, SphereFormer is the strongest competitor, narrowly beating CurveCloudNet on A2D2 (55.1 vs. 54.1) and nuScenes (79.5 vs. 78.0), but CurveCloudNet surpasses it on all object-level tasks (KortX: 78.9 vs. 69.7; ShapeNet: 83.1 vs. 79.5) and on KITTI (69.5 vs. 69.0). MinkowskiNet, Cylinder3D, and PolarNet are consistently outperformed by CurveCloudNet across all datasets where they report results.

## Relation to Prior Work

CurveCloudNet builds directly on the point-based backbone lineage (PointNet++ set abstraction and feature propagation, DGCNN graph convolution, and improvements from PointMLP/PointNext/RandLANet) for its lower-resolution point operations, while introducing a new curve-based representation for higher-resolution processing. It departs from both the point-based camp (which treats points as unstructured sets) and the voxel/discretization camp (which imposes global geometric grids like polar or cylindrical coordinates) by exploiting the 1D polyline structure inherent in physical laser traversals. The closest prior work, CurveNet [61], also reasons along curves but constructs them via guided random walks on uniformly sampled points rather than recovering them from actual scanner timestamps, and does not scale to large outdoor scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing trade-off in 3D point cloud processing: point-based backbones excel on small objects but fail to scale to large outdoor scenes, while sparse-voxel backbones scale well but incur discretization errors on small or irregularly distributed point sets. By parameterizing the point cloud as a curve cloud, CurveCloudNet claims to resolve this trade-off with a single architecture that is both efficient at scale and accurate on individual objects, validated across five datasets spanning ±1 m to ±70 m scenes and three distinct laser patterns. Key limitations include restriction to laser-scanned data with explicit beam IDs and timestamps (not uniformly sampled point clouds), and the modest margins over SphereFormer on the largest scenes. Open questions raised by the authors include extending to virtual curves for non-laser data, curve-to-curve communication, and curve self-attention.
