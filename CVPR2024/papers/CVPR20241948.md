## Problem

Conventional post-training quantization for diffusion models applies a single shared rounding function across all denoising timesteps, despite activation distributions varying dramatically between early and late steps, and constructs calibration sets from randomly sampled timesteps that fail to capture the true latent distribution. Both issues compound to produce large quantization errors that significantly degrade generated image quality, especially at low bitwidths (6-bit).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241948/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion models (DDIM at 32×32/64×64, LDMs/Stable Diffusion at 256×256) and produces quantized versions with 6-bit or 8-bit weights and activations using uniform quantization. For unconditional generation, input is standard Gaussian noise; for conditional generation, input is a text prompt or class label. The quantized model performs the same 100-step iterative denoising as the full-precision model but with integer arithmetic, and the calibration set consists of only 1024 latent images generated at actively selected timesteps.

## Method

APQ-DM introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, distribution-aware quantization partitions all T timesteps into G groups (typically 8) and assigns a dedicated rounding/clipping function per group; a differentiable search jointly optimizes all group parameters and learnable importance weights by quantizing activations through every group function and summing outputs weighted by σ_g, with an entropy penalty pushing weights toward one-hot to select the best group per timestep. Second, an active timestep selection criterion derived from a generalized structural risk minimization bound combines the entropy of importance weights (measuring distributional uncertainty) with a UCB-style sampling-frequency term (1/(N_t+1)) to iteratively choose which timesteps generate the next batch of calibration images, balancing exploitation of informative timesteps against exploration of under-sampled ones.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (DDIM, W8A8), APQ-DM achieves FID 4.24 and IS 9.07, essentially matching the full-precision baseline (FID 4.23, IS 9.07), while the best prior method Q-Diffusion reaches only FID 4.78. At the more challenging W6A6 setting, APQ-DM attains FID 6.57 on CIFAR-10 versus 9.19 for Q-Diffusion and 11.28 for PTQ4DM. On LSUN-Bedroom (W8A8), APQ-DM improves FID from 7.48 (PTQ4DM) to 6.46 and IS from 2.23 to 2.55. For class-conditional ImageNet generation with LDMs (W8A8), APQ-DM achieves FID 11.58 and IS 179.13, outperforming PTQ4DM (FID 12.59, IS 161.75) by a substantial margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APQ-DM consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and bitwidth setting. Against LSQ (shared quantization with random calibration), the gap is dramatic: on CIFAR-10 W6A6, APQ-DM achieves FID 6.57 versus LSQ's 35.96, a more than 5× improvement, because LSQ's single rounding function cannot handle the diverse activation distributions across timesteps. Against PTQ4DM (step-wise quantization with heuristic timestep selection), APQ-DM improves FID by 1.02 on LSUN-Bedroom W8A8 (6.46 vs. 7.48) and by 4.71 on CIFAR-10 W6A6 (6.57 vs. 11.28), with the advantage growing at lower bitwidths where quantization errors are more impactful. Against Q-Diffusion (step-wise quantization with a different calibration strategy), APQ-DM leads by 0.54 FID on CIFAR-10 W8A8 (4.24 vs. 4.78) and by 1.85 FID on CIFAR-10 W6A6 (6.57 vs. 9.19). The paper attributes the gains to two factors: distribution-aware group-wise quantization that avoids overfitting from per-timestep parameters, and SRM-guided active timestep selection that yields more informative calibration images than random or heuristic sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) line of work from conventional vision networks to diffusion models, building directly on PTQ4DM [31] and Q-Diffusion [20], which introduced step-wise quantization functions to handle the diverse activation distributions across denoising timesteps. The key departure is replacing per-timestep quantization (which overfits with limited calibration data) with a distribution-aware grouping strategy that partitions timesteps into a small number of groups via differentiable search, combined with an SRM-based active timestep selection for calibration set construction that replaces the random or heuristic sampling used in prior diffusion-quantization work. The differentiable search framework and SRM principle are borrowed from broader optimization and statistical learning literature but are novel in their application to diffusion model quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying large diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) on resource-constrained devices, as the method achieves near-full-precision generation quality at 6-bit with negligible additional inference cost. A notable open question is whether the grouping strategy and SRM-based calibration selection generalize to video diffusion models, autoregressive diffusion, or even lower bitwidths (4-bit) where quantization errors compound more severely. The paper also leaves open whether the differentiable search overhead during calibration (10 epochs over 1024 images) is acceptable in production pipelines, and whether the approach can be combined with sampling-path shortening methods for further speedup.
