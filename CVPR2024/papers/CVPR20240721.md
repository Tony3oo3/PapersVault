## Problem

Prior perpetual view generation methods are restricted to a single scene domain (e.g., nature photos in InfiniteNature-Zero) or a single scene type (e.g., cave-like environments in SceneScape), preventing the generation of diverse, multi-environment journeys. This paper addresses the broader problem of perpetual 3D scene generation from an arbitrary starting point, where the goal is to synthesize a long sequence of diverse yet geometrically and semantically coherent 3D scenes connected along a continuous camera trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240721/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single user-provided image (real photo or generated art) or a text description, pairing them into a unified representation via DALL-E 3 (text-to-image) or a VLM (image-to-text). It outputs a potentially unbounded sequence of 3D colored point clouds representing coherently connected scenes, renderable as a fly-through video; the same input can produce different journeys across runs due to stochastic outpainting and LLM sampling. The sequence length is user-controlled (up to 30 scenes demonstrated), and the point cloud representation is maintained incrementally rather than as a single global scene.

## Method

WonderJourney is a fully modular, training-free framework with three components: an LLM that autoregressively generates scene descriptions (style, objects, background) with memory of all past scenes, a text-driven visual generation module that produces 3D point clouds, and a VLM that validates outputs for artifacts like photo borders or out-of-focus objects. The visual module lifts the current scene image to a point cloud via monocular depth estimation (MIDAS), refines depth using SAM-based frontal-plane modeling (replacing depth with segment median when disparity range is small) and sky segmentation (OneFormer), then places a new camera, renders a partial view, and outpaints it with Stable Diffusion conditioned on the next scene description. Geometric consistency is enforced through a depth alignment loss that adapts the depth estimator to match analytically computed background and foreground depths, and a re-rendering consistency check that resolves occlusion/disocclusion depth inversions. A 'complete-as-you-go' process iteratively inserts additional cameras along the trajectory to fill in the scene, and the entire pipeline requires no training, leveraging only pretrained off-the-shelf models.

## Results

Since perpetual 3D scene generation is a new task without a standard benchmark, the paper evaluates via human preference with 400 participants (200 per baseline comparison, 12 binary-choice questions each). WonderJourney is preferred over InfiniteNature-Zero on all four axes: diversity (92.7%), visual quality (94.9%), scene complexity (91.5%), and overall interestingness (88.6%). Against SceneScape, WonderJourney is preferred on diversity (88.8%), visual quality (83.4%), scene complexity (80.0%), and overall interestingness (90.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WonderJourney is strongly preferred over both baselines on every evaluated axis. Against InfiniteNature-Zero, the margin is largest on visual quality (94.9%) and diversity (92.7%), which the authors attribute to InfiniteNature-Zero being trained exclusively on nature photos and thus only synthesizing nature scenes, whereas WonderJourney naturally transitions into diverse scene types (e.g., houses, mountaineers) connected to the starting nature scene. Against SceneScape, WonderJourney leads most decisively on overall interestingness (90.3%) and diversity (88.8%); the paper notes that SceneScape's textured-mesh representation causes all generated journeys to converge into cave-like scenes and that its limited white space prevents the generation of new objects. The smallest margin is against SceneScape on scene complexity (80.0%), suggesting SceneScape's single-scene mesh approach still produces moderately complex geometry within its constrained domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task—perpetual 3D scene generation—by generalizing prior perpetual view generation work (Infinite Nature, InfiniteNature-Zero, SceneScape) from single-domain (nature photos) or single-scene (cave) settings to diverse, multi-domain 3D scenes starting from any user-provided input. Rather than training a new end-to-end model, it repurposes a modular pipeline of off-the-shelf components (MIDAS depth, SAM segmentation, OneFormer sky segmentation, Stable Diffusion outpainting, an LLM for scene descriptions, and a VLM for validation) into a coherent generation loop. The closest conceptual neighbors are text-guided video generation works (TaleCrafter, VideoDirectorGPT), but those produce hard-cut video clips rather than geometrically connected 3D scenes, and single-scene 3D generation methods (Text2Room, ZeroNVS) that do not address long-range coherence across diverse environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, training-free design means the framework can be upgraded as any individual component (depth estimator, outpainter, LLM, VLM) improves, making it a practical scaffold for future perpetual 3D generation research. Key limitations include reliance on monocular depth estimation (inherent boundary and sky inaccuracies), the 'complete-as-you-go' process being computationally expensive for long trajectories, and the point cloud representation not scaling well in GPU memory. Open questions include whether 3D geometric consistency can be improved beyond the current depth-alignment and re-rendering heuristics, whether the approach can support interactive user control mid-journey, and whether quantitative 3D metrics (beyond user preference) can be established for this new task.
