## Problem

Existing MLLMs use a single language decoder to process all modalities, causing modality interference where fine-tuning for multi-modal tasks degrades pure-text performance. Prior approaches either freeze the LLM (limiting modality collaboration) or fine-tune it (risking catastrophic forgetting of language capabilities), leaving no mechanism to simultaneously exploit cross-modal synergy and preserve modality-specific representations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (processed at 224×224 during pre-training and 448×448 during instruction tuning), text instructions, and video clips (treated as image sequences). Images are encoded by ViT-L/14 into patch tokens, then compressed by a visual abstractor using 64 learnable queries to reduce the sequence length from O((P+L)²) to O((K+L)²) attention computation. Output is free-form generated text: image captions, VQA answers, video descriptions, or general text responses, all produced autoregressively by the LLaMA-2-7B decoder.

## Method

The core contribution is the Modality-Adaptive Module (MAM) embedded within each layer of the LLaMA-2-7B language decoder. MAM applies modality-separated operations: distinct layer normalizations for visual and text tokens, separate key and value projection matrices per modality (W^K₀, W^K₁, W^V₀, W^V₁), while sharing the query projection and FFN across modalities. This design enables cross-modal attention in a shared semantic space (via shared queries) while preserving modality-specific feature representations (via separate K/V projections), preventing granularity mismatch between dense visual features and discrete text embeddings. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: (1) pre-training on ~348M image-text pairs with the vision encoder, visual abstractor, and MAM trainable while the LLM backbone is frozen, and (2) joint instruction tuning on both text-only and multi-modal instruction data with the entire model (including the vision encoder) trainable, using layer-wise learning rate decay (factor 0.9) on the vision encoder to retain low-level visual representations while adapting higher-level semantics.

## Results

mPLUG-Owl2 (8.2B) achieves state-of-the-art performance on 8 classic vision-language benchmarks, including 137.3 CIDEr on COCO captioning, 85.1 CIDEr on Flickr30K (zero-shot), 79.4% on VQAv2, and 54.3% on TextVQA (zero-shot, OCR-free). On 5 recent zero-shot multi-modal benchmarks, it ranks first or second, scoring 64.5 on MMBench, 36.2 on MM-Vet, and 62.9 on Q-Bench. Notably, it also achieves SOTA on pure-text benchmarks, reaching 53.4 on MMLU, 45.0 on BBH, and 79.9 on ARC-Easy, surpassing LLaMA-2-Chat by 7.2, 9.4, and 8.3 points respectively. On video QA, it attains 67.1% accuracy on TGIF-QA and 65.4% on MSVD-QA in a zero-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest generalist baselines, mPLUG-Owl2 outperforms Qwen-VL-Chat (9.6B) on COCO captioning (137.3 vs. 131.9), Flickr30K (85.1 vs. 81.0), VQAv2 (79.4 vs. 78.2), OKVQA (57.7 vs. 56.6), and VizWizQA (54.5 vs. 38.9), while slightly trailing on GQA (56.1 vs. 57.5). Compared to LLaVA-1.5 (7.2B), it leads on VQAv2 (79.4 vs. 78.5), VizWizQA (54.5 vs. 50.0), and TextVQA without OCR (54.3 vs. 46.1), but falls behind on GQA (56.1 vs. 62.0) and MME (1450.19 vs. 1510.70). On zero-shot multi-modal benchmarks, mPLUG-Owl2 surpasses LLaVA-1.5 on MM-Vet (36.2 vs. 30.5) and Q-Bench (62.9 vs. 60.7), and edges out Qwen-VL-Chat on Q-Bench (62.9 vs. 61.6) and MMBench (64.5 vs. 60.6). On pure-text benchmarks, it dominates all LLaMA-2 family variants (LLaMA-2, LLaMA-2-Chat, Vicuna-v1.5, WizardLM), gaining +7.2 over LLaMA-2-Chat on MMLU and +9.4 on BBH. On video QA, it outperforms Video-ChatGPT on MSVD-QA (65.4 vs. 64.9) and TGIF-QA (67.1 vs. 51.4) under GPT-assisted evaluation, and beats InstructBLIP on MSRVTT-QA (23.6 vs. 22.1) under exact matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

mPLUG-Owl2 is a direct architectural evolution of mPLUG-Owl, retaining its Perceiver-based visual abstractor (itself derived from Flamingo's cross-attention design) and modularized pipeline, but introducing the Modality-Adaptive Module (MAM) to address a specific weakness in prior MLLMs: the granularity mismatch between dense visual features and discrete text embeddings. Where BLIP-2, LLaVA, and MiniGPT-4 project visual features into the language space and treat both modalities symmetrically, mPLUG-Owl2 decouples key/value projections and layer normalizations per modality while sharing query projections and FFN layers, enabling cross-modal attention without forcing identical representational treatment. The two-stage training paradigm (vision-language pre-training followed by joint text+multimodal instruction tuning with a trainable vision encoder) extends the LLaVA/mPLUG-Owl recipe by keeping the vision encoder unfrozen throughout, motivated by the observation that frozen encoders limit interpretation of complex visual content like scene text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically relevant contribution is the demonstration that, with proper architectural separation (MAM), joint multimodal instruction tuning can *improve* pure-text benchmarks (MMLU, BBH, ARC) rather than merely avoiding catastrophic forgetting—a finding that challenges the common assumption that multimodal fine-tuning is a zero-sum trade-off against language capability. However, the gains are modest (2–4 points over LLaMA-2-Chat), the MME score lags behind LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen-VL-Chat, and the MAM adds per-layer parameter overhead that may not scale favorably to much larger LLMs. Open questions include whether the modality-collaboration benefit generalizes to additional modalities (audio, 3D), whether the effect persists with more diverse and larger instruction datasets, and whether the trainable-vision-encoder strategy remains beneficial at higher resolutions or with stronger vision backbones.
