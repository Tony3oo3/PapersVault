## Problem

Real-world vision datasets have fixed distributions, limited annotation types, and cannot be reconfigured for controlled experiments, while existing synthetic alternatives each sacrifice a critical property: 3D reconstruction datasets lack object-level customizability, artist-designed scenes lack physical plausibility, and 3D simulators lack photorealism and ready-made tooling for CV researchers. No prior system simultaneously offers photorealistic rendering, physically plausible object states (articulation, fluids, cloth), and fine-grained parameter control across scene, object, and camera levels.

## Input / Output

The generator consumes user-specified constraints at three levels—scene (lighting, object category swaps), object (joint angles, semantic predicates like 'filled' or 'folded', spatial relations like 'inside' or 'ontop'), and camera (intrinsics, pose, trajectory)—and produces photorealistic RGB frames with a full suite of free annotations: instance/semantic segmentation, 2D/3D bounding boxes, depth, surface normals, optical flow, point clouds, scene graphs, unary state labels, binary predicate labels, and continuous values (e.g., joint openness fraction, container fill ratio). All labels are derived directly from the simulation state at render time, so adding new annotation modalities incurs no additional labeling cost.

## Method

BVS is built on OmniGibson, combining NVIDIA Omniverse's path-traced renderer with a physics engine to guarantee both photorealism and physical plausibility (no object penetration or levitation). The asset library comprises 8,841 object models (including articulated, fluid-capable, and soft-body objects) across 1,937 categories and 1,000 scene instances augmented from 51 artist-designed raw scenes, with collision meshes refined via V-HACD/CoACD and manual design. The generation pipeline proceeds as: (1) select a scene and randomize objects within semantic categories, (2) procedurally place and configure objects to satisfy user-specified predicates (spatial relations, articulation states, fluid filling, cloth folding) while the physics engine validates plausibility, (3) sample camera poses or trajectories using occupancy grids and hand-crafted heuristics to avoid occlusion, (4) randomize lighting and camera intrinsics per user specification, and (5) render frames with all requested annotations extracted from the simulation state. A key differentiator is the predicate-based labeling system that goes beyond standard geometric annotations to provide semantic state and relational labels (e.g., 'cabinet drawer 3 is 70% open', 'bowl is filled with water') directly usable for training and evaluation.

## Results

On the holistic scene understanding benchmark (Table 3), BVS-generated data preserves the relative performance ordering of SOTA models across four tasks compared to real datasets: Grounding DINO leads open-vocab detection (AP 44.7 vs. GLIP 41.4, RAM 41.3), iDisc leads depth estimation (δ1 0.12 vs. NVDS 0.10, DPT 0.09), Grounding SAM leads open-vocab segmentation (AP 59.2 vs. OpenSeeD 57.3, ODISE 57.1), and NICE-SLAM leads point cloud reconstruction (Comp. Ratio 66.3 vs. GradSLAM 50.0). For the sim2real object relation prediction task, the model trained solely on 12.5k BVS synthetic images achieves 0.839 F1 on the real test set, substantially outperforming zero-shot CLIP (0.271 F1) by a margin of 0.568. Parametric evaluation across five domain-shift axes (articulation, lighting, visibility, zoom, pitch) reveals that current SOTA models show significant performance degradation under out-of-distribution conditions, with visibility below 0.5 causing the steepest AP drops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11 SOTA models across four tasks on BVS-generated images to validate that BVS serves as a faithful proxy for real datasets. In open-vocabulary detection, Grounding DINO (AP 44.7) outperforms GLIP (41.4) and RAM (41.3) on BVS, mirroring the COCO ordering (63.0 > 61.4 > 60.8). For depth estimation, iDisc achieves the best δ1 (0.12) on BVS, consistent with its top NYUv2 score (0.94), while DPT trails (δ1 0.09 vs. 0.90). In open-vocabulary segmentation, Grounding SAM leads on BVS (AP 59.2) but OpenSeeD leads on ADE (15.0 vs. 14.8), showing a minor ordering swap. For point cloud reconstruction, NICE-SLAM dominates GradSLAM on both BVS (Comp. Ratio 66.3 vs. 50.0) and Replica (89.3 vs. 67.9). In the sim2real object relation prediction task, the BVS-trained model achieves 0.839 F1 on real images, dramatically outperforming zero-shot CLIP (0.271 F1) by over 3x, demonstrating that task-specific training on synthetic data is far more effective than relying on general-purpose vision-language models for fine-grained spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

BVS extends the BEHAVIOR-1K embodied AI benchmark and its OmniGibson simulator by adding a full CV-oriented data generation pipeline with photorealistic rendering via NVIDIA Omniverse, 8,841 object models, and 1,000 scene instances. It departs from prior synthetic datasets (InteriorNet, OpenRooms, Hypersim) that offer photorealism but lack physical plausibility and articulated-object states, and from 3D simulators (iGibson, Habitat 2.0, ProcTHOR, Kubric) that guarantee physics but sacrifice visual quality and CV-specific tooling. The predicate-prediction model in §4.3 is adapted from Migimatsu & Bohg (ICRA 2022), repurposed here as a testbed for sim2real transfer rather than as a robotics grounding method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BVS fills a concrete gap: no prior system simultaneously offers photorealism, physical plausibility, articulated/semantic object states, and a user-facing toolkit for generating labeled CV datasets. The demonstration that relative model rankings on BVS-generated images correlate with those on real benchmarks (COCO, NYUv2, ADE) validates synthetic data as a faithful evaluation proxy, while the parametric robustness experiments reveal that current SOTA detectors and segmentors degrade sharply on simple axes like partial occlusion and object articulation. Open questions include whether the photorealism gap narrows with future renderers, whether the approach extends to outdoor or large-scale urban scenes, and whether the sim2real transfer for state/relation prediction generalizes beyond the five tested predicates.
