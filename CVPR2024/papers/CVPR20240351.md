## Problem

Existing SGG methods model predicate representations by aggregating cues across all subject-object pairs sharing the same predicate, but large visual variations inherent in identical predicates under different triplets make this aggregation unreliable. Additionally, the long-tail distribution in SGG leaves tail predicates with insufficient triplet types and sample quantities, and prior debiasing strategies (re-sampling, re-weighting, mixture of experts) fail to directly enrich the limited patterns of tail classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a scene graph consisting of entity bounding boxes with class labels and predicate labels for each subject-object pair. A pre-trained Faster R-CNN with ResNeXt-101 backbone generates N entity proposals, and GloVe word embeddings of predicted entity class labels provide semantic features that are fused with visual features via Hybrid Attention. The final output is a set of <subject, predicate, object> triplets with associated bounding boxes, evaluated under three protocols: PredCls (GT boxes and labels), SGCls (GT boxes), and SGDet (fully end-to-end).

## Method

DRM introduces a dual-granularity architecture where a 2-layer Hybrid Attention predicate branch captures coarse-grained cues shared across diverse subject-object pairs, while a parallel 2-layer Hybrid Attention triplet branch (initialized with concatenated subject-predicate-object features) captures fine-grained cues specific to each triplet type. Dual-granularity constraints apply category-aware supervised contrastive losses on projected predicate and triplet features to prevent feature space collapse and enforce each branch to focus on its respective granularity. For long-tail mitigation, the DKT strategy computes per-category Gaussian distributions of frozen predicate and triplet features, calibrates tail-category covariances by blending them with those of the most similar head categories (weighted by inverse Euclidean distance), and generates synthetic tail samples from the calibrated distributions. The relation classifier is then fine-tuned on a balanced dataset combining real and synthetic instances, enriching tail predicate patterns without altering the backbone.

## Results

On Visual Genome VG150, DRM w/o DKT achieves 70.2/72.1 R@50/100 on PredCls, 44.3/45.2 on SGCls, and 34.0/38.9 on SGDet, outperforming the previous best biased method PE-Net by 2.0–2.9% at R@100 across all three tasks. With the DKT strategy, DRM achieves 47.1/49.6 mR@50/100 on PredCls and 27.8/29.2 on SGCls, surpassing the best unbiased baseline SHA+GCL by 5.5%, 4.9%, and 3.2% at mR@100 on PredCls, SGCls, and SGDet respectively. On Open Image V6, DRM w/o DKT reaches 40.5/41.4 wmAP_rel/phr and 47.9 score_wtd, exceeding PE-Net (36.6/37.4, 44.9) and SQUAT (34.9/35.9, 43.5). On GQA200, DRM achieves 41.9/43.5 mR@50/100 on PredCls, outperforming SHA+GCL (41.0/42.7) and VETO (21.2/22.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRM w/o DKT establishes new state-of-the-art on biased SGG, outperforming PE-Net (CVPR'23) by 2.0%, 2.9%, and 2.0% at R@100 on PredCls, SGCls, and SGDet respectively, and surpassing HL-Net and RU-Net by similar margins. For unbiased SGG, DRM with DKT outperforms the strongest multi-expert baseline SHA+GCL (CVPR'22) by 5.5%, 4.9%, and 3.2% at mR@100 across the three tasks, and exceeds CaCao (ICCV'23) by 5.9% at mR@100 on PredCls. On Open Image V6, DRM w/o DKT achieves 47.9 score_wtd, beating PE-Net (44.9) and SQUAT (43.5) by 3.0 and 4.4 points. On GQA200, DRM outperforms VETO (ICCV'23) by 17.7% at mR@100 on PredCls and beats SHA+GCL by 0.4% average at mR@100. The R@K metric drops for DRM with DKT relative to DRM w/o DKT (e.g., 43.9 vs. 70.2 on PredCls R@50), which the authors attribute to the model reclassifying ambiguous head predicates (e.g., 'on') into more specific tail predicates (e.g., 'sitting on'), a trade-off consistent with prior unbiased SGG work.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRM departs from the dominant SGG paradigm of modeling predicate cues in a coarse, predicate-only manner (as in MOTIFS, VCTREE, and PE-Net) by introducing an explicit fine-grained triplet branch alongside the predicate branch, arguing that visual variations within a single predicate are large but within a single triplet are small. For the long-tail sub-problem, it moves beyond the re-sampling, re-weighting, and mixture-of-experts strategies used by BGNN, SHA+GCL, and VETO, instead directly calibrating tail-class feature distributions via Gaussian variance transfer from the most similar head classes. The architecture reuses the Hybrid Attention mechanism from SHA/GCL [6] and the Faster R-CNN + ResNeXt-101 backbone common to recent SGG work, while the supervised contrastive losses and Gaussian-based knowledge transfer are the principal new components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-granularity framing offers a principled way to reconcile the tension between predicate-level generalization and triplet-level specificity, a tension that has limited prior predicate-refinement approaches. The DKT strategy addresses the root cause of long-tail SGG (insufficient tail patterns) rather than its symptoms, and the consistent gains across VG150, Open Image V6, and GQA200 suggest the approach is not dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the Gaussian distribution assumption holds for highly multimodal tail classes, whether the two-stage training pipeline (pre-train then freeze-and-transfer) limits end-to-end optimization, and whether the dual-granularity idea generalizes to other structured-prediction tasks beyond SGG.
