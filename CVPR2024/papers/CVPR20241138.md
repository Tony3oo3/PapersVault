## Problem

Sign Language Production (SLP) has been largely unexplored due to the absence of large-scale 3D datasets and the reliance of existing methods on 2D joint regressions or intermediate gloss representations, both of which limit the realism and semantic fidelity of generated motions. Only ~5% of television programs are interpreted into sign language, underscoring the practical need for realistic 3D signing avatars that can be directly driven by text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained English text sentence as input, encoded via a frozen CLIP-ViT-L-14 sentence embedding (not word-level tokens), and produces a temporal sequence of SMPL-X body pose, hand pose, and facial expression parameters on a canonical shape. The output is defined in the low-dimensional pose space rather than vertex space, making it directly animatable on a 3D avatar without any post-hoc mesh fitting step.

## Method

The core generative model is a DDPM-style diffusion process operating on concatenated SMPL-X pose and expression sequences, with a denoising network built around a novel anatomically informed GNN that breaks permutation equivariance by assigning each joint a unique learnable token embedding and using anisotropic (neighbor-specific) message passing over the SMPL-X kinematic tree. Text conditioning is injected via a gating mechanism on the CLIP sentence embedding, and temporal coherence is enforced by an autoregressive LSTM decoder (4 layers) that processes the denoised pose sequence frame-by-frame. The training data is a newly curated 3D-annotated version of How2Sign (35K clips, 16K+ word tokens of ASL), where SMPL-X parameters are obtained through a hybrid pipeline: OSX initialization, Mediapipe-guided hand joint refinement, a PCA-based anatomical pose prior loss, and a temporal smoothness regularizer. Ablations show that the GNN encoder, pose/expression token embeddings, and CLIP sentence encoding are each critical, with the GNN enabling high-frequency hand articulations that permutation-equivariant MLP baselines cannot capture.

## Results

On the SGNify mocap dataset, the proposed fitting pipeline achieves the lowest mean per-vertex error across all body parts: 46.42 mm (body), 16.17 mm (left hand), and 15.23 mm (right hand), outperforming OSX (47.32/18.34/18.12 mm) and SGNify (55.63/19.22/17.50 mm). On the How2Sign SLP test set, the proposed diffusion model outperforms all four SLP baselines on MPVPE, MPJPE, FID, and DTW, with its BLEU-4 back-translation score matching the BLEU-2 of the second-best method (Stoll et al.). In a user study with 15 ASL-fluent participants, the generated signs received a 5.8/10 alignment score (vs. 8.7 for ground truth) and achieved 40% top-1 and 80% top-2 accuracy in a sign-to-text identification task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 3D fitting quality (Table 1), the proposed pipeline beats all six baselines on every body part, with the largest margins over FrankMoCap (78.07→46.42 mm body) and PIXIE (60.11→46.42 mm body); it edges out OSX by ~1 mm on body and ~2 mm on hands. For SLP (Table 2), the proposed method outperforms all four SLP baselines—Saunders et al. [49], Saunders et al. [48], Hwang et al. [30], and Stoll et al. [54]—on MPVPE, MPJPE, FID, and DTW; Stoll et al. is the closest competitor but still trails substantially, with the proposed BLEU-4 matching Stoll's BLEU-2. In the user study, the proposed method scores 5.8/10 on text-alignment while Stoll et al. fails to achieve a reasonable score. Against general-purpose motion diffusion models (Tevet et al. [56] and Chen et al. [13], Table 3), the proposed anatomically-informed GNN encoder yields lower MPVPE (31.47 vs. 36.11/35.23 mm), lower FID (1.56 vs. 2.45/2.15), and lower DTW (7.83 vs. 8.92/8.26), confirming that the graph structure is critical for capturing high-frequency hand articulations that linear-layer-based motion models miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sign language production (SLP) task from 2D joint regression and gloss-based intermediate representations to direct 3D SMPL-X avatar generation, building on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the OSX whole-body fitting pipeline (Lin et al., 2023) for dataset annotation. The key architectural departure is replacing permutation-equivariant MLP layers with an anatomically-informed GNN using anisotropic message passing over the SMPL-X kinematic tree, motivated by the observation that equivariance limits the network's ability to model high-frequency hand articulations. For text conditioning, the paper departs from word-level embeddings used in prior SLP work by adopting CLIP sentence embeddings, arguing that sign language's non-word-for-word grammar benefits from holistic semantic encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of the first large-scale 3D sign language dataset (How2Sign-3D, 16K+ word tokens with SMPL-X annotations) addresses a critical bottleneck that has limited SLP research to small German sign language corpora. The user study with 15 ASL-fluent participants (40% top-1, 80% top-2 accuracy in identifying correct text from generated signs) provides rare perceptual validation from the target community, moving beyond purely quantitative metrics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other sign languages with different grammatical structures, whether real-time generation is feasible for practical avatar deployment, and how the method scales to longer utterances beyond the How2Sign clip lengths.
