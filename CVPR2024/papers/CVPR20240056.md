## Problem

Existing token reduction methods for Vision Transformers rely on a single criterion (e.g., similarity or informativeness) to decide which tokens to fuse, leading to suboptimal matching that either over-merges informative foreground tokens or collapses dissimilar tokens. This causes a persistent speed-accuracy trade-off where reducing FLOPs inevitably degrades classification performance. The paper argues that token fusion must jointly consider multiple relational criteria to minimize information loss.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard 224×224 RGB images tokenized into 196 patches (16×16) by a ViT backbone (DeiT-T, DeiT-S, T2T-ViT, or LV-ViT), and outputs a single class label over 1000 ImageNet-1K categories. A key tunable parameter is the number of tokens reduced per layer (r), which controls the FLOPs-accuracy operating point; the same model can be evaluated at different r values after a single fine-tuning pass. The method is also applicable in a training-free plug-and-play mode on pre-trained ViTs.

## Method

MCTF defines a multi-criteria attraction function W(x_i, x_j) that combines three factors via temperature-weighted products: cosine similarity (to merge redundant tokens), inverse informativeness based on one-step-ahead attention scores (to protect informative tokens), and inverse token size (to prevent over-merging). Tokens are paired via bidirectional bipartite soft matching, which halves the quadratic matching cost and captures relations in both directions. The one-step-ahead attention mechanism measures informativeness using the attention map from the *next* layer rather than the previous one, addressing the observed inconsistency between consecutive-layer attention maps; after fusion, the attention map is approximated by aggregating the pre-computed next-layer scores without recomputing dot products. During fine-tuning, a token reduction consistency loss (MSE between class tokens produced at the target r and a randomly sampled smaller r') regularizes the model to maintain consistent representations across different reduction levels, acting as a form of token-level data augmentation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MCTF achieves 72.7% top-1 accuracy with 0.7G FLOPs on DeiT-T (+0.5% over the 72.2% base model at 1.2G FLOPs) and 80.1% top-1 accuracy with 2.6G FLOPs on DeiT-S (+0.3% over the 79.8% base model at 4.6G FLOPs), representing approximately 44% FLOPs reduction while improving accuracy. Without any fine-tuning, MCTF at r=16 on pre-trained DeiT-S retains 79.2% accuracy, surpassing training-based methods like A-ViT (78.6%) and DynamicViT (79.3%). On LV-ViT-S, MCTF reaches 83.4% accuracy at 4.2G FLOPs, outperforming all other token reduction methods (EViT 83.0%, BAT 83.1%, SPViT 83.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCTF outperforms all 13 compared token reduction methods on both DeiT-T and DeiT-S. On DeiT-T, MCTF (72.7%) surpasses the closest competitor BAT (72.3%) by 0.4% while using fewer FLOPs (0.7G vs 0.8G), and beats ToMe (71.3%) by 1.4% at the same FLOPs budget. On DeiT-S, MCTF (80.1%) leads BAT (79.6%) by 0.5% and EViT (79.5%) by 0.6%, while also matching SPViT's FLOPs (2.6G) with a 0.8% accuracy advantage. Among token pruning methods, MCTF's advantage is most pronounced over A-ViT (78.6% on DeiT-S, a 1.5% gap) and IA-RED² (79.1%, a 1.0% gap). In the training-free setting (Table 3), MCTF consistently beats ToMe across all reduction levels, with the gap widening to +7.0% on DeiT-T and +3.8% on DeiT-S at r=20. On LV-ViT-S, MCTF is the only method that improves over the base model (83.4% vs 83.3%), while all other reduction methods (EViT, BAT, DynamicViT, SPViT) cause degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token fusion line of work (ToMe, SPViT, EViT, BAT) by replacing single-criterion matching with a multi-criteria attraction function combining similarity, informativeness, and token size. It builds directly on ToMe's bipartite soft matching but generalizes it to a bidirectional variant, and departs from prior methods' reliance on previous-layer attention scores by introducing one-step-ahead attention to better estimate token informativeness. The token reduction consistency training strategy is a novel addition that has no direct precedent in the token reduction literature, drawing loosely on consistency regularization ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear: achieving ~44% FLOPs reduction while simultaneously improving Top-1 accuracy (rather than merely preserving it) makes MCTF a strong candidate for deployment-constrained ViT inference. However, the evaluation is limited to ImageNet-1K classification, and open questions remain about whether the multi-criteria fusion and consistency loss transfer to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation) or to larger foundation models like CLIP where the quadratic cost is most acute. The finding that consecutive-layer attention maps can differ substantially, motivating one-step-ahead attention, is a useful diagnostic that may inform other efficiency techniques.
