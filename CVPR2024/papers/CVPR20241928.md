## Problem

Video-based BEV perception methods like StreamPETR require large-scale multi-view driving video datasets, but acquiring and annotating such data is costly and risky. Prior generative efforts in autonomous driving produced only single-view or image-only outputs, leaving the generation of multi-view videos paired with BEV layout sequences largely unexplored. The two core challenges are maintaining temporal and cross-view consistency while ensuring generated content aligns precisely with the conditioning annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241928/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes BEV layout sequences (3D bounding boxes, road maps, camera poses) converted into 19-channel perspective images (10 depth, 3 bounding box, 3 road map, 3 camera pose channels), free-form text prompts for global scene attributes, and optionally a conditional image. It produces 6-view panoramic driving videos at 256×512 spatial resolution with 8 frames, where views are concatenated along the width dimension to form a joint latent of shape H×(W×V)×T×C. The first stage can also output standalone multi-view images, enabling elevation of image-only datasets to video datasets.

## Method

Panacea builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1 and introduces a decomposed 4D attention UNet that replaces prohibitively expensive full HWVT attention with three targeted modules: intra-view spatial attention, cross-view attention restricted to adjacent views, and cross-frame attention over spatially aligned temporal patches. A two-stage pipeline first trains a multi-view image generator (56k steps), then trains a video extension stage (40k steps) that conditions on the first-stage output concatenated with the diffused input, decomposing spatial and temporal synthesis. BEV layout sequences are injected via a ControlNet branch processing the 19-channel perspective images, while CLIP-encoded text prompts provide coarse-grained control over weather, time, and scene. Inference uses DDIM sampling with 25 steps, and the two-stage design allows the same architecture to serve as both an image and video generator by bypassing temporal modules.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, Panacea achieves an FVD of 139 and FID of 16.96, substantially outperforming all baselines. In terms of controllability, generated samples yield 68% relative NDS compared to real data when evaluated with a pre-trained StreamPETR model. When used for data augmentation, combining Panacea-generated samples with real data boosts StreamPETR's NDS from 46.9 to 49.2 (+2.3 points), and elevating image-only data to video data yields a +5.8 NDS improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Panacea is the only method that simultaneously supports both multi-view and multi-frame generation, whereas BEVGen and BEVControl produce only multi-view images (no temporal dimension) and DriveDreamer produces only single-view video. On FID, Panacea (16.96) outperforms BEVGen (25.54) by 8.58 points and BEVControl (24.85) by 7.89 points, indicating significantly better image fidelity. On FVD, Panacea (139) dramatically outperforms DriveDreamer (452) by over 3×, reflecting far superior temporal coherence. In the controllability evaluation (Table 2), Panacea's full two-stage pipeline achieves 68% relative NDS on the validation set, while its first-stage image-only output reaches 72%, confirming that generated samples closely align with the conditioned BEV annotations. The data augmentation experiments (Table 3) further show that Panacea-generated data, when combined with real data, improves StreamPETR's NDS by 2.3 points over real-data-only training, validating the practical utility of the generated samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

Panacea composes three established building blocks—Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework, VLDM's temporal attention for video, and ControlNet for conditional injection—into a unified multi-view video generation pipeline for driving. It departs from prior BEV-conditioned generation work (BEVGen, BEVControl), which produced only single-frame images, and from single-view driving video generators (DriveDreamer, VLDM), by introducing a decomposed 4D attention mechanism (cross-view + cross-frame) that jointly enforces spatial and temporal coherence across all camera views. The two-stage image-then-video pipeline is a practical architectural choice that has no direct precedent in the driving generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Panacea provides the first practical pipeline for generating unlimited annotated multi-view driving videos from BEV layout sequences, directly addressing the data bottleneck for video-based BEV perception methods like StreamPETR. The introduced Gen-nuScenes dataset and the demonstrated ability to elevate image-only datasets into video datasets (yielding a 5.8-point NDS gain) open a path for broader adoption of temporal perception without requiring new video collection. However, the 2.3-point mAP degradation when using generated video data, the domain gap between synthetic and real validation data, and the limited 8-frame sequence length at 256×512 resolution remain open challenges that constrain real-world deployment and simulation use.
