## Problem

Spike cameras record photons at ultra-fine temporal granularity, but Poisson photon arrival, thermal noise, and clock-signal quantization introduce fluctuations that make individual spike intervals unstable and unable to directly reflect light intensities. Existing spike camera image reconstruction (SCIR) methods aggregate spatial-temporal information but do not explicitly model or suppress these fluctuation sources, limiting reconstruction fidelity especially under low light or high motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240726/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous binary spike stream S ∈ B^(H×W×T) sampled at 40 kHz and segments it into 5 temporal sub-streams (61 frames total) centered around the target moment t₀ and four reference moments. It outputs a single reconstructed grayscale image at t₀. Experiments use the REDS-SCIR benchmark at 256×256 (training) and 384×512 (evaluation) with three simulated light-intensity levels (η = 1.00, 0.75, 0.50), plus 12 real-captured scenes for no-reference evaluation.

## Method

The BSF network has two core modules. The Multi-order DSFT Fusion (MODF) module exploits a proven theorem that the reciprocal of the (1,1)-order differential of spike firing time (DSFT) is an unbiased estimator of light intensity under quantization effects; it extends this to higher-order DSFT (1,2), (2,1), (2,2) to further suppress Poisson noise, then fuses features from all orders via a weight-shared extractor and convolutional fusion. The Multi-granularity Alignment (MGA) module aligns reference features to the key moment in a pyramidal fashion: at each level, a Cross-Attentional Patch-level Alignment (CAPA) with a local k_p×k_p search strategy performs coarse patch-level alignment, followed by deformable convolution for fine pixel-level refinement. The network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss on the REDS-SCIR dataset for 60 epochs using Adam, achieving state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on synthetic data and BRISQUE/PIQE/HOSA on real-captured data with only 2.48M parameters.

## Results

On the REDS-SCIR benchmark, BSF achieves PSNR of 39.76 / 39.09 / 37.76 dB and SSIM of 0.970 / 0.964 / 0.951 at light intensity factors η = 1.00 / 0.75 / 0.50, outperforming the strongest supervised baseline Spk2ImgNet♣ (39.16 / 38.27 / 36.59 dB) by 0.6–1.2 dB across all settings. On real-captured data (no ground truth), BSF attains the best no-reference scores: BRISQUE 18.53, PIQE 23.48, and HOSA 25.52, beating the next-best method (WGSE) by over 6 points on BRISQUE. The model uses only 2.48 M parameters, fewer than Spk2ImgNet (3.90 M) and WGSE (3.81 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSF outperforms all nine baselines across every metric and every light-intensity setting. Against the strongest supervised competitor Spk2ImgNet♣, BSF gains +0.60 dB PSNR at η=1.00 (39.76 vs 39.16), +0.82 dB at η=0.75, and +1.17 dB at η=0.50, with consistent SSIM and LPIPS improvements. WGSE♣ is the second-closest method but trails BSF by 0.79–1.01 dB PSNR. The training-free filtering methods (TFP, TFI, MAHTF) lag by 10+ dB PSNR, and the neuronic-model method TFSTP is the weakest overall (~20 dB). On real-captured data, BSF achieves the best BRISQUE (18.53 vs 23.43 for WGSE♣) and PIQE (23.48 vs 25.08 for FireNet♣), while HOSA scores are closely matched with WGSE (25.52 vs 25.66). Notably, BSF uses fewer parameters (2.48 M) than both Spk2ImgNet (3.90 M) and WGSE (3.81 M), and far fewer than ETNet (22.18 M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the differential of spike firing time (DSFT) concept from Spike2Flow [67], originally designed for optical flow estimation, as the core spike representation for image reconstruction. It departs from prior SCIR methods (Spk2ImgNet, WGSE, SSML) that operate directly on binary spikes or use wavelet-based representations, by introducing a theoretically grounded multi-order DSFT fusion (MODF) that explicitly addresses quantization effects and Poisson noise. The multi-granularity alignment (MGA) module extends the deformable-convolution-based alignment in Spk2ImgNet with a novel cross-attentional patch-level alignment (CAPA) for coarse initialization, addressing matching errors caused by spike fluctuations that prior methods did not explicitly model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Theorem 4.1 providing a principled, unbiased-estimation justification for reciprocal DSFT under quantization effects gives the spike representation a theoretical foundation that prior heuristic approaches lacked, and the multi-order extension offers a systematic way to trade off temporal stability against motion blur. The REDS-SCIR benchmark with three light-intensity levels is a useful community resource for evaluating robustness to illumination variation. However, the theoretical analysis assumes constant photon arrival, limiting its direct applicability to dynamic scenes, and the performance gains over the strongest baseline (Spk2ImgNet♣) are modest (~0.5–1 dB PSNR), leaving open whether the added architectural complexity (pyramidal CAPA + DCN) is necessary or whether simpler alignment strategies could achieve similar results.
