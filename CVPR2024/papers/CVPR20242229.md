## Problem

Existing event-camera line-based motion estimation relies on a polynomial (Gröbner basis) solver that is restricted to exactly five events, depends on expensive and numerically unstable root-finding, and uses a non-minimal four-DoF line representation with singularities for lines near the yz-plane. No linear, extensible-to-N-events, singularity-free solver existed for jointly recovering line geometry and partial camera velocity from event manifolds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242229/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N≥5 events (pixel coordinates, μs-resolution timestamps, polarity) belonging to a single 3D line, together with IMU angular-rate readings used to motion-correct bearing vectors into a common reference frame over a short time window (~0.5 s). It outputs a minimal three-DoF line parameterization in angle-axis form (with a fixed unit-distance scale that eliminates auxiliary constraints), two observable velocity components perpendicular to the line, and—after fusing M≥2 lines via a geometric averaging scheme—the full 3D linear camera velocity up to an unobservable global scale.

## Method

The core insight is that reparametrizing the 3D line via the angle-axis representation of a rotation matrix R_ℓ (whose columns e₁^ℓ, e₂^ℓ, e₃^ℓ span a line-centered frame) renders the event-line incidence relation linear in the unknowns (e₂^ℓ, e₃^ℓ, u_y^ℓ, u_z^ℓ). Stacking N such constraints yields an N×6 matrix A whose null-space solution (via SVD) gives a globally optimal, closed-form estimate; the four sign-ambiguous solutions are enumerated and degenerate cases are fully characterized by rank deficiency of A. For full-velocity recovery, each line's partial observation is converted into a homogeneous linear constraint by enforcing that the 90°-rotated measured velocity is perpendicular to the true projected velocity, and stacking M such constraints is again solved by a single SVD—avoiding the Schur-complement elimination used in prior work. The solver is embedded in a GC-RANSAC loop with NAPSAC spatially coherent sampling, an angular reprojection residual for inlier selection, and optional local refinement via either the overdetermined linear solver or Levenberg-Marquardt on the algebraic residual.

## Results

The proposed linear solver runs in 3.25 μs (avg.) for five events, over 600× faster than the Gröbner basis solver (2046 μs), while achieving a 0.00% error rate (≥0.1°) compared to 1.00% for the polynomial solver. On real-world VECtor sequences, the full pipeline (linear solver + non-linear optimization) achieves mean/median direction errors of 16.5°/14.6° (mountain-normal), 22.1°/20.7° (desk-normal), and 19.9°/15.0° (sofa-normal), outperforming both baselines across all sequences. In noisy simulations, using 10 events per line reduces mean pixel-noise error from 5.53° (5 events) to 0.46°, demonstrating the benefit of the overdetermined formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gröbner basis solver [12] (the most directly comparable method), the proposed linear solver wins decisively on every metric: it is 600× faster (3.25 μs vs. 2046 μs), eliminates the 1% failure rate at 0.1° error, and achieves lower direction error on all three real-world sequences (e.g., 16.5° vs. 33.5° mean on mountain-normal, a 50% reduction). Against CELC+opt [30], the proposed method also wins on all sequences (e.g., 19.9° vs. 24.0° mean on sofa-normal), and additionally does not require the spatial-temporal plane clustering assumption that limits CELC+opt to certain sub-sequences. The local refinement step (non-linear optimization over 10 inliers) provides an additional 16–18% mean error reduction over the linear-only solver, confirming that the linear solution serves as a strong initialization for further refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Gao et al. (ICCV 2023), which established the incidence relation between events and 3D lines under constant linear velocity and solved it with a Gröbner-basis polynomial elimination tailored to exactly five events. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an angle-axis (SO(3)) line representation with three degrees of freedom, which the authors show makes the incidence system linear in the unknowns, replacing polynomial root-finding with a single SVD. The velocity-averaging scheme also departs from the Schur-Complement formulation in [12], using a geometrically-motivated homogeneous linear constraint instead. More broadly, the work sits within the event-based motion estimation literature (Peng et al., Ieng et al., Seok and Lim) but is the first to provide a linear, N-point, singularity-free solver for the line-manifold fitting problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 600× speedup (3.25 µs vs. 2046 µs) and elimination of the 1% numerical failure rate make line-based event motion estimation viable in real-time RANSAC pipelines, which was impractical with the polynomial solver. The extensibility to N > 5 events and the explicit characterization of degenerate cases and solution multiplicity provide a cleaner theoretical foundation for future work. Open questions include incorporating uncertainty into partial velocity readings, asynchronous fusion over time, and integration with IMU measurements—directions the authors flag as next steps. The formulation's compatibility with standard-camera line detections also suggests broader applicability beyond event sensors.
