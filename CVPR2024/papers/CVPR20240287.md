## Problem

Spatio-temporal transformers for video deblurring are limited to short temporal windows (typically 2–4 frames) because dense self-attention scales quadratically with sequence length, preventing access to distant frames that contain sharp information. Simultaneously, bidirectional feature propagation is highly sensitive to inaccurate optical flow estimated from blurry frames, causing error accumulation that degrades restoration quality over longer propagation paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240287/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurry video sequence of T frames (T=48 at test time) and produces a deblurred video sequence at the same resolution. Optical flows are pre-computed by a frozen RAFT network on a downsampled version of the input, and the blur map is derived non-parametrically from these flows. Training uses 256×256 random crops with L1 loss; evaluation is performed at full resolution (1280×720 on GoPro, 256×256 on DVD).

## Method

BSSTNet introduces a non-learnable blur map computed from squared forward/backward optical flow magnitudes, which identifies sparse blurry regions in space and time. This map drives two key mechanisms: (1) Blur-aware Bidirectional Feature Propagation (BBFP), where sharp maps (complement of blur maps) serve as additional conditions for deformable convolution offsets and masks, preventing propagation of blurry pixels from neighboring frames; and (2) Blur-aware Spatio-temporal Sparse Transformer (BSST), which selects only blurry windows as query tokens and sharp windows as key/value tokens, reducing the attention computation to a small subset of the full spatio-temporal volume. This sparsity allows BSST to use a 48-frame temporal window with only ~133 GFLOPs, whereas a standard spatio-temporal transformer would exceed GPU memory at that length. The architecture stacks multiple BSST blocks with alternating odd/even frame selection in key/value spaces to further halve the KV token count.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, BSSTNet achieves 35.98 PSNR and 0.9792 SSIM, outperforming the previous best method Shift-Net+ (35.88 PSNR, 0.9790 SSIM) by 0.10 dB. On the DVD dataset, BSSTNet reaches 34.95 PSNR and 0.9703 SSIM, improving over Shift-Net+ by 0.26 dB in PSNR and 0.0013 in SSIM. In terms of efficiency, BSSTNet uses 133 GFLOPs and 28 ms runtime per frame, reducing FLOPs by 13 GFLOPs and achieving a 1.6× speedup over Shift-Net+ (146 GFLOPs, 45 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSSTNet outperforms all eight baselines on both the GoPro and DVD datasets. The closest competitor is Shift-Net+ [10], which BSSTNet surpasses by 0.10 dB (GoPro) and 0.26 dB (DVD) in PSNR while also using fewer FLOPs (133 vs. 146 GFLOPs) and running 1.6× faster (28 ms vs. 45 ms). Among transformer-based methods, RVRT [12] (34.92 PSNR on GoPro, 34.30 on DVD) and VRT [11] (34.81/34.27) trail BSSTNet by roughly 1.0–1.1 dB, highlighting the benefit of the longer temporal window enabled by sparse attention. RNN-based methods (RNN-MBP [28], STDANet [24]) and earlier approaches (STFAN [26], ARVo [9], NAFNet [2]) are outperformed by a wider margin of 2.3–7.3 dB in PSNR, confirming that the combination of blur-aware sparse spatio-temporal attention and blur-guided bidirectional propagation yields substantially better restoration than prior propagation-only or small-window transformer designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

BSSTNet sits at the intersection of two dominant paradigms in video deblurring: bidirectional feature propagation (inherited from BasicVSR++'s flow-guided alignment) and spatio-temporal self-attention (introduced by VRT). It repurposes FuseFormer's soft split/composition and enlarged K/V window machinery as the structural backbone of its transformer, then introduces a non-learnable blur map—derived from RAFT optical flows—as a sparsity signal that simultaneously prunes attention tokens and gates deformable-convolution offsets during propagation. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating blur-region sparsity as a first-class architectural constraint rather than an incidental property, enabling longer temporal windows (48 frames vs. 2 in VRT) at comparable FLOPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The blur-map-driven sparsity strategy offers a practical recipe for extending temporal context in video transformers without proportional memory growth, a bottleneck that has constrained the field since VRT. The finding that top-50% token selection guided by blur maps recovers nearly all of the full-attention quality (34.95 vs. 34.98 dB) while cutting FLOPs to ~43% suggests that most spatio-temporal attention computation in deblurring is redundant. Limitations include reliance on optical-flow quality for the blur prior (which degrades under extreme blur or occlusion), evaluation on only two synthetic datasets, and the fact that the approach targets motion blur specifically; whether the same sparsity principle transfers to other degradations (defocus, sensor noise) or to longer video sequences remains open.
