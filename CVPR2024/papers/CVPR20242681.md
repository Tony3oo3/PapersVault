## Problem

Single-image turbulence removal is severely ill-posed, causing substantial performance drops in diverse real-world scenes, while multi-frame methods require 10–16 consecutive captures that are impractical when scene motion or capture constraints apply. The paper identifies an underexploited physical property—turbulence strength decreases at longer wavelengths due to the dispersive nature of the atmosphere—as a means to bridge this gap with only a pair of simultaneously captured images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242681/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of simultaneously acquired images: a standard RGB frame and a narrow-band frame (680 nm central wavelength, 40 nm bandwidth) that is effectively red-dominant, lacks color and high-frequency detail, and carries higher noise. The output is a single turbulence-free RGB image at the same spatial resolution as the input. During training, images are cropped to 512×512 from the Places dataset but processed at 224×224; the narrow-band image is synthesized by re-scaling Zernike coefficients per Hardie et al. and reweighting RGB channels to (0.95, 0.04, 0.01) to match real narrow-band photography.

## Method

NB-GTR exploits the scaling relation between turbulence angles at two wavelengths (derived from Ingensand et al.) to extract a shared turbulence field from the RGB–narrow-band pair. A shared-weight lightweight UNet (dual turbulence encoder) extracts multiscale turbulence cues from both images, and a re-turb U-Net with spatially adaptive modulation reconstructs each turbulent input from a clean reference under those cues, enforcing meaningful extraction via a consistency loss. The de-turb module is a multiscale Transformer (Restormer-style) with MHSA blocks at four encoder levels (2, 3, 3, 4 blocks) that fuses RGB and narrow-band features via overlapping patches, while a CBAM-based conjunction mechanism merges the two sets of turbulence cues at each scale and injects them into skip connections to guide up-sampling. The full network is trained end-to-end with a combined ℓ₁ consistency loss (re-turb outputs vs. turbulent inputs, weighted 0.4 each) and an ℓ₁ reconstruction loss (output vs. ground truth) for 300K iterations using Adam with cosine annealing from 5e-5 to 1e-6.

## Results

On synthetic turbulence data (Places dataset), NB-GTR achieves 25.00 PSNR and 0.8199 SSIM, substantially outperforming all five baselines. The strongest baseline, TurbNet [25] in its dual-image setting, reaches 23.18 PSNR and 0.7498 SSIM, yielding a gap of 1.82 dB in PSNR and 0.0701 in SSIM in favor of NB-GTR. Even the best general restoration method, FocalNet [7], trails by 2.20 dB in PSNR. The dual-image setting consistently improves over single-image for all baselines, confirming the value of the narrow-band input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NB-GTR decisively outperforms all five baselines on both PSNR and SSIM in the dual-image setting. Against the closest competitor TurbNet [25] (23.18 PSNR / 0.7498 SSIM), NB-GTR gains 1.82 dB and 0.0701 SSIM. FocalNet [7] (22.80 / 0.7147) and AT-Net [39] (22.18 / 0.6877) trail by 2.20 dB and 2.82 dB respectively. N-IDR [22] (20.72 / 0.6231) and Burstormer [9] (21.37 / 0.6236) are the weakest, with NB-GTR exceeding them by over 3.6 dB in PSNR. Notably, N-IDR actually degrades slightly when given the dual input (20.77→20.72 PSNR), suggesting its grid-deformation model cannot exploit the narrow-band modality, whereas NB-GTR's dedicated conjunction mechanism is specifically designed to leverage it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the atmospheric turbulence removal literature by introducing a new input modality: a simultaneously captured narrow-band image alongside the standard RGB frame. It builds on the wavelength dependence of turbulence established by Hardie et al. [14] and the dispersion-based alignment formalism of Ingensand et al. [15], but departs from prior multi-spectral work (which could not reconstruct color images) by targeting RGB restoration guided by a single narrow-band observation. Architecturally, it combines a Restormer-style multiscale Transformer [41] with a dual-encoder turbulence extraction module and a re-turb consistency mechanism, drawing on spatially adaptive modulation [21] and CBAM [38] for feature interaction. The P2S turbulence simulator [24] is used for training data generation, consistent with recent physics-driven restoration methods [17, 25].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical middle ground between single-image turbulence removal (convenient but ill-posed) and multi-frame approaches (robust but requiring 10–16 frames and stable scenes), needing only a pair of simultaneously captured images. However, the real-world evaluation is purely qualitative with no quantitative metrics, and the method requires specialized hardware (a 680 nm narrow-band filter with 40 nm bandwidth), limiting immediate adoption. Open questions include generalization to different filter configurations, dynamic scenes with motion between the two captures, and whether the narrow-band guidance benefit persists under stronger turbulence or longer distances. The parallel-camera setup introduces parallax that the authors acknowledge but do not quantify.
