## Problem

Few-shot learning systems are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, and existing robust FSL methods rely on either visual similarity learning or class concept learning in isolation. These two paradigms exhibit complementary robustness profiles—similarity learners are vulnerable to weak attacks due to fragmented per-class clusters, while class concept learners resist stronger attacks through clearer class separation—but no prior work has unified them to capture robustness across the full range of attack strengths.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images from FSL benchmarks (Mini-ImageNet at 84×84, CIFAR-FS at 32×32, FC100 at 32×32) organized into N-way K-shot episodes with support and query sets. At test time, the co-distilled unified embedding model is paired with a rebuilt multinomial logistic regression head constructed from the episode's support samples, producing a class label for each query image. The adversarial threat model uses ℓ∞-norm bounded perturbations with radius ε=8/255 and step size α=2/255.

## Method

RESISTANCE maintains two parallel network branches—a similarity learner (prototype-based, N-way per episode) and a class concept learner (global Z-class classifier)—and co-distills their parameters into a unified embedding model via exponential moving average, periodically redistributing the unified parameters back to each branch to prevent divergence. Adversarial samples for each branch are seeded with cross-branch class-wise global adversarial initialization perturbations (GAIP), which are iteratively optimized to be disruptive across all three networks (similarity, concept, and unified) simultaneously, replacing standard random initialization. A branch robustness harmonization module computes a relative adversarial robustness score (ratio of KL divergences) and downweights the learning rate of the more robust branch via a tanh-based schedule, ensuring balanced optimization. The framework also supports single-step adversary generation (N-FGSM, RS-FGSM, GradAlign) for ~3× training speedup while retaining most of the multi-step robustness.

## Results

On the 5-way 5-shot setting with ResNet-12, RESISTANCE achieves 68.79% clean / 50.52% Auto-Attack (AA) robust accuracy on Mini-ImageNet, 74.83% / 58.76% on CIFAR-FS, and 51.69% / 34.66% on FC100 (ε=8/255). The largest gain is on CIFAR-FS, where RESISTANCE improves clean accuracy by ~9% and AA robustness by ~8% over the strongest baseline (DFSL). In the 5-way 1-shot setting with ResNet-12, RESISTANCE reaches 50.28% / 33.71% (Mini-ImageNet), 55.78% / 41.57% (CIFAR-FS), and 37.75% / 23.18% (FC100), again leading all baselines on both clean and robust metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESISTANCE outperforms all six baselines (AQ, R-MAML, ST, GR, DFSL, TROBA) on every dataset and both 1-shot and 5-shot settings for both clean and robust accuracy. The most dramatic margin is on CIFAR-FS 5-shot (ResNet-12): RESISTANCE gains +8.99% clean and +8.12% AA over DFSL (74.83 vs 65.84; 58.76 vs 50.64), and +7.84% clean / +7.85% AA over GR. On Mini-ImageNet 5-shot, the advantage is +3.84% clean and +4.02% AA over DFSL; on FC100 5-shot, +3.96% clean and +3.69% AA. In cross-domain transfer (Table 5), RESISTANCE also leads on all three transfer directions (M→C, M→F, C→M) for both PGD and AA robustness. Notably, RESISTANCE with single-step N-FGSM (5.3 h training) still surpasses the multi-step R-MAML (15.6 h) and GR (10.7 h) on both clean and robust accuracy, demonstrating that the co-distillation framework is robust to the adversary generation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESISTANCE unifies two previously separate paradigms in adversarially robust few-shot learning: visual similarity learning (AQ, DFSL) that learns per-episode prototypes, and class concept learning (GR, ST) that learns global Z-class classifiers. The paper's key departure is the explicit analysis that these paradigms produce structurally different decision boundaries (multi-cluster vs. single-cluster per class), yielding complementary robustness profiles across attack strengths, and the first systematic exploitation of this complementarity via parameter-level co-distillation (EMA) rather than output-level ensembling or feature concatenation. The cross-branch class-wise global adversarial initialization perturbations extend universal adversarial perturbation ideas [25, 34] to a multi-branch, class-conditioned setting, and the single-step adversary exploration builds on fast adversarial training methods (N-FGSM, RS-FGSM, GradAlign).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why combining similarity and concept learners outperforms either alone or same-type ensembles, and offers a practical recipe (parameter co-distillation + harmonization) that practitioners can adopt to improve robustness across the full range of perturbation strengths. The single-step adversary results are practically important, reducing training time from ~17h to ~5h while maintaining near-multi-step robustness. Open questions include whether the co-distillation framework generalizes to non-episodic or long-tail settings, whether the complementary robustness insight extends to other modality pairs (e.g., text + vision), and how the approach scales to larger backbones or datasets beyond the three standard FSL benchmarks.
