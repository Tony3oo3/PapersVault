## Problem

Traditional dense 3D reconstruction pipelines require accurate camera intrinsics and extrinsics as prerequisites for triangulation, making them brittle, complex, and prone to cascading failures across sequential stages (keypoint matching, SfM, bundle adjustment, MVS). DUSt3R addresses this by eliminating the need for any prior camera calibration or pose information, instead directly regressing a dense 3D scene representation from raw image pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240747/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network accepts a pair of RGB images (or a collection for multi-view) at up to 512 pixels in the largest dimension and outputs two dense pointmaps (W×H×3) both expressed in the first image's coordinate frame, along with per-pixel confidence maps. The pointmaps are regressed up to an unknown global scale factor. From these outputs, depth maps (z-coordinate), camera intrinsics (via focal-length optimization), relative poses (via Procrustes alignment or PnP-RANSAC), absolute poses, and dense pixel correspondences (via 3D nearest-neighbor search) are all derived as straightforward post-processing steps.

## Method

The architecture is a Siamese ViT-Large encoder feeding a cross-attention ViT-Base decoder with DPT regression heads, initialized from CroCo cross-view completion pretraining. The key design choice is the pointmap representation: both output pointmaps are expressed in the same canonical frame (the first image's), which smoothly unifies monocular (f(I,I)) and binocular reconstruction and implicitly encodes the inter-view relationship without explicit camera parameters. Training uses a single confidence-weighted 3D Euclidean regression loss with scale normalization (average point-to-origin distance), requiring no geometric constraints at inference. For multi-view scenes, a global alignment optimization (Eq. 5) jointly refines per-pair poses, scales, and world-coordinate pointmaps via gradient descent, minimizing 3D projection errors rather than 2D reprojection errors as in traditional bundle adjustment, converging in seconds on a GPU.

## Results

On multi-view depth estimation without GT poses, intrinsics, or depth range, DUSt3R 512 achieves an average relative error of 4.73 and inlier ratio of 64.52 across KITTI, ScanNet, ETH3D, DTU, and Tanks & Temples, outperforming methods that use GT camera poses (e.g., Robust MVD Baseline: 75.1 rel, 6.3 τ). For multi-view pose estimation on CO3Dv2, DUSt3R with global alignment reaches 96.2% RRA@15 and 86.8% RTA@15, substantially surpassing PoseDiffusion (80.5% RRA@15). On monocular depth, DUSt3R 512 achieves 6.50 Rel on NYUv2 and 8.08 on BONN (best among all compared methods), while on the Map-free localization benchmark it attains 115.8px VCRE and 0.98m median translation error, beating RoMa (128.8px, 1.23m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On multi-view pose estimation (CO3Dv2), DUSt3R with global alignment achieves 96.2% RRA@15, outperforming PoseDiffusion (80.5%), PosReg (53.2%), PixSfM (33.7%), and COLMAP+SPSG (36.1%); on RealEstate10K it reaches 67.7 mAA(30) vs. 48.0 for PoseDiffusion. For monocular depth, DUSt3R 512 matches or beats supervised baselines (DPT-BEiT, NeWCRFs) on indoor datasets (BONN: 8.08 Rel, NYUv2: 6.50 Rel) but trails the zero-shot SlowTv on outdoor scenes (DDAD: 13.88 vs. 12.63; KITTI: 10.74 vs. 6.84). On the Map-free localization benchmark, DUSt3R achieves the best VCRE (115.8px) and median translation error (0.98m), surpassing RoMa (128.8px, 1.23m) and FAR (137.0px, 1.48m), though it slightly trails RoMa on pose AUC (0.393 vs. 0.407). In multi-view depth, DUSt3R without any GT camera parameters outperforms methods that use GT poses on ETH3D (2.91 rel vs. 7.1 for Robust MVD) and DTU (3.52 rel vs. 42.6), while remaining competitive on KITTI and T&T. On DTU 3D reconstruction, DUSt3R (1.741 overall) does not match domain-specific methods like GeoMVSNet (0.295) or Gipuma (0.578), which leverage GT poses and train on DTU, but achieves sub-3mm accuracy without any camera calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUSt3R builds directly on the CroCo cross-view completion pretraining paradigm for its Siamese ViT encoder-decoder architecture and weight initialization, but departs fundamentally from the traditional SfM+MVS pipeline by eliminating the need for explicit camera intrinsics and extrinsics. Where prior direct RGB-to-3D methods (DeMon, DeepV2D) still require ground-truth camera parameters and output depthmaps plus relative poses, DUSt3R introduces the pointmap representation—a dense 2D field of 3D points expressed in a canonical frame—that implicitly encodes both scene geometry and inter-view relationships without any projective camera model. The global alignment procedure further departs from bundle adjustment by optimizing 3D point-to-point distances rather than 2D reprojection errors, yielding a simpler and faster multi-view fusion strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying monocular depth, multi-view depth, relative/absolute pose estimation, pixel matching, and dense 3D reconstruction into a single zero-shot model, DUSt3R removes the brittle sequential pipeline (keypoint matching → SfM → BA → MVS) that has dominated 3D vision for decades, and its plug-and-play nature (no calibration, no finetuning) makes it immediately applicable to in-the-wild photography. Key limitations include the inherent scale ambiguity (predictions are up-to-scale), reduced accuracy on sub-millimeter tasks like DTU compared to explicit triangulation methods, and reliance on a connectivity graph that may struggle with very large or sparsely-overlapping scenes. Open questions include whether the pointmap paradigm can be extended to dynamic scenes, whether metric scale can be recovered without external depth sensors, and how the approach scales to hundreds or thousands of views.
