## Problem

State-of-the-art Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) tend to process images as a 'bag of objects,' failing to capture compositional structure such as object attributes and inter-object relationships. While scene graphs (SGs) are a natural structured representation for this, they require expensive annotations and finetuning on SG data causes catastrophic forgetting of pretrained capabilities. The paper asks whether compositional knowledge can be extracted from an LMM at inference time using SG representations without any annotated SG data or model finetuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242708/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a free-form text task prompt (e.g., a VQA question, captioning instruction, or classification prompt) as input. It produces a final text response (option letter, short answer, or detailed description depending on the task) with an intermediate scene graph in JSON format that encodes objects, their attributes, and pairwise relationships. The JSON structure is explicitly enforced in the generation prompt to facilitate reliable parsing by the LMM, and the generated SG is kept compact (optimal at ~256 tokens) to remain token-efficient within the LMM's limited context window shared between image and text tokens.

## Method

CCoT is a two-step zero-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting procedure applied at inference time. In Step 1, the LMM is prompted with the image, the original task prompt, and a dedicated scene-graph generation prompt (S_in) that instructs it to output a JSON-formatted SG capturing objects, attributes, and relationships; including the task prompt conditions the SG to be task-relevant rather than a generic scene description. In Step 2, the LMM is re-prompted with the image, the generated SG, a brief context sentence ('Use the image and scene graph as context...'), the original task prompt, and a short answer-extraction sub-prompt to produce the final response. The key insight is that the self-generated SG acts as a structured intermediate reasoning step that forces the model to explicitly attend to compositional structure before answering, improving performance without any parameter updates. Ablations confirm that the JSON format, the structured SG representation (vs. free-form captions), and the absence of bounding-box coordinates are all important for the method's effectiveness.

## Results

CCoT improves all four evaluated LMMs across every benchmark without fine-tuning. On compositional benchmarks, gains are largest: InstructBLIP-13B-CCoT achieves 26.0 Wino-Text (+13.2) and 62.9 WHOOPS! VQA (+14.6), while GPT-4V-CCoT reaches 64.0 Wino-Text and 54.5 Wino-Image. On general multimodal benchmarks, improvements are more modest but consistent: GPT-4V-CCoT scores 74.0 on SEEDBench-Image (+4.9) and 91.2 on LLaVA-Bench In-the-Wild (+2.0), and LLaVA-1.5-13B-CCoT reaches 70.7 on MMBench (+3.7). Notably, GPT-4V-CCoT surpasses the previous Winoground SOTA (SGVL, 42.8 Wino-Text) by over 21 points, despite SGVL being finetuned on annotated scene graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCoT consistently outperforms the zero-shot CoT (ZS-CoT) baseline across all four LMMs on compositional benchmarks, with the largest gap on InstructBLIP (26.0 vs. 15.8 Wino-Text) and LLaVA-1.5 (39.8 vs. 36.8 Wino-Text); ZS-CoT actually degrades performance on several splits, likely because it was designed for text-only LLMs and ignores visual structure. On general multimodal benchmarks, CCoT also beats ZS-CoT (e.g., InstructBLIP: 56.9 vs. 37.6 SEED-I; LLaVA-1.5: 69.7 vs. 66.7 SEED-I). Against the previous Winoground SOTA SGVL (finetuned on annotated scene graphs, 42.8 Wino-Text), GPT-4V-CCoT achieves 64.0 Wino-Text without any fine-tuning or ground-truth SG annotations. In the SEEDBench-Image comparison (Table 2), LLaVA-1.5-13B-CCoT (69.7 overall) outperforms MMCoT (34.4, a finetuning method), DDCoT (58.0), and VidIL (68.9), confirming that structured scene-graph prompting surpasses both caption-based and finetuning-based multimodal CoT approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCoT extends the zero-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm (Kojima et al., 2022) to the multimodal setting by replacing free-form text reasoning with structured scene graph generation as the intermediate step. It departs from prior scene-graph-based VL methods like SGVL (Herzig et al., 2023), which require expensive ground-truth SG annotations and finetuning, by generating SGs entirely at inference time. Compared to other multimodal CoT approaches (MMCoT, DDCoT, VidIL) that rely on captions or finetuned reasoning traces, CCoT's use of generated SGs in JSON format provides a more structured compositional representation without any training data or model modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that CCoT is a drop-in, zero-shot prompting technique applicable to any LMM architecture without finetuning, making it immediately deployable and avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The finding that a zero-shot method outperforms SGVL (finetuned on ground-truth SGs) on Winoground suggests that structured intermediate representations at inference time can substitute for supervised training on compositional data. Limitations include dependence on LLM context length, reduced utility for language-heavy tasks (e.g., document understanding), and the observation that gains diminish for smaller models (7B shows negligible improvement). Open questions include whether the SG structure is truly necessary versus any sufficiently detailed structured description, and whether this approach transfers to video or 3D scene understanding.
