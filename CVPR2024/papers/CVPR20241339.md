## Problem

Generalizable novel view synthesis from sparse image pairs has been dominated by light field transformers that render fast but produce no interpretable 3D scene representation, while 3D Gaussian splatting yields real-time renderable 3D scenes but requires per-scene optimization over dozens of images. Bringing Gaussian splatting into a feed-forward, generalizable setting introduces two unsolved challenges: SfM camera poses carry an arbitrary per-scene scale factor that must be recovered, and directly regressing Gaussian positions via gradient descent falls into local minima because the non-differentiable pruning and splitting heuristics used in single-scene 3D-GS are unavailable.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of 256×256 RGB images with their SfM-reconstructed camera poses (extrinsics and intrinsics, defined only up to an unknown per-scene scale factor) and produces a set of pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian primitives—each with a 3D mean, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients—that parameterize the scene's radiance field. At inference, these Gaussians are rendered via rasterization-based splatting in ~2 ms per frame, yielding an explicit, editable 3D scene rather than a black-box light field. The model is trained at 256×256 to match the resolution supported by the strongest baseline.

## Method

pixelSplat combines a two-view epipolar transformer encoder with a probabilistic Gaussian prediction head. The encoder (ResNet-50 + ViT-B/8, DINO-pretrained) produces per-pixel features, which are then refined by epipolar cross-attention that associates each pixel with positionally-encoded depths triangulated from the opposite view, thereby resolving the SfM scale ambiguity; a subsequent self-attention layer in-paints depths for pixels lacking cross-view correspondences. For Gaussian prediction, instead of directly regressing depth, the network outputs a discrete probability distribution over Z depth buckets plus per-bucket offsets; a depth bucket is sampled from this distribution, and the Gaussian's opacity is set equal to the probability of the sampled bucket. This reparameterization trick makes the otherwise non-differentiable sampling operation differentiable: gradients that increase a Gaussian's opacity also increase the probability of re-sampling that depth, effectively allowing Gaussians to be 'spawned' or 'deleted' during training without non-differentiable heuristics. The full model is trained end-to-end with MSE + LPIPS loss under Adam, using a curriculum that gradually increases the inter-frame distance between reference views.

## Results

On RealEstate10k, pixelSplat achieves 26.09 PSNR, 0.863 SSIM, and 0.136 LPIPS, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On ACID, it reaches 28.27 PSNR, 0.843 SSIM, and 0.146 LPIPS. In terms of efficiency, pixelSplat renders at 0.002 s per frame (vs. 1.309 s for Du et al. and 13.340 s for GPNR) and requires only 14.4 GB of training memory (vs. 314.3 GB for Du et al. and 3789.9 GB for GPNR), a roughly 650× reduction in per-scene inference cost over the next-fastest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pixelSplat outperforms all three baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) on both RealEstate10k and ACID. Against the strongest baseline, Du et al. [10] (a light-field transformer with epipolar attention), pixelSplat gains +1.39 dB PSNR on ACID and +1.31 dB on RealEstate10k, while reducing rendering time from 1.309 s to 0.002 s (~650×) and training memory from 314.3 GB to 14.4 GB. Against GPNR [46], the gains are larger: +2.99 dB PSNR on ACID and +1.98 dB on RealEstate10k, with a ~6670× speedup in rendering. Against pixelNeRF [58], the improvement is most dramatic: +7.30 dB PSNR on ACID and +5.66 dB on RealEstate10k, reflecting the advantage of an explicit 3D Gaussian representation over a neural radiance field conditioned on 2D features. The LPIPS gap is particularly notable (0.146 vs. 0.218 for Du et al. on ACID), indicating substantially better perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

pixelSplat adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from its original single-scene, per-scene-optimization setting into a generalizable feed-forward pipeline, and repurposes Epipolar Transformers (He et al., 2020) specifically to resolve the per-scene scale ambiguity inherent in SfM-reconstructed poses. It departs from light field transformers (Du et al., GPNR) that sacrifice 3D interpretability for rendering speed, demonstrating instead that a primitive-based representation can be both fast and explicit. The key methodological departure from single-scene 3D-GS is replacing non-differentiable adaptive density control (pruning/splitting) with a differentiable probabilistic sampling scheme that allows gradient flow through primitive placement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that 3D Gaussians can be predicted end-to-end in a single forward pass from just two images, producing an explicit, editable 3D scene at 650× the rendering speed of the next-fastest generalizable baseline. The probabilistic reparameterization trick (coupling Gaussian opacity to the probability of its sampled depth bucket) is a general-purpose solution to the local-minima problem in differentiable primitive regression that could transfer to other primitive-based representations. Limitations include billboard-like artifacts from out-of-distribution views, transparent reflective surfaces, and evaluation only at 256×256 resolution; the authors suggest combining the framework with diffusion models and removing the camera-pose requirement as natural next steps.
