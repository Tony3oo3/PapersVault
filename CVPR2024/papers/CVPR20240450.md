## Problem

Existing similarity measures either rely on discriminative features optimized to separate samples (contrastive models like CLIP) or on raw information-theoretic quantities dominated by incompressible randomness, neither of which captures the high-level conceptual relations humans perceive. The paper asks whether one can define a principled, interpretable notion of conceptual similarity that aligns with human judgement without being explicitly trained on human similarity scores, while also proving that no canonical (representation-independent) definition of such similarity can exist.

## Input / Output

The method accepts any pair of data points—two images, two text sentences, or an image and a text sentence—and produces a conceptual distance as a continuous function of description complexity C (a curve, not a single number), along with a scalar AUC score for comparison. Unusually, the output also includes the specific natural-language descriptions selected at each complexity level, providing an interpretable explanation of where and why the two samples diverge. Inputs are consumed by generating candidate descriptions via a captioning/encoding model (LLaVA for images, LLaMA-13B for text), which are then re-evaluated under a conditional generative model or via Bayes' rule using only the encoder.

## Method

CC:DAE formulates, for each sample x, the problem of finding the optimal natural-language description h under a coding-length constraint ℓ(h) ≤ C, relaxed to a distribution q(h) over descriptions to enable gradient-free optimization. The solution takes the closed form q_x*(h|C) ∝ p_code(h)·p(x|h)^λ(C), where λ is chosen to satisfy the KL constraint, and all required expectations are estimated via importance sampling from a mixture proposal π(h) = ½p(h|x₁)+½p(h|x₂). The conceptual distance at complexity C is the mean of two asymmetric terms Δ_{2→1}(C) and Δ_{1→2}(C), each measuring the reconstruction-loss gap between a sample's own optimal description and the other sample's description. A single similarity score is obtained as the AUC of d_{x₁,x₂}(C) up to C_max, and the paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that any sufficiently expressive decoder class renders all samples structurally identical, justifying the restriction to natural-language descriptions as a non-canonical but useful hypothesis space.

## Results

On the STS sentence similarity benchmarks (Spearman correlation ×100), CC:DAE with LLaMA-13B achieves an average of 67.3, outperforming all zero-shot baselines including CLIP-ViTL14 (67.0) and Meaning as Trajectories (65.3), with a relative error reduction of 5.8% over the next best same-backbone method. On the CxC-SIS image similarity benchmark, CC:DAE achieves 81.44 Spearman correlation, reducing error by 33.5% relative to CLIP-ViTB/16 (72.08). On the cross-modal CxC-SITS benchmark, CC:DAE scores 67.71, outperforming CLIP (64.60) by 8.8% relative error reduction. On SICK-R, which tests compositional knowledge, CC:DAE reaches 74.3, surpassing even the contrastive-trained SimCSE-BERT (72.2) and CLIP (68.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP-ViTL14 (contrastive-trained), CC:DAE wins on average (67.3 vs 67.0) and by a large margin on SICK-R (74.3 vs 68.6, +5.7 points), but loses on STS12 (56.7 vs 67.7) and STS13 (67.9 vs 68.5). Against SimCSE-BERT (also contrastive-trained), CC:DAE trails significantly on average (67.3 vs 76.3), which the authors acknowledge since SimCSE was explicitly trained on human similarity annotations; however, CC:DAE still outperforms SimCSE on SICK-R (74.3 vs 72.2), suggesting better compositional generalization. Against Meaning as Trajectories [28] (the closest prior method, which the paper shows is a special case of CC:DAE at λ=1), CC:DAE wins on average (67.3 vs 65.3) and on most individual STS splits, while on CxC-SIS the two are essentially tied (81.44 vs 81.47) and CC:DAE slightly edges ahead on CxC-SITS (67.71 vs 67.63). The Cond. Likelihood baseline (direct conditional probability comparison) performs poorly across all benchmarks (avg 45.6 on STS, 29.46 on CxC-SITS), confirming that simple likelihood comparison is insufficient for measuring conceptual similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CC:DAE reframes Kolmogorov's Structure Function by replacing formal programs with natural language descriptions and introducing a stochastic relaxation (Eq. 2) that makes the optimization tractable via LLM sampling. It generalizes 'Meaning as Trajectories' (Kong et al., 2023), which the authors show is a special case at a fixed complexity level, while adding the full complexity sweep and interpretability. Conceptually, the paper departs from the contrastive-learning paradigm (CLIP, SimCSE) by arguing that similarity should be measured through optimal *description* rather than adversarial *discrimination*, and from information-theoretic distances (NCD) by grounding 'structure' in human-interpretable language rather than algorithmic incompressibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides an interpretable, zero-shot measure of conceptual similarity that outperforms contrastive-trained models (CLIP) and all prior zero-shot baselines on standard benchmarks, with the added benefit of revealing *which* descriptive features differentiate two samples at each granularity level. Theorem 4.1 formalizes a fundamental limitation: no sufficiently expressive decoder can recover non-trivial structure, justifying the non-canonical choice of natural language as the hypothesis space. A key limitation is that the description space is restricted to text, preventing the method from capturing similarities arising from visual arrangement or spatial composition; extending to visual-feature descriptions is identified as important future work.
