## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion frequently misinterpret directional relationships in prompts (e.g., generating 'bowl on book' when prompted 'book on bowl') because the CLIP text encoder treats sentences as bags of words, producing nearly identical [EOT] embeddings for object-swapped prompts. This paper formalizes the task of Relation Rectification—making the model distinguish and correctly render the direction of a given relationship—and identifies the indistinguishable [EOT] embeddings as the root cause.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single text prompt containing a directional relationship and outputs a standard generated image via the frozen Stable Diffusion pipeline (PNDM scheduler, 30 steps, classifier-free guidance). During training, it consumes pairs of object-swapped prompts (e.g., '<A, R, B>' and '<B, R, A>') along with 100 exemplar images per prompt, and the RRNet produces a low-dimensional adjustment vector that is additively applied to the [EOT] token embedding before it conditions the U-Net. A tunable weight λ ∈ [0,1] controls the strength of the adjustment, trading off relational accuracy against image fidelity (FID).

## Method

RRNet models each object-swapped prompt pair as a directed heterogeneous graph with three node types—objects (V_O), relations (V_R), and a dedicated ΔEOT node—and processes it with a Heterogeneous Graph Convolutional Network whose weight matrices are type-specific. The HGCN aggregates CLIP word embeddings from object and relation nodes into the ΔEOT node, producing an adjustment vector h_ΔEOT that is added to the original [EOT] embedding (V_eot* = V_eot + λ·h_ΔEOT) to separate the embeddings of the two OSPs. Training uses a combined loss: a positive denoising loss that aligns the adjusted embedding with exemplar images, and a negative loss that penalizes the swapped prompt's embedding on the same images, forcing [EOT] separation. An object-node disentanglement mechanism (with Gaussian noise perturbation) prevents individual object features from blending into the relation representation. Only the lightweight HGCN is trained (~20 min per relationship on one V100); both the CLIP text encoder and the SD U-Net remain frozen, preserving general generation quality.

## Results

On the Relation Rectification Benchmark, RRNet (λ=0.6) achieves 69.7% positional relation accuracy (Qwen-VL-Chat) and 50.0% action relation accuracy, compared to 46.7% and 39.9% for vanilla Stable Diffusion and 50.9% and 27.3% for Personalized Diffusion. Object Generation Accuracy (OGA) improves from 89.8% (SD) and 84.4% (Personalized Diffusion) to 97.0% with RRNet. The trade-off is image fidelity: FID increases from 83.73 (SD) to 100.78 (RRNet λ=0.6), though the authors note that λ=0.4–0.6 offers the best balance between relation accuracy and image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RRNet against two baselines: vanilla Stable Diffusion (SD) and a Personalized Diffusion variant that fine-tunes the CLIP text encoder. Against SD, RRNet (λ=0.6) improves positional relation accuracy by 23.0 percentage points (69.7% vs. 46.7% on Qwen) and action relation accuracy by 10.1 points (50.0% vs. 39.9%), while also raising OGA from 89.8% to 97.0%. Against Personalized Diffusion, the gains are even larger on action relations (50.0% vs. 27.3%, a 22.7-point improvement) and OGA (97.0% vs. 84.4%). The cost is image fidelity: FID degrades from 83.73 (SD) to 100.78 (RRNet λ=0.6), and Personalized Diffusion sits in between at 91.93. A user study with 63 evaluators confirms the advantage, with RRNet preferred 75.56% of the time versus 16.98% for SD and 7.46% for Personalized Diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as a frozen backbone and addresses a specific failure mode identified in the VLM literature: CLIP-based text encoders treat prompts as bags-of-words, making directional relations indistinguishable (Yuksekgonul et al., 2022). It departs from personalized diffusion methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) that modify text embeddings for subject-driven generation, and from compositional generation approaches (GLIGEN, ControlNet, Attend-and-Excite) that rely on auxiliary layout inputs or attention manipulation. The key conceptual departure is using a Heterogeneous GCN to explicitly encode the directed graph structure of object-relation-object triplets, producing a lightweight adjustment vector on the [EOT] embedding rather than retraining or augmenting the diffusion pipeline itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, diagnosable explanation for why diffusion models fail on directional relations (near-identical [EOT] embeddings for object-swapped prompts) and a minimal-intervention fix that preserves the base model's general capabilities. The introduced Relation Rectification Benchmark (21 relations, 4200 images) fills a gap in evaluation tooling for relational generation. However, the FID degradation at higher adjustment strengths (λ) reveals an unresolved quality-accuracy trade-off, and the evaluation's reliance on VLM chatbots (Qwen-VL, LLaVA) to judge relation correctness introduces a potential circularity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes with more than two entities, whether it transfers to other diffusion architectures, and whether the 21-relation benchmark is sufficient to claim general relational understanding.
