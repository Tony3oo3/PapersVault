## Problem

All existing model inversion (MI) defenses rely on dependency-minimization regularization that directly conflicts with the classification training objective, causing significant utility degradation and requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning. This paper asks whether transfer learning—specifically, limiting which layers see private data—can improve MI robustness without modifying the training objective at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241293/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The target model T is a standard K-way classifier (CNN or ViT) that takes RGB facial images as input and outputs an identity class label. The MI adversary, given white-box access to T and a public dataset, reconstructs the private training image via GAN-guided optimization. The defense operates entirely at training time: the model architecture and inference pipeline remain unchanged, so the input/output contract is identical to an unmodified classifier.

## Method

TL-DMI decomposes the target model into an encoder E (early layers) and a classifier C (late layers), then applies a two-stage transfer learning procedure: (1) pre-train both E and C on a public dataset with no identity overlap with the private set, and (2) fine-tune only C on the private dataset while freezing E. A Fisher Information analysis—conducted for the first time on the MI task—reveals that the earliest layers carry the most information exploitable by MI reconstruction, while the latest layers are most important for classification. By preventing private information from being encoded in the frozen early layers, TL-DMI reduces the number of parameters that memorize private data, degrading MI attack accuracy by 20–70% across 20 attack setups while incurring only 1–6% natural accuracy loss. The method requires no change to the training loss, no additional regularization, and no grid-search hyperparameter tuning, making it trivially applicable to any architecture.

## Results

Across four SOTA MI attack setups (VMI, LOMMA, PPA on ResNet-18/101), TL-DMI consistently achieves the best MI robustness trade-off (Δ metric) compared to BiDO. The most striking result is PPA on ResNet-18, where TL-DMI reduces attack accuracy from 88.46% (no defense) to 22.36% while retaining 91.12% natural accuracy (Δ=21.32), versus BiDO's 76.56% attack accuracy (Δ=4.12). On PPA with ResNet-101, TL-DMI drops attack accuracy to 31.82% (Δ=10.75) vs. BiDO's 67.26% (Δ=3.46). When combined with BiDO, TL-DMI further reduces attack accuracy by 27.36% (46.23%→18.87%) while improving natural accuracy by 1.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is BiDO (Peng et al., KDD 2022), the current SOTA MI defense based on bilateral dependency optimization regularization. TL-DMI outperforms BiDO on the Δ robustness metric in every evaluated setup: 2.22 vs. 1.13 (VMI/ResNet-34), 3.58 vs. 2.91 (LOMMA/VGG-16), 21.32 vs. 4.12 (PPA/ResNet-18), and 10.75 vs. 3.46 (PPA/ResNet-101). The gap is most dramatic under the PPA attack, where TL-DMI reduces attack accuracy by 54–55 percentage points (88.46%→22.36% on ResNet-18; 83.00%→31.82% on ResNet-101) while BiDO only achieves 12–16 point reductions. TL-DMI also maintains higher natural accuracy than BiDO in most setups (e.g., 62.20% vs. 61.14% on VMI; 83.41% vs. 80.35% on LOMMA), indicating a better utility–robustness trade-off. The paper also notes that TL-DMI is significantly easier to implement (no hyperparameter grid search) and extends to architectures (MaxViT, ResNeSt-101) and datasets (Stanford Dogs, VGGFace2) that BiDO has not been evaluated on.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the existing MI defense paradigm, which relies on dependency minimization regularization (MID, BiDO) that explicitly conflicts with the classification objective. Instead, TL-DMI repurposes the standard two-stage transfer learning framework (pre-training on public data, fine-tuning on private data) by selectively freezing early layers during fine-tuning, thereby limiting which parameters encode private information. The method is further justified by a novel Fisher Information analysis of layer importance for the MI task, extending FI-based parameter importance from continual learning and generative tasks to model inversion for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TL-DMI offers a remarkably simple, hyperparameter-light defense that is readily applicable across diverse architectures (CNNs, Vision Transformers) without the intensive grid search required by BiDO, making it far more practical for real-world deployment. The finding that early layers are critical for MI reconstruction but not for classification provides a useful mechanistic understanding that could inform other privacy-preserving training strategies. However, the work is limited to classification tasks and facial image domains; extending to detection, segmentation, or non-facial domains remains open, as does understanding how TL-DMI interacts with other privacy threats like membership inference.
