## Problem

Existing learning-based ISP methods learn a single average mapping between sRGB and RAW images, ignoring the fact that real camera ISPs adapt their operations (denoising, tone mapping, etc.) to camera parameters such as exposure time, ISO sensitivity, aperture, and focal length. This leads to degraded reconstruction quality, and prior network architectures (simple stacked convolutions or single invertible flows) are too simplistic to capture the full complexity of real ISP pipelines.

## Input / Output

The forward ISP takes a single-channel RAW image (Bayer pattern) plus EXIF camera parameters (exposure time, ISO, aperture, focal length, white balance coefficients, color correction matrix) and outputs a 3-channel sRGB image; the inverse ISP reverses this. Camera parameters are read directly from the EXIF metadata of the captured JPEG, requiring no modification to the camera pipeline. Training uses 448×448 patches, and the model is fine-tuned per camera model after multi-camera pre-training.

## Method

ParamISP decomposes each ISP direction into four subnetworks: CanoNet (fixed, weight-free demosaicing, white balance, and color-space conversion driven by canonical parameters), LocalNet (a U-Net with CBAM that predicts a residual for local operations like denoising and sharpening, conditioned on handcrafted features and a parameter feature vector), GlobalNet (predicts coefficients for N=4 iterative stages of quadratic transformation and gamma correction for global tone mapping), and ParamNet (converts optical parameters into a conditioning vector via a non-linear equalization layer that applies 15 diverse mappings—x, 1/x, √x, log x, sin(log x), etc.—to handle scale differences, followed by FC layers). A 20% random dropout of individual optical parameter vectors during training prevents overfitting to a subset of parameters. The forward and inverse networks are trained end-to-end with L1 losses in a two-stage protocol (multi-camera pre-training followed by single-camera fine-tuning), and at inference they operate independently rather than as a coupled invertible pair.

## Results

On sRGB-to-RAW reconstruction (Table 4), ParamISP achieves an average PSNR of 41.01 dB across five cameras (D7000, D90, D40, S7, A7R3), surpassing the best prior method InvISP (39.08 dB) by 1.93 dB and CycleISP (38.82 dB) by 2.19 dB, while using only 0.7M parameters. On RAW-to-sRGB reconstruction (Table 5), ParamISP reaches 35.81 dB average PSNR, outperforming InvISP (33.97 dB) by 1.84 dB. ParamISP wins on every individual camera in both directions, with the largest margins on D7000 (+5.01 dB over InvISP in RAW) and the smallest on S7 forward (+0.06 dB over InvISP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParamISP outperforms all four baselines on every camera in both reconstruction directions. Against the non-learnable UPI [4], ParamISP gains roughly 10–18 dB in RAW and 10–16 dB in sRGB, reflecting the fundamental limitation of a fixed parametric pipeline. Against CIE XYZ Net [3], the improvement is approximately 8 dB (RAW) and 5 dB (sRGB), attributable to ParamISP's learned local and global tone modules versus CIE XYZ Net's simpler convolutional and single-quadratic design. The closest competitor is InvISP [29] (a normalizing-flow invertible network): ParamISP leads by 1.93 dB average in RAW and 1.84 dB in sRGB, with the margin ranging from +0.06 dB (S7 forward) to +5.01 dB (D7000 inverse). CycleISP [30] is competitive in RAW reconstruction (within ~2.2 dB on average) but is excluded from the forward comparison because its architecture requires the input sRGB image during the forward pass, making it unsuitable for applications that modify the intermediate RAW image (e.g., deblurring, HDR). Notably, ParamISP achieves these gains with the smallest model size (0.7M vs. 1.3–3.1M for the other learned methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learned ISP line of work (UPI, CIE XYZ Net, CycleISP, InvISP) by conditioning the forward and inverse ISP networks on optical camera parameters (exposure time, sensitivity, aperture, focal length) extracted from EXIF metadata. Prior methods either used only canonical parameters (Bayer pattern, white balance, CCM) or ignored parameters entirely, learning an average ISP. The key architectural departure is ParamNet, which handles the heterogeneous scales and non-linear distributions of optical parameters via a non-linear equalization layer and random dropout, feeding a feature vector that modulates U-Net-based local and iterative gamma/quadratic global tone subnetworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By faithfully modeling how real ISPs adapt to camera parameters, ParamISP enables more accurate RAW-level processing (deblurring, HDR, camera-to-camera transfer) from sRGB images without requiring RAW captures. The paper demonstrates that all four optical parameters contribute to reconstruction quality, though the exact mechanism by which aperture and focal length influence ISP behavior remains unclear. A key open question is whether pre-training across heterogeneous cameras introduces conflicting parameter-conditioned behaviors, and whether the approach generalizes to cameras whose ISP parameterization differs substantially from the training set.
