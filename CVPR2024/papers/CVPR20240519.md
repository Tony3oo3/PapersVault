## Problem

Dense and semi-dense feature matchers waste computation on matching-redundant regions (non-overlapping or repetitive areas), limiting their accuracy. Prior redundancy-reduction methods like SGAM rely on explicit semantic segmentation, which is impractical due to labeling errors, semantic ambiguity, and closed-set label constraints. The paper asks how to reduce matching redundancy in a general, label-free manner that works across diverse scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240519/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MESA takes a pair of RGB images as input, applies SAM to segment each into areas, and produces area-to-area correspondences that are then fed to a downstream point matcher (e.g., DKM, LoFTR, ASpan, QuadT) for precise point correspondences. The learning-based similarity module operates on 320×320 (indoor) or 480×480 (outdoor/odometry) square crops of matched areas, and the final area match is a single fused region per source area, with an energy threshold (T_Emax) to reject unmatched areas.

## Method

MESA constructs a multi-relational Area Graph (AG) from SAM segments, where nodes are areas organized into scale-hierarchy levels and edges encode two relations: directed inclusion edges (parent–child) and undirected adjacency edges (spatial neighbors). The AG is converted into two graphical models: an Area Markov Random Field (AMRF) that formulates area matching as a binary-labeling energy minimization solvable by Graph Cut, and an Area Bayesian Network (ABN) that exploits conditional independence along inclusion edges to skip redundant similarity computations. Area similarity is computed by a Siamese CNN with self- and cross-attention that performs patch-level binary classification (predicting whether each 1/8-resolution patch appears in the other area), yielding a similarity score as the product of two activity-map expectations. A final global matching energy, considering parent, child, and neighbor structures in both source and target graphs, refines the Graph Cut solution to select the single best area match, enabling robust matching under repetitive patterns.

## Results

On the ScanNet1500 indoor pose estimation benchmark, MESA combined with DKM achieves 33.4% AUC@5° (+13.61% over DKM alone), 55.0% AUC@10° (+8.48%), and 72.0% AUC@20° (+5.42%), setting a new state-of-the-art. MESA also delivers +15.26% AUC@5° improvement for QuadT and +7.42% for ASpan. On the MegaDepth1500 outdoor benchmark, MESA_DKM reaches 61.1% AUC@5° (new SOTA), while MESA_LoFTR gains +7.58% AUC@5° over LoFTR alone. In area matching on ScanNet1500, MESA achieves 67.98% AOR versus 60.59% for SGAM with SEEM segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MESA consistently outperforms SGAM (with SEEM semantic segmentation) across all four point matchers on indoor pose estimation, with the largest gap for DKM: MESA_DKM achieves 33.4% AUC@5° versus 30.6% for SEEM+SGAM_DKM (+13.61% vs. +4.12% improvement over DKM alone). MESA_DKM also surpasses all standalone recent methods including CasMTR (27.1%), PATS (26.0%), and SiLK (18.0%) on AUC@5°. For semi-dense matchers, MESA_QuadT gains +15.26% AUC@5° over QuadT alone, substantially exceeding SGAM's +2.53% improvement, while MESA_ASpan matches SGAM_ASpan at 27.5% AUC@5°. On the outdoor MegaDepth1500 benchmark, MESA_LoFTR achieves 56.8% AUC@5° (+7.58%), outperforming OETR+LoFTR (54.8%) and surpassing standalone ASTR (58.4%) and CasMTR (59.1%) only when combined with DKM (61.1%). The paper notes that MESA's advantage is most pronounced for dense matchers (DKM) where matching redundancy is most harmful, while gains for semi-dense methods are comparable to SGAM due to their coarse-to-fine pipelines being less sensitive to area overlap ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

MESA extends the Area to Point Matching (A2PM) framework introduced by SGAM, which established that matching semantic areas before point matching reduces redundancy. The key departure is replacing SGAM's reliance on explicit semantic segmentation (e.g., SEEM) with SAM's implicit-semantic segmentation, eliminating sensitivity to labeling errors and closed-set label limitations. The novel Area Graph (AG) with dual edge types (inclusion and adjacency) and its conversion into AMRF/ABN graphical models for energy-minimization-based matching is the paper's core architectural contribution, building on classical Graph Cut and Potts model machinery. The learning-based area similarity via Siamese CNN with cross-attention draws on the patch-level classification paradigm from recent point matchers like LoFTR and ASpan.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MESA addresses a practical bottleneck—matching redundancy—that limits the accuracy of dense and semi-dense feature matchers, and does so by leveraging a widely available foundation model (SAM) rather than task-specific semantic segmentation, making it broadly applicable across indoor, outdoor, and driving scenes. The graph-based formulation (AMRF + ABN) provides an interpretable and efficient solution path, and the ablation showing that SAM's implicit semantic outperforms explicit semantic segmentation is a useful signal for the community. Limitations include dependence on SAM's segmentation quality, the need to train a separate similarity model, and added computational overhead from graph construction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video sequences or 3D scenes, and whether the graph hierarchy can be constructed more efficiently for very large or highly repetitive scenes.
