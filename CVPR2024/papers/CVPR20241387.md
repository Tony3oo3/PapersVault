## Problem

Existing gradient compression methods for distributed learning combine sparsification and quantization in a two-stage sequential pipeline, which restricts the solution space and prevents the two operations from being jointly optimized. This leads to suboptimal compression strategies that are architecture-specific and fail to generalize across different network types. The paper asks whether sparsification and quantization can be unified into a single end-to-end optimization to eliminate this suboptimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241387/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a per-layer gradient tensor of arbitrary dimension d and produces a compressed gradient along with a per-element mixed-precision quantization mask. The bit-width candidate set is restricted to {0, 2, 4, 8} bits, where 0-bit corresponds to sparsification (zeroing out the element). The assignment must satisfy a global communication budget c (total bits across all elements), and the output mask determines which elements are dropped (0-bit), quantized at 2-bit, 4-bit, or 8-bit precision.

## Method

JointSQ's key insight is to treat sparsification as 0-bit quantization, thereby reformulating the two-stage sparsify-then-quantize pipeline as a single mixed-precision quantization problem over n bit-width levels. The optimal per-element bit-width assignment is formulated as a Multiple-Choice Knapsack Problem (MCKP), where each gradient element is a group, each bit-width choice is an item with weight equal to the bit-width and profit equal to the noise reduction from Eq. (11). The MCKP is solved by a Greedy Allocation step that selects items in order of highest Incremental Profit Density (O(d log k) complexity), followed by a Reallocation fine-tuning phase that iteratively swaps bit-widths between boundary elements to further reduce compression noise in O(1) per iteration. An adaptive parameter R dynamically adjusts the number of non-zero elements per layer based on observed noise reduction, and the paper proves that JointSQ's minimum achievable compression noise is always ≤ that of any two-stage Co-compressor, since the latter's solution space is a strict subset of JointSQ's.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 at 20× compression, JointSQ achieves 91.74% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-20 (vs. 91.75% full-precision SGD), 93.62% on ResNet-110, and 84.42% on SimpleViT, matching or exceeding the uncompressed baseline across all four architectures tested. At the extreme 1000× compression ratio, JointSQ retains 91.55% on ResNet-20 (only 0.2% loss) and 90.78% on VGG-16. On ImageNet (ResNet-101) at 1000×, JointSQ incurs just 0.18% top-1 accuracy drop (74.72% vs. 74.9% baseline), and on PTB language modeling at 1000× it achieves 76.38 perplexity, a 1.46 improvement over the full-precision baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JointSQ consistently outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and compression ratio tested. Against the joint-compression baselines QLSGD [5] and AC-SGD [37], JointSQ leads by 0.48–1.91% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 at 20× (e.g., 91.74% vs. 91.26% and 89.88% on ResNet-20) and by 0.34–1.94% at 1000×. Against the sparsification-only baseline DGC [21], JointSQ gains 0.23% on ResNet-20 (91.55% vs. 91.32%) and 2.66% on VGG-16 (90.78% vs. 88.12%) at 1000×. On CIFAR-100 at 32×, JointSQ surpasses signSGD [6] by 3.86% (75.04% vs. 71.18%) and MCGQ [23] by 0.79% (75.04% vs. 74.25%), while on ImageNet at 1000× it beats MCGQ by 0.18% and DGC by 0.26%. On PTB at 1000×, JointSQ achieves 76.38 perplexity, improving over DGC by 0.47 and MCGQ by 1.29. Notably, JointSQ is the only method that maintains near-lossless accuracy on the compression-sensitive ViT architecture (84.42% vs. 76.82% for QLSGD at 20×), highlighting its architectural generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate gradient compression techniques—sparsification and quantization—by reframing sparsification as 0-bit quantization, thereby transforming the standard two-stage sequential Co-compression pipeline into a single mixed-precision quantization problem. It builds directly on the unbiasedness and bounded-variance analysis of Co-compressors established in prior work (e.g., AC-SGD [37]) and on the mixed-precision quantization framework with n levels [33]. The key departure from prior joint methods (QLSGD, AC-SGD, MCGQ) is that those still treat sparsification and quantization as separate stages with independently chosen parameters, whereas JointSQ jointly optimizes bit-width assignment across all elements via a per-layer Multiple-Choice Knapsack Problem solved by a greedy algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JointSQ provides a principled, architecture-agnostic compression strategy that achieves 1000× compression with under 0.2% accuracy loss and 1.4×–2.9× wall-clock speedup in multi-node settings, making it practically relevant for bandwidth-constrained edge and federated learning deployments. The MCKP-based allocation is lightweight (O(d log k) for greedy allocation, O(1) per reallocation step), so the method adds negligible compute overhead. Open questions include whether the dynamic compression-ratio schedule shown in preliminary experiments (Section 4.4) can be formalized, how the approach scales to very large models (e.g., billion-parameter LLMs) where per-element bit-width assignment may become costly, and whether the mild accuracy gains over full-precision SGD observed on some datasets reflect a genuine regularization effect or are dataset-specific.
