## Problem

Existing unsupervised visible-infrared person re-identification methods rely exclusively on deep features from the final layer to learn cross-modality representations, which is insufficient under the combined pressure of intra-modality variation and inter-modality discrepancy, leading to negative optimization. The nuanced detail information carried by shallow-layer features—critical for capturing modality-invariant pedestrian attributes—is systematically disregarded by prior work, leaving a gap in how cross-modality supervision is derived and how reliable neighbors are selected.

## Input / Output

The method consumes visible and infrared person images resized to 288×144, processed through a dual-path architecture where modality-specific shallow patch embeddings (IBN + CNN) and a shared TransReID transformer backbone produce both shallow and deep class tokens. Retrieval similarity is computed as the cosine distance between the concatenation of shallow and deep class tokens. Pseudo identity labels are generated offline via DBSCAN clustering, and the CRA module refines cross-modality labels at the start of each epoch before training resumes.

## Method

SDCL builds on a dual-path transformer with two modality-specific shallow patch embedding layers and a modality-shared transformer, extending the augmented dual-contrastive learning baseline by adding shallow contrastive losses alongside deep ones. The Collaborative Neighbor Learning (CNL) module computes homogeneous (intra-modality) and heterogeneous (inter-modality) neighbor sets from both shallow and deep similarities, then takes their intersection to obtain reliable neighbors, applying contrastive losses on these refined sets to dynamically reduce label noise. The Collaborative Ranking Association (CRA) module exploits the ranking consistency between shallow and deep cross-modality similarity lists to assign refined cross-modality pseudo labels via majority voting over top-k intersections, then smooths these labels using a combined shallow-deep intra-modality similarity matrix. The overall loss combines identity-level contrastive loss (shallow + deep, both modalities) with the four-directional neighbor learning loss, and the CRA-refined labels are used to build a modality-shared memory for cross-modality contrastive training in the final 20 of 50 epochs.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (all-search), SDCL achieves 64.49% Rank-1, 63.24% mAP, and 51.06% mINP, surpassing the best prior unsupervised method GUR (63.51% Rank-1) by ~1%. On RegDB (visible-to-infrared), SDCL reaches 86.91% Rank-1 and 78.92% mAP, exceeding GUR (73.91% Rank-1) by ~13%. On SYSU-MM01 (indoor-search), SDCL attains 71.37% Rank-1 and 76.90% mAP. The method also outperforms several supervised baselines including DDAG (54.75%), AGW (47.50%), and MSO (58.70%) on SYSU-MM01 all-search Rank-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDCL outperforms all 15 unsupervised baselines on both SYSU-MM01 and RegDB. Against the strongest prior unsupervised method GUR [51], SDCL gains ~1% Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01 all-search (64.49% vs. 63.51%) and a substantial ~13% on RegDB visible-to-infrared (86.91% vs. 73.91%). SDCL also surpasses the uni-semi-supervised DPIS [35] (58.4% Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01) despite using no visible identity annotations. Among supervised methods, SDCL exceeds DDAG [54] (54.75%), AGW [57] (47.50%), and MSO [12] (58.70%) on SYSU-MM01 all-search Rank-1, and is competitive with MCLNet [16] (65.40%), though it trails the top supervised approaches such as SGIEL [11] (77.12%), PartMix [20] (77.78%), and PMCM [34] (75.54%). On RegDB, SDCL's 86.91% Rank-1 (V→I) surpasses all unsupervised methods but remains below the best supervised entries (e.g., SGIEL at 95.35%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ADCA framework [50], which established a dual-path transformer with augmented dual-contrastive learning for unsupervised VI-ReID, by introducing shallow modality-specific patch embeddings alongside the deep modality-shared transformer features. Prior US-VI-ReID methods (ADCA, PGM, H2H, CHCR, GUR) uniformly relied on deep features from the ultimate layer for both feature learning and pseudo-label generation, disregarding the detail information in shallow layers. The key conceptual departure is the argument that under multiple discrepancies (intra-modality variation and inter-modality gap), deep features alone are insufficient for reliable neighbor selection and cross-modality label association, motivating the two new modules—CNL for dynamic neighbor refinement and CRA for global ranking-based label association—that explicitly exploit shallow-deep consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that shallow features, long overlooked in US-VI-ReID, provide meaningful complementary signal for cross-modality alignment is a useful observation that could inform other cross-modality retrieval tasks. The method's ability to outperform several supervised baselines while remaining fully unsupervised is practically significant for real-world deployment where cross-modality annotation is prohibitively expensive. However, evaluation is limited to two relatively small benchmarks (SYSU-MM01, RegDB), and the added complexity of dual memory structures, two new modules, and the late-stage CRA schedule raises questions about scalability and robustness to larger, more diverse datasets. An open question is whether the shallow-deep collaboration principle generalizes beyond person ReID to other cross-modality matching problems.
