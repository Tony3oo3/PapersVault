## Problem

Most open-vocabulary object detection methods transfer vision-language alignment from pretrained VLMs (e.g., CLIP) to off-the-shelf detectors like Faster-RCNN, but this knowledge transfer suffers from information attenuation due to domain gaps, limiting generalization to novel categories. Additionally, existing built-in detector approaches (e.g., OV-DETR) rely on fixed prediction slots or manually designed anchors that produce excessive redundant proposals, causing high computational cost and poor convergence when handling multiple class queries simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224, yielding 14×14 patches with 16×16 patch size) and a text query (category name or prompt) as input. The image is encoded by a DINOv2 ViT backbone into patch-level embeddings, while the text is encoded by a CLIP text encoder into a single query embedding. The output consists of bounding boxes (x, y, h, w) and class labels, where the number of predictions is determined adaptively by the anchor proposal network rather than a fixed set of 100 DETR-style slots. An RPN is used only during the region-word alignment training phase and is discarded at inference.

## Method

BIND employs a two-stage Encoder-Decoder framework. In stage 1, a DINOv2 ViT image encoder and CLIP text encoder are trained for region-word alignment: an RPN generates region proposals, RoIAlign extracts region embeddings, and a bipartite matching problem (solved via Hungarian algorithm) pairs regions with caption nouns, optimized with focal loss. In stage 2, a 6-layer DETR-style Transformer decoder (width 256, 8 heads) is trained on annotated detection data, using global image features as keys/values and anchor-augmented query embeddings as queries. The key architectural contribution is the Anchor Proposal Network, which computes a positional filter via max-pooled and normalized query-image patch similarity, then applies it as a Hadamard mask over learned positional embeddings to produce only high-likelihood proposals—eliminating the need for fixed prediction slots and reducing both training and inference computation. DINOv2 is chosen over CLIP for the image encoder because its self-supervised pretraining yields segment-level features more sensitive to local regions, which is critical for region-word alignment.

## Results

On the OV-COCO benchmark, BIND with ViT-B/16 achieves 36.3 AP50 on novel classes, outperforming all prior methods (best prior: OADP at 35.6). Scaling to ViT-L/16 (BIND*) further improves novel AP50 to 41.5. On OV-LVIS, BIND (ViT-B/16) achieves 29.4 APr (rare category mAP), surpassing CFM-ViT (28.8) and CORA (28.1), while BIND* (ViT-L/16) reaches 32.5 APr. Inference speed is 0.21 s/img on COCO, roughly 2.4× faster than CORA (0.50 s/img) at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OV-COCO, BIND (ViT-B/16) achieves 36.3 novel AP50, beating the strongest transfer-based method OADP (35.6) by 0.7 and the prior built-in detector CORA (35.1) by 1.2. On OV-LVIS, BIND (ViT-B/16) reaches 29.4 APr, surpassing CFM-ViT (28.8), CORA (28.1), and Ro-ViT (28.0), with a larger gap over distillation-based methods like OADP (21.9) and OV-DETR (21.0). Scaling to ViT-L/16, BIND* achieves 41.5 novel AP50 on COCO and 32.5 APr on LVIS, maintaining the lead over all baselines. In terms of efficiency, BIND (ViT-B/16) runs at 0.21 s/img on COCO versus CORA's 0.50 s/img at similar accuracy (36.3 vs. 41.7 novel AP50 with CORA's RN50×4), and on LVIS BIND (ViT-B/16) at 0.39 s/img is faster than OWL-ViT (ViT-L/14, JAX) at 0.42 s/img while achieving higher APr (29.4 vs. 31.2). The paper also notes that CORA's base-class AP50 (35.5) is substantially lower than BIND's (54.7), suggesting CORA overfits to novel classes at the expense of base-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIND positions itself against the dominant transfer-based paradigm in open-vocabulary detection (CLIP → Faster-RCNN), arguing that knowledge transfer causes information attenuation. It builds on the DETR-style Transformer architecture and OV-DETR's open-vocabulary formulation, but replaces OV-DETR's per-class conditional matching with a learned anchor proposal network that adaptively generates proposals from image-query interactions. The choice of DINOv2 over CLIP as the visual encoder departs from the CLIP-centric trend in the field, motivated by DINOv2's segment-level feature maps being more suitable for region-word alignment. The bipartite matching formulation for region-word alignment draws on Hungarian matching, while the overall two-stage encoder-decoder design synthesizes ideas from contrastive pre-training, DETR, and RPN-based proposal generation into a unified built-in detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The built-in detector framing eliminates a known failure mode (information loss during VLM-to-detector transfer) and the anchor proposal network delivers a practical 3× inference speedup (0.21s vs 0.63s per image on COCO) while matching or exceeding prior accuracy, making open-vocabulary detection more deployable. However, the method still requires an RPN during training and a two-stage curriculum, adding implementation complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large vocabularies beyond LVIS's 1203 classes, whether the DINOv2 advantage over CLIP persists with larger-scale pre-training data, and whether the anchor proposal network generalizes to other detection paradigms (e.g., instance segmentation or video detection).
