## Problem

Existing camera pose estimation in NeRF and SLAM pipelines optimizes discrete SE(3) parameters per keyframe, ignoring the inherent continuity of camera motion. This discrete treatment causes inaccurate accumulation of high-frequency or asynchronous measurements (IMUs, event streams), loss of fine-grained motion details, and forfeits the continuous motion prior that could regularize optimization. The paper asks whether representing poses as a continuous neural function of time can address all three issues simultaneously across diverse sensor setups.

## Input / Output

The PoseNet takes a scalar time value t (embedded via sinusoidal harmonic functions) and outputs a 7-dimensional vector [v; q] representing translation and unit quaternion rotation. In practice, the network is queried at arbitrary timestamps — the exact microsecond of each asynchronous event or each IMU sample — rather than at fixed keyframe intervals, and the predicted pose is composed with an initial estimate (T_i = T_init ∘ T_refine). The broader system produces a continuous camera trajectory, a refined neural radiance field, and a neural implicit scene representation, all optimized end-to-end from RGB, depth, event, or IMU inputs.

## Method

The core architecture is an 8-layer MLP (256-dim hidden units, ReLU) with sinusoidal time embedding that maps t → [v, q], where the final layer applies tanh to constrain outputs before quaternion normalization. This PoseNet is jointly optimized with the scene INR via photometric and geometric losses, replacing discrete per-frame pose variables with a single set of network parameters θ_p. A key additional contribution is the intrinsic motion frame: the full pose is decomposed as T = T_o ∘ T_I where T_o (slowly-changing reference) and T_I (low-DOF intrinsic motion) are each modeled by separate PoseNets, with an ℓ₁-relaxed ℓ₀ DOF loss on T_I's normalized Euler angles and translation to enforce sparsity. For IMU fusion, the continuous representation enables tight coupling by directly supervising the quaternion time derivative (via auto-differentiation) with measured angular velocity, eliminating the drift inherent in discrete integration.

## Results

On the NeRF-from-inaccurate-poses task (LLFF real data), PoseNet achieves a 12/12 success rate versus BARF's 9/12, with average PSNR of 19.58 dB vs. 17.50 dB and average translation error of 0.320 vs. 10.777 (BARF diverges on 3 scenes). In dense visual SLAM on Replica, the full method with intrinsic motion achieves 0.49 cm ATE-RMSE, nearly halving NICE-SLAM's 1.06 cm. On EUROC with IMU fusion, tight coupling yields 6.16 cm average ATE-RMSE, outperforming NICE-SLAM (which fails on v102, v202, v203) and approaching DROID-SLAM's 2.2 cm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BARF [27] on NeRF pose refinement, PoseNet wins decisively: it succeeds on all 12 scenes (12/12) while BARF diverges on 3 (Fortress/2, Orchids/4, Room/4), and even where BARF succeeds, PoseNet achieves lower rotation/translation error and higher PSNR (e.g., Room: 25.32 vs. 21.78 dB). In dense SLAM on Replica, ScanNet, and TUM RGB-D, the method with intrinsic motion outperforms NICE-SLAM [63] by 54% (0.49 vs. 1.06 cm), 17% (8.93 vs. 10.70 cm), and 81% (2.48 vs. 13.28 cm) in ATE-RMSE, respectively, and also beats Vox-Fusion [60], ESLAM [20], and DI-Fusion [16]. On EUROC with IMU fusion, tight coupling (6.16 cm avg) outperforms NICE-SLAM (which fails on 3/6 sequences) and loose coupling (6.77 cm), though it remains behind the sparse-feature DROID-SLAM [54] (2.2 cm) and ORB-SLAM [36]. For event-based NeRF, PoseNet reduces translation error by 50–85% over EventNeRF [46] across all six Chair/Hotdog setups (e.g., 6-view: 22.68 vs. 59.31 cm TE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of optimizing discrete SE(3) pose parameters (as in BARF, NICE-SLAM, and EventNeRF) by representing camera motion as a continuous neural function of time (PoseNet), drawing on the sinusoidal embedding idea from NeRF and the continuous-time estimation literature (B-splines, Gaussian processes) while replacing those basis functions with a trainable MLP. It integrates directly into the NICE-SLAM tracking pipeline and the EventNeRF radiance-field framework, and introduces the first IMU fusion in a NeRF+SLAM setting. The intrinsic motion frame—a decomposition of 6-DOF pose into a low-DOF manifold plus a rigid offset—is a novel conceptual addition not present in prior continuous-time SLAM or NeRF pose-refinement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play PoseNet offers a broadly applicable drop-in replacement for discrete pose variables across NeRF, event-based reconstruction, and dense SLAM, with the added benefit of free velocity/acceleration via autodifferentiation. However, practical impact is tempered: on EUROC the method still trails sparse SOTA trackers (DROID-SLAM) by a wide margin, and the intrinsic-motion gains are inconsistent across datasets (large on Replica, marginal on ScanNet). Open questions include whether the continuous-pose prior helps in truly online, long-duration settings, whether the low-DOF assumption generalizes beyond indoor vSLAM, and how the approach scales to multi-camera or large-scale outdoor scenes.
