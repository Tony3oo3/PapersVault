## Problem

Multi-view wire art (MVWA) — a static 3D sculpture that reveals different 2D images from different viewpoints — is extremely difficult to create even for skilled artists, and prior computational methods (voxel carving, 3D path-finding) are rule-based, transparent but brittle, collapsing on complex or arbitrary visual inputs. No existing system could generate MVWA from free-form text prompts or rough scribbles, leaving the creative process locked behind expert 3D wire organisation and reverse-projection reasoning.

## Input / Output

The system takes three user-specified conditions — one per mutually orthogonal viewpoint (X, Y, Z) — each of which can be a text prompt (e.g., 'a simple drawing of a bicycle'), a scribble, or a combination of text and visual layout via ControlNet (canny edges, HED boundaries, poses, etc.). The output is a set of 3D cubic Bézier curves (each defined by four 3D control points) forming a single connected wire sculpture; an upper bound of three viewpoints is enforced due to increasing 3D wire-space conflict. The curves are optimised per-instance over 2000 iterations and can be rendered digitally (AR/VR) or physically fabricated.

## Method

DreamWire represents the 3D wire art as a collection of independent cubic 3D Bézier curves and proves that projecting a 3D Bézier curve onto a plane is exactly equivalent to a 2D Bézier curve whose control points are the projections of the original 3D control points, enabling the use of the differentiable 2D renderer DiffVG for 3D curve optimisation. The control points are optimised via a multi-view Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss that feeds each projected view through a latent diffusion model (or ControlNet for visual conditions) conditioned on the corresponding user prompt, following the DreamFusion paradigm. To ensure physical connectivity, a minimum spanning tree (MST) regularisation is added: wires are treated as graph vertices, pairwise endpoint distances form edge weights, and Prim's algorithm yields a spanning-tree cost that penalises disconnected or overly spread configurations. The final objective balances the multi-SDS aesthetic loss against the MST connectivity loss via a hyperparameter λ, and the entire pipeline is a per-instance optimisation (no MVWA training data required) that distills knowledge from the pretrained diffusion prior.

## Results

Under visual control (DINO similarity to target sketches), the baselines outperform DreamWire: ShadowArt achieves 79.62% (DINO-V2-Base) and 83.82% (DINO-V2-Giant), while DreamWire-V scores 69.08% and 72.76% respectively. Under text control (CLIP Score), DreamWire-T achieves 37.21%, surpassing ShadowArt (34.37%) and MVWA (34.81%) by roughly 2.4–2.8 points. Adding MST regularisation (λ) slightly reduces CLIP Score (36.52% for Ours-T-λ) and DINO similarity but improves structural connectivity of the 3D wire sculpture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two rule-based baselines: ShadowArt [22] (geometric optimisation of shadow hulls) and MVWA [14] (visual hull reconstruction via back-projection and 3D path-finding). On the visual-control setting (DINO similarity to user-provided line drawings), both baselines clearly outperform DreamWire: ShadowArt leads by ~10.5 points on DINO-V2-Base (79.62 vs. 69.08) and ~11 points on DINO-V2-Giant (83.82 vs. 72.76), with MVWA in between. The authors attribute this gap to their optimisation-based approach suffering from under/overfitting in different regions, whereas the search-based traditional methods can more precisely match fixed layout constraints. However, on the text-control setting (CLIP Score), DreamWire-T achieves 37.21%, beating ShadowArt (34.37%) by 2.84 points and MVWA (34.81%) by 2.40 points — a capability neither baseline possesses, as both require explicit 2D line-drawing inputs and cannot generate arbitrary visual content from text. The MST regularisation variant (Ours-T-λ) trades ~0.7 CLIP points for shorter inter-wire connections, improving physical manufacturability at a small aesthetic cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamFusion/SDS optimisation paradigm from NeRF-based 3D representation to a discrete set of 3D Bézier curves, leveraging the elegant (and proven) equivalence between projecting a 3D Bézier curve and computing a 2D Bézier from projected control points to reuse the DiffVG differentiable renderer. It builds on VectorFusion and SketchDreamer for the vector-graphics generation pipeline and multi-conditional ControlNet guidance, but departs fundamentally from prior MVWA methods (Hsiao et al. 2018; Mitra & Pauly 2009), which relied on rule-based voxel carving and 3D path-finding, by replacing those hand-crafted geometric heuristics with a learned generative prior. The Prim's-algorithm-based MST regularisation for enforcing wire connectivity is a novel loss design not present in any of the cited predecessors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that diffusion-model priors can be repurposed to optimise an entirely new 3D parameterisation (sparse Bézier curves) for a task with essentially zero training data, opening a template for applying generative priors to other data-scarce 3D design problems. Key limitations include a hard cap of three viewpoints (due to 3D wire-space conflict), underfitting/overfitting of Bézier parameters relative to the search-based baselines on visual control, and poor handling of non-smooth polyline targets. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond three views, whether richer line representations (e.g., variable-width strokes, splines of higher degree) could close the gap with traditional methods on layout-constrained inputs, and how to ensure physical stability of the resulting sculpture beyond the MST heuristic.
