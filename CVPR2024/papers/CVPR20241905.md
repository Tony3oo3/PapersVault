## Problem

Existing learning-based isosurface extraction methods, particularly Neural Dual Contouring (NDC), rely on supervised training against QEF-minimized vertex positions from axiomatic Dual Contouring. This introduces biases from the ill-defined QEF minimization, makes the methods sensitive to imperfect or noisy SDF inputs, and prevents end-to-end joint optimization of implicit functions and meshes. The paper asks whether mesh vertex prediction can be learned entirely from the input SDF grid without any explicit mesh ground truth.

## Input / Output

The SDC network takes a signed distance function discretized on a regular 3D grid (64³ or 128³) and outputs one vertex position per grid cell, from which a quad mesh is assembled using standard Dual Contouring connectivity rules (sign changes on grid edges). For the single-view reconstruction variant, the input is an RGB image encoded by a ResNet18 backbone, and the pipeline jointly produces both a learned SDF (via a DeepSDF MLP) and the extracted mesh in a single feed-forward pass. The DIN regularizer additionally consumes a pre-trained SDC with frozen weights to compute mesh-based distance penalties during implicit network training.

## Method

SDC replaces the QEF-based vertex supervision in Neural Dual Contouring with two self-supervised losses computed solely from the input SDF grid and the predicted mesh. The distance loss (L_D) minimizes the squared difference between |SDF| values at grid nodes and the point-to-face distance from those nodes to the reconstructed mesh, while the normal consistency loss (L_N) aligns mesh face normals with SDF gradient normals interpolated at edge zero-crossings, ensuring first-order agreement. A 6-layer 3D CNN predicts vertex displacements per cell, and Gaussian noise augmentation (σ = e/3) during training improves robustness to sign-flipping perturbations. The same distance-consistency principle is inverted to regularize Deep Implicit Networks: a frozen SDC extracts a mesh from the DIN's predicted SDF grid, and the DIN is penalized for SDF values that deviate from the resulting mesh's distance function (L_SDR). For single-view reconstruction, the image encoder, DIN, and SDC are jointly fine-tuned end-to-end, minimizing a combined objective of SDF supervision, the DIN regularizer, and the SDC distance loss.

## Results

On the ABC dataset with analytical SDF input, SDC achieves the best Chamfer Distance (3.3×10⁻³), Normal Consistency (94.9%), Edge Chamfer Distance (3.2×10⁻³), and Level-Set Discrepancy (11.3×10⁻³) while producing only 9.7 self-intersections per mesh (vs. 43.1 for NDC and 96.3 for DC). On the unseen Thingi10K domain, SDC maintains its lead with CD=3.6, NC=68.0%, ECD=10.9, and LSD-P=11.6, outperforming all baselines. As a DIN regularizer on ShapeNet, SDC improves 3D IoU from 83.9% (CSDF baseline) to 86.0% and reduces LSD-A from 3.1 to 2.7. For single-view reconstruction, the end-to-end SDC pipeline achieves CD=9.1, SSIM=88.5%, and LSD-A=7.7, surpassing MeshSDF (CD=11.3, SSIM=84.1%) without test-time optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDC outperforms all five meshing baselines (MC, NMC, DC, NDC, FlexiCubes) on every metric across both ABC and Thingi10K, with the largest gains in self-intersection count: SDC produces 9.7 SI on ABC vs. 43.1 (NDC) and 96.3 (DC), and 15.7 on Thingi10K vs. 70.4 (NDC) and 105.6 (DC). MC and FlexiCubes produce zero self-intersections but sacrifice reconstruction quality (CD 4.7/4.5 vs. 3.3 for SDC on ABC), as their vertex placement is restricted to grid edges. NDC, the closest supervised competitor, is beaten by SDC on CD (3.3 vs. 3.6), NC (94.9% vs. 94.3%), ECD (3.2 vs. 3.5), and SI (9.7 vs. 43.1) on ABC. For DIN regularization (Table 2), SDC as a regularizer beats CSDF (CD 2.6 vs. 3.0, 3D IoU 86.0% vs. 83.9%), Eikonal constraint (CD 2.6 vs. 3.2), and NDC-based regularization (CD 2.6 vs. 2.9, 3D IoU 86.0% vs. 84.8%). In single-view reconstruction (Table 3), the end-to-end SDC pipeline outperforms MeshSDF (CD 9.1 vs. 11.3, SSIM 88.5% vs. 84.1%) and DISN (CD 9.1 vs. 13.0, SSIM 88.5% vs. 82.0%) without requiring test-time optimization, and also beats its own ablation WoBW (CD 9.1 vs. 9.9, SSIM 88.5% vs. 86.4%), confirming the benefit of joint training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neural Dual Contouring (NDC) by replacing its supervised QEF-based vertex training with two geometrically motivated self-supervised losses (distance consistency and normal alignment) that depend only on the input SDF grid. It also departs from the standard decoupled pipeline where DINs are trained independently and meshing is applied a posteriori, by introducing a mesh-based regularizer that enforces consistency between the learned SDF and the extracted mesh during DIN training. The single-view reconstruction pipeline follows MeshSDF's architecture but replaces test-time optimization with a feed-forward SDC pass, enabling joint end-to-end training of the implicit representation and the mesh.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for axiomatically computed ground-truth meshes, SDC removes a source of bias and enables end-to-end optimization of both SDF and mesh, which prior supervised meshing methods could not support. The DIN regularizer is a broadly applicable technique that could improve any implicit-surface learning pipeline by ensuring the learned distance field is geometrically coherent with its extracted mesh. Limitations include the fixed regular grid (capping resolution) and the absence of theoretical guarantees for manifoldness or self-intersection-free output; the authors identify adaptive grids and strict manifoldness conditions as natural next steps.
