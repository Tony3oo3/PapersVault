## Problem

State-of-the-art reference-based counting methods like CounTR require manually annotated density maps or bounding boxes for training, which are expensive to produce for dozens of objects per image. This paper asks whether the strong visual representations learned by self-supervised methods (e.g., DINO) are already sufficient to learn counting without any human annotation whatsoever.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a 224×224 RGB image and 1–3 exemplar crops (each resized to 64×64) that specify which object type to count. It outputs a spatially dense prediction map at the input resolution; the final count is obtained by summing all pixel values in this map. During training, the exemplars are simply crops taken from the synthetic Self-Collage itself, and the supervision signal is a Gaussian-blob density map constructed from known paste locations—no manual labels are involved at any stage.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-supervised training pipeline built on 'Self-Collages': objects from ImageNet-1k are segmented (via unsupervised spectral cluster voting) and pasted onto SUN397 backgrounds, with k-means clustering of DINO CLS embeddings (K=10,000) providing unsupervised object categories. The UnCounTR architecture reuses a frozen DINO ViT-B/16 as both the image encoder and exemplar encoder (placing both in the same feature space), followed by a transformer-decoder feature interaction module that enriches image features with exemplar information, and a 4-convolutional-block decoder that upsamples to a density map. Training uses MSE loss against Gaussian pseudo-labels, with the key finding that keeping the entire DINO backbone frozen outperforms fine-tuning, as it prevents the model from overfitting to collage artefacts. The model is trained on only 3–19 target objects per image yet generalizes to the much higher counts (up to 3701) in FSC-147, matching or exceeding supervised CounTR on low-count splits.

## Results

On FSC-147, UnCounTR achieves MAE 5.60 / RMSE 10.13 / τ 0.27 on the low split (8–16 objects), MAE 9.48 / RMSE 12.73 / τ 0.34 on the medium split (17–40 objects), and MAE 67.17 / RMSE 189.76 / τ 0.26 on the high split (41–3701 objects), all without any manual annotations. On the full FSC-147 test set, UnCounTR reports MAE 35.77 and RMSE 130.34. On the MSO dataset, UnCounTR achieves MAE 1.07 and RMSE 2.32, outperforming the supervised CounTR (MAE 2.34, RMSE 8.12). On CARPK, UnCounTR reports MAE 30.35 and RMSE 35.67, compared to CounTR's MAE 19.62 and RMSE 29.70.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnCounTR outperforms both FasterRCNN and DETR on MAE across all three FSC-147 splits (e.g., 5.60 vs. 7.06/6.92 on low; 9.48 vs. 19.87/19.33 on medium; 67.17 vs. 109.12/109.34 on high), despite being trained entirely without annotations while the detectors are supervised. However, DETR achieves lower RMSE on the low split (8.20 vs. 10.13), and FasterRCNN is slightly better on low-split RMSE (8.46 vs. 10.13). Against the supervised CounTR, UnCounTR dramatically outperforms on the low split in RMSE (10.13 vs. 48.11) and MAE (5.60 vs. 6.58), but CounTR retains a clear advantage on the medium (MAE 4.48 vs. 9.48) and high (MAE 20.50 vs. 67.17) splits, reflecting UnCounTR's limited training range of 3–19 objects. On MSO, UnCounTR beats CounTR by a large margin (MAE 1.07 vs. 2.34; RMSE 2.32 vs. 8.12). On CARPK, CounTR still leads (MAE 19.62 vs. 30.35), though UnCounTR's cross-dataset degradation is smaller (15% improvement over its FSC-147 score vs. CounTR's near-doubling of MAE).

## Relation to Prior Work

UnCounTR builds directly on the CounTR architecture (transformer decoder with learnable tokens for density-map regression) and the DINO self-supervised ViT backbone, but departs fundamentally from the supervised training paradigm by replacing all manual annotations with synthetic 'Self-Collages' constructed via copy-paste of DINO-clustered objects onto scene backgrounds. The data generation strategy repurposes the copy-paste augmentation technique previously used only to enrich annotated training sets [2, 65] into the sole source of supervision, while the unsupervised segmentation from spectral cluster voting [51] replaces manual object masks. This positions the work at the intersection of self-supervised representation learning (DINO, BYOL) and reference-based counting, showing that the two can be composed into a fully annotation-free pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that reference-based object counting can be learned end-to-end without any human annotations, opening a path toward unsupervised visual understanding for tasks beyond classification and segmentation. The practical limitation is significant: the model is trained on at most 19–20 objects per image yet must generalize to counts up to 3731, resulting in a large MAE gap (35.77 vs. 11.95 for CounTR) on the full FSC-147 test set, though it matches or exceeds supervised methods on low-count splits. Open questions include whether scaling the collage construction to higher counts, using DINOv2, or refining high-count predictions (as in UnCounTRv2) can close this gap, and whether the approach extends to more complex scene understanding tasks like scene graphs or unsupervised instance segmentation.
