## Problem

Existing VLMs like CLIP perform poorly in fine-grained domains because they cannot effectively encode visual attributes beyond recognizing category names, and simply appending LLM-generated attribute descriptions at test time yields negligible or even negative gains (e.g., CUB: 50.5% → 50.7%). The core gap is the absence of large-scale, aligned image-text datasets in fine-grained domains, making it impossible to train VLMs to leverage the rich attribute knowledge that LLMs can generate for these categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images paired with category-level text descriptions (visual attributes, habitat, geographic location, and taxonomy) generated by prompting GPT-4, where images and texts are grouped by class without explicit image-to-text correspondences (bag-level supervision). At test time, the model receives an unseen image and a set of LLM-generated descriptions per candidate class, producing a class label by averaging similarity scores across all texts for each class. The unusual aspect is that training pairs are stochastically sampled within categories, meaning a given image may be paired with a text describing an attribute not visible in that particular image.

## Method

The core contribution is a category-level contrastive loss that modifies CLIP's standard batch-level objective to handle multiple image-text pairs from the same category within a single batch, aggregating similarity across all same-category pairs rather than treating each pair as an independent positive-negative example. During training, images and texts within each category are stochastically paired, and the model is fine-tuned with this loss while maintaining EMA (exponential moving average) encoders to prevent overfitting on small datasets. The key insight is that random pairing within categories is more robust than biasing toward similar image-text pairs or using visibility annotations, and the model learns correct attribute associations despite the noise. At inference, the predicted class is determined by averaging the softmax-normalized similarity scores across all LLM-generated texts for each candidate class, which proves more robust than geometric mean alternatives.

## Results

On fine-grained domains using ViT-B/32, CLIP^FT+A achieves 53.34% on CUB-200, 71.63% on Stanford Cars, 36.41% on FGVC Aircrafts, 77.05% on Flowers-102, and 93.71% on Food-101, yielding an average improvement of 4–5% over pre-trained CLIP across 12 datasets. On the challenging NeWT benchmark (50 binary tasks), the method achieves 61.90 MAP versus CLIP's 60.25, a 4.1% relative error reduction. When trained on external datasets (iNaturalist, NABirds) and tested on CUB, the method still improves over CLIP by 3–5%, demonstrating cross-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pre-trained CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), the proposed CLIP^FT+A consistently wins across all five fine-grained datasets in Table 1, with the largest gain on FGVC Aircrafts (+7.14 points, 29.27→36.41) and substantial gains on Flowers-102 (+5.27) and Food-101 (+5.39). Against CLIP-A-self (Maniparambil et al., ICCV 2023), which trains a GPT-4-guided adapter with only 16 images per class using ViT-B/16, the proposed method outperforms on every dataset in Table 2: e.g., 75.78 vs. 72.90 on Stanford Cars, 40.75 vs. 33.00 on FGVC Aircrafts, and 81.82 vs. 70.50 on EuroSAT. The paper also shows that simply appending LLM-generated attributes to CLIP at test time (CLIP+A) yields negligible or even negative gains on fine-grained domains (e.g., Stanford Cars drops from 69.72 to 69.47), confirming that fine-tuning with the category-level contrastive loss is essential for the model to learn meaningful image–attribute associations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's contrastive image-text pretraining objective, modifying it into a category-level loss to handle 'bag-level' supervision where images and LLM-generated descriptions are only coarsely aligned within a class. It departs from prior prompt-tuning work (CoOp, CoCoOp) that learns context vectors, and from LLM-augmented zero-shot methods (Menon et al., CuPL, Maniparambil et al.) that use GPT descriptions only at test-time or train lightweight adapters, by instead fine-tuning both CLIP encoders end-to-end with a stochastic image-text pairing strategy. The closest prior work, GIST and I2MVFormer, also generate category-level texts via LLMs but rely on CLIP-similarity-based pairing or human annotations, whereas this paper shows that random pairing within a category is more robust and requires no human effort.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the method requires only $1–5 in GPT-4 API costs and under 5 minutes of training on small datasets, making LLM-generated fine-grained descriptions a scalable substitute for expensive human annotation. The finding that habitat and geographic priors are as effective as visual appearance cues for natural-domain classification opens a new axis of information for zero-shot recognition that prior work largely ignored. Key limitations include reliance on LLM factual accuracy (96% on spot checks but unverified at scale), the need for domain-specific training images, and the fact that the bag-level supervision is a workaround rather than a principled solution to the image-text alignment problem. Open questions include whether the approach extends to domains where LLMs lack reliable knowledge, and whether the stochastic pairing strategy generalizes to truly unstructured multi-attribute settings.
