## Problem

Existing anti-customization methods for diffusion models (AdvDM, Anti-DreamBooth) randomly sample timesteps during adversarial noise optimization, but gradients become near-zero at large timesteps where the noisy latent is close to pure Gaussian noise, rendering a large fraction of optimization steps ineffective. These methods also ignore the internal frequency-domain perception of the diffusion model at different timesteps and the distinct roles of features at different U-Net decoder layers, leading to suboptimal identity disruption.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of at least 15 clear face portrait images per individual (from CelebA-HQ or VGGFace2) and produces a single perturbed face image with imperceptible adversarial noise constrained to an L∞ budget of η=16/255. The perturbed image is then fed as training data into DreamBooth fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion v2.1; success is measured by whether the customized model fails to reproduce the user's identity (via RetinaFace detection failure and ArcFace cosine similarity) across multiple unseen text prompts.

## Method

SimAC introduces two targeted improvements integrated into Anti-DreamBooth's alternating surrogate-perturbation framework. First, an adaptive greedy time interval selection algorithm iteratively samples five timesteps, computes gradient magnitudes, and prunes the interval around the minimum-gradient timestep while retaining the maximum-gradient region, narrowing the search to an optimal window of length ≤100 where adversarial optimization is effective (supported by a formal theorem showing the expected gradient magnitude increases after pruning). Second, a feature interference loss is added to the standard diffusion denoising objective: based on PCA analysis revealing that deeper U-Net decoder residual blocks (layers 9–11) encode high-frequency texture information, the loss maximizes the L2 distance between intermediate features of the clean and perturbed images at these layers, ensuring the adversarial noise specifically disrupts the high-frequency components that carry identity. The combined objective L = L_cond + λL_f is optimized via PGD with step size 0.005 over 50 epochs.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, Anti-DB+SimAC achieves the lowest Identity Score Matching (ISM) and highest Face Detection Failure Rate (FDFR) across all four test prompts. The most dramatic gain appears on the prompt 'a dslr portrait of sks person', where FDFR rises from 86.80% (Anti-DB) to 96.87% and ISM drops from 0.19 to 0.12. On VGGFace2, similar improvements hold, with FDFR reaching 96.33% and ISM dropping to 0.11 on the same prompt. Image quality metrics (BRISQUE, SER-FIQ) remain comparable to baselines, indicating the protection does not excessively degrade the perturbed image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Anti-DB+SimAC consistently outperforms Anti-DB (its base framework) on both FDFR and ISM across all four prompts on CelebA-HQ. The largest FDFR gain is +10.07 points on 'a dslr portrait of sks person' (96.87 vs 86.80) and +49.04 points on 'sks person looking at the mirror' (91.90 vs 42.86), where the mirror prompt is particularly challenging for baselines. ISM drops from 0.19 to 0.12 on the DSLR prompt and from 0.22 to 0.12 on the mirror prompt, indicating substantially weaker identity leakage. PhotoGuard is the weakest baseline across all prompts, with FDFR values below 51% and ISM values generally higher than the other methods. AdvDM sits between PhotoGuard and Anti-DB, with FDFR ranging from 35–70% depending on the prompt. On VGGFace2, the same ranking holds: Anti-DB+SimAC achieves the best FDFR (up to 96.33%) and lowest ISM (0.09–0.31) across all prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anti-customization line initiated by AdvDM (Liang et al., ICML 2023) and Anti-DreamBooth (Le et al., ICCV 2023), both of which use adversarial noise optimized via the diffusion denoising loss to protect images from DreamBooth-style personalization. The key departure is a diagnostic analysis showing that prior methods waste optimization steps at large timesteps where gradients vanish, and that U-Net decoder layers encode progressively higher-frequency information. SimAC addresses both gaps with an adaptive greedy timestep selection algorithm and a feature interference loss on deep decoder layers, plugging into existing frameworks (primarily Anti-DreamBooth) rather than proposing a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain and gradient-magnitude analyses provide a useful diagnostic for anyone designing adversarial attacks against diffusion models, explaining why naive random timestep sampling is suboptimal. However, the practical impact is limited: the method is tested only on face images with SD v1.4/v2.1, black-box robustness degrades sharply under model mismatch (e.g., FDFR drops from 96.9% to 20.5% for one prompt when transferring from v2.1 to v1.4), and the approach is inherently tied to the specific architecture of Stable Diffusion's U-Net. Open questions include whether the timestep-selection and feature-interference principles generalize to other diffusion backbones (e.g., DiT-based models), non-face subjects, and stronger customization methods like multi-concept or style transfer.
