## Problem

Existing video-based music recommendation systems select tracks based solely on content compatibility (e.g., matching mood or genre to video theme) but cannot incorporate individual user preferences, adapt recommendations through dialogue, or explain their choices. This leaves users without a mechanism to refine suggestions iteratively or understand why a particular track was recommended, resulting in a less satisfying and opaque experience.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240834/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system operates in two dialogue turns: the first turn takes only a 120-second video clip (processed as twelve 10-second segments at 5 fps) and retrieves an initial candidate music from a pool of 2,000 tracks; the second turn additionally accepts the candidate music (10-second clip) and a free-form user text prompt expressing preferences (mood, genre, instruments, artist, etc.) to refine the recommendation from a test pool of 500 tracks. The output is a ranked list of music tracks plus a natural-language reasoning statement generated by the sentence generator, which at inference also receives the recommended track's title to ensure correct naming.

## Method

MuseChat comprises two modules. The Music Recommendation Module uses a novel MVT-Fusion architecture that encodes the candidate music with an AST encoder, the user prompt with a frozen CLIP text encoder, and video frames with a frozen CLIP image encoder (temporally averaged). Self-attention layers are applied to the music and text branches, followed by bidirectional cross-modal attention (audio-to-text and text-to-audio) to produce a fused music-text vector, which is then summed with the video vector to form a tri-modal representation. This is trained with a Contrastive Multiview Coding loss that pulls the tri-modal embedding toward the target music's AST cls-token embedding while pushing away other pool members. The Sentence Generator Module fine-tunes Vicuna-7B via LoRA (rank 32) on attention and output layers, projecting AST music embeddings through a linear layer into the text embedding space and training with next-token prediction on reasoning statements from the simulated dialogues.

## Results

On the MuseChat dataset (98,206 video-music-conversation quartets), MuseChat achieves R@1=32.79%, R@5=63.92%, R@10=76.53%, Median Rank=2, and Success Rate=40.49% for the two-turn conversational music retrieval task. For reasoning quality, MuseChat attains a BERTScore F1 of 0.9676 and a human-evaluated overall MOS of 4.46/5, substantially outperforming both the text-only Vicuna-7B (BERTScore 0.9453, MOS 3.07) and the music-embedding-only variant (BERTScore 0.9526, MOS 2.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuseChat outperforms all four external baselines on every retrieval metric. Against ViML [36] (the strongest prior method incorporating text prompts), MuseChat gains +10.18% on R@1 (32.79 vs. 22.61) and +9.97% on R@10 (76.53 vs. 66.56), demonstrating the benefit of jointly fusing candidate music, video, and user text rather than relying on a single text description. Compared to MVPt [42], which handles only video-to-music retrieval without user interaction, MuseChat improves R@1 by +12.08% (32.79 vs. 20.71) and achieves a Success Rate of 40.49% versus MVPt's 20.71% (equivalent to its R@1 since it cannot refine in a second turn). Against VM-NET [20], the improvement is dramatic: R@1 jumps from 5.31% to 32.79%. For the reasoning task, MuseChat (using both music title and embeddings) surpasses the frozen Vicuna-7B [58] baseline by +2.23 points on BERTScore F1 (0.9676 vs. 0.9453) and by +1.39 points on human-evaluated overall MOS (4.46 vs. 3.07), while also achieving far higher correctness scores (4.63 vs. 3.07) thanks to the music title input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—video-to-music retrieval (MVPt, ViML, VM-NET), conversational recommender systems, and multi-modal LLMs—into a unified dialogue-based system, none of which had previously been combined for the video-music domain. The MVT-Fusion module departs from prior two-tower retrieval architectures by introducing cross-modal attention between candidate music and user text before fusing with video, rather than treating modalities independently. The sentence generator repurposes Vicuna-7B with LoRA adapters and a music-embedding projection, extending the multi-modal LLM paradigm (MiniGPT-4, Video-LLaMA) to the specific task of music explanation grounded in audio features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 98,206-sample conversational dataset is the first of its kind for video-music recommendation and provides a benchmark for interactive, preference-aware retrieval that prior single-shot systems cannot address. The two-turn success rate (40.49% vs. 20.71% single-turn) quantifies the practical value of user feedback in narrowing recommendations, directly tackling the cold-start and preference-drift problems. Limitations include reliance on GPT-3.5 for synthetic dialogue generation (introducing potential distributional bias), a proprietary MVP retrieval model, and the two-turn constraint; the authors note that a fully integrated single-LLM system combining retrieval and reasoning remains an open direction.
