## Problem

3D point cloud datasets are individually small (e.g., ScanNet has only 1.6K scans) compared to 2D image datasets at million scale, limiting representation learning. Naively merging multiple 3D datasets for joint training causes negative transfer due to large domain gaps in sparsity, scene complexity, and data source (real vs. synthetic), degrading performance below single-dataset baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241050/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds from multiple heterogeneous datasets (indoor: ScanNet, S3DIS, Structured3D; outdoor: SemanticKITTI, nuScenes, Waymo) conditioned on a single learnable 256-dimensional domain prompt vector per dataset. It outputs per-point semantic labels for segmentation and, more broadly, generalizable scene-level representations transferable to instance segmentation, classification, and detection. The categorical alignment additionally consumes CLIP text embeddings of class names as a shared label-space anchor across datasets.

## Method

PPT introduces two mechanisms to enable multi-dataset synergistic training with a single shared-weight backbone. First, Prompt-driven Normalization (PDNorm) replaces standard normalization layers throughout the backbone with prompt-conditioned versions, where a learnable domain prompt c is linearly projected to produce dataset-specific scale γ(c) and shift β(c) parameters, analogous to adaptive instance normalization in style transfer. Second, Language-guided Categorical Alignment projects point representations into a unified space and aligns them with CLIP text embeddings of class names via InfoNCE loss, with negative samples restricted to each dataset's own category space to resolve label-space inconsistencies. The framework is backbone-agnostic (validated on SparseUNet and PTv3), supports both supervised and unsupervised pre-training, and employs zero-initialization of prompt parameters with a 0.1× learning rate scaling to stabilize early training.

## Results

On indoor semantic segmentation, PPT with SparseUNet achieves 76.4 mIoU on ScanNet Val (+4.2 over baseline) and 72.7 mIoU on S3DIS Area5 (+7.3), while with PTv3 it reaches 78.6 mIoU on ScanNet Val (+1.1). On outdoor benchmarks, PPT with SparseUNet improves SemanticKITTI Val mIoU from 63.8 to 71.4 (+7.6) and nuScenes Val from 73.3 to 78.6 (+5.3); with PTv3, it reaches 72.3 on SemanticKITTI Val (+1.5) and 81.2 on nuScenes Val (+0.8). The method also achieves 31.9 mIoU on ScanNet200 Val (+6.9 over SparseUNet) and 42.1 mAP on ScanNet instance segmentation (+4.7 over baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior SOTA segmentation methods, PPT with SparseUNet (f.t.) achieves 76.4 mIoU on ScanNet Val, surpassing StratifiedFormer (74.3), PTv2 (75.4), and PointNeXt (71.5) by 2.1, 1.0, and 4.9 points respectively. Among pre-training baselines on the same SparseUNet backbone, PPT outperforms MSC (75.5), CSC (73.8), and PointContrast (74.1) by 0.9, 2.6, and 2.3 points on ScanNet Val. With the stronger PTv3 backbone, PPT pushes ScanNet Val to 78.6 mIoU, exceeding PTv3 alone (77.5) and all prior methods. On outdoor SemanticKITTI, PPT+PTv3 reaches 72.3 Val mIoU, beating SphereFormer (67.8) by 4.5 points and Cylinder3D (64.3) by 8.0 points. On S3DIS Area5, PPT with SparseUNet achieves 72.7 mIoU, outperforming StratifiedFormer (72.0) and PTv3 (73.4 is higher, but PPT+PTv3 reaches 74.7). The consistent gains across both indoor and outdoor settings with a single shared-weight model demonstrate the effectiveness of multi-dataset synergistic training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt tuning paradigm from 2D (VPT, ECCV 2022) into the 3D pre-training setting, repurposing learnable prompts not to adapt a frozen backbone to a downstream task but to condition a jointly-trained model on dataset-specific domain contexts during training. It builds directly on Masked Scene Contrast (MSC, CVPR 2023) for the unsupervised pre-training component, adding multi-dataset synergistic training on top. The Prompt-driven Normalization design draws an explicit analogy to adaptive instance normalization in style transfer, while the Language-guided Categorical Alignment borrows the CLIP text-embedding + InfoNCE recipe from vision-language alignment. The paper positions itself against 2D multi-dataset segmentation work (e.g., label-shift handling, unified label spaces) but argues the 3D domain gap, sparsity, and long-tail nature make the problem substantially harder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies negative transfer in 3D multi-dataset training—a problem that silently undermines the straightforward 'just pool more data' strategy that 2D vision has relied on for scale. By showing that a single shared-weight model with domain prompts can outperform single-dataset training on every dataset simultaneously, it provides a practical recipe for scaling 3D representation learning without requiring a single massive annotated dataset. Key open questions remain: whether the framework extends to joint indoor+outdoor training, whether more advanced unsupervised objectives (beyond MSC) benefit equally, and whether multi-task training (segmentation + detection + classification) can be unified under the same prompt mechanism. The authors also note the current exploration is limited in scope and the prompt adapter design may be sub-optimal.
