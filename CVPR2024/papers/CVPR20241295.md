## Problem

Existing semantic NeRF methods treat semantic prediction as an additional per-pixel 'label rendering' task, ignoring contextual relationships between nearby pixels and objects. This leads to unclear boundary segmentation and abnormal predictions within objects, and prevents the radiance field and semantic field from benefiting each other during joint optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241295/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N=10 multi-view posed RGB images (640×480 for Replica, 320×240 for ScanNet) with known camera extrinsics as input. Rather than rendering discrete class labels, it produces continuous semantic embedding features in novel views that are fed into a U-Net-based perception head to yield semantic or instance segmentation maps, alongside RGB novel-view reconstructions. During training, unrendered regions of the semantic feature map are filled with corresponding regions from the novel-view 2D feature map to form a complete image-level input for the segmentation head.

## Method

GP-NeRF builds on GNT's coordinate-aligned feature field formulation, using a shared Field Aggregation Transformer to co-aggregate radiance and semantic-embedding fields from reference views, and a shared Ray Aggregation Transformer to perform joint volumetric rendering of both fields in novel views. The key architectural choice is rendering rich semantic features (not labels) that are decomposed into four multi-scale levels and processed by a U-Net decoder for context-aware segmentation. Two self-distillation losses are introduced: a 2D Semantic Distill Loss that aligns rendered features with novel-view teacher features (with a gradient block on the shared CNN encoder to prevent feature collapse), and a Depth-Guided Semantic Distill Loss that applies per-point similarity or anti-similarity constraints based on proximity to ground-truth depth, preserving geometric consistency of the semantic field.

## Results

On the generalized semantic segmentation task, GP-NeRF achieves 48.53 mIoU on Replica and 59.92 mIoU on ScanNet, outperforming the strongest baseline Semantic-Ray by 6.94% and 2.77% respectively. In the fine-tuning setting, GP-NeRF reaches 87.72 mIoU on Replica and 93.84 mIoU on ScanNet, surpassing Semantic-Ray_ft by 11.76% and Semantic-NeRF by 2.60%. For instance segmentation on Replica, GP-NeRF achieves 89.50 AP^0.75, exceeding DM-NeRF by 8.47%. On reconstruction quality (ScanNet), GP-NeRF attains 29.37 PSNR (generalized) and 29.60 PSNR (fine-tuned), matching or exceeding GNT and Semantic-Ray.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GP-NeRF consistently outperforms all five baselines across both datasets and both evaluation settings. Against Semantic-Ray [22] (the closest competitor), GP-NeRF gains 6.94% mIoU on Replica and 2.77% on ScanNet in the generalized setting, and 11.76% on Replica and 2.78% on ScanNet after fine-tuning. In the fine-tuning regime, GP-NeRF also surpasses the per-scene optimized Semantic-NeRF [54] by 2.60% mIoU on ScanNet, while Semantic-Ray_ft trails GP-NeRF by 12.66% on Replica. For instance segmentation on Replica, GP-NeRF (89.50 AP^0.75) beats DM-NeRF [40] (81.03) by 8.47%, and also outperforms 2D-only segmenters M-RCNN (75.68) and Swin-T (77.14). On reconstruction quality, GP-NeRF matches GNT [38] (29.37 vs 28.96 PSNR generalized; 29.60 vs 29.55 fine-tuned) while simultaneously achieving superior segmentation, demonstrating that the joint radiance-semantic optimization does not sacrifice rendering fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GP-NeRF builds directly on the generalized NeRF framework of GNT (Transformer-based cross-view feature aggregation) and NeuRay, extending them from pure radiance rendering to joint radiance + semantic-embedding field construction. It departs fundamentally from the dominant 'label rendering' paradigm of Semantic-NeRF, DM-NeRF, Panoptic-NeRF, and Semantic-Ray, which treat semantic prediction as an independent per-pixel regression task. Instead, GP-NeRF renders semantic-aware features into a 2D segmentation head (U-Net decoder) for context-aware perception, and introduces two self-distillation losses (2D Semantic Distill and Depth-Guided Semantic Distill) to prevent the network from 'cheating' the distillation objective by collapsing all point features to a single prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that jointly optimizing radiance and semantic fields through shared attention maps can simultaneously improve both reconstruction quality and segmentation accuracy, addressing a trade-off observed in prior work like NeRF-RPN. The identification of the 'cheating' failure mode (where the shared encoder learns less discriminative features to trivially satisfy distillation) and its resolution via gradient blocking is a practically useful insight for any feature-distillation setup in NeRF. However, evaluation is limited to indoor scenes (Replica, ScanNet) with a relatively simple U-Net decoder, and the depth-guided loss requires GT depth supervision, leaving open questions about scalability to outdoor/urban scenes, other perception tasks (detection, depth), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific Transformer aggregation architecture.
