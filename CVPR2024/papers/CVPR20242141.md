## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, Imagen, GLIDE) consistently produce unrealistic hands with incorrect finger counts, implausible poses, and poor hand-object interactions, despite billions of parameters and millions of training images. While parametric hand models (SMPL, MANO) encode rich geometric and topological priors useful for pose animation and hand-object interaction tasks, the steps to learn these representations from text prompts and map them into pixel space within a T2I pipeline remain open problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image containing a human with realistic hands. Internally, the pipeline generates SMPL-H parameters (51 joint rotations and 10 shape parameters) as an intermediate 3D representation, from which MANO hand parameters are extracted. The image generation is conditioned on three hand representations: 2D spatial joint locations (quantized into 1000 positional bins), 3D hand vertices (encoded via Basis Point Sets), and 6D joint rotations, all jointly embedded with the text.

## Method

HanDiffuser is a two-stage diffusion architecture. The first component, Text-to-Hand-Params (T2H), is a transformer encoder-only diffusion model (following MDM) that generates SMPL-H pose and shape parameters conditioned on CLIP text embeddings, trained on 450K text–SMPL-H pairs. The second component, Text-Guided Hand-Params-to-Image (T-H2I), fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1.4 with a novel Text+Hand encoder that extends CLIP ViT-L/14 by adding positional tokens for 2D hand joints and MLP branches for hand vertices (via BPS) and joint rotations, producing joint text-and-hand embeddings that condition the U-Net denoiser. The key design choice is generating full SMPL-H parametric models rather than skeletons, since SMPL-H encodes richer geometric/topological priors and is more robust to noise. The two components are trained independently and run end-to-end at inference, with camera parameters randomly sampled to ensure hand visibility.

## Results

HanDiffuser achieves the best scores across all five evaluation metrics: FID 13.918, KID 4.07×10⁻³, FID-H 27.550, KID-H 2.11×10⁻², and hand detection confidence 0.978. In user studies, HanDiffuser scores 3.51/5 on hand plausibility and 4.23/5 on prompt relevance, outperforming fine-tuned Stable Diffusion (2.74 and 3.83) and the 2D-joints-only variant (3.30 and 4.11). 55% of HanDiffuser's plausibility ratings are 'Good' or better, compared to 27% for fine-tuned Stable Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HanDiffuser outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion, it reduces FID from 29.005 to 13.918 (52% improvement) and FID-H from 34.372 to 27.550, while raising hand confidence from 0.887 to 0.978. Against fine-tuned Stable Diffusion (trained on the same human-centric data), HanDiffuser still leads by a wide margin (FID 13.918 vs. 20.056; FID-H 27.550 vs. 31.219). Against ControlNet (which uses ground-truth hand skeleton images as additional input at inference), HanDiffuser achieves lower FID (13.918 vs. 18.694) and higher hand confidence (0.978 vs. 0.969) while requiring only a text prompt. The authors note that FID-H and KID-H for Stable Diffusion variants are optimistic, since the hand detector fails to localize severely malformed hands, making the true gap even larger.

## Relation to Prior Work

HanDiffuser extends the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion by replacing the standard CLIP text encoder with a novel Text+Hand encoder that jointly embeds text, 2D spatial hand joints, 3D hand vertices (via BPS), and 6D joint rotations. The Text-to-Hand-Params component borrows the denoising-by-prediction formulation from MDM (motion diffusion) but applies it to SMPL-H body and MANO hand parameters rather than skeletal motion, and the overall two-stage design (generate 3D hand params → condition image synthesis) departs from both ControlNet-style approaches that require external skeleton inputs at inference and post-hoc inpainting methods like HandRefiner that depend on the quality of an initially generated hand. The work sits at the intersection of text-to-motion generation (which typically ignores hands) and text-to-image generation (which typically produces unrealistic hands), bridging the gap by using parametric hand models as an intermediate representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hand artifacts remain one of the most visible and persistent failure modes in production T2I systems, and HanDiffuser offers an end-to-end solution requiring only a text prompt at inference, unlike ControlNet-based alternatives that need additional skeleton or mesh inputs. The ablation confirming that all three hand representations (spatial joints, joint rotations, vertices) contribute meaningfully provides practical guidance for hand-conditioned generation pipelines. However, the method is limited to single-person scenes with relatively simple hand-object interactions, and the authors explicitly flag open challenges including multi-person hand-hand interactions, highly dexterous tasks (e.g., origami), and maintaining hand identity/shape consistency across multiple images of the same person. The reliance on estimated SMPL-H parameters from training data (with low-confidence estimates rejected) also means the approach inherits errors from the upstream pose estimation pipeline.
