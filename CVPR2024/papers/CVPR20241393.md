## Problem

Class-incremental learning (CIL) suffers from catastrophic forgetting largely because discriminative models require expanding a classification head for each new task, which biases the network toward the current task. While generative multi-modal models (GMMs) have shown strong image understanding capabilities, no prior work has formulated CIL within a generative framework for image classification, leaving open how to convert free-form generated text into discrete class predictions and how to adapt standard CIL benchmarks to a generative training paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241393/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, or variable resolution for ImageNet-R) as input and produces a class label as output. Rather than outputting a probability vector over classes, the model first generates a short text sentence in the template 'This is a photo of [CLS]', then encodes both the generated text and all seen class-name strings with a frozen CLIP text encoder, and selects the class whose embedding has the highest cosine similarity to the generated text's embedding.

## Method

The architecture follows MiniGPT-4/BLIP-2: a frozen EVA-CLIP ViT-g/14 image encoder feeds into a Q-Former, whose output is projected by a trainable linear layer into the token space of a frozen LLaMA-based auto-regressive decoder. During training on each incremental task, the model is fine-tuned (only the projection layer is updated, with 1–2 epochs at a very low learning rate) to generate concise label-format sentences rather than verbose image captions, using standard cross-entropy loss over the target text tokens. At inference, the decoder produces a predicted sentence, the content within '[CLS]' is extracted, and a CLIP text encoder computes cosine similarity between this generated text and the text embeddings of all previously seen class names; the nearest class is the prediction. This design eliminates the expanded classification head entirely, so the model's 'classifier' is the fixed text decoder plus the frozen text encoder, which structurally prevents the task-bias that drives forgetting in discriminative CIL.

## Results

On Tiny-ImageNet (conventional CIL), GMM achieves 84.23% average accuracy and 79.17% last-task accuracy over 20 tasks with exemplars, outperforming all discriminative pre-trained baselines (e.g., CODA-Prompt: 77.43% avg, 66.32% last). On ImageNet-R (10 tasks), GMM reaches 89.41% last-task accuracy with exemplars vs. 73.88% for CODA-Prompt. In few-shot CIL on mini-ImageNet, GMM achieves 75.18% accuracy after 8 incremental sessions with only 14.17% performance drop, surpassing CODA-Prompt (61.14%, PD 34.23) by over 14 points. On CIFAR-100 few-shot CIL, GMM attains 81.47% final accuracy with a PD of just 10.06, compared to 59.32% and PD 34.23 for CODA-Prompt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMM decisively outperforms all conventional CIL methods (EWC, LwF, iCaRL, EEIL, UCIR, PASS, DyTox) by 25–65 points on Tiny-ImageNet, confirming that generative multi-modal pre-training provides a far stronger starting point than training from scratch. Against discriminative pre-trained prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt), GMM is slightly behind in short-sequence settings (5 tasks, no exemplars) where ImageNet-21K pre-training overlaps heavily with the test data, but surpasses them by 5–15 points in longer sequences (10–20 tasks) where classifier bias accumulates. On ImageNet-R, GMM with exemplars achieves 89.41% vs. 73.88% (CODA-Prompt) and 72.92% (L2P), a 15.5-point gain. In few-shot CIL on mini-ImageNet, GMM exceeds CODA-Prompt by 14.04 points on the final task (75.18% vs. 61.14%) and reduces performance drop from 34.23 to 14.17; it also outperforms all few-shot-specific methods (TOPIC, CEC, F2M, MetaFSCIL, Entropy-reg) by 26+ points. On CIFAR-100 few-shot CIL, GMM achieves 81.47% final accuracy with PD 10.06, beating CODA-Prompt (59.32%, PD 34.23) by 22 points and the best few-shot method MetaFSCIL (49.97%, PD 24.53) by 31.5 points. The Linear Probe baseline (74.38% on 5-task Tiny-ImageNet) confirms that the gains come from the generative pipeline rather than the large ViT backbone alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the MiniGPT-4/BLIP-2 generative multi-modal architecture (frozen EVA-CLIP ViT-g/14 encoder + Q-Former + LLaMA decoder) for class-incremental learning, departing fundamentally from the discriminative paradigm that dominates CIL research. While VAG (Shao et al., 2023) formulated CIL as continual label generation, it was restricted to NLP; this work is the first to transfer that generative-label-generation idea to image classification. The key architectural departure from prior CIL methods (EWC, iCaRL, L2P, CODA-Prompt, etc.) is the elimination of the expanded classifier head entirely, replacing it with a fixed text encoder that matches generated text against class names via cosine similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the root cause of catastrophic forgetting in CIL—the classifier's bias toward the current task due to head expansion—can be circumvented by simply not having a classifier head, which is a conceptually clean insight. However, the method's advantage is setting-dependent: it underperforms discriminative pre-trained backbones in short-sequence and early-task settings where ImageNet-21K overlap provides a shortcut, and the authors themselves note the design is 'embarrassingly simple' with significant room for advancement. Open questions include whether integrating continual-learning objectives into the GMM pre-training process could further reduce training costs, and whether this generative approach extends to more complex downstream tasks beyond classification.
