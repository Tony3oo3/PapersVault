## Problem

In vision-language pre-training with hard negative sampling (especially GRIT sampling), unpaired but semantically valid image-text combinations are treated as negatives, creating false negatives that can reach 16–23% of all in-batch negatives. This confuses the contrastive and matching losses, degrading downstream performance, yet the issue has been largely overlooked compared to the more studied problem of false positives from noisy captions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240953/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4M unique images (5M image-text pairs from MSCOCO, Visual Genome, Conceptual Captions, and SBU Captions) through a 12-layer ViT image encoder and a 6-layer BERT-based text encoder. During training, the Connection Discriminator (Con-D) receives hard-negative candidates selected by GRIT sampling and outputs a match probability per candidate; the training model then produces ITC cosine similarities over [CLS] tokens, ITM binary match predictions, and MLM token predictions. The pre-trained model is fine-tuned for image-text retrieval (COCO, Flickr30K), VQA, and NLVR2.

## Method

MAFA introduces two complementary components built on GRIT sampling. First, Efficient Connection Mining (ECM) uses a separately pre-trained Connection Discriminator to inspect the hardest in-batch negatives: if Con-D assigns a match probability above 0.8, the pair is relabeled as a positive for ITC and ITM losses and additionally fed into MLM, effectively converting false negatives into new positive connections without extra forward passes on the training model. Second, Smoothed ITC (S-ITC) applies label smoothing to the contrastive loss, mixing one-hot targets with a uniform distribution so that all negatives receive non-negligible soft labels—critical under GRIT sampling where most negatives are likely false negatives, and where prior soft-labeling approaches (momentum distillation, consistency loss) fail to distribute mass broadly enough. The combined objective is L = L_S-ITC^ECM + L_MLM^ECM + L_ITM^ECM, and the paper shows that converting false negatives into positives outperforms merely discarding them, and that managing false negatives yields larger gains than removing false positives via caption refinement.

## Results

On the 4M-Noisy dataset, MAFA achieves 78.0/61.2 COCO TR/IR R@1, 96.1/84.9 Flickr TR/IR R@1, 82.52 NLVR2 dev, and 75.75 VQA test-standard, outperforming GRIT-VLP by +1.6/+1.4 on COCO R@1 and +1.1 on NLVR2 dev. When trained on the 4M-Clean dataset, MAFA further improves to 79.4/61.6 COCO TR/IR R@1 and 82.66 NLVR2 dev. Notably, MAFA trained on only 4M images surpasses ALBEF trained on 14M images on COCO retrieval (78.0 vs 77.6 TR R@1, 61.2 vs 60.7 IR R@1) and Flickr TR R@1 (96.1 vs 95.9), while remaining competitive with BLIP (14M) on NLVR2 (82.52 vs 82.67 dev).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAFA (4M) decisively outperforms its direct predecessor GRIT-VLP (4M) across all metrics: +1.6 COCO TR R@1 (78.0 vs 76.6), +1.4 COCO IR R@1 (61.2 vs 59.6), +1.1 Flickr IR R@1 (84.9 vs 82.9), and +1.12 NLVR2 dev (82.52 vs 81.40). Against ALBEF (4M), MAFA gains +4.9 COCO TR R@1 and +4.4 COCO IR R@1, confirming that managing false negatives is far more impactful than the original ALBEF design. MAFA (4M) also surpasses BLIP (4M-Clean) on every retrieval and reasoning metric (e.g., 78.0 vs 75.5 COCO TR R@1, 82.52 vs 79.70 NLVR2 dev), suggesting that addressing false negatives outweighs the benefit of removing false positives via caption refinement. At the 14M scale, MAFA (4M) beats ALBEF (14M) on COCO retrieval (78.0 vs 77.6 TR, 61.2 vs 60.7 IR) and Flickr TR (96.1 vs 95.9) but trails on Flickr IR (84.9 vs 85.6) and VQA (75.75 vs 76.04). MAFA (4M) remains below BLIP (14M) on most metrics (e.g., 78.0 vs 80.6 COCO TR R@1, 75.75 vs 77.62 VQA), though the gap narrows substantially on NLVR2 (82.52 vs 82.67 dev). In the BLIP-2 compatibility experiment, MAFA improves zero-shot VQA by +4.2 on VQAv2 (50.8 vs 46.6) and +5.2 on OK-VQA (29.0 vs 23.8), while BLIP-2+GRIT without MAFA collapses (65.9 COCO TR R@1 vs 82.6 baseline), underscoring that false negatives are especially harmful in high-capacity models.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAFA builds directly on GRIT-VLP's grouped mini-batch sampling strategy and ALBEF's ITC+ITM+MLM training paradigm, but identifies a critical blind spot in this lineage: the hard negatives mined by GRIT sampling are frequently false negatives due to the many-to-many correspondence of image-text pairs, a problem the paper argues has been overlooked. It departs from the BLIP family's focus on false positives (noisy captions) by introducing two complementary mechanisms—Efficient Connection Mining (a pre-trained discriminator that converts false negatives into additional positives) and Smoothed ITC (label smoothing applied to the contrastive loss)—that specifically target the false-negative problem. The method is positioned as orthogonal to and potentially more impactful than false-positive mitigation, and its compatibility with BLIP-2 demonstrates generality across the BLIP model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that managing false negatives can outweigh the impact of removing false positives challenges the field's prevailing emphasis on caption cleaning and suggests that the many-to-many structure of web-crawled datasets is an underexploited source of positive signal. The dramatic failure of BLIP-2+GRIT without false-negative management (TR R@1 dropping from 82.6 to 65.9) highlights a practical risk for practitioners adopting hard negative mining at scale. Open questions include whether the Connection Discriminator approach scales to much larger datasets (e.g., 129M+), whether the threshold-based conversion of false negatives to positives remains reliable as model capacity grows, and whether the benefits persist in fully generative (stage-2) settings beyond the limited zero-shot VQA evaluation provided.
