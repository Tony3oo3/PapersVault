## Problem

Existing unsupervised visual grounding methods, particularly Pseudo-Q, rely on 2D pixel coordinates to infer spatial relationships, which leads to ambiguous or incorrect descriptions (e.g., confusing front/rear with left/right). Additionally, they depend on manually designed prompt templates to organize object information into queries, constraining the diversity and comprehensiveness of generated language expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241813/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only unlabeled RGB images as input—no queries, bounding boxes, or any other annotations are required. Internally, it leverages GRiT (pre-trained on Visual Genome) for object detection and description, a depth estimation model for 3D spatial reasoning, and TextBlob for syntactic parsing. The output is a set of pseudo box-query pairs (up to 48 queries sampled per image from 6 selected objects) used to train a TransVG grounding model end-to-end.

## Method

Omni-Q comprises three modules: (1) an Object Perception Module that uses GRiT to extract bounding boxes and natural-language descriptions, then applies TextBlob syntactic parsing to decompose descriptions into noun phrases, adjectives, colors, and clothing attributes, with a non-duplication filter to ensure discriminative expressions; (2) a 3D Spatial Relation Module that uses a depth estimation model to classify objects into foreground/background planes, then combines horizontal, vertical, and depth relationships to produce both absolute positions (image-region descriptors) and pairwise relative positions (e.g., 'in front of', 'to the left of'); (3) a Spatial Graph Module that constructs a directed graph where nodes hold multiple description candidates per object and edges encode spatial relationships, then generates diverse queries via random walks of up to N nodes—eliminating the need for rigid prompt templates while enabling flexible, multi-object compositional descriptions.

## Results

On the three RefCOCO variants, Omni-Q achieves top-1 accuracy of 67.39/71.64/61.94 (val/testA/testB) on RefCOCO, 46.22/51.71/38.60 on RefCOCO+, and 65.99/63.62/62.36 (val-g/val-u/test-u) on RefCOCOg, surpassing the prior unsupervised SOTA Pseudo-Q by up to 17.37% and the best weakly-supervised method DTWREG by up to 22.75%. On ReferItGame and Flickr30K Entities, Omni-Q reaches 52.91% and 65.23% top-1 accuracy respectively, outperforming all unsupervised and weakly-supervised baselines. Notably, on RefCOCOg Omni-Q (65.99%) nearly matches the fully supervised TransVG (66.56%), and the method can replace ~60% of ground-truth annotations without performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-Q decisively outperforms the primary unsupervised baseline Pseudo-Q across all five datasets, with the largest gains on RefCOCOg (+16.17% val-g) and RefCOCO (+13.39% testA), confirming that 3D spatial relations and graph-based query generation address Pseudo-Q's core weaknesses. Against the strongest weakly-supervised method DTWREG, Omni-Q wins by a wide margin on RefCOCOg (65.99 vs 43.24, +22.75%) and RefCOCO (67.39 vs 39.21, +28.18% val), and is competitive on RefCOCO+ testB (38.60 vs 38.08). On RefCOCOg, Omni-Q nearly matches the fully supervised TransVG (65.99 vs 66.56 val-g), a remarkable result for an unsupervised method, though it still trails TransVG on RefCOCO (67.39 vs 80.32) and RefCOCO+ (46.22 vs 63.50). On ReferItGame, Omni-Q (52.91%) surpasses all weakly-supervised baselines (best: Wang et al. [34] at 38.39%) and Pseudo-Q (43.32%) but remains well below fully supervised TransVG (69.76%). On Flickr30K Entities, Omni-Q (65.23%) beats Pseudo-Q (60.41%) and all weakly-supervised methods (best: Liu et al. [25] at 59.27%) but lags TransVG (78.47%). The generalization study (Table 4) further shows Omni-Q trained on ReferItGame queries transfers better to RefCOCO series than Pseudo-Q, with gains of +8.75 to +12.22% over Pseudo-Q's cross-dataset performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Omni-Q builds directly on Pseudo-Q (Jiang et al., CVPR 2022), the most representative unsupervised visual grounding method, which generates pseudo queries from unlabeled images using a detector, attribute classifier, and fixed prompt templates. The paper departs from Pseudo-Q along two axes: it replaces 2D-only spatial heuristics with a 3D spatial relation module that incorporates depth estimation to resolve front/rear ambiguities, and it substitutes manually designed prompt templates with a spatial graph structure that enables flexible, diverse query generation via random walks. The object perception module also shifts from separate detector-plus-attribute-classifier pipelines to a unified object understanding model (GRiT), while the downstream training backbone (TransVG) is shared with Pseudo-Q for fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the claim that Omni-Q can replace approximately 60% of ground-truth annotations while matching fully supervised performance, which would substantially reduce labeling costs for visual grounding datasets. The 3D spatial relation formulation addresses a genuine failure mode of prior unsupervised methods (confusing depth ordering with 2D left/right), and the spatial graph eliminates brittle prompt engineering. However, the paper acknowledges that incorrect object expressions from the unsupervised pipeline remain a limitation, and the spatial graph module contributes only 0.87% in ablation, suggesting its practical impact is modest relative to the 3D spatial component. Open questions include whether the depth-threshold heuristic generalizes to complex scenes and how to filter low-quality pseudo queries at scale.
