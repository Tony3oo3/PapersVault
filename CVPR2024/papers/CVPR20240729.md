## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models cannot replicate a specific motion from a reference video while generating diverse visual variations in new contexts. Naive extensions of image customization methods entangle appearance and motion, and existing video-to-video approaches either require auxiliary depth maps or fine-tune all attention layers, making them computationally expensive and prone to preserving the original subject's shape rather than transferring only the motion pattern.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 8-frame video clip (at low resolution) depicting the target motion and a text prompt describing the desired new context (e.g., 'A chicken is walking in a city'). During training, the prompt is deliberately simplified to an appearance-invariant form (e.g., 'A bird is flying' instead of 'A bird is flying above a lake in the forest') to decouple motion from background. The output is a 29-frame video at 576×320 resolution, produced via a cascaded pipeline: low-resolution keyframes from the fine-tuned U-Net, followed by temporal interpolation and spatial super-resolution. No depth maps or additional structural guidance are required at either training or inference time.

## Method

VMC fine-tunes only the temporal attention layers within the key-frame generation U-Net of the Show-1 cascaded video diffusion model, leaving spatial self-attention, cross-attention, frame interpolation, and super-resolution modules frozen. The core training signal is a novel motion distillation objective that defines motion vectors as residual differences between consecutive noisy latent frames (δv_t^n = v_t^{n+c} − v_t^n), then aligns the ground-truth motion vector with its Tweedie-denoised estimate by maximizing cosine similarity between the corresponding epsilon residuals (δε_t^n vs. δε_θ,t^n). This residual-based formulation preserves low-frequency motion trajectories while suppressing high-frequency motion-unrelated noise. Combined with appearance-invariant prompts that strip background descriptors from the text conditioning, the approach isolates motion information and enables one-shot adaptation in under 5 minutes on 15 GB VRAM. At inference, DDIM inversion of the input video seeds the fine-tuned keyframe generator, and the unmodified interpolation and super-resolution stages extend the result to the full 29-frame output.

## Results

On the 24-video evaluation set, VMC achieves the best CLIP text alignment (0.801) and temporal consistency (0.959), narrowly surpassing VideoComposer (0.798, 0.958). The most significant gains appear in the user study: VMC scores 4.42/5 on motion preservation and 4.54/5 on appearance diversity, compared to the best baselines of 3.50 (Tune-A-Video) and 3.43 (VideoComposer) respectively. User-rated text alignment and temporal consistency also show large margins, with VMC at 4.56 and 4.57 versus the next-best baselines at 2.96 and 3.03.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VMC outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor on CLIP-based automatic metrics is VideoComposer (0.798 vs. 0.801 text alignment; 0.958 vs. 0.959 temporal consistency), but the gap widens dramatically in the user study, where VMC leads by roughly 1 point on motion preservation (4.42 vs. 3.45) and appearance diversity (4.54 vs. 3.43). Tune-A-Video, which fine-tunes self-, cross-, and temporal attention layers, achieves the highest baseline motion preservation score (3.50) yet still trails VMC by nearly a full point, and it has the lowest CLIP text alignment (0.758). Gen-1 sits in the middle on CLIP metrics but lags on user-rated appearance diversity (3.17). Control-A-Video is consistently the weakest baseline across all six metrics, scoring below 2.1 on user-rated text alignment and temporal consistency. Qualitatively, the baselines struggle to decouple motion from appearance (e.g., producing a shark-shaped airplane), whereas VMC successfully transfers motion to entirely new subjects and backgrounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot video customization paradigm established by Tune-A-Video [32] by restricting fine-tuning to only the temporal attention layers of a cascaded video diffusion model (Show-1 [34]), rather than all self-, cross-, and temporal-attention layers. The core methodological departure is a novel motion distillation objective that aligns residual vectors between consecutive noisy latent frames, decoupling motion from appearance in a way that prior appearance-customization methods (DreamBooth, etc.) cannot achieve. The appearance-invariant prompting strategy further differentiates VMC from standard fine-tuning pipelines that rely on faithful text descriptions of the input video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that temporal attention layers alone suffice for motion transfer is a useful architectural insight for the video diffusion community, suggesting that motion priors are concentrated in a small, identifiable subset of parameters. The extreme efficiency (15 GB VRAM, <5 min training) makes motion customization accessible on consumer hardware, potentially enabling per-user or per-scene motion personalization at scale. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (Show-1), a small 24-video test set, and 8-frame inputs, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer videos, larger models, or more complex multi-object motions. The lack of a Motion Director comparison (no public code) also limits the strength of the empirical claims.
