## Problem

Existing self-training methods for source-free domain adaptive segmentation (SFUDA) lack comprehensive denoising mechanisms, causing them to fall into class, domain, and confirmation biases simultaneously. The paper identifies that pseudo-label noise is concentrated in unstable samples—those whose pixel predictions undergo large variations during self-training—and that this noise does not diminish as training progresses, leading to error accumulation and under-adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242015/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained segmentation model (e.g., DeepLab-v2/ResNet-101 or SegFormer/MiT-B5) and unlabeled target-domain RGB images at standard resolutions (e.g., 2048×1024 for Cityscapes, 1280×720 for ACDC/BDD100K), with no access to source data. It produces an adapted model that outputs pixel-level segmentation masks, along with a per-pixel uncertainty weight map ω that modulates the self-training loss. The stable/unstable partition is determined by an evolution stability (ES) score computed as the sum of cosine similarities between initial and current predictions across all pixels, with the top-k% (default 5%) forming the stable set.

## Method

SND divides target samples into stable and unstable sets via evolution stability, then denoises unstable samples through bi-level optimization: the inner loop updates the model on unstable samples with initial weights, while the outer loop optimizes the uncertainty map ω by enforcing that the inner-updated model also achieves low loss on stable samples. A gradient analysis (Eq. 8) reveals that this bi-level denoising is only unbiased when stable and unstable samples are highly correlated in domain factors; otherwise, non-intersecting loss gradients cause the ω estimate to be zero or biased. To enforce correlation, SND retrieves the nearest stable neighbor for each unstable sample using two domain proxies: a low-frequency Fourier amplitude spectrum vector (style) and row/column-wise pseudo-label histogram vectors (spatial layout). Missing categories in the retrieved neighbor are compensated by object-level copy-paste from per-category stable sets, ensuring balanced class coverage. The entire procedure is embedded in a student-teacher framework where the teacher provides pseudo-labels and the student is updated with the learned ω, with the teacher refreshed via exponential moving average.

## Results

On the four SFUDA semantic segmentation benchmarks, SND (DeepLab-v2 + ResNet-101) achieves mIoU of 55.6% (GTA→Cityscapes), 54.1% (Synthia→Cityscapes), 48.1% (Cityscapes→ACDC), and 47.8% (GTA→BDD100K). When combined with DTST's minority-class resampling, the combined method (DTST+SND) reaches 58.1%, 55.9%, 49.6%, and 49.5% respectively, surpassing the second-best method by 2.7%, 3.1%, 3.2%, and 4.2% on the four tasks. In the continual adaptation setting (SegFormer + MiT-B5), SND achieves 61.8% average mIoU across Fog/Night/Rain/Snow, outperforming CoTTA (59.2%) and SFDASEG (59.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SND outperforms all baselines on the two real-to-real and open-compound tasks: on Cityscapes→ACDC, SND (48.1%) beats SFDASEG (46.4%) by 1.7% and DTST (45.4%) by 2.7%; on GTA→BDD100K, SND (47.8%) beats SFDASEG (45.3%) by 2.5% and DTST (45.2%) by 2.6%. On GTA→Cityscapes, standalone SND (55.6%) slightly trails CrossMatch (56.4%) by 0.8%, but the combined DTST+SND (58.1%) surpasses CrossMatch by 1.7% and DTST alone by 2.7%. On Synthia→Cityscapes, SND (54.1%) beats SFDASEG (52.3%) by 1.8% and DTST (50.7%) by 3.4%, while DTST+SND (55.9%) edges out CrossMatch (55.6%) by 0.3%. The combined method consistently yields the largest gains on the hardest tasks (compound domain BDD100K: +4.2% over second-best; adverse weather ACDC: +3.2%), confirming that SND's denoising mechanism is most beneficial under severe domain shift. In the continual adaptation setting (Table 2), SND (61.8% avg mIoU) outperforms CoTTA (59.2%), SFDASEG (59.1%), and HCL (58.7%) by 2.6–3.1% across Fog, Night, Rain, and Snow sub-domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bi-level optimization framework from meta pseudo-label learning (Pham et al., CVPR 2021; Wu et al., AAAI 2021) and repurposes it for pixel-level pseudo-label denoising in source-free domain adaptation, a setting where prior meta-learning approaches were limited by their dependence on source data. It operates within the student-teacher self-training paradigm (FixMatch, Sohn et al., NeurIPS 2020) and the vanilla self-training baseline of Araslanov & Roth (CVPR 2021), but departs from the dominant uncertainty-estimation strategies (probability thresholding, perturbation consistency, classifier voting) by introducing an evolution-stability metric that tracks how much each sample's prediction changes over training iterations. The copy-paste augmentation from DACS (Tranheden et al., WACV 2021) is repurposed not for domain mixing but for category compensation of retrieved stable neighbors, addressing class imbalance in the denoising signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SND provides a unified denoising mechanism that simultaneously mitigates class, domain, and confirmation biases in SFUDA, which prior methods typically address in isolation. The architecture-agnostic design (validated on both DeepLab-v2/ResNet-101 and SegFormer/MiT-B5) makes it broadly applicable, and the ~6% mIoU gain over the best uncertainty-estimation alternatives across four diverse transfer settings is practically meaningful. However, the method requires ~10k iterations of vanilla self-training before the stability-based division can be computed, adding a non-trivial warm-up cost, and the bi-level optimization introduces additional per-step computation. Open questions include whether the stability metric generalizes to video segmentation or multi-source SFUDA, and whether the top-k% stable-set selection can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
