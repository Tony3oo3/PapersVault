## Problem

Existing RGB-video-based avatar methods either map raw SMPL pose vectors directly to appearances via NeRF MLPs (which lack any appearance information) or rely on low-capacity latent embeddings such as global codes or joint-structured feature lines, both of which produce blurry results and poor generalization to novel poses. The core challenge is how to effectively exploit the rich pixel-level image evidence available in multi-view training videos to guide the implicit network in learning fine-grained, pose-dependent dynamic details like garment wrinkles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB videos (11–24 synchronized cameras) with known SMPL/SMPL-X body poses for all frames, and at inference takes a driving SMPL pose plus a 3D ray coordinate to render a novel-view image. A key preprocessing step back-projects every visible pixel onto the posed SMPL mesh and converts it to the fixed SMPL UV parameterization, so the texture maps are defined on a canonical 2D manifold rather than per-view image space. The output is a pose-conditioned NeRF (represented as an MLP with VolSDF) that renders RGB images under arbitrary novel poses, including poses from the AIST++ dataset not seen during training.

## Method

TexVocab constructs a discrete vocabulary of (body-part pose, texture-map feature) pairs: for each training frame, all available views are back-projected onto the posed SMPL surface, averaged in the UV domain, and passed through a frozen ResNet-34 to yield a texture feature map. The 24 SMPL joints are grouped into 5 body parts (main body, left/right legs, left/right arms) to capture kinematic-chain effects on cloth dynamics, and M key body parts are sampled per part via farthest-point sampling in quaternion rotation space. At query time, the driving pose is decomposed into body parts, K nearest key body parts are retrieved per part, and their texture features are interpolated with inverse-distance weights; the resulting feature is sampled at the UV coordinate of the 3D point, modulated by a skinning-weight-wise attention (from SCANimate), and fed into the NeRF MLP alongside positional encodings of the canonical coordinate and viewing direction. Training uses a combined color MSE, mask MAE, LPIPS perceptual, and eikonal loss, with the SDF-to-density conversion following VolSDF.

## Results

On the THuman4.0 'subject00' sequence, TexVocab achieves 36.52 PSNR / 0.9896 SSIM / 10.83 LPIPS* / 12.31 FID on training poses and 32.09 PSNR / 0.9832 SSIM / 13.40 LPIPS* / 18.79 FID on novel poses, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On the DeepCap 'S2' sequence, TexVocab reaches 26.33 PSNR, 0.017 LPIPS, and 17.84 FID, surpassing NeuralActor (23.53 PSNR, 0.066 LPIPS, 19.71 FID). The method is evaluated on 6 multi-view sequences from THuman4.0 (24 views), ZJU-MoCap (21–23 views), and DeepCap (11 views), with novel poses drawn from held-out video segments and the AIST++ dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexVocab outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor PoseVocab (joint-structured feature lines), TexVocab gains +2.46 PSNR on training poses and +1.96 PSNR on novel poses, with FID dropping from 22.88→12.31 (training) and 28.10→18.79 (novel), indicating substantially sharper garment wrinkles and edges. Against TAVA, AniNeRF, and ARAH—which rely on pose vectors or per-frame latent codes as NeRF conditions—TexVocab leads by 10–14 PSNR on novel poses, confirming that pixel-aligned texture features are far more informative than global codes or feature lines for decoding fine-grained dynamics. Against NeuralActor (which predicts texture maps from SMPL normal maps via a vid2vid model), TexVocab achieves +2.8 PSNR, 4× lower LPIPS (0.017 vs 0.066), and lower FID (17.84 vs 19.71) on DeepCap, showing that directly gathering texture maps from multi-view RGB evidence avoids the information bottleneck of low-frequency normal-map driving signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

TexVocab extends the PoseVocab line of work by replacing its joint-wise vocabulary with a body-part-wise encoding that preserves kinematic chain structure, and by substituting learned feature lines with explicit pixel-aligned texture maps gathered from multi-view images. It builds on NeRF for rendering, VolSDF for SDF-to-density conversion, and SCANimate's skinning-weight attention, but departs from the broader trend of using global latent codes (AniNeRF, ARAH) or per-frame embeddings by grounding the pose-conditioned feature in actual image evidence. The conceptual shift is from 'learn a compact code that encodes dynamics' to 'use the dynamics already visible in the images as direct conditions for the network.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The body-part-wise decomposition addresses a practical limitation of joint-wise encoding: cloth wrinkles are governed by kinematic chains rather than individual joint rotations, and this structural insight yields measurable gains in pose generalization (FID 18.79 vs 28.10 over PoseVocab on novel poses). However, the method is constrained to dense multi-view settings and tight-fitting garments that conform to SMPL topology, leaving sparse-view and loose-clothing scenarios open. A key open question is whether the texture vocabulary approach can be adapted to monocular or few-view inputs without the complete UV coverage that multi-view back-projection provides.
