## Problem

Existing large-kernel ConvNets largely copy architectures from Swin Transformer or ConvNeXt without exploiting the unique property of large kernels—seeing wide without going deep—leaving their architectural design under-addressed. Additionally, while transformers have demonstrated universal perception across audio, video, point cloud, and time-series modalities, it remains unexplored whether ConvNets can achieve comparable cross-modal generality with a single unified architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All modalities are converted into 2D embedding maps of shape B × C' × H × W before entering the network: audio spectrograms become single-channel maps (C'=1), time-series sequences are reshaped via Corrformer's embedding layer into single-channel maps, point clouds are projected into three-view images at 224×224 (C'=3), and video frames are spatially concatenated (e.g., 16 frames of 224×224 yield an 896×896 map). The network outputs task-specific predictions—class labels, segmentation masks, bounding boxes with instance masks, or forecasted scalar values—using standard downstream heads (UPerNet, Cascade Mask R-CNN, or a linear decoder) without any modality-specific architectural changes.

## Method

The paper proposes four architectural guidelines that decouple the three effects a conv layer normally provides (enlarging ERF, increasing spatial pattern hierarchy, and adding depth): (1) SE Blocks efficiently increase depth via inter-channel communication; (2) a Dilated Reparam Block places parallel dilated small-kernel convs alongside a large kernel to capture sparse patterns, then merges everything into a single non-dilated large kernel at inference via a transpose-convolution equivalence; (3) large kernels (13×13) are reserved for middle- and high-level stages while 3×3 kernels handle low-level features; (4) when scaling depth, added blocks use small 3×3 kernels rather than more large kernels. The resulting LarK/SmaK block design achieves 88.0% ImageNet top-1, 55.6% ADE20K mIoU, and 56.4% COCO box AP, and the same architecture—without modality-specific customization—delivers state-of-the-art results on time-series forecasting (outperforming Corrformer), audio recognition (98.5% on Speech Commands V2 without pretraining), point cloud classification (93.2% OA on ModelNet-40), and video recognition (54.8% on Kinetics-400 without pretraining).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, UniRepLKNet-XL achieves 88.0% top-1 accuracy at 131 img/s throughput, matching InternImage-XL's accuracy while running 15% faster, and outperforming ConvNeXt-XL (87.8%) and DeiT III-L (87.7%). At the smaller scale, UniRepLKNet-S reaches 83.9% accuracy at 1265 img/s, surpassing ConvNeXt-S (83.1%) and SLaK-S (83.8%) in both accuracy and speed. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, UniRepLKNet-XL achieves 55.6% mIoU (multi-scale), and on COCO detection it reaches 56.4 AP_box / 49.0 AP_mask. Notably, as a generalist model with no modality-specific customization, UniRepLKNet-S outperforms the specialist transformer Corrformer on time-series forecasting (MSE 7.602 vs 7.709 for temperature) and achieves 98.5% accuracy on Speech Commands V2 without pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet, UniRepLKNet consistently outperforms ConvNeXt V2 at every scale (e.g., +0.8% for A, +0.6% for F, +0.5% for P, +0.4% for N) while also running 17–19% faster. At the T-level, UniRepLKNet-T (83.2%) slightly trails InternImage-T (83.5%) but runs 39% faster (1804 vs 1292 img/s); at the S-level, UniRepLKNet-S (83.9%) beats both ConvNeXt-S (83.1%) and SLaK-S (83.8%) in accuracy and throughput. At the XL level, UniRepLKNet-XL ties InternImage-XL at 88.0% but is 15% faster, and clearly outperforms CoAtNet-3 (87.6%) and DeiT III-L (87.7%) in both accuracy and speed. On ADE20K, UniRepLKNet-XL achieves 55.6% mIoU (MS), surpassing InternImage-XL (55.3%) and ConvNeXt-XL (54.0%); on COCO, UniRepLKNet-XL reaches 56.4 AP_box, edging out InternImage-XL (56.2%). In cross-modality experiments, UniRepLKNet-S as a generalist ConvNet outperforms the specialist transformer Corrformer on time-series forecasting (MSE 7.602 vs 7.709 for temperature, 3.865 vs 3.889 for wind speed) with fewer parameters (132M vs 155M), and achieves 98.5% on Speech Commands V2 without pretraining, beating Audio-MAE (98.3%) and SSAST (97.8%) which require AS-2M pretraining. On Kinetics-400 video, UniRepLKNet-S (54.8%, no pretraining) outperforms other generalist methods ImageBind (50.0%) and Meta-Transformer (47.3%), though it remains behind specialist video models like MViTv2-B (81.2%) that use ImageNet pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the large-kernel ConvNet paradigm initiated by RepLKNet, which first demonstrated that very large kernels (31×31) with structural reparameterization could rival Vision Transformers. While RepLKNet followed Swin Transformer's architecture and SLaK followed ConvNeXt's, UniRepLKNet departs by proposing four architectural guidelines specifically designed for large-kernel ConvNets, decoupling the roles of receptive field expansion, spatial pattern hierarchy, and representational depth. The base block design inherits from ConvNeXt (DW conv + FFN) and ConvNeXt V2 (GRN unit), but the key novelty is the Dilated Reparam Block, which uses dilated small kernels in parallel with a large kernel and merges them at inference via a transpose-convolution equivalence, and the strategic placement of large kernels only in middle/high-level stages with small kernels and SE blocks handling the rest.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single ConvNet architecture, with only modality-specific preprocessing (reshaping data into 2D embedding maps), achieves competitive or state-of-the-art results across five modalities—image, audio, video, point cloud, and time-series—without any architectural customization. This challenges the prevailing assumption that transformers are necessary for non-vision modalities, particularly given that UniRepLKNet-S outperforms Corrformer (Nature Machine Intelligence) on large-scale weather forecasting. Limitations include the higher training cost of dilated branches, modest video results (54.8% on Kinetics-400, behind specialist models), and unexplored applications to vision-language models, cross-attention scenarios, and generation tasks. The architectural guidelines (especially using small kernels when scaling depth and placing large kernels only in mid/high stages) provide practical design principles likely to influence future large-kernel ConvNet research.
