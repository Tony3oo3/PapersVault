## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models are limited to erasing fewer than five concepts simultaneously and struggle to balance generality (blocking synonyms) with specificity (preserving unrelated concepts). The paper identifies three root causes: residual concept information embedded in co-existing words via cross-attention, degradation of specificity when finetuning predictions at early denoising steps (before spontaneous symmetry breaking), and performance collapse from catastrophic forgetting or inter-concept interference when scaling to many concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240386/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (SD v1.4) and a set of target phrases expressing concepts to remove; it does not require concept synonyms or access to the original training data. It outputs a finetuned model whose cross-attention projection matrices and per-concept LoRA adapters have been modified so that prompting with any target phrase or its synonyms no longer produces images of that concept, while unrelated concepts (including those sharing common words) remain intact. Images are generated at 512×512 resolution using the DDIM sampler over 50 steps.

## Method

MACE operates in three stages. First, a closed-form cross-attention refinement (CFR) modifies the W_k and W_v projection matrices so that the keys and values of words co-existing with a target phrase are remapped to those of the same words in a generic/super-category prompt, eliminating residual concept traces without altering the target phrase's own keys. Second, a distinct LoRA module is trained per concept using a loss that suppresses attention-map activations in image regions identified by Grounded-SAM segmentation, with concept-focal importance sampling (CFIS) that biases timestep selection toward later denoising steps (t₁=200, t₂=400) to avoid perturbing the early sampling trajectory before spontaneous symmetry breaking. Third, a closed-form LoRA fusion (CFLF) integrates all per-concept LoRA modules by solving a least-squares objective that maps each concept's text embeddings through its respective LoRA while preserving prior knowledge, preventing inter-module interference and catastrophic forgetting without sequential or parallel finetuning.

## Results

On object erasure (CIFAR-10), MACE achieves the highest harmonic mean H₀ across 9 of 10 classes (e.g., 92.03% for airplane, 91.15% for automobile), balancing low efficacy accuracy (Acc_e ≈ 7–10%), high specificity (Acc_s ≈ 95–99%), and low synonym accuracy (Acc_g ≈ 10–15%). On explicit content erasure, MACE reduces NudeNet-detected explicit items from 743 (SD v1.4) to 111, outperforming all finetuning baselines. On 100-concept artistic style erasure, MACE achieves the best overall score H_a = 5.99 (CLIP_s − CLIP_e) with FID-30K of 12.71, surpassing UCE (H_a = 4.39, FID = 77.72) and all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACE outperforms all six finetuning baselines across all four tasks. FMN [71] and AC [30] fail to meaningfully erase concepts (Acc_e remains ~96–99% on CIFAR-10; CLIP_e barely drops on style erasure), indicating they remove only superficial features rather than the concept itself. ESD-u and ESD-x [16] achieve strong efficacy (Acc_e ≈ 7–33%) but sacrifice specificity (Acc_s drops to 85–97%), and their outputs deviate from human likenesses when erasing celebrities. UCE [17] is the closest competitor on efficacy but its specificity degrades rapidly beyond 10 concepts (Acc_s falls to 32% in the ablation without CFIS) and its FID on MS-COCO collapses to 77.72 when erasing 100 styles. SLD-M [59] shows moderate performance but is outperformed by MACE on both efficacy and specificity in every setting. On explicit content, MACE (111 detected items) halves the count of the next-best ESD-u (123) and substantially outperforms SA [19] (292) and the retraining baseline SD v2.1 [52] (586).

## Relation to Prior Work

MACE builds on LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and on the linear associative memory perspective for closed-form weight editing, but departs from prior concept erasure methods (ESD, FMN, UCE, AC, SLD-M) in three key ways: (1) it explicitly targets residual concept information embedded in co-existing words via closed-form cross-attention refinement, which prior methods overlook; (2) it introduces concept-focal importance sampling to restrict LoRA training to post-SSB timesteps, preserving specificity for polysemous phrases; and (3) it replaces sequential or parallel fine-tuning with per-concept LoRA modules fused via a closed-form objective, enabling scaling to 100 concepts without catastrophic forgetting or inter-concept interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to erase 100+ concepts simultaneously while maintaining specificity is practically critical for T2I model providers preparing to release new versions, as retraining from scratch on curated data is prohibitively expensive and only yields marginal gains (as shown by SD v2.1 vs. SD v1.4). The SSB-based insight that early denoising steps encode shared structure across related concepts provides a principled explanation for why prior uniform-timestep fine-tuning degrades specificity. A key open question is whether the observed performance decline from 10 to 100 concepts will prevent scaling to thousands of concepts in next-generation models, and whether the approach generalizes beyond SD v1.4 to newer architectures.
