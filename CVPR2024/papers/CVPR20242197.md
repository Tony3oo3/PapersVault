## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP exhibit spurious correlations that degrade worst-group accuracy, yet existing mitigation methods either require costly group annotations or involve computationally expensive full-model retraining. This paper asks whether group robustness can be improved in CLIP through lightweight, annotation-free fine-tuning that avoids retraining the entire model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242197/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images encoded by a frozen CLIP visual encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT), producing a class label via a trainable projection head. Only the projection head parameters are updated during training; all upstream visual and text encoder layers remain frozen. The calibration set is constructed by first training the projection head with cross-entropy (ERM) and then selecting misclassified samples, so the input to the contrastive recalibration phase is a curated subset of the training data rather than the full set.

## Method

CFR (Contrastive Feature Recalibration) operates in two stages: (1) a calibration set is assembled from samples misclassified by an ERM-tuned projection head on frozen CLIP features, and (2) a contrastive loss recalibrates these samples by pulling their projected features toward an EMA-estimated class centroid (built from correctly-predicted same-class samples via Dynamic Positive Centroid Sampling) while pushing them away from negative samples (either randomly or via nearest-neighbour sampling). A holistic cosine-similarity loss over the full training set is added to prevent overfitting on the small calibration set. The final loss is a weighted sum of the calibration contrastive loss and the holistic cosine loss, and only the projection head is optimized throughout, making the approach parameter-efficient and group-label-free.

## Results

On Waterbirds with CLIP-ResNet50, CFR (DPS+RNS) achieves 76.93% WGA, substantially outperforming ERM (45.64%) and all semi-supervised baselines (AFR 48.38%, JTT 61.68%, CnC 61.21%, Con-Adapter 69.89%). With the ViT backbone, CFR (DPS+RNS) reaches 88.23% WGA on Waterbirds, approaching the fully supervised GroupDRO (90.82%). Across all four benchmarks, CFR (DPS+RNS) yields 15.25–28.55 WGA points over AFR, 9.77–20.22 over JTT, and 8.55–9.34 over CnC with ResNet-50, and up to 14.81–15.39 points over the same baselines with ViT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFR (DPS+RNS) decisively outperforms all four semi-supervised baselines across every dataset and backbone: on Waterbirds-ResNet50 it gains +28.55 WGA over AFR (76.93 vs 48.38), +15.25 over JTT (76.93 vs 61.68), +15.72 over CnC (76.93 vs 61.21), and +7.04 over Con-Adapter (76.93 vs 69.89). Against supervised methods, CFR-ResNet50 matches or exceeds DFR on Waterbirds (76.93 vs 73.22) and MetaShift (81.54 vs 83.08, close), but trails GroupDRO on CelebA (73.66 vs 84.09) and CheXpert (54.44 vs 68.29). With the ViT backbone, CFR narrows the gap to supervised methods considerably: it reaches 88.23 WGA on Waterbirds vs GroupDRO's 90.82, and 64.11 on CheXpert vs DFR's 68.09. The DPS+RNS variant consistently outperforms the RPS+RNS variant by 3–5 WGA points, confirming that the dynamic positive centroid sampling is critical. Overall, CFR is the strongest semi-supervised method in the comparison, approaching supervised performance with ViT while remaining competitive with ResNet-50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Deep Feature Reweighting (DFR) result of Kirichenko et al. (ICLR 2023), which showed that retraining only the final layer suffices for group robustness, by removing DFR's reliance on group annotations. It repurposes the contrastive learning framework of SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) into a feature-recalibration objective applied to the CLIP projection head, with a calibration set built from misclassified samples rather than group-labeled data. The work also positions itself against the broader semi-supervised group-robustness literature (JTT, CnC, AFR) and the CLIP-specific fine-tuning line (S-CS/S-CL of Yang et al., 2023), arguing that the combination of contrastive recalibration and the absence of group labels is underexplored for pre-trained vision-language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the group-annotation bottleneck that hinders deployment of group-robust models in real-world, resource-constrained settings, while keeping the computational footprint minimal (only the projection head is trained). However, a performance gap versus fully supervised methods (e.g., GroupDRO) persists, particularly with ResNet backbones, and the method still requires class labels—only group labels are eliminated. Open questions include whether the misclassified-sample calibration strategy generalizes to multi-class or open-set settings, whether the NNS underperformance relative to RNS reflects a fundamental limitation of top-k sampling at small batch sizes, and whether the approach extends to other foundation models beyond CLIP.
