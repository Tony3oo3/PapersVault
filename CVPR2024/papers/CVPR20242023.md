## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models struggle to faithfully render complex prompts involving exact object quantities, precise attribute binding, spatial relationships, and multi-subject compositions, because natural language lacks the structured expressiveness of pixel-based representations. This gap limits both generation accuracy and the ability to perform fine-grained, instruction-driven edits on previously generated images.

## Input / Output

For generation, the system takes a free-form text prompt and produces both a structured semantic panel (a list of visual concepts, each with a text description, bounding box, top-6 colors from a 156-color palette, and up to 8 keypoints) and a final RGB image. For editing, it accepts a previously generated image paired with its semantic panel plus a natural-language instruction (or manual panel manipulation), and outputs an updated image where only the bounding-box regions of modified concepts are re-synthesized while the rest of the latent is preserved via a binary mask.

## Method

Ranni introduces a semantic panel as a generative middleware that decomposes text-to-image generation into two sub-tasks. In the text-to-panel stage, a Llama-2 13B model fine-tuned with LoRA (6.25M parameters per attribute task) uses chain-of-thought prompting to sequentially parse the prompt into visual concepts and their attributes. In the panel-to-image stage, a 3B-parameter latent diffusion model is fine-tuned to consume the panel as a control signal: each attribute is encoded differently (CLIP text embeddings, binary bounding-box masks, indexed learnable color embeddings, and binary keypoint heatmaps), aggregated via learnable convolution layers, and added to the denoising network input, while cross-attention maps are further restricted per-object during inference. Training data (50M image-text pairs from LAION and WebVision) is automatically annotated with Grounding DINO, SAM, and Farthest Point Sampling, then augmented with LLaVA-generated captions and rule-based pseudo samples. For editing, six unit operations (add, remove, replace, resize, reposition, attribute revision) update the panel, and a binary mask confines re-denoising to only the changed regions, enabling both manual GUI editing and fully automatic LLM-driven chatting-based generation.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, Ranni achieves the best score across all six alignment metrics: 0.6893 (color binding), 0.6325 (texture binding), 0.4934 (shape binding), 0.3167 (spatial relationship), 0.2720 (quantity), and 0.6400 (multi-object). The most substantial gains are on spatial relationship (+0.107 over the next-best SDXL(bpr) at 0.2097) and quantity (+0.038 over SD v2.1 at 0.2337), confirming that the semantic panel middleware most strongly benefits tasks requiring precise spatial arrangement and object counting. Ranni also outperforms its own base model (0.5446/0.5970/0.4732/0.1833/0.2337/0.5579) on every metric, demonstrating that the panel control signal enhances an existing diffusion model without retraining from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ranni outperforms all quantitative baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest inference-time methods (GORS at 0.6603 color, 0.6287 texture; Attn-Exct at 0.6400 color, 0.5963 texture), Ranni leads by 0.029 and 0.004 respectively, showing that explicit panel control surpasses attention-manipulation tricks. The gap widens dramatically on spatial relationship, where Ranni (0.3167) exceeds SDXL(bpr) (0.2097) by over 50% relatively, and on quantity (0.2720 vs. 0.2337 for SD v2.1), tasks where no prior method exceeds 0.21. For multi-object generation, Ranni (0.6400) edges out SDXL(bpr) (0.6221) and SDXL(b2r) (0.5905). In qualitative comparisons (Fig. 3), Ranni matches or exceeds LLM-grounded Diffusion, DALL-E 3, and Midjourney on prompt fidelity while maintaining generation quality. For instruction-based editing (Fig. 4), Ranni better preserves non-edited regions and supports more flexible operations (e.g., position swapping) compared to InstructPix2Pix and MagicBrush, which rely on implicit prompt-to-edit mapping rather than explicit panel updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ranni extends the LLM-assisted text-to-image generation line (LLM-grounded Diffusion, VPGen, LayoutGPT) by replacing simple object-location inference with a richer 'semantic panel' that encodes bounding boxes, colors, keypoints, and text descriptions per concept. It builds on latent diffusion backbones (Stable Diffusion/SDXL) and incorporates ControlNet-style conditioning, but departs from prior controllable-generation work by treating the panel as a full generative middleware rather than a single auxiliary signal. The editing component generalizes instruction-based editing (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush) from pixel-level prompt modification to explicit manipulation of structured visual concepts, enabling composable multi-round edits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic panel provides a practical, inspectable, and editable intermediate representation that bridges the gap between free-form text and pixel-level generation, making diffusion models more transparent and user-controllable. The chatting-based editing loop (LLM parses natural instructions → updates panel → diffusion regenerates) demonstrates a viable path toward conversational image creation systems. Open questions include how well the panel scales to highly abstract or novel compositions where LLMs lack reliable spatial/attribute knowledge, whether the four-attribute representation is sufficient for complex scenes, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation.
