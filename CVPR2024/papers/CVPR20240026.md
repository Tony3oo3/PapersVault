## Problem

State-of-the-art surface normal estimation methods adopt general-purpose dense prediction architectures (CNNs, ViTs) with inductive biases designed for other tasks like depth estimation or semantic segmentation, ignoring two task-specific biases: the per-pixel ray direction that constrains which normals are visible, and the rotational relationships between neighboring normals that encode piecewise-smooth scene structure. This limits both prediction accuracy and generalization to images captured with out-of-distribution camera intrinsics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of arbitrary resolution and aspect ratio together with camera intrinsics (focal lengths and principal point), which are encoded as focal-length-normalized coordinates and injected into intermediate network layers. It outputs a per-pixel unit surface normal map at full input resolution, produced by an initial coarse prediction at H/8 × W/8 followed by five iterative refinement steps and convex upsampling. Because the architecture is fully convolutional, no image resizing or positional-encoding interpolation is needed for new resolutions or aspect ratios.

## Method

The core idea is to recast surface normal estimation as iterative relative-rotation estimation between neighboring pixels. A lightweight CNN extracts a coarse feature map from which an initial normal, hidden state, and context are obtained; a ConvGRU unit then recurrently updates the hidden state over five iterations. At each iteration, for every pixel the network predicts three quantities per neighbor: a rotation angle (sigmoid × π), a 2D unit vector encoding the image-plane projection of the rotation axis (whose 3D direction is recovered via the perpendicularity constraint with the neighbor's normal), and a fusion weight (sigmoid). Rotated neighbor normals are fused and passed through a proposed ray-ReLU activation that zeroes the component along the viewing ray, guaranteeing visibility. The entire model is trained end-to-end with an angular loss on a 160K-image meta-dataset for five epochs on a single GPU, requiring no 3D data augmentation or auxiliary supervision.

## Results

On out-of-distribution camera intrinsics, the method achieves 34.9° mean angular error on Sintel and 28.9° on Virtual KITTI, substantially outperforming the ViT-based SOTA Omnidata v2 (40.5° and 37.5° respectively). On standard indoor benchmarks (NYUv2, ScanNet, iBims-1), it matches or slightly exceeds Omnidata v2 (e.g., 16.4° vs 17.2° mean on NYUv2; 17.1° vs 18.2° on iBims-1). Notably, these results are obtained with only 160K training images (vs. 12M for Omnidata), 40% fewer parameters (72M vs 123M), and 12 hours of training on a single GPU (vs. 2 weeks on 4×V100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SOTA baseline Omnidata v2 [29] (ViT-based, trained on 12M images with 3D augmentation), the proposed method wins decisively on out-of-distribution intrinsics: 34.9° vs 40.5° mean on Sintel (5.6° gap) and 28.9° vs 37.5° on Virtual KITTI (8.6° gap), while also improving on NYUv2 (16.4° vs 17.2°) and iBims-1 (17.1° vs 18.2°). On ScanNet the two methods tie (16.2° mean), and on OASIS Omnidata v2 has a marginal 0.2° advantage in mean error, though the paper notes OASIS ground truth is limited to small flat patches and does not faithfully reflect performance. Against EESNU [2] (CNN-based with aleatoric uncertainty), the proposed method outperforms on all six datasets, with the largest gaps on Sintel (34.9° vs 42.1°) and Virtual KITTI (28.9° vs 51.9°). Against Omnidata v1 [14] (U-Net on 12M images), the method wins on every dataset, and against the OASIS Hourglass baseline [8] it achieves large improvements across the board (e.g., 34.9° vs 43.1° on Sintel). The key differentiator is the explicit ray-direction encoding, which enables robust generalization to unseen camera intrinsics without positional-encoding interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using general-purpose dense prediction architectures (translation-equivariant CNNs or ViTs with learned positional encodings) for surface normal estimation, arguing that task-specific inductive biases are being overlooked. It builds on the ConvGRU-based iterative refinement architecture from Iron-Depth [3] and the camera-aware convolution idea from CAM-Convs [17], but repurposes them for normals: ray direction is encoded as dense input rather than as positional encoding, and the output is recast from direct normal regression to pairwise relative rotation (axis-angle) between neighboring pixels. The ray ReLU activation is a novel constraint that has no direct analogue in prior normal estimation work, though it is conceptually parallel to ReLU for positive depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that encoding physically-motivated inductive biases (per-pixel ray direction, inter-pixel rotation structure) can match or exceed a ViT-based SOTA trained on 75× more images, suggesting that task-aware architecture design can substitute for data scale in generalization. The fully convolutional design also sidesteps the positional-encoding inter/extrapolation problem that limits ViT-based methods to fixed resolutions and aspect ratios. A key limitation is the requirement for camera intrinsics, which the authors acknowledge restricts applicability to truly uncalibrated in-the-wild images; they propose using the model itself for camera calibration as future work. The rotation-estimation component shows modest quantitative gains but clear qualitative improvements in piecewise smoothness and boundary sharpness, leaving open whether more sophisticated inter-pixel constraints (e.g., higher-order neighborhood relationships) could yield larger benefits.
