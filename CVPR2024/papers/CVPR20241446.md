## Problem

Existing panoptic segmentation and tracking datasets are sourced from self-driving vehicles or internet videos, creating significant domain gaps for robotic applications in human-crowded environments. They typically offer single data modalities, limited object classes, lack temporal tracking information, or miss the 360-degree panoramic spatial perspective that real-world mobile robots require for comprehensive environment understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 20,000 RGB images at 752×480 resolution sampled at 1 Hz from 54 robot-collected videos, along with 4,000 merged 360° panoramic images (stitched from 5 camera views) and 4,000 synchronized 3D point clouds. Outputs include 428K panoptic masks with 27K tracking tracklets (up to 117 seconds long, averaging 16s), with a distinctive multi-label annotation scheme where objects behind glass or hanging on walls receive multiple class labels (affecting 9% of objects). The dataset supports both closed-world (43 known classes) and open-world (29 unknown classes) evaluation, with floors treated as trackable 'thing' objects rather than background.

## Method

JRDB-PanoTrack is built on the JRDB dataset collected by a social mobile manipulator robot, with annotations produced through an open-ended class list (72 total: 61 thing, 11 stuff) where any clearly visible object is labeled, followed by multi-round senior-annotator quality control. The key methodological contribution is the introduction of OSPA_PS and OSPA²_PT metrics, which extend the Optimal Sub-Pattern Matching framework to handle multi-label panoptic segmentation and tracking—addressing limitations of existing metrics (VPQ, PTQ, STQ) that are threshold-based, penalize ID recovery, or cannot handle multi-label scenarios. The dataset defines four benchmarks (CW/OW panoptic segmentation and tracking) with 9,365 training and 9,280 test images across 27 sequences each. SOTA methods (Mask DINO, Mask2Former, Kmax-Deeplab for segmentation; ByteTrack, OC-SORT, BoT-SORT for tracking; FC-CLIP, ODISE for open-world) are evaluated using both standard metrics and the proposed OSPA variants, with 3D evaluation using pseudo-labels projected from 2D annotations onto point clouds.

## Results

On the CW panoptic segmentation benchmark, Mask DINO achieves the best PQ of 36.57 (with COCO pretraining) and lowest OSPA of 0.64, outperforming Mask2Former (PQ 33.25) and Kmax-Deeplab (PQ 32.52). For OW panoptic segmentation, ODISE-C leads with PQ 11.07 and OSPA 0.85, while FC-CLIP achieves PQ 10.06 and OSPA 0.87. In CW panoptic tracking, the DINO + BoT-SORT combination achieves the highest STQ of 14.50 and IDF1 of 31.61, while in OW tracking, FC-CLIP+ + BoT-SORT reaches STQ 9.01 and IDF1 14.51. All methods show substantially lower performance than on standard benchmarks (e.g., FC-CLIP drops from PQ 26.8 on ADE20K to 10.06 here), underscoring the dataset's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In CW panoptic segmentation, Mask DINO [18] clearly outperforms Mask2Former [7] and Kmax-Deeplab [37] by 3.3 and 4.1 PQ points respectively, attributed to its larger number of object queries handling crowded scenes. In OW panoptic segmentation, ODISE-C [34] (PQ 11.07) edges out ODISE-L (10.57) and FC-CLIP [38] (10.06), but FC-CLIP achieves the lowest OSPA (0.87 vs. 0.85 for ODISE variants), indicating better spatial localization despite lower overall PQ. For CW panoptic tracking, BoT-SORT [1] consistently outperforms ByteTrack [40] and OC-SORT [5] in STQ and IDF1 across all segmentation front-ends (e.g., DINO+BS: STQ 14.50 vs. DINO+BT: 9.00 vs. DINO+OS: 7.70), though OC-SORT achieves the lowest OSPA^2 (0.793 with DINO), reflecting fewer spurious tracklets. In OW tracking, FC-CLIP+ (in-domain trained) + BoT-SORT achieves the best STQ (9.01) and IDF1 (14.51), significantly surpassing cross-domain ODISE-C + BoT-SORT (STQ 8.32, IDF1 10.80) and FC-CLIP + BoT-SORT (STQ 4.71, IDF1 9.52). For 3D tasks, MaskPLS [22] leads in segmentation (PQ 7.02, IoU 15.13) while Mask4D [36] leads in tracking (LSTQ 27.87, OSPA^2 0.860), with DSNet [13] trailing both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the JRDB dataset family (JRDB, JRDB-Act, JRDB-Pose) by adding 428K panoptic segmentation masks and 27K tracking annotations, shifting the focus from human-centric perception to full environment understanding. Methodologically, it adapts the OSPA metric from multi-target tracking to panoptic segmentation and tracking, addressing limitations of existing metrics (VPQ, PTQ, STQ) that cannot handle multi-label scenarios or penalize ID recovery. It positions itself against car-centric (Cityscapes, Waymo, NuScenes) and internet-sourced (PanopticCOCO, VIPSeg) datasets by introducing a robotic-platform, 360°-panoramic, multi-modal (RGB + point cloud) benchmark with both indoor and outdoor crowded scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark combines a mobile robot platform, panoramic coverage, multi-label annotations (objects behind glass), and open-world evaluation in a single resource. SOTA methods show substantial performance drops (e.g., FC-CLIP PQ falls from 26.8 on ADE20K to 10.06 here), and no open-world panoptic tracking method currently exists, leaving a significant open problem. The multi-label annotation scheme (9% of objects) challenges the community to develop evaluation and modeling approaches beyond single-label assumptions. Open questions include whether the knowledge-transfer results (JRDB→COCO improvement) generalize beyond Mask DINO, and how to properly exploit multi-class pixel annotations in training rather than the current single-label preprocessing workaround.
