## Problem

Visual object tracking is fragmented across task-specific models for RGB, RGB+N, RGB+M, and RGB+D/T/E tracking, each requiring customized architectures and suffering from limited training data. No existing unified framework simultaneously handles all these modalities while preserving the temporal matching mechanism that underlies human visual attention, and prior multi-task trackers either ignore non-RGB inputs or fail to capture cross-modal temporal correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241636/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The unified framework accepts RGB template and search frames (patchified into ViT tokens) plus a task-specific auxiliary input X: an initial bounding box (RGB), a BERT-encoded language description (RGB+N), a target mask replacing the bounding box (RGB+M), or per-frame depth/thermal/event maps (RGB+D/T/E). It outputs bounding box coordinates via a box head and a segmentation mask via an auxiliary head supervised by BoxInst during pretraining and by ground-truth masks during RGB+M finetuning. For RGB+M tracking, inference uses the first-frame mask and the previous-frame predicted mask for online matching without cropping, while other tasks use Hanning-window-cropped search regions.

## Method

OneTracker is a two-stage framework: a Foundation Tracker (ViT-B with 12 transformer encoder layers) is pretrained on LaSOT, TrackingNet, and GOT-10K to learn strong RGB temporal matching, then adapted to RGB+X tasks as a Prompt Tracker via parameter-efficient finetuning. Cross Modality Tracking Prompters (CMT Prompters) inject multimodal features at each transformer layer as residual prompts—using cross-attention for language and additive linear fusion for depth/thermal/event/mask—while Tracking Task Perception (TTP) Transformer layers insert low-rank adapters (rank 16) into the QKV projections and FFN linear layers of each encoder block, following the adapter formulation of He et al. Only 2.8M parameters are trainable (vs. 99.83M for full finetuning), preserving the pretrained temporal matching knowledge while enabling task-specific adaptation. The key design choice is treating all auxiliary modalities as prompts to a frozen foundation model rather than adding parallel fusion branches, unifying the architecture across all six tracking tasks.

## Results

OneTracker achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 6 tracking tasks and 11 benchmarks. On RGB tracking, the Foundation Tracker reaches 70.5 AUC on LaSOT and 83.7 AUC on TrackingNet, surpassing the previous best (GRM at 69.9 AUC on LaSOT). On RGB+X tasks, the Prompt Tracker (with only 2.8M trainable parameters) leads on every benchmark: 69.7 AUC on OTB99 (RGB+N), 60.9 F-score on DepthTrack (RGB+D), 67.2 PR on LasHeR (RGB+T), 76.7 MPR on VisEvent (RGB+E), and 82.5 J&F on DAVIS17 (RGB+M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RGB tracking (LaSOT), OneTracker's Foundation Tracker outperforms the strongest tracking-specific model GRM by 0.6 AUC (70.5 vs. 69.9) and all unified trackers (UniTrack 64.6, UTT 65.3, Unicorn 69.1, OmniTracker 69.1, UNINEXT 69.2). On RGB+N tracking (OTB99), the Prompt Tracker surpasses the best language-guided tracker JointNLT by 4.4 AUC (69.7 vs. 65.3) and 5.9 precision (91.5 vs. 85.6). On RGB+D/T/E tracking, OneTracker consistently beats the closest prompt-based competitor ViPT by 1.5 F-score on DepthTrack (60.9 vs. 59.4), 2.1 PR on LasHeR (67.2 vs. 65.1), and 0.9 MPR on VisEvent (76.7 vs. 75.8). On RGB+M tracking (DAVIS17), OneTracker edges out ViPT by 0.7 J&F (82.5 vs. 81.8), though it trails task-specific models with memory mechanisms (e.g., STARKS50 at 91.1 J&F on DAVIS16) due to its memory-free design relying only on the first and previous frame. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the full Prompt Tracker (2.8M params) outperforms full fine-tuning (99.83M params) on most RGB+X benchmarks, validating the parameter-efficient approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneTracker sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: large-scale pretraining for visual tracking (GRM, OSTrack, MixFormer) and parameter-efficient transfer learning via adapters and prompt tuning (He et al. [38], ProTrack, ViPT). Unlike prior unified trackers (UniTrack, UTT, Unicorn, OmniTracker) that handle only RGB inputs, and unlike ProTrack/ViPT that apply prompt tuning exclusively to RGB+D/T/E, this paper extends the pretrain-then-prompt paradigm to all RGB+X modalities (language, mask, depth, thermal, event) under a single architectural template. The CMT Prompter and TTP Transformer layers are the specific mechanisms that generalize the adapter concept from NLP-style PETL to cross-modal tracking fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a single codebase and training recipe that covers six tracking task families with only 2.8M trainable parameters per downstream task, reducing the engineering overhead of maintaining separate trackers. The acknowledged gap versus memory-based models (XMem, STTN) on RGB+M tracking highlights that prompt-based approaches may struggle with long-range temporal dependencies, leaving open whether richer temporal mechanisms can be integrated into the prompt-tuning framework. The ablation showing that mask embeddings degrade with deeper CMT integration suggests that not all modalities benefit equally from deep cross-modal fusion, a finding that could inform future multi-modal architecture design.
