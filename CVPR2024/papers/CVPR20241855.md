## Problem

State-of-the-art 360° room layout methods split into two camps: regression-based approaches that directly predict per-column geometry but require large decoders and still rely on heuristics for occluded regions, and segmentation-based approaches that are easier to learn and naturally model occlusion but need non-differentiable post-processing to recover layout polygons. This paper asks whether the two formulations can be unified so that a simple segmentation-style prediction is differentiably converted into the high-level geometric regression that regression methods produce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360° equirectangular panorama (H×W×3) as input and outputs a K-edge 2D floor-plan polygon (sequence of 2D coordinates) plus a scalar layout height, with the camera-to-floor distance fixed at 1.6 m to resolve scale ambiguity. Internally, the network predicts a pixel-level density logit map (H×W×1) whose upper and lower halves correspond to ceiling and floor planes respectively; the polygon is then rendered from this density field rather than being directly regressed. Secondary polygons rendered from sampled interior camera positions are unioned with the primary polygon to recover occluded layout regions.

## Method

Seg2Reg reformulates the 2D layout prediction as a floor-plan density field and applies a 'flattened' volume rendering algorithm (adapted from NeRF) to differentiably compute 1D per-column layout depth: rays are cast on the 2D floor plane, K sample points are evaluated for opacity via Softplus-activated density, and alpha blending yields the expected distance to the layout boundary. The network is a lightweight CNN backbone (ResNet-34 or HRNet-18) with an all-MLP decoder that predicts the density logit map, requiring only ~0.02 M decoder parameters versus 30–92 M for regression-based decoders. Training uses a cross-entropy loss on the alpha-blending weight distribution (guiding where each ray 'stops') rather than a direct depth regression loss, combined with a binary segmentation auxiliary loss on the density logit. A novel LayoutWarp augmentation performs geometric transformations in 3D space (camera height adjustment, random vertex perturbation) and back-warps the panorama, enabling principled 360°-compatible data augmentation that preserves ground-truth layout consistency.

## Results

On MatterportLayout, Seg2Reg with HRNet-18 achieves 85.27% 2D IoU and 82.83% 3D IoU, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art LGT-Net (HRNet-18) by +0.66 and +0.57 points respectively. On the larger Zillow Indoor dataset, the method reaches 92.50% 2D IoU and 90.73% 3D IoU, improving over LGT-Net (HRNet-18) by +0.11 and +0.12. On Stanford2D3D, Seg2Reg achieves 87.24% 3D IoU versus 85.83% for LGT-Net, while on PanoContext it is slightly behind (87.23% vs. 87.53%). The all-MLP decoder uses only 0.015M parameters, roughly 1000× fewer than the 13M-parameter LGT-Net decoder, while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest regression-based baseline LGT-Net [18], Seg2Reg wins on MatterportLayout (+0.66 2D IoU, +0.57 3D IoU with HRNet-18) and Zillow Indoor (+0.11 2D IoU, +0.12 3D IoU), but is marginally behind on PanoContext (87.23% vs. 87.53% 3D IoU) while clearly winning on Stanford2D3D (87.24% vs. 85.83%). Against earlier regression methods—HorizonNet [34], HoHoNet [35], and LED2-Net [37]—Seg2Reg achieves 1.4–2.3 points higher 3D IoU on MatterportLayout. Against segmentation-based baselines LayoutNet v2 / DuLaNet v2 [53] and AtlantaNet [31], the improvement is even larger, exceeding 7 points in 3D IoU on MatterportLayout. Notably, the paper's own reproductions of all baselines (using PanoLayoutStudio with SWA and unified training) consistently outperform the original paper reports, suggesting that the training recipe contributes meaningfully to the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

Seg2Reg bridges two established lines of 360° room layout estimation: segmentation-based methods (LayoutNet, DuLaNet, AtlantaNet) that predict per-pixel layout but require non-differentiable post-heuristics to recover geometry, and regression-based methods (HorizonNet, HoHoNet, LED2-Net, LGT-Net) that directly predict per-column depth but demand large decoders to capture global scale. The key conceptual departure is repurposing NeRF-style volume rendering in a 'flattened' 2D setting, where rays march over a predicted floor-plan density field rather than through 3D space, thereby converting a low-level segmentation output into high-level geometric regression without learned decoder parameters. The LayoutWarp augmentation framework generalizes PanoStretch by performing geometric transformations in 3D polygon space rather than on the 2D equirectangular image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ultra-lightweight all-MLP decoder (0.02M parameters) matching or exceeding 52–92M-parameter regression decoders suggests that the rendering algorithm itself can substitute for learned geometric reasoning, a principle that may transfer to other tasks where a differentiable projection from pixel-level predictions to structured geometry is needed. The PanoLayoutStudio codebase, with unified training recipes (SWA, RandAug, modern backbones) and reproduced baselines, provides a stronger and fairer benchmark for the community. Open questions include why segmentation-based methods favor certain backbones (HRNet-18) while regression methods prefer others (ResNet-34), and whether NeRF-inspired techniques (mesh extraction, few-shot regularization) can further improve layout estimation from sparse or noisy inputs.
