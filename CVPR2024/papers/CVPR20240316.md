## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-video generation methods produce clips with minimal motion and limited visual dynamics, particularly when input content falls outside the training distribution. The paper argues that text instructions alone are insufficient to specify complex scenes and intricate motions, and that a more direct conditioning mechanism is needed to unlock high-dynamic video synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240316/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt, a first-frame image (obtained via a T2I model like Stable Diffusion V2.1 or user-provided), and an optional last-frame image (which can be a coarse sketch or draft) as input, and produces a 16-frame video clip. For long-video generation, the last frame of each generated clip is fed as the first-frame instruction for the next clip in an autoregressive chain. The last-frame instruction is intentionally treated as a soft guide rather than a hard constraint, so users can supply rough drafts without expecting pixel-exact replication.

## Method

PixelDance is a 1.5B-parameter latent diffusion model built on a 3D UNet (2D spatial layers plus 1D temporal convolution and attention layers), with text injected via CLIP cross-attention and image instructions encoded by a frozen VAE and concatenated along the channel dimension with the noised video latents. The key design choice is how the last-frame instruction is handled: during training, it is randomly sampled from the last three ground-truth frames, perturbed with noise, and dropped with 25% probability; during inference, it is applied only for the first τ of T denoising steps and then removed, allowing the model to generate temporally coherent continuations rather than rigidly converging to the target frame. This τ parameter gives users a dial to control how strongly the ending constrains the generation. The model is trained jointly on WebVid (~10M clips), 500K self-collected watermark-free clips, and LAION-400M image-text pairs using DDPM with ε-prediction, enabling it to learn motion dynamics from image instructions and generalize to out-of-domain styles such as cartoons and sci-fi.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, PixelDance (1.5B params, 10M data) achieves an FVD of 381 and CLIPSIM of 0.3125, substantially surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ModelScope (FVD 550) and Make-A-Video (CLIPSIM 0.3049). On UCF-101, PixelDance attains IS 42.10, FID 49.36, and FVD 242.82, outperforming all baselines across all three metrics. In human evaluation, PixelDance is preferred over Gen2 and PiKa on both text faithfulness and video quality with a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelDance (1.5B params) outperforms all 14 baselines on UCF-101, achieving FVD 242.82 versus the next-best Dysen-VDM (325.42) and VidRD (363.19), a gap of over 80 FVD points. On MSR-VTT, it achieves the best CLIPSIM (0.3125) and FVD (381), beating ModelScope (FVD 550) by 31% and Make-A-Video (9.7B params, FVD 367.23 on UCF-101) in overall quality despite using fewer parameters. Against transformer-based methods like CogVideo (15.5B, FVD 701.59) and MagicVideo (FVD 699.00), PixelDance achieves roughly 3× lower FVD. In the human evaluation, PixelDance is preferred over both Gen2 and PiKa on text faithfulness and video quality. For long video generation (1024 frames), PixelDance's autoregressive approach yields lower FVD and smoother temporal variation than TATS-AR [9] and both LVDM-AR and LVDM-Hi [17].

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelDance builds directly on the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) for its VAE and denoising architecture, and on the temporal extension of 2D UNets to 3D variants (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2022) for video modeling. Its key departure from prior T2V and image-conditioned video generation work (e.g., Gen-2, PiKa, ModelScope) is the introduction of a dual image-instruction conditioning scheme—first and last frame—alongside text, with specific training techniques (random last-frame selection, noise perturbation, and stochastic dropping) designed to make the last frame a soft guide rather than a hard constraint. This positions the work as a principled extension of the single-image-conditioned paradigm, where the last frame instruction provides an additional degree of control over motion dynamics and scene transitions that text alone cannot express.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively compact 1.5B-parameter model trained primarily on the coarse-annotated WebVid dataset can outperform substantially larger T2V systems on standard benchmarks, suggesting that conditioning design (image instructions) may matter as much as model scale for video generation quality. The out-of-domain generalization to styles absent from training data (comics, sci-fi) and the ability to produce multi-minute narratively coherent videos via autoregressive chaining are practically significant milestones. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer sequences without quality degradation, how sensitive performance is to the quality of user-provided last-frame drafts, and whether the technique transfers to other generative architectures beyond UNet-based diffusion.
