## Problem

Dynamic 3D head reconstruction from monocular RGB videos is heavily under-constrained due to depth ambiguity, complex facial movements, and varying lighting. While neural-field-based parametric head models (e.g., NPHM) offer richer geometry than mesh-based 3DMMs, they have not been adapted for monocular RGB tracking, and existing tracking methods either rely on fixed-topology templates or require per-video training. The paper bridges this gap by building a generalizable, neural-field-based parametric head model specifically tailored for inverse rendering from monocular video.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular RGB video (15 fps, 12 s per sequence) along with per-frame facial silhouettes (from video matting) and 2D facial landmarks (from a detector); preprocessing also removes the torso via semantic segmentation. It outputs time-invariant latent codes for shape (z_geo) and appearance (z_app), per-frame expression codes (z_exp), head pose (rotation and translation), and a 9-dimensional spherical harmonics lighting vector. Geometry is represented as a signed distance field in canonical space, appearance as a texture field, and dynamics as a backward deformation with two hyper-dimensions, yielding a fully animatable head including hair and mouth interior. Tracking runs at approximately 1.2 frames per minute.

## Method

MonoNPHM composes three neural components: (1) a canonical SDF built from an ensemble of local MLPs centered on 65 predicted facial anchor points, (2) a canonical texture field whose local MLPs are conditioned on hidden geometry features h_geo rather than raw spatial coordinates, ensuring that RGB gradients meaningfully update geometry and expression codes during inverse rendering, and (3) a backward deformation network augmented with two hyper-dimensions to handle topologically challenging expressions (e.g., mouth opening, eye closing). Tracking formulates an energy minimization over latent codes, poses, and lighting, using NeuS-style volumetric rendering of backward-warped rays in posed space, a 3-band spherical harmonics shading term, and a landmark loss computed by numerically inverting the backward deformation via iterative root finding to project anchor points into image space. The model is trained end-to-end as an auto-decoder on the NPHM dataset with direct SDF and RGB supervision, and tracking proceeds in two stages: a per-frame warm-up followed by joint optimization over all parameters with temporal smoothness regularization.

## Results

On 20 self-recorded Kinect sequences (180 frames each, 5 participants), MonoNPHM achieves the best L1-Chamfer distance of 0.0024 m, outperforming the strongest baseline MICA Tracker (0.0030 m) by approximately 20%. It also attains the highest normal cosine similarity (0.940 vs. 0.932 for MICA) and the best Recall@2.5 mm (0.785 vs. 0.654 for MICA), indicating substantially better geometric coverage of the ground-truth surface. The method further demonstrates single-image reconstruction on FFHQ, showing the learned prior generalizes to sparse observations and extreme lighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoNPHM outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the strongest mesh-based tracker MICA (0.0030 m Chamfer), MonoNPHM reduces the L1-Chamfer distance by 20% to 0.0024 m and improves Recall@2.5 mm from 0.654 to 0.785, reflecting its ability to reconstruct complete heads including hair and mouth interior. DECA, the other mesh-based baseline, lags further behind at 0.0034 m Chamfer and 0.644 recall. Among field-based methods, IMavatar (0.0054 m) and HeadNeRF (0.0049 m) suffer from under-constrained geometry due to their high-capacity neural networks, while NHA (0.0055 m) performs worst overall, with only 0.490 recall. The gap is most pronounced on Recall@2.5 mm, where MonoNPHM's 0.785 exceeds the next-best MICA by 13 percentage points, confirming that the SDF-based volumetric rendering with landmark loss yields substantially more complete geometric reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoNPHM extends NPHM [27], a neural parametric head model using SDF-based local MLPs and facial anchor points, by adding a geometry-conditioned texture field, switching from forward to backward deformation, and augmenting the deformation with hyper-dimensions to handle topologically challenging expressions. It departs from the broader neural-field face modeling literature (HeadNeRF, IMavatar, PanoHead), which has focused on multi-view or single-image reconstruction, by formulating the problem as model-based photometric tracking with NeuS-style volumetric rendering. The landmark loss, obtained by numerically inverting the backward deformation field via iterative root finding, is a novel bridge between implicit geometry and the discrete 2D landmark supervision that is standard in mesh-based trackers like MICA and DECA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural parametric head model to perform dynamic 3D reconstruction from monocular RGB videos, making a class of high-capacity implicit models practical for the common webcam-only scenario. The 20% Chamfer distance improvement over the strongest mesh-based baselines demonstrates that neural implicit priors can outperform PCA-based 3DMMs when properly regularized. Key limitations include slow tracking speed (~1.2 frames/minute), a simple 3-band spherical harmonics lighting model, and evaluation on only 20 self-recorded Kinect sequences, leaving open questions about generalization to diverse in-the-wild conditions and real-time applicability.
