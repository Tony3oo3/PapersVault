## Problem

Prior semantic scene completion (SSC) methods for autonomous vehicles produce low-resolution, heavily occluded voxel occupancy that is unsuitable for simulation-ready 3D environments. This paper tackles the harder problem of extrapolating fine-grained, high-resolution scene geometry beyond both LiDAR occlusions and the sensor's field of view from sparse, noisy scans — a task the authors term outdoor scene extrapolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242099/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 5 or 10 registered, accumulated LiDAR scans from an ego-vehicle trajectory (simulated with raycasting plus coordinate and pose noise during training) as input, operating within a 38.4×38.4×8 m volume. It produces a high-resolution 3D mesh at 10 cm³ voxel resolution via local implicit functions, extending geometry beyond the maximum LiDAR elevation and into occluded regions such as building roofs, trees, and parked cars. The coarse stage operates at 20 cm³, and the final mesh is obtained by decoding unsigned distance fields from a pretrained local-implicit autoencoder.

## Method

hGCA is a two-stage hierarchical generative model built on Generative Cellular Automata (GCA). The coarse stage applies GCA's sparse, local transition kernel at 20 cm³ for T₁=30 steps, augmented with a lightweight bird's-eye-view (BEV) planner that encodes the input via PointPillars-style pillar aggregation and a 2D U-Net, then injects global context into the sparse U-Net decoder through SPADE-style mean/variance normalization per pillar; the planner is additionally supervised to predict very low-resolution dense occupancy (z_r=4) as an auxiliary loss. The fine stage uses cGCA to upsample to 10 cm³ over T₂=15 steps, generating per-cell latent features in a pretrained local-implicit autoencoder space that are decoded into a continuous unsigned distance field and meshed. Both stages are trained with infusion training (binary cross-entropy against ground truth at each recursive step), and the upsampler is trained independently on ground-truth coarse geometry to avoid stochasticity-induced instability, preserving spatial scalability throughout.

## Results

On synthetic scenes (5 LiDAR scans), hGCA achieves IoU of 35.43 on CARLA and 59.79 on Karton City, dramatically outperforming deterministic SSC baselines (e.g., JS3CNet: 5.28/3.92, SG-NN: 3.86/2.30) in scene completeness. On the Street CD metric (evaluating occluded geometry), hGCA achieves 3.36 on Karton City, comparable to GCA (3.34) and far superior to deterministic methods (46–53). hGCA also achieves the highest generation diversity (TMD = 3.19 on CARLA) among all methods, exceeding GCA (1.45) and cGCA (2.25). The planner module trades a small amount of ReSim fidelity for improved global consistency, as confirmed in ablation studies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against deterministic SSC baselines (SCPNet, JS3CNet, SG-NN, ConvOcc), hGCA generates far more complete geometry: Karton City IoU of 59.79 vs. 3.64–70.28, and Street CD of 3.36 vs. 46.76–53.39, confirming that conservative deterministic models fail to extrapolate occluded geometry. Against the closest generative baselines GCA and cGCA, hGCA achieves comparable Street CD (3.36 vs. 3.34/4.16) while producing the highest diversity (TMD 3.19 vs. 1.45/2.25), at the cost of slightly lower IoU (59.79 vs. 74.95/68.23) due to the planner's global-consistency regularization. The paper notes that the planner trades some per-region fidelity for scene-wide coherence, visible qualitatively as consistent building walls and fences. On real Waymo-open LiDAR, hGCA qualitatively outperforms all baselines in completeness and sim-to-real fidelity, though quantitative evaluation is limited by the lack of full ground-truth geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generative Cellular Automata (GCA) framework [46, 47] from indoor scene completion to large-scale outdoor street scenes, introducing a hierarchical coarse-to-fine architecture (hGCA) with a lightweight bird's-eye-view planner to inject global context into GCA's inherently local sparse convolutions. It deliberately departs from prior semantic scene completion (SSC) methods (JS3CNet, SCPNet) that use discriminative autoencoders supervised by accumulated LiDAR scans, arguing that such approaches produce low-resolution, occlusion-ridden geometry unsuitable for simulation. The upsampling stage leverages cGCA's local implicit functions [47] to promote voxel output to continuous meshes, while the planner borrows architectural ideas from PointPillars [21] and SPADE-style spatial conditioning [27].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

hGCA takes a concrete step toward simulation-ready 3D street environment creation from sparse, unlabeled LiDAR scans — a byproduct of AV deployment — rather than requiring dense multi-sensor captures or manual authoring. The strong sim-to-real generalization (trained on CARLA/Karton City, evaluated on Waymo) and anecdotal evidence of generating novel objects (three-wheelers, complex trees) from geometric cues suggest a path toward diverse, scalable world generation. However, the method is too slow for real-time use, produces inconsistent geometry (e.g., roofs), lacks textures/materials, and the novel-object generation claims remain unvalidated beyond qualitative examples. Open questions include whether the hierarchical approach can be accelerated, whether the planner can be replaced by learned global attention, and how to rigorously evaluate generative 3D scene quality on real data where ground truth is inherently incomplete.
