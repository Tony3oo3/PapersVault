## Problem

Industrial recognition systems (QR code reading, text OCR, defect detection) frequently fail when products are covered by wrinkled transparent films, which introduce specular highlights, altered light transmittance, and texture distortions. Existing highlight-removal methods model only the specular component and cannot decouple the full range of film-induced degradations, and no dataset exists for this problem in real industrial settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242182/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four single-shot polarized images (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) from a monochrome polarization sensor (Sony IMX250MZR), demosaicked via EARI to full resolution, along with derived AoP and DoP maps computed from Stokes parameters. The output is a single grayscale reconstructed image representing the original material surface with all film-induced degradations removed. The dataset comprises 315 industrial scenarios (QR codes, text, products) with paired film-covered and uncovered captures under fixed lighting and camera geometry.

## Method

The core insight is that specular highlight is the only light component whose intensity varies with polarization angle, so minimizing it via angle optimization yields a spatial prior that localizes highlight regions. An Angle Estimation Network (lightweight RDN) takes the four polarized images plus AoP and DoP maps and regresses a pixel-wise angle map A. A Polarization-based Location Model then applies Malus's Law with Stokes-derived I_max and I_min to compute the prior P = I_m + I_d + I_max·cos²(A) + I_min·sin²(A), which suppresses I_h. A second RDN (Reconstruction Network) takes the four polarized images concatenated with P and reconstructs the original diffuse reflection I_m, decoupling both I_h and I_d. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with L1 loss at learning rate 5×10⁻⁵ (halved every 2×10⁴ iterations) on the paired industrial dataset using 10-fold cross-validation.

## Results

On the Film Removal dataset evaluated with 10-fold cross-validation, the proposed method achieves a mean PSNR of 36.48 and SSIM of 0.9824, outperforming the strongest baseline Restormer (PSNR 34.32, SSIM 0.9731) by over 2 dB in PSNR. The method also substantially improves downstream industrial tasks: QR code reading rate jumps from 27.58% (Restormer) to 72.41% (Ours), approaching the 100% ground-truth rate. The low variance across folds (PSNR σ=0.57, SSIM σ=1.23×10⁻⁵) indicates stable and robust performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: two general image restoration models (Uformer, Restormer) and two highlight-removal methods (SHIQ, Polar-HR). The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on both PSNR and SSIM, with the largest gap over the highlight-removal methods (SHIQ: +14.9 dB PSNR; Polar-HR: +14.3 dB PSNR) and a clear margin over the strongest restoration baseline Restormer (+2.16 dB PSNR, +0.0093 SSIM). On the downstream QR code reading task, the advantage is even more pronounced: the method achieves a 72.41% read rate versus 27.58% for Restormer, 20.69% for Uformer, 19.54% for Polar-HR, and 8.05% for SHIQ. The highlight-removal baselines (SHIQ, Polar-HR) fail to model the full film degradation (transmittance, texture), producing only partial highlight suppression, while the general restoration baselines (Uformer, Restormer) generate fake artifacts in highlight regions rather than recovering true content. The polarization-guided prior is the key differentiator, enabling accurate localization of specular highlights that neither category of baseline can exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem space by extending the well-studied task of polarization-based specular highlight removal (Nayar, Umeyama, Zhang, Wen/Polar-HR) to the broader challenge of removing all degradations introduced by a wrinkled transparent film, including light transmittance and material texture effects. Rather than treating the imaging as a binary decomposition (transmission + reflection) as prior highlight-removal methods assume, the authors introduce a three-component physical model (I_m + I_d + I_h) and a physics-informed prior built on Stokes parameters and Malus's Law to explicitly locate specular regions before reconstruction. The reconstruction backbone is a standard Residual Dense Network, so the methodological novelty lies in the polarization-based angle estimation network and the Polarization-based Location Model that feeds a spatial prior into the restoration pipeline, rather than in the network architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial recognition pipelines: the 72.41% QR code reading rate (vs. 27.58% for Restormer) demonstrates that film removal is not merely an image-quality improvement but a prerequisite for downstream task reliability in manufacturing. The new paired polarized dataset (315 industrial scenarios) fills a gap that prevents reproducible research on this problem. Open questions include generalization beyond the specific industrial optical setup (fixed camera/lighting geometry), whether the simplified elliptical polarization model (σ=90°) holds for more complex film materials, and whether the approach extends to other transparent overlays (plastic wrap, glass, water droplets) in uncontrolled environments.
