## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detectors require a predefined category list at inference time, severely limiting their practical utility in open-world scenarios. Unlike human perception, which interprets objects hierarchically across multiple granularities, current OVD systems can only match objects to given category names and cannot produce their own descriptions. This paper addresses both limitations by enabling detection with user-provided categories and, critically, generating multi-granularity hierarchical labels when no categories are supplied.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image and an optional text vocabulary list. In detection mode, the list constrains which objects are localized; in generative mode (no list provided), the model autonomously proposes foreground objects and outputs three-level hierarchical labels formatted as 'phrase | category | parent category' (e.g., 'a red sports car | car | vehicle'). Objectness scores in generative mode are re-calibrated by computing text-encoder similarity between the object query and its generated phrase/category fields. The model also supports image-level caption generation as an auxiliary output, and operates at 320×320 resolution during captioner pretraining before moving to higher resolution in fine-tuning.

## Method

DetCLIPv3 couples a transformer-based open-vocabulary detector (Swin backbone + pixel encoder + object decoder with deformable attention) with a Qformer-inspired object captioner that replaces cross-attention with deformable attention for dense prediction. The captioner consumes object queries from the detector's final decoder layer, prepends a special [OBJ] task token, and is trained with a standard language modeling loss to emit hierarchical labels; an auxiliary image-level captioning objective (using 32 learnable image queries) further enriches its generative capacity. Training data is the GranuCap50M dataset, constructed via a four-step auto-annotation pipeline: InstructBLIP recaptioning of noisy web image-text pairs, GPT-4-based entity extraction into {phrase, category, parent category} triplets, LLaVA instruction tuning for scalable annotation of 200M pairs, and pseudo-labeling of bounding boxes with a pre-trained OV detector. A three-stage training scheme—(1) OV detector training on annotated detection/grounding data, (2) captioner pretraining on GranuCap50M at 320×320 with the detector frozen, (3) holistic fine-tuning on 600k high-resolution samples with all parameters unfrozen—makes large-scale image-text learning computationally tractable while preserving detection quality.

## Results

On the LVIS minival benchmark, DetCLIPv3 with a Swin-T backbone achieves 47.0 zero-shot fixed AP, outperforming DetCLIPv2 (40.4), GLIPv2 (29.0), and GroundingDINO (27.4) by 6.6, 18.0, and 19.6 AP respectively. The Swin-L variant reaches 48.8 AP on LVIS minival and 41.4 AP on LVIS val. For dense captioning on Visual Genome V1.2, DetCLIPv3 (Swin-L) achieves 19.7 AP, surpassing the previous SOTA GRiT (15.5 AP) by 4.2 AP. On the COCO-O distribution shift benchmark, the Swin-L model attains 48.8 AP with an effective robustness score of +27.0, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetCLIPv3 decisively outperforms all prior open-vocabulary detectors on LVIS minival: the Swin-T model beats DetCLIPv2 by 6.6 AP (47.0 vs. 40.4), GLIPv2 by 18.0 AP, and GroundingDINO by 19.6 AP; the Swin-L model beats DetCLIPv2 by 4.1 AP (48.8 vs. 44.7). Notably, the Swin-L model's rare-class AP (49.9) exceeds its frequent-class AP (47.8), indicating strong long-tail generalization from the GranuCap50M pretraining. On dense captioning (VG V1.2), DetCLIPv3 (Swin-L) achieves 19.7 AP, surpassing GRiT (15.5 AP) by 4.2 AP and CapDet (15.4 AP) by 4.3 AP. For zero-shot generative detection on COCO, DetCLIPv3 (Swin-L) achieves 57.3 NovelAP50, exceeding the best discriminative baseline DetPro (43.3) by 14.0 points, though its OverallAP50 (49.3) trails ViLD (51.3) which was trained on base classes. On COCO-O distribution shift, DetCLIPv3 (Swin-L) attains 48.8 AP with +27.0 effective robustness, outperforming GLIP (Swin-L, 48 AP, +24.9) and DINO (Swin-L, 42.1 AP, +15.8). In fine-tuning on ODinW13, the Swin-T DetCLIPv3 (71.1 AP) surpasses the larger Swin-L DetCLIPv2 (70.4 AP), and on LVIS mini the Swin-L model reaches 60.5 AP, exceeding OWL-ST+FT (56.2 AP) by 4.3 AP despite using far less pretraining data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DetCLIPv3 is a direct evolution of the DetCLIP/DetCLIPv2 line of work, inheriting their parallel text-input formulation and noun concept corpus while adding a generative object captioner inspired by Qformer/BLIP-2. The OV detector component borrows the text-guided pixel feature selection from GroundingDINO but drops its computationally heavy cross-modal fusion modules. The auto-annotation pipeline extends the re-captioning literature (BLIP, PixArt-Alpha) by leveraging VLLMs (InstructBLIP, LLaVA) and GPT-4 to produce multi-granularity object entities at scale, a step beyond prior OVD work that relied on NLP parsers like NLTK or SpaCy for entity extraction. The key conceptual departure from all prior OVD methods is the shift from a purely discriminative paradigm (detect given categories) to a generative one (detect and describe without predefined categories).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative OVD paradigm broadens the practical applicability of open-vocabulary detectors to scenarios where no category list is available, and the hierarchical label output (phrase | category | parent category) provides richer object interpretation than flat class names. The GranuCap50M dataset and the VLLM-based auto-annotation pipeline are reusable assets for the broader vision-language community. However, the authors acknowledge that evaluation of generative detection remains incomplete due to the absence of suitable benchmarks, and the system currently lacks instruction-based control, pointing toward future integration with LLMs for more flexible visual understanding.
