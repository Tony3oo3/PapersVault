## Problem

Enabling arbitrary user control over text-to-image diffusion models typically requires substantial training on large annotated datasets, making it cumbersome and infeasible for most users. Existing conditional generation methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, LoRA) each require bespoke architectures and large-scale training, and none provide a unified framework for diverse control types such as drag-based manipulation, identity preservation, and spatially-aligned guidance under a single recipe.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240675/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt plus an optional control signal (pose/depth/edge map, a reference image with sparse point correspondences for drag manipulation, or a reference image for identity/appearance preservation) and produces a controlled generated image at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution. Readout heads operate directly on latent U-Net features (64×64 or 128×128 latent resolutions) without requiring a latent decoder, and are queried at every sampling timestep to produce intermediate readout estimates (pose maps, depth maps, edge maps, correspondence feature maps, or appearance similarity feature maps) that drive the guidance gradient.

## Method

Readout Guidance trains lightweight readout heads on top of a frozen diffusion model's U-Net decoder features, extending the Diffusion Hyperfeatures architecture with learned projection layers, bilinear upsampling, and layer-mixing weights, plus timestep conditioning. Two head types are defined: spatially-aligned heads (pose, depth, edges) trained with per-pixel MSE against pseudo-labels from off-the-shelf models, and relative heads (correspondence, appearance similarity) trained with contrastive or triplet/hinge losses on video data. At sampling time, the classifier guidance update rule is replaced with a regression formulation: the gradient of a distance function between a user-defined reference (or a reference image's readout) and the predicted readout is backpropagated through the readout head to nudge the diffusion model's noise prediction. Crucially, the distance function need not be spatially aligned, enabling arbitrary spatial relationships like point correspondences or global appearance matching. The heads require as few as 100 training examples and at most 3 hours on a single A100 GPU, and can be combined with classifier-free text guidance or stacked with multiple readout heads simultaneously.

## Results

The primary quantitative evaluation is pose control on 100 MSCOCO validation images, measured by Percentage of Correct Keypoints (PCK) at threshold α=0.05. Readout Guidance trained on only 8.5k PascalVOC images achieves PCK comparable to T2I-Adapter trained on 3M LAION-Aesthetics images (350× less data). Combining T2I-Adapter with Readout Guidance yields a 30% absolute PCK improvement (2.3× relative) over T2I-Adapter alone. In terms of efficiency, the method requires as few as 100 training examples, 49MB of added parameters (vs. 1.4GB for ControlNet), and at most 3 hours of training on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DragDiffusion [53] on drag-based manipulation, Readout Guidance produces qualitatively superior results, particularly for large out-of-plane 3D motions (e.g., fully rotating a tiger's head) where DragDiffusion only slightly translates the nose; additionally, Readout Guidance does not require a user-provided foreground mask. Against ControlNet [70], Readout Guidance is complementary rather than competitive: the paper demonstrates that applying Readout Guidance on top of ControlNet corrects 'last-mile' pose artifacts that ControlNet alone produces. Against T2I-Adapter [40] on pose control, Readout Guidance trained on 8.5k images matches T2I-Adapter trained on 3M images in PCK, and combining the two (T2I-Adapter + Readout Guidance) improves PCK by 30% (α=0.05) or 2.3× over T2I-Adapter alone. The paper also notes that Readout Guidance requires 350× less training data than T2I-Adapter and 2000× less than ControlNet (100 pairs vs. 200k) while adding only 49MB of parameters versus ControlNet's 1.4GB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier guidance framework of Dhariwal & Nichol [13] by replacing discrete classifiers with learned regression readout heads operating on frozen diffusion features, and builds its head architecture on Diffusion Hyperfeatures [36] while adapting it from single-timestep feature extraction to multi-timestep guidance. It positions itself as orthogonal to adapter-based conditional generation (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, LoRA), which modulate diffusion features, whereas Readout Guidance operates at sampling time and can be stacked on top of any such adapter. Prior sampling-time guidance methods (Depth-Aware Guidance, Sketch-Guided Diffusion, Mid-U Guidance) each addressed a single control type with hand-designed or task-specific losses; this paper unifies spatially-aligned and relative (cross-image) properties under a single architecture and training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic reduction in training cost: as few as 100 supervised pairs and ~49MB of added parameters versus 200k pairs and 1.4GB for ControlNet, making custom conditional control accessible on a single consumer GPU in hours. The composability with existing adapters (demonstrated 30% PCK improvement over T2I-Adapter alone) suggests a path toward stacking lightweight guidance heads for increasingly complex multi-constraint generation without retraining the base model. Key limitations include increased sampling-time memory and runtime from backpropagating through intermediate features, and occasional generation of unrealistic or cartoonish images that satisfy the readout constraints. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or 3D generation, whether the data-efficiency gains hold for more complex or open-vocabulary controls, and how to mitigate the unrealistic-image failure mode.
