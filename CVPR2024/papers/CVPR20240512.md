## Problem

Existing video frame interpolation methods rely on linear motion assumptions or task-oriented flow prediction that overlook latent acceleration information between input frames, making them ineffective for curvilinear and quadratic motion scenarios. Additionally, supervising predicted motions is difficult because ground-truth optical flows are unavailable, and existing pseudo-label strategies introduce undesired knowledge that is sub-optimal for the VFI task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240512/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes two consecutive RGB video frames (I_0 and I_1) and synthesizes a single intermediate frame I_t at an arbitrary temporal position 0<t<1. During training, the teacher model additionally receives the ground-truth intermediate frame I_t as a third input, while the student model operates on only the two input frames. Training uses 224×224 patches cropped from 448×256 Vimeo90K triplets, and evaluation covers resolutions up to 4K on the Xiph dataset.

## Method

IQ-VFI introduces an Implicit Acceleration Estimation Network (IANet) that applies shuffle operations, 3D convolutions, and N progressive fusion modules (with multi-scale temporal aggregation) to extract a latent acceleration prior P from spatio-temporal features. This prior is then used by an Implicit Motion Modulation Module (IMM) within a two-stage VFI backbone (PWC-Net motion estimator + U-Net RefineNet) to progressively convert linear flows into quadratic flows via dynamic 1×1 convolution modulation and a two-path local refinement scheme. Training follows a teacher-student knowledge distillation paradigm: the teacher (seeing three frames) learns task-oriented acceleration and motion knowledge, while the student (seeing two frames) is guided by an implicit acceleration distillation loss (L_IA, L1 distance on P) and an adaptive implicit motion distillation loss (L_IM, L1 distance on flows masked by interpolation-error regions), jointly optimized with a Laplacian reconstruction loss.

## Results

IQ-VFI achieves state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM on most benchmarks: 36.60/0.982 on Vimeo90K, 35.48/0.970 on UCF101, 40.24/0.991 on SNU-FILM Easy, and 34.72/0.905 on Xiph-4K. On Vimeo90K, it outperforms the closest competitor AMT by 0.07 dB and VFIFormer by 0.10 dB. On SNU-FILM Medium, it leads AMT by 0.12 dB. The method achieves the best or near-best results across all four datasets, with the only losses being on SNU-FILM Extreme (25.45 vs. EMA-VFI's 25.59) and Xiph-2K (36.68 vs. EMA-VFI's 36.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IQ-VFI outperforms the strongest motion-based baselines AMT (CVPR'23) and VFIFormer (CVPR'22) on Vimeo90K by 0.07 dB and 0.10 dB respectively, and on SNU-FILM Medium by 0.12 dB over AMT. Against EMA-VFI (CVPR'23), IQ-VFI wins on Vimeo90K (+0.10 dB), UCF101 (+0.06 dB), SNU-FILM Easy (+0.66 dB), SNU-FILM Medium (+0.38 dB), and Xiph-4K (+0.17 dB), but trails on SNU-FILM Extreme (−0.14 dB) and Xiph-2K (−0.06 dB). VFIFormer reports OOM on Xiph-2K/4K, making direct comparison impossible at those resolutions. Among earlier methods, IQ-VFI leads RIFE (ECCV'22) by 0.95 dB on Vimeo90K and IFRNet (CVPR'22) by 0.40 dB, confirming that the quadratic motion modeling and knowledge distillation strategy provide consistent gains over both two-stage and task-oriented paradigms. The margins over the top-3 competitors (AMT, VFIFormer, EMA-VFI) are modest (0.03–0.17 dB), indicating a highly competitive field, while the gap over older baselines (ToFlow, DAIN, CAIN) is substantial (1.8–2.9 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage VFI paradigm (off-the-shelf motion estimator + RefineNet) by incorporating AMT's all-pairs correlation and mutual update modules into its RefineNet decoder, and IFRNet's mutual update mechanism for joint flow-feature refinement. The key conceptual departure is modeling intermediate motion as quadratic rather than linear, introducing a latent acceleration prior estimated by a dedicated IANet with progressive fusion modules. Unlike prior knowledge distillation approaches in VFI (RIFE, IFRNet) that use off-the-shelf optical flow as pseudo labels, this work trains a task-oriented teacher regularized against overusing ground-truth information, and distills both acceleration priors and task-oriented flows to the student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic motion model addresses a genuine limitation of linear VFI methods in handling curvilinear trajectories, and the finding that directly predicting acceleration from two frames without supervision is non-trivial (IANet alone yields no gain) provides useful insight into the difficulty of implicit motion modeling. However, the practical gains are modest (0.07–0.1 dB over SOTA), and the authors themselves note that how to implicitly and effectively explore video motion properties for VFI remains an open question. The knowledge distillation strategy preventing teacher overfitting to ground-truth frames is a transferable idea, but the overall contribution is incremental within an already well-explored task.
