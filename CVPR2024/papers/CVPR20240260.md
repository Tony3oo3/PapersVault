## Problem

Capturing closely interacted two-person motions from monocular videos remains largely unsolved: single-person methods fail under heavy inter-human occlusions because they ignore interpersonal context, while multi-person methods prioritize relative joint positions over precise pose interactions. Additionally, no large-scale, high-quality benchmark dataset dedicated to closely interacted two-person motion capture exists in the community.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240260/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes monocular RGB video frames at 512×512 resolution and processes them in sliding windows of length 64 with half-overlap. It outputs a 269-dimensional motion representation per person (angular/linear velocities, root height, local joint positions, joint rotations, foot contact, plus initial direction and translation), FOV-normalized root translations, and per-person salience scores. The query-based design fixes exactly two queries per type (Pose, Transl, Prob), encoding the two-person assumption directly into the architecture.

## Method

The core contribution is reaction priors: a motion VAE encodes each person's motion into a latent Gaussian, and an invertible neural network (INN) bi-directionally maps the action latent distribution to the reaction latent distribution, exploiting the symmetry of two-person interactions so that either person can serve as the conditioning signal. These priors are injected into a decoder-only Transformer pose estimator as both a training regularizer (KL divergence between the estimated reaction latent and the prior-predicted latent) and a test-time optimizer (iteratively refining latent parameters against a data term and prior term). The pose estimator uses interaction-aware self-attention that decomposes attention into intra-human (skeleton-local) and inter-human (all-joint cross-person) passes, and implements query-based tracking by concatenating previous-frame translation estimates as additional queries. The Dual-Human dataset (~2k sequences captured with Xsens + HTC Vive, fitted to SMPL-X with SDF-based collision and contact annotations, rendered via BEDLAM) provides the training and evaluation substrate, with noise augmentation during prior training to simulate realistic MoCap estimation errors.

## Results

On the three interacted-motion benchmarks (Hi4D, CHI3D, Dual-Human), the proposed method achieves the best MPJPE on all three datasets (75.0, 71.9, and 63.4 mm respectively) and the best translation error on all three (106.7, 228.5, and 112.1 mm). It also attains the best PA-MPJPE on Dual-Human (51.2 mm) and is competitive on Hi4D (59.7 mm) and CHI3D (47.8 mm), where it trails CLIFF by 0.4 and 1.9 mm respectively. The largest gains over baselines appear in translation accuracy, where the method reduces error by 40–60% compared to top-down and bottom-up approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four MoCap baselines (CLIFF, 4D-Humans, BEV, TRACE) on MPJPE across all three benchmarks, with the largest margin over BEV (up to 19.7 mm on Hi4D) and a consistent 3–10 mm advantage over the strongest competitor TRACE on Dual-Human. Translation error shows the most dramatic improvement: 106.7 mm on Hi4D versus 179.9 mm for TRACE and 285.8 mm for CLIFF, and 112.1 mm on Dual-Human versus 120.9 mm for TRACE. The method is slightly behind BEV on Hi4D PA-MPJPE (59.7 vs. 59.3 mm) and behind CLIFF on CHI3D PA-MPJPE (47.8 vs. 45.9 mm), but leads on every other metric. In a separate prior comparison (Table 3), the reaction priors also outperform BUDDI's diffusion-based proxemics priors (64.1 vs. 67.3 mm MPJPE, 115.2 vs. 126.1 mm translation on Dual-Human), confirming that the invertible motion-VAE formulation is more effective than static single-frame spatial priors for temporal MoCap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends single-person motion priors (VPoser, HuMoR) to the two-person setting by introducing reaction priors built on a motion VAE and an invertible neural network (INN), repurposing the INN's bidirectionality to model the interchangeable nature of action-reaction pairs. It departs from the closest prior work, BUDDI, which learns static single-frame proxemic priors via diffusion; here the priors are temporal (operating on motion clips in latent space) and invertible, enabling seamless role switching between the two subjects. The pose estimator itself is a query-based decoder-only Transformer (in the lineage of 4D-Humans and Group Pose) augmented with an interaction-aware self-attention that separates intra-joint and inter-joint relationships, and a query-based tracking mechanism that avoids a separate tracking module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Dual-Human dataset (2k sequences with multi-view renderings, vertex-level contact annotations, and text descriptions) fills a notable gap in the interacted MoCap literature, where prior datasets are either small (Hi4D: 100 motions), lack images (Inter-Human), or suffer from occlusion-induced inaccuracies. However, the method is constrained to exactly two people and to interaction types well-represented in the training data; the authors acknowledge that large λ_prior values on non-interacting inputs produce false interaction motions. Open questions include whether the reaction-prior framework generalizes to N-person interactions, whether the INN's invertibility advantage persists at larger scale, and whether the approach transfers to real-world (non-synthetic) monocular video without the clean multi-view supervision used for dataset construction.
