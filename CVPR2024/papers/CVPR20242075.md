## Problem

Neural rendering methods for novel view synthesis rely on pre-computed camera poses from SfM pipelines like COLMAP, which are slow and fail on textureless or repetitive scenes. Prior attempts to eliminate this dependency (BARF, NeRFmm, Nope-NeRF) either require near-ground-truth initial poses, are restricted to forward-facing scenes, or take ~30 hours to train and degrade under large camera motions such as 360° orbits. The implicit representation of NeRFs makes joint optimization of camera poses and scene geometry particularly difficult, as poses can only be updated indirectly through ray-casting gradients.

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporally ordered sequence of unposed RGB images along with known camera intrinsics as input, and produces per-frame camera poses (quaternion rotation + translation) plus a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene that renders novel views in real time. A monocular depth network (DPT) is used only for initial point lifting, not as a training loss. Because the pipeline processes frames sequentially, it is restricted to video streams or ordered image collections rather than unordered photo sets.

## Method

CF-3DGS exploits the explicit point-cloud nature of 3D Gaussians to decouple pose estimation from scene optimization via a two-level strategy. A *local* 3DGS initializes a small Gaussian set from monocular depth for frame t, then optimizes a learnable SE(3) transformation (quaternion + translation) to align those Gaussians to frame t+1, yielding the relative pose in 5–10 seconds with all Gaussian attributes frozen. A *global* 3DGS then progressively grows the scene representation: each new frame's relative pose is chained to the first frame, the new frame is added to the optimization set, and the Gaussian set is densified at the same cadence as new frames arrive, keeping growth focused on previously unobserved regions. This sequential, frame-by-frame growth leverages temporal continuity to keep each pose update small and well-conditioned, avoiding the large-movement optimization failures that plague implicit NeRF-based approaches.

## Results

On Tanks and Temples, CF-3DGS achieves a mean PSNR of 31.28 dB, SSIM of 0.93, and LPIPS of 0.09 for novel view synthesis, outperforming the next-best baseline Nope-NeRF (26.34 dB PSNR) by nearly 5 dB. For camera pose estimation on the same dataset, CF-3DGS reports mean RPE_t of 0.041, RPE_r of 0.069°, and ATE of 0.004, matching or exceeding Nope-NeRF on most metrics. On the more challenging CO3D-V2 (360° object-centric videos), CF-3DGS achieves ~29.69 dB PSNR on the best scene and ~22.14 dB on the hardest, while reducing pose errors (RPE_t, RPE_r, ATE) by 2–5× over Nope-NeRF. Training takes approximately 2 hours per scene versus ~30 hours for Nope-NeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CF-3DGS decisively outperforms all four NeRF-based pose-unknown baselines (Nope-NeRF, BARF, NeRFmm, SC-NeRF) on novel view synthesis across both Tanks and Temples and CO3D-V2, with a consistent 4–9 dB PSNR advantage over the strongest competitor Nope-NeRF. On camera pose estimation for Tanks and Temples (small camera motion), CF-3DGS is on par with Nope-NeRF: it achieves lower RPE_t (0.041 vs 0.080) and ATE (0.004 vs 0.006) but slightly higher RPE_r (0.069° vs 0.038°), which the authors attribute to relying solely on photometric loss without the chamfer-distance constraint used by Nope-NeRF. On CO3D-V2 with large 360° camera motions, CF-3DGS surpasses Nope-NeRF by a wide margin on all pose metrics (e.g., RPE_r drops from ~1.9° to ~0.4°), demonstrating substantially greater robustness. Compared to the original 3DGS trained with COLMAP poses (Table 7), CF-3DGS achieves comparable quality (31.28 vs 30.20 dB mean PSNR), confirming that the SfM-free pipeline does not sacrifice rendering fidelity. Training time is ~2 hours per scene versus ~30 hours for Nope-NeRF, a 15× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), originally designed for posed-view rendering, as a vehicle for joint camera pose estimation and novel view synthesis without SfM preprocessing. It departs from the NeRF-based pose-estimation lineage (BARF, NeRFmm, Nope-NeRF, SC-NeRF) by exploiting the explicit point-cloud representation: relative pose estimation reduces to optimizing a small SE(3) transformation on a frozen local Gaussian set, avoiding the indirect ray-casting optimization that makes NeRF-based approaches fragile under large motion. The progressive-growing strategy—sequentially ingesting frames and densifying the global Gaussian set—leverages video temporal continuity in a way that prior NeRF methods could not easily replicate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the COLMAP dependency eliminates a major practical bottleneck and failure mode (textureless/repetitive regions) in 3DGS and NeRF pipelines, and the ~15× speedup over Nope-NeRF (2 h vs. 30 h) makes pose-free reconstruction far more accessible. The near-parity with COLMAP-initialized 3DGS (Table 7) suggests the SfM step may be dispensable for many real-world applications. However, the method is restricted to ordered video streams; extending it to unordered image collections (where temporal continuity is absent) remains an open and non-trivial challenge that limits its immediate applicability to the broader photo-collection use case.
