## Problem

Previous gaze following architectures are single-person, requiring a separate forward pass for each individual in a scene, which is highly inefficient especially when additional modalities are involved. While recent transformer-based multi-person methods (Tu et al., Tonini et al.) exist, they use a fixed set of learnable embeddings to jointly decode person identity and gaze target, necessitating a post-hoc matching step that makes reliable evaluation and integration into larger systems difficult. This paper addresses both limitations by maintaining the original per-person task formulation while enabling simultaneous multi-person prediction.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB scene image (224×224) and, for each detected person, a head crop (224×224) and normalized bounding box coordinates in [0,1]⁴, all obtained from an off-the-shelf head detector trained on CrowdHuman. It produces a 64×64 gaze heatmap per person, a binary in-vs-out label, and a normalized 2D gaze direction vector. The number of people processed at inference is unconstrained by the architecture, though training uses a fixed N_p^tr (default 2) with padding for missing detections.

## Method

Sharingan encodes each person's gaze information into a single location-aware gaze token by passing the head crop through a ResNet-18 gaze backbone (pretrained on Gaze360), projecting the resulting embedding to the token dimension, and adding a learned bounding-box embedding. These gaze tokens are concatenated with ViT-base image tokens (initialized from a multimodal MAE) and processed jointly through a standard transformer encoder, allowing scene and person information to interact via self-attention. A novel Conditional DPT decoder then performs multi-scale gaze prediction by progressively fusing image feature maps at four resolutions with person-specific conditioning via element-wise dot-product between each gaze token and the corresponding image features, followed by residual convnet fusion and upsampling. Training uses a combined loss of pixel-wise MSE on the heatmap, angular loss on the gaze vector, and binary cross-entropy for in-out classification, with the model first trained on GazeFollow and then fine-tuned on video datasets by unfreezing only the decoder and in-out head.

## Results

On GazeFollow, Sharingan achieves AUC 0.944, Avg. Dist. 0.113, Min. Dist. 0.057, and PLAH 0.667, setting new state-of-the-art on all metrics. On VideoAttentionTarget (fine-tuned), it reaches Dist. 0.107, PLAH 0.738, and AP 0.891; on ChildPlay, Dist. 0.106, PLAH 0.600, and AP 0.990. Notably, the model trained only on GazeFollow without fine-tuning already outperforms the prior multi-person method (Jin [16]) by 0.013 on Avg. Dist. (0.113 vs 0.126) and 0.02 on Min. Dist. (0.057 vs 0.076), demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sharingan outperforms the only prior multi-person method (Jin [16]) by 0.013 on Avg. Dist. (0.113 vs 0.126) and 0.02 on Min. Dist. (0.057 vs 0.076) on GazeFollow, and by 0.027 on Dist. (0.107 vs 0.134) on VideoAttentionTarget, all using only RGB images. Against the strongest single-person multimodal method (Gupta [12], I+D+P), Sharingan matches on Avg. Dist. (0.113 vs 0.114) and Min. Dist. (0.057 vs 0.056) on GazeFollow while requiring no depth or pose inputs and supporting multi-person inference in a single forward pass. On VideoAttentionTarget, Sharingan (0.107) beats Gupta [12] (0.110) and Tafasca [35] (0.109); on ChildPlay, Sharingan (0.106) edges out Tafasca [35] (0.107) and Gupta [12] (0.113). The GazeFollow-trained model without fine-tuning already surpasses all single-person baselines on cross-dataset evaluation (e.g., Dist. 0.113 vs 0.134 for Gupta [12] image-only on VideoAttentionTarget), highlighting strong generalization. Sharingan also achieves the best AP on VideoAttentionTarget (0.891) and ChildPlay (0.990) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sharingan departs from the dominant 2-tower CNN paradigm in gaze following (scene saliency branch + head-crop gaze branch) by unifying both into a single transformer where person-specific information is compressed into one controlled gaze token that interacts with image tokens via self-attention. It builds directly on DPT's multi-scale decoding architecture, extending it to be conditioned on person tokens (Conditional DPT), and draws on DETR-style set prediction ideas from Tu et al. and Tonini et al. while explicitly rejecting their fixed-embedding-plus-matching formulation in favor of the original task setup where head boxes are given. The gaze backbone (ResNet-18 pretrained on Gaze360) and the ViT encoder (initialized from multimodal MAE) connect the work to standard pretraining pipelines, but the key architectural departure is the single-token person encoding that replaces the attention maps and gaze cones used in prior CNN-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to predict gaze for all people in a scene with a single forward pass addresses a practical bottleneck that has limited the deployment of gaze following in real-world multi-person settings (robotics, social scene understanding). The strong cross-dataset generalization (GazeFollow-trained model outperforming the only comparable multi-person method on VideoAttentionTarget without fine-tuning) suggests the architecture captures transferable scene-person interaction priors. The paper's main limitation is that image and gaze tokens share the same transformer weights, making it difficult to disentangle how scene and person information are combined; the authors suggest selective fusion as a future direction. Broader implications include extending the architecture to richer social scene analysis (gestures, interactions, depth) and to video with temporal modeling.
