## Problem

Medical foundation models pre-trained on large-scale diverse datasets exhibit strong general feature extraction but underperform task-specific methods on individual medical tasks, and their growing deployment costs are prohibitive for hospital-level use. No prior work has explored decomposing a medical foundation model into multiple lightweight, domain-specific expert models that retain both specialization and transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242012/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes medical images (X-rays, histopathology slides, ultrasound, etc.) as input and outputs class labels for specific anatomical regions or conditions. During training, each mini-batch carries a one-hot task label vector M ∈ R^{B×T} that routes samples to the corresponding expert module; during deployment, parameter fusion collapses the expert into the shared ShuffleNetV2 backbone so the deployed model has identical size and FLOPs to a standard single-task model (1.25M params, 5.32% compression ratio vs. the 23.51M-param ResNet-50 foundation model).

## Method

LoRKD decomposes a foundation model into a shared backbone F_s (ShuffleNetV2) plus T low-rank expert modules, where each convolution W_0 is augmented per task t by a rank-r factor pair (B_t, A_t) so the t-th task computes (W_0 + B_t A_t)h_t. Unlike standard LoRA, W_0 is also updated to accumulate common knowledge across tasks. The key efficiency innovation is the Efficient Knowledge Separation Convolution (EKS Conv.), which uses the one-hot task label M and a Hadamard product to aggregate all T expert weights into a single per-sample weight W' ∈ R^{B×C_out×C_in×k×k}, implemented as a group convolution with group number B, so all T tasks are processed in one forward pass rather than T separate passes. Training combines task-level cross-entropy with KL-divergence distillation from the frozen foundation model, and after training, task knowledge is switched at deployment by simply adding or removing the low-rank product B_t A_t from W_0, keeping the deployed model size constant.

## Results

On the three pre-training datasets, LoRKD achieves the best average accuracy: 56.26% on Radimagenet (11 tasks), 83.50% on MedMNIST (10 tasks), and 75.73% on Med-MT (8 tasks), while using only ~2M parameters (vs. 23.51M for the foundation model). On downstream transferability (7 datasets), LoRKD expert models reach 84.18% average accuracy when pre-trained on Radimagenet, outperforming the foundation model (80.34%) and all baselines. The method also achieves the highest mutual information gap (MIG) scores, confirming stronger knowledge disentanglement compared to KF and other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KF (Yang et al., ECCV 2022), the closest knowledge-decomposition method, LoRKD wins decisively on all three pre-training datasets: +5.25% on Radimagenet (56.26 vs. 51.01), +25.63% on MedMNIST (83.50 vs. 57.87), and +12.64% on Med-MT (75.73 vs. 63.09), while using less than half the parameters (2.21M vs. 5.01M on Radimagenet). Against MoCo-MTL (Fernando et al., ICLR 2022), LoRKD achieves +0.56% on Radimagenet, +7.71% on MedMNIST, and +23.72% on Med-MT, with the largest gap on Med-MT where MoCo-MTL collapses to 52.01%. Against Aligned-MTL (Senushkin et al., CVPR 2023), LoRKD leads by +0.47% on Radimagenet, +1.81% on MedMNIST, and +22.73% on Med-MT. On downstream transferability (Table 2), LoRKD also outperforms KF by +3.52% average on Radimagenet-pretrained models (84.18 vs. 80.66) and +2.81% on MedMNIST-pretrained models (83.08 vs. 80.27), while deploying at the same parameter count as the baseline (1.25M) thanks to parameter fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LoRA low-rank factorization (originally designed for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of frozen backbones) as a mechanism for explicit knowledge decomposition, where the shared backbone W₀ is no longer frozen but co-trained as a common-knowledge carrier alongside task-specific low-rank expert modules. It departs from knowledge distillation, which transfers complete knowledge into a single student, by instead splitting knowledge into separate lightweight experts, and from multi-task learning, which optimizes a shared encoder for all tasks simultaneously, by explicitly separating gradients per task. The closest prior work is KF (Factorizing Knowledge in Neural Networks), which decomposes a model into a common knowledge network and task-specific networks via mutual information optimization; LoRKD replaces this with explicit gradient separation through a novel EKS Convolution that achieves per-sample parameter aggregation in a single forward pass via group convolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck for medical foundation models: the tension between generality (large, expensive models) and specialization (task-specific performance). The parameter fusion mechanism ensures that each deployed expert model has identical size and FLOPs to the base architecture (ShuffleNetV2), making the approach directly deployable in resource-constrained clinical settings without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to transformer-based medical foundation models, 3D volumetric imaging, and whether the number of decomposable experts scales gracefully beyond the 8–11 tasks tested. The method is currently validated only on CNN backbones and 2D medical images, limiting immediate applicability to the growing ecosystem of vision-transformer and diffusion-based medical models.
