## Problem

Existing multi-view photometric stereo (MVPS) methods that fuse per-view normal maps into 3D shapes either smooth out high-frequency surface details or require hours of optimization, making scanner-level reconstruction impractical. The core gap is the lack of a fast and accurate multi-view normal fusion method that can exploit the expressive power of modern neural implicit representations without incurring prohibitive training costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240225/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view surface normal maps (estimated by photometric stereo, e.g., SDM-UniPS), binary object masks, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. Normal maps are initially in camera space and are rotated into world space before use. During training, the method samples 2048 patches of 3×3 pixels per batch from all input views, with the scene bounded in a unit sphere. The output is a learned neural SDF (multi-resolution hash encoding + shallow MLP) whose zero-level set defines the 3D surface, extractable as a mesh.

## Method

SuperNormal optimizes a neural SDF parameterized by multi-resolution hash encoding and a shallow MLP via SDF-based volume rendering (NeuS-style sigmoid opacity), supervised by normal consistency, mask BCE, and eikonal regularization. The key efficiency contribution is directional finite difference (DFD): instead of axis-aligned finite difference requiring six extra SDF evaluations per point, DFD computes the SDF gradient by solving a 3×3 linear system using finite differences along three non-coplanar directions (the viewing ray plus two image-plane axes), reusing SDF samples already computed for volume rendering. To make this reuse possible, the paper introduces patch-based ray marching: a 3×3 pixel patch is sampled, and a plane parallel to the image plane is marched along the center ray, with neighboring rays intersecting the same plane so their SDF values serve as spatial neighbors for DFD. This eliminates second-order backpropagation through the hash encoding and avoids redundant SDF evaluations, yielding ~2× speedup over automatic differentiation and ~3× over axis-aligned finite difference with no measurable quality loss.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT-MV benchmark (5 objects, 20 views each), SuperNormal achieves the best average L2 Chamfer distance of 0.194 mm and the best F-score of 0.962 (τ_F = 0.5 mm), outperforming all compared MVPS methods. The method completes reconstruction in 48 seconds on a single RTX 4090Ti, making it the fastest neural implicit-surface-based method (vs. 70 min for MVAS and 8 hrs for PS-NeRF) and only slightly slower than the feedforward MVPSNet (37 s). The ablation on SDF gradient computation (Table 3) shows DFD reduces total training time from 78.1 s (AD) to 48.3 s while maintaining equivalent accuracy (CD 0.194 vs. 0.195).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperNormal outperforms all five neural MVPS baselines on both Chamfer distance and F-score. Against the closest neural competitor MVAS [4] (CD 0.307, F 0.816), SuperNormal improves CD by 37% and F-score by 14.6 points while being 87× faster (48 s vs. 70 min). Against PS-NeRF [31] (CD 0.293, F 0.853), the gains are 34% in CD and 10.9 points in F-score, with a 600× speedup (48 s vs. 8 hrs). Against the classical B-MVPS [16] (CD 0.203, F 0.941), SuperNormal edges ahead on both metrics (CD 0.194, F 0.962) without requiring manual mesh initialization. Against MVPSNet [35], which is the only method faster (37 s), SuperNormal still achieves substantially better geometry (CD 0.194 vs. 0.282, F 0.962 vs. 0.865), demonstrating that the 11-second overhead buys meaningful quality. R-MVPS [23] and UA-MVPS [12] are clearly outperformed, with R-MVPS showing the worst CD (0.794) and F-score (0.487) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperNormal sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: neural implicit surface reconstruction via SDF-based volume rendering (NeuS, NeuS2, VolSDF) and multi-view photometric stereo (PS-NeRF, MVAS, UA-MVPS). It departs from prior neural MVPS methods by replacing color-image supervision with normal-map supervision and by introducing multi-resolution hash encoding for the SDF, which prior MVPS methods (which relied on dense MLPs) did not exploit. The key technical departure is directional finite difference combined with patch-based ray marching, which reuses volume-rendering SDF samples for gradient approximation—avoiding both the double-backpropagation problem that forced CUDA re-implementations in NeuS2/PermutoSDF and the redundant SDF evaluations of axis-aligned finite difference used in Neuralangelo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SuperNormal reduces neural MVPS runtime from hours to under a minute while achieving the best geometry accuracy on the DiLiGenT-MV benchmark, and the DFD technique is demonstrated to be a general accelerator for any SDF-based volume rendering pipeline (validated on Neuralangelo/DTU). This makes scanner-level 3D reconstruction feasible with a smartphone, a video light, and a turntable, lowering the barrier to high-fidelity 3D capture. Open questions include the unresolved fault artifacts (present regardless of gradient computation strategy), the method's sensitivity to normal-map quality (it fails with monocular normal estimators like OmniData), and whether the approach extends to scene-level or dynamic reconstruction.
