## Problem

Diffusion models for pose-guided human video generation produce temporally inconsistent results (flickering, jittering, identity drift) due to the inherent stochasticity of the denoising sampling process. Existing remedies—spatio-temporal attention, large-scale motion dataset training, or architecture modifications—fail to eliminate this inconsistency because it is rooted in the randomization of each independent sampling step rather than in the model's learned representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241682/cropped_page0_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image of the target character (used for CLIP identity conditioning), a sequence of driving poses in either OpenPose keypoints or DensePose format, and a single video of the target character for personalization finetuning. It outputs a 512×512 temporally consistent human performance video where the character's appearance remains coherent across all frames while following the prescribed motion. Notably, no large-scale motion dataset is required—only one video of the target person is needed for adaptation.

## Method

DiffPerformer constructs an iterative optimization loop between a pose-guided video diffusion model (VideoComposer finetuned on a single video with pose conditioning and CLIP reference embeddings) and a hashing-based implicit video representation (IVR) that factorizes the video into a canonical texture space and a temporal deformation field. The IVR 'distills' the noisy diffusion output into a temporally smooth video, which is then encoded into VAE latent space and used as the denoising initialization for the diffusion model to recover fine-grained details—closing a mutually beneficial feedback loop. Crucially, noise is added in the VAE latent space rather than pixel space to preserve the temporal consistency of the guidance signal. A 3D-aware human flow constraint, computed from SMPL meshes with the driving pose shape transformed to match the reference character, explicitly enforces pose-appearance correspondence and corrects misalignment under large-magnitude motions. The VAE decoder is also finetuned on the target video to improve photorealistic detail recovery.

## Results

On the test poses, DiffPerformer achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: PSNR 30.72, SSIM 0.69, LPIPS 0.22, FID 36.00, L1 4.33×10⁻⁵, FID-VID 22.32, and FVD 254.39. The method leads the closest GAN-based competitor Fast-Vid2Vid by 0.32 dB in PSNR and 44.4 in FVD, while outperforming the two diffusion-based baselines (DreamPose and DisCo) by substantially larger margins on both image-level and video-level metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPerformer outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the GAN-based Fast-Vid2Vid, the gains are moderate but consistent: +0.32 dB PSNR, +0.05 SSIM, −0.07 LPIPS, and −44.4 FVD, indicating better temporal coherence while matching per-frame quality. Against DreamPose, the advantage is dramatic across the board—PSNR improves by 2.72 dB, SSIM by 0.27, and FVD drops from 529.13 to 254.39 (a 52% reduction)—reflecting DreamPose's well-documented struggles with challenging motions and identity drift. Against DisCo, DiffPerformer leads by +0.99 dB PSNR, +0.27 SSIM, and −71.73 FVD; notably, DisCo achieves a slightly lower FID-VID (23.67 vs 22.32 is actually a win for DiffPerformer), confirming that the implicit video representation and 3D-aware human flow together yield superior temporal consistency. Qualitatively, the paper reports that Fast-Vid2Vid and DisCo produce facial artifacts, DreamPose loses face identity and body shape, while DiffPerformer preserves identity and pose alignment even under large-magnitude motions such as dancing.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffPerformer builds on VideoComposer as its pretrained video diffusion backbone and adopts CoDef's temporal deformation field design for its implicit video representation, augmented with InstantNGP's multi-resolution hash encoding to capture high-frequency details. Conceptually, it departs from prior pose-guided video generation methods (DreamPose, DisCo, Ma et al.) that attempt to fix temporal inconsistency through architecture modifications (spatio-temporal attention, disentangled control) or large-scale training, by instead borrowing the text-to-3D optimization paradigm—where a 3D proxy enforces view consistency—and repurposing it for the temporal domain. The iterative joint optimization loop between the smooth implicit representation and the generative diffusion model, along with the 3D-aware human flow loss, are the paper's primary architectural and loss-function contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, lightweight alternative to the prevailing strategy of modifying diffusion architectures for temporal consistency, showing that a single-video finetuning plus an implicit representation proxy can substantially reduce flickering without large-scale datasets. However, the method is constrained to a single character per video, does not handle moving cameras well, and incurs significant runtime cost due to the iterative diffusion sampling. Open questions include whether the implicit-representation-as-proxy idea generalizes to multi-character scenes, longer sequences, or other controllable generation tasks, and whether the iterative loop can be accelerated or replaced by a learned surrogate.
