## Problem

Single-image 3D generation via multi-view diffusion models faces an inherent trade-off between visual consistency with the input view and diversity of novel views, yet existing methods (e.g., SyncDreamer) apply a single classifier-free guidance scale that simultaneously affects both dimensions, preventing independent control. Additionally, no standardized metric exists for quantitatively assessing the diversity of generated multi-view images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242043/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (arbitrary resolution, following SyncDreamer's conditioning) and produces N=16 novel-view images at predefined camera viewpoints, which are then fed into NeuS for 3D mesh reconstruction. The pipeline also supports a text-to-image-to-3D mode where a text prompt is first rendered into a 2D image by an off-the-shelf text-to-image model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) before entering the multi-view generation stage. Generation of 4 diverse instances × 16 views completes in ~1 minute on an A100 GPU with 50 DDIM steps.

## Method

HarmonyView decomposes the single classifier-free guidance scale s in multi-view diffusion sampling into two independent scales: s₁ controls visual consistency between the target view and the input view, while s₂ controls diversity across novel views. This is achieved by introducing two implicit classifiers derived via Bayes' rule: p(r⁽ⁿ⁾|xₜ⁽ⁿ⁾, xₜ⁽¹ˑᴺ⁾) for input-target consistency and p(xₜ⁽¹ˑᴺ⁾|xₜ⁽ⁿ⁾, r⁽ⁿ⁾) for multi-view diversity, each yielding a separate score gradient term. The final noise prediction (Eq. 7) sums the base conditional prediction with s₁-weighted and s₂-weighted correction terms, enabling independent tuning of consistency and diversity without retraining. The paper also introduces the CD score, a reference-free metric computed as the ratio of CLIP-image-encoder-based diversity to CLIP-text-encoder-based semantic variance, which closely correlates with human diversity judgments.

## Results

On the GSO dataset for 3D reconstruction, HarmonyView achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.0187 and volumetric IoU of 0.6401, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline SyncDreamer (0.0261 / 0.5421) by ~28% and ~18% respectively. For novel-view synthesis, HarmonyView scores PSNR 20.69, SSIM 0.825, LPIPS 0.133, E_flow 1.945, and CD score 0.792, leading all baselines on every metric. On in-the-wild images, HarmonyView attains the highest CD score (0.804) and top user Likert ratings across quality (3.958), consistency (3.479), and diversity (3.813).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HarmonyView outperforms all seven baselines on both Chamfer distance and volumetric IoU for 3D reconstruction. The closest competitor, SyncDreamer [19], is beaten by 28% in Chamfer distance (0.0187 vs. 0.0261) and 18% in IoU (0.6401 vs. 0.5421), reflecting superior multi-view geometric coherence. Zero123 [18] trails by 45% in Chamfer and 27% in IoU, while SDS-based methods (Realfusion [22], Magic123 [29]) and direct 3D generators (Point-E [25], Shap-E [13]) lag further behind, with Realfusion showing the weakest IoU (0.2741). One-2-3-45 [17] achieves a moderate IoU of 0.4086 but a relatively high Chamfer of 0.0629, indicating shape-level errors. In the novel-view synthesis task (Table 2), HarmonyView also leads on all five metrics, with the largest gains over SyncDreamer in E_flow (1.945 vs. 2.071) and CD score (0.792 vs. 0.717), confirming that the decomposed guidance improves both consistency and diversity simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

HarmonyView builds directly on SyncDreamer's multi-view diffusion architecture, which jointly models N novel views conditioned on a single input view using classifier-free guidance. The key departure is a conceptual decomposition of the single guidance scale in Eq. (3) into two independent scales (s₁ for input-view consistency, s₂ for novel-view diversity), derived via Bayes' rule from two implicit classifiers. This positions the work within the broader 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm (Zero123, Viewset Diffusion, Magic123) but contributes a sampling-time technique rather than a new architecture or training objective, and introduces the CD score as a reference-free diversity metric absent from prior evaluation protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and previously underexplored tension in single-image 3D generation: the trade-off between multi-view geometric coherence and novel-view diversity. The decomposition provides practitioners with explicit, interpretable knobs to tune this balance, and the CD score fills a practical gap in evaluation. Limitations include evaluation primarily on the GSO household-object dataset, dependence on a specific multi-view diffusion backbone (SyncDreamer), and the additional forward pass cost. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes, whether the CD score remains discriminative across diverse object categories, and whether similar decompositions apply to other conditional diffusion settings beyond multi-view generation.
