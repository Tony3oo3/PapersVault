## Problem

No existing method can detect procedural mistakes in egocentric videos in an online (causal) and open-set manner. Prior approaches are either offline, requiring full-video segmentation before flagging errors, or supervised, requiring labeled examples of specific mistake types, making them unsuitable for real-time monitoring where novel, unseen failures may occur.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241976/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes egocentric video frames sequentially up to the current timestep τ (causal, no future access), using pre-extracted TSN frame-level features for the recognition branch. The anticipation branch receives a text prompt containing context sequences from correct procedures and the current symbolic action history. Output is a per-step binary mistake/correct label derived from the alignment of the two branches, along with the recognized and anticipated action labels. The full pipeline runs at 0.02 fps on a single NVIDIA Quadro P6000.

## Method

PREGO is a dual-branch architecture: a step-recognition branch (MiniRoad, an RNN-based online action detector) classifies the current action from frames up to time τ, while a step-anticipation branch uses a pre-trained LLM (GPT-3.5 or LLAMA) in a zero-shot in-context learning setup to predict the next action from the action history. The key insight is mapping actions to a symbolic alphabet (numerical indices, semantic labels, or random symbols) so the LLM operates as a pure pattern-completion machine over sequences, abstracting away semantic content. Mistake detection is a simple binary comparison: a step is flagged as erroneous when the recognized action diverges from the anticipated one. Training follows a one-class paradigm—only correctly executed procedures are used—so the model learns normal procedural flows and flags any deviation, requiring no fine-tuning of the LLM and no labeled mistakes.

## Results

On Assembly101-O, PREGO with oracle step recognition and LLAMA achieves the best F1 score of 46.3 (Precision 30.7, Recall 94.0), outperforming BERT (F1 31.8) by 45.6% and OadTR for MD (F1 20.7) by 124%. With the MiniRoad step recognition module (non-oracle), PREGO+LLAMA still reaches F1 41.8 on Assembly101-O, more than doubling OadTR's F1 of 20.7. On Epic-tent-O, the best PREGO configuration (Oracle+LLAMA) achieves F1 19.1, while the non-oracle PREGO+MiniRoad+LLAMA configuration scores F1 12.0, still surpassing OadTR (F1 10.2) and BERT (F1 10.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PREGO outperforms all baselines on both datasets. Against BERT (oracle setting), PREGO+LLAMA achieves a 45.6% F1 improvement on Assembly101-O (46.3 vs. 31.8), primarily due to much higher recall (94.0 vs. 20.0) at the cost of lower precision (30.7 vs. 78.2), indicating BERT is overly conservative and misses most mistakes. Against OadTR for MD (the only non-oracle video-based baseline), PREGO with the same AodTR step recognition module and LLAMA anticipation achieves a 102% F1 improvement on Assembly101-O (35.8 vs. 20.7), attributed to OadTR's fixed 64-frame window being insufficient for procedures averaging 7 minutes. The One-step memory baseline (F1 21.3 on Assembly101-O) confirms that considering only the immediately preceding action is far too limited for procedural reasoning. Notably, PREGO's advantage is most pronounced in recall, suggesting that symbolic LLM-based anticipation captures long-range procedural dependencies that frame-level video methods and simple transition matrices cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

PREGO combines two previously separate threads: online action detection (via MiniROAD's RNN-based step recognition) and LLM-based symbolic pattern completion (following the 'LLMs as general pattern machines' framing of Mirchandani et al.). It departs from prior procedural mistake detection work (Assembly101, HoloAssist, ATA), which is uniformly offline and supervised, by formulating the problem as one-class classification where only correct procedures are seen at training time. The key architectural departure is replacing video-based anticipation (as in OadTR) with a zero-shot LLM that reasons over symbolic action sequences, effectively subsuming action inter-dependency graphs with implicit pattern learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to address online, open-set procedural mistake detection in egocentric videos, a setting directly relevant to real-time safety monitoring in manufacturing and healthcare. However, absolute performance remains modest (best F1 ≈ 46% on Assembly101-O), and the video recognition branch is the clear bottleneck (oracle vs. non-oracle gap of ~11–54% in F1). The small dataset sizes (190 and 29 procedures) and 0.02 fps throughput limit immediate deployment, and the authors explicitly flag the need for large-scale procedural datasets as future work. The finding that random symbols outperform semantic labels for sequence anticipation raises an open question about whether procedural structure is purely syntactic rather than semantic.
