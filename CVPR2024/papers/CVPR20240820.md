## Problem

Existing text-conditioned image-to-video (TI2V) generation methods require computationally expensive training on video-text datasets and specialized architecture designs to jointly condition on both text and image inputs. This paper asks whether a pretrained text-to-video (T2V) diffusion model, trained only for text conditioning, can be repurposed for TI2V generation at inference time without any optimization, fine-tuning, or introduction of external modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240820/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB starting image and a free-form text prompt as input, and produces a video of M+1 frames (default 16 frames at 256×256) where the first frame is exactly the provided image. Generation is autoregressive—one new frame is synthesized per complete diffusion sampling pass—so the output length is unbounded in principle (128-frame videos are demonstrated). The starting image may be a real photograph or a frame synthesized by the backbone T2V model itself.

## Method

TI2V-Zero operates entirely at inference time on a frozen ModelScopeT2V latent diffusion model (3D U-Net denoiser with a frame autoencoder). The core mechanism is a 'repeat-and-slide' strategy: a queue of K latent frames is initialized by repeating the encoded starting image, and at each reverse denoising step the first K frames of the latent sequence are replaced with noisy versions of this queue, forcing the temporal attention layers to condition exclusively on the provided context while the U-Net predicts only the single new frame. After each frame is generated, the queue slides forward by dequeuing the oldest latent and enqueuing the new one. To improve temporal coherence, DDPM-based inversion (adding T full noise steps to the queue) replaces random Gaussian initialization, and a multi-step resampling loop (borrowed from Repaint inpainting) is applied within each denoising step to harmonize predicted and conditioning latents. No parameters are trained or updated; the entire pipeline is a sampling-time modulation of the frozen model.

## Results

On the MUG dataset, TI2V-Zero with resampling achieves FVD 180.09, sFVD 267.17±74.72, and tFVD 252.77±39.02, substantially outperforming DynamiCrafter (FVD 1094.72, sFVD 1359.86±257.73, tFVD 1223.89±105.94). On UCF-101, TI2V-Zero with resampling reaches FVD 477.19 and tFVD 1306.75±271.82, again beating DynamiCrafter (FVD 589.59, tFVD 1540.02±199.59). All results are for 16-frame videos at 256×256 resolution, with 1000 generated videos used for distribution estimation. The OPEN dataset (open-domain, no ground truth) is evaluated qualitatively only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DynamiCrafter [67], a recent open-domain TI2V framework built on the VideoCrafter1 T2V foundation model that requires a learnable image encoding network and full fine-tuning to enable image conditioning. TI2V-Zero, which is entirely zero-shot (no fine-tuning, no additional modules), outperforms DynamiCrafter on every reported metric: on MUG it reduces FVD from 1094.72 to 180.09 (≈6× improvement), sFVD from 1359.86 to 267.17, and tFVD from 1223.89 to 252.77; on UCF-101 it lowers FVD from 589.59 to 477.19 and tFVD from 1540.02 to 1306.75. The second comparison point is the backbone ModelScopeT2V [60] itself (used as a reference in Table 2): TI2V-Zero-Fake (starting from ModelScopeT2V-generated frames) produces more realistic videos than ModelScopeT2V alone (FVD 366.41 vs. 985.82 against real videos), confirming that the zero-shot image conditioning adds meaningful value beyond the base T2V model. Qualitatively, DynamiCrafter struggles to preserve identity details from the input image and produces less diverse motion, whereas TI2V-Zero faithfully starts from the given frame and maintains subject appearance across the generated sequence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against prior TI2V frameworks (MAGE, DragNUWA, DynamiCrafter) that require costly training on video-text datasets or additional learnable modules for image conditioning. It departs from this paradigm by treating a frozen pretrained T2V latent diffusion model (ModelScopeT2V) as a generative prior and manipulating only its inference-time sampling process. The key technical ingredients—DDPM-based inversion for noise initialization and iterative resampling for detail preservation—are borrowed from the image inpainting/editing literature (Repaint, DDPM) and repurposed here for video frame synthesis. The novel 'repeat-and-slide' strategy, which forces temporal attention to attend exclusively to provided/synthesized frames rather than model-generated ones, is the paper's core architectural contribution, motivated by the observed failure of a naive frame-replacing baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, training-free nature of TI2V-Zero makes it immediately applicable to any image without domain-specific fine-tuning, and its autoregressive design naturally extends to long video generation (demonstrated at 128 frames). However, the method is constrained by the backbone model's quality, produces occasional blurring and flickering artifacts, and is considerably slower than GAN or standard video diffusion approaches since a full diffusion process runs per frame. Open questions include whether the repeat-and-slide strategy transfers to more powerful video diffusion foundation models, whether faster ODE-based samplers (DPM-Solver) can reduce the per-frame cost, and whether the approach generalizes beyond 256×256 resolution and 16-frame clips where it was evaluated.
