## Problem

Image stitching inherently produces non-rectangular boundaries that are visually unappealing. Existing rectangling solutions each suffer from a critical flaw: cropping discards image content, inpainting introduces unrelated content, and warping-based methods distort non-linear structures and introduce edge artifacts and discontinuities. This paper reformulates rectangling as a generative problem solvable by diffusion models rather than a regression or warping problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242588/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stitched RGB image with irregular (non-rectangular) boundary and a binary mask delineating valid image content as input, and produces a geometrically corrected rectangular-boundary image as output. The MDM operates at 256×192 or 512×384 resolution with only 2 DDIM sampling steps, while the CDM refinement stage uses 200 sampling steps. The final output preserves the original scene content while eliminating white margins and boundary artifacts.

## Method

RecDiffusion is a two-stage diffusion framework. First, a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) generates a rectangling motion field via an image-to-motion diffusion process conditioned on the stitched image and its mask, then warps the input to produce a geometrically correct but artifact-laden intermediate. Second, a Content Diffusion Model (CDM) refines this intermediate through an image-to-image diffusion process. The key innovation is a weighted sampling mask (WSM) inspired by the Rank-Nullity Theorem: a confidence mask is computed from three sub-masks (input validity, displacement magnitude, and white-edge detection), and during each CDM sampling step, confident regions retain the warped image content while non-confident regions are replaced by CDM-generated content, formalized as r' = M√(ᾱ_t)r̂ + (I−M)r'. This strategy combines the geometric accuracy of warping with the generative fidelity of diffusion to eliminate local distortions and restore missing boundary content.

## Results

On the DIR-D test set, RecDiffusion achieves FID 3.63, SSIM 0.7733, and PSNR 22.21, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art deep rectangling method (Nie et al.) by 0.51 in FID, 0.059 in SSIM, and 0.93 dB in PSNR. Against inpainting baselines (Palette, Stable Diffusion 2.1), RecDiffusion improves SSIM by over 0.44 and PSNR by over 7.7 dB, while also achieving a substantially lower FID (3.63 vs. 15.58 for SD 2.1). Zero-shot generalization on the APAP-conssite dataset further confirms robustness, with RecDiffusion producing fewer structural artifacts than both He et al. and Nie et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RecDiffusion outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against the traditional warping method of He et al. [9], it improves SSIM from 0.3775 to 0.7733 and PSNR from 14.70 to 22.21, while He et al. does not report FID. Against the previous deep-learning SOTA of Nie et al. [32], RecDiffusion gains 0.51 in FID (3.63 vs. 4.14), 0.059 in SSIM (0.7733 vs. 0.7141), and 0.93 dB in PSNR (22.21 vs. 21.28), with qualitative gains in eliminating white-edge artifacts and reducing line discontinuities. Against inpainting baselines, the advantage is even more pronounced: Palette [38] scores SSIM 0.3315 / PSNR 14.49, and Stable Diffusion 2.1 [37] scores FID 15.58 / SSIM 0.3276 / PSNR 14.23, both far below RecDiffusion, confirming that generative inpainting introduces content inconsistent with the original scene. On the zero-shot APAP-conssite generalization test, RecDiffusion avoids the roof/branch twisting seen in He et al. and the chimney breakage and flower-bed displacement observed in Nie et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established rectangling paradigm of He et al. (2013) and Nie et al. (2022), which both rely on predicting a mesh or motion field and applying a deterministic warp to the stitched image. Instead, RecDiffusion reframes rectangling as a two-stage generative problem: a Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) produces the corrective motion field, and a Content Diffusion Model (CDM) refines residual artifacts, with the sampling loop guided by a weighted mask derived from the Rank-Nullity Theorem decomposition in Wang et al. (2023). The conceptual shift is from regression-based warping to iterative generative refinement, leveraging the denoising prior of diffusion models to handle the ill-posed nature of single-image motion prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RecDiffusion demonstrates that a general-purpose generative framework (DDIM + classifier-free guidance) can outperform task-specific regression architectures for rectangling, suggesting diffusion models may be a viable backbone for other motion-correction tasks (rotation, video stabilization, wide-angle rectification). The weighted sampling mask strategy—splitting confident and uncertain regions via an RNT-inspired decomposition—is a lightweight, transferable technique. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (DIR-D and APAP-conssite), the MDM is trained on pseudo-motion fields from Nie et al. rather than ground-truth annotations, and the modest quantitative gains (SSIM +0.06, PSNR +1 over the prior SOTA) leave open whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or more diverse scene types.
