## Problem

Dense feature matching methods that train backbones from scratch overfit to limited 3D supervision data, while frozen ImageNet features lack the robustness needed for challenging real-world changes in viewpoint, illumination, and scale. Although DINOv2 foundation model features are far more robust, they are inherently coarse and cannot provide the fine-grained localizability required for precise correspondences. Additionally, prior methods apply the same L2 regression loss at both coarse and fine stages, ignoring that the coarse matching distribution is multimodal while the conditional refinement is unimodal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240600/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RoMa takes two RGB images (trained at 560×560) and produces a dense warp W^{A→B} mapping every pixel in image A to a sub-pixel location in image B, along with a per-pixel matchability confidence score p(x^A). The warp is estimated in a coarse-to-fine cascade: an initial global match at coarse resolution (stride 14) is successively refined through four refiner stages (strides 8, 4, 2, 1), each predicting a residual offset and logit adjustment conditioned on the previous estimate.

## Method

RoMa decouples the feature encoder into a frozen DINOv2 ViT for coarse features and a trainable VGG19 ConvNet for fine features, exploiting the finding that features specialized for coarse matching or refinement outperform jointly-trained features. A 5-block Transformer decoder (8 heads, hidden dim 1024, no position encodings) replaces the standard ConvNet decoder, predicting a 64×64 grid of anchor probabilities rather than directly regressing coordinates, which enables multimodal representation. The loss is split into two paradigms motivated by a diffusion-based theoretical model: a regression-by-classification loss (negative log-likelihood over anchor bins) for the coarse global matcher, and a generalized Charbonnier robust regression loss for the refinement stages, whose gradients decay as |x|^{-1/2} to handle outliers.

## Results

RoMa sets a new state-of-the-art across all evaluated benchmarks. The headline result is a 36% improvement on the WxBS benchmark (mAA@10: 80.1 vs. DKM's 58.9), where most prior methods fail. On IMC2022, RoMa achieves mAA@10 of 88.0, a 26% relative error reduction over the previous best (ASpanFormer, 83.8). On MegaDepth-1500 pose estimation, RoMa reaches AUC@5° of 62.6 (vs. PMatch 61.4), and on ScanNet-1500 it achieves the first AUC@20° score above 70 (70.9 vs. DKM 68.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMa outperforms all 15 compared baselines on every benchmark. The most dramatic margin is on WxBS, where RoMa (80.1) surpasses DKM (58.9) by 21.2 points and LoFTR (55.4) by 24.7 points, while detector-based methods (DISK 35.5, SuperPoint+SuperGlue 31.4) lag far behind. On IMC2022, RoMa (88.0) beats the closest competitor ASpanFormer (83.8) by 4.2 points and DKM (83.1) by 4.9 points. On MegaDepth-1500, RoMa (AUC@5° 62.6) edges out PMatch (61.4) and DKM (60.4), with larger gaps over older methods like LoFTR (52.8) and LightGlue (51.0). On ScanNet-1500, RoMa (AUC@20° 70.9) leads DKM (68.3) and PMatch (67.4), while SuperGlue (51.8) and PDC-Net+ (57.1) trail substantially. On InLoc visual localization, RoMa (60.6/79.3/89.9 at 0.25m/0.5m/1.0m) improves over DKM (51.5/75.3/86.9) and CasMTR (53.5/76.8/85.4) on DUC1, confirming gains transfer to downstream pose estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoMa builds directly on DKM's coarse-to-fine dense matching pipeline (GP match encoder, sequential ConvNet refiners) but makes three key departures: replacing the trained/fine-tuned backbone with frozen DINOv2 features for coarse matching paired with VGG19 for fine features, substituting the ConvNet match decoder with a position-encoding-free Transformer that predicts anchor probabilities, and splitting the loss into regression-by-classification (coarse) and robust Charbonnier regression (fine). This positions the paper against the prevailing trend of training or fine-tuning matching-specific backbones, arguing instead that frozen self-supervised foundation model features provide superior robustness when combined with task-specialized fine features. The regression-by-classification formulation connects to prior work on stereo disparity and pose regression, while the robust regression loss draws on the Charbonnier family used in optical flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 36% relative improvement on WxBS and consistent SOTA across IMC2022, MegaDepth, ScanNet, and InLoc establish RoMa as a new strong baseline for dense feature matching, and the frozen-DINOv2-plus-specialized-ConvNet recipe offers a practical, computationally cheaper alternative to end-to-end training. The finding that VGG19—worse than ResNet50 for coarse matching—outperforms it as fine features reveals an inherent tension between coarse robustness and fine localizability that may inform future architecture design. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or video, whether the Transformer decoder's cost is prohibitive for real-time applications, and whether the frozen-feature strategy transfers to other correspondence tasks beyond two-view matching.
