## Problem

Existing human avatar methods face a fundamental trade-off: NeRF-based approaches capture high-frequency details but suffer from ambiguous backward mapping between posed and canonical spaces and prohibitively slow volume rendering, while mesh-based methods provide efficient motion control but cannot represent fine geometric details like hair, glasses, and thin structures. Additionally, MLP-based deformation fields used for motion in NeRF avatars are incompatible with standard animation pipelines (skeletal animation, blend shapes, mesh editing). The paper seeks a representation that achieves photorealistic detail, explicit mesh-driven motion control, and real-time rendering on both GPU and mobile devices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence with pre-registered mesh templates (SMPL for full-body, FLAME for head), camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and segmentation masks as input. It produces a hybrid 3D representation consisting of ~10k+ Gaussians each parameterized by a mesh embedding (triangle index, barycentric coordinates, displacement) plus per-Gaussian rotation, scale, color, and opacity. The output is directly portable to Unity as three files (.ply for Gaussians, .json for embeddings, .fbx for the rigged mesh), enabling real-time rendering at 300+ FPS on an RTX 3090 and 30 FPS on an iPhone 13, with animation driven purely by mesh deformation.

## Method

SplattingAvatar embeds 3D Gaussians on a canonical triangle mesh using Phong surface parameterization: each Gaussian's position is defined by barycentric coordinates (u,v) on triangle k plus a displacement d along the interpolated normal. The mesh deforms with animation, and pose-dependent rotation (barycentric interpolation of per-vertex quaternions derived from canonical-to-posed triangle frame changes) and scaling (ratio of posed to canonical triangle area) are applied multiplicatively to the canonical Gaussian properties, while color and opacity remain pose-invariant. Training employs lifted optimization that simultaneously updates Gaussian parameters and embedding coordinates; when a barycentric update crosses a triangle boundary, the residual displacement is re-expressed in the adjacent triangle (a 'walking on triangles' procedure implemented in C++). A scaling regularization penalizes Gaussians that become both long and thin to prevent needle-like artifacts in novel poses, and the standard 3DGS densification (clone/split/prune) is adapted so that new Gaussian positions are projected back onto the mesh via a mini optimization over embeddings.

## Results

On head avatars (Table 1), SplattingAvatar achieves PSNR 28.19 / SSIM 0.931 / LPIPS 0.063 with FLAME meshes and PSNR 28.86 / SSIM 0.931 / LPIPS 0.060 with NHA meshes, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On full-body avatars (Table 2, PeopleSnapshot), the method reaches average PSNR ~31.8 and SSIM ~0.981 across four subjects, surpassing InstantAvatar (avg PSNR ~30.3) and Anim-NeRF (avg PSNR ~28.9) by 1.5–3 dB in PSNR. The method also achieves over 300 FPS on an RTX 3090 and 30 FPS on an iPhone 13 in a Unity implementation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplattingAvatar outperforms all five baselines on the primary head-avatar metrics. Against NHA [16], the gain is substantial: +7.9 dB PSNR and +0.048 SSIM for Ours+NHA, reflecting NHA's texture-atlas limitation on fine details like hair and eyes. Against INSTA [53], the improvement is +2.4 dB PSNR and −0.020 LPIPS, with the paper attributing the gap to INSTA's fixed mesh-vertex attachment versus SplattingAvatar's trainable embedding that redistributes Gaussians toward texture-rich regions. Against PointAvatar [51], the margin is smaller (+0.35 dB PSNR, +0.018 SSIM) but consistent, and the authors note that SplattingAvatar renders roughly two orders of magnitude faster due to forward splatting rasterization versus PointAvatar's LBS-based deformation field. On full-body PeopleSnapshot, SplattingAvatar beats InstantAvatar [21] by ~1.5 dB PSNR and ~0.005 SSIM on average, and Anim-NeRF [7] by ~2.9 dB PSNR; however, LPIPS values are close (0.018–0.029 vs. 0.017–0.027), indicating comparable perceptual quality where the pixel-level advantage is most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for appearance with a triangle mesh (SMPL/FLAME) for motion, departing from NeRF-based avatar methods (InstantAvatar, Anim-NeRF) that rely on MLP-based linear blend skinning fields and from hybrid part-segmentation approaches (AvatarReX, DELTA, SCARF) that assign different representations to body, hair, and clothing. The key conceptual departure is the trainable embedding of Gaussians on the mesh via barycentric coordinates and displacement (inspired by the Phong Surface / lifted optimization of Shen et al., 2020), which allows Gaussians to explicitly follow mesh deformation without any learned deformation network. Unlike PointAvatar, which uses explicit point primitives with LBS-based deformation, SplattingAvatar achieves two orders of magnitude faster rendering while maintaining comparable quality through the mesh-embedding mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving 300+ FPS on a modern GPU and 30 FPS on an iPhone 13 for photorealistic animatable avatars opens the door to real-time XR, gaming, and telepresence applications that were previously infeasible with NeRF-based methods. The tight coupling to standard mesh animation pipelines (skeletal animation, blend shapes, Mixamo rigging) makes the representation directly compatible with existing game-engine workflows. Key limitations include dependence on the driving mesh's motion representation quality (no separate motion channels for hair or clothing) and sensitivity to limited pose variation in training data, leaving open the question of how to handle topological changes and complex garment dynamics within a single unified mesh.
