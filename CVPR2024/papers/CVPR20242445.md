## Problem

State-of-the-art action localization methods either outsource bounding box detection to a pre-trained Faster R-CNN (two-stage, adding 246 GFLOPs) or adopt DETR-style encoder-decoder architectures with learnable queries (single-stage but complex and hard to train). The paper asks whether a vision transformer backbone alone, trained with a bipartite matching loss directly on its output tokens, can perform both detection and classification without any decoder or learnable queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242445/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 16-frame video clip resized to 256×256 (no cropping, fixed aspect ratio) sampled at stride τ=4, and produces L independent prediction triplets ⟨bounding box, actor/no-actor probability, multi-label action class vector⟩ where L equals the number of selected output tokens (256 or 512 depending on token selection strategy). Bounding boxes are parameterized as normalized [x_c, y_c, h, w] with a 2D spatial bias centered on the corresponding patch grid position. At inference, only triplets with actor likelihood exceeding a threshold θ are retained as detections.

## Method

BMViT removes the last pooling layer of MViTv2-S to increase output resolution to 8×16×16 = 2048 spatio-temporal tokens, then selects a subset: central-frame tokens (t=⌊T/2⌋) feed the bounding box and actor-likelihood MLP heads, while temporally pooled tokens feed the action classification head, ensuring one-to-one correspondence across all three heads. Training uses the Hungarian Algorithm to bipartite-match the L output triplets to ground-truth instances, minimizing a combined cost of generalized IoU loss, L1 box loss, binary actor loss, and multi-label cross-entropy action loss. The key architectural difference from DETR is that the backbone's own output tokens serve as fixed 'queries' rather than separate learnable embeddings, eliminating the need for a transformer decoder with self- and cross-attention layers. This yields a single MViTv2-S backbone plus three small MLP heads (121 GFLOPs total) that surpasses the two-stage MViTv2-S + Faster R-CNN pipeline by +3 mAP on AVA 2.2.

## Results

On AVA 2.2, BMViT with MViTv2-S backbone (121 GFLOPs, 256×256 resolution) achieves 30.0 mAP, surpassing the two-stage MViTv2-S (26.8 mAP, 65+246 GFLOPs) by +3.2 mAP while using fewer total FLOPs. Scaling to a ViT-B backbone pre-trained on K400+MAE, BMViT reaches 31.4 mAP at 350 GFLOPs, approaching EVAD's 32.1 mAP (425 GFLOPs) with ~18% fewer FLOPs. On UCF101-24, BMViT achieves 85.6 mAP (MViTv2-S) and 90.7 mAP (ViT-B†@288), and on JHMDB51-21 it reaches 80.7 and 88.4 mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMViT (MViTv2-S, 30.0 mAP) outperforms all two-stage MViT-based methods on AVA 2.2: MViTv2-S (26.8), MViTv2-B (28.1), and MeMViT (28.5), while also using fewer total FLOPs (121 vs. 311–455+246). Among single-stage baselines, BMViT surpasses WOO (25.4 mAP) by +4.6 and STMixer (27.8 mAP) by +2.2, and is comparable to TubeR (reported as similar mAP in the text). At the ViT-B scale, BMViT (31.4 mAP, 350 GFLOPs) trails EVAD (32.1 mAP, 425 GFLOPs) by 0.7 mAP but with ~18% fewer FLOPs. On UCF101-24, BMViT with ViT-B†@288 (90.7 mAP) beats EVAD (85.1 mAP) by +5.6, while on JHMDB51-21 it lags behind EVAD (88.4 vs. 90.2 mAP) by 1.8 mAP, though the authors note their model achieves 95% precision and 97% recall, suggesting room for accuracy improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two ideas from the object detection literature—DETR's bipartite matching loss (Carion et al., 2020) and OWL-ViT's observation that a ViT's un-pooled output tokens can serve as independent prediction slots (Minderer et al., 2022)—and applies them to video action localization. It departs from both the dominant two-stage paradigm (external Faster R-CNN + ROI align + classifier, as in MViTv2, MeMViT, VideoMAE) and the emerging single-stage DETR-based approaches (TubeR, STMixer, EVAD) that still require a transformer decoder with learnable queries. The key conceptual move is recognizing that a multi-scale video transformer's spatio-temporal output tokens already play the role of object queries, making the encoder-decoder split unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is a substantially simpler deployment pipeline: no external person detector (saving 246 GFLOPs), no DETR decoder, and no learnable queries—just a video backbone plus three small MLP heads. However, the paper itself reveals an important open question: scaling to larger backbones (Hiera-L) yields sub-par performance compared to the two-stage counterpart, suggesting that end-to-end training of large models with bipartite matching remains unsolved. The fixed-aspect-ratio requirement and the sensitivity to token selection strategy also limit generality. Whether this encoder-only matching paradigm can be made to scale with model size and diverse input geometries is the key direction for future work.
