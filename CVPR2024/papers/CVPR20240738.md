## Problem

PAC-NeRF's two-step optimization (learn geometry from first frames, then optimize physics with frozen geometry) makes it highly sensitive to initial geometry quality, causing failure in sparse-view settings where few initial frames provide insufficient geometric information. A straightforward fix—training the Eulerian grid with all video frames—fails because grid representations in world space cannot receive gradients from the Lagrangian particle-based physical simulation, so physical constraints cannot be enforced during geometry optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240738/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences of a dynamic continuum object (as few as 3 training views out of 11 total), along with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics and collision geometry (e.g., ground plane). It outputs a voxel-based geometric representation (volume density and color fields) and material-specific physical parameters—Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio for deformable solids, viscosity and bulk modulus for Newtonian fluids, yield stress and plastic viscosity for non-Newtonian fluids, or friction angle for granular media. The iterative procedure also synthesizes novel first-frame views from the corrected geometry to augment training data in subsequent iterations.

## Method

Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO) optimizes both particle positions and particle features (volume density, color) directly in Lagrangian (material) space rather than in Eulerian grid space, so that gradients from the differentiable MPM simulation propagate into the geometric representation and enforce physical constraints during geometry correction. This is applied after the standard PAC-NeRF physical property optimization to correct geometry that was poorly learned from sparse initial frames. The paper further introduces iterative geometry-physics optimization: after LPO corrects the particle geometry, novel first-frame views are rendered from the updated particles and combined with the original sparse views to retrain PAC-NeRF from scratch, breaking the chicken-and-egg coupling between geometry and physics. Full recalculation (rather than repeated G2P/P2G conversions) is used to avoid accumulation of interpolation artifacts across iterations.

## Results

On the PAC-NeRF benchmark (9 scenes, 3 training views, 8 test views), LPO improves geometric correction over the PAC-NeRF-3v baseline from 27.39 to 29.22 PSNR (+1.83 dB) and over the improved PAC-NeRF-3v† baseline from 28.47 to 30.11 PSNR (+1.64 dB), while the full-view upper bound is 35.99 PSNR. For physical identification (4 iterations), LPO⁴ reduces the mean absolute error in Young's modulus (log₁₀ scale) from 0.449 to 0.067 on the Bird scene and from 0.277 to 0.061 on the Torus scene compared to PAC-NeRF-3v. The iterative geometry-physics optimization further improves PSNR to 30.34 (from 30.11 for single-pass LPO on 3v†), confirming the chicken-and-egg benefit of alternating geometry and physics refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole external baseline is PAC-NeRF [Li et al., ICLR 2023], evaluated in three configurations: full-view (11 views, upper bound), 3-view (PAC-NeRF-3v), and an improved 3-view variant with sparse-view regularizers (PAC-NeRF-3v†). LPO consistently outperforms both 3-view baselines on all geometric correction metrics: +1.83 dB PSNR over PAC-NeRF-3v and +1.64 dB over PAC-NeRF-3v†, closing roughly 30% of the gap to the 11-view upper bound. For physical identification, LPO⁴ reduces absolute errors in most material parameters (e.g., log₁₀(E) on Bird: 0.449→0.067; log₁₀(κ) on Letter: 6.772→0.174) compared to PAC-NeRF-3v, while PAC-NeRF-3v†+LPO⁴ achieves the best overall stability. Ablation variants (+LPO-F, +LPO-P, +GO) all underperform the full LPO, confirming that joint optimization of particle positions and features within MPM constraints is essential; the Eulerian-grid alternative (+GO) in particular cannot enforce physical constraints and degrades on complex scenes like Bird.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PAC-NeRF (Li et al., ICLR 2023), which introduced a hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian representation for geometry-agnostic system identification by coupling voxel-based NeRF rendering with a differentiable Material Point Method (DiffMPM). The key departure is identifying PAC-NeRF's two-step optimization (freeze geometry from first frames, then optimize physics) as a critical bottleneck in sparse-view settings, and proposing Lagrangian Particle Optimization (LPO) to correct geometry in the particle space where MPM physics is naturally defined. The iterative geometry-physics optimization loop further extends the framework by exploiting the mutual dependency between geometric accuracy and physical property estimation, a chicken-and-egg relationship the authors make explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and common limitation—sparse-view settings—where PAC-NeRF's frozen-geometry assumption causes cascading failures in both reconstruction and physical identification. The conceptual insight that geometry should be optimized in Lagrangian (particle) space rather than Eulerian (grid) space to respect physical constraints is clean and potentially transferable to other physics-informed NeRF architectures. However, the method requires longer training time, is sensitive to the initial PAC-NeRF state, and the physical property improvements are inconsistent across scenes and material types, leaving open whether the iterative scheme converges reliably in more challenging regimes. The authors note that combining LPO with other few-shot NeRF regularizers (as demonstrated with PAC-NeRF-3v†) is complementary, suggesting a path toward more robust sparse-view system identification.
