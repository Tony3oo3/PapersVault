## Problem

Vision language models struggle with detailed region-level understanding because vision encoders have limited spatial awareness and existing training data provides only coarse, short region captions (e.g., 8.46 words in ReferCOCOg). Prior region-aware MLLMs either encode region positions as text (overloading the language decoder with spatial interpretation) or restrict input to bounding boxes, leaving the potential of arbitrary-shaped region-specific visual representations under-explored.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 336×336 RGB image, one or more regions of interest in any shape (bounding box or segmentation mask), and a text instruction containing `<region>` placeholder tokens. These placeholders are replaced at inference time with semantic region-level embeddings (obtained via Mask Pooling over refined visual features) that are injected directly into the language decoder's token sequence. Outputs include free-form region descriptions, closed-set class labels (80 COCO categories), region indices for referring expression comprehension, and multi-turn reasoning responses.

## Method

RGPT builds on a frozen CLIP ViT-L backbone and introduces a feature refinement module of two stride-2 deconvolution layers that upsamples the low-resolution feature map by 4×, enabling Mask Pooling to extract region-level features from arbitrary-shaped masks. Image-level features are obtained via adaptive pooling to control sequence length, and both image and region features pass through a shared two-layer MLP connector into a Vicuna-7B language decoder. The key training innovation is task-guided instruction prompting: custom prompt templates specify the exact output format for closed-set tasks (e.g., 'output only the class name from the 80 COCO categories'), converting vision tasks into VQA-style responses aligned with the LLM's generation pattern. For data, the authors build RecapD (213K images, 1.5M regions, 87.14 words/caption) via a two-stage GPT-assisted pipeline that first generates a global image caption, then prompts LLaVA with that caption plus the human-annotated class name to produce detailed region descriptions, with CLIP-based filtering to remove noisy samples. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training (freeze visual encoder and LLM; train refinement module and MLP) on joint image-level and region-level data, followed by fine-tuning (freeze only the visual encoder; update refinement, MLP, and LLM) on the enriched RecapD dataset plus task-guided prompts on COCO and ReferCOCOg.

## Results

On COCO-2017 val set for region-level object classification, RGPT achieves 70.0 mAP and 80.61% accuracy, surpassing the strongest feature-based baseline ASM (69.3 mAP) and all VLM baselines (best: PVIT at 64.53% Acc). For region-level captioning on RefCOCOg, RGPT attains 16.9 METEOR and 109.9 CIDEr, outperforming GRIT (15.2/71.6) and Kosmos-2 (14.1/62.3). On Referring Expression Comprehension (ReferCOCOg), RGPT scores 86.44 (val) and 86.96 (test), exceeding MiniGPT-V2 (84.44/84.66) and Shikra (82.27/82.19). On the POPE object hallucination benchmark, RGPT achieves 87.80% accuracy on the Random split, competitive with InstructBLIP (88.57%) and superior to LLaVA (86.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGPT outperforms all VLM baselines on COCO-2017 region classification: it beats PVIT (64.53% Acc) by 16.1 points, GPT4RoI (64.01%) by 16.6 points, Shikra (53.91%) by 26.7 points, and LLaVA (40.04%) by 40.6 points. Against the strongest feature-based method ASM (69.3 mAP), RGPT achieves a marginal mAP gain (70.0 vs. 69.3) while also reporting accuracy (80.61%), a metric ASM does not provide. On region captioning (RefCOCOg), RGPT's CIDEr of 109.9 substantially exceeds GRIT (71.6), SLR (66.2), and Kosmos-2 (62.3), with METEOR also leading at 16.9. For REC on ReferCOCOg, RGPT (86.44 val / 86.96 test) surpasses MiniGPT-V2 (84.44/84.66) by ~2 points, Shikra (82.27/82.19) by ~4 points, and Kosmos-2 (60.57/61.65) by a large margin. On the POPE hallucination benchmark, RGPT (87.80% Random) is slightly below InstructBLIP (88.57%) but clearly ahead of Shikra (86.90%), LLaVA (86.00%), MiniGPT4 (79.67%), and the dialogue-oriented models MM-GPT (50.10%) and mPLUG-Owl (53.97%).

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionGPT builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (frozen CLIP ViT-L + MLP connector + Vicuna-7B) and its multi-turn instruction-tuning paradigm, but departs from prior region-aware MLLMs (Shikra, KOSMOS-2, MiniGPT-2) that encode spatial information purely as text coordinates, and from GPT4RoI/ASM that use RoI-aligned features restricted to bounding boxes. The key architectural departure is a lightweight feature refinement module (two stride-2 deconvolution layers) combined with Mask Pooling to support arbitrary-shaped regions, plus task-guided instruction prompts that convert closed-set vision tasks into constrained VQA. The GPT-assisted two-stage caption generation pipeline (global caption → region-specific description conditioned on class name) is a novel data construction approach that repurposes LLaVA as an annotation tool rather than a model component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that modest architectural modifications to existing VLMs—rather than new pretraining objectives or larger models—can substantially improve region-level understanding, offering a practical recipe for practitioners who want to add spatial grounding to LLaVA-style systems. The RecapD dataset (1.5M regions, 87.14 words/caption vs. 8.46 in ReferCOCOg) is a significant resource that could benefit the broader region-level VLM community. However, limitations remain: evaluation on classification uses ground-truth boxes (not predicted ones), the feature refinement is limited to 4× upsampling, and the GPT-assisted captions may inherit systematic biases from LLaVA's own hallucination tendencies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video regions, 3D volumes, or requires fundamentally different spatial encodings for very fine-grained or overlapping regions.
