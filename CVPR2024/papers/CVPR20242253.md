## Problem

Diffusion models' iterative denoising process suffers from instability because discretization errors cause the denoised sample to oscillate around its ground truth, requiring lengthy timesteps to converge. Additionally, the well-known frequency evolution principle—where low-frequency components are recovered first and high-frequency details emerge later—is largely unexploited in the sampling process. The paper seeks a training-free mechanism that leverages all prior samples to stabilize denoising while respecting this frequency evolution.

## Input / Output

MASF is a training-free sampling plug-in that operates on pre-trained diffusion models (pixel-space ADM or latent-space U-ViT) at resolutions from 64×64 to 256×256, accepting Gaussian noise plus optional class labels or text prompts and producing a synthesized RGB image. At each denoising timestep, the method internally maps the current noisy sample x_t to the estimated clean sample x_0^t, decomposes it into four Haar wavelet subbands of half spatial resolution, and reconstructs the full-resolution image via inverse DWT before feeding it back to the denoising network. The entire procedure adds only ~0.97% computational overhead to the sampling loop.

## Method

MASF reframes iterative denoising as model optimization and applies a moving average over all prior estimated clean samples x_0^t (rather than noisy x_t) to avoid distribution shift across timesteps. The estimated sample is decomposed via Haar wavelet DWT into four subbands (ll, lh, hl, hh), and a separate moving average is maintained for each frequency component with an adaptive spatial weight w_t that increases reliance on the moving average where the sample is evolving sharply. A dynamic frequency weighting scheme β_f(t) then modulates the contribution of each subband during IDWT reconstruction: the low-frequency weight decreases linearly while high-frequency weights increase linearly over timesteps, aligning stabilization priority with the natural frequency evolution of diffusion. The refined sample is fed back into the denoising network, making MASF seamlessly compatible with DDIM, DDPM, DPM-Solver++, UniPC, F-PNDM, and classifier guidance without any retraining.

## Results

On ImageNet 128×128 (class-conditional, 50K samples, guidance scale 1.0), MASF consistently reduces FID across all solvers and NFEs. The headline result is DDIM+MASF achieving FID 4.73 at 25 NFE (vs. 6.14 for DDIM alone), and F-PNDM+MASF reaching FID 3.26 at 25 NFE (vs. 3.30). On MS-COCO text-conditional generation, MASF applied to DDIM drops FID from 35.10 to 25.67 at 10 NFE (a 9.43 absolute improvement). The method adds only 0.97% extra computational cost to the sampling process.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASF is evaluated as a plug-in module on top of five sampling solvers: DDPM, DDIM, DPM-Solver++, UniPC, and F-PNDM. It wins on every comparison across all NFEs (10–25) and all datasets (ImageNet, MS-COCO, LSUN, FFHQ). The largest relative gains appear at low NFE where denoising instability is most severe: DDIM+MASF improves FID by 1.79 at 10 NFE on ImageNet 128 (11.18→9.39) and by 9.43 on MS-COCO (35.10→25.67). Among the high-order solvers, UniPC+MASF achieves the best overall FID of 3.50 at 25 NFE on ImageNet 128, outperforming DPM-Solver++(2M)+MASF (3.79) and F-PNDM+MASF (3.26 at 25 NFE but only 5.60 at 15 NFE). MASF also works with classifier guidance at all tested scales (0.0–4.0), with the best result of FID 4.73 at scale 1.0 and 25 NFE. The method never degrades performance relative to its base solver, confirming its compatibility as a training-free, solver-agnostic enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

MASF builds directly on the DDIM and DDPM sampling frameworks, modifying the denoising update by replacing the per-step estimated sample x₀ᵗ with a global moving average. Conceptually, it departs from momentum-based samplers (which track only the most recent 1–3 steps) by ensembling *all* prior samples, and from frequency-aware diffusion architectures (e.g., Spectral Diffusion, Wavelet Diffusion) by requiring no retraining or specialized network design. The key positioning is that MASF is a training-free, solver-agnostic plug-in that exploits the known low-to-high frequency evolution of diffusion denoising through a simple Haar wavelet decomposition, rather than learning frequency dynamics end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: a 0.97% overhead that consistently reduces FID across four datasets, five solvers, multiple resolutions, and guidance scales makes MASF an easy win for practitioners already deploying diffusion pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to pixel-space models at ≤256×256 and a single-level Haar decomposition; whether the frequency-domain moving average transfers to latent-space models at higher resolutions, multi-level wavelet decompositions, or video/3D generation remains open. The dynamic weighting scheme (β_f(t)) is heuristic (linear ramp), and its interaction with more sophisticated solvers or classifier-free guidance at scale is unexplored.
