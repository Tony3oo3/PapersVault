## Problem

Traditional YOLO detectors are confined to a fixed set of predefined categories, limiting their use in open-world scenarios. Existing open-vocabulary detectors (e.g., GLIP, Grounding DINO) rely on heavy backbones like Swin-L and require online text encoding at inference, making them slow and impractical for edge deployment. Whether lightweight one-stage detectors can be pre-trained to achieve strong open-vocabulary detection at real-time speeds remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242159/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a set of text prompts (category names, noun phrases, or object descriptions) as input. During training, an online vocabulary of up to 80 nouns is sampled per mosaic batch; during inference, a user-defined offline vocabulary is pre-encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into fixed embeddings. The model outputs bounding boxes paired with object embeddings in R^D, which are matched to text embeddings via L2-normalized dot product with a learnable affine transformation (scaling α and shift β) to produce category scores.

## Method

YOLO-World builds on YOLOv8's Darknet backbone and introduces a Re-parameterizable Vision-Language PAN (RepVL-PAN) that fuses text and image features at multiple scales. The T-CSPLayer injects text guidance into image features via max-sigmoid attention over text embeddings, while the Image-Pooling Attention updates text embeddings with image context using multi-head attention over 27 max-pooled patch tokens from three feature levels. Training uses a region-text contrastive loss that reformulates detection as region-text matching with cross-entropy, combined with IoU and distributed focal losses for box regression. A key deployment insight is re-parameterization: at inference, the offline text embeddings are folded directly into convolutional and linear layer weights, eliminating the text encoder entirely and enabling 52 FPS on a single V100. Pre-training unifies detection (Objects365), grounding (GQA, Flickr30k), and pseudo-labeled image-text data (CC3M-Lite, generated via GLIP + CLIP filtering) into a single region-text contrastive objective over 100 epochs.

## Results

On the LVIS minival benchmark (zero-shot, Fixed AP), YOLO-World-L pre-trained on Objects365 + GoldG + CC3M-Lite achieves 35.4 AP at 52.0 FPS on a single V100 (re-parameterized), outperforming DetCLIP-T (34.4 AP) by 1.0 AP while running 20× faster (52.0 vs. 2.3 FPS). The smaller YOLO-World-S variant (13M params) reaches 26.2 AP at 74.1 FPS, demonstrating that lightweight YOLO backbones can be effectively pre-trained for open-vocabulary detection. After fine-tuning on COCO for only 80 epochs, YOLO-World-L achieves 53.3 AP, surpassing YOLOv8-L trained from scratch for 500 epochs (52.9 AP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOLO-World-L (35.4 AP) outperforms all prior open-vocabulary detectors pre-trained on similar data: it beats DetCLIP-T (34.4 AP) by 1.0 AP while achieving 20× higher inference speed (52.0 vs. 2.3 FPS), surpasses GLIPv2-T with Cap4M (29.0 AP) by 6.4 AP, and exceeds Grounding DINO-T with Cap4M (27.4 AP) by 8.0 AP. Notably, YOLO-World achieves these gains with far fewer parameters (48M vs. 155–232M) and a much lighter backbone (YOLOv8-L vs. Swin-T). On COCO fine-tuning (Table 6), YOLO-World-L reaches 53.3 AP in just 80 epochs, edging out YOLOv8-L (52.9 AP, 500 epochs) and YOLOv7-X (52.6 AP, 300 epochs), confirming strong transfer from open-vocabulary pre-training. On LVIS fine-tuning (Table 7), YOLO-World-L (34.1 AP, 20.4 AP_r) surpasses BARON (29.5 AP, 23.2 AP_r) in overall AP but trails slightly on rare categories, while clearly outperforming YOLOv8-L (26.9 AP) by 7.2 AP. The speed advantage is decisive: YOLO-World-S runs at 74.1 FPS versus 0.12 FPS for GLIP-T, making it the only method in the comparison suitable for real-time edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLO-World extends the YOLOv8 single-stage CNN architecture with a CLIP text encoder and a novel RepVL-PAN that injects language information into multi-scale image features via Text-guided CSPLayers and Image-Pooling Attention. It departs from the dominant open-vocabulary detection paradigm (GLIP, Grounding DINO, DetCLIP) that relies on heavy transformer backbones (Swin-L) and online text encoding at inference, instead demonstrating that a lightweight 13–48M parameter CNN can achieve competitive zero-shot performance through region-text contrastive pre-training. The re-parameterization of text embeddings into convolutional weights and the 'prompt-then-detect' paradigm represent a practical departure from prior methods that require a text encoder at every inference step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that open-vocabulary detection does not inherently require large transformer backbones, challenging the prevailing assumption that model scale is the primary driver of open-vocabulary capability. The re-parameterization trick makes deployment on edge devices feasible, and the open-sourced codebase positions YOLO-World as a practical baseline for real-world open-vocabulary applications. However, zero-shot performance (35.4 AP on LVIS) still trails the largest transformer-based methods trained on more data, and the approach inherits CLIP's limitations in text understanding. Open questions include whether the re-parameterization strategy generalizes to more complex linguistic inputs (full sentences, compositional descriptions) and whether the speed-accuracy gap can be closed with further pre-training scale.
