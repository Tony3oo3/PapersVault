## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion models suffer from text-image misalignment (attribute binding errors, counting failures, relation confusion) in complex multi-object scenes, yet their intrinsic compositional reasoning remains underexploited. Existing remedies either impose inductive biases on cross-attention maps or delegate layout planning to LLMs with intermediate bounding-box states, both of which bypass the model's own reasoning capacity. No prior work has investigated whether strengthening a T2I model's discriminative (understanding) abilities can directly improve its generative alignment.

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a free-form text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image via iterative denoising in SD's latent space; during inference, a self-correction step modifies the latent z_t at each timestep using gradients from the discriminative adapter before the U-Net predicts noise. For the discriminative probing tasks, the adapter consumes a 2D feature map F_t ∈ R^{h×w×d} extracted from an intermediate U-Net block (fused with position and timestep embeddings) and outputs either a scalar matching score (cosine similarity between projected query representations and CLIP text embeddings) for ITM, or a bounding box (x, y, w, h) with a confidence probability for REC. The adapter uses N learnable queries, partitioned into M matching queries and N−M object-prior queries.

## Method

DPT is a two-stage paradigm built on Stable Diffusion. In Stage 1 (Discriminative Probing), all SD parameters are frozen and a DETR-inspired Transformer adapter (encoder + decoder with N learnable queries) is attached to an intermediate U-Net feature map; it is trained with a contrastive ITM loss (bidirectional T→I and I→T) and a DETR-style REC loss (Hungarian matching on grounding probability, L1, GIoU, plus a text-to-object contrastive term) to probe the model's global matching and local grounding abilities. In Stage 2 (Discriminative Tuning), LoRA parameters are injected into SD's cross-attention layers and jointly fine-tuned with the adapter under the same discriminative objectives, allowing gradients to reshape the U-Net's intermediate activations and thereby improve generative alignment without sacrificing image quality. As an inference-time bonus, a self-correction mechanism updates the latent at each denoising step via ẑ_t = z_t + η·∂s(z_t, y)/∂z_t, where the gradient flows through the discriminative adapter, steering generation toward better prompt alignment.

## Results

On text-to-image generation, DPT with SD-v2.1 achieves CLIP scores of 35.83 (COCO-NSS1K, ID), 40.23 (CC-500, OOD), and 38.39 (ABC-6K, MD), consistently outperforming the base SD-v2.1 (34.96, 39.24, 37.53) and all baselines. On T2I-CompBench, DPT reaches 60.59 on Color and 58.24 on Spatial composition, substantially exceeding SD-v2.1 (48.21, 46.83) and Attend-and-Excite (53.72, 48.53). On TIFA, DPT achieves 84.49 VQA accuracy versus 81.35 for SD-v2.1. On discriminative tasks, DPT* (priority-discriminative selection) attains 39.18 overall on MSCOCO-HN ITM and 66.70 on RefCOCO testA, surpassing all generative baselines by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPT consistently outperforms the base Stable Diffusion (v1.4 and v2.1) on all three generation benchmarks, with the largest gains on CC-500 (OOD) where CLIP improves from 39.24 to 40.23 and BLIP-M from 85.45 to 90.72. Against Attend-and-Excite, DPT wins on CLIP across all three datasets (e.g., 35.83 vs. 34.95 on COCO-NSS1K) and on BLIP-M for CC-500 (90.72 vs. 90.03), while HN-DiffusionITM is surpassed by DPT on CLIP and BLIP-M for both SD versions. On T2I-CompBench, DPT dominates on Color (60.59 vs. 53.72 for Attend-and-Excite, 48.21 for SD-v2.1) and Spatial (58.24 vs. 48.53, 46.83), though HN-DiffusionITM edges out DPT slightly on the Composite metric (30.99 vs. 30.95). On discriminative tasks (Table 3), DPT* achieves 39.18 overall on MSCOCO-HN and 66.70 on RefCOCO testA, exceeding HN-DiffusionITM (33.96, 35.47) and Diffusion Classifier (30.88, 10.91) by substantial margins, while even the Stage-1 probing-only variant (38.52, 53.28) already surpasses all fine-tuned generative baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion as the base generative model and departs from two dominant lines of work for improving text-image alignment: cross-attention manipulation methods (StructureDiffusion, Attend-and-Excite) that impose linguistic inductive biases, and LLM-based layout planning methods (LayoutLLM-T2I, LayoutGPT) that rely on intermediate bounding-box representations. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that a T2I model's discriminative abilities (probed via ITM and REC) reflect its intrinsic compositional alignment, and that explicitly tuning these discriminative capacities through a DETR-style adapter plus LoRA fine-tuning can improve generation without external structural priors. Prior work (Peekaboo, Diffusion Classifier, DiffusionITM) demonstrated that diffusion U-Net features support zero-shot or fine-tuned discrimination, but treated the generative model as a frozen feature extractor; DPT is the first to close the loop by back-propagating discriminative objectives into the generative model's parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DPT paradigm offers a practical, parameter-efficient recipe (LoRA + lightweight adapter + optional inference-time self-correction) for improving T2I alignment that does not require prompt parsing, bounding-box intermediates, or architectural modifications, making it broadly applicable to any diffusion-based T2I system. The self-correction mechanism, which uses discriminative gradients to nudge latents during denoising, is a lightweight inference-time technique that could be combined with other guidance strategies. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion to other diffusion architectures or autoregressive T2I models, how to resolve the observed trade-offs between multi-task discriminative objectives (GM vs. LG), and whether the generative-discriminative synergy extends to more complex compositional or counterfactual reasoning tasks.
