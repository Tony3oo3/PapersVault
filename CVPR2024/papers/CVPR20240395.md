## Problem

Diffusion models inherit and amplify biases from large-scale training data, producing homogeneous outputs under specific conditions (e.g., nearly all-male programmers for a text prompt). Existing diversity-enhancing methods—re-weighted losses, classifier guidance, prompt engineering—are task-specific and do not generalize across different diffusion model architectures or conditioning types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (class-conditional DDPM or text-conditional StableDiffusion), a conditioning signal (class label or text prompt such as 'a headshot of a person'), and a small set of diverse reference images (e.g., 50 per class from ImageNet, or 50 balanced-attribute faces from CelebA with 25 positive/25 negative). It outputs a fine-tuned diffusion model that generates images whose distribution better covers the reference distribution. Fine-tuning uses LoRA adapters on attention layers with M=5 generated images per batch and N=50 reference images per condition.

## Method

The core contribution is a set-based Diversity Reward that measures the distributional gap between M generated images and N reference images in a learned feature space (InceptionV3 for class-conditional, CLIP visual encoder for text-conditional), instantiated as either negative MMD with a polynomial kernel or Gaussian Process-based mutual information with an RBF kernel. To convert this set-level reward into per-image dense supervision, the paper applies marginal utility from submodular set cover, assigning each generated image a distinct reward equal to the change in total diversity reward when that image is removed. The diffusion model is then fine-tuned via policy gradient (PPO-style with importance sampling and clipping) in the standard MDP formulation where each denoising step is an action, maximizing the sum of individual diversity rewards across the batch. The framework is model-agnostic, requiring no architecture-specific modifications, and is validated on both class-conditional (ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100) and text-conditional (StableDiffusion on CelebA attributes) settings.

## Results

On ImageNet-128×128 class-conditional generation at guidance scale s=4.0, the proposed MMD reward improves Recall from 36.15 (baseline) to 47.66 while reducing FID from 24.49 to 23.42; at s=1.0, Recall rises from 55.10 to 59.63 with FID dropping from 19.86 to 19.19. On CIFAR-100, the MMD variant achieves Recall 0.69 and FID 2.70, matching or slightly exceeding the retraining-based CBDM (Recall 0.67, FID 2.72) while only fine-tuning a small subset of parameters via LoRA. For unbiased text-to-image generation with StableDiffusion, the GP-MI reward achieves the lowest KL divergence across all six CelebA attributes (e.g., 2×10⁻⁴ for 'young' vs. 0.578 for raw SD), approaching the near-zero values of the task-specific ITI-GEN method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the class-conditional baseline (Dhariwal & Nichol [8]), the proposed RL fine-tuning consistently improves Recall by 9–11.5 points across all guidance scales (e.g., +11.5 at s=4.0, +4.5 at s=1.0) while also reducing FID, confirming that diversity gains do not come at a quality cost. On CIFAR-10/100, the method matches or slightly exceeds the retraining-based Class-Balancing Diffusion Model (CBDM, Qin et al. [27]) in FID (2.70 vs. 2.72 on CIFAR-100; 2.98 vs. 3.03 on CIFAR-10) and Recall (0.69 vs. 0.67; 0.67 vs. 0.65), while only fine-tuning LoRA parameters rather than retraining from scratch. For text-to-image generation, the method substantially outperforms prompt-engineering baselines: Ethical Intervention (Bansal et al. [3]) and Hard Prompt Searching (Ding et al. [9]) show high KL divergence on most attributes (e.g., 0.531 and 0.371 for 'eyeglasses'), whereas Ours (GP-MI) achieves 1.5×10⁻⁴. Prompts Debiasing (Chuang et al. [7]) and Custom Diffusion (Kumari et al. [19]) are intermediate but still 100–1000× worse in KL than the proposed method on most attributes. The closest competitor is ITI-GEN (Zhang et al. [40]), which achieves near-zero KL (e.g., 1×10⁻⁶ for 'male') but requires manually specifying diversity ratios and is designed exclusively for StableDiffusion; the proposed method achieves comparable KL values (e.g., 2×10⁻⁴ for 'male') while remaining a generalizable framework applicable to any diffusion model architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL fine-tuning framework for diffusion models established by Black et al. [5] and Fan et al. [10, 11], which formulate the reverse diffusion process as an MDP and apply policy gradients with single-image rewards (e.g., ImageReward). The key departure is replacing single-image rewards with a set-based Diversity Reward that measures distributional overlap (via MMD or GP-based mutual information) between generated and reference image populations, drawing on probabilistic two-sample testing [13] and submodular marginal utility [23] to decompose the set-level signal into per-image rewards. Unlike prior diversity methods that are task-specific—retraining with re-weighted losses [27], classifier-based guidance [33], or prompt engineering/tuning [9, 40]—the proposed framework is model-agnostic and applies uniformly to both class-conditional and text-conditional diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, lightweight path to bias mitigation: rather than retraining from scratch or manually crafting prompts, practitioners can fine-tune a small subset of parameters (via LoRA) guided by a small reference set, automatically adapting to the diversity ratio encoded in those references. However, the evaluation is limited in scope—only the first 100 ImageNet classes are fine-tuned, reference sets are small (50 images), and the diversity metric is a proxy that may not capture all dimensions of bias simultaneously. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full ImageNet or larger text-to-image benchmarks, how it behaves when multiple bias axes (gender, race, age) must be balanced concurrently, and whether the set-based reward generalizes to other modalities beyond images.
