## Problem

Conventional optical flow models treat estimation as a discriminative regression task, which limits their ability to capture the underlying probabilistic distribution of dense correspondence fields and generalizes poorly under occlusion, motion blur, and brightness changes. While a prior generative attempt (DDVM) applied a standard UNet-based diffusion framework to optical flow, it incurred 20–30× higher computational cost and lacked task-specific architectural design, leaving a gap for an efficient, purpose-built generative paradigm for flow estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241623/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of consecutive RGB frames (I₁, I₂) encoded by a Twins-SVT backbone into dual feature maps and a 4D correlation volume. It outputs a per-pixel 2D displacement vector field, normalized to the range {−b, b} with b = 0.5. At inference, the flow is generated from a standard Gaussian noise sample via K = 3 DDIM denoising steps (σ = 0), making the reverse process deterministic and requiring no additional sampling overhead beyond the recurrent decoder iterations.

## Method

FlowDiffuser reformulates optical flow estimation as a conditional denoising diffusion process: during training, Gaussian noise is progressively added to ground-truth flow, and the network learns to reverse this process conditioned on the cost volume, context features, and RAFT hidden states. The core architectural contribution is the Conditional Recurrent Denoising Decoder (Conditional-RDD), which embeds a RAFT-style GRU recurrent loop inside each denoising step, replacing the standard UNet decoder with a lightweight motion-encoder → GRU → flow-head pipeline. A key innovation, Hidden State Denoising (HSD), introduces a sub-network G(·) that predicts an intermediate latent flow (fewer GRU iterations than training) in place of the full f₀ prediction, reducing variance and enabling a striding factor λ = 0.2 to suppress long-range error accumulation. An Embedding Enhancement (EE) module amplifies the time-embedding signal through learned scale/shift convolutions, and the reverse process uses DDIM (σ = 0) with only 3 steps, keeping inference cost close to the underlying RAFT baseline while delivering consistent gains across Sintel and KITTI.

## Results

FlowDiffuser achieves the best average rank (1.1) across all seven evaluation metrics on Sintel and KITTI. On the Sintel training set, it records an EPE of 0.86 (clean) and 2.19 (final), outperforming the previous best FlowFormer++ (0.90/2.30). On KITTI-15 training, it achieves an EPE of 3.61 and F1-all of 11.8%, surpassing GAFlow (3.92/13.9) and FlowFormer++ (3.93/14.1). On the Sintel test set, FlowDiffuser attains 1.02 (clean) and 2.03 (final) EPE, and on KITTI-15 test it reaches 4.17% F1-all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowDiffuser outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the four training-set metrics (Sintel clean/final EPE and KITTI EPE/F1-all), achieving the best values in each. On the Sintel test set, it leads on clean EPE (1.02 vs. 1.03 for GMFlow+) but trails FlowFormer++ on final EPE (2.03 vs. 1.94) and is slightly behind GAFlow (2.05). On KITTI test F1-all, FlowDiffuser (4.17%) is marginally behind RAFT-it (4.31%), TransFlow (4.32%), and OCTC (4.33%). Against the closest generative competitor DDVM [31], FlowDiffuser improves Sintel clean EPE from 1.48 to 0.86 and KITTI F1-all from 14.1% to 11.8%, while also exhibiting substantially more stable predictions. The compatibility ablation (Table 3) further shows that plugging Conditional-RDD into RAFT, GMA, KPA-Flow, and SKFlow yields 3–9% gains over each respective baseline, confirming broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowDiffuser departs from the dominant discriminative regression paradigm for optical flow by reformulating the task as a conditional denoising diffusion process, building directly on the DDPM/DDIM framework (Ho et al., Song et al.) while replacing the standard UNet denoiser with a RAFT-inspired recurrent decoder. This positions it as a targeted alternative to DDVM (Saxena et al.), which applied a generic diffusion model to flow and suffered 20–30× slower inference; FlowDiffuser's Conditional-RDD and Hidden State Denoising (HSD) are designed specifically to make diffusion tractable within the RAFT recurrent pipeline. The method is explicitly modular, plugging into existing RAFT-like decoders (GMA, KPA-Flow, SKFlow) rather than proposing a monolithic architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a generative, noise-to-flow formulation consistently improves generalization over discriminative baselines across four different backbone decoders, suggesting the probabilistic framing captures motion ambiguity (occlusion, blur) that point-estimation regression cannot. The HSD mechanism—using a sub-network trained with more iterations to produce latent intermediate flow predictions—offers a practical recipe for reducing diffusion steps without sacrificing accuracy, making the approach deployable. Open questions include whether the generative framing extends to other dense-prediction tasks (depth, segmentation) with similar efficiency gains, and whether the modest but consistent EPE improvements translate to downstream tasks (tracking, 3D reconstruction) where flow uncertainty matters more than mean accuracy.
