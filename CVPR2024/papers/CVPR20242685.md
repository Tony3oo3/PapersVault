## Problem

Single-photon cameras (QIS, SPAD) produce 1-bit binary frames with enormous data throughput, making continuous operation infeasible for low-power and bandwidth-limited applications. The paper asks whether a full color image can be generated from a single binary frame, a problem that is particularly difficult for standard colorization because the input suffers from unknown and potentially extreme exposure variation (over- or under-exposure) that degrades image details and breaks colorizer assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242685/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 1-bit binary image (512×512 for AFHQ, 256×256 for CelebA-HQ) along with its input exposure index τ and a target exposure index, and produces exposure-corrected images that are not necessarily binary (they can be continuous-valued), followed by a colorized RGB image. In burst mode, multiple exposure-corrected images at predefined exposure levels are generated from the single input and jointly fed to a burst colorizer equipped with Cross Non-Local Fusion blocks. The colorizer itself is trained exclusively at one fixed exposure, so all exposure mismatch is handled by the synthesis module.

## Method

The core contribution is a filter atom-coefficient decomposition (F = φΛ) applied to the convolutional layers of a Pix2Pix or CycleGAN generator, where the filter atoms Λ are parameterized as the solution of a neural ODE whose integration interval is controlled by the input-to-target exposure pair. A 6-layer MLP defines the ODE dynamics dΛ/dθ̃ = g(θ̃_input, θ̃_target, Λ_init; W), so that integrating from θ̃_input to θ̃_target yields the filter atoms for the desired exposure, and the full filter is reconstructed as φΛ. Theorem 3.1 proves that Lipschitz-continuous filter atoms induce controlled, continuous exposure changes in the output. This exposure synthesis module is modular and sits upstream of a standard colorizer (Pix2Pix for single-image, Pix2Pix + CNLF for burst), which is trained only at one fixed exposure and never sees augmented data, achieving lower FID than baselines that require 3× training data or larger generators.

## Results

On the AFHQ and CelebA-HQ datasets, AtomODE-CycleGAN achieves the lowest MSE across all five categories (1.15–1.87), while AtomODE-Pix2Pix attains the highest Relative Linearity scores (0.9994–0.9998), indicating the smoothest exposure transitions. In terms of FID for colorization quality, AtomODE-Pix2Pix achieves the best scores on four of five categories (e.g., 57.88 on Cat, 40.08 on Female), and AtomODE-CycleGAN leads on Dog (87.43) and Wild (35.75). Both proposed methods substantially outperform the DNI-based baselines in MSE (e.g., 1.15 vs. 3.75 for Cat) and RL (0.9948 vs. 0.9816), while also reducing FID by 4–10 points in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DNI-based baselines (Pix2Pix-DNI and CycleGAN-DNI), both AtomODE variants achieve substantially lower MSE (e.g., 1.15 vs. 3.75 on Cat; 1.18 vs. 5.91 on Dog) and higher RL scores (0.9948–0.9998 vs. 0.9780–0.9844), confirming that neural ODE-controlled filter atoms produce smoother and more accurate exposure transitions than linear network interpolation. On FID, AtomODE-Pix2Pix improves over Pix2Pix-DNI by 4–10 points in most categories (e.g., 57.88 vs. 61.89 on Cat, 40.08 vs. 39.81 on Female), while AtomODE-CycleGAN shows larger gains over CycleGAN-DNI (e.g., 87.43 vs. 220.90 on Dog). Against SAVI2I, the proposed methods win decisively on all three metrics across every category, with MSE reductions of 20–60× and FID improvements of 50–200 points. DLOW is the closest competitor on MSE (e.g., 2.44 vs. 2.29 on Cat; 1.37 vs. 1.87 on Female), but its RL scores are markedly lower (0.9489–0.9798 vs. 0.9948–0.9998), indicating less consistent exposure progression. On FID, DLOW is competitive on CelebA-HQ (62.57 Male, 63.31 Female) but lags on AFHQ (92.30 Cat, 173.65 Dog), whereas AtomODE-Pix2Pix achieves the best FID on four of five categories overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of single-photon imaging reconstruction, image colorization, and continuous parameter control. All prior single-photon reconstruction methods (QIS and SPAD) require multiple binary frames, and all prior colorization methods assume a well-exposed grayscale or multi-bit input; this work is the first to frame colorization from a single 1-bit frame as a solvable problem. Methodologically, it repurposes the atom-coefficient filter decomposition from Qiu et al.'s line of work as the parameterization that a neural ODE (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2018) continuously modulates, replacing the discrete network-weight interpolation used by DNI with a differentiable, parameter-efficient continuous trajectory. The base generative architectures (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN) are standard, but the ODE-controlled filter atoms constitute a new architectural pattern for exposure-adaptive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in single-photon imaging: the 150 Gb/s data throughput at 150k fps makes continuous operation infeasible for low-power, wireless, and space applications. By demonstrating that a single binary frame can be expanded into a full exposure burst and then colorized, it opens a path toward extreme data compression (1 bit/pixel) for future AR/VR and embedded sensing. However, the real-data validation is limited to printed images and a prototype QIS, and the method has not been tested at video frame rates or on scenes with highly non-uniform illumination. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-spectral or 3D single-photon imaging, and whether the neural ODE integration cost is acceptable at the frame rates these sensors actually operate at.
