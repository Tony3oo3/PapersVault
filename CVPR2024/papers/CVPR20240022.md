## Problem

Existing text-to-image style transfer methods either remain confined to low-level features like color and brushstrokes (conventional AST) or, when using diffusion-based personalization (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, LoRA), suffer from laborious finetuning, overfitting to style reference content, and poor preservation of textual semantics. No prior framework simultaneously captures high-level artistic elements such as composition and distinctive expression while faithfully adhering to the text prompt.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and one or more style reference images as input. Style references are processed through a pretrained VGG network to extract activations at three hierarchical levels (relu3_3, relu4_3, relu5_3), from which mean and standard deviation statistics are computed and mapped to 9 learnable style embeddings. The output is a 512×512 stylized image generated by a Stable Diffusion V1.5 backbone over 50 sampling steps with classifier-free guidance scale 9. For multi-reference or style-mixing scenarios, embeddings from different references are concatenated or averaged at corresponding hierarchical levels before generation.

## Method

ArtAdapter introduces a multi-level style encoder that maps VGG second-order statistics at three depths through distinct MLPs into 9 pseudo-word style embeddings, which are concatenated with text embeddings and jointly processed by the text encoder. Within the diffusion backbone's cross-attention layers, an Explicit Adaptation mechanism applies learnable residual weights (ΔW) exclusively to the key and value projections of style encodings while keeping the text pathway frozen, ensuring style-specific adaptation without degrading linguistic generalization. An Auxiliary Content Adapter (ACA), active only during training and only in the first 20% of denoising steps, injects weak content structure into the deepest UNet block with random color augmentation to prevent the style pathway from absorbing content semantics. A fast finetuning step adds rank-1 residual vectors to style-related activations, requiring only 25 iterations (~1 minute on a single GPU) to refine zero-shot style fidelity. Style mixing is achieved by concatenating embeddings from different references at their respective hierarchical levels, enabling controlled fusion of texture, tone, and composition from distinct artworks.

## Results

On single-reference style transfer, ArtAdapter (with fast finetuning) achieves a style similarity of 0.707 and aesthetics score of 5.601, the best style score among all methods except T2I-Style-Adapter (0.822), while maintaining a text similarity of 0.255 that far exceeds T2I-Style-Adapter's 0.181. In the zero-shot setting, ArtAdapter already reaches 0.656 style and 5.532 aesthetics, competitive with InST and FreeDoM. For multi-reference transfer, ArtAdapter attains the highest style score (0.680) and competitive text score (0.258) versus TI (0.678/0.265) and LoRA (0.678/0.254). In the user study (7-point scale), ArtAdapter leads all baselines on text (4.74), style (4.76), and overall quality (4.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conventional AST methods (CAST, StyTr²), ArtAdapter substantially outperforms on style similarity (0.707 vs. 0.583/0.577) and aesthetics (5.601 vs. 5.410/5.308), though it trails slightly on text similarity (0.255 vs. 0.297/0.299), reflecting the trade-off of stronger style adherence. Versus T2I-Style-Adapter, ArtAdapter sacrifices style score (0.707 vs. 0.822) and aesthetics (5.601 vs. 5.705) but recovers text fidelity by 0.074 (0.255 vs. 0.181), which the authors argue is critical for controllable T2I generation. Compared to InST and FreeDoM, ArtAdapter wins on both style (0.707 vs. 0.660/0.634) and aesthetics (5.601 vs. 5.255/5.059) while matching or exceeding text scores. In multi-reference transfer, ArtAdapter edges out TI and LoRA on style (0.680 vs. 0.678) and text (0.258 vs. 0.265/0.254), with a modest aesthetics gap (5.610 vs. 5.712/5.701). The user study confirms these trends: ArtAdapter leads TI by 0.25 on text and 0.41 on style, and leads LoRA by 0.83 on text and 0.68 on style, while also achieving the highest overall quality score (4.43 vs. 4.38/4.18). DALL-E3 is compared qualitatively only; the paper reports it struggles to faithfully reproduce style features despite leveraging ChatGPT-generated prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

ArtAdapter builds directly on the T2I-Adapter architecture [31] for its Auxiliary Content Adapter, repurposing it to provide weak content guidance that disentangles content from style during training. Conceptually, it departs from prior T2I style transfer methods (InST, T2I-Style-Adapter, FreeDoM) that rely on CLIP image embeddings for style conditioning, instead extracting multi-level style features from VGG second-order statistics (mean and std of relu3_3, relu4_3, relu5_3) and injecting them as pseudo-token embeddings into the text encoder. The Explicit Adaptation mechanism distinguishes itself from LoRA-style full-model adaptation by surgically modifying only the style-related key/value projections in cross-attention while freezing text pathways, a design that echoes the parameter-efficiency goals of LoRA [20] and SVDiff [17] but with a stricter architectural constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in T2I style transfer—content leakage from style references (e.g., a face appearing in every output when the reference contains one)—through a clean training-time mechanism (ACA) that is discarded at inference. The ~1-minute fast finetuning (25 iterations on a single GPU) makes personalized style adaptation accessible without the multi-hour LoRA/DreamBooth pipelines, which has direct practical value for creative tooling. However, the authors acknowledge that high-level style embeddings inadvertently incorporate lower-level features, causing interference in style mixing, leaving the full disentanglement of hierarchical style components as an open problem. The approach is also tied to the SD 1.5 backbone and VGG feature extraction, raising questions about transferability to newer diffusion architectures and whether the three-level VGG decomposition is optimal.
