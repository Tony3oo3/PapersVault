## Problem

Cross-domain few-shot learning methods typically fine-tune a large fraction of backbone parameters on tiny support sets, risking overfitting, and rely on nearest-centroid classifiers that ignore inter- and intra-class variance, producing poorly separated feature clusters. This paper addresses both limitations by drastically reducing the number of tunable parameters and introducing a sample-aware discriminative loss that explicitly accounts for hard positives and negatives during adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242171/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images resized to 224×224 pixels. During meta-testing, it receives an N-way K-shot support set (e.g., 5 labeled images per class) used to fine-tune lightweight task-specific parameters, and a query set of unlabeled images whose class labels are predicted. The final feature representation is a concatenation of [cls] embeddings from the last d_f=4 ViT layers (dimension 1536), and classification assigns each query to the nearest class centroid via cosine similarity.

## Method

DIPA pre-trains a ViT-small backbone with masked image modeling (IBOT) to obtain task-agnostic features, then adapts them to a target task by attaching per-layer scaling (γ) and shifting (β) parameters to the layer normalization, MLP, and multi-head attention sub-layers, reducing tunable parameters to 0.3–0.5% of the total. A variable tuning depth d_t (7 for seen, 9 for unseen domains) restricts adaptation to only the deeper, less transferable layers. Fine-tuning on the support set uses a proxy anchor loss with learnable class anchors that apply stronger gradients to the most dissimilar positive examples and the most similar negative examples, encouraging well-separated and compact clusters. At inference, query images are classified by nearest mean centroid computed from the adapted feature embeddings.

## Results

On the Meta-Dataset benchmark (13-way 5-shot), DIPA achieves 83.3% average accuracy across all domains under the MDL setting, improving over the prior best (eTT) by 5.0%. Under the more challenging SDL setting (pre-trained on ImageNet only), DIPA reaches 82.5% average accuracy, surpassing the best prior method (TSA) by 5.0% overall, with gains of 7.7% on seen and 5.3% on unseen domains. In the SDL-E setting, DIPA achieves 84.0% average accuracy, outperforming PMF by 6.5% while fine-tuning only 0.08% of model parameters versus PMF's 100%. DIPA is 3.3×–4.3× more parameter-efficient than existing methods during meta-testing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the MDL setting, DIPA outperforms eTT [59] (the closest self-supervised competitor) across all 10 common seen and unseen domains, with the largest gap on CIFAR-100 (+10.4%) and MS-COCO (+6.7%), while being up to 32.5× more parameter-efficient. Against TSA+SSA [53], DIPA wins in 4 of 5 unseen domains (Traffic Sign +6.4, MS-COCO +6.7, CIFAR-10 +4.5, CIFAR-100 +10.4) without requiring MixStyle augmentation, and is up to 24.7× more parameter-efficient. In the SDL-E+MDL setting, DIPA matches ATTNSCALE [2] exactly on seen domains (82.9%) and unseen (79.1%) but is 4.3× more parameter-efficient (0.08M vs 0.26M fine-tuned params); it also surpasses PMF [28] by 3.3% on unseen domains despite PMF using an additional meta-training stage and 100% parameter fine-tuning. In the harder SDL setting, DIPA beats TSA [37] by 7.7% on seen and 5.3% on unseen domains, and outperforms ProtoNet [52] and CTX [17] across all 13 domains. In the SDL-E+SDL setting, DIPA exceeds PMF [28] by 2.6% (seen), 4.4% (unseen com.), and 6.5% (all) without any meta-training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIPA builds on two distinct lines of work: the scale-and-shift parameter-efficient tuning paradigm from Lian et al. (NeurIPS 2022), which adjusts first- and second-order feature statistics, and the proxy anchor loss from Kim et al. (CVPR 2020), a discriminative metric-learning objective. It departs from the dominant cross-domain few-shot adaptation strategies—residual adapters (TSA), visual prompts (eTT), FiLM layers (Simple CNAPS, FLUTE), and full-backbone fine-tuning (PMF)—by replacing them with a minimal per-layer (γ, β) linear transformation applied only to deeper layers, combined with a sample-aware loss that explicitly accounts for intra- and inter-class variance during fine-tuning. The use of masked image modeling (MIM) for pre-training, rather than the more common DINO, further distinguishes the pipeline from prior ViT-based few-shot methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on the Meta-Dataset benchmark while fine-tuning only 0.3–0.5% of model parameters (versus 9–100% in prior methods), making the approach substantially cheaper to deploy in data-scarce, multi-domain settings. The simplicity of the adaptation mechanism—no auxiliary networks, no matrix multiplications, no prompts—suggests a broadly applicable recipe for parameter-efficient domain adaptation beyond few-shot learning. Limitations include the fixed per-layer linear form (no layer-specific flexibility) and the reliance on just two tuning-depth values (7 for seen, 9 for unseen domains), which the authors acknowledge may be suboptimal for individual datasets. Open questions include whether per-dataset or per-task tuning depths, and more expressive (e.g., low-rank) transformations, could close the remaining gap to full fine-tuning without sacrificing efficiency.
