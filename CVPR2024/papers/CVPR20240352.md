## Problem

Plane-based decomposition methods (e.g., HexPlane) reduce training time for dynamic NeRFs but struggle to capture high-fidelity texture details in complex motion. When standard 2D discrete wavelet transforms are applied to these plane representations, they fail due to shift variance (small spatial shifts disrupt oscillation patterns) and poor direction selectivity (the DWT checkerboard pattern blends ±45° orientations), producing ghosting artifacts around moving objects. No existing decomposition-based approach simultaneously addresses both limitations while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view images with per-frame timestamps (e.g., 21 synchronized GoPro videos at 2028×2704 and 30 fps for Plenoptic; monocular 360° synthetic video for D-NeRF) and produces rendered novel-view images at arbitrary camera poses and time steps. Internally, the 4D spatiotemporal volume is decomposed into six 2D plane representations (XY, ZT, XZ, YT, YZ, XT) at a spatial grid of 512 and temporal grid of 300, each parameterized by direction-aware wavelet coefficients rather than raw pixel values. For static scenes, the decomposition reduces to three planes plus three vectors in a TensorRF-like factorization.

## Method

DaReNeRF replaces the raw plane grids in HexPlane-style decomposition with learned dual-tree complex wavelet transform (DTCWT) coefficients: each 2D plane is represented by 12 direction-aware coefficients (6 real + 6 imaginary) plus one approximation coefficient, recovered via inverse DTCWT to yield shift-invariant, direction-selective features aligned along six distinct orientations. A trainable binary mask (applied via a stop-gradient Heaviside–sigmoid gating) sparsifies the 2^d redundant wavelet coefficients, and the resulting sparse representation is compressed with run-length encoding followed by Huffman coding. A compact MLP regresses RGB color from concatenated appearance features and view direction, while opacity is read directly from density features; images are synthesized via differentiable volumetric rendering. Training minimizes a photometric L2 loss plus total-variation regularization on the wavelet coefficients and a mask-sparsity penalty, using a coarse-to-fine resolution schedule and an emptiness-voxel acceleration structure.

## Results

On the Plenoptic Video dataset (all scenes average), DaReNeRF achieves the best PSNR of 32.258 dB with D-SSIM 0.012 and LPIPS 0.084, trained in 4.5 GPU hours. The sparse variant DaReNeRF-S achieves 32.102 dB PSNR with a compact 244 MB model size, outperforming HexPlane (31.705 dB, 252 MB) and Mix Voxels-X (31.730 dB, 500 MB) while matching their training time. On the D-NeRF monocular dataset, DaReNeRF reaches 31.95 dB PSNR without a deformation field, surpassing HexPlane (31.04 dB) and K-Planes (31.05 dB) but trailing 4D-GS (33.30 dB) which uses deformation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DaReNeRF outperforms all decomposition-based baselines on the Plenoptic Video dataset: it exceeds HexPlane by 0.55 dB PSNR (32.258 vs 31.705) at 100k steps, beats Mix Voxels-X by 0.53 dB, and surpasses K-Planes-hybrid by 0.63 dB, while also achieving the lowest D-SSIM (0.012). The sparse variant DaReNeRF-S (244 MB) matches HexPlane's model size (252 MB) while gaining 0.39 dB PSNR, making it the best performance-to-size trade-off among non-Gaussian methods. On the D-NeRF monocular dataset, DaReNeRF (31.95 dB) outperforms all non-deformation methods (HexPlane 31.04, K-Planes 31.05, D-NeRF 30.50) and even TiNeuVox-S (30.75), but is outperformed by deformation-based 4D-GS (33.30) and TiNeuVox-B (32.67), which exploit topological constraints unavailable to DaReNeRF. On static scenes (NSVF), DaReNeRF achieves 36.24 dB PSNR versus 34.67 dB for the DWT-based masked wavelet NeRF, a 1.57 dB gain at comparable sparsity (~94% vs 97%). The paper notes that 4D-GS achieves lower LPIPS (0.064–0.150) but higher perceptual artifacts in some cases, while DaReNeRF's advantage is most pronounced in texture fidelity and motion reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

DaReNeRF extends the plane-based 4D decomposition framework of HexPlane by replacing the raw plane parameters with direction-aware wavelet coefficients recovered via an inverse dual-tree complex wavelet transform (DTCWT). The key conceptual departure is from standard 2D DWT-based representations (as in Masked Wavelet NeRF), which the authors identify as suffering from shift variance and direction ambiguity—limitations that manifest as ghosting artifacts in dynamic scenes. For static scenes, the method follows a TensorRF-like decomposition, and the compression pipeline (learned masks + RLE + Huffman encoding) is adapted from Masked Wavelet NeRF. Unlike deformation-field methods (D-NeRF, TiNeuVox), DaReNeRF does not assume topological invariance, making it applicable to scenes with topology changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The direction-aware representation offers a drop-in upgrade for plane-decomposition NeRFs that addresses a well-known signal-processing weakness (DWT's checkerboard/direction ambiguity) without requiring additional supervision like depth or optical flow. The 2× training time reduction over HexPlane at superior quality makes it practically attractive for dynamic scene applications. However, the method's lower compactness compared to DWT (requiring 12 wavelet coefficient maps vs. 3) limits its ability to achieve sub-1MB model sizes, and it struggles with extremely sparse observations due to the absence of a deformation field. Open questions include whether more compact direction-aware constructions exist and whether the approach generalizes to even higher-dimensional or unbounded scenes.
