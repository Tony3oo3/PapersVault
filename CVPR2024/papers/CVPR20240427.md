## Problem

While data heterogeneity in federated learning has been extensively studied, device heterogeneity (varying computational and communication capacities) and state heterogeneity (dynamic client availability) remain poorly understood due to the absence of real-world benchmarks and evaluation metrics. Existing FL methods are typically evaluated in homogeneous simulations or with manually designated stragglers, which fail to capture the complex interplay of device speeds, network fluctuations, and client disconnection patterns that degrade performance in practice.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes a baseline device database of ~10,000 mobile devices (characterized by computational and communication latency) and a 136k-entry state database, from which DPGMM and DPCSM sample 100-device or 100-state subsets at controlled heterogeneity levels by tuning K_n, σ (devices) or startRank, α (states). For FL training, each client receives 10 local images (COVID-FL) or client-specific validation/test splits (OpenImage), and a frozen pre-trained ViT-B backbone is used. Outputs include test accuracy under deadline-based or readiness-based aggregation, total training time, and six Monte Carlo simulation metrics (DevMC-R/T, StatMC-R/T, InterMC-R/T) that correlate with actual FL performance rather than raw statistical divergence.

## Method

FLHetBench introduces two Dirichlet process-based samplers: DPGMM draws device speeds from a Gaussian mixture whose component means are perturbed by a controllable σ around the baseline mean, with Dirichlet-distributed client assignments; DPCSM uses a stick-breaking construction over states sorted by a single-state metric, with startRank and α controlling the quality and diversity of sampled states. Six heterogeneity metrics are computed via Monte Carlo simulation of the FL training loop, capturing successful participation rate and bias (deadline-based) or total communication cost (readiness-based), rather than relying on statistical divergence alone. The proposed BiasPrompt+ method combines two mechanisms: (1) a gradient surgery-based staleness-aware aggregation that projects stale client gradients onto the normal plane of the fresh gradient average, retaining only the non-conflicting component to maximize resource utility from slow or delayed clients; and (2) a BiasPrompt module that prepends learnable prompt embeddings (fast weights) to each ViT layer's input and updates only bias terms (stable weights) alongside server momentum, reducing communication and computation to a small fraction of full-model training while maintaining accuracy.

## Results

On OpenImage under deadline-based strategy, BiasPrompt+ achieves 35.75% test accuracy under severe interplay heterogeneity (InterMC-R=0.56), 33.79% under moderate (InterMC-R=0.73), and 35.24% under mild (InterMC-R=0.86), consistently outperforming all six baselines. The closest competitor, FetchSGD, reaches 20.78%, 25.62%, and 33.60% respectively, yielding gaps of 15.0, 8.2, and 1.6 percentage points. Under readiness-based strategy (Fig. 9), BiasPrompt+ also achieves the shortest training time across all heterogeneity levels, confirming its dual advantage in both accuracy and communication efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiasPrompt+ wins against all six baselines across every heterogeneity level on OpenImage. FetchSGD is the strongest competitor, reaching 33.60% under mild heterogeneity (only 1.64 pp behind BiasPrompt+), but degrades sharply to 20.78% under severe heterogeneity because it discards stale client updates, wasting low-capacity participants. FedProx and FedDyn, which add local regularization or dynamic parameters, suffer up to 10% accuracy drops even at mild heterogeneity (InterMC-R=0.9) due to staleness of less-active clients' extra parameters. FedKD (knowledge distillation) and FedBuff (asynchronous buffered aggregation) perform poorly under moderate-to-severe heterogeneity (16.24% and 17.11% at moderate, respectively), as they do not explicitly address wall-clock time or resource utilization. FedAVG, the standard synchronous baseline, shows the most uniform but weakest performance (14.84–23.48%), confirming that no single-axis fix (communication efficiency alone or asynchrony alone) suffices; BiasPrompt+ addresses both simultaneously via gradient-surgery staleness-aware aggregation and fast-weight prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FL heterogeneity literature by shifting focus from the well-studied data heterogeneity to device and state heterogeneity, which prior benchmarks (FedScale, FS-Real, FLASH) have only partially addressed. Methodologically, BiasPrompt+ repurposes visual prompt tuning (Jia et al., ECCV 2022) as fast weights (Tieleman & Hinton, ICML 2009) for parameter-efficient FL, and adapts gradient surgery (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020) into a staleness-aware aggregation rule that projects stale gradients onto the orthogonal complement of fresh gradients. The sampling methods (DPGMM, DPCSM) build on Dirichlet process nonparametrics to generate controlled heterogeneity levels, going beyond the single-point or fixed-distribution device data used in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLHetBench fills a concrete gap: prior FL evaluations either used homogeneous device simulations or manually designated stragglers, and the paper demonstrates that these straggler-mimic strategies fail to reproduce real-world training-time degradation. The finding that wall-clock time inflation and low resource utilization (rather than statistical divergence alone) drive performance loss reframes how the community should design and evaluate heterogeneity-aware FL methods. However, BiasPrompt+ still degrades under severe heterogeneity, and the benchmark is validated on only two datasets with a single ViT-B architecture, leaving open whether the metrics and method generalize to other model families, tasks, or larger-scale deployments.
