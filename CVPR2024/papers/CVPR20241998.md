## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for fibrosis staging from liver biopsy images treat tissue patches as graph nodes or MIL instances, learning spatial relationships between patches without explicitly modeling the vessel-fiber interactions that define fibrosis progression. This omission limits both staging accuracy and clinical interpretability, since fibrosis is fundamentally characterized by fiber deposition around and between vessels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241998/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes liver biopsy whole-slide images (WSIs) stained with Sirius Red or Masson's Trichrome, which are first segmented via mean shift to extract vessel and fiber regions. Only image tiles containing detected vessels or fibers are converted into graph representations, rather than processing the entire slide uniformly. The model outputs a fibrosis stage label (FIB 0-4) along with per-tile importance scores, where the number of tiles contributing to the WSI-level feature is adaptively determined by a saturation threshold rather than a fixed top-k.

## Method

XFibrosis transforms each relevant image tile into a primal-dual graph pair: a vessel-induced primal graph whose nodes are vessel centroids connected via Delaunay triangulation, and a fiber-induced dual graph whose nodes correspond to the primal graph's edges (i.e., fiber regions between vessels). Node embeddings concatenate CNN-extracted morphological features with 2D position encodings. A primal-dual graph convolution module (PDGCN) then performs three operations: two separate GCN layers propagate information within the vessel and fiber graphs independently to capture each micro-architecture, and an edge-enhanced GCN layer exploits the primal-dual correspondence to propagate joint vessel-fiber interaction features. WSI-level features are aggregated via adaptive TopK mean pooling over tile-level representations, and training minimizes a cross-entropy loss combined with an ordinal regularization term that penalizes predictions far from the true fibrosis stage.

## Results

On the Liver-Fibrosis-SR dataset (132 WSIs), XFibrosis achieves a mean AUC of 84.00% and accuracy of 53.63%, outperforming the best MIL baseline IBMIL (AUC 82.40%, accuracy 45.76%) and the best fibrosis-specific GNN baseline GNN-Fibrosis (AUC 68.50%, accuracy 43.46%). On the smaller FibrosisMT-v1.0 dataset (47 WSIs), the method reaches a mean AUC of 92.82% and accuracy of 64.40%, surpassing IBMIL (AUC 84.45%, accuracy 60.21%) by a substantial margin. Per-stage AUCs (FIB≥1 through FIB≥4) are also consistently the highest across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XFibrosis outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets across every reported metric. Against the strongest MIL baseline IBMIL, it gains +1.60 AUC and +7.87 accuracy points on Liver-Fibrosis-SR, and a larger +8.37 AUC and +4.19 accuracy on FibrosisMT-v1.0. Against the fibrosis-specific GNN baseline GNN-Fibrosis, the improvement is more dramatic: +15.50 AUC and +10.17 accuracy on Liver-Fibrosis-SR, and +19.46 AUC and +13.24 accuracy on FibrosisMT-v1.0. General GNN baselines (GATv2, ARMA, HGNN) all fall well below XFibrosis, with HGNN being the closest at AUC 70.67 vs. 84.00 on Liver-Fibrosis-SR. The paper attributes the gap to XFibrosis's explicit vessel-fiber graph representation, which captures micro-architectural topology that patch-based MIL and generic patch-graph GNNs cannot model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in WSI-based fibrosis staging, where graph nodes are regular grid image patches (as in GNN-Fibrosis [40]) or individual cells/nuclei, by instead grounding graph structure in explicit biological entities: vessels become nodes (via Delaunay triangulation) and fibers become edges (via the dual graph). The primal-dual graph construction draws on classical graph theory [18, 38] but repurposes it for histopathology, so that the two graphs simultaneously encode vessel topology and fiber morphology while preserving their connectivity. The graph convolution module extends standard GCNs with an edge-enhanced layer that propagates information across the primal-dual boundary, going beyond node-only message passing used in prior GNN-based histopathology methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit vessel-fiber modeling offers a clinically grounded interpretability story: the graph structure directly mirrors the histological definition of fibrosis stages (FIB-1 through FIB-4), and the importance-score heatmaps align with known fibrosis regions. However, the empirical evidence is constrained by very small datasets (132 and 47 WSIs) with high variance, limiting confidence in the reported gains. Open questions include whether the primal-dual formulation generalizes to other histopathology tasks involving distinct tissue components, whether the mean-shift segmentation bottleneck can be replaced with learned segmentation, and whether the approach scales to larger, multi-center cohorts.
