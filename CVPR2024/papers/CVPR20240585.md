## Problem

Few-shot NeRF methods that synthesize pseudo novel views via depth-guided warping typically mask out disoccluded (unreliable) regions to avoid inconsistent supervision, but this discards geometric information that could aid multi-view consistency. The paper identifies that while absolute color in these void regions is unreliable, relative depth ordering between neighboring pixels remains consistent, offering an underexploited self-supervised signal for geometry learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240585/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–4 sparse RGB views with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics and accumulates coarse depth maps from the current radiance field density. Warped novel views are generated periodically (every 1000 training steps) by deforming input pixels through cross-view transformations, and a binary unreliability mask is computed from void pixels and cross-view depth correlation. The output is a DVGO-style voxel grid storing per-voxel RGB features and density, rendered at 3 FPS, with training completing in ~7 minutes for a 360° scene.

## Method

ReVoRF builds on DVGO's voxel grid with a coarse-to-fine scheme and introduces a bilateral geometric consistency loss that treats reliable and unreliable warped regions differently: reliable regions receive standard color-and-density reconstruction supervision, while unreliable regions receive a relative depth consistency loss (adapted from Mertan et al.) that penalizes inconsistent depth ordering between the rendered depth and a DPT-estimated depth on the warped image. A reliability-aware voxel smoothing loss modulates the standard DVGO smoothing by a per-voxel factor derived from ray accumulation through reliable regions, and a reliability-guided learning strategy scales per-voxel gradients to prioritize reliable zones early in training. The warping process is iterative—depth maps and warped RGB are refreshed every 1000 steps—allowing the reliability mask and depth supervision to refine as the radiance field improves.

## Results

On the Realistic Synthetic 360° dataset (4-view setting), ReVoRF achieves a PSNR of 20.72 dB, SSIM of 0.848, and LPIPS of 0.179, outperforming the previous best method GeCoNeRF (19.78 dB PSNR) by approximately 1 dB while training in 7 minutes versus GeCoNeRF's >2 hours. On the LLFF dataset (3-view setting), ReVoRF attains the best SSIM (0.644) and LPIPS (0.316) among all compared methods, with a PSNR of 19.26 dB that is competitive with SparseNeRF (19.86 dB) and VGOS (19.35 dB). The method trains a full 360° scene in 7 minutes at 3 FPS rendering speed, making it one of the fastest few-shot approaches while delivering the highest reconstruction fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Realistic Synthetic 360° dataset, ReVoRF surpasses GeCoNeRF—the closest prior method that also exploits warped views—by ~1 dB in PSNR (20.72 vs. 19.78) and 0.006 in LPIPS (0.179 vs. 0.185), though GeCoNeRF retains a lead in SSIM (0.880 vs. 0.848). Against VGOS, the fastest competing voxel method, ReVoRF gains 1.81 dB in PSNR and 0.023 in SSIM at only a 4-minute training cost increase (7 vs. 3 min). On LLFF (3-view), ReVoRF achieves the best SSIM (0.644) and LPIPS (0.316) but trails SparseNeRF (19.86 dB) and VGOS (19.35 dB) in PSNR by 0.6 and 0.09 dB respectively, while training in 11 minutes versus SparseNeRF's >2 hours. ReVoRF dominates 3DGS on both datasets across all three metrics (e.g., 20.72 vs. 17.55 PSNR on 360°), confirming that the voxel-based radiance field approach with reliability-aware regularization substantially outperforms the Gaussian splatting baseline in the few-shot regime. Overall, the method trades a small SSIM gap to GeCoNeRF on 360° for large gains in PSNR, LPIPS, and training speed, and achieves the best perceptual quality (SSIM + LPIPS) on LLFF.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReVoRF builds directly on DVGO's voxel grid optimization framework for fast radiance field training, adopting its coarse-to-fine scheme and differentiable voxel smoothing as the architectural backbone. Its key conceptual departure is from GeCoNeRF, which discards unreliable (disoccluded) regions in warped novel views via self-occlusion masking; ReVoRF instead argues that these 'unreliable' regions retain valid relative depth ordering and should be exploited through a bilateral geometric consistency loss. The relative depth regularization term draws on the ranking-based depth consistency formulation from Mertan et al., repurposing it as a self-supervised signal for geometric coherence in regions where absolute color supervision is absent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for few-shot reconstruction: 7 minutes to train a 360° scene with a ~5% PSNR gain over prior few-shot methods makes high-quality sparse-view reconstruction accessible in near-real-time workflows. The core insight—that relative depth relationships survive warping even where absolute appearance does not—opens a path to extracting more supervision from limited views without requiring additional sensors or pre-trained models. However, the voxel-based representation inherently smooths fine geometric details, and the method has not been validated on large-scale or dynamic scenes, leaving open whether the reliability-aware smoothing strategy scales beyond small, static objects.
