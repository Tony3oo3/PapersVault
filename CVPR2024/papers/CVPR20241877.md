## Problem

Reconstructing 3D human interactions from single images is challenging due to mutual occlusion and the need to model relative depth and body proximity between two people. Existing single-person mesh estimators are not trained to reason about inter-person contact, and prior multi-person methods either require ground-truth contact annotations at test time or do not model contact at all. No generative model existed that captures the joint distribution of two people in close social interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241877/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single RGB image of two people in close proximity and consumes detected 2D keypoints (OpenPose/ViTPose) plus initial BEV multi-person regressor estimates as conditioning signals. The output is full SMPL-X parameters (21×3 pose, 10-dim shape, 3-dim global orientation, 3-dim translation) for both individuals, yielding two 3D meshes with correct relative placement. The diffusion model itself operates on the concatenated parameter vector of both people (not joint locations), and can also generate interaction samples unconditionally from pure noise.

## Method

BUDDI is a transformer-based denoising diffusion model trained to learn the joint distribution over SMPL-X parameters of two interacting people, using an x0-prediction parameterization with a cosine noise schedule over 1000 steps. The key architectural choice is operating directly on full body-model parameters (pose, shape, orientation, translation) rather than joint locations, so the model captures body surface geometry relevant to contact. For reconstruction, BUDDI serves as a data-driven prior inside an optimization loop: the current estimate is initialized via conditional DDIM sampling, then refined by minimizing a composite loss combining 2D reprojection error, a winding-number-based interpenetration penalty, and a score-distillation-inspired diffusion loss that performs a single diffuse-denoise step at t=10 to pull the estimate toward plausible social interactions conditioned on the regressor output.

## Results

On FlickrCI3D Signatures, BUDDI achieves 66 mm joint PA-MPJPE, a 38% improvement over BEV (106 mm), and the best PCC at r=10 mm (44%) among all methods. On Hi4D, BUDDI reduces joint PA-MPJPE to 98 mm versus BEV's 136 mm, with the largest per-action gains on hugging (58 mm improvement) and kissing (54 mm). On CHI3D, BUDDI achieves 68 mm joint PA-MPJPE versus BEV's 96 mm. In a perceptual study, BUDDI's unconditional samples are preferred over real data 44.4% of the time (50% being the upper bound of indistinguishability).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BUDDI outperforms BEV [42] on all three datasets in joint PA-MPJPE: 66 vs. 106 mm on FlickrCI3D Signatures, 98 vs. 136 mm on Hi4D, and 68 vs. 96 mm on CHI3D. Against the Contact Heuristic baseline, BUDDI achieves comparable or better joint PA-MPJPE (66 vs. 68 mm on Flickr) while substantially outperforming it on PCC (44% vs. 34% at r=10 mm), indicating the heuristic captures proximity but not the nuanced contact patterns. The non-optimization baselines (Transformer, BUDDI gen.) achieve lower joint PA-MPJPE than BEV but worse per-person accuracy, showing that direct regression from BEV input is insufficient. The VAE baseline performs worst on joint PA-MPJPE across all datasets (101/138/103 mm), suggesting that optimizing in a VAE latent space is less effective than the diffusion-based score distillation approach. BUDDI's advantage is most pronounced on complex close-contact actions like hugging and kissing on Hi4D, where the data-driven proxemics prior provides the strongest guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based 3D human motion generation (MDM, PhysDiff, EDGE) by shifting the state space from joint locations to full SMPL-X body parameters, enabling the model to reason about body surfaces and interpersonal contact. It departs from prior multi-person reconstruction methods (Fieraru et al., REMIPS, BEV) that require ground-truth contact annotations at test time, instead learning a generative proxemics prior that eliminates this dependency. The optimization framework borrows the score-distillation loss concept from 3D object creation (DreamFusion) and adapts it to the two-person SMPL-X parameter space, building on the broader lineage of data-driven priors in human mesh fitting (SMPLify, V-Poser, ProHMR, POSA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By learning a generative prior over 3D social proxemics, this work removes the need for explicit contact annotations during reconstruction, opening the door to scalable 3D social interaction understanding from in-the-wild images. The perceptual study showing near-indistinguishable quality (44.4% vs. 50% upper bound) validates that the learned distribution captures realistic interaction structure. Key limitations include restriction to exactly two people, absence of hand poses, and reliance on a small set of MoCap datasets for ground truth; future work could extend to groups, incorporate pixel-level conditioning, and address fine-grained interaction like finger pose and facial expressions.
