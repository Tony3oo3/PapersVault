## Problem

Existing simulated annealing algorithms restart training with a fixed network structure, ignoring the continuity between cycles and losing temporal knowledge inheritance. Meanwhile, the re-parameterization (Rep) technique—designed solely for lossless inference acceleration—has never been explored as a training mechanism, despite its ability to compress multi-branch knowledge into a single convolution without information loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242684/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels, identical to any CNN classifier. The distinguishing aspect is that the network topology itself changes during training: it alternates between a multi-branch training form and a single-branch deployed form, yet the final inference model has zero additional parameters or FLOPs relative to the baseline architecture.

## Method

RepAn makes the irreversible Rep operation recyclable by inserting two recovery stages between annealing cycles: (i) branch expansion, which adds randomly initialized parallel branches scaled by a learnable factor λ (starting at 0 to preserve inherited outputs, then ramping up), and (ii) BN restoration, which inverts the BN-fusion equation to recover normalization layers so gradients flow properly. Each cycle then proceeds: merge branches via Rep → expand new branches → restore BN → train (optionally with knowledge distillation) → repeat. The λ scheduler and optional KD teacher guide the network to retain previously learned features while the new branches absorb additional representations, producing an implicit ensemble/incremental learning effect. The approach is architecture-agnostic, compatible with any Rep-compatible block (RepVGG, DBB, ACNet, MobileOne), and requires no extra inference-time cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, RepAn improves RepVGG-A1 by 6.38% (64.90% → 71.28%) and ResNet-18(DBB) by 4.53% (73.44% → 77.97%) top-1 accuracy. On ImageNet, the method achieves up to 2.80% improvement over baselines (ResNet-50: 76.31% → 77.76%) and reaches 81.60% top-1 on RepVGG-B3, setting a new state-of-the-art among Rep methods. On downstream tasks, RepAn-pretrained backbones improve COCO mAP by 0.5 (ResNet-18: 31.7 → 32.2) and Cityscapes mIOU by 0.6 (ResNet-18: 74.9 → 75.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepAn consistently outperforms all compared Rep methods on ImageNet. Against RepVGG [17], the method gains +0.86% on A1 (75.24 vs 74.46 via ODBB comparison) and +1.08% on B3 (81.60 vs 80.52). Against DBB [16], RepAn achieves 76.50% vs 74.33% on ResNet-34 (+2.17%) and 77.76% vs 76.71% on ResNet-50 (+1.05%). Against DyRep [35], the closest competitor, RepAn wins by +0.48% on ResNet-50 (77.76 vs 77.08) and +0.48% on RepVGG-B3 (81.60 vs 81.12). Against OREPA [32], RepAn leads by +1.46% on ResNet-34 (76.50 vs 75.04). Against ODBB/RepNAS [66], RepAn gains +0.53% on A1 (75.24→76.50 via r34 comparison) and +0.63% on B3 (81.60 vs 80.97). On CIFAR-100, RepAn also beats ACNet [14] by +0.43% (78.37 vs 77.94) and DBB [16] by +1.01% (77.97 vs 76.96). MobileOne [58] is used only in the branch-count ablation (Table 5), where RepAn's multi-branch strategy shows diminishing returns beyond k=3 branches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the re-parameterization (Rep) technique, originally introduced by RepVGG [17] as a one-way deployment operation for inference acceleration, into a reversible training mechanism. It draws conceptual inspiration from DyRep [35], which dynamically compresses branches during training to reduce FLOPs, but inverts the logic: instead of compressing to save compute, RepAn compresses (via lossless branch merging) and then re-expands to inherit knowledge across annealing cycles. The method also builds on simulated annealing / SGDR-style restarts and incremental learning, but introduces structural changes (branch expansion + BN restoration) between cycles rather than simple weight re-initialization, making it the first work to exploit Rep's lossless compression property as a training modality for accuracy gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RepAn offers a general, drop-in training paradigm compatible with all existing Rep architectures (RepVGG, DBB, ACNet, MobileOne, etc.) that improves accuracy without any additional inference-time cost, which is practically attractive for deployment-sensitive settings. The 6.38% CIFAR-100 and 2.80% ImageNet gains are meaningful, though the ImageNet improvement is more modest and comes at the cost of additional training cycles. Open questions include how the number of annealing cycles and the λ schedule interact with dataset scale, whether the approach extends to non-convolutional backbones (e.g., ViT-based Rep variants), and whether the incremental-learning framing can be formalized more rigorously beyond the ensemble analogy.
