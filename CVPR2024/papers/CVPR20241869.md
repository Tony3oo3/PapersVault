## Problem

Existing VFI methods are constrained by limited receptive fields, causing sub-optimal performance when handling large motion where object correspondences between frames are difficult to locate due to large pixel displacements. Prior large-motion approaches either sacrifice small-motion generalization (XVFI) or require excessively deep networks that still struggle with fast-moving small objects (FILM). This paper addresses the gap by selectively integrating global-level information only where local flow estimation fails.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241869/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB input frames I_0, I_1 (tested at 1280×720, 2K, and 4K resolutions) and a scalar timestep t, and produces a single synthesized intermediate frame Î_t. The local feature branch operates at H/8 × W/8 resolution for its deepest features, while the sparse global matching branch uses a pretrained GMFlow encoder at H/8 × W/8 to extract discriminative global features. The sparsity ratio (1/8, 1/4, or 1/2 of pixels) controls how many points receive global matching, trading off correction coverage against computational cost.

## Method

The pipeline uses a two-step strategy: first, a CNN+Transformer hybrid local feature branch (inspired by EMA-VFI) estimates initial intermediate flows F̃_{t→0} and F̃_{t→1} at H/8 × W/8 resolution; second, a sparse global matching branch identifies flaws via difference maps D_0, D_1 (constructed by backward-forward warping and occlusion masking), selects top-k defective points, and performs global matching using a pretrained GMFlow feature extractor to produce sparse flow compensation. The bidirectional sparse flow is shifted to intermediate flow compensation via forward warping, then a flow merge block (inspired by RAFT's convex sampling) adaptively blends the initial local flows with the global compensation using learned R×R neighborhood weights. Training follows a transfer-learning protocol: pretraining the local branch on Vimeo90K (small motion), then fine-tuning only the sparse global matching block on X4K1000FPS (large motion) with the local branch and global feature extractor frozen.

## Results

On the curated large-motion benchmarks (X-Test-L, SNU-FILM-L, Xiph-L), the full model (Ours-1/2-Points) achieves 30.99 dB PSNR on X-Test-L-2K, 29.91 dB on X-Test-L-4K, 28.88 dB on SNU-FILM-L hard, 23.62 dB on SNU-FILM-L extreme, 30.93 dB on Xiph-L-2K, and 29.25 dB on Xiph-L-4K. The sparse global matching branch contributes up to +0.66 dB PSNR over the local-branch-only model on X-Test-L-4K, and even correcting only 1/8 of points yields +0.48 dB. On small-to-medium motion (SNU-FILM easy/medium), performance is maintained at 40.15/36.05 dB PSNR, comparable to VFIFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (Ours-1/2-Points) outperforms all six baselines on 5 out of 6 large-motion benchmark settings, losing only to BiFormer on X-Test-L-4K (29.91 vs. 30.11 dB PSNR). The largest margins are on Xiph-L-2K (+1.32 dB over BiFormer, +0.75 dB over RIFE) and X-Test-L-2K (+0.67 dB over BiFormer, +0.91 dB over RIFE). On SNU-FILM-L extreme, the method edges out BiFormer by 0.07 dB (23.62 vs. 23.55) and XVFI by 0.63 dB. FILM is unable to run at 4K resolution (OOM), and EMA-VFI-small trails the proposed method by 1.4–2.3 dB across all settings. On small-to-medium motion (SNU-FILM easy/medium), the method matches VFIFormer within 0.01–0.04 dB, confirming that the sparse global matching branch does not degrade performance in low-motion regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RIFE/EMA-VFI lineage of CNN+Transformer hybrid architectures for local intermediate flow estimation, and borrows the convex-sampling merge mechanism from RAFT. Its key departure from prior large-motion VFI methods (XVFI, FILM, BiFormer) is a two-step sparse strategy: rather than forcing the entire network to have a global receptive field (which is prohibitively expensive at VFI resolutions), it first estimates flows locally, then uses a difference map to identify only the defective regions and applies global matching (via a pretrained GMFlow feature extractor) exclusively at those sparse points. The paper also introduces curated large-motion benchmark subsets (X-Test-L, SNU-FILM-L, Xiph-L) by analyzing motion magnitude and sufficiency, filling a gap where existing benchmarks mix small and large motion cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sparse global matching approach offers a practical middle ground between local-only methods (fast but fail on large motion) and full global matching (accurate but computationally infeasible at 4K VFI resolutions), and the difference-map-guided selection shows that correcting only ~12.5% of points yields most of the benefit. Limitations include the two-stage training protocol (pretrain on Vimeo90K, finetune on X-Train with frozen backbone), dependence on a separately pretrained GMFlow feature extractor, and the fact that the method is specifically tuned for large motion with limited gains on small-to-medium motion. Open questions include whether the sparse matching can be made fully end-to-end, whether the difference map generalizes to occlusion-heavy or multi-object scenes, and whether the approach extends to other flow-based tasks beyond VFI.
