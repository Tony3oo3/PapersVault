## Problem

Existing 2D-3D cross-modal retrieval methods require large amounts of labeled data from both modalities, which is prohibitively expensive in practice. Under label scarcity, standard pseudo-labeling strategies produce noisy and overconfident predictions—especially for samples at class distribution boundaries—and the intrinsic heterogeneous gap between 2D and 3D representations further hinders effective cross-modal alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241980/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 2D images (encoded by a pre-trained ResNet-18) and 3D point clouds (encoded by a pre-trained DGCNN), both projected into a shared 256-dimensional embedding space. Both labeled and unlabeled samples from each modality are used during training, while at test time a query from one modality retrieves ranked results from the other. The evaluation metric is mean average precision (MAP) over the retrieval ranking.

## Method

FIVE introduces two key mechanisms within a semi-supervised framework. First, Fine-grained Prototypical Voting replaces the single per-class centroid with K fine-grained prototypes (generated via K-means on labeled features); each unlabeled sample retrieves its M nearest prototypes, which collectively vote for a pseudo-label, yielding more robust assignments at distribution boundaries. Second, Heterogeneous Mixup linearly fuses deep representations of cross-modal pairs sharing the same (pseudo-)label using a Beta-distributed coefficient, then performs soft InfoNCE-style similarity learning in a newly projected shared space to reduce the modality gap in a noise-tolerant manner. The entire objective is optimized via a bi-level sharpness-aware minimization scheme that simultaneously minimizes the loss and its local sharpness, providing robustness against residual pseudo-label noise.

## Results

FIVE achieves average MAP of 93.50% (2D→3D) and 91.80% (3D→2D) on 3D MNIST, 84.44% and 83.76% on ModelNet10, and 72.94% and 71.48% on ModelNet40, evaluated across 200–800 (or 800–3200) labeled samples. On average, FIVE outperforms the second-best baseline by 4.74% on 3D MNIST, 12.94% on ModelNet10, and 22.10% on ModelNet40. The advantage is most pronounced under extreme label scarcity (e.g., 200 labels), where FIVE reaches 91.68% MAP on 3D MNIST 2D→3D versus 83.30% for the next-best method (ALGCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIVE outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and both retrieval directions. On 3D MNIST, the closest competitor is ALGCN (89.10% avg 2D→3D), which leverages an additional GNN; FIVE surpasses it by 4.40 points without introducing extra network parameters. On ModelNet10, RONO (72.75%) and ALGCN (77.21%) are the strongest baselines, yet FIVE still leads by 11.69 and 7.23 points respectively. The gap widens dramatically on ModelNet40, where FIVE (72.94%) exceeds the best baseline CLF (61.86%) by over 11 points in 2D→3D retrieval. Notably, the performance margin is largest at the lowest label budget (200 labels), confirming that FIVE's prototypical voting and heterogeneous Mixup are most effective under severe label scarcity. The four DA-* variants [48] consistently underperform the dedicated 2D-3D methods (CLF, RONO), suggesting that adapting text-image retrieval architectures to 2D-3D is suboptimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cross-modal retrieval methods (CLF, RONO) that map 2D and 3D data into a shared embedding space by introducing a semi-supervised training paradigm. It draws on prototype-based semi-supervised learning (K-means clustering for fine-grained class structure) and adapts Mixup from the image domain to the cross-modal feature space, where it interpolates 2D and 3D representations with matching semantics. The bi-level optimization with sharpness-aware minimization (SAM/ASAM lineage) is repurposed here not for adversarial robustness but to mitigate pseudo-label noise, a departure from its typical use in classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—annotating 2D-3D pairs is expensive or infeasible when color/textual cues are missing—and shows that a small number of labeled samples plus abundant unlabeled data can substantially close the gap to fully supervised performance. However, experiments are limited to relatively small-scale datasets (≤12K samples) with pre-trained backbones (ResNet-18, DGCNN), and the baselines include text-image retrieval methods merely adapted to 2D-3D, which may understate the true competitive landscape. Open questions include whether the prototypical voting strategy scales to open-vocabulary or zero-shot settings, and whether the bi-level optimization overhead is justified at larger scale.
