## Problem

Existing neural 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, NeuS, etc.) learn a neural field for the entire scene, leaving the reconstruction of a user-specified target object unaddressed. Obtaining multi-view consistent segmentation of a target object is labor-intensive, and while SAM enables zero-shot 2D segmentation, it operates on a single view and its rich encoder features have not been leveraged to improve 3D reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241540/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (e.g., 49–64 views at 1600×1200 on DTU, 20–62 views at 1008×756 on LLFF) together with user-provided point and box prompts on a single annotated view. It produces a 3D SDF surface mesh of the target object, multi-view consistent 2D segmentation masks for all views, and a learned 3D occupancy field. Notably, only one view requires manual annotation; the 3D occupancy field propagates the segmentation to all remaining views, and SAM encoder feature maps at 256×256 resolution are distilled into the 3D field.

## Method

NTO3D is a two-stage pipeline built on the Instant-NSR architecture (two MLPs with multi-resolution hash encoding for SDF and color). In Stage 1, a 3D occupancy field is trained iteratively: it renders coarse 2D masks via a max-over-ray formulation (foreground pixel implies at least one foreground voxel on the ray; background pixel implies all voxels are background), which are then used as K-means centers and bounding-box prompts for SAM to generate refined masks, supervised back into the occupancy field via binary cross-entropy until convergence. In Stage 2, a lightweight output head is added to the neural field to predict per-point SAM encoder features, which are volume-rendered and supervised with L1 loss against the 256×256 SAM feature maps, alongside standard photometric and Eikonal losses for color and SDF. The key insight is that the iterative 3D occupancy field resolves cross-view consistency from a single prompt, while distilling SAM's learned features into 3D injects semantic and structural knowledge that improves surface reconstruction quality.

## Results

On the DTU dataset (15 scenes), NTO3D achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.73 mm in the w/ mask setting, outperforming NeuS (0.77), IDR (0.90), SPIn-NeRF (1.02), and SA3D (1.11). For segmentation quality, NTO3D's SAM-refined masks (M_SAM) reach a mean IoU of 0.983, surpassing DINO-based SPIn-NeRF (0.945) and SA3D (0.923). In the ablation, the full NTO3D pipeline achieves PSNR 33.06 and SSIM 0.9520, compared to 29.94/0.8914 for the Instant-NSR baseline, demonstrating that both the 3D occupancy field and 3D SAM feature field contribute meaningfully to reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTO3D outperforms all compared baselines on Chamfer distance in the w/ mask setting, with the closest competitor being NeuS (0.77 vs. 0.73). Against rendering-based methods (ISRF, SPIn-NeRF, SA3D), NTO3D achieves 0.11–0.50 mm lower Chamfer distance, partly because those methods rely on density fields and require post-hoc projection to extract geometry. For segmentation IoU, NTO3D's iterative 3D occupancy field + SAM pipeline (0.983) clearly beats DINO-based SPIn-NeRF (0.945), SA3D (0.923), and ISRF (0.660), while simple prompt projection (0.522) fails due to inaccurate depth reprojection. In the w/o mask setting, NTO3D is not directly compared (it requires user prompts), but NeuS (0.84) and UNISURF (1.02) show that removing mask supervision degrades all methods. The ablation confirms that the 3D occupancy field alone reduces CD from 0.82 to 0.76, and adding the 3D SAM feature field further improves PSNR from 32.44 to 33.06, indicating complementary contributions of the two proposed components.

## Relation to Prior Work

NTO3D builds directly on the NeuS/Instant-NSR lineage of SDF-based neural surface reconstruction with multi-resolution hash encoding, and repurposes the Segment Anything Model (SAM) as a zero-shot 2D segmentation oracle. It departs from prior target-object reconstruction attempts (SA3D, SPIn-NeRF, ISRF) by introducing an iterative 3D occupancy field that lifts multi-view 2D masks into a unified volumetric representation, and by distilling SAM encoder features into a 3D feature field via volume rendering—neither of which appears in earlier density-field-based or DINO-based approaches. The method thus sits at the intersection of neural implicit representation and foundation-model feature transfer, extending the whole-scene reconstruction paradigm to user-specified target objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NTO3D demonstrates a practical recipe for combining a large 2D foundation model (SAM) with a 3D neural field to achieve interactive, single-prompt target object reconstruction without laborious multi-view annotation, which is a meaningful workflow improvement for practitioners. The key limitation is the hard dependency on SAM's segmentation quality: if SAM fails on a challenging scene, the entire pipeline degrades, and the authors acknowledge that PEFT fine-tuning of SAM would be needed. Open questions include whether the 3D occupancy field approach generalizes to dynamic or partially occluded objects, whether the SAM feature distillation transfers to other foundation models, and whether the method scales to large-scale outdoor scenes beyond the controlled DTU/LLFF/BlendedMVS benchmarks.
