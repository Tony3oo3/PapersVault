## Problem

Existing Referring Image Segmentation methods, designed for natural images, struggle with the extreme spatial scale variation and diverse object orientations inherent in aerial imagery, leading to significant performance degradation on remote sensing data. Moreover, the only prior RRSIS dataset (RefSegRS) is limited to 4,420 images at 512×512 resolution with only 3 expression attributes, insufficient to train models robust to the full range of scale and rotation challenges in real-world aerial scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240793/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an 800×800 RGB remote sensing image (spatial resolution 0.5 m–30 m) and a natural-language referring expression (drawn from 20 object categories and 7 attributes) as input, and produces a binary pixel-level segmentation mask for the referred target. The RRSIS-D benchmark contains 17,402 image-caption-mask triplets where mask sizes span from tiny sub-object fragments to regions exceeding 400,000 pixels, and annotations were generated semi-automatically via SAM with manual refinement, then converted to RefCOCO format for compatibility with standard RIS tooling.

## Method

RMSIN comprises a Compounded Scale Interaction Encoder (CSIE) and an Oriented-aware Decoder (OAD). The CSIE stacks four Intra-scale Interaction Modules (IIM), each combining a Various Receptive Branch (multi-kernel convolutions with sigmoid pixel-wise gating) and a Cross-modal Alignment Branch (scaled dot-product attention with visual features as query and BERT language features as key/value), followed by a Cross-scale Interaction Module (CIM) that concatenates downsampled multi-stage features, applies multi-scale depth-wise convolutions to build a flattened sequence, performs cross-attention with local relationship compensation (LRC), and routes outputs through a Scale-aware Gate. The OAD performs top-down fusion of the four CSIE stages, replacing half the convolution layers with Adaptive Rotated Convolution (ARC), which predicts n rotation angles and corresponding weights from input features, re-parameterizes static kernel weights via inverse rotation matrices and bilinear interpolation, and computes a weighted sum of the rotated kernels to produce orientation-aware segmentation features. The visual backbone is a Swin Transformer pre-trained on ImageNet-22K and the language backbone is BERT-base; training uses AdamW (lr 3e-5, polynomial decay) for 40 epochs on 4× RTX 2080 GPUs.

## Results

On the proposed RRSIS-D dataset, RMSIN achieves 65.10% mIoU (val) and 64.20% mIoU (test), surpassing the closest competitor LAVT by 3.64% and 3.16% respectively. In terms of precision, RMSIN reaches 74.66% P@0.5 (val) and 74.26% P@0.5 (test), outperforming LAVT (69.54%/69.52%) by over 5 points. The advantage is particularly pronounced at higher IoU thresholds, where RMSIN gains over 3.0% in P@0.5, P@0.6, and P@0.7 over LAVT, indicating superior boundary accuracy for small and rotated objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RMSIN outperforms all eight baselines across nearly every metric on both validation and test splits. Against the strongest competitor LAVT [58], RMSIN gains +5.12% P@0.5 (val), +4.25% P@0.7 (val), and +3.64% mIoU (val), with similar margins on the test set. The first RRSIS-specific model LGCE [63] (Yuan et al.) trails RMSIN by 6.56% in mIoU (val), confirming that the larger RRSIS-D dataset and the rotated multi-scale architecture yield substantial improvements over the prior RRSIS baseline. Among the conventional RIS methods (RRN, CSMA, LSCM, CMPC, BRINet, CMPC+), all of which use ResNet-101 backbones, RMSIN leads by 13–19% in mIoU, highlighting the domain gap between natural-image RIS and remote sensing. The gap between Swin-B-based methods (LGCE, LAVT, RMSIN) and R-101-based methods is roughly 9–14% in mIoU, underscoring the importance of the visual backbone for aerial imagery. Notably, RMSIN's advantage over LAVT is most pronounced at moderate-to-high IoU thresholds (P@0.5–P@0.7), suggesting the ARC module and multi-scale interaction specifically improve boundary precision for rotated and small-scale targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the referring image segmentation (RIS) literature—dominated by transformer-based cross-modal fusion methods like LAVT and LGCE—into the remote sensing domain, where extreme scale variation and arbitrary object orientations break assumptions that hold for natural images. Architecturally, it borrows the Adaptive Rotated Convolution concept from rotated object detection and repurposes it for pixel-level mask generation, while the Compounded Scale Interaction Encoder (IIM + CIM) generalizes multi-scale feature fusion strategies previously applied in detection-oriented remote sensing work. The dataset construction pipeline, leveraging SAM with bounding-box prompts for semi-automated mask generation, departs from the fully manual annotation used in the prior RefSegRS benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 17,402-triplet RRSIS-D dataset is the largest benchmark for referring remote sensing segmentation and will likely become the standard evaluation set for this nascent task, enabling more rigorous comparison than the 4,420-image RefSegRS. The ARC-in-decoder design offers a transferable recipe for incorporating orientation awareness into any segmentation pipeline dealing with arbitrarily oriented objects. Open questions include whether the SAM-assisted annotation introduces systematic biases (the authors acknowledge domain-gap variability), whether the approach generalizes to other aerial tasks (e.g., panoptic or instance-level referring), and whether the 0.5 m–30 m spatial resolution range captures the full diversity of operational satellite imagery.
