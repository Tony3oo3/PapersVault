## Problem

Existing zero-shot composed image retrieval (ZS-CIR) methods like Pic2Word and SEARLE train their projection modules on restricted, pre-defined text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of [ ]'), which limits backbone scalability and generalizability to diverse text conditions. Additionally, their training pipelines require sequential forward passes through both visual and textual encoders, making them computationally expensive and difficult to scale to larger backbones like CLIP ViT-G.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241468/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a reference image and a free-form text condition as a composed query, and outputs a ranked list of retrieved images from a gallery. The image is projected into the CLIP token embedding space via a learned 3-layer MLP, inserted into the prompt template 'a photo of [ ] that [cond]', and standard text-to-image retrieval is performed. During training, no images are needed at all—only 5.5M text captions (CC3M + StableDiffusion prompts) are used, with an average token length of ~12.56 per caption, making the training corpus only 571 MB versus ~430 GB for the corresponding images.

## Method

LinCIR introduces a language-only training paradigm for ZS-CIR built around a novel self-supervision called Self-Masking Projection (SMP). Instead of projecting image embeddings to the token space (as in Pic2Word/SEARLE), LinCIR projects the text latent embedding z_c through a 3-layer MLP φ into the token embedding space, then replaces all keyword tokens (consecutive adjectives and nouns, identified via POS tagging) in the original caption with this projected embedding. The training objective is an MSE loss between the original text embedding and the embedding of the keyword-masked caption, forcing the [ ] token to encode a one-word summarization of the input's essential semantics. To bridge the modality gap between text and image embeddings at inference, a carefully designed random noise n ~ Unif(0,1) × N(0,1) is added to the text embedding before projection during training, which empirically outperforms standard Gaussian noise by enforcing diverse norm magnitudes in the high-dimensional CLIP space. Only the φ module is trained (with the textual encoder frozen), enabling ViT-G training in 48 minutes on 8 A100 GPUs—16× faster than Pic2Word and 17× faster than SEARLE at the same backbone size.

## Results

On CIRCO (the paper's primary benchmark), LinCIR with CLIP ViT-G achieves 19.71 mAP@5, substantially outperforming SEARLE (13.20) and Pic2Word (5.54) at the same backbone size. On FashionIQ, LinCIR ViT-G reaches 45.11 Average R@10, surpassing the state-of-the-art supervised method Combiner (38.32) by 6.79 points. On CIRR full R@10, LinCIR ViT-G scores 76.05 vs. SEARLE's 75.11 and Pic2Word's 69.23. Training the ViT-G backbone takes only 48 minutes on 8 A100 GPUs, roughly 16× faster than Pic2Word and 18× faster than SEARLE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinCIR consistently outperforms both Pic2Word and SEARLE across all four benchmarks (CIRCO, GeneCIS, FashionIQ, CIRR) and all three backbone sizes (ViT-L, ViT-H, ViT-G). The performance gap widens dramatically at ViT-G: on CIRCO mAP@5, LinCIR scores 19.71 versus SEARLE's 13.20 and Pic2Word's 5.54, indicating that Pic2Word and SEARLE's restricted training prompt ('a photo of [ ]') fails to scale with larger backbones. On FashionIQ Average R@10, LinCIR ViT-G (45.11) surpasses the supervised Combiner method (38.32) by 6.79 points, making it the first zero-shot CIR method to beat a supervised baseline on this benchmark. On CIRR full R@10, LinCIR ViT-G (76.05) edges out SEARLE (75.11) and clearly beats Pic2Word (69.23), though the margin over SEARLE is modest. On GeneCIS average R@3, LinCIR ViT-G (33.54) leads SEARLE (32.46) and Pic2Word (29.50), with the largest gap on the 'Focus Attribute' and 'Change Attribute' subtasks where attribute-level reasoning is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinCIR extends the projection-based zero-shot CIR paradigm established by Pic2Word and SEARLE, which learn a module φ to map image latent embeddings into the CLIP token embedding space for text-to-image retrieval. The key conceptual departure is inverting the projection direction: instead of projecting visual embeddings, LinCIR projects *textual* latent embeddings, enabling training entirely on text corpora via a novel Self-Masking Projection (SMP) self-supervision. The modality-gap mitigation builds on Nukrai et al.'s noise-injection strategy for language-only training, but replaces Gaussian noise with a randomized-variance distribution (Unif(0,1)×N(0,1)) that empirically bridges the gap more effectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 48-minute ViT-G training time (vs. hours or days for prior methods) and the 3,400× storage reduction make large-backbone ZS-CIR practically accessible, removing a major barrier to scaling. The finding that Pic2Word and SEARLE *degrade* at ViT-G while LinCIR improves is important: it suggests that restricted training prompts are a fundamental bottleneck for backbone scalability, not just a minor limitation. Open questions include whether SMP generalizes to other VLMs (the paper shows a BLIP extension but with limited evaluation), whether the noise-distribution finding transfers to other language-only training tasks, and whether the approach can handle multi-image or video retrieval settings.
