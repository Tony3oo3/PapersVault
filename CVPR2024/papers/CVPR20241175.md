## Problem

Autocalibration of a perspective camera with constant intrinsics from point correspondences has traditionally relied on Kruppa's equations (requiring fundamental matrices) or modulus constraints (requiring projective reconstruction), both of which suffer from known degeneracies and fail to enforce positive-semidefiniteness of the DIAC under noise. No prior work provided a complete taxonomy of minimal autocalibration problems or a depth-based formulation that avoids fundamental matrix compatibility issues entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D homogeneous point correspondences from 2 or 3 views (typically 5–7 points depending on the intrinsics prior) along with an optional partial intrinsics prior encoded as linear constraints on K (e.g., zero skew s=0, square pixels f=g). It outputs the full 5-parameter calibration matrix K (focal lengths f, g; principal point u, v; skew s) together with the unknown 3D point depths λ_ip for each point in each view. The formulation is designed so that any subset of the 5 intrinsics can be fixed a priori, with the solver adapting to the remaining unknowns.

## Method

The core contribution is a depth-based formulation that eliminates camera extrinsics from the projection equations, replacing them with constraints stating that Euclidean distances between 3D points are invariant across views, expressed as polynomial equations in the unknown depths and the DIAC ω = K^{-T}K^{-1}. The DIAC is parametrized using (f², g², s/g) to exploit invariance under sign flips, and the resulting system is solved via numerical homotopy continuation with an offline/online pipeline: offline, all solutions for a synthetic instance are tracked via monodromy and parameter homotopy; online, solutions are tracked to the real problem instance. To handle over-constrained cases, the paper introduces minimal relaxations—subsets of depth equations selected via a 4-coloring of point pairs—and provides a complete combinatorial taxonomy of 80 problem groups (by intrinsics prior, number of views, and number of points), identifying the fewest-solution relaxations for efficient solving. Three practical solvers (ffuv0, fguv0, fguvs) are implemented in Julia using HomotopyContinuation.jl and embedded in MSAC/RANSAC frameworks for robust estimation on real data.

## Results

On Fountain-P11, the fully uncalibrated fguvs solver achieves Δfg = 0.035, Δuv = 0.064, Δs = 0.008, and Re = 1.075, outperforming all Kruppa-based and BnB baselines on focal length and reprojection error. The partially calibrated ffuv0 solver (square pixels, zero skew) is the overall best method, reaching Δfg = 0.017 and Re = 0.449 on Fountain-P11 and Δfg = 0.017, Re = 0.672 on Herz-Jesu-P8. In the COLMAP integration (Table 3), fguv0 initialization reduces Δfg from 0.335 (COLMAP guess) to 0.0058 on Fountain-P11 and from 0.651 to 0.072 on KITTI-Depth, approaching ground-truth-initialized performance (0.0013 and 0.0401 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kruppa-8 [30] (Luong & Faugeras, IJCV 1997), all three proposed solvers dominate: fguvs achieves Δfg = 0.035 vs. 0.260 (7.4× lower) and Re = 1.075 vs. unavailable (inconsistent reconstruction) on Fountain-P11, while Kruppa-8 also suffers 3–6% failure rates due to indefinite DIAC estimates. Against Kruppa-6 [42] (Schaffalitzky, ECCV 2000), the closest competitor in point count, fguvs reduces Δfg from 0.137 to 0.035 (3.9×) and Re from 2.891 to 1.075 on Fountain-P11, and from 0.098 to 0.041 on Herz-Jesu-P8. Against the state-of-the-art BnB methods [36] (Paudel & Van Gool, ECCV 2018), fguvs wins decisively on focal length (Δfg 0.035 vs. 0.127 for Kruppa BnB, a 3.6× improvement on Fountain-P11) and reprojection error, but loses narrowly on principal point (Δuv 0.064 vs. 0.058 for Kruppa BnB on Fountain-P11). The partially calibrated solvers ffuv0 and fguv0 extend these advantages further, with ffuv0 achieving the best scores across nearly all metrics on both datasets. The paper also notes that Kruppa-based methods exhibit a known degeneracy (optical centers on a sphere with axes through the center) that does not affect the proposed depth-equation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimal Euclidean reconstruction framework of four points in three calibrated views [24, 40] to the uncalibrated case, and adopts the algebraic-geometric minimal problem formalism (rank conditions, variety irreducibility) from the PLMP line of work [8]. It departs from the two dominant autocalibration paradigms—direct methods based on Kruppa's equations and the DIAC [14, 28, 30], and stratified methods relying on a projective reconstruction and modulus constraints [39, 51]—by formulating constraints directly on image points, unknown 3D depths, and a partially specified calibration matrix K. The key conceptual departure is the use of depth-consistency equations (vanishing of inter-view Euclidean distance differences) parameterized through the image of the absolute conic ω, which sidesteps fundamental-matrix compatibility issues and the well-known Kruppa degeneracy when optical centers lie on a sphere [49].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete taxonomy of 80 minimal autocalibration problem groups (organized by prior knowledge of K, number of views, and number of points) provides a reference that was previously absent, and the practical solvers demonstrate that direct recovery of K (rather than the DIAC) avoids the positive-semidefiniteness failures that plague Kruppa-based methods under noise. The COLMAP integration shows that in challenging scenes with few matches (KITTI-Depth), the solver's K estimates are more reliable than those obtained through bundle adjustment refinement alone, suggesting a practical path toward more robust SfM pipelines. Limitations include substantially higher runtime (up to ~13× slower per iteration than Kruppa-6) and the restriction to constant intrinsics across views; extending the framework to varying intrinsics, more than three views, or non-perspective cameras remains open.
