## Problem

Event cameras offer exceptional temporal resolution (>10,000 fps) and dynamic range (>120 dB) ideal for real-time traffic monitoring, yet their potential in static traffic monitoring remains largely unexplored. Existing event-based traffic datasets are predominantly ego-motion, lacking the static roadside perspective essential for comprehensive traffic surveillance. This paper addresses the gap by introducing eTraM, the first fully event-based traffic monitoring dataset captured from a static perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240875/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input consists of asynchronous event streams (tuples ⟨x,y,p,t⟩) from a Prophesee EVK4 HD camera at 1280×720 resolution, mounted at ~6 m height with a ~35° pitch angle. Events are denoised via spatiotemporal filtering (requiring ≥2 same-polarity neighbors within a 10 ms window in the 8-neighborhood) and binned into 30 Hz synchronous frames for annotation. The output comprises over 2M 2D bounding boxes with class labels across 8 traffic participant classes (cars, trucks, buses, trams, pedestrians, bikes, bicycles, wheelchairs) plus object IDs enabling multi-object tracking, spanning 10 hours of data across intersections, roadways, and local streets.

## Method

Data was acquired over 8 months at multiple sites around ASU Tempe campus, systematically covering daytime, nighttime, twilight, and varied weather conditions. A spatiotemporal filter discards events lacking sufficient same-polarity neighbors within a 10 ms temporal window in the 8-neighborhood, mitigating the heightened sensor noise observed in nighttime recordings. The filtered events are partitioned into 30 Hz frames and manually annotated using CVAT, yielding the 70/15/15 train/val/test split. Baseline evaluation trains three SOTA detectors from scratch—RVT (Recurrent Vision Transformer), RED (Recurrent Event-camera Detector), and YOLOv8—on 7 hours of data over 2–3 days on A100 GPUs, reporting AP50 per traffic category and scene. Generalization experiments specifically probe transferability to nighttime (day-only vs. day+night training) and unseen intersection scenes (held-in vs. held-out test sets), revealing that event-based models require explicit nighttime training data despite illumination invariance.

## Results

On eTraM, the tensor-based event detector RVT achieves the best overall AP50 of 0.539 (PED 0.309, VH 0.717, MM 0.313), followed by RED at 0.491 (PED 0.303, VH 0.649, MM 0.197), while the frame-based YOLOv8 trails significantly at 0.178 (PED 0.134, VH 0.314, MM 0.086). Vehicle detection consistently outperforms pedestrian detection across all methods and scenes. In the generalization study, models trained on day+night data (RVT: VH 0.761, PED 0.254; RED: VH 0.673, PED 0.422) substantially outperform day-only models on unseen nighttime data (RVT: VH 0.566, PED 0.166; RED: VH 0.374, PED 0.354), and models generalize well to unseen intersections (held-out AP50 within ~0.1 of held-in).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three detection methods on eTraM: two tensor-based event detectors (RVT and RED) and one frame-based detector (YOLOv8). RVT consistently outperforms both RED and YOLOv8 across all scenes and lighting conditions, achieving an overall AP50 of 0.539 versus RED's 0.491 and YOLOv8's 0.178. The gap between tensor-based and frame-based methods is most pronounced for micro-mobility (RVT 0.313 vs. YOLOv8 0.086) and pedestrians (RVT 0.309 vs. YOLOv8 0.134), suggesting that temporal recurrence in event tensors captures motion cues that single-frame representations miss. In the nighttime generalization experiment, adding just 45 min of nighttime data to the training set boosts RVT's vehicle AP50 from 0.566 to 0.761 and RED's from 0.374 to 0.673, confirming that event-based models do not automatically transfer to nighttime without explicit nighttime training. For unseen-scene generalization, both RVT and RED maintain comparable AP50 on held-out intersections (RVT: 0.628 VH / 0.529 PED) versus held-in intersections (0.449 VH / 0.316 PED), indicating strong spatial transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first fully event-based traffic monitoring dataset from a static (roadside) perspective, departing from the dominant ego-motion paradigm in prior event-based driving datasets (DDD17, DDD20, DSEC, 1 Megapixel, Gen1). While prior static event datasets exist (DVS-Pedestrian, DVS-OUTLAB), they are limited in scope—either single-class or non-traffic scenarios. The baseline evaluation repurposes SOTA event-based detectors (RVT, RED) and a frame-based method (YOLOv8) on this new static monitoring setting, and the spatiotemporal denoising pipeline builds on the neighborhood-consistency filtering of Brosch et al. [7].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

eTraM fills a clear gap in the event-based vision literature by providing a large-scale (10 hr, 2M boxes), multi-scene, multi-condition benchmark specifically designed for static traffic monitoring, a setting critical for real-world ITS deployment. The nighttime generalization finding—that event cameras' illumination invariance does not automatically transfer to nighttime without explicit nighttime training data—challenges a common assumption and has practical implications for deployment pipelines. Open questions include whether domain adaptation or synthetic nighttime event generation can close this gap, whether the dataset scales to more geographically diverse scenes, and how the low event sparsity (30× fewer events than ego-motion) affects detection performance at scale.
