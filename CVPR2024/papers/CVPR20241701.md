## Problem

Existing practical solvers for the P2P1L and P1P2L minimal absolute pose problems require computing roots of degree-4 and degree-8 univariate polynomials, respectively. Although Galois group analysis (Duff et al., 2022) showed these problems decompose into lower-degree subproblems, no practical solver exploiting this structure had been implemented. This paper closes that gap by providing algebraically optimal solvers that reduce the required polynomial degrees to 2 and 4.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solvers consume calibrated 3D-2D correspondences: either two 3D points with their 2D projections plus one 3D line with its 2D projection (P2P1L), or one 3D point with its 2D projection plus two 3D lines with their 2D projections (P1P2L). The output is the full camera extrinsic pose — a 3×3 rotation matrix and a 3-vector translation — recovered up to a small number of discrete solutions (up to 4 for P2P1L, up to 8 for P1P2L). The solvers are valid generically; a separate coplanar-case variant is provided for P2P1L, and a frame-rotation stabilization handles a near-degenerate configuration in P1P2L.

## Method

Both solvers begin by rigidly transforming the input into a special reference frame (inherited from Ramalingam et al.) that fixes the camera center and simplifies the projection equations. The linear projection constraints are then used to express the translation and most rotation entries as linear functions of a small set of free rotation variables. Substituting these into the rotation-matrix orthonormality constraints yields a system of bivariate polynomial equations; a change of variables (ratio of two rotation entries) collapses the system to a single univariate polynomial — quadratic for P2P1L, quartic for P1P2L. The P1P2L solver additionally rotates the world frame to align the first 3D line with the z-axis, eliminating a division-by-zero degeneracy that causes numerical instability when the line is nearly parallel to the image plane. The resulting solvers are elementary (requiring only square roots and standard quadratic/quartic formulas), are ~4–6× faster than the E3Q3-based state of the art, and achieve superior numerical stability on both synthetic and real Oxford Multi-view data within a LORANSAC pipeline.

## Results

On synthetic noiseless data (1e5 samples), the proposed P2P1L solver achieves a mean rotation error of 5.3e-12 rad and max rotation error of 1.2e-07 rad, compared to 2.8e-05 and 2.7 rad for the 3Q3 baseline. The P1P2L solver (stabilized) achieves mean rotation error of 1.2e-07 rad and max of 0.010 rad, versus 3.3e-05 and 2.60 rad for 3Q3. In raw solver runtime, the P2P1L solver runs in 313.8 ns (≈6× faster than 3Q3's 1860.6 ns) and the P1P2L solver in 504.0 ns (≈4× faster than 3Q3's 1967.1 ns). Within a full LORANSAC pipeline on the Oxford Multi-view dataset, the P2P1L solver achieves a 0.94× average speedup over 3Q3 (slightly slower due to scoring overhead) but is 2.14× faster than Ramalingam+SVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 3Q3 solver [Kukelova et al., CVPR 2016], the proposed method wins decisively on both metrics: raw solver runtime is 6× faster for P2P1L (313.8 ns vs 1860.6 ns) and 4× faster for P1P2L (504.0 ns vs 1967.1 ns), while max rotation error drops from 2.7 rad to 1.2e-07 rad (P2P1L) and from 2.60 rad to 0.010 rad (P1P2L). Against Ramalingam et al. [ICRA 2011], the speedup is even larger—approximately 28× for P2P1L (313.8 ns vs 8897.5 ns for the SVD variant)—and the max rotation error improves by roughly four orders of magnitude. Within the full LORANSAC pipeline on the Oxford Multi-view dataset, the P2P1L solver is marginally slower than 3Q3 on average (0.94× speedup) because RANSAC's scoring and local-optimization steps dominate wall-clock time, but it remains 2.14× faster than Ramalingam+SVD. For P1P2L, the in-RANSAC speedup over 3Q3 is only 0.59× on average, which the authors attribute to scenes where a larger fraction of time is spent on model scoring rather than the minimal solver itself. Final pose accuracy in RANSAC is identical across all solvers, confirming that the speed and stability gains come without any accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key threads: Ramalingam et al. [25] (ICRA 2011), who first solved P2P1L and P1P2L using degree-4 and degree-8 polynomials via special reference frames, and Duff et al. [9] (2022), who showed via Galois group computation that these problems decompose into simpler subproblems but did not provide practical solvers. The paper's core contribution is turning the theoretical decomposition from [9] into explicit, implementable solvers that achieve the algebraically optimal polynomial degrees (quadratic for P2P1L, quartic for P1P2L). It departs from the E3Q3-based approach [17] that had become the practical state of the art, replacing a general-purpose quadric-intersection solver with problem-specific elementary algebra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since minimal solvers are called thousands of times inside RANSAC, the 4–6× speedup and improved numerical stability have direct practical impact on pose estimation pipelines in localization, SLAM, and SfM. The algebraic optimality result is clean and definitive, closing a gap between what Galois theory predicted was possible and what was actually implemented. Limitations include the need to switch between solver variants for coplanar vs. generic configurations, and the P1P2L stabilization trick (rotating the world frame) which fails in the coplanar case. An open question is whether similar Galois-theoretic decompositions can be systematically applied to other mixed-feature minimal problems beyond the two addressed here.
