## Problem

Placing a target object into a specific location of a scene image with identity consistency—without per-object fine-tuning—remains unsolved. Reference-based editing methods (e.g., Paint-by-Example, ObjectStitch) preserve only semantic consistency and fail on untrained categories, while tuning-based customization methods (e.g., DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) require ~1 hour of fine-tuning per object and cannot specify the target scene or location.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target object image (background removed and center-aligned), a scene image, and a location box or optional coarse shape mask as input. At inference, the location box is expanded into a square with a 2.0× amplifier ratio, cropped, and resized to 512×512 for the diffusion model, enabling arbitrary scene aspect ratios and object scales. The output is a full-resolution composited image where the object is seamlessly regenerated at the specified location with harmonized lighting and pose, requiring no per-object parameter tuning.

## Method

AnyDoor decomposes object representation into two complementary channels: (1) an ID extractor using DINOv2 (with background removal) that produces 257 discriminative tokens (global + patch) projected to 1024-dim and injected via cross-attention into every UNet layer, and (2) a detail extractor (ControlNet-style encoder) that consumes a high-frequency map—computed via horizontal/vertical Sobel filters on the grayscale object, masked by an eroded contour—stitched into the scene at the target location, plus a shape mask, producing hierarchical feature maps concatenated with UNet decoder features. The base generator is Stable Diffusion V2.1 with the UNet encoder frozen and decoder tuned. Training leverages video datasets (YouTubeVOS, UVO, etc.) to capture appearance variation of the same object across frames, combined with high-quality image datasets for scenario diversity, and employs adaptive timestep sampling that biases video samples toward early denoising steps (structure/pose learning) and image samples toward late steps (texture/detail learning).

## Results

In the user study (15 annotators, 30 image groups, 4 proposals each), AnyDoor achieves a Fidelity score of 3.06/4, substantially outperforming Paint-by-Example (2.10) and Graphit (2.11), while also leading in Quality (3.04 vs. 2.71 and 2.65). On Diversity, AnyDoor scores 2.88, slightly below Paint-by-Example (3.04) but marginally above Graphit (2.84), which the authors attribute to the fact that reference-based methods only preserve semantic consistency and thus have a larger space for variation. Qualitative comparisons (Figs. 5–6) confirm that AnyDoor preserves instance-level identity (e.g., logos, patterns, facial features) for untrained concepts where all baselines degrade to semantic-level consistency or suffer from multi-subject attribute confusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two reference-based baselines with quantitative scores (Paint-by-Example and Graphit), AnyDoor wins decisively on Fidelity (+0.95 and +0.95) and Quality (+0.33 and +0.39), while conceding a small Diversity gap to Paint-by-Example (−0.16) that the authors explain as an artifact of semantic-only guidance allowing more pose variation. In qualitative comparisons (Fig. 5), IP-Adapter and Stable Diffusion inpainting fail to preserve distinguishing features such as a dog-face logo on a backpack or the color pattern of a sloth toy, whereas AnyDoor retains these instance-specific details. For tuning-based methods (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, Cones) evaluated on multi-subject composition (Fig. 6), all three suffer from cross-subject attribute leakage (e.g., one subject's pattern appearing on another), while AnyDoor composes multiple objects without any per-object fine-tuning and without such confusion. Paint-by-Example performs acceptably on trained categories (dog, cat) but degrades sharply on novel concepts, a gap AnyDoor closes through its DINOv2-based ID tokens and high-frequency detail maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyDoor sits at the intersection of reference-based image editing (Paint-by-Example, ObjectStitch) and customized subject-driven generation (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, Cones), but departs from both in critical ways. Where prior reference-based methods rely on CLIP image encoders that capture only semantic-level information, AnyDoor substitutes DINOv2 self-supervised features for discriminative instance identity, and where tuning-based methods require per-object fine-tuning, AnyDoor operates entirely zero-shot. The ControlNet-style detail injection and the use of video data to learn appearance variation (adaptive timestep sampling) are novel architectural and data-strategy choices that distinguish it from both lineages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyDoor provides a practical, tuning-free solution for object-level image customization that directly addresses a gap between semantic-level editing and per-object fine-tuning, with immediate applications in virtual try-on, compositing, and content creation. The finding that self-supervised DINOv2 features substantially outperform CLIP for identity preservation (DINO Score 64.1 vs. 31.5) challenges the default assumption in the field and may influence future reference-based generation designs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (the paper acknowledges struggles with small text/logos), whether the video-data strategy generalizes to domains without natural temporal variation, and how well the method handles occlusion and complex multi-object interactions beyond the demonstrated cases.
