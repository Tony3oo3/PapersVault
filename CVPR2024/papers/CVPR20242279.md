## Problem

Dilated convolution expands the receptive field by increasing the dilation rate, but this simultaneously reduces the effective bandwidth to 1/D of the original, causing gridding artifacts when local feature frequency exceeds the sampling rate. The conventional practice of fixing a single global dilation rate as a hyperparameter is suboptimal because different spatial regions (e.g., object boundaries vs. smooth backgrounds) have fundamentally different frequency content and thus require different dilation rates to balance bandwidth and receptive field.

## Input / Output

FADC operates as a drop-in replacement for standard dilated convolution layers within existing CNN backbones (ResNet, PIDNet, HorNet, etc.), consuming standard RGB images at resolutions such as 1024×2048 (Cityscapes) or 640×960 (ADE20K/COCO). It produces pixel-wise segmentation masks for semantic segmentation, and when integrated with detection frameworks (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN), also yields bounding boxes and instance masks. The module adds only ~0.5M parameters and ~9G FLOPs over the base architecture, making it suitable for real-time deployment (e.g., 37.7 FPS on Cityscapes).

## Method

FADC comprises three complementary strategies grounded in Fourier analysis of dilated convolution. (1) AdaDR predicts a spatially variant dilation rate per pixel via a lightweight convolutional head, assigning small D to high-frequency regions (object boundaries) to preserve bandwidth and large D to low-frequency regions (backgrounds, object centers) to expand the receptive field, formalized as maximizing total receptive field minus lost high-frequency power. (2) AdaKern decomposes each kernel into a low-frequency component (kernel-wise mean, acting as a low-pass filter) and a high-frequency residual, then dynamically scales each by per-channel weights λ_l and λ_h predicted from global pooling, thereby increasing the effective bandwidth of the frequency response. (3) FreqSelect applies four octave-spaced binary masks in the Fourier domain to decompose features into frequency bands [0,1/16), [1/16,1/8), [1/8,1/4), [1/4,1/2], then spatially reweights each band with learned selection maps that suppress high frequencies in non-critical areas, encouraging the network to adopt larger dilation rates and thus a larger overall receptive field (from ~441 to ~1100 pixels in dilated ResNet-50).

## Results

On Cityscapes validation, FADC consistently improves mIoU across architectures: PSPNet gains +2.6 (77.8→80.4), DeepLabV3+ gains +1.1 (79.2→80.3), and Mask2Former gains +1.2 (79.4→80.6), all with fewer additional FLOPs than DCNv2. On ADE20K, ResNet-50-FADC achieves 44.4 mIoU (+3.7 over baseline), surpassing the heavier ResNet-101 (42.9), while HorNet-B-FADC reaches 51.1 mIoU, outperforming InternImage-B (50.8) and DiNAT-B (49.6). For real-time segmentation, PIDNet-M-FADC achieves 81.0 mIoU at 37.7 FPS, beating the larger PIDNet-L (80.9 mIoU at 31.1 FPS). On COCO object detection, adding AdaKern+FreqSelect to DCNv2 boosts box AP from 41.3 to 42.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADC outperforms DCNv2 on all three Cityscapes architectures (PSPNet: 80.4 vs 79.7; DeepLabV3+: 80.3 vs 79.9; Mask2Former: 80.6 vs 80.4) while requiring fewer additional parameters (+0.5M vs +0.7M) and significantly fewer FLOPs (+9.2G vs +24.5G). Against the adaptive dilation baseline ADC [78], FADC achieves 81.5 vs 80.7 mIoU on DeepLabV3+ with Dilated-ResNet-101. On ADE20K, ResNet-50-FADC (44.4 mIoU) surpasses ResNet-101 (42.9) by 1.5 points, and HorNet-B-FADC (51.1) beats InternImage-B (50.8), DiNAT-B (49.6), and ConvNeXt-B-dcls (49.3). For real-time segmentation, PIDNet-M-FADC (81.0 mIoU, 37.7 FPS) exceeds PIDNet-L (80.9 mIoU, 31.1 FPS) in both accuracy and speed. On COCO detection, AdaKern+FreqSelect added to DCNv2 improves box AP by 0.9 (41.3→42.2), and FreqSelect added to DiNAT-S improves box AP by 0.5 (49.3→49.8) and mask AP by 0.6 (43.9→44.5). FreqSelect also boosts InternImage-T on ADE20K by 0.8 mIoU (47.9→48.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dilated convolution (Yu et al., 2017) by reframing the dilation rate assignment as a frequency-domain trade-off between effective bandwidth and receptive field, departing from prior adaptive dilation methods (ADCNN, learnable spacings) that rely on empirical or scale-based heuristics. It also builds on the modulation mechanism from Deformable ConvNets v2 (Zhu et al., 2019) for per-pixel dilation prediction, but avoids the spatial deviation problem inherent to deformable sampling. The frequency analysis connects to the aliasing/gridding artifact literature but offers a principled solution (spatially adaptive dilation + frequency reweighting) rather than the empirical multi-rate stacking or learned low-pass filters used previously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FADC provides a plug-in frequency-domain framework that can be integrated into diverse architectures (CNNs, deformable convolutions, dilated attention in transformers), making it broadly applicable beyond a single model family. The receptive field improvement from ~440 to ~1100 pixels with dilated ResNet-50 is substantial and addresses a long-standing limitation of fixed-rate dilation. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain perspective generalizes to other sampling-based operations (e.g., strided convolutions, attention sparsification), and whether the three-component design (AdaDR + AdaKern + FreqSelect) can be simplified without losing the frequency-balancing benefit. The authors note future work on extending the analysis to deformable/dilated attention and designing FADC-specific architectures.
