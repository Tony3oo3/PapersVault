## Problem

Existing Retrieval and Deformation (R&D) methods for 3D shape reconstruction assume target shapes are pre-aligned in a canonical pose and struggle with partially-observed objects under significant occlusion. No prior framework jointly handles arbitrary-pose canonicalization, semantic part segmentation, occlusion-robust retrieval, and deformation in a single self-supervised pipeline, forcing practitioners to rely on laborious manual alignment or separate pose estimation networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud of 2,500 points in an arbitrary SE(3) pose (simulated via random cropping at 10–50% occlusion) and a pre-curated database of full source meshes (e.g., 1,419 models in PartNet). It produces a deformed 3D shape tightly fitting the target, per-point part segmentation labels, M part centers, and the identity of the retrieved source shape. Retrieval operates on per-part feature tokens (M × C dimensional) rather than a single global embedding, with a region-weighted L1 distance where each part's contribution is scaled by its fraction of visible points.

## Method

ShapeMatcher jointly trains four modules—Canonicalization, Segmentation, Retrieval, and Deformation—using cross-task consistency losses so each task supervises the others without ground-truth annotations. Canonicalization employs VN-MLP (Vector Neurons) to progressively decouple translation, rotation, and scale, yielding affine-invariant point-wise features; segmentation uses two 4-layer MLPs to predict part labels and centers purely from these features; retrieval aggregates per-part features into tokens and matches them against database shapes via a region-weighted L1 distance that down-weights occluded parts; and deformation applies part-center-guided neural cage offsets to warp the retrieved shape onto the target. Training proceeds in three stages: first the full branch learns canonicalization and segmentation, then the partial branch is introduced with partial-full consistency losses, and finally retrieval and deformation are jointly trained with regional supervision that excludes occluded regions from the Chamfer Distance ground truth.

## Results

On PartNet full shapes under arbitrary poses, ShapeMatcher achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.200, substantially outperforming the best baseline U-RED+PE (0.725) by 72%. On ComplementMe, ShapeMatcher reports 0.294 average CD versus 2.688 for U-RED+PE, an 85.2% improvement. On the real-world Scan2CAD dataset (zero-shot transfer from PartNet training), ShapeMatcher achieves 0.375 average CD, reducing Chamfer Distance by 92–96% compared to U-RED across chair, table, and cabinet categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Uy et al. (CVPR 2021) and U-RED (arXiv 2023), each tested both with and without an offline pose estimation (PE) module. ShapeMatcher dominates all four baseline variants across every dataset and category. On PartNet full shapes, ShapeMatcher (0.200 avg CD) beats the strongest baseline U-RED+PE (0.725) by 3.6×, and Uy et al.+PE (2.219) by 11×. On partial inputs at 25% occlusion, ShapeMatcher (0.776) outperforms U-RED+PE (1.625) by 52%, with the gap widening to 3× at 50% occlusion (1.194 vs 3.628). On the zero-shot Scan2CAD real-world test, ShapeMatcher (0.375) reduces Chamfer Distance by 92–96% over U-RED and 91–98% over Uy et al., confirming strong domain generalization without any real-world training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Retrieval and Deformation (R&D) pipeline established by Uy et al. (CVPR 2021) and U-RED (2023) by unifying four previously decoupled stages—canonicalization, segmentation, retrieval, and deformation—into a single self-supervised framework. For canonicalization, it builds directly on Vector Neurons and VN-MLP [13, 30] for SE(3)-invariant feature extraction, and on [8] for scale normalization, rather than relying on separate pose-estimation networks. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of cross-task consistency losses that allow the four modules to supervise each other during joint training, and a region-weighted retrieval strategy that replaces the global-embedding approach of prior R&D methods to handle occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual alignment or a separate pose-estimation stage, ShapeMatcher addresses a major practical barrier to deploying R&D methods on real-world scans, and the zero-shot transfer from synthetic PartNet to Scan2CAD (92–98% CD reduction over baselines) suggests strong domain generalization. The fully self-supervised segmentation (no part annotations required) is a notable practical simplification. However, evaluation is limited to three object categories (chairs, tables, cabinets) and two baseline methods, and the authors themselves note future work on extending to downstream tasks like robotic grasping, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more diverse or articulated object classes.
