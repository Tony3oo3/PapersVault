## Problem

Generating large-scale 3D scenes cannot simply reuse 3D object synthesis methods because scenes contain complex spatial configurations and objects at varying scales. While BEV maps provide a practical way to specify scene layout, they are ambiguous about fine-grained appearance, causing severe inconsistencies (jittering, blurring) when stitching locally generated scenes into a global one. The paper addresses how to make BEV-conditioned scene generation equivariant so that local scenes compose seamlessly at arbitrary scale.

## Input / Output

The generator consumes a 2D BEV map (specifying object positions, types, and scales), a randomly sampled latent code from a Gaussian, and a camera pose encoded as a 2D Fourier feature grid over global coordinates. It produces a 3D radiance field parameterized by a U-Net output feature map (32 channels) lifted to 3D via Cartesian product with z-axis positional embedding, which is then volume-rendered into 256×256 RGB images. For infinite-scale synthesis, a global BEV is divided into sliding local patches, each rendered independently and stitched into a continuous navigating video or panoramic view.

## Method

BerfScene uses a U-Net with StyleGAN blocks that takes a 2D Fourier feature map as input and progressively down-samples and up-samples features, with each block incorporating the BEV map via Spatial Encoding Layers (SEL) and modulating features with the latent code through modulated convolutions. The key architectural insight is enforcing equivariance to the BEV map through two anti-aliasing mechanisms: (1) a wide margin of padding around the BEV input to prevent absolute positional information from leaking into internal representations, and (2) low-pass FIR filters applied before every down-sampling step to restrict signal bandwidth per the Nyquist criterion. The output 2D feature map is lifted to 3D by Cartesian product with z-axis positional embedding, and color/density are decoded by an MLP via standard volume rendering. Training follows EG3D's dual-discriminator adversarial setup with R1 and density regularization, and inference at infinite scale simply slides a window over the global BEV, rendering and composing local scenes with guaranteed consistency.

## Results

On CLEVR, BerfScene achieves a FID of 0.96, substantially outperforming the closest 3D-aware baseline CC3D (3.61) and EG3D (4.67). On 3D-Front, it attains a FID of 36.78 and EQT of 15.76 dB, beating CC3D (42.88 FID, 14.74 EQT) and EG3D (80.70 FID). On CARLA, BerfScene records a FID of 40.7, improving over CC3D (45.2) and EG3D (46.8). The EQT metric, measuring equivariance under translation, confirms that BerfScene maintains the highest consistency across local coordinate shifts among all 3D-aware methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BerfScene is compared against four baselines: StyleGAN2 (2D GAN), GSN (3D GAN), EG3D (3D GAN), and CC3D (BEV-conditioned scene generation). Among 3D-aware methods, BerfScene achieves the best FID on all three datasets: 0.96 on CLEVR (vs. CC3D's 3.61 and EG3D's 4.67), 36.78 on 3D-Front (vs. CC3D's 42.88 and EG3D's 80.70), and 40.7 on CARLA (vs. CC3D's 45.2 and EG3D's 46.8). On the equivariance metric (EQT), BerfScene edges out CC3D on CLEVR (22.02 vs. 21.94 dB) and leads more clearly on 3D-Front (15.76 vs. 14.74 dB), confirming superior consistency for large-scale scene composition. StyleGAN2 achieves a lower FID on CARLA (16.89) and 3D-Front (31.01), but as a 2D generator it lacks explicit camera control and 3D consistency, which the paper notes is the key differentiator. GSN performs poorly on 3D-Front (FID 130.70), indicating that its 3D inductive bias is insufficient for complex multi-object scenes without BEV conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

BerfScene builds directly on EG3D's geometry-aware GAN architecture and dual-discriminator training, repurposing its radiance field representation for scene-level generation. It departs from CC3D, which also conditions on BEV maps but lacks the equivariance mechanism needed for seamless multi-patch composition, and from InfiniCity/SceneDreamer, which enforce continuity through explicit 3D voxel structures at significant computational cost. The key conceptual move is importing anti-aliasing principles from alias-free GANs (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2021)—specifically wide padding margins and low-pass filters before downsampling—to make the BEV-conditioned generator equivariant, thereby enabling divide-and-conquer composition of local scenes into arbitrarily large global scenes without explicit 3D constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivariant BEV-conditioned formulation offers a practical path to infinite-scale 3D scene generation without the memory and loading overhead of explicit 3D structures, and the BEV map provides an intuitive, editable interface for scene layout control (translation, restyling, insertion/removal). However, the method is limited to static scenes, constrained camera viewpoints during inference, and imprecise attribute control (e.g., specifying a color in the BEV does not guarantee the output matches). Open questions include extending to dynamic scenes, broadening the observation distribution during training, and incorporating CLIP-based supervision for finer-grained semantic control—directions the authors explicitly flag as future work.
