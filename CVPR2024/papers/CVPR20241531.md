## Problem

Meshflow estimation from event cameras is an unexplored task: while event-based dense optical flow has been studied, no prior work estimates the spatially smooth sparse motion field (meshflow) that captures global camera motion while rejecting dynamic object motion. Additionally, existing event flow datasets lack high resolution (≤640×480), dynamic objects, and meshflow labels, making it impossible to train or evaluate such a model at practical resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241531/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive event sequences E(t_{k-1}, t_k) and E(t_k, t_{k+1}) at 1280×720 resolution, converted into 3D event volumes, with two temporal modes (dt=1 at 60 Hz and dt=4 at 15 Hz). EEMFlow directly outputs a 16×16 meshflow (sparse motion vectors at mesh vertices), while EEMFlow+ outputs dense optical flow at full input resolution. For evaluation, meshflow predictions are upsampled to input resolution to compute EPE against upsampled ground truth.

## Method

EEMFlow uses a lightweight encoder-decoder architecture (1.24M params) rather than the recurrent refinement structure common in prior flow networks. A three-level pyramid encoder with shared weights extracts multi-scale features, which are pooled to 1/64 resolution and combined via Dilated Feature Correlation (DFC)—a cost volume construction that samples densely near the center and sparsely at larger offsets, enabling a wide search radius with reduced computation. The decoder employs group shuffle convolutions to regress meshflow directly at the 16×16 GT resolution. For dense optical flow (EEMFlow+), a coarse-to-fine residual decoder is added, and a Confidence-induced Detail Completion (CDC) module replaces bilinear upsampling: it fuses a self-corrector branch (dense convolutions producing corrected flow and confidence maps) with a self-correlation branch (self-attention finding matching fine regions) to preserve sharp motion boundaries at object edges.

## Results

On the HREM dataset (dt=1), EEMFlow achieves the lowest average EPE of 5.50, outperforming FlowFormer (6.08), KPAFlow (6.31), and ERAFT (7.68), while using only 1.24M parameters and 7 ms inference time — 38.7× faster than FlowFormer (281 ms) and 25.5× faster than ERAFT (93 ms). For dt=4, EEMFlow again leads with 24.45 average EPE versus 25.38 (KPAFlow) and 27.24 (ERAFT). On DSEC for dense optical flow, EEMFlow+ achieves the best 2PE (3.93), 3PE (2.15), and AE (2.67), with a competitive EPE of 0.75 (vs. TMA's 0.74), at 39.2 FPS — 5.2× faster than TMA (7.55 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEMFlow outperforms all seven compared flow networks on HREM (dt=1) in average EPE, with the largest margins over ERAFT (5.50 vs. 7.68, −28%) and EVFlownet (5.50 vs. 8.57, −36%), while using 76% fewer parameters and 92% less inference time than ERAFT. Against the strongest baseline FlowFormer, EEMFlow reduces EPE from 6.08 to 5.50 (−9.5%) while running 38.7× faster (7 ms vs. 281 ms) with 8× fewer parameters. On DSEC for dense optical flow, EEMFlow+ ties TMA on EPE (0.75 vs. 0.74) but wins on 2PE (3.93 vs. 3.97), 3PE (2.15 vs. 2.30), and AE (2.67 vs. 2.68), while running 5.2× faster (39.2 vs. 7.55 FPS). EEMFlow+ also surpasses EFlowformer on 3PE (2.15 vs. 2.45) and AE (2.67 vs. 2.68), and outperforms ADMFlow and ERAFT on all DSEC metrics. The efficiency advantage is consistent: EEMFlow's 1.24M parameter count is the smallest among all compared methods, and its 7 ms latency is an order of magnitude below the next fastest (PWCNet at 42 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the event-based optical flow estimation framework established by ERAFT (which processes two consecutive event sequences) and the 3D volume representation from Zhu et al., but departs fundamentally from the recurrent refinement paradigm used by ERAFT, PWC-Net, and FlowFormer in favor of a lightweight encoder-decoder with multi-scale global optimization. The key conceptual departure is introducing meshflow—a sparse, spatially smooth motion field previously defined only for RGB images—as the target of event-based estimation, bridging the gap between dense optical flow and global homography. The dilated feature correlation and group shuffle convolutions are efficiency-oriented modifications to standard cost-volume and decoder designs, while the CDC module for optical flow upsampling draws on self-attention to address edge blurring that bilinear interpolation introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HREM dataset is the first to provide high-resolution (1280×720) event data paired with both meshflow and dense optical flow labels across dynamic scenes and extreme conditions, filling a clear gap in the event-based vision literature. The 39× inference speedup over SOTA flow networks while maintaining competitive accuracy makes real-time event-based motion estimation (video stabilization, autonomous driving) practically viable on embedded hardware. However, the primary meshflow results are validated on synthetic Blender-rendered data, leaving open whether the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff holds on real-world event streams with sensor noise and calibration artifacts. The authors note that extending the framework to 3D scene flow by integrating depth data is a natural next step.
