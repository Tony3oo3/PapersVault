## Problem

Existing scene understanding datasets capture the world from a single viewpoint (egocentric, third-person, or panoramic) and typically a single modality (video or audio), whereas humans perceive scenes holistically by integrating multiple viewpoints and sensory channels simultaneously. No prior dataset provides a truly 'panoptic' perspective that combines multiple camera viewpoints with rich multi-modal data (audio, directional binaural delay, location, text) within the same real-world scene, leaving a gap in how we benchmark comprehensive scene understanding.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides temporally aligned 360° panoramic video (5760×2880, 25 FPS), a de-warped third-person front view cropped via optical flow from the spherical panorama, egocentric binocular video (2432×1216, 60 FPS) from Snapchat Spectacles 3, four-channel directional audio with estimated interaural time delay, GPS coordinates, and textual scene descriptions. Each of the 232 examples spans ~6.2 minutes on average, containing 1–5 egocentric clips scattered throughout the 360° recording. Outputs span five benchmark tasks: scene class labels (28 categories), dense temporal action segments (38 action instances), cross-modality retrieval rankings, learned multi-modal representations, and transfer performance on THUMOS14.

## Method

The core contribution is the 360+x dataset itself, collected with an Insta 360 One X2 (static 360° capture) and Snapchat Spectacles 3 (worn by a capturer visible in the 360° frame) across 28 scene categories in five countries, with privacy-preserving face blurring. For multi-modal fusion, the paper employs a hierarchical attention mechanism rather than simple concatenation: directional binaural delay features act as an attention query over audio features, and the enriched audio features then serve as a query over video features, creating a synergistic representation. Benchmark experiments use I3D (video), VGGish (audio), and ResNet-18 (binaural delay) as backbones with a linear head, evaluated across five tasks including video classification, temporal action localisation (ActionFormer, TriDet, TemporalMaxer), cross-modality retrieval, and self-supervised representation learning (video pace + clip order prediction extended to multi-modal). A key empirical finding is that self-supervised pre-training on 360+x outperforms supervised pre-training on the same data, and that 360+x pre-training transfers better to THUMOS14 than Kinetics400 pre-training, especially at higher IoU thresholds.

## Results

On the 360+x dataset, video scene classification reaches 80.62% Avg. Precision using all three views (360°+Front+Ego) with V+A+D modalities, compared to 51.95% for egocentric-only video. For temporal action localisation, TriDet with 360+x pre-trained extractors and V+A+D modalities achieves 17.6% Avg. mAP, nearly doubling the 10.5% baseline (video-only, Kinetics400-pretrained). On THUMOS14 dataset adaptation, pre-training on 360+x yields 71.9% Avg. mAP versus 69.5% for Kinetics400 pre-training, with the combined two-step approach reaching 73.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses three TAL frameworks—ActionFormer [32], TemporalMaxer [24], and TriDet [21]—as evaluation backbones rather than direct competitors, comparing baseline extractors (Kinetics400/AudioSet pre-trained) against 360+x pre-trained extractors. Across all three methods, adding audio (V+A) over video-only (V) with baseline extractors yields a 5.2–7.2% Avg. mAP gain, and adding directional binaural delay (V+A+D) with 360+x pre-trained extractors provides a further 0.3–0.9% improvement. TriDet consistently outperforms ActionFormer and TemporalMaxer by 1.5–2.1% Avg. mAP under the same configuration, making it the strongest TAL framework on this dataset. For dataset adaptation to THUMOS14, 360+x pre-training alone surpasses Kinetics400 pre-training by 2.4% Avg. mAP (71.9% vs. 69.5%), with the gap widening to 7.2% at IoU=0.7, indicating the dataset's richer multi-view content transfers better to fine-grained temporal boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of several dataset lines—panoramic scene understanding (KITTI-360, EGOK360), egocentric video analysis (EPIC-Kitchens, Ego4D), and audio-visual datasets (AudioSet, VGGSound)—but departs from all of them by simultaneously capturing 360° panoramic, third-person front, egocentric monocular, and egocentric binocular views alongside multi-channel audio with directional binaural delay, GPS, and scene descriptions. For the benchmark, it reuses standard backbones (I3D, VGGish, ResNet-18) and TAL architectures (ActionFormer, TriDet, TemporalMaxer) but introduces a hierarchical attention mechanism for multi-modality fusion, motivated by prior evidence that naive feature concatenation degrades multi-modal performance. The activity taxonomy is inherited from ActivityNet, and the scene categories are derived from the Places/WordNet hierarchy, grounding the dataset in established ontologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 360+x dataset offers the first unified benchmark where each viewpoint and modality can be ablated in isolation, providing clear evidence that multi-view and multi-modality integration yields substantial gains (up to ~22% in classification). The finding that self-supervised pre-training (video pace + clip order) outperforms supervised pre-training on both classification and TAL is a notable conceptual result, though it is demonstrated on a single dataset with a fixed model family, leaving open whether it generalises to larger-scale or more diverse settings. The dataset's relatively small scale (232 examples, ~244K seconds) compared to Ego4D or Kinetics limits its immediate utility for large-scale training, and the fixed-camera collection protocol constrains the range of egocentric interactions captured. Open questions include whether the multi-view benefits persist at scale, whether the hierarchical attention fusion is optimal versus learned cross-modal transformers, and how the directional binaural signal interacts with more sophisticated spatial audio models.
