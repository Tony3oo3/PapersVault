## Problem

Existing deep learning turbulence mitigation methods are either single-frame (fundamentally limited by the ill-posedness of the inverse problem) or multi-frame architectures adapted from generic video restoration that lack turbulence-specific inductive biases. Additionally, prior methods suffer from high computational complexity that impedes practical deployment, and their training data is synthesized with suboptimal physics models that restrict generalization to real-world turbulence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DATUM consumes a sequence of turbulence-degraded RGB video frames (30 frames for dynamic scenes, 36 for static scenes during training; 16 for resource-constrained baselines) at 960×540 resolution and produces a single turbulence-free restored frame. The network also outputs an intermediate inverse tilt field used to rectify shallow features before final reconstruction. For static scenes, the loss is computed between a single ground truth and all output frames, while evaluation uses the average score of the central four output frames.

## Method

DATUM is a bidirectional recurrent network that translates three classical TM stages—frame-to-reference registration, lucky fusion, and blind deconvolution—into differentiable deep modules. The Deformable Attention Alignment Block (DAAB) registers each frame's high-level feature to a recurrently updated reference using multi-group deformable attention guided by SPyNet optical flow, while the Gated Reference Update Module (GRUM) adaptively fuses the reference with deeper embeddings via a gating mechanism. A Multi-head Temporal-Channel Self-Attention (MTCSA) module performs spatially adaptive fusion across a 5-frame sliding window in the temporal-channel dimension. A twin decoder first predicts an inverse tilt map to rectify shallow features (supervised by a Charbonnier loss against tilt-only ground truth), then jointly decodes rectified shallow and deep features into the final clean image. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a weighted combination of tilt and reconstruction Charbonnier losses, achieving state-of-the-art quality at 10× the speed of prior multi-frame TM methods.

## Results

On the ATSyn-dynamic benchmark, DATUM achieves an overall PSNR of 29.42 dB and SSIM_CW of 0.8781, outperforming the best prior method RVRT (28.77 dB / 0.8756) by 0.65 dB while using 5.75M parameters (vs. 13.50M) and running at 9.17 FPS (vs. 2.43 FPS). On ATSyn-static, DATUM reaches 26.76 dB PSNR and 0.9102 SSIM_CW, and on the real-world Turbulence Text dataset it attains 93.55% CRNN recognition accuracy, surpassing the next-best method (ESTRNN at 87.10%). DATUM is approximately 10× faster than prior turbulence-specific methods (TMT at 0.80 FPS, TSRWGAN at 0.87 FPS) while delivering the highest quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DATUM outperforms all seven baselines across every turbulence level and both scene modalities. Against the closest competitor RVRT [38] (a general video restoration transformer with guided deformable attention), DATUM gains 0.65 dB PSNR on ATSyn-dynamic and 1.05 dB on ATSyn-static, while being 2.3× smaller and 3.8× faster. The turbulence-specific transformer TMT [72] trails by 1.15 dB overall on ATSyn-dynamic and 2.25 dB on ATSyn-static, and is 11× slower (0.80 vs. 9.17 FPS). The earlier turbulence GAN TSRWGAN [26] is the weakest multi-frame TM baseline at 26.45 dB, reflecting its limited turbulence simulation range. Single-frame TurbNet [44] scores only 24.22 dB on the TMT synthetic set, confirming the advantage of multi-frame temporal aggregation. Among general video deblurring methods, ESTRNN [74] is the fastest (27.65 FPS) but sacrifices 1.29 dB PSNR, while RNN-MBP [76] is the weakest deblurring baseline at 27.22 dB. VRT [37], a non-recurrent video restoration transformer, achieves 28.02 dB but is the slowest method tested (0.17 FPS) due to its non-recurrent design.

## Relation to Prior Work

DATUM positions itself as a translational bridge between classical multi-frame turbulence mitigation (frame-to-reference registration, lucky fusion, blind deconvolution) and deep learning, embedding these classical stages as inductive biases (DAAB, MTCSA, twin decoder) within a recurrent network. It builds directly on the Zernike-based turbulence simulator of Chimitt & Chan [12, 13], extending it with a continuous Cn² path technique and a more physically grounded PSF representation to produce the ATSyn dataset. The method departs from prior deep TM networks (TMT, TSRWGAN) that were adapted from generic video restoration architectures without turbulence-specific design, and from single-frame methods that ignore the temporal dimension entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup over prior multi-frame TM methods while simultaneously outperforming them makes turbulence mitigation more deployable in real-time long-range imaging systems, addressing a key practical barrier. The ATSyn dataset, with its physics-aligned parameter space (focal length, F-number, distance, wavelength), provides a more representative training ground than prior synthetic datasets, and the paper demonstrates that retraining existing methods on ATSyn alone improves their real-world generalization. Open questions include whether the recurrent design scales to very long sequences, how the method handles extreme turbulence beyond the tested Cn² range, and whether the translational framework generalizes to other stochastic degradation problems.
