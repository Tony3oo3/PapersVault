## Problem

Prior conversational gesture generation methods evaluate motion using non-textured meshes or skeletal representations, which obscure subtle nuances like smirks, grimaces, and eye gaze that are critical for human perception of conversational dynamics. Additionally, existing approaches rely on either VQ-based or diffusion-based generation alone, failing to simultaneously capture the diversity of expressive gestures (e.g., pointing, clapping) and the high-frequency motion details (e.g., finger wag) needed for realistic conversation.

## Input / Output

The method takes Wav2Vec features extracted from both the target speaker's and the conversation partner's audio streams (2 × d_a × T), and produces face expression codes (256-dim vectors per frame), body/hand joint angles (3 rotation angles per joint at 30fps), and a final photorealistic video via a personalized cVAE renderer. The body pipeline operates at two temporal resolutions: coarse guide poses at 1fps are generated autoregressively, then refined to 30fps by the diffusion model. The face and body are modeled by separate networks because the face is tightly coupled to speech (lip sync) while the body exhibits weaker audio correlation and greater gesture diversity.

## Method

The core architecture is a two-stage body model combined with a separate face diffusion model. For the body, an autoregressive transformer first predicts discrete motion tokens (quantized via a residual VQ-VAE) at 1fps to produce diverse guide poses, then a DDPM diffusion model conditioned on both audio and these guide poses infills high-frequency 30fps motion. For the face, a DDPM diffusion model is conditioned on audio features and lip vertices from a pre-trained lip regressor, using cross-attention for conditioning and FiLM for timestep encoding. The key insight is that VQ sampling provides diversity in coarse pose selection (enabling 'extreme' gestures like pointing or clapping) while diffusion provides temporal smoothness and fine-grained detail, and the coarse-to-fine 1fps→30fps hierarchy prevents autoregressive drift. The final motion is rendered through a personalized cVAE avatar (from [5]) that produces registered geometry and view-dependent texture for photorealistic video output.

## Results

On the Photorealistic Conversational Dataset, the proposed method achieves the best (lowest) geometric realism score FDg=2.94 and kinetic realism score FDk=0.96, while simultaneously attaining the highest diversity scores: Divg=2.98, Divk=2.36, and Divsample=3.58. These diversity numbers approach ground-truth levels (GT Divg=3.09, Divk=2.50), indicating the generated motion is both realistic and varied. In the perceptual A/B evaluation, approximately 70% of Mechanical Turk evaluators prefer the proposed method over the strongest baseline (LDA) in both mesh and photorealistic renderings, with a notable shift from 'slightly prefer' to 'strongly prefer' when viewing photorealistic avatars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two external baselines: SHOW [40], a VQ-VAE + autoregressive transformer method, and LDA [2], an audio-to-motion diffusion model. The proposed method outperforms SHOW on all five metrics, with particularly large gains in kinetic realism (FDk 0.96 vs. 2.60) and temporal diversity (Divk 2.36 vs. 0.77), reflecting that the VQ-only approach produces overly static, low-variance motion. Against LDA, the proposed method wins on geometric realism (FDg 2.94 vs. 5.08), geometric diversity (Divg 2.98 vs. 2.45), kinetic diversity (Divk 2.36 vs. 1.88), and cross-sample diversity (Divsample 3.58 vs. 2.68), while also achieving slightly better kinetic realism (FDk 0.96 vs. 1.04). The ablation 'Ours w/o P' (audio-only diffusion, comparable to LDA) confirms that the guide-pose conditioning from the VQ transformer is the key factor driving the diversity improvement. In the perceptual study, the proposed method is preferred over LDA by ~70% of evaluators in both visualization settings, and evaluators are significantly more likely to correctly identify ground truth when viewing photorealistic avatars versus meshes, underscoring the paper's central claim about evaluation methodology.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of audio-driven motion synthesis and photorealistic avatar rendering. It builds on DDPM-style diffusion models (Ho et al.) and classifier-free guidance for the generative backbone, on residual VQ-VAE quantization for the autoregressive guide-pose transformer, and on the cVAE-based full-body avatar renderer of Bagautdinov et al. for the final photorealistic output. Conceptually, it departs from prior gesture-generation work (SHOW, LDA) in two key ways: (1) it combines VQ-based diversity with diffusion-based high-frequency detail in a coarse-to-fine pipeline rather than relying on either mechanism alone, and (2) it frames the task as dyadic conversation (modeling both speaker and listener dynamics) rather than monologue, while evaluating on photorealistic avatars instead of non-textured meshes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first multi-view dyadic conversational dataset with full photorealistic renderings of face, body, and hands, which is a practical resource for the community. Its perceptual evaluation provides empirical evidence that mesh-based evaluation systematically underestimates the importance of subtle motion details (smirks, grimaces, gaze shifts), suggesting the field should shift toward photorealistic evaluation protocols. Key limitations include the small subject count (4 people, 8 hours), the restriction to short-range audio (no long-range linguistic understanding like counting), and the need for per-subject renderer training. Open questions include whether the VQ+diffusion coarse-to-fine paradigm generalizes to longer sequences, more diverse populations, and real-time interactive settings.
