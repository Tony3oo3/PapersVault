## Problem

Traditional stereo MRFs rely on hand-crafted pairwise potential functions and message passing procedures that cannot model complex spatial relationships, leading to over-smoothed disparity maps and convergence difficulties. Although deep learning has improved the unary matching cost, the overall accuracy of MRF-based methods remains severely limited by these manually designed components. No prior work had a fully data-driven MRF formulation that simultaneously learns both potential functions and message passing while retaining the graph inductive bias essential for handling ambiguity in stereo matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB stereo images and produces a dense disparity map. Internally, a siamese CNN extracts features at 1/8 and 1/4 of the original resolution; the Disparity Proposal Network prunes the full disparity range [0, z_max] down to k=4 candidate labels per pixel, and the final disparity is selected via a winner-takes-all strategy over 8×8 disparity offsets and probabilities decoded from each candidate label's latent embedding. The model runs in under 100 ms on an RTX 3090.

## Method

The core contribution is a Neural MRF (NMRF) grounded in mean-field variational inference, where the fixed-point update equation is parameterized as a neural message passing network with self-attention for embedding aggregation. The probabilistic graph has two edge types: inter-pixel (neighbor) edges that allow compatible labels to support each other via content-adaptive positional bias over 3D relative positions, and intra-pixel (self) edges that enforce competition among candidate labels of the same pixel, making the winning label more prominent in the posterior. A Disparity Proposal Network (DPN) first identifies top-k disparity modals via 1D max-pooling and then refines them through cross-shaped window attention to exchange matching features across distant pixels, making the MRF inference tractable at high resolution. After N_i layers of alternating neighbor/self message passing at 1/8 resolution, a single-label refinement stage at 1/4 resolution further sharpens boundaries. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a proposal loss (bipartite matching to ground-truth modals, inspired by DETR) and a disparity loss over the posterior distribution.

## Results

On SceneFlow test, NMRF achieves 0.45 px EPE and 4.50% Bad-1.0, the best on both metrics among all compared methods. On KITTI 2012, it attains 1.59% bad-2.0 (non-occluded) and 1.01% bad-3.0 (non-occluded), while on KITTI 2015 it reaches 1.28% D1-bg, 3.13% D1-fg, and 1.59% D1-all — ranking 1st on both KITTI leaderboards among all published methods. The full pipeline runs in 0.09 s per image pair, faster than 100 ms. In zero-shot cross-domain evaluation (trained only on SceneFlow), NMRF achieves 4.2% (KITTI-12), 5.1% (KITTI-15), 7.5% (Middlebury), and 3.8% (ETH3D) outlier ratios, surpassing methods specifically designed for robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMRF outperforms all 13 compared methods on nearly every KITTI 2012/2015 metric. Against the strongest local methods (LEAStereo, ACVNet, IGEV-Stereo), it improves KITTI-15 D1-bg from 1.37–1.40% to 1.28% and KITTI-12 bad-2.0-noc from 1.71–1.90% to 1.59%, while running 2–3× faster (0.09 s vs. 0.18–0.30 s). Against prior global MRF-based methods, the gains are dramatic: D1-bg drops from 2.85% (LBPS) and 2.58% (PBCP) to 1.28%, a >50% reduction, and runtime improves from 68 s (PBCP) to 0.09 s. On SceneFlow, NMRF reduces Bad-1.0 by 42.5% over LEAStereo (4.50% vs. 7.82%) and by 10.4% over ACVNet (4.50% vs. 5.02%). In zero-shot cross-domain transfer, NMRF beats both DSMNet (6.2/6.5/8.1/6.2) and CREStereo++ (4.7/5.2/–/4.4) on all four held-out datasets, despite neither being specifically designed for robustness. The only metric where NMRF is not the absolute best is KITTI-15 D1-FG (3.13% vs. IGEV-Stereo's 2.67%), though it still ties for best on D1-ALL (1.59%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning-based stereo MRFs (MC-CNN, Hybrid CNN-CRF, GANet, LBPS) by replacing both the hand-crafted pairwise potential and message passing with data-driven neural networks, building on the theoretical observation from Dai et al. that neural message passing can be derived from embedded mean-field inference on probabilistic graphical models. The Disparity Proposal Network borrows the cross-shaped window attention mechanism from CSWin Transformer for efficient long-range feature exchange, while the overall graph structure and variational inference framework are grounded in Hamilton's graph representation learning. The key departure from prior deep stereo methods is that NMRF is the first to make the entire MRF—unary terms, pairwise terms, and message passing—fully learnable while preserving the graph inductive bias that gives MRFs their spatial coherence properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NMRF provides a principled, fully data-driven alternative to the dominant convolutional cost aggregation and iterative recurrent refinement paradigms in stereo matching, and its Disparity Proposal Network is a reusable component for any dense matching task requiring search space pruning. The strong zero-shot cross-domain generalization (outperforming purpose-built methods like DSMNet and CREStereo++) suggests the MRF graph structure itself acts as a useful inductive bias for robustness, which is an open question worth further investigation. The authors note the framework could extend to optical flow, but scalability to very high-resolution images and the sensitivity to the number of candidate labels remain practical considerations. The discovery that intra-pixel self-edges (intra-label competition) contribute meaningfully to accuracy challenges the long-standing assumption in stereo MRFs that only inter-pixel smoothness matters.
