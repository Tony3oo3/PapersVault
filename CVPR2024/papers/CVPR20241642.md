## Problem

In stereo egocentric 3D pose estimation, the heatmap-to-3D lifting step suffers from two critical flaws: (1) standard CNN encoders merge all joint heatmaps into a single embedding, losing per-joint correspondence and failing to capture relationships between spatially distant joints, and (2) conventional MLP decoders treat all joints uniformly, ignoring that body extremities are often occluded while proximal joints (e.g., neck) have clear visual cues. This leads to inaccurate 3D positions, especially for obscure joints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed stereo joint heatmaps (2N_J × 64×64) and limb heatmaps (2N_L × 2×64×64) produced by a frozen ResNet-18 U-Net that processes stereo RGB images (2×256×256×3). Joint heatmaps encode probabilistic 2D positions, while limb heatmaps embed 3D relational information via trigonometric functions of the camera view angle. The output is a set of N_J' 3D joint positions (N_J' × 3) expressed relative to the user's root, where N_J' may differ from N_J (e.g., pelvis and head in UnrealEgo lack heatmaps and are estimated via a learnable offset projection).

## Method

EgoTAP introduces two components. First, the Grid ViT Heatmap Encoder arranges all stereo joint heatmaps into a single grid image, splits it into 16×16 patches (16 per heatmap), and encodes them with a 3-layer Vision Transformer using multi-head self-attention across all patches; per-heatmap patch embeddings are then concatenated and re-encoded into a compact joint feature, preserving per-joint correspondence and enabling long-range communication between spatially distant heatmaps. Second, the Propagation Network is a 2-layer LSTM-inspired Propagation Unit (PU) that traverses the skeletal tree from the head (root) to extremities, taking the parent joint's hidden state, a relational feature from limb heatmaps, and the child joint's own feature as inputs. Crucially, the PU includes additional forget gates computed solely from the child joint's feature, which suppress propagated parent/relational information when the child joint has clear visual cues, restricting predictive estimation to genuinely obscure joints. The final 3D position per joint is obtained by concatenating the second-layer PU hidden state with the Grid ViT joint feature and applying a linear projection.

## Results

On UnrealEgo, EgoTAP achieves 41.06 MPJPE and 35.39 PA-MPJPE, representing a 23.9% reduction in MPJPE and 17.7% decrease in PA-MPJPE over the previous state-of-the-art (Ego3DPose: 53.99 / 43.02). On EgoCap, the method reaches 55.38 MPJPE and 45.24 PA-MPJPE, surpassing EgoGlass by 22.6% in MPJPE and Ego3DPose by 9.4% in PA-MPJPE. The largest per-category improvement is 31.3% MPJPE reduction in 'Crouching-Forward,' while the smallest is 8.8% in 'Crawling,' reflecting the method's dependence on visible body cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoTAP outperforms all three baselines on both datasets across both metrics. Against Ego3DPose (the strongest prior method), EgoTAP reduces MPJPE from 53.99 to 41.06 on UnrealEgo (−23.9%) and from 69.45 to 55.38 on EgoCap (−20.3%), with PA-MPJPE improvements of 17.7% and 9.4% respectively. Against EgoGlass, the gap is even larger on UnrealEgo (81.55 → 41.06, −49.7% MPJPE), though EgoGlass does not report PA-MPJPE on EgoCap. Against the UnrealEgo baseline method, EgoTAP improves MPJPE by 35.3% on UnrealEgo and by 21.8% on EgoCap. The paper notes that the smaller PA-MPJPE gains (vs. MPJPE) suggest prior methods capture relative joint geometry reasonably well but suffer from global coordinate bias, which EgoTAP corrects more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stereo egocentric heatmap-to-3D lifting pipeline established by UnrealEgo and Ego3DPose, which both rely on CNN encoders to compress joint heatmaps into a single embedding before MLP-based 3D decoding. EgoTAP departs from this lineage in two ways: it replaces the CNN encoder with a Grid ViT that arranges all joint heatmaps into a single grid image and applies self-attention across patches, preserving per-joint correspondence; and it introduces a Propagation Network with LSTM-inspired Propagation Units that explicitly traverses the skeletal hierarchy to propagate information from camera-proximate (visible) parent joints to distal (occluded) child joints. The limb heatmap representation with embedded 3D trigonometric information is adopted from Ego3DPose, and the frozen ResNet-18 U-Net heatmap estimator follows the UnrealEgo architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23.9% MPJPE reduction over the prior state-of-the-art (Ego3DPose) on UnrealEgo is a meaningful improvement for a task where egocentric self-occlusion makes accurate extremity estimation particularly difficult, and the Grid ViT + PU design offers a principled alternative to the ad-hoc CNN-then-MLP lifting that has been standard in this sub-field. However, the method is tightly coupled to a specific frozen heatmap estimator and a particular stereo egocentric setup (downward-facing, head-mounted), limiting immediate generalizability to other camera geometries or monocular settings. The smaller gains on EgoCap (real-world data) and the dependence on accurate limb heatmaps (which degrade on smaller datasets) suggest that the propagation mechanism's benefit is contingent on the quality of upstream heatmap estimation. Open questions include whether the grid ViT encoding strategy transfers to other probabilistic 2D representations (e.g., keypoint confidence maps) and whether the fixed skeletal propagation order could be made adaptive.
