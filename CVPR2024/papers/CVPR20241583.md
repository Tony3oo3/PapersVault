## Problem

Hierarchical VAEs suffer from posterior collapse, where data information fails to propagate to higher-level latent variables, rendering them uninformative. The paper shows from an information theory perspective that existing mitigations—architecture modifications and additional training objectives—either fail to explicitly regularize higher-level inference or disrupt the hierarchical dependencies between latent levels, leaving the sequential inference process inadequately constrained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241583/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (FashionMNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 32×32, TinyImageNet 64×64, STL-10 32×32) and sequentially infers L-level latent variables (e.g., 5 levels with dimensions {128, 64, 32, 16, 8} for FashionMNIST) in either bottom-up or top-down order. The output is a hierarchy of latent representations where lower levels encode fine-grained details and higher levels capture increasingly abstract semantics, plus a reconstructed image decoded from the lowest-level latent. Evaluation uses top-level latent representations for linear SVM classification and MINE-estimated mutual information with the input.

## Method

The paper formulates the sequential inference of a hierarchical VAE as a Markov Decision Process where the encoder acts as the policy network, each inferred latent variable is an action, and a novel skip-generative path (marginalizing out lower-level latents via integration) provides a differentiable reward measuring the information content of the current latent given all previously inferred ones. The Q-value function accumulates discounted future rewards, so maximizing it regularizes not only the current latent's information content but also its influence on all subsequent higher-level latents, preserving hierarchical dependencies. Because the reward is differentiable through the reparameterization trick, policy gradient is implemented by direct gradient ascent on the Q-value without requiring high-variance estimators like REINFORCE. The approach is applied to both bottom-up (HVAE+RL) and top-down (LVAE+RL) inference schemes, requiring no additional networks or modules beyond the standard hierarchical VAE architecture.

## Results

On FashionMNIST, HVAE+RL achieves 78.23% linear SVM classification accuracy and LVAE+RL achieves 78.48%, both substantially outperforming the closest baseline IVAE (76.25%) and the single-level VAE (75.08%). On CIFAR-10, HVAE+RL reaches 46.46% accuracy versus IVAE's 39.26% and VAE's 39.98%, a gap of over 6 points. The estimated mutual information Ĩ(x, z_L) between inputs and the top-level latent variable also improves markedly: HVAE+RL obtains 17.01 on CIFAR-100 and 11.88 on TinyImageNet, compared to 8.91 and 3.92 for IVAE respectively, confirming that the learned hierarchy retains rich data information at higher levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines spanning single-level (VAE, AAE) and hierarchical (HVAE, LVAE, BIVA, IVAE) architectures. The most dramatic gains are over ELBO-trained hierarchical VAEs: HVAE+RL improves FashionMNIST accuracy from 10.73% (HVAE) to 78.23%, and LVAE+RL from 19.70% (LVAE) to 78.48%, confirming that posterior collapse in the ELBO-trained models renders their top-level representations nearly useless. BIVA, despite its bidirectional architecture, also suffers severe collapse (28.67% on FashionMNIST, near-chance on CIFAR-100), and is outperformed by over 50 points. Against the strongest baseline IVAE, which adds layer-wise mutual information regularization, the RL methods still win consistently: +2.0 points on FashionMNIST, +7.2 on CIFAR-10, +5.7 on CIFAR-100, +4.7 on TinyImageNet, and +5.8 on STL-10. Against single-level VAE and AAE, the hierarchical RL methods also lead by 3–8 points, demonstrating that the learned hierarchy itself adds representational value beyond a flat latent space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hierarchical VAE family (HVAE, LVAE, BIVA) and the information-theoretic analysis of variational objectives, but departs from both major lines of posterior-collapse mitigation: architecture modifications (residual connections, delta-VAEs) and objective modifications (IVAE's layer-wise mutual information regularization). The key conceptual departure is formulating the sequential inference of a hierarchical VAE as a Markov decision process and applying policy gradient RL with a skip-generative-path reward, rather than treating each level's regularization independently. While RL has been applied to generative models (SeqGAN, diffusion model fine-tuning), this is the first work to use it specifically to regularize the multi-level inference process of a hierarchical VAE while preserving inter-level dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled information-theoretic argument that neither architecture changes nor per-level regularization can fully prevent posterior collapse, and that the regularization must account for a latent variable's influence on all subsequent levels—a requirement naturally captured by the Q-value function. The empirical finding that marginal likelihood (ELBO) quality is largely decoupled from representation quality challenges a common assumption in VAE-based representation learning. However, the practical impact is somewhat limited by the niche status of hierarchical VAEs in modern representation learning; the authors themselves note the method's more promising extension is to hierarchical diffusion models with trainable encoders, which remains future work.
