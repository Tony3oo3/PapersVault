## Problem

While PCA provides a standard solution for fitting a flat to a set of points, no established method exists for fitting a flat to a set of other flats of possibly varying dimensions. Existing approaches based on Riemannian centers of mass on affine Grassmannians or Stiefel manifolds are mathematically complex, computationally expensive, and lack natural properties such as equivariance to rigid transformations and unoriented representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240049/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts m flats of arbitrary (and possibly varying) dimensions l_i in the ambient space R^d, each encoded as a squared distance field pair (Q_i, r_i) where Q_i is a d×d symmetric PSD matrix and r_i is a d-vector, along with a target output dimension k. It produces a single k-flat in standard parameter form (A, b) with A ∈ R^{d×k} orthonormal and b orthogonal to the column space of A. The squared distance field representation is unoriented and homogeneous (defined up to scale), making it invariant to the choice of normal frame for each input flat.

## Method

The core insight is to represent each flat as its squared distance field (Q, r) and interpret the fitting procedure as a projected mean: first compute the arithmetic mean Q* = ΣQ_i and r* = Σr_i in the ambient space of symmetric PSD matrices and Euclidean vectors, then project onto the manifold of k-flats by eigendecomposing Q* and selecting the k eigenvectors with smallest eigenvalues as the basis A, with displacement b = (I − AA^T) Q*⁺ r*. This yields a closed-form solution whose computational complexity is dominated by a single O(d³) eigendecomposition, identical to PCA. The approach is equivariant to all rigid transformations and reflections, unoriented, and naturally bisects angles and distances between two flats. For robustness against outliers, the L2 mean can be replaced by an L1 mean computed via Weiszfeld's algorithm before the same projection step, yielding the Median-SDF variant.

## Results

On the primary benchmark (reconstructing a line from 20 planes in ℝ³), Median-SDF achieves the best minimum distance d_min across most noise levels (e.g., 0.1192 vs. Graff's 0.2174 at σ=0.5; 0.1870 vs. 0.3411 at σ=1.0) and the best principal angle θ in low-noise settings. Under 50% outliers, Median-SDF maintains d_min=0.3857 while Mean-SDF degrades catastrophically to 15.06, and Graff reaches 0.3917. In the archery multi-view application, the proposed method produces reconstructions close to ground truth, whereas the rank-minimization baseline [16] is significantly off. Running time is orders of magnitude faster than iterative Graff optimization, scaling only as O(d³) for the eigendecomposition regardless of input flat dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Riemannian center of mass on the affine Grassmannian (Graff, [22]), Median-SDF consistently achieves lower d_min in all noise-only conditions (e.g., 0.1192 vs. 0.2174 at σ=0.5, a ~45% reduction) and in all outlier conditions above 20%, while being competitive on principal angle θ. Mean-SDF also beats Graff on d_min in low-noise settings but is not robust to outliers, with d_min exploding to 15.06 at 50% outliers versus Graff's 0.3917. Against the rank-minimization approach from Hartley & Zisserman [16] in the archery multi-view reconstruction, the proposed method yields reconstructions consistently close to ground truth, while rank-minimization is significantly off due to its dependence on the non-unique normal representation of planes. The proposed method is also orders of magnitude faster than the iterative Graff optimization, as the core computation reduces to a single eigendecomposition of a d×d matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical PCA framework for fitting a flat to a set of points to the more general problem of fitting a k-flat to a set of flats of arbitrary (possibly varying) dimensions. It departs from the dominant Riemannian-geometry approach—computing the Karcher mean on the affine Grassmannian via Stiefel-manifold optimization [22,23]—by showing that representing flats as squared distance fields (Q, r) and summing them reduces the problem to a single eigendecomposition, analogous to PCA. The paper also contrasts with Plücker/Grassmann-Plücker coordinate methods, noting their orientation dependence and the complexity of projecting back onto the Grassmann-Plücker relations, and with the rank-minimization approach from multi-view geometry [16], whose noise properties are ill-defined due to non-unique normal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that a method with the same O(d³) complexity as PCA achieves equivariance to rigid transformations and symmetry (angle/distance bisection) properties that the Riemannian Stiefel-based approach provably lacks, while being orders of magnitude faster in practice. The L1/median variant (Median-SDF) provides robustness to outliers without the complexity of Riemannian Lp centers of mass. Open questions include extending the framework to clustering, mixture models, and L∞ norms (smallest enclosing ball), as well as handling degenerate spectral cases where the eigenvalue gap vanishes. The method's applicability to LiDAR registration, multi-view line reconstruction, and other engineering tasks where flats of mixed dimensions arise naturally suggests broader utility beyond the demonstrated archery example.
