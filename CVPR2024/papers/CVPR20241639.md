## Problem

Reflective surface reconstruction from sparse views is fundamentally difficult because view-dependent specular reflections violate the photometric consistency assumption underlying multiview stereo, while the limited number of views introduces shape-radiance ambiguity that prevents disentanglement of geometry from reflectance. Existing methods address either reflective surfaces (requiring dense captures) or sparse views (relying on RGB photometric consistency), but no prior work handles both challenges simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241639/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 sparse polarized images per object, where each image is a snapshot polarization capture at four angles (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) yielding 1224×1024 pixel values, along with binary silhouette masks and calibrated camera poses. It outputs a continuous signed distance field (SDF) representing the object surface, from which meshes and per-view surface normal maps can be extracted. The polarized inputs are converted to 3-element Stokes vectors (s₀, s₁, s₂) and angle-of-polarization (AoP) maps, which serve as the supervisory signals rather than raw RGB intensities.

## Method

NeRSP combines two complementary cues derived from polarized images to resolve the shape ambiguity under sparse views. The geometric cue exploits the fact that AoP directly encodes the surface azimuth angle (up to π and π/2 ambiguities); multi-view consistency of these azimuth angles yields a linear system T(x)n(x)=0 that constrains surface normals without depending on reflectance. The photometric cue uses the full polarimetric image formation model (Muller matrix with pBRDF decomposition into diffuse and specular Stokes components) to predict observed Stokes vectors from a candidate surface. The network follows a NeuS-style SDF architecture with volume rendering, augmented by a diffuse radiance MLP and a RoughnessNet for specular prediction (using Ref-NeRF's integrated positional encoding). Training minimizes a combined loss of L1 Stokes-vector photometric error, the geometric linear-system residual, binary cross-entropy mask loss, and Eikonal regularization. The key insight is that neither cue alone suffices under sparse views—geometric cues cannot localize depth along rays, and photometric cues suffer from shape-radiance ambiguity—but their intersection uniquely identifies the correct surface point and normal.

## Results

On the synthetic SMVP3D dataset (6 views), NeRSP achieves the lowest Chamfer distance across all five objects, ranging from 3.43 (HEDGEHOG) to 5.59 (SNAIL), compared to the next-best method MVAS which ranges from 5.37 to 8.01. On the real-world RMVP3D dataset, NeRSP achieves the smallest average Chamfer distance of 6.13, outperforming NeRO (7.48), MVAS (7.86), S-VolSDF (10.91), and PANDORA (11.14). For surface normal estimation, NeRSP also achieves the lowest mean angular error in average on the synthetic dataset, with particularly large gaps on detail-rich objects like DAVID and DRAGON where competing methods exceed 20° MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRSP outperforms all four baselines on every object in both the synthetic and real-world evaluations. Against NeRO (reflective-surface specialist using RGB), NeRSP reduces Chamfer distance by roughly 36% on HEDGEHOG (3.43 vs 5.39) and by a dramatic margin on DAVID (4.16 vs 45.8), where NeRO's shape-radiance disentanglement from sparse RGB views breaks down. Against S-VolSDF (sparse-view specialist using RGB), NeRSP wins on all objects and avoids the complete failure (N/A) that S-VolSDF exhibits on DRAGON. Against MVAS (geometric-cue-only polarized method), NeRSP consistently improves by 1–3 Chamfer units, with the largest gap on SNAIL (5.59 vs 8.01), confirming that the photometric cue is essential to resolve residual ambiguities. Against PANDORA (photometric-cue-only polarized method), NeRSP achieves substantially lower errors (e.g., 4.16 vs 7.86 on DAVID, 4.77 vs 17.4 on DRAGON), demonstrating that the geometric cue is equally critical. On the real RMVP3D dataset, NeRSP's average Chamfer distance of 6.13 is 18% lower than the next-best NeRO (7.48) and 45% lower than PANDORA (11.14).

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRSP sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: neural 3D reconstruction for reflective surfaces (NeRO, PANDORA) and sparse-view reconstruction (S-VolSDF, SparseNeuS). It inherits its network architecture and photometric cue from PANDORA's polarization-aided neural decomposition, its SDF-based volume rendering from NeuS, and its view-dependent specular modeling from Ref-NeRF. The key departure is the explicit integration of the geometric cue (multiview azimuth consistency from MVAS) alongside the photometric cue, rather than relying on either in isolation as prior polarization-based methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that only 6 polarized views suffice for reflective surface reconstruction, NeRSP makes practical capture of glossy objects significantly cheaper than dense-view approaches. The introduction of RMVP3D with aligned GT meshes fills a gap in the polarization 3D reconstruction literature, where prior datasets (e.g., PANDORA) lacked ground-truth geometry for quantitative evaluation. Open questions remain around inter-reflections and polarized environment lighting, which the authors acknowledge as unhandled; combining NeRSP's sparse-shot formulation with incident Stokes field modeling (NeISF) is a natural next step.
