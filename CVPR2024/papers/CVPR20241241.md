## Problem

Human avatar reconstruction typically demands expensive multi-view capture setups or large image collections, making it impractical for casual use. Existing few-shot approaches either treat the human as a static scene (ignoring articulation) or rely on text-based generation that cannot faithfully reconstruct a specific individual's identity. This paper asks whether a small set (as few as 2) of unconstrained, dynamically posed images suffices to build a fully animatable 3D avatar.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241241/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2–8 RGB images (512×512) with arbitrary viewpoints and articulated poses, obtained from a personal photo album or video keyframes. Off-the-shelf tools extract per-image SMPLX/MANO pose and expression coefficients, plus masks, normals, and depth maps. The output is a drivable tetrahedral representation parameterized by viewpoint R, pose θ, and expression ψ, which renders 512×512 novel-view images and supports free-pose articulated animation of both body and hands.

## Method

HaveFun builds a drivable tetrahedral representation by integrating SMPLX/MANO skinning weights and expression blendshapes into a DMTet grid, so that the differentiable mesh topology can be deformed to arbitrary poses while preserving a canonical human geometry prior for initialization. Optimization proceeds in two complementary phases: a few-shot reference stage that enforces LPIPS, normal, depth, and mask reconstruction losses on aligned views (with a Laplacian normal constraint replacing normal/depth supervision for hands), and a few-shot guidance stage that applies an SDS loss conditioned on Zero123's image+viewpoint diffusion prior to hallucinate plausible textures for unseen regions. Crucially, the diffusion prompt is the input image's CLIP feature rather than a text caption, avoiding the identity drift inherent in text-based methods. The two phases are combined as L = L_recon + λ_sds · L_sds, with λ_sds tuned per shot count (0.01–0.05) to balance fidelity against over-smoothing.

## Results

On FS-XHumans (body), HaveFun achieves PSNR 24.0/25.6/26.8, SSIM×10² 95.5/96.3/96.7, and LPIPS×10² 4.2/3.5/3.0 for 2-/4-/8-shot settings, substantially outperforming SelfRecon (PSNR 20.7 at 100-shot) and TeCH (PSNR 21.0 at 1-shot). On FS-DART (hand), HaveFun reaches PSNR 26.6/26.7/26.3 and LPIPS×10² 5.8/5.5/5.2, beating SelfRecon (PSNR 21.7 at 8-shot) by over 5 dB. Notably, 2-shot results are within ~1 dB of 8-shot, demonstrating strong data efficiency, and for hands some 2-shot metrics even exceed 8-shot due to limited additional information from lateral views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HaveFun outperforms both baselines across all metrics on both benchmarks. Against SelfRecon [30] (a video-based method), HaveFun gains ~6 dB PSNR on FS-XHumans (26.8 vs 20.7) even when SelfRecon uses 100 frames of video versus HaveFun's 8 images, and ~5 dB on FS-DART (26.6 vs 21.7 at 2-shot). Against TeCH [26] (a text-guided one-shot method), HaveFun achieves 5.8 dB higher PSNR on FS-XHumans (26.8 vs 21.0) and substantially lower LPIPS (3.0 vs 6.5). The paper attributes SelfRecon's weakness to its inability to learn effectively from limited data and lack of expression handling, while TeCH's text-caption-based approach introduces identity drift and caption-biased features (e.g., 'caucasian' dominating lateral head views) rather than faithful reconstruction. HaveFun's advantage is most pronounced in preserving facial identity and hand detail (palm wrinkles), where visual-prompt SDS guidance avoids the ambiguity inherent in text descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DMTet from static scene reconstruction to articulated human modeling by integrating SMPLX/MANO skinning weights and blendshapes into the tetrahedral grid, creating a 'drivable' representation that can deform to arbitrary poses. For texture generation in unseen regions, it adapts the SDS loss from DreamFusion but replaces text conditioning with image-conditioned Zero123 diffusion, arguing that direct visual prompts preserve identity more faithfully than text captions (as demonstrated against TeCH). The two-phase optimization (few-shot reference losses + few-shot SDS guidance) departs from prior sparse-view NeRF work that relies on geometric regularizers, instead leveraging a pre-trained diffusion prior for appearance completion in a dynamic, articulated setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: reconstructing an animatable avatar from as few as 2 casual photos removes the need for expensive multi-view rigs or long video captures, making avatar creation accessible for AR/VR and metaverse applications. Key limitations include imperfect expression handling (SMPLX blendshapes constrain facial fidelity, causing 8-shot to underperform 2-shot for faces when training data has diverse expressions) and reliance on parametric body/hand models that limit geometric expressiveness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to extreme poses with heavy self-occlusion, whether real-time rendering is feasible with the current optimization pipeline, and whether the image-conditioned SDS can be replaced by more controllable generative priors as diffusion models evolve.
