## Problem

Existing MLLMs rely on vision encoders pretrained on coarsely aligned image-text pairs (e.g., CLIP), which limits their ability to extract fine-grained spatial details and high-level semantic content, leading to hallucinated and inaccurate responses. Simply tuning the vision encoder requires massive pretraining data or suffers from catastrophic forgetting, making it impractical. The paper asks how to enrich the visual branch of an MLLM with both fine-grained spatial-aware knowledge and high-level semantic evidence without incurring prohibitive pretraining costs.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image, a natural-language instruction, and a set of image tags (objects, scenes, actions, attributes) produced by the off-the-shelf RAM tagging model. The tags are not concatenated directly into the prompt; instead, they are injected through a soft-prompting mechanism that prepends a learnable token to a tailored instruction sentence, allowing the model to adaptively select useful tags. Outputs include free-form text captions, short VQA answers, and normalized bounding-box coordinates in the format [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] for referring-expression tasks.

## Method

LION injects visual knowledge at two levels. At the fine-grained level, a Vision Aggregator (two sequential transformer blocks with self-attention, cross-attention, and FFN) fuses multi-level hidden features from the EVA-CLIP encoder to capture spatial details, and is trained on visual grounding tasks (REC/REG from Visual Genome). At the high-level semantic level, image tags from RAM are incorporated via a soft-prompting method that embeds a learnable token into the instruction template, mitigating the negative impact of imperfect tag predictions. To resolve the internal conflict between image-level and region-level VL tasks, the paper proposes a three-stage instruction-tuning strategy: stage 1 tunes the Q-Former and an image-level adapter on captioning/VQA, stage 2 tunes the Vision Aggregator and a region-level adapter on grounding, and stage 3 jointly trains both using a Mixture-of-Adapters with a task-specific router that dynamically gates adapter contributions per FFN layer. The router uses trainable per-task gating vectors to modulate each adapter's output, enabling the unified model to specialize on either holistic or fine-grained tasks without mutual interference.

## Results

On image-level VL tasks (Table 1), LION-4B achieves the best or second-best performance on 8 out of 9 benchmarks, with headline numbers of 72.96% top-1 accuracy on VSR, 104.87 CIDEr on TextCaps, and 54.91% on IconQA. LION-12B further improves to 73.77% on VSR, 108.76 CIDEr on TextCaps, and 57.33% on OKVQA. On region-level REC tasks (Table 2), LION-12B achieves 86.36% average accuracy in the fine-tuning setting, surpassing Ferret-13B (85.57%) and Pink (84.25%). On MMBench, LION scores 73.4 overall, outperforming the previous best mPLUG-Owl2 (66.0) by 7.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LION-4B outperforms the re-implemented InstructBLIP(T5XXL) on 7 of 9 benchmarks, with the largest gains on VSR (+4.5%, 72.96 vs 68.46) and GQA (+1.8%, 49.50 vs 47.74), while slightly trailing on TextCaps (104.87 vs 105.44). Compared to the original InstructBLIP†(T5XXL) trained with the larger in-house WebCapFilt dataset, LION-4B still leads on all zero-shot benchmarks, demonstrating better generalization with less training data. On REC tasks, LION-4B achieves 54.98% zero-shot average, surpassing Kosmos-2 (52.21%) and Pink (51.00%), with the largest margin on RefCOCOg (64.74 vs 60.57). In the fine-tuning REC setting, LION-12B (86.36% avg) edges out Ferret-13B (85.57%) and Pink (84.25%) using a simpler vision aggregator and existing datasets rather than Ferret's extensive custom grounding data. On MMBench, LION (73.4) substantially outperforms Shikra (60.2), InstructBLIP (33.9), and mPLUG-Owl2 (66.0), with particularly strong gains in relational reasoning (78.4 vs 53.1 for Shikra). On POPE hallucination evaluation, LION achieves the best F1 under adversarial settings (84.71 vs 82.49 for Shikra and 77.32 for InstructBLIP), though InstructBLIP retains a slight edge on Random F1 (89.27 vs 88.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

LION builds directly on the InstructBLIP/BLIP-2 paradigm (frozen EVA-CLIP vision encoder + Q-Former + FlanT5 LLM) and extends it by injecting dual-level visual knowledge: fine-grained spatial-aware features via a Vision Aggregator and region-level visual grounding tasks, and high-level semantic evidence via RAM-generated image tags with soft prompting. It departs from concurrent grounding-focused MLLMs (Kosmos-2, Shikra, Pink, Ferret) that treat visual grounding as just another instruction-tuning task, by explicitly identifying and resolving the internal conflict between image-level and region-level tasks through a three-stage instruction-tuning strategy with Mixture-of-Adapters. The soft prompting mechanism for imperfect tags is a lightweight adaptation of prompt tuning, repurposed here not for a specific task objective but to let the model adaptively select useful information from noisy external predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the 'obvious' approach of jointly training on image-level and region-level VL tasks in a single stage catastrophically degrades grounding performance (3.78 vs. 54.46 average REC), revealing a previously underappreciated task-conflict problem in MLLM instruction tuning. The stage-wise solution is practical and does not require the massive grounding pretraining corpora (e.g., GRIT-20M) used by Kosmos-2, making fine-grained spatial reasoning more accessible. Open questions include whether the stage-wise strategy generalizes to other task-conflict scenarios (e.g., video, 3D), whether the reliance on an external RAM model for tags limits scalability, and whether the Mixture-of-Adapters router can handle more than two task types without combinatorial complexity.
