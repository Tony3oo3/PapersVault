## Problem

Multi-view depth estimation methods depend heavily on accurate camera poses, which are often noisy or unavailable in real-world autonomous driving (e.g., SLAM drift, long-term deployment without recalibration). Under noisy poses, existing multi-view and single-view/multi-view fusion methods degrade severely—sometimes performing worse than single-view alone—yet no prior work systematically evaluates or addresses this robustness gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242653/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a reference RGB image I_0, n−1 source RGB images (n=3 in practice), camera intrinsics, and relative camera poses, and produces a fused depth map d_fuse at H/4 × W/4 resolution via a soft-weighted sum over 256 log-spaced depth bins. Beyond the final depth, the system outputs per-branch confidence maps (M_s, M_m) and a wrapping confidence map M_w that encodes sub-pixel geometric consistency, all at quarter resolution. Input resolutions are 1936×1216 (DDAD) and ~1241×376 (KITTI).

## Method

AFNet is a two-branch CNN sharing a ConvNeXt-T backbone: a single-view branch decodes multi-scale features into a depth probability volume and confidence map, while a multi-view branch constructs a 4D cost volume by warping source features into 128 hypothesis planes, regularizes it with a 2D hourglass, and fuses single-view depth features via a residual connection to recover lost semantics. The key contribution is the Adaptive Fusion (AF) module, which performs pixel-wise selection between the two branches guided by three confidence maps—M_s and M_m (learned matching ambiguity) and M_w (a wrapping confidence computed by forward-warping source images with the predicted multi-view depth and camera poses, then convolving the warped images with the reference to detect geometric inconsistency). This mechanism causes the network to automatically fall back to single-view predictions in textureless regions, dynamic objects, or under pose noise, while retaining multi-view precision where geometry is reliable. Training uses a combined L1 depth loss on all three outputs (d_s, d_m, d_fuse) plus a confidence loss with valid-pixel masking to prevent outlier interference.

## Results

On DDAD, AFNet achieves AbsRel 0.088, SqRel 1.41, and RMSE 7.23, outperforming the best multi-view method IterMVS (AbsRel 0.104) and the best fusion method MaGNet (AbsRel 0.112) by over 15% in AbsRel. On KITTI Eigen split, AFNet reaches AbsRel 0.039, SqRel 0.121, and RMSE 1.743, a 27.8% AbsRel reduction over the previous SOTA MaGNet (0.054). Under noisy pose conditions (Table 7), AFNet maintains the lowest AbsRel across all noise levels (δ=0 to 0.05) and achieves the best robustness-aware relative error (R-Rel 0.168 vs. 0.195 for MaGNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFNet outperforms all 15 baselines on both DDAD and KITTI. On DDAD, it beats the strongest single-view method AdaBins (0.164 AbsRel) by 46%, the strongest multi-view method IterMVS (0.104) by 15%, and the strongest fusion method MaGNet (0.112) by 21%. On KITTI, AFNet (0.039 AbsRel) surpasses the best single-view Metric3D (0.053) by 26%, the best multi-view IterMVS (0.057) by 32%, and the best fusion MaGNet (0.054) by 28%. Under noisy poses (Table 7), classical cost-volume methods like CasMVS and MVSNet degrade severely (AbsRel jumps from ~0.11–0.13 to 0.19–0.21 at δ=0.05), while AFNet remains stable (0.092→0.164) and always stays above single-view performance. Under real SLAM poses from ORB-SLAM2 (Table 8), AFNet is the only method that maintains near-GT accuracy (0.061–0.070 AbsRel) even when the stereo SLAM trajectory error reaches ATE=0.4, whereas MoRec degrades to 0.345–0.381 and IterMVS to 0.169–0.182. In the dynamic object region (Table 5), AFNet's fusion output (SqRel 1.484) improves over its own multi-view branch (1.879) by 21% and beats MaGNet (2.500) by 41%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFNet builds directly on the two-branch architecture of MVS2D [43] and the cost-volume construction paradigm of MVSNet [44], but departs from both by replacing the Epipolar Attention Module with a standard cost volume and, more critically, by introducing an adaptive fusion module that dynamically selects between single-view and multi-view predictions using a wrapping confidence map. Prior fusion methods (MaGNet [1], MVS2D [43]) assume ideal camera poses and naively combine branches, which the paper shows leads to performance worse than single-view alone under noisy poses. The paper also carves new ground by proposing a systematic robustness benchmark with graded synthetic pose noise and real SLAM-derived poses, a dimension of evaluation absent from prior multi-view stereo work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For autonomous driving practitioners, the finding that widely-used multi-view stereo methods degrade catastrophically under realistic SLAM pose noise (sometimes below single-view baselines) is a practically critical warning that changes deployment assumptions. The adaptive fusion framework offers a general recipe for combining geometric and semantic depth cues with graceful degradation, and the robustness benchmark provides a reusable evaluation protocol the field lacked. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger view counts or real-time constraints, whether the wrapping confidence map generalizes to non-driving scenes with different motion patterns, and how the method behaves when both pose and intrinsics are uncertain simultaneously.
