## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) underperform on visual document understanding because they typically process low-resolution images (224×224), causing severe text distortion, and lack document-oriented instruction tuning. Directly increasing resolution to 1536×1536 generates ~9216 visual tokens that exceed LLM context windows and incur quadratic attention costs, making high-resolution document processing computationally infeasible with standard architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242555/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HRVDA accepts 1536×1536 RGB document images and free-form text instructions, producing structured text outputs spanning seven task types: key-value pair extraction, full OCR transcription, VQA answers, visual grounding bounding boxes (x,y,x+w,y+h), table element reconstruction, image captions, and document classification labels. The model is OCR-free, meaning it reads text directly from pixels without an external OCR pipeline, and the instruction templates are diversified via ChatGPT-generated prompt variants (50 per task) to improve generalization.

## Method

HRVDA introduces a two-stage visual token filtering pipeline built on a Swin Transformer encoder and LLaMA-2-7B decoder. First, a PSENet-based content detector assigns a binary content probability to each patch; a skip connection in each Swin block (h^{j+1} = p·F^j(h^j) + (1−p)·h^j) bypasses attention for content-agnostic windows, and patch merging propagates content probability via max-pooling while thresholds increase from shallow to deep layers to preserve global layout. Second, an instruction filtering module concatenates encoded visual tokens with instruction tokens, applies a self-attention + FFN fusion layer, and uses a 2-layer MLP to discard instruction-agnostic visual tokens. Training follows a four-stage curriculum: (1) supervised content detector training using OCR/detection ground-truth coordinates, (2) image encoder pretraining with m-BART cross-attention for full-text recognition, (3) instruction filtering module training with layout-dependent threshold scheduling, and (4) LoRA (rank 8) fine-tuning of the LLM on a document-oriented instruction dataset covering 17 real and synthetic sources.

## Results

HRVDA (1536×1536 resolution) achieves state-of-the-art results on 9 out of 12 document understanding benchmarks. Headline numbers include 89.3 F1 on CORD, 91.0 F1 on SROIE, 72.1 ANLS on DocVQA, 43.5 ANLS on InfographicsVQA, and 73.3 ANLS on TextVQA. In terms of efficiency, with both filtering thresholds set to 0.5, HRVDA reduces total forward-inference latency to 3.64 s on a V100 GPU, a 61% speedup over Qwen-VL (9.47 s) despite operating at 3.4× higher input resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against encoder-decoder baselines Donut and SeRum, HRVDA wins on all 12 datasets, with particularly large margins on InfographicsVQA (43.5 vs. 11.6/13.5 ANLS) and TabFact (72.3 vs. 54.6/58.3 F1), attributable to the LLM decoder's stronger semantic reasoning. Versus the MLLM baselines Qwen-VL, mPLUG-Doc, and UReader (all operating at 224–448 px), HRVDA dominates on every dataset where both report results, e.g., 73.3 vs. 63.8/52.6/57.6 ANLS on TextVQA and 67.6 vs. 66.3/57.4/59.3 on ChartQA. The three exceptions where HRVDA does not achieve the top score are: DocVQA (Pix2Struct 76.6 vs. HRVDA 72.1 ANLS), VisualMRC (UReader 221.7 vs. HRVDA 211.5 CIDEr), and TextCaps (CogVLM 144.9 vs. HRVDA 125.3 CIDEr), the latter two likely reflecting the advantage of natural-scene semantic cues that high-resolution document tuning does not fully exploit. In the efficiency comparison (Table 3), HRVDA at (ε_c=0.5, ε_i=0.5) runs in 3.64 s total versus Qwen-VL's 9.47 s on the same V100 GPU, confirming that the token-pruning pipeline more than compensates for the 3.4× higher input resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

HRVDA extends the projection-based MLLM paradigm (LLaVA, BLIP-2) by replacing the standard ViT encoder with a Swin Transformer, making it the first MLLM to leverage window-based attention and hierarchical patch merging for visual document understanding. It departs from the query-based information bottleneck (Q-Former, Visual Abstractor) in favor of a direct self-attention + MLP classification approach for instruction filtering, arguing that learnable queries are unsuitable for fine-grained prediction tasks. The content filtering mechanism repurposes token pruning ideas from DynamicViT and SparseViT but adapts them to the sparse-content structure of document images, using a pluggable PSENet detector to identify content-bearing patches and exploiting Swin's window structure to skip attention computation on blank regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck: MLLMs cannot process the high resolutions that document understanding demands without prohibitive compute costs. The dual filtering strategy (content-agnostic + instruction-agnostic token removal) is a practical recipe that could be adopted by other MLLM pipelines working on structured visual inputs. However, the approach is tightly coupled to document-specific structure (sparse content, localized instructions), limiting generalizability to natural scene understanding. Open questions include whether the filtering thresholds can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-tuned, whether the method scales beyond 1536×1536 as GPU memory allows, and whether the content detector introduces a latency floor that undermines the efficiency gains at very high resolutions.
