## Problem

Existing subject-driven customization methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion, P+) are designed to invert appearance features and either completely fail to capture high-level action semantics or entangle action features with subject appearance, causing contamination when generalizing to unseen subjects (e.g., generating a gorilla with human arms). The task of learning a co-existing action from a few exemplar images and transferring it to arbitrary new subjects—humans or animals—remains unaddressed by prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241769/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Training takes a set of 10 exemplar images all depicting the same action performed by different people, along with action-agnostic prompt templates (action descriptions manually removed). At inference, the learned layer-wise identifier tokens are inserted into a text prompt specifying a new subject (e.g., 'A panda <A>'), and the system produces 512×512 images via Stable Diffusion v2-1-base with a 50-step DDIM sampler (guidance scale 7.5). The identifiers are reusable across subjects without retraining, enabling free combination of the learned action with any character or animal description.

## Method

ADI (Action-Disentangled Identifier) learns action-specific tokens through two mechanisms. First, it expands the semantic conditioning space by initializing one identifier token per cross-attention layer (L tokens total) rather than a single token, increasing representational capacity for high-level action features. Second, it constructs gradient masks from sample triples: for an anchor image x^(a,c), a context-different pair x^(a,c̄) (same action, different person) and an action-different pair x^(ā,c) (same person, different action, generated via a quick one-shot DreamBooth training) are used to compute gradient differences w.r.t. each identifier token. The context-different mask preserves channels with small gradient difference (action-invariant), while the action-different mask preserves channels with large gradient difference (context-specific); the final mask is their intersection, and only unmasked channels contribute to the identifier update. Training runs 3000 steps with AdamW (lr 2e-4) and a masking ratio β=0.6, discarding 60% of channels per update to block action-agnostic feature inversion.

## Results

On ActionBench, ADI achieves 60.33% action accuracy, 85.87% subject accuracy, and 51.09% total accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest baseline, Stable Diffusion (text-only), reaches only 27.17% total accuracy, while the best subject-driven method P+ achieves 20.92%. ADI's action accuracy (60.33%) more than doubles that of the next-best baseline ControlNet (41.30%), and its total accuracy exceeds the best baseline by 23.92 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADI decisively outperforms all seven baselines on total accuracy (51.09% vs. 27.17% for the best baseline, Stable Diffusion). Among subject-driven customization methods, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, and ReVersion show near-zero action accuracy (1.63–2.45%), confirming their complete inability to invert action features despite high subject fidelity. ControlNet achieves the highest baseline action accuracy (41.30%) by conditioning on skeleton images, but its subject accuracy drops to 42.66%, yielding a lower total than even text-only Stable Diffusion. Custom Diffusion and P+ partially capture action knowledge (29.62% and 26.90% action accuracy) but sacrifice subject accuracy (53.53% and 80.16%), indicating appearance leakage into the learned identifiers. ADI uniquely combines high action accuracy (60.33%) with strong subject accuracy (85.87%), demonstrating that gradient masking effectively decouples action from context without degrading subject generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subject-driven customization paradigm (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, P+) by shifting the inversion target from appearance to action, building on P+'s per-layer tokens to expand the semantic conditioning space. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of gradient-level disentanglement: rather than relying on pixel-level reconstruction loss (which biases toward low-level features), ADI constructs sample triples and uses gradient invariance across context-different and action-different pairs to identify and mask action-agnostic channels. This positions the work at the intersection of textual inversion and feature disentanglement, departing from prior customization methods that treat the identifier as a monolithic representation of the subject.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of established customization methods—near-zero action accuracy (1.6–2.5%)—that motivates the need for action-specific learning, and the ActionBench provides the first systematic evaluation framework for this task. However, the benchmark is small (8 actions, 10 exemplar images each, 23 subjects), and the action-different pair generation depends on a separate DreamBooth training step, introducing a practical dependency. Open questions include whether the gradient masking strategy generalizes to multi-action or temporal action sequences, whether it transfers to other diffusion architectures, and whether the 30.26% channel overlap between the two masks indicates a fundamental limitation of the pairwise invariance approach.
