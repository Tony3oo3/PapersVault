key_contribution: Identifies the insufficient labeling problem in open-ended video QA (only one labeled answer per question despite multiple correct answers) and proposes RADI, a parameter-free ranking distillation framework that uses a teacher model's answer rankings with adaptive soft margins (via Monte Carlo Dropout + Sinkhorn optimal transport) or partial listwise sampling (Exp/Zipf) to enrich label information, achieving consistent SOTA improvements on five OE-VQA benchmarks with only 30M trainable parameters.
limitations: The method is evaluated exclusively with FrozenBiLM as the base architecture, and the insufficient-labeling ablation relies primarily on iVQA (the only dataset with multiple ground-truth answers), leaving open whether the ranking-distillation principle generalizes to other backbones or multi-label tasks. The two-stage teacher-student pipeline adds training complexity, and the OT-based margin adaptation is truncated to top-2500 answers, which may limit scalability to substantially larger answer vocabularies.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that feature distillation (FitNet) and relation distillation (RKD) consistently *degrade* performance under the insufficient-labeling setting, while ranking-based distillation substantially outperforms distribution distillation (DKD, CTKD) by 1.5–2.5 points on Acc@1. ## Problem

Open-ended video QA (OE-VQA) is inherently a multi-label classification task, yet existing benchmarks provide only one labeled answer per question due to annotation costs. State-of-the-art methods treat all unlabeled answers as negatives, ignoring numerous potentially correct answers and limiting open-world generalization. This paper identifies this 'insufficient labeling problem' and seeks to mitigate it without additional manual annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240821/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (10–20 uniformly sampled frames) and a natural language question as input, and produces a ranked list over a large answer vocabulary (ranging from 911 to 3,589 answers depending on the dataset) with a predicted score for each candidate. The backbone is FrozenBiLM, which concatenates frame and question embeddings as sequential input to a frozen DeBERTa model, updating only lightweight adapter and LayerNorm modules (~30M trainable parameters). During training, only one positive answer is available per sample (except iVQA with five), while evaluation uses the full ground-truth answer set.

## Method

RADI is a two-stage ranking distillation framework: a teacher model is first trained with incomplete labels via standard classification loss, then its answer rankings are used as soft supervision to train a student model. To avoid overconfidence in the imperfect teacher, two parameter-free distillation strategies are proposed. The pairwise approach estimates teacher uncertainty via Monte Carlo Dropout on the last layer, then solves an Optimal Transport problem (via the Sinkhorn algorithm) to assign adaptive soft margins that relax constraints on uncertain pairs. The listwise approach performs partial listwise learning by constructing a hot list (top-k answers) and a cold list (sampled via Exp- or Zipf-distribution), then applying standard listwise losses (ListNet, ListMLE, LambdaLoss, etc.) only on this sampled subset. Both approaches are parameter-free, incur no extra inference cost, and are more robust to teacher bias than distribution distillation.

## Results

On five OE-VQA benchmarks, RADI-P achieves top-1 accuracy of 43.5% on iVQA, 44.1% on ActivityNet-QA, 55.8% on MSVD-QA, 48.2% on MSRVTT-QA, and 69.9% on TGIF-FrameQA, consistently outperforming the previous SOTA (FrozenBiLM) by 1.1–3.9 points across datasets. RADI-L achieves comparable results (42.9%, 44.1%, 56.0%, 48.1%, 70.0%) with the same 30M trainable parameters. On the insufficient-labeling evaluation (iVQA with only one labeled answer per question), RADI-P improves Acc@1 by 2.7 points and nDCG@5 by 3.1 points over the FrozenBiLM baseline, while RADI-L improves Hit@5 by 4.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADI-P and RADI-L outperform all 13 baselines on every dataset where results are reported. Against the strongest prior SOTA FrozenBiLM [53] (the base architecture), RADI gains +3.9 Acc@1 on iVQA, +0.9 on ActivityNet-QA, +1.0 on MSVD-QA, +1.2 on MSRVTT-QA, and +1.3 on TGIF-FrameQA. Larger models such as SViTT (255M params, 43.2 on ActivityNet-QA) and MERLOT (223M, 43.1 on MSRVTT-QA) are surpassed by RADI with only 30M trainable parameters. Among the distillation baselines in Table 2, RADI-P (43.2 Acc@1, 68.3 Hit@5, 52.7 nDCG@5) beats DKD [64] (41.4/67.4/51.5), CTKD [28] (41.3/66.7/51.0), and Vanilla KD [17] (41.3/66.7/51.2) by 1.8–2.7 Acc@1 points, while feature distillation (FitNet [39]) and relation distillation (RKD [34]) both underperform the FrozenBiLM baseline, confirming that label-space ranking information is more effective than feature-space matching for this problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on FrozenBiLM (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022) as the base video-QA architecture and reframes the standard knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton et al., 2015) as a learning-to-rank problem. It departs from distribution distillation by relaxing the matching constraint from absolute probability distributions to relative answer rankings, and from vanilla LTR methods by introducing robustness mechanisms: Monte Carlo Dropout–based uncertainty estimation combined with Sinkhorn optimal transport for adaptive pairwise margins, and ranking-based (Exp/Zipf) sampling for partial listwise distillation. The work also positions itself against simpler label-augmentation schemes (label smoothing, pseudo labeling) and other KD variants (FitNet, RKD, DKD, CTKD), arguing that ranking-based soft supervision is uniquely suited to the multi-label, label-scarce nature of OE-VQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes the 'insufficient labeling problem' in OE-VQA—a practical limitation that prior SOTA methods have implicitly ignored by treating all unlabeled answers as negatives. RADI's parameter-free design (no extra inference cost, only ~10–50% training overhead) makes it directly deployable on top of existing OE-VQA pipelines. Open questions include whether the ranking-distillation principle generalizes to other multi-label tasks with incomplete annotations (e.g., multi-label image classification, open-vocabulary detection), whether the teacher-student capacity gap can be further exploited, and whether the OT-based margin adaptation scales efficiently to answer sets far beyond the ~3.5K tested here.
