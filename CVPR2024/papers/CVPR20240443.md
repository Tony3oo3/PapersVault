## Problem

Vision-based semantic occupancy prediction requires operating on a 3D latent space, but dense voxel representations incur cubic time and space complexity that limits scalability in perception range and resolution. Existing compression strategies like BEV or TPV projections reduce cost at the expense of geometric information, degrading fine-grained occupancy accuracy. No prior work has explored a pure sparse 3D representation that is simultaneously lossless in geometry and computationally efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240443/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view surround camera images (6 views at 704×256 or 1600×900 for nuScenes) or a single monocular image (for SemanticKITTI), encodes them with a 2D backbone (ResNet-50 or EfficientNet-B7), and lifts features to a 3D volume of 128×128×16 (nuScenes) or 128×128×10 (SemanticKITTI) with 128 channels via Lift-Splat-Shoot using predicted depth. The output is a per-voxel semantic label across the full 3D volume (16+1 classes for nuScenes, 19+1 for SemanticKITTI), produced by a sparse transformer head that generates masks only for occupied voxels and assigns a single learnable token to all empty voxels.

## Method

SparseOcc converts the LSS-lifted dense 3D volume into a COO-format sparse tensor (retaining only the ~20% non-empty voxels) and performs all subsequent 3D operations on this sparse representation. A sparse latent diffuser propagates features to adjacent empty regions using 3D sparse convolutions with spatially decomposed orthogonal kernels (k×k×1, k×1×k, 1×k×k) for completion and submanifold convolutions for context aggregation, reducing kernel complexity from O(k³) to O(3k²). A four-scale sparse feature pyramid is built with strided sparse convolutions and fused via lightweight sparse interpolation rather than deformable attention, expanding receptive fields while preserving sparsity. The prediction head adapts Mask2Former to a sparse variant: a binary classifier first filters out empty voxels, then queries are decoded only against occupied voxel features plus one shared empty-voxel token, reducing mask prediction complexity from O(Nq·HWDC) to O(Nl·Nq·C + HWZ). This design achieves 74.9% FLOPs reduction over the dense C-CONet baseline while improving mIoU from 12.8% to 14.1%, attributed to the sparse representation's natural avoidance of hallucinations on empty voxels.

## Results

On nuScenes-Occupancy, SparseOcc achieves 21.8% IoU and 14.1% mIoU, outperforming the dense 3D baseline C-CONet (20.1% IoU, 12.8% mIoU) while reducing FLOPs by 74.9% (455G vs. 1810G) and cutting 3D inference latency from 2.18s to 0.19s. On SemanticKITTI, SparseOcc reaches 36.48% IoU and 13.12% mIoU with only 393G FLOPs, matching OccFormer's accuracy (36.50% IoU, 13.46% mIoU) at 44.2% of its computational cost. The method also surpasses TPVFormer by 80.8% relative mIoU improvement on nuScenes-Occupancy while using 59.8% fewer FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseOcc outperforms all dense 3D representation baselines: it beats C-CONet [33] by +1.7% IoU and +1.3% mIoU on nuScenes-Occupancy while using 74.9% fewer FLOPs, and surpasses OpenOccupancy [33] by +2.5% IoU and +3.8% mIoU. Against the TPV-based TPVFormer [13], SparseOcc achieves a dramatic +6.5% mIoU gain (14.1% vs. 7.8%) on nuScenes-Occupancy with 59.8% lower FLOPs, and +1.8% mIoU on SemanticKITTI (13.12% vs. 11.36%) at 58.5% fewer FLOPs. On SemanticKITTI, SparseOcc substantially outperforms the monocular scene completion methods MonoScene [4] (+9.6% mIoU), LMSCNet [27] (+6.4% mIoU), 3DSketch [5] (+5.6% mIoU), AICNet [16] (+4.8% mIoU), and JS3C-Net [37] (+2.8% mIoU). The closest competitor is OccFormer [41], which achieves marginally higher mIoU (13.46% vs. 13.12%) and IoU (36.50% vs. 36.48%) on SemanticKITTI but requires 889G FLOPs—more than double SparseOcc's 393G—making SparseOcc the more efficient choice at near-parity accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseOcc departs from the dominant paradigm of dense 3D volume processing (C-CONet, OccFormer) and lossy projected representations (BEV, TPV) by adopting a pure sparse tensor representation for the 3D latent space, drawing directly from the point cloud processing literature (SpConv, submanifold convolution). It builds on LSS for the 2D-to-3D lifting stage, on Cylinder3D's kernel decomposition strategy for efficient 3D convolutions, and on Mask2Former's query-based mask prediction for the transformer head, but re-architects all 3D operations to operate exclusively on non-empty voxels in COO format. The conceptual lineage is thus: dense 3D CNNs → projected (BEV/TPV) representations → sparse 3D processing, with the paper arguing that the last step is both more efficient and more accurate for occupancy prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 74.9% FLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding dense-baseline accuracy makes SparseOcc a practical candidate for real-time deployment of occupancy networks, and the paper explicitly proposes it as a new baseline. The observation that sparsity avoids hallucinations on empty voxels challenges the implicit assumption that denser representations are always more informative, though this benefit is demonstrated primarily on nuScenes-Occupancy (14.1% vs 12.8% mIoU) while on SemanticKITTI SparseOcc remains slightly below OccFormer (13.12 vs 13.46 mIoU). Open questions include whether the sparse representation advantage persists at higher spatial resolutions or with larger perception ranges, and whether the LSS lifting stage (which itself produces the sparsity) can be replaced by more accurate depth estimation without undermining the efficiency gains.
