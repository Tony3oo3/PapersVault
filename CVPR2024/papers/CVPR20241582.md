## Problem

Conditional diffusion models for tasks like super-resolution, inpainting, and image editing require 20–200 sampling steps for high-quality results, making real-time deployment impractical. Existing acceleration strategies either distill the unconditional model first and then fine-tune for the new condition, or fine-tune first and then distill, both of which sacrifice the pretrained generative prior or require the original text-to-image training data. The paper asks whether one can directly distill a fast conditional model from an unconditional pretrained LDM in a single stage without these compromises.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion LDM and accepts a variety of image conditions (low-resolution images, binary masks, depth maps, or source images) paired with a text prompt or editing instruction. A ControlNet-style duplicated encoder processes the condition, and its features are blended into the main U-Net encoder via a learnable scalar μ (initialized to zero). The output is a single high-quality image generated in 1–4 DDIM-style sampling steps, with the default conditional guidance using an ℓ₂ distance in latent space to avoid the blurriness or over-saturation observed with pixel-space or perceptual losses.

## Method

CoDi adapts a pretrained unconditional LDM by attaching a zero-initialized conditional encoder (ControlNet architecture) whose features are injected into the U-Net via a learnable mixing scalar μ. The key training objective is a conditional consistency loss that simultaneously enforces self-consistency in the noise-prediction space (matching the EMA target network's noise estimate at a nearby ODE step) and conditional generation fidelity in the signal-prediction space (matching the ground-truth image). Crucially, the intermediate latent ẑ_s used for the consistency target is predicted by the adapted (training) model's own PF-ODE rather than a frozen teacher, which the authors show harmonizes the otherwise conflicting optimization directions of distillation and conditional fine-tuning. A parameter-efficient variant (PE-CoDi) freezes the entire backbone and updates only the duplicated conditional encoder, reducing additional parameters to ~364 M while retaining competitive quality.

## Results

On real-world super-resolution (DF2K), CoDi achieves FID 19.637 and LPIPS 0.2656 in just 4 sampling steps, nearly matching the 250-step LDM baseline (FID 19.200, LPIPS 0.2639) while outperforming all other 4-step methods. The parameter-efficient variant PE-CoDi (364M params vs. 1.22B for full CoDi) reaches FID 25.214, still surpassing GD-II (23.675) and CM-II (27.810). On inpainting (ImageNet), PE-CoDi attains FID 14.700 and LPIPS 0.2231 in 4 steps, beating GD-II (15.950) and CM-II (17.710). On depth-to-image (WebLI), PE-CoDi achieves FID 23.047 and CLIP 0.2874, outperforming GD-II (26.513) and ControlNet (29.780) at the same 4-step budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDi (full model) decisively outperforms all competing 4-step distillation methods on super-resolution: it beats GD-II by 4.0 FID points (19.637 vs. 23.675), CM-II by 8.2 points (19.637 vs. 27.810), and ControlNet by 14.9 points (19.637 vs. 34.56). The full CoDi model is within 0.4 FID of the 250-step LDM baseline (19.637 vs. 19.200), effectively matching 62× more sampling steps. The parameter-efficient PE-CoDi (364M additional params) consistently wins over GD-II, CM-II, and ControlNet across all three tasks (SR, inpainting, depth-to-image), with the largest margin on depth-to-image (FID 23.047 vs. 29.780 for ControlNet). On inpainting, PE-CoDi (FID 14.700) even surpasses the 50-step ControlNet (14.895) while using only 4 steps. The only method CoDi does not beat is the 250-step LDM itself, but at 50 steps LDMs (19.231) is still slightly ahead of CoDi (19.637), confirming that the distillation gap is minimal. Notably, CoDi also outperforms the non-distilled LDM at 4 steps (29.266) by 9.6 FID points, showing that distillation is essential even for the base model at low step counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDi unifies two previously separate stages—conditional fine-tuning (à la ControlNet) and diffusion distillation (à la Consistency Models or Progressive Distillation)—into a single training procedure. It inherits the self-consistency formulation and skip-connection boundary condition from Consistency Models [47], the velocity parameterization from Progressive Distillation [39], and the duplicated-encoder conditional module from ControlNet [57], but departs from all of them by enforcing consistency in the *adapted* model's PF-ODE rather than a frozen teacher's, and by jointly optimizing a noise-space consistency loss with a signal-space conditional guidance loss. This single-stage 'conditional distillation' avoids the quality degradation that arises when distilling an already-fine-tuned model or fine-tuning an already-distilled one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 1–4 step conditional generation (SR, inpainting, depth-to-image, instruction editing) that matches or exceeds 200+ step baselines, with a parameter-efficient variant (PE-CoDi, 364 M params) that outperforms full ControlNet (1.22 B) at the same step count, enabling task-specific adapters to share a frozen backbone. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex or multi-modal conditions (e.g., simultaneous depth + pose + segmentation), how it behaves at substantially higher resolutions, and whether the single-stage formulation generalizes to video or 3D generation. The paper does not explore training stability at scale or the sensitivity to the conditional encoder architecture beyond the ControlNet-style duplication.
