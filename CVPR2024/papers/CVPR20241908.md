## Problem

Existing segmentation MLLMs like LISA rely on a single [SEG] token to trigger mask decoding, which forces them to produce exactly one mask per instruction. This design cannot handle Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation (GRES), where a single expression may reference multiple objects simultaneously or describe objects entirely absent from the image, leading to incorrect or hallucinated masks in both cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241908/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224 for the MLLM's CLIP-ViT-L/14 encoder and 1024×1024 for SAM's ViT-H encoder) and a free-form text referring expression that may name multiple targets or include non-existent objects. Output is a variable-length sequence of segmentation masks: each [SEG] token in the autoregressive output triggers one SAM query to decode a mask, while each [REJ] token directly assigns an all-zero mask without invoking the mask decoder, so the number of output masks matches the number of referenced entities rather than being fixed at one.

## Method

GSVA extends LISA's MLLM+SFM architecture by introducing two mechanisms: (1) multiple weight-sharing [SEG] tokens, each preceded by the specific referring expression for its target (e.g., 'front zebra:[SEG], right zebra:[SEG]'), which acts as implicit in-context learning to disambiguate which object each token should segment; and (2) a novel [REJ] token that the MLLM learns to predict for absent targets, bypassing SAM's mask decoder entirely and assigning empty masks. The MLLM (LLaVA with Vicuna-7B/13B or Llama2-13B) autoregressively generates the token sequence, all [SEG] token embeddings are projected via an MLP into SAM's prompt space to produce multiple segmentation queries, and the frozen SAM ViT-H backbone decodes the corresponding masks. Training proceeds in two stages: 50k-step pretraining on LISA's mixed dataset augmented with gRefCOCO, followed by 10-epoch finetuning on gRefCOCO, using LoRA adapters on the LLM.

## Results

On the GRES benchmark gRefCOCO, GSVA-Llama2-13B (finetuned) achieves 70.04 gIoU and 66.02 N-acc on the validation set, 73.29 gIoU on Test-A, and 65.45 gIoU on Test-B, setting a new state-of-the-art. The 7B variant (GSVA-Vicuna-7B ft) reaches 66.47 gIoU and 62.43 N-acc on validation, surpassing the strongest non-LLM baseline ReLA (63.60 gIoU, 56.37 N-acc) by ~3% gIoU and ~6% N-acc. On classic RES (RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg), GSVA-Llama2-13B (ft) attains 79.2/70.3/75.7 cIoU on validation splits, and on REC it reaches 89.16/79.74/85.47 Prec@0.5, outperforming LISA by 2.8–5.9% cIoU and 3–7% Prec@0.5 across splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSVA decisively outperforms all non-LLM GRES baselines (MattNet, LTS, VLT, CRIS, LAVT, ReLA) on gRefCOCO: the best GSVA variant (Llama2-13B ft) exceeds ReLA by +6.4 gIoU and +9.7 N-acc on validation, and by +3.3 gIoU on Test-A. Against the primary MLLM baseline LISA, GSVA wins by large margins across all three LLM backbones (Vicuna-7B, Vicuna-13B, Llama2-13B) and both pretrained/finetuned settings; the gap is most pronounced on N-acc (e.g., 62.43 vs. 54.67 for 7B ft, 66.02 vs. 57.49 for Llama2-13B ft), confirming that the [REJ] token is the key differentiator for empty-target rejection. On classic RES, GSVA-Llama2-13B (ft) surpasses PolyFormer-L (76.0/69.3/69.2 cIoU) and ReLA (73.8/66.0/65.0) on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg validation, and also exceeds X-Decoder and SEEM on RefCOCOg. On REC, GSVA-Vicuna-7B (ft) matches or slightly exceeds u-LLaVA-7B (full-ft) on RefCOCO (86.27 vs. 86.04 Prec@0.5) while using only LoRA-level adaptation, and GSVA-Llama2-13B (ft) leads all baselines by 3–5% across all REC splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSVA builds directly on LISA's (Lai et al., 2023) MLLM-plus-SFM architecture, where a single [SEG] token bridges a language model to a segmentation decoder. The key departure is twofold: replacing the single [SEG] token with multiple weight-sharing [SEG] tokens (each preceded by its referring expression, framed as implicit in-context learning) to handle multi-target queries, and introducing a [REJ] token to explicitly reject empty targets. The GRES task itself was defined by Liu et al. (2023), and GSVA positions itself as the first MLLM-based solution to it, drawing conceptual inspiration from dynamic neural networks for the adaptive, input-dependent behavior of the shared-weight tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The [REJ] token offers a clean, model-agnostic mechanism for empty-target rejection that could be adopted by other segmentation MLLMs beyond the LISA-style architecture, and the multi-[SEG] design addresses a practical gap critical for embodied AI (robot navigation, human-robot interaction) where instructions routinely reference multiple or nonexistent objects. Limitations include reliance on SAM as the segmentation backbone, evaluation restricted to 2D image segmentation on COCO-derived datasets, and the fact that the referring-expression-before-[SEG] prompt format is somewhat rigid. Open questions include whether this approach extends to video segmentation, 3D scenes, or open-vocabulary settings where the number and identity of targets are not known a priori.
