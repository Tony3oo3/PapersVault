## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods are shown to be harmful for misclassification detection (MisD), and vice versa, creating a fundamental tradeoff that makes the simple MSP baseline still the best unified failure detector. The paper identifies that jointly optimizing MisD and OOD detection objectives during training leads to conflicts because the two failure types have different sensitivity levels, and that even sequence learning (train MisD first, then OOD) suffers from catastrophic forgetting of reliability knowledge during the second phase.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241776/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-subset) as input and produces a single failure detection score (MSP by default) that is compared against a threshold to yield a binary reject/accept decision. No auxiliary outlier data, misclassified data, or additional model parameters are required at inference time; the entire procedure operates on the same training data used for the initial classifier, making it practical for deployment where only in-distribution labeled data is available.

## Method

RCL is a three-step reliable continual learning paradigm: (1) obtain a pre-trained classifier with strong MisD ability (e.g., trained with CRL or FMFP); (2) fine-tune it for T epochs using LogitNorm as the target OOD-detection objective, while applying Fisher Information–based weight regularization (EWC-style) to penalize changes to parameters important for the existing MisD knowledge; (3) perform Weight Space Interpolation (WSI) by linearly ensembling all intermediate models along the tuning trajectory (θ₀ through θ_T) to capture diverse reliability knowledge at different tuning stages. The key insight is that decoupling MisD and OOD objectives in sequence, combined with selective parameter preservation and trajectory ensembling, avoids the conflicts of joint learning while preventing catastrophic forgetting of reliability. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requires no auxiliary data, and can be applied to selective layers (e.g., only the last ResNet block) for efficiency.

## Results

On CIFAR-100/ResNet110, RCL built on FMFP achieves unified failure detection AUROC of 86.75% and AURC of 122.69, substantially outperforming the MSP baseline (83.60% AUROC, 143.68 AURC). On CIFAR-10/ResNet110, RCL (FMFP+RCL) reaches 95.39% failure detection AUROC and 9.98 AURC, versus 91.07% and 18.81 for MSP. RCL improves OOD detection AUROC by 8.18% over CRL and 6.21% over FMFP while preserving or slightly improving their MisD performance. On the larger WRN-28-10 architecture, RCL (CRL+RCL) on CIFAR-100 achieves 88.79% failure detection AUROC, again surpassing MSP (86.66%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCL consistently outperforms all OOD detection baselines (MSP, Energy, MaxLogit, KNN, LogitNorm) on unified failure detection: on CIFAR-100/ResNet110, Energy and MaxLogit degrade failure AUROC to ~80% (vs. MSP's 83.60%), while KNN and LogitNorm roughly match MSP; RCL (CRL+RCL) reaches 87.73% AUROC, a 4+ point gain. Against MisD baselines, RCL preserves their strong MisD performance (e.g., FMFP's 86.86% MisD AUROC stays at 87.11% with RCL) while boosting OOD AUROC from 72.91% to 79.12%, yielding the best overall failure detection. Among continual learning strategies tested in Table 4, DER achieves the best failure AUROC (89.71%) but introduces additional parameters; regularization-based methods (EWC, SI, MAS) are effective and parameter-free, with EWC (the Fisher-based RWC used in RCL) achieving 87.73% AUROC. The paper confirms that joint learning of CRL+LogitNorm is counterproductive (MisD AURC worsens from 76.93 to 80.42 on CIFAR-100), validating the sequential RCL approach over naive objective combination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified failure detection formulation introduced by Jaeger et al. [22], which calls for jointly evaluating misclassification detection (MisD) and out-of-distribution (OOD) detection. It repurposes the Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) regularization from Kirkpatrick et al. [25]—originally designed to preserve classification accuracy across sequential tasks—to instead preserve reliability knowledge (MisD ability) during fine-tuning for a new objective (OOD detection). The Weight Space Interpolation strategy draws on model soup literature [21, 47, 48] but differs by ensembling all intermediate tuning steps rather than just endpoints, and by operating under the constraint of weight regularization. Conceptually, the paper departs from the natural joint-learning approach by demonstrating that MisD and OOD objectives conflict during simultaneous optimization, motivating a sequential/continual learning paradigm instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world deployment requires rejecting both misclassified known-class examples and unknown-class OOD inputs, yet prior methods optimize for one at the expense of the other. The RCL paradigm is attractive for practitioners because it requires no auxiliary outlier data, no additional parameters, and only ~10 epochs of fine-tuning on top of an existing classifier. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet110 and WRN-28-10 (ImageNet results deferred to appendix), and the absolute improvements over the MSP baseline, while consistent, are moderate. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to vision transformers and larger datasets, whether the MisD→OOD ordering is always optimal, and whether the approach generalizes to other failure types beyond these two.
