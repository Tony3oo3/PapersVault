## Problem

3D hand-mesh reconstruction from a single image is bottlenecked by the limited diversity of existing real-world hand-object datasets in hand appearances, poses, views, and backgrounds. Rendering-based synthetic data suffers from a noticeable syn-to-real gap, while prior generative approaches (e.g., HOGAN) address only novel view synthesis and lack evidence of consistent downstream improvement. The paper tackles how to generate realistic, diverse, and novel hand-object interaction images with reliable 3D annotations that consistently boost reconstruction performance.

## Input / Output

The generative pipeline takes a 3D hand mesh and object pose as input, projects them into 256×256 normal and texture maps plus a quaternion-encoded hand orientation, and produces 128×128 RGB images whose 3D annotations are available for free. The downstream reconstruction models then consume a single RGB image of a hand-object interaction and regress MANO coefficients or 3D vertex coordinates. Edge-case filtering discards generated samples where a pre-trained reconstruction model produces joint/vertex errors exceeding predetermined thresholds.

## Method

HandBooster combines a conditional classifier-free diffusion model (DDPM) with a novel condition creator. The synthesizer uses content-aware conditions—normal map, texture map, and quaternion hand orientation embedded into the latent space at multiple UNet stages—to guide generation, with a pixel-wise foreground-weighted L2 loss on predicted RGB. The condition creator prepares grasping poses via optimization-based simulation with a three-criteria validation (contact, no intersection, no self-penetration), then applies two similarity-aware sampling strategies: an intra-distribution farthest-pose-sampling (FPS) using cosine distance on canonical pose vectors to ensure even coverage within real and synthetic pose sets, and a cross-distribution sampling that assigns each synthetic pose a probability inversely proportional to its aggregate similarity to all real poses, adaptively balancing novelty against redundancy.

## Results

On DexYCB (S0, root-relative), HandBooster reduces J-PE from 14.0→11.9 mm for HandOccNet, 14.2→13.2 mm for MobRecon, and 14.0→12.9 mm for H2ONet, while boosting F@5 from 51.5→55.6, 50.8→51.8, and 51.3→52.1 respectively. On the more challenging HO3D benchmark (root-relative), gains are larger: HandOccNet J-PE drops from 25.1→21.1 mm, MobRecon from 25.2→23.4 mm, and H2ONet from 26.3→24.0 mm. The boosted H2ONet on HO3D (J-PE 8.2 under PA) surpasses multi-frame Deformer (9.4) and approaches AMVUR (8.3), demonstrating that single-view reconstruction with HandBooster data can rival or exceed multi-frame methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HandBooster is a data-augmentation framework rather than a new reconstruction model, so it is evaluated by boosting three existing baselines (HandOccNet, MobRecon, H2ONet) and comparing before/after performance. All three baselines improve consistently across every metric on both DexYCB (S0 and S1 splits) and HO3D, with the largest gains on HO3D root-relative J-PE (up to 4.0 mm reduction for HandOccNet). On HO3D under Procrustes alignment, the boosted H2ONet (J-PE 8.2) surpasses multi-frame Deformer (9.4) and AMVUR (8.3), and the boosted HandOccNet (8.5) matches H2ONet's multi-frame result (8.5). On DexYCB S0, the boosted HandOccNet (root-relative J-PE 11.9) outperforms the multi-frame H2ONet (13.7) by 1.8 mm. MobRecon shows the smallest gains (≈1 mm J-PE reduction) due to its mobile-friendly design with only 0.46G FLOPs and 8.23M parameters, limiting its capacity to exploit the additional data. The FID comparison (2.91 for HandBooster vs. 8.52 for ObMan rendering) confirms the realism advantage of the generative approach over traditional rendering-based synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classifier-free diffusion framework (Ho & Salimans, 2022; Ho et al., 2020) and the prediction/loss formulation from Karras et al. (2022), repurposing them for a task-specific conditional generation of hand-object interaction images. It departs from prior rendering-based synthesis (ObMan, YCB-Afford, ArtiBoost) by replacing physically-based rendering with a learned generative model to close the syn-to-real gap, and from prior generative approaches like HOGAN by extending beyond novel-view synthesis to encompass diverse grasping poses, hand appearances, and backgrounds simultaneously. The similarity-aware sampling strategies (intra-distribution FPS and cross-distribution adaptive sampling) are novel contributions that go beyond simple data augmentation, deliberately exploring the generative space for poses absent from the training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—limited diversity in hand-object datasets—by providing a model-agnostic data synthesis pipeline that consistently improves three architecturally distinct baselines across two benchmarks, with the boosted single-view methods even surpassing multi-frame approaches. The FID comparison (2.91 vs. 8.52 for ObMan) quantifies the realism gap that rendering-based methods suffer from, validating the diffusion-based approach. Open questions include whether the method scales to more diverse hand shapes/appearances beyond the MANO parameterization, whether it generalizes to object categories outside YCB, and the computational cost of generating large-scale synthetic datasets at this fidelity.
