## Problem

In subject-driven text-to-image synthesis, learnable subject embeddings and fine-tuned networks tend to dominate the generation process, causing the model to ignore attributes specified in the text prompt (e.g., style, scene, composition). This 'content ignorance' limits output diversity and fidelity to user intent. Existing remedies modify the training process with additional regularization, but the paper seeks an inference-time solution that requires no retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a subject reference image (mapped to a subject embedding via a learnable token or an encoder) and a text prompt containing a pseudo-word S* as input, and produces a single generated image that is simultaneously faithful to the subject identity and the full text description. The subject-agnostic condition c0 is constructed at inference time by either replacing S* with a generic noun (e.g., 'a dog') for token-based methods or zeroing the subject embedding and its attention mask for encoder-based methods. The approach is purely inference-time and requires no modification to the trained model weights.

## Method

Subject-Agnostic Guidance (SAG) introduces a Dual Classifier-Free Guidance (DCFG) scheme that combines two guidance passes at each denoising step. The first pass (Weak CFG) computes a weighted difference between the subject-aware condition c and the subject-agnostic condition c0, using a time-varying weight w_t that is a piecewise-constant function: a value r during early iterations (where structure is established) and −1 during later iterations (where subject details are refined). The second pass (Null CFG) applies standard classifier-free guidance with a constant weight w to the Weak CFG output, using the null condition to preserve diversity. The key insight is that coarse image structure is formed in early diffusion steps, so suppressing subject information early allows the model to correctly construct scene layout and style, after which the subject-aware condition is reintroduced to restore identity. This mechanism is agnostic to how the subject is encoded and plugs into both optimization-based (Textual Inversion) and encoder-based (ELITE, SuTI) pipelines without any training changes.

## Results

On the quantitative comparison (Table 1), ELITE-SAG achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CLIP-T 0.344 (text alignment), CLIP-I 0.790 (image/subject alignment), and DINO 0.671 (subject fidelity). The most notable gains are in subject fidelity, where ELITE-SAG surpasses DreamBooth (0.651) by 0.020 and ELITE (0.586) by 0.085 on DINO. In the user study (Table 2), over 50% of raters prefer ELITE-SAG over each baseline on all three criteria (subject alignment, text alignment, quality), with the strongest preference (84%) over Textual Inversion on quality.

|---|---|---|---|
| DreamBooth [37] | 0.315 | 0.785 | 0.651 |
| Textual Inversion [16] | 0.339 | 0.751 | 0.571 |
| ELITE [49] | 0.342 | 0.751 | 0.586 |
| **ELITE-SAG (ours)** | **0.344** | **0.790** | **0.671** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELITE-SAG outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against DreamBooth, it improves CLIP-T from 0.315 to 0.344 (+0.029), CLIP-I from 0.785 to 0.790 (+0.005), and DINO from 0.651 to 0.671 (+0.020), showing that SAG recovers text alignment without sacrificing subject fidelity. Against Textual Inversion, the gains are larger on subject metrics (DINO: 0.571 → 0.671, +0.100) while also improving text alignment (CLIP-T: 0.339 → 0.344). Against the closest baseline ELITE, ELITE-SAG achieves a marginal CLIP-T improvement (0.342 → 0.344) but a substantial DINO gain (0.586 → 0.671, +0.085), indicating that the dual CFG mechanism primarily rescues subject fidelity that the encoder-based method loses. In the user study, ELITE-SAG is preferred over 50% of the time in every comparison, with the largest margins over Textual Inversion (64–84% preference) and ELITE (56–80% preference).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier-free guidance [19] into a Dual CFG (DCFG) scheme specifically for subject-driven text-to-image synthesis, where prior methods (Textual Inversion [16], DreamBooth [37], ELITE [49], SuTI [10]) suffer from subject embeddings dominating the generation and ignoring text-prompt attributes. Rather than modifying the training process with additional regularization (as prior work does), SAG operates entirely at inference time by constructing a subject-agnostic condition (replacing the learned subject token with a generic noun, or zeroing the subject embedding) and applying a time-varying guidance weight that suppresses subject information during early denoising steps. The approach is method-agnostic, plugging into both optimization-based (Textual Inversion) and encoder-based (ELITE, SuTI) pipelines without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAG offers a practical, training-free remedy for the content-ignorance problem that plagues subject-driven synthesis, making it immediately adoptable across existing pipelines with minimal code changes. The time-varying guidance schedule (suppressing subject in early steps, reintroducing it later) aligns with known diffusion dynamics where structure forms first, providing an intuitive mechanism for balancing subject fidelity against text alignment. Limitations include dependence on the underlying generation model's quality for uncommon content, and the quantitative gains, while consistent, are modest on text-alignment metrics (CLIP-T: 0.342→0.344) though more pronounced on subject fidelity (DINO: 0.586→0.671). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-subject or video generation settings, and whether more sophisticated subject-agnostic conditions (beyond simple noun substitution) could yield further gains.
