## Problem

Existing single-view 3D reconstruction methods either rely on slow per-shape optimization (e.g., SDS-based diffusion distillation) or struggle to directly regress 3D Gaussians from images due to their discrete, non-structural, and high-dimensional nature. This paper tackles the gap of achieving fast, feed-forward, category-agnostic 3D reconstruction that leverages the rendering speed and quality of Gaussian Splatting without requiring per-object optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240217/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and its camera pose as input. The image is encoded by a pre-trained DINOv2 ViT into 768-dimensional patch tokens (patch size 14), with camera information injected via adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) at each transformer layer. The output is a 16,384-point cloud (upsampled from an initial 2,048-point coarse prediction), a triplane feature field (three 32×32×512 axis-aligned planes), and per-point 3D Gaussian attributes (offset position, opacity, anisotropic scale, rotation quaternion, and spherical harmonic coefficients), which together enable real-time novel view rendering via differentiable splatting.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid Triplane-Gaussian representation that separates explicit geometry (point cloud) from implicit Gaussian properties (triplane features), making the learning problem tractable. Two transformer-based decoders operate in parallel: a point cloud decoder (6-layer, 2048 learnable tokens) predicts coarse geometry, which is then densified to 16,384 points via two-step Snowflake point deconvolution augmented with projection-aware conditioning from the input image; a triplane decoder (10-layer, 3×32×32 tokens) predicts the implicit feature field, with geometry-aware encoding that projects point cloud features (via PointNet with local pooling) onto the triplane planes to improve alignment. A final MLP decodes 3D Gaussian attributes by querying the triplane at each point position and concatenating projected local image features (RGB, DINOv2 features, mask, distance transform). The full pipeline is trained end-to-end on Objaverse-LVIS (46K models) using a combination of Chamfer Distance, Earth Mover's Distance, and rendering losses (MSE, mask, SSIM, LPIPS), achieving sub-second reconstruction and 48 ms rendering per view.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, TGS achieves a Chamfer Distance of 21.04×10⁻³ and Volume IoU of 0.401 for single-view 3D reconstruction, substantially outperforming Point-E (CD 68.56, IoU 0.181), Shap-E (CD 79.84, IoU 0.218), and One-2-3-45 (CD 75.56, IoU 0.338). For novel view synthesis, TGS reaches PSNR 23.15, SSIM 0.87, and LPIPS 0.13, surpassing PixelNeRF (PSNR 20.64), Zero-1-2-3 (PSNR 17.57), and One-2-3-45 (PSNR 15.55). The method completes full reconstruction in 0.14 s and renders novel views in 3 ms, orders of magnitude faster than all baselines (78 s for Point-E, 14 s for One-2-3-45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGS dominates all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the native 3D generative models, Point-E produces sparse point clouds with holes (CD 68.56, IoU 0.181) and Shap-E suffers from generation collapse (CD 79.84, IoU 0.218), while TGS achieves CD 21.04 and IoU 0.401 with explicit point-cloud geometry. One-2-3-45, which relies on Zero-1-2-3-generated multi-view images and SparseNeuS, yields coarse geometry (CD 75.56) and the lowest novel-view PSNR (15.55) due to accumulated diffusion errors. Zero-1-2-3, a pure 2D diffusion approach, generates plausible but inconsistent novel views (PSNR 17.57) and can hallucinate entirely different objects. PixelNeRF, the strongest feed-forward NeRF baseline, reaches PSNR 20.64 but is outperformed by TGS by 2.51 dB in PSNR and 0.05 in SSIM, while TGS renders 1363× faster (3 ms vs. 4.09 s). The speed advantage stems from TGS's feed-forward transformer architecture combined with efficient Gaussian splatting rasterization, eliminating per-shape optimization entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the transformer-based reconstruction paradigm established by LRM, which decodes a triplane NeRF representation from image tokens via cross-attention, and extends it by replacing the implicit NeRF rendering with a hybrid Triplane-Gaussian representation that leverages the fast splatting pipeline of 3D Gaussian Splatting. It also incorporates projection-aware conditioning from PC² and Snowflake point deconvolution for point cloud upsampling, but departs from prior work by separating explicit geometry (point cloud) from implicit Gaussian attributes (triplane), motivated by the failure of directly regressing 3D Gaussians from latent tokens. The key conceptual departure from SDS-based and diffusion-based methods is the elimination of per-shape optimization entirely in favor of a feed-forward transformer pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-second reconstruction time (0.14s) and near-instant rendering (3ms) make single-view 3D reconstruction practically viable for interactive applications like AR/VR and rapid 3D content creation, where prior methods required 27–78 seconds. The hybrid representation design—explicit points for geometry, implicit triplane for Gaussian properties—offers a principled solution to the non-structural, high-dimensional nature of 3D Gaussians that has blocked their use in generalizable settings. Limitations remain: backside texture is blurry due to the non-probabilistic nature of the model, and rendering quality is sensitive to initial geometry errors. Open questions include whether combining with GAN or diffusion priors can address the backside ambiguity, and whether the approach extends to multi-object or scene-level reconstruction.
