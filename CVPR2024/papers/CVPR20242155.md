## Problem

Existing medical image segmentation methods suffer from two coupled limitations: they require training or fine-tuning a new model for each new task (infeasible for most clinical settings), and they produce a single deterministic mask that ignores the well-documented inter-annotator variability in what constitutes the correct segmentation. No prior framework simultaneously generalizes to unseen segmentation tasks via in-context learning and outputs a distribution of plausible segmentations to capture this ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242155/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Tyche takes a target image, a context set of S=16 image-segmentation pairs that implicitly defines the task, and K Gaussian noise channels (K_tr=8 at training, flexible at inference). It outputs K diverse binary segmentation masks in a single forward pass (Tyche-TS) or via K augmented forward passes through a pretrained deterministic ICL network (Tyche-IS). The context set is sampled from the development split of the target dataset, so the model never sees out-of-distribution data during training, and the number of output candidates can be varied at test time without retraining.

## Method

Tyche-TS employs a UNet-like architecture (4 encoder + 4 decoder levels, 64 features, Leaky ReLU) whose core building block is a novel SetBlock that enables set-to-set interaction between K candidate feature maps and S context feature maps. SetBlock computes the mean of candidate features, passes it through a CrossBlock with the context set, then broadcasts the result back to each candidate via channel concatenation and convolution, allowing predictions to inform one another. Training uses a best-candidate loss that backpropagates only through the prediction with minimum Dice loss among K candidates, encouraging the network to produce diverse solutions rather than collapsing to a single mode. Tyche-IS instead applies in-context test-time augmentation (ICTTA)—Gaussian noise, blurring, and intensity inversion—to both the target and the context set of a pretrained deterministic ICL model (UniverSeg), generating K stochastic predictions without any retraining. Ablations confirm that SetBlock is the critical architectural component for diversity, while simulated multi-annotator training data and standard-deviation features provide negligible gains.

## Results

On multi-annotator out-of-distribution data (context size 16, 8 predictions), Tyche-TS achieves best candidate Dice of 0.88 (Hippocampus), 0.91 (LIDC-IDRI), 0.95 (Prostate Task 1), 0.93 (Prostate Task 2), and 0.60 (STARE), outperforming all in-context and interactive baselines on every dataset. Tyche-TS also matches or exceeds stochastic upper bounds trained specifically on each O.D. dataset: it beats ProbaUNet on LIDC-IDRI (0.91 vs 0.86) and STARE (0.60 vs 0.59), and is within 0.01–0.03 of the best upper bound on the remaining tasks. In terms of GED², Tyche-TS achieves 0.22, 0.40, 0.09, 0.15, and 0.62, competitive with task-specific probabilistic models. Tyche-TS runs in 18.09 ms for 8 predictions with only 1.7M parameters, versus 2,857 ms for SegGPT (370M params) and 170,000 ms for CIDM (85.6M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tyche outperforms all three in-context baselines (UniverSeg, SegGPT, SENet) on every O.D. dataset in best candidate Dice; the largest gaps are on LIDC-IDRI (Tyche-TS 0.91 vs UniverSeg 0.67, SENet 0.00) and STARE (Tyche-TS 0.60 vs SegGPT 0.02, SENet 0.30). Against interactive methods, Tyche-TS beats fine-tuned SAM by 0.17 on Hippocampus (0.88 vs 0.71) and by 0.10 on LIDC-IDRI (0.91 vs 0.55), and beats SAM-Med2D by 0.36 on LIDC-IDRI (0.91 vs 0.42). Compared to stochastic upper bounds trained specifically on each O.D. dataset, Tyche-TS matches or exceeds ProbaUNet on LIDC-IDRI (0.91 vs 0.86) and STARE (0.60 vs 0.59), and is within 0.03 of the best upper bound on Hippocampus and both Prostate tasks. On GED², Tyche-TS is competitive with upper bounds on most datasets (e.g., 0.40 vs ProbaUNet 0.51 on LIDC-IDRI; 0.62 vs ProbaUNet 0.76 on STARE) but trails slightly on Hippocampus (0.22 vs ProbaUNet 0.13). The key distinction is that Tyche achieves these results without ever seeing the O.D. datasets during training, whereas the stochastic upper bounds are trained, tuned, and evaluated on those same datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tyche unifies two previously separate research threads: in-context learning (ICL) segmentation, exemplified by UniverSeg and its CrossBlock mechanism, and stochastic/probabilistic segmentation, represented by Probabilistic UNet, PhiSeg, and diffusion-based methods. The core architectural contribution, SetBlock, extends CrossBlock from a single-target-to-context interaction to a set-to-set interaction among K prediction candidates, enabling them to communicate and diverge. The best-candidate loss (backpropagating only through the min-Dice prediction) draws on multiple-choice learning, while Tyche-IS repurposes test-time augmentation in a novel way by applying it jointly to both the target and the context set rather than ensembling averaged predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tyche addresses a practical bottleneck in clinical and research settings: the need to retrain or fine-tune a model for every new segmentation task, while simultaneously acknowledging that a single deterministic mask often fails to capture genuine inter-annotator ambiguity. The finding that a 1.7M-parameter model can match task-specific stochastic networks (trained directly on the evaluation data) without ever seeing those datasets is practically significant for resource-constrained biomedical environments. Open questions include whether the captured stochasticity truly reflects aleatoric uncertainty versus model artifacts, how the approach extends to 3D volumes, and whether the fixed context size of 16 remains sufficient for highly heterogeneous tasks. The ablation showing that simulated multi-annotator training data provides negligible improvement suggests the diversity mechanism (SetBlock + best-candidate loss) is doing most of the work, which is both reassuring and a hint that the synthetic data design may be suboptimal.
