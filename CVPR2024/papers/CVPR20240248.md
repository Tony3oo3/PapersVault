## Problem

Traditional CNN-based restoration networks introduce non-linearity through fixed activation functions (ReLU, sigmoid, etc.), which adds computational overhead and constrains the non-linear response to a predefined form. The paper asks whether non-linearity can instead be learned directly from data through higher-order convolutions, and whether this yields a more general and efficient framework for image/video restoration.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single degraded RGB image (motion-blurred or sensor-noisy) or a short video clip with sensor noise, and outputs a restored image or video at the same spatial resolution. For video, the 3D spatio-temporal Volterra filter operates over a temporal window of length 2L+1 frames to capture inter-frame dynamics. The U-Net encoder reduces spatial resolution by a factor of 8 (H/8 × W/8) in the latent space before the decoder upsamples back to full resolution.

## Method

MR-VNet replaces activation functions with second-order Volterra layers, where each layer computes a linear convolution plus a quadratic (pixel-interaction) convolution: X_z = F_z^1(X_{z-1}) + F_z^2(X_{z-1}). Two practical approximations are provided: a lossless version that discards symmetric kernel terms, and a lossy version that factorizes the quadratic kernel into Q rank-1 separable convolutions (W_aq * X · W_bq * X), reducing complexity from O(P²) to O(QP). The architecture is a U-Net with 4 encoder blocks (4 Volterra layers each), 1 middle block, and 4 decoder blocks (1 Volterra layer each), with skip connections. The paper proves that NAFNet's simple gate is a Q=1 special case of this formulation and that the Volterra filter can approximate any continuous activation function, making it a strictly more general non-linearity mechanism.

## Results

On the GoPro deblurring benchmark, MR-VNet-LLA achieves 34.04 dB PSNR and 0.969 SSIM with 96 GMACs, outperforming the strongest baseline NAF-Net (33.69 dB, 0.967 SSIM, 65 GMACs) by 0.35 dB while using comparable compute. On SIDD denoising, MR-VNet-LLA reaches 40.58 dB PSNR and 0.963 SSIM, surpassing NAF-Net (40.30 dB) and Restormer (40.02 dB). On the CRVD video denoising task, MR-VNet-LYA achieves 41.93 dB PSNR and 0.985 SSIM with 163 GMACs, beating LLVD-L (41.41 dB, 117 GMACs) and RVIDEFormer (41.29 dB, 287 GMACs). The lossy approximation variant (Q=2) achieves 33.85 dB on GoPro with only 47 GMACs, the lowest compute among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR-VNet outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every dataset and metric. On GoPro deblurring, MR-VNet-LLA (34.04 dB) beats NAF-Net (33.69 dB) by 0.35 dB, MPR-Net (33.31 dB) by 0.73 dB, and Restormer (32.92 dB) by 1.12 dB, while using fewer GMACs than MPR-Net (96 vs. 778.2) and Restormer (96 vs. 140). On SIDD denoising, MR-VNet-LLA (40.58 dB) leads NAF-Net (40.30 dB) by 0.28 dB and Restormer (40.02 dB) by 0.56 dB. On REDS deblurring, MR-VNet-LLA (29.92 dB) surpasses NAF-Net (29.09 dB) by 0.83 dB and MAXIM (28.93 dB) by 0.99 dB. On CRVD video denoising, MR-VNet-LYA (41.93 dB) exceeds LLVD-L (41.41 dB) by 0.52 dB and RVIDEFormer (41.29 dB) by 0.64 dB, while using fewer GMACs than RVIDEFormer (163 vs. 287). The Q=2 lossy variant achieves the lowest compute (47 GMACs on GoPro) among all methods while still outperforming NAF-Net, demonstrating that the Volterra formulation delivers superior quality at reduced or comparable computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Volterra series formulation (a classical signal-processing tool for nonlinear system identification) to image/video restoration by replacing activation functions with second-order convolutions in a U-Net architecture. It builds directly on the cascaded second-order Volterra filter approach from [18] (originally for video action recognition) and extends the separable-kernel approximation from VolterraNet [3] from rank-1 to higher ranks (Q=2,4,8). A key conceptual contribution is showing that NAFNet's simple gate (element-wise multiplication of two convolved feature maps) is mathematically equivalent to a rank-1 approximation of the quadratic Volterra kernel, thereby unifying two apparently distinct design philosophies under one framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA restoration quality at substantially lower computational cost (47–96 GMACs vs. 65–1235 GMACs for competing methods), making high-quality restoration feasible on resource-constrained devices. The theoretical unification of NAFNet as a special case provides a principled path for future work: increasing the kernel rank Q is a natural knob to trade accuracy against complexity. Open questions include whether third- or higher-order Volterra terms yield meaningful gains beyond second order, whether the approach transfers to other restoration sub-tasks (super-resolution, compression artifact removal), and whether the lossless approximation's 96 GMACs can be further reduced without quality loss.
