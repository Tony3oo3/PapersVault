## Problem

Existing information bottleneck (IB)-based deep multi-view clustering methods rely on variational approximation with auxiliary neural estimators to lower-bound mutual information, introducing approximation error and uncertainty into high-dimensional multi-view representation learning. This paper asks whether mutual information can be directly and deterministically fitted from data without any distributional assumptions or variational surrogates, yielding an analytically tractable IB objective for MVC.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabelled multi-view collection {X^v ∈ R^{N×D^v}}_{v=1}^V where views may have heterogeneous dimensions and modalities (e.g., RGB images, text features, depth sensor readings) and produces a single clustering assignment C partitioning the N samples into K clusters. No labels, cluster prototypes, or pairwise similarity matrices are required; the only hyperparameters are the number of views V, cluster count K, and trade-off scalars γ and β (both fixed at 0.01 across all experiments).

## Method

DIB replaces variational MI estimation with a deterministic measurement built on the eigenvalues of a normalized Gaussian kernel Gram matrix, which directly fits Rényi's α-order entropy (Proposition 1) and admits closed-form analytical gradients (Proposition 2). A view-specific four-layer fully connected encoder E^v is trained to minimize I_α(X^v; Z^v) via backpropagation, producing a deterministic compact representation Z^v = E^v(X^v) per view without any stochastic sampling or KL-divergence regularization. On top of these representations, a triplet consistency discovery module applies contrastive learning to maximize feature consistency (cross-view high-level features), cluster consistency (cross-view semantic labels), and joint consistency (within-view feature-to-label alignment), all combined with the compression loss in a single differentiable objective (Eq. 15) optimized end-to-end with Adam.

## Results

DIB achieves the best ACC across all 8 dataset configurations, reaching 99.86% on MNIST-USPS, 99.00% on BDGP, 88.95% on Handwritten, 59.06% on ESP, 60.40% on Flickr, and 84.79% on Caltech-5V. On the most challenging ESP dataset, DIB improves ACC by 3.04% over MFLVC, 12.01% over CVCL, 8.38% over AONGR, 1.46% over GCFAgg, and 9.49% over SDMVC. The method also leads on NMI in every setting, with the largest margin on Caltech-5V (78.34% vs. 78.08% for SDMVC) and Flickr (40.23% vs. 38.88% for DCIB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIB outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest deep MVC baselines, DIB gains 3.04% ACC over MFLVC, 12.01% over CVCL, 8.38% over AONGR, 1.46% over GCFAgg, and 9.49% over SDMVC on ESP. Among IB-based methods, DIB surpasses the closest competitor ConGMC by 0.61% ACC on ESP (59.06 vs. 58.45), 0.35% on Flickr (60.40 vs. 60.05), and 1.01% on Caltech-5V (84.79 vs. 83.78). On MNIST-USPS and BDGP, DIB edges out the near-saturated SDMVC (99.86 vs. 99.82 and 99.00 vs. 96.80 ACC respectively). The largest absolute margins appear on Handwritten (88.95 vs. 88.80 for SMKKM, the second-best) and Caltech-4V (75.64 vs. 74.79 for SDMVC), where the deterministic compression and triplet consistency jointly provide a clear advantage over both shallow and variational IB approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck principle for multi-view clustering by replacing the standard variational approximation (Alemi et al., 2017) with a deterministic mutual information measurement based on eigenvalues of normalized kernel Gram matrices, drawing on the established connection between Rényi's α-order entropy and Parzen-window kernel density estimation (Giraldo et al., 2015; Príncipe, 2010). It departs from prior IB-based deep MVC methods (DMIM, DMIB, ConGMC, DCIB) that rely on auxiliary neural estimators to approximate posterior distributions, instead computing MI analytically from data pairs. The triplet consistency discovery mechanism (feature, cluster, joint) builds on contrastive learning formulations already used in ConGMC and CVCL, but is applied here to deterministic rather than stochastic representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical alternative to variational MI estimation, showing that kernel Gram matrix eigenvalues provide a differentiable, distribution-free path to parameterizing the IB objective—potentially reducing the uncertainty that plagues variational approaches in high-dimensional multi-view spaces. However, the practical impact is bounded: the Gram matrix construction is O(N²) in memory and eigenvalue computation, limiting scalability to the relatively small datasets tested (largest: 12,154 samples). Open questions include whether this kernel-based MI measurement remains informative at scale, how the choice of kernel bandwidth σ affects performance in practice, and whether the deterministic compression generalizes beyond the four-layer MLP encoders used here. The authors themselves suggest layer-by-layer DNN training as a natural next step.
