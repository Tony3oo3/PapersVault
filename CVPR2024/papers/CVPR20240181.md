## Problem

Global translation estimation in SfM is highly sensitive to low-parallax feature matches and collinear camera motion when relying solely on relative translations. Existing methods that incorporate feature tracks into the objective function are either implicit (sensitive to relative translation errors in 3D point representation) or explicit (noisy under feature track outliers). The paper identifies that the failure of prior explicit methods like 1DSfM stems from an inappropriate objective function and inaccurate input observations, rather than from the explicit formulation itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise feature matches (2D-2D correspondences between image pairs) and pre-computed global camera rotations, organized into a view track graph where camera nodes are connected by relative translation edges and 3D point nodes are connected to cameras by feature ray edges. It outputs camera positions in a global coordinate frame and 3D point coordinates, with the scale and position ambiguity resolved via camera-to-camera inequality constraints. The two-stage optimization first solves a convex L1-norm problem via ADMM, then refines with a non-convex angle-based L2-norm objective under IRLS.

## Method

HETA (Hybrid Explicit Translation Averaging) is a two-step explicit framework that simultaneously estimates camera positions and 3D points. Before optimization, relative translations are re-estimated using the epipolar coplanarity constraint with unnormalized normal vectors (preserving the sin α weight that reflects parallax-dependent accuracy), and feature tracks are selected by sorting on maximum parallax angle until all cameras are covered at least N times. The objective function uses the cross-product-form distance metric rather than the scale-form, avoiding redundant scale variables that hinder convergence under disparate baseline/depth scales. The first stage minimizes L1-norm residuals via ADMM for outlier robustness, and the second stage refines with an unbiased angle-based L2-norm objective under IRLS with Cauchy loss, yielding both accurate and robust camera and point estimates.

## Results

On the KITTI sequential dataset, HETA achieves median/mean camera position errors of 0.2/0.4 m (seq 06) and 0.1/0.3 m (seq 03) after BA, dramatically outperforming LUD (20.2/46.5 m on seq 06), CReTA (14.9/39.6 m), and PGILP (2.9/17.4 m). On the unordered 1DSfM dataset, HETA registers the highest number of images (e.g., 3951/3989 on TFG, 1807/1838 on PIC) while maintaining the best or near-best median errors (e.g., 0.1 m on ALM, ELS, MND, ND, NYC, PDP, PIC, ROF, YKM). The 1DSfM explicit baseline fails catastrophically on KITTI with errors of 1e2 m, and LiGT also degrades severely on several sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HETA decisively outperforms all five baselines on the KITTI sequential dataset, where low parallax and near-collinear camera motion are the primary challenges. The explicit 1DSfM baseline [54] fails catastrophically with errors of 1e2 m on most sequences, and LiGT [8] (implicit, feature-track-only) also degrades severely (e.g., 85.0/2e2 m on seq 00, 63.5/1e2 m on seq 09). Among methods using relative translations, LUD [38] and CReTA [32] remain 10–100× worse than HETA on difficult sequences (e.g., seq 06: 20.2/46.5 and 14.9/39.6 m vs. HETA's 0.2/0.4 m). PGILP [30] (implicit, parallax-based) is the closest competitor on KITTI but still lags by 3–40× on median error. On the unordered 1DSfM dataset, HETA achieves comparable or slightly better median accuracy than LUD and CReTA while registering significantly more images (e.g., 3951 vs. 3864 on TFG; 1807 vs. 1797 on PIC), demonstrating superior robustness to the higher outlier rates in Internet-sourced feature matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the global translation estimation subproblem of SfM, building directly on the LUD framework [38] for relative translation re-estimation while departing from it by preserving the parallax-angle-dependent weighting (sin α) of epipolar plane normals rather than normalizing them. It systematically categorizes prior feature-track-based methods into explicit (jointly optimizing 3D points) and implicit (constraining cameras via represented points) families, then argues that the cross-product distance metric is superior to the scale-form objective used in 1DSfM [54] and PGILP [30]. The hybrid formulation—combining re-estimated relative translations with a curated subset of feature tracks—repurposes ideas from both the relative-translation-only line (LUD, CReTA) and the feature-track line (LiGT, PGILP) into a unified two-step L1-then-L2 optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical guidance for SfM practitioners: the finding that explicit methods are as efficient as implicit methods dispels a common assumption that jointly optimizing 3D points inflates problem complexity, and the cross-product-form objective's convergence advantage over scale-form is a useful design choice for robust optimization pipelines. However, the broader challenge of feature match outliers in global SfM remains unresolved, and the authors acknowledge that neural-network-based feature matching is needed for wider adoption. The method's reliance on accurate global rotations (estimated separately) and a predefined parallax threshold limits its applicability in extremely degenerate scenes.
