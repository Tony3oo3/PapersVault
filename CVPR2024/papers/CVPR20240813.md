## Problem

Existing availability attacks that inject unlearnable noise into data are easily neutralized by adversarial training, and the few robust methods (REM, EntF) still offer limited protection. Two specific gaps are identified: REM's surrogate model is effectively non-robust despite its optimization structure, and prior work has ignored the original EM definition that models trained on unlearnable examples should perform at random-guessing level on clean samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240813/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled training dataset of RGB images (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet subset at 224×224) and produces per-sample L∞-bounded unlearnable noise (ρu = 8/255) via a trained surrogate model. The resulting unlearnable examples (clean image + noise) are imperceptible perturbations designed so that any downstream model—whether standard or adversarially trained—achieves near-random accuracy on clean test data. The noise is model-agnostic at deployment: generated once by ResNet-18, it transfers to VGG-16, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, and Wide ResNet-34-10.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage (min-max)-min optimization to train a robust surrogate model from scratch. In the inner min stage, PGD generates unlearnable noise δu that minimizes the surrogate's loss on clean examples. In the outer min-max stage, the surrogate undergoes adversarial training on the unlearnable examples (x + δu + δa), extracting robust features so that the noise it produces naturally resists adversarial training in downstream poisoned models. A key differentiator from REM is the reordering: REM uses min-(min-max) with a non-robust surrogate, while this work uses (min-max)-min with a robust one, ensuring the noise targets robust features. Additionally, the paper introduces a Differentiable Average Randomness (DAR) constraint—MSE between the surrogate's predicted probability vector and the uniform distribution—added to the outer objective to explicitly enforce random-guessing behavior on clean samples, a constraint absent in all prior methods.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with adversarial training radius ρa=4/255, the proposed method reduces test accuracy to 23.12%, compared to 48.16% for the best prior robust method (REM) and 88–89% for non-robust baselines (EM, TAP, NTGA, EntF). On CIFAR-100 under the same setting, the method achieves 23.00% versus 27.35% for REM. On the ImageNet subset at ρa=4/255, the method reaches 31.64% versus 41.66% for REM, representing a ~10 percentage-point improvement in protection. The method consistently outperforms all baselines across all three datasets and all adversarial training radii from 1/255 to 4/255.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: three non-robust methods (EM, TAP, NTGA) and two robust methods (REM, EntF). All non-robust baselines are effectively neutralized by adversarial training: at ρa=4/255 on CIFAR-10, EM (88.62%), TAP (88.02%), NTGA (88.96%), and EntF (89.52%) all retain near-clean accuracy, while the proposed method drops to 23.12%. Among robust methods, REM is the strongest prior baseline but still yields 48.16% on CIFAR-10 and 27.35% on CIFAR-100 at ρa=4/255, compared to 23.12% and 23.00% for the proposed method—a gap of ~25 and ~4.3 percentage points respectively. On the ImageNet subset at ρa=4/255, the proposed method (31.64%) outperforms REM (41.66%) by ~10 percentage points. The proposed method wins on every dataset, every adversarial training radius, and every model architecture tested (VGG-16, ResNet-18, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, WRN-34-10), with the largest margins observed on CIFAR-10 where the gap over REM reaches 25+ percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the unlearnable examples line of work initiated by EM (Huang et al., ICLR 2021) and its robustness-oriented successors REM (Fu et al., ICLR 2022) and EntF (Wen et al., ICLR 2023). It departs from REM by reordering the optimization from min-(min-max) to (min-max)-min, arguing that REM's surrogate model is effectively non-robust because ρ_a < ρ_u collapses the adversarial step, making it structurally equivalent to EM. It also departs from EntF by rejecting feature-level constraints in favor of a full adversarial-training-based surrogate, and introduces an Average Randomness Constraint (DAR) that formalizes EM's original 'random guessing' definition, which prior robust methods had never enforced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AI companies face increasing scrutiny over unauthorized data usage, robust availability attacks are a practically important defense mechanism, and this paper provides a more principled optimization framework that consistently outperforms prior methods by up to ~30% under adversarial training across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet subsets. Key limitations include the substantial computational cost of adversarial training for noise generation (problematic at ImageNet scale) and notably weaker protection when only a fraction of data is poisoned. Open questions include whether the min-max-min structure can be made more efficient, whether partial-protection scenarios can be addressed without degrading to near-clean performance, and whether incorporating misleading semantic information could strengthen protection further.
