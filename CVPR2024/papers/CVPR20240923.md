## Problem

Existing human-human interaction datasets are limited by inaccurate body motions from RGB-based extraction (suffering occlusions and penetrations), absence of dexterous hand gestures, coarse textual annotations, and missing annotations for causal interaction order (actor-reactor asymmetry) and subject relationships/personalities. These gaps prevent a unified study of both perceptual and generative tasks for human-human interactions at the level of detail required for practical applications in surveillance, AR/VR, and robotics.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 11,388 SMPL-X motion sequences (body pose θ∈R^{N×55×3}, shape β∈R^{N×10}, translation t∈R^{N×3}) captured at 120 fps with ±0.15 mm accuracy, paired with 34,164 fine-grained text descriptions averaging ~35 words at human-part level (coarse body, finger movements, relative orientations), 40 action categories, causal actor-reactor order labels, and Big Five personality plus 5-category relationship annotations for 89 subjects. Synthetic multi-view RGB videos (1920×1080, 30 fps, 6 viewpoints, 4 scenes) are rendered via Unreal Engine. The 8 downstream tasks consume different subsets of these annotations as conditioning signals and produce SMPL-X motions, textual captions, category labels, order predictions, or personality/relationship assessments.

## Method

The capture system combines an OptiTrack optical MoCap rig (20 PrimeX 22 infrared cameras at 2048×1088, 120 fps) for full-body motion with Noitom PNS inertial gloves for occlusion-robust finger tracking, spatially aligned through a shared triangular bracket of reflective markers at the wrists and temporally synchronized via timecodes. Captured keypoints are fitted to the SMPL-X parametric model via a weighted least-squares optimization with part-specific weights, and sequences are segmented into atomic interaction snippets. The paper then formulates a unified benchmark of 8 tasks across 4 categories (text-conditioned generation, action-conditioned generation, reaction generation, interaction recognition, captioning, causal order inference, stylized generation, personality assessment) and evaluates state-of-the-art diffusion, transformer, and GCN-based methods on the test split, establishing baselines and evaluation metrics for tasks that previously lacked standardized benchmarks.

## Results

On the Inter-X dataset, InterGen achieves the best text-conditioned generation quality with R Precision Top-1 of 0.207, FID of 5.207, and MModality of 3.686, while ComMDM performs worst on R Precision (Top-1: 0.090). For action-conditioned generation, Actformer attains the best FID (8.067) and action recognition accuracy (94.5%), while MDM leads in multimodality (22.042). In human reaction generation, AGRoL dominates all four metrics (FID 4.386, Acc 92.5%, Div 12.204, Multimod 20.199). For skeleton-based interaction recognition, MS-G3D achieves the best Top-1 accuracy of 83.30% and Top-5 of 97.09%, which the authors note is still unsatisfactory given the dataset's hand-gesture and action/reaction diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Across the four evaluated tasks, no single method dominates all metrics, highlighting the dataset's difficulty. In text-conditioned generation, InterGen [54] leads on R Precision (Top-1 0.207 vs. 0.184 for T2M [34] and 0.203 for MDM [78]) and FID (5.207 vs. 5.481 for T2M), while ComMDM [71] and TEMOS [66] trail significantly on R Precision (0.090 and 0.092) and FID (29.27 and 29.26). For action-conditioned generation, Actformer [87] achieves the best FID (8.067) and accuracy (94.5%), outperforming ACTOR [65] (FID 9.392, Acc 85.5%) and MDM [78] (FID 12.426, Acc 89.6%), though MDM edges out Actformer on multimodality (22.042 vs. 16.187). In reaction generation, AGRoL [25] wins all four metrics (FID 4.386, Acc 92.5%), beating RAIG [76] (FID 6.372, Acc 90.8%) and MDM [78] (FID 6.747, Acc 90.3%), while MDM-GRU [20,78] is consistently worst. For recognition, MS-G3D [58] achieves the best Top-1 (83.30%) over CTR-GCN [18] (82.19%), HD-GCN [50] (77.40%), 2s-AGCN [72] (75.22%), and ST-GCN [89] (64.62%), but the authors stress that even the best result is far from satisfactory, underscoring room for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the human-human interaction dataset lineage by combining optical MoCap (OptiTrack) with inertial hand tracking (Noitom PNS) to achieve a level of body and finger fidelity that prior RGB-based datasets (InterHuman, NTU120, Chi3D) could not. It departs from the InterHuman [54] line of work—which introduced text-annotated interaction sequences—by adding four annotation modalities absent from all prior datasets: dexterous hand gestures via SMPL-X, fine-grained part-level text descriptions, explicit actor-reactor causal order, and Big Five personality/relationship metadata. The benchmark then repurposes existing generative and perceptual methods (diffusion models, transformers, GCNs) under a unified evaluation protocol, rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the currently largest human-human interaction MoCap dataset (11K sequences, 8.1M frames, 40 action categories), Inter-X is positioned to become a standard testbed for the field, and the benchmark reveals that even strong SOTA methods (e.g., MS-G3D at 83.30% Top-1, InterGen with moderate FID) still struggle with the added complexity of hand gestures and diverse reactions—suggesting substantial headroom for future work. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the absence of facial expressions and the restriction to atomic (short) interaction snippets rather than long, multi-transition sequences, which limits ecological validity. Open questions include whether personality and relationship annotations can meaningfully drive stylized generation, and whether causal order inference can generalize to real-world surveillance settings.
