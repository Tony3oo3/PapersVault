## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised scene graph generation (WSSGG) methods rely on rule-based parsers and static knowledge bases (e.g., WordNet) to form triplets from image captions, but this process suffers from two overlooked issues: semantic over-simplification (fine-grained predicates like 'lying on' are collapsed into coarse ones like 'on') and low-density scene graphs (many triplets are discarded when any component fails to match the KB). These issues produce incomplete, long-tailed supervision that degrades SGG model performance even when grounding is correct.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with their natural-language captions (from COCO, Conceptual Captions, or Visual Genome caption datasets) and produces a scene graph of localized triplets, each containing subject/object bounding boxes and entity/predicate class labels from the target dataset's vocabulary. The LLM operates purely on the caption text (no image input), while a pre-trained object detector (Faster R-CNN) provides candidate regions for the subsequent grounding step. The final localized triplets serve as pseudo-labels for training a standard supervised SGG model in the SGDet setting.

## Method

LLM4SGG replaces both the rule-based parser and the KB-based alignment in the WSSGG pipeline with a pre-trained LLM (ChatGPT) prompted via Chain-of-Thought and in-context few-shot learning, requiring no fine-tuning. Chain-1 extracts triplets from both the original caption and an LLM-generated paraphrase, preserving fine-grained predicates and increasing triplet density. Chain-2 aligns extracted entities and predicates with the target dataset's predefined class lexicons by asking the LLM to find semantically relevant lexemes, replacing rigid synonym/hypernym lookups. The resulting unlocalized triplets (344K vs. 154K with the conventional pipeline) are then grounded over image regions using existing methods (SGNLS or VS³) and used as pseudo-labels to train a conventional SGG model. The entire LLM-based triplet construction is a one-time preprocessing step, not part of the training loop.

## Results

On the Visual Genome SGDet task using COCO captions, VS³+LLM4SGG achieves R@100 of 10.43 and mR@100 of 8.18, substantially outperforming the best prior WSSGG baseline VS³ (R@100=8.01, mR@100=3.25). The mR@100 improvement is particularly dramatic: +4.93 absolute points over VS³, reflecting the method's ability to recover fine-grained predicates that were previously lost to semantic over-simplification. On GQA, VS³+LLM4SGG reaches mR@100 of 6.51 versus 1.81 for VS³ alone, a 3.6× relative gain, while also improving R@100 from 6.97 to 10.38. The method is also data-efficient, outperforming the full-data VS³ baseline with only 5K training images (7.8% of the 64K used by the baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM4SGG is applied as a plug-in to existing WSSGG grounding methods (SGNLS and VS³) and consistently improves them across all metrics. Compared to VS³ (CVPR'23), the strongest WSSGG baseline, LLM4SGG yields +2.31 R@50 and +4.23 mR@50 on COCO captions, with the mR@K gain being the most striking evidence that the method recovers fine-grained predicates lost to semantic over-simplification. Against SGNLS (ICCV'21), the improvement is smaller in absolute terms (+1.29 R@50, +1.57 mR@50) because SGNLS's grounding step fails to localize ~29% of the additional triplets that LLM4SGG produces. LLM4SGG also outperforms Li et al. (MM'22) and LSWS (CVPR'21) by wide margins on both R@K and mR@K. On GQA, where 44 out of 100 predicates have zero frequency under the conventional pipeline, VS³+LLM4SGG achieves mR@100 of 6.51 versus 1.81 for VS³ alone, confirming that the gains generalize to datasets with more complex and diverse predicates. The fully-supervised Motif baseline (R@100=36.36) remains far ahead, but LLM4SGG narrows the mR@100 gap from 4.32 points (VS³ vs. Motif) to 0.61 points (VS³+LLM4SGG vs. Motif) on COCO captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the WSSGG pipeline established by SGNLS and VS³, which focus on grounding unlocalized triplets onto image regions (Step 4), but deliberately shifts attention to the upstream triplet formation steps (parsing and class alignment) that prior work has treated as a fixed preprocessing stage. It departs from the conventional reliance on rule-based scene parsers (Scene Parser) and static knowledge bases (WordNet) by replacing both with a single LLM (ChatGPT) guided by Chain-of-Thought prompting and in-context few-shot examples, drawing on NLP techniques rather than vision-specific architectures. The method is designed as a plug-in enhancement: it produces improved unlocalized triplets that feed into existing grounding methods without modifying them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the bottleneck in WSSGG from grounding (Step 4) to triplet quality (Steps 2–3), showing that semantic over-simplification and low-density graphs caused by heuristic parsers and rigid KB lookups are the primary limiting factors. The substantial mR@K gains (e.g., 2.5× for VS³ on COCO) and the data-efficiency result (7.8% of training images outperforming the full-data baseline) suggest that pseudo-label quality, not quantity, is the key lever for weakly-supervised SGG. Key limitations include dependence on a proprietary black-box LLM (cost and reproducibility concerns) and the fact that the grounding step still discards a non-trivial fraction of triplets in some methods; open questions include whether smaller open-source LLMs can match ChatGPT's performance and whether LLMs can further improve the grounding step itself.
