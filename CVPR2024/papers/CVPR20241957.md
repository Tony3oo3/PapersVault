## Problem

Existing referring video segmentation methods reduce a natural language expression to a single sentence embedding, conflating static image-level cues with temporal motion cues. This causes image-level features to poorly comprehend motion expressions, while static cues can overshadow motion cues and disrupt temporal perception. Additionally, prior approaches treat all frames uniformly, failing to distinguish between fleeting actions (e.g., 'flying away') and long-term motions (e.g., 'walking from leftmost to rightmost').

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames (resized to 640×360) and a natural language expression encoded by RoBERTa. The sentence is decomposed via an external NLP tool into static cues (nouns, adjectives, prepositions) and motion cues (verbs, adverbs), each augmented with the full sentence embedding for context. Output consists of per-frame binary segmentation masks for the target object, selected by thresholding class scores from the Motion Decoder's video tokens multiplied with Mask2Former's mask features.

## Method

DsHmp decouples referring video segmentation into two complementary stages: static perception, where static cues are injected into learnable queries via cross-attention and fed to Mask2Former to segment candidate objects at image-level, and motion perception, where motion cues guide a Hierarchical Motion Perception (HMP) module operating on the resulting object tokens. HMP uses Hungarian matching to establish object trajectories, then iteratively applies motion-cue-weighted cross-attention and adjacent-token merging across N_h=3 stages, progressively expanding the temporal receptive field from individual frames to the full video. A Motion Decoder then identifies the target among candidates using motion cues. An object-wise contrastive loss with a memory bank of per-target feature centroids (prioritizing same-category negatives) is applied to the video tokens to sharpen discrimination between visually similar objects with distinct motion patterns.

## Results

On the motion-focused MeViS dataset, DsHmp achieves 46.4% J&F (43.0% J, 49.8% F), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art LMPM by a remarkable 9.2% J&F. On Ref-YouTube-VOS with Video-Swin-Base, DsHmp reaches 67.1% J&F, outperforming the prior best SOC by 1.1%. On A2D-Sentences and JHMDB-Sentences (Video-Swin-Base), DsHmp achieves 59.8% and 45.8% mAP respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on all five evaluated datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DsHmp achieves state-of-the-art on all five datasets, winning every head-to-head comparison. The most dramatic gain is on MeViS, where DsHmp (46.4% J&F) surpasses LMPM [8] (37.2%) by 9.2% and ReferFormer [56] (31.0%) by 15.4%, confirming the benefit of decoupled static/motion perception for motion-heavy expressions. On Ref-YouTube-VOS (Video-Swin-Base), DsHmp (67.1% J&F) edges out SOC [40] (66.0%) by 1.1% and TempCD [49] (65.8%) by 1.3%, with larger margins over earlier methods like HTML [15] (63.4%) and OnlineRefer [55] (62.9%). On Ref-DAVIS17, DsHmp (64.9% J&F) beats SOC [40] (64.2%) by 0.7%. On A2D-Sentences and JHMDB-Sentences, DsHmp outperforms the nearest competitor SgMg [43] by 1.3% and 0.8% mAP respectively, while also surpassing R2-VOS [29] and MTTR [2] by wider margins. The consistent but modest gains on Ref-YouTube-VOS and Ref-DAVIS17 (vs. the large MeViS gain) are attributed to those datasets containing more image-level descriptions that do not strictly require motion understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mask2Former's query-token architecture for image-level segmentation and on LMPM's (MeViS, ICCV 2023) object-token-based motion capture, which established the motion-expression video segmentation benchmark. It departs from prior referring video segmentation methods (ReferFormer, LMPM, SOC) that treat the language expression as a monolithic sentence embedding, instead explicitly decoupling static and motion cues via NLP-based word classification and routing them to separate perception pathways. The hierarchical motion perception module extends LMPM's uniform temporal treatment by introducing a multi-scale token-merging hierarchy, while the contrastive learning with a memory bank is borrowed from instance discrimination literature and adapted to the video-token setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of static and motion perception offers a clean conceptual framework that could be adopted by other multimodal video understanding tasks where language contains heterogeneous cue types. The 9.2% J&F gain on MeViS is substantial, but improvements on other datasets are more modest (1.2% on Ref-YouTube-VOS, 0.7% on Ref-DAVIS17), suggesting the approach's benefit is most pronounced when motion expressions are the primary discriminative signal. Open questions include whether the reliance on an external NLP tool for sentence parsing limits robustness to ambiguous or novel expressions, whether the hierarchical token-merging scales gracefully to very long videos, and whether the contrastive memory bank approach generalizes beyond the specific object categories in the training set.
