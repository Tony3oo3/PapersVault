## Problem

Scene coordinate regression (SCR) methods struggle to scale to large-scale scenes because of a fundamental dilemma: the network must be invariant to different observations of the same landmark while simultaneously discriminating visually similar but unrelated observations. Without ground truth 3D supervision, the network must implicitly triangulate points from reprojection constraints alone, and in large scenes, ambiguous patches from different locations corrupt this process. Prior SCR methods address this by training ensembles of sub-region networks or requiring 3D model supervision, both of which increase model size, training cost, and query latency.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single RGB query image and produces per-pixel 3D scene coordinate predictions, which are fed into a PnP+RANSAC solver to yield a 6-DOF camera pose. The network consumes two types of pre-computed encodings: per-patch local features from ACE's buffer and a 256-dimensional global feature from the R²Former retrieval model (pretrained on MSLS). Training requires only posed RGB images—no depth maps, meshes, or SfM point clouds—and the entire map is stored in a single ~9 MB network, compared to 16–205 MB for ensemble baselines.

## Method

GLACE integrates pre-trained global encodings (from R²Former) with ACE's local encodings to inject co-visibility information into the SCR pipeline. The key mechanism is a feature diffusion technique: Gaussian noise (σ equal to the retrieval margin m) is added to the global encoding and the result is projected back onto the unit sphere, which implicitly groups reprojection constraints across co-visible views while preventing the network from overfitting to trivial per-ray solutions. A novel position decoder replaces ACE's fixed center offset with a convex combination of K-Means cluster centers over training camera positions, making the output distribution multimodal and better suited to large-scale scenes. The entire system is trained with a dynamic tanh reprojection loss (no 3D supervision) using large buffers (64M–128M samples) and distributed multi-GPU training, and a single small MLP head maps the concatenated (diffused global + local) encoding to the final coordinate prediction.

## Results

On Cambridge Landmarks, GLACE achieves a median position error of 14 cm (0.3° rotation) with a 13 MB model, outperforming Poker (ACE×4, 16 MB) by 17% (17 cm) and single ACE (4 MB) by 44% (25 cm). On the integrated i19 indoor dataset, GLACE (9 MB) reaches 93.4% of frames within 5 cm/5° (SfM poses), approaching ACE×19 (78 MB) at 97.8% while using 8.7× less storage. On Aachen Day, GLACE (27 MB) achieves 20.8% at 0.5 m/5°, surpassing ACE×50 (205 MB) at 17.2% with a 7.6× smaller map and no depth supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLACE outperforms all SCR baselines on Cambridge Landmarks: it reduces median position error from 25 cm (ACE, 4 MB) and 19 cm (DSAC* Full, 28 MB) to 14 cm, and beats the ensemble Poker (ACE×4, 16 MB) at 17 cm by 3 cm. On the i19 integrated indoor dataset, GLACE (9 MB) achieves 93.4% (SfM) / 87.0% (D-SLAM) within 5 cm/5°, closely matching ACE×19 (78 MB) at 97.8% / 90.9% while using 8.7× less storage, and substantially outperforming ACE×4 (16 MB) at 42.0% / 36.5%. On Aachen Day, GLACE (27 MB) reaches 20.8% at 0.5 m/5°, exceeding ACE×50 (205 MB) at 17.2% with a 7.6× smaller map, though it trails ESAC×50 (1400 MB, depth-supervised) at 59.6%. On small-scale 7 Scenes, GLACE (9 MB) at 95.6% is slightly below ACE (4 MB) at 97.1%, indicating a minor trade-off in compact indoor settings. Overall, GLACE closes the gap with feature-matching methods (hLoc at 11 cm, pixLoc at 15 cm) on Cambridge Landmarks while maintaining a single-model, depth-free pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLACE builds directly on ACE (Brachmann et al., CVPR 2023), which established the SCR pipeline trained purely from reprojection loss without 3D model supervision. The key conceptual departure is identifying the 'receptive field dilemma' in large-scale SCR—where invariance to viewpoint changes conflicts with the need to discriminate visually similar but spatially distinct locations—and resolving it by injecting co-visibility information via pre-trained global encodings (R2Former) with a novel feature diffusion mechanism. The position decoder also departs from ACE's unimodal offset parameterization by predicting a convex combination of K-Means cluster centers, enabling multimodal output distributions suited to large scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SCR method to achieve state-of-the-art performance on large-scale outdoor scenes (Cambridge Landmarks, Aachen) with a single compact network (~9–27 MB) and no 3D model or depth supervision, eliminating the need for ensembles that multiply training time, memory, and query latency. The feature diffusion technique—adding calibrated Gaussian noise to global features to enforce a smoothness prior in feature space rather than image space—is a lightweight, scene-agnostic augmentation that could transfer to other tasks requiring soft grouping of observations. Open questions include whether the approach degrades when the pre-trained global encoder (R2Former) is mismatched to the target scene's visual domain, and whether the method extends to truly city-scale or multi-building environments beyond the tested landmarks.
