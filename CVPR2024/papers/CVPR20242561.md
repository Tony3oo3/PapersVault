## Problem

3D indoor object detection from multi-view images is ill-posed because, without explicit geometry, image features can be incorrectly projected to occluded or unobserved 3D locations. Existing end-to-end methods (e.g., ImVoxelNet) aggregate features along rays without occlusion awareness, while two-stage pipelines (reconstruct-then-detect) suffer from geometry noise and lack of end-to-end trainability, both leading to inaccurate detections in cluttered indoor scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242561/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed multi-view RGB images along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and outputs 3D axis-aligned bounding boxes (ScanNet, 18 categories) or oriented bounding boxes (ARKitScenes, 17 categories) with per-class confidence scores. Internally, the pipeline produces a sparse point cloud with 32-channel aggregated features at 1 cm³ voxel resolution as the intermediate representation fed to the detector, while the reconstruction stage operates at a coarser 4 cm³ voxel grid.

## Method

CN-RMA couples an Atlas-based multi-view stereo module (ResNet50-FPN 2D backbone + 3D CNN encoder-decoder) that predicts a rough Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) with a FCAF3D sparse-convolutional detector, bridged by a novel Ray Marching Aggregation (RMA) module. RMA samples 300 points per pixel along each camera ray, computes per-interval opacity from the TSDF via a NeuS-style sigmoid formulation, and accumulates transmittance in the NeRF manner to assign each sample point a weight reflecting its visibility. Image features are then voted into 3D space weighted by these opacity values, so occluded regions receive suppressed contributions, and only points near the reconstructed surface (opacity > θ_rma = 0.05) are retained. Training follows a three-stage schedule: pre-train the MVS module with reconstruction loss, freeze it and pre-train the detector with detection loss, then jointly fine-tune the full network with a weighted sum of both losses (λ = 0.5).

## Results

On ScanNet, CN-RMA achieves mAP@0.25 of 58.6 and mAP@0.5 of 36.8, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ImGeoNet (54.8 / 28.4) by 3.8 and 8.4 points respectively. On ARKitScenes, CN-RMA reaches mAP@0.25 of 67.6 and mAP@0.5 of 56.5, outperforming ImGeoNet (60.2 / 43.4) by 7.4 and 13.1 points. Compared to the strongest two-stage baseline (Atlas+FCAF3D), CN-RMA gains 3.2 / 3.0 on ScanNet and 16.3 / 15.9 on ARKitScenes, demonstrating the benefit of end-to-end occlusion-aware aggregation over decoupled reconstruction-then-detection pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CN-RMA outperforms all six baselines on both datasets. Against the previous SOTA ImGeoNet [28], it gains 3.8 mAP@0.25 and 8.4 mAP@0.5 on ScanNet, and 7.4 / 13.1 on ARKitScenes, with the larger ARKitScenes margin attributed to the lower-resolution depth maps (192×256 vs. 480×640) making occlusion handling more critical. The end-to-end method ImVoxelNet [25] trails by 11.9 / 13.4 on ScanNet and 40.3 / 52.2 on ARKitScenes, confirming that unprojection-based aggregation without geometry awareness is insufficient for cluttered indoor scenes. NeRF-Det [36], which incorporates NeRF but lacks explicit occlusion-aware voting, is surpassed by 5.1 / 9.4 on ScanNet and 28.1 / 34.6 on ARKitScenes. Among two-stage baselines, Atlas+FCAF3D is the strongest (55.4 / 33.8 on ScanNet), yet CN-RMA still leads by 3.2 / 3.0, while NeuralRecon+FCAF3D lags further behind (51.5 / 31.6), showing that reconstruction quality alone does not close the gap without end-to-end feature aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CN-RMA sits at the intersection of end-to-end 3D reconstruction (Atlas) and sparse-convolution 3D detection (FCAF3D), bridging them with a novel Ray Marching Aggregation module that borrows the TSDF-to-density mapping from NeuS and the transmittance accumulation from NeRF. It departs from two-stage pipelines (reconstruct-then-detect) by making the entire pipeline differentiable, and from prior end-to-end methods like ImVoxelNet and NeRF-Det by explicitly modeling occlusion during the 2D-to-3D feature voting step rather than treating all points along a ray equally. The conceptual lineage is: NeRF/NeuS provide the mathematical machinery for soft surface weighting, and this paper repurposes it not for rendering but for occlusion-aware feature aggregation in a detection pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that occlusion-aware feature aggregation is critical for indoor 3D detection, where object stacking and complex geometry make naive unprojection highly unreliable (27.5 mAP gap between VA and RMA). The RMA module is a general-purpose component that could transfer to other multi-view 3D scene understanding tasks beyond detection. Limitations include reliance on the quality of the rough TSDF (though RMA is more robust than hard depth-based selection), restriction to indoor scenes with known camera poses, and the need for a multi-step pre-training/fine-tuning schedule that increases training complexity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to outdoor or unposed settings, and whether better reconstruction (e.g., NeRF-based) would further close the gap.
