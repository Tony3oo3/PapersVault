## Problem

Existing AI agent systems either represent characters as 2D pixelated figures (Generative Agents) or constrain 3D agents to a small set of predefined actions without genuine socialization capability. A critical gap remains in simultaneously modeling sophisticated human body language for interaction and the long-term evolution of socio-psychological states (personality, trust, intimacy, relationships) that underpin realistic social behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241497/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes natural-language motion descriptions, retrieved motion priors from a curated database, and structured character psychological states (Big Five traits, motivations, central beliefs, trust, intimacy) to produce synchronized dual-person 3D body motion sequences. The brain-to-body interface is a dictionary-like structured text message with pre-set keys (<speech>, <motion>, <place>) that is interpretable by both the LLM and a regex parser. An extension accepts monocular human video as input, translating it into motion descriptions so virtual characters can respond to human players.

## Method

The framework couples two modules: SocioMind, an LLM-based 'digital brain' that uses IPIP psychological test items as few-shot exemplars to ground personality in behavior, applies social cognitive psychology principles for episodic memory reflection, and employs a topic-proposal negotiation mechanism between characters to drive long-term story progression; and MoMat-MoGen, a 'digital body' that first retrieves a high-quality motion pair via coarse-to-fine text-and-kinematics motion matching, then diversifies it with a diffusion model featuring a Dual-path Semantic-Modulated Attention (DSMA) module that cross-attends both characters' motion features, retrieved priors, and text embeddings to model interaction. During inference, a contact loss computed from joint distances in the retrieved prior is added to the denoising objective to enforce physically natural interaction patterns. An Active-Passive Mechanism assigns one character as the motion-pair generator while the other retains discretion to approve or reject the suggested passive behavior.

## Results

On the InterHuman test set, MoMat-MoGen achieves the best FID (5.674), MM Dist (3.790), and Diversity (8.021) among all compared methods, while also attaining the highest R Precision at Top-1 (0.449) and Top-2 (0.591). It ties with ReMoDiffuse on R Precision Top-3 (0.666) but surpasses it on FID by 0.69 and on Diversity by 0.065. In the integrated user study (Table 2), the full DLP pipeline (SocioMind + MoMat-MoGen) scores 7.17/9 on script quality and 6.77/9 on motion quality, outperforming the best alternative combination (GA + MoMat-MoGen) by 1.29 and 0.65 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMat-MoGen outperforms all single-person motion generation baselines (TEMOS, T2M, MDM, ComMDM, MotionDiffuse) by a wide margin on every metric, with FID improvements of 1.4–11.7 points over the closest single-person method (MotionDiffuse, FID 12.663). Against the two most relevant interactive baselines, MoMat-MoGen beats ReMoDiffuse on FID (5.674 vs. 6.366), MM Dist (3.790 vs. 3.802), Diversity (8.021 vs. 7.956), and R Precision Top-1/Top-2, while tying on Top-3; it also beats InterGen on FID (5.674 vs. 5.918), MM Dist (3.790 vs. 5.108), all R Precision scores, and Diversity (8.021 vs. 7.387). The sole metric where MoMat-MoGen trails is MultiModality: InterGen scores 2.141 vs. 1.295, and MDM reaches 2.355, suggesting the retrieval-based prior constrains output variety in the multimodality sense even as it boosts per-sample quality. In the social-intelligence evaluation (Figs. 4–5), SocioMind outperforms Generative Agents on both controllability (central belief, motivation, personality, relationship) and long-term consistency (plots and psychological states). In the integrated user study (Table 2), the full DLP pipeline (SocioMind + MoMat-MoGen) achieves the highest overall score (6.88/9), beating the GA + InterGen combination (4.93/9) by nearly 2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: LLM-based social agents (Generative Agents, Co-LLM-Agents) and text-driven motion synthesis (ReMoDiffuse, InterGen, MotionDiffuse). It departs from prior agent work by replacing 2D pixelated avatars or fixed action sets with fully articulated 3D bodies driven by a novel MoMat-MoGen paradigm that synergizes motion matching (for quality) and diffusion-based motion generation (for diversity), augmented by a Dual-path Semantic-Modulated Attention module and a contact loss for interaction fidelity. On the cognitive side, SocioMind extends the Generative Agents architecture by grounding personality in IPIP-derived persona instructions, adding a psychology-based reflection loop, and introducing a topic-proposal planning mechanism for long-term narrative coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLP is the first framework to jointly address social cognition and articulated 3D body motion in autonomous characters, opening a path toward more lifelike virtual companions, interactive entertainment, and social simulation. The IPIP-based persona instruction approach offers a practical, interpretable mechanism for controlling LLM agent behavior that could transfer to other agent domains. Key limitations include the restriction to two-character interactions, the reliance on prompt-based (rather than learned) psychological modeling, and the need for a curated motion database for the matching component. Open questions include scaling to multi-agent social groups, real-time inference, and whether the psychological reflection mechanism generalizes beyond the four tested relationship archetypes.
