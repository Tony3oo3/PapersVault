## Problem

Existing deep video compression (DVC) methods for machine vision tasks require training a dedicated encoder-decoder pair for each downstream task (detection, tracking, etc.), making deployment inflexible and costly. Unlike traditional codecs that adapt a single decoder to multiple objectives via encoder-side mode control, no prior DVC framework allows a single pre-trained decoder to serve multiple machine vision tasks through encoder-side adjustments alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240579/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence (short side downsampled to 512 for MOT17; 512×512 training / 576×576 evaluation for ImageNet VID) and frozen pre-trained DVC codec weights (FVC, DCVC, or TCM) as input. It produces a compressed bitstream optimized for a target machine vision task, a per-GoP binary structure vector (1 = standard P frame, 0 = P_m frame), and a per-element skip/no-skip mask over motion and residual/contextual feature maps. The decoder architecture and weights remain entirely unchanged, and the encoder can be switched back to its original mode to restore full human-viewing reconstruction quality.

## Method

The framework introduces two encoder-side control modules that operate without modifying pre-trained DVC weights. First, a Dynamic Vision Mode Prediction (DVMP) network (2 conv layers + 3 ResBlocks) consumes hyperprior mean/variance estimates and uses Gumbel-Softmax to produce a binary mask deciding whether each feature element is entropy-coded (1) or replaced by its hyperprior-predicted mean (0), generating low-bitrate P_m frames that retain object and motion information. Second, a GoP Selection network performs pre-analysis (object detection masks + RAFT optical flow) followed by a prediction head (conv blocks + adaptive average pooling + linear layers) that outputs a per-frame P/P_m assignment via Gumbel-Softmax during training and softmax-thresholding at inference, dynamically adapting the GoP structure to video content. Training is two-stage: DVMP is trained single-frame with L = R + λ_m·L_task, then the GoP Selection network is trained multi-frame with L = R̄ + λ_g·L̄_task while DVMP weights are frozen. All P and P_m frames reference the preceding I/P frame (never another P_m frame) to limit error propagation, and the entire pipeline achieves 25–45% BD-RATE savings across MOT, VOD, and VAR tasks with a single shared decoder.

## Results

On the MOT17 multi-object tracking task (DCVC as anchor), the proposed encoder control achieves average BD-RATE savings of −40.26% (Ours+DCVC), −33.11% (Ours+FVC), and −44.54% (Ours+TCM), substantially outperforming the unmodified baselines. For video object detection on ImageNet VID, the method saves approximately 21.76% and 20.42% bitrate versus FVC and TCM at equal mAP50. For video action recognition on UCF101, it achieves ~21.81% and 26.03% bitrate savings for TCM and FVC respectively, using pre-trained mode prediction networks without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is evaluated against three pre-trained deep video codecs (DCVC, FVC, TCM) and the traditional HEVC standard. On MOT17, the controlled encoders dramatically outperform all baselines: Ours+DCVC saves 40.26% average BD-RATE versus DCVC's 0% (anchor), while Ours+TCM achieves 44.54% savings, surpassing even TCM's own 30.97% improvement over DCVC. FVC alone provides only 7.44% average savings, whereas Ours+FVC reaches 33.11%. HEVC, the strongest traditional baseline, achieves 24.78% average savings but notably degrades MOTP (+15.80%), indicating poor preservation of tracking precision. On ImageNet VID (VOD), the method saves ~21.76% and ~20.42% bitrate versus FVC and TCM at equal mAP50. On UCF101 (VAR), it saves ~21.81% and ~26.03% for TCM and FVC respectively, confirming generality across tasks without retraining the downstream models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three mainstream deep video compression codecs—FVC (residual), DCVC (conditional), and TCM (conditional)—and departs from the prevailing 'one-to-one codec' paradigm in coding-for-machine research, where each downstream task requires a dedicated encoder-decoder pair (as in DeepSVC, Tian et al., and Lin et al.). Instead, it draws inspiration from traditional codec mode prediction (e.g., PSNR vs. SSIM optimization in HEVC) and Hu et al.'s slimmable mode prediction to introduce an encoder-side controller that adapts a fixed pre-trained DVC decoder to multiple machine vision tasks. The GoP selection component extends the hand-crafted DivGoP structure by learning to predict frame types per GoP, motivated by a DFS search showing that optimal GoP structures vary across videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in deployment simplicity: a single pre-trained decoder serves detection, tracking, and action recognition simultaneously, eliminating the need for task-specific codec pipelines. The finding that naively fine-tuning a DVC for a downstream task actually increases bitrate (due to inter-frame reference degradation) provides a useful negative result that validates the encoder-control philosophy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to generative or perceptual DVC codecs, how it performs on very long or highly dynamic sequences, and whether the GoP selection can be further reduced in latency for real-time encoding at scale.
