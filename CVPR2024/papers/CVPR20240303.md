## Problem

Developing classifiers for subjective visual concepts (e.g., 'gourmet tuna,' 'unsafe content') traditionally requires users to manually label ~2,000 images over 30+ minutes, and crowd workers produce inconsistent labels that misalign with the user's concept definition. Existing Agile Modeling frameworks reduce iteration time but still demand substantial repetitive human labeling, creating a barrier to rapid deployment of user-specific vision models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The user provides a concept name, an optional natural-language description, and a small 100-image validation set; the system then autonomously mines candidate images from LAION using LLM-generated and mutated search queries. During annotation, the pipeline consumes RGB images and outputs a binary positive/negative label accompanied by a chain-of-thought rationale. The final deliverable is a lightweight 3-layer MLP (128-128-128) over frozen CLIP or ALIGN image embeddings, deployable in cost-sensitive settings without the heavy LLM/VLM inference stack.

## Method

Modeling Collaborator orchestrates three AI components—PaLM 2 (LLM), PaLI-X VQA, and PaLI-X MMIT captioning—into an automated annotator that replaces human labeling. The LLM first decomposes the subjective concept into atomic objective attributes (e.g., 'contains tuna,' 'is a sandwich'), generates targeted VQA questions, and then performs chain-of-thought reasoning over the VQA answers and image caption to emit a final label with rationale. A shallow MLP is trained on CLIP/ALIGN features using these LLM-generated labels as a teacher (student-teacher distillation), followed by multiple rounds of active learning with stratified sampling over LAION to mine hard positives and negatives. Data mining is also LLM-driven: the model generates diverse positive and hard-negative search queries with iterative mutations to ensure broad visual-mode coverage, reducing reliance on user-crafted queries.

## Results

On the Agile Modeling dataset (14 concepts), Modeling Collaborator achieves an overall F1 of 0.74, outperforming PaLI-X (0.72), CLIP (0.62), and CuPL (0.62). Gains are most pronounced on hard/subjective concepts where F1 reaches 0.68 vs. 0.65 for PaLI-X, and on the Hateful Memes dataset where F1 jumps to 0.66 vs. 0.51 for PaLI-X (+15%). In the distilled-model evaluation (Table 2), the fully automated annotator achieves 0.66 auPR, matching crowd-rater quality (0.65) while requiring only 100 user annotations instead of ~2,000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PaLI-X (the strongest zero-shot VQA baseline), Modeling Collaborator wins on F1 across all concept groups: +2% overall, +3% on hard concepts, and a substantial +15% on Hateful Memes (0.66 vs. 0.51), though PaLI-X retains a precision advantage on some easy concepts. Against CLIP and CuPL (contrastive zero-shot methods), the gains are larger—F1 improves from 0.62 to 0.74 overall and from 0.55/0.56 to 0.68 on hard concepts—because contrastive models suffer from very low precision (0.47–0.56) while the LLM-based annotator achieves 0.78 precision. In the distilled-model comparison (Table 2), the fully automated annotator (0.66 auPR) outperforms crowd raters (0.65) and PaLI-X (0.63) while staying within 2% of the user's own annotations (0.68), and it does so with only 100 user-labeled images versus ~2,000 in traditional Agile Modeling. On Hateful Memes specifically (Table 3), the teacher model (F1 0.66) beats CLIP (0.57), CuPL (0.51), and PaLI-X (0.51), and even the lightweight student model (F1 0.56) surpasses a CLIP+MLP trained on 8.5K human labels (F1 0.48).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Agile Modeling [51], which formalized user-in-the-loop classifier development for subjective visual concepts but required users to manually label ~2,000 images. The key departure is replacing human annotation with an LLM-orchestrated pipeline (PaLM 2 + PaLI-X VQA + chain-of-thought) that decomposes subjective concepts into atomic visual questions, drawing on the broader tool-use LLM paradigm (Toolformer, AVIS, ViperGPT). For deployment, it adopts the student-teacher distillation and active learning loop from Agile Modeling, training a lightweight MLP on CLIP/ALIGN features labeled by the automated annotator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing human annotation from ~2,000 images to ~100 plus a few natural-language interactions eliminates the primary bottleneck in building classifiers for subjective concepts (content moderation, aesthetic assessment, wildlife conservation). However, the system remains constrained by the quality of its underlying VLMs (PaLI-X), can degrade on simple concepts where a direct VQA prompt suffices, and CLIP features limit discrimination for certain visual modes (e.g., physical vs. depicted stop signs). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-class or fine-grained subjective tasks, how LLM biases propagate into the distilled model, and whether the 100-image validation set requirement can be further reduced.
