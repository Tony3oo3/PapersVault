## Problem

Existing point cloud upsampling methods predict dense coordinates indirectly through sparse feature extraction and dedicated upsampling modules, which biases results toward the input sparse cloud, disrupts intrinsic point-wise mappings, and introduces noise sensitivity via CD loss. This paper reframes point cloud upsampling as a conditional generation problem, directly modeling the gradient of the dense point cloud data distribution to capture ground-truth geometric details without an explicit upsampling module.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241353/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse point cloud (2048 points in experiments) as the conditioning signal and generates a dense point cloud at an arbitrary upsampling rate R (e.g., 2048×R output points). During training, patches of 256 points are upsampled to 1024-point ground truth; during inference, the rate factor R is specified as a condition, enabling single-model generation across scales (2×–128×). Midpoint interpolation of the sparse cloud produces guidance points that are added to the denoising input at each reverse step.

## Method

PUDM is a conditional DDPM with a dual-branch architecture: a Conditional Network (C-Net, PointNet++ U-Net with self-attention) encodes the sparse cloud and is supervised to reconstruct it (enforcing one-to-one point-wise mapping), while a Noise Network (N-Net, same architecture) predicts the diffusion noise with an inherent one-to-one mapping between noise and dense cloud. A bidirectional Transfer Module at the U-Net bottleneck exchanges features between the two branches via cross-attention. A parameterized rate factor r is injected as an additional condition (analogous to class labels) to enable high-quality arbitrary-scale generation from a single training run. Training minimizes the standard DDPM noise-prediction MSE plus a reconstruction MSE on the C-Net (dual mapping loss), decoupling the objective from Chamfer Distance and thereby improving noise robustness.

## Results

On PUGAN at 4× upsampling, PUDM achieves CD=0.131 and HD=1.220, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Grad-PU (CD=0.245, HD=2.369) by 46% and 48% respectively. At 16× upsampling, PUDM further reduces CD to 0.082 and HD to 1.120, maintaining a clear margin over Grad-PU (CD=0.108, HD=2.352). On the more challenging PU1K benchmark at 4×, PUDM reports CD=0.217 and HD=2.164 versus Grad-PU's CD=0.404 and HD=3.732. PUDM also demonstrates superior noise robustness, e.g., at τ=0.05 Gaussian noise it achieves CD=0.618 vs. Grad-PU's 0.978.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUDM decisively outperforms all eight baselines on both CD and HD across PUGAN (4× and 16×) and PU1K (4×). The closest competitor, Grad-PU [9], is surpassed by a wide margin: at 4× on PUGAN, PUDM reduces CD by 46% (0.131 vs. 0.245) and HD by 48% (1.220 vs. 2.369); at 16×, the HD gap widens to 52% (1.120 vs. 2.352). The only metric where PUDM does not lead is P2F at 4× on PUGAN, where Grad-PU edges ahead by 0.019 (1.893 vs. 1.912), a difference the authors attribute to point-surface asymmetry. On PU1K, PUDM nearly halves CD (0.217 vs. 0.404) and HD (2.164 vs. 3.732) relative to Grad-PU while matching its P2F (1.477 vs. 1.474). Under Gaussian noise (τ=0.05), PUDM maintains CD=0.618 while Grad-PU degrades to 0.978, and at τ=0.1 the gap persists (0.853 vs. 1.118), confirming the noise-robustness advantage from decoupling CD loss during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm in point cloud upsampling (PU-Net, MPU, PUGAN, Dis-PU, PU-GCN, PU-EVA, NePS, Grad-PU), which all rely on explicit upsampling modules to predict dense coordinates from sparse features. Instead, PUDM reframes PCU as a conditional generation problem built on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020), where the sparse point cloud serves as a condition and the model learns the score function of the dense point cloud distribution. The dual mapping paradigm and rate prior are novel architectural additions that address two specific limitations of applying DDPM to PCU: weak 3D feature perception in the conditional branch and fixed-scale training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PUDM provides the first systematic demonstration that conditional diffusion models can outperform purpose-built upsampling architectures on PCU, with particularly strong noise robustness explained by the score-matching objective's natural alignment with Gaussian perturbations. The rate prior mechanism for arbitrary-scale generation without retraining is a practical contribution that avoids the multi-task training cost of prior methods. Open questions include whether the T=1000-step iterative inference can be accelerated (e.g., via DDIM or consistency distillation), how the approach scales to scene-level point clouds with millions of points, and whether the uniform-noise performance gap can be closed by modifying the noise schedule.
