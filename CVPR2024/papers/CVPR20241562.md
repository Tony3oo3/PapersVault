## Problem

Existing concept factorization and NMF methods preserve local geometric structure using only first-order neighbors, which is insufficient to capture the full latent structure of data. High-order neighbor relationships, which reveal more complex and complementary patterns, have been rarely exploited in the CF/NMF community. Additionally, standard CF factor matrices lack an explicit group structure that directly encodes cluster assignments.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{d×n} (which may contain mixed signs, unlike standard NMF), a target number of clusters c, and a neighbor order A (set to 3 in experiments). Neighbor relationships are encoded via a binary KNN kernel (K=5) converted into a Markov transition probability matrix, which is then raised to successive powers and symmetrized to form the fine-grained probability matrix P_A. The output consists of a score matrix W, a coefficient matrix V (constrained to be a relaxed indicator via V^T V = I_c), and cluster assignments obtained by applying K-means to V.

## Method

Figer-CF constructs a fine-grained neighbor graph by fusing cross-order Markov transition probability matrices P^a (a=1..A), symmetrized as (P^a + (P^a)^T)/2, to capture comprehensive multi-step random-walk neighbor relationships. It then formulates clustering as bipartite graph partitioning: a symmetric nonnegative matrix S = [[0, (WV^T)^T], [WV^T, 0]] is built from the factor product, and its graph Laplacian L is penalized via Tr(F^T L F) to enforce exactly c connected components (by Ky-Fan's theorem). The objective combines a kernelized reconstruction term (using P_A in place of X^T X), an L2 regularizer on W, and the Laplacian term, solved by alternating updates: F via eigendecomposition of L, and W and V via multiplicative update rules derived from auxiliary functions. Theoretical convergence is guaranteed by showing monotonic decrease of the objective under each update, and the method naturally extends to mixed-sign data since P_A is nonnegative.

## Results

Figer-CF achieves the best overall clustering accuracy on Jaffe (97.7% ACC, 97.0% NMI, 97.7% PUR) and PIX (94.0% ACC, 95.0% NMI, 94.0% PUR), outperforming the strongest baselines EWNMF (95.3% ACC on Jaffe) and GLS-MKNMF (82.0% ACC on PIX) by 2.4 and 12.0 percentage points in ACC, respectively. On the more challenging Semeion dataset, Figer-CF attains the highest NMI (62.6%) and PUR (68.2%), though OPMC edges it slightly in ACC (65.4% vs. 63.5%). Across all 60 evaluation cases (4 datasets × multiple class counts × 3 metrics), Figer-CF ranks first in 47 cases, second in 8, and third in 5, making it the only method to appear in the top three in every single case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Figer-CF clearly dominates on Jaffe, achieving 97.7% overall ACC versus 95.3% for EWNMF (the strongest baseline) and 90.6% for WNMF, with a 2.4-point margin over the runner-up. On PIX, the advantage is even more pronounced: Figer-CF reaches 94.0% ACC compared to 82.0% for GLS-MKNMF and 81.0% for EWNMF, a gap of over 12 points. On Semeion, Figer-CF is not the absolute best in ACC (OPMC scores 65.4% vs. Figer-CF's 63.5%), but it leads in NMI (62.6% vs. OPMC's 54.6%) and PUR (68.2% vs. 65.5%), indicating more consistent cluster structure. On COIL20, the paper reports Figer-CF outperforms both EWNMF and GLS-MKNMF, which are the strongest baselines on that dataset. Notably, Figer-CF is the only method to rank in the top three across all 60 evaluation cases (4 datasets × multiple class splits × 3 metrics), while baselines like OPMC and CNMF show large performance drops on larger subsets (e.g., OPMC drops from 100% to 62% ACC on PIX as class count increases).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Factorization (CF) / Convex NMF framework by introducing two key ingredients from different research threads: (1) a fine-grained cross-order neighbor graph built from Markov transition probability matrices (borrowed from graph clustering and random-walk literature), and (2) a bipartite graph partitioning formulation that enforces explicit cluster structure on the coefficient matrix via a graph Laplacian. Prior CF methods (CNMF, Kernel-NMF, and their subspace-clustering-connected variants) relied exclusively on first-order neighbor information or global kernel similarity; this work is the first to fuse multi-step random-walk probabilities into the CF objective while simultaneously imposing a bipartite partitioning constraint. The bipartite Laplacian term also departs from the standard Frobenius-norm regularization on WV^T used in recent CF-subspace clustering hybrids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-order neighbor relationships (up to 3-step random walks) provide genuinely complementary information beyond first-order neighbors, yielding consistent 2–7% improvements in clustering accuracy across all tested configurations. The bipartite partitioning gives the coefficient matrix a clear block-diagonal group structure, making the factorization more interpretable for clustering. However, all experiments are confined to four small-scale digit/face benchmarks (≤2,500 samples), leaving open whether the approach scales to large or high-dimensional data, whether the fixed A=3 heuristic generalizes, and how the method compares to modern deep or contrastive clustering approaches. The convergence guarantee, while elegant, follows standard auxiliary-function techniques and does not address the non-convexity of the joint W–V subproblem.
