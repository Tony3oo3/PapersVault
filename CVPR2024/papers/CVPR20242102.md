## Problem

Existing SLAM systems that integrate neural rendering for photorealistic mapping (e.g., Nice-SLAM, ESLAM, Co-SLAM) rely entirely on implicit representations decoded by MLPs or hash grids, making them computationally expensive, unable to run on portable or embedded devices, and dependent on dense depth supervision for convergence. This fundamentally contradicts SLAM's original goal of real-time operation on resource-constrained platforms for autonomous exploration.

## Input / Output

The system consumes monocular, stereo, or RGB-D image sequences at resolutions up to 1200×680 and produces three outputs: 6-DoF camera poses (tracked at ~42 FPS on desktop), a persistent hyper primitives map (point clouds with ORB descriptors, 3D Gaussian parameters, and 4th-order spherical harmonic coefficients), and photorealistic novel view renderings at up to ~1000 FPS on desktop or ~100 FPS on Jetson AGX Orin. Unlike prior implicit-representation SLAMs, the system does not require a pre-defined bounding volume for ray normalization and operates without external depth estimators in monocular mode.

## Method

Photo-SLAM maintains a hybrid 'hyper primitives map' where each primitive is a 3D Gaussian (position, rotation, scaling, density) augmented with an ORB feature descriptor and spherical harmonic color coefficients, enabling a factor-graph-based LM solver to handle localization via explicit 2D-3D correspondences while 3D Gaussian splatting handles photometric rendering. Two key innovations address the challenge of online incremental mapping without dense depth: (1) geometry-based densification, which actively creates additional hyper primitives from inactive 2D feature points (using depth from RGB-D, stereo matching, or nearest-neighbor interpolation for monocular), and (2) Gaussian-Pyramid-based (GP) learning, a progressive training scheme where optimization starts at a coarsely downsampled pyramid level and iteratively refines to full resolution while simultaneously densifying primitives, ensuring thorough optimization before fine details are introduced. The photometric loss combines L1 and SSIM terms, optimized with fixed-rate SGD, and the entire pipeline runs in parallel threads (localization, geometry mapping, photorealistic mapping, loop closure) fully implemented in C++ and CUDA.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, Photo-SLAM achieves 33.30 PSNR / 0.926 SSIM / 0.078 LPIPS in monocular mode and 34.96 PSNR / 0.942 SSIM / 0.059 LPIPS in RGB-D mode, while rendering at 911 and 1084 FPS respectively with only 5–6 GB GPU memory. Localization accuracy (ATE RMSE) is competitive: 1.09 cm (mono) and 0.60 cm (RGB-D), comparable to DROID-SLAM (0.73 / 0.63 cm) and ESLAM (0.57 cm). On the TUM RGB-D dataset, Photo-SLAM reaches 20.97–22.74 PSNR across three sequences, and on EuRoC stereo it achieves 13.95–17.07 PSNR. The system runs at real-time speed (~18–20 FPS tracking, ~95–116 FPS rendering) even on a Jetson AGX Orin embedded platform.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Photo-SLAM dominates all photorealistic-mapping baselines on rendering quality and speed. In monocular mode, it achieves 33.30 PSNR versus 16.31 (Nice-SLAM*), 23.25 (Orbeez-SLAM), and 21.17 (Go-SLAM), while rendering at 911 FPS compared to <1.1 FPS for all three. In RGB-D mode, Photo-SLAM (34.96 PSNR, 1084 FPS) outperforms ESLAM (30.59 PSNR, 2.6 FPS), Co-SLAM (30.25 PSNR, 3.7 FPS), Nice-SLAM (26.16 PSNR, 0.6 FPS), Go-SLAM (24.16 PSNR, 0.4 FPS), and BundleFusion (23.84 PSNR, no rendering FPS reported). The closest competitor is Point-SLAM (34.63 PSNR), but it requires >2 hours of offline processing and 24 GB GPU memory, whereas Photo-SLAM completes in <2 minutes with 5 GB. For localization, Photo-SLAM (0.60 cm RMSE) is on par with ESLAM (0.57 cm) and Go-SLAM (0.57 cm) in RGB-D, and slightly behind DROID-SLAM (0.63 cm) but far ahead of ORB-SLAM3 (1.83 cm). On the EuRoC stereo benchmark, Photo-SLAM matches ORB-SLAM3's localization accuracy (4.0–4.4 cm vs 4.4–4.5 cm on MH sequences) while being the first system to provide online photorealistic rendering with stereo input.

## Relation to Prior Work

Photo-SLAM sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: classical feature-based SLAM (ORB-SLAM3's factor-graph localization with LM solver) and explicit radiance field rendering (3D Gaussian Splatting). It departs from the dominant neural-SLAM paradigm (Nice-SLAM, ESLAM, Co-SLAM, Orbeez-SLAM) that relies on implicit representations decoded by MLPs and optimized via ray sampling, which the authors argue is too resource-hungry for portable deployment. The key conceptual move is replacing ray-sampled implicit features with a 'hyper primitives' map—3D Gaussians augmented with ORB descriptors—so that localization uses explicit geometric correspondences while photometric optimization backpropagates through a tile-based splatting renderer. The Gaussian-Pyramid-based progressive training and geometry-based densification are novel contributions that address the specific challenge of incremental (online) mapping without dense depth supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Photo-SLAM demonstrates real-time photorealistic mapping (~100 FPS rendering) on an embedded Jetson AGX Orin, a milestone for deploying immersive SLAM on actual robots rather than desktop workstations. The ablation revealing that naive densification without progressive training actively degrades quality (more Gaussians become 'encumbrances' with inaccurate positions) is a useful cautionary finding for the 3DGS community. Open questions include scalability to very large or unbounded outdoor scenes (the outdoor stereo results in Fig. 9 show noticeably lower fidelity), long-term drift in extended sessions, and whether the 3-level Gaussian pyramid generalizes to scenes with vastly different texture complexity. The system also does not produce a mesh, limiting downstream applications that require geometric surfaces.
