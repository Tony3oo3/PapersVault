## Problem

Large-scale HR land-cover mapping is hindered by the scarcity of accurate pixel-level labels over wide-span areas, while existing LR historical labels suffer from severe geospatial mismatch (e.g., a single 30 m label pixel must guide 900 target 1 m pixels). Prior approaches either still depend on partial HR labels or require multi-stage, non-end-to-end pipelines with human interventions, and their feature extractors cannot simultaneously preserve local HR details and model global contexts across diverse landforms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241714/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes HR remote-sensing images (1 m or 0.25–0.5 m/pixel, 3–4 spectral bands) together with LR historical land-cover labels (10 m or 30 m/pixel) as the sole training supervision, with resolution gaps up to 120×. It outputs a pixel-wise HR land-cover segmentation map at the same spatial resolution as the input image, requiring no HR annotations at any stage of training or inference.

## Method

Paraformer parallelly combines a downsampling-free CNN branch (five serial resolution-preserving blocks with parallel 1×1, 3×3, and 5×5 convolutions at stride 1, channel counts inversely proportional to kernel size) with a 12-layer Transformer branch that encodes downsampled CNN features as dense patches for global context and upsamples back to HR resolution with skip connections. A Pseudo-Label-Assisted Training (PLAT) module iteratively intersects the CNN branch's primal prediction with the LR label to produce mask labels, setting inconsistent pixels to void so they are excluded from the loss. The total objective combines a standard cross-entropy loss between the CNN branch prediction and the LR label with a Mask-CrossEntropy loss between the final fused prediction and the refined mask labels, enabling end-to-end, HR-label-free optimization.

## Results

On the Chesapeake Bay dataset (30× resolution gap, 16 classes), Paraformer achieves a mean mIoU of 64.65% across six states, outperforming the closest competitor L2HNet (63.08%) by 1.57 percentage points. On the Poland dataset, Paraformer leads all baselines across four LR label sources: 56.57% (FROM_GLC10, 40× gap), 55.19% (ESA_GLC10), 55.07% (Esri_GLC10), and 49.39% (GLC_FCS30, 120× gap), with margins of 3.06–6.42% over L2HNet and 10–23% over CNN-Transformer hybrids. The model uses 109.4M parameters and 141.3G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Paraformer outperforms all 14 baselines on average mIoU across both datasets. On Chesapeake Bay, it leads L2HNet (the strongest weakly-supervised competitor) by 1.57% (64.65% vs. 63.08%), though L2HNet edges ahead in Virginia (69.39% vs. 68.01%) and West Virginia (55.43% vs. 52.62%). On the Poland dataset, Paraformer's advantage widens substantially: it beats L2HNet by 6.42% (FROM_GLC10), 3.06% (ESA_GLC10), 4.29% (Esri_GLC10), and 5.77% (GLC_FCS30 at 120× gap). CNN-Transformer hybrids (TransUNet, ConViT, MobileViT, DC-Swin) trail Paraformer by 8–18% on Poland, indicating that simply combining local and global features without resolution preservation or label refinement is insufficient for extreme resolution mismatches. Pure CNN methods (UNet, HRNet, LinkNet) and pixel-to-pixel RF perform worst, with mIoU dropping below 55% on Chesapeake Bay and below 50% on Poland, confirming that neither local-only features nor shallow architectures can bridge the geospatial gap between LR labels and HR images.

## Relation to Prior Work

Paraformer extends the authors' own L2HNet [28] by adding a parallel Transformer branch to the existing resolution-preserving CNN backbone, addressing L2HNet's limitation in modeling global contexts across diverse landforms. It departs from prior weakly supervised land-cover mapping pipelines (WESUP, the 2021 DFC winners) that required multi-stage training or human intervention by introducing the PLAT module, which uses a simple intersection of primal CNN predictions with LR labels to iteratively refine supervision in an end-to-end fashion. The paper also positions itself against general CNN-Transformer hybrids (TransUNet, ConViT, CoAtNet) that, while capturing both local and global features, do not specifically address the geospatial resolution mismatch inherent in using LR labels for HR segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: Paraformer provides a single, end-to-end trainable framework for updating large-scale HR land-cover maps from readily available LR products (NLCD, ESA WorldCover, etc.) without any HR annotations, directly addressing a major bottleneck in periodic land-cover monitoring. The PLAT module's simplicity (a pixel-wise intersection as a mask) is elegant and easy to adopt, though its effectiveness depends on the CNN branch producing reasonably accurate primal predictions early in training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to temporal change detection, whether the intersection-based mask discards too much supervisory signal in highly mismatched regions, and how performance scales to truly continental or global extents beyond the two tested areas.
