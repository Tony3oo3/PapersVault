## Problem

Small CLIP models are essential for resource-constrained applications, yet prior distillation approaches like TinyCLIP require the teacher and student to share the same architecture style (e.g., ViT-to-ViT or ResNet-to-ResNet), limiting practical applicability. This paper asks: what distillation strategies effectively transfer knowledge from a large teacher CLIP to a small student CLIP across arbitrary architecture pairs, and why do some strategies outperform others?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241634/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes (image, text) pairs from CC3M+12M (or Laion-400M for the teacher), with images at 224×224 and text as natural language captions. The student model produces L2-normalized image and text feature embeddings that are aligned to the teacher's embeddings via the chosen distillation loss; when embedding dimensions differ, a linear projection head maps student features to the teacher's dimension. At inference, the distilled model outputs zero-shot classification scores over ImageNet prompt templates and ranked image-text similarity scores for cross-modal retrieval on MSCOCO and Flickr.

## Method

The paper proposes and systematically compares six distillation losses: Contrastive Relational Distillation (KL divergence between teacher and student contrastive distributions), Feature Distillation (MSE on L2-normalized embeddings), Masked Feature Distillation (MSE with MAE-style patch masking on the student input), Gradient Distillation (MSE on gradients of the CLIP loss w.r.t. embeddings), Interactive Contrastive Learning (student embedding as anchor contrasting teacher embeddings in an InfoNCE loss), and Augmented Feature Distillation (a linear fusion encoder concatenating student and teacher embeddings fed into the CLIP loss). The key empirical finding is that the simplest strategy—plain MSE feature mimicry—achieves the best individual performance, and the unified combination FD+ICL+CRD is the best overall. The authors explain this by showing that distillation gains correlate directly with the cosine/CKA similarity between teacher and student features: methods that more directly maximize this similarity (FD, ICL) outperform those that only indirectly encourage it (CRD, GD, AFD). Crucially, all losses operate solely on final output embeddings, making the approach architecture-agnostic and applicable to any ViT-to-CNN or ViT-to-ViT teacher-student pair.

## Results

Using ViT-L/14 pretrained on Laion-400M as teacher, CLIP-KD achieves 57.5% zero-shot top-1 ImageNet accuracy for ViT-B/16 (baseline 37.0%) and 55.4% for ResNet-50 (baseline 35.3%), representing 20.5% and 20.1% absolute gains over the original CLIP without distillation. On CC3M+12M-trained models, the unified FD+ICL+CRD method improves ViT-T/16 by 4.3% ImageNet top-1 accuracy (30.6%→34.9%) and yields 2.0%–3.9% R@1 gains on CC3M retrieval. Cross-modal retrieval on MSCOCO and Flickr shows consistent improvements of 2.1%–4.3% and 4.9%–8.8% R@1 respectively across five student architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is TinyCLIP [Wu et al., ICCV 2023], which uses affinity mimicking and weight inheritance for CLIP distillation. CLIP-KD outperforms TinyCLIP by 1.8% ImageNet top-1 accuracy and 1.7% average cross-modal retrieval R@1 across all comparable settings. Specifically, for ViT-T/16 distilled from ViT-B/16 (Laion-400M), CLIP-KD reaches 42.6% ImageNet accuracy versus TinyCLIP's 40.8%; for ViT-B/16 distilled from ViT-L/14, CLIP-KD achieves 57.5% versus TinyCLIP's 55.4%. A critical practical advantage is that CLIP-KD supports arbitrary teacher-student architecture pairs (e.g., ViT-L/14 teacher → ResNet-50 student, yielding 55.4% ImageNet accuracy), whereas TinyCLIP requires the same architecture style and cannot be applied to cross-architecture distillation. The paper notes that TinyCLIP's weight inheritance mechanism is the limiting factor, restricting its applicability to same-style ViT-to-ViT or ResNet-to-ResNet pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical knowledge distillation framework (Hinton et al., 2015) to the CLIP contrastive language-image pre-training setting, drawing on the InfoNCE-based contrastive loss as the base training objective. It departs from the closest prior work, TinyCLIP (Wu et al., 2023), which relies on weight inheritance requiring the teacher and student to share the same architecture style (e.g., ViT-to-ViT or ResNet-to-ResNet); CLIP-KD instead operates purely on final output embeddings, enabling arbitrary cross-architecture distillation (e.g., ViT teacher to CNN student). The masked feature distillation variant borrows the patch-masking algorithm from MAE (He et al., 2022), and the interactive contrastive learning component is grounded in mutual information maximization between teacher and student encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, architecture-agnostic distillation recipe for CLIP models, offering practitioners clear guidance (FD+ICL+CRD as the default unified method) for compressing CLIP in resource-constrained deployments. A notable open question is the counter-intuitive finding that a larger teacher (ViT-L/14, 72.8% ImageNet) underperforms a smaller one (ViT-B/16, 67.1%) when distilling ViT-T/16, suggesting an unresolved capacity-gap problem between very large teachers and small students. The 20%+ zero-shot ImageNet gains achievable by distilling from Laion-400M-pretrained teachers highlight a practical pathway to transfer large-scale pre-training knowledge without retraining on the full dataset, though the method's ceiling and behavior at even larger teacher scales remain unexplored.
