## Problem

Current 3D Human Mesh Reconstruction (HMR) systems are evaluated primarily on 3D metrics (mm-level joint/vertex errors) and do not reflect 2D reprojection accuracy, which is critical for AR applications like virtual try-on where small 3D errors become glaringly visible pixel misalignments. DensePose provides pixel-accurate 2D association but no 3D mesh, while HMR systems lack pixel-level grounding for vertex positions. This paper bridges the gap by unifying both tasks into a single system that achieves competitive DensePose accuracy while delivering an accurate 3D mesh.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (typically a tight crop around the person, as in selfie-style AR use cases) and produces a low-poly 3D body mesh with per-vertex XYZ coordinates, per-vertex visibility scores, and DensePose UV coordinates for every body pixel. Depth is predicted relative to the sternum root (via a separate RootNet) in pixel units under a weak perspective camera model, and the low-poly mesh can optionally be upsampled to high-poly via a trained MLP. The system is designed for real-time mobile inference, with the smallest variant (MeshPoseXS, 21M params) running at 124 FPS.

## Method

MeshPose operates in two stages within a single end-to-end CNN. First, a novel VertexPose layer produces an H×W×V heatmap tensor where each vertex is localized via argsoftmax, and per-pixel UV is computed by barycentric interpolation over the highest-scoring triangle; this is trained with two new weakly-supervised losses (Barycentric Cross-Entropy and UV Consistency) that exploit DensePose UV annotations without requiring direct vertex ground-truth. Second, a regression branch performs 1D integral regression to estimate per-vertex depth relative to the sternum, along with a visibility score that differentiably fuses the VertexPose 2D position with a fallback regressed position for occluded or out-of-crop vertices. Visibility is supervised both from partial DensePose annotations and from differentiable rendering against segmentation masks. The entire pipeline uses only standard CNN layers (no custom ops), making it deployable on mobile NPUs/GPUs.

## Results

On DensePose-COCO, MeshPose(HRNet32) achieves the best DensePose AP of 47.87 and AR of 57.62, outperforming the next-best HMR method (CLIFF at 30.99 AP) by over 50%. On 3DPW, MeshPose(HRNet32) reports MPJPE of 76.08 mm, PA-MPJPE of 46.73 mm, and PVE of 92.70 mm, which is competitive with the best 3D methods (CLIFF: 69.00/43.00/81.20 mm). The lightweight MeshPoseXS variant (21.25M params, 124 FPS) retains competitive 3D accuracy (79.15/49.71/96.49 mm) while being the fastest and smallest model in the comparison. On 2D COCO Keypoints, MeshPose(HRNet32) leads with AP-All of 47.30 and AP-80% of 71.20, far exceeding all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshPose(HRNet32) dominates on all 2D reprojection metrics: its DensePose AP of 47.87 exceeds CLIFF (30.99), NIKI (25.11), PARE (30.02), and all non-parametric methods (Metro 9.28, Graphormer 13.57, FastMetro 13.76, PointHMR 19.58) by a wide margin of 17–39 points. On 3D accuracy (3DPW), CLIFF achieves the best MPJPE (69.00 mm) and PA-MPJPE (43.00 mm), with MeshPose(HRNet32) trailing by ~7 mm on MPJPE and ~4 mm on PA-MPJPE, while MeshPoseXS (79.15/49.71 mm) is within 10 mm of CLIFF. The non-parametric transformer methods (Metro, Graphormer, FastMetro) use 3–10× more parameters (153–243M vs. 46M) yet achieve substantially worse DensePose scores (9–14 AP vs. 48 AP) and comparable or worse 3D accuracy. MeshPoseXS is the most efficient model in the table (21.25M params, 124 FPS) while maintaining 3D accuracy within 10 mm of the best, making it the clear winner for mobile/real-time deployment. The UV-regression DensePose baseline (DP-HRNet48, AP 62.74) still outperforms VertexPose (SP-HRNet48, AP 63.32) only marginally, confirming that the vertex-based approach is competitive with direct UV regression on the DensePose task itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshPose departs from the dominant top-down paradigm in HMR (predict full mesh via FK, IK, or global regression) by adopting a bottom-up strategy: first localize sparse mesh vertices in 2D via dense heatmaps, then lift them to 3D. The 2D localization is trained entirely from DensePose ground-truth through two novel weakly-supervised losses (barycentric cross-entropy and UV consistency), repurposing a dataset originally designed for continuous UV regression. The 3D lifting stage adapts the integral regression and weak-perspective camera formulation from I2L-MeshNet [35] and PLIKS [43], while the visibility-weighted fusion of 2D and regressed coordinates draws on differentiable rendering techniques. The paper explicitly positions against parametric FK-based, IK-based, recurrent-refinement, and non-parametric (transformer/GNN) HMR methods, arguing all suffer from poor 2D reprojection accuracy that 3D-only metrics fail to capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that 2D reprojection accuracy (measured via DensePose metrics) is a critical but previously under-evaluated dimension of HMR quality, particularly for AR applications like virtual try-on where pixel-level alignment is user-visible. The practical impact is significant: a lightweight, standard-layer-only architecture achieving real-time mobile inference with >50% better DensePose metrics than prior HMR systems opens a path for deploying mesh-accurate AR on consumer devices. Limitations include hand-flattening artifacts from the upsampler and reliance on a weak-perspective camera model that degrades under strong perspective distortion. An open question is whether the vertex-based DensePose supervision strategy generalizes to multi-person or heavily occluded scenes beyond the single-person setting evaluated here.
