## Problem

While the existence of utility-fairness trade-offs in classification is well established, two questions remain unanswered: what are the optimal trade-offs, and how can they be numerically quantified from data for a specific task and demographic attribute? Prior work studied only one type of trade-off, provided bounds rather than precise characterizations, and was largely restricted to demographic parity, leaving separation-based fairness notions like Equalized Opportunity unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240537/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

U-FaTE consumes a data triplet (X, Y, S) — raw images or tabular features, a target label, and a sensitive demographic attribute — and produces two trade-off curves (DST and LST) parameterized by λ ∈ [0,1), each mapping fairness violation to achievable utility. The curves partition the utility-fairness plane into three regions: fully possible, possible with extra data, and impossible. A downstream MLP classifier trained on the fair representation yields the final class prediction and the corresponding fairness metric values (DPV, EOD, or EOOD).

## Method

U-FaTE composes a neural feature extractor (ResNet-18) with a fair encoder whose parameters are solved in closed form via a generalized eigenvalue problem, avoiding the instability of adversarial or iterative optimization. The key mechanism is an HSIC-like dependence measure over a universal RKHS that captures all non-linear dependencies, adapted to handle both unconditional independence (for demographic parity) and conditional independence (for equalized opportunity and equality of odds) by operating on data conditioned on Y=y. Training alternates between minibatch SGD updates to the feature extractor and exact closed-form updates to the encoder at each iteration. For the LST, the representation is not constrained to be a function of X; instead, the model takes (Y, S) as input to estimate the oracle trade-off inherent to the label distributions alone. This design yields stable, reproducible trade-off estimates that span the full range of λ values, unlike prior methods such as K-T_Opt which fail to cover the entire front.

## Results

On FairFace, U-FaTE-DST achieves 96.17% accuracy with 0.263 EOD, 97.93% accuracy with 1.126 EOOD, and 94.39% accuracy with 3.082 DPV, substantially outperforming all FRL baselines on both utility and fairness. The LST (oracle) reaches 100% accuracy with zero EOD/EOOD, confirming that separation-based fairness metrics do not inherently force a utility sacrifice. Across CelebA and FolkTables (Figs. 3a–3f), the gap between LST and DST is ~20% accuracy in low-fairness regions and ~40% in high-fairness regions for EOD/EOOD, while for DPV the gap is ~20% at low fairness and shrinks with increasing unfairness. Evaluation of 100+ zero-shot CLIP and 900+ supervised pre-trained models reveals that most are far from the estimated DST, with only a handful of models trained on OpenAI WIT or ImageNet22K approaching or even surpassing the DST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-FaTE-DST dominates all five FRL baselines on FairFace across all three fairness metrics: for EOD it achieves 96.17% accuracy with 0.263 unfairness versus ARL (93.39%, 1.34), FairHSIC (91.02%, 1.33), and OptNet-ARL (92.94%, 1.70); for EOOD it reaches 97.93% accuracy with 1.126 unfairness versus ARL (91.60%, 3.04) and FairHSIC (93.43%, 2.13); and for DPV it attains 94.39% accuracy with 3.082 unfairness versus ARL (92.49%, 6.09) and OptNet-ARL (93.33%, 5.80). Among baselines, K-T_Opt [29] is closest to the DST curve and most stable due to its closed-form solver, but it fails to span the full range of fairness values. ARL [34] is the most unstable (highest variance in Figs. 3) yet can span the entire fairness range. FairHSIC [23] finds the closest single point to both LST and DST when optimizing for EOOD (Dist_LST = 0.498), while OptNet-ARL [28] performs slightly best among baselines for DPV reduction (Dist_DST = 0.316). The paper further shows that 100+ zero-shot CLIP models and 900+ supervised pre-trained models are substantially farther from the DST than any FRL method, with only a few models trained on OpenAI WIT or ImageNet22K approaching or surpassing the DST boundary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on K-T_Opt (Sadeghi et al., TMLR 2022), adopting its HSIC-like universal dependence measure and closed-form solver, but extends it in three key ways: it generalizes from unconditional to conditional dependence to support separation-based fairness definitions (EO, EOO) beyond demographic parity; it introduces a second trade-off (LST) alongside the previously studied DST; and it operates on raw data via a feature extractor rather than pre-extracted features. Prior theoretical work (Zhao et al., McNamara et al.) established bounds or end-points for a single trade-off under restricted label settings, while this paper provides a full numerical characterization of both trade-offs for arbitrary label types and multiple fairness definitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-region decomposition of the utility-fairness plane (possible, possible with extra data, impossible) gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic: whether to invest in better optimization or better data. The large-scale evaluation of over 1000 pre-trained models reveals that out-of-the-box representations are substantially far from achievable trade-offs, quantifying a gap that was previously only acknowledged qualitatively. A key limitation is that U-FaTE provides no convergence or optimality guarantees, so the estimated trade-offs are likely suboptimal upper bounds; whether tighter estimates or provably optimal solutions exist remains open. The finding that EOD and EOOD permit 100% accuracy at zero unfairness in the LST (unlike DPV) reinforces their practical superiority but also raises the question of whether real-world data always admits such a clean separation.
