## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D part segmentation methods that transfer 2D VLM predictions to 3D suffer from three issues: (1) occluded or undetected 3D regions lack corresponding 2D predictions, (2) inconsistent VLM predictions across views cause negative transfer, and (3) geometric evidence across multiple shapes is not exploited, leading to suboptimal 3D segmentation. These methods directly average or vote over 2D predictions without a mechanism to filter unreliable knowledge or leverage 3D geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N points) or mesh shape, renders it into V multi-view 2D images (default 10 views), and accepts R text prompts describing target semantic parts. The VLM (GLIP for bounding-box or CLIP for pixel-wise predictions) processes each view with the text prompts to produce 2D predictions, which are back-projected to 3D as knowledge units. The output is a per-point part probability vector (N×R) from which the final segmentation is obtained by argmax. The method supports both zero-shot (no labels) and few-shot (8 labeled shapes per category) settings, and can optionally use generated shapes from DiT-3D as additional knowledge sources.

## Method

PartDistill is a bidirectional cross-modal distillation framework with a 2D teacher (VLM) and a 3D student (frozen Point-M2AE encoder + 4-layer MLP distillation head). Forward distillation back-projects VLM predictions (bounding boxes or connected components) into 3D knowledge units and trains the student via a masked cross-entropy loss weighted by per-point confidence scores, allowing the student to interpolate over uncovered regions using geometric features. The key innovation is backward distillation: after the student integrates teacher knowledge, each knowledge unit is re-scored by the fraction of points where teacher and student predictions agree (Eq. 4), suppressing low-quality units (e.g., falsely detected boxes) and amplifying reliable ones. A second forward distillation pass with re-scored knowledge then produces the final segmentation. The framework generalizes to both bounding-box VLMs and pixel-wise VLMs, and supports test-time alignment (single shape, frozen encoder) or pre-alignment (collection of unlabeled shapes) for the student network.

## Results

On ShapeNetPart (zero-shot, CLIP, point cloud), PartDistill with pre-alignment achieves 63.9% mIoU, surpassing PointCLIPv2 (48.4%) by 15.5% and OpenScene (52.9%) by 11.0%. On the mesh-data setting with GLIP, PartDistill(Pre) reaches 56.3% mIoU versus SATR's 32.3% (24% improvement). On PartNetE (zero-shot, GLIP, point cloud), PartDistill(TTA) attains 39.9% mIoU, outperforming PartSLIP (27.3%) by 12.6%. In the few-shot setting on PartNetE, PartDistill achieves 65.9% mIoU, exceeding PartSLIP (59.4%) by 6.5% and the best non-VLM baseline Point-M2AE (56.4%) by 9.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartDistill consistently outperforms all ten baselines across every setting. Against the CLIP-based pixel-wise methods, PartDistill(Pre) beats PointCLIP [48] (31.0%) by 32.9% and PointCLIPv2 [52] (48.4%) by 15.5% on ShapeNetPart overall mIoU, with the largest per-category gains on Laptop (+30.8%) and Guitar (+26.7%). Against OpenScene [34] (52.9%), which uses the same Point-M2AE backbone and CLIP, PartDistill(Pre) gains 11.0%, attributed to the backward distillation mechanism that suppresses low-quality 2D predictions. On the mesh-data track with GLIP, PartDistill(Pre) (56.3%) surpasses SATR [1] (32.3%) by 24.0%, largely because distillation mitigates semantic leaking from bounding boxes. On PartNetE zero-shot, PartDistill(TTA) (39.9%) exceeds PartSLIP [24] (27.3%) by 12.6%. In the few-shot PartNetE setting, PartDistill (65.9%) outperforms PartSLIP (59.4%) by 6.5%, and dominates all non-VLM baselines: PointNet++ [35] (20.4%), PointNext [36] (40.6%), ACD [12] (23.2%), Prototype [51] (44.3%), and fine-tuned Point-M2AE [47] (56.4%), confirming that the distillation framework itself—rather than the backbone—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the direct 2D-to-3D transfer paradigm used by PointCLIP, PointCLIPv2, PartSLIP, and SATR, which simply average or weight multi-view VLM predictions to produce 3D segmentation. Instead, PartDistill introduces a cross-modal teacher-student distillation framework where a 3D point-cloud student network learns from 2D VLM knowledge while simultaneously extracting geometric features from multiple shapes. The bidirectional distillation (forward 2D→3D and backward 3D→2D re-scoring) extends prior 2D-to-3D distillation work like OpenScene and CLIP2Scene, which only perform unidirectional transfer and target scene segmentation rather than part-level segmentation. The framework also repurposes generative models (DiT-3D, SAP) as a source of unlabeled 3D shapes for distillation, a practical addition not explored in prior zero-shot 3D segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial mIoU gains (15%+ on ShapeNetPart, 12%+ on PartNetE) demonstrate that the identified failure modes of direct 2D-to-3D transfer (uncovered regions, inconsistent predictions, lack of cross-shape geometric reasoning) are significant and addressable. The bidirectional distillation mechanism—using the student's integrated 3D knowledge to re-score and suppress low-quality 2D teacher predictions—is a reusable principle that could apply to other cross-modal transfer settings. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that VLMs are pre-trained for object-level rather than part-level recognition, capping the quality of the teacher signal; exploiting multiple complementary VLMs or part-specific VLMs is identified as future work. The ability to substitute real training shapes with generative-model outputs broadens practical applicability when labeled or even unlabeled shape collections are unavailable.
