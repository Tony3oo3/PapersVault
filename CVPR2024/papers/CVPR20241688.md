## Problem

Pluralistic image inpainting—generating multiple diverse yet coherent completions for masked images—is difficult in standard encoder-decoder architectures because the reconstruction objective discourages conditioning on random variables, and models tend to ignore latent priors when visible cues suffice. While generative transformers (e.g., MaskGIT) offer fine-grained control over diversity through iterative token prediction, adapting this framework to inpainting raises unresolved questions about how to robustly encode partial images into discrete latent codes and how to decode predicted tokens into pixels that respect the observable region.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB partial image and a binary mask specifying the region to fill, and produces multiple diverse complete inpainted images. The mask is downsampled to a 16×16 token grid (1/16 image resolution) via a restrictive rule controlled by hyperparameter α, so small masked regions may still yield valid tokens while large holes are left for the transformer to predict. Three mask settings are evaluated: small random holes, large random holes, and a challenging 80%-area centered box.

## Method

The method is a three-stage pipeline that fundamentally separates feature reasoning from generative modeling. Stage 1 uses a restrictive partial convolution encoder (with threshold α=0.5) that only processes visible and near-visible regions to predict discrete VQGAN token labels, avoiding contamination from masked pixels. Stage 2 employs a BERT-based bidirectional transformer that iteratively predicts missing tokens via parallel decoding with cosine scheduling and adaptive temperature annealing (starting at t=1, annealing factor 0.9), sampling from predicted distributions to ensure diversity. Stage 3 couples the predicted token features with smooth partial-image priors extracted by a standard partial convolution encoder through an averaging operation, then decodes via a GAN generator trained with adversarial loss (R1-regularized) and LPIPS perceptual loss, ensuring coherent blending at mask boundaries.

## Results

On Places365 (256×256), the method achieves FID of 1.02 (small mask), 2.82 (large mask), and 13.30 (box mask), outperforming all baselines in every setting. On CelebA-HQ (256×256), FID scores are 2.70, 5.04, and 12.79 for the three mask settings respectively. For sample diversity (LPIPS-based, 100 samples per image under the box mask), the method scores 0.29±0.06 on Places and 0.28±0.05 on CelebA-HQ, ranking first on CelebA-HQ and second on Places (behind MaskGIT's 0.39±0.05, which comes at a large FID cost).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on FID across every mask setting on both datasets. Against MAT (the strongest pluralistic inpainting baseline), the gap is most pronounced under the challenging box mask: 13.30 vs. 17.50 FID on Places and 12.79 vs. 15.18 on CelebA-HQ, while also achieving higher diversity (0.29 vs. 0.26 on Places; 0.28 vs. 0.10 on CelebA-HQ). LaMa, a deterministic Fourier-convolution method, is consistently worse on FID (e.g., 19.48 vs. 13.30 on Places Box) and does not produce pluralistic outputs. MaskGIT achieves the highest diversity score on Places (0.39±0.05) but at a severe quality cost (FID 52.71 vs. 13.30), confirming that its lack of a partial-image-aware decoder leads to incoherent compositing. Pluralistic [48] degrades substantially under the box mask (FID 86.57 on Places) and is omitted from diversity scoring there, indicating failure to generate coherent results in extreme settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to adapt the generative transformer framework (MaskGIT) to image inpainting, repurposing its bidirectional transformer and parallel decoding algorithm for a conditional completion task. It builds on VQGAN's discrete codebook for representing images as latent tokens and modifies the standard partial convolution (Liu et al.) into a 'restrictive' variant with a threshold parameter α that prevents feature propagation into densely masked regions. The key conceptual departure from prior inpainting work is the explicit separation of feature reasoning (encoding only visible regions) from generative modeling (predicting missing tokens), rather than modeling the interaction between observed and missing regions within a single encoder-decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves consistent state-of-the-art FID and competitive diversity scores across three mask settings on two benchmarks, validating that the separation paradigm benefits large-mask pluralistic inpainting. However, inference is slower than single-pass methods due to iterative sampling, and the model still struggles with semantically salient objects (people, furniture). Open questions include whether training with semantic labels could improve object completion, how the approach scales to higher resolutions, and whether the restrictive convolution design generalizes beyond the 256×256 setting tested here.
