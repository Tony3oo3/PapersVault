## Problem

Generating realistic 3D human-object interaction (HOI) animations from text descriptions remains largely unsolved because existing methods either treat objects as passive in static scenes or fail to capture the complex reciprocal forces between humans and objects. The significant disparity in pose parameterization between humans (SMPL-H) and objects (translation/rotation) further complicates unified generation, and prior text-driven animation approaches lack explicit modeling of contact dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing an HOI action (e.g., 'a person bends over, lifts a large box with both hands') and produces a 3D HOI animation as a sequence of 175-dimensional pose states (10-dim SMPL-H shape β, 159-dim SMPL-H pose θ, 3-dim object translation τ, 3-dim object rotation γ) at 30 FPS over 6–10 seconds. The human body is parameterized via SMPL-H while objects use rigid-body translation and rotation, and the animation is constrained to a single human interacting with a single object.

## Method

HOIAnimator employs dual Perceptive Diffusion Models (PDM): a human-centric diffusion model G^H and an object-centric diffusion model G^O that jointly denoise their respective pose sequences under a shared text condition (frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 + 2 transformer layers). The two models communicate via Perceptive Message Passing (PMP), which has two stages: (1) object passage, where fully-connected networks L_w and L_b produce dynamic weights φ and biases φ from the object latent code to modulate the human latent code, and (2) dual flow, where a cross-attention aggregation module F_dual exchanges features bidirectionally before the latent decoder. A separate Interaction Contact Field (ICF) diffusion model G^I predicts per-vertex signed distance fields (1,500 sampled points per mesh) encoding contact/penetration probability between human and object surfaces, conditioned on the same text prompt. The ICF is injected into the PDM via cross-attention (Eq. 8), guiding the animation toward physically plausible contact. Training is supervised with L2 losses on both human and object sequences over T=1000 denoising steps, using 4-layer transformers (512-dim latent) and Adam at lr=0.0002 on 4× 3090Ti GPUs for 250k steps.

## Results

On the BEHAVE dataset, HOIAnimator achieves the best R Precision across all ranks (Top-1: 0.526, Top-2: 0.719, Top-3: 0.781), the lowest Vertex Distance (0.118 vs. 0.523 for the next-best InterGen), and the best FID (0.623). It also attains the highest Penetration score (0.643) and lowest MM Dist (7.521), while its Diversity (9.526) is nearly on par with real motions (9.534). The ablation study confirms that removing PDM, PMP, or ICF each degrades performance, with PDM removal causing the largest drop in FID (0.823) and Penetration (0.564).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOIAnimator outperforms all six baselines on every metric except Diversity, where MDM (9.537) and real motions (9.534) are marginally higher than HOIAnimator's 9.526. Against the closest competitor InterGen [21], HOIAnimator improves R Precision Top-3 from 0.734 to 0.781, reduces Vertex Distance from 0.523 to 0.118 (a 77% reduction), lowers FID from 0.717 to 0.623, and raises Penetration from 0.613 to 0.643. Against PriorMDM [31], the gains are even larger: Top-3 R Precision improves from 0.727 to 0.781 and FID drops from 0.853 to 0.623. T2M [11] and MLD [6] trail further behind, with T2M achieving only 0.673 Top-3 R Precision and 0.944 FID, while MLD reaches 0.701 and 0.859 respectively. The paper notes that InterGen was adapted from a two-human interaction framework to the HOI setting, and MLD required a VAE encoder to convert the dataset into latent codes before diffusion-based generation, making the comparison somewhat asymmetric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-motion generation line (T2M, MDM, PriorMDM) by introducing explicit object dynamics alongside human motion, and builds on multi-entity interaction frameworks like InterGen and Scene Diffuser. The key architectural departure is the dual perceptive diffusion model (separate human-centric and object-centric denoisers) coupled with a Perceptive Message Passing mechanism, rather than a single unified diffusion process over a concatenated state. The Interaction Contact Field, which models contact probability via a learned SDF-based diffusion, departs from prior HOI works that treat objects as passive or rely on simple collision detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOIAnimator is among the first frameworks to generate 3D human-object interaction animations directly from free-text prompts, filling a gap between text-to-human-motion and HOI animation literature. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (BEHAVE) with a small number of baselines, and the authors acknowledge limitations with complex sequential interactions, multi-object scenes, and non-rigid objects. Open questions include whether the dual-diffusion architecture scales to richer interaction vocabularies and whether the ICF generalizes beyond the 1,500-point sampling used here.
