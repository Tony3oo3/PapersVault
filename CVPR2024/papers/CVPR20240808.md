## Problem

Camera-only 3D object detection struggles with depth estimation and robustness in adverse conditions, while existing radar-camera fusion methods rely on LiDAR-designed encoders (e.g., PointPillars) that are sub-optimal for radar data and use simple fusion strategies (concatenation, SE-modules) that cannot handle spatial misalignment between radar and camera BEV features caused by radar azimuth errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240808/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images (256×704 or 448×800) and multi-sweep radar point clouds annotated with RCS and Doppler velocity as features. The camera stream accumulates BEV features across temporal frames (following BEVDet4D), while radar points are accumulated over multiple sweeps. Output is 3D bounding boxes with class, size, orientation, and velocity, evaluated via nuScenes NDS/mAP and VoD mAP metrics, with inference at 21–28 FPS on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

RCBEVDet introduces two key components. RadarBEVNet extracts radar BEV features via a dual-stream backbone (a PointNet-style MLP stream for local features and a transformer stream with distance-modulated self-attention for global context, bridged by an Injection/Extraction cross-attention module) and an RCS-aware BEV encoder that uses radar cross-section as an object-size prior to scatter each point's feature to multiple nearby BEV pixels with a Gaussian weight map, alleviating the sparsity of naive point-to-voxel scattering. The Cross-Attention Multi-layer Fusion (CAMF) module then dynamically aligns radar and camera BEV features using bidirectional deformable cross-attention (reducing complexity from O(H²W²C) to O(HWC²K)) to correct radar azimuth misalignment, followed by channel-and-spatial CBR fusion layers. Training is two-stage: the camera stream (BEVDepth with temporal accumulation) is pre-trained, then the radar stream and CAMF are trained with the camera parameters frozen.

## Results

On the nuScenes val set, RCBEVDet achieves 56.8 NDS and 45.3 mAP with ResNet-50 backbone at 21.3 FPS, outperforming the best camera-only method (SOLOFusion, 53.4 NDS) and the best prior radar-camera method (CRN, 56.0 NDS). On the nuScenes test set, RCBEVDet with V2-99 backbone reaches 63.9 NDS and 55.0 mAP, surpassing CRN with ConvNeXt-B (62.4 NDS) by 1.5 NDS. On the VoD dataset, RCBEVDet achieves 49.99 mAP (entire area) and 69.80 mAP (region of interest), exceeding RCFusion by 0.34 and 0.57 mAP respectively. Notably, RCBEVDet reduces velocity error (mAVE) by 37.5% compared to CRN under the R50 setting (0.220 vs. 0.344).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCBEVDet consistently outperforms all prior radar-camera fusion methods across backbone settings on nuScenes val: it beats CRN by 0.8 NDS (R50) and 0.5 NDS (R18), CRAFT by 4.6 NDS (DLA34), and CenterFusion by 11.0 NDS (DLA34). Against camera-only baselines, RCBEVDet (R50) surpasses SOLOFusion by 3.4 NDS and StreamPETR by 2.8 NDS, confirming the benefit of adding radar. On the nuScenes test set, RCBEVDet (V2-99) achieves 63.9 NDS, edging out SparseBEV and StreamPETR (both 63.6 NDS) and beating CRN (ConvNeXt-B) by 1.5 NDS despite using a smaller backbone. On VoD, RCBEVDet marginally outperforms RCFusion by 0.34 mAP (entire area) and 0.57 mAP (ROI). In robustness experiments (Table 7), RCBEVDet degrades far less than CRN under radar dropout: when all radar is dropped, RCBEVDet retains 62.1 Car mAP versus CRN's 43.8, demonstrating the effectiveness of the cross-attention alignment module.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCBEVDet builds directly on the BEVDepth camera backbone and BEVDet4D temporal fusion for the image stream, while departing from prior radar-camera fusion methods (CenterFusion, CRAFT, CRN, RCFusion) that rely on LiDAR-designed encoders like PointPillars and simple element-wise or SE-based fusion. The paper introduces a purpose-built radar encoder (RadarBEVNet) with a dual-stream point/transformer backbone and RCS-aware scattering, and replaces naive BEV concatenation with a deformable cross-attention alignment module (CAMF) borrowed conceptually from Deformable DETR. This positions the work as a systematic rethinking of both the radar feature extraction and the cross-modal fusion stages, rather than a drop-in improvement to an existing pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RCBEVDet demonstrates that a well-designed radar encoder and attention-based alignment can close much of the gap between radar-camera fusion and camera-only methods while preserving real-time inference (21–28 FPS), making it practically relevant for cost-sensitive autonomous driving deployments. The robustness analysis showing significantly smaller performance degradation under radar dropout (0.9 mAP vs. 4.5 mAP for CRN) suggests the cross-attention alignment produces genuinely more decoupled multi-modal features. Open questions include whether the dual-stream backbone and RCS-aware scattering generalize to other radar types (e.g., 4D imaging radar with richer angular resolution) and whether the frozen-camera-stream training protocol limits the upper bound of fusion quality.
