## Problem

Existing hyperspectral reconstruction methods rely on deeper networks or additional self-attention modules to capture non-local spatial-spectral mutation features, but the limited scope of attention modules and the irrelevance of attentions across different dimensions make this approach inefficient, yielding large parameter counts with only marginal accuracy gains. Additionally, the physical imaging constraint (the optical path) is typically overlooked in end-to-end training, limiting reconstruction fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241752/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D compressed measurement (H×W for optical filter-based systems, or H×(W+d(Nλ−1)) for CASSI) together with the corresponding 3D mask matrix M∈ℝ^{H×W×Nλ} as input, and reconstructs a full 3D hyperspectral cube of shape H×W×Nλ with 28 spectral channels spanning 450–650 nm. The mask is not merely a fixed forward operator but is actively injected into the network through a Mask Attention module, allowing the same architecture to generalize across different physical imaging systems (CASSI, Fabry-Perot filter arrays, metasurface HSI).

## Method

SPECAT organizes Cumulative-Attention Blocks (CABs) in a U-Net hierarchy (N₁=2, N₂=1) where each CAB performs a two-stage cumulative attention: a Hierarchical Spatial Attention (HSA) using Swin-style windowed multi-head attention first identifies spatially salient regions per spectral band, and a global spectral-wise attention then recovers fine-grained spectral details across bands using the spatially-attended features as tokens. A Mask Attention module multiplies the spectral queries by a learned function of the physical mask, embedding the optical-path constraint directly into the attention computation. Training uses a Dual-domain Loss Function that jointly penalizes reconstruction error in the object domain and projection-domain mismatch (‖M⊙X_out − Y₀‖₂), integrating the forward imaging model without extra inference cost. This design achieves 40.37 dB average PSNR with only 0.29 M parameters and 12.4 GFLOPs, roughly 5% of the parameters and 10% of the FLOPs of the prior SOTA (SST-LPlus at 9.71 M / 162.1 GFLOPs).

## Results

On the optical filter-based HSI benchmark (10 scenes, 256×256, 28 spectral bands), SPECAT achieves an average PSNR of 40.37 dB and SSIM of 0.986, outperforming the best prior method (SST-LPlus, 39.13 dB) by 1.24 dB while using only 0.29M parameters (vs. 9.71M) and 12.4 GFLOPs (vs. 162.1). This represents a ~34× reduction in parameters and ~13× reduction in computational cost. On real CASSI system data, SPECAT achieves reconstruction quality comparable to SOTA methods with the same parameter budget, showing particular strength in spectral dimension recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPECAT outperforms all 11 baselines on both PSNR and SSIM across the 10-scene optical filter-based HSI benchmark. Against the strongest Transformer-based competitor SST-LPlus [27] (39.13 dB, 9.71M params, 162.1 GFLOPs), SPECAT gains 1.24 dB PSNR while using only 3% of the parameters and 8% of the FLOPs. Compared to the best Deep Unfolding methods PADUT-12stg [30] (38.96 dB) and DAUHST-9stg [29] (38.53 dB), SPECAT improves by 1.41 dB and 1.84 dB respectively, with roughly 1/18th the parameters. Against earlier spectral-wise Transformers (MST-L [25] at 35.48 dB, MST++ [44] at 36.03 dB, CST-L [28] at 36.83 dB), the gains range from 3.5 to 4.9 dB. Traditional iterative methods (TwIST [21], DeSCI [23]) and CNN-based approaches (TSA-Net [26], GAP-net [49], BIRNAT [50]) are substantially outperformed by 2.8–17.2 dB. On real CASSI data, SPECAT matches SOTA spatial quality but shows a clear advantage in spectral fidelity, correctly recovering wavelength-dependent contrast patterns that other methods blur.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPECAT builds directly on the spectral-wise Transformer lineage initiated by MST (Cai et al., CVPR 2022), adopting its spectral-dimension attention and mask-attention mechanism, and on Swin Transformer's windowed multi-head attention for spatial feature extraction. The key conceptual departure is the Cumulative-Attention Block (CAB), which arranges spatial and spectral attention hierarchically—first identifying spatial mutation regions per band via Swin-style windowed attention, then applying spectral attention across bands—drawing an analogy to hierarchical attention networks for document classification. This contrasts with prior E2E methods (SST, CST, MST++) that either separate spatial and spectral modules or simply stack more attention layers and depth, and with Deep Unfolding networks (DAUHST, PADUT) that embed physical models iteratively at high parameter cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–30× reduction in parameters (0.29M vs. 5–10M) while simultaneously improving PSNR by ~1.2 dB over SOTA is practically significant for on-chip and embedded hyperspectral systems where memory and compute are tightly constrained. The paper's evaluation on optical filter-based HSI (Fabry-Perot, metasurface, LCTF) positions SPECAT as a viable reconstruction backbone for next-generation miniaturized spectrometers. A notable limitation is that spatial detail recovery on real CASSI data still lags behind the best SOTA methods, suggesting the cumulative-attention design may be more naturally suited to the non-correlated encoding of filter-based systems than to the shifted encoding of classical CASSI. Open questions include whether the hierarchical spatial-then-spectral ordering generalizes to other snapshot imaging modalities or to higher spectral resolutions.
