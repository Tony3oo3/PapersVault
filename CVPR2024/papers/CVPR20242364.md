## Problem

Existing radiology report generation methods suffer from feature degradation when prior knowledge is encoded by a separate embedding network (e.g., BERT) that is inconsistent with the language generation model, causing prediction shifts during decoding. Additionally, prior approaches apply general knowledge uniformly to all cases rather than tailoring it to individual patient diagnoses, and they fail to enhance attention on the pivotal discriminative regions of radiology images where lesions are often subtle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242364/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single chest X-ray image as input, processed by a visual extractor (ResNet-101 or ViT-B/16) into patch-level features. It also consumes a pre-computed expert report set (indexed by disease class) to build instance-level knowledge representations, and auxiliary logits from a Swin Transformer-based DiagnosisBot and a ResNet34-based Anomaly Locator. The output is a free-text radiology report generated autoregressively by a 6-layer Pre-Norm Transformer decoder, with no fixed template or structured format.

## Method

EKAGen unifies the prior-knowledge embedding network with the language generation model to prevent feature degradation, then constructs Instance-level Expert Knowledge by computing a per-disease prototype (PKS) in the shared embedding space, selecting the nearest original representations as Rectified Knowledge Support (RKS), and retrieving only the RKS entries corresponding to diseases diagnosed for the specific query image. An Aggregate Discriminative Attention Map (ADM) is generated by aggregating Grad-CAM activation maps across all valid disease classes, applying thresholding and morphological dilation to produce a foreground mask that suppresses background via a Hadamard product with a scaling factor γ=0.4. To counteract feature erosion from background suppression, a Global Information Self-Distillation (GID) strategy distills knowledge from an iteratively updated teacher (same architecture trained on full images) into the student using KL divergence, with the teacher's weights updated only when the student outperforms it. The final training objective combines cross-entropy language loss with the KL distillation loss scaled by δ=0.01.

## Results

On IU X-Ray, EKAGen (RN-101) achieves BLEU-1 of 0.526, BLEU-2 of 0.361, BLEU-3 of 0.267, and BLEU-4 of 0.203, setting new state-of-the-art on all four BLEU metrics. On MIMIC-CXR, EKAGen (RN-101) reaches BLEU-1 of 0.419 and BLEU-2 of 0.258, outperforming the previous best (METransformer) by 2.6% and 0.8% respectively. For clinical efficacy on MIMIC-CXR, EKAGen achieves Precision 0.517, Recall 0.483, and F1-Score 0.499, improving over the best prior method by 4.6%, 4.8%, and 8.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EKAGen (RN-101) achieves the best BLEU-1 through BLEU-4 on both datasets, narrowly surpassing KiUT on IU X-Ray (0.526 vs. 0.525 BLEU-1) and METransformer on MIMIC-CXR (0.419 vs. 0.393 BLEU-1, a 2.6% gain). However, EKAGen trails KiUT on METEOR (0.214 vs. 0.242) and ROUGE-L (0.404 vs. 0.409) on IU X-Ray, which the authors attribute to KiUT's synonym-aware symptom graph, and trails METransformer on METEOR (0.157 vs. 0.160) on MIMIC-CXR due to METransformer's word-voting strategy favoring phrase alignment. On clinical efficacy (MIMIC-CXR), EKAGen dominates all baselines: Precision 0.517 vs. DCL's 0.471, Recall 0.483 vs. Clinical-BERT's 0.435, and F1-Score 0.499 vs. Clinical-BERT's 0.415. Among memory-driven and contrastive baselines (R2GenCMN, R2GenRL, CA, DCL), EKAGen leads by a wide margin on all BLEU metrics on both datasets. The ViT-B/16 variant of EKAGen underperforms the ResNet-101 variant on both datasets, suggesting CNN-based local feature extraction is more effective for capturing fine-grained pathological details in chest X-rays.

## Relation to Prior Work

EKAGen extends the knowledge-based radiology report generation line (GSKET, PPKED, KiUT, METransformer) by identifying and addressing a specific failure mode: prior methods encode expert knowledge with separate embedding networks (BERT, Graph), causing feature degradation when word vectors are mapped back to the generation space. The paper departs from this by unifying the knowledge embedding network with the Language Generation Model and introducing instance-level knowledge retrieval via a DiagnosisBot, rather than applying general knowledge uniformly to all cases. The Aggregate Discriminative Attention Map repurposes Grad-CAM in a multi-disease aggregation setting with morphological dilation, and the Global Information Self-Distillation strategy introduces a self-updating teacher (unlike standard frozen-teacher distillation) to counteract feature erosion from background suppression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified-embedding insight is a clean, practical fix for a problem that likely affects many knowledge-augmented generation pipelines beyond radiology, and the 8.4% F1 improvement in clinical efficacy metrics suggests the approach captures disease-specific semantics more faithfully than prior methods. However, the pipeline's effectiveness is gated by DiagnosisBot accuracy (a separate multi-class classifier), and the paper does not deeply analyze error propagation when misdiagnosis occurs. Open questions include whether the instance-level knowledge retrieval generalizes to rarer diseases with very few support samples, whether the approach transfers to other imaging modalities (CT, MRI), and whether the GID self-distillation loop remains stable at scale.
