## Problem

Existing video object grounding methods for egocentric videos output only bounding boxes or single points, which are insufficient for highly-overlapping, occluded, or non-grid-aligned objects common in first-person procedural videos. Pixel-level grounding of referred objects typically requires expensive spatial annotations (segmentation masks or mouse traces) for every mentioned object, making large-scale training infeasible. No benchmark or weakly-supervised framework existed for pixel-level segmentation of objects mentioned in narrations of egocentric videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short egocentric video clip (T frames of H×W pixels) and a narration sentence containing N noun phrases (objects) as input, and produces a binary segmentation mask of size H×W for each referred object at each frame. Rather than predicting masks from scratch, the model operates over M SAM-generated mask proposals per frame (from a 32×32 point grid with NMS at 0.9) and selects the best-matching proposal per phrase. Training uses only video-narration pairs from Ego4D (250k cooking clips, 4 frames sampled per 1-second clip), with no pixel-level or bounding-box annotations for the referred objects.

## Method

ROSA uses a CLIP-based Dual Encoder: a Context-Aware Region Encoder that passes each frame through L−1 ViT layers and then applies attention masking in the final layer so the CLS token attends exclusively to a given mask proposal (sharing computation across all proposals), and a Context-Aware Phrase Encoder that averages a narration-aware embedding (token pooling) with a localized EOT-token embedding from CLIP's text encoder. Training employs a Global-Local Contrastive Learning framework: a global Video-Narration Alignment loss (InfoNCE) that aggregates region-phrase cosine similarities via a Temporal Adaptive Pooling with a dynamically increasing temperature α, and a local Region-Phrase Alignment loss using pseudo-labelled alignments with both a standard R2P InfoNCE term and a P2R MIL-NCE term that treats all temporally-aligned regions as positives. At inference, grounding is achieved by selecting the SAM mask proposal with the highest cosine similarity to the phrase embedding in the learned joint space. The model is trained on Ego4D narrations and evaluated zero-shot on VISOR-NVOS, VOST, and YouCook2-BB.

## Results

On VISOR-NVOS, ROSA (ViT-L/14) achieves J&F of 42.4%, outperforming the strongest spatially-supervised baseline GroundedSAM (39.5%) by 2.9 points and the simple SAM+CLIP baseline (24.0%) by 18.4 points. On VOST, ROSA reaches J_ins of 26.7%, surpassing GroundedSAM (25.3%) and ODISE (21.1%). For zero-shot generalization to the third-person YouCook2-BB dataset, ROSA achieves 37.93% macro box accuracy, comparable to methods trained directly on YouCook2 (e.g., SCL at 42.80%) and far exceeding the zero-shot CoMMa†+SAM baseline (6.63%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROSA (ViT-L/14) outperforms all seven baselines on both VISOR-NVOS and VOST. Against the strongest spatially-supervised baseline, GroundedSAM (J&F 39.5%), ROSA gains 2.9 points on VISOR-NVOS J&F and 1.4 points on VOST J_ins, despite using only video-narration supervision rather than bounding-box annotations. ODISE, which uses class-specific mask-text supervision, lags significantly behind ROSA (30.9% vs. 42.4% J&F on VISOR-NVOS). The untrained SAM+CLIP baseline (24.0% J&F) is a strong reference point; ROSA with the same ViT-B/16 backbone improves it by 14.1 points. CoMMa†+SAM, adapted for point-level grounding, performs worst among trained methods (20.3% J&F), confirming that pixel-level mask proposals are superior to point prompts for this task. On YouCook2-BB, ROSA's zero-shot macro box accuracy (37.93%) approaches methods trained on YouCook2 (SCL: 42.80%, STVG: 41.67%) and exceeds the zero-shot CoMMa†+SAM by over 31 points, while also surpassing CoMMa's point accuracy by 10.1 points (69.35% vs. 59.25%).

## Relation to Prior Work

ROSA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CLIP-based vision-language pre-training, which prior works have shown is difficult to adapt for pixel-level region representations; (2) SAM-based mask proposal generation, which provides class-agnostic segmentation candidates without labels; and (3) weakly-supervised phrase grounding in images and videos, which has so far been limited to bounding boxes or points. The closest prior work is Video Narration Grounding (Voigtlaender et al., 2023), which also grounds noun phrases to segmentation masks but operates on third-person videos and relies on mouse-trace spatial annotations. ROSA departs by formulating the problem in the egocentric setting, eliminating all spatial supervision, and introducing a dual-encoder architecture with a temporal adaptive pooling mechanism that has no direct precedent in image-based phrase grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VISOR-NVOS benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior egocentric dataset paired segmentation masks with object-referencing narrations, making pixel-level grounding in first-person video unevaluable. The finding that weakly-supervised training (video-narration pairs only) can match or exceed models trained with bounding-box annotations (e.g., GroundedSAM) suggests that the quality of mask proposals and the alignment objective matter more than the strength of spatial supervision. However, the large gap to the SAM upper bound (42.4 vs. 73.0 J&F) and the absence of temporal modeling indicate the task remains far from solved. Open questions include whether temporal consistency can close this gap, whether the approach generalizes beyond the cooking domain, and how to handle the ambiguity of multiple instances of the same object class without explicit disambiguation signals.
