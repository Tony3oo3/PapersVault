## Problem

Existing text-to-motion methods generate body motions only, ignoring the rich two-hand gestures that are essential for realistic human movement. No prior approach can simultaneously condition hand generation on both explicit text prompts and implicit body dynamics, and the field suffers from severe data scarcity for fine-grained, finger-level hand motion annotations paired with body descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241399/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded by a frozen CLIP-ViT-L-14) and a body motion sequence of 22 SMPLH joints (positions + 6D rotations per frame) as input, and produces a 30-joint two-hand motion sequence in global space (positions + 6D rotations per frame). The body motion is consumed as a clean conditioning signal concatenated with noised hands in the diffusion denoiser, while text is injected as a token embedded with the noise-level timestep. The output hands are expressed in the same global coordinate frame as the body, requiring a forward-kinematics projection step to align the locally-parameterized text-conditioned hands before blending.

## Method

BOTH2Hands employs a two-stage pipeline: two parallel DDPM-based denoisers (body-to-hand and text-to-hand) are first trained independently, then a cross-attention transformer blends their outputs. The body-to-hand denoiser concatenates noised hand joints with the clean body sequence and predicts the clean hand motion via L1 reconstruction; the text-to-hand denoiser operates on local hand parameters (IK-derived relative rotations and wrist-relative positions) to focus on gesture, then projects back to global space via FK. The blending transformer (N=4 blocks, 512-dim latent, 4 heads) alternates the K/V inputs between body-conditioned and text-conditioned hand embeddings across successive attention layers, so each hand sequence attends to the other in turn. Training uses a weighted L1 blending loss (w_B=0.8, w_T=0.2) that supervises the final output against a convex combination of both conditioned hands, with 1000 diffusion timesteps and AdamW at lr=1e-4.

## Results

On the BOTH57M test set, BOTH2Hands achieves the best FID (0.201), MM-Dist (1.392), Diversity (3.969), MModality (1.312), and Top-3 R-Precision (0.115) among all compared methods, approaching the ground-truth values (FID 0.181, MM-Dist 1.391, Diversity 3.980). The text-only variant (BOTH2Hands-Text) further improves FID to 0.198 and MM-Dist to 1.391. The authors note that metric improvements over baselines are marginal, attributing this to the insensitivity of standard single-condition metrics (R-Precision, FID) to multi-conditioned hand generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOTH2Hands outperforms all four baselines on FID, MM-Dist, Diversity, and MModality, with the largest gap over T2M-GPT (FID 0.201 vs. 0.461) and MLD (FID 0.201 vs. 0.296). Against MDM, BOTH2Hands improves FID by 0.056 and MModality by 0.039, while MDM retains a slight edge on Top-1 and Top-2 R-Precision (0.039/0.077 vs. 0.037/0.075). Compared to Ego-Ego (body-conditioned only), BOTH2Hands achieves substantially better FID (0.201 vs. 0.287) and MModality (1.312 vs. 1.240), confirming the benefit of incorporating text conditioning. T2M-GPT achieves the highest Top-1 R-Precision (0.042), but this is attributed to its text-only focus; it lags significantly on FID and diversity, indicating less realistic hand motions. The authors emphasize that qualitative results (Fig. 4, 6) show clearer advantages for BOTH2Hands in following both text and body conditions simultaneously, which the quantitative metrics understate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: text-to-motion generation (MDM, T2M-GPT, MLD) and body-to-hand motion synthesis (Body2Hands, EgoEgo). It departs from both by introducing a hybrid conditioning setting where hand motions are generated simultaneously from explicit text prompts and implicit body dynamics, rather than treating these as separate tasks. Architecturally, it builds on DDPM for the denoising backbone, borrows the FK-based body-to-hand concatenation from EgoEgo, and adopts MDM's text-token embedding strategy, then combines the two parallel diffusion streams via a cross-attention transformer inspired by InterGen's shared-weight design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BOTH57M dataset (57.4M frames, 32-camera capture, finger-level annotations) addresses a critical data bottleneck for two-hand motion generation and is the first to provide paired body and hand text annotations at this granularity. However, the paper itself acknowledges that standard motion-generation metrics (R-precision, FID) show only marginal improvements, revealing a gap in how the field evaluates multi-conditioned, fine-grained hand synthesis. Open questions include developing hand-specific evaluation metrics, extending the approach to interactive or real-time settings, and whether the cross-attention blending generalizes to more complex multi-person or object-interaction scenarios.
