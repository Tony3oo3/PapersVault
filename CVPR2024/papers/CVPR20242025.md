## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: SDS-based approaches (e.g., DreamFusion) inherit the diversity of 2D diffusion but suffer from slow optimization (~30 min), multi-face artifacts, and mode-seeking behavior, while direct 3D diffusion models trained on limited 3D datasets (e.g., ShapeNet, Objaverse) produce fast but low-diversity results with poor generalization to complex prompts. No prior method simultaneously achieves fast inference, high diversity, and strong text alignment without relying on iterative score distillation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single free-form text prompt as input and produces a textured 3D triangle mesh in approximately 10 seconds. Internally, it first generates 4 multi-view normal maps at 256×256 resolution (front, left, right, back views aligned to world coordinates), then generates 4 corresponding multi-view RGB images at 256×256 that are upscaled to 1024×1024 via Real-ESRGAN. The final mesh is constructed by fusing the normal maps through space carving and differentiable rasterization, with textures projected via UV parameterization and Poisson blending.

## Method

The core idea is to fine-tune a pre-trained 2D latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2.1) into a multi-view 2.5D diffusion model that directly models the joint distribution of multi-view normal maps, thereby inheriting the generalization of large-scale 2D training while capturing 3D structural consistency. The key architectural modification is cross-view attention: at each self-attention layer, intermediate features from all 4 view branches are concatenated as queries and values, enabling mutual conditioning without additional trainable parameters. Training uses 500K Objaverse objects rendered from 32 viewpoints for 2.5D supervision, jointly with 65M filtered COYO-700M images (80/20 ratio) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. For 3D reconstruction, the generated normal maps are fused via space carving initialization followed by ~200 steps of differentiable rasterization optimization (nvdiffrast) minimizing L1 normal and alpha losses plus a normal consistency regularization. A second, normal-conditioned multi-view diffusion model generates appearance, with the normal latent injected via an extended first convolution layer initialized to zero.

## Results

On the text-to-3D generation benchmark (50 unseen prompts from DreamFusion), Direct2.5 achieves the best FID on both Objaverse renderings (82.32) and COCO validation set (126.01), and the second-best CLIP score (0.267) behind only ProlificDreamer (0.269). Its Inception Score (8.11) is slightly below MVDream (8.18) and ProlificDreamer (9.46). Most notably, the full pipeline runs in ~10 seconds, compared to 35 minutes for MVDream, 5 hours for ProlificDreamer, and 50–115 minutes for DreamFusion/Fantasia3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Direct2.5 decisively outperforms the direct 3D diffusion baselines (Point-E and Shap-E) on every metric: FID drops from ~104 to 82 on Objaverse and from ~164 to 126 on COCO, while CLIP improves from 0.22–0.24 to 0.267, reflecting far better text alignment and generalization. Against the SDS-based methods, Direct2.5 achieves a substantially lower FID than DreamFusion (82.3 vs 125.9) and Fantasia3D (82.3 vs 132.9) on Objaverse, and a higher CLIP score than both (0.267 vs 0.245 and 0.207), while running 5–11× faster. Compared to the closest competitor MVDream, Direct2.5 wins on FID (82.3 vs 117.7 on Objaverse; 126.0 vs 133.1 on COCO) and CLIP (0.267 vs 0.262), but trails slightly on IS (8.11 vs 8.18) and is ~21× faster (10 s vs 35 min). ProlificDreamer edges out Direct2.5 on IS (9.46 vs 8.11) and CLIP (0.269 vs 0.267) but has a much worse FID (121.6 vs 82.3 on Objaverse) and requires ~5 hours per sample. The paper also demonstrates that Direct2.5 preserves sample diversity (multiple distinct outputs per prompt), whereas MVDream's SDS-based optimization collapses to near-identical results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves a middle path between two established paradigms for text-to-3D generation: SDS-based methods (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer, MVDream) that distill 2D diffusion knowledge into 3D optimization, and direct 3D diffusion models (Point-E, Shap-E) trained on limited 3D datasets. It borrows the cross-view attention mechanism from MVDream [33] for multi-view consistency but departs fundamentally by operating on 2.5D normal maps rather than RGB images, and by replacing SDS with a one-pass differentiable rasterization pipeline (space carving + marching cubes + nvdiffrast optimization). The fine-tuning strategy from Stable Diffusion v2.1 on Objaverse renderings, combined with COYO-700M 2D data to prevent catastrophic forgetting, positions the work as a practical bridge inheriting 2D diffusion's generalization while explicitly modeling 3D geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-second generation time (vs. 35 minutes for MVDream, 50+ minutes for DreamFusion) makes diverse, high-fidelity text-to-3D generation practically usable in interactive or production settings, and the preservation of diffusion-model diversity (demonstrated against MVDream's mode-seeking collapse) addresses a core limitation of SDS. Key limitations remain: only 4 views are generated, leaving top/bottom/concavity regions unobserved, and texture quality is constrained by the rendering fidelity of 3D training data. Open questions include whether scaling to more views or higher-resolution 2.5D representations can close the remaining quality gap, and whether the 2.5D intermediate can be extended to dynamic or articulated objects.
