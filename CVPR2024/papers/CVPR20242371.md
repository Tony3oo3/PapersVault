## Problem

Existing ego-exo datasets record time-synchronized videos in the same environment, but real-world demonstration following requires bridging asynchronous procedural actions performed in different places and at different times. No dataset or benchmark existed to study how models can associate, anticipate, and plan across these asynchronous ego-exo viewpoints, leaving a critical gap for embodied AI agents that must learn from human demonstrations in novel settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242371/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmarks consume egocentric videos (with calibrated gaze positions from Pupil Invisible Glasses), exocentric demonstration videos, and dense temporal-bounded text captions. Gaze is integrated by spatially cropping egocentric frames around gaze positions rather than concatenating gaze as a separate channel. Outputs are structured as multiple-choice video associations (20 candidates), multi-label verb/noun predictions for the next 1-second action, sequences of K=8 coarse action steps, pairwise skill rankings given an exo reference clip, and fine-grained video captions with start/end timestamps.

## Method

EgoExoLearn is a 120-hour dataset (432 ego + 315 exo videos) where participants replicate exocentric demonstrations in different environments, annotated with four-stage multi-level labels (coarse actions, fine verb/noun per hand, skill rankings, and dense captions). Four benchmarks are defined: cross-view association (TimeSformer-B + CLIP text encoder trained with contrastive loss, initialized from EgoVLP), cross-view action anticipation/planning (evaluated via three transfer directions—unsupervised domain adaptation with CLIP+TA3N, knowledge distillation, and co-training), cross-view referenced skill assessment (RAAN extended with triplet loss or relation network to incorporate an exo reference), and cross-view referenced captioning. A key empirical finding is that gaze-cropped egocentric inputs substantially narrow the ego-exo performance gap across all benchmarks, suggesting visual attention is a critical signal for cross-view alignment.

## Results

On the cross-view association benchmark, the best fine-tuned model (EgoExo co-training + gaze) achieves 55.3% Ego2Exo and 51.1% Exo2Ego Top-1 accuracy, substantially above the 12.5% random baseline but still far from saturation. For cross-view action anticipation, co-training with gaze yields the best class-mean Top-5 recall of 43.8% (Ego-V) and 53.3% (Ego-N), while for action planning it achieves the lowest Edit distance of 75.6 (Exo) and 79.0 (Ego) at K=8. In cross-view referenced skill assessment, RAAN+triplet loss with gaze reaches 82.2–84.1% ranking accuracy across four action types. Across all benchmarks, gaze consistently provides 3–6 absolute points of improvement, and co-training on both views outperforms single-view training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot vision-language models, EgoVLP [72] leads with 32.1%/28.9% (Ego2Exo/Exo2Ego) test accuracy, outperforming LaViLa [158] (28.7%/25.7%) and InternVideo [129] (30.6%/21.7%); however, adding gaze to InternVideo narrows the gap to 33.3%/32.2%, surpassing EgoVLP on Exo2Ego. In the fine-tuned setting, the paper's EgoExo co-training baseline with gaze (55.3%/51.1%) beats the exo-only model (45.4%/46.9%) by ~10 points on Ego2Exo, demonstrating that cross-view training is essential. For skill assessment, RAAN [23] (78.5–82.5%) outperforms Who's Better [22] (74.8–77.5%) across all four actions, and adding the exo reference via triplet loss yields only marginal gains (e.g., 79.09→78.73 without gaze on Cutting), suggesting current models still struggle to exploit cross-view demonstrations. Gaze provides the most consistent improvement across all benchmarks: +5.3 points for Ego-only association, +7.1 points for Ego-V anticipation, and +3.6 points for RAAN skill ranking, highlighting gaze as an underexploited signal for ego-exo bridging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the 'ego-exo demonstration following' setting, where egocentric recordings are made in different environments and at different times from exocentric demonstrations, emulating human observational learning. It departs from existing ego-exo datasets (Ego4D, Assembly101, H2O, HOMEAGE, CharadesEgo) that either record synchronized multi-view footage in the same environment or focus on 3D pose/activity understanding, and from the conceptually similar but much smaller AE2 dataset that targets clip-level view-invariant representations. Methodologically, the baselines repurpose established techniques—contrastive video-language pretraining (EgoVLP, CLIP), unsupervised domain adaptation, knowledge distillation, and pairwise ranking (RAAN)—but apply them to the novel asynchronous cross-view problem for the first time, with gaze as an additional modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoExoLearn fills a critical gap for embodied AI: no prior dataset captures the realistic scenario where an agent must learn from a demonstration recorded elsewhere and from a different viewpoint, which is essential for leveraging the vast pool of public instructional videos. The benchmarks reveal that even the best models achieve only ~55% cross-view association accuracy (vs. 12.7% random), and the marginal gains from adding exo references in skill assessment indicate that current architectures fundamentally struggle with asynchronous cross-view bridging. The finding that naive gaze-cropping substantially narrows the ego-exo performance gap across all tasks opens a promising but underexplored direction. Open questions include how to scale these findings to more complex, open-vocabulary tasks, how to handle the inherent length mismatch between edited demonstrations and full procedural recordings, and whether the gaze signal's effectiveness persists in more cluttered or dynamic real-world settings.
