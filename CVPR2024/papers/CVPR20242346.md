## Problem

Pre-trained visual encoders (e.g., MAE-based ViTs) produce task-agnostic representations that cannot optimally serve diverse downstream tasks requiring different invariances and symmetries. While attention-based models offer some inherent adaptability, the paper argues that explicitly modulating visual features per task is a critical missing design choice for multi-task robot policies, and that a single policy can outperform specialized single-task policies when visual features are properly adapted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242346/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives the last 3 RGB frames (h×w×3, processed as a batch through the ViT), a proprioception vector p_t ∈ R^{d_a}, and a task embedding e ∈ R^{d_e}. The policy input is a concatenation of two frame-difference representations, the current frame representation, proprioception, and the task embedding. The output is a continuous action vector in the union of all task action spaces, with a masking procedure applied during training to restrict predictions to the valid action subspace of the current task.

## Method

The method inserts task-conditioned 'middle' adapters (one 2-layer MLP after each ViT self-attention block, with residual connections) and a 'top' adapter (after a learned token-aggregation layer) into a frozen MAE-pretrained ViT backbone (VC-1). All adapters and a single multi-task MLP policy are conditioned on a shared task embedding e, which for known tasks is projected from a 1-in-K one-hot vector via a learned linear layer. Training uses behavior cloning over expert demonstrations from 12 tasks across Adroit, DMC, and MetaWorld, with only adapter, aggregation, embedding, and policy weights updated. For few-shot adaptation to unseen tasks, the task embedding is estimated by SGD-minimizing the MSE between policy predictions and expert actions on 5 demonstrations, requiring no weight fine-tuning of the backbone or adapters.

## Results

On the known-task setting across 12 tasks from Adroit, DMC, and MetaWorld, the proposed multi-task policy with conditioned middle and top adapters (row f) achieves 54.8±1.2 average test performance across all tasks, outperforming the single-task VC-1 baseline (row a) at 46.3±0.4 by 8.5 points. Per-benchmark, the best configuration reaches 42.3 (Adroit), 60.0 (DMC), and 54.5 (MetaWorld) test scores. Ablations confirm that conditioning adapters on the learned task embedding is critical: replacing it with a random vector (row g) or a random known-task embedding (row h) collapses performance to 3.8 and 4.6 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the single-task policy setup from Majumdar et al. [20], where a separate policy is trained per task using non-adapted VC-1 CLS-token features. The proposed multi-task policy with task-conditioned middle and top adapters (row f) outperforms this baseline by 8.5 points on average test performance (54.8 vs. 46.3), despite being a single shared policy rather than 12 specialized ones. Notably, the single-task baseline is validated only on its own task (giving it an advantage), making the multi-task result more impressive. The comparison also reveals that simply switching to a multi-task policy without adapters (row b) actually underperforms the single-task baseline (42.5 vs. 46.3), demonstrating that the task-conditioned visual adapters are the key design choice enabling the multi-task policy to surpass per-task specialists. Ablations with random or randomly-picked task embeddings (rows g, h) show near-zero performance, confirming that the learned task embedding space is essential rather than a trivial conditioning signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VC-1/CortexBench framework of Majumdar et al. [20] by replacing their single-task policies with a unified multi-task policy and introducing task-conditioned visual adapters (building on the adapter architecture of Houlsby et al. [10] and the visual adapter placement of Sharma et al. [31]). The key departure is that adapters are not task-specific but are instead modulated by a shared learned task embedding, enabling a single set of adapter weights to serve all tasks. The few-shot task embedding estimation via optimization over a small set of demonstrations is a novel mechanism not present in prior adapter or multi-task robotics work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that task-conditioned visual feature adaptation is a critical design choice for multi-task robot policies, showing that a single policy with adapted features can match or exceed specialized single-task policies. The few-shot generalization to unseen tasks (new objects, new manipulation types) without any weight fine-tuning is practically appealing for deploying agents in new environments. However, all experiments are in simulation (MetaWorld, Adroit, DMC) with only 12+15 tasks, and few-shot success is inconsistent—some tasks achieve high performance while others (e.g., button-press-wall) fail entirely. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real-world embodiments, scales to hundreds of tasks, and benefits from alternative pre-training objectives beyond MAE.
