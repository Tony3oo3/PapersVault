## Problem

Images contain substantial redundant information, making efficient vision-language pre-training challenging. Existing masking strategies face a trade-off: random patch dropout (e.g., FLIP) improves training speed but provides no additional learning signal, while semantically-aware masking (e.g., ACLIP) improves representations but requires expensive attention maps or separate grouping mechanisms. The paper asks whether a simple, lightweight criterion can identify coherent visual structures to mask, combining the efficiency of random dropout with the representational benefit of structured masking.

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images, patchified into 16×16 patches, and text captions truncated or padded to 77 tokens. Before clustering, each patch is pixel-normalized (zero mean, unit standard deviation) so that similarity is computed on relative intensity patterns rather than absolute brightness. The output is a 512-dimensional image embedding (via a class token MLP) and a 512-dimensional text embedding for contrastive alignment, plus a binary patch mask whose ratio varies per image; a minimum mask ratio β enforces a lower bound on masked patches to keep batched tensor shapes uniform, with attention masks handling the residual variability.

## Method

The core mechanism is a single-iteration, anchor-based clustering performed on raw patch features at every training step. A small random subset of patches (<5%) is selected as anchors; pairwise cosine similarity is computed across all patches, and every patch within a distance threshold r of any anchor is grouped into that anchor's cluster. All patches in the selected clusters are then masked out, forcing the model to associate caption words with visually absent structures purely from context. The distance metric can optionally blend RGB cosine similarity with the ViT patch-embedding cosine similarity via a weight α that linearly ramps from 0 to 1 over training, incorporating positional encoding information. Training uses the standard symmetric InfoNCE loss (CLIP-style) on Conceptual 12M with a ViT-B/16 image encoder, and the entire clustering step is differentiable-free and requires no extra forward pass or attention computation, keeping training speed comparable to FLIP while yielding +5.5% average zero-shot classification improvement over it.

## Results

The best variant (Ours-Embedding) achieves 38.2% average zero-shot classification accuracy across 12 datasets, with a training time of only 0.64× that of CLIP. On ImageNet-1K specifically, Ours-RGB0.3 reaches 36.6% and Ours-Embedding reaches 36.3%, both surpassing CLIP's 36.1%. In linear probing, the method gains +1.8% on ImageNet, +3.1% on CIFAR-10, and +4.2% on CIFAR-100 over CLIP. For zero-shot retrieval on MS-COCO, Ours-RGB0.3 achieves 35.87 R1 (text→image) and 23.65 R1 (image→text), outperforming CLIP (34.60 and 23.49) and FLIP (32.62 and 22.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (no masking), Ours-Embedding improves average zero-shot classification by +2.1% while training 36% faster (0.64× vs 1.00×), winning on 11 of 12 datasets. Against FLIP (random patch dropout at 50%), the method achieves a +5.5% average improvement at comparable training speed (0.64× vs 0.53×), with particularly large gains on CIFAR-100 (+6.5%) and ImageNet-A (+1.7%). Against FLIPAttn (attention-guided masking), the method delivers +2.6% average accuracy while being substantially faster (0.64× vs 1.06×), since it avoids the extra forward pass needed to compute attention maps. On zero-shot retrieval (MS-COCO text→image R1), Ours-RGB0.3 leads all baselines at 35.87, ahead of CLIP (34.60), FLIPAttn (33.66), and FLIP (32.62). On SUGARCREPE language composition, the method matches or exceeds baselines on Relation tests and improves Object and Attribute scores by +3.9% and +3.0% over FLIP respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive vision-language pre-training framework of CLIP by introducing a cluster-based patch masking strategy that sits between FLIP's random dropout and ACLIP's attention-guided masking. It draws on two distinct lineages: the Masked Image Modeling literature (MAE, SimMIM) for using pixel-level features as a similarity signal, and masked region classification in VLP (UNITER, VL-BERT) for the learning signal of predicting caption words for occluded visual structures. The key departure from prior masking work is that it avoids both the semantic grouping overhead of object-level methods and the two-pass attention computation of ACLIP, instead using a single K-Means-like iteration over raw RGB (or shallow embedding) features to form clusters that are then masked wholesale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical contribution is a drop-in masking strategy that simultaneously speeds up training (~36% faster than CLIP) and improves representation quality (+2.1% average over CLIP, +5.5% over FLIP) without architectural changes, making it attractive for practitioners scaling VLP. The finding that untrained pixel intensities suffice to capture coherent object-part structure is a useful simplification, though it is grounded in classical segmentation intuition. Open questions include whether the uniform distance threshold generalizes across diverse image distributions, whether the approach scales beyond ViT-B/16 on CC12M, and whether the modest language-composition gains (comparable on Relation, improved on Object/Attribute) hold at larger scale where bag-of-words tendencies are more pronounced.
