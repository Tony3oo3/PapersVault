## Problem

Existing point-based adversarial attacks on 3D point clouds inevitably produce easily detectable outlier points due to the trade-off between imperceptibility and adversarial strength, while shape-based attacks eliminate outliers but introduce irrational deformations that remain perceptible. The paper identifies that this trade-off is difficult to resolve because prior methods treat all surface regions equally, ignoring that human vision is less sensitive to perturbations in areas with complex, high-curvature-variation surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean unordered 3D point cloud (m×3 coordinate matrix) and produces an adversarial point cloud of the same cardinality, where each point's displacement is computed as a weighted combination of Gaussian kernel functions centered at a small number of selected deformation centers. For the physical attack extension, the adversarial point cloud is reconstructed into a mesh, 3D-printed, and re-scanned to form the final physical adversarial input, with benign resampling and rigid transformations applied to ensure adversarial strength derives purely from shape deformation rather than point-level artifacts.

## Method

HiT-ADV is a shape-based adversarial attack that conceals deformation perturbations in visually insensitive regions. A two-stage search module first computes a combined saliency-imperceptibility (SI) score per point—where the imperceptibility component measures the standard deviation of local surface curvature—then selects a small set of central points in complex-surface areas as deformation centers. Perturbations are generated via Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression using Gaussian kernels centered at these points, with both the center displacements and kernel bandwidths σ optimized iteratively. Three regularization terms constrain the solution: a kernel loss limiting perturbation magnitude and kernel flatness, a hide loss that aligns σ with local curvature complexity (smaller σ on complex surfaces, larger on smooth ones), and a Chamfer distance loss. For physical robustness, a MaxOT-based rigid transformation and benign resampling step are applied before each iteration to suppress digital adversarial strength from non-shape-altering artifacts.

## Results

HiT-ADV achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) across all four model-dataset combinations (PointNet/DGCNN on ModelNet40/ShapeNet Part), while simultaneously attaining the best imperceptibility scores: CSD of 0.4709 (ModelNet40/PointNet) and 0.1946 (ModelNet40/DGCNN), and Uniform of 0.2883 and 0.2867 respectively. On ShapeNet Part, CSD drops to 0.9810 (PointNet) and 0.6629 (DGCNN), still the lowest among all methods. Against four defense methods (SRS, SOR, DUP-Net, AT), HiT-ADV retains 84.95–99.76% ASR on PointNet and 66.82–100% on DGCNN, substantially outperforming all baselines which drop to 11–86%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiT-ADV dominates on the CSD metric across all settings, reducing curvature standard deviation difference by 50–90% compared to the next-best method (e.g., 0.4709 vs. 0.9500 for 3D-ADV on ModelNet40/PointNet; 0.1946 vs. 1.0206 for 3D-ADV on ModelNet40/DGCNN). On the Uniform metric, HiT-ADV also achieves the lowest values (0.2883/0.2867 on ModelNet40; 0.1874/0.1870 on ShapeNet Part), outperforming even KNN [35] which uses explicit KNN regularization. The KNN distance metric is the one area where HiT-ADV is slightly behind KNN [35] (0.7447 vs. 0.6077 on ModelNet40/PointNet), but remains competitive with other methods. Against defenses (Table 2), HiT-ADV's advantage is most dramatic: under SOR it retains 87.86% ASR on PointNet versus 18.76% for GeoA³ and 24.25% for 3D-ADV, and under adversarial training it achieves 99.76% (PointNet) and 100% (DGCNN) while all baselines drop below 78%. The shape-based baselines MeshAttack [49] and Eps-iso [5] achieve comparable ASR but suffer from much higher CSD (2.2–3.7) and visible surface distortions, confirming that HiT-ADV's concealment-in-complex-areas strategy is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiT-ADV builds on the C&W-style gradient optimization framework for point cloud attacks (Xiang et al., 2019) and the Gaussian kernel deformation mechanism introduced by Liu et al. (2020), but departs from both by introducing a principled region-selection strategy: rather than applying perturbations uniformly or minimizing them globally, it explicitly targets areas of high local curvature variation where human visual sensitivity is lowest. The saliency scoring follows Zheng et al. (2019), and the physical-attack pipeline borrows the MaxOT rigid-transformation suppression from Dong et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure from prior shape-based attacks (MeshAttack, Eps-iso) is the argument that imperceptibility should be achieved by *where* perturbations are placed, not by *how small* they are made, which reframes the imperceptibility-adversarial-strength trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that point-based adversarial training, a standard defense, is ineffective against shape-based attacks, exposing a gap in current 3D robustness research that remains open. The physical-attack validation (3D printing + re-scanning) is important for real-world LiDAR security, though the demonstration is limited to a small number of objects. A key open question is whether the curvature-based hiding strategy generalizes to other 3D tasks (segmentation, detection) or to point cloud models with different inductive biases. The introduced CSD metric, while intuitive, is a new evaluation criterion that the community has not yet adopted, so its long-term utility is uncertain.
