## Problem

Multi-person 3D pose estimation from Wi-Fi signals remained an unsolved challenge: prior work had achieved single-person 2D/3D pose and multi-person 2D pose, but no system could estimate 3D poses for multiple people simultaneously. Naively extending the 2D Person-in-WiFi framework to 3D (via 3D heatmaps and part affinity fields) failed to converge due to excessive network size, slow training, coarse spatial resolution, error accumulation from non-end-to-end post-processing, and prohibitive storage requirements (~2TB for all frames).

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw Wi-Fi Channel State Information (CSI) from 1 transmitter and 3 receivers (each with 3 antennas, 30 subcarriers, 20 time samples), yielding a 1×3×3×30×20 tensor per frame. After tokenization, this becomes 180 tokens of dimension 1×60 (concatenated amplitude and PhaseFi-denoised phase per subcarrier), upsampled to 1×256. The output is a set of up to 100 predicted 3D poses (14 keypoints × 3 coordinates each) with confidence scores, from which G actual persons are selected via Hungarian matching (training) or top-confidence ranking (inference), followed by a refine decoder that adjusts only the selected bodies' joint coordinates.

## Method

Person-in-WiFi 3D formulates multi-person 3D pose estimation as a set-prediction problem solved end-to-end by a Transformer with three modules: a 6-layer Wi-Fi encoder (multi-head self-attention + FFN) that processes 180 spatial-temporal tokens with learnable embeddings; a 3-layer DETR-style pose decoder with 100 learnable queries that predicts cumulative offsets (Deformable-DETR-inspired) to produce initial 3D poses; and a 3-layer refine decoder that fine-tunes joint coordinates for only the G matched bodies. Training uses a set-based Hungarian loss combining focal loss for person classification and MSE for keypoint regression, eliminating the need for separate pose grouping. The key architectural insight is replacing the 2D predecessor's heatmap/PAF regression and post-hoc grouping with direct set prediction, reducing storage from ~2TB to ~200MB and enabling 54fps inference versus ~10s per frame for the 3D-extended baseline.

## Results

Person-in-WiFi 3D achieves MPJPE of 91.7 mm in single-person, 108.1 mm in two-person, and 125.3 mm in three-person scenarios, with an overall MPJPE of 107.2 mm across all test samples. Per-dimension errors (MPJDLE) are 53.5 mm (horizontal), 49.4 mm (vertical), and 62.4 mm (depth). The largest per-joint errors occur at the hands (182.1 mm left, 202.9 mm right), while the hips show the smallest (~73–75 mm). The system runs at 54 fps for 3D pose output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Person-in-WiFi 3D outperforms the independently reproduced WiPose baseline on overall MPJPE (91.7 mm vs. 101.8 mm) and on both vertical (43.3 vs. 47.8 mm) and depth (50.0 vs. 58.0 mm) dimensions, while WiPose holds a marginal 0.3 mm edge on the horizontal dimension (42.1 vs. 42.4 mm). The paper also contextualizes its results against camera-based multi-person 3D pose estimators (54–70 mm MPJPE) and a mmWave radar system (29.9 mm MPJPE), positioning Person-in-WiFi 3D as comparable in accuracy while offering privacy and occlusion advantages. Applying PETR directly to the Wi-Fi task without the proposed enhancements yields 219.0 mm MPJPE, underscoring the contribution of the Wi-Fi-specific encoder and refine decoder design. The authors note that their upper bound is constrained by the Azure Kinect SDK annotations, which degrade beyond three persons, so true performance may be higher with more accurate ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Person-in-WiFi (multi-person 2D pose from Wi-Fi) to the 3D setting by replacing the heatmap-and-part-affinity-field pipeline with a DETR-style set-prediction architecture, drawing on Deformable-DETR's per-layer offset prediction and PETR's refinement decoder. It also generalizes single-person 3D Wi-Fi pose estimation (WiPose, GoPose, Winect) to multiple simultaneous persons, a combination that prior work explicitly could not handle due to the absence of person-segmentation or keypoint-grouping strategies. The tokenization of CSI samples with spatial-temporal embeddings and the use of Hungarian matching for set-based pose assignment are the key architectural departures from the convolutional, heatmap-based lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first system to achieve multi-person 3D pose estimation from commodity Wi-Fi, this work opens a privacy-preserving, occlusion-robust sensing modality for applications like elderly care and smart homes where cameras are impractical. The released 97K-frame dataset is the first publicly available Wi-Fi pose dataset, which should accelerate follow-up research. However, significant open questions remain: cross-environment generalization collapses (626 mm MPJPE), the Azure Kinect SDK annotations cap the achievable accuracy, and arm-joint errors (~200 mm) are roughly double body-joint errors, limiting fine-grained action recognition. The consistent ~17 mm per-person error increment suggests the bottleneck is algorithmic rather than a fundamental Wi-Fi signal limitation, pointing toward better architectures or more diverse training data as the path forward.
