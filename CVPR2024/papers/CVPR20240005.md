## Problem

Monocular 3D lane detection methods rely on discrete representations (grids, anchors, keypoints) or limited continuous curves that cannot easily incorporate physical priors about lane structure and road geometry. This makes them prone to unpredictable behavior in rare or out-of-distribution scenarios due to limited training data. The paper asks how to build a flexible continuous 3D lane representation that can analytically enforce physical constraints (parallelism, smoothness, curvature) as soft regularization, while also learning geometry-aware 3D features without relying on flat-ground assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular front-view RGB image at 360×480 resolution and produces 64 lane proposals, each parameterized as a full 3D B-Spline (degree 3, 10 control points) where each control point is defined by two scalar offsets (α_k, β_k) from an initial proposal along its normal directions. Additionally, the network outputs a per-proposal visibility spline (sigmoid-activated, same B-Spline configuration) and a 26×16 BEV grid encoding the estimated road surface height z at each cell, which is supervised by a regression loss against interpolated lane annotation heights.

## Method

LaneCPP introduces a full 3D B-Spline lane representation where each control point's degrees of freedom are constrained to two normal-direction offsets (one in the x-y plane, one in the y-z plane) relative to diverse initial proposals, resolving the overfitting ambiguity of unconstrained 3D spline regression. Physical priors are enforced as analytical regularization losses computed directly on the parametric curves: a parallelism loss penalizes cosine distance between unit tangents of neighboring lines at normal point pairs, a surface smoothness loss penalizes cosine distance between cross-product-derived surface normals, and a curvature loss thresholds second-order tangent derivatives in the x-y and z directions separately. For 3D feature extraction, a spatial transformation module inspired by Lift-Splat-Shoot lifts multi-scale front-view features along rays onto S=5 plane-based road surface hypotheses, weights them by a learned depth distribution, and aggregates weighted sums onto a BEV grid, with an auxiliary surface regression loss anchoring the learned height map to ground truth. The full training objective combines focal losses for lane presence and category, a regression loss for spline parameters, binary cross-entropy for visibility, the prior regularization terms, and the surface regression loss, all optimized with Adam on a modified EfficientNet backbone.

## Results

On the real-world OpenLane benchmark, LaneCPP achieves a state-of-the-art F1-Score of 60.3%, outperforming the previous best (BEV-LaneDet at 58.4%) by +1.9% and Anchor3DLane-T (54.3%) by +6.0%. It also attains the lowest geometric errors across all ranges: X-near 0.264 m, X-far 0.310 m, Z-near 0.077 m, and Z-far 0.117 m. On the synthetic Apollo 3D Synthetic dataset, LaneCPP reaches 97.4% F1 on balanced scenes (best) and 96.2% on rare scenes (second to BEV-LaneDet's 97.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaneCPP outperforms all eight baselines on OpenLane F1-Score, with the largest margins over Gen-LaneNet (+28.0%) and 3D-LaneNet (+16.2%). Against the closest competitor BEV-LaneDet (58.4%), LaneCPP gains +1.9% F1 while dramatically reducing Z-far error from 0.631 m to 0.117 m (−81%), indicating far superior 3D geometric accuracy. Compared to Anchor3DLane-T (54.3%), the F1 gap is +6.0%, and X-errors are marginally better (0.264 vs 0.275 m near), while Z-errors are substantially lower (0.077 vs 0.105 m near). On the synthetic Apollo 3D Synthetic dataset, LaneCPP achieves the best F1 on balanced scenes (97.4% vs BEV-LaneDet's 96.9%) but trails BEV-LaneDet slightly on rare scenes (96.2% vs 97.6%), suggesting the physical priors provide less advantage on the simpler synthetic scenarios. The per-scenario breakdown on OpenLane highlights a +5.9% gain on up/down-hill sequences over BEV-LaneDet, directly attributable to the road-surface-aware spatial transformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaneCPP extends the authors' own 3D-SplineNet [32] by upgrading its 2D (x,z) B-Spline lane representation to a full 3D (x,y,z) formulation, resolving the monotonicity limitation that restricted the prior work to synthetic data. The key conceptual departure is the use of physical priors—line parallelism, surface smoothness, and curvature—as soft regularization losses computed analytically on the continuous spline, in contrast to hard-constraint approaches (deep declarative networks) or discrete-representation-based priors (GP, SGNet) that require simplifications. For spatial transformation, the paper moves beyond the flat-ground IPM assumption prevalent in 3D-LaneNet, Gen-LaneNet, and CurveFormer, instead adopting a depth-classification strategy inspired by Lift-Splat-Shoot [31] that models multiple road surface hypotheses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that encoding physical knowledge as differentiable regularization on a continuous parametric representation yields measurable gains in both detection accuracy and geometric precision, particularly in challenging real-world scenarios (up/down hills, occlusions, poor visibility) where purely data-driven methods degrade. This provides a principled path toward more predictable and safety-oriented lane detection without requiring additional inference-time computation. Open questions include whether the plane-based surface hypotheses can be replaced with richer geometry, whether the parallelism indicator function can be made more robust to weakly converging line pairs, and whether scenario-specific prior weighting could further improve performance on rare driving situations.
