## Problem

Conventional VOS methods rely on high-quality visual inputs for similarity-based matching between query and memory frames, but under low-light conditions the degraded image quality causes inaccurate matching and significant performance drops. No prior work has leveraged event cameras—which provide high dynamic range and rich motion/edge cues—to assist VOS in low-light settings, and no dedicated dataset combining frames and events under low illumination existed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-light APS frames (cropped to 465×465 on LLE-DAVIS or 256×256 on LLE-VOS), temporally synchronized event streams converted into voxel grids of shape B×H×W, and a single initial segmentation mask annotation (semi-supervised setting). It outputs a per-frame binary segmentation mask for each tracked object, with the mask at each time step stored in a memory bank to guide subsequent matching.

## Method

Built on AOT's matching-based architecture with a memory bank, the framework introduces two modules. The Adaptive Cross-Modal Fusion (ACMF) module merges multi-scale image and event features, then uses the combined feature as an adaptive filter via element-wise multiplication to suppress noise; channel and spatial attention further extract edge cues from events to restore structural details lost in low-light images. The Event-Guided Memory Matching (EGMM) module generates a guided signal from concatenated event and mask features in memory, which is multiplied with the previous key K_{t-1} to filter misaligned object-region features before computing attention with the current query; the matching result is combined with the current feature and decoded into a mask. Training uses a composite BCE + Soft Jaccard loss over 50K iterations with AdamW (lr 2e-4, weight decay 0.07) on four A800 GPUs, with standard augmentations plus random temporal reversal of video and event sequences.

## Results

On the real-world LLE-VOS dataset, the proposed method achieves an overall J&F of 0.678 (J=0.702, F=0.653), outperforming the best one-stage baseline DeAOT (J&F=0.633) by 4.5% and the best two-stage baseline Zero-DCE+AOT (J&F=0.607) by 7.1%. On the synthetic LLE-DAVIS dataset, the method reaches J&F=0.628 (J=0.602, F=0.654), surpassing AOT (J&F=0.559) by 6.9% and Zero-DCE+AOT (J&F=0.561) by 6.7%. The method also shows strong indoor performance (J&F=0.749) on LLE-VOS, indicating particular strength in controlled low-light environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four one-stage VOS methods (STCN, XMem, AOT, DeAOT) and four two-stage pipelines (Zero-DCE followed by each of the above). The proposed method wins on all comparisons across both datasets. On LLE-VOS, it outperforms the strongest one-stage baseline DeAOT by 4.5% in J&F (0.678 vs. 0.633) and the strongest two-stage baseline Zero-DCE+AOT by 7.1% (0.678 vs. 0.607). On LLE-DAVIS, the gains are even larger: +6.9% over AOT and +6.7% over Zero-DCE+AOT. Notably, the two-stage Zero-DCE enhancement actually degrades performance in real-world extremely low-light settings (e.g., Zero-DCE+DeAOT scores 0.606 vs. DeAOT alone at 0.633), whereas the event-assisted approach consistently improves over its image-only counterpart. The gap is most pronounced in indoor scenes (J&F 0.749 vs. 0.680 for DeAOT), suggesting event data is particularly valuable when illumination is most constrained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AOT (Associating Objects with Transformers) matching-based VOS architecture by introducing event camera data as an auxiliary modality under low-light conditions. It departs from the prior two-stage paradigm (Zero-DCE enhancement followed by standard VOS) by jointly processing frames and events end-to-end, and from the broader event-based vision literature (event segmentation, event-based low-light enhancement) by being the first to apply events specifically to the VOS task. The ACMF module draws on cross-modal attention mechanisms, while EGMM extends the memory-matching paradigm of AOT/XMem with an event-and-mask-guided filtering step to correct unreliable matching under degraded illumination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the first real-world event-based low-light VOS dataset (LLE-VOS) and the synthetic LLE-DAVIS dataset fills a critical gap that previously prevented systematic evaluation in this setting. A notable finding is that mask-only guidance in the matching module actually degrades performance substantially (J&F drops from 0.601 to 0.464), revealing that imprecise low-light masks are more harmful than helpful without event-based correction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to static or slowly-moving objects (where event signals are sparse), whether the synthetic low-light simulation adequately captures real-world sensor noise characteristics, and how the method scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-object scenes.
