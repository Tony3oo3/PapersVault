## Problem

iToF depth imaging requires four measurements with 90° phase shifts captured at different times, so any 3D motion between captures introduces ghosting and blur artifacts in dynamic scenes. Existing compensation methods rely on 2D optical flow, which cannot losslessly represent 3D motion, and suffer from photometric inconsistencies caused by both the heterogeneous phase shifts across modes and depth-wise displacement, making direct flow estimation ill-posed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240825/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes five consecutive alternating 4-mode iToF measurements (m_{θ0}^{t-4} through m_{θ0}^{t}) plus the modulation frequency as a conditioning signal, operating on 448×448 patches at 20 MHz or 30 MHz. It outputs the four phase-shifted measurements reconstructed at the target time t, from which depth is extracted via standard iToF demodulation. The intermediate outputs include LLT coefficient maps (K, B), three optical flow fields, and depth-dependent photometric residual maps.

## Method

The core insight is decomposing iToF measurement variation into cross-mode flow (mode-dependent photometric change plus 2D pixel shift) and uni-mode flow (depth-wise motion-induced photometric residual within the same mode), enabling sequential and separate estimation. The LCTM exploits a motion-insensitive local linear transform (LLT) derived from the two-feature property at edges to map pixel intensities between modes without explicit motion constraints, then uses a cascaded encoder-decoder (SFE) to estimate optical flow between LLT-mapped and actual measurements for pixel alignment. The UPCM, built on a compact 3D CNN (three downsampling, two upsampling layers), estimates the depth-dependent photometric residual by modeling the first-order Taylor expansion of the iToF measurement with respect to phase change. All modules are trained end-to-end with MAE losses on simulated data from Blender ray-traced iToF rendering, achieving 75% faster runtime and 38% lower depth error than the prior SOTA.

## Results

On the combined CB-ToF, CB-ToF-Extension, and iToF-flow supplement test sets, the proposed method achieves a depth MAE (MAE_d) of 4.72 cm and a photometric MAE (MAE_p) of 1.51, substantially outperforming all baselines. Compared to the SOTA method FFN [28], the depth error is reduced by 37% (4.72 vs. 7.58 cm) while runtime drops by 75% (0.081 s vs. 0.32 s on RTX 2080 Ti). The method also achieves a 0% mask rate (warping-failure rate), the lowest among all compared approaches, and uses 7.75 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FFN [28] (the SOTA iToF-specific optical flow method), the proposed method reduces depth MAE from 7.58 cm to 4.72 cm (−37%) and photometric MAE from 3.79 to 1.51 (−60%), while cutting runtime from 0.32 s to 0.081 s (−75%). Against MOM [11] (the fastest baseline at 0.028 s), the proposed method is roughly 3× slower but achieves a 67% lower depth error (4.72 vs. 14.28 cm) and a 77% lower photometric error (1.51 vs. 6.72). Against the pre-trained RGB optical flow net RAFT [31], the proposed method reduces depth error by 69% (4.72 vs. 15.40 cm) and runs 3.3× faster (0.081 s vs. 0.27 s), confirming that generic RGB flow is poorly suited to the photometric inconsistencies of iToF measurements. The proposed method also achieves a 0% mask rate, whereas RAFT, FFN, and MOM all exhibit 0.45–0.82% warping failures. In speed-generalization tests (up to 2× the training speed), the proposed method maintains the lowest error across all speed ratios, and on real-world Sony IMX518 data it more accurately recovers finger gaps and fast-moving box contours than both FFN and MOM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior iToF motion-compensation methods (MOM, FFN) that rely on 2D optical flow alignment by explicitly decomposing measurement variation into two physically-motivated components: cross-mode flow (mode alternation) and uni-mode flow (depth-wise motion). The cross-mode transfer leverages the local linear transform (LLT) concept originally developed for RGB-NIR deblurring [37], repurposing it to model the motion-insensitive linear mapping between different iToF demodulation phases. The uni-mode correction draws on the inverse-square law and first-order Taylor expansion of the iToF measurement model, while the spatial flow estimation reuses the hierarchical encoder-decoder architecture from FlowNet. The overall framework is inspired by dynamic cross-modality imaging (HDR, hyperspectral video) where alternating measurements of different modalities must be temporally aligned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

iToF is the dominant cost-effective 3D sensing technology in consumer and industrial applications, yet dynamic scenes remain a persistent weakness; this paper's 75% runtime reduction alongside 38% accuracy improvement over the SOTA (FFN) is practically significant for real-time deployment. The clean physical decomposition into cross-mode and uni-mode flows provides an interpretable framework that could guide future iToF sensor design and post-processing. However, validation is primarily simulation-based with only two real-world scenes, and the LLT two-feature assumption may degrade in highly textured or multi-edge regions. Open questions include generalization to higher resolutions, more extreme motion speeds, and integration with multi-sensor fusion pipelines.
