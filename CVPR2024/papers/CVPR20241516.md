## Problem

Existing character animation methods either target narrow domains (fashion, dance) or rely on CLIP-based image conditioning that discards fine-grained spatial details, leading to appearance inconsistency and temporal jitter. No prior approach simultaneously achieves generalizability across arbitrary characters and faithful preservation of intricate appearance details in generated video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference character image (any domain: full-body, portrait, cartoon, humanoid) and a pose sequence rendered as OpenPose skeleton images (extracted via DWPose). During training, frames are resized to 768×768; at inference, the driving pose skeleton is rescaled to match the reference character's proportions. Output is a 24-frame video clip per denoising pass, with longer videos assembled via temporal aggregation across batches using a DDIM sampler with 20 steps.

## Method

Built on Stable Diffusion's denoising UNet extended for multi-frame inputs, the architecture introduces three components: (1) ReferenceNet, a parallel UNet with identical structure and SD-initialized weights that extracts spatial detail features from the reference image, integrated at each layer via spatial-attention (replacing self-attention) to preserve fine-grained appearance; (2) a lightweight Pose Guider (four 4×4 conv layers with zero-convolution output) that adds pose features directly to the noise latent; and (3) a temporal self-attention layer (initialized from AnimateDiff) inserted after spatial and cross-attention blocks to model inter-frame continuity. CLIP image encoder features additionally feed cross-attention for semantic conditioning. Training is two-stage: first, single-frame denoising trains the UNet, ReferenceNet, and Pose Guider jointly; second, only the temporal layer is trained on 24-frame clips while all other weights are frozen, preserving per-frame quality while adding temporal smoothness.

## Results

On the UBC Fashion Video Dataset, Animate Anyone achieves SSIM 0.931, PSNR 38.49, LPIPS 0.044, and FVD 81.6, substantially outperforming the next-best method BDMM (FVD 148.3) and the SD-I2V baseline (FVD 175.4). On the TikTok dance dataset, it reaches FVD 171.9 versus 225.5 for SD-I2V and 292.8 for DisCo. On the Ted-talk dataset, it achieves FVD 80.5, marginally beating TPSMM (80.7) and significantly outperforming DisCo (223.5) and SD-I2V (158.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Animate Anyone outperforms all pose-guided baselines across all three benchmarks. On UBC Fashion, it leads BDMM by 66.7 FVD points (81.6 vs. 148.3) and DreamPose by 157.1 points (81.6 vs. 238.7), while also surpassing the SD-I2V composite baseline (SD + ControlNet + IP-Adapter + AnimateDiff) by 93.8 FVD points. On TikTok, it beats DisCo by 120.9 FVD points (171.9 vs. 292.8) and SD-I2V by 53.6 points, despite DisCo leveraging large-scale human attribute pre-training. On Ted-talk, the margin over TPSMM is minimal (FVD 80.5 vs. 80.7), but it still wins on SSIM (0.832 vs. 0.830) and PSNR (33.91 vs. 33.81), and decisively outperforms DisCo (FVD 223.5) and SD-I2V (FVD 158.3). For general image-to-video methods (AnimateDiff, Gen2), the comparison is qualitative only since they lack pose control; the paper shows they struggle with substantial character movements and long-term appearance consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework to multi-frame video generation, inheriting the denoising UNet architecture and pretrained weights. It draws on AnimateDiff's temporal attention design for inter-frame modeling and adapts ControlNet's condition encoder architecture into a lightweight Pose Guider. The key departure from prior character animation methods (DreamPose, DisCo) is the introduction of ReferenceNet—a symmetric UNet that extracts spatial detail features from the reference image and injects them via spatial attention—replacing the CLIP image encoder approach that loses fine-grained appearance information. The two-stage training strategy (single-frame denoising followed by temporal layer training) is a practical recipe that prior image-to-video methods did not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously achieve domain-general character animation (arbitrary characters, not just fashion or dance) and fine-grained appearance consistency, opening practical applications in retail, entertainment, and virtual character creation. The ReferenceNet + spatial attention design offers a broadly applicable recipe for image-conditioned generation where spatial misalignment between reference and target prevents direct ControlNet-style conditioning. Limitations include instability in hand movements, difficulty generating unseen body parts during large motions, and the inherent computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling. Open questions include whether the two-stage training paradigm generalizes to longer video sequences and whether the ReferenceNet approach can be extended to multi-reference or video-to-video settings.
