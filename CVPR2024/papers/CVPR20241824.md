## Problem

Naive knowledge distillation from a Vision-Language Model (VLM) teacher to a vision-only student improves in-domain accuracy but fails to transfer the VLM's superior out-of-distribution generalization, especially in a black-box setting where only input-output access is available and the student must be initialized from a separate pre-trained model (e.g., ImageNet). The core challenge is that the student's pre-trained features are misaligned with the VLM's embedding space, and directly enforcing similarity causes catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241824/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a class label via cosine similarity against the VLM's frozen text embeddings (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') used directly as classifier weights—no additional classifier training is needed at inference. During training, only labeled images are required (no image-text pairs), and the VLM teacher is queried in a black-box manner for its image and text embeddings. The student backbone (ViT-B/16, ViT-S/16, DeiT-S/16, or ResNet-50) is initialized from ImageNet-1K pre-trained weights, while the teacher is a CLIP ViT-B/16 model.

## Method

VL2V-ADiP operates in three stages: (1) Align—train a linear projection head on a frozen student backbone to map its features into the VLM's shared embedding space, minimizing negative cosine similarity to both the VLM's image embeddings and the text embedding of the ground-truth class; (2) Distill—freeze the projection head and fine-tune the student backbone with the same dual-alignment loss, transferring rich visual features from the image encoder and domain invariance from the text encoder; (3) Predict—use the VLM's text embeddings directly as the classifier, since the alignment in Stages 1–2 implicitly makes them compatible with the student's feature space. The key insight is that text embeddings encode domain-invariant class concepts (trained over ~400M diverse pairs) while image embeddings capture input-specific details; distilling from both simultaneously preserves the student's pre-trained features while injecting the VLM's generalization. A companion self-distillation variant (VL2V-SD) applies the same principle in a white-box setting by distilling the VLM's text encoder invariances into its own image encoder.

## Results

On the standard Domain Generalization benchmarks (OfficeHome, Terra-Incognita, VLCS, PACS, DomainNet) with a ViT-B/16 student and ImageNet-1K initialization, VL2V-ADiP achieves an average OOD accuracy of 75.48% and average ID accuracy of 89.02%, outperforming the strongest baseline RISE by ~1.5% in OOD accuracy. In the white-box setting, VL2V-SD achieves 77.73% average OOD accuracy with CLIP ViT-B/16 initialization, surpassing the best prior DG method (MIRO at 76.66%) and the CLIP zero-shot baseline (70.60%). The method also transfers effectively to lower-capacity students (ViT-S/16, DeiT-S/16, ResNet-50), consistently improving OOD accuracy by 1.5–3.4% over the KD and ERM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL2V-ADiP achieves the best average OOD accuracy (75.48%) across all five DG datasets, outperforming the strongest black-box distillation baseline RISE (73.97%) by 1.51% and the best DG method SAGM (72.15%) by 3.33%. On individual datasets, VL2V-ADiP wins on OfficeHome (85.74 vs. 83.75 for DART), Terra-Incognita (55.43 vs. 53.24 for SAGM), PACS (94.94 vs. 93.54 for RISE), and DomainNet (59.38 vs. 58.05 for DART), but trails RISE on VLCS (81.90 vs. 83.70). Compared to standard knowledge distillation KD (71.84% OOD), the proposed method gains 3.64% in OOD accuracy while maintaining comparable ID accuracy (89.02% vs. 89.20%). In the white-box setting (Table 2), VL2V-SD (77.73% OOD) outperforms CLIP zero-shot (70.60%), WiSE-FT (75.80%), MIRO (76.66%), and RISE (71.45%), confirming that text-embedding-driven self-distillation is the key mechanism. The method also substantially outperforms SimKD (61.87%) and Text2Concept (52.82%), which fail to handle the multimodal teacher–unimodal student mismatch. Notably, VL2V-ADiP achieves these gains without expanded prompt engineering (unlike RISE) and without access to the teacher's intermediate features (unlike feature-distillation methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the standard knowledge distillation framework (Hinton et al.) to a VLM-to-vision setting, but departs from prior KD work by recognizing that naive output distillation from CLIP fails to transfer its OOD generalization. The key conceptual departure is the empirical finding that CLIP's domain invariance resides in its text embeddings rather than its image encoder, motivating a two-stage Align-then-Distill procedure that projects a pre-trained student into the VLM's embedding space before fine-tuning. This positions the work at the intersection of CLIP fine-tuning methods (LP-FT, WiSE-FT, CLIPood), domain generalization techniques (SWAD, MIRO, DART, SAGM), and black-box distillation (RISE, SimKD), while introducing the practical constraint of only input-output access to the teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The vendor-client black-box distillation paradigm is a realistic deployment scenario for expensive VLMs, and this paper provides a principled method for transferring their generalization to cheaper student models without access to teacher weights. The insight that text embeddings carry domain invariance while image embeddings do not is a useful diagnostic for understanding VLM behavior more broadly. However, the gains over the strongest baselines are modest (~1.5% OOD), the approach requires an ImageNet-pretrained student, and it remains unclear how well the method scales to larger VLMs, non-classification tasks, or settings where the student architecture differs substantially from the teacher. An open question is whether the Align stage could be replaced by learned adapters or other parameter-efficient modules to reduce the dependency on a strong pre-trained initialization.
