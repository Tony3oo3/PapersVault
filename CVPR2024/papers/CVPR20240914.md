## Problem

Existing visual-LLMs (e.g., BLIP-2, LLaVA) produce highly descriptive textual answers yet exhibit near-random performance on basic spatial reasoning tasks such as determining whether one object is to the left or right of another. No prior work has demonstrated how to inject spatial awareness into a generic V-LLM through purely textual coordinate-based instruction fine-tuning without introducing architectural modifications or specialized location tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240914/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (224×224 for LocVLM-B, 336×336 for LocVLM-L) or sequences of video frames, combined with text prompts that may embed image-space coordinates as plain text. Coordinates are discretized via integer-valued binning (224 or 336 bins per axis) so each coordinate value occupies at most 3 BPE tokens, avoiding the 6-token cost of floating-point representations. Outputs are natural language responses that can include coordinate-based location descriptions (e.g., bounding boxes or point coordinates) or object descriptions for a queried region, all generated through standard next-token prediction without any auxiliary prediction heads.

## Method

LocVLM retains the standard LLaVA architecture (CLIP ViT encoder + linear/MLP adapter + LLaMA LLM) and introduces three instruction fine-tuning objectives—Location Prediction, Negative Prediction, and Reverse-Location Prediction—that train the LLM to both consume and generate image-space coordinates as text. The key insight is that representing locations as discrete integer bins (rather than floating-point values or specialized tokens) yields optimal performance across VQA, region description, and video tasks while keeping the architecture unmodified. A pseudo-data generation pipeline uses the model's own stage-1 checkpoint to produce object-level captions beyond COCO categories (self-training) and leverages SEEM panoptic segmentation for pseudo-bounding boxes in video (weak supervision), enabling scaling to the video domain. Training follows a two-phase schedule: adapter-only pre-training on CC3M, then joint adapter+LLM instruction fine-tuning on the 200K-sample Localize-Instruct-200K dataset, with an optional third video fine-tuning phase on ActivityNet.

## Results

On the spatial reasoning benchmark (COCO-Spatial-27K), LocVLM-B achieves 69.5% accuracy in direct VQA and 76.5% with in-context learning, substantially outperforming LLaVA (55.1%/55.1%) and BLIP-2 (45.5%/14.7%). On Image VQA, LocVLM-B reaches 47.3% on GQA and 50.3% on VQAv2 validation (zero-shot), while LocVLM-L achieves 63.5% on GQA and 78.2% on VQAv2 validation. On Video VQA, LocVLM-Vid-B achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot accuracy of 37.4% on ActivityNet-QA, 51.2% on MSRVTT-QA, 66.1% on MSVD-QA, and 51.8% on TGIF-QA. For region description, LocVLM-B scores 26.0/26.2 METEOR on RefCOCOg val/test, surpassing Shikra (19.7/19.5) and Kosmos-2 (14.3/14.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocVLM consistently outperforms LLaVA (the primary architectural baseline) across all evaluated tasks: spatial reasoning (69.5% vs 55.1% direct, 76.5% vs 55.1% ICL), Image VQA (47.3% vs 44.7% GQA zero-shot), Video VQA (37.4% vs 35.2% ActivityNet-QA), hallucination (88.3% vs 61.9% on Hal-COCO), and region description (26.0 vs 13.5 METEOR on RefCOCOg). Against the closest concurrent work Shikra [8], LocVLM wins on spatial reasoning, hallucination (88.3% vs 86.2% Hal-COCO; 75.2% vs 58.7% Hal-ADE), and region description (26.0 vs 19.7 METEOR), while Shikra achieves slightly higher VQA accuracy (75.3% vs 78.2% on VQA-V) but uses VQA training data that LocVLM does not. On video VQA, LocVLM-Vid-B surpasses Video-ChatGPT [42] by 2.2 points on ActivityNet-QA (37.4% vs 35.2%) and by 1.2–1.8 points on the other three benchmarks. LocVLM-B trails ViperGPT (175B LLM) by 0.8 points on GQA zero-shot (47.3% vs 48.1%), though ViperGPT uses a model 25× larger. On the POPE hallucination benchmark, LocVLM-B achieves 87.9% accuracy (Random) and 78.8% (Adversarial), outperforming LLaVA (50.4%/49.7%) and matching or exceeding BLIP-2 and Shikra across all three POPE splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA visual instruction tuning framework by adding three coordinate-based instruction fine-tuning objectives (LocPred, NegPred, RevLoc) that teach the LLM to read and write image-space coordinates as plain text. It departs from concurrent localization-in-VLM work (Kosmos-2, Ferret, Shikra) that introduces specialized location tokens or architectural modifications, instead treating coordinates as ordinary text within the existing LLM vocabulary. The video domain adaptation follows Video-ChatGPT's frame-averaging strategy, and the pseudo-data generation leverages self-training with the model's own stage-one outputs to overcome COCO's limited object vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SOTA V-LLMs perform near-randomly on binary spatial questions (left/right, above/below) despite generating fluent image descriptions highlights a fundamental gap in current vision-language models, and the paper shows that a simple textual coordinate fine-tuning objective closes much of this gap without architectural changes. The broad improvements across five task categories (spatial reasoning, image VQA, video VQA, hallucination, region description) suggest spatial awareness is a transferable capability rather than a task-specific skill. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to 3D spatial reasoning, more complex multi-object relational queries, and whether the discrete binning representation limits fine-grained localization at higher resolutions.
