## Problem

Existing text-to-3D human generation methods rely on text-to-image diffusion models that lack understanding of 3D geometry and view direction, resulting in Janus artifacts, smooth geometry, fake 3D details (e.g., wrinkles and hair that don't follow surface curvature), and over-saturated textures. Even methods incorporating parametric body models like SMPL fail to resolve these fundamental perceptual limitations of the underlying 2D diffusion prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241799/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt augmented with view-dependent text (e.g., 'front view') and body-aware text (e.g., 'upper body') as input, and produces a textured 3D human mesh with approximately 400K faces and a 2K-resolution texture map. The full generation pipeline requires 15K iterations for geometry and 10K for texture (~2 hours on a single RTX 3090), with final renders at 1024×1024 resolution. The output is exportable as standard mesh and texture map files suitable for downstream animation and rendering.

## Method

HumanNorm introduces a normal diffusion model with two components: a normal-adapted diffusion model fine-tuned to generate high-fidelity normal maps from prompts (with view-dependent and body-aware text conditions), and a normal-aligned diffusion model (built via ControlNet) that generates color images conditioned on normal maps to ensure texture-geometry alignment. For geometry, the method uses DMTET as the 3D representation optimized with both normal SDS and depth SDS losses, augmented by a progressive strategy that gradually unmasks high-frequency positional encoding components and adds a progressive SDF loss to prevent geometric distortions. For texture, a multi-step SDS loss recovers images through multiple denoising steps to avoid over-saturation, combined with a VGG-based perceptual loss to maintain natural appearance. Normal maps are transformed from world to camera coordinates before training to reduce view-dependent variation and encourage the model to focus on geometric detail.

## Results

HumanNorm achieves the best FID of 92.5 and highest CLIP Score of 31.70 among all compared methods, evaluated over 30 prompts with 120 rendered/generated images per prompt. The FID is computed between rendered views of generated 3D humans and Stable Diffusion V1.5 images, while CLIP Score measures prompt-image alignment. The generation process requires 15K iterations for geometry and 10K for texture, taking approximately 2 hours on a single RTX 3090 GPU, producing 1024×1024 outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanNorm outperforms all six baselines on both FID and CLIP Score. Against the strongest text-to-3D human baselines, HumanNorm reduces FID by 18.8 over DreamHuman (92.5 vs. 111.3) and by 27.5 over TADA (92.5 vs. 120.0), while improving CLIP Score by 1.55 and 1.05 respectively. Against general text-to-3D content methods, the margins are larger: FID drops by 28.1 over Fantasia3D, 50.3 over TEXTure, 52.7 over DreamFusion, and 60.1 over LatentNeRF. Qualitatively, HumanNorm produces more detailed geometry (clothing wrinkles, distinct facial features) and less over-saturated textures compared to DreamHuman and TADA, which rely on SMPL-X/imGHUM priors but still exhibit fake 3D details and smooth geometry. The ablation studies confirm that the normal-adapted/depth-adapted diffusion models are critical for geometry quality, the normal-aligned diffusion model prevents blurry misaligned textures, and the multi-step SDS loss eliminates over-saturation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HumanNorm extends the diffusion-guided 3D generation framework established by DreamFusion (SDS loss) and the geometry-texture disentanglement pipeline of Fantasia3D, but departs from both by replacing the generic T2I diffusion model with purpose-built normal-adapted and normal-aligned diffusion models. Where Fantasia3D aligned rendered normal maps against the RGB image distribution (an ill-posed objective), HumanNorm trains a dedicated text-to-normal diffusion model so that geometry is guided by a distribution that actually matches the target modality. The normal-aligned diffusion model, built on ControlNet, further distinguishes this work from prior texture-generation approaches that lack explicit geometric conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The normal-map-as-intermediate-representation strategy offers a practical and generalizable recipe for improving 3D geometry quality in any SDS-based pipeline, potentially transferable beyond humans to other object categories. However, the method still requires a rigged skeleton for animation, may produce undesired shading, and struggles with fine details like fingers—limitations the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether the normal-adapted diffusion approach scales to more complex scenes, whether PBR-based material estimation can eliminate the shading artifacts, and whether the 2-hour generation time per model is acceptable for interactive applications.
