## Problem

Multi-view human scene analysis in bird's-eye view (BEV) has traditionally required either pre-given camera calibration parameters, a real top-view image (e.g., from a UAV), or depth sensors such as LiDAR. This paper addresses the calibration-free setting where the only input is a set of RGB images from first-person views, and the goal is to simultaneously recover both the camera poses and the subject positions/orientations in a unified virtual BEV — a problem that had not been studied before.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240166/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N (N ≥ 2) synchronous RGB images from first-person-view cameras as the sole input, with no BEV image, no camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and no depth information. It produces a single virtual BEV plane containing 3-DOF poses (x, y, θ in meters and degrees) for every camera and every subject, along with cross-view subject identity assignments. At inference time the number of views is unconstrained: one view is designated as reference and all others are registered into it.

## Method

The framework alternates between subject and camera registration, exploiting their mutual dependence. A View-Transform Module (VTM) first uses PifPaf for 2D skeleton detection and a lightweight fully-connected LocoNet to regress each person's BEV position and face orientation per view. A Spatial Alignment Module (SAM) then matches subjects across views via ResNet-50 appearance features and computes K candidate relative camera poses by solving a 2D rotation-translation alignment on matched subject pairs. A centroid-distance selection strategy picks the final camera pose from the K candidates, after which subjects are fused across views using spatial distance, angle difference, and appearance similarity matrices with cycle-consistency and uniqueness constraints solved via union-find and a Prim-algorithm-based hierarchical maximum spanning subgraph. A self-supervised loss further trains the appearance network by matching its predicted similarity matrix against a spatial-aware pseudo-label derived from the registration results, closing the loop between the two registration tasks.

## Results

On CSRD-II, the proposed method achieves a camera position error of 0.89 m (Cam.Pos.Avg) and orientation error of 5.78° (Cam.Ori.Avg), with 72.4% of predictions within 1 m and 94.8% within 15° of ground truth. For subject registration, the method reaches 0.75 m average position error and 14.67° average orientation error, with 81.4% of subjects within 1 m. On the real-world CSRD-R dataset, the method achieves 82.50%–86.31% detection accuracy across 2–4 views, demonstrating cross-domain generalization from synthetic to real data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on both camera and subject registration. For camera registration, the best baseline Monoloco++ [8] achieves 3.00 m position error and 21.84° orientation error, while the proposed method reduces these to 0.89 m and 5.78° respectively—a 3.4× improvement in position and 3.8× in orientation. Key-point matching methods (SIFT, LoFTR, SuperGlue, CVNet, R2Former) essentially fail, producing 7–14 m position errors due to the large field-of-view differences and human-dominated scenes lacking distinctive textures. DMHA [23], which requires a real BEV image as input, still lags behind with 5.99 m error. For subject registration, the proposed method (0.75 m avg. position error) beats Monoloco++ (1.32 m) by 43% and MVDetr/MVDet (2.41–2.44 m, which require camera calibration) by over 3×. The self-supervised subject association strategy improves F1 from 66.78% (ResNet-50 baseline [24]) to 85.98%, approaching the oracle supervised result of 86.43%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating a joint camera-and-subject registration problem in BEV that departs from three established lines: multi-view pedestrian detection (which requires pre-given camera calibration), complementary-view analysis (which requires a real BEV image from a UAV), and classical camera pose estimation (which relies on local descriptor matching that fails under large viewpoint differences). It repurposes deep human localization (PifPaf + LocoNet), multi-view geometry (rigid alignment of subject positions), and person Re-ID features (ResNet-50) into a unified framework where subject registration and camera registration are solved alternately through their mutual dependence. The use of union-find and a Prim-inspired hierarchical spanning subgraph for subject matching under cycle-consistency and uniqueness constraints is a novel algorithmic contribution not seen in prior multi-view detection or Re-ID literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both the need for pre-calibrated cameras and a dedicated BEV sensor, this work addresses a significant practical deployment barrier for multi-view human scene analysis in surveillance, social understanding, and outdoor detection. The ablation showing that the method is robust to missing camera orientation supervision (w/o GT δ_θ drops performance only marginally) is practically important since orientation is hard to annotate in the wild. Open questions include scalability beyond 5 views, handling dynamic or occluded scenes, extending to non-pedestrian subjects, and closing the remaining synthetic-to-real gap (the real-world case in Figure 8 still shows occasional wrong fusions). The introduced CSRD datasets (synthetic and real) provide a much-needed benchmark for this previously unstudied problem.
