## Problem

One-stage end-to-end keypoint regression methods for multi-person pose estimation are efficient but produce a single deterministic output, making them prone to missed or false detections in crowded or occluded scenes where pose ambiguity is high. No prior one-stage approach models the image-conditioned pose distribution to reason about such ambiguity in a generative fashion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240365/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and outputs a fixed set of N_i=100 candidate poses, each consisting of 2D keypoint coordinates (14 for CrowdPose, 17 for COCO) and a human bounding box with a class score. During inference, the model starts from pure Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises via DDIM sampling over T steps, with each step conditioned on image features extracted by a ResNet-50 backbone and a deformable-attention Transformer encoder. Keypoint coordinates are normalized by head length and bounding-box offset before entering the diffusion process.

## Method

DiffusionRegPose reformulates one-stage keypoint regression as a denoising diffusion process: during training, GT poses are corrupted with Gaussian noise (signal scale ζ=5) and the network learns to predict clean poses, boxes, and class scores via a diffusion decoder. A key architectural choice is the attention-based information interaction between pose denoising and human detection: coarse keypoint tokens are expanded from human-box queries (Human-to-Keypoint module) and cross-attended with noisy-pose self-attention tokens, enabling mutual guidance between the two sub-tasks. To handle occluded keypoints, the paper introduces a probabilistic completion step that fits a multivariate Gaussian over visible keypoints (using Cholesky decomposition) and solves a least-squares problem to assign rational coordinates to invisible ones. At inference, DDIM sampling progressively refines noisy poses over T iterative steps, with the full image-encoding pipeline (backbone → encoder → detection decoder → cross-attention → diffusion decoder) executed at each step.

## Results

On CrowdPose, DiffusionRegPose (ResNet-50) achieves 72.7 AP, 91.1 AP50, and 79.3 AP75, surpassing the closest end-to-end competitor ED-Pose (69.9 AP) by 2.8 AP overall and by a notable 4.0 AP on the hardest crowded subset (AP_H: 64.9 vs 60.9). On COCO val2017, it reaches 72.5 AP with ResNet-50, outperforming all end-to-end one-stage methods (GroupPose 72.0, ED-Pose 71.6, PETR 68.8) and bottom-up methods using heavier HRNet-w32 backbones. The human detection AP (AP_b) also improves by 2.9% over ED-Pose on CrowdPose (63.1 vs 60.2), confirming better robustness to occlusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most directly comparable end-to-end method ED-Pose (same ResNet-50 backbone and BR+KR loss), DiffusionRegPose wins on every CrowdPose metric: +2.8 AP overall, +1.6 AP_E, +2.7 AP_M, and +4.0 AP_H, with the largest gain in the hardest crowded scenes. On COCO, it also beats ED-Pose by 0.9 AP (72.5 vs 71.6) and GroupPose by 0.5 AP (72.5 vs 72.0), while surpassing PETR by 3.7 AP and QueryPose by 3.8 AP. Among top-down methods, DiffusionRegPose is competitive with HRFormer-B on CrowdPose (72.7 vs 72.4 AP) despite using a much lighter ResNet-50 backbone, but trails the strongest top-down entry DiffusionPose (75.9 AP) on COCO, which relies on HRNet-w32 and an external detector. It decisively outperforms all bottom-up baselines (HrHRNet, DEKR, SWAHR, LOGO-CAP) on both datasets by at least 2.9 AP, and exceeds the ResNet-50 top-down SimpleBaseline by 11.9 AP on CrowdPose. The human detection AP_b (63.1) also surpasses ED-Pose (60.2) by 2.9 points on CrowdPose, indicating the diffusion denoising process improves instance-level localization under occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the DiffusionDet paradigm [3]—which reframes object detection as iterative denoising of noisy boxes—to the multi-person pose estimation domain, converting deterministic keypoint regression into a diffusion-based sampling process. Architecturally, it builds on the ED-Pose [41] decoder design and loss formulation, but introduces a cross-attention mechanism that couples pose denoising with human detection in a mutually informative loop, a design absent in prior one-stage methods (PETR, QueryPose, GroupPose). It also departs from prior diffusion-based pose work (DiffPose, Diffupose, D3DP) by operating entirely in 2D without requiring a separate 2D-to-3D lifting step, and from DiffusionPose [26] by being fully end-to-end rather than relying on an external human detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized weakness of deterministic regression—its inability to model pose ambiguity under occlusion—by introducing a generative sampling process, yielding a meaningful 4.0 AP_H gain on CrowdPose's hardest subset. The probabilistic keypoint completion via MLE under a multivariate Gaussian (solved with Cholesky decomposition) is a practical, low-cost technique that could be adopted by other regression-based pose estimators. However, the human detection AP (46.5%) remains substantially below dedicated detectors (56.4%), and the iterative DDIM sampling at inference introduces latency that may limit real-time deployment; whether the diffusion formulation scales to even denser scenes or benefits from stronger backbones (e.g., Swin, ViT) remains open.
