## Problem

Existing multi-modal 3D representation learning methods (e.g., ULIP, OpenShape) under-utilize the 2D image modality relative to text, because rendered images from 3D shapes suffer a domain gap from natural images on which CLIP was pre-trained, and because image features (visual attributes) and text features (semantics) encode subtly different information, making simultaneous alignment of a single 3D feature with both modalities sub-optimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242051/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes triplets of 3D point clouds, multi-view rendered 2D images (4 views per shape found optimal), and text descriptions generated by vision-language models like BLIP. It produces two decoupled 3D feature sub-spaces per shape: a vision-focusing feature and a semantic-focusing feature. At inference, zero-shot classification sums similarity scores from both sub-spaces against CLIP text embeddings, while linear probing concatenates the two sub-space features before a single learnable linear layer.

## Method

TAMM is a two-stage pre-training framework built on three lightweight adapter modules. In Stage 1, a CLIP Image Adapter (CIA)—a two-layer MLP with a residual connection—is fine-tuned via contrastive learning on synthetic image-text pairs to re-align rendered image features with text features, closing the domain gap (contrastive accuracy rises from 40.1% to 60.9%). In Stage 2, two independent adapters (Image Alignment Adapter and Text Alignment Adapter) are attached to the 3D backbone, decoupling the 3D feature into a visual sub-space aligned with the adapted image space and a semantic sub-space aligned with the text space, each via contrastive loss over multi-view rendered images. The 3D encoder (Point-BERT or SparseConv) is trained end-to-end with these adapters while CLIP encoders remain frozen, and the two-stage ordering (CIA first, then Dual Adapters) outperforms joint one-stage training.

## Results

On the most challenging long-tailed Objaverse-LVIS benchmark (1,156 categories), TAMM pre-trained on the Ensembled dataset with Point-BERT achieves 50.7% Top-1 zero-shot accuracy, surpassing OpenShape (46.8%) by +3.9% and ULIP (26.8%) by +23.9%. On ModelNet40, TAMM reaches 85.0% Top-1 zero-shot and 93.5% linear probing accuracy, and on the 5-way 10-shot few-shot setting it achieves 99.0% accuracy (vs. 96.1% for OpenShape). On real-world ScanNet recognition, TAMM attains 49.4% mean Top-1 accuracy, outperforming OpenShape (45.6%) and CLIP² (38.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMM consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluation task. Against the two most directly comparable multi-modal pre-training methods, ULIP [45] and OpenShape [23], TAMM gains +23.9% and +3.9% Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS respectively (Ensembled, Point-BERT), with the gap widening on the long-tailed distribution. On ModelNet40 zero-shot, TAMM (85.0%) surpasses OpenShape (84.4%) and ULIP (75.1%); on ScanObjectNN it leads by +3.5% over OpenShape (55.7% vs. 52.2%). Against earlier CLIP-based 3D methods, TAMM dominates: PointCLIP [51] and PointCLIP v2 [56] achieve only 4.7% and 63.6% Top-1 on ModelNet40 respectively, while CLIP2Point [19] and ReCon [32] (ShapeNet-only pre-training) score 49.5% and 61.2%. On the real-world ScanNet recognition task, TAMM (49.4% avg) beats OpenShape (45.6%) by +3.8% and CLIP² [49] (38.5%) by +10.9%, confirming gains transfer to uncontrolled real-world scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAMM extends the multi-modal 3D representation learning paradigm established by ULIP and OpenShape, which align 3D point cloud features with CLIP-extracted image and text features via contrastive learning. The key departure is the identification of two specific failure modes in prior methods: a domain gap between rendered 3D-projected images and the natural images on which CLIP was trained, and a fundamental focus mismatch between image features (visual attributes) and text features (semantics). TAMM addresses these through a two-stage pipeline with three lightweight adapters (CIA, IAA, TAA), repurposing the adapter concept from domain adaptation literature for the first time in multi-modal 3D pre-training, and introducing a decoupled dual-subspace strategy for 3D features that has no direct precedent in the ULIP/OpenShape line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the image modality, long assumed to be equally useful as text in CLIP-based 3D pre-training, was actually contributing far less than expected due to a solvable domain shift — a finding that reframes how practitioners should think about multi-modal 3D pre-training. The adapter-based approach is lightweight and modular, making it easy to integrate into existing pipelines, and the consistent gains across multiple 3D backbones (Point-BERT, SparseConv), datasets, and downstream tasks (zero-shot, linear probing, few-shot, real-world) suggest broad practical applicability. However, the authors acknowledge they could not validate the approach at billion-parameter scale, leaving open whether the decoupling strategy and domain adaptation remain effective as 3D backbones grow substantially larger. The finding that image and text contribute nearly equally in TAMM (vs. text dominating in prior methods) also raises the question of whether similar modality-imbalance issues exist in other cross-modal transfer settings beyond 3D.
