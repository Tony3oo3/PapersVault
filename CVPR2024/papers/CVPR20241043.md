## Problem

Existing exposure correction methods face a fundamental efficiency-quality tradeoff: dense pixel-wise transformations scale linearly with resolution (e.g., SID requires 219G FLOPs at 1024×1024), while learnable 3D LUTs are efficient but perform only global transformations that ignore local context, producing sub-optimal contrast in specific regions. Additionally, prior LUT-based methods rely on uniform downsampling that discards color information in texture-rich areas, limiting the LUT's learned color mapping capability.

## Input / Output

The method accepts over- or underexposed RGB images (including non-uniform illumination where both over- and underexposure coexist) at arbitrary resolutions up to 3840×2160 UHD, and outputs a single exposure-corrected RGB image at the same resolution. The network internally downsamples to 256×256 for feature extraction, but the 3D LUT-based global transformation is resolution-independent and is applied directly to the full-resolution input, enabling real-time processing of high-resolution images without tiling.

## Method

CoTF integrates three components: (1) a global transformation using multiple image-adaptive 3D LUTs (3 basis LUTs of dimension 17³, linearly combined with content-dependent weights) that adjusts overall appearance and preserves high-frequency details; (2) a pixel-wise encoder-decoder at low resolution with a simplified ENC module that captures low-frequency local context; and (3) a Relation-Aware Modulation (RAM) module that performs cross-resolution interaction via channel-dimension cross-attention between the high-resolution global features and low-resolution pixel features, avoiding blurring from naive upsampling. An adaptive sampling strategy predicts image-dependent sampling intervals (via a lightweight CNN + Softmax + accumulation) to densely sample colorful regions and sparsely sample flat regions, providing higher-quality input data for 3D LUT learning. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with L1, perceptual, and SSIM losses, requiring only 1.81G FLOPs and 0.0095s per 1024×1024 image.

## Results

On the MSEC dataset, CoTF achieves the best average PSNR of 23.44 dB, SSIM of 0.8728, and LPIPS of 0.1232, outperforming the next-best FECNet+ERL (23.15 dB PSNR). On SICE, CoTF reaches 21.51 dB PSNR and 0.7151 SSIM, surpassing FECNet (20.96 dB) and ENC-DRBN (20.49 dB). On the LCDP dataset, CoTF attains 23.89 dB PSNR, 0.8581 SSIM, and 0.1035 LPIPS while requiring only 1.81G FLOPs and 0.0095 s inference time on a 1024×1024 image—roughly 2% of FECNet's FLOPs and 93% fewer than LCDPNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTF outperforms all 19 baselines on PSNR across all three datasets. On MSEC, it leads FECNet+ERL by 0.29 dB and FECNet by 0.32 dB in average PSNR, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.1232 vs. 0.1419 for FECNet). On SICE, CoTF surpasses FECNet by 0.55 dB PSNR and ENC-DRBN by 1.02 dB. On LCDP, CoTF improves over the second-best LCDPNet by 0.65 dB PSNR and 0.0161 SSIM. In terms of efficiency, CoTF requires only 1.81G FLOPs—just 2% of FECNet's 94.61G and 93% fewer than LCDPNet's 27.12G—while running in 0.0095 s, making it 80% faster than LCDPNet and 92.5% faster than FECNet on 1024×1024 inputs. Traditional methods (HE, CLAHE, LIME, WVM) and Retinex-based approaches (RetinexNet, URetinexNet) trail by 5–8 dB in PSNR, confirming the advantage of learned exposure correction. The only method faster than CoTF is Zero-DCE++ (0.0024 s), but it lags by 5.47 dB in PSNR, illustrating the quality–speed trade-off that CoTF resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms in exposure correction: learnable 3D LUTs (Zeng et al. [49], AdaINT [42]) that perform efficient global color mapping, and pixel-wise encoder-decoder networks (SID [9], MSEC [1], FECNet [16]) that learn dense spatially-varying transformations. It builds directly on the image-adaptive 3D LUT formulation of Zeng et al. [49] and the exposure normalization module of ENC [15], but departs from both by (1) combining global and local transformations in a single collaborative framework via a cross-resolution relation-aware modulation module, and (2) replacing the uniform bilinear downsampling used by all prior LUT-based methods with a content-adaptive sampling strategy that learns where to concentrate sampling density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CoTF achieves near-SOTA quality at roughly 2% of the FLOPs of comparable pixel-wise methods (1.81G vs. 94.61G for FECNet) and can process 4K UHD images in real-time on a single GPU where most competing methods run out of memory. This makes high-quality exposure correction viable on mobile and embedded platforms for applications like autonomous driving. Open questions include whether the collaborative global-plus-local framework generalizes beyond exposure correction to other enhancement tasks (denoising, super-resolution), whether the adaptive sampling strategy transfers to other LUT-based or downsampling-dependent pipelines, and whether the fixed 17³ LUT dimension is optimal or whether larger/smaller tables would shift the quality-efficiency tradeoff.
