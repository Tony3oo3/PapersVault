## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution methods such as StableSR inevitably introduce perceptual artifacts—implausible textures, spurious details, and noise—during iterative denoising, violating the content integrity of the source image. Existing approaches either preserve structural fidelity at the cost of artifacts or suppress artifacts by over-smoothing, and no training-free mechanism simultaneously addresses both failure modes within the latent diffusion pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242614/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single low-resolution RGB image, bicubically upsamples it to the target resolution, and encodes it into the latent space of a pre-trained LDM (StableSR). It outputs a high-resolution RGB image at arbitrary integer scales (×2, ×3, ×4) after 100 denoising steps (half the standard 200). No additional training data, degradation models, or auxiliary networks are required at inference time beyond the pre-trained encoder, decoder, and the PAL artifact detector.

## Method

SARGD is a training-free inference-time intervention with two alternating mechanisms. Reality-Guided Refinement (RGR) decodes the current latent at each step, runs the PAL artifact detector to produce a binary artifact mask, and replaces masked latent regions with values from a realistic latent (the initial encoding of the upscaled LR image), thereby removing implausible content. Self-Adaptive Guidance (SAG) counteracts the over-smoothing introduced by RGR: it computes a 'reality score' (the normalized fraction of artifact-free pixels in the refined latent) and, when the score improves, updates the guidance latent by blending the current artifact-free latent back into the stored reference, so the guidance progressively sharpens rather than remaining a static bicubic-upsampled encoding. The two phases alternate over 100 steps, and the final latent is decoded to the HR output. This design reduces inference steps by 2× while improving PSNR by ~3–4 dB over StableSR across all benchmark datasets.

## Results

SARGD achieves substantial PSNR gains over StableSR across all five benchmark datasets and all three upscaling factors. On Set5, PSNR improves from 28.78 to 32.64 dB at ×2 (+3.86 dB), from 29.09 to 33.06 at ×3 (+3.97 dB), and from 29.36 to 32.27 at ×4 (+2.91 dB). SSIM improvements are equally notable, e.g., 0.805→0.899 on Set5 ×2 and 0.694→0.817 on B100 ×2. Perceptual metrics also improve: LPIPS drops from 0.165 to 0.118 (Set5 ×2) and DISTS from 0.102 to 0.086 (Manga109 ×2), while SARGD reduces inference steps by 2× (from 200 to ~100 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARGD is compared against two baselines: LDM† (Latent Diffusion Model fine-tuned on the same dataset as StableSR) and StableSR (the current state-of-the-art diffusion-based SR). SARGD decisively outperforms both on every metric and every dataset/scale combination. Against StableSR, PSNR gains range from +2.91 dB (Set5 ×4) to +4.14 dB (Set14 ×2), SSIM gains reach +0.123 (B100 ×2: 0.694→0.817), LPIPS drops by up to 0.082 (Manga109 ×2: 0.118→0.083), and DISTS drops by up to 0.016 (Manga109 ×2: 0.102→0.086). Against LDM†, the margins are even larger (e.g., +4.78 dB PSNR on Set5 ×2). Notably, SARGD is training-free and reduces inference steps by 2× (peak quality at ~100 steps vs. 200 for baselines), making it both more accurate and faster than both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARGD is a training-free, inference-time refinement layer that plugs into the StableSR pipeline (a pre-trained latent diffusion model for SR) and operates within the standard DDPM sampling loop. Its two core mechanisms—Reality-Guided Refinement (RGR) and Self-Adaptive Guidance (SAG)—repurpose the PAL perceptual-artifact detector (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) as a per-step mask generator, a use that departs from prior artifact-detection work which was designed for post-hoc image authentication rather than iterative latent-space correction. Conceptually, the method sits between the architecture-modification approaches of earlier diffusion-SR models (SR3, pixel-aware Stable Diffusion) and the purely generative StableSR, adding a corrective feedback loop without any retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play method, SARGD can be applied to any pre-trained diffusion SR model without retraining, and the demonstrated 2× reduction in required sampling steps (peak quality at ~100 steps vs. 200) offers a practical efficiency gain. However, the per-step overhead of decoding, running PAL, and re-encoding partially offsets this, and the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to PAL's detection accuracy on diverse content. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video SR, non-standard degradations (blur, noise, compression), or diffusion architectures beyond the LDM/StableSR family, and whether the PSNR gains come at the cost of perceptual richness that LPIPS/DISTS only partially capture.
