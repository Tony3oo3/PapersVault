## Problem

Reassembly tasks in 2D (jigsaw puzzles) and 3D (fractured object reconstruction) have been addressed by separate methods with incompatible assumptions: optimization-based 2D solvers lack robustness to missing pieces and scale poorly, while learning-based 2D methods cannot handle rotated pieces, and 3D reassembly has lagged behind in performance. No prior learning-based approach unifies both dimensions or simultaneously handles translation and rotation for 2D puzzles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242156/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of M pieces—either 2D square image patches (initialized with random positions in [-1,1] and random rotations from Z4) or 3D point-cloud fragments of 1,000 points each (translated to origin with random rotations)—and outputs the continuous translation and rotation for every piece that restores the coherent structure. The graph formulation is cardinality-agnostic, so the same model handles puzzles from 36 to 900 pieces or 3D objects with 2 to 20 fragments without retraining. For 3D, rotations are parameterized as unit quaternions; for 2D, as [cos(θ), sin(θ)] pairs optimized in the continuous domain.

## Method

DiffAssemble frames reassembly as a denoising diffusion task over a complete graph whose nodes encode each piece's equivariant visual features, translation, and rotation. A forward Markov chain injects Gaussian noise into poses (with SO(3)-manifold-aware diffusion for 3D rotations), and an L-layer UniMP attention-based GNN learns to predict the original pose at each reverse step, trained with separate L2 translation loss and Frobenius-norm rotation loss. Rotation-equivariant feature encoders (VN-DGCNN for 3D point clouds, Z4-equivariant ResNet-18 for 2D patches) provide inductive bias that lets the network associate input-space rotations with feature-space transformations. To scale to large graphs (up to 900 nodes), the architecture incorporates Exphormer's expander-graph sparsity with virtual global nodes, pruning 80% of edges while preserving global connectivity and halving memory consumption.

## Results

On the Breaking Bad 3D reassembly benchmark, DiffAssemble achieves RMSE(R) of 73.3°, RMSE(T) of 14.8×10⁻², and Part Accuracy of 27.5%, outperforming the previous SOTA (SE(3)-Equiv) on all three metrics simultaneously. On 2D jigsaw puzzles, DiffAssemble reaches 99.51% accuracy on PuzzleCelebA 6×6 and 97.76% on 12×12, surpassing all optimization-based methods. With 30% missing pieces, DiffAssemble retains 96.92% on CelebA 6×6 and 76.49% on 12×12, while the best optimization baseline drops to 33.45% and 21.78% respectively. For large puzzles (30×30, 900 pieces), DiffAssemble solves in 5 seconds at 95% accuracy versus 55 seconds at 58% for the fastest optimization method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3D reassembly (Breaking Bad), DiffAssemble outperforms all five baselines on every metric: it beats SE(3)-Equiv (previous SOTA) by 4.6° in RMSE(R), 1.9×10⁻² in RMSE(T), and 19.4 points in Part Accuracy (27.5% vs 8.1%), and surpasses DGL (best in translation) by 8.1° in rotation while matching its translation accuracy. On 2D puzzles, DiffAssemble wins on PuzzleCelebA across all sizes (e.g., 99.51% vs 98.63% for Yu on 6×6) but loses to Yu and Huroyan on PuzzleWikiArt in the standard setting (e.g., 90.65% vs 94.62% for Yu on 6×6), which the authors attribute to WikiArt's diverse visual content lacking the semantic structure that DiffAssemble exploits. However, with 30% missing pieces, DiffAssemble dramatically outperforms all optimization baselines on both datasets (e.g., 76.49% vs 21.78% for Yu on CelebA 12×12), demonstrating superior robustness. The efficiency gap is also notable: DiffAssemble solves 900-piece puzzles in 5 seconds versus 55 seconds for Gallagher, the fastest optimization method, while achieving 95% vs 58% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffAssemble repurposes the diffusion probabilistic model framework (DDPM/DDIM) from generative modeling to pose estimation, treating reassembly as an iterative denoising problem over piece translations and rotations. The architecture combines UniMP attention-based message passing with Exphormer's sparse expander-graph mechanism for scalability, and adopts SO(3)-manifold diffusion (Leach et al.) to handle 3D rotations. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work by unifying 2D jigsaw puzzles and 3D fractured-object reassembly under a single graph-diffusion formulation, whereas previous approaches treat these as fundamentally different problems requiring separate optimization or learning pipelines. The use of rotation-equivariant feature encoders (VN-DGCNN for 3D, Z4-equivariant ResNet for 2D) as inductive bias is a key design choice absent from prior reassembly methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-based method to handle both rotation and translation simultaneously for 2D puzzles, and it achieves an 11× speedup over the fastest optimization-based solver while being far more robust to missing pieces (96.9% vs. 18–33% accuracy at 30% missing on 6×6). The unified 2D/3D framework suggests reassembly tasks share sufficient structural commonality to be addressed by one architecture, potentially accelerating progress in applications like fresco reconstruction and molecular docking. Key limitations include high GPU memory consumption (mitigated but not eliminated by sparsity), a notable performance gap on semantically diverse images (WikiArt), and the fact that 3D results, while SOTA, still show substantial RMSE in rotation (~73°). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real-world scans with noise and partial observability, and whether memory can be further reduced for very large assemblies.
