## Problem

Personalized Federated Learning (PFL) suffers from two underexplored inconsistencies: (1) intra-client inconsistency, where the asynchronous replacement of shared backbone parameters with global updates creates a knowledge mismatch with the locally-trained personalized head, and (2) inter-client inconsistency, where under-trained 'straggler' clients inject poorly-optimized parameters into server aggregation, degrading the collaborative model for all active clients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240853/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 (10 classes) or CIFAR-100 (100 classes), partitioned non-IID via a Dirichlet distribution (β ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}) across 20 clients. The 6-layer CNN produces a class label as output, with the backbone (shared) and classifier head (personalized) decoupled. Client participation is controlled by an online ratio P ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 1}, simulating varying numbers of stragglers.

## Method

FedAS introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Federated Parameter Alignment (PA) localizes the received global backbone before local training: the client generates feature embeddings H_i using the previous round's locally-tuned backbone, then minimizes an MSE loss to align the new global backbone's outputs with H_i, bridging the local-global knowledge gap. Second, Federated Client Synchronization (CS) computes the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix (t-FIM) for each client's model at the end of local training; since under-trained stragglers exhibit lower t-FIM values, the server normalizes these values into aggregation weights, reducing the influence of stragglers on the global update. The two modules operate at different levels—PA at the client side and CS at the server side—and are shown to be independently beneficial and synergistic in ablation studies.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FedAS achieves 91.03% top-1 accuracy under the most favorable setting (β=0.1, P=1) and 89.92% under the most challenging setting (β=0.1, P=0.2), outperforming the strongest baseline FedALA (90.06% and 89.61% respectively). On CIFAR-100, FedAS reaches 56.37% (β=0.1, P=1) and 52.41% (β=0.1, P=0.2), surpassing the best baseline FedPAC (52.03% and 45.08%) by up to 4.34 percentage points. Across all 12 β×P configurations on both datasets, FedAS consistently achieves the highest accuracy, with average improvements of 0.08–6.89 points over the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAS outperforms all nine baselines across every β and P configuration on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The closest competitor is FedALA, which FedAS surpasses by 0.08–0.97 points on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 91.03 vs. 90.06 at β=0.1, P=1) and by 2.83–4.34 points on CIFAR-100. FedPAC is the second-closest on CIFAR-10 (within 0.08–1.20 points) but trails FedAS by 2.42–6.89 points on CIFAR-100. FedBABU, which performs well under mild heterogeneity, degrades more sharply as β increases, while FedAS maintains a steadier accuracy curve. FedPer and LG-FedAvg show the largest gaps under high heterogeneity (β=0.5), where FedAS leads by 2.8–13.8 points. FedProto is particularly vulnerable to low client participation (P=0.2), dropping to 58.58% on CIFAR-10 (β=0.5) versus FedAS's 75.93%, highlighting FedAS's robustness to stragglers. FedAvg-P and FedProx-P, the non-personalized baselines, consistently underperform all PFL methods by 5–25 points, confirming the necessity of personalization under non-IID conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-decoupling paradigm in Personalized Federated Learning (PFL), where the backbone is shared and the head is personalized (as in FedPer, FedBABU, FedPAC, FedALA). It departs from prior PFL work by explicitly identifying and addressing two overlooked failure modes: intra-client inconsistency caused by asynchronous replacement of shared parameters, and inter-client inconsistency caused by under-trained stragglers. The Parameter Alignment module repurposes a self-distillation-style MSE loss to localize the incoming global backbone, while the Client Synchronization module introduces the Fisher Information Matrix trace as a novel aggregation weighting criterion in the federated setting—a use of FIM previously confined to continual learning (EWC) and optimization (K-FAC) but not to federated aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FIM-based weighted aggregation offers a principled, lightweight mechanism to handle stragglers without requiring explicit client selection or communication overhead, which is practically relevant for real-world federated deployments with unreliable participants. However, the method assumes homogeneous model architecture across all clients, limiting its applicability to heterogeneous-structure scenarios. Open questions include whether the diagonal FIM approximation remains informative at scale, whether the alignment loss interacts well with more complex personalization strategies (e.g., multi-head or adapter-based), and whether the approach generalizes beyond CIFAR-scale image classification to larger, more diverse benchmarks.
