## Problem

Pre-trained vision language models (e.g., CLIP, MaskCLIP) produce noisy and inconsistent dense pseudo-labels for 3D semantic segmentation, with label conflicts arising within individual views, across multiple views of the same scene, and across different scenes. Existing label-free methods rely solely on cross-modal alignment with these unreliable pseudo-labels, resulting in scattered and ambiguous 3D feature spaces. This paper exploits the occlusion-free, geometry-rich nature of point clouds to establish reliable intra-modal correlations that counteract the inconsistent cross-modal supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240436/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds (sampled from indoor ScanNet scenes or outdoor nuScenes scenes) together with multi-view RGB images and text class prompts processed by a frozen MaskCLIP model to generate 2D pseudo-labels, which are projected onto 3D points via depth-filtered back-projection. At inference, only the 3D encoder (MinkowskiNet14 for ScanNet, SPVCNN for nuScenes) is retained, producing a per-point semantic class label over the target vocabulary. The correlation-learning vision transformers are discarded after training.

## Method

The framework hierarchically captures intra-modal correlations at three scales. First, intra-set label refinement clusters points into geometric consistency sets via normal-based over-segmentation and replaces noisy pseudo-labels with the mode label within each set. Second, a 3-layer vision transformer performs intra-scene correlation learning by attending over sampled points within each scene, with gradients that pull correlated points toward the same text embedding and push them apart from others. Third, a cross-scene vision transformer processes points from multiple scenes in a single batch, enforcing consistent feature distributions across scenes. A KL-divergence feedback distillation loss then aligns the 3D model's raw output features with the aggregated correlation-enhanced features, transferring the correlation-learning capability into the 3D encoder. Training is staged over 120 epochs: cross-entropy only (epochs 1–10), plus intra-scene correlation (epoch 11+), plus inter-scene correlation and feedback distillation (epoch 20+).

## Results

On the ScanNet indoor dataset, the method achieves a state-of-the-art 36.6% mIoU, surpassing the previous best (CLIP2Scene) by 7.8% mIoU and the concurrent SAM-based method (Chen et al.) by 3.1% mIoU, while using only 8.2M parameters and 14 GPU·h of training time. On the outdoor nuScenes dataset, the method achieves 23.0% mIoU, improving over CLIP2Scene by 2.2% mIoU, though it trails Chen et al. (26.8%) by 3.8% due to the latter's additional 2D segmentation model for label correction. The method matches CLIP2Scene's parameter count (8.2M) while requiring only 3.5× more training time (14 vs. 4 GPU·h), and is substantially lighter than Chen et al.'s 31.7M-parameter model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on ScanNet: it beats MaskCLIP (14.2%) by 22.4% mIoU, MaskCLIP+ (21.6%) by 15.0%, OpenScene (16.8%) by 19.8%, and CLIP2Scene (25.6%) by 11.0%. Against the strongest concurrent baseline, Chen et al. (33.5%), the method gains 3.1% mIoU on ScanNet while using 30% less training time (14 vs. 20 GPU·h) and 4× fewer parameters (8.2M vs. 31.7M). On nuScenes, the method surpasses CLIP2Scene (20.8%) by 2.2% mIoU and MaskCLIP+ (15.3%) by 7.7%, but falls short of Chen et al. (26.8%) by 3.8% mIoU; the authors attribute this gap to Chen et al.'s additional 2D segmentation model that corrects the particularly poor CLIP pseudo-labels in the outdoor setting. Overall, the method achieves the best ScanNet result with the lowest parameter count among all compared approaches, demonstrating that hierarchical intra-modal correlation learning is a more efficient path to compact 3D features than SAM-based label refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP2Scene cross-modal transfer learning framework by introducing a hierarchical intra-modal correlation learning module that operates at three scales: intra-set (geometric consistency sets with voting-based label refinement, building on Chen et al.'s geometric set consistency), intra-scene (vision transformer attention over sampled points within each scene), and inter-scene (cross-scene attention over batched multi-scene features). Unlike the concurrent work of Chen et al. [4], which relies on SAM segmentation masks to denoise pseudo-labels at significant training cost, this paper addresses the same pseudo-label inconsistency problem purely through intra-modal 3D point correlations, avoiding the need for an additional 2D segmentation model. The feedback distillation mechanism (KL loss) then transfers the learned correlation structure back into the 3D backbone, a design choice that distinguishes it from prior methods that discard auxiliary modules at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured intra-modal correlations in 3D point clouds can substantially mitigate the noisy pseudo-label problem that plagues CLIP-based label-free segmentation, achieving SOTA on ScanNet (36.6% mIoU) with lower training cost than SAM-based alternatives. However, the method's effectiveness is dataset-dependent: on nuScenes, where CLIP pseudo-labels are so poor that even refined labels are predominantly wrong, the gains shrink and the method underperforms Chen et al. [4]. The authors acknowledge that intra-modal correlations within images and texts remain unexplored, and the reliance on geometric consistency sets (normal-based over-segmentation) may limit applicability to scenes with complex or ambiguous geometry. Open questions include whether the hierarchical correlation framework generalizes to open-vocabulary settings beyond the fixed category set and whether the inter-scene attention component scales to very large scene collections.
