## Problem

Existing category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) methods rely solely on local features extracted at support keypoints to predict or refine query keypoints, which is inadequate when support keypoints are occluded or blurred. These methods neglect the inherent and universal information that keypoints possess across arbitrary object classes — information that humans can perceive without any reference image. The paper asks whether class-agnostic potential keypoints (meta-points) can be learned without support to complement the limited local information from a few annotated examples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image and N support images (1-shot or 5-shot) with annotated keypoints (K ranging from 8 to 68 per class) as input. It produces 2D keypoint coordinates on the query image along with visibility scores for M=100 meta-points. The support images are consumed in two ways: their annotated keypoint locations drive a bipartite-matching assignment to determine which meta-point corresponds to which desired keypoint, and their local features (via soft ROI pooling) plus their own meta-embeddings provide refinement signals. In N-shot settings, support features and cost matrices are averaged across shots.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, M=100 learnable meta-embeddings interact with ResNet-50 pyramid feature maps (R=3 levels) through a Progressive Deformable Point Decoder (L=3 layers) that uses deformable attention with the previous layer's points as reference to progressively refine meta-point coordinates and embeddings without any support. Second, a bipartite-matching assignment (based on a cost matrix combining slacked L1 distances and visibility scores computed on the support image) maps meta-points to desired keypoint identities, after which a second decoder refines the assigned meta-points using concatenated query meta-embeddings, support meta-embeddings, and ROI-pooled support keypoint features. A slacked regression loss (with ReLU-gated L1 and a slack hyperparameter λ=0.1) reduces immature gradients on auxiliary decoder layers, and a binary visibility classifier filters out non-existent meta-points for occluded parts.

## Results

On the MP-100 dataset, MetaPoint+ achieves 86.17 PCK@0.2 in the 1-shot setting and 90.02 in the 5-shot setting, outperforming the strongest baseline CapeFormer (85.31 and 89.30 respectively). The meta-point prediction alone (MetaPoint, without support-based refinement) reaches 79.31 and 79.83 PCK@0.2, already competitive with POMNet (79.70 and 80.71). Under the stricter mPCK metric (averaging thresholds 0.05–0.2), MetaPoint+ achieves 72.23 (1-shot) and 77.12 (5-shot), surpassing CapeFormer by ~1.6 and ~1.7 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaPoint+ outperforms CapeFormer [41], the most competitive prior method, by 0.86 points (1-shot) and 0.72 points (5-shot) on PCK@0.2, with larger margins under the stricter mPCK metric (~1.6–1.7 points). Against POMNet [48], the improvement is more substantial: +6.47 (1-shot) and +9.31 (5-shot) on PCK@0.2. The few-shot baselines (ProtoNet [42], MAML [17], Fine-tune [35]) are far behind, with the best (Fine-tune) reaching only 63.58/64.61 PCK@0.2. Notably, MetaPoint without any support-based refinement (79.31/79.83) is already on par with POMNet, demonstrating that the learned meta-points capture meaningful class-agnostic keypoint structure. In the cross super-category evaluation (Table 3), MetaPoint+ wins on all four super-categories (Human Body, Human Face, Vehicle, Furniture) against CapeFormer, with the largest gain on Vehicle (+1.11) and Furniture (+1.18).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CAPE paradigm established by POMNet and CapeFormer, which extract features at support keypoints and match them to query pixels. Instead, it introduces learnable meta-embeddings that interact with query feature maps via a progressive deformable point decoder to predict class-agnostic potential keypoints (meta-points) without any support, drawing conceptual inspiration from category-agnostic proposal learning (e.g., Faster R-CNN's region proposals). The support keypoints then serve only to assign identities via bipartite matching and provide local detail features for refinement, inverting the dependency structure of prior methods. The progressive deformable point decoder and slacked regression loss are technical contributions borrowed from and adapted to the deformable DETR lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The meta-point framework offers a more principled decomposition of CAPE into an inherent-structure stage and a support-refinement stage, which is particularly valuable when support keypoints are occluded or ambiguous. However, the empirical gains over CapeFormer are modest (≈0.9 PCK@0.2 in 1-shot, ≈0.7 in 5-shot), and the base MetaPoint without refinement actually underperforms CapeFormer, suggesting the meta-point prior alone is not yet a strong enough signal. Open questions include whether meta-points generalize beyond the 100-class MP-100 benchmark, whether the approach extends to 3D pose or dense keypoint fields, and whether the M=100 meta-embeddings scale gracefully to classes with very different keypoint counts.
