## Problem

Off-the-shelf dense predictors (depth, normal, segmentation) fail on imaginative T2I-generated images due to an irreducible domain gap between real-world training data and free-form synthetic content. Adapting pre-trained T2I diffusion models to dense prediction is non-trivial because these models are inherently stochastic while dense prediction requires deterministic input-to-output mappings, and fine-tuning risks destroying the generalizability that makes the prior useful.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241336/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) as input and produces a pixel-aligned prediction map of the same spatial resolution in RGB space (H×W×3), covering depth, surface normals, semantic segmentation, albedo, and shading. For segmentation, the diffusion model operates on color-coded label maps and converts predictions to categorical labels by nearest-color assignment at inference. All outputs are generated via a 5-step deterministic reverse diffusion process, requiring no noise sampling or text prompts at test time.

## Method

DMP reformulates the standard stochastic diffusion process as a deterministic chain of linear interpolations between the input image x and the target output y (y_t = √ᾱ_t · y + √(1−ᾱ_t) · x), eliminating Gaussian noise entirely so the mapping from input to output is fully deterministic. The U-Net is fine-tuned with v-prediction parameterization to predict the velocity field at each interpolation step, and the reverse process progressively 'demorphs' from the input image to the final prediction over 5 steps. To preserve the pre-trained model's generalizability while learning the target task, only low-rank (LoRA) adapters on the attention layers are updated, keeping the bulk of the Stable Diffusion weights frozen. This design allows the model to generalize from 10K bedroom-domain training images to arbitrary unseen images, including T2I-generated content in diverse styles.

## Results

On 3D property estimation (Table 1), DMP achieves Normal L1 of 0.0514 (in-domain) and 0.0872 (out-of-domain), and Depth RMSE of 0.1020 (in-domain) and 0.1360 (out-of-domain), outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On semantic segmentation (Table 2), DMP reaches a mean mIoU of 0.6127 and mean accuracy of 0.7409, surpassing the next-best SPADE (mIoU 0.4276, Acc 0.6074) by roughly 18 and 13 percentage points respectively. On intrinsic image decomposition (Table 3), DMP attains Albedo MSE of 0.0041 (in-domain) and 0.0064 (out-of-domain), and Shading MSE of 0.0051 and 0.0070, again the best across all settings. All results are obtained with only 10K bedroom-domain training images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMP outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three tasks. Against the GAN-based methods (SPADE, DRIT++), DMP reduces Normal L1 by ~27% (0.0514 vs 0.0708 in-domain) and Depth RMSE by ~26% (0.1020 vs 0.1379 in-domain), with even larger gaps out-of-domain. Among the diffusion-based baselines, SDEdit and DDIB suffer from the stochasticity mismatch, yielding Normal L1 of 0.2599 and 0.1849 respectively—4–5× worse than DMP's 0.0514. IP2P (both hard and learned variants) and VISII perform worst overall, with Normal Ang errors exceeding 0.44 in-domain versus DMP's 0.1156. On semantic segmentation, DMP's mean mIoU of 0.6127 exceeds SPADE (0.4276) by 43% relatively, while all diffusion-based baselines (SDEdit, DDIB, IP2P, VISII) collapse to mIoU below 0.17. The advantage is most pronounced on out-of-domain and arbitrary test sets, where off-the-shelf predictors fail and DMP still produces faithful estimates thanks to the pre-trained T2I prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMP builds on the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as a frozen backbone and repurposes it for deterministic dense prediction by replacing the standard noise-to-image diffusion schedule with an interpolation between the input image and the target output, drawing on the α-blending idea from Heitz et al. This departs from prior image-to-image translation approaches with diffusion models (SDEdit, DDIB, IP2P, VISII), which retain stochasticity and text conditioning, and from generative-prior methods for dense prediction (e.g., Bhattad et al. with StyleGAN, Gong et al. with diffusion representations) that are task-specific or category-limited. The use of LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and v-prediction as the U-Net parameterization are borrowed from the LLM adaptation and progressive distillation literatures respectively, but their combination within a deterministic diffusion reformulation for multi-task dense prediction is the paper's core architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pre-trained T2I model, fine-tuned with LoRA on only 10K labeled images from one domain, can produce faithful dense predictions (depth, normals, segmentation, albedo, shading) on arbitrary unseen images, outperforming both GAN-based translators and other diffusion-based image-to-image methods by substantial margins. A key limitation is that all ground truth is pseudo-labeled by off-the-shelf predictors, so the method's ceiling is bounded by those models' quality, and all evaluation is on T2I-generated synthetic images rather than real photographs. Open questions include whether the deterministic interpolation formulation transfers to tasks with genuinely different output modalities (e.g., 3D point clouds), whether real-world training data with true labels would unlock further gains, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video dense prediction.
