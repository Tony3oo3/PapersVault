## Problem

No existing 3D shape matching method simultaneously guarantees geometric consistency (preservation of neighbourhood relations under the matching) and global optimality. Learning-based state-of-the-art methods produce matchings with local geometric inconsistencies, while optimization-based methods that enforce consistency require initial pre-matchings or rely on heuristics that yield only approximate solutions. This paper closes the gap by formulating 3D shape matching as a path-based problem that admits globally optimal, geometrically consistent solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes two 3D surface meshes (source X and target Y) with the same genus, decimated to 1000 triangles, along with vertex-wise features extracted by a pretrained network (Cao et al.). The source mesh is internally discretised into a SpiderCurve via a Christofides TSP approximation, which duplicates vertices at self-intersection points so that |V^C| ≥ |V^X|. The output is a dense vertex-to-vertex correspondence represented as a cyclic shortest path in the product graph, solved to global optimality by Gurobi in roughly 100 seconds per instance.

## Method

The core idea is to represent the source 3D shape as a SpiderCurve—a long self-intersecting curve that traces the entire surface—and then match it to the target shape by finding a cyclic shortest path in the product graph P = C × Y. The matching is formulated as an integer linear program with four constraint families: (CYC) forces the path to traverse every layer of P, (CON) enforces path continuity at each product vertex, (PES) couples product vertices at self-intersection points so that existing intersections in C are preserved on Y, and (ANS) forbids the path from visiting any vertex of Y more than once, thereby preventing new self-intersections. The cost vector is built from vertex-wise feature differences using a robust loss function. Unlike prior product-graph approaches that use Dijkstra-style shortest-path algorithms, the ILP formulation allows the self-intersection constraints to be integrated directly, and the authors empirically show that Gurobi solves all instances to global optimality despite the problem involving up to 2×10⁶ binary variables.

## Results

The paper evaluates geodesic error (normalized by square root of shape area, following the Princeton protocol) on FAUST, SMAL, and DT4D-H (intra-class and inter-class), with all shapes decimated to 1000 triangles. The method achieves the lowest mean geodesic error on FAUST, SMAL, and DT4D-H intra-class, and is on par with the state-of-the-art learning-based method (Cao et al.) on DT4D-H inter-class. In the ablation study on 500-triangle FAUST pairs, the full method achieves a mean geodesic error of 0.034, compared to 0.035–0.036 when individual constraints (PES, ANS) or the robust loss are removed, and 0.213 when using WKS features instead of learned features. All 400 test instances are solved to global optimality in under 200 s using Gurobi.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cao et al. [14] (the state-of-the-art learning-based method and the feature extractor used by the proposed method), SpiderMatch achieves lower mean geodesic error on FAUST, SMAL, and DT4D-H intra-class, and is on par on DT4D-H inter-class; the key advantage is the absence of local geometric inconsistencies that plague the learning-based baseline. Against Ren et al. [53] (functional map + point map hard constraint), SpiderMatch clearly wins on all four settings, as Ren et al. produces severe left-right flips due to intrinsic symmetries and large mismatches. Against Eisenberger et al. [22] (iterative functional map alignment), SpiderMatch wins on all settings; Eisenberger et al. suffers from unaligned shapes (requiring heuristic pre-alignment) and also produces left-right flips and local mismatches. Against Roetzer et al. [55] (the only other geometrically consistent method), SpiderMatch matches the geometric consistency guarantee while additionally achieving global optimality and running substantially faster (≈100 s at 1000 triangles vs. requiring decimation to 450 triangles for acceptable runtimes).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalises the product-graph shortest-path formalism for 2D-to-3D shape matching (Lähner et al. 2016; Roetzer et al. 2023) to the 3D-to-3D setting by introducing a SpiderCurve discretisation of the source shape, reducing the intractable 4D product-space problem to a 1D cyclic path problem. It departs from the dominant deep-learning paradigm (functional maps, DiffusionNet-based methods) by casting matching as an integer linear programme solved with Gurobi, and from prior geometrically consistent methods (e.g., Roetzer et al. 2022) by eliminating the need for initial pre-matchings or search-space pruning. The SpiderCurve itself is extracted via the Christofides TSP approximation, a repurposing of a classical combinatorial algorithm for shape representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D shape matching method to simultaneously guarantee global optimality and geometric consistency, filling a gap explicitly identified in the literature. The practical limitation is that the ILP has exponential worst-case complexity (up to 2×10⁶ binary variables), and the ~100 s runtime per 1000-triangle pair, while competitive, is orders of magnitude slower than deep-learning baselines. Open questions include whether a polynomial-time algorithm exists for the SpiderMatch ILP, whether alternative curve discretisations could improve the SpiderCurve quality, and whether the approach extends to partial or topologically noisy shapes. The method also requires same-genus shapes and still depends on a pretrained feature extractor (Cao et al.) for competitive accuracy.
