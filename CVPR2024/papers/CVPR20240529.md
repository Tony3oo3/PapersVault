## Problem

Existing indoor 3D point cloud datasets (S3DIS, ScanNet, ScanNet++) are either captured by RGB-D cameras or annotated on Poisson-reconstructed meshes, lacking the non-uniform point distributions (scanning holes, scanning lines) and anomalies (reflection noise, ghosts) inherent to real LiDAR scanning. This gap causes deep models trained on these datasets to generalize poorly to actual real-scanned point cloud data, significantly impeding practical deployment in robotics, smart homes, and digital twins.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 3.6 billion raw 3D points captured by a Leica BLK2GO mobile laser scanner (±3 mm accuracy at 10 m, 420 K points/s, 0.5–25 m range) across 30,000 m² of indoor space spanning learning, working, and living scenes. Each point carries a semantic label from 24 categories and an instance label; 17 of the 24 categories additionally receive 3D bounding boxes for object detection. Crucially, the points preserve authentic LiDAR characteristics—non-uniform density over a 2–7 m scanning radius, scanning lines, holes, reflection noise from specular surfaces, and ghost points from moving persons—rather than the uniform voxel-like distributions of reconstructed-mesh datasets.

## Method

This is a dataset and benchmark contribution rather than a new architecture. Acquisition uses the Leica BLK2GO with GrandSLAM dual-positioning (fusing LiDAR+IMU SLAM with panoramic-camera visual SLAM), with routes pre-planned by professional surveyors and data collected manually in ~1-hour sessions to replicate real-world interference. A custom Point Cloud AnnoTation (PCAT) system enables point-level semantic and instance labeling directly on raw points, consuming approximately 550 working hours for the full dataset. The benchmark evaluates eight SOTA methods (PointNet, PointNet++, KPConv, RandLA-Net, ASIS, 3D-BoNet, VoteNet, GroupFree3D) across semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and object detection. A key empirical finding is that models trained on LiDAR-Net outperform those trained on S3DIS, ScanNet, or ScanNet++ when tested on unseen real-scanned rooms, validating the importance of preserving raw point distributions. The paper further identifies severe class imbalance (floors/ceilings/walls exceed 80% of points while monitors constitute 0.16%) and the 2–7 m vs. 2–5 m distribution mismatch between LiDAR and RGB-D as fundamental open challenges.

## Results

On LiDAR-Net, the best semantic segmentation result is achieved by KPConv with 44.29% mIoU, followed by RandLA-Net (32.60%), PointNet++ (28.81%), and PointNet (16.27%). For instance segmentation, 3D-BoNet leads with 39.82% mPre and 24.63% mRec, while ASIS achieves 37.32% mPre and 25.08% mRec. For object detection, GroupFree3D attains 46.80% mAP@0.25 and 18.86% mAP@0.5, outperforming VoteNet (44.83% and 16.71%). Crucially, when models trained on LiDAR-Net are tested on unseen real-scanned data, they achieve 39.75% mIoU, 32.92% mPre, 20.39% mRec, 32.96% mAP@0.25, and 9.22% mAP@0.5, substantially outperforming models trained on S3DIS, ScanNet, or ScanNet++ across all three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four semantic segmentation baselines, KPConv (44.29% mIoU) clearly outperforms RandLA-Net (32.60%), PointNet++ (28.81%), and PointNet (16.27%), though per-category analysis reveals no single method dominates all classes (e.g., RandLA-Net achieves 42.02% IoU on trees vs. KPConv's 7.94%, while KPConv scores 58.02% on sofas vs. RandLA-Net's 0.00%). For instance segmentation, 3D-BoNet edges out ASIS on precision (39.82% vs. 37.32%) while ASIS slightly leads on recall (25.08% vs. 24.63%). In object detection, GroupFree3D surpasses VoteNet on both mAP@0.25 (46.80% vs. 44.83%) and mAP@0.5 (18.86% vs. 16.71%). The most compelling comparison is cross-dataset: models trained on LiDAR-Net outperform those trained on S3DIS by 4.17 mIoU, on ScanNet by 6.57 mIoU, and on ScanNet++ by 16.15 mIoU when evaluated on real-scanned data, with particularly dramatic gains in object detection (9.22% vs. 0.28–1.67% mAP@0.5), demonstrating that raw LiDAR point distributions are critical for real-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiDAR-Net carves new ground in the indoor 3D point cloud dataset lineage (S3DIS → ScanNet → ScanNet++ → LiDAR-Net) by being the first to provide point-level semantic and instance annotations directly on raw mobile LiDAR scans rather than on reconstructed meshes or uniformly sampled points. While ScanNet++ also uses a laser scanner (Faro Focus Premium), it annotates Poisson-reconstructed meshes, losing the non-uniform scanning lines, holes, and anomalies (reflection noise, ghosts) that characterize authentic LiDAR data. The paper also distinguishes itself from outdoor LiDAR datasets (Semantic3D, Toronto-3D, etc.) by targeting indoor scenes with dense, multi-room coverage including corridors and stairs, and by using a mobile scanner (Leica BLK2GO) rather than static terrestrial or airborne systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-dataset generalization experiment (Table 4) provides the first quantitative evidence that models trained on mesh-sampled points (S3DIS, ScanNet, ScanNet++) systematically underperform on real LiDAR data, with LiDAR-Net-trained models achieving substantially higher mIoU, mPre/mRec, and mAP across all three tasks. The paper identifies two open challenges that remain unresolved: (1) the distribution mismatch between the 2–5 m range typical of RGB-D/mesh-sampled data and the 2–7 m range of real LiDAR, and (2) the extreme class imbalance where a handful of structural categories (floor, ceiling, wall) dominate >80% of points while critical small objects (monitors, houseplants) constitute <0.2%. These findings suggest that the field's reliance on mesh-sampled benchmarks may have masked generalization gaps that only become visible on authentic sensor data, and that class-imbalance-aware methods for 3D point clouds remain an active research need.
