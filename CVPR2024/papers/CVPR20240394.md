## Problem

Amodal completion—recovering the hidden pixels of occluded objects—remains challenging for generative AI because existing two-step approaches (predict amodal mask, then synthesize pixels) are ill-posed due to the diversity of valid completions. Additionally, off-the-shelf diffusion inpainting models suffer from co-occurrence bias, regenerating similar occluders or contextually compatible objects instead of completing the target, and there is no principled way to judge whether a completion has succeeded.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image containing an occluded query object, its binary modal mask, and a text prompt specifying the object's semantic category. If the object touches the image boundary, the image and mask are conditionally padded to allow completion beyond the frame, so the output amodal completion image may be larger than the input. The pipeline also consumes auxiliary outputs from grounded segmentation (Grounding DINO + SAM) and instance-wise depth ordering to identify occluders, and produces both the final amodal completion image and its corresponding amodal mask.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free Progressive Occlusion-aware Completion pipeline built on Stable Diffusion v2 inpainting. Each iteration identifies occluders via grounded segmentation and depth ordering, then applies Mixed Context Diffusion Sampling: the image background is temporarily swapped to a clean gray backdrop to break co-occurrence bias, diffusion inpainting runs to an intermediate timestep k, unsupervised clustering of UNet decoder features (layer D3) extracts a pseudo-complete object mask from the noisy latent, and this object is composited onto a separately generated object-removed background before the remaining N−k denoising steps complete the image. The pipeline iterates until no occluders remain, and a counterfactual curation system—outpainting the completed object and checking whether its mask area grows beyond a 20% threshold—provides a training-free completeness judgment.

## Results

On a pseudo-occluded dataset of 3,000 objects (2,500 from COCO, 500 from Open Images), the proposed method achieves the best CLIP similarity (0.290/0.267) and lowest DreamSim (0.184/0.185) and LPIPS (0.110/0.141) on hard cases (50–80% occlusion), outperforming all three baselines. On easy cases (20–50% occlusion), it matches LaMa on DreamSim (0.096 vs. 0.098) and LPIPS (0.054 vs. 0.054) while leading on CLIP (0.290 vs. 0.288). In a user preference study across 110 cases, the proposed method is preferred 90.9% of the time, compared to 7.3% for LaMa, 1.8% for SSSD, and 0.0% for Inst-Inpaint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every metric in both easy and hard settings. Against SSSD [60] (GAN-based amodal completion), the gap is largest on hard cases: DreamSim improves from 0.315/0.334 to 0.184/0.185 and LPIPS from 0.166/0.225 to 0.110/0.141, reflecting SSSD's tendency to produce visual artifacts and incomplete objects. Against Inst-Inpaint [56] (diffusion-based object removal), the advantage is even more pronounced, with DreamSim on hard cases dropping from 0.451/0.446 to 0.184/0.185, since Inst-Inpaint removes objects but struggles to regenerate them. LaMa [47] (large-mask inpainting) is the closest competitor on perceptual metrics—nearly matching on easy-case DreamSim (0.098 vs. 0.096) and LPIPS (0.054 vs. 0.054)—but the authors note it produces blurry boundaries and similarly colored fills that inflate metric scores without true object completion. The user preference study (90.9% vs. 7.3% for LaMa) confirms that the metric-level parity with LaMa does not translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-step amodal completion paradigm (predict amodal mask, then synthesize pixels) by directly leveraging a pre-trained latent diffusion inpainting model (Stable Diffusion v2) with a progressive, occlusion-aware pipeline. It builds on grounded segmentation (Grounding DINO, SAM) and depth-ordering models for occluder identification, and on the LaMa removal inpainter for background cleanup, but introduces the Mixed Context Diffusion Sampling technique as a novel intervention into the diffusion denoising process to break co-occurrence bias. The counterfactual curation system draws on counterfactual image networks [35] but repurposes the idea for a training-free completeness check specific to amodal completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, progressive pipeline offers a practical path to photorealistic amodal completion in natural images without the domain gap of synthetic datasets or the fidelity limitations of GAN-based methods. However, the evaluation relies on a self-constructed pseudo-occlusion dataset and compares against relatively weak baselines (no other diffusion-based amodal completion method), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to truly diverse natural occlusions. Key limitations remain for small objects enclosed by large occluders, shadow-induced artifacts, and pose-dependent co-occurrence, and the counterfactual curation rule (0.70 accuracy) still trails human judgment (0.83), suggesting room for learned completeness predictors.
