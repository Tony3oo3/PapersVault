## Problem

Automatic cinemagraph generation from a single landscape image has been limited by methods that either require reference videos, train large synthesis networks from scratch, or operate at low resolutions. A key obstacle is that navigating the low-dimensional latent space of a pre-trained StyleGAN cannot preserve fine spatial details or produce content-preserving motion, because the latent space is highly semantic-condensed and lacks explicit spatial structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single landscape image (e.g., from the Sky Time-Lapse or LHQ datasets) and produces a seamlessly looping cinemagraph video at 1024×1024 resolution, where most of the scene remains static while a small region (sky, water) animates. The pre-trained StyleGAN generator is used without any fine-tuning; only lightweight auxiliary networks (an encoder for inversion, an MLP mask predictor, and a motion generator) are trained. Style interpolation in W+ space additionally allows the user to shift the appearance of the generated cinemagraph while preserving structure.

## Method

The core contribution is Multi-Scale Deep Feature Warping (MSDFW), which warps intermediate convolutional features of a pre-trained StyleGAN at five resolutions (layers 10, 12, 14, 16, 18) rather than operating solely in the latent space. GAN inversion recovers both W+ latent codes and the deep feature D^10 via a trained encoder, enabling high-fidelity reconstruction of landscape details that latent codes alone cannot capture. A motion generator predicts an Eulerian motion field, which is refined by a segmentation mask separating static and dynamic regions, then integrated via Euler steps to produce future and past displacement fields. Forward warping (splatting) of the multi-scale deep features using joint splatting with a looping weight α_t = 1 − t/N composites the warped features to synthesize each frame, avoiding the tearing artifacts of RGB warping and the blurriness of single-scale warping. The final frame is assembled by compositing the warped dynamic region with the original static region, optionally applying a style-interpolated latent code for appearance changes.

## Results

On the Sky Time-Lapse test set (224 videos), StyleCineGAN achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: static consistency LPIPS 0.0062, MS-SSIM 0.9962, RMSE 1.9430, and motion quality LPIPS 0.0131, MS-SSIM 0.7237, RMSE 20.8948, FID 39.21. In the user study (17 participants, 10 LHQ samples), the method scores 4.37/5 for static consistency and 3.86/5 for motion quality, outperforming the closest competitor T2C (3.75 and 2.96). In side-by-side comparisons, participants prefer StyleCineGAN over T2C 77.8% of the time for static consistency and 73.9% for motion quality, and over AL 99.4% and 92.8% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleCineGAN outperforms all four cinemagraph baselines on every reported metric. Against AL [6], the gap is largest (static RMSE 1.94 vs 8.90; motion FID 39.2 vs 53.7), as AL's recurrent backward warping produces visible stretching artifacts. Against EMF [9], the advantage is moderate but consistent (static LPIPS 0.0062 vs 0.0103; motion FID 39.2 vs 45.8), with EMF's from-scratch encoder-decoder struggling to reconstruct fine details. Against DL [17], the closest competitor on static consistency (RMSE 1.94 vs 2.10), StyleCineGAN still leads on all motion-quality metrics (FID 39.2 vs 41.6), benefiting from deep-feature-based inversion that preserves texture. Against T2C [19], the margin narrows (static LPIPS 0.0062 vs 0.0063; motion LPIPS 0.0131 vs 0.0159) but StyleCineGAN still wins on all seven metrics and in user preference (77.8% vs 22.2% for static consistency). Against MoCoGAN-HD [34] (unconditional video generation), StyleCineGAN shows dramatically better content preservation: LPIPS, RMSE, and MS-SSIM between the first and subsequent frames remain near-constant over 60 frames, whereas MoCoGAN-HD diverges sharply, confirming that latent-space navigation alone cannot maintain spatial structure for cinemagraphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of prior work. First, it diverges from cinemagraph generation methods (EMF, DL, T2C, AL) that train encoder-decoder or motion-generator networks from scratch, instead leveraging a fixed pre-trained StyleGAN as the synthesis backbone. Second, it departs from unconditional video generation methods that navigate StyleGAN's latent space (MoCoGAN-HD, StyleVideoGAN) by operating in the deep feature space rather than W+ codes, motivated by the observation that latent codes are too low-dimensional to preserve high-frequency landscape details. The multi-scale deep feature warping (MSDFW) module is the key architectural novelty, combining forward warping at multiple resolutions with joint splatting for seamless looping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a pre-trained StyleGAN can serve as a frozen, high-fidelity synthesis engine for cinemagraph generation at 1024×1024 without any fine-tuning, significantly reducing the training cost compared to from-scratch approaches. The ability to edit style and appearance via latent code interpolation adds a practical creative control dimension. However, the method is limited to landscape scenes (skies, fluids), struggles with thin structured objects due to low-resolution feature warping, and cannot resolve inherent motion ambiguity in static images—extending to more diverse motion types (rotating, flapping, articulated) remains an open direction.
