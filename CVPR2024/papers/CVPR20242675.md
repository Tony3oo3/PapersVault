## Problem

Most face encoders used for identity preservation in reenactment are trained on pose-imbalanced datasets, causing encoding confidence to drop sharply for large or extreme poses and degrading identity fidelity. Additionally, existing benchmark datasets lack sufficient large-pose face pairs, making it impossible to properly evaluate or compare large-pose reenactment performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242675/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image (providing identity and style) and a reference face image (providing pose and expression), both processed at 256×256 pixels with 3DDFA2 landmark annotations. The output is a single reenacted target face image that transfers the reference's pose and expression onto the source's identity. Internally, the pipeline operates in a 3D shape parameter space (DECA/FLAME β, ψ, θ) as an intermediate representation before image synthesis.

## Method

PASL comprises three modules: (1) six Pose-Adapted face Encoders (PAEs), each trained on a specific pose-pair subset (ff, ss, pp, fs, sp, fp) to provide pose-robust identity embeddings that outperform general-purpose encoders like ArcFace and VGGFace2 at large yaw angles; (2) a Cycle-consistent Shape Generator (CSG) modified from DECA that fuses source identity shape (β_s) with reference action (ψ_r, θ_r) into a recomposed shape, then enforces cycle-consistency by re-encoding and re-decoding so the output shape remains close to the source in identity space; (3) an Attention-Embedded Generator (AEG) that uses a multi-headed transformer encoder-decoder to learn self-attention within the shape code and cross-attention between the shape code and a StyleGAN2-style source style code, with AdaIN applied throughout the decoder for style preservation. Training proceeds in two stages (self-reenactment with ground-truth targets, then cross-reenactment from scratch) under a combined objective of pose-adapted identity loss, cycle-consistent shape loss, style loss, VGG perceptual loss, and adversarial loss.

## Results

On the cross-reenactment task, PASL achieves the best FID across all three benchmarks: 18.1 on MPIE-LP, 38.4 on VoxCeleb1, and 37.9 on VoxCeleb2-LP. Identity preservation (CSIM measured by PAE) reaches 0.46 on MPIE-LP, 0.68 on VoxCeleb1, and 0.52 on VoxCeleb2-LP, all leading the comparison. Pose transfer accuracy (ARD) is 2.24° on MPIE-LP and 2.05° on VoxCeleb1, and perceptual quality (LPIPS) reaches 0.21 on MPIE-LP. PASL outperforms the closest competitor, Dual Generator (DG), by 6.1 FID on MPIE-LP, 6.7 FID on VoxCeleb1, and 6.3 FID on VoxCeleb2-LP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASL outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is Dual Generator (DG) [14], which PASL beats by 6.1 FID on MPIE-LP (18.1 vs 24.2), 6.7 FID on VoxCeleb1 (38.4 vs 45.1), and 6.3 FID on VoxCeleb2-LP (37.9 vs 44.2), while also achieving higher CSIM (0.46 vs 0.28 on MPIE-LP; 0.52 vs 0.39 on VoxCeleb2-LP) and lower ARD. HyperReenact [2] is the second-best in identity preservation (CSIM 0.31 on MPIE-LP, 0.45 on VoxCeleb2-LP) but lags significantly in FID (85.2 vs 18.1 on MPIE-LP) and ARD (6.65° vs 2.24°). FOMM [24] and Bi-layer [32] degrade severely under large-pose conditions, with FOMM's ARD reaching 10.91° on MPIE-LP and Bi-layer's FID exceeding 100 on both large-pose datasets. On VoxCeleb1 (common pose range), PASL still leads all methods, confirming that the pose-adapted design does not sacrifice performance in the standard evaluation setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3D-shape-based face reenactment (building on DECA/FLAME decomposition as used by HeadGAN and Face2Face) and identity-preserving face encoders (VGGFace2, ArcFace, AdaFace). Its key departure is replacing the single frontal-biased identity encoder with six pose-adapted encoders (PAEs) trained on specific yaw ranges, effectively a piece-wise approximation of the nonlinear pose-identity mapping. The Cycle-consistent Shape Generator modifies DECA by adding a re-encode/re-decode loop to enforce identity-shape consistency, and the Attention-Embedded Generator introduces a transformer (self-attention on shape codes, cross-attention with StyleGAN2-style codes) between the shape space and the pixel-space generator, a combination not seen in prior hybrid reenactment methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MPIE-LP and VoxCeleb2-LP benchmarks addresses a genuine gap: prior face reenactment evaluations on VoxCeleb1 or MPIE contain too few large-pose pairs to meaningfully test pose-robust identity preservation, so reported SOTA numbers may overstate real-world performance at extreme yaw. The PAE strategy—training separate encoders per pose band—is a simple, transferable idea that could benefit any face recognition or verification pipeline dealing with pose imbalance. Open questions include whether the six-encoder design scales gracefully (e.g., to 3D pose with roll/pitch), whether the cycle-consistency constraint in CSG generalizes beyond DECA's parameterization, and whether the transformer-based AEG can be replaced by lighter-weight attention without losing the reported gains.
