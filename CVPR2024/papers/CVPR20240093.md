## Problem

Weakly supervised referring image segmentation (RIS) must learn pixel-level semantics from image-text pairs alone, producing coarse and imprecise masks. A natural remedy—integrating the SAM foundation model to refine coarse localizations into precise masks—fails or even degrades performance because noisy attention responses mislead SAM and SAM's edge-oriented, semantic-unaware nature causes excessive focus on salient object parts rather than the full referent. The paper addresses both failure modes simultaneously through a point prompting framework and a curriculum learning strategy.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and a free-form natural language expression as input and produces a fixed-size set of K predictions, each comprising a segmentation mask, a point prompt of M points (each with normalized 2D coordinates and a positive/negative classification score), and a referent confidence score. At inference, the mask with the highest confidence is selected as the final output. The point prompts are the key intermediate representation: positive points guide SAM toward the referent interior while negative points explicitly suppress noisy or misleading regions, a mechanism absent in prior box- or mask-based prompting approaches.

## Method

PPT uses a trainable, lightweight DETR-style point generator (20.7M parameters) to bridge frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoders and a frozen ViT-B SAM decoder. Learnable point queries interact with multi-stage CLIP image features and word-level text features via alternating cross-attention, then regress point coordinates, positive/negative point scores, and referent confidence through separate MLP heads. The critical design insight is that negative point prompts inherently distinguish noise and misleading context (e.g., a green t-shirt in 'man in green') from the true referent, resolving SAM's segmentation ambiguity. Training follows a three-stage multi-source curriculum: (1) semantic-alignment on object-centric ImageNet pseudo-labels plus simple single-referent RIS data, (2) RIS learning with relation-augmented ImageNet data (Mosaic composition and image fusion) plus multi-referent RIS data with iteratively selected pseudo-referents, and (3) domain refinement fine-tuning exclusively on RIS data to close the distribution gap. Pseudo-labels for ImageNet are generated via CLIP+SAM on object-centric images, while RIS pseudo-labels are extracted per noun phrase to handle expression complexity.

## Results

PPT achieves mIoU of 46.76 (val), 45.33 (test A), and 46.28 (test B) on RefCOCO; 45.34 (val), 45.84 (test A), and 44.77 (test B) on RefCOCO+; and 42.97 (val-G) on G-Ref. These represent average mIoU improvements of 11.34%, 14.14%, and 6.97% over the previous best weakly supervised methods (Shatter, Chunk, and TRIS respectively). On precision metrics, PPT reaches P@0.5 of 50.19% and P@0.7 of 36.22% on RefCOCO val, surpassing the prior best by 25.2% and 27.2% respectively, while using only 20.7M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPT outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset partition. Against the strongest weakly supervised competitor Shatter [18], PPT gains 11.34% average mIoU on RefCOCO (46.12 vs. 34.78), 16.96% on RefCOCO+ (45.32 vs. 28.35), and 14.10% on G-Ref (42.97 vs. 28.87). Compared to TRIS [37], which shares a pseudo-label-then-train strategy, PPT improves by 15.59%, 14.44%, and 6.97% on the three datasets respectively. Notably, naively adding SAM to TRIS (TRIS+SAM) actually degrades performance (e.g., 25.56 vs. 31.17 on RefCOCO val), confirming the paper's observation that noisy CLIP attention maps mislead SAM; PPT avoids this by generating negative point prompts that suppress background interference. Against Chunk [27], the prior best on RefCOCO+ among weakly supervised methods, PPT leads by 14.06 mIoU points on the val split. On precision at stricter thresholds, PPT's P@0.7 of 36.22% nearly quadruples Shatter's 6.35% and Chunk's 9.02%, indicating substantially tighter mask boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends weakly supervised referring image segmentation (RIS) by bridging frozen CLIP encoders and frozen SAM decoders through a trainable, DETR-inspired point generator, departing from prior weakly supervised RIS methods (TRIS, Shatter, Chunk) that rely on noisy pseudo-labels from attention maps or Grad-CAM. It also introduces a multi-source curriculum learning strategy that progressively trains from object-centric ImageNet data to scene-centric RIS data, a training paradigm not previously applied to this task. The use of negative point prompts to suppress background noise and the augmentation of object-centric images with spatial relationships represent novel repurposings of SAM's prompting interface and Mosaic-style data augmentation, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naively combining CLIP and SAM for weakly supervised RIS actually degrades performance, a finding that cautions against treating foundation models as plug-and-play refinements. The 11–14% mIoU gains over prior weakly supervised methods with only 20.7M trainable parameters (vs. 142M for TRIS) suggest a practical, parameter-efficient path toward pixel-accurate segmentation without mask annotations. Open questions include whether the curriculum strategy generalizes beyond spatial relationships to richer compositional semantics, whether the approach scales to video or 3D segmentation, and how sensitive performance is to the choice and quantity of object-centric auxiliary data.
