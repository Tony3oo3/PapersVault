## Problem

Video transformers for action recognition rely exclusively on RGB, which is insufficient for Activities of Daily Living where visually similar actions (e.g., PourFromBottle vs. PourFromKettle) and multi-viewpoint observations demand fine-grained and view-invariant representations. Existing RGB+pose fusion methods require depth sensors or expensive 3D pose estimation at inference, introducing latency and hardware constraints that limit real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241698/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes only an RGB video clip (T×H×W×3) and outputs a single action class label, with no pose input or additional computational overhead. During training, 2D skeleton joint coordinates and 3D skeleton sequences are additionally provided as supervision signals for the auxiliary modules. The video is tokenized into spatio-temporal patches of size τ×p×p, and the 2D/3D skeletons are aligned to these token grids via max-pooling (2D) or temporal resampling (3D) to match the transformer's spatial and temporal resolution.

## Method

PI-ViT (π-ViT) inserts two plug-in modules into a pretrained TimeSformer backbone: a 2D Skeleton Induction Module (2D-SIM) and a 3D Skeleton Induction Module (3D-SIM). 2D-SIM, placed near early layers, predicts a binary token-skeleton map (presence/absence of each joint per visual token) via a fully-connected head, using binary cross-entropy to enforce fine-grained appearance supervision on joint-localized RGB regions. 3D-SIM, placed near deeper layers, projects pooled visual tokens into the embedding space of a frozen Hyperformer 3D skeleton model and minimizes MSE alignment loss (global or local) plus a cross-entropy classification loss on the hallucinated skeleton features, inducing viewpoint-agnostic motion knowledge. The total training loss is the sum of the main classification loss and both auxiliary losses; at inference, both modules are simply removed, leaving a standard video transformer that requires no pose input.

## Results

On Toyota-Smarthome (the real-world ADL benchmark), π-ViT using only RGB at inference achieves 72.9% mCA on cross-subject, 55.2% on CV1, and 64.8% on CV2, outperforming the TimeSformer backbone by +6.6%, +10.4%, and +6.9% respectively. On NTU120, π-ViT reaches 91.9% top-1 on cross-subject and 92.9% on cross-setup (RGB-only inference), surpassing Video Swin (91.4%/92.1%) and TimeSformer (90.6%/91.6%). On NTU60, π-ViT achieves 94.0% CS and 97.9% CV, matching or exceeding all prior RGB-only methods. When 3D poses are additionally available at inference, π-ViT+3D Poses reaches 73.1% CS on Toyota-Smarthome and 95.1% CS on NTU120, competitive with the SoTA PoseC3D (95.3% CS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Toyota-Smarthome, π-ViT (RGB-only inference) outperforms all prior RGB-only methods: it beats Video Swin by +3.1% CS and +18.6% CV1, and TimeSformer by +4.5% CS and +5.2% CV1. Remarkably, π-ViT without any poses at inference also surpasses RGB+Pose methods that require 3D poses at test time, e.g., VPN+++3D Poses (71.0% CS) and PoseC3D (53.8% CS). On NTU120, π-ViT achieves the best RGB-only result (91.9% CS, 92.9% CSet), edging out Video Swin (91.4%/92.1%) and TimeSformer (90.6%/91.6%), but falls short of PoseC3D (95.3%/96.4%) in the RGB+Pose category. On NTU60, π-ViT leads all RGB-only baselines (94.0% CS vs. TimeSformer's 93.0% and Video Swin's 93.4%) and is competitive on CV (97.9% vs. ViewCon's 98.0%). The paper notes that PoseC3D, while strong on NTU, drops to 53.8% CS on Toyota-Smarthome, suggesting its reliance on high-quality lab poses limits real-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the video transformer lineage (TimeSformer, Video Swin, MotionFormer) and skeleton-based action recognition (Hyperformer, PoseC3D, 3Mformer), but departs from prior RGB+Pose fusion methods (STAR-Transformer, 3D-Def-Transformer, VPN++) that require pose inputs at inference time. The key conceptual departure is treating pose information as a training-time-only signal: rather than fusing modalities at inference, PI-ViT uses pose-aware auxiliary tasks (2D joint classification and 3D feature alignment) to reshape the RGB representations, then discards the pose modules entirely. The paper also explicitly contrasts with traditional knowledge distillation, showing that all four standard KD variants (feature/logit → class/distillation token) fail to transfer skeleton knowledge into video transformers, motivating the proposed alignment-in-different-embedding-space approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The train-with-poses, infer-without-poses paradigm removes the practical barrier of requiring depth sensors or expensive 3D pose estimation at deployment, making video transformers viable for real-world ADL monitoring where such infrastructure is unavailable. The finding that 2D-SIM helps on real-world Smarthome but not on controlled NTU datasets highlights that fine-grained appearance discrimination is a genuinely different challenge from lab-based recognition, and that pose induction addresses it specifically. Open questions include whether this induction strategy generalizes beyond ADL to other fine-grained or multi-view action domains, and whether the approach extends to multi-person or occluded scenarios where skeleton annotations are unreliable.
