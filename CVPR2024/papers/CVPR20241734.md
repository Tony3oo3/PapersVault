## Problem

Single-source domain generalization for object detection suffers from distribution shift in unseen environments, but existing strategies—domain-invariant feature learning and data augmentation alone—both fail because they produce non-causal, biased features. The paper identifies three specific biases (data bias, scene-level attention bias, and object-level prototype bias) caused by scene confounders and object attribute confounders, and is the first to formalize SDG object detection from a causal perspective using a Structural Causal Model.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and, during training, simultaneously processes both the original image and a GLT-augmented version through a shared Faster R-CNN backbone (ResNet-101). At inference, the model produces bounding boxes and class labels for seven object categories (person, car, bike, rider, motor, bus, truck) while maintaining the same parameter count as vanilla Faster R-CNN; the only architectural change is that features fed into the RPN are gated by a learned attention map rather than passed directly.

## Method

The Unbiased Faster R-CNN (UFR) combines three modules guided by a Structural Causal Model that defines the ideal feature mapping as invariant under scene and object-attribute perturbations. (1) A Global-Local Transformation module augments data in both the frequency domain (Fourier transform with a randomized band-pass filter) and the spatial domain (SAM-segmented objects undergo independent random augmentations), with the final image a weighted fusion of both. (2) A Causal Attention Learning module computes a sigmoid attention map from the backbone and enforces an attention invariance loss (Dice loss between binarized attention maps of original vs. augmented images), then multiplies the attention map onto backbone features before the RPN to select scene-level causal features. (3) A Causal Prototype Learning module imposes an explicit KL-divergence constraint on ROI feature distributions across augmentations and an implicit prototype contrastive loss that pulls same-category prototypes together and pushes different-category prototypes apart across augmentations. The total training loss is the supervised detection loss plus weighted attention and prototype losses, and the model is trained end-to-end with SGD for 80k iterations.

## Results

On the headline Night-Clear scene, UFR achieves 40.8% mAP, outperforming the strongest baseline CLIP-Gap by 3.9% and vanilla Faster R-CNN by 5.0%. On the source Daytime-Clear scene, UFR reaches 58.6% mAP (a 2.4% gain over FR), confirming that the causal constraints do not sacrifice in-domain performance. Across all four unseen target domains, UFR consistently achieves the best mAP, with gains of 5.2% (Dusk-Rainy), 5.0% (Night-Rainy), and 6.1% (Daytime-Foggy) over vanilla Faster R-CNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFR outperforms all seven baselines on every evaluated scene. Against the two purpose-built SDG methods, UFR beats CLIP-Gap [38] by 3.9% mAP on Night-Clear, 0.9% on Dusk-Rainy, 0.5% on Night-Rainy, and 1.1% on Daytime-Foggy, and beats SDGOD [44] by 4.2% on Night-Clear and 6.1% on Daytime-Foggy. The four feature-normalization baselines (SW, IBN-Net, IterNorm, ISW) all underperform vanilla Faster R-CNN on most unseen scenes, with IterNorm dropping to 29.6% mAP on Night-Clear versus FR's 35.8%, confirming that domain-invariant normalization is insufficient for single-source generalization. Notably, UFR's largest per-category gains on Night-Clear come in Car (+16.3% over FR) and Person (+12.8%), while it slightly trails FR on Motor (14.7 vs. 17.5) and Rider (26.4 vs. 29.2), suggesting the causal prototype constraint is less effective for smaller, less structurally distinctive object classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-source domain generalization (SDG) object detection line initiated by SDGOD [44] and CLIP-Gap [38], but departs from both by rejecting their core assumptions: domain-invariant features are inherently biased, and data augmentation alone without feature constraints fails to capture causal structure. It draws on the causal-inference-for-vision thread (Xu et al. [45] for frequency-domain augmentation, Lv et al. [22] for causality-inspired representation learning) and repurposes SAM [15] as a segmentation tool for local data augmentation. The key conceptual departure is constructing a task-specific Structural Causal Model that decomposes feature bias into scene-confounder-driven attention bias and object-attribute-confounder-driven prototype bias, then addressing each with a dedicated module (Causal Attention Learning and Causal Prototype Learning) on top of a standard Faster R-CNN backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal SCM provides a principled diagnostic framework for understanding why SDG detectors fail, and the ablation showing that causal constraints deliberately sacrifice source-domain accuracy (60.4% → 58.6% mAP on Daytime-Clear) while gaining 3–6% on unseen scenes is a useful quantitative illustration of the in-domain/out-of-domain trade-off. However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset with five weather conditions and one detector architecture (Faster R-CNN + ResNet-101), and the SAM dependency adds a heavy pre-processing step that limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether the SCM structure and the attention/prototype decomposition transfer to other detection paradigms (e.g., DETR-based detectors), whether the causal framing generalizes beyond weather-condition shifts to more diverse domain gaps, and whether the explicit KL constraint on ROI features can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives.
