## Problem

Existing neural avatar methods handle either dynamic facial animation or relightability, but not both simultaneously. Static relightable NeRFs (ReNeRF, NeLFs) cannot animate, while dynamic avatars (MVP, NeRFBlendShapes) bake in illumination. Furthermore, prior relightable approaches assume distant point lights and fixed camera positions, precluding nearfield lighting effects and nearfield viewpoint changes such as dolly-zoom.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a tracked 3D face mesh (expression blendshape parameters from an actor-specific local model), an arbitrary 3D light position or environment map, and a 3D camera position with focal length, and produces a photorealistic RGB render of the head via volumetric rendering of 16,384 primitives (each an 8×8×8 RGBα voxel grid). Training uses ~1,800 OLAT frames per subject captured with 10 cameras and 32 individually-addressable LED bars at 1024×768 resolution, with per-frame tracked mesh geometry and a single 3D light position per frame.

## Method

The method extends the Mixture of Volumetric Primitives (MVP) architecture by replacing the single global view vector in the appearance branch with per-primitive local light and view directions computed as l_k = p_olat − R_k·t_k and v_k = p_cam − R_k·t_k, where R_k·t_k is the world-space center of each primitive. These 6-channel directions are bilinearly downsampled and concatenated at every layer of a 7-layer fully-convolutional appearance network, so early layers see approximately global directions while deeper layers specialize per-primitive appearance for nearfield effects. The mesh encoder is a modified Deep Appearance Model with the texture branch removed, driving the model purely from expression geometry to enable artistically novel expressions at inference. Training is end-to-end on OLAT sequences (one LED bar lit per frame, interleaved with full-on frames every 3rd frame) using a matting loss to suppress background primitives, requiring only a sparse 32-bar LED setup rather than a dense light stage.

## Results

On held-out validation data across three subjects, the method achieves PSNR values of 27.3–39.8 dB, MAE of 2.88–5.27, SSIM of 0.826–0.899, and LPIPS of 0.278–0.390 across three evaluation conditions (novel light, novel performance, and both). The method consistently outperforms the re-implemented TRAvatar* baseline on all four metrics in every condition and for every subject. Qualitatively, the method supports nearfield point-light relighting, dolly-zoom camera effects, and arbitrary environment-map illumination—capabilities that TRAvatar* cannot achieve due to its fixed lighting basis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline: TRAvatar* (a re-implementation of the lighting branch from Yang et al. 2023), evaluated on held-out novel light directions, novel performances, and both simultaneously across three subjects. The authors state their method "clearly outperforms TRAvatar* in terms of both qualitative render quality and quantitative analysis in all cases." The most significant advantage is functional: TRAvatar* relies on a fixed lighting basis and can only evaluate novel directions via barycentric interpolation within that basis, whereas the proposed method generalizes to arbitrary light directions and supports nearfield lighting effects that TRAvatar* cannot model at all. On the quantitative side, the method achieves higher PSNR and SSIM and lower MAE and LPIPS in the majority of subject-condition pairs, with the largest gaps appearing in the novel-light setting (e.g., S1: 32.19 vs. 29.68 PSNR; S3: 39.75 vs. 28.86 PSNR). The comparison is limited by the scarcity of publicly available relightable-and-animatable neural avatar methods with released code, which the authors acknowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: the Mixture of Volumetric Primitives (MVP) architecture for animatable neural heads [25] and the OLAT-based relighting framework from ReNeRF [50] for static neural avatars. The key architectural departure is replacing the single global view/light vector in MVP's appearance branch with per-primitive local directions computed from the geometry branch's world-space transformations, which enables nearfield lighting and viewpoints. Unlike the concurrent TRAvatar [51], which adds a linear lighting branch requiring a dense fixed basis, this method uses a learned appearance branch conditioned on continuous local directions, allowing interpolation to novel light positions and nearfield effects without an expensive light stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously support full facial animation, arbitrary environment-map relighting, and nearfield light/viewpoint effects in a single neural head representation, trained on data from a modest 32-LED-bar setup rather than a dense light stage. The practical impact for the entertainment industry is significant: artists can drive the avatar with novel expressions, reposition lights in the scene, and simulate camera moves like dolly-zoom without re-capture. Open limitations include the restriction to frontal head geometry (no 360°), inability to extrapolate extreme expressions beyond training data, and lack of control over gaze and hair motion, all of which point toward future work on full-head capture and richer semantic controls.
