## Problem

Existing VLM-based unsupervised domain adaptation methods adapt either the visual or textual branch of CLIP in isolation, overlooking the modality gap phenomenon where vision and text features remain distinctly distributed. The paper identifies that different samples are best classified by different modalities—visually straightforward items benefit from the vision branch while semantically ambiguous items benefit from the text branch—yet no prior method synergistically combines both modalities for adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single forward pass through frozen CLIP to extract vision features (d_v-dimensional) and text features from naive prompts of the form '[DOMAIN] photo of a [CLASS]', requiring no iterative forwarding through CLIP during training. At inference, the output is a class label produced by a fixed-weight ensemble (w=0.3) of the VAC linear classifier prediction and the LAC cosine-similarity prediction against CLIP text features. The entire pipeline operates on pre-extracted features stored in memory, reducing GPU memory to under 3 GB even on DomainNet's 345 classes.

## Method

UniMoS disentangles CLIP-extracted vision features into language-associated components (LAC) and vision-associated components (VAC) via two linear separators regularized by an orthogonal loss to enforce disentanglement. LAC is trained by distilling CLIP's zero-shot similarity scores (with ACDE debiasing to correct class-imbalance bias) onto the target domain, while VAC is trained on K-means pseudo-labels generated from ensemble outputs, augmented with an information maximization loss to enforce intra-class compactness and inter-class diversity. A dynamic weight generator W(VAC) adaptively blends VAC and LAC predictions during training, with the LAC output detached from the computational graph to preserve pretrained semantics. A modality discriminator, trained only on source data to distinguish LAC from VAC, is then frozen on the target domain to align both components across domains by updating only the separation networks.

## Results

On Office-Home, UniMoS achieves 77.9% average accuracy across 12 adaptation tasks, outperforming the strongest CLIP-based baseline PADCLIP (76.6%) by +1.3% and the best non-CLIP method (EIDCo/ICON at 75.8%) by +2.1%. On VisDA-2017, UniMoS reaches 88.1% average accuracy, slightly trailing PADCLIP (88.5%) but surpassing all other methods. On DomainNet, UniMoS achieves 63.6% average, matching PADCLIP (63.7%) while significantly outperforming DAPrompt (59.8%) and CLIP zero-shot (56.2%). On Mini-DomainNet, UniMoS attains 78.0% (ResNet50) and 87.3% (ViT-B), setting new state-of-the-art among CLIP-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMoS outperforms PADCLIP (the strongest full-tuning CLIP baseline) by +1.3% on Office-Home (77.9% vs 76.6%) and achieves comparable performance on DomainNet (63.6% vs 63.7%), while slightly trailing PADCLIP on VisDA-2017 (88.1% vs 88.5%) due to CLIP's incompatibility with synthetic source images. Among CLIP-based non-tuning methods, UniMoS leads DAPrompt by +3.4% (77.9% vs 74.5%) and ADCLIP by +2.0% (77.9% vs 75.9%) on Office-Home, with similar margins on VisDA and DomainNet. Against all non-CLIP UDA baselines (EIDCo, ICON, KUDA, ATDOC, etc.), UniMoS achieves a clear +2.1% advantage over the best (75.8%) on Office-Home. On Mini-DomainNet, UniMoS achieves 78.0% (ResNet50) and 87.3% (ViT-B), surpassing ADCLIP (75.2%/86.9%) and DAPrompt (74.8%/85.8%) by 2.8–3.4%. Notably, UniMoS requires only 0.79M trainable parameters and 0.5H training time on VisDA versus PADCLIP's 23.5H, yielding a >47× speedup while matching or exceeding accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against prior CLIP-based UDA methods (DAPrompt, PADCLIP, ADCLIP) that adapt either the visual or textual branch in isolation, arguing that the modality gap literature (Liang et al. 2022; Jiang et al. 2023) reveals each modality carries unique, complementary cues. It draws on Domain Separation Networks (Bousmalis et al. 2016) for the orthogonal disentanglement principle and on K-means deep clustering (Caron et al. 2018) for pseudo-label generation, but departs from both by applying separation *posteriorly* to frozen CLIP features rather than training a VLM from scratch. The key conceptual departure is the 'split-to-merge' paradigm: explicitly decomposing a single vision feature into language-associated and vision-associated subspaces, training each with modality-appropriate objectives, then recombining via a learnable ensemble weight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: by training only a handful of linear layers (0.79M parameters) on pre-extracted CLIP features, UniMoS achieves a 47× training speedup over PADCLIP and requires under 3 GB GPU memory versus 22 GB for DAPrompt on DomainNet, making CLIP-based UDA accessible on modest hardware. A notable limitation is that on VisDA-2017 (synthetic→real) and DomainNet's qdr target, the method trails PADCLIP because CLIP was never exposed to those visual domains, suggesting the frozen-backbone assumption has a ceiling. Open questions include whether the modality separation generalizes to tasks beyond classification (e.g., detection, segmentation) and whether the approach extends to VLMs with larger modality gaps or different architectures beyond CLIP.
