## Problem

Deterministic regression-based image restoration methods minimize pixel-level error and thus produce blurry outputs lacking fine-grained texture details. Directly applying diffusion models to restoration is impractical because their inherent stochasticity conflicts with the high structural fidelity that restoration demands. The paper addresses how to harness diffusion's strength in generating realistic textures while preserving content fidelity and enabling efficient, generalizable adaptation across diverse restoration tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240883/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (motion-blurred, defocus-blurred, rainy, hazy, snowy, or with raindrops) as input, processed in 256×256 patches, and outputs a restored image with realistic texture details. Internally, a lightweight U-Net initial predictor first produces a coarse structural estimate from the degraded input, which then conditions the diffusion denoiser. The diffusion model operates on the residual between the target clean image and its edge-preserving smoothed version, so it only needs to synthesize the texture layer rather than the full image content.

## Method

DTPM uses a two-stage training strategy. In Stage I, a conditional diffusion model (built on NAFNet-style denoising blocks with depth-wise convolutions, Channel Attention, SimpleGate, and AdaLN timestep injection) is pre-trained on ~55K high-quality images from DIV2K and Flickr2K to learn texture priors via residual learning, with a ResNet-18 semantic code injected into each block as a global constraint. In Stage II, most pre-trained parameters are frozen and lightweight Conditional Guidance Adapters (CG-Adapters) are inserted between layers; each adapter adds a residual branch that fuses the initial predictor's coarse structural output with the current features via MLPs, enabling parameter-efficient fine-tuning for specific tasks without catastrophic forgetting. Sampling uses DDIM with 4, 10, or 50 steps, and the initial predictor requires no separate supervision—its gradients flow through the diffusion loss.

## Results

On Snow100K-S (desnowing), DTPM-4 Step achieves PSNR 37.01, SSIM 0.9663, and LPIPS 0.034, outperforming the best diffusion baseline SnowDiff64 (PSNR 36.59, LPIPS 0.035) and the best regression baseline Restormer (PSNR 35.03, LPIPS 0.047). On Outdoor-Rain (deraining & dehazing), DTPM-4 Step reaches PSNR 30.99 and LPIPS 0.0635, surpassing Restormer (PSNR 29.97, LPIPS 0.074) and RainHazeDiff64 (PSNR 28.38, LPIPS 0.067). On GoPro (motion deblurring), DTPM-50 Step achieves the best FID of 6.61 and LPIPS of 0.061, though Restormer retains a PSNR advantage (33.20 vs. 31.11). Across all five tasks, DTPM consistently improves perceptual quality (LPIPS/FID) while maintaining competitive distortion metrics, with a clear trade-off where more sampling steps reduce LPIPS at the cost of PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTPM-4 Step outperforms all 12 baselines on Snow100K-S in PSNR (37.01 vs. 36.59 for SnowDiff64) and SSIM (0.9663 vs. 0.9626), while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.034 vs. 0.035). On Outdoor-Rain, DTPM-4 Step beats Restormer by +1.02 dB PSNR and 0.0105 LPIPS, and surpasses the diffusion-based RainHazeDiff64 by +2.61 dB PSNR, demonstrating that the texture-prior approach recovers more structural fidelity than both regression and prior diffusion methods. On GoPro motion deblurring, Restormer retains a PSNR edge (33.20 vs. 32.09 for DTPM-4 Step), but DTPM-50 Step achieves a substantially lower FID (6.61 vs. 19.33) and LPIPS (0.061 vs. 0.084), indicating far superior perceptual realism. On DPDD defocus deblurring, DTPM-4 Step matches Restormer's PSNR (25.98) while improving SSIM (0.823 vs. 0.811) and LPIPS (0.153 vs. 0.178). On RainDrop removal, DTPM-4 Step leads in PSNR (32.72 vs. 32.43 for RainDropDiff128) and SSIM (0.9440 vs. 0.9334), with DTPM-50 Step achieving the best LPIPS (0.0439 vs. 0.058). Overall, DTPM wins or ties on perceptual metrics across all five tasks, with the largest margins over diffusion-based baselines (WeatherDiff variants) and consistent PSNR gains over regression baselines (Restormer, NAFNet, MPRNet).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on NAFNet's lightweight denoising block architecture and DvSR's stochastic refinement paradigm (using a U-Net initial predictor), but departs from both by restricting the diffusion process to texture layers via residual learning rather than modeling the full image distribution. Unlike WeatherDiff, which applies patch-based diffusion directly to degraded images, DTPM pre-trains a texture prior on ~55K high-quality images (DIV2K + Flickr2K) and then adapts it to downstream tasks through parameter-efficient Conditional Guidance Adapters, avoiding catastrophic forgetting of the learned texture knowledge. The use of ResNet-18 semantic embeddings as a global constraint on texture generation is a novel conditioning mechanism not present in prior diffusion-based restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a 4-step DDIM sampling schedule matches or exceeds the perceptual quality of 50-step diffusion baselines (e.g., WeatherDiff), making diffusion-based restoration more deployable. The texture/structure decomposition offers a principled way to harness diffusion's generative strengths while mitigating its stochasticity, a design choice that could inform future generative restoration frameworks. Limitations include the notable PSNR–LPIPS trade-off (PSNR drops ~1 dB from 4 to 50 steps), evaluation primarily on synthetic degradations, and the reliance on a pre-trained ResNet-18 for semantic conditioning that may not generalize to out-of-domain content. Open questions include whether the texture prior can be extended to video restoration or whether the adapter mechanism scales to more diverse degradation types without retraining.
