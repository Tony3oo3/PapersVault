## Problem

Pre-trained foundation model features (DINOv2, Stable Diffusion) achieve strong semantic correspondence but systematically fail on geometry-aware cases where semantically identical parts differ only in their spatial relationship to the object's overall orientation (e.g., left vs. right paw). These ambiguous cases account for ~60% of keypoints in SPair-71k, and state-of-the-art methods perform up to 30% worse on this subset, revealing a fundamental limitation in how current features encode instance geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241265/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source and target RGB image pair, resized to 960² and 840² respectively to extract Stable Diffusion and DINOv2 features at a shared 60×60 resolution. Training additionally requires sparse keypoint annotations (17 keypoints per instance in AP-10K). At inference, the output is a dense correspondence field: for each query pixel, a similarity map over the target feature map is computed and refined via window soft-argmax to yield sub-pixel correspondence coordinates.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight 4-bottleneck post-processor (5M parameters) on top of fused SD+DINOv2 features, trained with a dense soft-argmax objective that back-propagates gradients from sparse keypoint annotations to all spatial locations, combined with pose-variant augmentation (double/single/self flip) that exploits the features' inherent global pose awareness to generate geometrically meaningful supervision from a single flipped forward pass. At test time, a zero-shot adaptive pose alignment step uses an Instance Matching Distance (IMD) metric to select the optimal viewpoint augmentation of the source image before matching, and a window soft-argmax (15×15 for supervised, 11×11 for unsupervised) replaces global argmax to enable sub-pixel reasoning while avoiding noise from distant pixels. The paper also introduces the AP-10K benchmark (261k training, 36k test pairs across 54 species) built from animal pose annotations, which serves as both a pre-training resource and a cross-species/cross-family evaluation suite.

## Results

On SPair-71k, the zero-shot method achieves 65.4 PCK@0.10 (5.5p over SD+DINO's 59.9), while the supervised method with AP-10K pre-training reaches 85.6 PCK@0.10 (11.0p over SD+DINO(S)'s 74.6). On the geometry-aware subset, the supervised method scores 82.3 PCK@0.10 versus 67.6 for SD+DINO(S), reducing the relative gap between geometry-aware and standard sets from 9.38% to 3.86%. On the new AP-10K intra-species benchmark, the supervised method achieves 87.5 PCK@0.10, outperforming the previous best (SD+DINO(S)) by 10.5p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the zero-shot setting, Ours-Zero-Shot† (65.4 PCK@0.10 on SPair-71k) outperforms SD+DINO (59.9) by 5.5p, DINOv2+NN (53.9) by 11.5p, DIFT (52.9) by 12.5p, and ASIC (36.9) by 28.5p, with the largest gains on geometry-ambiguous categories like Cat (82.7 vs. 78.6) and Train (83.8 vs. 66.2). In the supervised setting, the full method with AP-10K pre-training (85.6 PCK@0.10) surpasses SD+DINO(S) (74.6) by 11.0p, DHF (64.9) by 20.7p, CATs++ (59.8) by 25.8p, and SCorrSAN (55.3) by 30.3p. On the geometry-aware subset specifically, the supervised method (82.3 PCK@0.10) beats SD+DINO(S) (67.6) by 14.7p, demonstrating that the gains are concentrated where geometric ambiguity is highest. On the new AP-10K benchmark, the method generalizes well across intra-species (87.5), cross-species (85.8), and cross-family (78.4) settings, consistently outperforming SD+DINO(S) by 10.5p, 11.8p, and 12.6p respectively. On PF-Pascal, the supervised method achieves 98.0 PCK@0.15, marginally exceeding SD+DINO(S) (96.9), indicating that the gains are most pronounced on challenging, high-variation datasets rather than easy ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the fused SD+DINO feature architecture from [61] (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2023), which established that combining Stable Diffusion and DINOv2 features yields strong zero-shot semantic correspondence. Rather than proposing a new feature extractor, the authors identify a specific geometric blind spot in those features and address it through targeted post-processing: a test-time pose alignment strategy, a dense soft-argmax training objective, pose-variant augmentation, and a window soft-argmax inference module. The work also connects to the broader semantic correspondence pipeline literature (CATs, SCOT, DHF) and repurposes the AP-10K animal pose estimation dataset as a correspondence benchmark, extending the evaluation scope beyond the intra-species setting of SPair-71k to cross-species and cross-family matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that nearly 60% of SPair-71k keypoints are geometry-ambiguous and that SOTA methods degrade by up to 30% on this subset provides a concrete, measurable diagnostic for foundation model feature limitations that the field has lacked. The AP-10K benchmark (261k training pairs, 5× larger than SPair-71k, with cross-species and cross-family splits) fills a critical gap in evaluation scale and diversity. Limitations remain for small instances and extreme deformations, and the approach relies on 2D pose alignment rather than explicit 3D geometry, leaving open whether richer geometric priors (e.g., spherical constraints from concurrent work [31]) could further close the gap. The simplicity of the fixes (0.32% extra runtime) makes them immediately adoptable in existing pipelines.
