## Problem

Existing weakly supervised monocular 3D detection methods rely on additional training data—LiDAR point clouds (WeakM3D) or multi-view/multi-frame images (WeakMono3D)—which severely limits their applicability and deployment cost. The paper asks whether depth from an off-the-shelf single-view depth estimator can be exploited to achieve competitive 3D detection without any extra training data and with minimal inference overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240754/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a single-view RGB image, its 2D bounding box annotations, and pseudo depth maps generated by a frozen off-the-shelf estimator (PENET); no 3D box labels or LiDAR/multi-view data are used. At inference, only the Monocular 3D Detection Network (MDN) runs on the RGB image alone, producing per-object 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation), 2D boxes, category, and a scalar uncertainty estimate—eliminating the depth estimator entirely. The 3D predictions are projected back to 2D for the projection-based losses during training.

## Method

SKD-WM3D employs a self-knowledge distillation framework with two subnetworks sharing a DLA-34 backbone: a Depth-Guided Self-Teaching Network (DSN) that fuses depth features (from a depth head supervised by pseudo depth maps) with global features via CrossAttention, SelfAttention, and an FFN to form 3D-like representations, and a Monocular 3D Detection Network (MDN) that uses RoIAlign and a 2D-to-3D head to predict 3D boxes directly from image features. Knowledge flows from DSN to MDN through an uncertainty-aware distillation loss that inversely weights the SmoothL1 box-consistency term by the average prediction uncertainty (capped at α=0.1) plus an L2 penalty on uncertainty, so certain knowledge is emphasized while uncertain predictions are down-weighted. A gradient-targeted transfer modulation strategy dynamically rescales the distillation gradients for each network based on their respective 2D projection losses, prioritizing the lagging network early in training and enabling reciprocal feedback later. At inference, only the lightweight MDN operates, yielding 30.3 FPS on KITTI—faster than both WeakM3D (13.9 FPS) and MonoDistill (25.0 FPS)—since no depth estimation or fusion modules are needed.

## Results

On the KITTI validation set (IoU=0.5), SKD-WM3D achieves AP_BEV/AP_3D of 55.47/50.21 (Easy), 44.35/41.57 (Moderate), and 41.86/36.92 (Hard) for the Car category, surpassing WeakMono3D by 1.52/2.56 AP_BEV on Moderate and 1.79/0.58 AP_3D on Hard. On the KITTI test set (IoU=0.7), it reaches 15.71/8.95 (Easy), 10.15/5.54 (Moderate), and 8.08/4.53 (Hard), outperforming WeakM3D by 3.89/3.92 AP_BEV/AP_3D on Easy. On nuScenes validation, SKD-WM3D achieves AP 0.242, ATE 0.795, ASE 0.231, and AAE 0.659, improving over WeakM3D on all four metrics. Inference speed reaches 30.3 FPS, faster than WeakM3D (13.9 FPS) and MonoDistill (25.0 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among weakly supervised methods, SKD-WM3D clearly outperforms WeakM3D [34] on Moderate (44.35 vs. 38.02 AP_BEV) and Hard (41.86 vs. 30.17 AP_BEV) categories, attributed to WeakM3D's reliance on sparse LiDAR point clouds that degrade at long range. It also surpasses WeakMono3D [42] on Moderate (44.35 vs. 42.83 AP_BEV) and Hard (41.86 vs. 40.07 AP_BEV) while using only a single-view image rather than multi-view or multi-frame inputs. On the KITTI test set (stricter IoU=0.7), the margins widen: SKD-WM3D leads WeakM3D by 4.49 AP_BEV on Moderate and 4.00 on Hard, and leads WeakMono3D by 1.35 AP_BEV on Moderate. Notably, SKD-WM3D's performance is on par with fully supervised methods like M3D-RPN [1] (53.35/48.53 Easy) and MonoGRNet [35] (52.13/47.59 Easy) despite using no 3D annotations. On nuScenes, it improves over WeakM3D by 0.028 AP and reduces ATE from 0.814 to 0.795. Inference speed of 30.3 FPS exceeds both WeakM3D (13.9 FPS) and MonoDistill (25.0 FPS), confirming the efficiency advantage of eliminating depth estimation at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior weakly supervised monocular 3D detection methods (WeakM3D, WeakMono3D) that rely on LiDAR point clouds or multi-view/pseudo-multi-view images as auxiliary supervision, instead grounding the entire pipeline on a single-view RGB image with pseudo-depth from a frozen off-the-shelf estimator (PENET). Conceptually, it repurposes the self-knowledge distillation paradigm—previously applied to classification and feature refinement—into a dual-network architecture where a depth-guided self-teaching network acts as a soft teacher for a monocular 3D detection network, eliminating the need for any external 3D annotations or additional sensor data. The uncertainty-aware distillation loss and gradient-targeted transfer modulation are novel loss/optimization designs specific to this distillation setup, drawing on Laplace-distribution uncertainty modeling and projection-loss-based gradient scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the dependency on costly LiDAR sensors and multi-camera rigs, making weakly supervised 3D detection deployable in settings where only a single RGB camera is available, while also achieving faster inference (30.3 FPS vs. 13.9 FPS for WeakM3D) by discarding depth modules at test time. The performance is competitive with several fully supervised methods, suggesting that pseudo-depth plus self-distillation can largely close the 3D annotation gap. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to heavily occluded scenes, adverse weather, and diverse object categories beyond cars, and whether the reliance on a specific off-the-shelf depth estimator limits robustness when depth quality degrades.
