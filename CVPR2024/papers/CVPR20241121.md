## Problem

Transferable targeted 3D adversarial attacks remain entirely unexplored: existing 3D attack methods either lack transferability, cannot specify a target class, or produce visually unnatural perturbations when optimized directly in high-dimensional mesh space. The paper aims to fill this gap by generating 3D adversarial examples that are simultaneously targeted, transferable across models, visually natural, and physically realizable via 3D printing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241121/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of multi-view images of a 3D object (no pre-existing mesh required) and produces a textured 3D mesh whose vertex colors and coordinates have been adversarially perturbed. The output mesh is designed to be 3D-printable, and its rendered views from arbitrary viewpoints should be misclassified as a user-specified target label by black-box classifiers. Evaluation renders 100 random-viewpoint images per object using NvDiffRast, MeshLab, or Blender, and physical tests use 3D-printed objects photographed from 360° with a smartphone at 10 fps.

## Method

TT3D first reconstructs an initial 3D mesh from multi-view images using grid-based NeRF (Instant NGP) with Marching Cubes, then performs adversarial fine-tuning in the NeRF parameter space rather than directly in mesh vertex-color space. The key innovation is a dual optimization that simultaneously perturbs the appearance feature grid parameters (Θ_G_tex) and the texture MLP weights (Θ_M_tex), embedding adversarial signals at both the foundational feature level and the higher-level decision-making layers to improve transferability while avoiding overfitting. Vertex coordinates are also explicitly optimized as a geometric auxiliary perturbation. Naturalness is enforced through a composite regularization combining rendered RGB consistency, Chamfer distance, Laplacian smoothing, and mesh edge-length losses, while an EOT scheme integrating 3D pose/distance/viewpoint shifts with 2D contrast and blur transformations bridges the digital-to-physical gap.

## Results

On the IM3D dataset, TT3D's dual optimization (MLP+Grid) achieves an average ASR of ~65% across 10 victim models when the surrogate is ResNet-101, and ~55% when the surrogate is DenseNet-121, compared to <5% for the enhanced mesh-based baseline on all non-surrogate models. In the physical world, 3D-printed adversarial objects maintain ASR of 76–92% across three different backgrounds and 360° viewpoints. Cross-task transferability is also demonstrated: 76.94% ASR on zero-shot detection (Grounding DINO) and 32.33% on image captioning (BLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is the enhanced MeshAdv [49] method, which directly optimizes vertex colors and coordinates in the high-dimensional mesh space. TT3D's dual optimization (MLP+Grid) dramatically outperforms this baseline in transferability: when the surrogate is ResNet-101, the mesh-based method achieves only 0.84–8.73% ASR on the 10 non-surrogate victim models, while TT3D achieves 51.54–78.85% (a gap of roughly 50–75 percentage points). The same pattern holds with DenseNet-121 as surrogate: mesh-based scores 1.10–3.91% on other models versus 36.49–70.98% for TT3D. Both methods achieve near-perfect ASR on their own surrogate (~99%), confirming the attack is effective in the white-box setting; the critical difference is that TT3D generalizes across architectures (CNNs and Transformers) while the mesh-based approach essentially overfits to the surrogate. The ablation further shows that Grid-only optimization already surpasses MLP-only in transferability (e.g., 64.85% vs. 40.48% on RN-50 with ResNet-101 surrogate), and combining both yields the best results, validating the dual-optimization design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D adversarial attacks (MeshAdv, DTA, ACTIVE, FCA) that directly optimize vertex colors or texture maps in high-dimensional mesh space, instead performing adversarial fine-tuning in the parameter space of grid-based NeRF (Instant NGP). It also extends the 2D transferable targeted attack literature (Li et al., Zhao et al., Naseer et al.) into the 3D domain, which the authors explicitly identify as vacant. A key conceptual departure is the dual optimization over both the appearance feature grid and the shallow MLP, which the authors argue perturbs at both foundational and decision-making levels of the rendering pipeline, combined with eliminating the need for a pre-existing 3D mesh by reconstructing from multi-view images via Marching Cubes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical security concern: 3D adversarial objects that can be physically printed and mislead multiple vision models (classification, detection, captioning) from arbitrary viewpoints, which is more threatening than 2D adversarial patches. The physical-world validation with 3D-printed objects under varying backgrounds and viewpoints (81–91% ASR) demonstrates real-world applicability. Limitations include evaluation on only 100 objects from 30 ImageNet categories, modest cross-task transferability (32% for captioning), and reliance on a white background during optimization that may not fully capture real-world lighting complexity. Open questions include whether the NeRF-space optimization approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, larger objects, or adversarial training defenses.
