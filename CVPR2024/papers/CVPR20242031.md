## Problem

Partial-to-partial shape matching—finding correspondences between two incomplete 3D shapes where only a subset of each shape overlaps—remains largely unsolved. Existing approaches either require closing holes to reduce the problem to full-to-full matching (losing information for low-overlap shapes), rely on supervised training data that is often unavailable, or fail to enforce geometric consistency (neighboring triangles mapping to neighboring triangles). No prior method achieves geometrically consistent partial-to-partial matching without these limitations.

## Input / Output

The method takes two oriented, manifold partial 3D meshes (with boundaries) and vertex-wise deep features extracted by a pretrained spectral shape matching network (Cao et al. [11]). It produces triangle-to-triangle correspondences over an extended set of 'degenerate triangles' (vertices, edges, and faces), which implicitly defines the overlapping region. The matching is non-bijective: triangles may remain unmatched, and a triangle in one shape can map to a vertex or edge in the other to accommodate different discretizations and non-isometric deformations. A coarse-to-fine scheme solves the problem at reduced resolution and upsamples via nearest-neighbor search in feature space.

## Method

The core contribution is a non-linear integer program over triangle product spaces that encodes geometric consistency via a product orientation constraint (∂Γ = 0), ensuring neighboring non-boundary triangles remain neighbors after matching. The objective minimizes mean matching cost (L2 distance in feature space) over the number of matched triangles, making it a fractional program; this is solved by iterating over fixed-cardinality integer linear programs (ILPs) for each possible number of matchings x. A novel pruning algorithm exploits a Lipschitz-like bound (g(x+1) − g(x) ≤ 1) to identify intervals of x that cannot contain a better solution, drastically reducing the number of ILPs that must be solved. SOTA unsupervised deep features from Cao et al. [11] provide the matching costs, and the entire pipeline is training-free for the matching step itself. The ILPs are solved with Gurobi via YALMIP, with a time-limit heuristic that selects the best solution among subproblems that terminate within budget.

## Results

On the intra-class CP2P TEST'24 dataset, the proposed method achieves a mean IoU of 69.29, outperforming Sm-comb (57.86) and DPFM (54.93). On the novel inter-class PARTIALSMAL dataset, it reaches a mean IoU of 64.34 versus 54.76 (Sm-comb) and 48.31 (DPFM). In terms of correspondence quality (geodesic error), the method also achieves the lowest error curves on both datasets, with an AUC GeoErr of 61.25 on CP2P TEST. The method is the only one that simultaneously predicts the overlapping region and produces geometrically consistent correspondences without requiring hole-closing or annotated training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two existing partial-to-partial shape matching methods: Sm-comb [37] (a learning-free combinatorial solver that requires closing holes in partial shapes) and DPFM [3] (a supervised deep learning method requiring annotated training data). The proposed method wins on both evaluation metrics (mean IoU and geodesic error) across both datasets. On CP2P TEST, it improves mean IoU by 11.4 points over Sm-comb and 14.4 points over DPFM; on the more challenging inter-class PARTIALSMAL dataset, the margins widen to 9.6 and 16.0 points respectively. Sm-comb's reliance on hole-closing leads to poor overlapping region predictions (it often predicts the entire shape as matched or places the overlap at the wrong position), while DPFM produces noisy predictions due to its supervised nature. The geodesic error curves (Figure 8) confirm that the proposed method yields consistently lower correspondence errors than both baselines on both datasets, with the gap being particularly pronounced at low error thresholds where geometric consistency matters most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometrically consistent integer programming framework for shape matching from full-to-full (Windheuser et al. [39,43,44]) and partial-to-full (Ehm et al. [18]) to the partial-to-partial setting, which had previously been addressed only by methods requiring annotated training data (DPFM [3]) or hole-closing of partial shapes (Sm-comb [37]). The key conceptual departure is formulating a non-linear (fractional) integer program over triangle product spaces with a novel pruning algorithm, rather than the linear programs used in prior geometrically consistent matchers. The authors also integrate SOTA unsupervised deep features [11] as matching costs, bridging the deep-learning and combinatorial-optimization paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Partial-to-partial matching is a practically critical setting (e.g., incomplete 3D scans) that has been largely neglected, and this work provides the first geometrically consistent solution without requiring annotations or hole-closing. The new PARTIALSMAL inter-class dataset is a valuable resource for evaluating non-isometric partial matching beyond the intra-class CP2P benchmark. However, the method's 10-hour worst-case runtime and the fact that geometric consistency is only guaranteed at coarse resolution levels limit immediate practical adoption. Open questions include extending the approach to implicit shape representations and reducing the computational cost of the ILP subproblems.
