## Problem

Existing deep unfolding networks for compressive sensing struggle to capture fine-grained image details at low CS ratios (≤0.10), producing noticeable distortions and blurriness. This stems from two deficiencies: inadequate extraction and fusion of multi-scale feature information, and poor balancing of information across different iterative reconstruction stages, leading to information loss and confusion in the final output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241013/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes compressed measurements b = Φx (where images are divided into non-overlapping blocks and flattened into vectors) and a jointly learned sampling matrix Φ as input, and produces a full-resolution reconstructed image as output. For the CS-MRI extension, the sampling matrix is a partial Fourier transform (sub-sampling mask × DFT) applied to k-space data. The network operates with a default of 8 reconstruction stages and is trained end-to-end with an L2 loss between the reconstructed and ground-truth images.

## Method

CPP-Net derives three iteration submodules (X, V, Y) from the primal-dual hybrid steps of the Chambolle-Pock Proximal Point Algorithm, replacing the fixed sparse basis Ψ with a learnable deep operator D(·) solved via soft thresholding. The core architectural innovation is the Dual Path Fusion Block (DPFB), which extends the proximal operator structure to extract and fuse multi-scale features through two parallel pathways: one using transposed convolutions with residual connections, and another using Multi-Scale Transformer Blocks (MSTBs) that perform cross-attention between full-resolution and downsampled feature maps via Global Average Pooling along width and height dimensions. An Iteration Fusion Strategy (IFS) then concatenates outputs from all K reconstruction stages along the channel dimension and applies Depth-wise Channel Attention Blocks for adaptive weighted fusion, maximizing utilization of information across stages. The entire network is trained end-to-end with per-stage learnable regularization parameters (α, β, λ) and a shared L2 loss.

## Results

On General100 at a CS ratio of 0.04, CPP-Net achieves 29.16 dB PSNR / 0.9159 SSIM, outperforming the next-best method OCTUF (28.35/0.8122) by 0.81 dB. At the more challenging CS ratio of 0.10, CPP-Net reaches 33.16/0.9159 on General100 and 29.93/0.8537 on Set14, surpassing CASNet (32.78/0.9099) and OCTUF (32.77/0.9084) respectively. Averaged across four CS ratios (0.01–0.25), CPP-Net leads on all seven natural-image benchmarks, with average PSNR gains of 0.5–1.4 dB over the strongest baselines. In the CS-MRI extension (Brain dataset, Pseudo Radial masks), CPP-Net achieves 37.05/0.9351 average PSNR/SSIM, exceeding HiTDUN (35.90/0.9159) by 1.15 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPP-Net consistently outperforms all 11 natural-image CS baselines across every dataset and CS ratio tested. The largest margins appear at low CS ratios: on General100 at 0.04, CPP-Net leads OCTUF by 0.81 dB and AutoBCS by 2.06 dB in PSNR; on Set14 at 0.04, the gap widens to 3.19 dB over OCTUF and 4.16 dB over DPC-DUN, reflecting CPP-Net's multi-scale fusion advantage under extreme undersampling. At the standard 0.10 ratio, CPP-Net edges out the closest competitors (CASNet, OCTUF, FSOINet) by 0.3–0.5 dB on General100 and 0.5–0.9 dB on Set14 and Urban100. Among DUN-based methods, CPP-Net surpasses OCTUF (cross-attention transformer) and DGUNet+ (PGD unfolding) on all settings, while also beating pure-DL methods like CSformer and TCS-Net. In the CS-MRI extension, CPP-Net exceeds the strongest DUN baseline HiTDUN by 1.15 dB average PSNR and the best pure-DL method RDN by 2.45 dB, confirming the transferability of the CP-PPA unfolding framework beyond natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPP-Net belongs to the deep unfolding network (DUN) lineage that maps iterative optimization solvers into trainable network architectures, following ISTA-Net, ADMM-CSNet, AMP-Net, OCTUF, and DGUNet+. Its key conceptual departure is adopting the Chambolle–Pock Proximal Point Algorithm (CP-PPA), a primal-dual hybrid scheme, as the unfolding backbone rather than the more common ISTA (primal-only proximal gradient) or ADMM formulations. On top of this backbone, the paper introduces two architectural innovations not present in prior DUNs: the Dual Path Fusion Block (DPFB) with Multi-Scale Transformer Blocks for cross-scale feature aggregation, and the Iteration Fusion Strategy (IFS) for attentional weighting across reconstruction stages. The paper explicitly positions these as remedies for the multi-scale detail loss and inter-stage information degradation it identifies in existing DUNs at low CS ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing DUNs degrade noticeably (blurring, distortion) at CS ratios ≤ 0.10, which is precisely the regime where CS offers the greatest sampling savings. The multi-scale fusion mechanism (DPFB + MSTB/MSCA) and cross-stage IFS are modular design choices that could be transplanted into other unfolding-based inverse-problem solvers. However, the paper does not isolate how much of the gain comes from the CP-PPA backbone itself versus the multi-scale fusion modules, leaving open whether a simpler ISTA-based backbone with the same DPFB/IFS would perform comparably. The authors note future extensions to denoising, deraining, and super-resolution, but the current scope is limited to natural image CS and CS-MRI with block-based processing, which constrains applicability to very large or non-block-structured images.
