## Problem

Current VQA benchmarks evaluate visual recognition and compositional reasoning but do not test whether multi-modal language models can perform counterfactual reasoning—imagining how scene contents would change under hypothetical conditions that did not actually occur. Existing counterfactual VQA work (CF-VQA, VQA-CP) addresses distribution shifts between train and test sets rather than the model's ability to reason about 'what if' scenarios embedded directly in the question text, leaving a critical gap in assessing a core human cognitive ability.

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs an RGB image with a text question that embeds a counterfactual presupposition (e.g., 'How many cats would be there if the TV was off?'), making questions substantially longer than standard VQA (13.15 words average vs. 5.88 in VQAv2). Outputs are exact numerical values for counting questions, binary yes/no for boolean questions, or a four-option multiple-choice selection in the synthetic subset. The counterfactual presupposition requires the model to first ground visual concepts in the image and then reason about how those concepts would change under the stated hypothetical, rather than simply reporting what is observed.

## Method

The paper introduces C-VQA, a benchmark with two components: C-VQA-Real (3,144 pairs built on VQAv2 images) and C-VQA-Synthetic (3,000 procedurally generated images with six question templates for flower- and dot-counting). C-VQA-Real questions are annotated via a two-stage process—manual annotation of 200 exemplars per question group followed by ChatGPT generation using in-context examples and chain-of-thought decomposition—then verified through two manual passes checking image relevance and answer correctness. Questions are stratified into three difficulty levels: numerical direct (one-step arithmetic change), numerical indirect (multi-step spatial/causal reasoning, e.g., recognizing cats on a TV screen), and boolean (fact-reversal presuppositions). The evaluation protocol tests 12+ models spanning neuro-symbolic (ViperGPT, VisProg with multiple code LLMs) and end-to-end architectures (LLaVA, MiniGPT4, BLIP2, InstructBLIP, CogVLM, Qwen-VL, GPT-4V), comparing original vs. counterfactual accuracy, probing chain-of-thought prompting, and analyzing gender-related bias via COCO-annotated subgroups.

## Results

On C-VQA-Real, all evaluated models suffer substantial accuracy drops when counterfactual presuppositions are added to questions. The best original-question performers (ViperGPT at 83.8% Numerical Direct, 95.0% Boolean; Qwen-VL at 65.1% Numerical Direct, 88.1% Boolean) drop by 12–67 percentage points on their counterfactual counterparts. Even GPT-4V, evaluated on a 10% subset, drops 35.8 points on Numerical Direct (50.4→14.6) and 17.9 points on Boolean (65.9→48.0). On C-VQA-Synthetic, most models perform at or below the 25% random-guess level, indicating a failure to generalize to out-of-distribution visual content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the central comparison is each model's original-question accuracy versus its counterfactual-question accuracy. No model family—neuro-symbolic (ViperGPT, VisProg) or end-to-end (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL, CogVLM, MiniGPT)—consistently handles counterfactual questions; all show drops of 10–67 points. Neuro-symbolic models, despite generating executable code, suffer the largest drops on complex multi-step reasoning (ViperGPT: −48.4 on Numerical Indirect, −66.8 on Boolean), underperforming end-to-end models like LLaVA-1.5-13B (−25.2, −27.3) on those subgroups. Among end-to-end models, Qwen-VL-Chat achieves the highest original accuracy (65.1/69.2/88.1) but also one of the largest drops (−34.4/−39.8/−38.8). GPT-4V, the strongest proprietary model tested, still drops 35.8 points on Numerical Direct, confirming that even frontier models cannot reliably perform counterfactual visual reasoning. Chain-of-thought prompting (Table 4) provides no meaningful improvement, with some models actually degrading further.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the causal-reasoning concept of counterfactual presuppositions (from Pearl's framework) into the VQA setting, explicitly distinguishing its formulation from prior 'counterfactual VQA' work (CF-VQA, VQA-CP) which uses the term to denote train/test distribution shift rather than hypothetical scenario reasoning within a single question. It extends the VQAv2 evaluation paradigm by modifying questions with 'what if' clauses that require the model to mentally alter scene state before answering, and it draws on the CLEVR-style synthetic generation pipeline for out-of-distribution testing. The evaluation spans both neuro-symbolic (ViperGPT, VisProg) and end-to-end (LLaVA, BLIP2, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL, CogVLM) MLLM paradigms, positioning the work as a diagnostic benchmark rather than a new method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no current MLLM family—including GPT-4V—can reliably handle counterfactual visual reasoning, with performance drops up to 40% and chain-of-thought prompting providing no meaningful rescue. This exposes a fundamental gap between the perceptual grounding that modern MLLMs excel at and the hypothetical scenario manipulation that human cognition handles naturally. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on counterfactual data, new prompting strategies, or architectural changes (e.g., explicit scene-graph manipulation) can close this gap, and whether the observed gender bias in counterfactual reasoning reflects a broader fairness issue in MLLM reasoning pipelines. The synthetic domain results also highlight that domain generalization and counterfactual reasoning are compounding challenges that current models cannot address simultaneously.
