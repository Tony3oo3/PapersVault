## Problem

Fine-grained building attribute segmentation (land use, floor level) from satellite imagery alone cannot distinguish facade-level details. Existing cross-view geometric projection methods fail to capture complete facades of tall buildings and introduce severe distortion away from the camera center, while standard BEV methods concentrate features unevenly at building wall edges rather than distributing them across building interiors, degrading fusion with satellite features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 satellite image (covering ~70×70 m) paired with a 360° street-view panorama as input, and produces per-pixel segmentation masks for building land use and floor level in the top-down satellite view. Monocular depth maps are estimated from the panorama to enable 3D point cloud construction, and the satellite branch additionally outputs a building footprint segmentation that guides the reprojection step. The two views are not required to be center-aligned (as in the Vigor dataset), with offset values used for BEV feature translation.

## Method

SG-BEV uses a two-branch architecture with SegNext MSCAN-B2 as the shared backbone. The satellite branch extracts top-down features and a building footprint mask; the street-view branch converts the panorama into a 3D point cloud via monocular depth estimation and spherical coordinate mapping, then applies the proposed Satellite-Guided Reprojection (SGR) module, which computes a per-point offset Δ = log(1 + d − d₀) × α (where α is derived from the satellite footprint area in a 3×3 grid) to push features from building walls toward their interiors before deformable attention projects them onto the BEV plane. A cross-view fusion module then aligns BEV features to satellite features using a learned 2D flow field (predicted by convolutional layers) and integrates them via channel concatenation, a 3×3 spatial convolution, and an adaptive global-average-pooling + 1×1-conv gating mechanism. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 6e-5, step decay, 50 epochs) on Cityscapes-pretrained weights.

## Results

On four cross-view datasets (OmniCity, Brooklyn, Boston, Vigor), SG-BEV achieves an average mIOU of 42.72% across land-use and floor-level segmentation tasks, outperforming the best satellite-only baseline (SegNext, 32.59% avg mIOU) by 10.13% and the best cross-view baseline (BEVFormer, 37.50% avg mIOU) by 5.21%. The largest gains appear on floor-level segmentation (e.g., 40.64% vs. 30.81% mIOU on OmniCity), where facade information from street views is most critical. On the Brooklyn dataset, SG-BEV reaches 47.19% mIOU for land use and 49.51% for floor level, the highest values reported across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against satellite-only baselines, SG-BEV outperforms SegNext (the strongest satellite method) by 6.16–15.37 mIOU points across tasks, with the largest gap on floor-level segmentation (e.g., +15.37 on OmniCity, +14.96 on Brooklyn), confirming that street-view facade information is essential for floor counting. FarSeg and SAN trail SegNext on most tasks and are consistently outperformed by SG-BEV by 10–20 mIOU points. Among cross-view baselines, geometric projection methods (ST, GP) suffer from feature distortion away from the camera center, yielding 4–10 mIOU lower than SG-BEV on most tasks; GP outperforms ST because it projects features directly rather than via image-plane transformation. BEVFormer is the closest competitor, correctly identifying building categories at edges but failing in building interiors due to its concentration of features on walls; SG-BEV's SGR module addresses this, achieving +4.13 to +7.38 mIOU over BEVFormer on average. SG-BEV wins on every dataset and every task against all six baselines without exception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Bird's Eye View (BEV) paradigm—originally developed for autonomous driving and robot navigation (LSS, BEVFormer)—for the cross-view building attribute segmentation task, where prior work relied on geometric projection (spherical transforms, homography-based mapping) or attention/diffusion-based feature transfer (Workman et al., Wojna et al.). The key departure is the Satellite-Guided Reprojection (SGR) module, which addresses a specific failure mode of BEV in this setting: features concentrate on building walls rather than filling footprints, a problem that does not arise in driving scenarios where the ground plane dominates. The cross-view feature fusion module with flow-based alignment extends standard concatenation fusion to handle residual misalignment from depth estimation errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical recipe for integrating street-view facade information into satellite-view segmentation, a gap that limits urban planning and building management applications. However, the method depends on monocular depth estimation accuracy and a preliminary building footprint prediction from the satellite branch, creating a potential error-propagation chain that is not deeply analyzed. Open questions include generalization to cities with very different building morphologies (e.g., dense Asian megacities vs. the tested US cities), handling of temporal changes in building appearance, and whether the SGR module's 3×3 grid-based α computation scales to irregular or very large footprints.
