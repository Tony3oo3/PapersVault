## Problem

Monocular 3D detectors achieve strong performance on cars but degrade significantly on large objects (trucks, buses, trailers), a gap that persists even on nearly balanced datasets like KITTI-360. The paper identifies the root cause as the noise sensitivity of depth regression losses (L1, L2) for larger objects, where greater object size amplifies the impact of depth prediction errors on localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240401/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a single frontal RGB image (KITTI-360) or multi-camera surround-view images (nuScenes, 6 cameras at resolutions from 256×704 to 640×1600), which are encoded into BEV features. It produces two outputs: a BEV foreground segmentation map at 200×200 resolution (supervised by dice loss) and 7-DoF 3D bounding boxes (BEV 2D position, elevation, 3D dimensions, yaw). The segmentation map is concatenated with the original BEV features as an additional channel before the detection head, since the BEV map alone lacks elevation and height information.

## Method

SeaBird introduces a sequential (rather than parallel) pipeline where a BEV segmentation head trained with dice loss precedes the Mono3D detection head, with the predicted segmentation map concatenated to BEV features as input to the detector. The key theoretical contribution is a mathematical proof that dice loss exhibits lower gradient variance than L1 or L2 regression losses for large objects under noise, yielding better SGD convergence and thus superior AP. Training follows a two-stage protocol: first, the BEV segmentation head is trained exclusively with dice loss to fully exploit its noise-robustness for localizing large objects; second, both heads are jointly fine-tuned with detection losses plus dice loss. Segmentation is restricted to foreground detection categories (not background) to mitigate negative transfer, and a BEV feature shortcut is retained to the detection head to recover elevation information absent from the segmentation map. The pipeline is plug-and-play, demonstrated by integrating it into two SoTA BEV detectors (BEVerse and HoP) by reorganizing their parallel heads into the sequential design.

## Results

On KITTI-360 Val, PBEV+SeaBird achieves 27.84% AP3D50 mAP and 44.84% AP3D25 mAP, with a particularly strong 13.22% AP_Lrg (large objects) compared to the best frontal monocular baseline MonoDLE at 22.88% mAP and only 0.94% AP_Lrg. On the KITTI-360 Test leaderboard, PBEV+SeaBird reaches 4.64% AP3D50 mAP, surpassing all monocular baselines (best: DEVIANT at 0.88%) and even outperforming legacy LiDAR methods (L-BoxNet at 4.08%). On nuScenes Val, HoP+SeaBird (V2-99, 640×1600) achieves 52.7% mAP and 60.2% NDS, improving over the HoP baseline (49.6% mAP, 58.3% NDS) with the largest gain on AP_Lrg (+3.8 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On KITTI-360, SeaBird (both I2M+SeaBird and PBEV+SeaBird) decisively outperforms all six frontal monocular baselines (GrooMeD-NMS, MonoDLE, GUP Net, DEVIANT, Cube R-CNN, MonoDETR) on every metric, with the most dramatic gap on AP_Lrg: PBEV+SeaBird scores 13.22% vs. 0.94% for the best frontal method (MonoDLE). PBEV+SeaBird also surpasses both legacy LiDAR baselines (L-VoteNet at 3.40% and L-BoxNet at 4.08% AP3D50 mAP on Test). On nuScenes, SeaBird consistently improves its host detectors: HoP+SeaBird (V2-99) gains +3.8 AP_Lrg, +2.6 AP_Car, +2.7 AP_Sml, and +1.9 NDS over the HoP baseline; BEVerse-S+SeaBird gains +3.7 AP_Lrg and +1.8 NDS over BEVerse-S. Against other nuScenes SoTA methods (PETRv2, VEDet, FrustumFormer, MV2D, CAPE, SparseBEV), HoP+SeaBird (V2-99) achieves the highest AP_Lrg (40.3%) among methods without CBGS or future frames, though methods using CBGS (SA-BEV, FB-BEV, CAPE) or future frames (SparseBEV) still lead on overall mAP and NDS. The lengthwise AP analysis (Fig. 5) confirms that SeaBird's advantage is concentrated on objects >10 m in length, where baselines degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeaBird extends the BEV segmentation line of work (Image2Maps, PanopticBEV) by repurposing the dice loss—originally designed for segmentation—as a mechanism to improve monocular 3D detection, rather than treating segmentation and detection as parallel auxiliary tasks. It departs from the standard Mono3D paradigm of regressing depth with L1/L2/Smooth-L1 losses by proving that a classification-style loss in BEV space is more noise-robust for large objects, and from prior joint-learning approaches (BEVerse, M2BEV) that use parallel heads and often suffer negative transfer, by adopting a sequential two-stage protocol. The detection head (BoxNet) and the backbone architectures (Swin, ResNet, V2-99) are borrowed from existing BEV detectors, making SeaBird a plug-in module rather than a new architecture from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Large-object detection failures (trucks, buses, trailers) are a documented cause of fatal AV accidents, yet prior explanations focused on data scarcity or receptive field size; this paper identifies loss-function noise sensitivity as a concrete, addressable cause and provides a simple fix. The plug-and-play design (add a BEV segmentation head with dice loss, then concatenate) makes it easy to retrofit onto existing detectors, as demonstrated on both BEVerse and HoP. Limitations include the simplified single-layer, one-class theoretical setting, the modest NDS improvement (and slight NDS drop) on nuScenes with R101, and the reliance on GT 3D boxes to generate BEV segmentation labels. Open questions include whether the two-stage protocol is truly necessary versus a well-tuned joint loss, whether the result generalizes to other segmentation losses (e.g., Tversky), and whether the gains persist under domain shift or adversarial conditions.
