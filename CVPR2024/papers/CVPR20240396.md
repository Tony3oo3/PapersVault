## Problem

Extracting reliable semantic features from untextured 3D shapes (meshes or point clouds) for shape correspondence remains challenging: classical geometric descriptors capture shape but not semantics, while learning-based 3D methods require training data and fail to generalize to unseen categories. Image foundational models (diffusion models, DINOv2) have learned powerful semantic features, but cannot be directly applied to untextured 3D geometry due to the absence of textures, potential non-manifold topology, and lack of connectivity in point clouds.

## Input / Output

The method accepts untextured meshes (including non-manifold) or raw point clouds along with a text prompt, and produces 1280-dimensional per-point semantic feature descriptors. Correspondence is computed via cosine similarity in the shared feature space for point-to-point matching, or by feeding descriptors into a vanilla Functional Maps pipeline for continuous surface-to-surface maps. The method requires no training, no UV parameterization, and no surface reconstruction, operating directly on the raw input geometry.

## Method

DIFF3F renders the input shape from 100 uniformly distributed camera views to obtain depth and normal maps, which condition ControlNet to guide Stable Diffusion in producing textured images. During the diffusion forward pass, intermediate UNet features are extracted at multiple timesteps (from T/4 to 0) and weighted-aggregated, then fused with DINOv2 features from the textured renderings (α=0.5) to combine spatial and semantic signals. Features are unprojected back to 3D surface points using known camera parameters, with a ball query (radius = 1% of bounding box diagonal) promoting local neighborhood consensus. The key insight is that although multi-view textured images are mutually inconsistent, the diffusion features are robust enough to be directly averaged across views, effectively denoising view-specific artifacts. This entire pipeline is zero-shot, reusing intermediate features already computed during the depth-guided image generation step without any additional training or optimization.

## Results

On SHREC'19 (human shapes), DIFF3F achieves 26.41% correspondence accuracy at 1% error tolerance with 1.69 average error, outperforming all baselines. On SHREC'20 (non-isometric animal shapes), DIFF3F reaches 72.60% accuracy with 0.93 error, more than doubling the best baseline (SE-ORNet at 31.70%). On TOSCA (isometric animal shapes), DIFF3F scores 20.27% accuracy, trailing SE-ORNet (33.25%) and DPC (30.79%). On FAUST, DIFF3F achieves a 5.29 cm average geodesic error on high-resolution human scans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFF3F clearly outperforms all baselines on SHREC'19 (26.41% vs. 21.41% for SE-ORNet, 17.40% for DPC, 2.10% for 3D-CODED, 4.37% for FM+WKS) and dominates on SHREC'20 (72.60% vs. 31.70% for SE-ORNet, 31.08% for DPC, 4.13% for FM+WKS), where the non-isometric deformations expose the limitations of purely geometric descriptors. On TOSCA, however, DIFF3F (20.27%) underperforms SE-ORNet (33.25%) and DPC (30.79%), likely because TOSCA shapes are highly isometric and the baselines were trained on that specific dataset. 3D-CODED performs extremely poorly on TOSCA (0.5% accuracy) due to its reliance on template deformation and large annotated training sets unavailable for animal shapes. A key practical advantage is that DIFF3F requires no training data or fine-tuning, is class-agnostic, and handles both meshes and point clouds, whereas DPC and SE-ORNet are trained on specific human or animal datasets and 3D-CODED requires extensive ground-truth annotations. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that the TEXTure+DINO baseline (17.20% on SHREC'19) is notably weaker than DIFF3F's diffusion-based approach, confirming that inconsistent multi-view diffusion features, when properly aggregated, yield richer semantic descriptors than consistent textures with DINO features alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFF3F sits at the intersection of multi-view rendering-based 3D analysis (Huang et al., Projective Analysis) and the recent observation that image diffusion models produce emergent per-pixel semantic features (Tang et al.). It departs from prior 3D correspondence methods (DPC, SE-ORNet, 3D-CODED) that require 3D training data by instead distilling features from pre-trained image foundational models (Stable Diffusion, DINOv2) via ControlNet-conditioned multi-view texturing. Unlike 3D Highlighter or Distilled Feature Field, which extract features from rendered images, DIFF3F uses ControlNet in-the-loop so that the texturing task itself drives the diffusion model to produce semantically discriminative intermediate features, which are then unprojected and aggregated onto the 3D surface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a zero-shot, training-free semantic descriptor for untextured 3D shapes that generalizes across species, pose, and topology without category-specific data — a significant practical advantage over learned 3D methods that struggle on unseen categories. The finding that inconsistent multi-view textured renderings still yield robust features when aggregated (because aggregation acts as implicit denoising) challenges the assumption that consistent texturing is a prerequisite for meaningful feature extraction. Open questions include handling self-occluded regions, reducing inherited biases from diffusion model training data, and extending to volumetric representations (NeRFs, distance fields). The TOSCA underperformance relative to baselines on isometric shapes suggests the semantic advantage is most pronounced for non-isometric deformations, leaving room for hybrid geometric-semantic approaches.
