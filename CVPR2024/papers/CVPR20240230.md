## Problem

Existing methods for adapting CLIP to referring image segmentation rely on unidirectional prompting or two-stage mask-classification pipelines, both of which fail to capture sufficient cross-modal interaction for pixel-level text-pixel alignment and ignore global context about multiple instances and their relationships. Additionally, SAM requires positional priors (e.g., point prompts) to produce accurate masks, but generating these prompts from CLIP in a differentiable, end-to-end manner remains unsolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 480×480 for the CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder and 1024×1024 for the SAM ViT-B/16 encoder, yielding 30×30 and 64×64 patch grids respectively) and a natural language description of variable length. It outputs a binary segmentation mask at 16× the SAM patch resolution (1024×1024), produced by SAM's mask decoder conditioned on three types of prompts derived from the prompt-tuned CLIP.

## Method

Prompt-RIS bridges CLIP and SAM end-to-end through a bidirectional Cross-Modal Prompting (CMP) mechanism: at every CLIP encoding layer, learnable tokens attend to the opposite modality's features via cross-attention and are injected as prompt tokens, enabling fine-grained text-pixel alignment without increasing sequence length. The prompt-tuned CLIP then generates three prompt types for SAM — a coarse mask upsampled into dense mask prompts, a differentiable point prompt obtained via Gumbel-Softmax over the coarse mask response map, and text prompts projected from CLIP text features conditioned on SAM image features. To sharpen instance discrimination, Instance Contrastive Learning (ICL) samples b sentences per image and applies a Dice-coefficient-based contrastive loss that pulls masks of same-instance descriptions together while pushing apart masks of different-instance descriptions, weighted by each mask's IoU with ground truth to prevent misleading signals from poor predictions.

## Results

On RefCOCO, Prompt-RIS achieves 76.36 oIoU and 78.10 mIoU (val), outperforming the previous best CGFormer by 1.61 and 1.17 points respectively. On the more challenging G-Ref dataset with longer and more diverse expressions, the method reaches 64.79 oIoU and 69.17 mIoU (val-G), surpassing CGFormer by 2.28 and 3.38 points. On RefCOCO+, which emphasizes attribute descriptions, the gains are most pronounced: 67.06 oIoU and 71.13 mIoU, improving over CGFormer by 2.52 and 2.57 points. The method also leads on open-vocabulary generalization, exceeding CGFormer by over 2.5 mIoU points on unseen categories across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prompt-RIS consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset split and both metrics (oIoU and mIoU). Against the strongest prior method CGFormer [41], the gains range from 1.17 mIoU (RefCOCO val) to 3.38 mIoU (G-Ref val-G), with the largest improvements on G-Ref where expressions are longer and more diverse. Compared to other CLIP-based methods CRIS [43] and ETRIS [44], Prompt-RIS leads by 6–10 mIoU points, and even when restricted to the CLIP-only component (without SAM), it still exceeds them by ~2.5 mIoU, isolating the contribution of Cross-Modal Prompting. Against transformer-based methods LAVT [46] and DMMI [12], the advantage is 5–8 mIoU points, reflecting the benefit of leveraging SAM's segmentation prior. On open-vocabulary generalization (Table 2), Prompt-RIS surpasses CGFormer by over 2.5 mIoU on unseen categories across all three datasets, confirming that the prompt-learning approach preserves CLIP's zero-shot knowledge more effectively than fine-tuning-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage 'mask-classification' paradigm for adapting CLIP to RIS (used by CRIS, ETRIS, and others), where a mask generator produces proposals that CLIP then classifies. Instead, it bridges CLIP and SAM end-to-end through prompt learning, where the prompt-tuned CLIP generates mask, point, and text prompts that directly drive SAM's decoder. The Cross-Modal Prompting method extends prior unidirectional prompt learning (Co-CoOp's V2L, MaPLe's L2V) to a bidirectional scheme applied at every CLIP encoding layer, and the Instance Contrastive Learning loss is a novel addition not seen in prior RIS prompt-learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a clean, modular recipe for transferring CLIP's open-vocabulary knowledge and SAM's segmentation quality to RIS without fine-tuning either backbone, which is practically attractive as both models continue to evolve. The consistent 2–3 point gains across all three benchmarks and the strong unseen-category generalization (Table 2) suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the CLIP→SAM prompt pipeline scales to video or higher-resolution inputs, whether the Gumbel-softmax point selection remains stable at larger spatial resolutions, and whether the bidirectional prompting overhead justifies its modest gain over L2V alone in compute-constrained settings.
