## Problem

Existing self-supervised facial representation learning methods (e.g., BYOL, PCL, MCF) enforce consistency only at the image level, treating each face as a whole and ignoring the spatial consistency of local facial regions (eyes, nose, mouth) across augmented views. This oversight limits the generalization of learned representations to diverse downstream facial analysis tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241448/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image, applies two random augmentations to produce two views, and feeds each through a ResNet-50 encoder to obtain a spatial feature map (C×H×W) and a global latent vector. It produces N=8 heatmaps (each H×W, representing a probabilistic distribution over facial region clusters), N local facial region embeddings (each D-dimensional, obtained via heatmap-weighted average pooling of the feature map), and one global facial embedding. The heatmaps are learned end-to-end without ground-truth masks, and the entire pipeline uses a single 1-layer Transformer decoder for mask prediction.

## Method

FRA extends BYOL's Siamese online/momentum architecture with a Transformer decoder that takes the encoder's feature map and N learnable positional embeddings (acting as facial queries) to predict N facial mask embeddings. Heatmaps are generated by computing cosine similarity between these mask embeddings and per-pixel projected feature maps, followed by softmax normalization along the channel dimension. Training uses two jointly optimized losses: (1) a semantic relation loss that formulates heatmap learning as a self-supervised deep clustering problem, aligning per-pixel cluster assignments between the online and momentum networks via cross-entropy with Sinkhorn-Knopp normalization and entropy maximization; and (2) a semantic consistency loss that matches both global embeddings and heatmap-pooled local region embeddings across augmented views using negative cosine similarity. The key distinction from supervised segmentation is that no ground-truth masks are needed—the facial region structure emerges purely from the self-supervised clustering objective.

## Results

On facial expression recognition, FRA (fine-tuned) achieves 66.16% top-1 accuracy on AffectNet, 89.95% on RAF-DB, and 89.78% on FERPlus, outperforming BYOL (65.65/89.53/89.25) and PCL (60.77/85.92/85.87). On CelebA facial attribute recognition, FRA (FT) reaches 92.02% averaged accuracy, surpassing BYOL (91.56%) and PCL (91.48%). On face alignment, FRA achieves 2.91 NME on 300W (full) and 4.11 NME on WFLW, comparable to SOTA supervised methods using hourglass backbones (STAR: 2.87 on 300W) while using a unified ResNet-50 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRA consistently outperforms all self-supervised pre-training baselines across all three downstream tasks. On FER, FRA (FT) beats BYOL by 0.51/0.42/0.53 points on AffectNet/RAF-DB/FERPlus, and beats PCL by a large margin (5.39 points on AffectNet). On CelebA, FRA (FT) achieves 92.02% vs. BYOL's 91.56% and PCL's 91.48%. On face alignment, FRA achieves 2.91 NME on 300W, beating BYOL (3.03) and PCL (3.35) while remaining competitive with SOTA supervised methods using hourglass backbones (STAR: 2.87). Against the weakly-supervised FaRL (86M ViT-B/16 with text supervision), FRA (24M ResNet-50, no text) achieves better results on all three tasks (66.16 vs. 64.85 on AffectNet, 92.02 vs. 91.88 on CelebA, 2.91 vs. 3.08 on 300W). When FRA pre-trained weights initialize the SOTA EAC architecture, the resulting FRA(EAC) surpasses the original supervised EAC on all FER datasets (e.g., 90.76 vs. 90.35 on RAF-DB), demonstrating the transferability of the learned representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BYOL non-contrastive self-supervised framework by adding a local facial region consistency objective, departing from prior facial pre-training methods (Bulat et al., FaRL, MCF, PCL) that enforce only image-level global consistency. The heatmap prediction mechanism is directly inspired by MaskFormer's mask classification approach in supervised segmentation, repurposing learnable positional embeddings as 'facial queries' that attend over encoder features to discover regions. The learning of these heatmaps is formulated as a deep clustering problem (à la SwAV), aligning per-pixel cluster assignments between online and momentum networks rather than relying on ground-truth masks. Conceptually, the paper bridges instance discrimination (global view matching) and spatially-aware representation learning (local region matching) in a unified self-supervised objective for faces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a single 24M-parameter ResNet-50 backbone, pre-trained with FRA, can serve as a competitive unified backbone across both classification (FER, facial attributes) and regression (face alignment) tasks, matching or exceeding task-specific architectures like hourglass networks. This reduces the need for architecture selection per task and avoids the computational overhead of methods like PCL (3× forward/backward passes) or the text supervision required by FaRL. Open questions include whether the discovered heatmaps are truly semantically meaningful (vs. merely useful for alignment), whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D faces, and whether the heuristic choice of N=8 heatmaps scales to more complex facial analysis tasks. The method is also limited to 2D face images and relies on a fixed number of regions, which may not capture all relevant facial sub-structures.
