## Problem

Existing imbalanced multimodal learning methods identify and correct modality dominance only at the dataset level, yet in realistic scenarios the dominant modality can vary from sample to sample. There is no fine-grained, theoretically grounded metric to observe per-sample modality contribution, making it impossible to target the specific samples where a modality is underutilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242527/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multimodal inputs (audio + 10-second video clips on Kinetics Sounds; RGB + optical flow on UCF-101) and outputs a class label at inference. During training, it additionally computes per-sample Shapley contribution scores for each modality; when re-sampling a low-contributing modality, all other modalities are zeroed out so the encoder is trained in isolation on that single modality's input.

## Method

The paper introduces a Shapley-value-based sample-level modality valuation that assigns each modality a contribution score φ^i for every individual sample by averaging marginal contributions over all modality permutations. A theoretical analysis (Remark 1) shows that when φ^i < 1, the multimodal prediction's benefit converges toward the subset excluding modality i, causing collapse toward uni-modal behavior. To counteract this, after a warm-up phase the method identifies low-contributing modalities per sample and re-samples their inputs with a frequency inversely proportional to (1 − φ^i), while masking all other modalities to zero for targeted discriminative training. Two variants are provided: a fine-grained sample-level method that re-samples each sample's weak modality individually, and a more efficient modality-level method that estimates average contribution on a 20% subset and applies a uniform re-sampling probability to the globally weaker modality. The approach is fusion-agnostic and is validated on Concatenation, CentralNet, MMTM, and MBT architectures, as well as on a newly constructed MM-Debiased dataset where dataset-level imbalance is removed but sample-level imbalance persists.

## Results

On Kinetics Sounds (audio+video), the sample-level method achieves 66.92% accuracy and 71.84% mAP, outperforming the Concatenation baseline (62.30% / 67.95%) by over 4 points. On UCF-101 (RGB+optical flow), it reaches 83.52% accuracy and 88.89% mAP versus 81.15% / 86.15% for the baseline. On the proposed MM-Debiased dataset, where dataset-level modality discrepancy is minimized, the sample-level method achieves 84.71% accuracy while most existing imbalanced-learning baselines drop below the 83.22% Concatenation baseline, confirming the importance of sample-level valuation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fusion baselines (Table 1), the sample-level method (66.92% KS Acc) outperforms all seven compared strategies, including the strongest fusion baseline Gated (62.72%) and Deep MKL (63.61%), with gains of 3–6 points. Among imbalanced multimodal learning methods (Table 2), the sample-level method beats G-Blending (66.24%) by 0.68 points on KS and AGM (63.96%) by nearly 3 points; on MM-Debiased, OGM-GE, Greedy, and PMR all fall below the Concatenation baseline (83.08%, 83.08%, 82.88% vs. 83.22%), while the sample-level method reaches 84.71%. The modality-level variant is slightly weaker than sample-level but more computationally efficient, and still surpasses all baselines on all three datasets. When combined with cross-modal interaction modules (Table 3), the sample-level method further improves CentralNet (67.35→67.89% on KS), MMTM (64.23→64.40%), and MBT (47.02→47.53%), confirming generality across architectures. The ablation against naive and reversed re-sampling (Table 4) shows that targeted re-sampling guided by Shapley valuation (66.92%) outperforms random re-sampling (64.69%) and that reversed re-sampling (59.03%) actively harms performance, validating that the valuation signal—not re-sampling per se—drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the use of Shapley values in multimodal learning from the dataset-level evaluation of Hu et al. (SHAPE) to a sample-level modality valuation metric, providing a fine-grained diagnostic of per-sample modality contribution. It departs from existing imbalanced multimodal learning methods (OGM-GE, Greedy, PMR, G-Blending, AGM) that estimate modality discrepancy only at the dataset level and modulate optimization accordingly, by showing that the discrepancy is inherently sample-dependent and that dataset-level heuristics can be counterproductive. The re-sampling enhancement strategy is a practical, architecture-agnostic intervention guided by the Shapley-based valuation, building on the general principle that improving a modality's discriminative ability increases its contribution (formalized in Remark 2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that existing imbalanced multimodal learning methods can actively harm performance when the dataset-level modality preference assumption is violated, as demonstrated on the newly introduced MM-Debiased dataset. The Shapley-based sample-level valuation offers a theoretically grounded, fusion-agnostic diagnostic that practitioners can adopt to identify which modality is under-contributing for specific samples. Open questions remain around the computational cost of exact Shapley computation (n! permutations), the natural irreducible differences between modalities that the method cannot fully equalize, and whether the approach extends to the imbalanced contribution problem in multimodal large language models (e.g., GPT-4V's text bias).
