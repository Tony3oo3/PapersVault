## Problem

Existing 3D human pose estimation methods treat the human body as a monolithic unit, ignoring fine-grained guidance available from individual body parts (head, body, arms, legs) and accessible textual/kinematic information such as action class and speed. This neglect of part-level and text-based implicit supervision limits denoising quality in diffusion-based approaches, particularly for complex or rare poses where depth ambiguity and inter-joint coupling are most severe.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D keypoint sequence X ∈ R^{N×J×2} (243 frames × 17 joints for Human3.6M) along with text prompts encoding the action class, body-part labels (person, head, body, arms, legs), and speed, all processed through a frozen CLIP text encoder. It outputs a 3D pose sequence Y ∈ R^{N×J×3} via iterative DDIM denoising with 20 parallel hypotheses and 10 timesteps at inference, followed by joint-wise reprojection-based multi-hypothesis aggregation (JPMA) to select the best joint from each hypothesis. For multi-human scenarios, each of C characters is processed independently and results are stacked along the character dimension.

## Method

FinePOSE is a diffusion-model denoiser built on a MixSTE backbone with three core blocks. The FPP block constructs 7 fine-grained part-aware prompts (person, action class, speed, head, body, arms, legs) by encoding each text via CLIP, retaining the first 4 tokens, and wrapping them with learnable modifier vectors initialized from N(0, 0.02²) to form a prompt embedding P ∈ R^{77×D}. The FPC block injects P, the 2D keypoints, and a sinusoidal timestamp embedding into the noisy 3D pose via concatenation and addition, then applies a spatial transformer (MHSA) for intra-frame joint relationships, a multi-head cross-attention layer where queries come from pose features and keys/values come from the prompt embedding to achieve fine-grained prompt-pose communication, followed by a temporal transformer and a spatial-temporal transformer with permutation operations. The PTS block fuses the prompt embedding with the timestamp via positional encoding and applies adaptive per-channel scaling and bias (Hadamard product with linear projections) to the cross-attention output, enabling noise-level-aware refinement at each denoising step. Training minimizes MSE between ground-truth and predicted 3D poses with AdamW (lr=6e-5, weight decay=0.1) over 100 epochs; inference uses DDIM sampling with H=20 hypotheses and M=10 iterations, aggregating joints via JPMA reprojection.

## Results

On Human3.6M with detected 2D keypoints (CPN), FinePOSE achieves 31.9 mm MPJPE and 25.0 mm P-MPJPE, surpassing the previous SOTA D3DP (35.4 / 28.7) by 3.5 mm and 3.7 mm respectively. With ground-truth 2D keypoints, FinePOSE reaches 16.7 mm MPJPE and 12.7 mm P-MPJPE, edging out MotionBERT (16.9 mm) by 0.2 mm. On MPI-INF-3DHP, FinePOSE attains 26.2 mm MPJPE, 98.9% PCK, and 80.0% AUC, improving over GLA-GCN by 1.6 mm, 0.4%, and 0.9% respectively. In the multi-human EgoHumans setting, FinePOSE achieves 34.3 mm average MPJPE versus D3DP's 35.4 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FinePOSE outperforms all 13 compared baselines on both Human3.6M evaluation protocols. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor D3DP [34], FinePOSE reduces MPJPE by 3.5 mm (31.9 vs 35.4) and P-MPJPE by 3.7 mm (25.0 vs 28.7) under the detected-keypoint setting, and by 1.7 mm (16.7 vs 18.4) under the ground-truth setting. On the GT-keypoint protocol, FinePOSE also beats the non-diffusion SOTA MotionBERT [59] by 0.2 mm in MPJPE (16.7 vs 16.9) and introduces a P-MPJPE of 12.7 mm where MotionBERT reports none. On MPI-INF-3DHP, FinePOSE surpasses GLA-GCN [48] (the prior best) by 1.6 mm in MPJPE (26.2 vs 27.8) while also improving PCK (98.9% vs 98.5%) and AUC (80.0% vs 79.1%). In the multi-human EgoHumans experiment, FinePOSE achieves 34.3 mm average MPJPE, outperforming D3DP's 35.4 mm, with the largest per-action gain on Lego-assembling (26.7 vs 29.0 mm). The per-action breakdown (Table 2) shows consistent improvements across all 15 Human3.6M action classes, with the largest gains on 'Photo' (−7.2 mm) and 'SittingDown' (−5.3 mm) relative to D3DP.

## Relation to Prior Work

FinePOSE extends the diffusion-based 3D HPE line initiated by DiffPose and D3DP by injecting fine-grained textual guidance into the denoising process. It adopts D3DP's JPMA multi-hypothesis aggregation for inference and uses MixSTE as the spatial-temporal transformer backbone, but departs from both by introducing a CLIP-based fine-grained part-aware prompt learning mechanism that encodes action class, body-part identities (head, body, arms, legs), and kinematic speed as learnable modifiers. This positions the work at the intersection of prompt learning (CoOp, CoCoOp) and diffusion-based pose estimation, where prior prompt-based HPE methods like ActionPrompt only used coarse action-class text without per-part granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-granularity textual knowledge about body-part movements can serve as effective implicit supervision for diffusion-based 3D HPE, yielding consistent SOTA improvements across single-human benchmarks and a reasonable extension to multi-human scenarios. However, the multi-human extension relies on a simple per-character inference plus stacking strategy rather than modeling inter-person interactions, and the diffusion-based pipeline remains computationally expensive for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the learned part-aware prompts generalize to unseen action classes or novel body configurations, and whether the prompt mechanism can be made more efficient to reduce inference cost.
