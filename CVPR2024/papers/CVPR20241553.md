## Problem

Existing CIL distillation methods inherit knowledge from a single teacher, but teachers with different prediction mechanisms are better suited to different tasks, and inheriting diverse knowledge can improve compatibility with new classes. The key challenge is how to effectively find diverse teachers from a basic model without the prohibitive cost of retraining multiple models from scratch with different seeds or architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241553/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels. Unusually, the model architecture contains multiple small prediction branches (each comprising the last M layers and classifier) that share the earlier feature-extraction stages of the base network. During distillation, the student receives knowledge from an ensemble of teacher branches whose output logits are averaged, rather than from a single frozen teacher model.

## Method

MTD finds diverse teachers by applying three techniques to small branches copied from the base model: (1) weight permutation, which uses random permutation matrices to teleport branch parameters from one low-loss region to another while preserving output equivalence; (2) feature perturbation, which adds Gaussian noise (δ ~ N(0,I)) scaled by α to each branch's input to break the permutation-induced equivalence and create distinct optimization trajectories; (3) diversity regularization, which minimizes the mean absolute cosine similarity between teacher embeddings to encourage mutually orthogonal representations. The branches are optimized during the class-balanced fine-tuning phase using a combined loss of cross-entropy, KL distillation from previous teachers, KL distillation from the current model, and the diversity regularization term. The key empirical finding is that only one additional branch (n=2 total teachers) is needed, as adding more teachers degrades both ensemble quality and inter-teacher diversity.

## Results

The paper reports average incremental accuracy (A%) on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1000 under B50/B500 settings with 5, 10, and 25 incremental steps. MTD-T (teacher ensemble) applied to PODNet achieves 67.64% on CIFAR-100 B50-5S, 78.47% on ImageNet-100 B50-5S, and 69.65% on ImageNet-1000 B500-5S, improving over the PODNet baseline by 2.57%, 2.15%, and 1.32% respectively. Applied to AFC, MTD-T reaches 67.40% (CIFAR-100 B50-5S), 77.85% (ImageNet-100 B50-5S), and 70.40% (ImageNet-1000 B500-5S), outperforming the AFC baseline by 1.46%, 0.58%, and 1.33% respectively. Gains are largest in the 25-step setting, where MTD-T improves PODNet by up to 5.49% on ImageNet-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTD-T consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and step count. On CIFAR-100 B50-5S, PODNet+MTD-T (67.64%) surpasses the strongest non-MTD baseline AANet (65.97%) by 1.67% and GeoDL (65.14%) by 2.50%. On ImageNet-100 B50-5S, PODNet+MTD-T (78.47%) edges out AANet (77.98%) by 0.49%, while AFC+MTD-T (77.85%) trails AANet slightly but still beats PODNet (76.32%) and AFC (77.27%) baselines. The largest absolute gains appear in the 25-step regime: on ImageNet-100 B50-25S, PODNet+MTD-T improves from 63.05% to 68.54% (+5.49%), and on ImageNet-1000 B500-25S, AFC+MTD-T improves from 63.40% to 65.01% (+1.61%). Notably, in some ImageNet settings (10 and 25 steps with AFC), the student model (MTD-S) exceeds the teacher ensemble (MTD-T), indicating that the simple logit-averaging ensemble is not always optimal. Overall, MTD provides a plug-and-play improvement of 0.5–5.5% over both PODNet and AFC without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-teacher distillation paradigm in CIL (as in LwF, iCaRL, PODNet, AFC) to a multi-teacher setting, where the key departure is how diverse teachers are obtained. Rather than retraining full models with different seeds or architectures (as in general multi-teacher distillation literature), MTD uses weight permutation to teleport parameters into different low-loss regions, then breaks the resulting output invariance via feature perturbation and diversity regularization. The closest prior CIL work, DT-CIL, uses two teachers trained on disjoint class subsets, whereas MTD generates multiple teachers from a single base model as lightweight shared-backbone branches, drawing on the permutation-invariance and mode-connectivity literature to motivate the design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that multi-teacher distillation becomes nearly free in CIL: one small branch per teacher shares most feature-extraction layers, so the overhead is minimal. The empirical finding that diversity (measured by prediction difference matrices) correlates with CIL gains while maintaining teacher accuracy provides a useful design principle for future ensemble-based continual learning. A notable limitation is that permutation matrices are chosen randomly rather than in a data-driven way, and the paper itself identifies this as the main open direction. The observation that more than two teachers degrades performance due to crude logit averaging and reduced pairwise diversity also highlights an unresolved challenge in knowledge integration.
