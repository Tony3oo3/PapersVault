## Problem

State-of-the-art instructional image editing models like InstructPix2Pix generate edited images that only partially align with the editing instructions, because their training data pipeline lacks explicit human preference alignment. Moreover, humans naturally prefer selective, minimal edits, but this preference is not captured in existing training objectives. Applying the RLHF paradigm that works for LLMs to diffusion-based editing models is technically challenging due to the hundreds of denoising steps required per sample, making on-policy RL infeasible.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB input image (encoded to latent space via a pre-trained VAE) and a free-form text editing instruction (e.g., 'remove the red arch') as joint conditions, and produces a full edited RGB image. The reward model additionally consumes the original input image alongside the instruction and candidate edited image to output a scalar preference score. During inference with the condition reward loss, a fixed text prompt indicating the highest quality tier is appended to the instruction condition.

## Method

HIVE is a three-stage framework: (1) supervised fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion v1.5 on a new 1.1M triplet dataset generated via GPT-3 instruction synthesis and Prompt-to-Prompt image editing, augmented with cycle-consistency pairs from invertible instructions (e.g., 'add a dog' ↔ 'remove the dog'); (2) training a BLIP-based reward model on 3.6K human-ranked image-instruction pairs using a Bradley-Terry pairwise loss; (3) fine-tuning the diffusion model with human feedback via two novel offline-RL-derived losses that avoid expensive sampling: a Weighted Reward Loss that reweights the standard denoising objective by exp(R/η), and a Condition Reward Loss that quantizes the reward into a five-level text prompt appended to the conditioning, treating preference as an additional label. Both losses are derived from the optimal policy of a KL-regularized reward maximization objective, making them computationally comparable to standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On the synthetic evaluation dataset (15,652 image pairs), HIVE achieves a superior trade-off between CLIP image similarity (consistency with the input) and directional CLIP similarity (consistency with the edit instruction) compared to both IP2P-Official and IP2P-Ours, as shown in the Pareto-style plot (Fig. 5). In the user study on the 1K real-image evaluation dataset, IP2P-Ours receives approximately 30% more favorable votes than IP2P-Official, and HIVE receives approximately 25% more favorable votes than IP2P-Ours. On the most improved verb category ('add'), HIVE raises the success rate from 23.5% (IP2P-Ours) to 28.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HIVE against two variants of InstructPix2Pix [7]: the official model (IP2P-Official) and a retrained version using HIVE's larger 1.45M training dataset (IP2P-Ours). On the synthetic CLIP-similarity trade-off plot (Fig. 5), HIVE dominates both baselines, achieving higher image consistency at every level of directional similarity. In the 1K user study, IP2P-Ours already outperforms IP2P-Official by ~30% in favorable votes, confirming the value of the expanded dataset; HIVE then further outperforms IP2P-Ours by ~25%, demonstrating that human-feedback reward fine-tuning provides a substantial additional gain beyond data scaling alone. The ablation in Fig. 11(b) also shows that HIVE's condition-reward-loss formulation beats a naive scalar-reward-conditioning baseline (HIVE-Reward) by 25.8% in user votes, indicating that the text-prompt reward conditioning is important for stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIVE extends InstructPix2Pix's instructional image editing framework by importing the RLHF paradigm from InstructGPT, but departs from the standard PPO-based approach by formulating the alignment problem through offline RL objectives (weighted reward loss and condition reward loss) that avoid the prohibitively expensive on-policy sampling required by diffusion models. The reward model architecture leverages BLIP's vision-language pretraining to jointly encode the input image, editing instruction, and edited output, and the training pipeline is augmented with cycle-consistency data generated by inverting editing instructions. Concurrent works on text-to-image alignment (ReFL, ImageReward, Aligning T2I) address the simpler single-condition generation task, whereas HIVE tackles the harder two-condition (image + instruction) editing setting where ground-truth edited images are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HIVE demonstrates that human feedback provides a consistent and meaningful improvement (≈25% preference gain) for instructional editing, validating the RLHF paradigm for diffusion-based image manipulation and opening a path toward conversational, preference-aware editing. The released 1.1M training, 3.6K reward, and 1K evaluation datasets are practical resources for the community. However, the paper identifies clear limitations—counting, spatial reasoning, and object grounding remain failure modes, and the method cannot rescue cases where all candidate outputs are equally wrong—suggesting that data quality and base-model capability remain bottlenecks that feedback alone cannot overcome.
