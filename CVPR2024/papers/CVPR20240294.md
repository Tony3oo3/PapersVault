## Problem

Black-box knowledge distillation (B2KD) requires compressing a cloud-hosted teacher model into an edge-deployable student without access to the teacher's parameters, gradients, or internal structure, while also respecting limited bandwidth, potentially hard-only API responses, and user data privacy. Existing B2KD methods either send raw local images to the cloud (leaking user privacy) or rely solely on direct logit-level alignment, which provides a single gradient direction and underperforms under distribution shift and limited query budgets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes random noise vectors whose dimensionality equals the number of classes C (sampled from a standard Gaussian), a black-box teacher accessible only via API returning either soft probability vectors or hard class indices, and a small set of local unlabeled images used solely to train the GAN discriminator. The output is a lightweight student CNN (e.g., MobileNet, ResNet8/32) trained without ground-truth labels. Crucially, only GAN-synthesized privacy-free images are transmitted to the cloud server—never the user's original local data—keeping the data exchange cost around 120 MB versus 11–28 GB for competing methods.

## Method

MEKD operates in two stages. First, a DCGAN is trained with its generator's input dimension set to C (the class count) and an information-maximization loss that pushes the teacher's response to generated images toward 1.0, so the generator learns an inverse mapping f_G ≈ f_T⁻¹ from logit-space to image-space. Second, the generator is frozen and grafted behind both teacher and student: each model's softened logits are fed into f_G to produce high-dimensional image points, and the student is trained to minimize the L1 (or L2) distance between these image points, combined with a standard KLD logit loss weighted by β. The key theoretical insight is that minimizing this image-space distance drives student logits toward the boundary of the latent-space cell containing the teacher's logits, yielding a gradient direction complementary to direct KLD alignment and provably equivalent to minimizing the Wasserstein distance between the two models' output distributions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet56→ResNet8), MEKD(soft) achieves 85.36% top-1 accuracy with only 10K query samples, outperforming the best B2KD baseline DB3KD (78.47%) by ~7 points. On CIFAR-100 (VGG13→VGG11), MEKD(soft) reaches 64.76%, surpassing DB3KD (63.48%) and AM (62.17%). On Tiny ImageNet (ResNet110→ResNet32), MEKD(soft) attains 50.87%, exceeding DB3KD (47.95%) and AM (47.72%). The soft and hard response variants perform within 1% of each other across all datasets, demonstrating robustness to response type.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEKD outperforms all three dedicated B2KD baselines (KN, AM, DB3KD) on every dataset and both response types. On CIFAR-10, MEKD(soft) gains +4.7% over DB3KD (85.36 vs 78.47) and +10.5% over AM (85.36 vs 74.89); on CIFAR-100, it leads DB3KD by +1.3% and AM by +2.6%. Against traditional full-data KD methods (KD, ML, DKD) that use 50K–100K samples, MEKD with only 10K queries is competitive on easy tasks (MNIST, CIFAR-10) but trails by 1–5% on harder ones (CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet), which is expected given the 5–10× data reduction. DAFL (zero-shot, no local data) is substantially weaker, confirming that even a small local dataset helps. On the out-of-domain SVHN experiment (Table 6), MEKD(soft) achieves 86.45% (ResNet56→MobileNet) vs. DB3KD's 90.15%, but at a data exchange cost of ~120 MB versus DB3KD's ~20.8 GB, making MEKD far more practical for bandwidth-constrained edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box knowledge distillation (B2KD) line initiated by Hinton et al.'s KLD-based KD and subsequent response-based methods (ML, DKD), as well as B2KD-specific approaches like Knockoff Nets, Active Mixup, and DB3KD. Its key conceptual departure is repurposing a frozen DCGAN generator as an 'inverse mapping' of the teacher function, so that alignment is achieved by reducing distance in high-dimensional image space rather than directly minimizing KLD in logit space. The deprivatization step—synthesizing query images via GAN to avoid sending real local data to the cloud—addresses a privacy gap that prior B2KD methods (which typically send original local images) do not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical solution to cloud-to-edge model compression under strict data-exchange budgets (~120 MB vs. ~20.8 GB for DB3KD) and provides theoretical grounding (Wasserstein-based cell-boundary optimization) for why image-space distance reduction yields a distinct gradient direction from logit-space KLD. However, the approach is heavily dependent on a well-trained GAN, and mode collapse remains a significant limitation for complex datasets like ImageNet, where the generator must capture diverse patterns. Open questions include whether the method scales to non-visual modalities, whether more stable generative models (diffusion, VAEs) could replace GANs to mitigate mode collapse, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the inverse mapping.
