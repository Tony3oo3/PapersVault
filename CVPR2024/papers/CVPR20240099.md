## Problem

Existing language-based driving benchmarks cover only a subset of autonomous driving tasks and rely on incomplete information (single-view images without temporal or spatial context), making them insufficient for holistic driving understanding. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a comprehensive multi-view video dataset spanning the full perception→prediction→risk→planning pipeline and a model architecture that fuses temporal, multi-view, and spatial (BEV) information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240099/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives 6-camera multi-view video (3 uniformly sampled frames per view, resized to 224×224) and a natural-language instruction, producing a free-form text response. The 91K instruction-response pairs in NuInstruct are generated via a SQL-based pipeline over NuScenes annotations, covering 17 subtasks across four categories (Perception, Prediction, Risk, Planning with Reasoning), with evaluation using MAE, Accuracy, MAP, and BLEU depending on the task type.

## Method

BEV-InMLLM extends a base MLLM (BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, or Video-LLaMA) with two trainable modules while freezing all pre-trained weights. First, a Multi-view Q-Former concatenates per-view visual tokens from all 6 cameras and uses K_mv=32 learnable queries with cross-attention to produce compact multi-view visual semantics. Second, a BEV injection module extracts instruction-aware BEV features by cross-attending a concatenation of instruction tokens and K_bev=32 learnable BEV queries against frozen BEV features (from LSS or BEVFormer at 200×200 resolution), then injects these into the multi-view features via residual cross-attention. The instruction tokens in the BEV Q-Former allow the model to selectively attend to spatially relevant regions, and the entire BEV injection module is plug-and-play for any existing MLLM.

## Results

On NuInstruct, BEV-InMLLM consistently outperforms all three base MLLMs across all 17 subtasks. The most notable gains appear in Planning with Reasoning (BLEU), where MiniGPT-4+BEV-InMLLM reaches 35.6 vs. 26.3 for the base (9.3-point improvement), and Video-LLaMA+BEV-InMLLM reaches 35.2 vs. 25.3 (9.9-point improvement). For spatial-sensitive perception tasks, BEV-InMLLM reduces Distance MAE from 30.2 to 23.6 (MiniGPT-4) and Speed MAE from 6.2 to 3.1, while boosting Closest accuracy from 20.2 to 32.9. The MV-MLLM intermediate step already yields ~5–6% gains on Risk and Planning subtasks, confirming the value of multi-view temporal information before BEV injection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three base MLLMs—BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, and Video-LLaMA—each augmented with the proposed MV-MLLM and BEV-InMLLM modules under identical fine-tuning settings. BEV-InMLLM wins on every subtask across all three base models, with the largest absolute gains in Planning with Reasoning (BLEU): +8.4 for BLIP-2 (24.9→33.3), +9.3 for MiniGPT-4 (26.3→35.6), and +9.9 for Video-LLaMA (25.3→35.2). The intermediate MV-MLLM step already captures most of the multi-view and temporal benefit, improving Risk subtasks by ~5% and Planning by ~6% over the base, but BEV injection adds a further 2–5% on spatial-sensitive tasks (Distance, Speed, Motion, Closest). MiniGPT-4+BEV-InMLLM achieves the single best Planning score (35.6), while Video-LLaMA+BEV-InMLLM leads on Same-Road accuracy (34.6) and Crossing (26.1). No baseline variant ever surpasses the corresponding BEV-InMLLM on any of the 17 subtasks, confirming the plug-and-play BEV injection module's consistent benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Q-Former-based MLLM lineage (BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, Video-LLaMA) by adding a multi-view Q-Former for video inputs and a plug-and-play BEV injection module that leverages pre-trained BEV extractors (LSS, BEVFormer, BEVDet) from the autonomous driving literature. It departs from prior language-driving datasets (Talk2Car, DRAMA, DriveGPT4, Talk2BEV, NuScenes-QA) by covering all four driving stages—perception, prediction, risk, and planning with reasoning—under multi-view, temporal, and spatial constraints simultaneously, using a structured SQL-based generation pipeline rather than GPT-4 prompting or manual annotation. The key architectural departure is treating BEV features as a frozen, language-aligned token stream injected via cross-attention, rather than replacing the ViT backbone or training a new BEV encoder from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NuInstruct (91K multi-view video-QA pairs, 17 subtasks) is currently the most comprehensive language-driving benchmark, and the SQL-based generation method offers a reproducible, scalable alternative to LLM- or human-annotated data. The plug-and-play BEV injection module is practically attractive because it requires no retraining of the BEV extractor and can be attached to any existing MLLM. However, the dataset is limited to NuScenes (no traffic lights, no 3D detection tasks), and the 9% improvement, while consistent across three base MLLMs, is measured only on the authors' own benchmark, leaving open whether the gains transfer to other driving scenarios or larger-scale MLLMs. A key open question is whether the instruction-aware BEV Q-Former generalizes to tasks not represented in the 17 subtasks.
