## Problem

Camera ISP pipelines introduce compounded, often unknown degradations (sensor noise, demosaicing artifacts, compression, and suboptimal hyperparameter settings) that are extremely difficult to model or synthesize, preventing supervised learning of clean/degraded image pairs. This limits the generalization of deep networks for blind image restoration and their adaptability to downstream vision tasks such as detection and segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240520/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single ISP-degraded RGB image as input and produces a refined degradation-independent representation (DiR) in a canonical latent space. This latent code can be decoded via a learned decoder into a restored RGB image, or fed directly into a task-specific network (e.g., Mask R-CNN for detection, DeepLabV3/SegFormer for segmentation). During training, the DiRNet consumes pairs of degraded images from the same source, while the alignment stage uses synthesized degraded/clean pairs generated by simulating the ISP pipeline on DIV2K images.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage self-supervised + supervised framework. In Stage I, a VAE-type DiRNet learns a baseline DiR by maximizing multi-view mutual information (MMI) between the latent code and two degraded views of the same image, using a JSD-based mutual information lower bound; a separate DfRNet (InfoVAE) learns a degradation-free representation reference from high-quality images. In Stage II, an alignment network refines the baseline DiR toward the DfR space, guided by a 'pilot DfR' (the DfRNet's output on the degraded image) through a G-Conv module with adaptive kernels and a spatial attention branch. The alignment network is jointly trained with a task-specific auxiliary network, minimizing a combined loss of representation alignment, task loss, and pixel-level reconstruction, while DiRNet and DfRNet remain frozen.

## Results

On real-world ISP-degraded image restoration, the proposed DiR method achieves the best non-reference quality scores across all four test datasets: NIQE of 6.57 (PolyU-Real), 9.06 (NC12), 8.96 (Nam), and 8.62 (DND), outperforming the strongest competitor MPRNet (6.12 on PolyU-Real) and DIL (7.86 on DND). For object detection on COCO, the method reaches mAP@0.5:0.95 of 43.3, surpassing Sequential-tuned ISP (42.3) and Attention-aware ISP (41.0). On ADE20k segmentation under high-noise conditions, the method achieves mAcc of 44.5 (DeepLabV3) and 52.9 (SegFormer), exceeding DNF by 7.6% and 6.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For image restoration, the DiR method outperforms all eight competing methods (BM3D, CBDNet, LIR, MPRNet, Uformer, SCPGab, DIL, SDAP) on all three no-reference metrics across all four real-world datasets, with the largest margins on BIQA (e.g., 51.9 vs. 48.1 for LIR on PolyU-Real, 53.6 vs. 49.8 for LIR on Nam). For object detection on COCO, the method achieves mAP@0.5:0.95 of 43.3, beating Sequential-tuned ISP [39] (42.3) by 1.0 points and Attention-aware ISP [38] (41.0) by 2.3 points, while the default ISP baseline scores only 21.4. On COCO segmentation, the method reaches mAP@0.5:0.95 of 34.8, outperforming Sequential-tuned (32.6) and Attention-aware (31.5). On ADE20k under high-noise conditions, the method surpasses DNF [20] by 7.6% mAcc (DeepLabV3) and 6.7% mAcc (SegFormer), and PyNET [16] by even larger margins (12.8% and 7.1% mAcc respectively). Notably, the method also outperforms expert-tuned ISP hyperparameters on downstream tasks, demonstrating that degradation-independent representations better preserve semantically critical features than perceptually optimized ISP settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes information-theoretic representation learning tools—multi-view mutual information maximization (Federici et al., 2019), variational interaction information bounds (Hwang et al., 2020), and JSD-based MI estimation (Nowozin et al., 2016)—within a VAE/InfoVAE framework to address the specific problem of ISP pipeline degradations. It departs from prior blind restoration work (which attempts to directly invert unknown degradations) and from ISP hyperparameter tuning approaches (which optimize camera settings) by instead learning a latent representation that is invariant to degradation type and severity. The two-stage design—self-supervised baseline DiR followed by supervised alignment to a degradation-free reference via a G-Conv and spatial-attention alignment network—is a novel architectural combination not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ISP degradation problem is practically important because camera-captured images feed into both human viewing and machine vision pipelines, yet the compound, often unknown degradation mechanisms make supervised restoration intractable. The self-supervised core of the method (requiring only degraded images) is a practical advantage, and the demonstrated generality across restoration, detection, and segmentation suggests the learned representation could serve as a reusable front-end for camera-based systems. Limitations include reliance on synthetic ISP degradation simulation during training (which may not capture all real-world failure modes), the imperfect 'degradation-free' surrogate ground truth from real cameras, and the two-stage training pipeline that adds engineering complexity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, multi-sensor fusion, or truly adversarial/unseen degradation distributions.
