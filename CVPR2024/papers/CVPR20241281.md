## Problem

Existing video recognition datasets are built almost exclusively on landscape mode videos, yet portrait mode short-form videos from social media have become dominant and introduce unique challenges: subject-centric framing with minimal background, egocentric content, and heavy reliance on verbal communication. No dedicated dataset or systematic study existed to characterize these format-specific challenges for video recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes portrait mode video clips (aspect ratio > 1, duration 2 s–1 min) and optionally synchronized audio, producing a single class label from 400 fine-grained categories. Frames are resized by the shorter side and center-cropped to 224×224 squares (or rectangular crops such as 288×192 for transformer models), with 16 frames sampled per clip; audio is processed separately via a frozen VGG sound-classification backbone. The dataset is strictly single-label: videos ambiguous across multiple categories are discarded during annotation, and deduplication via UniformerBase features (cosine similarity > 0.98, Louvain clustering) removes roughly 25% of candidates before human labeling.

## Method

The paper introduces PortraitMode-400, a 76k-video dataset whose 400-category taxonomy is built data-driven from ~38k Douyin search queries and organized in a three-level hierarchy (9 domains → 400 leaf categories), with professional annotation and 20% double-review for quality assurance. For controlled cross-format analysis, the authors construct S100-PM and S100-LM subsets from Kinetics-700 with matched taxonomies and per-category video counts, enabling direct comparison of landscape- versus portrait-trained models. The empirical study probes spatial bias via sliding-window accuracy heatmaps, compares Inception-style random scaling versus shorter-side resizing (finding the latter is preferable for transformers on portrait-only data), and shows that rectangular crops (288×192) outperform square crops for transformer architectures while degrading CNN performance. Temporal self-attention (Uniformer-S) yields 4.5–4.6% gains over frame-level mean pooling, and adding frozen audio features provides ~2.4 points improvement, confirming the multimodal and temporally rich nature of portrait mode content.

## Results

On the cross-mode evaluation (S100-PM), X3D-M trained on portrait mode videos achieves 52.0% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the landscape-trained model by 7.5 points (44.5%). For preprocessing, the Inception-style random scaling improves X3D-M by 2.2% on S100-PM (54.2% vs 52.0%) but degrades Uniformer-S by 2.3% (39.7% vs 42.0%), revealing architecture-dependent optimal strategies. Rectangular training crops (288×192) boost Uniformer-S to 45.4% and MViTv2-S to 45.5% on S100-PM versus 42.0% and 41.0% with square 224×224 crops. Temporal self-attention in Uniformer-S yields a 4.6% gain on PM-400 (50.3% vs 45.7%), and adding audio improves PM-400 accuracy by 2.4 points (57.0% vs 54.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses three model architectures—X3D-M (CNN), Uniformer-S (hybrid), and MViTv2-S (transformer)—as testbeds rather than proposing a new method. In the cross-mode comparison, all three models show that portrait-mode training is essential for portrait-mode evaluation: X3D-M gains 7.5 points (52.0% vs 44.5%), Uniformer-S gains 1.9 points (42.0% vs 40.1%), and MViTv2-S gains 7.3 points (41.0% vs 33.7%) when trained on PM vs LM videos and tested on S100-PM. For data augmentation, the Inception-style random scaling consistently benefits X3D-M (up to +2.2%) but consistently hurts both transformer models (Uniformer-S drops 2.3% on S100-PM, MViTv2-S drops up to 4.1%), indicating that landscape-mode conventions do not transfer to portrait-only data. Rectangular training crops (288×192) improve both transformers by 3.4–4.5 points over square crops but degrade X3D-M by 1.2 points, suggesting the fixed square receptive field of CNNs is incompatible with elongated inputs. Temporal self-attention (Uniformer-S vs frame-based Uniformer-frames) yields a 4.6% gain on PM-400 and 4.5% on Kinetics-400, while audio fusion adds a consistent ~2.4 points on both PM-400 and 3Massiv.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first dataset (PortraitMode-400) dedicated to portrait mode video recognition, departing from the landscape-mode-centric tradition of video benchmarks (Kinetics, UCF101, HMDB51). While 3Massiv includes portrait mode videos, it targets coarse multilingual concept recognition with only 34 classes, whereas PM-400 provides a fine-grained, data-driven taxonomy of 400 action categories sourced from Douyin search queries. The empirical study repurposes standard video recognition architectures (X3D, Uniformer, MViTv2) but investigates how the portrait format changes optimal preprocessing, spatial attention patterns, and the relative importance of temporal and audio signals—questions not addressed in prior landscape-mode-focused work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As short-form portrait videos dominate social media (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), this dataset and its empirical findings provide a necessary testbed for understanding how video recognition models must adapt to vertical, subject-centric, audio-rich content. The paper reveals that landscape-mode training protocols (Inception-style augmentation, square crops) do not transfer optimally to portrait mode, and that spatial information is distributed differently (middle-to-lower vs. center), suggesting that current architectures and pipelines may be suboptimal for the dominant video format in consumer applications. Open questions include whether purpose-built architectures for portrait mode can close the cross-mode gap, how findings generalize beyond Douyin-sourced content, and whether the 400-class taxonomy captures the full diversity of portrait mode video content.
