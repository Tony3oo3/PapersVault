## Problem

Synthetic data has empowered third-person-view vision models, but its application to embodied egocentric perception tasks remains largely unexplored. The critical challenge is simulating natural human movements that effectively steer head-mounted cameras to capture faithful egocentric representations of complex, dynamic 3D worlds, without relying on pre-defined global paths or expensive mocap data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes 3D scenes (e.g., Replica, LaMAR, EgoBody environments), SMPL-X body models with diverse skin textures and 3D clothing from BEDLAM, camera rig parameters supporting fisheye and pinhole models (e.g., Project Aria, HoloLens), and target locations. The egocentric camera is placed between the two eyeballs on the SMPL-X mesh, with viewing direction perpendicular to the eye-bone plane, and multi-camera rigs are supported. Outputs include photorealistic RGB with simulated motion blur, depth maps, surface normals, segmentation masks, world positions, optical flow, camera pose trajectories, and SMPL-X parameters for the target subject.

## Method

EgoGen's core is a novel egocentric perception-driven motion synthesis model built on collision-avoiding motion primitives (CAMPs). A C-VAE-based generative model produces 0.5-second motion clips (20 frames of 67 body surface markers) from a 128D latent action space, conditioned on 2 seed frames. A PPO policy selects CAMPs using a compact egocentric sensing representation (N rays cast from the camera midpoint with 2D collision detection) as a depth proxy, avoiding the instability and cost of full depth rendering. Training uses a two-stage RL scheme: pretraining with relaxed penetration constraints followed by finetuning with strict SDF-based termination, plus an attention reward that incentivizes the virtual human to look toward targets. Composable CAMPs enable emergent multi-agent crowd behaviors without multi-agent RL, as each virtual human independently navigates using the same policy while treating others as dynamic obstacles via bounding boxes.

## Results

On motion synthesis (Table 1), EgoGen's CAMPs achieve 100% success rate in moving-obstacle, 2-human, and 4-human scenarios, outperforming GAMMA* (92–96%) and DIMOS* (70–88%), while reducing human-human penetration to 0% (vs. 27.6–60.4% for GAMMA* and 10.7–41.7% for DIMOS*). On the LaMAR mapping/localization benchmark, augmenting with EgoGen synthetic images yields 76.7% recall at (1°, 10 cm), surpassing real-data-only LaMAR (66.9%) and pose-perturbation augmentation (74.9%). For egocentric camera tracking on GIMO, EgoGen pretraining reduces pose error from 1.83 to 1.67 and rotation error from 0.74 to 0.62. On EgoBody HMR, synthetic pretraining lowers depth-based G-MPJPE by 22.9% (117.7→90.7 mm) and MPJPE by 20.7% (82.2→65.2 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAMMA* [117] and DIMOS* [119] on motion synthesis, EgoGen's CAMPs achieve a perfect 100% success rate in all dynamic scenarios (moving obstacles, 2-human, 4-human), compared to 92–96% for GAMMA* and 70–88% for DIMOS*. The penetration advantage is dramatic: EgoGen reports 0% human-human penetration in multi-human scenes, while GAMMA* reaches 27.6% (2 humans) and 60.4% (4 humans), and DIMOS* reaches 10.7% and 41.7% respectively. On the LaMAR mapping/localization task, EgoGen augmentation (76.7% recall) outperforms both the real-data-only baseline (66.9%) and the pose-perturbation method of Ng et al. [58] (74.9%), while also producing the longest track (5.2105 m). For egocentric camera tracking on GIMO, EgoGen pretraining consistently improves over scratch training on all three metrics (pose, rotation, translation). On EgoBody HMR, synthetic pretraining yields substantial gains over real-data-only training for both depth (22.9% G-MPJPE reduction) and RGB (5.9% MPJPE reduction) inputs, confirming that EgoGen data effectively compensates for the scarcity of real egocentric annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoGen builds directly on the generative motion primitive framework of GAMMA [117] (C-VAE-based latent action space) and DIMOS [119] (scene-aware motion synthesis), but departs from both by replacing their reliance on pre-computed navigation meshes and global path planning with an egocentric ray-casting sensing proxy that drives a PPO policy over collision-avoiding motion primitives (CAMPs). This closes the perception-action loop that prior synthetic data generators for egocentric views (e.g., XR-EgoPose [97], which depends on mocap) left open. The two-stage RL scheme (pretraining with relaxed penetration constraints, then finetuning with SDF-based strict termination) and the composable CAMP architecture for emergent multi-agent behavior without multi-agent RL are the key methodological departures from the single-agent, static-scene setting of [117, 119].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoGen addresses a genuine bottleneck in egocentric perception research: the scarcity of large-scale, richly annotated training data for HMD-based tasks. By demonstrating that synthetic data from autonomous virtual humans can consistently improve SOTA methods on mapping/localization (LaMAR), camera tracking (GIMO), and human mesh recovery (EgoBody), the paper validates a scalable, privacy-preserving data augmentation pipeline. However, human-scene interaction remains coarse (no hand manipulation, sitting, or lying), attention is modeled with fixed goals rather than predicted intent, and the dynamic-environment evaluation uses relatively simple obstacle configurations. Open questions include whether the egocentric sensing proxy scales to outdoor or highly cluttered scenes, and whether the motion diversity is sufficient to cover the full behavioral distribution of real HMD users.
