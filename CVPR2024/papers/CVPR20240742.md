## Problem

Existing 3D generative models are fundamentally limited by low-resolution representations (typically ≤256³) and cannot scale to large outdoor scenes such as those in autonomous driving. While 2D-prior-based methods (e.g., diffusion score distillation) offer diversity, they require expensive test-time optimization and suffer from multi-view inconsistency. The paper asks whether a purely 3D generative prior, built on sparse voxel hierarchies, can simultaneously achieve high resolution (up to 1024³) and large scene scale (100m × 100m) in a single feed-forward pass.

## Input / Output

The model accepts optional global conditions (CLIP-encoded text prompts, category labels, or a single LiDAR scan) and optional coarse-level voxel edits, and produces a multi-level sparse voxel hierarchy where each level carries per-voxel attributes (TSDF, surface normals, semantic labels). The finest grid reaches 1024³ resolution (millions of active voxels) for objects or 1024³ over a 102.4m × 102.4m patch (10 cm voxel size) for driving scenes, all generated in under 30 seconds. Because the output is a hierarchy of sparse grids rather than a single dense volume, users can intervene at any resolution level to edit geometry before finer levels are regenerated.

## Method

XCube factorizes the joint distribution over a sparse voxel hierarchy into a cascade of per-level latent diffusion models, each conditioned on the decoded grid and attributes of the coarser level (a Markov chain induced by the geometric containment of finer grids within coarser ones). A sparse-structure VAE at each level encodes the voxel grid and its attributes into a compact continuous latent at the coarser resolution, using sparse convolutions with progressive pruning in the decoder to predict novel voxels. The diffusion backbone is a 3D sparse U-Net (adapted from DDPM) with v-parameterization, AdaGN timestep injection, and cross-attention for text; conditions from the coarser level are injected by concatenating their features to the network input. The entire pipeline runs on a custom GPU framework built on the VDB data structure, which stores 3.4M voxels in ~11 MB and processes 1024³ scenes in milliseconds, achieving ~3× speedup and ~0.5× memory over TorchSparse. A refinement network appended to each VAE decoder mitigates error accumulation across hierarchy levels by being trained on noise-augmented posterior samples.

## Results

On ShapeNet (1-NNA, lower is better), XCube achieves the best Chamfer Distance and Earth Mover's Distance across all three categories: Airplane (CD 52.85, EMD 49.75), Chair (CD 53.99, EMD 48.60), and Car (CD 57.96, EMD 54.43). In a user study on Objaverse text-to-3D, 79.2% of 900 pairwise comparisons favor XCube over Shap-E. On the Waymo Open Dataset, 66.3% of 900 user-study comparisons prefer XCube's generated scenes over ground-truth scans, and the model generates 1024³-resolution scenes with millions of voxels in under 30 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XCube achieves the lowest 1-NNA scores (both CD and EMD) across all three ShapeNet categories, outperforming every baseline. Against point-based methods PVD and LION, XCube leads by 4–17 points in CD, reflecting the resolution advantage of generating millions of voxels versus 2048 points. Triplane-based NFD is competitive on simpler categories (Airplane CD 57.55, Chair CD 54.87) but degrades sharply on Car (CD 69.49, EMD 71.96) due to its inability to capture intricate geometry like suspensions. Dense-voxel NWD and sparse-voxel LAS-Diffusion are limited by low grid resolutions (≤128³), while 3DShape2VecSet over-compresses its unstructured latent, yielding the worst Car scores (CD 86.85, EMD 80.91). In the text-to-3D user study against Shap-E, XCube wins 79.2% of 900 pairwise comparisons, with the paper attributing the gap to higher geometric fidelity and finer detail from the hierarchical sparse voxel representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

XCube extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) and hierarchical/cascaded diffusion (Blattmann et al., 2023; Ho et al., 2022) to the 3D domain by coupling a hierarchy of sparse-structure VAEs with a hierarchy of diffusion models over sparse voxel grids. It departs from prior 3D generative models (LION, NFD, NWD, LAS-Diffusion) that either use a single diffusion level, are limited to ≤256³ resolution, or lack support for arbitrary per-voxel attributes. The key architectural departure is the use of the VDB data structure (Museth, 2013) as the underlying sparse storage, enabling a custom 3D learning framework that is ~3× faster and uses ~0.5× the memory of TorchSparse, which prior sparse-voxel 3D generators did not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first demonstrations of purely 3D generative priors scaling to 1024³ resolution and 100 m × 100 m outdoor scenes at 10 cm voxel size, a regime previously infeasible for feed-forward 3D generation. The hierarchical formulation enables multi-scale user editing and conditional generation from a single LiDAR scan, opening practical applications in autonomous driving and robotics. The authors acknowledge that text-to-3D quality is still bounded by the gap between 3D and image dataset scale, and future work on image-conditioning and using the learned prior as a foundation model for downstream tasks remains open.
