## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs are specialized for either image or video understanding: image-focused methods allocate many visual tokens for fine spatial detail but cannot model temporal relationships, while video-focused methods sacrifice per-frame spatial resolution to fit more frames. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified visual representation that lets a single model handle both images and videos with a limited, content-adaptive number of visual tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images or video clips (all frames resized to 224×224) alongside a text query, and produces free-form text responses including conversational replies, detailed descriptions, and multiple-choice answers. A key design detail is that the number of visual tokens is dynamic: images and short videos use fewer tokens, while longer videos are assigned proportionally more tokens (default temporal clustering ratio of 1/16 of frames), yet the total remains well below fixed-token baselines (112 vs. LLaVA's 256). The same trained model processes both modalities without any architectural modification or re-alignment.

## Method

Chat-UniVi represents both images and videos as a set of dynamic visual tokens obtained through a parameter-free, progressive token-merging procedure based on DPC-KNN density-peaks clustering. For images, visual tokens from a CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder are clustered and merged by averaging within each cluster; for videos, frames are first grouped into temporal events via DPC-KNN on mean-pooled frame features, and then spatial merging is applied within each event so tokens expand only over frames belonging to the same event. A three-step multi-scale aggregation (64→32→16 clusters) concatenates features from each merging level, providing the LLM with both high-level semantic and low-level detail representations, which are projected into language embeddings via a trainable matrix W. Training follows a two-stage scheme: first pre-training W with frozen LLM and vision encoder on image-caption pairs, then joint instruction tuning on a mixed dataset of image and video conversations using Vicuna-7B. The parameter-free clustering is critical—the authors attribute its superiority over parameterized alternatives to gradient instability in multimodal conversation training that hinders convergence of learned merging parameters.

## Results

On GPT-4-based image understanding (Table 1), Chat-UniVi (7B, 112 visual tokens) achieves 84.1/74.2/93.7/84.2 on Conversation/Detail/Reason/All, surpassing LLaVA-7B† (78.8/70.2/91.8/80.4) while using only 44% of the visual tokens. On GPT-based video understanding (Table 2), Chat-UniVi scores 57.8/58.2/69.2/57.8/56.2 on Correct/Detail/Context/Temporal/Consistency, outperforming the best video-specific baseline Video-ChatGPT (48.0/50.4/52.4/39.6/47.4) by 9.8 points on Temporal understanding. On zero-shot video QA (Table 4), Chat-UniVi leads on all four benchmarks, e.g., 55.0% on MSRVTT-QA vs. 49.3% for Video-ChatGPT and 69.0% on TGIF-QA vs. 51.4% for Video-ChatGPT. On ScienceQA fine-tuning (Table 3), Chat-UniVi reaches 88.78% average accuracy, exceeding LLaMA-SciTune (86.11%) and LLaMA-Adapter (85.19%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against image-focused baselines, Chat-UniVi (7B, 112 tokens) clearly outperforms LLaVA-7B† on all four GPT-4 image metrics (e.g., Conversation 84.1 vs. 78.8, All 84.2 vs. 80.4), but trails LLaVA-13B on Detail (74.2 vs. 75.3), Reason (93.7 vs. 96.5), and All (84.2 vs. 85.1), while winning on Conversation (84.1 vs. 83.1). Against video-focused baselines, Chat-UniVi dominates across the board: on GPT-based video evaluation it beats Video-ChatGPT by +9.8 on Temporal (57.8 vs. 39.6) and +16.8 on Context (69.2 vs. 52.4), and beats Video-LLaMA by +18.6 on Correct (57.8 vs. 39.2). On zero-shot video QA, Chat-UniVi surpasses Video-ChatGPT on all four datasets (MSRVTT-QA 55.0 vs. 49.3, MSVD-QA 69.3 vs. 64.9, TGIF-QA 69.0 vs. 51.4, ActivityNet-QA 46.1 vs. 35.2). On the POPE object-hallucination benchmark, Chat-UniVi with multi-scale representation outperforms LLaVA-7B† on POPE-R accuracy (85.19 vs. 72.16) but falls short of InstructBLIP-13B (88.57) and LLaVA-13B (64.12 on POPE-R, though InstructBLIP leads on all three POPE variants). On ScienceQA fine-tuning, Chat-UniVi (88.78%) beats LLaMA-SciTune (86.11%) and LLaMA-Adapter (85.19%) but remains below LLaVA-13B (90.92%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA two-stage training paradigm (pre-train a projection matrix, then joint instruction tuning) and the CLIP vision encoder, but departs from prior multimodal LLMs by introducing a unified dynamic visual token representation that handles both images and videos simultaneously. Where LLaVA and similar image-focused methods use a fixed grid of 256 tokens, and video-focused methods like Video-ChatGPT sacrifice spatial resolution for temporal coverage, Chat-UniVi employs DPC-KNN density-peaks clustering to progressively merge semantically similar tokens, yielding a variable number of tokens that adapt to content complexity. The multi-scale aggregation (three merging steps) and the parameter-free clustering are the key architectural departures from prior dynamic-token methods, which the authors note cannot be directly extended to video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single 7B model with only 112 visual tokens can outperform both image-specialized (LLaVA-13B) and video-specialized (Video-ChatGPT, VideoChat) models on their respective benchmarks suggests that unified multimodal training is not merely feasible but advantageous, reducing the need for separate model families. The finding that parameter-free DPC-KNN clustering outperforms parameterized alternatives—attributed to gradient instability in multimodal training—offers a practical design principle for future vision-language architectures. Open questions include scalability to very long videos (beyond the 1/16 temporal clustering ratio tested), whether the approach generalizes to other modalities (audio, 3D), and whether the multi-scale representation's hallucination-reduction benefit persists at larger model scales.
