## Problem

Existing click-based interactive segmentation algorithms use previous probability maps as network input, but the shape and detail information contained in those maps is not effectively propagated to current predictions. No prior method explicitly enhances or extracts beneficial information from the probability maps before feeding them to the segmentation network, leaving their potential underutilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240039/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives four input channels: the RGB image, a click map encoding foreground/background clicks, the original previous-round probability map P^{t-1}, and a modulated probability map P̃^{t-1} produced by gamma correction. These are concatenated, embedded into tensors matching the image spatial size, and element-wise summed before entering the backbone. The output is a per-pixel probability map P^t that is thresholded at 0.5 to yield the binary segmentation mask Y^t for the current round.

## Method

MFP introduces three key components. First, probability map modulation applies gamma correction within a circular window around the current click: for foreground clicks, probabilities are raised toward 1 via P^{1/γ}; for background clicks, they are lowered toward 0 via P^{γ}, with γ assigned per-pixel based on either Euclidean distance or probability distance from the click (switching from probability-distance to Euclidean after the 7th click). Second, a late-fusion branch extracts probability-related features from P^{t-1} and P̃^{t-1} using two Xception convolution blocks, then concatenates and fuses them with the backbone feature map through four additional Xception blocks before the segmentation head, strengthening the influence of probability information on the final prediction. Third, a fully recursive training strategy replaces random click sampling: training starts at the object center and iteratively places the next click near the largest error region, up to 24 clicks per image, ensuring every click has a meaningful previous probability map for modulation. The framework is implemented on ResNet-34, HRNet-18, and ViT-B backbones and trained with normalized focal loss.

## Results

On the COCO+LVIS-trained setting (Table 3), MFP (ViT-B) achieves NoC@85 of 1.34 on GrabCut, 1.35 on Berkeley, 3.37 on DAVIS, and 3.26 on SBD, and NoC@95 of 1.70, 4.68, 9.23, and 11.65 respectively. MFP attains the best NoC score in 7 out of 12 evaluated settings and ranks second in the remaining 5. In the same-backbone comparison (Table 4), MFP outperforms all competing methods in 28 out of 36 settings across ResNet-34, HRNet-18, and ViT-B backbones, with the largest margins on SBD (e.g., NoC@85 of 3.21 vs. 3.28 for SimpleClick on ViT-B, and 3.92 vs. 5.18 for CDNet on ResNet-34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimpleClick (same ViT-B backbone), MFP wins on all 12 NoC scores in the COCO+LVIS setting, with the largest gap on DAVIS NoC@95 (9.23 vs. 10.04) and SBD NoC@85 (3.26 vs. 3.43). Against iCMFormer (also ViT-B), MFP wins on 7 of 12 scores but trails on Berkeley NoC@90 (1.90 vs. 1.86) and SBD NoC@90 (5.34 vs. 5.30). Against FocalClick (SegF-B3), MFP wins on 8 of 12 scores but is slightly behind on Berkeley NoC@95 (4.68 vs. 4.63) and SBD NoC@95 (11.65 vs. 11.55). Against EMC-Click (HRNet-32), MFP wins on 10 of 12 scores, losing only on GrabCut NoC@85 (1.34 vs. 1.30) and tying on GrabCut NoC@90 (both 1.42). Against older methods like RITM, CDNet, PseudoClick, GPCIS, BRS, f-BRS, and FocusCut, MFP achieves the best or near-best scores across all four datasets, with particularly large margins on SBD (e.g., NoC@85 of 3.26 vs. 4.30 for CDNet and 3.59 for RITM). In the same-backbone ablation (Table 4), MFP with ResNet-34 beats CDNet on 10 of 12 NoC scores, and MFP with HRNet-18 beats RITM, PseudoClick, and EMC-Click on the majority of metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFP extends the standard pipeline established by RITM [27] and SimpleClick [21], which feed previous probability maps as additional network input for click-based interactive segmentation. The key departure is that prior methods treat previous probability maps as a passive input signal, whereas MFP actively modulates them via gamma correction guided by the current click's label and location, and further introduces a late-fusion branch that extracts probability-related features separately before combining them with backbone features. The recursive training strategy also departs from the iterative sampling used in RITM, ensuring every training click has a meaningful previous probability map to modulate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete underutilization of information in the standard interactive segmentation pipeline—previous probability maps carry shape details that existing architectures fail to propagate—and offers a lightweight fix (gamma correction + late fusion) that consistently improves results across three backbone families. This suggests that the bottleneck in current methods may lie less in backbone capacity and more in how auxiliary inputs are integrated, a direction worth exploring for other dense-prediction tasks with iterative refinement. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object or video settings, whether the fixed hyperparameters (N=7, R_max=100) generalize across domains, and whether the additional input channels and fusion blocks impose meaningful latency costs in real-time applications.
