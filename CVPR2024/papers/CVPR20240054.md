## Problem

Automatic background music generation for free-style videos faces three intertwined challenges: the absence of high-quality video-music datasets with fine-grained annotations and temporal alignment, the inability of existing transformer-based models to flexibly control different musical aspects (rhythm vs. melody) with interpretable signals, and the lack of mechanisms to sequentially align music timing with video content such as shot transitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240054/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip as input, which is sampled into T frames and segmented into T segments. Visual features are extracted per-frame via a frozen Video CLIP encoder, while per-segment natural language captions (generated by BLIP over 8-frame windows) are encoded by a frozen BERT model. The output is a 2-channel binary piano roll tensor of shape 2×128×128 (onset/sustain × time steps × MIDI pitch bins), representing one 8-bar (32-beat) segment of music, which can be rendered to audio.

## Method

Diff-BGM builds on the Polyffusion latent diffusion framework and introduces a feature selector that switches conditioning signals based on the denoising timestep: language (semantic) features guide melody and atmosphere in early high-noise stages (timestep > t₀), while dynamic video features control rhythm in later low-noise stages (timestep ≤ t₀). A segment-aware cross-attention layer fuses video features into the diffusion denoiser, where the noisy latent serves as Query and the condition feature as Key/Value. A specially designed binary mask restricts each query position to attend only to k=8 adjacent key positions, enforcing short-term temporal alignment between music and video segments. The model is trained with a standard noise-prediction diffusion objective conditioned on the selected features, using a 1000-step Gaussian noise schedule and Adam optimizer at learning rate 5e-5.

## Results

On the BGM909 test set, Diff-BGM achieves P@5/P@10/P@20 retrieval precision of 13.28%/23.91%/44.10%, surpassing CMT (10.56%/18.59%/36.40%) and Riffusion (9.12%/15.71%/34.29%) by 4.91–10.39 percentage points. On music quality, Diff-BGM's PCHE (2.840) and GPS (0.601) are closest to the real-data reference (2.717, 0.708) among all methods. On the SymMV dataset, Diff-BGM further outperforms V-MusProd with P@5 of 19.0 vs. 15.7 and average rank of 19.4 vs. 25.4, confirming superior video-music temporal alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CMT [4], Diff-BGM wins on all video-music correspondence metrics (P@5: 13.28% vs. 10.56%, P@20: 44.10% vs. 36.40%) and produces music quality metrics closer to the real-data reference on PCHE (2.840 vs. 2.398; real = 2.717) and SI (0.521 vs. 0.761; real = 0.486). Against Riffusion [6], Diff-BGM leads by a wider margin on retrieval (P@5: 13.28% vs. 9.12%) and on all three music quality metrics, reflecting Riffusion's lack of video conditioning. On the SymMV benchmark, Diff-BGM outperforms V-MusProd [39] on retrieval precision (P@5: 19.0 vs. 15.7, P@20: 47.6 vs. 44.8) and average rank (19.4 vs. 25.4), though V-MusProd is slightly closer to real on PCHE (2.607 vs. 2.738; real = 2.538). Subjectively, Diff-BGM is preferred over CMT by 75–84% of participants across all quality and correspondence dimensions, and scores closest to human-created music (3.33 vs. 3.48 overall) among all automated methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Polyffusion diffusion framework for polyphonic score generation by introducing video conditioning and temporal alignment, adapting the latent diffusion cross-attention architecture from image synthesis to the music domain. It departs from the dominant transformer-based approaches for video BGM generation (CMT, V-MusProd) by arguing that diffusion's iterative denoising process naturally supports staged control—semantic features guide melody in early steps while dynamic features guide rhythm in later steps—offering better interpretability than end-to-end autoregressive generation. The segment-aware cross-attention with a short-term context mask is a novel architectural addition not present in either Polyffusion or prior video-music alignment work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BGM909 dataset with its comprehensive annotations (chords, beats, key signatures, shot detection, captions) and the proposed retrieval-precision and diversity metrics fill a gap in evaluation infrastructure for video-music generation that prior datasets (MAESTRO, POP909, SymMV) left unaddressed. However, the dataset is small (909 samples, piano-only), the generated music is limited to 8-bar segments, and the subjective scores (3.33/5) remain noticeably below human-created music (3.48/5), indicating substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the staged feature-selection strategy generalizes to longer compositions, richer instrumentation, or real-time generation, and whether the quality-correspondence trade-off observed in the ablation (SAC-Attn improves alignment but reduces music quality) can be resolved.
