## Problem

Published age estimation results are unreliable due to two persistent issues: (1) most datasets (especially MORPH) are evaluated with random splits that leak the same person across train and test sets, inflating performance, and (2) methods simultaneously alter multiple pipeline components, making it impossible to attribute gains to the claimed loss function or decision layer. The paper demonstrates that the apparent decade-long performance improvements are largely artifacts of these evaluation flaws rather than genuine methodological advances.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB facial image, detected and aligned via RetinaFace landmarks, cropped to complete head coverage, and resized to 256×256 pixels with ImageNet normalization. It outputs a continuous age estimate (regression), evaluated by Mean Absolute Error. The benchmark protocol requires subject-exclusive (identity-disjoint) splits at 60/20/20 (train/val/test) with matched age distributions, and mandates reporting both intra-dataset and cross-dataset generalization results.

## Method

The paper establishes a unified evaluation protocol with subject-exclusive splits across seven datasets and systematically ablates each pipeline component (backbone, loss function, facial alignment, coverage, resolution, input transform) while holding all others fixed. The key finding is that loss function and decision layer choices (OR-CNN, DLDL, SORD, Mean-Variance, Unimodal) produce negligible differences compared to backbone architecture, pretraining data volume, or preprocessing choices. Leveraging this insight, the authors propose a simple baseline: a frozen FaRL ViT-B-16 backbone (pretrained on 50M facial images from LAION via contrastive and masked-patch objectives) with a 2-layer MLP head (512 neurons, ReLU) trained with standard cross-entropy, which achieves statistically significant improvements over all specialized loss functions on most datasets.

## Results

With IMDB-WIKI pre-training on ResNet-50, all specialized loss functions (OR-CNN, DLDL, DLDL-v2, SORD, Mean-Variance, Unimodal) yield intra-dataset MAE values within ~0.1–0.3 of the cross-entropy baseline across all seven datasets (e.g., on CLAP2016: CE=4.49, OR-CNN=4.13, DLDL=4.18, Mean-Var=4.28), and the Friedman–Nemenyi test confirms no statistically significant difference. The proposed FaRL+MLP backbone achieves the best intra-dataset MAE on AgeDB (5.64), AFAD (3.12), CLAP2016 (3.38), and UTKFace, matching cross-entropy on CACD2000 (3.96) but performing worse on MORPH. Cross-dataset MAE degrades substantially for all methods (e.g., CLAP2016→AgeDB: 7.35 for CE vs. 5.81 intra-dataset), and no loss function shows a significant generalization advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central finding is that, under a controlled protocol with IMDB-WIKI pre-training, none of the six specialized loss functions—OR-CNN [21], DLDL [12], DLDL-v2 [13], SORD [10], Mean-Variance [22], or Unimodal [17]—achieves a statistically significant improvement over the plain cross-entropy baseline (Friedman–Nemenyi test, p=0.05). With random initialization on small datasets, OR-CNN, DLDL, and Mean-Variance show marginal gains (~0.1–0.3 MAE) attributable to implicit regularization, but these vanish with pre-training. The proposed FaRL+MLP backbone (ViT-B-16 frozen features + 2-layer MLP, cross-entropy loss) significantly outperforms all ResNet-50 variants on CLAP2016 (3.38 vs. 4.13–4.49), AgeDB (5.64 vs. 5.78–5.90), and UTKFace, while matching cross-entropy on CACD2000 (3.96) and underperforming on MORPH due to distribution mismatch with its LAION pre-training data. Cross-dataset, no method demonstrates a meaningful generalization advantage, confirming that the loss function choice is negligible compared to pipeline factors like pre-training data volume, input resolution, and facial coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not introduce a new age estimation method but instead critically re-examines the evaluation practices underlying the entire age estimation literature. It re-implements and re-benchmarks the major loss-function-based approaches (OR-CNN, DLDL, SORD, Mean-Variance, Unimodal) under a unified protocol with subject-exclusive splits, departing from the random-splitting convention that dominates prior publications. The paper also leverages FaRL, a large-scale facial representation model pretrained on 50 million image-text pairs, as a frozen backbone with a simple MLP head, representing a shift away from the ResNet/VGG backbones and task-specific losses that have defined the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that specialized ordinal loss functions contribute negligibly to performance compared to pretraining data volume, facial alignment, coverage, and resolution fundamentally reframes where the field should direct its research effort. The public code, exact data splits, and proposed evaluation protocol address a reproducibility gap that has made prior results difficult to compare. Open questions include whether the FaRL backbone's underperformance on MORPH (attributed to distribution mismatch with LAION) can be resolved through fine-tuning, whether the evaluation protocol generalizes to other ordinal regression tasks beyond age, and whether the community will adopt subject-exclusive splitting as the default standard.
