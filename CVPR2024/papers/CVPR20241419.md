## Problem

Despite strong QA accuracy, current vision-language models for VideoQA may not genuinely ground their predictions in relevant video content, instead relying on language shortcuts or spurious correlations with irrelevant visual context. No prior benchmark or methodology existed to systematically evaluate whether VideoQA answers are visually substantiated, making it impossible to assess the trustworthiness of these models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241419/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives 32 uniformly sampled frames from a ~40-second video, a text question, and a set of candidate answers (multi-choice format). It produces both a predicted answer and a temporal grounding segment expressed as a Gaussian confidence interval (μ ± γσ) over the video duration. The NExT-GQA benchmark evaluates grounding via intersection-over-prediction (IoP), which checks whether the predicted window falls inside the ground-truth segment rather than requiring exact boundary matching, reflecting that any sufficient visual evidence should count as valid grounding.

## Method

The paper proposes a model-agnostic temporal grounding mechanism that learns differentiable Gaussian masks (parameterized by mean μ and standard deviation σ) over the video's temporal axis, integrated into existing dual- or stacked-style transformer VLMs. The base NG formulation jointly optimizes the QA loss and a Gaussian-weighted token aggregation that localizes the relevant moment. The NG+ variant adds a cross-modal self-supervision objective: the grounded video segment is pulled toward the question embedding and pushed away from hard negatives (other questions from the same video) and easy negatives (questions from other videos), with 10% of positives augmented via GPT-4 rephrasing. This contrastive grounding term is combined with the QA term via a trade-off parameter α, enforcing that the localized content is relevant to both the question and the answer. The approach requires no temporal labels during training and is applied to backbones including Temp[CLIP] and FrozenBiLM, achieving 73.1% QA accuracy on NExT-QA while substantially improving grounding metrics.

## Results

On the NExT-GQA test set, the proposed NG+ method applied to FrozenBiLM achieves 70.8% QA accuracy (73.1% on the full NExT-QA test set) and 17.5% grounded QA accuracy (Acc@GQA), while the post-hoc baseline for the same backbone yields 69.1% QA and 15.8% GQA. For temporal grounding, NG+ FrozenBiLM reaches 9.6 mIoU and 6.1 IoU@0.5, compared to 7.1 mIoU and 4.4 IoU@0.5 for the post-hoc baseline. In contrast, human annotators achieve 93.3% QA accuracy with 82.1% grounded QA accuracy and 61.2 mIoU, revealing a large gap between current VLMs and human-level visual grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed NG+ method outperforms all compared baselines on grounded QA accuracy: it beats IGV (10.2% → 17.5% Acc@GQA for FrozenBiLM) and SeViLA (16.6% → 17.5%) while also achieving higher QA accuracy (70.8% vs. 50.1% and 68.1% respectively). Against VGT (50.9% QA, 12.7% GQA) and VIOLETv2 (52.9% QA, 12.8% GQA), NG+ FrozenBiLM leads by a wide margin on both metrics. Notably, SeViLA achieves the best standalone grounding among all baselines (29.5 mIoP, 21.7 mIoU), surpassing NG+ FrozenBiLM (24.2 mIoP, 9.6 mIoU), which the authors attribute to SeViLA's pretraining on localization supervision. However, SeViLA's grounding does not translate into proportionally better grounded QA, highlighting the importance of joint QA-grounding optimization. The NG+ Temp[CLIP] variant shows the largest grounding improvement over its post-hoc counterpart (6.6 → 12.1 mIoU, +5.5), demonstrating that the cross-modal self-supervision is particularly effective for dual-style architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NExT-QA benchmark with temporal grounding labels to create NExT-GQA, and adapts end-to-end Gaussian mask learning (from prior weakly-supervised video grounding work by Zheng et al.) into large VLMs for the first time. It departs from fully-supervised grounded VQA benchmarks (TVQA, VidSTG) by operating under weak supervision, and from prior VideoQA methods (VGT, VIOLETv2, FrozenBiLM, SeViLA) that optimize QA accuracy without evaluating whether predictions are visually grounded. The cross-modal self-supervision objective (NG+) is a novel addition that regularizes the grounding module via question-video contrastive learning, going beyond the answer-only supervision used in the naive Gaussian approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that SoTA VLMs achieve ~69% QA accuracy but only ~16% grounded QA accuracy, while humans reach 82% grounding—challenges the field's implicit assumption that high QA scores reflect genuine visual understanding. The NExT-GQA benchmark (10.5K temporal labels) provides a much-needed diagnostic tool for evaluating whether VLMs are truly grounded or exploiting language shortcuts. Open questions include how to close the large gap with human grounding performance, whether the findings generalize beyond multi-choice QA, and how to integrate grounding supervision into pretraining rather than post-hoc fine-tuning. The NG+ method, while effective, demands additional memory and training time, limiting its immediate adoption in resource-constrained settings.
