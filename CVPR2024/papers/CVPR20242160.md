## Problem

Extending representation learning to rare modalities (3D point cloud, depth, audio, tactile, EEG) is hindered by the scarcity of large-scale paired data, making it infeasible to train modality-specific encoders from scratch with the same data budgets that made vision-language models successful. While pretrained ViTs encode rich visual knowledge with strong generalization, no efficient mechanism exists to repurpose their frozen parameters for processing fundamentally different input signals without extensive retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw modality signals in diverse formats: 3D point clouds (8,192–10,000 points grouped via FPS+KNN), depth maps (in-filled and converted to disparity), 5-second audio clips (128-dim log Mel filterbank features at 10ms hop), GelSight RGB tactile frames, and 128-channel EEG sequences (5–95 Hz). These are tokenized into token embeddings, passed through the Lens and frozen ViT, and produce a single normalized feature vector in CLIP's shared embedding space. When plugged into an MFM, the same pipeline additionally yields text captions, QA answers, or generated images from any input modality without instruction tuning.

## Method

VIT-LENS introduces a lightweight modality-specific adapter (the Lens) that bridges the input-space gap between raw non-visual signals and a frozen pretrained ViT, treating the ViT as a universal multi-modal processor. Two Lens variants are provided: S-Attn (stacked self-attention blocks, optionally initialized from pretrained ViT weights) for image-like inputs such as depth maps, and Iter-CS-Attn (iterative cross-self-attention inspired by Perceiver) for long or structurally dissimilar inputs like point clouds, audio spectrograms, and EEG, which also reduces ViT input length. Training uses multimodal contrastive learning (symmetric InfoNCE) to align the ViT-encoded modality features with anchor features (image and/or text) extracted by a frozen foundation model (OpenCLIP), updating only the ModEmbed and Lens parameters while keeping the ViT frozen. A key practical advantage is that when the frozen ViT is the same encoder used inside an MFM (e.g., EVA01-g14 in InstructBLIP or SEED), the trained Lens plugs in directly, transferring the MFM's instruction-following and generation capabilities to any new modality with zero additional training.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D classification, VIT-LENS-G (trained on OpenShape-Triplets excluding LVIS) achieves 50.1% top-1 accuracy on Objaverse-LVIS, surpassing the prior SOTA (OpenShape-PointBERT at 39.1%) by 11.0 points, and 86.8% on ModelNet40. On depth-only scene classification, VIT-LENS-L with image+text anchors reaches 68.5% on NYU-v2 and 52.2% on SUN RGB-D, outperforming ImageBind-H (54.0% and 35.1%) by 14.5 and 17.1 points respectively. For audio, VIT-LENS-L (I+T) attains 26.7 mAP on AudioSet and 75.9% on ESC-50, exceeding AudioCLIP (25.9 mAP, 69.4%). On EEG visual concept classification, VIT-LENS-L (I+T) scores 42.7% test accuracy versus 19.2% for DreamDiffusion-L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIT-LENS consistently outperforms all 3D baselines: on Objaverse-LVIS (No LVIS), it beats OpenShape-PointBERT by 11.0 points (50.1% vs 39.1%) and ULIP-PointBERT by 28.7 points; on ScanObjectNN it surpasses OpenShape-SparseConv by 4.9 points (59.8% vs 54.9%). For audio, VIT-LENS-L (I+T) exceeds AudioCLIP on AudioSet mAP (26.7 vs 25.9) and ESC-50 (75.9% vs 69.4%), and outperforms ImageBind-H on VGGSound (31.7% vs 27.8%) and Clotho R@1 (8.1 vs 6.0). On depth classification, VIT-LENS-L (I+T) dominates ImageBind-H by 14.5 points on NYU-v2 (68.5% vs 54.0%) and 17.1 points on SUN-D (52.2% vs 35.1%), narrowing the gap to the supervised SOTA (Omnivore) to 8.2 and 12.7 points respectively. For tactile material classification, VIT-LENS-L (I+T) achieves 65.8% versus ImageBind-B's 24.2%, a 41.6-point margin, and outperforms CMC linear probing (54.7%) by 11.1 points. On EEG, VIT-LENS-L (I+T) more than doubles DreamDiffusion-L's test accuracy (42.7% vs 19.2%). In video retrieval (MSRVTT), VIT-LENS-L slightly edges out ImageBind-H (R@1: 37.6 vs 36.8) but falls short of the zero-shot SOTA VAST (R@1: 49.3), which uses video data in pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIT-LENS extends the cross-modal contrastive alignment paradigm established by CLIP and ImageBind, but departs fundamentally in its architectural strategy: rather than training modality-specific encoders jointly (ImageBind) or from scratch (ONE-PEACE), it repurposes a frozen pretrained ViT as a universal feature processor and trains only lightweight modality-specific 'Lens' adapters (self-attention or Perceiver-style cross-attention blocks). This positions the work at the intersection of transfer learning and multimodal representation learning, drawing on the Perceiver architecture for variable-length input compression and on OpenCLIP's feature space as the alignment target. The plug-and-play integration with MFMs (InstructBLIP, SEED) without instruction tuning extends the line of work on modality-agnostic LLM interfaces (e.g., PandaGPT, ImageBind-LLM) by eliminating the need for domain-specific tuning data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical recipe for extending foundation models to data-scarce modalities (tactile, EEG, 3D) without collecting large-scale paired data or retraining the backbone, which is directly relevant to embodied AI and assistive technology. The plug-and-play MFM integration showing emergent any-modality-to-image generation and captioning without instruction tuning is a compelling demonstration of compositional capability transfer. However, the authors acknowledge that VIT-LENS inherits biases from the pretrained ViT and is not ready for real-world deployment; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to modalities with no natural image or text anchor, and whether the frozen-ViT assumption holds as modality diversity grows beyond the five tested.
