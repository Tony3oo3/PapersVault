## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) methods require expensive GPS-tagged ground-satellite image pairs for supervised training, making them impractical for large-scale deployment. Without such labels, the model faces a cold-start problem: it has no optimization direction to align the significant imaging and spatial gaps between ground and satellite views. Moreover, standard unsupervised image retrieval techniques (clustering, class-level alignment) are inapplicable because CVGL is a fine-grained, non-class, one-to-one spatial correspondence task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241800/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 360° ground panorama image as query and a database of GPS-tagged satellite images as references, producing the most similar satellite image (and thus geographic location) via cosine similarity in a learned embedding space. Ground panoramas are first geometrically projected to bird's-eye-view (BEV) images using a spherical transform, then converted to fake satellite-style images via CycleGAN to serve as proxy positives during cold-start. Encoders are ConvNeXt-Small (224×224) or ConvNeXt-Base (384×384) with a SAFA spatial attention module, trained with a learnable-temperature Symmetric InfoNCE loss augmented by label smoothing to handle noisy pseudo-labels.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In the cold-start stage, a Correspondence-Free Projection (CFP) bridges the cross-view gap without any ground-truth pairs: a geometric spherical transform maps ground panorama pixels to BEV coordinates, and a CycleGAN trained on unpaired BEV/satellite images fills occluded regions and matches satellite imaging style, yielding fake satellite images aligned one-to-one with ground panoramas. Self-supervised contrastive learning then trains two separate encoders with intra-view instance discrimination and cross-view ground-fake alignment objectives. In the semi-supervised stage, an Adaptive-Mutual-Matching (AMM) mechanism refines pseudo-labels by requiring mutual top-1 retrieval between ground and satellite images, filtering pairs where the top-1/top-2 similarity gap exceeds a threshold, and progressively lowering that threshold (curriculum learning) to introduce harder samples as the model improves, ultimately achieving performance comparable to fully supervised methods.

## Results

In the fully unsupervised setting (0% ground-truth labels), Ours-base achieves R@1 of 92.56 on CVUSA, 84.58 on CVACT Val, and 60.53 on CVACT Test, approaching the performance of fully supervised methods like TransGeo (94.08 / 84.95) and GeoDTR (93.76 / 85.43). With only 10% labeled data (semi-supervised), Ours-base* reaches R@1 of 94.88 on CVUSA, 87.89 on CVACT Val, and 65.30 on CVACT Test, surpassing the fully supervised Sample4Geo (97.83 / 87.49 / 60.57) on CVACT Val and CVACT Test. On the large-scale CVACT Test set (92,802 pairs), the unsupervised model nearly matches Sample4Geo (60.53 vs. 60.57 R@1) without any labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The unsupervised Ours-base (0% GT) outperforms all baselines except the three most recent supervised methods (Sample4Geo, TransGeo, GeoDTR) on CVUSA and CVACT Val, winning by 1.5–1.4 R@1 over L2LTR (92.56 vs. 91.99) and DSM (92.56 vs. 91.96), and by 6.4 R@1 over SAFA (92.56 vs. 81.15). On CVACT Test, the unsupervised model nearly ties Sample4Geo (60.53 vs. 60.57 R@1) and beats L2LTR (60.53 vs. 58.33) without any labels. In the semi-supervised setting with 10% labels, Ours-base* surpasses the fully supervised Sample4Geo on CVACT Val (87.89 vs. 87.49 R@1) and by a larger margin on CVACT Test (65.30 vs. 60.57 R@1), demonstrating that unlabeled data can boost even state-of-the-art supervised methods. The gap to Sample4Geo on CVUSA remains the largest (94.88 vs. 97.83 R@1 with 10% labels), suggesting CVUSA's more uniform scene distribution is harder to exploit with limited supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire supervised CVGL literature (Sample4Geo, TransGeo, SAFA, etc.) by eliminating the need for ground-truth cross-view correspondences and GPS labels. It repurposes geometric projection (from Shi et al.) and CycleGAN to synthesize 'fake satellite' images as a cold-start mechanism, and introduces Adaptive-Mutual-Matching to exploit the non-class, fine-grained spatial-correspondence structure of CVGL—something that distinguishes it from standard unsupervised image retrieval methods that rely on class-level or instance-level clustering. The spatial attention module SAFA and ConvNeXt encoders are carried over from supervised CVGL, but the training paradigm shifts entirely to self-supervised contrastive learning followed by curriculum-based semi-supervised refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to operate CVGL without any human-annotated pairs, directly addressing the high cost of collecting GPS-tagged ground-satellite correspondences that limits real-world deployment. The semi-supervised results (e.g., 68.29 R@1 with only 1% labels on CVACT) suggest that a small seed of annotations plus unlabeled data can approach full supervision, which has practical value for scaling CVGL to new or changing scenes. However, the method still relies on localization and orientation alignment assumptions, and the homography-based projection struggles with complex 3D city scenes (VIGOR), leaving open the question of how to handle arbitrary viewpoint changes without depth or 3D information.
