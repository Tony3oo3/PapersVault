## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-quality real-time rendering on small scenes but fails to scale to large environments due to video memory limits (a 32 GB GPU can only hold ~11M Gaussians while a single small scene needs ~5.8M), prohibitively long optimization times, and appearance variations that cause 3DGS to produce large low-opacity floaters. Existing NeRF-based large-scene methods (Mega-NeRF, Switch-NeRF, Grid-NeRF) address scalability but still produce blurry results and render at sub-real-time speeds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse SfM point cloud and training images with estimated camera poses (downsampled 4× for training) as input, and produces a merged 3D Gaussian scene representation enabling real-time 1080p rendering. The scene is partitioned into a grid of cells (e.g., 8 cells in main experiments), each optimized independently on a separate GPU, then merged into a single non-overlapping scene. The decoupled appearance modeling CNN and per-view appearance embeddings are discarded after training, so inference uses only the standard 3DGS rasterization pipeline.

## Method

VastGaussian introduces a progressive data partitioning strategy that first divides the scene into cells by camera position on the ground plane, then progressively enriches each cell with additional cameras selected via an airspace-aware visibility criterion (projecting the cell's bounding box rather than just surface points) and additional SfM points covered by those cameras, ensuring sufficient supervision to suppress floaters and eliminate boundary artifacts. For appearance variation, a decoupled appearance modeling module concatenates a per-view appearance embedding with a 32× downsampled rendered image, feeds the result through a CNN to produce a pixel-wise transformation map, and applies it to the full-resolution rendering; the D-SSIM loss is computed on the raw rendering to enforce structural consistency while the L1 loss is computed on the appearance-adjusted image to absorb photometric differences. Each cell is optimized for 60,000 iterations with standard 3DGS adaptive densification, and after optimization, Gaussians outside the original (unexpanded) cell boundary are removed before merging all cells into a seamless scene whose total Gaussian count can far exceed what a single-GPU whole-scene optimization would allow.

## Results

On five large-scale scenes (Building, Rubble, Campus, Residence, Sci-Art from Mill-19 and UrbanScene3D), VastGaussian achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS across all scenes, with SSIM ranging from 0.804 to 0.885 and LPIPS from 0.121 to 0.151. On PSNR, VastGaussian achieves 23.50–26.92 dB, outperforming or matching all baselines. In terms of efficiency, VastGaussian trains in 2h25m–2h56m (vs. 17h–42h for NeRF methods and ~20h for Modified 3DGS) using only 10.4–11.9 GB VRAM per GPU, while rendering at 126–172 FPS at 1080p resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VastGaussian outperforms all four baselines on SSIM and LPIPS across every scene where data is available. Against the strongest NeRF-based baseline (Switch-NeRF), VastGaussian gains 0.21–0.24 in SSIM and reduces LPIPS by 0.10–0.36, while also improving PSNR by 1.4–2.1 dB. Compared to Modified 3DGS (the closest competitor), VastGaussian improves SSIM by 0.03–0.10 and LPIPS by 0.02–0.14, with a PSNR gain of 0.5–2.1 dB; the gap is largest on Campus (SSIM 0.816 vs. 0.712) where Modified 3DGS suffers from floaters. Grid-NeRF (grid branch) is competitive on Rubble and Residence but is unavailable for Building and Sci-Art, and its results are copied from the original paper due to non-reproducibility. In efficiency, VastGaussian trains ~8× faster than Modified 3DGS (2.5h vs. 20h) with ~3× less VRAM (11.9 GB vs. 31.2 GB), while rendering at comparable real-time speeds (126–172 FPS vs. 178–211 FPS) and ~500× faster than NeRF-based methods (0.12–0.31 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from small, object-centric scenes to large-scale environments, a setting previously dominated by NeRF-based methods (Mega-NeRF, Switch-NeRF, Grid-NeRF, Block-NeRF) that suffer from slow rendering and limited detail. The progressive data partitioning strategy departs from the grid-based or mixture-of-experts decompositions used by those NeRF methods by introducing an airspace-aware visibility criterion for camera selection, ensuring sufficient supervision to prevent floaters. The decoupled appearance modeling is a key departure from NeRF-W-style appearance embeddings fed into radiance MLPs during ray-marching; instead, a lightweight CNN generates pixel-wise transformation maps on the rasterized image, making the module discardable after training and preserving 3DGS's real-time rendering speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VastGaussian is the first method to achieve both high-fidelity reconstruction and real-time rendering on large scenes using explicit 3D representations, opening the door to practical applications in autonomous driving, aerial surveying, and VR that previously required either slow NeRF rendering or low-quality mesh-based approaches. The divide-and-conquer framework offers practical scalability benefits: parallel optimization across GPUs, the ability to expand scenes incrementally, and the capacity to fine-tune specific regions without full retraining. Open questions remain around optimal cell division strategies (the paper acknowledges no principled solution exists), storage and rendering speed degradation as scene size grows, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or extremely large (city-scale) scenes.
