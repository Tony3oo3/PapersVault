## Problem

Few-shot 3D keypoint detection requires both semantic understanding (to identify meaningful points) and precise geometric localization, yet existing methods are limited by either hand-crafted descriptors that lack semantic awareness or learned 3D models constrained by scarce 3D training data. No prior work has demonstrated that features from large pre-trained 2D vision foundation models can be back-projected onto 3D surfaces to provide the localized, semantically rich descriptors needed for accurate keypoint detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target 3D mesh and a small set of same-class shapes (typically 3) with annotated keypoints as input. The target mesh is rendered from 62 viewpoints, processed by a frozen 2D encoder, and features are back-projected onto the surface; 2048 candidate keypoint locations are obtained via farthest-point sampling. The output is a set of k keypoint locations (5–23 in KeypointNet) on the target mesh, extracted as argmax over an optimized selection matrix, with pairwise geodesic distances normalized to [0,1] for scale invariance.

## Method

B2-3D renders the 3D shape from 62 viewpoints, extracts per-patch features using a frozen DINO ViT-G encoder, and back-projects them onto the mesh surface using known camera intrinsics and extrinsics. A Gaussian geodesic reweighting step smooths the back-projected features along the surface to handle noisy visibility information, and features from all views are aggregated by averaging. For keypoint detection, the method formulates a joint optimization over a right-stochastic selection matrix that simultaneously minimizes feature mismatch (‖ŜᵀF_cand − F_kp‖₂) and pairwise geodesic distance distribution mismatch (‖ŜᵀD_candŜ − D_kp‖₂), preventing symmetry collapse by enforcing that detected keypoints match the global spatial distribution observed in the few-shot examples. The approach is learning-free and descriptor-agnostic, and the paper shows that DINO features substantially outperform CLIP, SAM, EfficientNet, and traditional HKS/WKS/SHOT descriptors in this setting.

## Results

On the KeypointNet dataset (airplane, chair, table classes), B2-3D achieves a mean relative IoU improvement of 93% over the previous state-of-the-art FSKD across all evaluation distance thresholds, with over 200% improvement at the tightest threshold of 0.02. B2-3D matches FSKD's IoU at a threshold of 0.1 already at a threshold of approximately 0.045, indicating substantially better localization accuracy. For part segmentation transfer on ShapeNet Part, back-projected DINO features with nearest-neighbor classification achieve 71.0% mean IoU, improving by nearly 2% over the previous SOTA NCP (69.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

B2-3D decisively outperforms all seven few-shot keypoint detection baselines (SIFT-3D, HARRIS-3D, ISS, D3Feat, USIP, UKPGAN, and FSKD) on KeypointNet across all distance thresholds, with the largest gap against the previous SOTA FSKD (93% mean relative IoU improvement). Among traditional geometry-based descriptors (HKS, WKS, SHOT), even the best (HKS) only matches simple farthest-point sampling, confirming that hand-crafted descriptors lack the semantic awareness needed for keypoint detection. When the same optimization module is paired with back-projected features from other 2D models, DINO features clearly outperform CLIP, SAM, and EfficientNet, though CLIP and EfficientNet features still beat FSKD. Notably, the paper observes that the smaller EfficientNet CNN outperforms the larger CLIP and SAM models, suggesting that task-specific properties of those models (joint image-text alignment, segmentation decoder) are not advantageous for this setting. For part segmentation transfer, B2-3D's DINO features with nearest-neighbor classification surpass NCP by ~2% IoU, validating the generalizability of the back-projected features beyond keypoint detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work transferring 2D foundation model knowledge to 3D shape analysis, which has previously focused on global tasks (shape classification via CLIP/PointCLIP) or mid-level tasks (part segmentation via SATR). The closest prior work, Morreale et al. [27], also uses pretrained 2D feature extractors for 3D shape matching, but processes features in the 2D domain before projecting correspondences back; B2-3D instead back-projects and aggregates raw features directly onto the 3D surface, making the approach more versatile for downstream tasks. For keypoint detection specifically, the paper departs from prior methods that rely on 3D-specific learned descriptors (FSKD, UKPGAN) or hand-crafted geometric signatures (HKS, WKS, SHOT) by showing that learning-free 2D encoder features, combined with a distribution-matching optimization, suffice for state-of-the-art performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The back-projection technique provides a general, training-free way to obtain rich semantic and geometric 3D features from any pre-trained 2D vision model, potentially applicable to a wide range of shape analysis tasks beyond keypoint detection (validated here with part segmentation transfer). Key open questions remain: how to handle non-existent keypoints on new shapes, detect unseen keypoint classes, and scale to more complex or textured real-world geometry. The dependence on multi-view rendering also introduces a computational overhead compared to pure 3D methods, and the evaluation is limited to three ShapeNet categories, leaving generalization to broader shape diversity untested.
