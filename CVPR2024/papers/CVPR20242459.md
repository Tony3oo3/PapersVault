## Problem

Existing probabilistic 3D human mesh estimation methods generate multiple hypotheses but lack a principled mechanism to select the highest-quality estimate from them, relying instead on ground-truth-dependent selection or simple averaging. This limits their practical utility in real-world scenarios where no ground truth is available and subtle quality differences between plausible poses must be discriminated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242459/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (cropped to 256×256 via GT bounding box) and produces M diverse 3D mesh hypotheses represented as SMPL swing (29 joint positions) and twist (23 limb rotations) parameters, generated in 4 DDIM steps. ScoreNet then assigns a scalar quality score to each hypothesis and aggregates the top-K (K=5) by averaging to yield the final mesh estimate. The framework also accepts hypotheses from external methods (ProHMR, HuManiFlow) as plug-and-play input to ScoreNet.

## Method

ScoreHypo comprises two components sharing a common architecture: HypoNet formulates mesh estimation as a reverse DDIM denoising process where a Transformer-based noise estimator (HypoFormer) is conditioned on multi-scale image features—low-resolution global features via cross-attention and high-resolution pixel-aligned local features sampled at predicted 2D joint positions—to generate diverse, image-consistent hypotheses. ScoreNet reuses the same backbone but replaces the noise-prediction head with a scalar scorer, trained via a differentiable pairwise probabilistic ranking loss that models the relative quality of hypothesis pairs using cross-entropy against ground-truth MPVE/MPJPE orderings. The key design insight is that ScoreNet learns a generalizable quality signal from the subtle differences between plausible hypotheses, enabling it to improve external methods' outputs by over 15% without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On H3.6M, ScoreHypo with M=100 hypotheses achieves MPJPE 38.4 mm and PA-MPJPE 26.0 mm, outperforming the best deterministic method DeFormer (MPJPE 44.8, PA-MPJPE 31.6) and the best probabilistic method HMDiff (MPJPE 49.3, PA-MPJPE 32.4). On 3DPW, ScoreHypo (M=100) reaches MPVPE 73.4 mm, MPJPE 63.0 mm, and PA-MPJPE 37.6 mm, surpassing the strongest deterministic baseline Zolly (MPVPE 76.3, MPJPE 65.0, PA-MPJPE 39.8) and the best probabilistic method HuManiFlow (MPJPE 65.1, PA-MPJPE 39.9). On the occlusion-challenging 3DPW-OC subset, ScoreHypo achieves MPJPE 73.9 mm and PA-MPJPE 48.7 mm, beating the prior best JOTR (MPJPE 75.7, PA-MPJPE 52.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScoreHypo consistently outperforms all 26 baselines across both H3.6M and 3DPW. Against the strongest deterministic methods, it reduces MPJPE by 6.4 mm over DeFormer (38.4 vs. 44.8) and by 2.0 mm over Zolly on 3DPW (63.0 vs. 65.0) at M=100. Among probabilistic competitors, the advantage is even larger: ScoreHypo (M=10) already beats HMDiff (M=25) by 6.9 mm in MPJPE on H3.6M (42.4 vs. 49.3) and HuManiFlow (M=100) by 2.1 mm in MPJPE on 3DPW (68.5 vs. 65.1 at M=10, but 63.0 vs. 65.1 at M=100). The scalability is notable—increasing M from 10 to 200 further reduces H3.6M MPJPE from 42.4 to 37.4 mm, a 5 mm gain that no baseline matches. On the occlusion subset 3DPW-OC, ScoreHypo achieves the best MPJPE (73.9) and PA-MPJPE (48.7), edging out JOTR (75.7, 52.2) by 1.8 and 3.5 mm respectively. Additionally, the plug-and-play ScoreNet module improves ProHMR and HuManiFlow by 13.7% and 17.2% in MPVE without any retraining, demonstrating cross-method generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScoreHypo sits at the intersection of diffusion-based generation and multi-hypothesis 3D human mesh estimation. It builds directly on DDPM/DDIM for the reverse denoising formulation and on HybrIK's swing/twist decomposition for the SMPL parameterization, while positioning itself against prior probabilistic estimators (ProHMR, HuManiFlow, HMDiff) that generate multiple meshes but rely on ad-hoc selection (min-GT-error or simple averaging). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a dedicated, learnable scoring module (ScoreNet) trained with a differentiable pairwise ranking loss, decoupling the 'generate diverse hypotheses' step from the 'select the best one' step within a unified architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ScoreNet's demonstrated cross-method generalizability: a scorer trained on HypoNet's hypotheses can be plugged into ProHMR or HuManiFlow without fine-tuning and reduce their MPVE by 13.7% and 17.2% respectively, suggesting that hypothesis quality assessment is a transferable sub-problem. This opens the door to a 'generate-then-score' paradigm for other probabilistic 3D reconstruction tasks. Open questions include whether the supervised ranking loss (which requires GT MPJPE/MPVE during training) can be replaced with self-supervised or physics-based scoring, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-person settings where temporal and inter-person consistency add further constraints.
