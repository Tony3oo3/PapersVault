## Problem

Featurizing millions of high-content screening (HCS) microscopy images for biological research remains challenging, and the current state-of-the-art weakly supervised learning (WSL) approaches do not scale: their performance degrades when trained on larger, less-curated datasets. This paper asks whether self-supervised masked autoencoders (MAEs) can serve as scalable learners of cellular biology, outperforming WSL as model and dataset sizes grow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241128/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume 256×256 random crops from 2048×2048×6 multi-channel Cell Painting fluorescence images (or brightfield variants), preprocessed with channel-wise self-standardization. The CA-MAE variant additionally accepts images with arbitrary numbers and orderings of channels at inference time by treating each channel as an independent modality. Outputs are fixed-dimensional embeddings (384/768/1024-dim for ViT-S/B/L) obtained by averaging patch embeddings from the final encoder layer, which are then aggregated per perturbation via spherical mean for downstream relationship prediction.

## Method

The paper trains MAEs with ViT (S/B/L) and U-Net backbones on five HCS datasets ranging from 125K to 93M images, introducing two key technical contributions. First, a Fourier-domain reconstruction loss (L1 on FFT magnitudes of masked patches, weighted at α=0.01) stabilizes training of large ViTs (e.g., ViT-L/8) by enabling the model to escape a saddle-point region in the loss landscape and achieve a 'double-descent' in reconstruction error. Second, a channel-agnostic MAE (CA-MAE) architecture applies a single shared linear projection and identical positional embeddings across all channels, creating C×N tokens with per-channel masking and separate decoders, so the trained encoder generalizes to unseen channel configurations at test time. The best model (ViT-L/8+ on RPI-93M) uses 8×8 patches, 75% mask ratio, Lion optimizer, batch sizes up to 16,384, and 128 A100 GPUs for over 20,000 GPU hours, achieving an 11.5% relative improvement over the best WSL model on hu.MAP relationship recall.

## Results

On RxRx3, the best MAE model (ViT-L/8+ trained on RPI-93M) achieves recall of .622/.443/.267/.484 on CORUM/hu.MAP/Reactome/StringDB (top and bottom 5% of cosine similarities), representing an 11.5% relative improvement over the best WSL model (ViT-L/16 on RxRx1-2M: .568/.397/.255/.472) on hu.MAP. Recall scales monotonically with training FLOPs, and the Fourier domain reconstruction loss (α=0.01) is critical for stabilizing large ViT-L/8 training. On JUMP-CP, the CA-MAE ViT-L/16+ achieves .95±.05 perturbation retrieval (vs. .53±.30 for CellProfiler), and the MAE ViT-L/8+ yields a minimum 20% relative improvement over CP-DiNO 1640 on StringDB recall at 5% FPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ImageNet-21k pretrained ViTs (Ridnik et al.), the MAE ViT-L/8+ on RPI-93M outperforms the best ImageNet model (ViT-L/16) by 17% on CORUM (.622 vs .531) and 23% on hu.MAP (.443 vs .360), confirming that domain-specific self-supervised pretraining substantially exceeds generic natural-image pretraining for HCS. Against the Sypetkowski et al. WSL baseline (DenseNet-161 w/ AdaBN on RxRx1), the MAE achieves 24% higher CORUM recall (.622 vs .502) and 22% higher hu.MAP recall (.443 vs .363); even the best WSL variant (ViT-L/16 on RxRx1-2M) trails the MAE by 9% on CORUM and 11.5% on hu.MAP. On JUMP-CP perturbation retrieval, the CA-MAE ViT-L/16+ (.95±.05) and MAE ViT-L/8+ (.78±.13) both substantially outperform CellProfiler (.53±.30), though the MAE models underperform CellProfiler on sibling retrieval (.02–.03 vs .13), suggesting MAE embeddings are overly discriminative for closely related perturbations. Against the external CP-DiNO 1640 platform (Sivanandan et al.), the MAE ViT-L/8+ achieves a minimum 20% relative improvement in StringDB recall at 5% FPR across three gene sets (.80/.23/.17 vs .53/.12/.14), though the authors note direct comparison is limited by differences in assay technology and cell lines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAE framework (He et al., CVPR 2022) to high-content screening microscopy, building on the MultiMAE multi-modal architecture (Bachmann et al., ECCV 2022) to propose a channel-agnostic variant that treats each fluorescence channel as a separate modality. It departs from the dominant weakly supervised learning paradigm for HCS representation learning (Sypetkowski et al., Caicedo et al.) by demonstrating that self-supervised masked reconstruction scales more favorably with dataset and model size. The Fourier-domain reconstruction loss draws on masked frequency modeling (Xie et al., ICLR 2022) but is repurposed here as a training-stability mechanism for large ViT backbones rather than a primary learning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that self-supervised MAEs are the scalable path forward for HCS representation learning, with recall of known biological relationships improving monotonically with FLOPs while WSL performance plateaus or degrades on larger, less-curated datasets. The channel-agnostic architecture addresses a critical practical barrier—cross-experiment transfer when channel configurations differ—and the JUMP-CP transfer experiment validates this, though the weaker sibling-retrieval performance highlights the need for task-specific fine-tuning strategies. Open questions include handling the large token sequences of small-patch ViTs (e.g., 6,144 tokens for ViT-L/8 on 6-channel images), developing alignment methods for application-specific objectives, and establishing community-wide benchmarks for HCS model evaluation.
