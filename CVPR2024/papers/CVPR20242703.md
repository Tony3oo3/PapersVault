## Problem

Existing BEV-based multimodal fusion approaches perform fusion only at the entire scene level, ignoring the inherent imbalance between small foreground instances and large background regions in the BEV plane. This makes it difficult to capture local context around object instances, and prior instance-level methods fail to exploit the collaboration between instance and scene features. The paper asks how to simultaneously formulate instance-level and scene-level multimodal context and elegantly integrate them for 3D object detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242703/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud (covering [-54m, 54m] in X/Y, [-5m, 3m] in Z, voxelized at 0.075m×0.075m×0.2m) and six synchronized multi-view RGB images at 384×1056 resolution as input. It produces 3D bounding boxes via a transformer decoder operating on a 180×180 BEV feature map, decoding the top-200 proposals with Hungarian matching, Focal loss for classification, and L1 loss for box regression. The point cloud is pillarized at 0.6m×0.6m×8.0m for the Point-to-Grid transformer, and the image encoder (Swin-T) is pre-trained on nuImage before end-to-end fine-tuning.

## Method

IS-FUSION introduces two complementary modules. The Hierarchical Scene Fusion (HSF) module first applies a Point-to-Grid transformer that projects each pillar's points onto image features, applies multi-head self-attention across points within a pillar, and pools to a grid-level feature; it then applies a Grid-to-Region transformer that performs inter-grid self-attention within M×M regions followed by shifted-window self-attention across regions to capture long-range scene context. The Instance-Guided Fusion (IGF) module selects top-K=200 instance candidates via a centerness heatmap, applies inter-instance self-attention to model relationships, aggregates local multimodal context around each instance using deformable attention over D=16 sampled locations, and finally propagates instance information back to the scene through an Instance-to-Scene cross-attention where every BEV grid queries the enriched instance features. This bidirectional instance-scene collaboration yields an instance-aware BEV representation that outperforms scene-level-only fusion by 4.3% mAP over BEVFusion on nuScenes.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, IS-FUSION achieves 73.0% mAP and 75.2% NDS, outperforming all prior multimodal 3D detection methods. With test-time augmentation and model ensemble (IS-FUSION†), it reaches 76.5% mAP and 77.4% NDS, setting a new state of the art on the nuScenes leaderboard. On the validation set, IS-FUSION attains 72.8% mAP and 74.0% NDS at 3.2 FPS, surpassing CMT (70.3% mAP) and SparseFusion (71.0% mAP) by 2.5% and 1.8% respectively while maintaining comparable inference speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IS-FUSION outperforms all 19 compared baselines on the nuScenes test set. Among the strongest multimodal competitors, it surpasses CMT [74] and SparseFusion [71] (both at 72.0% mAP) by 1.0% mAP, FocalFormer3D [11] (71.6% mAP) by 1.4% mAP, and BEVFusion [46] (70.2% mAP) by 2.8% mAP. On the validation set (Table 2), the margin over CMT widens to 2.5% mAP (72.8 vs. 70.3) and over SparseFusion to 1.8% mAP (72.8 vs. 71.0), while maintaining a competitive 3.2 FPS inference speed. Notably, IS-FUSION achieves the highest per-category scores on motorcycle (82.4 vs. 80.3 for FocalFormer3D) and trailer (67.3 vs. 65.4 for CMT), which are the rarest classes in the dataset (1.08% and 2.13% of instances respectively), suggesting the instance-level fusion is particularly effective for underrepresented categories. The LiDAR-only baseline (TransFusion-L, 65.5% mAP) is improved by 7.5% mAP through the multimodal instance-scene collaborative fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

IS-FUSION extends the BEV-based multimodal fusion paradigm established by BEVFusion [46] and DeepInteraction [77], which align point cloud and image features on a shared bird's-eye-view plane but perform fusion only at the global scene level. The key departure is the explicit introduction of instance-level fusion: the Hierarchical Scene Fusion module (Point-to-Grid and Grid-to-Region transformers) captures multi-granularity scene context, while the Instance-Guided Fusion module mines top-K instance candidates (following CenterPoint [84]), aggregates local multimodal context via deformable attention [92], and propagates instance information back to the scene through cross-attention. The decoder and LiDAR-only baseline follow TransFusion [1], and the shifted-window attention in the Grid-to-Region transformer is borrowed from Swin Transformer [45].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete limitation of BEV-based detection: foreground objects occupy very few grid cells relative to background, making scene-level fusion struggle with local instance context. The instance-scene collaboration mechanism is a practical and modular addition that could be integrated into other BEV-based detectors, and the strong results (72.8% mAP on nuScenes validation, outperforming BEVFusion by 2.6%) validate the approach. Limitations include evaluation only on nuScenes, reliance on a pre-trained image encoder, and added computational overhead from multiple transformer attention layers. An open question is whether the instance-scene collaboration generalizes to other instance-centric tasks (e.g., 3D segmentation, tracking) or to datasets with denser object distributions.
