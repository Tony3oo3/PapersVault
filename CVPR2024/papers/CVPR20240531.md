## Problem

Existing text-to-image evaluation methods (e.g., PickScore, ImageReward, CLIP-Score) reduce image quality to a single scalar score, providing no spatial localization of artifacts or misalignment and no identification of which prompt words are misrepresented. This paper introduces the first dataset and model that produce fine-grained, interpretable, and region-attributable feedback for T2I generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240531/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a generated RGB image and its associated text prompt as input. It produces two spatial heatmaps (implausibility/artifact and text-image misalignment), four scalar scores (plausibility, alignment, aesthetics, overall) on a standardized [0,1] scale derived from 5-point Likert ratings, and a modified prompt sequence where misaligned keywords are flagged with a special '_0' suffix. The ground-truth heatmaps are constructed from point annotations where each point expands into a disk of radius 1/20 image height, and multiple annotator heatmaps are averaged.

## Method

The RAHF model is a multimodal transformer combining a ViT image encoder and T5X text encoder, with bidirectional self-attention over concatenated image and text tokens (inspired by Spotlight and PaLI). Three prediction heads operate on the fused tokens: heatmaps are produced by reshaping image tokens into feature maps passed through conv/deconv/sigmoid layers; scores use conv + linear + sigmoid; and the misaligned keyword sequence is decoded by a T5X decoder trained with teacher-forcing cross-entropy. The authors compare a multi-head variant (7 separate heads) against an augmented-prompt variant (3 heads with task-specific strings like 'implausibility heatmap' prepended to the prompt), finding that the latter creates task-adaptive feature maps and outperforms the former on several tasks. Training uses a weighted combination of pixel-wise MSE (heatmaps), MSE (scores), and cross-entropy (sequence), with pretraining on WebLI natural image captioning for broader visual diversity.

## Results

On the RichHF-18K test set, the RAHF model (augmented prompt variant) achieves PLCC of 0.693 for plausibility, 0.600 for aesthetics, 0.474 for text-image alignment, and 0.580 for overall score, substantially outperforming all baselines. For implausibility heatmap prediction, the augmented prompt variant achieves MSE of 0.00920 (all data) and CC of 0.556 (non-empty GT), while for misalignment heatmap it achieves MSE of 0.00304 and CC of 0.212 (non-empty GT). For misaligned keyword sequence prediction, the model achieves token-level F1 of 43.9% (augmented prompt) and 43.3% (multi-head).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The RAHF model (augmented prompt) outperforms ResNet-50 on all four score types, with the largest gap on text-image alignment (PLCC 0.474 vs. 0.108) and plausibility (0.693 vs. 0.495). Against the off-the-shelf PickScore, RAHF dominates across the board, e.g., plausibility PLCC 0.693 vs. 0.010, confirming that a single preference score cannot capture fine-grained quality dimensions. The fine-tuned CLIP baseline is the strongest competitor, reaching PLCC 0.390 on plausibility and 0.398 on alignment, yet RAHF still leads by 0.30 and 0.08 PLCC respectively. For misalignment heatmap prediction, RAHF (MSE 0.00304, CC 0.212 on non-empty GT) substantially outperforms the CLIP gradient baseline (MSE 0.00817, CC 0.015), indicating that learned multimodal features capture misalignment regions far better than raw CLIP gradients. The multi-head variant is slightly weaker than the augmented prompt variant on plausibility (0.666 vs. 0.693) and misalignment heatmap (MSE 0.00303 vs. 0.00304, CC 0.206 vs. 0.212), while the augmented prompt variant is marginally better on implausibility heatmap (MSE 0.00920 vs. 0.01216), confirming that task-specific prompt augmentation helps the shared backbone specialize per prediction target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF-for-T2I line of work (ImageReward, PickScore, HPS) that reduces image quality to a single scalar score or binary preference, by introducing spatially-localized feedback (implausibility and misalignment heatmaps) and keyword-level text misalignment alongside four fine-grained scores. Architecturally, it adapts the Spotlight/PaLI vision-language paradigm (ViT + T5X with cross-modal self-attention) to a multi-task prediction setting, and builds its dataset on Pick-a-Pic images. The closest prior work, Zhang et al. [61], collected only artifact regions for inpainting; this paper broadens the feedback to misalignment regions, misaligned keywords, and multi-dimensional scores simultaneously, positioning itself as the first heterogeneous rich-feedback formulation for T2I evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released RichHF-18K dataset and RAHF model provide the first automatically predictable, spatially-grounded evaluation signal for T2I generation, enabling practical downstream uses (region inpainting, data filtering for finetuning, classifier guidance) that single-score metrics cannot support. The demonstrated generalization from Stable Diffusion-trained feedback to the architecturally distinct Muse model suggests the learned feedback is capturing generator-agnostic quality signals. Key limitations include the relatively small dataset (18K), reliance on a single source (Pick-a-Pic/SD variants), and notably weaker misalignment heatmap performance due to annotation ambiguity. Open questions include scaling to diverse generators, using rich feedback as an RL reward signal, and leveraging predicted heatmaps as spatial weighting maps during training.
