## Problem

Existing image editing methods for diffusion-generated images either operate purely in 2D image space (cutting, pasting, gradient-domain edits) or require expensive fine-tuning on large 3D datasets to support 3D-aware manipulation. There is no training-free approach that enables true 3D object edits—translations, rotations, and perspective changes—while preserving object identity and producing plausible secondary effects like occlusions, shadows, and lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240684/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (real photograph or diffusion-generated), a corresponding text prompt, a user-specified 3D rigid transformation (translation, rotation, or scale) applied to a selected object, and a depth map (either ground-truth from synthetic scenes or estimated via a monocular depth estimator like Zoedepth with a 55° FOV assumption). It outputs a single edited image where the target object has been 3D-transformed with consistent perspective, occlusion, and lighting changes. The method operates on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion v2 depth-conditioned) and requires no fine-tuning or additional training data.

## Method

The key insight is to lift intermediate diffusion decoder activations (layers 2 and 3) to 3D using a proxy depth map, apply the user's 3D transformation to the object's lifted 3D points (segmented via SAM), and project them back to image space to construct a 3D-aware flow field. The input image is first inverted via Null-Text Inversion to recover initial noise and record activations; these activations are then warped by the 3D-aware flow to produce edited activations. The edited depth is constructed separately for the transformed object (from 3D point distances) and the static background (via object removal + re-estimation), composited with Poisson blending. During regeneration, the diffusion process is guided by two energy terms: a local L2 matching term for the object region and an average-matching term for the background, applied with a cyclic layer schedule and limited to the first 38 of 50 denoising steps to allow the model to reconcile lighting and shadows in the final steps.

## Results

On the synthetic Benchmark dataset (50 scenes), Diffusion Handles achieves the best edit adherence with 0.85 IoU between the edited foreground object and ground truth, substantially outperforming ObjectStitch (0.37), Zero123 (0.52), and 3DIT (0.15). For identity preservation (measured by cycle-consistency L1×10 and LPIPS), the method scores 0.71 and 0.19 respectively, beating Zero123 (1.05, 0.31) and ObjectStitch (0.89, 0.25), and matching or slightly improving on 3DIT (0.74, 0.27). A user study on 11 PhotoGen edits confirms that users prefer Diffusion Handles over all baselines on all three goals: identity preservation, edit adherence, and plausibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion Handles outperforms all three baselines on edit adherence, achieving 0.85 IoU versus 0.37 (ObjectStitch), 0.52 (Zero123), and 0.15 (3DIT) — a particularly large margin over 3DIT, which struggles with generalization due to its fine-tuned synthetic prior. On identity preservation, the method achieves the best LPIPS score (0.19) and the best L1 score (0.71), narrowly beating 3DIT (0.74 L1, 0.27 LPIPS) and clearly surpassing Zero123 (1.05 L1, 0.31 LPIPS) and ObjectStitch (0.89 L1, 0.25 LPIPS). In the user study, Diffusion Handles was preferred over every baseline on all three evaluated goals (identity, edit adherence, plausibility), with users even occasionally rating edited outputs as more plausible than the original input images. The key qualitative advantage is that Diffusion Handles requires no fine-tuning or 3D data, whereas 3DIT and Zero123 both depend on fine-tuned models trained on 3D datasets, and ObjectStitch cannot perform true 3D rotations or perspective changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-based guidance framework of Diffusion Self-Guidance (DSG) [13], which warps intermediate diffusion features in 2D to reposition or resize objects, by lifting those activations to 3D surfaces using a proxy depth map, applying a user-specified 3D rigid transform, and projecting back to the image plane. It also builds on Null-Text Inversion [31] to recover the initial noise and activations from an input image. The key departure from prior 3D-aware editing methods (3DIT [30], Zero123 [27]) is that no fine-tuning, 3D object datasets, or category-specific models are required; the diffusion model's own prior handles occlusion, lighting, and shadow synthesis implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free, 3D-data-free method for object-level 3D edits (translation, rotation, scale) on diffusion-generated or real images, making 3D-aware creative editing accessible without the cost of fine-tuning or maintaining 3D asset libraries. The main limitation is reliance on depth quality: large rotations or edits that reveal previously occluded regions degrade identity preservation, and the authors note that shape priors or image-to-NeRF regularization could address this. Open questions include extending the approach to video (temporal consistency), removing the text prompt dependency, and whether future multi-channel generative models (albedo, normals, illumination) could replace the current RGB-only identity signal.
