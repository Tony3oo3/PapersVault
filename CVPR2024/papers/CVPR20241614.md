## Problem

Existing LiDAR segmentation models are trained per-dataset, per-task, and per-modality, yielding separate parameter sets that fail to generalize across domains. Naively merging heterogeneous LiDAR datasets for joint training causes severe performance degradation due to sensor discrepancies, distribution gaps, and conflicting label spaces, while prior multi-dataset approaches either require dataset-specific fine-tuning or collapse labels to a coarser set, losing fine-grained segmentation capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241614/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, M3Net takes a single LiDAR point cloud as input and produces per-point semantic labels (and optionally panoptic instance+semantic predictions) using one shared set of parameters across all datasets. During training, the method additionally consumes synchronized multi-view camera images and class-name text strings, which are projected onto point clouds via camera calibration matrices to form paired point-pixel and point-text samples for the alignment losses. Dataset-specific rasterization parameters (e.g., 0.05 m voxel size for SemanticKITTI and Waymo, 0.1 m for nuScenes) are applied to accommodate varying point densities.

## Method

M3Net introduces a three-space alignment framework to tame heterogeneous LiDAR data with a single parameter set. In the data space, it performs cross-sensor coordinate offsetting, dataset-specific rasterization, and decoupled batch normalization to reduce statistical shifts. In the feature space, a frozen VLM image encoder extracts semantically rich image features that are aligned with point encoder features via a cosine-similarity V2P loss; a domain-aware gating module concatenates image features from all datasets and applies a learned channel-wise modulation before the final alignment. In the label space, a frozen text encoder produces class-name embeddings that drive contrastive P2T and V2T losses, while a cross-modality I2P loss aligns image and point logits, preserving each dataset's original label mapping without collapsing to a coarser set. The overall objective combines these alignment losses with standard cross-entropy, Lovász-softmax, and L1 instance-regression losses, and the framework supports both semantic and panoptic LiDAR segmentation via a shared backbone (MinkUNet or PTv2+) and a DSNet-inspired instance extractor.

## Results

Using a single shared set of parameters, M3Net achieves 75.1% mIoU on SemanticKITTI test, 83.1% mIoU on nuScenes test, and 72.4% mIoU / 81.1% mAcc on Waymo Open val, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. On panoptic LiDAR segmentation, M3Net reaches 63.87 PQ on Panoptic-SemanticKITTI (surpassing the prior best Panoptic-PHNet by 2.17%) and 80.90 mIoU on Panoptic-nuScenes. In out-of-distribution robustness evaluation (Robo3D), M3Net attains 86.43 mCE / 85.77 mRR on SemanticKITTI-C and 91.03 mCE / 79.15 mRR on nuScenes-C, outperforming both single-dataset and naive joint training baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SemanticKITTI, M3Net (72.0% val / 75.1% test mIoU) surpasses the strongest prior methods—FRNet (68.7/73.3), PTv2+ (70.3/70.6), and SphereFormer (67.8/74.8)—by 1.7–3.3% mIoU on val and 1.8–4.5% on test. On nuScenes, M3Net (80.9% val / 83.1% test) edges out PTv2+ (80.2/82.6) and FRNet (79.0/82.5) by 0.7–1.9% mIoU. On Waymo Open, M3Net (72.4% mIoU, 81.1% mAcc) beats PTv2+ (70.6/80.2) but trails LidarMultiNet (73.8% mIoU, no mAcc reported) by 1.4% mIoU, though M3Net is the only method reporting both metrics. For panoptic segmentation, M3Net (63.87 PQ) outperforms Panoptic-PHNet (61.7 PQ) on Panoptic-SemanticKITTI but falls short on Panoptic-nuScenes PQ (71.70 vs. 74.70), while still leading in mIoU (80.90 vs. 79.70). In cross-dataset knowledge transfer (Table 3), M3Net consistently outperforms PPKT, SLidR, Seal, naive joint training, and single-dataset baselines across all five held-out datasets (RELLIS-3D, SemanticPOSS, SemanticSTF, SynLiDAR, DAPS-3D), with the largest gains on SynLiDAR (44.10% vs. 43.58% for Seal at 1% data). On robustness benchmarks, M3Net achieves the lowest mCE (86.43 on SemanticKITTI-C, 91.03 on nuScenes-C) among all compared methods, confirming superior corruption resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

M3Net extends the multi-dataset learning line (COLA, MDT3D, Uni3D, PPT) by rejecting the common practice of collapsing label spaces to a coarse unified set or requiring per-dataset fine-tuning, instead maintaining original per-dataset label mappings through language-guided alignment. It also departs from prior multi-modality LiDAR pre-training (PPKT, SLidR, Seal) by using a frozen VLM as a feature-space anchor rather than training cross-sensor contrastive objectives from scratch, and from domain adaptation methods (XMUDA, CONDA) by jointly training across all datasets rather than adapting between source and target. The panoptic segmentation module is directly adapted from DSNet's dynamic shifting network, and the overall architecture leverages standard backbones (MinkUNet, PTv2+) rather than proposing a new network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A single parameter set achieving SOTA on SemanticKITTI, nuScenes, and Waymo Open simultaneously is practically significant for autonomous driving deployments where maintaining separate models per sensor/dataset is costly. The pilot study quantifying how naive joint training degrades performance (especially with sensor density differences) provides useful empirical grounding for the alignment design. Open questions include whether the framework scales beyond three datasets, whether the VLM dependency (frozen image encoder + text encoder) limits applicability to datasets without synchronized cameras, and whether the multi-space alignment strategy transfers to other 3D perception tasks like detection or tracking.
