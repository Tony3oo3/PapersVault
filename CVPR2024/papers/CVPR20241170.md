## Problem

Existing open-loop end-to-end autonomous driving methods on nuScenes report state-of-the-art planning results, but it remains unclear whether complex perception pipelines are truly necessary or whether ego status (velocity, acceleration, yaw angle) alone suffices. The paper identifies that 73.9% of nuScenes scenes involve straightforward driving, that current metrics (L2 distance, collision rate) fail to capture safety-critical behaviors like running off the road, and that ego status disproportionately dominates planning outputs, masking the actual contribution of perception modules.

## Input / Output

The method consumes surround-view camera images at 256×704 resolution (smaller than UniAD/VAD) and optionally ego status vectors (velocity, acceleration, yaw angle, driving command). BEV features are generated at 128×128 resolution covering ~50m, with temporal context from 4 past timesteps concatenated along the channel dimension without spatial alignment. The output is a single predicted ego trajectory (position over time), supervised solely by L1 loss against ground-truth trajectories, with no auxiliary perception losses or annotations required.

## Method

BEV-Planner is a deliberately minimal baseline that generates BEV features via an R50 backbone, concatenates 4 historical BEV frames along channels (no alignment), squeezes them through a BEV encoder to 256 channels, and then performs a single cross-attention between a learnable ego query and the fused BEV features before an MLP decodes the final trajectory. The key design choice is the absence of any perception sub-tasks (detection, tracking, HD map) or human-labeled annotations—only trajectory L1 loss is used. The paper also introduces Ego-MLP, a reproduction of AD-MLP that removes the label-leaking historical trajectory input, and a new Curb Collision Rate (CCR) metric that rasterizes road boundaries at 0.1m resolution to measure how often predicted trajectories intersect drivable-area boundaries. Ablations show that adding ego status to the planner causes rapid convergence and dominates the learned BEV activations, while adding map perception worsens L2/collision on straight-driving scenes but improves CCR, revealing metric-dependent trade-offs.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop evaluation, the proposed BEV-Planner++ (ego status in both BEV and planner) achieves 0.35 m average L2 distance, 0.34% collision rate, and 3.16% Curb Collision Rate (CCR). The key finding is that Ego-MLP, a simple MLP using only ego status (velocity, acceleration, yaw angle), achieves 0.35 m L2 and 0.37% collision—on par with complex end-to-end methods like UniAD (0.46 m L2) and VAD (0.37 m L2). However, under the newly proposed CCR metric, Ego-MLP (2.93%) and BEV-Planner++ (3.16%) perform substantially worse than UniAD (1.59%) and VAD (2.47%), revealing that ego-status-dominated models produce trajectories that deviate from road boundaries more frequently.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniAD (ID-3, ego status in both modules), BEV-Planner++ matches on L2 (0.35 vs. 0.46 m) and collision (0.34% vs. 0.37%), but UniAD is nearly 2× better on CCR (1.59% vs. 3.16%), indicating UniAD's map-aware planning keeps trajectories on-road more reliably. Against VAD-Base (ID-6), BEV-Planner++ ties on L2 (0.35 vs. 0.37 m) and collision (0.34% vs. 0.33%), while VAD is better on CCR (2.47% vs. 3.16%). The most striking comparison is with Ego-MLP (ID-8): this simple MLP using only ego status achieves identical L2 (0.35 m) and comparable collision (0.37% vs. 0.34%) to BEV-Planner++, demonstrating that the entire perception pipeline contributes negligibly to L2/collision metrics when ego status is present. Against ST-P3 (ID-0), BEV-Planner++ is dramatically superior on all three metrics (0.35 m vs. 2.65 m L2), though the paper notes ST-P3's official implementation used partially erroneous ground truth. The GoStright baseline (ID-7) achieves 0.83 m L2 and 1.08% collision—worse than Ego-MLP but still competitive—while its CCR of 8.62% confirms that naive straight-line driving frequently leaves the road.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is primarily an empirical analysis that interrogates the open-loop end-to-end autonomous driving paradigm established by UniAD, VAD, and ST-P3 on nuScenes. It builds directly on AD-MLP's observation that ego status alone can match complex pipelines, but corrects AD-MLP's label leakage (use of ground-truth historical trajectories) and extends the analysis with perturbation experiments, a new Curb Collision Rate metric, and a perception-free BEV-Planner baseline. Conceptually, it departs from the prevailing narrative that richer perception modules (tracking, HD maps, occupancy) improve planning quality, showing instead that ego status dominates the learning signal and that existing metrics mask this over-reliance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the validity of SOTA claims in open-loop end-to-end driving on nuScenes, arguing that 73.9% straightforward driving scenarios and incomplete metrics create a misleading benchmark where ego status shortcuts masquerade as genuine planning capability. The finding that completely blanking camera inputs barely degrades planning (while perturbing ego velocity to 100 m/s produces catastrophic trajectories) raises serious safety concerns about models that are hypersensitive to a single input channel. Open questions remain: how to construct evaluation datasets with sufficient scenario diversity, how to design metrics that jointly capture collision avoidance, road adherence, and smoothness without trade-off gaming, and whether closed-loop evaluation can eventually replace the open-loop paradigm. The authors explicitly caution the community to reassess prevailing research before pursuing incremental SOTA on the current benchmark.
