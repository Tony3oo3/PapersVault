## Problem

Existing NeRF editing methods are task-specific, requiring separate pipelines for super-resolution, inpainting, colorization, and text-driven editing. The dominant 'render-edit-aggregate' approach (e.g., Instruct-NeRF2NeRF) produces a single deterministic edit that cannot capture the stochastic diversity users expect from content editing, and its complex iterative optimization offers no guarantee of robust multi-view consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained NeRF scene (built from N multi-view images via Nerfstudio) and a user-specified editing goal (text prompt, colorization, SR, or inpainting mask). A plug-and-play 2D image-to-image translator (InstructPix2Pix, DDColor, ResShift, or SAM+LaMa depending on the task) generates M edited images per view, yielding an N×M set of translated images. At inference, the user samples a 64-dimensional edit code from a learned Gaussian distribution and the translated NeRF renders a multi-view consistent edited 3D scene; different samples yield diverse yet coherent results.

## Method

GenN2N replaces the deterministic 'aggregate all 2D edits into one NeRF' strategy with a conditional 3D generative model that learns the full distribution of plausible 3D edits. A Latent Distill Module (frozen Stable Diffusion VAE encoder + trainable MLP) maps each edited image to a 64-dim edit code, constrained to a Gaussian via KL loss. The translated NeRF is a VAE-GAN with a differentiable volume renderer, trained with four losses: same-view reconstruction (L1 + LPIPS), a conditional adversarial loss where the discriminator receives rendered-vs-edited image pairs concatenated with a style-condition image to prevent novel-view blur, and a contrastive loss that pulls edit codes of different views sharing the same code together while pushing apart same-view codes from different edit styles, thereby disentangling viewpoint from edit content. At inference, sampling from the Gaussian latent space yields diverse, multi-view consistent edited NeRFs without any task-specific optimization.

## Results

On text-driven editing (Face, Fangzhou, Farm datasets), GenN2N achieves CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity of 0.2089, CLIP Direction Consistency of 0.9864, and FID of 137.74, outperforming Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (0.2021, 0.9828, 148.02). On colorization, GenN2N reaches a colorfulness score of 65.10 and FID of 35.04, substantially exceeding PaletteNeRF (CF 39.65) and DDColor+NeRF (FID 148.96). For super-resolution on LLFF, GenN2N attains PSNR 28.50, SSIM 0.913, and LPIPS 0.075, surpassing the task-specific NeRF-SR (PSNR 27.96, SSIM 0.897, LPIPS 0.094). On inpainting (statue dataset), GenN2N achieves PSNR 26.87 and SSIM 0.814, beating SPIn-NeRF (PSNR 24.37, SSIM 0.722).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenN2N outperforms Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [9] on all four tasks: text-driven editing (FID 137.74 vs. 148.02), colorization (FID 35.04 vs. 201.46), super-resolution (PSNR 28.50 vs. 20.30), and inpainting (PSNR 26.87 vs. 16.73). Against task-specific specialists, GenN2N beats PaletteNeRF [16] on colorization (CF 65.10 vs. 39.65), NeRF-SR [34] on super-resolution (PSNR 28.50 vs. 27.96, LPIPS 0.075 vs. 0.094), and SPIn-NeRF [24] on inpainting (PSNR 26.87 vs. 24.37, SSIM 0.814 vs. 0.722). The naive baselines that simply combine a 2D translator with NeRF optimization (InstructPix2Pix+NeRF, DDColor+NeRF, ResShift+NeRF, LaMa+NeRF) all perform substantially worse, confirming that direct multi-view optimization without the generative framework fails to maintain 3D consistency. GenN2N also demonstrates a unique advantage over Instruct-NeRF2NeRF in generative diversity: while Instruct-NeRF2NeRF produces nearly identical results across multiple training runs, GenN2N yields varied yet consistent edited scenes by sampling different edit codes from the learned Gaussian distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenN2N departs from the iterative 'render-edit-aggregate' pipeline of Instruct-NeRF2NeRF by replacing deterministic optimization with a conditional 3D VAE-GAN that models the distribution of all plausible edits, drawing architectural inspiration from 3D generative models like GRAF and EG3D. It repurposes off-the-shelf 2D image-to-image translators (InstructPix2Pix, DDColor, ResShift, LaMa) as plug-and-play front-ends, positioning itself against task-specific specialists (NeRF-SR, PaletteNeRF, SPIn-NeRF) by subsuming them under a single NeRF-to-NeRF translation formulation. The contrastive learning scheme for disentangling viewpoint from edit content in the latent code is a novel regularization not seen in prior NeRF editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying text-driven editing, colorization, super-resolution, and inpainting under one generative framework, GenN2N eliminates the need for task-specific 3D optimization schemes and naturally captures the stochastic nature of content editing (multiple valid 3D outcomes per instruction). Key limitations include the per-scene training cost of the VAE-GAN, dependence on the quality and consistency of 2D translators, and evaluation on relatively small datasets (single objects, short sequences). Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, larger-scale environments, or alternative 3D representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting, and whether the generative formulation can be made amortized to avoid per-scene optimization.
