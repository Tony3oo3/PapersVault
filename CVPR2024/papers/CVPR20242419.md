## Problem

Generating language-guided human motion in 3D scenes is difficult because (i) jointly modeling language, scene geometry, and motion in a single generative model creates a complex multimodal entanglement that hinders scene grounding and generalization, and (ii) existing human-scene-interaction datasets are small, lack diverse free-form language annotations, and offer limited scene layouts, making it hard to train data-hungry generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242419/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene as an RGB point cloud (N×6) and a free-form language description (M tokenized words) as input. It produces a human motion sequence parameterized as SMPL-X joint positions (J×3 per frame over F frames) and an intermediate affordance map (N×J) representing the normalized distance field between each scene point and each skeleton joint. The affordance map is computed via max-pooling of per-frame exponential-normalized ℓ₂ distances, providing a compact geometric summary that decouples scene grounding from motion synthesis.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage diffusion framework that uses scene affordance as an intermediate representation to decouple 3D scene grounding from conditional motion generation. Stage 1 (ADM) employs a Perceiver architecture with cross-attention between point features (key/value) and language+diffusion-step embeddings (query) to denoise an affordance map from Gaussian noise, trained with x₀-prediction MSE loss. Stage 2 (AMDM) uses a Point Transformer encoder to extract affordance features, then a Transformer backbone with cross-attention layers fuses these affordance features with CLIP text embeddings and noisy motion to generate the final joint-position sequence. This decomposition simplifies the joint distribution p(motion | scene, language) into p(affordance | scene, language) × p(motion | affordance, language), reducing the conditioning complexity and enabling better generalization to unseen scenes and descriptions even with limited paired training data.

## Results

On HUMANISE, the proposed two-stage model (Ours@Enc) achieves a goal distance of 0.156 m, contact score of 95.86%, quality score of 3.46±1.15, and action score of 4.47±0.84, substantially outperforming the cVAE baseline (goal dist. 0.422, contact 84.06%, quality 2.25) and one-stage diffusion variants. On HumanML3D, the method attains the best FID of 0.352 (vs. MDM's 0.544) and highest MultiModality of 2.835, while the encoder-adapted variant (Ours†) leads in R-Precision Top-1 (0.432) and MultiModal Dist. (3.430). On the novel generalization set with unseen scenes and descriptions, the method achieves a contact score of 71.98% (Ours@Enc) versus 46.64% for the one-stage baseline, confirming robustness to novel language-scene pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the cVAE baseline [84] on HUMANISE, the proposed method reduces goal distance by 63% (0.156 vs. 0.422 m), improves contact score by 11.8 points (95.86% vs. 84.06%), and raises quality and action scores by 1.21 and 0.81 respectively. Compared to the one-stage diffusion variants that bypass the affordance map, the two-stage approach achieves a 16% lower goal distance (0.156 vs. 0.185 for one-stage@Dec) and a 9.6-point higher contact score (95.86% vs. 86.43%), demonstrating that the explicit affordance intermediate representation is critical for accurate scene grounding. On HumanML3D, the method outperforms MDM [76] in FID (0.352 vs. 0.544) and MultiModality (2.835 vs. 2.799) while matching its diversity; against T2M [29], it achieves a 3.4× lower FID (0.352 vs. 1.067) but trails in R-Precision Top-1 (0.341 vs. 0.457) under the standard setting. The only metric where the method underperforms is APD (diversity), which the authors attribute to the model's higher grounding precision producing more consistent (less varied) motions rather than physically implausible diversity. On the unseen-scene generalization set, the method's contact score of 71.98% (Ours@Enc) nearly doubles the one-stage baseline's 46.64%, highlighting the affordance map's role in transferring interaction knowledge to novel geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Perceiver architecture [Jaegle et al., ICML 2021] for point-cloud feature extraction and the distance-field normalization from contact-aware motion forecasting [Mao et al., NeurIPS 2022] to define its affordance map. Conceptually, it departs from prior HSI generation work (HUMANISE, PROX, one-stage diffusion baselines) that jointly conditions on scene geometry and language in a single generative pass, instead decomposing the problem into a scene-grounding stage (ADM) and a motion-synthesis stage (AMDM) linked by an explicit affordance intermediate. This positions the work at the intersection of 3D vision-language grounding and conditional diffusion-based motion generation, extending the affordance concept from static scene understanding to a dynamic, language-conditioned intermediate representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The affordance-as-intermediate strategy offers a practical workaround for the severe data scarcity in language-scene-motion triplets, enabling better generalization to unseen scenes and descriptions without requiring massive paired datasets. The generalization evaluation on a curated set of 16 diverse scenes with novel Turkers-written prompts is a meaningful step toward assessing real-world applicability beyond in-distribution benchmarks. However, the reliance on diffusion models introduces slow inference, and the authors acknowledge that data limitations remain a fundamental bottleneck; future work on faster sampling or data-efficient training strategies would be needed for deployment. The approach also opens the question of whether richer affordance representations (e.g., temporal affordance, multi-object interaction affordance) could further improve complex multi-step interactions.
