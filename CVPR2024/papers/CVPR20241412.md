## Problem

Previous deep unfolding networks for single-pixel imaging either vectorize 2D images into 1D signals (producing prohibitively large measurement matrices) or rely on block-based patch sampling that is impractical for real SPI cameras. This mismatch between CS-oriented solvers and the physical constraints of SPI systems has limited the deployment of state-of-the-art reconstruction methods on actual hardware, particularly at large image scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D sub-sampled measurements Y ∈ R^(√M × √M) acquired via Kronecker SPI (Y = ΦXΨ^T with two independent measurement matrices) from a single-pixel detector, and produces a full-resolution 2D reconstructed image X̂ ∈ R^(√N × √N). In the real SPI prototype, the measurement matrices are cake-cutting Hadamard matrices displayed on a DMD, and the system handles images up to 768×1024 pixels at sampling ratios as low as 12.5%. The initialization is computed as X₀ = Φ^T Y Ψ before entering the unfolding stages.

## Method

HATNet unrolls the tensor ISTA computation graph into alternating tensor gradient descent (TGD) and deep denoising stages, where TGD exploits the Kronecker structure (Z_k = X_{k-1} + ρΦ^T(Y − ΦX_{k-1}Ψ^T)Ψ) to avoid the O(αN⁴) cost of vectorized gradient projection, reducing it to O((√α+α)N³). The denoiser is a three-level U-shaped encoder-decoder built from HAT blocks, each containing a residual dual-scale spatial self-attention (S-SA) that performs high-frequency attention in N-pixel windows and low-frequency cross-scale attention using average-pooled keys/values in pN-pixel windows, plus a channel-wise self-attention (C-SA) with a bottleneck MLP for global channel recalibration. Cross-stage skip connections (CSSC) fuse previous-stage decoder features into current-stage encoder features to mitigate information loss from the repeated signal-to-feature transformation inherent in deep unfolding. The network is trained end-to-end with supervised loss on 20,000 augmented images from BSD500 (or DIV2K for large-scale), using learnable measurement matrices in simulation and fixed Hadamard matrices in the real prototype.

## Results

On the Set11 benchmark, HATNet achieves the best PSNR/SSIM across all four sampling ratios: 27.98 dB / 0.8382 at 4%, 32.26 dB / 0.9182 at 10%, 37.24 dB / 0.9634 at 25%, and 42.05 dB / 0.9838 at 50%. The gains over the closest competitor SAUNet are consistent but modest (≈0.1–0.2 dB), while margins over earlier black-box and unfolding baselines (e.g., ReconNet, ISTA-Net+) reach 5–8 dB at low sampling ratios. In real-data experiments on a custom SPI prototype, HATNet also outperforms ISTA-TV, DGUNet+, OCTUF+, and SAUNet visually, and the Kronecker formulation reduces GPU memory from 10.74 GB to 3.02 GB and inference time from 0.55 s to 0.38 s compared to a vectorized formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HATNet outperforms all 11 compared baselines on Set11 at every sampling ratio. Against the most recent and strongest competitor SAUNet [51], the advantage is narrow but consistent: +0.18 dB at 4%, +0.11 dB at 10%, +0.13 dB at 25%, and +0.14 dB at 50% PSNR. The gap widens substantially against earlier unfolding networks: at 10% SR, HATNet leads DGUNet+ [34] by 1.33 dB, OCTUF+ [44] by 1.53 dB, and MADUN [42] by 2.06 dB. Against black-box and early unfolding baselines the margins are large—ReconNet [22] trails by 7.88 dB at 10% and ISTA-Net+ [69] by 5.62 dB. In real-data experiments on the authors' SPI prototype, HATNet produces the best visual quality among ISTA-TV [62], DGUNet+, OCTUF+, and SAUNet at 25% SR, and at large scale (768×1024, 12.5% SR) it clearly surpasses ISTA-TV. The Kronecker formulation also yields a 3.5× reduction in GPU memory (3.02 GB vs. 10.74 GB) and 31% faster inference (0.38 s vs. 0.55 s) compared to a vectorized SPI formulation of the same network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network (DUN) paradigm for compressive sensing reconstruction by replacing the standard vectorized ISTA with a tensor ISTA derived from the Kronecker product SPI model (Duarte & Baraniuk, 2011), building directly on the tensor ISTA formulation introduced in SAUNet (Wang & Yuan, 2023). The key departure from prior DUNs (ISTA-Net, AMP-Net, DGUNet+, OCTUF+) is the avoidance of vectorized 1D signal processing, which eliminates the prohibitively large measurement matrix and enables full-size sampling compatible with real SPI hardware. For the denoising module, the paper introduces a hybrid-attention Transformer (HAT) combining dual-scale spatial self-attention (inspired by Swin Transformer and PVT) with channel-wise self-attention (inspired by SE-Net), positioned within the broader trend of applying Transformers to low-level vision tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: previous SOTA DUNs for CS reconstruction rely on block-based or vectorized sampling that is incompatible with mainstream SPI cameras, limiting their real-world deployment. By demonstrating SOTA performance on a physical SPI prototype at 768×1024 resolution with Kronecker SPI, the work provides a viable path toward practical computational imaging in non-visible wavebands and through scattering media. The optical resolution experiment (sub-sampling + deep reconstruction outperforming full-sampling at equal measurement count) is a compelling demonstration of the potential of learned reconstruction. However, the large-scale comparison is limited to ISTA-TV (no re-trained deep baselines at 768×1024), and the SOTA margins over SAUNet on synthetic data are modest (~0.1–0.2 dB), leaving open whether the HAT denoiser's advantage persists at even larger scales or with different measurement matrices.
