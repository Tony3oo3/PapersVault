## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing methods are inefficient (requiring expensive fine-tuning or optimization), imprecise (deviating strongly from the input image), and limited in versatility (unable to support multiple simultaneous edits without interference). LEDITS++ addresses all three limitations with a parameter-free, architecture-agnostic approach that enables fast, precise, and multi-concept textual image manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241674/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and one or more free-form text edit instructions (e.g., 'add glasses', 'change background to beach') and produces a single edited image. It is architecture-agnostic, operating on both latent (Stable Diffusion 1.5, SD-XL) and pixel-based diffusion models. No user-provided masks, fine-tuning, or iterative optimization are required; the inversion can be truncated at 80–90% of total timesteps for further speedup, and resampling the inversion noise yields meaningful image variations.

## Method

LEDITS++ decomposes editing into three components: (1) perfect inversion derived for the second-order SDE DPM-Solver++ sampler, which reconstructs the input image exactly in as few as 20 total diffusion steps without any optimization; (2) a per-concept guidance formulation where each edit instruction e_i contributes an isolated guidance term γ_i that pushes the unconditioned noise estimate toward or away from the conditioned estimate, with independent scale s_e and direction per concept, enabling multiple simultaneous edits without cross-interference; (3) an implicit masking mechanism that intersects a coarse cross-attention mask (from the U-Net's attention layers, thresholded at the λ-th percentile) with a fine-grained noise-difference mask (from |ψ|, the absolute difference between conditioned and unconditioned noise estimates), restricting each edit's effect to semantically relevant image regions while preserving overall composition and object identity.

## Results

LEDITS++ achieves perfect reconstruction (RMSE = 0.00) with the fastest execution time (1.78 s) among all compared methods, outperforming Edit-friendly DDPM (10.36 s) by 6× and vanilla DDIM inversion (37.23 s) by 21×. On TEdBench, LEDITS++ with SD1.5 reaches a 75% success rate and 0.28 LPIPS, substantially outperforming Imagic with SD1.5 (55% SR, 0.56 LPIPS). On the more challenging TEdBench++, LEDITS++ with SD-XL achieves 87% SR and 0.34 LPIPS. In the CelebA multi-attribute editing experiment (10,000 images per method), LEDITS++ is closest to the ideal (high CLIP alignment, low LPIPS deviation) among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEDITS++ dominates all six baselines on reconstruction fidelity (RMSE = 0.00 vs. 0.13–0.81 for others) and execution speed (1.78 s vs. 2.10–349.98 s). Against SDEdit [26], LEDITS++ matches speed but eliminates the large reconstruction error (0.00 vs. 0.81 RMSE) while adding semantic grounding and multi-editing. Against Imagic [18], LEDITS++ is ~197× faster (1.78 s vs. 349.98 s) and achieves higher TEdBench success rate (75% vs. 55% with SD1.5) and lower LPIPS (0.28 vs. 0.56). Against vanilla DDIM inversion [42], LEDITS++ reduces RMSE from 0.22 to 0.00 and cuts runtime by 21×. Against Pix2Pix-Zero [30] and DiffEdit [9], LEDITS++ improves RMSE (0.00 vs. 0.20/0.13), is 32×/16× faster, and uniquely supports multi-editing. Against Edit-friendly DDPM [17], LEDITS++ ties on perfect reconstruction but is 6× faster (1.78 s vs. 10.36 s) and additionally provides semantic grounding and multi-concept editing, which DDPM inversion lacks entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEDITS++ bridges two previously separate lines of work: the perfect-inversion guarantee for DDPM established by Huberman-Spiegelglas et al. [17] and the fast higher-order ODE solvers (DPM-Solver++) of Lu et al. [23]. By deriving the inversion properties for the second-order SDE-DPM-Solver++ rather than the first-order DDPM, the paper achieves the same zero-reconstruction-error guarantee with far fewer diffusion steps. On the editing side, it departs from prior attention-only or fine-tuning-based approaches (Pix2Pix-Zero, Imagic, DiffEdit) by combining cross-attention masks with noise-estimate-based masks into an implicit grounding mechanism, and by formulating per-concept guidance terms that remain isolated during multi-editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's practical impact is significant: it eliminates the need for per-image fine-tuning or iterative optimization, runs in under 2 seconds on SD1.5, and is architecture-agnostic, making it immediately deployable with any text-to-image diffusion model. The introduced TEdBench++ benchmark addresses real gaps in the original TEdBench (ambiguous instructions, missing edit categories) and provides a more holistic evaluation protocol. Key open questions include the coherence trade-off (object identity can shift within masked regions for generic prompts), the dependence on the underlying DM's concept coverage, and whether user-provided masks can be seamlessly integrated to handle cases where automatic grounding is insufficient.
