## Problem

Single-view 3D reconstruction fundamentally struggles to recover occluded geometry, and the prevailing multi-view synthesis approach (generating novel camera views via diffusion) suffers from persistent multi-view consistency failures, especially across disparate viewpoints. The paper argues that slicing through an object is a more direct occlusion-revealing mechanism than changing camera angles: in the limit, infinitely many slices guarantee complete structural revelation, whereas infinitely many views may still leave parts hidden behind occluders.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 128×128 RGB image as input and produces a 3D triangle mesh via Marching Cubes over a learned signed distance function. An intermediate representation of 12 slice images (4 per axis along X, Y, Z) is predicted, each rendered without texture and with internal cavities kept as holes to expose back faces. For the Objaverse dataset, slicing is camera-aligned rather than object-aligned to handle arbitrary object orientations, and camera pose estimation is optional (applied only on ShapeNet).

## Method

Slice3D is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 predicts multi-slice images from the input view using either a U-Net regressor (with learnable slice indicator codes concatenated to the encoder latent to designate each spatial slice location) or a DDPM generator that denoises all 12 slices jointly along the color channel to enforce inter-slice consistency, conditioned on multi-layer VGG features of the input. Stage 2 is a coordinate-based transformer that, for each 3D query point, projects it onto the 2D planes of hierarchical CNN feature maps extracted from the slice images, gathers the corresponding feature vectors via bilinear interpolation, and aggregates them through pairwise attention to predict the signed distance at that point. The key architectural insight is that no camera rotation or multi-view consistency is needed at either stage, enabling straightforward use of convolutional features and avoiding the consistency failures inherent to multi-view synthesis. The entire system is trained end-to-end (for the regression variant) on a single A40 GPU with inference under 20 seconds.

## Results

On ShapeNet Chairs (estimated poses), Slice3D achieves CD 2.80, F1 2.43%, HD 6.75, outperforming DISN (CD 3.25, F1 2.34%, HD 7.84) and substantially beating diffusion-based methods like One-2-3-45 (CD 12.5, F1 1.01%, HD 11.7) and NeRF-From-Image (CD 19.5, F1 1.27%, HD 13.4). On ShapeNet Airplanes, Slice3D reaches CD 2.99, F1 2.83%, HD 6.45, again leading all baselines. On the cross-category Objaverse dataset, regression-based Slice3D (Ours(R)) achieves CD 16.6, F1 1.53%, HD 13.4, LPIPS 12.8, surpassing DISN (CD 28.2, HD 23.0, LPIPS 15.2) and One-2-3-45 (CD 35.4, HD 29.8, LPIPS 19.8) across all 3D and 2D metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Slice3D outperforms DISN [65] on all three metrics for both ShapeNet categories, with the gap widening when GT poses are used (e.g., Chairs CD 2.67 vs. 3.02). Against the diffusion+NeRF pipeline One-2-3-45 [28], Slice3D achieves roughly 4–8× lower CD and 2–4× lower HD, indicating far more accurate geometry; One-2-3-45 tends to produce dilated, detail-lacking shapes due to multi-view inconsistency. Auto-SDF [36] and NeRF-From-Image [42] (both latent-code-based) perform poorly on ShapeNet (CD 25.0 and 19.5 on Chairs), as their global latent representations fail to capture fine occluded details. SSDNeRF [6], even with GT poses, lags Slice3D by a large margin (CD 8.98 vs. 2.67 on Chairs), producing rougher surfaces. On the cross-category Objaverse benchmark, Slice3D (regression) beats DISN by 41% in CD (16.6 vs. 28.2) and One-2-3-45 by 53% in CD (16.6 vs. 35.4), while also leading in 2D metrics (PSNR 22.6 vs. 21.9/19.8; LPIPS 12.8 vs. 15.2/19.8), despite training on only 40K shapes versus the 800K used to fine-tune Zero-1-to-3 for One-2-3-45.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing paradigm of single-view 3D reconstruction that routes through multi-view synthesis (e.g., Zero-1-to-3 + NeRF pipelines like One-2-3-45 and RealFusion) by proposing multi-slice prediction as an intermediate representation instead. Conceptually, it draws on GANHopper's multi-hop domain translation idea and on slice-based reconstruction techniques (OReX, Cut-and-Approximate, NeuralSlice), but repurposes them for the single-view setting where slices are *predicted* from one image rather than provided as 3D cross-sections. The reconstruction module combines standard components—U-Net for slice regression, DDPM for slice generation, and a coordinate-based transformer for SDF prediction—in a pipeline that avoids the multi-view consistency problem entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's working assumption that multi-view synthesis is the most natural bridge from 2D to 3D, showing that a compact set of 12 slice images (4 per axis) can outperform 12+ synthesized views, particularly for severely occluded structures. Practically, the method trains on a single A40 GPU and infers in under 20 seconds, compared to 90 minutes for RealFusion, while using only 5% of the training data that One-2-3-45 relies on. Key limitations include the restriction to digital 3D models (physical objects cannot be sliced), the coarse 4-slice-per-axis resolution that may miss fine details on distant parts, and the open question of whether adaptive or denser slicing could further close the gap on complex geometries.
