## Problem

Existing person identification methods are predominantly restricted to walking patterns (gait recognition) or static whole-body images, overlooking the diverse daily activities people actually perform. Learning biometric features from RGB videos of varied activities is further complicated by appearance biases (clothing color, background) and spatio-temporal complexity, making it difficult to separate identity-relevant cues from irrelevant visual distractors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes face-blurred RGB video clips of 8 frames (stride 4) resized to 256×128, with hue-shifting applied for robustness. At inference, the model produces a biometric feature vector that is concatenated with an activity feature vector (the 'activity prior') to form the final identity embedding for gallery matching. Silhouette videos are used only during training to feed the bias-less teacher network and are not required at test time.

## Method

ABNet uses a ResNet3D-50 video encoder followed by a transformer decoder (actor head) that outputs entangled biometric and appearance features. Disentanglement is achieved through two complementary mechanisms: (1) knowledge distillation from a GaitGL teacher trained on binary silhouettes, which pulls the biometric component toward appearance-free representations via KL-divergence loss, and (2) a distortion branch that applies elastic transforms to alter identity while preserving appearance, creating hard-negative pairs for a contrastive loss that explicitly learns the appearance component. Joint training with an activity recognition head (cross-entropy on activity labels) enables the model to exploit activity context as a prior for identification. The total objective combines biometric loss (cross-entropy + triplet), activity loss, distillation loss, and distortion loss, and at inference the activity features are concatenated with the disentangled biometric features to form the final identity embedding.

## Results

On the same-activity View+ protocol, ABNet achieves Rank-1 accuracy of 78.76% (mAP 40.31) on NTU RGB-AB, 86.83% (mAP 57.31) on PKU MMD-AB, 45.84% (mAP 31.58) on Charades-AB, and 80.43% (mAP 52.71) on ACC-MM1-Activities. On the in-the-wild BRIAR-BGC3 dataset, ABNet reaches 34.38% R@1, 18.78% mAP, and 26.42% TAR@0.1%FAR. Across all five datasets, ABNet consistently outperforms the best prior method by approximately 2–4% in Rank-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABNet outperforms all 14 compared methods across all four primary datasets. Against the strongest image-based method (Video-CAL [15]), ABNet gains +3.27% R@1 on NTU RGB-AB (78.76 vs. 75.49), +7.24% on PKU MMD-AB (86.83 vs. 79.59), +1.93% on Charades-AB (45.84 vs. 43.91), and +2.95% on ACC-MM1-Activities (80.43 vs. 77.48). Against the best video-based method (PSTA [37]), the margins are even larger: +11.35% R@1 on NTU RGB-AB and +9.39% on PKU MMD-AB. On BRIAR-BGC3 (Table 3), ABNet achieves 34.38% R@1 versus 30.57% for Image-CAL [14] and 28.32% for Video-CAL [14], a +3.8% and +6.1% improvement respectively. The silhouette-based GaitGL [27] baseline, which is also used as the teacher in ABNet, lags significantly (e.g., 61.51% R@1 on NTU RGB-AB), confirming that RGB-based learning with disentanglement substantially surpasses pure silhouette approaches. Notably, ABNet's advantage is most pronounced on PKU MMD-AB and ACC-MM1-Activities, where diverse activity contexts amplify the benefit of the activity-prior and bias-disentanglement components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing person identification away from the dominant walking-centric paradigm (gait recognition, video ReID on walking sequences) toward arbitrary daily activities, repurposing activity recognition datasets as biometric benchmarks. Methodologically, it synthesizes ideas from cross-modal knowledge distillation (transferring silhouette-based gait features to an RGB student), elastic distortion as a negative-mining tool for appearance-bias disentanglement, and joint multi-task training (activity + identity), none of which had been combined in this way for person identification. The 3D CNN + transformer decoder backbone follows the standard video ReID recipe (ResNet3D-50, GaitGL), so the architectural contribution is modest; the novelty lies in the training objective and the problem setting rather than in a new network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The five curated benchmarks (NTU RGB-AB, PKU MMD-AB, Charades-AB, ACC-MM1-Activities, BRIAR-BGC3) fill a genuine gap: prior person-identification evaluations assume walking or static poses, leaving activity-diverse identification unmeasured. The face-restricted protocol and the finding that removing facial cues costs only ~0.5% accuracy suggest the learned features are genuinely body- and motion-based, which has practical implications for privacy-sensitive and mask-wearing scenarios. Open questions include whether the activity prior generalizes to unseen activity classes, how the method scales to longer videos (only 8 frames are sampled), and whether the reliance on Mask2Former for silhouette extraction during training limits deployment in real-time settings.
