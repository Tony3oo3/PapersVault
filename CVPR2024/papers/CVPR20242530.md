## Problem

Existing neural rendering representations for large-scale scenes cannot simultaneously capture macro-scale structure and micro-scale detail: UV-based and 3D-discretization methods suffer from parametrization distortion or limited feature-metric resolution, while implicit volumetric methods introduce surface ambiguity through dispersed weight distributions during volume rendering. Additionally, no real-captured benchmark exists for cross-scale, high-resolution novel view synthesis of scenes spanning square-kilometer areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242530/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view images at 5K/8K resolution (1,600–18,000 per scene) captured at unstructured distances ranging from 5 m to 10³ m, along with an MVS-reconstructed triangle mesh serving as a geometric proxy. It outputs novel-view RGB renderings at arbitrary camera poses, achieving an effective texture resolution of ~30 billion pixels across scenes averaging 1.4×10⁶ m² in area.

## Method

The core representation, hash featureized manifold, applies volumetric multi-resolution hash encoding to the mesh's bounding volume but queries hash features only at rasterized surface intersections, converting a redundant volumetric featurization into a concentrated surface-based one that propagates clean multi-view-consistent gradients. Two tailored enhancements improve cross-scale fidelity: surface multisampling (casting γ² rays per pixel and mean-pooling hash features for prefiltered, aliasing-free featurization) and manifold deformation (a tiny MLP that deforms hash features in latent space conditioned on view direction to tolerate geometric imperfections). A lightweight MLP-based neural shader then decodes the rasterized feature buffer together with the view direction map to synthesize view-dependent surface color, yielding a deferred rendering pipeline that is both expressive and efficient.

## Results

On the novel GigaNVS benchmark (7 real-world large-scale scenes, ~10⁶ m² each), XScale-NVS achieves a mean PSNR of 20.84, SSIM of 0.776, and LPIPS of 0.148, outperforming all baselines across all metrics. The headline result is a ~40% reduction in LPIPS relative to the second-best method ZipNeRF (0.148 vs. 0.243). On the public Tanks&Temples dataset, the method also leads with PSNR 29.66, SSIM 0.914, and LPIPS 0.079, surpassing iNGP (28.67 / 0.905 / 0.110) and 3DGS (28.62 / 0.903 / 0.123).

## Baselines & Comparisons

XScale-NVS outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across all three metrics. On GigaNVS, the largest gap is against Neuralangelo (LPIPS 0.148 vs. 0.482, a 3.2× improvement), reflecting that implicit volumetric methods suffer severe surface ambiguity on large-scale scenes. Against the closest competitor ZipNeRF, the method gains +0.75 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 39% (0.148 vs. 0.243), demonstrating substantially better perceptual fidelity. 3DGS and Meta Representation trail by ~0.8 dB PSNR and ~0.14 LPIPS, with 3DGS failing to capture micro-scale details and Meta Representation producing UV-distortion artifacts. On Tanks&Temples, the advantage narrows but remains consistent: +1.0 dB PSNR and 28% lower LPIPS over iNGP, the strongest baseline there. Notably, the method's 1M-triangle mesh variant still outperforms 3DGS with 3M Gaussians (PSNR 22.84 vs. 21.52 on TW-Pavilion), highlighting the efficiency of hash-based featurization over explicit 3D discretization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate families of NVS representations: explicit surface-based methods (3DGS, point-based graphics, UV-textured meshes) that suffer from limited local expressivity, and implicit volumetric methods (iNGP, ZipNeRF, Neuralangelo) that suffer from surface ambiguity due to dispersed volume-rendering weights. The key conceptual departure is using multi-resolution hash encoding (from iNGP [25]) as a featurization tool but restricting its query to surface intersections obtained via mesh rasterization, rather than sampling along rays through a volume. The manifold deformation module is explicitly inspired by the authors' prior work GianticNVS [39], while the deferred rendering pipeline follows the broader neural rasterization lineage (Thies et al., 3DGS, NPBG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GigaNVS dataset is the first real-captured benchmark for cross-scale, high-resolution NVS of square-kilometer-scale scenes, filling a critical gap where prior datasets were either synthetic, single-scale, or limited to small scenes. The demonstrated robustness to 90% mesh decimation with less than 1% metric degradation validates the core claim that hash encoding decouples feature resolution from geometric discretization, which has practical implications for memory and preprocessing pipelines. The stated limitation—incapability to handle incorrect geometry or occlusions—points toward future work on differentiable geometry refinement, and the method's dependence on an MVS mesh as input remains a practical bottleneck for scenes where MVS fails.
