## Problem

Volumetric neural rendering techniques successfully reconstruct opaque 3D objects using exponential transport models originally designed for translucent media, yet the mathematical justification for applying these models to opaque solids has remained heuristic. Previous derivations (e.g., NeuS, VolSDF) transform deterministic geometry representations into volumetric ones through ad hoc choices, and some violate fundamental physical constraints such as reciprocity. The paper asks: under what formal conditions can opaque solids be modeled with exponential volumetric transport, and what are the correct expressions for the resulting volumetric parameters?

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (from DTU, BlendedMVS, and NeRF Realistic Synthetic datasets) and learns a set of spatially varying scalar fields: a mean implicit function f, a vacancy field v, an isotropic density σ^∥, and a spatially varying anisotropy parameter α(x). Unlike prior methods that output a single SDF, the learned representation explicitly encodes stochastic geometry (occupancy, vacancy, and direction-dependent attenuation), enabling both mesh extraction via marching cubes on f and physically consistent novel view synthesis through the volume rendering equation.

## Method

The paper derives a rigorous volumetric representation for opaque solids from stochastic geometry first principles. It models an opaque solid as a random indicator function and proves (Theorem 4) that exponential transport holds if and only if the indicator function restricted to any ray is a continuous-time Markov process, yielding a closed-form attenuation coefficient σ_δ = |ω·∇log v(x)|. The coefficient is then decomposed into an isotropic density σ^∥ = ‖∇v‖/v and an anisotropic projected area σ_D^⊥ computed as the expected foreshortening over a distribution of normals D_x (Definition 5). Specializing to stochastic implicit surfaces with symmetric pointwise distributions (Gaussian, logistic, Laplace), Proposition 7 gives the final attenuation coefficient in terms of the mean implicit function f, its gradient, and a scale parameter s. The key practical innovation is a spatially varying anisotropy parameter α(x) that interpolates between fully anisotropic (surface-like) and fully isotropic (interior-like) behavior via a mixture normal distribution, correcting the reciprocity-violating ReLU in NeuS and the incorrect density in VolSDF.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, the proposed representation achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 1.57 (median 1.56), outperforming NeuS (2.17/1.99) and VolSDF (1.84/1.74). On the NeRF Realistic Synthetic dataset, the method achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.113 (median 0.057), substantially outperforming NeuS (0.201/0.085) and VolSDF (0.252/0.100). The improvements are most pronounced on NeRF RS, where the method reduces mean Chamfer distance by ~44% over NeuS and ~55% over VolSDF, reflecting the benefit of reciprocal attenuation and spatially varying anisotropy on scenes with complex geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior volumetric representations for opaque solids: NeuS [Wang et al., NeurIPS 2021] and VolSDF [Yariv et al., NeurIPS 2021]. The proposed method outperforms both baselines on all evaluated datasets (DTU and NeRF RS) in both mean and median Chamfer distance. Against NeuS, the improvement is ~28% on DTU (1.57 vs 2.17) and ~44% on NeRF RS (0.113 vs 0.201), primarily due to replacing NeuS's reciprocity-violating ReLU term with a reciprocal projected area and using a spatially varying anisotropy parameter. Against VolSDF, the improvement is ~15% on DTU (1.57 vs 1.84) and ~55% on NeRF RS (0.113 vs 0.252), benefiting from a correct density term (Gaussian vs. incorrectly applied Laplace CDF) and anisotropic surface behavior. Ablation results (Table 3) confirm that each theoretical correction—reciprocal projected area, correct density, spatially varying anisotropy, and Gaussian implicit distribution—contributes independently to the overall improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives volumetric representations for opaque solids from first principles using stochastic geometry, positioning itself as a rigorous foundation for the heuristic volumetric models used in NeuS and VolSDF. It builds on the stochastic geometry query framework of Bitterli et al. and the anisotropic attenuation decomposition from Jakob et al. and Heitz et al. (originally developed for microparticle media), adapting these tools to macroscopic solid geometry. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods (NeuS, VolSDF) were constructed by transforming deterministic SDFs into volumetric forms with ad hoc choices, whereas this paper proves that exponential transport holds if and only if the indicator function along a ray is a Markov process, and shows that NeuS and VolSDF correspond to specific (sometimes incorrect) probabilistic modeling choices within this unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous justification for why volumetric neural rendering can reconstruct opaque solid geometry, and identifies a concrete physical violation (reciprocity) in the widely-used NeuS ReLU term that also empirically degrades reconstruction quality. Open questions include extending the theory to non-exponential transport (which stochastic geometry suggests is more appropriate for Gaussian-process excursion sets), handling semi-transparent solids, and properly coupling geometry with global illumination to maintain reciprocity. For practitioners, the framework offers a principled design space (choice of implicit function distribution, normal distribution, spatially varying anisotropy) that can be plugged into existing pipelines with minimal code changes.
