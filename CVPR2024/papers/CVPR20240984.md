## Problem

State-of-the-art monocular metric depth estimation models are bottlenecked by their backbone resolution (e.g., ZoeDepth at 384×512), far below the 4K+ resolutions of modern cameras. Existing high-resolution strategies—guided super-resolution, implicit functions, and prior tile-based merging—suffer from error propagation, loss of high-frequency detail, or scale inconsistencies that mandate heuristic patch selection and post-processing corrections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240984/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 2160×3840 on UnrealStereo4K) and produces a pixel-aligned metric depth map at the original resolution. The image is internally split into fixed-size patches (540×960 or 270×480 depending on dataset) that match the native input resolution of the underlying base model, so the framework is model-agnostic and can wrap any pre-trained depth estimator. No post-processing (scale optimization, Gaussian blending) is required at inference.

## Method

PatchFusion decomposes high-resolution depth estimation into three phases: (i) a coarse network N_c predicts a globally consistent but low-detail depth map from the downsampled image, (ii) a fine network N_f predicts detailed but scale-inconsistent depth from individual patches, and (iii) a lightweight U-Net-style guided fusion network N_g merges both. The fusion network is augmented by a Global-to-Local (G2L) module that applies Swin Transformer shifted-window self-attention to the coarse features, preserving global scale context within each patch crop, and by multi-scale feature guidance injected at skip connections and decoder layers from both N_c and N_f. A Consistency-Aware Training (CAT) loss enforces L2 agreement on overlapping regions of intermediate features and depth predictions between two shifted crops, while Consistency-Aware Inference (CAI) extends the standard 16-patch grid with 33 shifted or 128 randomly sampled patches, updating the fused depth via a running-mean local ensemble—eliminating all pre- and post-processing steps.

## Results

On UnrealStereo4K, PatchFusion (R=128 random patches) achieves 98.47% δ₁, 0.0388 REL, 1.0655 RMS, 6.08 SiLog, and 0.849 SEE, improving RMSE by 17.3% over the ZoeDepth coarse baseline (1.2887). On MVS-Synth, the same configuration reaches 96.17% δ₁, 0.0589 REL, 0.8944 RMS, 9.77 SiLog, and 1.083 SEE, a 29.4% RMSE reduction over the coarse baseline (1.2676). The method also achieves the lowest Soft Edge Error (SEE) on both datasets, indicating superior boundary fidelity. On Middlebury 2014, zero-shot transfer from synthetic training to real images demonstrates competitive depth quality without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchFusion outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both UnrealStereo4K and MVS-Synth. Against the closest tile-based competitor BoostingDepth [27], PatchFusion (R=128) reduces RMSE from 1.1233 to 1.0655 (5.2%) on UnrealStereo4K and from 0.9535 to 0.8944 (6.2%) on MVS-Synth, while also achieving lower SEE (0.849 vs. 0.939) and CE. The guided super-resolution method Graph-GDSR [8] lags further behind, with RMSE of 1.2642 (UnrealStereo4K) and 1.2435 (MVS-Synth), roughly 15–28% worse than PatchFusion. The implicit-function approach SMD-Net [37] shows similar RMSE to Graph-GDSR (1.2817 / 1.2563) and is dominated on all metrics. The traditional monocular estimator iDisc [31] performs worst overall (RMSE 1.4035 / 1.4386), confirming that resolution-limited single-pass models cannot match tile-based strategies. Finally, compared to the raw ZoeDepth baselines, PatchFusion delivers the headline 17.3% and 29.4% RMSE improvements over the coarse variant, and also surpasses the fine (patch-wise) variant on consistency metrics despite the fine variant having slightly lower RMSE on MVS-Synth (0.9454 vs. 0.8944 for PatchFusion).

## Relation to Prior Work

PatchFusion extends the tile-based depth estimation paradigm introduced by BoostingDepth [27], which independently processes image patches and merges them, but departs from it in three key ways: adding high-level feature guidance to the fusion network, introducing a Global-to-Local (G2L) module built on Swin Transformer layers to preserve global context, and enforcing patch-overlap consistency during both training and inference. The framework is model-agnostic and is demonstrated on top of ZoeDepth [3], positioning itself as a drop-in enhancement over any pre-trained low-resolution metric depth estimator. It contrasts with alternative high-resolution strategies—guided depth super-resolution (Graph-GDSR) and implicit function approaches (SMD-Net)—by avoiding error propagation from downsampling and the loss of high-frequency detail inherent in those pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap between the ~0.2 MP input resolution of SOTA depth models and the 4K–8K resolutions of modern cameras, delivering 17.3% and 29.4% RMSE reductions on UnrealStereo4K and MVS-Synth respectively, all without post-processing steps like scale optimization or Gaussian blending. The end-to-end nature (no heuristic patch selection, no post-optimization) makes the framework significantly easier to deploy in production pipelines, and the demonstrated integration with ControlNet for depth-guided text-to-image generation highlights broader generative-modeling utility. Open questions include computational cost scaling with patch count, the absence of large-scale real-world high-resolution depth training data, and whether the consistency-aware training strategy transfers to other tile-based tasks beyond depth.
