## Problem

Existing unsupervised semantic segmentation methods built on self-supervised Vision Transformers produce patch-level features that lack explicit object-level semantic encoding, causing complex objects with diverse internal structures (e.g., a blanket with varying stripe patterns) to be fragmented into multiple segments or merged with neighboring objects. The paper addresses this gap by introducing object-centric representation learning that imposes intra- and inter-image object-feature consistency without any pixel-level annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240959/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images resized and five-cropped to 244×244, processed through a frozen DINO-pretrained ViT backbone to extract hierarchical attention key features from the last three transformer layers. At inference, the learned semantic features are clustered via minibatch K-means (cosine distance) and matched to ground-truth classes using the Hungarian algorithm, with Dense-CRF post-processing applied to the final segmentation map. A photometric augmentation generates a structurally identical augmented view used during training for cross-view consistency.

## Method

EAGLE introduces EiCue, a spectral cue derived by constructing a symmetric normalized graph Laplacian from two components: a color affinity matrix (RBF kernel on RGB patch distances with spatial constraints) and a semantic similarity matrix (S·Sᵀ from a learnable two-layer MLP segmentation head applied to concatenated ViT key features). Eigendecomposition of the Laplacian yields k eigenvectors (k=4, selected via eigengap analysis), which are clustered through a differentiable K-means with learnable centers to produce per-patch object assignments. Guided by these assignments, the method computes medoid-based object prototypes from projected features and applies an object-centric contrastive loss (ObjNCELoss) that pulls each patch toward its object prototype while pushing it away from other objects, enforced in both intra-image and inter-image (augmented view) directions. The total objective combines ObjNCELoss, a correspondence distillation loss from STEGO, and the eigen clustering loss, with a curriculum that gradually increases the contrastive loss weight during training.

## Results

On COCO-Stuff with ViT-S/8, EAGLE achieves 64.2% unsupervised accuracy and 27.2 mIoU, surpassing STEGO (48.3% Acc, 24.5 mIoU) by +15.9 Acc and +2.7 mIoU, and HP (57.2% Acc, 24.6 mIoU) by +7.0 Acc and +2.6 mIoU. In the linear probe setting, EAGLE reaches 76.8% Acc and 43.9 mIoU, outperforming STEGO by +2.4 Acc and +5.6 mIoU. On Cityscapes with ViT-B/8, EAGLE attains 22.1 mIoU unsupervised (vs. STEGO's 21.0 and HP's 18.4) and 33.4 mIoU linear, balancing both accuracy and mIoU where prior methods trade off between the two.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGLE consistently outperforms all compared baselines across both datasets and both evaluation protocols. On COCO-Stuff (ViT-S/8), EAGLE beats STEGO [15] by +15.9 Acc and +2.7 mIoU unsupervised, and HP [43] by +7.0 Acc and +2.6 mIoU; in the linear probe it gains +5.6 mIoU over STEGO and +1.2 mIoU over HP. On Cityscapes (ViT-B/8), EAGLE improves unsupervised mIoU over STEGO [15] by +1.1 and over HP [43] by +3.7, while matching HP's unsupervised accuracy (79.4 vs. 79.5) and exceeding both in linear mIoU (33.4 vs. 26.8 and 33.0). Against TransFGU [52], EAGLE gains +11.5 Acc and +9.7 mIoU on COCO-Stuff (ViT-S/8) and +3.9 Acc and +2.9 mIoU on Cityscapes (ViT-S/8). Compared to SlotCon [50], which also targets object-level representations, EAGLE achieves +21.8 mIoU and +21.8 Acc on COCO-Stuff unsupervised. Notably, on Cityscapes where class imbalance typically forces a trade-off between accuracy and mIoU, EAGLE is the only method to simultaneously lead in both metrics for the ViT-B/8 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGLE sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: (1) pretrained-feature-based USS frameworks like STEGO and HP, which distill DINO ViT features into semantic labels via correspondence or contrastive losses but operate at the patch level without explicit object semantics, and (2) spectral clustering methods (Ng et al., Melas-Kyriazi et al.) that exploit eigendecomposition of graph Laplacians to capture non-linear structure. The key departure is making the spectral cue *learnable and dynamic*: unlike Deep Spectral Methods which apply eigendecomposition to static feature vectors, EAGLE routes features through a trainable segmentation head before constructing the Laplacian, and couples the resulting EiCue with a novel object-centric contrastive loss (ObjNCELoss) that enforces intra- and inter-image object-feature consistency via medoid-based prototypes. This distinguishes it from slot-based object-centric methods (SlotCon, DINOSAUR) that rely on learned slots rather than spectral structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and persistent weakness in USS: patch-level features from self-supervised ViTs lack coherent object-level semantics, causing complex objects (e.g., a striped blanket) to be fragmented or merged with neighboring segments. EiCue provides a principled, label-free mechanism to recover object structure, and the object-centric contrastive loss gives a clean formulation for enforcing cross-image semantic consistency. Limitations include the reliance on a frozen DINO backbone (so gains are bounded by upstream pretraining quality), the computational cost of eigendecomposition on N×N matrices, and the heuristic eigengap-based selection of k. Open questions include whether the spectral cue can be made more efficient for high-resolution images, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D segmentation, and whether the object-level consistency loss generalizes beyond photometric augmentations to structural ones.
