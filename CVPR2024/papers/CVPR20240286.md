## Problem

Existing prompt-based continual learning methods suffer from two key limitations: (1) task-specific and task-shared prompts are learned in separate layers, ignoring their interaction, and (2) a fixed number of prompts is learned per task regardless of similarity to previous tasks, causing overfitting on fine-grained or highly similar task sequences. Additionally, the lack of fine-grained layer-specific prompt mechanisms limits the expressiveness of prompts for continual learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input, processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone into 16×16 patches, and outputs a class label prediction. Prompts are injected into 7 of the 12 transformer layers (determined by ablation). The text modality is used only at training time: GPT-3 generates class-level visual attribute descriptions for each incoming task, which are embedded via BERT to compute task similarity and determine how many new prompt generators to learn. At inference, only the image is required—no task ID or text input is needed.

## Method

ConvPrompt generates prompts via convolution over layer-wise shared embedding matrices (SE) that persist across all tasks. For each head and layer, M single-channel convolution kernels (prompt generators) are applied to the SE to produce M prompt components, which are then combined via input-dependent weights derived from cosine similarity between a projected [CLS] token and learnable prompt keys. This single-pass design (using each layer's own [CLS] embedding) avoids the two-pass overhead of CODA-Prompt. A GPT-3-driven task similarity measure dynamically sets the number of new prompt generators per task as J_t = (1 − sim_t)·J_max, reducing parameters when tasks are similar. L1 regularization on the shared embeddings and projection network parameters prevents overwriting of previously learned inter-task knowledge, while previously learned prompt generators are frozen to preserve task-specific information.

## Results

On the 10-task Split CIFAR-100, ConvPrompt achieves 88.87% average accuracy (A_T) with 4.75% forgetting (F_T), outperforming the prior SOTA CODA-Prompt (87.00% A_T, 4.78% F_T) while using only 2.0% trainable parameters versus CODA-Prompt's 4.6%. On Split CUB-200 (fine-grained), ConvPrompt reaches 80.2% A_T and 5.6% F_T, a 5.8-point accuracy gain over CODA-Prompt (74.40%). On ImageNet-R 10-task, ConvPrompt scores 77.86% A_T and 4.33% F_T versus CODA-Prompt's 74.24% and 4.92%, and the advantage grows to ~4.2 points at 20 tasks (75.1% vs 70.86%). Overall, ConvPrompt improves SOTA by ~3% on average across all three datasets while requiring roughly 40% fewer trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConvPrompt outperforms all seven baselines on both CIFAR-100 and CUB-200. Against the rehearsal-based ER (Chaudhry et al.), ConvPrompt gains ~6.6 points in A_T on CIFAR-100 (88.87 vs 82.30) and ~19.5 points on CUB-200 (80.2 vs 60.73), while also reducing forgetting from 16.30 to 4.75 on CIFAR-100. The regularization-based LwF (Li & Hoiem) is far behind at 64.56% A_T on CIFAR-100, confirming that prompt-based methods substantially outperform parameter-regularization approaches. Among prompt-based methods, ConvPrompt beats L2P (Wang et al.) by 6.1 points on CIFAR-100 and 18.0 points on CUB-200, and beats DualPrompt (Wang et al.) by 3.8 and 14.2 points respectively. The language-guided plugin LGCL (Khan et al.) improves L2P and DualPrompt modestly (e.g., DualPrompt+LGCL reaches 87.23% on CIFAR-100) but still trails ConvPrompt by 1.6 points. The closest competitor, CODA-Prompt (Smith et al.), is outperformed by ~1.9 points on CIFAR-100 and ~5.8 points on CUB-200, with ConvPrompt using less than half the trainable parameters (2.0% vs 4.6%). Finally, when combined with the full fine-tuning approach SLCA (Zhang et al.), ConvPrompt further improves SLCA by 1–2 points on ImageNet-R and CUB-200, though it is slightly below SLCA on CIFAR-100 (90.60 vs 91.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

ConvPrompt extends the prompt-based rehearsal-free continual learning line initiated by L2P, DualPrompt, and CODA-Prompt, which attach learnable vectors to frozen ViT self-attention layers. The key architectural departure is replacing directly-learned prompt components (CODA-Prompt) with prompts generated via convolution over task-shared embedding matrices, thereby coupling task-specific and task-shared knowledge within a single operation rather than in separate layers. Additionally, the paper introduces LLM-driven (GPT-3) class-attribute similarity to dynamically control the number of prompt generators per task, a mechanism not previously explored in continual learning. The single-pass prompt weighting using per-layer [CLS] tokens also contrasts with CODA-Prompt's two-pass design that relies on the final [CLS] embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of convolutional prompt generation and LLM-guided task similarity offers a practical recipe for parameter-efficient rehearsal-free CIL, achieving ~3% accuracy gains over CODA-Prompt with roughly 40% fewer trainable parameters and lower inference MACs. The LLM-based task similarity, while computationally cheap, opens a direction for leveraging external world knowledge in CL beyond mere prompt content. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/16 and three datasets, a fixed kernel size and prompt length that may not transfer to other backbones, and the LLM component's modest marginal contribution (~0.7% over simpler class-label similarity). Open questions include scalability to very long task sequences, applicability to other architectures (e.g., CNNs, larger ViTs), and whether the convolutional inductive bias generalizes beyond the tested kernel sizes.
