## Problem

Existing point cloud upsampling methods based on the FE-FX-CR paradigm only exploit spatial information and neglect the underlying topology of the shape, resulting in surface outliers, non-uniform distributions, and lost structural details. Moreover, most of these approaches require paired dense ground truth for supervised training, rendering them inapplicable to real-world scanned data where such pairs are unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241382/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse point cloud of N points in R³ and produces a dense upsampled point cloud of rN points, where r is the upsampling ratio (tested at 4×, 8×, and 16×). A key design choice is that the initial sparse input itself serves as the geometric reference for the Chamfer Distance loss, eliminating the need for a separate dense ground truth. The output is further regularized by a uniformity loss that enforces both local and non-local point spacing consistency across spherical patches.

## Method

SPU-PMD replaces the conventional FE-FX-CR pipeline with a cascaded sequence of mesh interpolators and deformers that progressively refine a deformable mesh to guide point placement. Each deformer operates in two stages: deformation A expands features via a Point Transformer encoder and a Recurrent Feature Aggregation (RFA) module—a GRU variant that applies its reset gate to current features rather than historical memory to prevent contradictory motion—then estimates node displacements through a Gate-based Coordinate Reconstruction (GCR) unit that jointly decides which points move and by how much. Deformation B further refines the mesh to close surface holes and improve uniformity. The interpolator associates upsampled points with features from their nearest spatial neighbors rather than duplicating latent codes, tying feature expansion to geometric topology. Training is fully self-supervised, combining Chamfer Distance (using the sparse input as reference) with a two-part uniformity loss applied at each deformation stage.

## Results

On the PU1K dataset (sparse 512-point input), SPU-PMD achieves CD 1.690×10⁻³, HD 13.568×10⁻³, and P2F 4.082×10⁻³, outperforming all supervised and unsupervised baselines on HD and P2F. At the dense 2048-point setting, it reaches CD 0.544×10⁻³, HD 4.926×10⁻³, and P2F 1.861×10⁻³. On PU-GAN, the model trained on PU1K generalizes well, achieving HD 10.603×10⁻³ and P2F 6.554×10⁻³ (sparse input), surpassing all methods except PU-CRN on CD. The method is self-supervised (no ground-truth dense point sets required), yet matches or exceeds supervised approaches on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPU-PMD is compared against six supervised methods (PU-Net, MPU, PU-GAN, Dis-PU, PU-GCN, PU-CRN) and two unsupervised methods (SAPCU, SPU-Net). On HD and P2F, SPU-PMD outperforms all eight baselines across every input density on PU1K; for example, at sparse 512 input it achieves HD 13.568 vs. PU-CRN's 18.835 (a 28% reduction) and P2F 4.082 vs. PU-CRN's 5.161. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is CD, where PU-CRN (supervised) is marginally better (1.611 vs. 1.690 at 512 input; 0.499 vs. 0.544 at 2048 input). Among unsupervised methods, SPU-PMD dominates SAPCU and SPU-Net by large margins—e.g., HD 13.568 vs. 30.237 (SAPCU) and 63.031 (SPU-Net) at sparse input. On the PU-GAN dataset, SPU-PMD trained on PU1K generalizes to unseen data, achieving HD 10.603 and P2F 6.554 (sparse), beating all baselines except PU-CRN on CD. On real-world KITTI LiDAR data, SPU-PMD visually preserves structural details (fences, cars) better than all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPU-PMD departs from the dominant FE-FX-CR (Feature Extraction–Feature Expansion–Coordinate Reconstruction) pipeline established by PU-Net, PU-GCN, and PU-CRN, reframing upsampling as progressive mesh deformation rather than direct coordinate regression. It inherits the self-supervised training philosophy of SPU-Net and SAPCU (no paired ground truth required) but replaces their reconstruction-overlap or implicit-surface strategies with an explicit mesh interpolation-and-deformation cascade. The encoder borrows Point Transformer for local shape context, and the Recurrent Feature Aggregation module is a GRU variant repurposed to track multi-step deformation history, linking the broader recurrent-network literature to the geometric mesh-editing tradition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for dense ground-truth pairs, SPU-PMD makes high-fidelity upsampling applicable to real-world LiDAR and RGB-D scans where paired data is unavailable, as demonstrated qualitatively on KITTI. The mesh-deformation framing offers a new inductive bias—topology-aware point placement—that could inspire hybrid approaches combining explicit geometric priors with learned features. However, quantitative evaluation is limited to small synthetic datasets (PU1K, PU-GAN) and the KITTI comparison is purely visual, leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale scenes, robustness to heavily corrupted inputs, and whether the mesh-construction step generalizes to non-manifold or open-surface geometries.
