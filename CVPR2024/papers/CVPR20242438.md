## Problem

Standard VLMs trained via contrastive learning on noisy web-scraped image-text pairs develop global, bag-of-words representations that lack spatial and compositional understanding, perform poorly on dense prediction tasks, and fail to generalize to non-English languages. While large unimodal foundational models (e.g., DINOv2, SAM, XGLM) capture rich visual and linguistic structure, they cannot directly align image and text. The paper addresses how to build a VLM that inherits the solid unimodal understanding of these teachers while still learning cross-modal alignment, without the heavy computational overhead of prior auxiliary-objective methods like SLIP or MaskCLIP.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed by a ViT-B/16 encoder into 196 patch tokens) and a text caption tokenized by the frozen teacher's tokenizer (XGLM-1.7B), with the text input being a learned linear projection from the teacher's frozen word embeddings rather than a learned embedding table. The primary output is a shared L2-normalized embedding vector per modality (mean of final-layer token features followed by a learned projection), used for contrastive alignment. Additionally, the per-patch token outputs from the vision encoder serve as dense features for zero-shot and linear-probe semantic segmentation, and the vision encoder's final representations are compatible with SAM's frozen decoder for instance segmentation.

## Method

SF-CLIP trains a ViT-B/16 vision encoder and a non-causal CLIP text encoder with a combined objective: the standard symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss plus masked feature distillation losses in both modalities. In the distillation step, the student receives a randomly masked version of the input (up to 25% of text tokens masked; no vision masking by default for ViT-B) and is trained via L2 loss to match the layer-normalized output representations of frozen teachers (SAM-H/16 + DINOv2-L/14 for vision, XGLM-1.7B for text) on both masked and unmasked positions. Two key design choices enable efficiency and multilinguality: (1) distillation is applied only to a random 12.5% subset of each mini-batch, keeping training throughput at 1.20× vanilla CLIP versus 2.28× for full-batch distillation; and (2) the text encoder inherits the teacher's word embeddings through a learned linear projection, which transfers the LLM's multilingual vocabulary without any non-English paired training data. The student and teacher must produce the same number of tokens, enforced by using the teacher's tokenizer.

## Results

On ImageNet zero-shot classification, LaSF-CLIP (trained on CC-12M) achieves 53.6% top-1 accuracy, a 5.2-point improvement over LaCLIP (48.4%) and 13.4 points over vanilla CLIP (40.2%). On YFCC-15M, SF-CLIP reaches 45.2% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy, surpassing SLIP (42.8%) and MaskCLIP (44.5%). For image-text retrieval on Flickr30K, LaSF-CLIP achieves 71.8 R@1 (I2T) and 59.9 R@1 (T2I), substantially outperforming LaCLIP (63.9 / 51.6). Training throughput is maintained at 2750 samples/sec (vs. 3300 for vanilla CLIP) thanks to batch subsampling of the distillation objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF-CLIP outperforms vanilla CLIP [44] across all evaluated tasks: on YFCC-15M it gains +7.6 points ImageNet zero-shot (45.2 vs 37.6) and +15.8 R@1 on Flickr30K I2T (68.7 vs 52.9). Against SLIP [37], SF-CLIP wins on ImageNet zero-shot (45.2 vs 42.8) and retrieval, while MaskCLIP [9] slightly edges SF-CLIP on Flickr30K I2T R@1 (70.1 vs 68.7) but SF-CLIP leads on ImageNet zero-shot (45.2 vs 44.5) and MS-COCO retrieval. On CC-12M, LaSF-CLIP achieves a decisive 5.2-point gain over LaCLIP [15] on ImageNet zero-shot (53.6 vs 48.4) and 7.9 R@1 on Flickr30K I2T (71.8 vs 63.9). Compared to SLIP$_{100ep}$ [17] (trained 4× longer), SF-CLIP still wins on ImageNet zero-shot (45.2 vs 45.0) and retrieval. For semantic segmentation, SF-CLIP's linear probe on Pascal-VOC reaches 69.4 mIoU, approaching CLIPDataComp-1B [18] (70.7) trained on 1B images, while zero-shot instance segmentation with a frozen SAM decoder [26] yields 45.0 mAP versus SAM's 57.8 mAP (trained on SA-1B). On multilingual retrieval (XTD10), SF-CLIP dramatically outperforms CLIP and SLIP in all non-English languages (e.g., Russian: 32.5 vs 1.1 R@5), a capability neither baseline possesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

SF-CLIP extends the CLIP contrastive pretraining paradigm by adding masked knowledge distillation from frozen unimodal foundation models (DINOv2/SAM for vision, XGLM for text), positioning itself between the self-supervision approaches of SLIP and MaskCLIP and the data-augmentation approach of LaCLIP. Unlike prior work that leverages pretrained vision encoders (LiT, Three Towers, SAM-CLIP), SF-CLIP distills from both modalities simultaneously and inherits the text teacher's word embeddings via a learned projection, which is a key departure enabling multilingual capability without multilingual training data. The batch subsampling strategy for the distillation objective is a practical departure from methods like SLIP and MaskCLIP that apply auxiliary losses to the full batch, trading a small performance cost for significantly higher training throughput.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SF-CLIP demonstrates that unimodal foundation models can serve as effective teachers for VLM training, offering a principled alternative to self-supervision or data re-captioning that avoids their computational overhead and hallucination risks. The emergent multilingual retrieval from a single-language training set via word-embedding inheritance is a practical path toward accessible multilingual VLMs without the cost of multilingual paired data. Key limitations include the requirement to share the text teacher's tokenizer, the inability to fine-tune an existing CLIP checkpoint, and the use of linear projections rather than transformer decoders for distillation, which may cap representational capacity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger ViT-L/H architectures and whether the multilingual generalization holds for truly low-resource languages beyond those covered by XGLM.
