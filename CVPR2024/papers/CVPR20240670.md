## Problem

Instance ImageGoal Navigation requires an agent to find a specific object instance from a goal image while rejecting visually similar distractors (e.g., multiple beds in a house). Existing ImageGoal Navigation methods adopt a simple Exploration-Exploitation paradigm that treats target identification as a one-shot binary decision, ignoring the sequential nature of confirming an object from varying distances and viewpoints. This paper addresses the gap by formulating instance re-identification as a sequential decision problem inspired by the human behavior of 'getting closer to confirm.'

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 640×480 egocentric RGB image, a corresponding depth image, a 512×512 goal image (with different camera height, look-at-angle, and FOV than the agent's camera), and sensor pose readings at each timestep. It outputs continuous navigation actions (linear velocity, angular velocity, camera pitch velocity, and velocity stop, each scaled to [-1, 1]) along with an internally maintained global semantic map and a goal map that guides the local path planner. The agent operates as a rigid-body cylinder (Hello Robot Stretch embodiment) in the Habitat simulator with a maximum linear speed of 35 cm/frame.

## Method

IEVE is a modular framework with five components: Instance Classification (Swin Transformer or segmentation-based), Online Mapping (Mask RCNN/InternImage segmentation + voxelized depth into a global semantic map), Switch Policy, Goal Mapping Policy, and Local Policy (Fast Matching Method). The core novelty is the Switch Policy, which uses DISK keypoint extraction and LightGlue matching to compute the number of matched keypoints ω between the goal image and current observation, then applies a distance-adaptive threshold function f_switch that sets higher keypoint thresholds at larger Euclidean distances and lower thresholds at closer ranges. This enables three operational modes: Exploration (RL-trained policy maximizing coverage), Verification (approaching a potential target for closer inspection), and Exploitation (navigating to a confirmed target). Only the Instance Classification and Exploration Policy modules require training, while the Switch Policy, Goal Mapping Policy, and Local Policy are rule-based, making the framework computationally efficient.

## Results

On the HM3D-SEM validation split, IEVE with Mask RCNN achieves 0.684 Success and 0.241 SPL, while IEVE with InternImage reaches 0.702 Success and 0.252 SPL. These results surpass the previous state-of-the-art Mod-IIN (0.561 Success, 0.233 SPL) by 0.123 and 0.141 in Success respectively, and by 0.008 and 0.019 in SPL. The method also outperforms the end-to-end RL baseline by 8.24× in Success (0.684 vs. 0.083) and the fine-tuned OVRL-v2 by 0.436 in Success (0.684 vs. 0.248).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IEVE decisively outperforms all three baselines on both Success and SPL. Against the end-to-end RL baseline (Krantz et al. 2022), IEVE with Mask RCNN achieves 8.24× higher Success (0.684 vs. 0.083), highlighting the limitations of fully end-to-end RL for instance-level navigation. Against the fine-tuned OVRL-v2 (Partsey et al. 2022), IEVE gains 0.436 in Success (0.684 vs. 0.248) and 0.123 in SPL (0.241 vs. 0.118), showing that the modular Exploration-Verification-Exploitation paradigm is far more effective than end-to-end visual representation learning for distinguishing similar instances. Against the previous state-of-the-art Mod-IIN (Krantz et al. 2023), IEVE improves Success by 0.123 with Mask RCNN (0.684 vs. 0.561) and by 0.141 with InternImage (0.702 vs. 0.561), while SPL improves modestly from 0.233 to 0.241/0.252. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that the Verification stage (EVE vs. EE) contributes a substantial 0.076–0.080 Success gain over a simple Exploration-Exploitation policy, validating the core design choice of the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard Exploration-Exploitation paradigm used in prior ImageGoal Navigation and IIN methods (notably Mod-IIN [28]) by inserting an intermediate Verification stage that treats target confirmation as a sequential decision problem rather than a one-shot binary match. The switch policy leverages local feature matching (DISK + LightGlue) with distance-adaptive thresholds, a design informed by the observation that re-identification confidence decays rapidly with Euclidean distance. The framework is modular and lightweight, requiring training only for the instance classifier and exploration policy, in contrast to end-to-end RL baselines that need billions of steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IIN task is more realistic than classical ImageGoal Navigation because the goal camera is disentangled from the agent camera in height, look-at-angle, and field-of-view, mirroring a user-supplied photo. The verification step directly addresses the core difficulty of distinguishing visually similar instances (e.g., two beds) from a distance, where a single-frame comparison is unreliable. Open questions include whether the three-stage EVEVE paradigm generalizes beyond IIN to other instance-specific navigation or search tasks, and whether richer visual reasoning (e.g., multi-view accumulation) can push the remaining 30% failure rate.
