## Problem

Incremental semantic segmentation of histopathological nuclei suffers from catastrophic forgetting when new cell types are added, and existing solutions either require access to old training data (impractical due to patient privacy) or rely on supervised detectors to identify future classes (requiring expensive pixel-level annotations). This paper addresses the gap of performing incremental nuclei segmentation without old-class samples while detecting visually similar future classes in a fully unsupervised manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241689/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes H&E-stained histopathological RGB images uniformly cropped to 320×320 pixels as input and produces pixel-level segmentation masks for all previously seen cell types. In the incremental setting, at each step only newly introduced classes carry ground-truth annotations; all old and future classes are treated as background, with the model expected to maintain accurate segmentation across all seen classes after each update. The backbone is a ResNet-101 pretrained on ImageNet, and the segmentation head outputs per-pixel logits over the cumulative class set.

## Method

The framework combines two key mechanisms on top of a PLOP+EWF base. First, a future-class awareness module computes class centroids (prototypes) in the old model's deepest feature space via masked average pooling, aggregates them across images with a running average, and then identifies background pixels whose features are sufficiently similar to any centroid (via an l2-normalized affinity matrix) to re-label them as 'unknown' for joint training. Second, a compatibility-inspired distillation scheme expands the old model's output to match the new model's class count by zero-padding new-class channels, then applies forward compatibility (transferring the old model's background and future-class predictions into the new-class prediction to inherit pre-learned knowledge) and backward compatibility (standard KD on background pixels) to resolve alignment conflicts and background shift. Feature-level retention is handled by multi-scale Local POD distillation, and final parameters are fused via Endpoints Weight Fusion. The total training loss is L_ce + L_cpd + λ·L_pod with λ=0.0001.

## Results

On MoNuSAC (the primary dataset), the proposed method achieves the highest mean mDice across all four incremental settings: 69.44 (1-1, 4 tasks), 70.16 (2-1, 3 tasks), 71.32 (2-2, 2 tasks), and 72.36 (3-1, 2 tasks). On the most challenging 1-1 setting, it outperforms the strongest baseline EWF by 3.54% on old-class mDice (68.11 vs. 64.57) and by 2.38% on mean mDice (69.44 vs. 67.06). On CoNSeP, the method achieves 69.44 mean mDice (1-1) and 71.08 (2-1), surpassing EWF by 0.36% and 0.64% respectively in mean mDice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 8 baselines spanning regularization (ILT, MiB, SDR), distillation (PLOP, REMINDER, CoNuSeg, IDEC), and weight-fusion (EWF) approaches. On MoNuSAC, the proposed method wins on mean mDice in all four settings, with the largest margin over EWF on the 1-1 protocol (+2.38% mean, +3.54% old-class). IDEC is the closest competitor on new-class performance (69.41 vs. 69.86 on 1-1), while EWF leads on old-class mDice in the 2-2 setting (65.90 vs. 65.43) but is still surpassed in mean. On CoNSeP, the method edges out EWF by 0.36% (1-1) and 0.64% (2-1) in mean mDice, with a notable +1.65% gain on new classes in the 2-1 setting (64.65 vs. 63.00). The gap to the joint-training upper bound narrows from ~5% (1-1) to ~2.1% (3-1) on MoNuSAC, indicating the method scales well with fewer incremental steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PLOP's Local POD feature distillation and pseudo-labeling strategy, and on EWF's endpoint weight fusion, using them as the base pipeline upon which two new components are layered. It departs from prior future-class pre-learning approaches (SSUL-M's salient object detector, MicroSeg's Mask2Former proposals) by identifying potential future classes in a fully unsupervised manner through class-centroid similarity in the old model's representation space, eliminating the need for annotated data. The compatibility-inspired distillation, framed via a software-interface analogy, extends standard knowledge distillation by redistributing the old model's background and future-class predictions into the new class channels, addressing the alignment conflict and background shift that arise when the new model has more output channels than the old one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical clinical bottleneck: updating nuclei segmentation models with new cell types without re-annotating or re-accessing old patient data, which is constrained by privacy and cost. The unsupervised future-class awareness is particularly relevant in histopathology where visually similar cell types (e.g., epithelia vs. lymphocytes) are common, but the authors acknowledge the approach may degrade when future classes are visually dissimilar to learned ones. An open question is whether the centroid-based similarity mechanism generalizes beyond histopathology to other incremental segmentation domains where class appearance overlap is less pronounced.
