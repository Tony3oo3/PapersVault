## Problem

State-of-the-art Video Object Segmentation methods require dense per-object mask annotations for every training frame, making dataset creation prohibitively expensive and limiting existing VOS datasets to a few thousand videos. This annotation bottleneck hinders the community's push toward larger-scale, language-connected video understanding. The paper asks whether spatio-temporally sparse point annotations can serve as a practical weak supervision signal that preserves most of the segmentation quality while drastically reducing annotation cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241831/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, each video provides a sparse subset of frames (≈10 out of T) with per-object point sets containing foreground, background, and ambiguous points (at least 20 px apart), replacing the conventional dense masks. At test time, the method receives only 1–10 foreground points on the reference frame (no mask) and must produce temporally consistent binary segmentation masks for every frame of the video. Because the points originate from VidLN mouse traces, each point is additionally linked to a noun in a natural-language caption, making the annotations multi-modal (video + text).

## Method

The paper introduces a semi-automatic annotation pipeline that converts VidLN mouse traces into verified point annotations: DynaMITe generates an initial pseudo-mask from a mouse trace segment, STCN propagates it across all frames to produce probability maps, and points are sampled from high/low/uncertain probability regions for rapid human verification (~1 s per point vs. ~80 s per mask). Two baselines are established: (i) Point-STCN, a minimal adaptation of STCN that consumes point supervision directly during training and point initialization at test time, and (ii) a pseudo-mask baseline that first converts points to masks via DynaMITe (or SAM) and trains standard STCN with a Huberised cross-entropy loss for robustness. Ablations on DAVIS show that 10 points on 10 frames saturate performance, and the pseudo-mask variant recovers >90 % of fully-supervised STCN quality (76.9 vs. 85.3 J&F) despite using only sparse, temporally-sparse point supervision at both training and test time.

## Results

On the DAVIS validation set, the Point-VOS pseudo-mask baseline (STCN trained on pseudo-masks generated from points, with pseudo-mask initialization at test time) achieves 76.9% J&F, reaching over 90% of the fully-supervised STCN performance (85.3% J&F). The point-only variant (Point-STCN) achieves 67.7% J&F, corresponding to roughly 80% of the fully-supervised quality. On the PV-YT benchmark, the best pseudo-mask configuration (PV-Oops+PV-Kinetics pre-training with SAM masks and fine-tuning) reaches 73.7% G, and on the PV-Oops benchmark, fine-tuning on PV-Oops data improves mean J&F from 57.1% to 64.9%. For the Video Narrative Grounding task, adding PV-Kinetics data improves UVO-VNG from 46.4% to 52.8% J&F (+6.4 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against fully-supervised STCN [Cheng et al., NeurIPS 2021], which achieves 85.3% J&F on DAVIS with dense mask supervision at both training and test time. The Point-VOS pseudo-mask baseline (setup ③) closes most of this gap, reaching 76.9% J&F (>90% of the fully-supervised score) despite a three-fold disadvantage: weak point supervision instead of masks during training, temporal sparsity (10 frames instead of all), and sparse point initialization instead of dense masks at test time. The point-only variant (setup ②) achieves 67.7% J&F (~80% of fully-supervised), showing that direct point-based training is substantially weaker than the pseudo-mask approach. The hybrid setup (④, masks for training but points at test time) scores 78.3%, indicating that the training supervision change (masks→points) causes a larger quality drop than the test-time change (masks→points). The paper also demonstrates that adding the newly annotated PV-Oops and PV-Kinetics data as pre-training consistently improves downstream performance: on PV-DAVIS, mean J&F rises from 65.6% (static pre-training only) to 71.6% (with both datasets and fine-tuning), and on PV-YT, G improves from 63.0% to 68.7% (73.7% with SAM masks).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on STCN (Cheng et al., NeurIPS 2021) as the backbone VOS method and DynaMITe (Rana et al., ICCV 2023) for interactive pseudo-mask generation, but departs fundamentally from the dense-mask supervision paradigm that all prior VOS methods (STCN, XMem, AOT, etc.) require for both training and test-time initialization. It also draws on point-supervised image segmentation (Cheng et al., 2022; Benenson & Ferrari, 2022) and the VidLN corpus (Mahadevan et al., CVPR 2023) for mouse-trace-based object localization, but is the first to combine spatio-temporally sparse point annotations for both training and testing in a video segmentation setting. Unlike TAP-Vid, which requires expensive point correspondences, and PET/BURST, which use points only at test time, Point-VOS uses random (non-corresponding) points on a sparse subset of frames throughout the pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× annotation speedup (0.95 s/point vs. ~80 s/mask) and the resulting 32K-video, 133K-object datasets address the central bottleneck limiting VOS dataset scale, making it feasible to create VOS benchmarks an order of magnitude larger than prior efforts. The finding that pseudo-masks from just 10 points on 10 frames recover over 90% of fully-supervised STCN performance (76.9 vs. 85.3 J&F on DAVIS) suggests that the field may not need dense masks for most practical VOS training, potentially reshaping how future datasets are annotated. Open questions include whether the 10-point/10-frame sweet spot generalizes to longer or more complex videos, whether the remaining ~8-point gap to full supervision can be closed with better pseudo-mask generators, and whether the multi-modal (point-to-noun) annotations can support truly open-vocabulary VOS beyond the current VNG evaluation.
