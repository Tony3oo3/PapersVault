## Problem

Existing MOT trackers rely on either motion or appearance cues (or a manually tuned blend of both), making them brittle across diverse scenarios characterized by varying motion complexity, target density, small targets, frame rates, and variation amplitude. No prior method generalizes well without scenario-specific hypothesis tuning or extensive engineering, limiting real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241706/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of consecutive RGB frames (t−1 and t) from a video stream, first processed by a YOLOX detector to yield N detections in frame t and M existing tracklets up to frame t−1. Features are extracted at 1/8 spatial resolution (H×W×D), and the association module outputs a tracklet-detection relation matrix P^rela ∈ R^{N×M} used for Hungarian matching. The RoIAlign grid size is dataset-dependent (3×3 for DanceTrack/SportsMOT, 2×2 for BDD100K/MOT17/MOT20), and the correlation pyramid depth S=3 with search radius R=4 are fixed hyperparameters.

## Method

GeneralTrack replaces instance-level association with a 'point-wise to instance-wise relation' pipeline in three stages. First, a Feature Relation Extractor computes a full 4D correlation volume (H×W×H×W) between dense CNN features of consecutive frames, capturing per-pixel visual similarity. Second, a Multi-scale Point-region Relation module pools the volume into a correlation pyramid and searches within center-based neighborhoods at each scale, producing a relation map that implicitly encodes a vision-and-geometry motion template adaptable to arbitrary displacements and frame rates. Third, a Hierarchical Relation Aggregation stage applies RoIAlign to extract part-wise relations for each tracklet, concatenates them with relative-position encodings, and passes through convolutions and an MLP to score each tracklet-detection pair, treating instances as flexible bodies rather than rigid ones. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a weighted binary cross-entropy loss on identity-matched pairs, with the feature extractor pre-trained on KITTI dense-flow/correspondence tasks.

## Results

GeneralTrack achieves state-of-the-art HOTA on four of five benchmarks: 64.0 on MOT17, 74.1 on SportsMOT, 59.2 on DanceTrack, and 63.1 on BDD100K (validation). On MOT17 it also leads in IDF1 (78.3) and MOTA (80.6), while on SportsMOT it attains the best IDF1 (76.4) and MOTA (96.8). On BDD100K test, GeneralTrack ranks 1st on the leaderboard with 57.87 mTETA. The only notable loss is on DanceTrack HOTA (59.2) versus MOTRv2 (69.9), which uses over 200× more training resources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeneralTrack outperforms ByteTrack on all five benchmarks in HOTA (e.g., 64.0 vs 63.1 on MOT17, 74.1 vs 64.1 on SportsMOT, 59.2 vs 47.7 on DanceTrack) and also reduces ID switches substantially (1563 vs 2196 on MOT17). Against GHOST, it wins on BDD100K (63.1 vs 61.7 HOTA), DanceTrack (59.2 vs 56.7), and MOT17 (64.0 vs 62.8), while matching or slightly exceeding on MOT20 (61.4 vs 61.2). Versus OC-SORT, GeneralTrack leads on HOTA for BDD100K, DanceTrack, and MOT17, ties on SportsMOT HOTA (74.1) but wins on IDF1 (76.4 vs 74.0), and trails slightly on MOT20 HOTA (61.4 vs 62.1). The one clear loss is on DanceTrack HOTA against MOTRv2 (59.2 vs 69.9), though the paper notes MOTRv2 requires over 200× more training resources and uses both YOLOX and MOTR. On BDD100K, GeneralTrack surpasses all baselines including MOTRv2 (72.7 IDF1 vs 72.7) and GHOST (63.1 vs 61.7 HOTA), securing 1st place on the leaderboard.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeneralTrack operates within the dominant tracking-by-detection paradigm but departs from prior methods that construct instance-level affinity matrices driven by either motion (e.g., ByteTrack, SORT) or appearance (e.g., DeepSORT, GHOST), or that manually balance the two with scenario-specific weights. Instead, it repurposes the 4D correlation volume machinery from optical flow and geometric correspondence estimation (RAFT, MatchFlow) to build dense point-wise relations between consecutive frames, then aggregates them hierarchically (point → part → instance) into tracklet-detection associations. This 'point-wise to instance-wise' framing is novel in MOT, drawing a direct conceptual bridge from dense correspondence literature to the data association problem, and eliminates the explicit motion-vs-appearance trade-off that has constrained prior trackers' generalizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's systematic identification of five tracking scenario attributes (motion complexity, variation amplitude, target density, small target, frame rate) and their mapping to where motion- vs. appearance-dominated methods fail provides a useful diagnostic framework for the community. The demonstrated domain generalization—training on BDD100K cars and tracking all classes, or transferring from BDD100K to SportsMOT/DanceTrack with only modest performance drops—suggests that low-level point-wise visual features are more transferable than instance-level representations, which could influence future tracker design. However, the method is limited to inter-frame (consecutive) relations and does not yet exploit longer temporal context, and the SOTA margins over strong baselines like GHOST and MOTRv2 are moderate (1–3 points), so the practical advantage over well-tuned existing trackers may be scenario-dependent.
