## Problem

In Non-Exemplar Class Incremental Learning, two critical issues persist beyond what knowledge distillation and prototype preservation address: (1) preserved prototypes of old classes inevitably drift in the feature space as the model is updated, losing their representational accuracy, and (2) features of new classes encroach on similar old-class regions, breaking classification boundaries. The paper identifies these as distinct failure modes that existing NECIL methods overlook.

## Input / Output

The model receives RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet and ImageNet-Subset) and outputs a class label via an expanded classification head. During each incremental stage, only current-stage data is available—no exemplars from prior stages are stored. The sole memory of old classes is a set of per-class feature prototypes (means of extracted features), which are augmented and fed through the classification head alongside new data. The backbone is a ResNet-18 producing 512-dimensional features.

## Method

FCS introduces two complementary components. First, a Feature Calibration Network (FCN), implemented as a single linear layer, is trained via optimal transport: it learns a transport plan mapping features extracted by the old model to those extracted by the new model on current-stage data, minimizing the L2 transport cost. This plan is then applied to calibrate old-class prototypes into the new model's feature space before training the classification head. Second, a Prototype-Involved Contrastive Loss (PIC) extends supervised contrastive learning by treating calibrated old-class prototypes as additional negative references in the denominator, pushing new-class features away from old-class regions while simultaneously compressing intra-class features. The overall objective combines cross-entropy, knowledge distillation, calibrated prototype classification, FCN transport loss, and PIC with weighted coefficients.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, FCS achieves 62.13%, 60.39%, and 58.36% top-1 accuracy for 5, 10, and 20 incremental stages respectively, outperforming the strongest non-exemplar baseline FeTrIL by 4.01%, 2.75%, and 4.69%. On TinyImageNet, FCS reaches 46.04%, 44.95%, and 42.57% (5/10/20 stages), surpassing FeTrIL by 3.12%, 2.54%, and 1.24%. On ImageNet-Subset (10 stages), FCS attains 61.76%, edging out FeTrIL (61.22%) by 0.54%. FCS also reports the lowest average forgetting on CIFAR-100 (12.20% at 5 stages) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCS outperforms all nine compared baselines across every dataset and stage setting. The closest competitor is FeTrIL [24], which freezes the backbone to preserve feature knowledge; FCS leads by 4.01% on CIFAR-100 (5 stages) but only by 0.54% on ImageNet-Subset, where FeTrIL's frozen backbone is more effective on larger data. Among prototype-based methods, PASS [42] and IL2A [41] suffer severe degradation as stages increase (e.g., PASS drops from 56.40% to 46.93% on CIFAR-100 from 5 to 20 stages), whereas FCS degrades more gracefully (62.13% → 58.36%). SSRE [43] and EDG [10] are competitive at 10 stages on CIFAR-100 (56.57% and 54.31%) but fall further behind at 20 stages (51.92% and 49.32% vs. FCS's 58.36%). Exemplar-based methods (iCaRL, EEIL) are substantially weaker, confirming the advantage of the non-exemplar setting when combined with effective anti-forgetting mechanisms. On average forgetting (CIFAR-100), FCS achieves the lowest values (12.20%, 16.70%, 15.90% for 5/10/20 stages), well below the next-best method IL2A (28.54%, 39.29%, 41.27%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NECIL line of work initiated by PASS and IL2A, which rely on prototype augmentation and knowledge distillation to preserve old-class knowledge without exemplars. It departs from prior prototype-based methods (PASS, IL2A, SSRE) by identifying two additional forgetting mechanisms—prototype drift within a single training stage and classification-boundary intersection between old and new classes—that earlier work overlooked. The Feature Calibration Network applies optimal transport (via a learned neural transport plan) to adapt prototypes across model updates, going beyond the between-stage interpolation of semantic drift compensation [38], while the Prototype-Involved Contrastive Loss repurposes prototypes as negative references in a contrastive objective rather than solely for classification-head training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FCS provides a principled, exemplar-free recipe that consistently outperforms prior NECIL methods across three benchmarks, with the FCN's benefit growing as the number of stages increases—suggesting the approach is particularly valuable for long incremental sequences. However, the method is validated only with ResNet-18 on relatively small datasets, and the ImageNet-Subset gain over the frozen-backbone FeTrIL is marginal (0.54%), raising questions about scalability to larger backbones (e.g., ViT) and longer task sequences. An open question is whether the optimal-transport calibration generalizes when the feature-space shift is highly non-linear, since the FCN is ultimately a single linear layer.
