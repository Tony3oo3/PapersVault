## Problem

Standard anomaly detection methods learn a fixed notion of normality and fail when irregularities are conditional to the specific instance, e.g., a three-legged chair may be normal for one design but anomalous for another. This paper introduces a conditional AD task where the definition of regularity is provided by a reference 3D shape, requiring the model to detect subtle geometric anomalies in a query image by comparing it against that shape across two modalities with a significant domain gap (textured photo vs. textureless 3D model).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB query image captured from an unknown viewpoint and N=20 grayscale multi-view images (256×256) rendered from the reference 3D shape at regularly sampled hemisphere viewpoints, along with known camera poses and depth maps for each view. It outputs a binary anomaly score (0 or 1) and, with an added regression head, a bounding box localizing the anomalous region. The reference shape is deliberately represented as textureless multi-view images rather than a mesh or point cloud, simulating a realistic scenario where the 3D model defines geometry but not material or color.

## Method

The Correspondence Matching Transformer (CMT) uses a shared ResNet18-FPN encoder for both query and multi-view images, augmented with a 3D Positional Encoding (Fourier encoding + MLP) that injects world-space 3D coordinates into each multi-view patch, forming a hybrid 2D-3D representation. A Correspondence-Guided Attention (CGA) network of B=3 transformer blocks applies a top-k sparse cross-attention (TKCA) mechanism that restricts each query patch to attend only to the k=100 most relevant multi-view patches, guided by a similarity matrix computed through a learned view-agnostic projection. A View-Agnostic Local Feature Alignment (VLFA) auxiliary task provides dense contrastive supervision: pseudo-correspondences are self-labeled for query-view pairs, while ground-truth correspondences are used for view-view pairs, both driving the shared projection to map same-object-part features to similar points regardless of viewpoint. The total loss combines binary cross-entropy for classification with the two alignment losses (weighted 0.5 each), and the key finding is that none of the three components (CGA, VLFA, 3DPE) alone suffices—their combination is necessary to establish fine-grained cross-modal correspondences from image-level supervision alone.

## Results

On the BrokenChairs-180K test set (26K images from unseen 3D shapes), the full CMT model achieves 84.7 AUC and 75.4% accuracy for conditional anomaly detection. This represents a 9.2-point AUC improvement over the strongest query-only baseline (ViT at 75.4 AUC) and a 7.6-point accuracy gain over the best 2D-3D shape retrieval baseline (Lin et al. at 67.8%). The model also achieves 56.5% average precision for anomaly bounding-box localization, and a perceptual study shows CMT (74.8% accuracy) outperforms human annotators (70.6%) on the same subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMT decisively outperforms all four external baselines. Against the query-only baselines, CMT gains 9.3 AUC points over ResNet18-FPN (84.7 vs. 74.6) and 9.3 AUC points over ViT (84.7 vs. 75.4), confirming that the 3D reference shape is essential for detecting fine-grained geometric anomalies. Against the two 2D-3D shape retrieval methods adapted to this task, CMT achieves 75.4% accuracy versus 64.9% for LFD (Grabner et al., 3DV'19) and 67.8% for Lin et al. (ICCV'21), a margin of 10.5 and 7.6 points respectively. The paper attributes the retrieval methods' underperformance to their reliance on global-level metric learning, which fails to capture the fine-grained local correspondences needed to detect subtle shape deviations. Notably, even a CMT variant without the correspondence-guided attention or 3D positional encoding (A: 76.1 AUC, 66.8% Acc) barely beats the query-only baselines, underscoring that the architectural innovations—not merely access to 3D data—are what drive the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of conditional anomaly detection and 2D-3D cross-modal correspondence learning. It departs from standard AD benchmarks (which define regularity at the class or instance level) by formulating anomaly detection as a comparison between a query image and a reference 3D shape, a setting the authors argue is absent from prior literature. Methodologically, it repurposes ideas from 2D-3D shape retrieval (LFD, cross-modal contrastive learning) and 2D-3D correspondence matching (2D3D-MatchNet, DGC-GNN) but redirects them toward fine-grained local anomaly detection rather than global retrieval or registration. The top-k sparse cross-attention and view-agnostic alignment mechanism are novel architectural contributions that go beyond standard transformer cross-attention by explicitly learning geometric correspondences across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BrokenChairs-180K benchmark is the first large-scale paired 3D-shape-and-image dataset for conditional anomaly detection, providing a concrete testbed for a practically motivated task (manufacturing quality control) that has no prior public benchmark. The human perceptual study (70.6% accuracy) confirms the task is genuinely challenging, and the model's modest 75.4% accuracy leaves substantial room for improvement. Key limitations constrain broader impact: the dataset covers only the 'chair' category, contains only synthetic renders, assumes rigid objects, and includes at most one anomaly per image. Open questions include generalization to other object categories, handling deformable or articulated objects, detecting appearance-based anomalies (fading, discoloration), and deployment on real-world inspection pipelines where reference 3D models may be imperfect.
