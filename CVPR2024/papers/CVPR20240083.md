## Problem

Camera-based 3D semantic scene completion struggles with occluded and shaded regions because existing methods ignore the information missing in those areas, producing distorted geometry. Prior approaches either rely on single-view BEV representations that cannot recover holistic 3D structure for variable-shaped obstacles, or use stereo matching that degrades in non-textured and occluded regions. The paper targets this gap by explicitly modeling how neighboring visible structures and cross-view spatial differences can compensate for invisible regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of stereo RGB images (KITTI cam2 and cam3) cropped to 1280×384 pixels and produces a 256×256×32 voxel grid (0.2 m per voxel) with 21 semantic classes (1 unknown, 1 free, 19 semantic). Internally, a 2D EfficientNetB7 backbone extracts features that are lifted into a 128×128×16 volumetric representation with 128 channels, which is then upsampled to full resolution by a 3D UNet for the final per-voxel prediction.

## Method

Bi-SSC introduces two core modules. The Spatial Sensory Fusion (SSF) module treats geometric features as queries and semantic features as keys/values, applying dual projections (W_in, W_out) to explore inter-image and out-image semantic spaces; an attention mask simulates occluded regions by zeroing attention weights where neighboring receptors disagree, and depth-derived attention scores (softmax over the depth dimension) are added to the attention logits to weight geometrically important pixels. The Cross-view Fusion (CVF) module performs bidirectional cross-attention between left and right SGF outputs, progressively propagating each view's advantages to the other, then refines the result with SSF against stereo-matching depth features. A Mutual Interactive Aggregation (MIA) block (adopted from StereoScene) fuses the refined dual-view features with stereo and geometric features into a final BEV representation, which a 3D UNet decodes into the voxel grid. Training uses a combined loss of semantic cross-entropy, geometric occupancy loss, class-weighted cross-entropy, and binary cross-entropy on depth for 30 epochs with AdamW.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI hidden test set, Bi-SSC achieves 45.10% IoU and 16.73% mIoU, setting a new state-of-the-art among camera-based SSC methods. The method outperforms the strongest stereo baseline StereoScene by +1.76 IoU and +1.36 mIoU, and surpasses the best mono baseline OccFormer by +10.57 IoU and +4.41 mIoU. On the curated SemanticKITTI-Complex subset (300 images emphasizing occlusion and shading), Bi-SSC retains 44.78% IoU and 16.0% mIoU, with only a 0.39 mIoU drop versus the full validation set, demonstrating superior robustness in challenging conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-SSC outperforms all five camera-based baselines on both IoU and mIoU. Against the closest stereo competitor StereoScene [20], Bi-SSC gains +1.76 IoU (43.34→45.10) and +1.36 mIoU (15.36→16.73), with consistent per-class improvements across all 19 semantic categories. Compared to VoxFormer-T [25], which uses up to four temporal stereo image pairs, Bi-SSC still leads by +1.89 IoU and +3.32 mIoU, indicating that the geometric-semantic fusion strategy is more effective than temporal aggregation alone. The gap over mono-input methods is substantially larger: Bi-SSC exceeds OccFormer [57] by +10.57 IoU and +4.41 mIoU, and MonoScene [3] by +10.94 IoU and +5.65 mIoU. On the SemanticKITTI-Complex subset, Bi-SSC's mIoU drops only 0.39 points (16.39→16.00), whereas VoxFormer-T drops 0.75 and StereoScene drops 1.50, confirming that the SSF and CVF modules provide meaningful occlusion robustness beyond what existing methods offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bi-SSC builds directly on the LSS (Lift, Splat, Shoot) framework for 2D-to-3D feature projection and adopts the Mutual Interactive Aggregation module from StereoScene as its final BEV aggregation step. It departs from prior camera-based SSC methods (MonoScene, VoxFormer, OccFormer, TPVFormer) that treat geometric and semantic features largely independently, instead proposing a bidirectional fusion where geometric features serve as queries and semantic features as keys/values within a masked attention mechanism. The Cross-view Fusion module extends standard stereo cross-attention by iteratively propagating features between left and right views before combining with stereo-matching depth, positioning the work at the intersection of stereo matching and BEV-based scene completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling occlusion through attention masks and cross-view spatial differences can meaningfully improve SSC in shaded and occluded regions, which are critical failure modes for autonomous driving perception. The introduction of SemanticKITTI-Complex, a hand-curated subset emphasizing challenging conditions, provides a useful stress-test benchmark for the community. However, the improvement over the closest stereo baseline (StereoScene) is modest (+1.36 mIoU), evaluation is limited to a single dataset, and the method's reliance on stereo input limits its applicability to monocular deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the geometric-semantic bidirectional fusion generalizes to multi-view or temporal settings and whether the approach can operate in real time.
