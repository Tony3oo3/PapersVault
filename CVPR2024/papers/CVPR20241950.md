## Problem

Existing multi-modal representation spaces such as ImageBind designate the image modality as the alignment center, producing an unbalanced embedding space where other modalities are sub-optimally represented. Additionally, relying solely on category names (e.g., 'Airplane') as text embedding centers fails to capture the full semantic diversity of visual data, limiting recognition accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241950/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts seven modalities—RGB images, point clouds, audio clips, thermal images, video clips, and event data—each encoded by a frozen modality-specific encoder followed by a single trainable linear projection layer. At inference, recognition is performed by computing the maximum cosine similarity between a modality embedding and a set of 50 LLM-augmented text embeddings (the 'embedding center') per category, rather than a single mean prompt embedding. The output is a class label for recognition or a unified multi-modal embedding for retrieval tasks.

## Method

UniBind replaces the image-centered contrastive alignment of ImageBind with a modality-agnostic, text-centered scheme: all modality embeddings are aligned to text embeddings generated from a knowledge base constructed by LLMs (GPT-4, LLaMA for category descriptions) and multi-modal LLMs (BLIP-2, LLaMA-Adapter for data descriptions). During training, a contrastive loss is imposed directly between each modality's visual embedding and its corresponding text description embedding, with other samples serving as negatives, ensuring all modalities are equally weighted in the shared space. At inference, an Embedding Center Localization module selects the top-50 most semantically relevant text embeddings per category (ranked by cosine similarity to a basic prompt) to form a richer, multi-point embedding center that establishes sharper category boundaries. The entire approach requires only a single linear layer per modality encoder (all backbone parameters frozen), reducing learnable parameters by 90% while delivering an average +6.36% zero-shot accuracy gain over prior methods.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting, UniBind (with PointBind backbone) achieves an average +6.27% top-1 accuracy improvement across 12 benchmarks spanning 6 modalities, with standout gains of +5.55% on ImageNet-1K (83.25%), +8.44% on Place-365 (53.84%), +20.83% on UrbanSound8K (62.56%), and +8.28% on N-Caltech-101 (59.26%). In the fine-tuning setting, UniBind achieves a new state-of-the-art +6.75% on ImageNet-1K (86.94%) and +7.54% on Place-365 (56.99%) while reducing 90% of learnable parameters. For cross-modal retrieval, UniBind delivers +17.96% on event-to-image R@20 with PointBind (55.66% vs. 37.70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniBind consistently outperforms ImageBind [13] and PointBind [15] in the zero-shot setting across all 12 benchmarks, with the largest gains on UrbanSound8K (+20.83%), Place-365 (+8.44%), and N-Caltech-101 (+8.28%). In the fine-tuning setting, UniBind surpasses Meta-Transformer [61] on ImageNet-1K (+6.75%) and Place-365 (+7.54%) but underperforms on UCF-101 (−4.75%) and UrbanSound8K (−2.51%), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced for high-category-count datasets. Against two-modality baselines, UniBind applied to CLIP [40] yields +10.35% on ImageNet and +9.19% on Place-365; with E-CLIP [65], it gains +3.83% on N-Caltech-101; with Audio-CLIP [16], +2.65% on ESC-50; and with Point-CLIP [59], +1.23% on ModelNet40. In the language-augmentation comparison (Table 6), UniBind outperforms Word-net Augmented [17] by +5.15% on Image and +1.50% on Point Cloud, and outperforms LLM-generated Prompts [66] by +3.66% on Image and +7.33% on Event, confirming that the full knowledge-base + embedding-center-localization pipeline is superior to simpler prompt strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniBind departs from the image-centered alignment paradigm established by ImageBind and extended by PointBind, where the image modality serves as the binding anchor for all other modalities. Instead, it makes alignment centers modality-agnostic by leveraging LLM-generated text descriptions (from GPT-4, LLaMA, BLIP-2, LLaMA-Adapter) as a knowledge base, and replaces simple category-name prompts with localized embedding centers constructed from top-50 semantically relevant descriptions. The method is architecturally minimal—only a trainable linear layer is added to frozen multi-modal encoders—making it a plug-in enhancement rather than a new backbone, and it is the first to incorporate the event modality into a unified multi-modal representation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality-agnostic alignment strategy addresses a recognized limitation of image-centered spaces (bias toward the image modality) and offers a practical, low-parameter recipe (90% fewer learnable parameters) that can be retrofitted onto any existing CLIP-style model without retraining encoders. The consistent gains across seven modalities and six backbone models suggest the LLM-augmented knowledge base is a broadly applicable technique for improving zero-shot and fine-tuned multi-modal recognition. However, the authors acknowledge that the robustness of the LLM-augmented method requires further enhancement, and the reliance on external LLMs for knowledge base construction introduces practical dependencies and potential hallucination risks that remain open questions for deployment.
