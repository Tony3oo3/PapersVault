## Problem

Achieving accurate, real-time full-body motion tracking on standalone VR HMDs is difficult because only head and hand positions are directly observed (making lower-body estimation under-constrained) and portable HMDs have severely limited compute. Prior Transformer-based methods process entire clips at each inference step, making them too slow for on-device deployment, while IMU-only methods suffer from positional drift. No existing approach unifies scalable sparse observations (HMD plus a variable number of wearable IMUs) in a single framework that runs in real-time on consumer HMDs.

## Input / Output

At each frame the model consumes a fixed 135-dim vector comprising HMD head and hand 6DOF signals (position, linear velocity, 6D rotation, angular velocity) plus optional IMU signals (6D rotation, angular velocity, acceleration) from the pelvis and both lower legs; missing IMU channels are zero-padded so the input dimensionality stays constant across the HMD, HMD+2IMUs, and HMD+3IMUs scenarios. Hand positions are additionally expressed relative to the head coordinate frame to aid shape estimation. The network outputs SMPL local pose parameters for 22 joints and body shape parameters, which are passed through differentiable forward kinematics to yield full-body joint positions and orientations as a 22×6 vector.

## Method

The core architecture is a lightweight Temporal-Spatial Feature Learning (TSFL) network of 2 stacked blocks, each containing per-component LSTMs (capturing temporal dynamics via a persistent hidden state) followed by a Transformer encoder that learns spatial correlations among the 8 input components. Because the LSTM retains history, the Transformer only attends over 8 spatial tokens per frame rather than 40–45 temporal frames, reducing per-layer cost by more than 5× versus prior clip-level Transformer methods. A separate shape head regresses SMPL β parameters online (instead of using a fixed default shape), and the differentiable SMPL model serves as the FK module to compute joint positions, which also provides a training signal to reduce kinematic-chain drift. Training is supervised with a composite L1 loss over root orientation, local pose, global pose, joint positions, and an acceleration-based smoothness term, using 40-frame clips on AMASS.

## Results

On the AMASS dataset (Protocol 1), HMD-Poser with HMD+3IMUs achieves the best MPJRE of 1.73° and MPJPE of 1.89 cm, substantially outperforming the next-best method PIP (2.45° / 4.54 cm). Even in the HMD-only scenario, HMD-Poser reaches 2.28° MPJRE and 3.19 cm MPJPE, beating all HMD-based baselines. In terms of inference speed, HMD-Poser runs at 205.7 FPS on an RTX 3080 GPU and 90.0 FPS on a PICO 4 HMD, the only method demonstrated to run in real-time on a consumer HMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HMD-Poser outperforms all five baselines across every metric in both AMASS protocols. In the HMD-only scenario, it beats the three HMD-based methods (AvatarPoser, AGRoL, AvatarJLM) by 0.42–0.66° in MPJRE and 1.84–2.65 cm in MPJPE, with the largest gap on H-PE (1.65 cm vs. 2.01–4.58 cm). Against the two 6-IMU methods (Transpose, PIP), HMD-Poser with HMD+3IMUs reduces L-PE from 6.53–6.76 cm to 2.46 cm and R-PE from 4.54–4.62 cm to 2.37 cm, demonstrating that combining HMD's reliable global position with leg IMUs eliminates the positional drift inherent to pure-IMU approaches. On inference speed, HMD-Poser (205.7 FPS GPU) is 1.8× faster than Transpose (123.0 FPS) and 108× faster than AvatarJLM (1.9 FPS), and is the only method shown to run at 90 FPS on a PICO 4 HMD.

## Relation to Prior Work

HMD-Poser carves new ground by unifying two previously separate lines of work: HMD-based full-body tracking (AvatarPoser, AGRoL, AvatarJLM) and IMU-based tracking (TransPose, PIP, TIP). Rather than committing to one sensor modality, it introduces a scalable input framework where HMD 6DOF data is optionally augmented by 2 or 3 body-worn 3DOF IMUs via zero-padding, a design no prior method supported. Architecturally, it departs from the pure-Transformer approaches (AvatarPoser, AvatarJLM) that are too computationally expensive for HMD deployment, instead combining LSTM for temporal state retention with a short-sequence Transformer for spatial correlation, and adds an online shape regression head that prior HMD-based methods entirely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of real-time full-body motion tracking running natively on a consumer HMD (90 FPS on PICO 4), which is a practical milestone for VR avatar applications. The newly released free-dancing dataset is the first to pair ground-truth 3D motions with real-captured HMD and IMU sensor data, exposing a significant performance gap (especially in hand position error) between synthetic and real inputs that prior work, trained and evaluated solely on AMASS, could not reveal. Open questions include whether the data-driven approach can be made less dependent on large-scale synthetic training, and how to handle the inherent IMU ambiguity for slow, uniform lower-body motions.
