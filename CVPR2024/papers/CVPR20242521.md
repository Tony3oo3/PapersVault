## Problem

Domain generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS) methods that remove style via feature normalization (e.g., whitening, instance normalization) suffer from style-content entanglement, inadvertently discarding content information critical for accurate segmentation. Existing approaches either require auxiliary datasets for domain randomization or external modules at inference time, limiting practical deployment. This paper seeks to blind the network to style variations without removing content and without relying on external data or modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242521/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of RGB images: the original image x and its augmented counterpart x_a (generated via strong color jittering to simulate style shift), both sharing identical content. The model (DeepLabV3+ with ResNet-50, ShuffleNetV2, or MobileNetV2 backbone) produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over 19 urban scene classes. Training images range from 960×720 (SYNTHIA) to 2048×1024 (Cityscapes), and the method operates without any additional input beyond the source-domain labeled data.

## Method

BlindNet introduces two complementary mechanisms. In the encoder, covariance alignment uses a Covariance Matching Loss (CML) to align the covariance matrices of instance-normalized features from the original and augmented images, making the network recognize different styles uniformly, and a Cross-covariance Loss (CCL) that converges only the diagonal of the cross-covariance to 1—preserving content information that full whitening would destroy. In the decoder, semantic consistency contrastive learning employs two InfoNCE-based losses: Class-wise Contrastive Learning (CWCL) builds a discriminative embedding space by contrasting same-pixel features across the image pair against different-class pixels, and Semantic Disentanglement Contrastive Learning (SDCL) uses the error mask between predicted and ground-truth segmentation to pull misclassified anchor features toward the correct class and away from the misclassified class. Both contrastive losses share a projection head, and the total objective combines cross-entropy with weighted CML, CCL, CWCL, and SDCL terms. No auxiliary datasets or inference-time modules are required.

## Results

Using DeepLabV3+ with ResNet-50 trained on GTAV, BlindNet achieves 45.72% mIoU on Cityscapes, 41.32% on BDD-100K, 47.08% on Mapillary, and 31.39% on SYNTHIA, setting new state-of-the-art across all four unseen target domains. When trained on Cityscapes, it reaches 60.18% mIoU on Mapillary and 47.97% on GTAV, again leading or closely matching the best competitors. The method requires no external dataset or additional inference module, maintaining the same 45.08M parameters and 277.78 GFLOPS as the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlindNet outperforms all nine baselines on every target domain when trained on GTAV with ResNet-50, achieving the highest mIoU on Cityscapes (45.72 vs. 44.74 for DPCL, the closest competitor), BDD-100K (41.32 vs. 40.59 for DPCL), Mapillary (47.08 vs. 46.33 for DPCL), and SYNTHIA (31.39 vs. 31.34 for WildNet). When trained on Cityscapes, it leads on Mapillary (60.18 vs. 59.81 for SANSAW), SYNTHIA (28.51 vs. 28.32 for SANSAW), and GTAV (47.97 vs. 47.28 for SANSAW), but trails SANSAW on BDD-100K (51.84 vs. 52.95). Compared to feature-normalization methods (IBN-Net, RobustNet, SANSAW), BlindNet consistently gains 2–8 mIoU points, confirming that covariance alignment preserves content information that direct style removal discards. Against domain-randomization methods (WildNet, SiamDoGe), BlindNet avoids the need for auxiliary datasets (WildNet requires ImageNet) while still surpassing them by 1–3 mIoU. The method also matches or exceeds DPCL (which uses an external contrastive module) without any additional inference-time module, and its computational cost (45.08M params, 10.03 ms) is identical to the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a departure from both major DGSS paradigms: Domain Randomization (DR) methods that require auxiliary datasets (e.g., WildNet, SiamDoGe) and Feature Normalization (FN) methods that remove style components via whitening or instance normalization (e.g., RobustNet, SANSAW, IBN-Net). Rather than removing style-sensitive factors from features, BlindNet introduces a covariance alignment strategy that aligns within-image covariance matrices across style-augmented pairs (CML) and converges only the diagonal of the cross-covariance to identity (CCL), explicitly preserving off-diagonal content information that prior FN methods discard. The decoder-side contrastive learning (CWCL + SDCL) repurposes InfoNCE for semantic consistency across augmented pairs, differing from prior DGSS contrastive approaches (e.g., DPCL, WildNet) that embed wild or multi-level prototypes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or superior DGSS performance without any external modules at inference time or auxiliary training datasets, making the method directly deployable with standard segmentation architectures. The ablation confirms each loss component contributes, and the approach generalizes across three backbones and five target domains. However, the augmentation strategy relies solely on color jittering, which may not capture the full breadth of real-world style variation (e.g., weather, sensor differences), and the authors themselves note that stabilizing the covariance alignment remains future work. An open question is whether the covariance alignment principle extends beyond urban scene segmentation to other domain-shift settings.
