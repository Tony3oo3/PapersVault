## Problem

Existing semantic line detectors either remove redundant lines via NMS without considering how the remaining group represents the scene layout, or assess harmony only through pairwise line relationships (e.g., maximal weight clique). Neither approach captures the higher-order, global harmony of a full set of semantic lines that jointly divide an image into coherent semantic regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241320/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 480×480) and produces an optimal combination of semantic lines selected from all 2^K subsets of K=8 reliable lines, along with a scalar composition score in [0,1] for each combination and a positional feature map P ∈ R^{HW×K} encoding the ternary side-of-line assignment for every pixel. The 1024 initial line candidates are parameterized in Hough polar space, and the final output is the single line combination with the highest predicted score.

## Method

SLCD operates in two stages: a modified S-Net filters 1024 Hough-space line candidates down to K=8 reliable lines via iterative NMS, then all 2^K line combinations are scored jointly by a dedicated network. The scoring network (ResNet50 encoder + cross-attention + regression head) first groups pixel features into M=8 semantic regions using a learnable region query matrix with column-wise softmax attention, guided by a novel SRS loss that maximizes KL divergence between region probability vectors on opposite sides of each ground-truth line. For each line combination, a compositional feature map Z is built by concatenating line-pixel features (X⊗B), region-interior features (X⊗(1−B)), and a positional feature map P derived from ternary masks indicating which side of each reliable line every pixel lies on. A regressor then predicts a single composition score per combination, and the highest-scoring combination is declared the optimal semantic line set. This whole-combination scoring replaces pairwise graph optimization, enabling the model to capture multi-line harmony that pairwise methods miss.

## Results

On the main semantic line detection benchmark (Table 1), SLCD achieves the highest HIoU on three of four datasets: 84.09% on SEL (+3.06 over HSLD), 76.21% on NKL (+1.92 over HSLD), and 68.85% on the newly introduced CDL dataset (+3.87 over HSLD). On SEL_Hard, SLCD scores 68.15%, slightly below DRM's 68.83% but well above all other baselines. In downstream applications, SLCD achieves 93.15% AUC_A on ICCV symmetry detection and 78.3% AA10° on vanishing point detection, both state-of-the-art among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLCD outperforms all four semantic line detection baselines (SLNet, DHT, DRM, HSLD) on SEL, NKL, and CDL, with margins of 3.06, 1.92, and 3.87 HIoU points over the strongest competitor HSLD respectively. The sole exception is SEL_Hard, where DRM edges out SLCD by 0.68 points (68.83 vs. 68.15), though DRM runs ~8.4× slower (0.952 spf vs. 0.114 spf). For vanishing point detection on AVA, SLCD surpasses both Zhou et al. [39] and Jin et al. [12] on AA2° (36.9 vs. 33.0 and 22.9) and AA10° (78.3 vs. 60.0 and 68.3), though it trails Zhou et al. on the stricter AA1° metric (16.6 vs. 18.5). For symmetry axis detection, SLCD achieves the best AUC_A on all three test sets (ICCV 93.15, NYU 93.51, SYM_Hard 88.03), beating the previous best Jin et al. [12] by 2.55, 0.73, and 3.30 points respectively, and outperforming all other symmetry methods (Cicconet, Elawady, Loy & Eklundh) by wider margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic line detection lineage from Lee et al. (SLNet, 2017) through Han et al. (DHT, 2020), Jin et al. (DRM, 2020), and Jin et al. (HSLD, 2021), all of which perform line detection followed by refinement via NMS or pairwise graph optimization. The key conceptual departure is from HSLD's pairwise harmony scoring (maximal weight clique over line pairs) to a group-level assessment where all lines in a candidate combination are processed simultaneously through a shared compositional feature map. The semantic feature grouping module borrows the cross-attention mechanism from segmentation transformers (CMT-DeepLab, k-means Mask Transformer), repurposing region queries to partition pixel features into semantic regions without explicit segmentation labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from pairwise to group-level harmony assessment provides a cleaner formulation of what 'harmonious' means for a set of lines, and the CDL dataset (9,100 images across seven composition classes) addresses the compositional homogeneity of prior benchmarks. The demonstrated applications—vanishing point detection, symmetry axis detection, and composition-based image retrieval—show that the learned positional feature maps carry transferable structural information beyond line detection itself. Limitations include the fixed Hough-space parameterization (N=1024 candidates), the modest runtime overhead (0.114 spf vs. 0.033 for DHT), and the reliance on a small number of reliable lines (K=8) that may underrepresent scenes with many semantic boundaries. Open questions include whether the cross-attention grouping is necessary at scale or whether simpler region partitioning suffices, and how the method generalizes to scenes with more than 8 dominant lines.
