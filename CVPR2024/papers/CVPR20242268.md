## Problem

Large multimodal models produce only textual descriptions and cannot generate pixel-level masks for image reasoning tasks. The closest prior work, LISA, handles single-target reasoning segmentation but cannot scale to multiple targets and depends on an external SAM model, adding ~50% computational overhead and capping performance at SAM's capability. No training dataset existed for multi-target reasoning segmentation with instance-level mask annotations tied to text descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242268/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed at 448x448 resolution by a CLIP-ViT-L/14 encoder) and a free-form text query that may reference an arbitrary number of targets through reasoning instructions (e.g., 'the fruit high in Vitamin-C') rather than explicit object names. It produces an interleaved autoregressive sequence of text tokens and segmentation codebook tokens; each codebook token group triggers the pixel decoder to emit a binary mask at the same spatial resolution as the input, with <SEG> markers in the text indicating where masks are inserted.

## Method

PixelLM replaces the external SAM dependency with a lightweight pixel decoder (L attention blocks operating top-down from coarse to fine scale) and a segmentation codebook of learnable tokens (N tokens per scale, fused via a linear projection) that the LLM processes autoregressively to encode target-relevant information into hidden states. A target refinement loss upweights BCE loss at pixels where multiple predicted masks overlap, reducing inter-target confusion. The model is fine-tuned end-to-end with LoRA on LLaVA-7B/13B using the newly constructed MUSE dataset (246k QA pairs over 0.9M instances, generated by GPT-4V from LVIS masks), achieving 50% lower TFLOPs than LISA while matching or exceeding its mask quality on MUSE, multi-referring, and refCOCO benchmarks.

## Results

On the newly introduced MUSE benchmark, PixelLM-Llama2-13B achieves 44.8 gIoU and 54.1 cIoU (Val overall), outperforming the strongest baseline LISA-Llama2-13B_aug (43.6 gIoU, 50.2 cIoU) while using 35% fewer TFLOPs (6.65 vs. 10.24). The 7B variant reaches 42.6 gIoU / 50.7 cIoU with only 3.57 TFLOPs—a 50% reduction over LISA-7B (7.16 TFLOPs). On multi-referring segmentation (MrefCOCO), PixelLM scores 72.7/76.2/68.1 (val/testA/testB), surpassing LISA_aug by 3.8/5.4/1.8 points. On single-target refCOCOg, PixelLM attains 69.3/70.5 (val/test), exceeding all non-LLM baselines including ReLA (65.0/66.0) and SEEM (65.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelLM consistently outperforms all LISA variants on MUSE: PixelLM-7B beats LISA-7B_aug by 0.6 gIoU and 4.6 cIoU while halving compute (3.57 vs. 7.16 TFLOPs); PixelLM-Llama2-13B beats LISA-Llama2-13B_aug by 1.2 gIoU and 3.9 cIoU with 35% fewer TFLOPs. The original LISA (single-target, SAM-dependent) lags far behind, scoring only 18.8 gIoU on MUSE Val versus PixelLM-7B's 42.6. On multi-referring segmentation, PixelLM surpasses LISA_aug by 3.8 gIoU on MrefCOCO val (72.7 vs. 68.9). On single-target refCOCOg, PixelLM (69.3/70.5) outperforms ReLA (65.0/66.0), SEEM (65.7), X-Decoder (64.6), and LISA (66.4/68.5), though it slightly trails LISA on refCOCO val (73.0 vs. 74.1) and testB (68.2 vs. 71.1). SEEM, a non-LLM segmentation-only model, performs worst on MUSE (13.6 gIoU), confirming that reasoning capability is essential for multi-target tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelLM directly extends LISA, the first LMM-based reasoning segmentation model, by removing its dependency on SAM for mask generation and extending from single-target to multi-target scenarios. Architecturally, it builds on the LLaVA paradigm (CLIP-ViT encoder + LLM) but introduces a segmentation codebook and a lightweight multi-scale pixel decoder that replace the external segmentation model entirely. The MUSE dataset, constructed via a GPT-4V-aided pipeline over LVIS annotations, fills a gap left by existing datasets (RefCOCO, ReasonSeg) that lack multi-target reasoning queries and instance-level mask annotations tied to natural language descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the SAM dependency, PixelLM cuts inference compute by ~50% while matching or exceeding LISA's mask quality, making pixel-level LMM reasoning more practical for deployment in robotics, autonomous driving, and image editing. The MUSE benchmark (246k QA pairs, 0.9M instances) provides the field with a standardized multi-target reasoning segmentation evaluation that was previously absent. Open questions include whether the lightweight decoder generalizes to very fine-grained or dense multi-instance scenes, whether the approach extends to video segmentation, and whether further scaling of the codebook or decoder yields diminishing returns beyond the modest gains observed with additional scales.
