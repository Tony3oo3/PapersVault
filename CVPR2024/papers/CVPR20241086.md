## Problem

Frame-by-frame image editing of videos produces temporally inconsistent results due to the high variance of generative diffusion models, while existing tracking-and-propagation approaches (optical flow, layered representations) are fragile and fail on videos with large motion or complex dynamics. The paper asks whether cross-frame attention inside a diffusion model can serve as a robust, implicit tracking mechanism that propagates edits across frames without explicit pixel correspondence, and whether this can be done at practical speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241086/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an input video of N frames at arbitrary aspect ratio (rescaled so the longer side is 512 px) and a natural-language editing instruction, and produces an edited video with the same number of frames and resolution. All frames are edited without temporal downsampling; a 120-frame 512×384 clip (4 s at 30 FPS) is generated in 14 s on 8 A100 GPUs. The model handles diverse aspect ratios without retraining and supports a wide range of edit types (stylization, character swap, local editing, attribute changes).

## Method

Fairy builds on InstructPix2Pix and introduces two key mechanisms. First, anchor-based cross-frame attention: K=3 anchor frames are uniformly sampled and their key/value vectors are cached at every attention layer and diffusion step; for each remaining frame, self-attention is replaced with cross-frame attention against these cached anchor features, so tokens attend to semantically corresponding regions in the anchors, implicitly tracking and propagating features without explicit optical flow. Second, equivariant finetuning: random affine transformations (rotation <5°, translation, scale, shear) are applied jointly to source and target images during a 50K-step fine-tune, making the model invariant to small camera-motion variations and further improving temporal consistency. Because each frame depends only on the cached anchor features and not on other frames, generation is fully parallelizable across GPUs, yielding a >44× speedup over prior methods.

## Results

Fairy achieves a latency of 13.8 seconds for a 4-second, 30 FPS, 512×384 video on 8 A100 GPUs, compared to 744 s (TokenFlow) and 608 s (Rerender), representing a >44× speedup. On quantitative metrics, Fairy attains the best Frame-Acc (0.819) and Tem-Con (0.974), surpassing Rerender (0.775 / 0.972) and TokenFlow (0.537 / 0.973). In a large-scale A/B user study over 1000 video-instruction pairs, Fairy wins 73% vs 16% against TokenFlow, 72% vs 26% against Gen-1, and 41% vs 36% against Rerender.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fairy is compared against three representative paradigms: Rerender (tracking-and-propagation via optical flow), TokenFlow (image-model adaptation with nearest-neighbor feature propagation), and Gen-1 (conditional video diffusion model). Against TokenFlow, Fairy dominates on every axis: 53× faster (8-GPU), higher Frame-Acc (0.819 vs 0.537), and a decisive 73% vs 16% A/B win rate. Against Rerender, Fairy is 44× faster, achieves higher Frame-Acc (0.819 vs 0.775), and wins the A/B test 41% to 36%, though the margin is narrower since Rerender benefits from a reduced key-frame interval (4 instead of 10) to handle fast motion. Against Gen-1 (evaluated on a smaller 100-video subset since it is not open-sourced), Fairy wins 72% to 26%, with Gen-1 tending to over-modify the entire scene rather than following the edit instruction faithfully. Overall, Fairy is the only method that simultaneously leads on speed, frame-level editing accuracy, and temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fairy builds directly on InstructPix2Pix as its base image-editing model and extends the spatial-temporal attention designs explored in Video-P2P and Text2Video-Zero into an anchor-based cross-frame attention mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant tracking-and-propagation paradigm (Rerender, TokenFlow, layered representations) by showing that cross-frame attention implicitly performs correspondence estimation and feature propagation, eliminating the need for explicit optical flow or pixel tracking. The equivariant finetuning strategy—a simple affine data augmentation applied to image editing pairs—is a novel contribution not present in prior video editing adaptations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44×+ speedup (via 8-GPU parallelization) and the ability to generate arbitrarily long videos without memory blowup make instruction-guided video editing practically accessible, a significant step beyond prior methods that were limited to short clips. The empirical validation that cross-frame attention scores serve as implicit correspondences (60–70% position accuracy on DAVIS without finetuning) provides a useful conceptual bridge between attention mechanisms and tracking. Limitations remain: quality is bounded by the underlying image model, and equivariant finetuning degrades rendering of inherently dynamic effects (lightning, flames). Open questions include whether the anchor-based design generalizes to other diffusion backbones or to tasks beyond instruction-guided editing, and whether the tracking-via-attention property holds in non-UNet architectures.
