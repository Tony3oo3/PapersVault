## Problem

Pre-trained Segment Anything Models (SAMs) achieve universal zero-shot object segmentation on RGB images, but the large distribution gap between RGB and event camera data prevents direct transfer. Existing event-based segmentation methods are confined to specific datasets or tasks and lack the generalization capability of large pre-trained models, leaving no universal event-centric segmentation framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242404/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3-channel time-voxel image (512×512) constructed from raw event streams as input; during training, a small fraction of event tokens are randomly replaced with RGB image tokens to retain pre-trained knowledge. The output is a pixel-level instance segmentation mask produced by SAM's promptable decoder (accepting points or bounding boxes), with ground truth masks generated by a well-trained SAM on the paired RGB images.

## Method

The core contribution is a correlation-aware weighted token distillation scheme that aligns event-domain ViT embeddings with RGB-domain counterparts. A multi-layer transition matrix H^(s) is approximated via a Markov-chain assumption over stacked self-attention layers, yielding per-token significance weights that quantify how much each intermediate embedding influences the final-layer output. The training loss applies an L1 penalty on the difference between RGB and event embeddings at selected intermediate layers, broadcast-weighted by these significance scores, so that regularization concentrates on pivotal tokens rather than uniformly across all positions. Only the last MLPs in transformer blocks {3,6,9,12} are fine-tuned (≈22% of parameters) while the rest of the SAM backbone remains frozen, preserving zero-shot generalization. The attention matrices used to compute the weights are taken from the teacher (RGB) network, as ablations show that student (event) attention matrices lead to degraded performance.

## Results

On the RGBE-SEG dataset, the proposed method achieves mIoU of 0.41 and aIoU of 0.55 (All difficulty), representing a ~9% improvement over the best competing methods in both metrics. On the zero-shot MVSEC dataset, it reaches mIoU of 0.38 (indoor) and 0.42 (outdoor), again outperforming all baselines. The method also achieves the best mP (0.59) among methods with competitive recall, balancing precision and recall more effectively than frame-reconstruction approaches like EVDistill (mP=0.63 but mR=0.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best mIoU (0.41) and aIoU (0.55) on RGBE-SEG, surpassing the strongest baseline E2VID (mIoU 0.32, aIoU 0.45) by 9% and 10% respectively. Frame-reconstruction methods (E2VID, ETNet) and E2VID-encoder-based methods (DTL, ESS, EVDistill) all underperform, with EVDistill achieving the worst aIoU (0.34) despite a high mP of 0.63, indicating it produces fewer but less accurate masks. Knowledge distillation baselines (NGA, LFI) show moderate mIoU (0.27–0.30) but lack the zero-shot generalization of the SAM-based approach. On the zero-shot MVSEC dataset, the method again leads in mIoU (0.38 indoor, 0.42 outdoor), outperforming DTL (0.32/0.34) and ESS (0.31/0.32), confirming robustness beyond the training distribution. The original SAM without fine-tuning (mIoU 0.26) demonstrates that naive application of SAM to event data is insufficient, validating the need for the proposed cross-modal distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Segment Anything Model (SAM) to event camera data, departing from prior event-based segmentation approaches (E2VID, ETNet, ESS, DTL, EVDistill) that either reconstruct pseudo-frames or repurpose E2VID as a fixed encoder. Its methodological lineage draws from cross-modal knowledge distillation, but introduces a key departure: instead of gradient-based or feature-map-level alignment, it uses the teacher SAM's self-attention matrices (approximated via a Markov chain formulation) to weight intermediate token embeddings, focusing regularization on pivotal tokens that most influence the final layer. The token-mixing strategy (replacing a small fraction of event tokens with image tokens) borrows from masked autoencoder ideas to preserve pre-trained knowledge during adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that SAM's zero-shot, prompt-based universal segmentation can be transferred to event data while largely preserving its generalization ability, opening a path toward event-based universal perception. A notable finding is that fine-tuning more parameters (all blocks vs. four MLPs) actually degrades performance, reinforcing that minimal adaptation is critical for preserving pre-trained model capabilities. Limitations include failure on texture-less regions (e.g., sky) due to event camera sensing characteristics, and the reliance on paired RGB-event data for training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution sensors, whether LLM integration can enable language-guided event segmentation, and whether the attention-weighting strategy generalizes to other pre-trained backbones beyond ViT-B.
