## Problem

In pre-trained model-based class-incremental learning, prompt-based methods share a single prompt pool, so optimizing prompts for new tasks overwrites those of former tasks, causing catastrophic forgetting. Expandable networks avoid this conflict by learning separate backbones per task, but they demand prohibitive memory for large PTMs and require exemplars to calibrate a unified classifier. The paper asks whether low-cost, task-specific subspaces can be constructed without exemplars to resolve cross-task conflict in PTM-based CIL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and outputs a single class label from the cumulative set of all seen classes. A key constraint is the exemplar-free setting: at each incremental stage, only the current task's data is available, so no old-class instances can be stored or replayed. The feature representation grows to b×d dimensions (b tasks × d backbone dim) as adapters accumulate, and classification is performed via cosine similarity between the expanded embedding and per-class prototype vectors.

## Method

EASE attaches a lightweight bottleneck adapter (down-projection → non-linearity → up-projection) as a residual side branch to each MLP layer of a frozen ViT, training one adapter per incremental task to encode task-specific features without disturbing prior adapters. At inference, features from the frozen backbone and all task adapters are concatenated into a holistic b×d embedding. To handle the classifier dimensionality mismatch without exemplars, EASE computes class-wise cosine similarity between old and new class prototypes in the co-occurrence subspace (where both sets coexist) and uses softmax-normalized weights to synthesize old-class prototypes in each new subspace via a weighted combination of new-class prototypes. Finally, a subspace reweighting step (α = 0.1) up-weights the logit contribution from the adapter matching the query's task, emphasizing task-specific features in the final ensemble decision.

## Results

EASE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all seven benchmark datasets using ViT-B/16-IN21K, with average accuracy (A̅) of 91.51 on CIFAR-100 (B0 Inc5), 92.23 on CUB-200 (B0 Inc10), 78.31 on ImageNet-R (B0 Inc5), 65.34 on ImageNet-A (B0 Inc20), 70.84 on ObjectNet (B0 Inc10), 81.11 on OmniBenchmark (B0 Inc30), and 93.61 on VTAB (B0 Inc10). The last-stage accuracy (A_B) reaches 85.80, 86.81, 70.58, 55.04, 57.86, 74.85, and 93.55 respectively. EASE outperforms the runner-up method by 4%–7.5% on ImageNet-R/A, ObjectNet, and VTAB, while using the same parameter scale as prompt-based methods and requiring zero exemplars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASE outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and both metrics (A̅ and A_B). Against the strongest prompt-based competitor CODA-Prompt, EASE gains +2.4% A̅ on CIFAR-100 (91.51 vs 89.11), +8.2% on ImageNet-R (78.31 vs 64.42), +11.8% on ImageNet-A (65.34 vs 53.54), and +9.7% on VTAB (93.61 vs 83.90). Against ADAM+Adapter (the best ADAM variant), EASE leads by +5.96% A̅ on ImageNet-R (78.31 vs 72.35) and +7.66% on ImageNet-A (65.34 vs 60.47), while maintaining a narrow +0.02% margin on CUB-200 (92.23 vs 92.21). Against SimpleCIL, EASE achieves +3.73% on ImageNet-R and +5.57% on ImageNet-A. In the exemplar-based comparison (Table 2), EASE with 0 exemplars surpasses FOSTER (81.73 vs 81.34 A̅ on ImageNet-R B100 Inc50) and DER (81.73 vs 80.48), while trailing FOSTER slightly on CIFAR-100 B0 Inc10 (92.35 vs 89.87 A̅) — actually EASE wins there too. EASE also dominates traditional CIL methods (LwF, SDC) by large margins, e.g., +23.3% A̅ over LwF on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

EASE bridges two previously separate lines of CIL research: expandable-network methods (FOSTER, DER) that learn a distinct backbone per task to avoid cross-task overwriting, and prompt-based PTM methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) that share a single prompt pool and suffer from prompt conflict. The paper transplants the subspace-expansion principle into the PTM setting by replacing full backbone expansion with lightweight AdaptFormer-style adapters, and replaces the exemplar-dependent unified classifier with a training-free semantic-guided prototype complement (drawing on the semantic-mapping idea of Ye et al. [65]). This positions EASE as a parameter-efficient, exemplar-free alternative that retains the conflict-avoidance benefit of expandable architectures without their memory cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Exemplar-free CIL is critical for privacy-sensitive and memory-constrained deployments, and EASE demonstrates that a simple adapter-plus-prototype recipe can match or exceed methods that store 20 exemplars per class. The semantic-guided prototype complement is a clean, training-free solution to the classifier-dimensionality mismatch that plagues any expanding-architecture approach, and could be adapted to other incremental settings (domain-incremental, task-incremental). Open questions include whether the assumption that class-relationship structure is preserved across adapter subspaces holds under large domain shifts, whether adapters can be further compressed for very long sequences, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-ViT backbones or multimodal models.
