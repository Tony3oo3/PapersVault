## Problem

Existing speech translation systems either operate solely on audio (A2A) or rely on 4-stage cascaded pipelines (ASR→NMT→TTS→TFG) to produce audio-visual output, creating a mismatch between the speaker's visible lip movements and the translated audio in video conferencing. No direct system exists that translates audio-visual speech to audio-visual speech, and no parallel AV2AV training corpus is publicly available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241627/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts any combination of modalities—audio-only, visual-only (lip movements), or full audio-visual face video—as input, and produces a synchronized pair of translated speech waveform and talking face video as output. Translation is many-to-many across 19 languages (mTEDx + VoxPopuli), and the zero-shot AV-Renderer preserves the source speaker's voice and facial identity in the translated output. The face video input is cropped to a 96×96 mouth region, and audio is resampled to 16 kHz.

## Method

The framework has three stages: (1) a multilingual AV-HuBERT (mAV-HuBERT), pretrained on ~7,000 hours of 100+ language AV data with modality dropout, extracts unified AV speech representations that are discretized into 1,000 K-means clusters (AV speech units); (2) a 12-layer transformer encoder-decoder translates between these units in a many-to-many setting, trained entirely on audio-only A2A data (~12,000 hours, 19 languages) by masking the visual stream during unit extraction, so the same model handles A2AV, V2AV, and AV2AV at inference; (3) a zero-shot AV-Renderer synthesizes raw audio (HiFi-GAN vocoder with a d-vector speaker embedding for voice preservation) and talking face video (modified Wav2Lip conditioned on AV speech units and source identity/pose features) in parallel, sharing a common length predictor for lip-audio synchronization.

## Results

On MuAViC (8 language directions), the proposed AV2AV (A8) achieves ASR-BLEU of 26.57/31.27/23.24/24.51 for X→En and 27.57/23.21/16.33/12.35 for En→X, using a single model for all directions. On LRS3-T, the proposed method with zero finetuning data (25.3 En-Es, 27.1 En-Fr) already outperforms AV-TranSpeech finetuned on 10 hours of AV2A data (21.5, 24.4). Under −5 dB SNR noise, the AV2AV input (17.31 BLEU) substantially outperforms audio-only cascaded baselines (3.73 BLEU), demonstrating strong noise robustness from multimodal inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior direct method AV-TranSpeech [62] on LRS3-T, the proposed method wins at every finetuning budget: with 0 hr it scores 25.3/27.1 (En-Es/En-Fr) versus AV-TranSpeech's 21.5/24.4 at 10 hr, and reaches 50.9/41.4 at 200 hr versus 45.2/33.6. On MuAViC, the proposed AV2AV (A8) is comparable to the 4-stage cascaded AVSR+NMT+TTS+TFG (A2) on X→En (e.g., 26.57 vs 28.70 Es-En) but trails by ~2–10 BLEU on En→X, where A2 uses four separate models. The proposed method clearly outperforms the 3-stage cascaded systems (A3, A4) on all X→En directions and the 2-stage A2A+TFG (A5) on most En→X directions. Under acoustic noise (Table 3), the AV-input proposed method (B7) maintains 17.31 BLEU at −5 dB SNR while the best audio-only cascaded baseline (B1) drops to 3.73, and the visual-only input (B6) stays constant at 10.36 regardless of noise level, confirming the robustness advantage of multimodal inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-unit speech translation paradigm (Kim et al. [11]) from audio-only to the full audio-visual domain by replacing HuBERT with a multilingual AV-HuBERT (mAV-HuBERT) that produces modality-agnostic representations. It departs from the prior AV2A work (AV-TranSpeech [62]) by producing both audio and visual outputs rather than audio alone, and from the 4-stage cascaded A2AV pipelines [15–19] by eliminating all intermediate text and speech stages. The face renderer component adapts Wav2Lip [27] to accept discrete AV speech units instead of raw audio, and the vocoder adds zero-shot speaker conditioning via d-vectors on top of the HiFi-GAN architecture used in prior A2A systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a single model that handles A2AV, V2AV, and AV2AV in a many-to-many setting with zero-shot speaker identity preservation in both audio and video, which is directly applicable to real-time multilingual video conferencing. The modality-agnostic training strategy (train on audio-only A2A data, infer with any modality combination) is a broadly useful insight that reduces the data bottleneck for multimodal speech tasks. However, V2AV performance remains substantially weaker than A2AV/AV2AV (BLEU ~10–18 vs. ~26–31), and the full pipeline's complexity (mAV-HuBERT + transformer + vocoder + face renderer) raises questions about real-time feasibility and error compounding across components.
