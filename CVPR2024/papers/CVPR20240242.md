## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image methods (e.g., Custom Diffusion, DreamBooth) cannot reliably capture multiple distinct concepts from a single reference image: they produce cluttered cross-attention maps that lead to appearance inconsistency with the input and cross-contamination between concepts (e.g., generating a chair when only a lamp is requested). The core gap is the lack of explicit guidance during optimization to ensure each learned concept's attention is both accurate and independent.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image containing two (or up to three) distinct concepts and a text prompt of the form 'V*_1 <class_1> and V*_2 <class_2>', where V*_i are learnable modifier tokens initialized from rare vocabulary. During inference, 30 diverse prompts are tested per image (10 combined, 10 targeting concept 1, 10 targeting concept 2), producing 16 samples per prompt via 50 DDIM steps at guidance scale 6. Training uses only the single image plus 200 regularization images retrieved from LAION-5B via CLIP similarity (>0.85) to mitigate language drift.

## Method

DisenDiff extends Stable Diffusion by updating only W_K and W_V matrices in cross-attention units alongside new token embeddings, and introduces an attention calibration mechanism with three coupled constraints. First, a binding loss (L_bind) minimizes the IoU between each modifier's attention map and its corresponding class token's attention map, leveraging the observation that frozen class tokens already roughly localize their concepts. Second, a separate-and-strengthen loss (L_s&s) minimizes the IoU between different class tokens' attention maps, decoupling concepts while preserving full coverage of each object. Third, a suppression step (element-wise squaring of attention values) sharpens boundaries and filters noisy low-activation patches before computing the s&s loss. A Gaussian filter is applied to all attention maps to alleviate pixel-wise softmax competition and encourage broader object coverage. The total loss combines the standard diffusion denoising objective with these attention-level constraints, applied at the 16×16 spatial resolution where the most semantic information resides.

## Results

DisenDiff is evaluated on ten datasets (each containing two distinct concepts per image) using two metrics: image-alignment (CLIP cosine similarity to the reference) and text-alignment (CLIP text-image similarity to the prompt), averaged over 30 prompts per image (10 combined, 10 per concept). DisenDiff achieves the highest image-alignment scores across all settings (Combined, Concept₁, Concept₂, and Mean), with the most pronounced advantage on Concept₂. Textual Inversion attains the highest text-alignment but the lowest image-alignment, while DisenDiff maintains text-alignment comparable to other methods while substantially outperforming them on reconstruction fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Textual Inversion (TI), DisenDiff wins decisively on image-alignment across all concept settings, while TI retains a slight edge on text-alignment — reflecting TI's inability to preserve concept appearance despite good prompt adherence. Against DreamBooth (DB), DisenDiff outperforms on both image-alignment and text-alignment; DB suffers from either missing key attributes or severe overfitting to the single training image. Against Custom Diffusion (CD), the closest competitor, DisenDiff matches or slightly leads on Combined and Concept₁ scores but shows a clear gap on Concept₂, where CD fails to decouple the second concept from the first (e.g., generating an unwanted chair alongside the target lamp). Overall, DisenDiff is the only method that simultaneously achieves top image fidelity and competitive text editability, with the advantage growing as the task shifts from combined to independent concept generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisenDiff extends the multi-concept personalization line initiated by Custom Diffusion [23] and DreamBooth [40], which learn new token embeddings and update cross-attention W_K/W_V matrices to capture user-specific concepts. The key departure is the problem setting: prior methods assume one concept per image or multiple images per concept, whereas this paper tackles the harder case of disentangling multiple distinct concepts from a single reference image. The core technical contribution is an attention calibration mechanism (binding loss, separate-and-strengthen loss, and suppression) that constrains cross-attention maps to be concept-specific, building on the observation that class-token attention already roughly localizes objects while modifier-token attention remains chaotic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a user with one photo containing multiple subjects (e.g., a couple, a pet and a toy) can now independently or jointly generate each concept in new contexts, a capability absent from prior personalization tools. The method's compatibility with LoRA and inpainting pipelines broadens its applicability to interactive editing workflows. However, the paper acknowledges significant limitations: disentangling fine-grained same-category subjects (e.g., two dog breeds) remains challenging, and performance degrades notably with three or more concepts, suggesting the attention calibration approach has a practical ceiling tied to the underlying T2I model's compositional capacity.
