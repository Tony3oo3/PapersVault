## Problem

Point cloud transformers face a fundamental efficiency bottleneck: KNN neighbor search and relative positional encoding consume 54% of forward time, preventing these models from scaling their receptive fields the way 2D vision and NLP have leveraged scale. This efficiency gap means point transformers cannot fully exploit the scaling principle—where model performance is driven more by scale than by intricate mechanism design—leaving them behind sparse convolutional backbones in large-scale pre-training settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241771/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw 3D point clouds (indoor scenes from ScanNet/S3DIS or outdoor LiDAR sweeps from nuScenes/SemanticKITTI/Waymo) and outputs per-point features through a U-Net encoder-decoder. A key preprocessing step serializes points by sorting them along space-filling curves (Z-order, Hilbert, and transposed variants) into a 64-bit integer code combining batch index and spatial order, which then enables efficient patch-based attention. The receptive field is configurable from 16 to 4096 points via patch size, with 1024 as the recommended default, and the same backbone supports semantic segmentation, instance segmentation (via PointGroup), and 3D object detection (via CenterPoint).

## Method

PTv3 replaces KNN-based neighborhood construction with point cloud serialization: points are sorted along space-filling curves (Z-order, Hilbert, and their axis-transposed variants) to create structured sequences where spatial neighbors remain adjacent. Attention is then performed as patch attention over these serialized sequences, with four interaction strategies—Shift Dilation, Shift Patch, Shift Order, and the proposed Shuffle Order (which randomly permutes the serialization pattern assignment across layers to capture diverse spatial relationships). Relative positional encoding is replaced by xCPE, a sparse convolutional layer with a skip connection prepended before each attention block, reducing forward-time overhead while preserving positional information. The overall architecture is a 4-stage U-Net with block depths [2,2,6,2] and [1,1,1,1] for encoder and decoder, using pre-norm with LayerNorm and Grid Pooling. This design achieves a 3.3× speedup and 10.2× memory reduction over PTv2 while expanding the receptive field from 16 to 1024 points, and further benefits from multi-dataset joint training (PPT) for additional gains.

## Results

On indoor semantic segmentation, PTv3 (trained from scratch) achieves 77.5% mIoU on ScanNet val and 77.7% mIoU on S3DIS 6-fold CV, outperforming PTv2 by 2.1% and 4.2% respectively. With multi-dataset joint training (PPT), these improve to 78.6% (ScanNet val) and 80.8% (S3DIS 6-fold). On outdoor benchmarks, PTv3 reaches 80.4% mIoU on nuScenes val and 70.8% on SemanticKITTI val (scratch), extending to 81.2% and 72.3% with PPT pre-training. In terms of efficiency, PTv3 achieves a 3.3× speedup in inference (44ms vs. 146ms for PTv2/16) and a 10.2× reduction in inference memory (1.2G vs. 12.3G) while expanding the receptive field from 16 to 1024 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTv3 consistently outperforms all compared baselines across indoor and outdoor benchmarks. Against its direct predecessor PTv2, PTv3 gains 2.1% mIoU on ScanNet val (77.5 vs. 75.4) and 4.2% on S3DIS 6-fold (77.7 vs. 73.5) when trained from scratch, with the gap widening to 5.2% and 7.3% under PPT pre-training. Compared to the strongest transformer baselines, PTv3 surpasses Swin3D by 1.1% on ScanNet val (77.5 vs. 76.4) and 0.8% on S3DIS 6-fold (77.7 vs. 76.9), and beats OctFormer by 1.8% on ScanNet val (77.5 vs. 75.7). Against sparse convolution baselines, PTv3 leads MinkUNet by 5.3% on ScanNet val (77.5 vs. 72.2) and 12.3% on S3DIS 6-fold (77.7 vs. 65.4). On outdoor segmentation, PTv3 exceeds SphereFormer by 2.0% on nuScenes val (80.4 vs. 78.4) and 3.0% on SemanticKITTI val (70.8 vs. 67.8). For Waymo object detection, PTv3 with CenterPoint achieves 70.5 mAPH with a single frame, surpassing FlatFormer (67.2) by 3.3% and PillarNet (67.2) by 3.3%, and maintains a 1.0% lead over FlatFormer in the 3-frame setting (73.0 vs. 72.0 mAPH).

## Relation to Prior Work

PTv3 is the direct successor to Point Transformer V2, but departs fundamentally from the KNN-based neighborhood construction and relative positional encoding that defined the PTv1/PTv2 lineage. It draws on the serialization paradigm introduced by OctFormer (octree-based ordering) and FlatFormer (window-based sorting), generalizing these into a unified framework using space-filling curves (Z-order and Hilbert) with four traversal variants. The design philosophy is explicitly guided by the scaling principle from recent 3D representation learning (PPT multi-dataset training), arguing that mechanism simplicity enables the receptive-field scaling that complex attention variants (shift-window, neighborhood attention) impede.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3× speedup and 10× memory reduction while expanding the receptive field from 16 to 1024 points breaks the long-standing accuracy-efficiency trade-off in point cloud transformers, making large-receptive-field attention practical for latency-sensitive applications. The broader implication—that sacrificing precision in locality-preserving mechanisms (KNN, RPE) is negligible after scaling—challenges the field's tendency toward increasingly complex attention designs. Open questions include whether serialization-based ordering can fully replace learned neighborhood structures for tasks requiring precise local geometry, and whether the approach extends naturally to other unstructured data modalities beyond point clouds.
