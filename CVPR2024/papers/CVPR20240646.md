## Problem

Evaluating text-to-image diffusion models on full prompt datasets (e.g., COCO's 40K+ prompts) is computationally prohibitive for the iterative evaluations needed during model development (schedule selection, weight tuning, design choices). The common workaround of random subsampling exhibits a poor accuracy-efficiency trade-off: even 1000 random prompts yield only 0.87 Kendall's Tau rank correlation, and more aggressive subsampling causes acute drops in quality and high variance. The paper asks whether a small, carefully selected representative subset can match the evaluation quality of much larger random subsets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full text prompt dataset (COCO val2014 with 40,504 prompts or DiffusionDB with 5,000 sampled prompts) and a model zoo of 78 Stable Diffusion configurations (12 model variants × 8 sampler schedules, with fixed seed=0 and guidance scale=7.5) as input. It produces a representative prompt subset of configurable size N' (e.g., 10, 50, or 100 prompts) that, when used to compute standard metrics (FID, CLIPScore, Aesthetic, ImageReward, HPS), yields model rankings with Kendall's Tau comparable to a 10× larger random subset. The subset is a one-time artifact reusable across all future evaluations on the same dataset.

## Method

FlashEval is an iterative evolutionary search algorithm operating jointly at the set level and prompt level. Each iteration constructs a large number of random candidate subsets from the current prompt population, evaluates their fitness via Kendall's Tau against ground-truth model rankings, and retains the top-performing subsets. The key innovation is a frequency-based prompt selection step: rather than keeping prompts with the highest individual KD (which fails because individually strong prompts can interfere with each other in combination), FlashEval retains prompts that appear most frequently across the top-performing subsets, ensuring they generalize well when combined with any other prompt. This iterative filtering progressively narrows the candidate pool, dramatically improving exploration efficiency over naive set-wise random search in the combinatorial space C(N,N'). The algorithm achieves KD ≈ 0.85–0.90 with only 50 prompts on unseen models, matching the quality of 500 randomly sampled prompts.

## Results

On COCO (40,504 prompts), FlashEval's 50-item subset achieves a Kendall's Tau of 0.851±0.004 on unseen models (random split), matching the 500-item random sampling baseline (0.829±0.000) — a 10× evaluation speedup. On DiffusionDB (5,000 prompts), FlashEval's 5-item subset (KD=0.789) surpasses the 100-item random sampling baseline (KD=0.763) on test models. Across all metrics (FID, CLIPScore, Aesthetic, ImageReward, HPS) and sub-tasks (random, model variants, schedulers), FlashEval consistently outperforms both prompt-level and set-level baselines, with the gap widening at smaller subset sizes (N'=10–50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashEval is compared against three baselines: Random Sampling (RS), prompt-level KD search (B3-prompt), and set-level KD search (B3-set). On COCO test models at N'=50, FlashEval (KD=0.851) outperforms B3-set (0.750±0.014) by ~10 points and B3-prompt (0.729) by ~12 points on the random split, while also exhibiting much lower variance (±0.004 vs ±0.014). Critically, FlashEval at N'=50 matches or exceeds RS at N'=500 (0.851 vs 0.829), demonstrating the claimed 10× speedup. On DiffusionDB, the advantage is even more pronounced: FlashEval at N'=5 (0.789) beats RS at N'=100 (0.763) on test models. B3-prompt degrades sharply at small N' (e.g., KD drops to 0.266 on model-variants sub-task at N'=5), confirming the paper's analysis that standalone prompt KD does not compose well into set-level rankings. B3-set suffers from insufficient search-space exploration, with high variance (±0.031 on DiffusionDB N'=5) that FlashEval's iterative frequency-based filtering effectively mitigates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the coreset/subset selection paradigm from training data (Sener & Savarese, Paul et al.) to the evaluation setting for text-to-image diffusion models, explicitly noting that insights from training subset selection do not transfer. It draws on evolutionary algorithms for the iterative search process and is closest in spirit to recent work on subset selection for language model evaluation [35], but is the first to address this problem for T2I diffusion models. The method builds on standard evaluation metrics (FID, CLIPScore, ImageReward, HPS, Aesthetic) without modifying them, positioning itself as a proxy accelerator rather than a replacement for existing evaluation protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× evaluation speedup (50 prompts matching 500 random prompts in ranking correlation) makes iterative model development—schedule selection, quantization tuning, architecture comparison—practically feasible, which was previously only done at final evaluation. A key open question is whether the searched subsets generalize to fundamentally new model families (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based architectures) beyond the Stable Diffusion zoo used for validation, since the search is conditioned on a specific model zoo. The method also requires a one-time search cost proportional to the dataset size, and its applicability to metrics that require distribution-level statistics (FID) is more constrained than for per-image metrics.
