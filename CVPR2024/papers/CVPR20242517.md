## Problem

Existing token pruning methods for Vision-Language Transformers operate on a single-modality basis, ignoring cross-modality semantic alignment, which causes tokens critical in one modality to be falsely pruned in the other. Additionally, prior VLT compression approaches use static, fixed compression ratios that cannot adapt to the varying computational complexity of different input instances.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts an image-text pair, where the image is patch-embedded into N visual tokens via a ViT encoder and the text is token-embedded into M language tokens via a BERT encoder. The number of retained tokens is not fixed: it varies per input instance and per transformer layer, determined dynamically by a learnable threshold. Downstream outputs are task-specific—binary match labels (NLVR2), ranked retrieval lists (Flickr30K/COCO), generated captions (COCO), or short text answers (VQA v2.0).

## Method

MADTP introduces two modules inserted into a pretrained VLT (CLIP or BLIP). The Multi-modality Alignment Guidance (MAG) module projects visual and language tokens into a shared dimension, then uses K learnable tokens as a common feature space: scaled dot-product attention between the learnable tokens and each modality's tokens yields token attention maps that encode cross-modal relevance, while a cosine-similarity loss (L_sim) forces the features extracted by the same learnable token from both modalities to align semantically. The Dynamic Token Pruning (DTP) module, placed between Self-Attention and FFN in every transformer block, computes a Token Importance Score (TIS) as the average of class-attention, self-attention, and MAG token-attention scores. A per-instance, per-layer threshold θ is derived by applying sparsemax to the MAG token attention maps (scaled by a temperature T) and taking the minimum of the resulting weighted TIS values; tokens below θ are pruned, and their information is preserved by weighting them with TIS and merging into a single residual token (EVit-style). Training minimizes L = L_task + α·L_sim from pretrained weights, with the temperature T dynamically adjusted per epoch to target a desired GFLOPs reduction.

## Results

On the NLVR2 visual reasoning task, MADTP compresses the BLIP model by 80% in GFLOPs (from 132.54 to 26.46) while retaining 79.22% test accuracy, a degradation of only 3.86% over the uncompressed model (83.08%). Remarkably, at a 30% reduce ratio, MADTP actually *exceeds* the uncompressed baseline (83.20% vs. 83.08% test accuracy) while cutting GFLOPs by 30%. On image-text retrieval (Flickr30K, COCO), image captioning (CIDEr), and VQA v2.0, MADTP consistently outperforms the UPop baseline at matched compression ratios, with the largest gains appearing at higher reduce ratios (e.g., +10.1% R@1 on COCO image-to-text retrieval at 75% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is UPop [35], a layer-wise dynamic parameter pruning method for VLTs. MADTP wins decisively across every reduce ratio on NLVR2: at 50% compression, MADTP achieves 82.85% test accuracy versus UPop's 77.61% (+5.24%), and at 80% compression the gap widens to 79.22% vs. 57.79% (+21.43%). The advantage grows monotonically with compression severity, suggesting UPop's static per-layer pruning ratios degrade sharply under aggressive compression while MADTP's instance-adaptive DTP module preserves more useful tokens. On image-text retrieval (Flickr30K and COCO), MADTP similarly outperforms UPop at both 50% and 75% reduce ratios for both CLIP and BLIP, with the largest single-metric gain being +10.1% R@1 on COCO image-to-text retrieval at 75% reduction (66.2% vs. 56.1%). On VQA v2.0, MADTP achieves 76.2% test-standard accuracy at 75% reduction versus UPop's 74.6%, while also reducing GFLOPs further (61.6 vs. 62.3). No comparison is made against ELIP [17] or CrossGET [36] with full numerical results, though these are discussed qualitatively in the related work.

## Relation to Prior Work

MADTP extends the unimodal dynamic token pruning line (DynamicViT, AdaViT, EVit) to the multimodal VLT setting, but its key conceptual departure is introducing explicit cross-modality alignment as a prerequisite for pruning. Prior VLT compression methods (Upop, ELIP, CrossGET) either prune parameters uniformly or use single-modality supervision to guide token removal, risking the deletion of tokens that are unimportant in one modality but critical in the other. By inserting learnable alignment tokens (MAG) that force shared semantic representations before computing importance scores, MADTP positions itself as the first VLT pruning framework that jointly considers layer-wise and instance-wise dynamics alongside cross-modal relevance, building on EVit's token-reorganization trick for information-preserving pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80% GFLOPs reduction with under 4% accuracy loss on NLVR2 is a practically significant efficiency gain for deploying VLTs on resource-constrained hardware, and the alignment-guided pruning principle could transfer to other multimodal architectures. However, the evaluation is limited to CLIP and BLIP on four datasets, with a single primary baseline (Upop), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger VLMs (BLIP-2, LLaVA) or to more than two modalities. The added MAG module introduces extra parameters and a similarity loss that must be tuned per task, and the dynamic threshold mechanism's sensitivity to batch composition (the 'max-keep' strategy) may limit real-world throughput gains.
