## Problem

Most decentralized personalized federated learning (DPFL) methods rely on undirected, symmetric communication topologies, which are prone to deadlocks under unstable wireless channels and force clients to aggregate models from neighbors at heterogeneous convergence levels, degrading personalized performance. The paper asks whether a directed, asymmetric collaboration topology combined with partial model exchange can simultaneously improve robustness, convergence speed, and personalization under data and computation heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

Each of 100 clients holds a non-IID subset of CIFAR-10 (32x32), CIFAR-100 (32x32), or Tiny-ImageNet (64x64) images, partitioned via Dirichlet (alpha=0.1, 0.3) or Pathological (c=2,5,10) distributions. The model is a ResNet-18 with group normalization, split into a shared feature-extraction backbone and a private linear classifier; only the shared backbone's gradients and push-sum bias weights are transmitted over the directed graph, while the classifier is updated purely locally. The output is a per-client personalized model (shared representation + private classifier) evaluated on each client's own test split.

## Method

DFedPGP performs three steps per communication round on a time-varying directed graph: (1) each client pulls the shared partial gradient and push-sum bias weight from its in-neighbors; (2) it alternately updates the private linear classifier (K_v local SGD steps) and the shared feature representation (K_u local SGD steps) using de-biased parameters z_i = u_i/mu_i; (3) it pushes the updated shared parameters and bias to its out-neighbors, then re-aggregates via push-sum to correct for the asymmetric mixing matrix (sum of p_{i,j} != 1). The directed topology lets each client independently select its out-neighbors, enlarging the effective feature search space without requiring reciprocal links. Theoretically, the paper proves an O(1/sqrt(T)) convergence rate in the general non-convex setting and shows that tighter connectivity (smaller q, smaller C) accelerates convergence.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DFedPGP achieves 88.85% (Dir-0.1), 85.61% (Dir-0.3), 91.26% (Pat-2), and 87.12% (Pat-5) test accuracy, outperforming the best baselines by 0.07–1.11%. On CIFAR-100, the gains are more pronounced: DFedPGP reaches 66.26% (Dir-0.1) and 57.66% (Dir-0.3), surpassing the next-best FedBABU by 6.03% and 5.29% respectively. In terms of convergence speed (Table 2), DFedPGP requires only 131 rounds to reach 80% on CIFAR-10 Dir-0.3, roughly half the 262–320 rounds needed by other methods. Under computation heterogeneity (Table 3), DFedPGP achieves 83.63% (Dir) and 91.83% (Pat) on CIFAR-10, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFedPGP outperforms all 8 baselines across every evaluated setting. Against centralized PFL methods, the largest margins appear on CIFAR-100: DFedPGP beats FedBABU by 6.03% (Dir-0.1) and 5.29% (Dir-0.3), and FedRep by 13.64% (Dir-0.1), indicating that the directed partial-gradient-push mechanism is especially beneficial under strong data heterogeneity with many classes. Among decentralized baselines, DFedPGP surpasses OSGP (the closest directed-graph competitor) by 1.47% on CIFAR-10 Dir-0.1 and 1.11% on CIFAR-100 Pat-10, while beating DFedAvgM by 2.12% on CIFAR-10 Dir-0.3 and 7.46% on CIFAR-100 Dir-0.1. Dis-PFL, which uses sparse training for personalization, trails DFedPGP by 1.04–10.61% depending on the setting. In the computation-heterogeneity experiment (Table 3), DFedPGP leads OSGP by 0.82% (Dir) and 0.22% (Pat), and FedRep by 0.55% (Dir) and 3.26% (Pat), confirming that partial gradient push mitigates the negative effect of aggregating models at different convergence levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: push-sum optimization on directed graphs (Nedić & Olshevsky 2016; Assran et al. 2019 SGP) and partial model personalization in federated learning (FedPer, FedRep, FedBABU, Pillutla et al. 2022). Prior directed DFL methods like OSGP focus on full-model exchange for a single consensus objective, while prior PFL methods assume undirected, symmetric topologies. DFedPGP departs from both by combining stochastic gradient push over directed, asymmetric topologies with a decoupled shared/personal parameter structure, enabling clients to selectively exchange only the shared feature gradients while retaining private classifiers locally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The directed topology addresses a practical pain point in decentralized FL—deadlock and suboptimal aggregation under heterogeneous communication and computation resources—without requiring a central server. The O(1/√T) convergence guarantee in the non-convex setting with partial participation provides theoretical grounding that prior directed DFL methods lacked for personalized objectives. Open questions include how to design principled (rather than random) neighbor selection strategies, whether the approach scales to larger networks or more complex architectures, and how the method performs under dynamic client dropout in real deployment settings.
