## Problem

Applying image harmonization methods frame-by-frame to composite videos causes severe inter-frame flickering, while existing video harmonization approaches (e.g., TCVHAN, CO2Net) only enforce short-term temporal consistency over a few consecutive frames without modeling long-term spatio-temporal appearance variation patterns. Additionally, standard relation-based temporal consistency metrics average over all frames, making them insensitive to the abrupt brightness jumps that constitute perceptually disruptive flickering.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 20-frame composite video at 256×256 resolution along with a per-frame binary foreground mask delineating the inharmonious region, and produces a harmonized video of identical size and frame count. During training, 5 frames are randomly sampled at varying frame rates per batch, while inference processes 20 or more consecutive frames. The foreground mask is concatenated with the video and jointly embedded into the token sequence, so the network explicitly conditions on which pixels require appearance adjustment.

## Method

The Video Triplet Transformer (VTT) decomposes spatio-temporal consistency into three complementary patterns processed by dedicated Transformer modules: (1) a Short-Term Spatial Transformer that adjusts spatial features within small neighbor-frame groups, (2) a Long-Term Global Transformer that captures gradual appearance variation trends across the full temporal sequence using a masked prediction strategy (inspired by BERT/MAE) to suppress redundant spatially-aligned features, and (3) a Long-Term Dynamic Transformer that employs deformable attention with learnable position offsets to align similar contextual appearances across frames despite object motion. The pipeline tokenizes input frames via patch embedding, applies multiple ST-ST layers for coarse spatial harmonization, then iteratively refines with stacked Triplet Transformer layers (ST-ST + LT-GT + LT-DT) whose outputs are summed, and finally decodes via a 2D-CNN. Training minimizes an L1 reconstruction loss plus a relation-based temporal consistency penalty (λ=5), and the masked prediction in LT-GT uses random 75% masking within temporal windows to maximize exposure to diverse appearance variation patterns.

## Results

On the HYouTube video harmonization benchmark, VHTT achieves PSNR 40.03, fPSNR 31.23, fMSE 90.35, R-RTC 0.03, and fR-RTC 1.26, setting new state-of-the-art across all spatial and temporal metrics. The most striking improvement is in temporal consistency: fR-RTC drops from 6.07 (CO2Net) to 1.26, a ~4.8× reduction, while fMSE improves from 154.20 (HT+) to 90.35 (~41% reduction). On secondary tasks, VHTT also leads: video enhancement on SDSD (PSNR 26.61 vs. 26.10 for VEN-Retinex; R-RTC 0.08 vs. 0.46) and video demoiréing (PSNR 28.56 vs. 23.71 for VDRTC; R-RTC 1.24 vs. 1.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VHTT outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Among image harmonization methods, HT+ [14] is the strongest spatial baseline (PSNR 38.55, fMSE 154.20) but suffers severe flickering (fR-RTC 12.51); VHTT improves PSNR by +1.48 and reduces fMSE by 41% while cutting fR-RTC by ~90%. The prior video harmonization method CO2Net [31] achieves the best temporal consistency among baselines (fR-RTC 6.07) but at 5190G FLOPs; VHTT matches and surpasses it on all metrics (fR-RTC 1.26) while using only 1727G FLOPs (~3× less). TCVHAN [17] is the weakest video baseline (fR-RTC 15.23), confirming that optical-flow-based alignment alone is insufficient. On the secondary video enhancement task (SDSD), VHTT beats VEN-Retinex [46] by +0.51 PSNR and reduces R-RTC from 0.46 to 0.08. On video demoiréing, VHTT surpasses VDRTC [8] by +4.85 PSNR (28.56 vs. 23.71) and slightly improves R-RTC (1.24 vs. 1.32), demonstrating the framework's generality beyond harmonization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions video harmonization as a problem of modeling spatio-temporal variation patterns, departing from prior frame-by-frame approaches (TCVHAN, CO2Net) that rely on optical flow or color-mapping consistency between adjacent frames. Architecturally, it adapts deformable attention from Deformable DETR for dynamic context alignment and borrows the masked prediction paradigm from BERT/MAE for long-term global appearance modeling, unifying both within a multi-scale Transformer (short-term spatial, long-term global, long-term dynamic). The proposed R-RTC/fR-RTC metrics extend relation-based temporal consistency evaluation by introducing an anchor-value mechanism to amplify flickering signals that standard averaging-based RTC metrics dilute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The triplet decomposition provides a clean, interpretable framework for video-to-video tasks that could generalize beyond harmonization (demonstrated on enhancement and demoiréing), and the fR-RTC metric addresses a genuine gap in how the field evaluates temporal artifacts. However, the work is limited to 20-frame sequences on a single synthetic dataset (HYouTube), and the masked prediction strategy, while effective, adds architectural complexity whose optimal masking ratio (75%) may not transfer to longer or more diverse video content. Open questions include scalability to minute-long videos, whether the triplet hierarchy is truly necessary versus a simpler multi-scale design, and whether the metric's HSV-Value-channel preference holds across diverse content types.
