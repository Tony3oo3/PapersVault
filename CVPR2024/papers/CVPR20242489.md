## Problem

State-of-the-art multi-person 3D motion estimation methods such as SLAHMR rely on purely kinematic optimization and ignore fundamental physical constraints, producing severe inter-person penetration, ground penetration, and foot skating—especially during close interactions with heavy occlusion. No prior method has incorporated a full-featured physics simulator into the multi-person motion estimation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242489/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes monocular RGB video but operates on SLAHMR's global kinematic pose estimates (SMPL-H parameters: global orientation, body pose, shape, and root translation per person per frame) rather than raw pixels. The body shape β is inherited directly from SLAHMR and held constant. Output is a temporally coherent sequence of physically compliant SMPL-H poses in world coordinates for all N interacting people (N=2 in experiments, but architecturally unbounded).

## Method

MultiPhys feeds SLAHMR's kinematic poses autoregressively into a Mujoco physics simulator where each person is represented as an articulated humanoid agent. A repurposed Universal Humanoid Controller (UHC), trained with PPO on the single-person AMASS dataset, generates joint-torque actions that drive each agent to track its reference pose while the shared simulation naturally enforces non-penetration between bodies and with the ground. The key technical contribution is the loop-N iterative refinement: at each timestep, the simulation state is updated for N_l iterations (N_l=2 in practice) while holding the reference pose fixed, allowing the policy to progressively close the gap on highly dynamic motions where a single step would lag. Because agents are controlled independently within one shared simulation, the policy requires no multi-person training data yet still produces physically consistent multi-person interactions.

## Results

On the three evaluation datasets (CHI3D, Hi4D, ExPI), MultiPhys reduces inter-person penetration by 7.4×, 7.2×, and 7.8× compared to SLAHMR (18.7 vs 139.3 mm, 51.1 vs 367.3 mm, 73.0 vs 567.3 mm), and ground penetration by 1.4×, 5.1×, and 31× respectively. Pose accuracy (W-MPJPE) is competitive with SLAHMR: 174.7 vs 177.1 mm on CHI3D, 118.1 vs 121.6 mm on Hi4D, and 250.9 vs 263.3 mm on ExPI. However, MultiPhys incurs higher acceleration error (7.4 vs 6.5, 9.6 vs 6.9, 17.1 vs 8.2 mm/s²) and slightly worse skating on CHI3D (2.7 vs 1.0 mm) due to the loop-N refinement step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SLAHMR (the state-of-the-art kinematic method), MultiPhys achieves a dramatic reduction in inter-person penetration across all three datasets (7–8× improvement) and ground penetration (1.4–31× improvement), while matching or slightly improving pose accuracy (W-MPJPE within 1–12 mm). The trade-off is higher acceleration error (jittering) and, on CHI3D, slightly worse skating, because SLAHMR directly optimizes for smoothness and benefits from the less dynamic motions in that dataset. Against EmbPose-MP (the multi-person adaptation of the physics-based single-person method), MultiPhys wins decisively on pose metrics (e.g., W-MPJPE 174.7 vs 214.7 on CHI3D, 118.1 vs 148.8 on Hi4D, 250.9 vs 386.4 on ExPI) because EmbPose-MP cannot handle inter-person occlusion and misplaces bodies. However, EmbPose-MP achieves lower skating on Hi4D (1.3 vs 3.5 mm) and ExPI (1.9 vs 2.3 mm), and lower penetration on Hi4D (39.8 vs 51.1 mm), partly because its poor spatial placement keeps bodies farther apart, artificially reducing penetration.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiPhys extends the single-person physics-based motion estimation line (EmbPose/UHC [19], SimPoE [44], PhysCap [30]) to the multi-person setting by placing multiple humanoid agents in a shared Mujoco simulation, so that inter-body collision constraints are enforced naturally. For the kinematic initialization, it relies on SLAHMR [39], a state-of-the-art multi-person motion estimator that performs global optimization but is physics-agnostic. The key architectural departure from prior single-person physics methods is the loop-N iterative refinement, which addresses the autoregressive error accumulation that arises when the RL policy must track highly dynamic multi-person reference poses. The paper positions itself as the first to incorporate a full-featured physics simulator into multi-person 3D motion estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that hard physical constraints (non-penetration, ground contact) can be enforced in multi-person motion recovery without sacrificing kinematic accuracy, yielding a 7× reduction in inter-body penetration and substantial ground-penetration and skating improvements over the kinematic baseline. A secondary but notable finding is that physics compliance also improves spatial placement (W-MPJPE) on CHI3D, suggesting that collision constraints act as an implicit spatial prior. Limitations include dependence on SLAHMR's initialization quality (2D keypoint failures cascade), the ad-hoc nature of loop-N (optimal N_l is dataset-dependent), and the lack of an explicit two-person interaction prior. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully beyond two people with close contact and whether a learned interaction prior could replace the generic collision model.
