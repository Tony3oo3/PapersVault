## Problem

Self-gated activation functions assume that a feature's importance is monotonically correlated with its intensity (the feature-filter inner product), but feature and filter norms bias this inner product, causing a mismatch between actual importance and explicit intensity. Prior solutions like IIEU address this via explicit norm-decoupling, which introduces complex gradients and potentially neutralizes the discriminative information carried by norms. The paper seeks a simpler mechanism that compensates for norm-induced biases without removing the informative norm cues.

## Input / Output

AdaShift is a drop-in activation replacement operating on normalized feature-filter inner products (post-BatchNorm or LayerNorm) within standard CNN backbones (ResNet, MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV2). It takes RGB images at standard resolutions (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) and produces task-appropriate outputs: class labels, bounding boxes, or pixel-wise segmentation masks. A critical constraint is that the shift factor Δ must be balanced with the input x̃ in scale (i.e., both should be Z-scored), otherwise the summation becomes imbalanced and accuracy drops.

## Method

AdaShift introduces an adaptive shift factor Δ into the re-weighting function of a self-gated activation: φ(x̃) = σ(x̃ + Δ)·x̃, where σ is a monotonic curve (e.g., Sigmoid). The shift is applied inside σ rather than to the feature itself, which ablations confirm is essential for effective recalibration. AdaShift-B computes Δ via LayerNorm on globally average-pooled channel statistics, capturing tensor-level non-local cues with negligible parameters. AdaShift-MA extends this with a minimalist self-attention module (replacing all linear projections with LayerNorm) over locally pooled patch statistics, adding finer-grained spatial context. The design is motivated by a Softmax-classification analysis showing that filter norms encode dataset-level non-local cues while feature norms encode local confidence signals, so gentle adaptive shifts preserve these cues unlike brutal norm-decoupling.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, AdaShift-B achieves 79.9% top-1 accuracy (+2.7% over ReLU) and AdaShift-MA achieves 80.3% (+3.1%), outperforming the previous SOTA IIEU (79.7%) with fewer parameters (25.7M vs. 25.6M) and comparable FLOPs. On ResNet-101, AdaShift-MA reaches 81.2% top-1 (+2.3% over ReLU), surpassing IIEU (80.3%). On CIFAR-100 with CF-ResNet-56, AdaShift-MA achieves 77.0% ± 0.4 top-1, improving over the best prior method Meta-ACON (75.7% ± 0.2) by 1.3 points, all with negligible additional parameters (~0.1M) and FLOPs over the ReLU baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaShift-B and AdaShift-MA consistently outperform all 17 compared activation functions across ImageNet and CIFAR-100. On ResNet-50 ImageNet, AdaShift-MA (80.3%) surpasses the prior SOTA IIEU (79.7%) by 0.6 points while using fewer FLOPs (4.2G vs. 4.2G) and only 0.1M more parameters than ReLU; it also beats Meta-ACON (78.0%) by 2.3 points and SMU (77.5%) by 2.8 points. On ResNet-101, AdaShift-MA (81.2%) leads IIEU (80.3%) by 0.9 points and Meta-ACON (78.9%) by 2.3 points. On CIFAR-100 (CF-ResNet-56), AdaShift-MA (77.0%) improves over Meta-ACON (75.7%), SiLU (75.3%), and GELU (75.3%) by 1.3–1.7 points. Notably, the paper reports that ResNet-50 with AdaShift can match Swin-T (a Transformer) in accuracy with lower cost and fewer parameters, and ResNet-50 with AdaShift outperforms the larger ResNet-101 with ReLU, demonstrating that the activation improvement compensates for model depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to the author's prior work IIEU (ICCV 2023), which first identified the norm-induced mismatched feature scoring problem in self-gated activations through a Multi-Criteria Decision-Making interpretation. While IIEU addressed this by explicitly decoupling feature and filter norms from the inner product, AdaShift departs from that paradigm by arguing that norms carry informative local and non-local cues that should be preserved rather than removed. Instead of norm-decoupling, AdaShift introduces a learnable adaptive shift factor Δ (computed via LayerNorm on channel statistics or a minimalist self-attention) that gently translates the input to the re-weighting function, positioning itself as a simpler and more efficient alternative to the broader family of dynamic self-gated activations (Swish, ACON, SMU, ErfAct, Pserf) that modify scaling or bounds but leave the norm-induced bias unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a near-zero-cost modification (a single LayerNorm on channel means) to the activation re-weighting curve yields 2.7–5.2 point Top-1 gains on ImageNet across ResNet, MobileNetV2, and ShuffleNetv2, enabling ResNet-50 to match Swin-T with fewer parameters under an improved training recipe. The conceptual reframing—that feature/filter norms are informative context rather than noise to be decoupled—challenges the working assumption in IIEU and opens a design space where activation functions can exploit tensor-level statistics without heavy gradient overhead. Open questions include whether the balanced-summation requirement generalizes to unnormalized inputs in MetaFormer/Transformer blocks (only briefly addressed in Supp), whether the minimalist attention in AdaShift-MA can be further simplified without accuracy loss, and whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities or generative models.
