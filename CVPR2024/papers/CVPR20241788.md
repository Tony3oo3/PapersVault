## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation methods face a fundamental trade-off: 3D proposal networks (e.g., OpenMask3D) miss small or geometrically ambiguous objects from rare classes, while 2D-to-3D back-projection approaches (e.g., OVIR-3D) produce fragmented proposals contaminated with background points due to occlusions and imperfect 2D mask alignment. No prior method effectively combines both sources to achieve high recall across the full category distribution, particularly for tail classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud with per-point RGB (N points in R^6), an RGB-D video sequence of T frames with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics (downsampled by a factor of 10 for efficiency), and a free-form text query. It outputs a set of K binary 3D instance masks (K×N matrix), each scored against the text embedding via average cosine similarity in CLIP space. Camera parameters are assumed known, and the 2D instance segmenter (Grounded-SAM) is run per-frame using dataset class names as prompts.

## Method

The core contribution is a 2D-Guided-3D Instance Proposal Module that constructs complete 3D proposals by hierarchically aggregating superpoint regions guided by 2D masks across multiple frames. Per-frame, superpoints are merged into candidate regions using IoU with projected 2D masks and cosine similarity in 3D feature space (from ISBNet's backbone) to filter background contamination. Across frames, agglomerative clustering with a hierarchical (binary-tree) merging order combines partial regions from different viewpoints into coherent 3D object proposals. These 2D-guided proposals are combined with class-agnostic 3D proposals from ISBNet via NMS. Finally, a 3D-aware Pointwise Feature Extraction accumulates CLIP image features onto the point cloud weighted by per-point visibility frequency across top-λ=5 views, enabling open-vocabulary classification via cosine similarity with text embeddings.

## Results

On ScanNet200 (198 classes), Open3DIS achieves AP 23.7, AP50 29.4, and AP25 32.8, outperforming the strongest open-vocabulary baselines OVIR-3D (AP 13.0) and OpenMask3D (AP 15.4) by margins of +10.7 and +8.3 respectively. On the tail classes (rare objects), Open3DIS reaches APtail 21.8, surpassing even the fully-supervised ISBNet (12.5) and Mask3D (17.9). On S3DIS, Open3DIS more than doubles the novel-class AP50 of prior methods (26.3 vs. 8.6 for PLA, 13.8 for Lowis3D). On Replica (zero-shot, 2D-only proposals), it achieves AP 18.1, beating OpenMask3D by +5.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Open3DIS decisively outperforms all open-vocabulary baselines on ScanNet200: it beats OVIR-3D [33] by +10.7 AP and OpenMask3D [45] by +8.3 AP, with the largest gains on rare (tail) classes where it scores 21.8 vs. 11.7 and 14.9 respectively. Against the fully-supervised ISBNet [36] and Mask3D [42], Open3DIS reaches ~96% and ~88% of their AP while exceeding both on APcom and APtail, demonstrating that 2D-guided proposals compensate for the lack of class-specific 3D supervision. On S3DIS, Open3DIS more than doubles the novel-class AP50 of PLA [8] (26.3 vs. 8.6) and Lowis3D [7] (26.3 vs. 13.8), confirming the advantage of leveraging 2D foundation models for unseen categories. On Replica in a strict zero-shot setting (no 3D proposal network), Open3DIS still surpasses OpenMask3D by +5.0 AP and OVIR-3D by +7.0 AP. The only comparisons where Open3DIS trails are against fully-supervised methods on head classes (APhead 27.8 vs. 38.6/39.8), which is expected given the open-vocabulary constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

Open3DIS builds on the OV-3DIS line initiated by OpenMask3D (3D class-agnostic proposals + CLIP scoring) and OVIR-3D (2D-to-3D back-projection), but departs from both by introducing a 2D-Guided 3D Instance Proposal Module that uses superpoint merging guided by multi-frame 2D masks and 3D feature similarity to produce geometrically coherent 3D proposals. It also extends the pointwise CLIP feature extraction of OpenMask3D with a visibility-weighted accumulation strategy. The 3D proposal backbone (ISBNet/Mask3D) and 2D segmenter (Grounded-SAM) are repurposed from their original fully-supervised and 2D-only settings, respectively, into a unified open-vocabulary pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~1.5× AP improvement on ScanNet200 and the particularly strong tail-class performance (AP_tail 21.8 vs. 14.9 for OpenMask3D) demonstrate that 2D foundation models can substantially rescue 3D instance segmentation for rare and small objects that 3D networks miss. The ablation showing that 2D-only proposals (18.2 AP) already surpass the best prior method (15.4 AP) highlights the underexploited potential of multi-view 2D guidance for 3D geometry. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is how to synergistically integrate the 2D-guided and 3D-native proposal modules rather than simply concatenating their outputs, which could yield further gains.
