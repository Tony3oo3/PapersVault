## Problem

SAM, a prompt-driven foundation model, cannot be directly applied to video camouflaged object detection because camouflaged objects are imperceptible to the naked eye, making reliable user-provided prompts infeasible. Existing VCOD methods rely on two-stage architectures that accumulate errors and generalize poorly, and none adapt SAM to this task. The paper addresses both the prompt reliability gap and the lack of temporal-spatial context in SAM's image encoder for video sequences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive RGB video frames at 352×352 resolution along with a windowed clip of T=7 frames for long-range consistency extraction. It produces a pixel-level segmentation mask via SAM's frozen decoder, conditioned on two learned prompts: a dense mask prompt (from a multi-scale decoder) and a sparse box or point prompt (from a residual-block head). The sparse prompt is a 4-dimensional coordinate vector refined by long-range consistency, while the dense prompt is a full-resolution binary mask.

## Method

TSP-SAM is an end-to-end framework that freezes SAM and trains only the prompt-learning modules. First, motion-driven self-prompt learning applies DCT to consecutive frames, establishes inter-frame correspondences via multi-head cross-attention separately on low-frequency (global deformation) and high-frequency (local edge motion) bands, then fuses the resulting implicit motion with PVT spatial features to generate both dense and sparse prompts. Second, a 3D ResNet18 extracts long-range temporal-spatial consistency from the windowed clip, which is cross-attended with SAM's image embedding to produce a refinement term that corrects the sparse prompt against inter-frame discontinuities. Third, the same long-range consistency is added directly to SAM's image embedding (temporal-spatial injection) to enhance the encoder's representational capacity. Training uses a joint loss combining SAM's prediction loss, a BCE+MSE sparse prompt loss, and a weighted cross-entropy + IoU dense prompt loss, with SAM's parameters held fixed throughout.

## Results

On MoCA-Mask, TSP-SAM with mask+box prompts (M+B) achieves mIoU of 38.8% and mDice of 45.8%, surpassing the previous SOTA SLT-Net (mIoU 31.0%, mDice 38.7%) by 7.8 and 7.1 percentage points respectively. On CAD2016, TSP-SAM (M+B) reaches mIoU of 43.8% and mDice of 54.3%, improving over SLT-Net (mIoU 34.2%, mDice 44.5%) by 9.6 and 9.8 percentage points. The model also generalizes well: trained on MoCA-Mask, it outperforms all baselines on the unseen CAD2016 dataset by 0.9%–14.7% across metrics, and achieves 62.84% mIoU on SegTrack-v2, surpassing SLT-Net by 9.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSP-SAM outperforms all 11 baselines on both MoCA-Mask and CAD2016 across every reported metric. Against the strongest video baseline SLT-Net [5], TSP-SAM (M+B) gains 7.8 pp in mIoU on MoCA-Mask (38.8% vs. 31.0%) and 9.6 pp on CAD2016 (43.8% vs. 34.2%), with consistent improvements of 1.3%–8.7% across all six metrics on MoCA-Mask. Among single-image COD methods, the best performer PUENet [44] reaches only 21.2% mIoU on MoCA-Mask, far below TSP-SAM's 38.8%, underscoring the critical role of temporal-spatial cues. On the generalization test (trained on MoCA-Mask, tested on CAD2016), TSP-SAM surpasses SLT-Net by up to 14.7% in mIoU and also outperforms PUENet (38.9% mIoU) by 4.9 pp. On the multi-object SegTrack-v2 dataset, TSP-SAM achieves 62.84% mIoU, exceeding SLT-Net by 9.9% and approaching the recent SMTC method. The mask+box prompt configuration consistently outperforms mask+point prompts, with the gap most pronounced on mIoU (38.8% vs. 34.5% on MoCA-Mask), attributed to the box prompt's superior boundary conveyance for irregularly shaped camouflaged objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: prompt-driven foundation models (SAM) and video camouflaged object detection (VCOD). It departs from prior VCOD methods (SLT-Net, MG, RCRNet) that rely on two-stage architectures for temporal-spatial modeling, instead adopting an end-to-end formulation where SAM's frozen backbone is guided by automatically learned prompts. The motion-driven self-prompt learning repurposes frequency-domain analysis (previously used in static COD by FSPNet and Zhong et al.) to capture inter-frame motion, while the long-range consistency module borrows 3D CNN temporal modeling to address a gap SAM was not designed to fill. As the first SAM-based camouflaged object detection framework, it extends the line of work adapting SAM to underperformed domains (MedSAM, SAM-Adapter) into the camouflage setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen 636M-parameter foundation model, guided by lightweight learned prompts (~90M trainable parameters), can outperform purpose-built VCOD architectures by 7.8–9.6% mIoU, suggesting that temporal-spatial prompt learning is a viable strategy for adapting SAM to visually challenging domains. However, the evaluation is limited to two small datasets (87 and 9 video sequences), and SAM accounts for 75% of inference time, raising practical deployment concerns. Open questions include whether unfreezing SAM's encoder would yield further gains, whether the approach generalizes to longer or more complex video sequences, and whether the frequency-domain motion perception advantage holds at larger scales or in synthetic/medical video settings.
