## Problem

Existing SE(3)-equivariant models for 3D point trajectory prediction rely on group convolutions, high-order representations, vector neurons, or fixed frame averaging, each introducing additional computational cost and sometimes limiting prediction accuracy. A key open question is how to design both the equivariant framework and the specific network architecture to balance expressiveness and efficiency for trajectory prediction tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with N points, each having a 3D position, 3D velocity, an SE(3)-invariant attribute vector (e.g., particle charge or velocity norm), and edge attributes encoding connectivity (stick/hinge indicators, distances). It outputs future 3D point positions and updated point attributes, with the guarantee that applying any SE(3) transformation to the input yields the correspondingly transformed output. The prediction is performed over a single frame interval (e.g., 30 frames for motion capture, 5000 frames for molecular dynamics), not as a sequential autoregressive rollout.

## Method

PT-EvNet achieves SE(3) equivariance through a three-stage pipeline: (1) unique pose-normalization via PCA where singular vector direction ambiguity is resolved by anchoring to the farthest point from the centroid, (2) a point position predictor F that applies N_K=4 learnable SE(3) pose transformations (parameterized with 6D rotation representations) to the normalized cloud, runs a shared Global-Local Motion Estimation Layer on each transformed pose, and aggregates predictions by inverse-transforming and averaging, and (3) inverse pose-normalization to recover the original frame. The Global-Local Motion Estimation Layer decomposes positions and velocities into global (centroid) and local (relative) components, then iteratively (N_S=4 steps) updates a shared global displacement and per-point local displacements via MLP-based message passing over a two-hop neighborhood, combining them additively for the final position estimate. The full network stacks three such pose-transformed prediction blocks, and SE(3) equivariance is formally proven for the entire composition.

## Results

On the CMU MoCap dataset, PT-EvNet achieves MSE of 4.8×10⁻² (Walking) and 18.7×10⁻² (Running), outperforming the previous best EqMotion (5.8 and 20.3) by 1.0% and 1.6% respectively. On MD17, PT-EvNet obtains the best MSE on 6 out of 8 molecules, with a particularly large improvement on Benzene (26.25×10⁻² vs. GMN's 48.12×10⁻²). On the Simulation dataset, PT-EvNet achieves the lowest MSE across all single-system configurations and most multiple-system configurations, e.g., 10.06×10⁻² on single (3,3) vs. FA-GNN's 11.14×10⁻².

## Baselines & Comparisons

PT-EvNet outperforms all nine baselines on both CMU subsets, with the closest competitor being EqMotion [33] (5.8 Walking, 20.3 Running) which uses equivariant multi-agent motion prediction with invariant interaction reasoning. FA-GNN [25], which applies frame averaging over 8 PCA-derived symmetry group elements, is notably weaker on Running (37.3 vs. 18.7), suggesting that fixed symmetry-group transforms are less effective than the learned pose transformations. On MD17, PT-EvNet beats GMN [15] (the previous best on most molecules) on 6 of 8 molecules, with the largest margin on Benzene (26.25 vs. 48.12), while GMN retains a slight edge on Ethanol (4.63 vs. 4.34) and Malonaldehyde (12.82 vs. 13.22). On the Simulation dataset, PT-EvNet dominates all single-system settings and most multiple-system settings, with the only exception being the (10,10) multiple system where FA-GNN [25] achieves 13.80 vs. PT-EvNet's 14.25. EGHN [14] and EGNN [26], which rely on equivariant message passing and graph hierarchy operations, are consistently outperformed by a wide margin across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PT-EvNet sits at the intersection of frame-averaging equivariant networks (FA-GNN [25]) and equivariant GNNs (EGNN, GMN, EGHN), but departs from both in a key architectural choice: rather than averaging over a fixed set of 8 PCA-derived symmetry group elements or performing equivariant message passing, it applies *learnable* SE(3) pose transformations (parameterized via 6D rotation representations) and aggregates predictions across these learned views. The unique PCA pose normalization with an anchor-point disambiguation strategy is borrowed from rotation-invariant point cloud analysis [36], and the global-local motion decomposition is inspired by classical rigid-body-plus-deformation splitting in physics. The paper explicitly positions itself against high-order equivariant methods (TFN, SE(3)-Transformers, LieConv) and vector-neuron approaches, arguing that the pose-transformed framework avoids their higher computational overhead while remaining provably SE(3)-equivariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learnable pose-transformation strategy offers a practical middle ground between the rigidity of fixed frame averaging and the expressiveness (but cost) of high-order equivariant layers, and the ablations confirm it outperforms both fixed and random transformations across all tested datasets. The global-local decomposition provides a physically interpretable inductive bias that may transfer to other motion-prediction settings. However, the paper only addresses single-step prediction; the authors note sequential trajectory prediction as future work, and the approach has not been tested on very large systems or non-SE(3) symmetry groups. Whether the learned pose transformations generalize across tasks without retraining, and how the method scales to thousands of particles, remain open questions.
