## Problem

Existing 3D plane estimation methods are either purely geometric (e.g., RANSAC on point clouds), which cannot distinguish semantically distinct but co-planar surfaces, or learning-based but limited to single images, producing per-pixel embeddings that are not 3D-consistent across views. The paper identifies that a surprisingly strong baseline comes from combining modern MVS reconstruction with sequential RANSAC, yet this still fails to segment adjacent co-planar instances (e.g., a picture frame from the wall). The gap is a method that leverages learned semantic cues while maintaining 3D consistency across an entire scene.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of posed RGB images (no explicit depth or 3D input required) and produces a planarized 3D mesh where each vertex is assigned to a plane instance, along with the underlying 3D-consistent embeddings. Depth, planar probability, and per-pixel embeddings are estimated per-frame by a shared-encoder network, then fused volumetrically into a TSDF; non-planar voxels (aggregated probability < 0.25) are excluded from the mesh. The online variant runs at 152 ms per keyframe on an RTX A6000, below the average 272 ms keyframe interval in ScanNetV2, using mean-shift clustering (25 ms) instead of full-scene RANSAC (131 ms).

## Method

The core contribution is a per-scene 3-layer MLP (128-dim hidden layers, 3-dim output, periodic activations) that maps any 3D point to a plane embedding, trained at test time by distilling per-pixel embeddings from a single-image plane estimator. The distillation loss (Eq. 1) is a push-pull objective: pairs of 3D points whose corresponding image-space embeddings are close (threshold t_e) AND whose normals align (threshold t_n) are pulled together, while all other pairs are pushed apart (threshold t_p). Geometry is provided by SimpleRecon (MVS depth + TSDF fusion), augmented with a planar-probability channel that prunes non-planar regions from the mesh before clustering. Plane grouping uses sequential RANSAC where inlier status requires both geometric distance < r_d and embedding distance < r_e, followed by merging of near-duplicate planes and connected-components splitting. This combination of learned 3D-consistent embeddings with geometric priors outperforms both the geometry-only RANSAC baseline and prior learning-based methods on segmentation and planar metrics.

## Results

On the ScanNetV2 test^planes split, the proposed method achieves the best segmentation scores (VOI 2.268, RI 0.957, SC 0.568) and the best planar metrics (fidelity 8.76, accuracy 7.98, chamfer 8.37) across all compared methods. On geometry, it achieves a chamfer distance of 5.30 and F1 of 64.92, which is comparable to the SR+RANSAC baseline (5.40 / 65.45) but significantly better than PlanarRecon (9.89 / 43.47). The method runs at interactive speeds (152 ms per keyframe) using mean-shift clustering, versus 131 ms per scene for the RANSAC variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest baseline, SR+RANSAC (geometry from SimpleRecon with sequential RANSAC but no 3D embeddings), is competitive on geometry (chamfer 5.40 vs. 5.30, F1 65.45 vs. 64.92) but is clearly outperformed on all segmentation metrics (VOI 2.507 vs. 2.268, SC 0.515 vs. 0.568) and planar metrics (accuracy 10.13 vs. 7.98, planar chamfer 9.78 vs. 8.37), demonstrating the value of 3D-consistent embeddings for instance separation. PlanarRecon, the prior state-of-the-art for 3D plane estimation from posed RGB, is substantially outperformed on every metric (e.g., planar chamfer 17.53 vs. 8.37, VOI 3.201 vs. 2.268). FineRecon+RANSAC is the strongest geometry competitor (chamfer 5.56) and even edges out the proposed method on planar fidelity (7.74 vs. 8.76), but loses on segmentation (SC 0.531 vs. 0.568) and planar accuracy (11.71 vs. 7.98). The single-image method PlaneRecTR+aggregation and the volumetric methods Atlas+RANSAC and NeuralRecon+RANSAC are all significantly worse across the board, with planar chamfer values of 19.14, 20.16, and 14.92 respectively. Notably, the paper shows that adding their 3D embeddings to any of the geometry backbones (Atlas, NeuralRecon, FineRecon, SR) consistently improves results over geometry+RANSAC alone (Table 2), confirming the embeddings are a general-purpose complement to 3D geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper distills per-pixel associative embeddings from single-image plane estimators (Yu et al. [82]) into 3D-consistent per-scene embeddings via a test-time-optimized MLP, drawing on the interactive labeling paradigm of iLabel [87] and iMap [69]. For geometry, it relies on SimpleRecon [58] (a lightweight multi-view stereo + TSDF pipeline) rather than the heavier 3D-convolution and recurrent architectures used by PlanarRecon [79], the closest prior multi-view plane estimation method. The key conceptual departure is replacing PlanarRecon's complex incremental detection-and-fusion pipeline with a simple per-scene embedding network plus sequential RANSAC clustering, while also introducing planar/non-planar probability as an additional TSDF channel to exclude non-planar regions from the mesh.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art plane estimation on ScanNetV2 at interactive speeds (152 ms/keyframe), making it practical for real-time AR and robotics applications where both geometric accuracy and semantic plane separation matter. The finding that a pure RANSAC-on-SimpleRecon baseline already outperforms the previous SOTA (PlanarRecon) reframes the problem: the bottleneck is not geometry quality but the lack of semantic/appearance cues to separate co-planar instances. Open questions include whether global optimization (e.g., energy-based fitting) could further improve over the greedy RANSAC, whether unobserved regions can be completed, and how the method scales to outdoor or highly non-planar scenes.
