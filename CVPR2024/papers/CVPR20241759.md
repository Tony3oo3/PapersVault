## Problem

Existing audio-visual contrastive architectures (e.g., ImageBind, CAVMAE) learn global representations that excel at cross-modal retrieval but fail to produce high-resolution, semantically aligned local features capable of localizing words or sounds within an image. No prior method has demonstrated that a self-supervised model can simultaneously ground both spoken language and non-speech sounds at pixel level, nor that it can automatically disentangle these two types of audio-visual association without explicit supervision.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10-second single-channel audio clips (resampled to 16 kHz for the HuBERT backbone) and a single randomly sampled video frame. It outputs dense per-pixel visual feature maps and per-time-step audio feature maps, each split into K=2 heads; inner products between these dense features directly yield segmentation heatmaps (no decoder), a scalar cross-modal similarity score, and head-specific activation patterns that separate sound from language. Evaluation uses binary segmentation masks from ADE20K scored with mAP and mIoU, plus 1000-way cross-modal retrieval accuracy.

## Method

DenseAV pairs a DINO ViT visual backbone with a HuBERT audio backbone, each followed by lightweight 'aligner' layers (LayerNorm + convolutions). The core innovation is a multi-head aggregation operator: it computes a full pairwise inner-product volume between dense local tokens (Eq. 1), then aggregates into a scalar similarity by max-pooling over heads and spatial dimensions while average-pooling over audio time (Eq. 2), encouraging the network to find strong local couplings regardless of object size or sound duration. Training uses a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss over in-batch negatives, augmented by a disentanglement regularizer that penalizes simultaneous activation of both heads and several stability terms. With two heads trained jointly on AudioSet (sound) and PlacesAudio (language), the heads naturally specialize—one localizing object sounds, the other tracking word meaning—without any label for this separation. DINO is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8) on Q/K/V attention layers while HuBERT is fully fine-tuned, with a 3000-step aligner warmup before full training for 800k steps.

## Results

On the newly introduced speech-prompted semantic segmentation task (ADE20K), DenseAV achieves 48.7% mAP and 36.8% mIoU, a 51% relative improvement over the best prior method (DAVENet at 32.2% mAP). On sound-prompted segmentation, DenseAV reaches 32.4% mAP and 25.5% mIoU, outperforming CAVMAE (26.0% mAP) by 6.4 points. For cross-modal retrieval, DenseAV achieves 94.2%/94.3% Acc@10 on PlacesAudio (I→A / A→I) and 68.2%/68.4% on AudioSet, surpassing ImageBind (64.5%/66.5% on AudioSet) with fewer than half the trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenseAV outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against DAVENet [23], the closest prior method for language grounding, DenseAV gains +16.5 mAP on speech segmentation (48.7% vs 32.2%) and +15.6 mAP on sound segmentation (32.4% vs 16.8%), while also achieving 94.2% vs 52.8% Acc@10 on PlacesAudio retrieval. CAVMAE [18] is the strongest sound-localization baseline (26.0% mAP) but still trails DenseAV by 6.4 mAP, and loses on retrieval (81.7% vs 94.2% on PlacesAudio I→A). ImageBind [17], the state-of-the-art multi-modal retrieval model, is particularly weak on localization (20.2% speech mAP, 18.3% sound mAP) and catastrophically fails on PlacesAudio retrieval (1.10% Acc@10), suggesting its class-token-based architecture cannot capture speech-specific local correspondences. The two earlier Harwath et al. baselines [21, 22] report only PlacesAudio retrieval (54.2–56.4% Acc@10) and are substantially outperformed. SimPool [44], the best single-head pooling strategy, achieves 35.3% speech mAP in the ablation (Table 4) versus DenseAV's multi-head 48.2%, confirming that the multi-head aggregation is critical for localization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DenseAV extends the line of audio-visual contrastive learning that began with Arandjelovic & Zisserman's MIL loss (ECCV 2018) for sound localization and Harwath et al.'s MISA loss (ECCV 2018) for word grounding, generalizing both into a unified multi-head aggregation operator that directly supervises dense local tokens rather than global pooled representations. It departs from the dominant paradigm of ImageBind, CAVMAE, and similar systems that form CLS-token or average-pooled features before the contrastive loss, arguing that this global aggregation is precisely what prevents local features from achieving cross-modal alignment. The architecture composes pretrained unsupervised backbones (DINO for vision, HuBERT for audio) with lightweight aligner layers and a novel multi-head similarity volume, positioning itself as a local-feature-first alternative to the global-representation contrastive approaches that currently dominate the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spontaneous disentanglement of language semantics from object sounds by two contrastive heads—achieved with no explicit supervision for this separation—suggests a general principle that multi-head contrastive learning can discover semantically distinct association types, with implications beyond AV grounding toward unsupervised concept discovery. The new prompted-segmentation datasets (speech and sound) provide a more rigorous and reproducible evaluation protocol than prior pointing-game or concept-counting metrics, and the dramatic gap between DenseAV and ImageBind on localization (while ImageBind excels at retrieval) highlights a fundamental limitation of global-representation contrastive learning that the field has underappreciated. Open questions include whether the disentanglement generalizes beyond two heads and two datasets, whether the approach scales to more complex scenes with overlapping sounds, and whether the DINO+HuBERT backbone combination is essential or whether other pretrained encoders would yield similar local alignment.
