## Problem

Transformer-based segmentation architectures like MaskFormer and Mask2Former achieve state-of-the-art results across multiple segmentation tasks but rely on computationally expensive attention operations that make them impractical for edge deployment and large-scale cloud inference. Existing efficient segmentation networks are restricted to a single task (semantic or panoptic), forcing redundant research and preventing a unified, deployable solution.

## Input / Output

PEM takes a single RGB image (2048×1024 on Cityscapes, variable resolution on ADE20K) and produces 100 binary masks of full image resolution, each paired with a class probability vector over K+1 classes (including a 'no object' class). Masks are generated by element-wise multiplication of refined query vectors with the highest-resolution pixel decoder features, while class scores come from a linear classifier on the queries. The same architecture and loss function handle both semantic and panoptic segmentation without architectural changes.

## Method

PEM replaces the standard masked cross-attention in Mask2Former's transformer decoder with a Prototype-based Masked Cross-Attention (PEM-CA) that selects a single prototype token per query via argmax on a masked similarity matrix, reducing the attention input from HW pixels to N tokens and replacing the full QK^T computation with a cheap element-wise product followed by a learned projection. The pixel decoder is redesigned as a fully convolutional FPN that recovers the benefits of deformable attention through two mechanisms: context-based self-modulation (CSM) modules that reweight channels using a globally pooled scene representation, and deformable convolutions that adaptively shift kernel sampling locations. The backbone (ResNet-50 or STDC1/STDC2) feeds four multi-scale feature maps into this FPN, whose three lower-resolution outputs serve as visual features for PEM-CA while the highest-resolution output is used for final mask prediction. Training uses AdamW with cosine schedule, BCE + Dice loss with deep supervision at each decoder stage, and 100 learnable object queries.

## Results

On panoptic segmentation, PEM achieves 61.1 PQ at 13.8 FPS on Cityscapes (the fastest multi-task architecture, trailing only Mask2Former's 62.1 PQ by 1.0) and 38.5 PQ at 35.7 FPS on ADE20K. On semantic segmentation, PEM reaches 79.9 mIoU at 13.8 FPS on Cityscapes and 45.5 mIoU at 35.7 FPS on ADE20K, outperforming all task-specific architectures on ADE20K by over 5 mIoU. PEM uses 35.6M parameters and 237G FLOPs (Cityscapes) / 47G FLOPs (ADE20K), roughly half the FLOPs of Mask2Former while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest multi-task baseline Mask2Former, PEM trades 1.0 PQ on Cityscapes panoptic (61.1 vs 62.1) for a 2× speedup (13.8 vs 4.1 FPS) and 54% fewer FLOPs; on ADE20K panoptic the gap widens to 1.2 PQ (38.5 vs 39.7) with PEM still ~1.8× faster. Against YOSO, the closest efficient competitor, PEM wins on every metric: +1.4 PQ and +2.7 FPS on Cityscapes panoptic, +0.5 PQ on ADE20K panoptic, +0.5 mIoU on Cityscapes semantic, and +0.8 mIoU on ADE20K semantic. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, PEM (45.5 mIoU) surpasses all task-specific architectures by a large margin, beating PIDNet-L (40.5 mIoU) by 5.0 mIoU while running at comparable speed. On Cityscapes semantic, PEM (79.9 mIoU) narrowly trails the task-specific PIDNet-L (80.9 mIoU) but exceeds it in FLOPs efficiency (240G vs 276G) and matches its speed. PEM consistently outperforms MaskFormer by 1.4–2.4 mIoU on semantic tasks and 3.4–4.6 PQ on panoptic tasks while being 2–4× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEM directly extends the MaskFormer/Mask2Former family of end-to-end set-prediction segmentation architectures, retaining their encoder-decoder structure and learnable-query formulation while replacing two computationally heavy components. The transformer decoder's masked cross-attention is swapped for a prototype-based mechanism (PEM-CA) that selects a single visual token per query via argmax similarity, drawing conceptual inspiration from SwiftFormer's element-wise attention but applying it to cross-attention rather than self-attention. The pixel decoder replaces Mask2Former's deformable-attention FPN with a fully convolutional FPN augmented by deformable convolutions and context-based self-modulation (CSM), effectively recovering the three properties of deformable attention (global context, dynamic weights, input-dependent receptive field) in a lighter convolutional form. This positions PEM as a bridge between the high-accuracy multi-task transformer architectures and the fast but task-specific efficient segmentation networks (PIDNet, STDC, DDRNet).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEM addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: transformer-based segmentation models like Mask2Former are too slow (4–7 FPS on Cityscapes) for edge or real-time applications, while efficient alternatives are typically single-task. By achieving near-SOTA accuracy at roughly 2× the speed of Mask2Former in a unified multi-task framework, PEM makes high-quality segmentation more accessible on resource-constrained hardware. Open questions include whether the prototype mechanism generalizes to higher-resolution inputs or additional tasks (e.g., instance segmentation), whether the multi-head prototype selection can be further simplified, and how the approach scales with larger backbones or very long sequences. The paper does not explore training from scratch or the sensitivity of the prototype count to the number of queries.
