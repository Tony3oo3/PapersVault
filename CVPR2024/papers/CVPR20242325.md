## Problem

Existing lane detection methods rely on a divide-and-conquer strategy where each output format (segmentation, anchor, parameter) requires a bespoke head network and one or more task-specific loss functions, resulting in complex architectures with many hyperparameters. No prior work has unified these heterogeneous detection formats into a single model, and it remains unexplored whether a plain sequence generation paradigm can replace all task-specific components while maintaining competitive performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242325/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 320×800) and a format prompt token (e.g., <Segmentation>, <Anchor>, <Parameter>) as input, and autoregressively generates a sequence of discrete tokens from a shared vocabulary of 1000 values. Depending on the prompt, the generated sequence encodes either 28 polygon vertex coordinates per lane (segmentation), 14 keypoint coordinates per lane (anchor), or 5 polynomial coefficients plus a vertical offset per lane (parameter), all normalized and quantized to [1, 1000] before de-quantization at inference.

## Method

Lane2Seq uses a ViT-Base (MAE-pretrained) image encoder and a 2-block transformer decoder (hidden 256, 8 heads) to generate lane descriptions token-by-token, trained with a single cross-entropy loss and no task-specific heads. To recover the task-specific knowledge lost by removing specialized modules, the authors propose Multi-Format Reinforcement Learning (MFRL): after supervised pretraining, the model is tuned with REINFORCE using format-specific reward functions built from evaluation metrics (matched Line-IOU, mIOU, Euclidean distance) minus false-positive penalties. The three rewards are combined with learned scale factors into a joint objective, allowing the model to optimize directly for detection quality rather than a proxy loss. This two-stage design—supervised pretraining for initialization followed by RL fine-tuning—avoids the convergence difficulties of training from scratch with reinforcement learning alone.

## Results

Lane2Seq achieves new state-of-the-art F1 scores of 97.95% on TuSimple and 97.42% on LLAMAS using the segmentation format with a ViT-Base encoder. On CULane, the segmentation format reaches 79.64% total F1, the anchor format 79.27%, and the parameter format 78.39%, all with a single unified transformer encoder-decoder and cross-entropy loss. The multi-format reinforcement learning tuning (MFRL) contributes substantial gains: +15.37% F1 for segmentation, +13.04% for anchor, and +10.25% for parameter formats on CULane.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TuSimple, Lane2Seq (segmentation) sets a new SOTA F1 of 97.95%, surpassing CLRNet (ResNet34) at 97.82% and GANet at 97.68%. On LLAMAS, Lane2Seq (segmentation) achieves 97.42% F1, outperforming the previous best CLRNet (DLA34) at 97.16% and LaneAF at 96.90%. On CULane, the segmentation format (79.64%) beats AtrousFormer (78.08%) and RESA (75.30%), while the anchor format (79.27%) outperforms CondLaneNet (78.74%) and ADNet (78.94%) but trails CLRNet (79.73%) by 0.46%, which the authors attribute to CLRNet's multi-scale detection. The parameter format (78.39%) significantly surpasses LSTR (64.00%) and Laneformer (77.06%) but falls short of BSNet (79.89%). Against the multi-task baseline YOLOP, Lane2Seq wins across all three formats (e.g., +4.41% F1 for segmentation, +8.96% for parameter) despite lacking any task-specific heads or losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lane2Seq extends the seq2seq paradigm for vision tasks pioneered by Pix2Seq (object detection) and SeqTrack (object tracking) to lane detection, replacing task-specific head networks and loss functions with a unified token-by-token generation framework. It departs from the dominant divide-and-conquer strategy in lane detection (segmentation heads, anchor refinement, polynomial regression) by encoding all three output formats as discrete token sequences conditioned on a format prompt. The MFRL tuning stage adapts the Task-Reward framework (Pinto et al., 2023) from object detection and segmentation to lane detection, introducing format-specific reward functions built from Line-IOU, mIOU, and Euclidean distance that were not available in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a plain ViT encoder-decoder with cross-entropy loss, augmented by RL-based tuning, can match or exceed methods with elaborate task-specific architectures across three lane detection paradigms, suggesting that architectural complexity may be less critical than previously assumed for this task. However, the token-by-token inference is slow (19–30 FPS) and degrades further with more than five lanes, limiting real-time deployment. MFRL is currently restricted to sequence-generation models, and whether the approach generalizes to other vision tasks or scales to more complex driving perception remains open. The modest SOTA margins (0.13–0.26% F1) indicate the method is competitive but not decisively superior to well-tuned specialized architectures.
