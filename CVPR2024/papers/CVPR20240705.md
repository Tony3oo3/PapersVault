## Problem

Existing noisy label detection methods rely on scalar training signals (e.g., loss or confidence) at a single epoch, which incompletely captures the temporal patterns that distinguish clean from noisy instances and fails to generalize across diverse noise types (symmetric, asymmetric, instance-dependent, human). The absence of ground-truth clean/noisy annotations for both training and validation further limits data-driven approaches, forcing reliance on hand-crafted heuristics that require per-dataset tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisily labeled RGB image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Clothing1M at 224×224) with an unknown noise rate and type, and produces a binary clean/noisy assignment for every training instance. Unusually, the input is consumed in two parallel streams: the original dataset and a corrupted subset (10% of instances with randomly flipped labels and weak augmentations), both fed through the same classifier to generate paired training dynamics trajectories. The output is a per-instance noisy-label indicator (v=1 or 0) determined by cluster assignment probability, evaluated via F1 score treating mislabeled instances as positives.

## Method

DynaCor introduces a label corruption strategy that augments the dataset with intentionally mislabeled instances (random label flipping + weak augmentation), providing a guaranteed-noisy reference to simulate the model's behavior on noisy labels without ground-truth annotations. A classifier is trained jointly on both original and corrupted data, and for each instance a training dynamics vector is collected as the trajectory of quantized logit differences (sign of the difference between the given-label logit and the max competing logit) across all epochs. A 1D-CNN dynamics encoder maps these trajectories to latent representations, which are then clustered into two groups (clean and noisy) using a self-enhancing target distribution with a Student-t kernel for cluster cohesion, plus an alignment loss that forces the mean representations of each cluster to match between the original and corrupted datasets. A novel validation metric—measuring the squared gap in noisy-cluster assignment probability between corrupted-noisy and original-clean instances—determines the optimal stopping epoch without requiring any clean validation data.

## Results

DynaCor achieves an average F1 score of 87.7% across nine noise settings on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 using CLIP w/ MLP (Table 1), outperforming the best baseline SIMIFEAT-R (84.7%) by +3.0%. With a randomly initialized ResNet34 backbone (Table 2), DynaCor still leads with 85.2% average F1, a +2.8% margin over the next best method (Avg.Encoder at 82.4%). The method shows particular strength on asymmetric and instance-dependent noise, where heuristic baselines like CORES collapse to 5.0% and 19.2% F1 on CIFAR-10, while DynaCor maintains 94.0% and 92.3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaCor outperforms all five baselines on average F1 across both backbones. Against the training-signal-based methods, the gap is most dramatic for CORES (50.9% avg), which catastrophically fails on asymmetric noise (5.0% on CIFAR-10) and instance-dependent noise (19.2%), while DynaCor scores 94.0% and 92.3% on those settings. AUM (83.7%) and CL (81.5%) are more stable but still trail DynaCor by 4.0% and 6.2% respectively, with CL particularly weak on CIFAR-100 symmetric noise (88.0% vs. DynaCor's 94.3%). The training-free SIMIFEAT variants (84.6–84.7%) are the closest competitors, but DynaCor still leads by ~3% and shows notably lower variance (e.g., ±0.38 vs. ±1.41 for SIMIFEAT-R on CIFAR-10 symmetric noise). With the weaker ResNet34 backbone, DynaCor's advantage widens further: it leads by +2.8% over Avg.Encoder and by +30.5% over AUM, confirming that the dynamics-clustering approach is robust to backbone quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaCor extends the training-dynamics-based line of noisy label detection (AUM, CORES, Avg.Encoder) by replacing single-epoch scalar heuristics with full temporal trajectories encoded by a 1D-CNN, and by learning cluster structure via a self-enhancing target distribution adapted from Deep Clustering Network [55]. Its key departure is the label corruption strategy: rather than relying on hand-crafted thresholds or GMMs to separate clean from noisy signals, it synthesizes a guaranteed-noisy subset through random label flipping, providing implicit supervision for the clustering objective. The alignment loss between original and corrupted representations is a novel mechanism that leverages the known asymmetry in detectability between symmetric (corrupted) and arbitrary (original) noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free-of-clean-data pipeline for noisy label detection that generalizes across symmetric, asymmetric, instance-dependent, and real-world human noise without per-dataset hyperparameter tuning—a persistent pain point in the literature. Its compatibility with DivideMix, GCE, SCE, and ELR suggests it can be dropped into existing robust-learning pipelines as a plug-in detector. Open questions include whether the label corruption assumption (symmetric noise as a proxy for arbitrary noise) holds in highly structured or domain-specific noise regimes, and whether the approach scales to very large datasets where training a full classifier for dynamics generation becomes costly.
