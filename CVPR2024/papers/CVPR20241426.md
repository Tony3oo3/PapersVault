## Problem

Normal integration from a single-view normal map must handle surface discontinuities caused by self-occlusion, but prior discontinuity-preserving methods (e.g., BiNI) model jump magnitudes only implicitly through bilateral weighting or graph cuts. This implicit treatment limits sparsity regularization, causing small and subtle discontinuities to be missed, resulting in inaccurate depth reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view normal map (captured under orthographic or perspective camera) and produces a depth map by averaging four vertex depths per quadrilateral in the integration graph. Optionally, a 3D mesh can be built directly from the quadrilateral graph without pixel-grid averaging, yielding near-identical shading to the ground-truth mesh. The integration domain is a discrete graph with one quadrilateral per pixel plus auxiliary edges bridging neighboring quadrilaterals, rather than the standard image grid.

## Method

The core idea is to introduce auxiliary edges in a discrete graph that explicitly represent the hidden gradient jumps across surface discontinuities, separating them from the data-driven gradients on quadrilateral edges. An iterative optimization alternates three steps: (1) solving a weighted least-squares depth equation via conjugate gradients, (2) reweighting auxiliary-edge residuals with IRLS to enforce l0 sparsity, and (3) filtering the depth gradients on auxiliary edges using a local-maximumness threshold scaled by tangentness change to build sparse jump magnitudes. A two-pass update alternating between a soft weight (λ_soft) and a hard weight (λ_hard), inspired by cosine annealing, heals erroneous jumps while reinforcing valid discontinuities across iterations. This explicit representation of jump magnitudes enables stronger sparsity regularization than implicit weighting schemes, allowing accurate detection of small discontinuities.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT dataset (9 objects), the proposed method achieves a mean absolute depth error of approximately 1.53, compared to 1.62 for BiNI and 4.22 for the Poisson solver. The method outperforms BiNI on 7 out of 9 objects, with the largest gains on Reading (0.15 vs. 0.32), Harvest (2.44 vs. 3.30), and Pot2 (0.19 vs. 0.24). Qualitative evaluation on a 27-model human body dataset (RenderPeople) further demonstrates that the method recovers small discontinuities missed by BiNI while matching its performance on large, obvious occlusion boundaries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Poisson solver (Horn & Brooks), the proposed method reduces mean absolute depth error by roughly 60–70% across all DiLiGenT objects, confirming that explicit discontinuity handling is essential for real-world reconstructions. Against the state-of-the-art BiNI (Cao et al.), the proposed method wins on 7 of 9 objects, with particularly large improvements on Reading (0.15 vs. 0.32, a 53% reduction) and Harvest (2.44 vs. 3.30, a 26% reduction), where small or subtle discontinuities are present. BiNI retains an edge on Cat (0.11 vs. 0.24) and Goblet (8.65 vs. 9.02), though the Goblet gap is small relative to the overall error magnitude. The paper attributes its advantage to the explicit auxiliary-edge representation combined with IRLS and gradient filtering, which enables stronger sparsity regularization than BiNI's smooth Heaviside-based bilateral weights, allowing detection of small jumps that BiNI's implicit weighting scheme misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discontinuity-preserving normal integration line of work, most directly building on BiNI (Cao et al., ECCV 2022), which handles surface jumps by iteratively weakening bilateral weights across occlusion boundaries. The key conceptual departure is shifting from implicit jump modeling (weakening or cutting edges in the original image grid) to explicit jump modeling via auxiliary edges that bridge neighboring quadrilaterals, allowing the magnitude of hidden gradients to be directly optimized. The optimization machinery draws on IRLS for l0-regularized sparsity and a two-pass cosine-annealing-inspired schedule to balance healing erroneous jumps against reinforcing valid ones, while the discrete quadrilateral-per-pixel graph structure echoes the DGP framework of Xie et al. (CVPR 2014) but adds the auxiliary-edge layer for discontinuity handling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit auxiliary-edge representation enables stronger sparsity regularization than the smooth Heaviside-based bilateral weights of BiNI, which is the mechanism that allows detection of small, subtle discontinuities that prior methods miss. However, the method still fails on noisy scans (e.g., fingers) and on surface islands where global translation/scale ambiguity of normal integration causes large depth errors—both acknowledged limitations. The authors suggest incorporating depth bias from data-driven models as a promising direction to address these failures, and note that robustness to noise and faster runtime remain open. The 120-second runtime for a 512×512 map (unoptimized PyTorch prototype) also limits immediate practical adoption in real-time or large-scale pipelines.
