## Problem

Existing speech-driven 3D body animation methods generate gestures directly from audio without explicitly modeling or controlling the emotional content of the output. The core difficulty is that the mapping from speech to body motion is a non-deterministic many-to-many relationship where content (rhythm, word utterances), emotion, and personal style are entangled, making it hard to independently control which emotion a generated gesture conveys.

## Input / Output

The method takes 10-second speech audio windows converted to 128 mel-frequency filterbank images (25 ms Hamming window, 10 ms shift) and produces 30 FPS SMPL-X upper-body pose sequences (47 joints in 6D rotation, no facial expressions or lower-body joints). For gesture editing, a second audio stream supplies the emotion or style latent while the first provides content and style, enabling cross-speaker emotion or style transfer. The audio disentanglement module additionally outputs three 256-dimensional latent vectors (content, emotion, style) that can be recombined at inference.

## Method

AMUSE decomposes the pipeline into three components: (1) an audio autoencoder with three separate DeiT-based encoders producing disentangled content, emotion, and style latents, trained with cross-reconstruction and classification losses to enforce disentanglement; (2) a temporal VAE motion prior (U-Net-like transformer with 9 layers, 4 heads) that learns a smooth 3D body motion distribution; and (3) a latent diffusion denoiser (same U-Net architecture as the prior encoder, hidden dim 1024) that generates motion in the VAE latent space conditioned on the concatenated content, emotion, and style vectors plus sinusoidal timestep encoding. The prior and denoiser are jointly trained via a 3-step forward pass with stop-gradient operations, using reconstruction, KL, alignment, and diffusion noise-prediction losses. At inference, DDIM with 50 steps produces diverse gesture samples, and swapping emotion or style latents from a second audio enables semantic gesture editing without retraining.

## Results

On the BEAT test set (72 sequences, 8 emotions), AMUSE achieves the best scores across all five metrics: SRGR 0.36, Beat Align 0.81, FGD 388.63, Diversity 25.06, and Gesture Emotion Accuracy 46.76%. The FGD improvement is particularly large — AMUSE scores 388.63 versus 762–1097 for all baselines — indicating substantially closer distributional match to ground-truth gestures. The emotion editing variant (Ours-EmoEdit) retains strong performance with BA 0.79, Div 24.68, and GA 34.18%, still outperforming all baselines on those three metrics. Ground-truth motions remain ahead (BA 0.83, Div 27.83, GA 64.04%), highlighting the residual gap between generated and real gestures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMUSE outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest baseline TalkSHOW-BEAT (an adapted version of TalkSHOW retrained on BEAT with emotion labels), AMUSE improves SRGR from 0.31 to 0.36, BA from 0.64 to 0.81, FGD from 808.99 to 388.63, and GA from 22.71% to 46.76%. The gap over the original TalkSHOW [101] is similar (FGD 762→389, GA 29.4→46.8). The diffusion-based DSG [97] and flow-based MoGlow [34] lag further behind, with FGD scores of 763 and 1097 respectively and GA below 23%. CaMN [55] and Habibie et al. [31] are the weakest baselines, with GA of only 14.2% and 16.7%. Notably, even the emotion-editing variant (Ours-EmoEdit) maintains BA 0.79 and GA 34.18%, exceeding all baselines on those metrics, demonstrating that the disentangled latents preserve discriminative emotional cues during cross-audio recombination.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMUSE builds on the latent diffusion framework for motion generation (Rombach et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2023) and the transformer VAE motion prior (Petrovich et al. 2021), adapting them to the speech-driven gesture setting. The audio encoder design follows the DeiT-based spectrogram transformer of Gong et al. (2021/2022), repurposed here for a three-branch disentanglement into content, emotion, and style latents. The key departure from prior speech-driven gesture methods (TalkSHOW, MoGlow, DSG, CaMN) is the explicit disentanglement of emotion and personal style from the driving audio, enabling controllable emotion and style editing at inference time—something none of the compared baselines provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to offer explicit, speech-only emotion control over 3D body gestures without requiring additional conditions (text labels, reference motions, or emotion classifiers at test time), opening a path toward emotionally expressive virtual agents and avatars. However, the gap to ground truth remains substantial across all metrics, and the model is limited to upper-body motion without facial expressions, lower-body locomotion, or semantic gesture types (deictic, metaphoric). The separate (non-end-to-end) training of the audio disentanglement module and the gesture generator, while practical, may limit the quality of audio-motion alignment. Future work on jointly learning face and body, incorporating language semantics, and extending to full-body motion would be needed for production-quality applications.
