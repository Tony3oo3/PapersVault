## Problem

LLMs can generate personalized text instructions but cannot produce the accompanying visuals that are critical for users to follow multi-step tasks. Naive pipelines that independently generate each step image with a frozen T2I model fail along three axes: images reflect the goal rather than the specific step, and successive images lack visual consistency (e.g., objects change color or count). The paper formalizes this as the new task of Illustrated Instructions and identifies goal faithfulness, step faithfulness, and cross-image consistency as the three key desiderata.

## Input / Output

The system takes an arbitrary text query (e.g., a cooking goal, a dietary restriction, or a user error description) as input. A pretrained LLM (GPT-4) first converts this into a structured goal plus up to N=6 step texts; StackedDiffusion then generates a spatially tiled latent of shape (N×32)×32 at 256px resolution, which is decoded into N individual step illustrations. The output is a fully rendered instructional article interleaving step text with corresponding images, where the text encoder (Flan-T5-XXL) is frozen and only the denoising U-Net is finetuned.

## Method

StackedDiffusion's core insight is to exploit the spatial consistency priors already learned by a pretrained T2I diffusion model by tiling all N step-image latents into a single spatial grid, so the U-Net denoises them jointly and naturally enforces cross-image consistency without any new parameters. For text conditioning, goal and step texts are separately encoded by the frozen Flan-T5-XXL encoder and concatenated with learned step-positional encodings (Φ_i) that demarcate which portion of the condition corresponds to which step, preventing the goal text from overshadowing individual steps. The U-Net is initialized from a pretrained LDM and finetuned for 14,000 steps on stacked WikiHow/VGSI instructional images, with a 0SNR noise schedule adjustment to handle the higher spatial resolution of the tiled latent. At inference, classifier-free guidance (dropout rate 0.05) is applied, and the denoised tiled latent is split and decoded into N images. The entire approach introduces no new learnable architectural components beyond the positional encodings, making it a lightweight adaptation of an existing T2I model.

## Results

StackedDiffusion achieves 61.5% step faithfulness (SF) and 74.3% goal faithfulness (GF), substantially outperforming the frozen T2I baseline (43.2% SF) and finetuned T2I baseline (52.4% SF). Its cross-image consistency (CIC) of 50.7 is nearly identical to ground truth (50.6), while its FID of 39.5 is the lowest among all generative methods (vs. 69.3 for frozen T2I and 53.5 for finetuned T2I). In human evaluation, StackedDiffusion is preferred over the frozen T2I baseline 78% of the time and over the finetuned T2I baseline 66.6% of the time, and is even preferred over ground truth articles 30% of the time in system-level comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GILL++ (the best accessible open-source multimodal generation model), StackedDiffusion achieves a 96.1% human preference win rate (GILL++ wins only 3.9%), as GILL struggles to produce sufficiently long articles, often omits illustrations entirely, and generates low-quality text and images. The frozen T2I baseline achieves high goal faithfulness (92.9%) but fails on step faithfulness (43.2%) because it generates images of the final goal rather than intermediate steps; the finetuned T2I baseline improves SF to 52.4% but still lags StackedDiffusion by 9.1 points and shows no cross-image consistency since images are generated independently. In system-level comparisons (Table 2), StackedDiffusion also outperforms retrieval-based approaches (LLM+CLIP at 15.6% win rate, Goal Retrieval at 33.1%) and both T2I baselines (33.8% and 34.0% win rates). Notably, even ground truth articles are preferred over StackedDiffusion only 70% of the time in the full system-level evaluation, meaning the generated articles are competitive with human-authored content.

## Relation to Prior Work

StackedDiffusion builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) [49] as its generative backbone, but departs from prior instructional-data work (WikiHow summarization, VGSI goal-step inference) that focused on understanding rather than generation. It contrasts with autoregressive multimodal models like GILL and CM3 that jointly predict text and image tokens, instead decoupling text generation (via a frozen LLM) from image generation (via a finetuned diffusion U-Net). The key architectural departure is spatial tiling: rather than introducing new cross-image attention or additional parameters, the method reshapes multiple step-image latents into a single spatially-extended latent, exploiting the T2I model's pre-existing within-image consistency prior to achieve cross-image coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes Illustrated Instructions as a task with three explicit desiderata (goal faithfulness, step faithfulness, cross-image consistency) and corresponding metrics, providing a structured evaluation framework that prior instructional-generation work lacked. The parameter-free spatial tiling trick is a clean, transferable idea for any multi-image generation setting where consistency matters. However, evaluation is confined to the narrow 'Recipes' subset of VGSI, the model is capped at 6 steps, and a visible gap with ground-truth articles remains; open questions include scaling to longer procedures, broader task categories, and extending the approach to video-based step illustrations.
