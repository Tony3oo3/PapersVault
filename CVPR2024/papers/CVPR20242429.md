## Problem

Vector-Quantized Image Modeling (VQIM) suffers from codebook collapse, where the majority of code vectors are never updated during training because existing methods learn codebooks from scratch in a code-independent manner. The paper argues that ignoring relationships between codes and good codebook priors is a key cause of this collapse, and that pretrained language models already contain superior codebooks with rich semantic relationships between tokens that have been largely unexploited for VQIM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242429/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images as input. The encoder produces continuous spatial vectors that are split along channels into an adjective vector and a noun vector, each independently quantized against its respective codebook (adjective codebook and noun codebook). The quantized vectors are concatenated and fed to the decoder to produce a reconstructed image, while the discrete token sequence (adjective codes + noun codes) serves as the image representation. The codebook itself is not a fixed parameter but is generated per forward pass by the transfer network.

## Method

VQCT transfers pretrained codebooks from language models (e.g., CLIP, GloVe) to VQIM by first filtering word embeddings to retain only vision-relevant tokens (adjectives and nouns) using part-of-speech knowledge from WordNet. A modifying graph is then constructed from text corpora, where edges encode adjective-noun modification relationships (e.g., 'sharp' modifies 'beak'). A three-layer Graph Convolutional Codebook Transfer Network (GCCTN) takes this graph as input and generates the VQIM codebook in a transfer mapping manner, making the codebook generative rather than directly learnable. This design enables cooperative optimization: even unselected code vectors are updated through their semantic relationships to active codes, alleviating codebook collapse. The framework is modular and can be plugged into existing VQIM methods (VQ-VAE, VQ-GAN) by replacing the directly-learned codebook with the transfer-generated one.

## Results

On four datasets (ADE20K, CelebA-HQ, CUB-200, MS-COCO), VQCT achieves the best FID and PSNR across nearly all settings. The most striking improvement is on CUB-200, where VQCT reaches FID 2.13 and PSNR 25.35, substantially outperforming the next-best Gumbel-VQ (FID 3.45, PSNR 22.11) and VQ-GAN (FID 3.63, PSNR 22.19). On MS-COCO, VQCT attains FID 9.82 and PSNR 21.46, edging out CVQ (FID 9.94, PSNR 20.48). On CelebA-HQ, VQCT achieves FID 5.02 and PSNR 25.18, beating CVQ (FID 5.19) and VQ-GAN (FID 5.66) while also matching VQ-VAE's pixel-level fidelity (l1 0.0699 vs 0.0719).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQCT outperforms all four baselines on FID and PSNR across all four datasets. Against VQ-GAN (the strongest baseline on most datasets), VQCT improves FID by 1.79 on ADE20K (20.25 vs 22.04), 0.64 on CelebA-HQ (5.02 vs 5.66), 1.50 on CUB-200 (2.13 vs 3.63), and 4.63 on MS-COCO (9.82 vs 14.45). Against CVQ, which also targets codebook collapse, VQCT achieves lower FID on every dataset (e.g., 2.13 vs 3.61 on CUB-200) and higher PSNR (e.g., 25.35 vs 22.29). Against Gumbel-VQ, the gap is largest on CUB-200 (FID 2.13 vs 3.45) and MS-COCO (FID 9.82 vs 15.30). VQ-VAE, while competitive on pixel-level metrics (l1/l2) for CelebA-HQ, is far behind on FID (36.08 vs 5.02), confirming that adversarial training plus codebook transfer is essential for perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQCT builds directly on the VQ-VAE encoder-decoder-quantization architecture and its adversarial extension VQ-GAN, but departs from the standard practice of learning codebooks from scratch in a code-independent manner. Rather than addressing codebook collapse through quantization tricks (SQ-VAE), online codebook restart (CVQ), or regularization (Reg-VQ), it introduces a codebook transfer perspective: pretrained language model embeddings (CLIP, GloVe) are filtered by part-of-speech into adjective and noun sub-codebooks, then mapped to the VQIM space via a three-layer graph convolutional network that models the modifying relationship between adjectives and nouns. This positions the work as a transfer-learning approach to codebook initialization, distinct from the concurrent SPAE which directly freezes PLM embeddings as the VQIM codebook without a learned transfer mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The codebook collapse problem is a persistent bottleneck in VQIM that limits the effective vocabulary size and thus reconstruction fidelity; VQCT's generative codebook design (where the optimization variable is the transfer network parameters rather than the code vectors themselves) offers a principled mechanism for cooperative code optimization that could complement existing collapse-mitigation strategies. However, the approach is currently limited to two part-of-speech categories at 256×256 resolution, and the codebook utilization improvement (27.9% → 42.2%) still leaves over half the codes unused, raising open questions about scalability to larger codebooks, additional linguistic categories, and higher resolutions. The vision-language alignment observed in the qualitative analysis is intriguing but anecdotal, and it remains unclear whether the semantic structure from text embeddings transfers meaningfully to abstract or compositional visual concepts beyond the bird and face datasets tested.
