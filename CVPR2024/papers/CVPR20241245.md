## Problem

Neural implicit head avatars achieve photorealistic rendering but rely on large MLPs that make real-time inference infeasible. Existing fast alternatives either use global blendshapes that cannot capture vertex-level local deformations (NeRFBlendshape) or condition a canonical-space NeRF on expression codes with a lightweight MLP that lacks sufficient capacity for extreme expressions (INSTA). The paper addresses how to simultaneously achieve real-time rendering speed, fine-grained expression controllability, and high-fidelity quality in a single 3D implicit avatar.

## Input / Output

Training requires only a monocular RGB video per subject (10 videos at VGA resolution, background-removed). At inference, the method takes FLAME 3DMM pose codes (neck, jaw, eyes) and expression codes along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and produces a rendered RGB image at 512×512 via volumetric ray marching. The 3DMM deformation is first converted to a UV-space displacement map that drives a U-Net, so the expression signal enters the network through a 2D convolutional pathway rather than as a direct MLP input.

## Method

The core representation attaches M=5 small multi-resolution hash tables to each of the 1772 FLAME mesh vertices, forming per-vertex local blendshapes. A U-Net operating on the UV displacement map predicts per-vertex blending weights and a per-vertex feature vector; the weights linearly merge the attached hash tables into expression-dependent embeddings. During volumetric rendering, each 3D query point retrieves embeddings from its K=3 nearest mesh vertices via hash encoding, concatenates them with the nearest vertex feature and positional encodings, and passes them through a 2-hidden-layer MLP to predict density and color. A hierarchical k-NN search (grouping query points into a 64-resolution 3D grid, first finding K'=12 neighbors of the voxel center, then refining to K=3 per point) accelerates the neighbor lookup by ~36% without visible quality loss. A per-frame warping field corrects tracking noise during training but is disabled at test time, keeping inference lightweight.

## Results

On a 10-subject monocular video benchmark, the proposed method achieves the best LPIPS (0.100) and ties NeRFBlendshape for the highest PSNR (22.77 dB), while rendering at 35.9 mean FPS at 512×512 resolution — the only method besides INSTA to reach real-time. It requires the fewest FLOPs (113 GFLOPs) among all compared approaches, roughly 2× less than INSTA (266) and 21× less than MonoAvatar (2385). SSIM is competitive at 0.795, slightly below MonoAvatar's 0.798 but well above INSTA (0.758) and PointAvatar (0.728).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against INSTA [42], the only other real-time method (46.2 FPS), the proposed approach wins decisively on all quality metrics: LPIPS 0.100 vs 0.149, SSIM 0.795 vs 0.758, and PSNR 22.77 vs 22.12, while also using fewer FLOPs (113 vs 266). Against NeRFBlendshape [14], the method matches PSNR (22.77) but improves LPIPS (0.100 vs 0.110) and SSIM (0.795 vs 0.793) while rendering 3.2× faster (35.9 vs 11.2 FPS). Against MonoAvatar [5], the quality is nearly identical (PSNR 22.77 vs 22.74, SSIM 0.795 vs 0.798, LPIPS 0.100 vs 0.114) but rendering speed improves by ~72× (35.9 vs 0.5 FPS). Against PointAvatar [41], the method dominates on every metric: better LPIPS (0.100 vs 0.117), SSIM (0.795 vs 0.728), PSNR (22.77 vs 21.12), and 7× faster (35.9 vs 5.0 FPS). The paper also notes that NeRFBlendshape produces severe artifacts on dynamic regions (mouth, eyebrows) and more floaters, while INSTA shows obvious artifacts on challenging expressions, further favoring the proposed method qualitatively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate lines of work: the 3DMM-anchored NeRF representation from MonoAvatar [5] (which achieves high quality but requires large MLPs and runs at ~0.5 FPS) and the multi-resolution hash encoding from Instant NGPs [27] (which enables fast inference but was designed for static scenes). The key architectural departure is attaching multiple small hash tables to each 3DMM vertex as per-vertex 'local blendshapes,' which contrasts with NeRFBlendshape [14]'s global blendshape-level hash tables and INSTA [42]'s canonical-space approach with expression codes as MLP inputs. The per-frame error-correction warping field is inherited from Nerfies [29], and the UV-space CNN for predicting blendshape weights follows the MonoAvatar pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving real-time rendering (35.9 FPS at 512×512) at quality comparable to MonoAvatar (0.5 FPS) removes a major practical barrier for deploying neural head avatars in VR, teleconferencing, and real-time applications. The hierarchical k-NN search is a practical acceleration technique that could generalize to other mesh-anchored neural field methods. However, the paper acknowledges floaters under distribution shift and instability in mouth-interior regions due to poor 3DMM tracking, and notes that the speed headroom could enable more expensive training strategies (adversarial losses, joint face fitting refinement) that remain unexplored. Whether per-vertex hash tables scale to full-body avatars or longer sequences with diverse expressions remains an open question.
