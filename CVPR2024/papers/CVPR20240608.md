## Problem

In uncalibrated multi-view photometric stereo, unknown light directions introduce a per-view linear ambiguity in surface normal estimation, making it difficult to fully exploit photometric stereo's detail-recovery capability for 3D shape reconstruction. This paper shows that constraining light sources to move together with the camera reduces these per-view ambiguities to a single global linear ambiguity, enabling more effective disambiguation and shape recovery under sparse viewpoints and few light sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240608/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images captured under a small number of unknown but camera-consistent light directions (e.g., 3 lights × 4 views), along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics from SfM or scene markers and binary foreground masks. It outputs a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from an optimized neural SDF, disambiguated per-view surface normal maps, and the learned 3×3 global ambiguity matrix. Notably, the light directions need not be calibrated; only their consistency relative to the camera across views is required, achievable with a simple rig or rotation stage.

## Method

The core factorization step uses Higher-Order Generalized SVD (HO-GSVD) to jointly decompose all multi-view observations into per-view surface normal maps and a shared set of light directions, reducing the per-view linear ambiguities of standard UPS to a single global 3×3 matrix X. This ambiguous normal supervision is then integrated into a NeuS-style neural SDF optimization: a normal consistency loss between volume-rendered SDF gradients and the disambiguated normals (N̂ᵢX) is jointly optimized with the SDF parameters and X via backpropagation, while color, mask, and Eikonal losses provide additional geometric constraints. A variance-based confidence estimation module weights the normal loss per pixel by tracking how consistently cross-view loss changes correlate during training, down-weighting unreliable pixels affected by shadows or specularity. Training employs a coarse-to-fine positional encoding schedule and separate learning rates for the MLP parameters and the ambiguity matrix.

## Results

On the main comparison (4 views, 3 light sources), the proposed method achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD) and mean angular error (MAE) on all 7 scenes. Headline CD values range from 0.85 (CUBE) to 8.83 (BLOBBY), compared to 0.90–95.2 for NeuS, 0.90–27.3 for MonoSDF, and 1.79–25.3 for PS-NeRF. For rendered SDF normal maps, the proposed method's MAE ranges from 2.28° (BLOBBY) to 14.8° (FROG), consistently outperforming all baselines by 3–30° depending on the scene. The disambiguated normal maps (MAE(Est.)) also show the best accuracy, ranging from 7.01° to 19.3°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all seven scenes. Against NeuS (pure MVS with no photometric stereo), the advantage is most dramatic: NeuS fails catastrophically on CUBE (CD 95.2 vs. 0.85) and FROG (CD 40.1 vs. 2.01), while the proposed method's global ambiguity reduction enables accurate shape recovery even from 4 sparse views. Against MonoSDF (monocular depth/normal priors), the proposed method wins on CD for all scenes (e.g., BUNNY: 1.79 vs. 7.61; BLOBBY: 8.83 vs. 27.3) and on MAE(SDF) by 3–11°, though MonoSDF is competitive on BEAR (CD 1.7 vs. 1.30) and CUBE (CD 0.90 vs. 0.85) where monocular cues are strong. Against PS-NeRF (per-view UPS + neural surface), the proposed method consistently achieves lower CD (e.g., BUNNY: 1.79 vs. 5.38; BLOBBY: 8.83 vs. 25.3) and lower MAE(SDF) (e.g., BLOBBY: 2.28° vs. 7.05°), demonstrating that the shared global ambiguity from HO-GSVD factorization yields more accurate disambiguation than per-view learning-based UPS, particularly under sparse light sources (only 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NeuS (Wang et al., 2021) by incorporating photometric stereo supervision into neural SDF optimization, and builds on the SVD-based uncalibrated photometric stereo of Hayakawa (1994) by replacing per-view SVD with Higher-Order Generalized SVD (Kempf et al., 2023) to exploit a constrained setup where lights move rigidly with the camera. The key conceptual departure from PS-NeRF (Yang et al., 2022) is that instead of estimating per-view normal maps via learning-based UPS and then disambiguating each independently, MVCPS reduces the per-view linear ambiguities to a single global ambiguity matrix that is jointly resolved during SDF optimization. This shifts the disambiguation burden from a per-view learning problem to a single 3×3 matrix learned end-to-end with the surface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constrained setup (camera + lights on a shared rig or rotation stage) is trivially implementable in practice, making high-fidelity photometric stereo accessible without per-view light calibration. The method's ability to recover detailed shapes from only 4 views and 3 light sources addresses a practical bottleneck for MVPS, and the variance-based confidence estimation provides a lightweight mechanism for handling shadows and specularities without additional sensors. The main limitation is the Lambertian assumption, which restricts applicability to diffuse materials; extending to non-Lambertian reflectance under sparse observations remains an open challenge. Whether the global-ambiguity formulation generalizes to more complex lighting geometries (e.g., area lights, non-rigid light motion) is also unexplored.
