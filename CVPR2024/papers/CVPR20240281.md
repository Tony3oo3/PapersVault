## Problem

Unlike LLMs, which undergo a dedicated alignment stage (SFT + RLHF) after pretraining, text-to-image diffusion models have lacked a principled method for learning from human preference data. Existing approaches—fine-tuning on curated high-quality images, reward backpropagation (DRaFT, AlignProp), or RL-based tuning (DPOK, DDPO)—either fail to generalize to open vocabulary, suffer from mode collapse, or increase inference cost. This paper introduces Diffusion-DPO, the first method to directly optimize a diffusion model on pairwise human preference data in an open-vocabulary setting.

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL-1.0 base or SD1.5) using 851K pairwise image preferences from the Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset, where each pair consists of a text prompt and two generated images labeled as preferred/dispreferred. At inference, the aligned model takes a text prompt and produces a single generated image at a fixed square resolution, with no additional inference-time cost over the base model. The training procedure requires both the policy model and a frozen reference model to compute noise-prediction differences on both the preferred and dispreferred images.

## Method

Diffusion-DPO adapts Direct Preference Optimization to diffusion models by defining a tractable notion of data likelihood via the evidence lower bound over the full diffusion path x_{0:T}. The key derivation reformulates the DPO objective over the joint path distribution, applies Jensen's inequality to push expectations outside the log-sigmoid, and then approximates sampling from the intractable reverse process with the forward noising process q(x_t|x_0). This yields a simple closed-form loss (Eq. 14) that compares the noise-prediction errors of the policy and reference models on the preferred versus dispreferred images, weighted by a signal-to-noise-ratio-dependent coefficient and a temperature parameter β. The loss encourages the model to improve denoising of the preferred image more than the dispreferred one, effectively learning an implicit reward model without explicit reward modeling or RL. The approach is derived as an off-policy multi-step RL algorithm and also shown to be equivalent to a noisy preference model perspective, connecting it to prior RL-based diffusion alignment methods.

## Results

On the PartiPrompts benchmark, DPO-SDXL is preferred over SDXL-base 70.0% of the time for General Preference, 64.3% for Visual Appeal, and 64.9% for Prompt Alignment (majority vote of 5 labelers). Against the full SDXL pipeline (base + refiner, 6.6B params), the 3.5B DPO-SDXL model still wins 69% on PartiPrompts and 64% on HPSv2. DPO-SDXL also achieves an HPSv2 reward of 28.16, topping the HPSv2 leaderboard, and is preferred 65% of the time over SDXL on the TEDBench image-editing task. On the Pick-a-Pic validation set, DPO-SDXL reaches 72.0% preference classification accuracy, exceeding all existing reward models (PickScore 64.2%, HPS 59.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPO-SDXL decisively outperforms SDXL-base across all three human-evaluation criteria on PartiPrompts (70.0% / 64.3% / 64.9% win rates) and on HPSv2 (64.7% General Preference). More impressively, the 3.5B-parameter DPO-SDXL (base architecture only) beats the full 6.6B-parameter SDXL pipeline (base + refiner) with 69% and 64% win rates on PartiPrompts and HPSv2 respectively, even on the People category where the refiner is strongest (67.2% vs. 73.4% over base alone). The paper also reports comparisons to Emu [11] and DDPO [61] in the supplementary material, though specific numbers are not in the main text. On the TEDBench image-editing task, DPO-SDXL is preferred 65% of the time versus 24% for SDXL (11% draws). The implicit reward model learned by DPO-SDXL achieves 72.0% preference accuracy, surpassing dedicated reward models like PickScore (64.2%) and HPS (59.3%), confirming that the DPO objective implicitly captures human preferences without a separate reward network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from LLMs to diffusion models by reformulating the intractable likelihood term via the ELBO, yielding a simple noise-prediction-based loss (Eq. 14). It departs from RL-based diffusion alignment (DPOK, DDPO) which suffer from limited vocabulary and instability, and from reward-backpropagation methods (DRaFT, AlignProp) which cause mode collapse and fail on nuanced rewards like text-image alignment. The key conceptual move is treating the diffusion reverse process as the policy and using the forward noising process as a tractable surrogate for sampling, connecting the result to an off-policy RL formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diffusion-DPO establishes the first stable, open-vocabulary preference alignment method for diffusion models, and the finding that a 3.5B base model outperforms the 6.6B base+refiner pipeline suggests human preference optimization can implicitly distill refinement capabilities without extra inference cost. The AI-feedback variant (using PickScore, HPSv2, CLIP) matching human-preference training opens a path to scaling alignment without expensive human annotation. Open questions include whether online (rather than offline) DPO further improves performance, how to handle personal or group-specific preferences, and whether the method transfers to video or 3D generation.
