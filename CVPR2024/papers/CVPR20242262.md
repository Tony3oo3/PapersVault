## Problem

Existing cloth-changing person ReID methods rely on appearance cues (clothing, color) that degrade over long periods, while 3D shape-based approaches require ground-truth body shape annotations that are unavailable for real images. This paper asks whether discriminative body shape features can be learned without any 3D ground-truth supervision, by leveraging the linguistic-visual alignment already embedded in a pre-trained vision-language model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242262/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a single RGB human image and produces a global identity feature vector (aggregated from local patch features via an MLP) used for cosine-similarity matching; no CLIP model or text input is needed at test time. During training, each image is additionally paired with 16 automatically assigned linguistic body-shape descriptors (e.g., 'muscular' vs. 'slender') generated by the frozen CLIP teacher, and the student is also trained on 10,000 synthetic texture-free SMPL mesh renders with known canonical shape parameters for 3D reconstruction supervision.

## Method

CLIP3DReID distills a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 teacher into a compact ResNet-50 student through three coupled mechanisms. First, 16 pairs of opposing body-shape phrases are scored against each image via CLIP text-image cosine similarity to auto-label the image, and the winning text embeddings are projected into the student's feature space. Second, local patch features of the student are aligned to these shape-aware text tokens using a Sinkhorn-regularized optimal transport loss, while global features are aligned to the CLIP image encoder output via an MSE loss through a projection MLP. Third, a 3D SMPL reconstruction loss regularizes the global identity feature to predict canonical body shape parameters from synthetic, clothing-free mesh renders, enforcing domain-invariant geometric reasoning without any real 3D ground truth. The total objective combines cross-entropy identity loss with weighted OT, global-alignment, and 3D-reconstruction terms (α=0.3, β=0.5, γ=0.3).

## Results

On the primary cloth-changing ReID benchmark Celeb-reID-light, CLIP3DReID achieves 26.3 mAP and 39.4 Rank-1, surpassing the best baseline 3DInvarReID (21.8 mAP, 37.0 Rank-1) by 4.5 mAP. On Celeb-reID, it reaches 19.2 mAP and 63.1 Rank-1, outperforming 3DInvarReID by 4.0 mAP. On the most recent CCDA dataset, CLIP3DReID gains 4.0 mAP and 4.4 Rank-1 over 3DInvarReID (25.7 vs. 21.7 mAP; 15.5 vs. 11.1 Rank-1). On short-term benchmarks, it achieves 88.4 mAP / 95.6 Rank-1 on Market-1501 and 61.2 mAP / 81.5 Rank-1 on MSMT17, both state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP3DReID outperforms all nine baselines on every ReID metric across all evaluated datasets. The closest competitor, 3DInvarReID (ICCV'23), is surpassed by 4.0 mAP on Celeb-reID, 4.5 mAP on Celeb-reID-light, 2.8 mAP on LTCC, 1.0 mAP on PRCC, and 4.0 mAP on CCDA. Among appearance-based methods, CAL (CVPR'22) and AIM (CVPR'23) trail by 5.5 and 2.6 mAP respectively on Celeb-reID-light. On the 3D body shape reconstruction task (HBW, CD-L2), CLIP3DReID (0.642) is slightly worse than 3DInvarReID (0.610) and SHAPY (0.632), though the authors note this is achieved without any real 3D ground-truth shape labels during training, relying solely on synthetic mesh images. On face-blurred Celeb-reID, CLIP3DReID maintains a 2.0 mAP advantage over 3DInvarReID (11.6 vs. 9.6), confirming robustness when facial cues are removed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: cloth-changing person ReID (building on CAL, 3DInvarReID, and ReIDCaps), CLIP-based knowledge distillation (following CLIPPING, RISE, and ZeroSeg), and linguistic body shape representation (inspired by BodyTalk and SHAPY). It departs from prior 3D-based ReID methods (3DSL, 3DInvarReID) by eliminating the need for ground-truth 3D body shapes, instead using synthetic SMPL meshes and CLIP-derived linguistic descriptors as supervision. The key conceptual departure is repurposing CLIP's text encoder not for general semantic alignment but specifically to produce image-specific body shape labels, then using optimal transport to align local visual patches with those shape-aware text tokens—a use of OT in ReID that prior work has not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that linguistic body shape descriptors, automatically assigned via CLIP, can serve as effective domain-invariant supervision for ReID without requiring 3D ground truth, which is a practical advantage given the scarcity of real 3D body scans. However, performance gains are more modest on lower-resolution datasets (LTCC, PRCC), suggesting the method's reliance on CLIP's semantic correlation may degrade with image quality. An open question is whether the fixed set of 16 paired descriptors is sufficient to capture the full diversity of human body shapes, and whether the approach extends to other appearance-irrelevant biometric cues (e.g., gait, build) beyond the current descriptor vocabulary.
