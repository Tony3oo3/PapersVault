## Problem

Pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models exhibit significant demographic biases in human generation, producing images skewed toward specific age, gender, and skin tone groups. Existing debiasing approaches—prompt editing, semantic guidance, or test-time tuning—rely on the model's internal knowledge and fail to substantially improve diversity without costly retraining of the backbone or its training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neutral text prompt (e.g., 'Photo of a doctor') and a set of K demographically diverse reference images retrieved from a pre-indexed external database (~330K face-containing images from MSCOCO and OpenImages-v6, filtered by a face detector and aesthetic scorer). At inference, CLIP image embeddings are pre-computed and stored, so no image encoder runs at generation time; the reference images are projected through a single linear layer into text-compatible tokens that are concatenated with the prompt to form a bimodal conditioning signal fed into the U-Net's cross-attention layers. The output is a 512×512 generated human image with improved demographic representation.

## Method

FairRAG conditions a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone on external reference images via a lightweight linear projector H(·) that maps CLIP image embeddings into a single text-compatible token, trained with a standard denoising loss while keeping all backbone parameters fixed. For fair retrieval, FairRAG employs a two-step post-hoc debiasing strategy: first, a debiased query (original prompt + 'with any age, gender skin tone') retrieves Top-N candidates, then a balanced sampling scheme assigns weights inversely proportional to the frequency of each intersectional demographic group's constituent attributes, selecting K references that over-represent underrepresented groups. At generation time, an additional transfer instruction ('with age, gender and skin tone of:') is appended to the prompt to guide implicit attribute transfer from the reference without explicitly specifying demographic values. This design requires only ~0.2 s of extra latency over the baseline and avoids the computational overhead of test-time tuning or heavy adaptor modules used in prior conditioning approaches.

## Results

FairRAG achieves an intersectional diversity score of 0.438, a CLIP alignment score of 0.146, and an FID of 51.8, outperforming the best non-RAG baseline (TextAug: 0.341 diversity, 0.144 CLIP, 74.1 FID) and the SDv2.1 baseline (0.188 diversity, 0.142 CLIP, 85.3 FID). The diversity gain over SDv2.1 is +0.250, while FID improves by 33.5 points. Inference latency increases only marginally from 2.8 s (SDv2.1) to 3.0 s (FairRAG) on a single A10G GPU with 20 denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairRAG wins on intersectional diversity against all five baselines, improving from 0.188 (SDv2.1) to 0.438, a gain of +0.250. Among non-RAG methods, TextAug is the strongest baseline (0.341 diversity), but FairRAG still leads by 0.097; Interven (0.333) and FairDiff (0.196) trail further behind. On CLIP alignment, FairRAG ties BaseRAG at 0.146 and slightly edges out TextAug (0.144) and SDv2.1 (0.142), while Interven actually degrades alignment to 0.132. On FID, FairRAG (51.8) is close to BaseRAG (49.4) and substantially better than all non-RAG baselines (74.1–93.9), indicating that the fairness interventions add only a small fidelity cost relative to the RAG conditioning itself. Notably, Interven and FairDiff both worsen FID relative to SDv2.1, whereas FairRAG improves it by 33.5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairRAG sits at the intersection of three research threads: fairness interventions for text-to-image models (prompt editing in Bansal et al., semantic guidance in Friedrich et al.), visual-reference conditioning for diffusion models (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, InstantBooth), and retrieval-augmented generation for diffusion (Blattmann et al., Re-Imagen). It departs from prior fairness work by externalizing the source of diversity to a curated image database rather than relying on the model's internal (biased) knowledge, and departs from prior conditioning work by replacing heavy adapter modules or test-time tuning with a single linear projector trained once and reused at inference. The concurrent ITI-Gen learns per-concept prompt embeddings from references, whereas FairRAG's single linear layer generalizes to arbitrary new images without additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FairRAG offers a practical, low-overhead path to improving demographic diversity in human image generation without retraining the backbone or modifying training data, making it attractive for deployment in existing production systems. However, it is limited to human generation, uses one-to-one reference mapping, and still produces anatomical disfigurements in small faces and limbs, suggesting the frozen backbone's representational limits remain a bottleneck. Open questions include extending the framework to non-human prompts, handling references that conflict with the user's prompt, and improving attribute transfer fidelity (the retrieved distribution already achieves higher diversity than the generated output, indicating the conditioning mechanism is the limiting factor).
