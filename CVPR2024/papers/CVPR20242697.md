## Problem

Existing graph-based neural architecture encoders rely on synchronous message passing that oversimplifies how information actually propagates through architectures, and while Graph Transformers offer global attention, they lack mechanisms to capture the directional information flows (forward pass and backpropagation) that define a neural architecture's fundamental behavior. No prior graph transformer was specifically designed to encode these flow dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242697/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neural architecture as a directed acyclic graph specified by a binary adjacency matrix and a one-hot node feature matrix (one row per operation type), requiring no complex preprocessing such as isomorphic sequence augmentation or specialized skip-connection handling. The output is a fixed-dimensional architecture embedding obtained by mean-pooling final-layer node embeddings, which is fed to a fully connected regressor trained with a margin ranking loss to produce a scalar performance score for relative comparison among architectures.

## Method

FLOWERFORMER stacks L FLOWER layers, each containing two modules. The flow encode module performs bidirectional asynchronous message passing: nodes are partitioned into topological generations, and embeddings are updated sequentially in forward order (mimicking inference) then in reverse order (mimicking backpropagation), using softmax-weighted messages and a GRU combine operator from DAGNN. The flow-aware global attention module applies masked multi-head attention where a reachability mask restricts each node to attend only to nodes sharing at least one directed path, encoding architecture-level flow dependencies. The two module outputs are summed, passed through a 2-layer MLP with skip connections and batch normalization, and the final node embeddings are mean-pooled to form the architecture representation. This design unifies local flow modeling (asynchronous MPNN) with global flow-aware attention in a single graph transformer, distinguishing it from both synchronous GNNs and unmasked graph transformers.

## Results

On the three computer vision benchmarks, FLOWERFORMER achieves the best Kendall's Tau across all training ratios (1%–50%). At the 50% training setting, it reaches 89.6 on NAS-Bench-101, 92.9 on NAS-Bench-201, and 77.5 on NAS-Bench-301, outperforming the strongest baseline (TA-GATES) by up to 3.3 points on NB-101. In the low-data regime (1% training), the margin is largest: 75.0 vs. 70.8 on NB-101 (a 4.2-point gap). On the cross-domain benchmarks, FLOWERFORMER leads on NAS-Bench-ASR (52.2 vs. 50.9 at 50%) and NAS-Bench-Graph (68.9 vs. 67.4 at 10%), though DAGNN edges ahead at 50% on NB-Graph (73.1 vs. 72.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOWERFORMER outperforms all six baselines on Kendall's Tau in the vast majority of settings across the three computer vision benchmarks. The largest margins are against NAR-Former (up to 9.8 points on NB-101 at 1%) and DAGFormer (up to 8.7 points on NB-101 at 50%), confirming that a generic graph transformer without flow-awareness is insufficient. Against the strongest flow-based baseline TA-GATES, FLOWERFORMER gains 3.3 points on NB-101 (50%) and 1.5 points on NB-301 (50%), attributed to the global attention mechanism that TA-GATES lacks. Against the strongest GNN baseline DAGNN, the gap is 3.7 points on NB-101 (50%) and 1.4 points on NB-301 (50%). On the cross-domain NAS-Bench-Graph, FLOWERFORMER wins at 1%, 5%, and 10% training but is narrowly outperformed by DAGNN at 50% (72.7 vs. 73.1); on NAS-Bench-ASR, FLOWERFORMER leads at 5%, 10%, and 50% but trails TA-GATES at 1% (31.1 vs. 34.0), where TA-GATES' convolution-mimicking update is particularly suited to speech architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOWERFORMER unifies three previously separate threads in neural architecture encoding: graph-based representation learning (building on DAGNN's message-passing operators), flow modeling (extending TA-GATES' local forward/backward simulation to a global, asynchronous topological-order scheme), and global attention (adapting DAGFormer's reachability-based masking to flow-path dependencies). It departs from TA-GATES by elevating flow capture from local operation-level simulation to architecture-level asynchronous message passing, and from NAR-Former by replacing sequence-based representation with a direct DAG encoding that avoids isomorphic augmentation. The paper positions itself as the first graph transformer specifically designed to capture information flows, filling a gap between generic GTs (GraphGPS, DAGFormer) that lack flow awareness and flow-based encoders (TA-GATES) that lack global attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLOWERFORMER provides a principled, domain-agnostic input representation (simple DAG + one-hot node features) that outperforms methods requiring complex preprocessing, making it broadly applicable across CV, GNN, and ASR architectures. The ablation results—particularly the 8.7-point gap between a plain GT and FLOWERFORMER—argue that flow-awareness is not merely a nice-to-have but a critical inductive bias for architecture encoding. Open questions include whether the flow-based masking generalizes to architectures with cyclic connections or dynamic control flow, and whether the margin ranking loss is optimal compared to other ranking or regression objectives. The method's reliance on labeled training architectures (even at 1%) remains a practical constraint for truly novel search spaces.
