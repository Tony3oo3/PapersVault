## Problem

Existing JPEG artifact removal networks require a conventional JPEG decoder as a preprocessing step, taking the decoded image as input despite the encoded spectrum containing strictly more information (by the data processing inequality). Moreover, most methods are dedicated to specific quality factors or rely on quality-factor estimation, limiting their flexibility. The paper asks whether a single network can directly decode JPEG bitstreams across all quality factors without any conventional decoder in the pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240128/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes raw quantized 8×8 DCT spectra for both luma (full resolution) and chroma (half resolution) components along with the corresponding quantization matrix Q, operating on 112×112 pixel patches. The luma and chroma spectra have different spatial dimensions (H×W vs. H/2×W/2), which the architecture must reconcile internally. The output is a full-resolution RGB image, with the chroma upsampling from half to full resolution handled implicitly by the local implicit neural representation rather than an explicit spatial resampling step.

## Method

JDEC comprises three components: a SwinV2-based encoder with a group spectra embedding layer that converts 8×8 DCT blocks into B×B (B=4) sub-block representations via block-diagonal DCT matrices to unify luma and chroma sizes; a Continuous Cosine Formulation (CCF) module that estimates trainable continuous frequencies (F_h, F_w) and amplitudes for each B×B block, reconstructing an enhanced spectrum as X̂ ⊗ (cos(πF_h⊗δ_h) ⊙ cos(πF_w⊗δ_w)); and a 5-layer MLP decoder that maps the CCF output to RGB pixel values as a local implicit neural representation. The CCF's key insight is that learning continuous cosine frequencies and amplitudes per block simultaneously performs dequantization and chroma upsampling, avoiding aliasing artifacts that discrete spectrum manipulation would introduce. Training is supervised with L1 loss over 112×112 patches, sampling quality factors uniformly from [10,100] in steps of 10, enabling a single model to decode any quality factor.

## Results

JDEC achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all three benchmark datasets (LIVE-1, BSDS500, ICB) at every tested quality factor (q=10–40), outperforming the strongest baseline FBCNN by up to 0.37 dB on ICB at q=10 (32.55 vs. 32.18 PSNR). At high quality factors (q=80–100), JDEC surpasses even the conventional JPEG decoder, reaching 45.14 dB PSNR on LIVE-1 at q=100 versus 43.07 dB for standard JPEG. The method also achieves the best SSIM scores, e.g., 0.968 on ICB at q=100 compared to 0.955 for JPEG and 0.951 for FBCNN. Notably, JDEC is the only method (alongside raw JPEG) that takes quantized spectra directly as input, eliminating the need for a conventional JPEG decoder in the pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JDEC outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and quality factor tested. Against the strongest flexible-QF baselines (FBCNN and QGAC), JDEC gains 0.18–0.37 dB PSNR on ICB (e.g., 32.55 vs. 32.18 at q=10) and 0.11–0.15 dB on LIVE-1 and BSDS500. At high quality factors (q=80–100, Table 3), JDEC surpasses the conventional JPEG decoder by up to 2.07 dB on LIVE-1 (45.14 vs. 43.07 at q=100) and 2.30 dB on ICB (43.61 vs. 41.31), while FBCNN and QGAC actually degrade below JPEG at q=95–100. DnCNN, a fixed-QF CNN, trails JDEC by 1.2–1.8 dB across all settings. Swin2SR, though competitive at q=10 on ICB (32.46 vs. 32.55), was trained on additional datasets including BSDS500 test images and only covers q∈[10,40], making it a less fair comparison. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that both the group spectra embedding and the CCF module contribute meaningfully, with removing both causing a ~1 dB drop.

## Relation to Prior Work

JDEC builds on two key threads: (1) the group spectra embedding from Park et al. [28], which enables processing raw JPEG DCT spectra without a conventional decoder, and (2) the SwinIR/SwinV2 transformer backbone [22, 25] for feature extraction. The paper's core departure is the Continuous Cosine Formulation (CCF) module, which borrows the implicit neural representation (INR) paradigm—where sinusoidal/cosine basis functions with learned frequencies and amplitudes reconstruct signals [5, 16, 20, 21, 32]—and applies it specifically to DCT block reconstruction. Unlike prior artifact removal methods that take decoded images as input, JDEC operates directly on quantized spectra, and unlike Bahat et al. [4] who also decoded from spectra, JDEC handles color components with trainable upsampling and targets high quality factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JDEC demonstrates that a single learned model can decode JPEG bitstreams across all quality factors (10–100) without a conventional decoder, outperforming both the standard JPEG decoder at q=100 and all prior artifact removal networks. The CCF module's ability to estimate continuous cosine spectra that track ground-truth dominant frequencies, even under extreme compression (q=10), suggests a principled way to invert quantization distortion in the spectral domain. Limitations include higher computational cost (224 ms, 38.9M params) compared to CNN-based baselines, and the approach is specific to the JPEG/DCT pipeline; whether the continuous-cosine INR strategy generalizes to other transforms (e.g., wavelet-based codecs) remains open. The paper also notes that extreme reconstruction (q=0) requires a separate JDEC+ variant, indicating the main model's practical range is bounded.
