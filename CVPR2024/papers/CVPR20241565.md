## Problem

Existing diffusion model-based image restoration methods require long serial sampling chains (100–1000 steps) to progressively denoise, making inference expensive and preventing gradient computation through the full chain. This limits both efficiency and the ability to understand or control how the noise initialization affects the restored output, particularly for tasks like super-resolution where identity preservation matters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241565/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded 256×256 image y together with a known linear degradation operator A (bicubic downsampling, Gaussian/anisotropic blur, grayscale averaging, or binary masks) and a Gaussian noise initialization x_T ~ N(0, I). It outputs a single restored high-quality image x_0* in a fully zero-shot manner with no training. The pseudo-inverse A† of the degradation is required; for unknown or non-linear degradations, a pre-trained restoration model can inject prior information at an intermediate state to guide the sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating the entire diffusion sampling chain as a multivariate deep equilibrium fixed point system: x_{0:T-1} = F(x_{0:T-1}; (x_T, y)). Proposition 1 provides an analytical expression showing each state x_{T-k} is a function of all subsequent states {x_{T-k+1}, …, x_T} and the degraded image y, enabling all timesteps to be evaluated in parallel rather than sequentially. The fixed point is solved via Anderson acceleration (typically 15 iterations) over a small number of DDIM timesteps (e.g., 20), replacing the long serial chain. For initialization optimization, DEQ inversion (implicit function theorem) computes gradients ∂L/∂x_T through the converged fixed point without storing the full computational graph, enabling gradient-based updates to the noise input to improve PSNR or steer generation toward a reference image.

## Results

On ImageNet 4× SR, DeqIR achieves 27.47 PSNR / 0.781 SSIM / 0.230 LPIPS with only 15 iterations, outperforming DDNM (26.49 PSNR, 100 steps) and DDRM (26.21 PSNR, 20 steps). On CelebA-HQ Gaussian deblurring, DeqIR reaches 47.18 PSNR / 0.992 SSIM / 0.019 LPIPS, the best among all zero-shot methods. The method requires only 15 Anderson-acceleration iterations (vs. 100–1000 for competing methods) while achieving the best or near-best results across all four restoration tasks (2×/4× SR, Gaussian/anisotropic deblurring) on both ImageNet and CelebA-HQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeqIR outperforms all zero-shot diffusion-based baselines (DPS, DiffPIR, DDRM, DDNM, ILVR) on the majority of metrics across both datasets, while requiring only 15 iterations versus 20–1000 for competitors. Against the strongest zero-shot baseline DDNM, DeqIR gains up to 0.98 dB PSNR on ImageNet 2× SR (32.35 vs. 31.81) and 1.07 dB on anisotropic deblurring (39.47 vs. 38.40), though DDNM retains a slight edge on ImageNet Gaussian deblurring PSNR (43.83 vs. 43.42). Against DDRM, DeqIR improves 4× SR PSNR by 1.26 dB on ImageNet (27.47 vs. 26.21) and 0.31 dB on CelebA-HQ (32.22 vs. 31.91). On inpainting (Table 2), DeqIR achieves the best text-mask PSNR (39.72) and is competitive with DDNM and RePaint on stripe masks. Against supervised methods (Table 5), DeqIR surpasses SRGAN, BSRGAN, and LDM on 4× SR but falls short of DiffIR (27.44 vs. 29.25 PSNR on ImageNet), which is trained specifically for the degradation. The GAN-based DGP baseline is substantially outperformed across all tasks, confirming the advantage of the diffusion prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DEQ-DDDIM line of work (Pokle et al., 2022), which applied deep equilibrium models to the diffusion sampling process, to the full image restoration pipeline that couples the denoiser with a degradation operator and its pseudo-inverse at every step. The key departure is an analytical reformulation (Proposition 1) that expresses the entire sequential sampling chain as a multivariate fixed point system, enabling all timesteps to be solved in parallel via Anderson acceleration rather than iterated one-by-one. This is non-trivial because the IR-specific coupling through A†A at each step breaks the simple single-variable fixed point structure of pure diffusion sampling. The DEQ inversion mechanism for gradient computation through the fixed point is borrowed from the broader implicit-model literature (Bai et al., 2019; Geng et al., 2021) but applied here to optimize the noise initialization, a capability that was impractical under sequential sampling due to memory constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parallel fixed-point formulation offers a principled alternative to the sequential sampling paradigm that has dominated diffusion-based IR, potentially reducing the number of network evaluations from 100+ to ~15 while matching or slightly exceeding quality. The DEQ inversion for initialization optimization opens a new axis of control (guiding generation direction, improving identity preservation) that was previously inaccessible without storing the full computational graph. However, the method still assumes a known or approximable linear degradation operator A, limiting its applicability to truly blind or highly non-linear degradations; the authors acknowledge this and mention a supervised variant as future work. Open questions include whether the parallel formulation extends to non-linear or unknown degradations, whether the convergence guarantees hold at larger image resolutions, and whether the modest wall-clock speedup over DDNM (12.1s vs 12.7s at 10 steps) scales favorably with multi-GPU parallelism as the paper suggests.
