## Problem

Existing multi-domain generalization (mDG) methods lack a unified learning objective and typically assume the target marginal distribution P(Y|D) is static across domains, an assumption that fails for regression and segmentation tasks. The paper further shows that prior methods only partially optimize what should be a single general objective, and that relaxing the static assumption without care introduces invalid causal pathways that degrade generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242059/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and produces task-specific outputs: class labels for classification, per-pixel depth maps for monocular depth estimation, or semantic segmentation masks. A key structural requirement is that the learnable Y-mapping ψ projects targets into the same RKHS dimensionality as the feature mapping φ(X) (e.g., an MLP for classification/regression, a ResNet-50 for segmentation), so that joint distributions P(φ(X), ψ(Y)) can be estimated per domain via VAE encoders and covariance matrices computed in a shared latent space.

## Method

The paper proposes the General Multi-Domain Generalization Objective (GMDG), a weighted sum of four terms: GAim1 minimizes the Generalized Jensen-Shannon Divergence (GJSD) of joint (φ(X), ψ(Y)) distributions across domains to learn D-independent conditional features; GAim2 maximizes the posterior via cross-entropy on both φ(X)→Y and ψ(Y)→Y predictions; GReg1 aligns features to a frozen pre-trained oracle distribution O via KL divergence; and GReg2 suppresses invalid causality (ψ(Y)→φ(X)) by minimizing the Conditional Feature Shift, reduced to minimizing |Σ_XY Σ_YY^{-1} Σ_YX| under a Gaussian assumption. The central novelty is the learnable Y-mapping ψ that relaxes the static P(Y|D) assumption, enabling the framework to handle tasks where targets carry domain-dependent structure. Theoretically, the paper derives an alignment Upper Bound (PUB) from GJSD with prior knowledge, proving that methods like MIRO which only minimize GReg1 leave the domain gap unresolved, and that all four terms are synergistic rather than independently sufficient.

## Results

On classification (DomainBed suite), GMDG with a single RegNetY-16GF oracle achieves 75.1% average accuracy, surpassing MIRO (74.1%) by 1.0%, and with SWAD reaches 78.2%, exceeding the SOTA SIMPLE++ (78.1%) which uses 283 pre-trained models as an ensemble oracle. On monocular depth estimation (NYU Depth V2), GMDG reduces average SILog from 13.05 (VA-DepthNet) to 12.25 and improves δ₁ from 79.09% to 82.52%. On mDG segmentation (GTAV→Cityscapes/BDD-100K/Mapillary), GMDG achieves 37.65% average mIoU, outperforming RobustNet (36.76%) and DeepLabv3+ (30.83%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMDG consistently outperforms MIRO across all oracle configurations: +0.4% avg with ResNet-50 (66.3 vs 65.9), +1.0% with RegNetY-16GF (75.1 vs 74.1), and +0.9% with SWAD (78.2 vs 77.3). The headline result is that GMDG + SWAD with a single RegNetY-16GF model (78.2%) marginally exceeds SIMPLE++ (78.1%), which relies on an ensemble of 283 pre-trained models. In segmentation, GMDG improves average mIoU by 0.89% over RobustNet (37.65 vs 36.76) and 6.82% over DeepLabv3+ (30.83), with the largest gain on the hardest unseen domain (Cityscapes: 38.62 vs 37.69). In regression, GMDG reduces average SILog by 0.79 over VA-DepthNet (12.25 vs 13.05) and 0.67 over the Swin-L backbone alone (12.93), with the most notable improvement when 'Home' is the unseen domain (14.14 vs 15.03 SILog). GMDG also surpasses all non-oracle baselines (ERM, CORAL, DANN, CDANN, MTL, MLDG, etc.) by 1.8–2.1% average accuracy in classification, confirming that the full four-term objective is necessary for optimal generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the mDG literature by introducing a Y-mapping ψ that projects targets into the same RKHS as inputs, relaxing the long-standing assumption that P(Y|D) is static across domains. It builds directly on the GJSD variational upper bound of Cho et al. [9] to derive an alignment upper bound (PUB), and subsumes objectives from DANN, CDANN, MDA, MIRO, SIMPLE, CORAL, and RobustNet as partial special cases. The key departure from MIRO/SIMPLE is the demonstration that prior-knowledge regularization (GReg1) alone is insufficient without concurrent domain-invariant feature learning (GAim1), and the identification of an 'invalid causality' pathway (ψ(Y)→φ(X)) that emerges when the static P(Y|D) assumption is relaxed—a side effect overlooked by MDA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GMDG framework provides a principled, task-agnostic objective that extends mDG beyond classification to regression and segmentation, where Y is continuous and the static P(Y|D) assumption is most problematic. The finding that certain terms (e.g., −H(P(φ(X)|D)) in CORAL-like objectives) can actively degrade performance when combined with strong prior knowledge is a useful caution for practitioners assembling multi-term losses. Limitations include the assumption of equal domain weights in the GJSD minimization, the Gaussian-like distributional assumption underlying the CFS simplification, and modest empirical gains (typically <1% in classification, small absolute improvements in regression/segmentation) that leave open whether the theoretical unification translates to large practical benefits at scale.
