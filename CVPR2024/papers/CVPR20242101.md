## Problem

Existing 3D articulated horse models (hSMAL, SMAL) are learned from rigid toy figurines, yielding low-resolution templates with limited shape expressiveness and no pose-dependent deformations. No prior model captures the muscle-level non-rigid deformations that occur during equine motion, despite horses having less subcutaneous fat and shorter hair than humans, making musculature directly observable and clinically relevant to health and performance assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242101/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes shape parameters β (PCA coefficients plus six limb-scaling dimensions for ears, legs, and tail) and pose parameters θ (38 joint rotations encoded as quaternions) to produce a 3D articulated horse mesh at 13,873 vertices (or 3,647 at low resolution) with realistic pose-dependent muscle deformations. During training, the network consumes 3D scan point clouds (resampled to 20,000 points) alongside pre-computed alignment parameters from ~3,600 cleaned dynamic scans of 50 horses captured with a 3dMD scanner.

## Method

VAREN is learned in two stages: first, a statistical shape model (PCA over aligned prototype scans) is built on an hSMAL+ template with an anatomical skeleton of 38 joints defined from real horse anatomy rather than optimization; second, a neural network learns pose-dependent deformations from dynamic scans. The key architectural novelty is a per-muscle deformation formulation: each of 13,873 vertices is assigned to one of 76 superficial muscles, and a per-muscle linear decoder D_i maps a muscle deformation variable β_m to vertex displacements. The variable β_m = A ∘ W_m(θ, β₁,₂) is computed via a learnable selection matrix A (76×154) that weights which joints influence each muscle, initialized from anatomical part adjacency, and a nonlinear network W_m that encodes pose and shape. This replaces the global linear pose blend shapes of SMPL and the joint-distance-weighted local models of STAR, yielding a more compact, interpretable, and accurate deformation model that explicitly links articulation to observable muscle deformation.

## Results

On the Out-shape test set (154 frames, 6 unseen horses), VAREN achieves a mean mesh-to-scan distance of 7.78 mm and Chamfer distance of 10.88 mm, outperforming STAR (8.36 mm / 11.03 mm), SMPL (8.84 mm / 11.38 mm), and the no-deformation baseline (10.07 mm / 12.00 mm). On the In-shape test set (275 frames, 5 horses), VAREN reaches 6.35 mm mesh-to-scan and 9.37 mm Chamfer distance. Wilcoxon significance tests confirm VAREN's improvement over STAR is statistically significant (p = 0.04 for Chamfer, p = 3.3×10⁻⁶ for mesh-to-scan).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAREN outperforms all three baselines on both test sets. Against the no-deformation baseline, VAREN reduces mesh-to-scan error by 22.9% (7.78 vs 10.07 mm) on the Out-shape set, confirming that pose-dependent muscle deformations are essential for accurate horse modeling. Compared to SMPL-style global linear pose deformations, VAREN achieves a 12.1% reduction in mesh-to-scan distance (7.78 vs 8.84 mm), showing that muscle-grouped deformations capture pose-dependent shape changes more effectively than global linear spaces. Against STAR, which adds joint-locality weighting to global deformations, VAREN still improves mesh-to-scan by 6.9% (7.78 vs 8.36 mm) with a statistically significant margin (p = 3.3×10⁻⁶). The comparison with hSMAL (21.92 mm Chamfer) highlights the benefit of learning from real 3D scans rather than rigid toys, with VAREN reducing error by roughly 50%. The In-shape results show a similar ordering, with VAREN achieving 6.35 mm mesh-to-scan versus STAR's 6.99 mm, indicating consistent gains even when the shape is known.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAREN extends the SMPL/hSMAL statistical shape model framework (PCA shape spaces, linear blend skinning, joint regressors) to horses, but makes two key conceptual departures. First, it replaces the joint-distance-based locality of STAR with per-muscle deformation regions, motivated by the fact that equine musculature is directly visible through short hair and low subcutaneous fat. Second, it grounds the kinematic skeleton in real anatomical joint locations (including scapula joints) rather than the optimization-derived joints of SMPL, making the model interpretable for veterinary and biomechanical use. The base alignment model hSMAL+ is itself a resolution-increased and shape-augmented version of the toy-learned hSMAL model, which VAREN supersedes by learning from real 3D scans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAREN provides the first large-scale dataset of real 3D horse scans (~4,000 cleaned scans from 50 horses of diverse breeds) and the first articulated horse model with anatomically grounded, muscle-based pose-dependent deformations, opening the door to markerless 3D motion capture, injury diagnosis, and performance analysis in the equestrian domain. The muscle-based formulation is more compact and interpretable than joint-distance alternatives, potentially enabling future work that couples the model with electromyography or muscle activation measurements. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other domestic species, whether the 50-horse shape space captures sufficient morphological diversity, and how the model performs under in-the-wild monocular reconstruction where occlusions and viewpoint ambiguity are far more severe than in the controlled scanning setup.
