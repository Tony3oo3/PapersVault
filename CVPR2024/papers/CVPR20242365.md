## Problem

In Exemplar-Free Class Incremental Learning (EFCIL), the model's attention to salient features drifts toward task-specific or background regions as new classes are learned, causing severe catastrophic forgetting. Existing saliency distillation approaches either require storing old samples (impossible in EFCIL) or enforce consistency across semantically different old and new classes, making them ineffective. The paper identifies this saliency drift as a key underexplored cause of forgetting and proposes a dedicated supervision strategy to mitigate it.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-Subset) and produces class labels as the primary output. Additionally, a lightweight decoder attached to the ResNet-18 backbone outputs combined saliency and boundary maps at full resolution, while Grad-CAM saliency maps are extracted at three intermediate stages of the encoder for boundary supervision. The teacher saliency and boundary maps are precomputed offline using CSNet, so no additional data storage is required during incremental training.

## Method

TASS introduces three complementary components to combat saliency drift in EFCIL. First, dilated boundary supervision binarizes and dilates CSNet-generated boundary maps, then applies a binary cross-entropy penalty at three mid-level backbone stages (via Grad-CAM) to prevent the model's attention from drifting into background regions while preserving task-adaptive plasticity within the foreground. Second, a task-agnostic low-level multi-task decoder predicts saliency and boundary maps with an L2 distillation loss against the CSNet teacher, anchoring stable, class-independent features across all incremental tasks. Third, saliency noise injection adds elliptical noise to random feature channels at each encoder stage and trains the network to denoise, building robustness against future drift without requiring ground-truth drift signals. The total loss is the sum of the standard CIL cross-entropy (plus any method-specific anti-forgetting term), the low-level distillation loss, and the dilated boundary loss, making TASS a plug-and-play module that can be integrated into existing EFCIL frameworks such as SSRE, PASS, and IL2A.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (10-task setting), TASS achieves 67.42% average accuracy, 57.93% last-task accuracy, and 17.78% average forgetting, outperforming the best EFCIL baseline SSRE (65.04/55.01/19.48) by ~2.4% in average accuracy and ~2.9% in last-task accuracy. On ImageNet-Subset (10-task), TASS reaches 72.60% average and 57.93% last accuracy versus SSRE's 67.69/57.51. TASS also surpasses exemplar-based methods: on CIFAR-100 10-task, it beats RRR (65.78 avg) and LUCIR (62.39 avg) despite using zero stored exemplars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TASS outperforms all exemplar-free baselines across every dataset and task sequence. Against the strongest EFCIL method SSRE, TASS gains +2.9% average and +2.9% last accuracy on CIFAR-100 (10-task), and +4.9% average on ImageNet-Subset (10-task). Against PASS and IL2A, the margins are larger: +5.6% and +9.8% average accuracy respectively on CIFAR-100 (10-task). TASS also surpasses exemplar-based methods that store 20 samples per class: it beats RRR by +1.6% average accuracy and LUCIR by +5.0% on CIFAR-100 (10-task) without retaining any past data. On longer sequences (20 tasks), TASS's advantage grows, e.g. +3.3% last accuracy over SSRE and +12.3% over PASS on CIFAR-100. TASS is also shown to be plug-and-play: adding it to MUC yields +20.8% average accuracy on CIFAR-100 (10-task), and to SSRE yields a consistent ~3% gain across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

TASS carves new ground by reframing catastrophic forgetting in EFCIL as a saliency drift problem, departing from the dominant paradigm of representation/embedding regularization (SSRE, PASS, IL2A) that focuses on feature-space constraints. It repurposes low-level vision tasks (saliency and boundary detection via CSNet) as task-agnostic auxiliary supervision, and introduces dilated boundary maps and elliptical saliency noise injection as novel regularization mechanisms. Unlike prior saliency-guided CIL methods (LwM, RRR) that require storing old samples or suffer from semantic gaps between old and new classes, TASS operates entirely on current-task data, making it compatible with the exemplar-free constraint. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module rather than a standalone framework, positioning it as complementary to existing EFCIL approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides direct quantitative evidence (IoU with DINO self-attention) that saliency drift is a major, previously underappreciated cause of forgetting in EFCIL, offering a concrete diagnostic for practitioners. The plug-and-play design means the technique can be adopted alongside any existing EFCIL method without architectural changes, lowering the barrier to improvement. A key limitation is the reliance on an external teacher model (CSNet) for generating saliency and boundary maps, which the authors acknowledge could raise fairness concerns in comparisons. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-CNN backbones (e.g., vision transformers), whether the low-level supervision can be replaced by self-supervised or pre-trained model signals, and how the method performs in more realistic streaming settings with non-uniform task sizes.
