## Problem

Camera-based person ReID systems are constrained by lighting conditions, complex backgrounds, privacy concerns, and heavy reliance on transient appearance cues. While Kinect and radar have been explored for 3D structural ReID, they suffer from limited range, poor resolution, or indoor-only operation. LiDAR-based person ReID in large-scale outdoor scenes had not been previously investigated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240268/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of 30 pedestrian point cloud frames, each upsampled/downsampled to 256 points and normalized by subtracting the bounding-box center, captured by Livox Mid-100 LiDARs at 10 Hz (~30,000 raw points per frame). The network outputs a 1024-dimensional feature vector per sequence, and identity matching is performed via cosine distance between query and gallery features. Evaluation follows the standard CMC/mAP protocol with cross-LiDAR recognition (no temporal overlap between query and gallery captures).

## Method

ReID3D combines multi-task pre-training with a novel encoder. Pre-training on the simulated LReID-sync dataset jointly optimizes point cloud completion (via a PCN folding decoder producing coarse 128-point and detailed 512-point shapes, supervised by Chamfer Distance) and SMPL shape parameter regression (MSE loss), guiding the encoder to capture intrinsic 3D body geometry. The ReID network's GCEE backbone uses DGCNN-style graph convolutions with dynamic KNN graphs updated in feature space at each layer, followed by a Complementary Feature Extractor (CFE) that processes each frame as a primary frame and the next as a supplementary frame; an Eraser module computes a correlation vector between the primary salient feature and all local descriptors of the supplementary frame, then binarizes a regional mask to remove already-discovered salient regions before extracting auxiliary features. A 4-layer transformer temporal module aggregates the sequence of complementary frame features into the final identity embedding, trained with cross-entropy plus batch-hard triplet loss.

## Results

On LReID, ReID3D achieves an overall Rank-1 accuracy of 94.0% and mAP of 83.28%, outperforming all four camera-based baselines. Under low-light conditions, ReID3D reaches 93.3% Rank-1, compared to the best camera method TCLNet at only 60.0%, demonstrating LiDAR's robustness to illumination. Under normal light, camera methods still lead (TCLNet: 98.6% Rank-1 vs. ReID3D: 94.3%), as they exploit rich appearance cues. Even without pre-training, B-ReID3D achieves 90.0% overall Rank-1, already surpassing all camera-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReID3D outperforms all four camera-based baselines on overall Rank-1 (94.0% vs. TCLNet 87.0%, SINet 81.0%, PiT 76.0%, STMN 75.0%) and overall mAP (83.28% vs. TCLNet 80.27%). The advantage is most dramatic under low light: ReID3D achieves 93.3% Rank-1 while the best camera method (TCLNet) drops to 60.0%, a 33.3-point gap, and STMN collapses to 30.0%. Under normal light, however, TCLNet (98.6%) and SINet (97.1%) still exceed ReID3D (94.3%) by 4.3 and 2.8 points respectively, as camera methods exploit appearance cues (clothing color, accessories) that LiDAR cannot capture. Even the unpretrained variant B-ReID3D (90.0% overall R1) beats every camera baseline, confirming that the 3D structural signal alone is sufficient to surpass 2D appearance-based ReID in challenging conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant camera-based person ReID paradigm (TCLNet, STMN, SINet, PiT), which relies on 2D appearance cues, by formulating ReID entirely on LiDAR point clouds. It draws conceptual inspiration from Kinect-based ReID (which used depth for 3D structure) and radar-based gait/identity recognition, but argues LiDAR's higher resolution and longer range make it viable for large-scale outdoor scenes where those sensors fail. The GCEE encoder adapts the Dynamic Graph CNN backbone [39] and borrows the complementary feature extraction idea from TCLNet [15], while the pre-training pipeline repurposes the PCN folding decoder [49] and SMPL [24] in a multi-task setup not previously applied to ReID.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first LiDAR-based ReID benchmark (LReID) and demonstrating that 3D structural features can match or exceed appearance-based methods overall (94.0 vs. 87.0 rank-1) while remaining robust to lighting (93.3 vs. 60.0 under low light), the paper opens a practical path for privacy-preserving, illumination-invariant surveillance. Key limitations include the modest scale (320 identities, single LiDAR model, outdoor-only), the absence of indoor or multi-LiDAR-type evaluation, and the reliance on a simulated pre-training set whose transfer to real-world diversity remains untested at scale. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-density LiDARs, larger identity pools, and whether the GCEE's complementary mechanism scales to longer sequences or more complex occlusion patterns.
