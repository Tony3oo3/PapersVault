## Problem

Existing multi-agent collaborative perception systems assume ideal communication and perception environments, neglecting real-world noise sources such as pose noise in vehicle localization, perception noise in sensor data, and motion blur from fast-moving objects. These noise sources cause feature misalignment during cross-agent fusion and degrade detection accuracy, yet robust collaborative perception under such conditions remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240570/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The ego agent receives its own LiDAR point cloud, point clouds from N-1 collaborators (transformed into the ego coordinate frame via pose-based alignment), and K historical point cloud frames from a memory bank. Point clouds are discretized at 0.4 m grid resolution and encoded into S=3 multi-scale BEV feature maps via a shared PointPillars backbone. The system outputs 3D detection boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) with per-anchor classification scores, evaluated at IoU thresholds of 0.50 and 0.70.

## Method

MRCNet introduces a two-stage communication protocol with a Multi-Scale Robust Fusion (MRF) module and a Motion Enhanced Mechanism (MEM). In MRF, a channel-wise max+avg pooling operation generates a semantic confidence map that selects only highly informative features for transmission, reducing bandwidth and noise propagation. In the first communication stage, collaborators apply an Improved Pyramid Sampling Aggregation (IPSA) module—concatenating pyramid-sampled cross-scale features with original features via 1×1 convolution—followed by a Multi-Scale Deformable Self-Attention (MDSA) module to fuse features robustly against pose-induced misalignment. In the second stage, the ego agent aggregates returned features using spatial-wise adaptive fusion (max/avg pooling + 3D convolution). MEM employs a Gated Motion Unit (GMU) built on a GRU architecture: it computes inter-frame motion context via BEV feature differencing, applies deformable convolution to adaptively sample historical features, and uses a channel-spatial attention update gate to selectively blend the refined historical feature with the current frame, thereby mitigating motion blur.

## Results

Under pose noise (Gaussian σ=0.2 m, 0.2°), MRCNet achieves the best AP@0.5/0.7 on all three datasets: 85.33/69.82 on V2XSim, 89.77/76.12 on OPV2V, and 85.00/66.31 on V2XSet. The largest margin over the strongest intermediate-fusion baseline is on V2XSim AP@0.7, where MRCNet leads Early Fusion (65.97) by 3.85 points and V2VNet (65.31) by 4.51 points. On OPV2V, MRCNet surpasses the next-best method (V2VNet, 88.69/74.93) by 1.08 and 1.19 points on AP@0.5 and AP@0.7 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRCNet outperforms all seven intermediate-fusion baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitors, the margins are: V2VNet (88.69/74.93 on OPV2V) is beaten by 1.08/1.19 AP points; CoBEVT (88.99/72.80 on OPV2V) by 0.78/3.32; AttFuse (84.37/66.27 on V2XSet) by 0.63/0.04; and V2X-ViT (86.74/75.70 on OPV2V) by 3.03/0.42. The gap widens substantially on V2XSim, where MRCNet leads V2VNet by 2.63/4.51 and Early Fusion by 0.39/3.85 on AP@0.5/0.7. Where2Comm and When2Com, which focus on communication efficiency rather than noise robustness, trail MRCNet by 7.76/12.80 and 11.97/17.30 AP@0.5/0.7 points on V2XSim respectively, confirming that bandwidth-reduction strategies alone do not address pose-noise-induced misalignment. Notably, Early Fusion achieves the highest AP@0.5 on V2XSim (84.94) but requires full raw-data transmission and still lags MRCNet on AP@0.7 by 3.85 points, highlighting MRCNet's advantage in precise localization under noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRCNet sits within the intermediate-fusion branch of collaborative perception (V2VNet, OPV2V/AttFuse, V2X-ViT, CoBEVT) and borrows its core attention machinery from Deformable DeTR's multi-scale deformable self-attention and the pyramid sampling aggregation lineage (TANet, D-ALIGN). Its departure from prior work is twofold: (1) it explicitly models real-world noise—pose error, perception distortion, and motion blur—that earlier frameworks assume away, and (2) it introduces a two-stage communication protocol with channel-wise feature selection to reduce bandwidth while preserving informative semantics. The GRU-based motion enhanced mechanism draws on temporal aggregation ideas from video person re-identification and deformable convolution, repurposing them for LiDAR BEV feature refinement in a multi-agent setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By targeting the noise robustness gap that most collaborative perception benchmarks ignore, MRCNet offers a practical recipe (multi-scale cross-semantic fusion + motion context) that practitioners deploying V2X systems can adopt without architectural overhaul. However, the evaluation is confined to three CARLA/OpenCDA-simulated datasets with simplified Gaussian noise models, and the paper does not address model heterogeneity, dynamic communication topologies, or real-sensor degradation, leaving open whether the robustness gains transfer to deployed systems. The authors themselves flag communication efficiency under varying network conditions and heterogeneous agent architectures as future directions.
