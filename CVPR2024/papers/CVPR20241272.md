## Problem

End-to-end text spotting eliminates error accumulation and sub-optimal performance of two-step pipelines but sacrifices modularity, requiring full retraining when swapping a single component. Two-step methods retain independent development of detectors and recognizers but cannot naturally share optimization signals across modules. The paper asks whether one can achieve end-to-end optimization benefits while preserving the modularity that makes two-step systems practical in competitions and production settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene text image as input. A pre-trained detector first predicts text bounding boxes; these boxes are used to crop both the original image (fed to the frozen recognizer backbone) and the detection backbone feature map (fed to the Bridge). The final output is a set of detected text regions paired with recognized text strings. Notably, the detector and recognizer are loaded from independently trained checkpoints and remain frozen throughout training—only the Bridge and lightweight Adapters are updated, so swapping a detector or recognizer requires training only the Bridge (~102 h vs. ~272 h for full end-to-end retraining).

## Method

The core mechanism is a Bridge module that injects large-receptive-field detection features into a locked recognizer without disrupting its pre-trained behavior. The Bridge applies a zero-initialized convolution to the cropped detection features, passes them through a single Transformer encoder layer, and projects back via a zero-initialized linear layer; the result is added residually to the recognizer backbone output. Because both the convolution and linear layers start at zero, the Bridge contributes nothing at initialization (reducing to the original recognizer output), yet gradients flow immediately, allowing the recognition head to gradually adapt to the detection features. To let the frozen detector and recognizer acquire end-to-end optimization signals, lightweight Adapters (two linear layers with a GELU activation, following Houlsby et al.) are inserted into the Transformer encoder of the detector and decoder of the recognizer, with only the Adapter weights and normalization-layer parameters updated during training. The total trainable parameters increase by only ~4.5 M over the two-step baseline, and the full Bridge+Adapter training completes in 10,000 iterations with a batch size of 8.

## Results

On Total-Text, DG-Bridge Spotter achieves 83.3% (None lexicon) and 88.3% (Full lexicon) end-to-end accuracy with 89.2% detection F-measure, outperforming the previous best ESTextSpotter (80.8%/87.1%) by 2.5% and 1.2% respectively. On CTW1500, the method reaches 69.8% (None) and 83.9% (Full), surpassing ESTextSpotter by 4.9% on the lexicon-free metric. On ICDAR 2015, TG-Bridge Spotter achieves 89.1% (Strong), 84.2% (Weak), and 80.4% (Generic), exceeding ESTextSpotter by 1.6%, 1.2%, and 2.3% respectively. The method also improves over its own two-step baseline (DPText-DETR+DiG at 77.5% None on Total-Text) by 5.8% while retaining full modularity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DG-Bridge Spotter (DPText-DETR + DiG + Bridge + Adapter) outperforms all end-to-end baselines on Total-Text, beating the strongest competitor ESTextSpotter [16] by 2.5% on the None lexicon (83.3% vs. 80.8%) and 1.2% on Full (88.3% vs. 87.1%). On CTW1500, the method achieves a 4.9% margin over ESTextSpotter on the None metric (69.8% vs. 64.9%) while tying on Full (83.9%), and surpasses SPTS v2 [36] by 6.2% on None. On ICDAR 2015, TG-Bridge Spotter (TESTR-det + DiG) leads all methods across Strong/Weak/Generic lexicons, exceeding ESTextSpotter by 1.6%/1.2%/2.3% and DeepSolo [62] by 2.3%/2.3%/3.5%. Compared to its own two-step baseline (DPText-DETR+DiG at 77.5% None), the Bridge adds 5.8% on Total-Text None and 0.7% on Full, confirming that the zero-initialized Bridge plus Adapter effectively mitigates error accumulation without sacrificing modularity. The method also outperforms the two-step baselines TESTR-det+MAERec (78.0% None) and DB+PARSeq (69.1% None) by 5.3% and 14.2% respectively on Total-Text None, demonstrating consistent gains across diverse detector-recognizer combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a third paradigm between the well-established two-step and end-to-end text spotting approaches, drawing on two key ideas from adjacent fields: the Adapter architecture from parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Houlsby et al., ICML 2019) and the zero-initialized residual connection from ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023). Unlike prior end-to-end methods (SwinTextSpotter, ESTextSpotter, DeepSolo) that require joint training of detection and recognition from a shared backbone, Bridging Text Spotting locks independently trained modules and inserts a lightweight trainable Bridge between them. The conceptual departure from prior work is that modularity and end-to-end optimization are treated as compatible rather than mutually exclusive, a framing not explored in the text spotting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: the paper demonstrates that replacing a detector in a Bridging system takes ~102 hours versus ~272 hours for full end-to-end retraining, and that only detection annotations are needed rather than both detection and recognition labels. This directly addresses the dominant reason competitions and production systems (PP-OCR, etc.) still use two-step pipelines. Open questions include whether the Bridge-Adapter pattern generalizes to multi-task systems beyond text spotting, and whether the approach degrades when the frozen modules are weakly trained. The method's reliance on high-quality pre-trained weights for both modules is a practical constraint that limits applicability in low-resource settings.
