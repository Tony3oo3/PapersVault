## Problem

In partial-label learning, each training example carries a candidate label set that includes the true label alongside false positives, creating label ambiguity that degrades model training. Existing identification-based methods rely on simple heuristics to iteratively update label confidences, which fail to simultaneously achieve high precision and high coverage in identifying true labels, leaving significant noise in the pseudo labels used during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242440/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images paired with a candidate label set S_i ⊂ {1,…,k} that is guaranteed to contain the true label, where the set is generated by flipping negative labels with probability q (ranging from 0.01 to 0.5 depending on dataset). At test time, the final prediction is the element-wise average of the softmax outputs from two identical Wide-ResNet-34-10 networks, producing a single class label. The candidate label set size varies per example and grows with q, directly affecting the difficulty of disambiguation.

## Method

CroSel trains two identical WRN-34-10 networks in a cross-supervised fashion: each network selects confident pseudo labels for the other using three criteria applied to a FIFO memory bank of the last t epochs of softmax outputs — the predicted label must lie in the candidate set, must not have flipped in the past t epochs, and must exceed a confidence threshold γ on average. Selected examples receive a standard cross-entropy loss, while all examples (selected and unselected) additionally receive a co-mix consistency regularization loss that crosses weak and strong augmentations to generate soft pseudo labels, then applies MixUp interpolation to produce 2n trainable pairs. The regularization weight λ_d decreases dynamically as the selection ratio grows, smoothly transitioning the training objective from regularization-dominated to supervised-dominated. At inference, the two model outputs are averaged to form the final prediction.

## Results

CroSel achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on all benchmark settings except CIFAR-10 (q=0.1), where CRDPLL edges it by 0.10%. On CIFAR-100 (q=0.1), CroSel reaches 84.07%, surpassing the next-best method PoP (82.39%) by 1.68%. On SVHN (q=0.3), CroSel attains 97.96%, outperforming CRDPLL (97.65%) by 0.31%. The method also selects true labels with over 99% accuracy and 93–99% selection ratio on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 across all noise levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CroSel outperforms all six baselines on 8 out of 9 settings, losing only to CRDPLL on CIFAR-10 (q=0.1) by a marginal 0.10% (97.31% vs. 97.41%). The most significant margin is on CIFAR-100, where CroSel beats PoP (the closest competitor) by 1.21–1.68% across noise levels, and surpasses PiCO by 9–14%. On SVHN, CroSel leads CRDPLL by 0.08–0.31% and PoP by 0.16–0.65%. The gap widens substantially on CIFAR-100, where identification-based methods like PRODEN and LWS drop to 52–72%, while CroSel maintains 84% accuracy. In the fine-grained CIFAR-100 setting (super-class candidates, q=0.5), CroSel achieves 83.34%, beating PoP (82.04%) and CRDPLL (81.53%) by over 1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CroSel builds on the identification-based paradigm in partial-label learning (PLL), where the true label is treated as a latent variable to be disambiguated from a candidate set, but departs from prior methods that rely on simple iterative heuristics (e.g., PRODEN's minimum-loss criterion, PiCO's contrastive disambiguation) by introducing a memory bank that tracks historical softmax predictions over multiple epochs. The cross-selection strategy is directly inspired by the co-teaching framework from noisy-label learning, where two models select clean samples for each other, but is adapted here to select confident pseudo-labels within candidate sets rather than filtering noisy labels. The co-mix regularization term repurposes MixUp and consistency regularization from semi-supervised learning to provide trainable targets for unselected examples, filling a gap that prior PLL methods leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CroSel demonstrates that combining historical prediction tracking with cross-model selection can achieve near-perfect true-label identification (>99% accuracy, >90% ratio) on standard PLL benchmarks, effectively reducing PLL to a supervised problem for most examples. The counter-intuitive finding that moderate noise levels can outperform low-noise settings—attributed to richer MixUp interpolation from larger candidate sets—challenges the assumption that less noise is always better. However, the method is validated only on CIFAR-type and SVHN datasets with WRN-34-10, leaving open questions about scalability to ImageNet-scale data, deeper architectures, and instance-dependent partial-label settings where candidate sets vary per example. The dual-model requirement also doubles training compute, which may limit practical adoption.
