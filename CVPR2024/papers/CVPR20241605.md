## Problem

Understanding how two sets of images differ is essential for auditing datasets, comparing model behaviors, and studying human cognition, yet manually inspecting thousands of images is impractical. No existing vision-language model can reason over large image sets to produce natural language descriptions of their differences, and prior approaches either limit themselves to single image pairs or rely on predefined text banks rather than free-form generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241605/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two image sets D_A and D_B (ranging from 100 to thousands of images each, e.g., 500 per set in ImageNet comparisons) and outputs a ranked list of natural language descriptions that are more true for D_A than D_B. The proposer operates on small random subsets of 20 images per set, while the ranker validates each candidate across all images in both sets. Candidates are scored by AUROC of CLIP image-text similarity and filtered by a t-test at p<0.05 for statistical significance.

## Method

VisDiff is a two-stage proposer-ranker framework. The proposer captions 20 randomly sampled images from each set using BLIP-2, then prompts GPT-4 to generate candidate difference descriptions from the caption pairs; this is repeated over multiple random samples and the union of candidates is collected. The ranker uses CLIP ViT-G/14 to compute cosine similarity between each image embedding and each candidate text embedding, then scores each candidate by the AUROC of these similarities in separating D_A from D_B. The key design insight is that the caption-based proposer (BLIP-2 + GPT-4) outperforms direct image-based (LLaVA, GPT-4V) and feature-based (BLIP-2 embedding subtraction) proposers because LLM reasoning compensates for caption information loss, while the CLIP feature-based ranker outperforms binary VQA rankers due to its continuous scoring and O(M+N) computational efficiency versus O(MN).

## Results

The best VisDiff configuration (BLIP-2+GPT-4 caption-based proposer with CLIP feature-based ranker) achieves Acc@1 of 88%, 75%, and 61% and Acc@5 of 99%, 86%, and 80% on PairedImageSets-Easy/Medium/Hard, respectively. On ImageNet-R/*, it reaches 78% Acc@1 and 96% Acc@5. The GPT-4V image-based proposer with CLIP ranker achieves the highest overall Acc@1 (95% on Easy, 86% on ImageNet-R/*) but underperforms on the Hard subset (57% vs. 61%) and is significantly more expensive to run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six proposer-ranker configurations rather than external methods. The caption-based proposer (BLIP-2+GPT-4) with CLIP feature ranker (the chosen VisDiff) outperforms the feature-based proposer (BLIP-2 embedding subtraction) by a large margin on PairedImageSets (e.g., 61% vs. 12% Acc@1 on Hard), because vector arithmetic fails when neither set has attributes absent in the other. The GPT-4V image-based proposer with CLIP ranker achieves the highest Acc@1 on Easy (95%) and ImageNet-R/* (86%) but drops to 57% on Hard, attributed to visual detail loss when aggregating 40 images into a single grid. The LLaVA-1.5 image-based proposer is weakest on ImageNet-R/* (27% Acc@1) and significantly trails the caption-based proposer on Medium/Hard. Among rankers, the CLIP feature-based ranker consistently outperforms both the LLaVA image-based and Vicuna caption-based rankers, with the advantage most pronounced on Hard (80% vs. 62% and 61% Acc@5), due to its continuous scoring and O(N) computational complexity versus O(MN) for the alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the proposer-ranker framework from D3 and D5 (which describe differences between text distributions using LLMs) to the visual domain, leveraging VLMs like BLIP-2, GPT-4, and CLIP as the underlying components. It extends prior difference captioning work (which operates on single image pairs) to the set level, where the goal is to find descriptions more true for one collection of thousands of images than another. The paper positions itself against GS-CLIP, which applied a similar CLIP-based retrieval approach but was limited to a pre-defined text bank and struggled with natural language generation, and against explainable CV methods (LIME, CAM, SHAP) that interpret model internals rather than describing data-level differences in natural language.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisDiff provides a practical, automated tool for surfacing interpretable differences in datasets and model behaviors at scale, with demonstrated applications spanning dataset auditing (ImageNet vs. ImageNetV2), model comparison (CLIP vs. ResNet), generative model analysis (SDv1 vs. SDv2), and cognitive science (memorability). The VisDiffBench benchmark (187 paired sets with ground-truth differences) fills a gap in evaluation for set-level visual reasoning. Key limitations include heavy reliance on CLIP/GPT/BLIP (inheriting their biases), no guarantee of finding all meaningful differences, and the need for human-in-the-loop interpretation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, 3D, or multimodal sets, and whether the proposer-ranker decomposition is optimal or whether end-to-end set-level reasoning models could eventually replace it.
