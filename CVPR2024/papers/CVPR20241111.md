## Problem

Existing reconstruction-based detectors for diffusion-generated images (e.g., DIRE, SeDID) require multi-step DDIM inversion and re-synthesis, taking over 2 seconds per image and introducing cumulative error uncertainty. Moreover, they treat the reconstruction error as a scalar feature, ignoring its spatial correlation with the raw image's local frequency content, which limits discriminative power and cross-generator generalizability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 256×256 for LaRE extraction and 224×224 for classification) and a text prompt (generic 'a photo' or class-specific 'a photo of CLS') as input, and outputs a binary real/fake score. LaRE is computed in the 32×32×4 latent space of Stable Diffusion V1.5, and the final classification is produced by a CLIP-pretrained ResNet-50 backbone followed by a single FC layer trained with binary cross-entropy loss.

## Method

LaRE² replaces full DDIM inversion with a single-step latent-space denoising loss: the image is encoded to latent x₀, noised to x_t via the closed-form forward process at t=200, and the denoising error is estimated with e=4 Monte Carlo samples, yielding an 8× speedup over DIRE. The key insight is that this reconstruction loss is spatially correlated with local image frequency (higher loss on high-frequency regions), so it serves as a spatially-aligned refinement signal rather than just a scalar. The Error-Guided Feature Refinement (EGRE) module exploits this in two ways: (1) an Error-guided Spatial Refinement module (ESR) injects the spatially-aligned LaRE into multi-head attention as an additive bias on attention scores, re-weighting which spatial locations the global query attends to; and (2) an Error-guided Channel Refinement module (ECR) applies a sigmoid gate derived from the globally-pooled LaRE to modulate channel-wise features. The spatially refined, channel-refined, and original global features are concatenated and passed through an FC layer for the final binary decision.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark (2.68M images, 8 generators), LaRE² achieves an average accuracy of 79.1% and average precision of 99.8%, surpassing the previous best method DIRE (67.2% ACC) by 11.9% ACC and 12.1% AP. The method also achieves 8× faster feature extraction compared to DIRE's multi-step DDIM inversion, reducing per-image cost from over 2 seconds to approximately 0.25 seconds. Per-generator, the largest gains are on SDV1.4 (87.3% vs. 73.7%), SDV1.5 (87.1% vs. 73.7%), and VQDM (84.4% vs. 63.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaRE² outperforms all five baselines across every one of the 8 generators on the GenImage benchmark. The closest competitor is DIRE [40], which also uses reconstruction error but requires full multi-step DDIM inversion; LaRE² improves over DIRE by 11.9% average ACC (79.1% vs. 67.2%) and 12.1% AP, with the largest per-generator gaps on VQDM (+21.0%), SDV1.4 (+13.6%), and SDV1.5 (+13.4%). The GAN-era detectors—CNNSpot [39] (61.7%), Spec [45] (65.1%), F3Net [29] (64.6%), and GramNet [19] (64.7%)—all fall 14–17 percentage points below LaRE², confirming that frequency- and texture-based cues designed for GAN detection transfer poorly to diffusion-generated images. Notably, LaRE² also achieves 8× faster feature extraction than DIRE, making it substantially more practical for real-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DIRE [40], which established reconstruction error as a discriminative feature for diffusion-generated image detection via full multi-step DDIM inversion. The key departure is replacing the expensive full inversion with a single-step denoising loss computed in the VAE latent space (LaRE), and further introducing the EGRE module that uses this error to refine image features spatially and channel-wise rather than treating it as the sole feature. The approach also builds on the broader line of work using pretrained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) as feature extractors for downstream detection tasks, and leverages CLIP-pretrained ResNet50 as the image backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× speedup over DIRE makes reconstruction-based detection practically deployable in real-world pipelines, while the 11.9%/12.1% ACC/AP gain over the prior SoTA on GenImage demonstrates that efficiency and accuracy need not be traded off. A notable open question is the dependence on a specific diffusion model (Stable Diffusion V1.5) for LaRE extraction, which raises questions about robustness if the detector's reference model diverges from the generator. The finding that class-specific prompts further improve LaRE suggests an unexplored direction for incorporating semantic context into reconstruction-based features.
