## Problem

Despite the critical role of on-device image denoising for mobile photography, existing real-world datasets are severely limited in sensor diversity and scene variety: SIDD covers only 5 camera sensors across 10 indoor scenes, and DND provides just 50 image pairs. This scarcity hinders the training of robust, generalizable denoising models, and no prior work simultaneously addresses dataset scale, sensor diversity, and efficient mobile inference in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (converted from RAW Bayer via a minimal ISP pipeline, with no internal denoising applied) and produces a denoised RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. Training uses 256×256 patches with MSE loss, while evaluation covers full-resolution images up to 8MP (4000×2000) on mobile hardware. The MIDD dataset additionally provides 20 noisy frames per scene alongside the mean-based ground truth, enabling burst-denoising variants, and is distributed in both PNG (RGB) and DNG (RAW) formats.

## Method

SplitterNet is a U-Net-based CNN (731K parameters) whose core mechanism is iterative tensor splitting along the channel axis at each encoding step, reducing the effective convolution cost on mobile accelerators, followed by concatenation of the split branches at each decoding step. At the bottleneck, a middle block combines spatial and channel attention to extract the most informative features. The model is trained with Adam (cosine-decay LR from 1e-4 to 7e-6) for 10–15 epochs on a single GPU, jointly optimizing for PSNR, SSIM, and runtime efficiency. It is deployed via TensorFlow Lite without quantization, achieving sub-second inference on 8MP images across all tested mobile GPUs and real-time (>30 FPS) HD processing on Snapdragon 8 Gen 3/2 and Dimensity 9000 NPUs.

## Results

On the MIDD dataset (720×480, Exynos 2200 Mali GPU), SplitterNet achieves PSNR 37.92 dB, SSIM 0.884, and a runtime of 27.7 ms, offering the best performance–runtime trade-off among all compared models. On the independent DPreview test set (294 camera sensors), the MIDD-trained SplitterNet reaches 21.75 dB PSNR, outperforming the SIDD-trained model (21.13 dB) by 0.62 dB and the MAI2021-trained model (20.55 dB) by 1.20 dB. On mobile NPUs (Snapdragon 8 Gen 3), the model processes 8MP images in 257 ms and achieves over 30 FPS at HD resolution, enabling real-time video denoising.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplitterNet outperforms NOAHTCV (the other MAI2021 winner) on both PSNR (37.92 vs. 37.81 dB) and SSIM (0.884 vs. 0.883) while running 2.7 ms faster (27.7 vs. 30.4 ms). Against Megvii (the MAI2021 PSNR winner), SplitterNet trades a small 0.13 dB PSNR loss (37.92 vs. 38.05) for a 2× runtime reduction (27.7 vs. 56.8 ms), yielding a superior performance–runtime trade-off. Compared to ResNet18 and U-Net, SplitterNet achieves higher PSNR (37.92 vs. 37.48/37.87) while being 3.5–6.7× faster. NAFNet, though achieving competitive PSNR (37.27), is too heavy for mobile inference and could not run on the target platform. In cross-dataset generalization (Table 4), the MIDD-trained SplitterNet leads on the DPreview benchmark by 0.62 dB over SIDD-trained and 1.20 dB over MAI2021-trained, confirming that dataset diversity drives robustness beyond what any single prior dataset provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

The SplitterNet architecture is a U-Net variant that introduces tensor splitting along the channel axis to reduce convolutional cost on mobile accelerators, a technique also explored in the MAI2021 challenge. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior mobile denoising work (SIDD, DND, MAI2021) primarily through its dataset: where SIDD covers only 5 sensors and 10 static indoor scenes, and DND offers just 50 image pairs, MIDD scales to 20 sensors, 1000 scenes, and 400K+ noisy/ground-truth pairs spanning diverse lighting and outdoor conditions. The model itself is positioned against recent efficient CNNs (NAFNet, KBNet, NOAHTCV, Megvii) that rely on attention mechanisms, whereas SplitterNet achieves competitive performance with a simpler split-and-concatenate design optimized for NPU/GPU deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIDD dataset and DPreview test set (294 cameras) fill a critical gap in real-world mobile denoising benchmarks, and the cross-dataset experiments showing MIDD-trained models generalize best (21.75 vs. 21.13 PSNR on DPreview) provide concrete evidence that sensor and scene diversity in training data matters more than dataset size alone. The practical deployment results—under 1 second for 8MP on all tested mobile GPUs and real-time HD on NPUs—make this a directly usable baseline for on-device photo processing pipelines. Open questions include whether the 20-burst averaging ground truth introduces subtle biases, whether the model's generalization holds for sensors with fundamentally different noise characteristics (e.g., stacked sensors, computational photography pipelines), and whether the dataset's 2015–2021 sensor range adequately covers next-generation hardware.
