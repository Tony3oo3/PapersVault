## Problem

Pre-training for visual autonomous driving remains largely unexplored because it requires features that simultaneously encode semantics, 3D geometry, and temporal dynamics for joint perception, prediction, and planning. Existing approaches either model only semantics (general vision pre-training), lack temporal modeling (depth estimation or masked scene rendering from Image-LiDAR pairs), or depend on expensive 3D annotations (supervised 3D detection or occupancy), making them insufficient for scalable end-to-end driving systems.

## Input / Output

ViDAR consumes 5 frames of historical multi-view images (6 cameras from nuScenes) and ego-motion conditions (next-frame coordinates and heading) to produce future point clouds at arbitrary 0.5-second intervals up to 3 seconds ahead. Internally, the model generates a 200×200 BEV feature map covering a 102.4m×102.4m area and a 200×200×16 occupancy volume spanning [-5m, 3m] in height; point clouds are extracted by casting rays from the sensor origin and locating the maximum-occupancy waypoint along each ray. The pre-training signal is the LiDAR point cloud at the target future timestamp, requiring no 3D box or occupancy annotations.

## Method

ViDAR comprises a History Encoder (BEVFormer), a novel Latent Rendering operator, and an auto-regressive Future Decoder. The Latent Rendering operator is the key architectural contribution: it computes ray-wise features via a feature expectation function (analogous to the distance expectation in differentiable ray-casting) and then differentiates each BEV grid's feature by weighting it with its conditional probability, which suppresses the 'ray-shaped feature' degeneracy where grids along the same ray collapse to similar values. Multi-group (16 parallel) Latent Rendering further diversifies geometric features across channels. The Future Decoder is a 6-layer transformer using Deformable Self-Attention, a Temporal Cross-Attention with ego-motion-aligned reference coordinates to handle coordinate-system shifts between frames, and FFN layers, iteratively predicting the next BEV feature from the previous one conditioned on ego-motion. Training uses a ray-wise cross-entropy loss on the occupancy volume rather than L1 depth loss, and the entire pipeline is pre-trained on Image-LiDAR sequences before transfer to downstream BEV encoders.

## Results

On nuScenes, ViDAR pre-training improves UniAD across all end-to-end autonomous driving tasks simultaneously: 3D detection NDS from 49.36% to 52.57% (+3.21%), tracking AMOTA from 38.3% to 42.0% (+3.7%), motion forecasting minADE from 0.75m to 0.67m (~10% reduction), and planning average collision rate from 0.27% to 0.23% (~15% reduction). For point cloud forecasting, ViDAR using only visual inputs outperforms the LiDAR-based 4D-Occ by ~33% Chamfer Distance reduction at 1s horizon. On 3D detection with BEVFormer-RN101, ViDAR-cls achieves 42.6% mAP / 51.8% NDS, surpassing 3D-detection supervised pre-training (41.5% mAP / 51.7% NDS) using only Image-LiDAR sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViDAR outperforms the LiDAR-based 4D-Occ [36] on point cloud forecasting by ~33% Chamfer Distance reduction at 1s and ~18% at 3s, despite using only camera inputs. Against the end-to-end system UniAD [27], ViDAR pre-training yields consistent gains on every sub-task: +3.21% NDS on detection, +3.7% AMOTA on tracking, ~10% minADE reduction on motion forecasting, and ~15% collision rate reduction on planning. In tracking, ViDAR-equipped UniAD (AMOTA 42.0%) surpasses all prior visual trackers including DQTrack-PETRv2 (44.6% with stereo), MUTR3D (29.4%), and QD3DT (24.2%). For motion forecasting, ViDAR (minADE 0.67m) substantially outperforms PnPNet (1.15m) and ViP3D (2.05m). In planning, ViDAR (Avg.Col 0.23%, Avg.L2 0.91m) beats VAD (0.41%, 1.05m), ST-P3 (0.71%, 2.11m), and LiDAR-based methods FF (0.43%) and EO (0.33%). For future occupancy prediction, ViDAR (VPQ-n 57.3, VPQ-f 36.4) outperforms BEVerse (54.3, 36.1), FIERY (50.2, 29.9), and StretchBEV (46.0, 29.0) in both near and far regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViDAR builds on BEVFormer's spatiotemporal transformer architecture as its history encoder and adapts the differentiable ray-casting and occupancy-to-point-cloud conversion mechanism from 4D-Occ (Khurana et al., CVPR 2023). However, it departs from prior autonomous driving pre-training work (depth estimation, masked scene rendering, supervised 3D detection/occupancy) by introducing visual point cloud forecasting as a unified pre-text task that simultaneously supervises semantics, multi-view 3D geometry, and temporal dynamics. The key architectural departure is the Latent Rendering operator, which replaces direct differentiable ray-casting to avoid the 'ray-shaped features' problem that degrades downstream transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical, annotation-free pre-training recipe for end-to-end visual autonomous driving that reduces reliance on expensive 3D box and occupancy labels, with demonstrated gains across perception, prediction, and planning simultaneously. The main limitation is that all experiments are confined to nuScenes with BEVFormer-family encoders, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger datasets, diverse sensor configurations, or non-BEV architectures. The authors explicitly flag scaling up to a foundation visual autonomous driving model as future work, and the current data scale remains modest for a pre-training claim.
