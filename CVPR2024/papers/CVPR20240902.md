## Problem

Supervised image denoising networks trained on real noise datasets (e.g., SIDD) overfit to the specific noise distributions present in training data and exhibit significant performance degradation on out-of-distribution real noise from different camera sensors, shooting environments, or ISP pipelines. This generalization gap is a relatively unexplored issue in the denoising literature, where most prior work focuses on in-distribution performance rather than robustness to unseen noise types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240902/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy RGB image (256×256 patches during training; 512×512 or 1024×1024 during evaluation) and produces a denoised RGB image of the same size. AFM is strictly a training-time augmentation: at inference, the mask generation network is entirely omitted and the denoising network operates standalone with no additional memory or computational cost. Training requires paired noisy-clean images from SIDD, while evaluation spans five OOD benchmarks (Poly, CC, HighISO, iPhone, Huawei).

## Method

AFM constructs adversarial training samples by mixing a noisy image I and its denoised prediction Î in the frequency domain via FFT, guided by a mask m produced by a lightweight adversarial mask generation network. The mask is trained to maximize the denoising error on the resulting I_hard while minimizing it on the complementary I_easy, ensuring the generated noise is both challenging and realistic (staying on the real-noise manifold as an interpolation of I and Î). Two mask variants are provided: AFM-E uses a U-Net to generate element-wise masks, while AFM-B uses a compact network to assign a single mixup value per circular frequency band. The denoising network is jointly trained on both the original and the adversarially mixed image, with the mask generator updated in alternating steps. The frequency-domain formulation is critical: it preserves real noise statistics, acts globally on the image to avoid spurious spatial artifacts, and enables the adversarial mask to target specific frequency regions where the denoiser is weakest.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution (OOD) average across five real-noise benchmarks (Poly, CC, HighISO, iPhone, Huawei), DnCNN trained with AFM-B achieves 38.56 dB PSNR and 0.9779 SSIM, outperforming the strongest SOTA supervised denoiser Restormer (38.17 dB / 0.9753) by +0.39 dB PSNR while using only 0.74M parameters vs. 26.1M. Across four architectures (DnCNN, CBDNet, MPRNet, NAFNet), AFM consistently improves OOD PSNR by +0.33 to +1.00 dB over the same architecture trained without AFM, with the largest gain on NAFNet (+1.00 dB). In-distribution (SIDD) performance is largely preserved, with at most a 0.27 dB PSNR drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SOTA supervised denoisers, DnCNN+AFM-B outperforms Restormer by +0.39 dB OOD PSNR (38.56 vs. 38.17), Uformer by +0.50 dB, and MIRNet-v2 by +0.52 dB, while using 35–70× fewer parameters and 4–24× fewer MACs. Against self-supervised methods, the gap is even larger: DnCNN+AFM-B exceeds R2R by +1.14 dB, AP-BSN by +1.90 dB, and CVF-SID by +5.46 dB in OOD PSNR. Among the four architecture baselines in Table 1, AFM improves OOD PSNR for DnCNN (+0.75 dB), CBDNet (+0.33 dB), MPRNet (+0.54 dB), and NAFNet (+1.00 dB), confirming the framework is model-agnostic. In the generalization-technique comparison (Table 3), AFM-B (38.56 dB) beats Random Frequency Mixup (38.17 dB), CutMix (37.96 dB), ASM-E (37.92 dB), Adversarial Training/PGD (37.18 dB), Input Mask (37.28 dB), and Dropout (37.57 dB), demonstrating that adversarial frequency-domain mixup is both necessary and superior to spatial-domain or random alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the mixup data augmentation concept by moving it from the spatial to the frequency domain, combined with an adversarial mask generation objective. It departs from prior generalization efforts in denoising—bias removal [15] and input/attention masking [3]—by instead constructing hard training examples that preserve the manifold of real noise rather than applying generic regularization. The adversarial training component is conceptually related to PGD-based adversarial training, but the key distinction is that AFM generates realistic noise variations (interpolations of real noisy and denoised images in frequency space) rather than synthetic perturbations, which the paper shows is critical for OOD robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights that even state-of-the-art denoisers like Restormer and Uformer suffer significant OOD degradation on real noise from different cameras and ISP pipelines, a gap that has been largely overlooked in favor of in-distribution benchmark improvements. AFM is model-agnostic and adds zero inference cost, making it a practical drop-in training modification for any supervised denoising pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other restoration tasks (super-resolution, inpainting), whether the adversarial component is strictly necessary given that random frequency mixup also yields gains, and whether the framework can be adapted to self-supervised or unsupervised settings where paired clean-noisy data is unavailable.
