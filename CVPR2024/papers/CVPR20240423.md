## Problem

Existing 3D human motion prediction models are trained offline with frozen parameters, making them inherently suboptimal for real-world streaming data where human motions exhibit concept drift over time. No prior work addresses online meta adaptation along the time direction, where recent predictive mistakes are explicitly exploited as the driving force for parameter adjustment to suit ever-changing motion contexts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240423/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observed 3D body joint sequence of N frames (each frame being J×3 coordinates, e.g., 22 joints on Human3.6M) and the prediction error from the immediately preceding sub-task, producing a predicted future sequence of T frames. The model operates in a streaming fashion where each sub-task (predicting T frames from N frames) is executed sequentially along time, and only the lightweight MoAdapter parameters are updated per sub-task while the generic backbone remains frozen. Evaluation uses MPJPE averaged over all sub-tasks at each timestamp, with prediction horizons of 80–400 ms on Human3.6M and up to 400 ms on 3DPW.

## Method

MoML introduces MoAdapter blocks (FC-MoAdapter with a GELU bottleneck, or GC-MoAdapter with a learnable graph convolution) inserted at the end of each network block as the sole adaptive parameters θ, while the generic backbone φ is shared across all contexts. Training follows a bilevel MAML-inspired scheme: the inner loop performs a few gradient steps on θ using a temporary prediction loss computed on the previous sub-task S_{s-1}, and the outer loop meta-updates φ across multiple contexts to find a 'smart' initialization that is close to every task-specific configuration. At inference, only θ is updated per sub-task via the temporary loss, keeping φ fixed. Fast-MoML replaces the gradient-based inner loop with a closed-form ridge regression solution on the last-layer weight matrix, reducing adaptation to a single matrix multiplication for real-time deployment.

## Results

On Human3.6M, MoML (both FC and GC variants) consistently reduces MPJPE across all three modified baselines (LTD, SPGSN, MotionMixer) at all prediction horizons (80–400 ms). At the 400 ms horizon, SPGSN-GC achieves the best overall performance, e.g., 39.8 mm on walking, 36.4 mm on eating, and 63.9 mm on discussion, compared to 41.5, 37.9, and 67.1 mm for the unmodified SPGSN. On CMU-Mocap (400 ms average), SPGSN-GC drops from 37.0 to 35.2 mm, and on 3DPW (400 ms average) from 64.5 to 61.6 mm. The paper notes that gains are minimal or absent for near-static activities (sitting, waiting) where the motion context barely changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper modifies three offline baselines—LTD (GCN+DCT), SPGSN (graph scattering), and MotionMixer (MLP-Mixer)—by inserting MoAdapters, and compares each against its unmodified version plus four unmodified reference models (Res.sup, DMGNN, MSR). MoML wins on virtually every activity–timestamp combination for all three modified baselines; for example, at 400 ms on walking, LTD drops from 46.1 to 42.2 mm (FC) / 42.7 mm (GC), SPGSN from 41.5 to 40.0 / 39.8 mm, and MotionMixer from 42.4 to 40.1 / 41.0 mm. The GC variant tends to edge out FC on spatially structured motions (walking, eating) while FC is slightly better on more dynamic sequences (discussion, directions). On CMU-Mocap and 3DPW, the same pattern holds: SPGSN-GC achieves the lowest 400 ms average (35.2 mm on CMU, 61.6 mm on 3DPW) versus 37.0 and 64.5 mm for the unmodified SPGSN. The one consistent exception is near-static activities (sitting, waiting), where adaptation provides little or no benefit because the motion context is essentially unchanged. Compared to vanilla MAML applied to the full network, MoML's selective-parameter adaptation yields comparable or better accuracy while being substantially faster at inference, since only the lightweight MoAdapter parameters are updated per sub-task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the bilevel optimization framework of MAML (Finn et al., 2017) and the lightweight adapter architecture from NLP parameter-efficient transfer learning (Houlsby et al., 2019; He et al., 2022) into the context of 3D human motion prediction, but departs from both in key ways: rather than adapting to novel tasks or domains, it adapts along the temporal axis of a single streaming sequence to handle concept drift. The Fast-MoML variant draws on differentiable closed-form solvers (Bertinetto et al., 2019) to replace gradient steps with a ridge-regression solution. Unlike prior meta-learning work in motion prediction that targets few-shot learning over unseen action categories, this paper addresses the orthogonal problem of temporal context shift within a continuous motion stream, and unlike online adaptation work in video segmentation or depth estimation that tackles inter-sequence domain shift, it focuses on intra-sequence drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic design (MoAdapter can be plugged into LTD, SPGSN, MotionMixer, etc.) makes this a practical recipe for bringing any existing offline predictor into a streaming deployment, which is a meaningful step toward real-world applications in robotics and autonomous driving. However, the improvements are modest (typically 1–3 mm MPJPE reduction), and the paper acknowledges failures on static motions where adaptation is unnecessary. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer prediction horizons, how it performs under truly non-stationary real-world conditions beyond the curated Human3.6M/CMU/3DPW splits, and whether the meta-learned initialization quality degrades as the motion distribution shifts more dramatically over time.
