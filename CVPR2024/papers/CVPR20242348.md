## Problem

While DETR and its variants have advanced object detection, the relationship between DETR's encoder-decoder architecture and the classical Faster R-CNN RPN-refiner paradigm has not been systematically dissected, leaving unclear which specific components drive Deformable DETR's performance gains over Faster R-CNN. Additionally, existing DETR detectors employ only a single proposal refiner (e.g., deformable attention), leaving unexplored whether multiple complementary refinement strategies can be combined for further gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242348/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (COCO format) and produces end-to-end bounding boxes with class labels via Hungarian matching, requiring no NMS post-processing. The HPR module is a plug-in decoder stage that operates on 300–900 object queries (point features from the RPN) and multi-scale feature maps from the neck network, and can be appended to any existing DETR decoder without modifying the backbone, neck, or RPN. Training uses a 12- to 36-epoch schedule with AdamW at lr 1e-4.

## Method

The paper first performs a step-by-step transformation of Faster R-CNN into Deformable DETR (Table 1), identifying that the performance gap is primarily attributable to the proposal refiner (deformable attention replacing RoI Align) and the neck network (deformable encoder replacing FPN), and that RoI Align is incompatible with Hungarian matching because it extracts excessive non-essential information from sharpened feature maps. Building on this, the Hybrid Proposal Refiner (HPR) designates one regional refiner (deformable attention, dynamic convolution, or regional cross attention) as the primary refiner and the other two as auxiliary refiners; each auxiliary refiner's self-attention and FFN intermediate features are injected into the primary refiner's corresponding layers via learnable weighted vector addition. Six HPR stages are stacked by default, each supervised by an independent detection loss (primary weight 1.0, auxiliary weights 0.5). A complementary data re-augmentation strategy duplicates normally augmented images and applies stronger augmentations (color jitter, geometric transforms) to the copies, forming a mixed training batch that synergizes with HPR.

## Results

With a ResNet-50 backbone and 36-epoch training, DDQ equipped with HPR and data re-augmentation achieves 54.9 AP on the COCO val set, surpassing the prior best ResNet-50 DETR (DDQ at 52.0 AP with 24 epochs) by +2.9 AP. With a Swin-L (IN-22K) backbone, AlignDETR with HPR and data re-augmentation reaches 60.0 AP at 36 epochs, and DDQ with HPR reaches 59.3 AP at 24 epochs. HPR yields consistent gains of +1.1 to +3.7 AP when plugged into existing DETR detectors (Deformable DETR, DINO, Align DETR, DDQ) under matched training budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The headline result (DDQ + HPR†, 54.9 AP at 36 epochs) outperforms DDQ (52.0 AP, 24 epochs) by +2.9 AP, DINO (51.2 AP, 36 epochs) by +3.7 AP, and DETA (51.6 AP, 24 epochs) by +3.3 AP on the COCO val set with ResNet-50. At matched 24-epoch budgets, DDQ + HPR† (54.2 AP) beats DDQ (52.0 AP) by +2.2 AP and Align DETR (51.3 AP) by +2.9 AP. HPR also improves Deformable DETR from 46.9 AP (50 epochs) to 51.9 AP (24 epochs), a +5.0 AP gain with fewer training epochs. With a Swin-L backbone, AlignDETR + HPR† reaches 60.0 AP (36 epochs), surpassing DINO (58.5 AP, 36 epochs) and DDQ (58.7 AP, 36 epochs) by +1.5 and +1.3 AP respectively. The paper also demonstrates that HPR is a drop-in module: it improves every tested DETR variant (Conditional DETR, DAB DETR, Deformable DETR, DINO, Align DETR, DDQ) by +1.1 to +3.7 AP without architectural changes to the backbone or neck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the DETR encoder-decoder architecture as a refined version of Faster R-CNN's RPN-refiner paradigm, then systematically transforms Faster R-CNN into Deformable DETR to isolate which components (deformable encoder, deformable attention, Hungarian matching, multi-stage refinement) drive the performance gap. The proposed Hybrid Proposal Refiner (HPR) synthesizes three existing regional refinement mechanisms—deformable attention (from Deformable DETR), dynamic convolution (from Sparse R-CNN), and regional cross attention (from DETR)—into a single module where one refiner acts as primary and the others inject auxiliary features. The data re-augmentation strategy extends batch augmentation by copying weakly augmented images and applying stronger augmentations to the copies, a distinction the authors explicitly draw from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HPR module is a practical, drop-in improvement for a wide range of DETR variants (Conditional DETR, DINO, DDQ, Align DETR, etc.), consistently adding 1.5–10.1 AP without architectural redesign, which makes it immediately useful for practitioners tuning existing detectors. The diagnostic finding that Hungarian matching sharpens feature activations and thereby degrades RoI Align (causing an 11.3 AP drop) provides a concrete explanation for why naive hybridization of two-stage and end-to-end components fails, guiding future architecture design. However, the conceptual reframing, while illuminating, does not yield a fundamentally new detection paradigm, and the absolute gains over the strongest baselines (e.g., DDQ, DINO) remain modest, leaving open whether further hybridization of refiners or alternative matching strategies could close the remaining gap to cascade-based detectors.
