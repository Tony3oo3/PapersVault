## Problem

Existing few-shot font generation methods based on GANs simultaneously generate complicated glyph structures and fine-grained style details in a single pass, which often produces broken structures, inconsistent styles, and artifacts. This paper observes that expert font designers follow a multi-stage workflow—building a structural template, transferring style from a source font, then refining local details—and asks whether this process can be formalized within a diffusion model to yield higher-quality results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242618/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source character image (providing the glyph structure to render) and 8 reference font images (providing the target style) as input, and produces a 128×128×3 RGB font image in the target style. All processing occurs in the VQ-VAE latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, with 1000 total diffusion steps. At inference, the structure construction stage (which would normally require iterative denoising from pure noise) is collapsed into a single forward noising step because the source image is directly available, reducing latency while preserving structural fidelity.

## Method

The key mechanism modifies the DDPM forward process in latent space by inserting a font transfer interval (t₁, t₂] where the target-font latent z₀ᵍ is gradually replaced by the source-character latent z₀ᶜ, while the noise sub-space remains unchanged. This yields three natural stages in the reverse process: structure construction (T→t₂, generating global glyph structure from the source), font transfer (t₂→t₁, transforming source font into target font under style conditioning), and font refinement (t₁→0, enhancing local details and visual appearance). Because the prediction network behaves differently in the transfer and refinement stages, the authors employ a dual-network architecture with two separate U-Net predictors (one per stage), each with its own style and content encoders. The entire framework is built on Stable Diffusion's VQ-VAE and U-Net backbone, trained with a simplified L2 loss on the predicted clean latent, and the optimal stage boundaries are t₁=200 and t₂=800 out of T=1000 steps.

## Results

On the UFSC (Unseen Fonts, Seen Contents) setting, MSD-Font achieves the best performance across all four metrics: RMSE 0.2439, PSNR 12.61, SSIM 0.7110, and LPIPS 0.1348. On the more challenging UFUC (Unseen Fonts, Unseen Contents) setting, MSD-Font again leads with RMSE 0.2475, PSNR 12.45, SSIM 0.7189, and LPIPS 0.1527. The closest competitor is FsFont on UFSC (RMSE 0.2443, PSNR 12.56) and NTF on UFUC (RMSE 0.2574, PSNR 12.11), with MSD-Font improving over NTF by 0.0099 RMSE and 0.34 PSNR on UFUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSD-Font outperforms all six baselines on every metric in both evaluation settings (UFSC and UFUC). On UFSC, the closest competitor is FsFont (RMSE 0.2443 vs. 0.2439; PSNR 12.56 vs. 12.61), with MSD-Font achieving a notable LPIPS advantage (0.1348 vs. 0.1468), indicating better perceptual quality. On UFUC, MSD-Font beats NTF (the second-best) by 0.0099 RMSE, 0.34 PSNR, and 0.0026 LPIPS, while also surpassing FsFont by 0.0034 RMSE and 0.16 PSNR. Component-based methods (LF-Font, MX-Font) and shape-deformation methods (DG-Font) trail significantly, with LF-Font showing the worst LPIPS (0.2787 on UFSC, 0.2564 on UFUC), reflecting broken structures and inconsistent styles. Diff-Font, a one-shot diffusion baseline, underperforms MSD-Font by a wide margin (RMSE 0.2811 vs. 0.2439 on UFSC), highlighting the benefit of the multi-stage transfer process over single-stage denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant GAN-based style-content disentanglement paradigm in few-shot font generation (LF-Font, DG-Font, MX-Font, FsFont) by reformulating the task within a latent diffusion framework. It builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models (Stable Diffusion) and DDPM, but introduces a key architectural departure: the font transfer process is embedded into the diffusion forward process at a predefined time interval, splitting the reverse denoising into three functionally distinct stages (structure construction, font transfer, refinement). The closest prior diffusion-based work, Diff-Font, performs one-shot generation without this multi-stage transfer decomposition, while NTF models font generation as a continuous transformation but within a GAN framework rather than diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-stage decomposition offers a principled way to address the long-standing failure modes of FFG (broken structures, inconsistent styles, artifacts) by assigning each to a dedicated generative stage, which could inspire similar staged decompositions in other image-to-image translation tasks. However, the practical impact is tempered by the increased inference time and model size inherent to diffusion-based generation compared to GAN baselines, and the performance gains over strong baselines like FsFont and NTF, while consistent, are moderate. An open question is whether the specific time-interval split (t₁=200, t₂=800) generalizes across different font scripts or resolution scales, and whether the dual-network design is truly necessary or whether a single well-regularized network could match it.
