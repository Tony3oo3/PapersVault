## Problem

Existing text-to-video generators naively repurpose image-generation U-Nets and diffusion frameworks designed for 64×64 px images, which ignores the spatial and temporal redundancy inherent in video. This leads to reduced motion fidelity, prohibitive computational cost (a full U-Net pass per frame), and limited scalability, ultimately producing dynamic images or motion artifacts rather than coherent videos with vivid motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241384/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded by a T5-11B text encoder) and Gaussian noise as input, and produces 16-frame videos at 512×288 px (16:9) or 288×288 px (square) at 24 fps. Generation uses a two-stage cascade: a first-stage model denoises at 36×64 px focusing on motion and scene structure, then a second-stage model upsamples to full resolution focusing on high-frequency details. During training, images are treated as infinite-frame-rate videos to unify the diffusion process across modalities.

## Method

The paper rewrites the EDM diffusion framework with an input scaling factor σ_in = s√T that preserves the optimal signal-to-noise ratio at the base resolution when generating high-resolution multi-frame videos, avoiding a train-inference mismatch caused by inter-frame redundancy. It replaces U-Nets with a scaled FIT (Far-reaching Interleaved Transformer) architecture that compresses the video into a small set of learnable latent tokens, on which joint spatiotemporal self-attention is performed, then writes results back to spatial-only patch tokens. This compressed representation enables global spatiotemporal modeling in a single pass, yielding 3.31× faster training and 4.49× faster inference than U-Nets while scaling to 3.9B parameters. The model is trained with the LAMB optimizer at batch size 2048, using classifier-free guidance, dynamic thresholding, and oscillating guidance at inference to improve text-video alignment and sample quality.

## Results

On UCF-101, Snap Video (512×288 px) achieves the best FVD of 200.2 and FID of 28.1, substantially outperforming the next-best PYoCo (FVD 355.2) and Make-A-Video (FVD 367.2). On MSR-VTT, Snap Video (288×288 px) attains the lowest CLIP-FID of 8.48 and FVD of 110.4, beating PYoCo (CLIP-FID 9.73) and Make-A-Video (CLIP-FID 13.17). In user studies against Gen-2, PikaLab, and Floor33, Snap Video is preferred for video-text alignment (81–81%), motion quantity (88–96%), and motion quality (71–79%), while trailing Floor33 on photorealism (76.3% vs. 44.3% for Gen-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Snap Video decisively outperforms all U-Net-based video diffusion baselines on UCF-101 FVD, reducing it from 355.2 (PYoCo) and 367.2 (Make-A-Video) to 200.2 at 512×288 resolution, and also achieves the best FID (28.1) among all methods reporting that metric. On MSR-VTT, Snap Video (288×288) achieves the lowest CLIP-FID (8.48) and FVD (110.4), surpassing PYoCo (CLIP-FID 9.73) and Make-A-Video (CLIP-FID 13.17), though it trails Make-A-Video on CLIPSIM (0.2793 vs. 0.3049), which the authors attribute to using T5 rather than CLIP text embeddings. In the user study, Snap Video is preferred over Gen-2 on video-text alignment (81%), motion quantity (96%), and motion quality (79%), but Gen-2 retains an edge on photorealism (44.3% preference for Snap Video). Against PikaLab and Floor33, Snap Video wins on all four user-study dimensions, with the largest margins on motion quantity (89% and 88% respectively). The consistent FVD advantage across both benchmarks is attributed to the joint spatiotemporal modeling on the compressed FIT representation, which the authors argue avoids the motion artifacts and 'dynamic image' failures seen in separable spatial-temporal U-Net architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant U-Net-based paradigm for text-to-video generation (Video LDM, Make-A-Video, PYoCo, Imagen Video) by replacing U-Nets with scaled FIT transformers that operate on a learnable compressed video representation, enabling joint spatiotemporal modeling rather than separable spatial-then-temporal computation. It also rewrites the EDM diffusion framework to handle the signal-to-noise ratio shift introduced by high-resolution video inputs via an input scaling factor σ_in, and addresses the image-video modality mismatch in joint training by treating images as infinite-frame-rate videos. Conceptually, the paper positions video as a first-class modality rather than a sequence of images, drawing an explicit analogy to video compression to motivate the compressed-representation approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that transformer-based video generators can be scaled to billions of parameters with practical training and inference costs (3.31× faster training, 4.49× faster inference than comparable U-Nets), opening a path to more scalable video generation architectures. The user study showing 96% preference over Gen-2 for motion quantity highlights that prior U-Net-based methods often produce 'dynamic images' rather than genuinely dynamic video, a limitation the joint spatiotemporal modeling directly addresses. Open questions include whether the compressed-representation approach generalizes to longer video sequences, whether the two-stage cascade can be replaced by end-to-end generation, and how the method performs on more diverse or longer-horizon motion beyond the 16-frame, 24fps setting evaluated here.
