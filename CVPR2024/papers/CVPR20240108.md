## Problem

Existing physical adversarial attacks against monocular depth estimation (MDE) models rely on 2D adversarial patches that only affect a small, localized region of the target vehicle and degrade significantly under viewpoint changes and adverse weather. No prior work has demonstrated a full-surface 3D camouflage attack that can make an entire vehicle vanish from MDE predictions across diverse viewing angles, weather conditions, and vehicle types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240108/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D vehicle mesh (e.g., car, bus, pedestrian), a frozen target MDE model, background scene images sampled from CARLA at 8400 camera poses (0–360° azimuth, 3–15 m distance), and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs a 128×128 adversarial texture seed that, after texture conversion, becomes a 512×512 world-aligned camouflage texture independent of the vehicle's original UV map. The seed is constrained to a 10-color palette for printability and is designed to be directly printable and pasteable onto physical vehicle surfaces.

## Method

3D²Fool optimizes a small 2D texture seed via backpropagation through a differentiable renderer, using two novel modules. The Texture Conversion (TC) module applies random geometric transforms (±90° rotation, flips), tiles the seed at a magnification factor τ=6, and randomly clips to 512×512, producing an object-agnostic texture that can be world-aligned onto any vehicle mesh without UV-map dependence. The Physical Augmentation (PA) module composites the rendered vehicle into diverse CARLA backgrounds and injects weather effects—polynomial-based exposure/shadow masks, depth-guided Gaussian rain and fog noise—followed by EoT saturation/contrast/brightness jitter to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap. The total loss combines a vehicle-vanishing adversarial term (MSE of inverse predicted depth to zero over the full vehicle mask), a Total Variation smoothness penalty on the seed, and a Non-Printability Score (NPS) loss restricting the seed to 10 randomly selected colors, weighted as L_total = L_a + 0.1·L_st + 5·L_nps.

## Results

On CARLA simulation, 3D²Fool achieves a mean depth estimation error (E_d) of 12.75 m and affected-region ratio (R_a) of 49.6% on Monodepth2, roughly 1.85× the best 2D-patch baseline (APARATE: 6.88 m, 26.5%). The advantage is consistent across all four MDE models, with E_d of 10.31 m (DepthHints), 6.78 m (ManyDepth), and 2.24 m (RobustDepth). In the physical world, a printed 3D texture on a 1:24 Tesla Model Y yields E_d of 10.21 m (outdoor) and 10.67 m (indoor) on Monodepth2, confirming the simulation-to-reality transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D²Fool outperforms all four 2D-patch baselines on every MDE model and every metric. The strongest baseline, APARATE [16], achieves E_d of 6.88 m on Monodepth2, while 3D²Fool reaches 12.75 m — an 85% relative improvement in depth error and nearly double the affected-region ratio (49.6% vs. 26.5%). APA [42] and SPOO [8] perform comparably to each other (E_d ≈ 5.6–6.1 m on Monodepth2) but degrade sharply on ManyDepth and RobustDepth, where 3D²Fool still maintains E_d above 6 m and 2 m respectively. SAAM [17] is the weakest baseline, barely exceeding the random-texture baseline on RobustDepth (0.31 m vs. 0.24 m). The gap widens on the most robust model (RobustDepth), where 3D²Fool achieves 2.24 m versus 0.95 m for APARATE, suggesting that full-surface 3D texture coverage and weather-aware augmentation provide a structural advantage that 2D patches cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D camouflage attack paradigm established for object detection (DTA, DAS, FCA, CAMOU) to the monocular depth estimation domain for the first time, where all prior physical attacks (SPOO, APARATE, SAAM, APA) relied on small 2D adversarial patches. The Texture Conversion module is inspired by DTA's repeated-texture projection but replaces the 3D rotation step with a differentiable renderer to avoid surface distortion, while the Physical Augmentation module goes beyond standard EoT by incorporating weather-specific perturbations (rain, fog, exposure) drawn from PBR-inspired rendering. The loss design combines a vehicle-vanishing MSE on the full object mask with Total Variation smoothness and a Non-Printability Score constraint, building on components from prior physical-attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single optimized 3D texture can make a vehicle vanish from MDE predictions across viewpoints, weather conditions, and object types (cars, buses, pedestrians), this work highlights a concrete safety gap in camera-only autonomous driving stacks that rely on monocular depth. The physical-world validation (10+ meter depth error on a printed 1:24 Tesla Model Y) makes the threat tangible. Open questions remain around black-box transferability, complex vehicle geometries where texture coverage is limited at certain angles, and whether the foggy-weather 'positive effect' observed here generalizes beyond Monodepth2's known weather sensitivity.
