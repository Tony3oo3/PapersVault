## Problem

Consistency learning for semi-supervised medical image segmentation typically relies on a single perturbation type (input, feature, or network), which limits the diversity of learned decision boundaries. Directly combining multiple perturbations, however, makes the consistency process uncontrollable and degrades performance. The paper addresses how to safely exploit the complementary benefits of mixed perturbations without losing control over the consistency signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242361/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D medical image slices (224×224 for Cross Teaching, 256×256 for BCP) from both a small labeled set and a larger unlabeled set. Each image is split into K=16 non-overlapping patches (k×k) for the displacement operations. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask; the method additionally produces two displaced composite images per input (one per direction) that are fed back through the networks for cross-supervision, and for labeled data the ground-truth labels undergo the identical patch displacement to supervise the new samples.

## Method

ABD introduces two complementary patch-displacement modules. ABD-R (for unlabeled data) computes per-patch confidence scores and output-distribution vectors from the predictions of weakly and strongly augmented images, then replaces the lowest-confidence patch in each image with the most semantically similar (minimum KL divergence) highest-confidence patch from the other image, producing two new samples that suppress unreliable regions while retaining complementary semantics. ABD-I (for labeled data) inverts this logic: the highest-confidence patch is replaced by the lowest-confidence patch from the counterpart image, and the label is displaced identically, forcing the model to learn from regions it previously found uncontrollable. The displaced samples are supervised via cross-entropy + Dice losses (labeled) or Dice cross-supervision between the two networks (unlabeled), with a Gaussian warmup schedule on the semi-supervised weight. The entire module is plug-and-play and was validated on top of both Cross Teaching (UNet + SwinUNet) and BCP (mean-teacher with two students).

## Results

On the ACDC dataset with 10% labeled data, Ours-ABD (BCP) achieves 89.81% DSC, 81.95% Jaccard, 1.46 95HD, and 0.49 ASD, setting a new state-of-the-art. With 5% labeled data, Ours-ABD (BCP) reaches 88.96% DSC and 0.52 ASD, surpassing BCP by 1.37% DSC. On PROMISE12 with 20% labeled data, Ours-ABD achieves 82.06% DSC and 1.33 ASD, outperforming the previous best SCP-Net (77.06% DSC) by 5.0% DSC. The method also improves the Cross Teaching baseline by 2.07% DSC (88.52 vs 86.45) on ACDC at 10% labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ACDC (10% labeled), Ours-ABD (BCP) outperforms all baselines: it beats SCP-Net by 0.12% DSC (89.81 vs 89.69), BCP by 0.97% DSC (89.81 vs 88.84), and Cross Teaching by 3.36% DSC (89.81 vs 86.45). On ACDC (5% labeled), the improvement over BCP is 1.37% DSC (88.96 vs 87.59) and over SCP-Net is 1.69% DSC (88.96 vs 87.27). On PROMISE12 (20% labeled), Ours-ABD achieves 82.06% DSC, surpassing SCP-Net (77.06%) by 5.0%, CCT (71.43%) by 10.6%, SLC-Net (68.31%) by 13.8%, URPC (63.23%) by 18.8%, and SS-Net (62.31%) by 19.8%. The method also reduces 95HD from 3.98 (BCP) to 1.46 and ASD from 1.17 (BCP) to 0.49 on ACDC, indicating substantially tighter boundary predictions. Notably, Ours-ABD (Cross Teaching) at 88.52% DSC slightly trails BCP (88.84%), suggesting the plug-and-play gain is larger when combined with a stronger baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency-learning paradigm for semi-supervised medical image segmentation, building directly on Cross Teaching (Luo et al., MIDL 2022) and BCP (Bai et al., CVPR 2023) as baselines. Prior work in this space relies on a single perturbation type (input, feature, or network) to avoid instability, but the authors identify that naively combining input and network perturbations degrades performance. Their departure is a confidence-guided patch displacement mechanism that selectively swaps unreliable regions between augmented views, enabling safe co-use of multiple perturbations without the out-of-control behavior observed in prior mixed-perturbation attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means ABD can be embedded into existing cross-supervision or mean-teacher frameworks without architectural changes, lowering the adoption barrier for practitioners. However, the method is validated on only two 2D-slice datasets (ACDC, PROMISE12) and two baselines, leaving open whether the confidence-guided displacement generalizes to 3D volumes, more complex multi-organ tasks, or substantially different network pairs. The authors themselves note that the current single-patch displacement is a simplification and plan to develop adaptive multi-patch strategies for more challenging cases.
