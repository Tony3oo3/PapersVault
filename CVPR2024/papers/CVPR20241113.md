## Problem

Previous co-speech motion synthesis works generate expressions or gestures independently, and the few holistic approaches either combine separate models or use deterministic CNN-based multitask learning that neglects the expression–gesture relationship and cannot capture the many-to-many mapping inherent in gesture generation. As a result, jointly generated expressions and gestures can be disharmonious and lack diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241113/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-length (streaming) speech audio clip and a person ID as input. Audio is encoded into low-level Mel-spectrogram and high-level HuBERT features, which are further processed by a shared Transformer encoder into mid-level speech representations. The output is a holistic 3D motion sequence where each frame concatenates gesture joint rotations in axis-angle format (3J dimensions) with facial expression blend-shape weights (C_exp dimensions), produced at 15 FPS (BEAT) or 30 FPS (SHOW) and connected smoothly across clips via overlapping frames.

## Method

DiffSHEG is a diffusion-based framework whose denoising network (UniEG-Transformer) enforces a unidirectional information flow from expression to gesture: the predicted clean expression at each diffusion step is fed into the gesture branch (with gradient detachment) so that expressions condition gestures without the reverse interference. Audio conditioning is achieved by channel-wise concatenation of speech embeddings with noisy motion features through a Motion-Speech Fusion Residual Block (LayerNorm + MLP predicting a residual), avoiding cross-attention and its associated mask complexity. Global style (person ID) and diffusion-step conditioning are injected via AdaIN blocks inside a style-aware Transformer that uses linear self-attention for efficiency. For arbitrary-length generation, FOPPAS performs partial autoregressive sampling by fixing overlapping frames from the previous clip and outpainting the remainder with a Repaint-based procedure adapted to 25-step DDIM, achieving ~31 FPS on a single RTX 3090. Training minimizes a weighted sum of noise-prediction MSE, velocity MSE, and Huber motion-reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the BEAT dataset, DiffSHEG achieves the best Fréchet distances for both expression (FED: 331.72) and gesture (FGD: 438.93), as well as the highest SRGR (0.251) among all baselines. On the SHOW dataset, DiffSHEG attains the best holistic FMD (0.00184), FED (0.00161), FGD (0.00271), and gesture diversity (0.810, close to ground truth 0.834). The method achieves real-time inference at ~31.5 FPS on a single RTX 3090 GPU (vs. 0.44 FPS for DDPM+Repaint), enabling streaming generation of arbitrary-length motion sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffSHEG outperforms all four BEAT baselines (CaMN, DiffGesture, DSG, LDA) on every Fréchet distance metric by a large margin (e.g., FGD 438.93 vs. 997.62 for LDA and 23700.91 for DiffGesture) and achieves the best SRGR (0.251 vs. 0.215 for LDA). On SHOW, DiffSHEG beats both TalkSHOW and LS3DCG on FMD, FED, FGD, and gesture diversity, with expression diversity (0.923) approaching ground truth (0.990). The one exception is LS3DCG's higher BA (0.947 vs. 0.902) and PCM (0.981 vs. 0.912) on SHOW, which the authors attribute to LS3DCG's jittering motion artificially inflating beat-alignment scores. On BEAT, DiffSHEG's gesture diversity (0.536) is lower than jittering-prone diffusion baselines (e.g., DiffGesture 3.284), but the paper argues this reflects smoother, more natural motion rather than inferior generation. User study results confirm DiffSHEG is dominantly preferred over all baselines on realism, expression-speech synchronism, gesture-speech synchronism, and diversity across both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffSHEG builds on the diffusion model lineage (DDPM, DDIM, Repaint) and positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: co-speech expression generation (talking heads) and co-speech gesture generation. It departs from prior holistic approaches (Habibie et al.'s deterministic CNN and Yi et al.'s VQ-VAE-based TalkSHOW) by being the first to explicitly model the joint expression-gesture distribution within a single generative diffusion framework, using a uni-directional expression-to-gesture information flow rather than treating the two modalities independently or symmetrically. The FOPPAS sampling strategy also departs from prior autoregressive diffusion methods (DiffGesture, DiffuseStyleGesture) that require fixed-length overlap and seed motions at training time, instead enabling flexible, training-free outpainting at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 30+ FPS real-time inference on a single GPU with streaming audio support, DiffSHEG makes synchronized expression-and-gesture generation practically deployable in digital humans, metaverse avatars, and embodied agents—applications where latency is critical. The uni-directional flow design, validated by ablations showing that gesture-to-expression conditioning degrades expression quality, offers a principled architectural insight for multi-modal motion generation. Open questions include generalization to full-body (lower-body) motion, multi-speaker or conversational settings, and whether the expression-to-gesture asymmetry holds across cultures and speaking styles.
