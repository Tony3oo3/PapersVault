## Problem

Self-calibration of two cameras from a fundamental matrix via the Bougnoux closed-form formula suffers from a generic singularity when the principal axes are coplanar—a very common configuration in practice—leading to inaccurate or even imaginary focal length estimates. Existing iterative refinements still invoke the Bougnoux formula internally, inheriting the same instability near degenerate configurations and remaining sensitive to noise in the fundamental matrix and errors in assumed principal point positions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3×3 fundamental matrix (typically estimated via RANSAC from LoFTR point correspondences on 640×480 or larger images), scalar focal-length priors (initialized at 1.2× the maximum image dimension, implying ~50° FoV), and 2D principal-point priors (usually the image center). It outputs the two focal lengths f₁, f₂ and the two principal points c₁, c₂, assuming square pixels. A secondary contribution is a lightweight real-focal-length check (RFC) that operates directly on the elements of a candidate fundamental matrix inside RANSAC to reject models yielding negative squared focal lengths before expensive scoring.

## Method

The paper formulates focal-length and principal-point estimation as a constrained optimization: minimize a weighted sum of squared deviations from priors subject to two Kruppa equations that guarantee K₁ᵀFK₂ is a valid essential matrix. Applying Lagrange multipliers yields a system of eight polynomial equations in eight unknowns, which is too complex to solve directly. Instead, an iterative scheme (inspired by iterative triangulation) substitutes the current estimates into the constraint derivatives at each step, reducing the problem to a system of two degree-4 polynomial equations in the two Lagrange multipliers. This 2×2 quartic system is solved exactly via Gröbner basis elimination (a 20×36 matrix reduction followed by eigenvalue extraction from a 16×16 companion matrix), and the solution minimizing |λ₁|+|λ₂| is selected. The iteration converges in a few steps even in degenerate configurations, and the entire pipeline is ~15–22 ms per pair in C++, far faster than competing iterative methods.

## Results

On the Phototourism dataset, the proposed method achieves a median focal length error of 0.149 (mAA_f of 23.95 at threshold 0.1) and a median pose error of 6.46° (mAA_p of 40.37 at 10°), substantially outperforming all baselines. On Aachen Day-Night v1.1, the method reaches a median focal length error of 0.232 (mAA_f 12.46) and median pose error of 9.53° (mAA_p 27.55). The method runs in ~3.6 ms per pair (C++/Eigen), roughly 180× faster than Hartley's LM-based approach (659 ms) and 55× faster than Fetzer's method (198 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines evaluated on the two-view self-calibration task (Bougnoux, Hartley, Fetzer, and the prior-only baseline) on both focal length and pose accuracy across both datasets. On Phototourism, the median focal length error drops from 0.215 (Bougnoux) and 0.250 (Hartley) to 0.149, while mAA_f at threshold 0.1 improves from 20.65 and 12.73 to 23.95. On Aachen Day-Night, the advantage is even more pronounced: median f_err of 0.232 vs. 0.295 (Bougnoux) and 0.336 (Hartley). For the equal-focal-length case on ETH3D (Table 3), the method achieves a median pose error of 16.51° vs. 17.98° (Sturm) and 36.18° (6-point minimal solver), while its median focal length error of 0.299 is slightly worse than the minimal solver's 0.291 but far better than Sturm (0.582) or Hartley (1.090). The method thus dominates on pose accuracy in all settings and is competitive or superior on focal length accuracy across all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-calibration-from-fundamental-matrix line initiated by Bougnoux's closed-form formula [2] and the iterative LM optimization of Hartley and Silpa-Anan [15]. The key conceptual departure is replacing numerical optimization with an algebraic iterative scheme inspired by iterative triangulation methods [21, 26]: each iteration reduces to solving a system of two degree-4 polynomial equations in two Lagrange multipliers via Gröbner basis, eliminating the need for the Bougnoux formula at any stage. The paper also introduces a simple polynomial-ratio degeneracy check for rejecting RANSAC hypotheses that yield imaginary focal lengths, a practical addition not present in prior self-calibration or RANSAC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-calibration accuracy directly impacts downstream SfM, visual localization, and AR pipelines, and the coplanar-principal-axis degeneracy is extremely common in real photography (objects centered in frame), making robustness to this configuration practically critical. The 180× speedup over the main iterative competitor (Hartley) and the broadly applicable RANSAC degeneracy check make this a drop-in improvement for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to more than two views with varying intrinsics, to radial distortion models, and whether the Gröbner-basis-based inner loop can be further accelerated for real-time applications.
