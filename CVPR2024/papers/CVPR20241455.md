## Problem

Transforming a NeRF scene to match a single RGBD observation of its non-rigidly deformed state remains unsolved: existing NeRF editing methods require manual user-defined transforms, dynamic NeRF approaches need densely sampled time, and single-view generative models (e.g., DreamGaussian) fail to recover accurate geometry. The paper tackles the automatic recovery of a non-rigid 3D scene flow from one RGBD image, without any user input or multi-view observation of the transformed state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241455/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained NeRF (Instant-NGP, 400 training views at 2880×2880 on a hemisphere) and a single RGBD image with known camera pose of the transformed scene. The depth channel is essential for lifting 2D correspondences into 3D and for resolving scale ambiguity. Outputs include novel-view renderings of the transformed scene (evaluated on 30 held-out poses), a 3D mesh extracted by applying the forward flow to marching-cubes vertices while preserving topology, and the forward/backward 3D scene flows themselves.

## Method

The transformation is modeled as a 3D scene flow defined as a KNN-weighted linear blend of per-vertex rigid transformations (rotation + translation) anchored on the original NeRF's marching-cubes mesh (~500k vertices, decimated to 2k for optimization). The backward flow is analytically invertible from the forward flow, eliminating the need for cyclic losses that constrain MLP-based flows in prior work. Correspondences between the NeRF and the single RGBD view are obtained via ASpanFormer dense matching against 200 hemisphere renders (augmented with 7 yaw rotations), followed by two-stage filtering: pixel-space neighbor-density scoring to resolve multi-view ambiguity, and 3D-space cluster-consistency to remove geometrically implausible pairs. The per-anchor rotation and translation parameters are optimized via an embedded deformation graph loss combining ARAP regularization with a translation consistency term on the filtered 3D correspondences, trained for 3k iterations with Adam.

## Results

On the proposed 113-scene dataset, NeRFDeformer achieves 25.9±4.2 PSNR, 0.924±0.034 SSIM, and 0.061±0.040 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming all baselines. For geometric reconstruction, it attains a Chamfer Distance of 1.46±2.9 (×1000), a success rate of 90.3%, and a Volume IoU of 0.666±0.20, substantially exceeding the next-best method SINE (6.40 CD, 63.7% success, 0.515 VmIoU). The method also demonstrates robustness to depth noise (SimKinect), with PSNR degrading only from 25.9 to 23.6 under the highest noise level (s.d. 1.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRFDeformer outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against Zero123-XL, the generative diffusion baseline, the method gains +11.8 PSNR (25.9 vs. 14.1) and reduces LPIPS by over 4× (0.061 vs. 0.265); Zero123-XL also cannot produce geometric output at all. Against DreamGaussian, the 3D-aware Gaussian splatting diffusion model, NeRFDeformer improves PSNR by +6.1 (25.9 vs. 19.8), reduces Chamfer Distance by 5× (1.46 vs. 7.36), and more than doubles the geometric success rate (90.3% vs. 33.6%). Against SINE, the closest prior NeRF-editing method using an MLP-based flow, NeRFDeformer achieves +3.8 PSNR (25.9 vs. 22.1), reduces CD by 4.4× (1.46 vs. 6.40), and raises the success rate from 63.7% to 90.3%; the paper attributes SINE's weaker performance to its cyclic MLP flow struggling with complex non-rigid transformations and its reliance on single-view correspondences from FlowFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRFDeformer draws on two distinct lineages: the embedded deformation graph framework from mesh shape manipulation (Sumner et al., 2007), which provides the ARAP regularization and anchor-point local rigid transformation structure, and ASpanFormer for dense detector-free image matching. It departs from the dominant MLP-based flow paradigm used in SINE, NeRFies, and D-NeRF by replacing learned flows with a weighted linear blending of per-vertex rigid transformations, yielding an analytically invertible flow without cyclic losses. Unlike NeRF editing methods that require explicit user input or dynamic NeRF methods that assume densely sampled time, this work formulates the problem as a fully automatic single-observation transformation, and unlike diffusion-based novel view synthesis (DreamGaussian, Zero123-XL), it conditions on a pre-trained NeRF rather than generating from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong for robotics and AR/VR pipelines where re-capturing multi-view data after a scene change is costly; a 90% mesh extraction success rate (vs. 63.7% for SINE) makes the approach viable for downstream tasks like collision-free manipulation. Key limitations include the hard dependency on accurate depth input and the requirement of a pre-trained NeRF, both of which restrict deployment to controlled or sensor-equipped settings. The authors identify relaxing the depth requirement (e.g., via shape priors) and grounding diffusion models through scene flow as natural next steps, and the 113-scene dataset, while purpose-built, is too small to establish broad generalization across object categories.
