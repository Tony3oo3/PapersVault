## Problem

Existing GAN-based quality enhancement methods for compressed images exhibit a pervasive enhancement bias towards the compression domain: their discriminators perceive compressed images as more realistic than raw images, and the resulting enhanced images align more closely with compressed counterparts than with raw originals. This occurs because prior methods only train the discriminator to distinguish enhanced from raw images, completely neglecting the compression domain's role in the enhancement pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a lossy-compressed RGB image (BPG at QP 27–47 or JPEG at QF 10–50) as input and produces a quality-enhanced RGB image at the same resolution. The compressed image is consumed in two roles simultaneously: as the generator's input to produce the enhanced image, and as a conditioning signal concatenated to the discriminator's input. The approach is model-agnostic and adds no inference overhead, meaning the generator architecture and forward pass remain unchanged from the underlying SR baseline (e.g., ESRGAN or RealESRGAN).

## Method

The method introduces two complementary mechanisms to mitigate the compression-domain bias. First, a conditional discriminator receives the compressed image as a key condition (concatenated with the image being scored), justified by a D-separation argument: in the causal graph I_R → I_C → I_E, the raw and enhanced images are conditionally independent given I_C, restoring the independence assumption that standard GANs require. Second, a domain-divergence regularization loss L_R is added to the generator objective, which penalizes the VGG-19 feature-space distance between the enhanced and compressed images only when that distance is smaller than the raw-to-compressed distance, thereby actively pushing the enhancement domain away from the compression domain. Together, these two components enable the discriminator to properly reject compressed images and guide the generator toward the raw domain, improving both perceptual and fidelity metrics across all tested baselines without modifying the generator architecture.

## Results

On BPG-compressed images (QP=37), the proposed method applied to RealESRGAN (Ours w/[44]) achieves FID 8.77, LPIPS 0.113, PSNR 30.6 dB, and MUSIQ 66.2, outperforming the RealESRGAN baseline (FID 10.6, LPIPS 0.116, PSNR 29.8 dB, MUSIQ 64.9) across all 11 metrics. Applied to ESRGAN (Ours w/[43]), it achieves FID 7.50 and LPIPS 0.114, the best perceptual scores among all methods, while also improving PSNR from 29.0 to 29.6 dB. In rate-distortion evaluation (BD-BR), the method reduces bitrate by up to 96.8% (CLIP-IQA) and 98.5% (HyperIQA) compared to ESRGAN while maintaining equivalent perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is model-agnostic and is evaluated as a plug-in on two GAN-based SR baselines (ESRGAN [43] and RealESRGAN [44]), consistently improving them across all 11 quality metrics. Against the seven fidelity-oriented baselines (AR-CNN, DCAD, DnCNN, CBDNet, RDN, RBQE, MPRNet), the SR-based methods (including the proposed enhancements) dominate on perceptual metrics (FID, LPIPS, DISTS) by large margins (e.g., FID 7.50–8.77 vs. 10.3–10.7), but the fidelity-oriented methods retain a PSNR advantage (31.2–31.7 dB vs. 29.6–30.6 dB). The proposed method closes this PSNR gap substantially: Ours w/[44] reaches 30.6 dB, surpassing RealESRGAN's 29.8 dB by 0.8 dB and approaching the fidelity-oriented range. In BD-BR rate-distortion evaluation, the method achieves the lowest bitrate at equivalent quality, with reductions of 24–98% over ESRGAN and 14–95% over RealESRGAN depending on the metric. Notably, RealESRGAN alone actually degrades FID relative to the compressed input (10.6 vs. 9.73), a problem the proposed method resolves (8.77), confirming that the enhancement bias was actively harmful.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GAN-based super-resolution lineage (SRGAN, ESRGAN, RealESRGAN) that has been repurposed for compressed-image quality enhancement, and on the conditional GAN framework of Mirza & Osindero (2014). It departs from prior work by identifying a previously unacknowledged failure mode: existing discriminators in these pipelines perceive compressed images as more realistic than raw images, causing the enhancement domain to collapse toward the compression domain rather than the raw domain. The paper introduces a conditional discriminator (using the compressed image as a key condition, justified via Bayesian D-separation) and a domain-divergence regularization loss to counteract this bias, both as model-agnostic add-ons to any existing SR-based enhancement pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bias analysis provides a new diagnostic lens for evaluating GAN-based restoration methods, revealing that the standard adversarial objective is insufficient when the 'real' and 'generated' distributions are correlated (as in compression→enhancement). The method's model-agnostic design and zero inference overhead make it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing enhancement pipelines. Limitations include evaluation restricted to BPG/JPEG on DIV2K and Flickr2K, and the domain-divergence regularization relies on a fixed VGG-19 feature extractor whose choice may affect generalization. Open questions include whether the same bias phenomenon and fix generalize to other degradation types (e.g., sensor noise, defocus) and to video quality enhancement.
