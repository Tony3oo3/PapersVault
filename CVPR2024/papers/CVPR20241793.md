## Problem

Multispectral pedestrian detectors trained on existing datasets (KAIST, CVC-14, FLIR) learn a spurious statistical correlation between pedestrian labels and the presence of thermal features, since these datasets are dominated by ROTO (day) and RXTO (night) data where thermal always reveals pedestrians. This modality bias causes detection failures on ROTX scenes—daytime situations where thermal radiation is blocked by glass or heat-insulating clothing—despite the pedestrian being clearly visible in RGB. No prior work identified this specific bias or provided a dataset to evaluate it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatially aligned RGB-thermal image pairs (640×512 for KAIST/FLIR, 640×471 for CVC-14) and outputs pedestrian bounding boxes with class labels via a Faster R-CNN-style detection pipeline. Unusually, inference does not rely on the standard posterior probability p(y|x_R, x_T) but instead uses a switchable total indirect effect (sTIE) score that conditionally subtracts the thermal direct effect depending on the detected input type (ROTO vs. RXTO/ROTX). The model also produces unimodal RGB and thermal prediction scores that are used both for mode determination and as auxiliary supervision.

## Method

The Causal Mode Multiplexer (CMM) framework formulates multispectral pedestrian detection as a structural causal model with a thermal direct link (X_T → Y) that causes modality bias. It introduces two learning schemes: a common mode that learns the total effect on ROTO data, and a differential mode that learns the total indirect effect on RXTO/ROTX data by subtracting the natural direct effect of thermal via counterfactual intervention. A Gumbel-softmax-based binary translation function compares unimodal RGB and thermal prediction scores to automatically determine the causal mode (K_mode = +1 for common, −1 for differential) at each training step. The CMM Loss optimizes a 'switchable total indirect effect' (sTIE) that seamlessly interpolates between TE and TIE, while a Log-Harmonic fusion combines multimodal and unimodal prediction scores. Built on the Uncertainty-Guided model [14], CMM achieves state-of-the-art performance on KAIST, CVC-14, and FLIR while dramatically improving generalization on the newly introduced ROTX-MP dataset (70.44 AP vs. 21.69 AP for the baseline).

## Results

On standard multispectral pedestrian detection benchmarks, CMM achieves the best overall performance: 8.54 MR on KAIST (All), 17.13 MR on CVC-14 (All), and 87.80 AP on FLIR, outperforming the strongest baseline (Kim et al.) by 0.13 MR, 1.57 MR, and 3.13 AP respectively. On the new ROTX-MP dataset (1000 thermal-obscured daytime images), CMM trained on KAIST achieves 70.44 AP, surpassing the next-best baseline (HFF at 36.95 AP) by over 33 AP points. When trained on CVC-14 or FLIR, CMM similarly dominates ROTX-MP with 34.96 and 57.09 AP respectively, while baselines drop to 5–13 AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMM is compared against four baselines: HFF (Halfway Fusion + Faster R-CNN), CFT (Cross-modality Fusion Transformer), MBNet, and Kim et al. (Uncertainty-Guided model, which serves as CMM's architectural backbone). On KAIST, CMM achieves the lowest All-set MR (8.54) and Day MR (9.60), but trails Kim et al. on Night (5.93 vs. 5.05). On CVC-14, CMM wins on Night (7.71 vs. 11.08) and All (17.13 vs. 18.70) but underperforms CFT on Day (27.81 vs. 18.81). On FLIR, CMM achieves the highest AP (87.80) over all baselines. The most striking gap appears on ROTX-MP: CMM trained on KAIST reaches 70.44 AP while HFF, CFT, MBNet, and Kim et al. score only 36.95, 3.64, 18.88, and 21.69 AP respectively—a margin of at least 33 AP over the runner-up, confirming that the causal debiasing strategy is the primary source of ROTX generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multispectral pedestrian detection line (KAIST, CVC-14, FLIR benchmarks with architectures like HFF, CFT, MBNet, and the Uncertainty-Guided model) by importing causal inference tools—structural causal models, counterfactual intervention, and total indirect effect—from the broader multimodal bias literature (VQA linguistic bias, deconfounded grounding). Rather than augmenting training data or adding regularization, it formulates modality bias as a learnable direct effect (X_T → Y) that can be pruned via a mode-switching loss, drawing on Pearl's decomposition of total effect into natural direct and indirect effects. The Gumbel-softmax-based mode determination and Log-Harmonic fusion are practical mechanisms that bridge the causal formalism to a standard Faster R-CNN-style detection pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a previously overlooked failure mode in multispectral detection—catastrophic degradation when thermal features are obscured in daytime (ROTX)—and provides both a principled causal remedy and a dedicated benchmark (ROTX-MP) to measure it. The large ROTX-MP gap (70.44 vs. 21.69 AP) suggests that conventional training implicitly memorizes a thermal-pedestrian co-occurrence prior rather than learning genuine multimodal reasoning, a concern that likely extends to other sensor-fusion tasks. Limitations include the relatively small ROTX-MP (1000 images, two scenarios), evaluation on a single detection architecture family, and the added complexity of multiple uni-modal heads and mode switching; whether the causal-mode framework generalizes to other multimodal settings (e.g., LiDAR+camera, audio+visual) remains open.
