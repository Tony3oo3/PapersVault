## Problem

State-of-the-art anatomically constrained face models (e.g., ALM) enforce bone-skin coupling as regularization terms within complex optimization problems, requiring minutes per frame on CPU and restricting anatomical constraints to sparse regions where bone is directly underneath the skin. In reality, the facial skin surface is densely constrained by underlying anatomy, but simulating this dense interaction through physical simulation is even more computationally expensive. The paper asks whether an implicit neural representation can learn a continuous, dense anatomical substructure that constrains every skin point while remaining fast to evaluate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240258/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model learning stage takes a set of N (20) registered 3D scan meshes of a single actor sharing vertex topology, plus a neutral shape with a rough skull and jaw bone estimate (fitted via Zoss et al.). At fitting time, the trained model accepts either dense 3D scan vertices or sparse 2D facial landmarks as constraints. The output is a continuously evaluable implicit function that, given a template query point, produces a 3D skin position along with the learned dense anatomical surface, per-point soft tissue thickness, per-point skinning weights, a 6-DOF jaw bone transformation, and spatially-varying expression blendshape coefficients. Unlike patch-based models, the expression coefficients are local and spatially varying, determined by a separate MLP conditioned on both a frame code and the query point.

## Method

The core architecture is an ensemble of five SIREN coordinate MLPs (B, D, N, K, E) that, given a template point c, predict the anatomy point, soft tissue thickness, anatomy normal, skinning weight, and a basis of corrective expression displacements. The skin surface is reconstructed in two stages: first, the neutral skin point is computed as s₀ = b₀ + d₀·n₀ (anatomical offset), then it is rigidly deformed via linear blend skinning with a learned jaw transformation and displaced by a weighted combination of expression blendshapes. The key insight is that the dense anatomical surface is learned end-to-end from only sparse bone-skin constraints (5–10% of vertices), with a thickness regularizer pushing unconstrained regions toward zero offset, effectively extending the anatomy to every skin point without requiring volumetric data. For fitting, the paper employs neural reparameterized optimization: frame codes are fed to two small MLPs that predict the jaw/head pose and the spatially-varying expression coefficients, avoiding the need to optimize thousands of per-vertex parameters directly and enabling convergence in ~1 second per frame on a single GPU.

## Results

On 3D performance fitting across 819 frames from 5 sequences of 5 different actors, AIM achieves an average fitting error of 0.31 mm, substantially outperforming global blendshapes (GBS, 0.83 mm) and patch blendshape models (PBS, 0.51 mm). The anatomical local model (ALM) achieves the lowest error at 0.09 mm but requires several minutes per frame, whereas AIM converges in a few seconds per frame. For 3D performance retargeting, AIM retargets each frame in 2–3 seconds compared to several minutes per frame for the prior state-of-the-art method of Chandran et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against global blendshapes (GBS, Lewis et al. 2014), AIM's local variant reduces fitting error from 0.83 mm to 0.31 mm (a 63% reduction), while the global variant of AIM (0.86 mm) performs slightly worse than GBS, confirming that locality is essential. Against the patch blendshape model (PBS, Chandran et al. 2023), AIM improves error from 0.51 mm to 0.31 mm without requiring manual patch layout or overlap hyperparameters. Against the anatomical local model (ALM, Wu et al. 2016), ALM achieves lower error (0.09 mm vs. 0.31 mm) but at a cost of several minutes per frame versus a few seconds for AIM, making AIM far more practical for production use. For 3D performance retargeting (Chandran et al. 2022), AIM provides qualitatively competitive results while retargeting each frame in 2–3 seconds instead of several minutes, and additionally disentangles rigid jaw motion from non-rigid soft tissue deformations without requiring hand-crafted design parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anatomical local model (ALM) of Wu et al. [47] and the local retargeting framework of Chandran et al. [10] by reformulating their sparse, optimization-based anatomical constraints as a jointly learned implicit neural representation. Where ALM enforces bone-skin coupling only in sparse regions via regularization terms within a costly constrained optimization, AIM learns a dense anatomical surface, soft tissue thickness, and normals for every skin point using an ensemble of SIREN coordinate networks, with linear blend skinning and expression blendshapes layered on top. The method also draws on the broader implicit neural representation literature (SIREN, NPHM, IMFace) but is the first to embed anatomical constraints directly inside an implicit facial blendshape model, eliminating the need for hand-crafted patch layouts or GPU solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for VFX and animation pipelines: AIM provides a drop-in replacement for traditional blendshape models that is orders of magnitude faster than ALM (seconds vs. minutes per frame) while enabling new capabilities like anatomy-based face sculpting and dense anatomical constraints without physical simulation. Key limitations include artifacts around the lip region from sparse anatomical supervision, the inability to skin the learned anatomy with expressions, and fitting accuracy (0.31 mm) that still trails ALM (0.09 mm). Open questions include whether the formulation extends to multi-identity morphable models, whether appearance can be integrated, and whether the dense anatomy learned from sparse bone supervision generalizes beyond the training expressions.
