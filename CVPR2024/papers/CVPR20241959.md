## Problem

Few-shot model compression for Vision Transformers remains largely unexplored, as existing few-shot methods target CNNs and fail to converge or transfer to ViT architectures. Moreover, the leading few-shot block-level pruning method (PRACTISE) produces only sparse compression options—just a handful of discrete model sizes—leaving hardware compute budgets underutilized. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing the first dense few-shot compression framework for ViTs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ViT (or CNN) model and a tiny unlabeled image set of 50–1000 samples (e.g., randomly drawn from ImageNet-1k at 224×224). It outputs a family of compressed models that densely populate a continuous range of MACs values, rather than a few discrete sizes. Notably, the training set requires no labels, and the method internally generates synthetic images via DeepDream-style optimization to serve as a metric set for block selection, eliminating any need for external data.

## Method

DC-ViT replaces each selected block's attention module with a small MLP that reuses a random subset of the original MLP's intermediate nodes (reducing dimension from 4d to 4d(1−r_d)), enabling dense compression by tuning the drop ratio r_d. Block selection proceeds via a block-wise trial: each block is individually compressed and finetuned using MSE feature mimicking on the tiny unlabeled set, then ranked by cross-entropy loss on a synthetic metric set generated from Gaussian noise through the pre-trained model. The k blocks with the lowest metric loss are then progressively compressed one at a time, with each stage finetuning only the front portion of the network up to the compressed block. This progressive strategy, combined with parameter reuse from the original MLP and partial (front-only) finetuning, yields roughly 10 percentage points higher top-1 accuracy than PRACTISE at matched MACs.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, DC-ViT-B pruned from ViT-Base with only 500 unlabeled samples achieves 83.73% top-1 accuracy at 8.3% MACs reduction, 82.74% at 16.7% reduction, and 80.18% at 24.5% reduction, outperforming PRACTISE by approximately 10 percentage points at every compression level. With 1000 samples, the gap narrows slightly but remains substantial (e.g., 81.60% vs. 73.28% at 24.5% MACs reduction). On CNN backbones (ResNet-34, MobileNetV2) with 50 samples, DC-ViT also leads PRACTISE by 1.9 and 0.5 percentage points respectively. Transfer to downstream tasks (CIFAR-10, CUB-200, etc.) shows the compressed DC-ViT-B retains or even exceeds the original ViT-B's accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-ViT decisively outperforms PRACTISE [43], the SOTA few-shot CNN compression method, by roughly 10 percentage points in top-1 accuracy on ViT-Base across all compression levels (e.g., 83.73% vs. 73.32% at 8.3% MACs reduction with 500 samples), while also achieving lower latency. On CNN backbones, DC-ViT beats FSKD [24] (72.2% vs. 45.3%), CD [2] (72.2% vs. 56.2%), and MiR [44] (72.2% vs. 64.1%) on ResNet-34 with 50 samples, and edges out PRACTISE on MobileNetV2 (69.8% vs. 69.3%). In Figure 1, DC-ViT compressed with <0.1% of ImageNet training images (unlabeled) matches or exceeds full-data methods Mini-DeiT-B [54], RegNetY-16GF [35], and VTP [56] at comparable MACs, and trails AutoFormer-B [6] and S²ViTE-B [7] by only 2–4 percentage points despite using orders of magnitude fewer labeled samples. On transfer learning (Table 7), the compressed DC-ViT-B achieves 98.0% on CIFAR-10 and 92.2% on CUB-200, matching or slightly exceeding the original ViT-B [13] and remaining competitive with EfficientNet-B7 [40].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot model compression line of work—dominated by CNN-specific methods like PRACTISE (block-level pruning), MiR (mimicking-then-replacing), and FSKD/CD (knowledge distillation)—to Vision Transformers, a domain the authors explicitly identify as unexplored. The key conceptual departure from PRACTISE is replacing sparse block dropping with dense compression: selectively removing the attention module while retaining a tunable fraction of the MLP, which yields a continuum of model sizes rather than a handful of discrete options. The synthetic metric set for block selection draws on DeepDream-style optimization, repurposing generative image synthesis as a diagnostic tool rather than a data augmentation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dense few-shot ViT compression addresses a practical deployment gap: hardware with a fixed compute budget needs a model that fits precisely, not one that undershoots by 20%. The 10+ percentage point improvement over PRACTISE with only 50–1000 unlabeled images (versus 1.28M labeled images for full-data methods) makes ViT compression viable in privacy-sensitive or data-scarce settings. However, the compression ratio is capped around 30% MACs reduction, and the method is currently limited to image classification; extending it to detection, segmentation, or higher compression ratios remains open. The finding that random MLP node selection matches or beats principled alternatives also suggests the field may be over-engineering component selection in few-shot regimes.
