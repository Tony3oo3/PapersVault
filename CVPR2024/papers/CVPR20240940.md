## Problem

Deep learning models learn multi-level feature spaces but do not explicitly encode part-whole hierarchical relationships among anatomical structures, a fundamental property of medical imaging. Existing self-supervised learning methods for medical images overlook anatomy hierarchies in their learning objectives, resulting in embeddings that lack the semantic structure needed to distinguish, compose, and decompose anatomical parts. This paper addresses the gap by introducing explicit part-whole hierarchy learning into the SSL training process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240940/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled chest radiographs and color fundus photographs, processing them at multiple anatomical scales from full images (H×W) down to (H/2^m × W/2^m) patches for m up to 4, with a 224² global view and eight 96² multi-scale crops per anchor patch. The pretrained model produces dense per-pixel anatomical embeddings (Eve-v2) that form semantically structured 'echo chambers'—distinct anatomical structures receive distinct embeddings while the same structure across patients yields nearly identical embeddings. For downstream use, the pretrained encoder is plugged into a U-Net for segmentation or a task-specific head for classification, requiring no architectural modification.

## Method

Adam-v2 unifies three learning branches under a shared student-teacher architecture with EMA-updated teacher weights, trained in a coarse-to-fine manner across anatomical scales. The localizability branch enforces consistency between an anchor patch and its multi-scale augmented crops via a self-distillation cross-entropy loss, learning discriminative embeddings for different anatomical structures. The composability branch decomposes a whole patch into n=4 non-overlapping parts, concatenates their student embeddings, and matches the aggregated result to the teacher's whole embedding via MSE, encoding parts-to-whole relations. The decomposability branch takes the student's whole embedding, passes it through a decomposability head to predict individual part embeddings, and matches them to the teacher's actual part embeddings via MSE, encoding whole-to-parts relations. The three losses are combined with equal weights (λ₁=λ₂=λ₃=1), and the framework is architecture-neutral, validated on ResNet-50, ViT-S, and ConvNeXt-B backbones.

## Results

In few-shot transfer, Adam-v2 achieves 73.59% Dice on 3-shot JSRT-Clavicle segmentation, surpassing the best large-scale model LVM-Med (56.87%) by 16.7 points and the best SSL baseline DenseCL (36.43%) by 37.2 points. On the ChestX-ray14 benchmark, the large-scale Adam-v2 (ConvNeXt-B, ~1M images) sets a new record of 83.4% mean AUC, outperforming LVM-Med (82.0%), Medical-MAE (83.0%), and RadImageNet (80.7%). In full fine-tuning across 10 downstream tasks, Adam-v2 consistently outperforms all 7 SSL baselines and the fully-supervised ImageNet model with statistical significance (p<0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adam-v2 outperforms all 8 baselines across every task and label fraction in the few-shot setting. Against the two large-scale medical models, Adam-v2 leads LVM-Med by 16.7 points (3-shot clavicle) and 7.4 points (3-shot heart), and RadImageNet by 18.1 and 13.8 points respectively. Against SSL baselines, the margins are even larger: Adam-v2 beats DenseCL (the strongest SSL method) by 37.2 points on 3-shot clavicle and 22.0 points on 3-shot heart. On the ChestX-ray14 benchmark, Adam-v2 (Large-scale) achieves 83.4% mAUC, edging out Medical-MAE (83.0%) and LVM-Med (82.0%) while using roughly half the pretraining data (~1M vs 1.3M images). In full fine-tuning, Adam-v2 surpasses all SSL methods (DINO, DenseCL, DiRA, TransVW, PCRL, Medical-MAE) and the fully-supervised ImageNet model with p<0.05 across all 10 downstream tasks, confirming that explicit part-whole hierarchy learning yields more generalizable representations than standard contrastive or masked-reconstruction objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work Adam [69], which learned dense anatomical embeddings via self-supervision, by explicitly incorporating part-whole hierarchical structure into the learning objectives through three new branches (localizability, composability, decomposability). The localizability branch builds directly on the DINO self-distillation framework [12], while the conceptual motivation draws from Hinton's GLOM [39] for representing part-whole hierarchies in neural networks. Unlike prior medical SSL methods (DiRA, TransVW, PCRL, Medical-MAE) that exploit anatomical consistency or reconstruction without encoding hierarchical relations, and unlike architecture-level approaches (capsule networks, hierarchical ViTs) that impose structure through network design, Adam-v2 encodes part-whole semantics into the embedding space through explicit training losses, making it architecture-neutral.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly learning anatomical part-whole hierarchies during self-supervised pretraining yields representations that are both more discriminative and more semantically structured than those from existing SSL methods, with particularly large gains in few-shot transfer (outperforming models trained on 1.3M images with only 3–24 labeled examples). The emergent interpolation and extrapolation properties—never explicitly trained—suggest the learned embedding space captures genuine spatial-anatomical relationships, opening the door to zero-shot anatomical reasoning. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine hierarchical learning generalizes to 3D volumetric data, whether the approach transfers to non-anatomical domains, and whether the gains persist as pretraining data scales further beyond ~1M images.
