## Problem

Existing monocular 3D face tracking methods rely on sparse landmarks and photometric supervision, which are computationally expensive and fail to capture fine facial motion in low-saliency regions. Training is typically done with weak supervision or synthetic data rather than high-quality 3D scan annotations, and current benchmarks neglect the temporal dimension of tracking. These limitations collectively prevent accurate, temporally consistent facial performance capture from readily available 2D video.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D video frames cropped to a face bounding box (detected by SFD, enlarged 20%, resized to 512×512) and produces a full FLAME 3DMM parameterization (identity shape β∈R³⁰⁰, expression φ∈R¹⁰⁰, skeletal pose θ) along with per-vertex 2D alignment (5023 vertices) with per-vertex Gaussian uncertainty, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and optional per-vertex deformations. The 2D alignment is represented as a dense UV-to-image flow field (64×64×2) rather than sparse keypoints, enabling reconstruction of fine details in regions without landmark coverage.

## Method

FlowFace is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 is a 2D alignment network that uses a SegFormer-b5 vision-transformer backbone to encode the face image, a learned multi-scale UV positional encoding module, and an iterative RAFT-based recurrent update block (3 iterations) that progressively refines a dense UV-to-image flow and per-pixel uncertainty map, supervised by Gaussian negative log-likelihood on both per-vertex positions and dense flow. Unlike prior work, it is trained on high-quality 3D scan annotations from FaceScape, Stirling, and FaMoS rather than synthetic or weakly supervised data. Stage 2 is an optimization-based 3D model fitting module that jointly fits FLAME parameters across all frames and views by minimizing an energy combining 2D alignment, FLAME shape/expression priors, temporal acceleration smoothing, a MICA neutral-shape prior for identity/expression disentanglement, and per-vertex deformation regularization. The key architectural insight is treating face alignment as a dense UV-to-image flow prediction problem (analogous to optical flow) rather than sparse keypoint regression, which enables topology-independent, per-vertex reconstruction of fine facial details.

## Results

On the proposed Multiface benchmark, FlowFace achieves a face-region SSME of 5.66 px (single-image) and 2.50 px (full sequence), outperforming the best public regression-based method (DECA: 6.14 px / 5.63 px) by 54% and 46% respectively. In terms of 3D reconstruction (chamfer distance), FlowFace achieves 1.37 mm (single-image) and 1.19 mm (sequence) on the face region, beating the photometric optimization baseline MPT (1.30 mm sequence) by 8%. On the FaceScape benchmark, FlowFace reduces CD to 2.21 mm (vs. 3.67 mm for HRN, a 38% improvement) and NME to 0.083 rad.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowFace outperforms all regression-based baselines (DECA, EMOCA, HRN, 3DDFAv2, PRNet, SADRNet) on both CD and SSME across every face region in the Multiface benchmark, with the largest SSME gap over HRN (5.66 vs. 7.91 px single-image) and 3DDFAv2/PRNet (5.66 vs. 8.45 px). Against the photometric optimization method MPT, FlowFace achieves 8% lower CD (1.19 vs. 1.30 mm sequence) and substantially lower SSME (2.50 vs. 5.74 px sequence), demonstrating that dense 2D alignment supervision can surpass photometric fitting for motion accuracy. On the NoW Challenge, FlowFace beats the prior key-point-only method Dense [42] by 13% in multi-view scan-to-mesh error (0.71 vs. 0.81 mm median) and outperforms MICA [57] by 4% in single-view mean error (1.07 vs. 1.11 mm), though TokenFace [38] achieves a slightly lower single-view median (0.76 vs. 0.87 mm). On FaceScape, FlowFace reduces CD by 38% over the best prior method HRN (2.21 vs. 3.67 mm). For downstream tasks, replacing MPT with FlowFace in INSTA improves LPIPS by 10.5% (public dataset) and 20.3% (Multiface), and augmenting CodeTalker training with FlowFace-generated meshes reduces lip vertex error by 8.8% on VOCASET.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowFace directly extends the dense-landmark 3D face reconstruction pipeline of Wood et al. [42], replacing their ResNet-based 2D alignment with a SegFormer ViT backbone and an iterative RAFT-style recurrent refinement module that predicts dense UV-to-image flow. It departs from prior optimization-based trackers (MPT, DECA, Face2Face) by using only 2D alignment as supervision rather than photometric or sparse landmark losses, and from regression-based methods by performing joint multi-frame, multi-view fitting with a parametric 3DMM. The integration of MICA [57] as a neutral shape prior and the addition of per-vertex deformations go beyond the FLAME blend-shape space used in most prior work, enabling identity details that standard 3DMMs cannot represent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proposed Screen Space Motion Error (SSME) metric and the Multiface-based benchmark address a genuine gap: prior evaluations relied on sparse key-points, photometric errors, or 3D reconstruction distances that either ignore temporal consistency or require rigid alignment that discards pose and intrinsics. The demonstrated downstream gains (10–20% LPIPS improvement on head avatar synthesis, 8.8% lip error reduction on speech-driven animation) show that tracking accuracy directly translates to practical quality in production pipelines. Key limitations remain: the two-stage pipeline is not end-to-end differentiable, training data is restricted to controlled in-the-lab captures, and the large margins on the self-constructed Multiface benchmark should be interpreted cautiously until independently reproduced.
