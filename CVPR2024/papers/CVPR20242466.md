## Problem

Existing Data-Free Knowledge Distillation (DFKD) methods generate synthetic samples from random noise that inherently lacks semantic information, forcing the generator to learn the full data distribution from scratch. This results in low-quality synthetic images and prohibitively long training times (25–30 hours on CIFAR-100), making large-scale DFKD on ImageNet practically infeasible for most state-of-the-art approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242466/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained teacher network, a list of class labels, and a frozen text encoder (CLIP, SBERT, or Doc2Vec) as inputs. The text encoder is queried exactly once to produce dense label-text embeddings (LTEs) via a prompt template (e.g., 'a class of a {class_name}'), which are then cached in a memory pool for all subsequent training iterations. The output is a trained student network (ResNet, VGG, or WideResNet variants) that emulates the teacher's predictions, along with the synthetic images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) generated during training.

## Method

NAYER replaces the random-noise generator input with a constant, semantically rich label-text embedding (LTE) that encodes inter-class relationships (e.g., 'dog' and 'cat' embeddings are closer than 'dog' and 'car'), making the input distribution structurally similar to the ground-truth data distribution and reducing generator convergence from ~2000 steps to 30–40 steps. To prevent the generator from collapsing to a single output due to the constant input, a Noisy Layer (BatchNorm + single Linear layer, output dim 1000) is inserted between the LTE and the generator and randomly reinitialized at every iteration, providing a layer-level random source that ensures diversity without sacrificing the LTE's semantic guidance. A K-to-1 variant feeds all K class LTEs through a single shared noisy layer to simultaneously generate one sample per class, reducing parameter count and enriching diversity through multiple gradient sources. Training proceeds adversarially: the generator and noisy layer are optimized to match teacher confidence while maximizing teacher–student KL divergence, and the student is updated on a memory buffer of stored synthetic images via KL distillation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WRN40-2/WRN16-2), NAYER (E=300) achieves 94.07% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous best SpaceshipNet (93.25%) and MAD (92.64%). On CIFAR-100 (WRN40-2/WRN16-2), NAYER reaches 71.72%, surpassing SpaceshipNet (69.95%) and CMI (68.75%). On ImageNet (ResNet50/ResNet50), NAYER achieves 68.92% versus DeepInv's 68.00% and FM's 57.37%. Training time is dramatically reduced: NAYER (E=100) completes CIFAR-10 in 2.05 hours versus 31.23 hours for DeepInv, a 15× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAYER (E=300) outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary WRN40-2/WRN16-2 setting for both CIFAR-10 (94.07% vs. best baseline SpaceshipNet at 93.25%) and CIFAR-100 (71.72% vs. SpaceshipNet at 69.95%). On TinyImageNet (R34/R18), NAYER achieves 64.17%, edging out SpaceshipNet (64.04%) and CMI (64.01%), while substantially surpassing MAD (62.32%) and KAKR variants (~48–50%). On ImageNet (R50/R50), NAYER (68.92%) beats DeepInv (68.00%) by 0.92 points and FM (57.37%) by 11.55 points, making it the first DFKD method to report competitive ImageNet results. The sole exception is CIFAR-10 R34/R18, where SpaceshipNet (95.39%) narrowly edges NAYER (95.21%). In terms of training time, NAYER (E=100) is 15× faster than DeepInv (2.05h vs. 31.23h on CIFAR-10) and 11× faster than CMI (2.05h vs. 24.01h), while simultaneously achieving higher accuracy than all methods that report training times.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAYER extends the adversarial data-free knowledge distillation framework established by DeepInv, CMI, MAD, and SpaceshipNet, which all generate synthetic samples from random noise inputs. The key conceptual departure is relocating the source of randomness from the input level to a dedicated noisy layer (a BatchNorm + Linear combination), while feeding the generator a constant label-text embedding (LTE) from a pretrained language model. This repurposes the inter-class geometric structure of text embeddings—previously exploited in multimodal and NLP settings—as a structured input signal for image generation, and the K-to-1 noisy layer design (one layer generating all class samples simultaneously) is a novel architectural choice not seen in prior DFKD or GAN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–15× training speedup (e.g., ~2 hours vs. ~30 hours on CIFAR-100) makes DFKD practically viable for larger-scale deployments where prior methods were prohibitively slow, and the simplicity of the approach (a single linear layer plus a one-time text encoding) lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the LTE-based generation strategy transfers to non-visual modalities or tasks where class labels lack natural language descriptions, and whether the speed-accuracy trade-off holds under more aggressive compression (e.g., very small student networks). The paper also demonstrates applicability to data-free quantization, suggesting the generation strategy is task-agnostic, but broader validation across diverse distillation settings remains to be done.
