## Problem

Diffusion-based decoders in neural image compression lack inductive bias for the multi-scale frequency structure of natural images, limiting their perceptual quality compared to GAN-based codecs. Additionally, existing Transformer-based entropy models rely on fixed clip-function positional encodings that cannot adaptively determine the optimal receptive field for each channel group, and parallel global context models (e.g., MEM) overlook positional encoding entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242060/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes an RGB image (256×256 crops during training; 768×512 Kodak and variable-resolution CLIC2020 images for evaluation) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. The encoder maps the input to a semantic latent y that is quantized and entropy-coded, while texture latent variables z_{1:T} are not stored but synthesized on-the-fly by a 500-step conditional blurring diffusion process during decoding, conditioned on the decoded semantic latent.

## Method

The decoder is a conditional non-isotropic (blurring) diffusion model operating in the DCT frequency domain, where each frequency component undergoes diffusion at a distinct rate governed by a combined Gaussian noise schedule and a blurring schedule d_t = exp(−λτ_t), enabling coarse-to-fine image synthesis. The entropy model adopts ELIC-style uneven channel grouping (16, 16, 32, 64, M−128) with a parallel bidirectional spatial context: a checkerboard convolution provides local context, and a Swin Transformer block (W-MSA + SW-MSA with checkerboard masks) extracts global spatial context. The key novelty is a learnable Laplacian-shaped relative positional encoding P_{Lap,j} = A_j² exp(−(|x|+|y|)/(2σ_j²)) applied within the self-attention, where A_j and σ_j are per-chunk parameters that dynamically widen or narrow the effective receptive field during joint optimization of the full codec. Training minimizes a rate-distortion-perception loss combining the diffusion ELBO (ε-prediction in pixel space), the entropy-coded rate, and an LPIPS term with β=0.9.

## Results

On the entropy model comparison (Table 2, Kodak dataset), the proposed parallel entropy model achieves a BD-rate of −8.25% relative to VVC with 196.3 ms decoding latency, outperforming MLIC (−7.89%, 207.4 ms) and ELIC (−5.71%, 156.9 ms). The Laplacian-shaped positional encoding reduces bitrate to 0.2347 bpp versus 0.2512 bpp for diamond-relative encoding and 0.2811 bpp without positional encoding (Table 1b). The blurring diffusion decoder with σ_B,max = 25 achieves FID 3.45, improving over the standard denoising diffusion baseline (FID 3.94). In the overall rate-perception comparison (Figure 4), the proposed codec achieves the best FID and LPIPS across all bitrates on both Kodak and CLIC2020, though at a slight PSNR cost relative to non-generative codecs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the rate-perception comparison (Figure 4), the proposed codec dominates all generative baselines—HiFiC [28], Multi-Realism [4], DIRAC [11], and CDC [42]—achieving the lowest FID and LPIPS at every bitrate on both Kodak and CLIC2020. On rate-distortion (PSNR), however, the method is outperformed by non-generative codecs (DGML, NSC, VVC) and is roughly competitive with CDC, confirming the expected rate-distortion-perception trade-off. For the entropy model specifically (Table 2), the proposed parallel variant achieves a BD-rate of −8.25% vs. VVC, beating MLIC (−7.89%) by 0.36 percentage points while also reducing decoding latency from 207.4 ms to 196.3 ms. Compared to ELIC's uneven-channel + local-context model (−5.71%), the proposed model saves an additional 2.54 percentage points in BD-rate. The serial variant reaches −9.38% BD-rate but requires >10³ ms, making the parallel version the practical choice. The Laplacian positional encoding ablation (Table 1b) shows a 0.0165 bpp saving over diamond-relative encoding and 0.0464 bpp over no positional encoding, validating the design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active threads in learned image compression: generative decoders (HiFiC, CDC, DIRAC) and adaptive entropy models (ELIC, Entroformer, MLIC). It departs from prior diffusion-based codecs by replacing the standard isotropic DDPM decoder with a non-isotropic blurring diffusion model that operates in DCT frequency space, imposing a coarse-to-fine inductive bias absent from CDC and DIRAC. On the entropy-model side, it extends ELIC's uneven channel grouping and Entroformer's Transformer-based global context by introducing a Laplacian-shaped positional encoding with per-chunk learnable parameters, replacing the clip-based diamond encoding of Entroformer with a smoother, adaptively-tuned receptive field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that frequency-aware inductive bias in the decoder can close the perceptual-quality gap between diffusion-based and GAN-based codecs while preserving text and semantic content better than HiFiC or CDC. The Laplacian positional encoding offers a principled alternative to clip-based receptive fields, and the observation that later channel chunks require narrower receptive fields provides a useful design heuristic for future entropy models. However, the 500-step diffusion decoder remains a significant computational bottleneck for practical deployment, and the perceptual gains come at a measurable PSNR cost, leaving open the question of whether fewer diffusion steps or distillation can retain the quality advantage at acceptable latency.
