## Problem

The ArIB framework for lossless image compression splits images spatially, which uniformly distributes latent variable information across subimages and causes inadequate use of latent variables when some subimages are excluded. Additionally, the growing autoregressive prior along the subimage sequence increases posterior collapse risk, and since all spatially-split subimages carry similar importance for latent variables, this risk is unavoidable. The paper asks whether a different splitting strategy can align subimage importance with autoregressive position to mitigate both issues.

## Input / Output

The method takes 8-bit RGB images (32×32 or 64×64 for training, 256×256 patches for DIV2K evaluation) and produces a lossless compressed bitstream encoded via ANS. The image is decomposed into 8 bit planes, with a fixed slice index s=4 separating significant planes (1:4, encoded with latent variables) from insignificant planes (5:8, used to provide initial bits). For single-image compression, the discretization precision k is computed per image from the available initial bits and clipped between 3 and 10.

## Method

ArIB-BPS replaces spatial splitting with bit plane slicing, arranging subimages from most significant plane (MSP) to least significant plane (LSP) so that planes most important for latent variables occupy early autoregressive positions with lower posterior collapse risk. A dimension-tailored autoregressive model handles three dimensions with matched complexity: a U-Net with diffusion-model architecture (Φ_p) for the plane dimension, a 4-stage checkerboard model with a single ResBlock (Φ_s) for the space dimension, and a non-network Bernoulli autoregression for the color dimension, with a fusion network Θ (ECANet + wider ResBlocks) combining plane and space features. Hierarchical latent variables (3 layers) are inferred top-down from the significant planes, with image-adaptive equal-mass discretization whose precision k is derived from the bit budget of the insignificant planes. A discretized sampling technique using logistic distributions and the Straight-Through Estimator is introduced for fine-tuning at low precision values.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ArIB-BPS achieves 3.06 BPD (dataset compression) and 3.07 BPD (single-image compression), outperforming the best normalizing-flow baseline iFlow (3.12 / 37.40 BPD) and LBB (3.12 / 42.98 BPD). On ImageNet64, it reaches 3.63 / 3.64 BPD versus iFlow's 3.70 / 38.12 BPD. On the high-resolution DIV2K dataset, ArIB-BPS attains 2.55 / 2.59 BPD, surpassing JPEG-XL (2.79) and iFlow (2.57 / —). Inference time is comparable to iFlow: ~15 ms/sample on CIFAR-10 and ~108 ms/sample on ImageNet64 versus iFlow's ~48 ms and ~58 ms respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArIB-BPS outperforms all 13 baselines in single-image compression across every dataset where a comparison is available, with the most dramatic gains over flow-based methods that suffer from large bits-back overhead (e.g., 3.07 vs. 37.40 BPD for iFlow on CIFAR-10 single-image). In dataset compression, ArIB-BPS achieves the best result on CIFAR-10 (3.06 vs. 3.12 for iFlow/LBB), ImageNet64 (3.63 vs. 3.70), and DIV2K (2.55 vs. 2.57 for iFlow), but trails iFlow slightly on ImageNet32 (3.91 vs. 3.88). Compared to the direct predecessor SHVC-ArIB, ArIB-BPS improves by more than 0.10 BPD on all low-resolution datasets in both compression settings. Against traditional codecs, ArIB-BPS surpasses JPEG-XL by 0.24 BPD on DIV2K and FLIF by 0.36 BPD on CIFAR-10. Inference time is competitive: ArIB-BPS runs at ~15 ms/sample on CIFAR-10 (vs. iFlow's 48 ms decode) and ~108 ms on ImageNet64 (vs. iFlow's 58 ms decode), confirming the paper's claim of comparable speed with superior compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the ArIB framework from SHVC-ArIB [28], which combines subimage autoregression with latent variable models for lossless compression. The key departure is replacing spatial image splitting with bit plane slicing (BPS), motivated by the observation that the importance of latent variables for global data modalities decreases monotonically from the most significant to the least significant bit plane. The autoregressive component draws on the checkerboard context model [9] for spatial dependencies and the non-network color autoregression from PixelCNN++ [29], while the latent variable machinery (hierarchical VAE, equal-mass discretization, bits-back coding) follows Hilloc [35] and LBB [12]. A novel dimension-tailored autoregressive model is introduced to handle the additional plane dimension without uniformly increasing network complexity across all dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BPS ordering provides a principled solution to posterior collapse in ArIB methods by placing subimages most important for latent variables in positions with weaker autoregressive priors, a structural insight that could inform other latent-variable-based compression designs. The dimension-tailored autoregressive model demonstrates that matching model complexity to dimension-specific difficulty (non-network for color, simple ResBlock for space, U-Net for plane) yields better performance than a uniform architecture, a practical design principle for multi-dimensional autoregressive models. The main limitation is that BPS complexity grows with bit-depth, restricting the method to 8-bit images; extending to high-bit-depth medical or scientific imaging would require handling multiple planes jointly. Single-image compression remains the primary practical advantage over flow-based methods, which is critical for real-world deployment where per-image overhead cannot be amortized.
