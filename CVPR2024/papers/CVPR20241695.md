## Problem

Tracking-by-detection MOT trackers rely on Kalman Filter motion prediction, which assumes constant velocity and direction, making them fail on non-linear motion scenarios such as dancers performing all-directional jumps or athletes with rapid acceleration/deceleration. Prior neural-network-based motion predictors (LSTM, Transformer, optical flow) either lack sufficient accuracy or are too computationally expensive for real-time deployment. No existing method simultaneously achieves accurate non-linear motion prediction and real-time speed.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes per-frame bounding box detections from a YOLOX detector and a conditioning vector C_f ∈ R^{n×8} built from the previous n=5 frames, where each frame contributes an 8-dimensional motion information vector (x, y, w, h, Δx, Δy, Δw, Δh). The D²MP outputs a single predicted motion vector M_{f,0} = (Δx, Δy, Δw, Δh) per object via one-step sampling from a standard normal distribution, which is added to the previous frame's box to yield the predicted bounding box. Association then matches predicted boxes against current-frame detections using two-stage Hungarian matching (high-score and low-score thresholds), producing tracks with consistent IDs.

## Method

DiffMOT introduces a Decoupled Diffusion-based Motion Predictor (D²MP) that formulates motion prediction as a generative denoising task. The forward process splits the standard data-to-noise mapping into two analytic sub-processes—data-to-zero (linear attenuation) and zero-to-noise (√t scaling)—enabling a closed-form reverse process that requires only one sampling step rather than thousands. A one-branch architecture parameterizes only the attenuation constant c_Θ (deriving z_Θ analytically from c_Θ), halving the network complexity versus the original two-branch decoupled diffusion design. The HMINet backbone uses 6 stacked multi-head self-attention layers to extract a condition embedding from historical motion, then fuses it with the noisy motion feature via a scale-and-shift motion fusion layer before an MLP outputs c_Θ. Trained with smooth L1 loss against ground-truth c, the full pipeline (YOLOX-X detection + D²MP prediction + ByteTrack-style association) runs at 22.7 FPS on an RTX 3090 while achieving 62.3% HOTA on DanceTrack and 76.2% on SportsMOT.

## Results

On DanceTrack (the primary non-linear motion benchmark), DiffMOT with YOLOX-X achieves 62.3% HOTA, 63.0% IDF1, 47.2% AssA, 92.8% MOTA, and 82.5% DetA at 22.7 FPS, outperforming the previous SOTA Deep OC-SORT by 1.0% in HOTA. On SportsMOT with detectors trained on train+val sets, DiffMOT reaches 76.2% HOTA and 76.1% IDF1, surpassing MixSort-OC by 2.1% in HOTA. On the pedestrian-dominant MOT17 dataset, DiffMOT achieves 64.5% HOTA, remaining competitive with SOTA methods despite being designed for non-linear motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DanceTrack, DiffMOT (62.3% HOTA) outperforms the previous SOTA Deep OC-SORT (61.3%) by 1.0%, C-BIoU (60.6%) by 1.7%, and the concurrent diffusion-based DiffusionTrack (52.4%) by 9.9%, with the largest gains in association metrics (IDF1 +1.5%, AssA +1.4% over Deep OC-SORT). On SportsMOT with train+val detectors, DiffMOT (76.2% HOTA) beats MixSort-OC (74.1%) by 2.1% and OC-SORT (73.7%) by 2.5%, while in the train-only setup it leads OC-SORT (71.9%) by 0.2%. On MOT17, DiffMOT (64.5% HOTA) is slightly below SparseTrack (65.1%) and Deep OC-SORT (64.9%) but above StrongSORT (64.4%) and MixSort-OC (63.4%), confirming it remains competitive in pedestrian-dominant linear-motion scenarios. The ablation in Table 5 further shows D²MP outperforms Transformer-based motion prediction (54.6% HOTA) by 1.1% and LSTM (51.2%) by 4.5% on DanceTrack validation, while the one-step one-branch D²MP-OB achieves 55.7% HOTA at 22.7 FPS versus 54.6% at 7.5 FPS for the two-branch 20-step variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffMOT builds directly on the decoupled diffusion model (DDM) framework, which splits the standard data-to-noise process into data-to-zero and zero-to-noise sub-processes, enabling analytic one-step sampling. For the tracking pipeline, it adopts ByteTrack's two-stage Hungarian association and YOLOX detection, while replacing the conventional Kalman Filter motion predictor with a diffusion-based generative model. Conceptually, it departs from prior non-linear motion predictors (LSTM, Transformer, optical-flow) by formulating motion prediction as a distributional generative task conditioned on historical trajectories, rather than a deterministic regression. It is positioned as the first work to introduce a diffusion probabilistic model into the MOT pipeline specifically for non-linear motion prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a diffusion-based motion predictor can operate at real-time speed (22.7 FPS) while outperforming Kalman Filter and Transformer-based alternatives on non-linear datasets, DiffMOT opens a new research direction for generative motion modeling in tracking. The practical impact is significant for applications involving dancers, athletes, or any scene with (ac/de)celeration and irregular direction changes where linear predictors fail. Limitations include dependence on a strong external detector (YOLOX-X), evaluation limited to two non-linear benchmarks plus MOT17, and the one-step sampling, while fast, still relies on the decoupled diffusion's analytic structure that may not generalize to more complex generative objectives. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D tracking, longer occlusions, or scenes with hundreds of objects.
