## Problem

Existing multimodal 3D pre-training frameworks (e.g., ULIP, OpenShape) rely on manually annotated category names and short metadata-derived descriptions as the language counterpart for 3D shapes, making them neither scalable to large datasets nor comprehensive in semantic detail. This paper asks: can we automatically generate rich, holistic language descriptions for 3D shapes using only the 3D data itself, eliminating all human annotation while improving multimodal 3D representation learning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pre-training pipeline takes only raw 3D shape data as input: point clouds are extracted at 10k, 8k, or 2k points (depending on downstream task), and 12 (Objaverse) or 30 (ShapeNet) equally spaced RGB images are rendered per shape. For each rendered image, BLIP-2 generates 10 candidate descriptions, which are ranked by CLIP image-text similarity and the top-1 is retained as the language modality. Downstream, the trained 3D encoder produces class labels for zero-shot or fine-tuned classification, or feeds into X-InstructBLIP with a frozen LLM to generate free-form 3D captions evaluated by CIDEr score.

## Method

ULIP-2's core contribution is a scalable triplet-creation paradigm: given a 3D shape, it renders images from a fixed set of holistic viewpoints and uses BLIP-2 (opt-6.7B) to generate detailed per-view descriptions, aggregating them into a comprehensive language modality that requires no human annotation. For pre-training, a 3D encoder (Point-BERT or PointNeXt) is trained to align its point-cloud features into the frozen OpenCLIP ViT-G/14 shared image-text feature space via two symmetric contrastive losses (3D-to-image and 3D-to-text), following the CLIP-style formulation. The key architectural upgrade over ULIP is scaling the vision-language backbone from SLIP ViT-B to OpenCLIP ViT-G/14, which provides a richer pre-aligned target space. The approach is essentially pseudo-self-supervised: all supervision comes from the 3D data itself plus the distilled knowledge of the large multimodal model, enabling training on any unannotated 3D dataset.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D classification, ULIP-2 (Point-BERT backbone, pre-trained on Objaverse + ShapeNet) achieves 50.6% top-1 accuracy on Objaverse-LVIS, surpassing the previous SOTA OpenShape (46.8%) by 3.8%, and 84.7% top-1 on ModelNet40. On standard fine-tuned 3D classification (ScanObjectNN hardest set), ULIP-2 with the lightweight PointNeXt backbone (1.4M parameters) reaches 91.5% overall accuracy, a new record on the benchmark. For 3D-to-language generation via X-InstructBLIP, ULIP-2 pre-trained encoders improve CIDEr score from 132.2 (ULIP) to 160.5, a 28.3% gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULIP-2 outperforms the previous SOTA OpenShape [22] by 3.8% top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS (50.6% vs 46.8%) and by 0.3% on ModelNet40 (84.7% vs 84.4%), despite using a simpler framework without manual captions or extra datasets. Compared to its direct predecessor ULIP [52] under identical pre-training data, ULIP-2 gains 15.4% top-1 on ModelNet40 and 24.9% on Objaverse-LVIS when pre-trained on Objaverse(no LVIS) + ShapeNet, demonstrating the impact of BLIP-2-generated holistic captions and the scaled-up ViT-G/14 backbone. On ScanObjectNN, ULIP-2 with PointNeXt (1.4M params) achieves 91.5% overall accuracy, surpassing I2P-MAE [59] (90.1%), Recon-s [34] (89.5%), P2P [47] (89.3%), and ACT [7] (88.2%), while using far fewer parameters than most competitors (e.g., 22.1M for Point-BERT, 15.3M for Point-M2AE [57]). For 3D captioning, ULIP-2 pre-trained encoders improve CIDEr by 28.3% over ULIP (160.5 vs 132.2) within the same X-InstructBLIP framework. ULIP-2 also substantially outperforms earlier zero-shot methods like PointCLIPv2 [62] (63.6% ModelNet40 top-1) and CLIP2Point [11] (49.5%) by large margins, confirming the advantage of holistic multi-view language descriptions over prompt-based pseudo-captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ULIP-2 directly extends ULIP, the first framework to align 3D point clouds, rendered images, and language in a shared feature space for 3D representation learning. The key conceptual departure is replacing ULIP's reliance on manually curated category names and metadata-derived captions with automatically generated holistic-view descriptions from BLIP-2, applied to multi-view renderings of each 3D shape. It also scales the frozen vision-language backbone from SLIP ViT-B to OpenCLIP ViT-G/14, and positions itself against the concurrent OpenShape, which achieved strong results but still depended on manual 3D annotations and a more complex data pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any human 3D annotation, ULIP-2 makes multimodal 3D pre-training applicable to arbitrary unlabeled 3D datasets, addressing the core scalability bottleneck that has limited prior work. The released ULIP-Objaverse (~800K shapes) and ULIP-ShapeNet (~52.5K shapes) triplets are substantial community resources. A key open question is whether this object-level paradigm transfers to scene-level 3D understanding, where the distribution and complexity differ substantially. The approach also naturally benefits from future improvements in large multimodal models, suggesting a compounding trajectory.
