## Problem

Image downscaling research has stalled due to the absence of a quantitative evaluation metric: full-reference IQA methods (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) require ground-truth LR images that do not exist, and no-reference IQA metrics rely on HR 'naturalness' statistics that are absent in LR images. This paper proposes IDA-RD, a process-based measure derived from Shannon's rate-distortion theory that evaluates how much information a downscaling algorithm preserves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241465/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N_X high-resolution RGB images (default 900 from FFHQ at 1024×1024) and an arbitrary downscaling algorithm f_ds to evaluate. It produces a single scalar IDA-RD score (lower = better) computed as the expected LPIPS distortion between each original HR image and N_Q (default 5) stochastic super-resolution reconstructions of its downscaled version. The downscaling algorithm is treated as a black box—no knowledge of its internals is required—and the metric works across scaling factors from 4× to 32×.

## Method

The core formulation treats downscaling as the encoding step and super-resolution as the decoding step in a rate-distortion model, defining the score S(f_ds) = E_x{E_{x̂|x}[D(x, x̂)]} where D is LPIPS. The critical challenge is that the SR function f_us must be both blind (applicable to any downscaling algorithm without retraining) and stochastic (producing a conditional distribution of HR reconstructions), which is infeasible for supervised many-to-many learning due to data scarcity. The authors overcome this by leveraging pre-trained deep generative models: either a StyleGAN generator with PULSE-style GAN inversion (optimizing latent codes so that G(z*) downscales to match the input LR image) or, more efficiently, a pre-trained SRFlow normalizing flow that explicitly models p(x̂|I_LR) via invertible mappings. SRFlow is preferred in the final metric for its lower computational cost while maintaining comparable discriminative power.

## Results

IDA-RD achieves perfect Spearman rank correlation (ρ = 1 or ρ = −1) with degradation levels across all tested degradation types (Gaussian blur, noise, contrast change, quantization), confirming monotonic sensitivity. On real-world 4× downscaling (FFHQ), the metric correctly orders traditional methods as Bilinear (0.090) < Bicubic (0.110) < Nearest Neighbor (0.512), while revealing that SOTA methods (DPID 0.127, L0-reg. 0.213, Perceptual 0.398) actually lose more information than Bilinear. Camera-captured LR images achieve the lowest score (0.047 on RealSR), validating that hardware downscaling preserves the most information. The metric scales correctly with scaling factor: IDA-RD increases from ~0.06 (4×) to ~0.23 (32×) for bicubic downscaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NR-IQA metrics (NIQE, BRISQUE, MANIQA, CONTRIQUE), IDA-RD is the only measure that correctly distinguishes degradation levels at extreme downscaling (e.g., 32×32 resolution), where NIQE fails entirely and BRISQUE gives incorrect scores; MANIQA and CONTRIQUE cannot separate σ=2.0 from σ=4.0 Gaussian blur and exhibit resolution bias. Against non-blind/non-stochastic SR backbones (ESRGAN, BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, SR3, RSR), the blind stochastic SRFlow-based IDA-RD produces far more discriminative scores, while ESRGAN-family methods fail to distinguish among downscaling algorithms at all. Among the six evaluated downscaling methods, IDA-RD reveals a counterintuitive finding: SOTA methods (DPID, L0-reg., Perceptual) all score worse than Bilinear (0.090), suggesting they sacrifice information content for perceptual appeal, though DPID and L0-reg. (0.127, 0.213) remain slightly better than Perceptual (0.398). The metric also successfully evaluates 'unknown downscaling' images from DIV2K (0.355) and camera-captured LR images from RealSR (0.047), the latter confirming that camera hardware downscaling preserves more information than any software method tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Shannon's rate-distortion theory as a formal framework for image downscaling evaluation, treating downscaling as the encoding step and super-resolution as the decoding step. The key enabling insight is that recent blind and stochastic super-resolution models—specifically SRFlow (a normalizing flow) and PULSE (a GAN-inversion approach)—satisfy the two requirements (blindness and stochasticity) that make the rate-distortion formulation practically computable, sidestepping the data scarcity problem that would otherwise require many-to-many LR-HR training pairs. The paper departs from both full-reference IQA metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and no-reference IQA metrics (NIQE, BRISQUE, MANIQA, CONTRIQUE), which the authors demonstrate are inapplicable to LR images due to the absence of ground-truth LR references and the lack of 'naturalness' statistics in downscaled images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This metric fills a long-standing gap that the authors argue has stalled progress in image downscaling research, providing the first process-based quantitative evaluation that does not require ground-truth LR images. A notable finding is that state-of-the-art downscaling methods (DPID, L0-regularized, Perceptual) score worse than simple Bilinear on IDA-RD, revealing that their perceptual-quality optimizations come at the cost of greater information loss—a trade-off invisible to visual inspection. Limitations include dependence on pre-trained generative models that are domain-specific (StyleGAN for faces, SRFlow trained on DIV2K/Flickr2K), and the metric's stability degrades on non-face real-world datasets at extreme scaling factors. Open questions include whether the metric generalizes to video, 3D, or non-photographic content, and whether future diffusion-based SR models can further improve its discriminative power.
