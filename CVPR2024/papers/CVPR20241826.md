## Problem

Prompt learning methods for vision-language models suffer from task overfitting: when soft prompts are fine-tuned on small task-specific datasets, they discard the general knowledge of the pre-trained VLM and perform worse than zero-shot CLIP on unseen classes. Existing regularization strategies require manual tuning of strength and are not universally beneficial, making it nontrivial to balance task-specific adaptation against preserving generalizability.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone augmented with 4 learnable soft prompt tokens per modality (visual and textual). It produces a class probability distribution over N_c classes via cosine similarity between prompted image and text embeddings. Training operates in a 16-shot regime (16 images per base class), and evaluation covers both base (seen) and new (unseen) classes across 11 datasets, as well as four out-of-distribution ImageNet variants for domain generalization.

## Method

ProMetaR formulates prompt learning as a bi-level (MAML-style) optimization that jointly meta-learns the soft prompts Θ and a gradient modulation function φ. In the inner loop, prompts are adapted using the sum of the loss gradient and a modulated regularizer gradient, where the modulation M^φ = σ(MLP_φ([g‖g_reg])) ⊙ g_reg adaptively scales the regularizer's contribution based on both gradients. In the outer loop, both Θ and φ are updated on an augmented validation set to minimize validation loss. To combat meta-overfitting in the single-task setting, task augmentation via manifold mixup interpolates features and labels between validation and training samples, generating diverse virtual tasks that act as a large meta-validation set. A Taylor-expansion analysis shows the objective simultaneously aligns training/validation gradients and aligns tuned-prompt gradients with the original VLM's general-knowledge gradients, explaining the improved task generalization.

## Results

On the base-to-base/base-to-new generalization setting (16-shot, 11 datasets), ProMetaR achieves 84.39% base accuracy, 76.93% new-class accuracy, and 80.49% harmonic mean, outperforming the best baseline PromptSRC (84.26/76.10/79.97) and the base method IVLP (82.51/73.35/77.66) by +1.88, +3.58, and +2.83 points respectively. On domain generalization (ImageNet → 4 OOD variants), ProMetaR attains 60.77% average accuracy, competitive with UNIGRAM (60.80%) and ahead of PromptSRC (60.65%) and RPO (60.28%). The method achieves the best average rank (1.36) across all 11 datasets in harmonic mean, compared to 2.73 for PromptSRC and 3.64 for MaPLe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMetaR outperforms all 8 baselines on the primary base-to-new benchmark. Against the strongest prompt-learning baseline PromptSRC, ProMetaR gains +0.13 on base, +0.83 on new, and +0.52 on harmonic mean; against MaPLe (the best multimodal prompt method), gains are +2.11/+1.79/+1.94. The largest improvements come over CoOp (+11.71 on new-class accuracy) and CoCoOp (+5.24 on new), confirming that meta-regularization most effectively rescues methods that overfit severely. Compared to UNIGRAM, which relies on large-scale external data for meta-learning, ProMetaR achieves +4.05 on base and +1.01 on new without any external data. On domain generalization, ProMetaR (60.77 avg) is marginally behind UNIGRAM (60.80) but ahead of all other methods including PromptSRC (60.65) and RPO (60.28). The method also serves as a plug-and-play module: when applied to CoOp it yields +5.14 harmonic mean gain, and to VPT +1.44, demonstrating broad applicability across prompting architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProMetaR sits at the intersection of prompt learning for vision-language models and meta-learning. It builds directly on MAML's bi-level optimization and one-step gradient update to formulate prompt adaptation, and uses MaPLe/IVLP as its base prompting architecture. The key departure from prior regularization-based prompt methods (ProGrad, PromptSRC) is that the regularizer itself is learned via meta-learning rather than fixed or heuristically tuned, and the key departure from UNIGRAM is that no external pre-training data is required. The task augmentation component repurposes manifold mixup to generate virtual tasks, addressing the 'single-task meta-learning' bottleneck that plagues gradient-based meta-learning in few-shot prompt tuning benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of ProMetaR (applicable to CoOp, VPT, IVLP, etc.) makes it a practical training wrapper that any prompt-learning pipeline can adopt without architectural changes. The finding that meta-learning alone degrades new-class generalization while task augmentation recovers it highlights a previously underappreciated failure mode in single-task meta-learning settings. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 16-shot image classification with CLIP-ViT-B/16, and the regularizer form (L1 distance to original embeddings) is specific to the prompt-tuning setup; whether the meta-regularization principle transfers to other parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigms or larger-scale tasks (detection, segmentation) remains open.
