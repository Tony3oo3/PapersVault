## Problem

Existing large-scale facial datasets are heavily biased toward near-frontal views, causing face generation, reenactment, and recognition models to produce distorted or low-quality outputs on extreme head poses (profile, pitched). Prior attempts like LPFF partially address this but are limited in scale (19k images), lack identity annotations, and cover only a subset of face-related tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241469/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests high-resolution face video clips from VFHQ (~5,000 clips) and CelebV-HQ, extracting per-frame bounding boxes, 5/68/478-point landmarks, quality scores, and identity labels. The final EFHQ dataset contains up to 450k extreme-pose images at 1024×1024 resolution, paired with frontal images of the same subject for reenactment and verification tasks. Task-specific sub-datasets are produced: 42,671 FFHQ-quality images for generation, the full set for reenactment, and 60,000 image pairs (frontal-frontal, frontal-profile, profile-profile) for a verification benchmark.

## Method

The core contribution is a meticulous multi-stage curation pipeline that filters and samples frames from VFHQ and CelebV-HQ. Pose estimation uses an ensemble of three heterogeneous estimators (SynergyNet 3DMM-based, DirectMHP landmark-free, and an in-house FacePoseNet augmented for extreme poses) with majority voting into a hierarchical binning scheme, followed by manual GUI-assisted review. For face generation, a brightness filter and the StyleGAN/EG3D preprocessing pipeline ensure FFHQ-comparable quality; for diffusion-based text-to-image, 478-point landmark condition images (Mediapipe-style connected edges) and structured text prompts (emotion, race, gender) are generated per image. The verification benchmark applies RetinaFace-based alignment filtering and random downscaling/compression to simulate varying image quality, yielding balanced 10k positive/negative pairs per pose scenario.

## Results

On the proposed EFHQ face verification benchmark (TAR@FAR=1e-3), state-of-the-art models suffer 5–37% drops on frontal-to-profile pairs compared to frontal-to-frontal: R18-ArcFace drops from 0.94 to 0.57 (−37%), while R100-AdaFace drops only from 0.99 to 0.94 (−5%). For face generation, adding EFHQ to FFHQ yields FID 3.9 on FFHQ test (vs. 6.2 for FFHQ+LPFF) with EG3D, and FID 83.39 vs. 119.74 for ControlNet fine-tuning on LPFF. For face reenactment, TPS trained on VoxCeleb1+EFHQ halves the AKD on the EFHQ test set (1.963 vs. 3.814) while maintaining comparable L1 on VoxCeleb1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LPFF [47], the closest competing extreme-pose dataset, EFHQ is substantially larger (450k vs. 19k images) and includes identity labels. In generation experiments, EG3D trained on FFHQ+EFHQ achieves FID 3.9 on the FFHQ test set versus 6.2 for FFHQ+LPFF, and the ControlNet fine-tuned on EFHQ reaches FID 83.39 vs. 119.74 for the LPFF-trained version (FID_eyepatch: 49.45 vs. 103.77). Against the pretrained ControlNet [49] baseline, the EFHQ fine-tuned model wins on all three metrics (NME 0.084 vs. 0.085 on LPFF; FID 83.39 vs. 119.74; FID_eyepatch 49.45 vs. 103.77). For face reenactment, both TPS [51] and LIA [43] trained on VoxCeleb1+EFHQ outperform their VoxCeleb1-only counterparts on the EFHQ test set: TPS reduces AKD from 3.814 to 1.963 and AED from 0.275 to 0.214, while LIA reduces AKD from 3.807 to 2.291 and AED from 0.266 to 0.233, with no degradation on the VoxCeleb1 test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in extreme-pose face data by curating 450K high-quality frames from VFHQ and CelebV-HQ video datasets, departing from the closest prior effort LPFF (19K images, no identity labels, generation-only) in both scale and multi-task applicability. Conceptually, it repurposes the video-to-frame extraction paradigm already used for VFHQ and CelebV-HQ but applies a novel ensemble pose-estimation pipeline (SynergyNet, DirectMHP, FacePoseNet with majority voting) and manual review to specifically target extreme-pose frames that standard pipelines discard. The downstream experiments position EFHQ as a drop-in supplement to FFHQ (for GAN/diffusion generation), VoxCeleb1 (for reenactment), and CPLFW (for verification), rather than proposing new architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a well-recognized but persistent gap: near-frontal bias in training data degrades all face-related models at profile and pitched views, yet no prior dataset simultaneously offered extreme-pose coverage, identity annotations, and multi-task readiness at this scale. The new pose-stratified verification benchmark (f2f/f2p/p2p) is a useful diagnostic tool that quantifies how much SOTA recognizers degrade under pose mismatch, and the finding that a larger ResNet100 model can underperform smaller ones on profile-profile pairs highlights a data-imbalance overfitting risk that the community should address. Open questions include whether 450K extreme-pose frames are sufficient to fully close the pose gap for all downstream tasks, and whether the pipeline's reliance on three pose estimators with majority voting generalizes to even more occluded or non-rigid scenarios.
