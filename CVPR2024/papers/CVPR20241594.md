## Problem

Existing LLM-based embodied agents in Minecraft assume all-seeing perception, while classical perception networks output only fixed results and cannot provide task-conditioned information. This paper tackles the compound challenge of solving long-horizon open-world tasks that are simultaneously process-dependent (requiring sequential sub-objective reasoning) and context-dependent (requiring selective perception of specific environmental elements like mobs, time, weather, and objects) in an embodied, ego-centric setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241594/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes ego-centric RGB screenshots from the MineDojo simulator along with a natural-language task instruction, inventory state, and localization data. It produces high-level action sequences (simple primitives like equip, move, craft — not keyboard/mouse commands) executed via the MineDojo API, a decomposed sub-objective list in natural language, and multi-round environmental perception descriptions. The perception output is not a single caption but a set of targeted, objective-conditioned answers gathered over multiple question-answering rounds, making it selective rather than exhaustive.

## Method

MP5 comprises five interacting modules: Parser (GPT-4 + RAG) decomposes the task into sub-objectives; Percipient (a LoRA-fine-tuned MLLM built on MineCLIP vision encoder + Vicuna-13B) answers targeted visual questions; Planner (GPT-4 + RAG + Performer Memory) schedules situation-aware action sequences; Performer executes actions via MineDojo; and Patroller (GPT-4) verifies outputs and relays feedback. The core mechanism is an Active Perception scheme: Patroller iteratively queries Percipient about specific items/factors relevant to the current sub-objective and action, accumulating responses into a Temporary Env. Info. Set that conditions both planning and execution. This multi-round, objective-conditioned perception replaces a single global caption, enabling the agent to detect when necessary context is present or missing and to trigger re-planning or continued execution accordingly. The Percipient is trained with parameter-efficient LoRA adapters on a Minecraft-specific instruction dataset, while Parser and Planner leverage retrieval-augmented generation over external knowledge (wiki, crafting recipes, Reddit tips).

## Results

On Context-Dependent Tasks, MP5 with Active Perception achieves 98.5% (Easy), 94.5% (Mid), 93.0% (Hard), and 91.0% (Complex) success rates, substantially outperforming LLaVA-1.5 (0.0% on Complex) and slightly exceeding GPT-4V (87.5% on Complex). On Process-Dependent Tasks, MP5 achieves 96.00% (Basic), 88.67% (Wooden), 76.00% (Stone), 52.00% (Iron), and 22.00% (Diamond) success rates. The 22% success rate on Diamond-level tasks (requiring >11 reasoning steps) represents the hardest long-horizon tasks evaluated, while the 91% on Complex context tasks (requiring perception of 4–6 key items) demonstrates strong scene understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.5, MP5 with Active Perception dominates across all difficulty levels, most dramatically on Complex tasks where MP5 achieves 91.0% versus LLaVA-1.5's 0.0%, and on Hard tasks (93.0% vs. 11.0%). Against GPT-4V, MP5 is highly competitive: it ties on Mid (94.5% both), slightly wins on Hard (93.0% vs. 92.5%) and Complex (91.0% vs. 87.5%), but marginally trails on Easy (98.5% vs. 100.0%). The ablation in Table 4 shows that removing situation-aware planning (w/o P.) drops Diamond-level success from 22.00% to 0.00%, and removing embodied execution (w/o E.) drops it to 14.00%, confirming both components are essential. In the Planner comparison (Table 6), GPT-4 as Planner achieves 22.00% on Diamond-level tasks, while GPT-3.5-turbo drops to 0.00% and Vicuna-13B achieves only 1.33% at Basic level, highlighting the critical role of strong reasoning in the planning module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based embodied agents in Minecraft (Voyager, DEPS, GITM), which use pre-trained LLMs as zero-shot planners but assume agents are all-seeing with perfect environmental knowledge. MP5 departs from this by introducing a goal-conditioned active perception module (a LoRA-fine-tuned MLLM, MineLLM) that engages in multi-round Q&A with a Patroller module, grounding planning and execution in actual observed visual context rather than privileged state. The system also repurposes retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over Minecraft-specific knowledge (wiki, crafting recipes, Reddit tips) to augment the Parser and Planner, building on the MineDojo/MineCLIP ecosystem for simulation and visual encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that active, objective-conditioned perception is not merely a nice-to-have but a critical enabler for embodied agents: removing it during execution drops success to 0% across all difficulty levels, a stark result that challenges the common assumption that high-level planning alone suffices. The 91% success rate on complex context-dependent tasks (requiring perception of 4–6 environmental elements) shows that multi-round MLLM-based perception can rival or exceed GPT-4V in domain-specific settings. However, the system's heavy reliance on GPT-4 for planning and its restriction to the MineDojo simulator limit immediate generalizability, leaving open whether the active perception paradigm transfers to other embodied domains or can be realized with open-source models.
