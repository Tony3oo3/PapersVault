## Problem

Existing scene text detectors focus on single-level text (word, line, or paragraph) without modeling the hierarchical relationships among different levels, limiting their ability to deliver complete semantic messages for scene understanding. Two-level approaches (e.g., CUTE, Unified Detector) rely on a two-stage paradigm where the layout stage is tightly coupled to and limited by the first-stage detection quality, and they cannot be easily extended to three or more levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene image (resized to 640×640 for training; 1120 or 800 height for testing depending on dataset) and produces three levels of text instance masks at H/4 × W/4 resolution along with their geometric layout relationships. The hierarchical output is structured so that word-level detections are grouped into lines via learned word-line relationships, and lines are further grouped into paragraphs via learned line-paragraph relationships, yielding a complete word-line-paragraph hierarchy in a single forward pass.

## Method

LayoutFormer uses a ResNet-50 backbone with a Transformer encoder to produce four-scale feature maps, then applies a Prior Location Sampler that selects only Top-K (K=32) representative foreground pixels per query from multi-scale features, reducing cross-attention complexity from O(NH'W'C) to O(NKC). Three Transformer decoders operate hierarchically: LineDecoder (4 layers, 256 queries) detects text lines as the base unit; WordDecoder (1 layer) splits lines into words via a decoupling mechanism where word queries are computed as X_line × X_per_line^T, avoiding full N_word self-attention; ParaDecoder (1 layer) aggregates lines into paragraphs by using line query features as cross-attention keys. Training uses multi-level text boxes directly as supervision (rather than relation matrices), with binary cross-entropy and dice losses at each level, and inference proceeds hierarchically from word boxes upward through learned inter-level relationships.

## Results

On MSRA-TD500 (line-level), LayoutFormer achieves state-of-the-art F-measure of 90.10% (R=88.32%, P=91.95%), outperforming the previous best (PCR, 87.0%) by 3.10%. On CTW1500, it reaches F=86.17%, and on Total-Text (word-level), F=87.12%. For hierarchical detection on HierText, LayoutFormer achieves PQ of 62.37 (line) and 53.76 (paragraph) for line-paragraph detection, and 49.24/46.21/42.97 for word/line/paragraph three-level detection, with an inference speed of 3.0 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MSRA-TD500, LayoutFormer achieves the best F-measure (90.10%), surpassing PCR (87.0%), FSG (88.1%), and Unified Detector (87.70%) by 3.10%, 2.00%, and 2.40% respectively. On CTW1500, LayoutFormer (F=86.17%) trails DPText-DETR (F=88.8%) by 2.63%, though it matches or exceeds most other methods. On Total-Text, LayoutFormer (F=87.12%) is slightly behind FSG (88.1%), Unified Detector (87.90%), and DPText-DETR (89.0%), which the authors attribute to those methods using more training epochs or larger pretraining datasets. For hierarchical line-paragraph detection on HierText, LayoutFormer (Line PQ=62.37, Para PQ=53.76) edges out Unified Detector with N=384 (62.23, 53.60) by 0.14% and 0.16% in PQ, while also outperforming all two-stage baselines (GCP API, GCN-PP, Mask-RCNN-Cluster, Max-DeepLab-Cluster). For three-level word-line-paragraph detection, no prior work exists, so LayoutFormer (Word PQ=49.24, Line PQ=46.21, Para PQ=42.97) serves as the first baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayoutFormer extends the Transformer-based detection paradigm (DETR, Mask2Former) to scene text by replacing Mask2Former's full-mask cross-attention with a Prior Location Sampler that selects only Top-K foreground features, reducing complexity from O(NH'W'C) to O(NKC). It departs from prior two-stage hierarchical approaches (CUTE, Unified Detector) that model inter-level relationships via affinity matrices or index labels on first-stage detection results; instead, LayoutFormer uses multi-level text boxes directly as supervision and learns word-line and line-paragraph relationships implicitly through dedicated decoders. The word decoupling mechanism (N_word = N_line × N_per_line) is a novel architectural choice that avoids the quadratic self-attention cost over all word queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework for three-level (word-line-paragraph) hierarchical text detection, providing a baseline for a task that previously had no end-to-end solution. The Prior Location Sampler is a broadly applicable efficiency technique for Transformer-based segmentation that could transfer beyond scene text. However, paragraph-level detection remains weak (42.97% PQ), inference speed is only 3.0 FPS, and the approach has not been validated on non-text hierarchical tasks, leaving open whether the hierarchical decoder architecture generalizes. The modest SOTA margins (0.14–0.16% PQ over Unified Detector) suggest the field still lacks a fundamentally better modeling of paragraph-level irregularity.
