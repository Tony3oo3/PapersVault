## Problem

When training and test distributions differ (domain shift, subpopulation imbalance), traditional data augmentation fails to bridge the gap, and existing cross-distribution generative methods require costly fine-tuning of diffusion models or complex multi-stage pipelines. This paper asks whether off-the-shelf large pre-trained models can be used as a plug-and-play, inference-only synthetic data generator that matches a target distribution from just a handful of unlabeled target images, without any model training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241844/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source dataset (e.g., 1000 real photos) and a small set of unlabeled target-distribution images (as few as 10–20 per class) as input. It produces a synthetic dataset of arbitrary size (e.g., 1000–1280 images per class) whose visual style and semantic attributes match the target distribution. The generation is controlled by a scalar edit-strength parameter C (drawn from N(c, 0.05)) that governs how strongly the distribution shift is applied, and optionally by ControlNet condition maps (Canny edges, HED, depth, segmentation) to preserve source image structure.

## Method

DoGE extracts a single Domain Gap Embedding Δz in CLIP latent space by computing the difference between the mean CLIP image embeddings of a randomly sampled source subset and the few target images (Eq. 1). To generate synthetic data, it adds C·Δz plus small Gaussian noise to each source image's CLIP embedding and feeds the result into Stable UnCLIP, a diffusion model that natively accepts CLIP embeddings as generation input, producing images in the target distribution. An optional ControlNet branch provides structural guidance (edge maps, depth, segmentation) to reduce deformation. A confidence-based cleaning step during downstream fine-tuning temporarily discards synthetic samples where the current model is highly confident but incorrect, preventing harmful generations from degrading training. The entire pipeline is inference-only with respect to the generative model, requiring no fine-tuning of Stable UnCLIP or CLIP.

## Results

On DomainNet unsupervised domain adaptation (Real→Painting/Infograph/Clipart/Sketch), DoGE achieves 44.00%, 18.71%, 45.61%, and 34.96% test accuracy respectively, outperforming all baselines by 2.5–10.3 points. On FMoW temporal domain adaptation (2002-12→2016-17), DoGE reaches 72.62% (Asia), 78.94% (Americas), and 78.14% (Oceania), again leading all methods. On the CelebA subpopulation shift task, DoGE attains 67.16% test accuracy versus 62.40% for RandAugment and 59.72% for DA-Fusion. In the GTA5→Cityscapes segmentation task, DoGE achieves 57.3% mIoU, surpassing DAFormer+DATUM (56.4%) while remaining inference-only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoGE outperforms all four primary baselines (RandAugment, DA-Fusion, DATUM, and Base) across every DomainNet and FMoW domain, with the largest margins on Sketch (+6.69 over DATUM) and Painting (+4.43 over DA-Fusion). On CelebA subpopulation shift, DoGE (67.16%) beats RandAugment (62.40%) by 4.76 points and DA-Fusion (59.72%) by 7.44 points. Against the fine-tuning-based LoRA generator, DoGE is complementary: LoRA alone yields 56.05% while LoRA+DoGE reaches 74.28% on CelebA. DoGE also improves all six UDA methods it is combined with (BSP, DANN, CDAN, MCD, MCC, MemSAC), with gains ranging from +0.45 (CDAN) to +2.87 (MCC) points on DomainNet Real→Painting. In segmentation, DoGE on top of DAFormer (57.3%) edges out DAFormer+DATUM (56.4%) by 0.9 points while requiring no fine-tuning of the generative model, whereas DATUM necessitates training a personalized Stable Diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoGE builds on CLIP's linear vector compositionality (Radford et al., 2021) to represent distributional differences as a simple mean-difference vector, and on Stable UnCLIP (Rombach et al., 2022) as a generation backbone that natively accepts CLIP image embeddings as input. It departs from prior cross-distribution data generation methods like DATUM and DomainStudio, which require domain-specific fine-tuning of diffusion models, by operating entirely in inference mode with no parameter updates. The approach also integrates ControlNet for structural preservation and borrows confidence-based filtering from Northcutt et al. (2021) for generation quality control, positioning itself as a composable, training-free alternative to both traditional augmentation (RandAugment) and fine-tuning-based generative augmentation (DA-Fusion, DATUM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, inference-only design lowers the barrier to cross-distribution data augmentation: practitioners need only a handful of unlabeled target images and no fine-tuning pipeline, making it immediately applicable in resource-constrained or rapidly shifting deployment scenarios. The method's demonstrated complementarity with UDA methods and LoRA fine-tuning suggests it can be layered onto existing adaptation stacks rather than replacing them. Key limitations remain: CLIP's latent space may not capture gaps it was never trained to represent (e.g., medical imaging), and the edit-strength hyperparameter C requires per-task tuning. Open questions include whether more expressive gap representations (beyond mean difference or PCA) could handle more complex or multi-modal distribution shifts, and whether the synthetic data can be leveraged more effectively through specialized training algorithms.
