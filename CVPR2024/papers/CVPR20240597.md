## Problem

Existing CLIP-based few-shot classification methods lack the flexibility to incorporate other pre-trained models that carry complementary prior knowledge. While CaFo explores ensembling multiple pre-trained models, it relies on heuristic deterministic weighting, requires extensive hyperparameter tuning, and cannot provide calibrated uncertainty estimates—critical limitations in low-data, high-risk settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240597/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed through frozen ResNet-50 backbones of CLIP, MoCo, and DINO) along with 1–16 labeled examples per class as the few-shot training set. It outputs a class label via argmax over the GP posterior mean and a per-sample predictive uncertainty estimate from the diagonal of the posterior covariance. The classification is formulated as label regression on one-hot targets, so the GP directly regresses C-dimensional probability vectors rather than a single scalar logit.

## Method

The paper proposes a Gaussian process regression framework where the prior mean is set to the zero-shot CLIP linear classifier (with learnable temperature τ and scale γ), injecting zero-shot knowledge as a data-independent prediction baseline. The prior kernel is an adaptive sum of deep kernels, each defined by a frozen pre-trained model (CLIP, MoCo, DINO) with an RBF base kernel and learnable per-dimension length-scales, so complementary inductive biases are integrated without retraining the backbones. All hyperparameters—noise variance, length-scales, τ, and γ—are tuned by maximizing the predictive likelihood on a validation split (or marginal likelihood under 1-shot ImageNet where no split is feasible), using Adam for ~100 steps. Because label regression preserves conjugacy, the posterior is available in closed form, enabling analytical uncertainty quantification, principled hyperparameter selection, and OOD detection via elevated predictive variance.

## Results

On ImageNet, the proposed GP ensemble achieves 63.07% top-1 accuracy at 1-shot and 70.77% at 16-shot, outperforming the strongest ensemble baseline Ens-LP† (61.77% and 69.83%) and Ens-CaFo (62.09% and 68.78%) across all shot settings. Against leading CLIP-based few-shot learners, the method leads by 1.72–5.92 points over CALIP-FS and by 7.82 points over CoOp at 16-shot. On OOD evaluation, the method attains 61.30% on ImageNet-V2 and 36.58% on ImageNet-Sketch, surpassing all ensemble baselines, and achieves the best TACE (0.0169) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all six compared baselines on ImageNet across every shot setting. Against the ensemble baselines, it beats Ens-CaFo by 0.98 points at 1-shot and 1.99 points at 16-shot, and surpasses Ens-LP† by 1.30 and 0.94 points respectively. Against leading CLIP-based few-shot learners, the advantage is more pronounced: at 16-shot, the method achieves 70.77% versus CALIP-FS (65.81%), Tip-Adapter-F (65.51%), CLIP-Adapter (63.59%), VT-CLIP (63.92%), and CoOp (62.95%), yielding margins of 4.96, 5.26, 7.18, 6.85, and 7.82 points respectively. On OOD robustness (Table 3), the method leads Ens-CaFo by 1.68 points on ImageNet-V2 and 0.46 points on ImageNet-Sketch at 16-shot. For calibration, the method achieves the best TACE (0.0169) compared to Ens-CaFo (0.0545), Ens-LP (0.0462), and Ens-LP† (0.0477), though its ECE (0.0786) is slightly worse than Ens-CaFo (0.0577).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Gaussian Process regression as a probabilistic ensemble framework for combining frozen pre-trained models (CLIP, MoCo, DINO) in few-shot image classification. It departs from standard Deep Kernel Learning by replacing the trainable feature extractor with frozen pre-trained model outputs, and from CaFo's heuristic logit-fusion ensemble by using GP kernel summation with analytically optimizable hyperparameters. The key conceptual move is assigning the zero-shot CLIP classifier to the GP mean function, thereby injecting strong prior knowledge that the kernel ensemble alone cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, uncertainty-aware alternative to the growing family of deterministic CLIP-based few-shot methods, which are prone to overfitting and cannot quantify predictive confidence. The finding that a simple GP with three frozen ResNet-50 backbones outperforms more elaborate prompt-learning and adapter-tuning pipelines suggests that the bottleneck in few-shot classification may be knowledge integration rather than model complexity. Open questions include scalability to larger class counts or more pre-trained models, whether the GP framework extends naturally to other modalities or tasks, and whether the label-regression approximation remains adequate for highly imbalanced or fine-grained settings.
