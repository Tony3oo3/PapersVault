## Problem

Radar-based automotive perception suffers from low spatial resolution, multipath reflections, ghost targets, and motion blur, which are further exacerbated by nonlinear object motion at low frame rates from an ego-centric viewpoint. Prior work such as TempoRadar exploits temporal relations over only two consecutive frames, which is insufficient to capture the full motion trajectory of fast-moving or nonlinearly moving objects, leading to incorrect feature-level and object-level association that propagates across frames.

## Input / Output

The method takes T consecutive bird's-eye-view (BEV) radar frames (1152×1152, center-cropped to 256×256) as input, with T=4 in the main experiments. Within the ETR module, frames are cyclically shifted and concatenated along the channel dimension for backbone feature extraction, so each frame's encoder input contains a different ordering of the U-frame window. The pipeline outputs oriented bounding boxes (center, width, length, orientation, sub-pixel offsets, and confidence heatmap) for detection, and at inference produces object tracks with persistent IDs via a Kalman-filter-based tracker (OC-SORT) augmented with pseudo-tracklet-based association.

## Method

SIRA comprises two modules: Extended Temporal Relation (ETR) and Motion Consistency Track (MCTrack). ETR generalizes TempoRadar's two-frame temporal relation layer to U≥2 frames by combining temporal window attention (masked multi-head cross-attention within U-frame windows) with temporally regrouped window attention (sub-frame patches from different windows are regrouped in a deformable temporal order for cross-window attention at O(TM/U × TM/U) complexity), inspired by Swin Transformer's shifted windows but applied along the temporal axis. MCTrack introduces a pseudo-direction estimation (DEst) decoder head using deformable convolution to predict object displacement between any pair of frames during training, supervised by a pseudo-direction loss that enforces inter-frame positional consistency. At inference, these pseudo-directions construct pseudo-tracklets independent of the Kalman filter state, which are used in association via a combined similarity metric (C^tracklet measuring GIoU between pseudo-tracklet observations and the KF trajectory, and C^angle measuring GIoU between the current observation and a rotation-corrected KF predicted state). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a bidirectional backward-forward pass through all T frames, jointly optimizing the oriented bounding box loss and the pseudo-direction loss.

## Results

On the Radiate dataset (train good weather split), SIRA-34(4) achieves 58.11 mAP@0.5 for oriented object detection and 47.79 MOTA / 51.13 IDF1 for multi-object tracking (with OC-SORT), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art TempoRadar-34(4) by +4.11 mAP@0.5 and +9.94 MOTA. On the more challenging 'train good & bad weather' split, SIRA-34(4) reaches 53.79 mAP@0.5, again outperforming all baselines. The improvement is consistent across both ResNet-18 and ResNet-34 backbones and across all tracking metrics (MOTA, IDF1, IDs, Frag, MT, PT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIRA consistently outperforms all single-frame baselines (RetinaNet, CenterPoint, BBAVectors) by large margins—e.g., SIRA-34(4) achieves 58.11 mAP@0.5 versus 50.17 for CenterPoint-34(1) and 51.26 for BBAVectors-34(1). Against the primary prior SOTA, TempoRadar-34(4), SIRA gains +1.10 mAP@0.5 in detection and +3.78 MOTA in tracking (47.79 vs. 44.01 with OC-SORT). The simple sequential extension SeTR-34(4) is also surpassed by SIRA by +1.50 mAP@0.5 and +3.22 MOTA, confirming that the structured window-attention design of ETR is more effective than naive sequential self-attention. Among trackers, pairing SIRA with OC-SORT yields the best MOTA (47.79) and IDF1 (51.13), while SIRA+CenterTrack (47.30 MOTA) is slightly lower, indicating that the pseudo-tracklet association metric in MCTrack synergizes best with observation-centric tracking. The ablation (Table 3d) further shows that using both C^tracklet and C^angle association metrics together is necessary for the full tracking gain over ETR alone (47.79 vs. 47.11 MOTA).

## Relation to Prior Work

SIRA directly extends TempoRadar (Li et al., CVPR 2022), which introduced a temporal relation layer operating on two consecutive BEV radar frames, by generalizing the cross-frame attention to an arbitrary window of U frames via a shifted-window mechanism borrowed from Swin Transformer. The key architectural departure is the Temporally Regrouped Window Attention (TRWA), which partitions per-frame features into sub-patches and reassembles them across windows in a deformable temporal order to enable cross-window interaction at sub-quadratic cost. On the tracking side, SIRA augments standard KF-based trackers (OC-SORT) with a pseudo-tracklet association metric derived from a learnable pseudo-direction estimator, going beyond the single-observation correlation used in prior SORT-family methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Radar-only perception remains a critical but underdeveloped modality for autonomous driving in adverse weather, and the +3.8 MOTA gain over the same 4-frame TempoRadar baseline (47.79 vs 44.01) demonstrates that longer-horizon temporal modeling and motion-consistent association are meaningful levers for radar tracking. The scalable window-attention design makes multi-frame radar perception practical without the quadratic cost of full temporal attention, which is important for real-time deployment. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (Radiate) and a single BEV radar configuration; generalization to other radar geometries, 3D radar, or multi-sensor fusion settings remains untested. An open question is whether the pseudo-tracklet association strategy transfers to non-KF trackers or to higher frame-rate regimes where nonlinear motion is less pronounced.
