## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning methods pre-select specific layers (e.g., classifier heads or feature extractors) for personalization, imposing a coarse-grained, shared partition across all clients. This layer-wise selection may not optimally balance global knowledge sharing with local personalization, and the fixed architecture ignores that the optimal personalized subnetwork varies with each client's local data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240848/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/CIFAR-10-C, 84×84 Mini-ImageNet, variable-resolution OfficeHome) and outputs class labels via a ResNet-18 backbone. Unusually, the training process also produces a per-client binary mask that partitions all network parameters into a globally aggregated subset and a client-specific personalized subset, with the personalized portion growing incrementally across communication rounds until reaching a user-defined personalization limit α.

## Method

FedSelect introduces an FL Gradient-based Lottery Ticket Hypothesis: parameters that change the most during local client training are the ones that should be personalized, while those changing the least should remain globally aggregated. The core sub-procedure, GradSelect, trains the client model via LocalAlt (alternating SGD passes on global and personalized parameters), then computes the element-wise absolute change in global parameters before and after training; the top p% by magnitude are promoted to personalized status via a binary OR with the existing mask, so the personalized subnetwork only grows over time. Global aggregation is performed only on parameters where each client's mask is 0, with per-parameter weighted averaging tracking which clients contribute to each position. This yields a first-of-its-kind parameter-wise (rather than layer-wise) personalization that adapts the subnetwork structure to each client's local distribution while maintaining comparable time complexity to existing alternating-minimization PFL methods.

## Results

FedSelect achieves the highest personalized accuracy across all four benchmark datasets: 82.25% on CIFAR-10, 72.05% on CIFAR-10-C, 38.69% on Mini-ImageNet, and 80.51% on OfficeHome. This represents an average improvement of over 2.4% over the strongest baseline (LG-FedAvg) across datasets. The method also demonstrates robustness to limited data, maintaining strong performance even with as few as 100 training samples per client on CIFAR-10, where baselines like FedBABU and FedPer degrade to 33.1% and 55.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSelect outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest baseline LG-FedAvg (77.65% on CIFAR-10), FedSelect gains +4.60 points, and against FedPAC (77.20%), it gains +5.05 points. The advantage is most dramatic on Mini-ImageNet, where FedSelect (38.69%) surpasses FedPAC (37.72%) by ~1 point and dramatically outperforms FedBABU (6.99%) by over 31 points. On OfficeHome, FedSelect (80.51%) edges out FedRep (80.07%) by 0.44 points, while FedPAC (66.61%) and FedBABU (65.63%) suffer severe degradation under domain shift. FedAvg without personalization performs poorly (27.70% on CIFAR-10), and even FedAvg+FT (75.30%) trails FedSelect by nearly 7 points, confirming that adaptive parameter selection provides substantial gains over simple fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSelect builds directly on the partial model personalization framework of Pillutla et al. (ICML 2022), reusing their LocalAlt alternating-update procedure as the inner training loop, but replaces the hand-specified layer partition with a data-driven, parameter-wise mask that grows over communication rounds. Conceptually, it repurposes the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis's iterative magnitude search (Frankle & Carbin, ICLR 2019) for a different objective: rather than pruning to zero for sparsity, FedSelect uses gradient-magnitude-based selection to decide which parameters to keep local versus aggregate globally. This departs from prior PFL methods (FedRep, FedBABU, FedPer, FedPAC) that fix the personalized subnetwork architecture a priori (e.g., always the final linear layer), and from LotteryFL (Li et al., 2020) which applies vanilla LTH pruning but with insufficient personalization due to low final sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, well-motivated mechanism for the open question of *which* parameters to personalize in PFL, showing that training-dynamics-based selection consistently outperforms both layer-wise heuristics and random partitions across four diverse datasets. The ablation confirming that the inverse hypothesis (personalizing least-changing parameters) degrades performance lends credibility to the core claim. However, the evaluation is limited to ResNet-18 in a cross-silo, full-participation setting, and the paper does not analyze communication overhead as the personalized subnetwork grows, nor does it address asynchronous or partial-participation regimes. Open questions include whether the gradient-magnitude criterion generalizes to transformer backbones, larger-scale deployments, and whether the growing mask introduces additional privacy considerations as more parameters remain local.
