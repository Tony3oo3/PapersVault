## Problem

Most superpixel oversegmentation methods require a priori initialization (seed grids, marker distributions) and user-defined termination criteria (superpixel count, iteration limits), making them sensitive to parameterization and unable to adapt to non-uniform detail distributions across an image. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a method that eliminates both initialization and manual termination while achieving significantly higher boundary adherence than existing approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image (or any multi-channel image, and optionally a binary mask for refining pre-segmented regions) and produces superpixel labels organized as a multi-level hierarchy, where each successive level adds finer detail. The only mandatory parameter is a minimum detail size in pixels (default 64); a target superpixel count is optional and, when omitted, the algorithm auto-terminates upon exhausting all color-channel information. The hierarchical output naturally yields ~172 segmentation levels per BSDS500 image, enabling multi-scale downstream use without re-running the algorithm.

## Method

HHTS iteratively selects the most inhomogeneous segment (prioritized by max per-channel standard deviation times segment area squared), computes its local histogram, and applies a 1D Laplace kernel to detect intensity-cluster boundaries as threshold candidates. An adjusted Cauchy weighting over the cumulative histogram favors balanced splits, and the selected threshold partitions the segment into foreground and background via connected-components labeling. This process is repeated across all three color spaces (RGB, LAB, HSV) simultaneously to capture diverse object outlines, building a hierarchy where homogeneity increases at each level. Auto-termination triggers when no segment contains more than one distinct intensity in any channel, eliminating the need for a target count. Segments smaller than the minimum detail size are merged back into their neighbors to preserve object coherence, analogous to forced connectivity in SLIC postprocessing.

## Results

On BSDS500, HHTS achieves the highest Boundary Recall (BR) across all compared methods: 0.8502 at 250 superpixels (vs. SH's 0.8080), 0.9326 at 600 (vs. SCAC's 0.8260), and 0.9626 at 1000 (vs. VSSS's 0.9188, APENet's 0.9204, LDFUNet's 0.9300, and CRTrees' 0.9624). HHTS also achieves the lowest Undersegmentation Error (UE): 0.0307 at 1000 superpixels, compared to 0.0324 (VSSS), 0.0680 (SCAC), and 0.0716 (CRTrees). The trade-off is lower Compactness (CO: 0.1215–0.1411 vs. 0.1953–0.4420 for baselines) and slightly lower Boundary Precision (BP: 0.0744 vs. 0.0996–0.1878). Runtime is competitive at ~448 ms per image for 600 superpixels, yielding 172 hierarchical levels at 2.6 ms per level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HHTS outperforms all 12 compared baselines on Boundary Recall (BR), the paper's primary metric: it beats SH by 4.2 pp (0.8502 vs. 0.8080) at 250 superpixels, SCAC by 10.7 pp (0.9326 vs. 0.8260) at 600, and VSSS by 4.4 pp (0.9626 vs. 0.9188) at 1000. Against the deep-learning methods APENet and LDFUNet, HHTS achieves 4.2 pp and 3.3 pp higher BR respectively, while also reaching these numbers with fewer superpixels (1000 vs. 1200/1300). HHTS also achieves the lowest Undersegmentation Error across all comparisons (0.0307 at 1000 vs. 0.0324 for VSSS and 0.0716 for CRTrees). The main trade-off is Compactness: HHTS scores 0.1215–0.1411 CO versus 0.1953–0.4420 for baselines, reflecting its irregular, boundary-following segment shapes. On Boundary Precision, HHTS (0.0744) is lower than APENet (0.1878) and LDFUNet (0.0996), but the paper argues the BR/BP balance remains superior to established methods like ETPS. Against CRTrees, HHTS matches BR (0.9626 vs. 0.9624) while halving UE (0.0307 vs. 0.0716) and auto-terminating at 1000 segments rather than requiring 2000.

## Relation to Prior Work

HHTS departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigms in superpixel oversegmentation: seed-based methods (SLIC, SEEDS, ETPS, VSSS) that require a priori initialization, and energy-optimization methods (CRS, ERS) that depend on predefined iteration limits. Instead, it repurposes classical histogram thresholding—augmented with a 1D Laplace filter for boundary detection and a Cauchy-weighted equal-partition criterion—into a top-down, multi-channel (RGB, HSV, LAB) hierarchical splitting scheme that auto-terminates when local color information is exhausted. The hierarchical structure echoes Superpixel Hierarchy and CRTrees, but the splitting mechanism (foreground/background separation via local histograms rather than graph cuts or contour relaxation) and the information-exhaustion stopping criterion are novel. The paper also positions HHTS as a post-processing refiner for both classical oversegmentation (ETPS) and deep-learning-based semantic segmentation (SAM), a use case not explored by prior superpixel methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of seed initialization and iteration-limit parameters addresses a persistent practical pain point in superpixel segmentation, particularly for applications requiring high boundary recall (medical imaging, stereo matching, SLAM). The 172-level hierarchy produced in ~448 ms per image (vs. 1–31 levels for competitors in the same budget) is a concrete efficiency gain for multi-scale pipelines. However, the method trades compactness and boundary precision for recall, produces irregularly shaped superpixels, and suffers boundary leakage in early iterations on smooth or compressed images. Open questions include whether the single-threaded CPU implementation scales to high-resolution or video inputs, whether depth or texture channels can be integrated into the histogram thresholding, and whether the SAM-refinement application generalizes beyond BSDS500-style natural images to domain-specific settings.
