## Problem

Attention-based video summarization models capture temporal dependencies between frames but ignore the visual significance of spatial content within each frame. Prior attempts to incorporate spatial importance (self-attention, multi-head attention, graph CNNs) require additional modules that incur substantial computational overhead, especially for long videos. The paper asks whether a single, lightweight mechanism can simultaneously encode both spatial and temporal relationships without these extra costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242453/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video as a sequence of T frames (H×W×3), extracts per-frame features via a frozen GoogleNet backbone (yielding T×D vectors), then replicates each feature to 3 channels and prepends a learnable CLS token to form an image-like tensor of shape 3×(T+1)×D. The output is a per-frame importance score in [0,1] for each of the T frames; at inference, shots are selected via a 0/1 knapsack algorithm under a 15% length constraint to produce the final summary video.

## Method

CSTA's core insight is to treat stacked frame features as a 2D image and apply a trainable 2D CNN (GoogleNet) to it, so that convolutional kernels simultaneously capture inter-frame (temporal) and intra-frame (spatial) relationships in a single forward pass, analogous to how a CNN learns patterns from natural images. The CNN's inherent ability to encode absolute positions (via padding and receptive fields) is exploited to locate visually salient attributes within the frame-feature grid. The architecture proceeds as: (1) Embedding — frozen GoogleNet extracts features, replicated to 3 channels, concatenated with a CLS token; (2) Attention Module — a trainable GoogleNet processes the key tensor, with adaptive pooling and a skip connection to produce same-shape attention maps; (3) Mixing Module — softmax is applied independently along the time axis (temporal importance) and dimension axis (spatial importance), and both weighted maps are element-wise multiplied with the value tensor and summed; (4) Classifier — adaptive pooling merges the CLS token, followed by FC-ReLU-Dropout-LayerNorm-FC-Sigmoid to yield per-frame scores. Training uses MSE loss against human-annotated frame scores, and the entire pipeline requires fewer MACs than prior spatiotemporal methods because the CNN acts as a single sliding-window operation rather than a pairwise attention over all frame pairs.

## Results

CSTA achieves the best overall rank (1) on SumMe with Kendall's τ = 0.246 and Spearman's ρ = 0.274, and the second-best rank (2*) on TVSum with τ = 0.194 and ρ = 0.255. These results surpass all temporal-only attention methods (e.g., VASNet: τ=0.160/0.160) and multi-modal methods (e.g., MSVA: τ=0.200/0.190) on SumMe, while on TVSum only DMASum (τ=0.203, ρ=0.267) edges out CSTA. In terms of efficiency, CSTA requires 9.78G MACs for score prediction on SumMe and 15.73G on TVSum, substantially fewer than SSPVS (20.72G/44.22G) and VJMHT (18.21G/28.25G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSTA outperforms all temporal-only attention baselines (VASNet, VJMHT, DSNet, HMT, MAAM) on both datasets, with the largest gap on SumMe where it leads VASNet by 0.086 in τ. Among spatiotemporal methods, CSTA beats RR-STG on SumMe (τ: 0.246 vs 0.211) and VSS-Net on TVSum (ρ: 0.255 vs 0.249), but trails DMASum on TVSum (τ: 0.194 vs 0.203; ρ: 0.255 vs 0.267) while DMASum performs poorly on SumMe (τ: 0.063). Against multi-modal methods, CSTA surpasses MSVA on SumMe (τ: 0.246 vs 0.200) and SSPVS on TVSum (τ: 0.194 vs 0.181), demonstrating that visual-spatial attention alone can match or exceed methods relying on external text or cross-task data. In terms of computational cost, CSTA's score-prediction MACs (9.78G on SumMe, 15.73G on TVSum) are lower than SSPVS (20.72G/44.22G) and VJMHT (18.21G/28.25G), while RR-STG's feature-extraction cost (54.82T/88.41T) is orders of magnitude higher due to its object-detection pre-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSTA builds on the CLS-token aggregation strategy from STVT [15] and the practice of adding positional encodings to attention maps (rather than inputs) from Apostolidis et al. [1]. It departs from prior spatiotemporal summarization methods (STVT, VSS-Net, DMASum, RR-STG) that design separate modules for spatial and temporal attention, instead repurposing a standard 2D CNN (GoogleNet) as a single sliding-window mechanism that captures both inter- and intra-frame relations simultaneously. The method also draws on the finding that CNNs implicitly encode absolute spatial positions [16, 21, 4], applying this property to frame-feature 'images' to locate critical visual attributes without explicit positional modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSTA demonstrates that a single CNN pass over stacked frame features can replace the multi-module spatial+temporal attention pipelines that dominate recent video summarization, achieving SOTA on SumMe and near-SOTA on TVSum with fewer MACs. The verification experiment showing that a plain CNN applied to frame features outperforms many dedicated summarization architectures on SumMe raises an open question about how much of the summarization signal is already captured by standard image backbones. Limitations include reliance on a frozen GoogleNet for feature extraction, evaluation on only two datasets, and the fact that the CNN verification does not fully isolate why it succeeds (position encoding vs. learned filters vs. channel structure). Future work on frame-feature-specific CNNs or jointly trained extraction+attention models could clarify the mechanism.
