## Problem

Existing image redundancy reduction methods rely on supervised signals (classification labels) to identify informative patches, which introduces a categorical inductive bias: the model preserves patches relevant to learned categories while discarding semantically meaningful content from unlabeled or unseen categories. This bias is inherent to how these methods are trained and limits their generalization to real-world scenes containing objects outside the training taxonomy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241846/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image divided into a ViT patch grid (e.g., 14×14 for ViT-B) and produces a per-patch informativeness score vector from which top-k patches are retained for downstream tasks. During training, only a randomly selected subset of visible patches (10% at 90% masking ratio) is fed into the ranking model via a sparse design, while at inference the model scores all patches simultaneously. A DPC-KNN clustering step additionally selects representative patches from homogeneous regions to complement the top-k boundary-favoring selection.

## Method

LTRP operates in two self-supervised stages. First, a frozen pre-trained MAE with a high 90% masking ratio reconstructs the image from visible patches; each visible patch is then individually removed (via masked attention) and the L1 distance between the two reconstructions yields a semantic density score, forming pseudo-labels. Second, a lightweight ranking model (ViT-S, ResNet-50, or even ViT-T) is trained with the ListMLE list-wise loss to predict these scores, learning to rank all patches by informativeness without any category supervision. The key insight is that at high masking ratios, removing a single semantically critical patch causes a perceptible reconstruction shift, making the MAE reconstruction difference a natural, category-agnostic proxy for patch importance. At inference, only the ranking model is retained (MAE is discarded), and top-k patches plus DPC-KNN-selected homogeneous patches are passed to downstream classifiers, yielding up to 32.7% GPU inference speedup with ≤0.5% accuracy loss.

## Results

On MS-COCO multi-label classification, LTRP achieves 82.8% mAP at keep ratio 0.75 and 81.8% at 0.5, outperforming the best unsupervised baselines by 1.1% and 1.4% and the best supervised methods by 0.7% at both ratios. On ImageNet-1K single-label classification, LTRP is competitive with state-of-the-art supervised methods while using no supervisory signals. For patch-level evaluation on unseen categories (excluding ImageNet-1K overlap), LTRP surpasses all competitors in IoU, F1, recall, and precision on both object detection (PASCAL VOC, MS-COCO) and semantic segmentation (MS-COCO, ADE20K) benchmarks. When deployed for efficient ViT with a ViT-T ranking model, LTRP accelerates GPU inference by 18.9%–32.7% with only 0.1%–0.5% top-1 accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against self-supervised activation-mapping baselines (MoCo with ResNet-50, DINO), LTRP clearly wins on both single-label and multi-label classification, as these methods were not designed for redundancy reduction and their activation maps are less targeted. Against supervised CAM (Grad-CAM with ViT), LTRP is roughly on par on ImageNet-1K single-label but pulls ahead on MS-COCO multi-label (by ~0.7% mAP) and substantially on unseen-category detection/segmentation, where Grad-CAM's precision degrades as keep ratio decreases. Among token-reduction methods (EViT, IA-RED², DynamicViT, AdaViT, DGE, ToMe), LTRP achieves the best mAP on MS-COCO multi-label at both evaluated keep ratios and the highest precision/recall on unseen-category object detection and semantic segmentation. The gap over DynamicViT (the second-best method) widens as the keep ratio decreases, confirming LTRP's superior preservation of unseen-category semantics. In the efficient-ViT setting (Table 2), LTRP with a ViT-T ranker matches or slightly trails the full-patch baseline in accuracy (−0.1% to −0.5%) while delivering 18.9%–32.7% GPU speedup, making it a practical drop-in for large ViT deployments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two well-studied components—masked autoencoder (MAE) reconstruction and learning-to-rank (LTR)—into a self-supervised pipeline for image redundancy reduction, a task previously dominated by supervised methods (Grad-CAM, EViT, IA-RED2) that suffer from categorical inductive bias. The key conceptual departure is using MAE's reconstruction sensitivity at high masking ratios (90%) as an unsupervised proxy for patch informativeness, then training a ranking model with ListMLE loss on the resulting pseudo-labels. Unlike prior self-supervised activation mapping (DINO, MoCo), which incidentally reveals patch importance but is not optimized for redundancy reduction, LTRP explicitly targets the ranking objective, and unlike token-pruning/merging methods (DynamicViT, ToMe, EViT), it does not require supervised fine-tuning to identify which patches to keep.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised framing eliminates the categorical inductive bias that causes supervised redundancy reduction to discard semantically meaningful but unlabeled content, making the approach more generalizable to open-world and multi-label scenarios. The patch-level evaluations on unseen categories (object detection and segmentation) provide strong evidence for this unbiasedness claim. Open questions include whether the pseudo-label generation cost (one MAE forward pass per visible patch) scales to very high-resolution images or video, whether the ranking model can be further distilled, and how the approach extends to modalities beyond 2D RGB images. The practical implication for efficient ViT deployment—achieving 18–33% inference speedup with <0.5% accuracy loss—is meaningful but moderate in magnitude.
