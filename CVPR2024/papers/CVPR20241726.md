## Problem

High-quality deepfakes contain subtle, localized blending artifacts that are easily lost through successive convolutional layers in standard CNN backbones, yet existing attention-based methods rely on implicit attention within single binary classifiers and fail to generalize to unseen manipulations. The paper addresses the gap between fine-grained artifact localization and cross-dataset generalizability by combining explicit attention on vulnerable blending regions with multi-task learning and pseudo-fake synthesis.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single cropped face frame resized to 384×384 pixels along with 68 facial landmarks extracted via Dlib, and produces three simultaneous outputs: a binary real/fake classification score, a spatial heatmap highlighting vulnerable (artifact-prone) pixels and their neighborhoods, and a 2D self-consistency matrix measuring pixel similarity relative to a randomly selected vulnerable point. During training, only real images are used; pseudo-fakes are generated on-the-fly via blending-based synthesis (BI or SBI) to provide ground-truth heatmaps and consistency targets.

## Method

LAA-Net introduces two complementary components. First, an explicit attention mechanism within a three-branch multi-task framework: a classification branch, a heatmap branch that regresses an adaptive Gaussian distribution centered at each vulnerable point (identified as argmax of the blending boundary mask B = 4M⊙(1−M)), and a self-consistency branch that computes a 2D matrix measuring similarity between a randomly chosen vulnerable pixel and all other mask pixels, reducing the original 4D consistency to 2D. Second, an Enhanced Feature Pyramid Network (E-FPN) that propagates low-level features from earlier layers to the final representation using a convolution–transpose-convolution path, then filters the incoming features through a sigmoid-weighted mask (1−sigmoid(E))^γw to suppress redundant contributions before concatenation, thereby preserving local texture cues without inflating feature redundancy. The total loss combines binary cross-entropy with focal loss on the heatmap and binary cross-entropy on the self-consistency matrix, weighted by λ₁=10 and λ₂=100.

## Results

LAA-Net (with SBI synthesis) achieves 99.96% AUC on the in-dataset FF++ benchmark and state-of-the-art cross-dataset AUC on CDF2 (95.40%), DFW (80.03%), and DFDC (86.94%). On DFD, the BI variant reaches 99.51% AUC, slightly edging out CADDM (99.03%) and AltFreezing (98.50%). The method also reports strong AP scores, e.g., 97.64% on CDF2 and 99.40% on DFD, confirming robustness across deepfake quality levels (Mask-SSIM ranging from 0.84 to 0.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAA-Net (w/SBI) outperforms the strongest prior methods on three of four cross-dataset benchmarks: CDF2 (95.40% vs. CADDM 93.88%, +1.52%), DFW (80.03% vs. CADDM 74.48%, +5.55%), and DFDC (86.94% vs. AUNet 86.16%, +0.78%). On DFD, the SBI variant (98.43%) is marginally below CADDM (99.03%) and AltFreezing (98.50%), but the BI variant (99.51%) surpasses both. Against attention-based baselines specifically, LAA-Net gains 27.14% AUC over Multi-attentional [55] and 19.57% over SFDG [50] on CDF2, demonstrating that explicit vulnerable-point attention combined with multi-task learning substantially improves generalization over implicit attention alone. On the in-dataset FF++ test, LAA-Net (99.96%) edges out CADDM (99.79%) and SBI (99.64%). The method also shows superior robustness to color perturbations (saturation, contrast) in Table 2, though it remains sensitive to Gaussian noise (53.9% AUC), a limitation shared with most prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAA-Net sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads in deepfake detection: multi-task learning with pseudo-fake synthesis (FaceXRay, PCL+I2G) for generalization, and attention-based methods (Multi-attentional, SFDG) for capturing localized artifacts. The paper departs from both by introducing an *explicit* attention mechanism (heatmap + self-consistency branches) that is tightly coupled to a blending-based pseudo-fake synthesis, rather than relying on implicit attention within a single binary classifier. Architecturally, it extends the standard FPN into an E-FPN that filters redundant features via a sigmoid-weighted mask before concatenation, drawing on the observation that feature redundancy acts as a regularizer. The self-consistency branch is adapted from Zhao et al. (ICCV 2021) but restricted to vulnerable points, reducing dimensionality from 4D to 2D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining explicit fine-grained attention with multi-task learning and a redundancy-aware FPN yields consistent SOTA across four cross-dataset benchmarks, bridging the gap between generalization-focused and artifact-focused detection. A notable limitation is extreme sensitivity to structural perturbations (Gaussian noise drops AUC to 53.9%), which limits practical deployment on compressed or noisy social-media content. Open questions include whether the vulnerable-point formulation generalizes to non-blending-based generation (e.g., diffusion models) and whether the temporal dimension, explicitly deferred to future work, is necessary for the most realistic deepfakes.
