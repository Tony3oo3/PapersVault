## Problem

Transformer-based 3D instance segmentation methods rely solely on geometric information, leading to two persistent failures: (1) confusion between instances with similar shapes but different semantic roles (e.g., chair vs. toilet), and (2) ambiguous boundary determination that causes misclassification of adjacent edge points. No prior approach explicitly injects semantic knowledge from language models into instance queries or provides dedicated edge supervision to sharpen instance boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241389/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a colored 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{Np×6} (xyz + RGB), voxelizes it into X0 voxels, and produces a binary instance segmentation mask M̂_f ∈ {0,1}^{X×Nq} via sigmoid-thresholded dot products between projected queries and full-resolution features. Additionally, it outputs per-query semantic class labels and a voxel-wise edge map Ê ∈ (0,1)^{X0×1} at full resolution. The edge map is generated by a lightweight MLP head on the backbone's F0 features and is used as auxiliary supervision rather than a final prediction.

## Method

EASE builds on a sparse convolutional U-Net backbone (producing multi-resolution features F0–F4) and a mask transformer decoder with Mask Module and Query Refinement blocks. Two key innovations differentiate it: (1) a Semantic Network that maps each query's predicted category to a CLIP text embedding, then fuses it with the query via channel-wise concatenation and a two-layer MLP with sigmoid, reinforcing queries with discriminative semantic priors at every decoder iteration; (2) an Edge Prediction Module that predicts voxel-wise edge probabilities from F0, supervised by pseudo edge labels computed offline via KNN (a point is labeled as edge if the count of distinct-instance-label neighbors exceeds a threshold τ). The edge head is trained with weighted binary cross-entropy to handle the extreme class imbalance between edge and interior voxels, and the total loss combines mask loss (BCE + Dice), classification cross-entropy, and the edge loss, all optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On ScanNetV2, EASE achieves mAP 60.2 / mAP50 77.2 (val) and mAP 59.8 / mAP50 78.9 (test), setting new state-of-the-art over MAFT (59.9/76.5) and QueryFormer (56.5/74.2). On S3DIS Area 5, EASE reaches AP 59.0 / AP50 71.6, outperforming QueryFormer (57.7/69.9) and Mask3D (56.6/68.4). On ScanNet200, EASE scores mAP 29.9 / mAP50 38.8 / mAP25 44.7, and on the outdoor STPLS3D benchmark it achieves mAP 64.5 / mAP50 80.8 / mAP25 86.9, exceeding the second-best Mask3D by +1.1/+1.6/+1.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASE outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. Against the closest transformer-based competitors, EASE edges out MAFT by +0.3 mAP on ScanNetV2 val (60.2 vs 59.9) and by +0.3 mAP on S3DIS Area 5 (59.0 vs 57.7 for QueryFormer, the strongest S3DIS baseline). On ScanNet200, EASE leads MAFT by +0.7 mAP (29.9 vs 29.2) and QueryFormer by +1.8 mAP. On the outdoor STPLS3D dataset, EASE surpasses Mask3D by +1.1 mAP (64.5 vs 63.4) and SoftGroup by a large margin of +17.2 mAP. The gains over older proposal- and grouping-based methods (GSPN, 3D-SIS, 3D-MPA, PointGroup) are substantial, with EASE exceeding them by 20–40 mAP points, confirming that the semantic prior and edge-aware modules provide consistent improvements over purely geometric approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

EASE builds directly on the transformer-based 3DIS lineage established by Mask3D, MAFT, and QueryFormer, which train instance queries for end-to-end mask prediction from sparse convolutional features. Its key departure is the injection of CLIP text embeddings as explicit semantic priors into the query refinement loop, connecting the 3DIS task to the broader trend of leveraging visual-language models for scene understanding. The edge prediction module, supervised by KNN-derived pseudo labels, draws on boundary-aware segmentation ideas from 2D/3D literature but repurposes them as a lightweight auxiliary head within the transformer decoder rather than a standalone boundary detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete and recurring failure modes in 3DIS—semantic confusion among visually similar objects and ambiguous instance boundaries—through two complementary, low-cost modules that can be grafted onto existing transformer-based pipelines. However, the semantic prior is closed-set (fixed category text descriptions), limiting immediate applicability to open-vocabulary 3DIS, and the edge labels are pseudo-generated rather than ground-truth, which may cap their utility in highly cluttered or outdoor scenes. The consistent but modest gains (+0.3 mAP over MAFT on ScanNetV2) suggest the approach is a practical refinement rather than a paradigm shift, and open questions remain about whether the semantic guidance generalizes to unseen categories or scales to larger, more diverse 3D environments.
