## Problem

Existing open-source image captioning methods face a fundamental trade-off: models like BLIP-2 and OFA produce overly succinct captions that omit essential visual details, while more expressive models like LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP generate long descriptions riddled with hallucinated objects and attributes. No prior open-source approach simultaneously achieves high detail and high fidelity without requiring proprietary-scale training data or compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240709/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For 2D captioning, the pipeline takes a single RGB image and produces a detailed natural-language caption; for 3D captioning, it takes two rendered views of a 3D object (compared to Cap3D's eight views) and produces a unified caption. The LLM orchestrator can follow arbitrary user instructions to control caption style, length, and focus (e.g., foreground-only descriptions), making the output format flexible rather than fixed. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying on pre-trained models called in sequence.

## Method

VFC is a three-step training-free pipeline: (1) Proposal, where two multimodal captioning models (LLaVA-1.5 and Kosmos-2 for 2D; LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP for 3D) independently generate initial detailed captions to maximize coverage of visual content; (2) Verification, where an LLM (GPT-4 or Llama-2) summarizes the proposals into a single caption, extracts an object checklist, and calls an open-vocabulary object detector (2D) or a VQA model answering five targeted questions (3D) to fact-check each claim; (3) Captioning, where the LLM revises the caption by retaining detected objects and removing undetected ones, then follows user instructions for final style. The key insight is that grounding tools (detection, VQA) serve as reliable 'fact-checkers' that a general-purpose captioner cannot self-verify, and chaining multiple captioners before verification captures a broader range of visual details than any single model.

## Results

On the 5,000-image COCO test set (Karpathy split), VisualFactChecker achieves 32.90% CLIP-Score and 73.61% CLIP-Image-Score, outperforming all open-source baselines and even the COCO human annotations (30.36% / 71.21%). On 1,000 Objaverse 3D objects, VFC reaches 34.01% CLIP-Score and 80.32% CLIP-Image-Score, surpassing Cap3D (33.44% / 79.88%). In pairwise per-image comparisons, VFC beats LLaVA-1.5 on 64.4% of images by CLIP-Score, and human/GPT-4V evaluations confirm consistent preference for VFC captions over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFC outperforms all five baselines on both CLIP-Score and CLIP-Image-Score. Against BLIP-2, the gap is largest (2.79 / 2.82 points), reflecting BLIP-2's tendency toward overly concise captions. InstructBLIP is the second-closest 2D baseline, trailing VFC by 1.45 / 0.66 points; its detailed captions still contain hallucinations that VFC's fact-checking step removes. LLaVA-1.5 and Kosmos-2 are the strongest individual captioners (32.08 and 32.32 CLIP-Score), but VFC's multi-model proposal plus detection-based verification yields a further 0.82 and 0.58 point gain respectively. For 3D captioning, VFC beats Cap3D by 0.57 CLIP-Score and 0.44 CLIP-Image-Score while using only 2 views versus Cap3D's 8. Notably, VFC also surpasses the COCO human annotations on both metrics, suggesting that LLM-summarized, fact-checked captions can exceed the information density of single human-written captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training a single MM-LLM for captioning by instead orchestrating multiple pre-trained open-source models (LLaVA-1.5, Kosmos-2, InstructBLIP) through an LLM in a training-free pipeline. It builds conceptually on LLM-as-summarizer approaches like Socratic models, ChatCaptioner, and IC3, but introduces a critical distinction: an explicit fact-checking step using open-vocabulary object detection (2D) or VQA (3D) to verify and remove hallucinated content before final caption generation. The pipeline also extends the captioning task from 2D images to 3D objects, going beyond Cap3D's single-model approach by applying per-view verification and multi-view synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a carefully designed training-free pipeline of existing open-source models can approach proprietary model quality (GPT-4V) for captioning, which is valuable for resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings. The CLIP-Image-Score metric offers a useful complementary evaluation tool that is more sensitive to hallucination than CLIP-Score alone. However, the pipeline's reliance on multiple sequential model calls introduces significant latency, and the fact-checking is limited to object presence (2D) or a small set of VQA questions (3D), leaving other hallucination types (spatial relationships, attribute errors) unaddressed. The authors note future work on making tool selection more automatic and adding more fact-checking components.
