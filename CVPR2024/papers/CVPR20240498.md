## Problem

Existing caricature generation methods either rely on deformation-based pipelines that prioritize shape exaggeration over identity preservation, or use T2I personalization approaches (Textual Inversion, Perfusion) that overfit on single reference images and suffer semantic interference when combining identity and style concepts. The paper addresses how to generate a plausible caricature from just a single photo and a rough freehand sketch while maintaining identity fidelity, accommodating unconstrained sketch deformations, and optionally transferring a separate style — all without the resource-intensive training of prior personalization methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240498/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a single reference portrait photo (RGB), a freehand black-and-white abstract sketch (edge map or line drawing of arbitrary complexity, from one-stroke outlines to detailed deformations), and optionally a style reference image. The output is a single caricature image that simultaneously preserves the identity from the photo, reflects the shape exaggerations encoded in the sketch, and adopts the visual style from the style image. Fine-tuning requires only 40 steps (~1 min) for identity and 100 steps (~2 min) for style on a single NVIDIA GTX 4090, with inference at 50 denoising steps using classifier-free guidance scale 9.

## Method

The core contribution is Explicit Rank-1 Model Editing (Explicit ROME), which applies rank-1 modifications to the Key and Value pathways of cross-attention layers exclusively at the concept token index, using a learnable pseudo word embedding (initialized from a superclass like 'man'/'woman') and a cosine-similarity-gated target output vector. This localizes editing to the identity/style concept position, preventing interference with other textual contexts and reducing trainable parameters by 30× compared to Perfusion. A Random Mask Reconstruction loss masks random patches of the reference image during training, forcing the model to rely on global identity and style features rather than local spatial details, thereby improving robustness to exaggerated sketch shapes. Concept Regularization (L2 on word embeddings plus cosine distance on CLIP text encodings against the superclass) further counters overfitting. At inference, a frozen T2I-Adapter provides sketch-based spatial conditioning, and Eq. (4) combines independently trained identity and style concepts additively at their respective token positions to produce the final caricature.

## Results

On the WebCaricature test set (20 identities, 4 styles, 12 edge maps → 960 pairs), the full method achieves the highest CLIP-Score for identity (0.671) and style (0.576) fidelity, while maintaining competitive shape fidelity (0.654). In the human study (15 raters, 300 responses per method), the method scores 4.4/5 on identity, 3.8/5 on style, and 4.2/5 on shape, yielding an overall score of 4.1/5 — substantially above Textual Inversion (2.9) and Perfusion (2.7). Fine-tuning requires only ~1 minute for identity and ~2 minutes for style on a single RTX 4090, making it 3× faster than Perfusion and 10× faster than Textual Inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Textual Inversion [13], the full method wins on all three CLIP-Score metrics (ID 0.671 vs 0.634, Style 0.576 vs 0.553, Shape 0.654 vs 0.633) and in the human study on every axis (Overall 4.1 vs 2.9). Against Perfusion [61], the method achieves a large identity gain (0.671 vs 0.536) and a style gain (0.576 vs 0.549), but concedes shape fidelity (0.654 vs 0.676); in the human study it still leads on overall score (4.1 vs 2.7). The three GAN-based deformation baselines — StyleCariGAN [27], CariGANs [6], and WarpGAN [57] — are compared qualitatively only (Figure 3): CariGANs and WarpGAN exhibit visible distortions and artefacts from landmark/control-point warping, while StyleCariGAN produces higher-fidelity outputs but is restricted to predefined scale-based exaggeration and cannot accept freehand sketch or style conditioning. The paper's key advantage over all five baselines is the ability to simultaneously condition on identity, style, and sketch shape in a single generation pass, a capability none of the compared methods support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant deformation-based caricature pipeline (CariGANs, WarpGAN, StyleCariGAN) by adopting a Stable Diffusion backbone with an off-the-shelf T2I-Adapter for sketch conditioning, shifting the paradigm from landmark/control-point warping to diffusion-based generation. On the personalization side, it extends Perfusion's Rank-1 Model Editing (ROME) by restricting edits to only the concept-token index in cross-attention layers (Explicit ROME), rather than modifying the full Value pathway, and introduces Random Mask Reconstruction to combat overfitting from single-image training. The combination of identity personalization, style transfer, and freehand sketch shape control in a single unified framework is the key architectural departure from prior caricature systems, which handled at most one of these conditioning signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for accessibility: a user needs only a photo, a rough sketch, and optionally a style reference, with fine-tuning completing in 1–2 minutes on a single GPU, removing the barrier of commissioning a professional caricaturist. The Explicit ROME technique—editing only at the concept index with cosine-similarity gating—offers a general recipe for multi-concept personalization without semantic interference, potentially applicable beyond caricature to other stylized generation tasks. Limitations include reliance on a single reference image per concept (no multi-image identity), a small evaluation set (20 identities, 4 styles, 12 shapes), and dependence on a pre-trained T2I-Adapter whose sketch-conditioning quality bounds the system. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-subject scenes, whether the identity-scale parameter generalizes across diverse sketch styles, and whether the method can handle temporal consistency for animated caricatures.
