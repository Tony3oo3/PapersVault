## Problem

State-of-the-art co-salient object detection (CoSOD) methods are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations (e.g., Jadena's joint exposure and noise attack), which drastically reduce detection accuracy. Existing diffusion-based purification methods like DiffPure remove perturbations without considering object-specific semantics, producing reconstructed images with artifacts that still degrade CoSOD performance. No prior method exploits the shared co-salient object identity across a group of images to guide purification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a group of N RGB images (resized to 768×768) in which an unknown number M of images have been adversarially perturbed by Jadena and the remaining N−M are clean. It outputs a purified group of N images reconstructed via concept-conditioned diffusion, which are then fed to a downstream CoSOD detector (GICD, GCAGC, or PoolNet) to produce saliency maps. The number of adversarial images M is not required as input, and the method treats all images uniformly during purification.

## Method

COSALPURE comprises two modules. First, group-image concept learning formulates the problem as textual inversion on a pre-trained T2I diffusion model: a single token c is optimized so that the text encoder's embedding for a placeholder word S* is replaced by c, minimizing the diffusion noise-prediction loss over all group images. This learned concept is empirically robust to adversarial perturbations because it captures high-level object semantics rather than low-level pixel patterns. Second, concept-guided diffusion purification maps each image through a continuous representation module (trained on ImageNet with PGD noise) into the latent space, applies forward diffusion for T=250 steps, and then performs reverse diffusion where the learned concept c is injected as an additional conditioning signal into the noise prediction function ε_θ(ẑ_t, t, c). This steers the reconstruction toward the correct object identity, preventing the artifacts that plague unconditional purification. The entire pipeline uses a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion architecture) with only the concept token and the continuous representation module requiring training.

## Results

COSALPURE achieves the highest co-salient object detection success rate (SR) across all four datasets (Cosal2015, iCoseg, CoSOD3k, CoCA) and all three CoSOD backbones (GICD, GCAGC, PoolNet). For example, on Cosal2015 with GICD, SR improves from 0.3493 (Source-Only) to 0.5602 with COSALPURE, compared to 0.4595 (DiffPure) and 0.4565 (DDA). On the most challenging CoCA dataset, COSALPURE lifts GICD SR from 0.1837 to 0.2409, while DiffPure actually degrades performance to 0.1706. COSALPURE also leads in AP, F_β, and MAE in the majority of settings, confirming consistent gains beyond the primary SR metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSALPURE consistently outperforms both DiffPure and DDA in SR across all 12 dataset–backbone combinations. Against DiffPure, the gains are substantial: on Cosal2015/GICD, COSALPURE achieves 0.5602 SR versus DiffPure's 0.4595 (+10.1 points), and on CoCA/GICD, DiffPure actually degrades below Source-Only (0.1706 vs. 0.1837) while COSALPURE reaches 0.2409. Against DDA, the advantage is smaller but still consistent: COSALPURE leads by roughly 3–7 SR points per setting (e.g., 0.5602 vs. 0.4565 on Cosal2015/GICD; 0.2409 vs. 0.2054 on CoCA/GICD). Table 2 further reveals that COSALPURE's edge is driven primarily by the adversarial subset: its 'adv' SR (e.g., 0.5416 on Cosal2015/GICD) far exceeds DiffPure (0.3560) and DDA (0.3079), while its 'clean' SR is comparable to or slightly below the baselines, confirming that the concept-guided purification specifically repairs damaged images without harming clean ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSALPURE sits at the intersection of two diffusion-model lines of work: adversarial purification via forward/reverse diffusion (DiffPure, Nie et al. 2022) and concept learning via textual inversion (Gal et al. 2022). It departs from DiffPure by injecting a group-level semantic concept into the reverse denoising step, addressing DiffPure's failure to preserve object identity during reconstruction. It also extends textual inversion from single-subject personalization to group-image concept extraction, where the learned token must be robust to partially adversarial inputs. The paper positions itself as the first defense method specifically designed for the CoSOD task, filling a gap left by general-purpose purification methods that ignore the group structure and shared semantics inherent to co-saliency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to explicitly exploit the group-level shared semantics of co-salient objects for adversarial defense, offering a principled reason why unconditional purification (DiffPure) can actually degrade CoSOD accuracy by introducing object-identity artifacts. The empirical finding that adversarial perturbations have limited impact on the learned concept token is practically important, as it means the defense does not require knowing which images are attacked. Open questions include generalization to attacks that alter high-level semantics, scalability to very large image groups, and whether the concept-guided purification can be extended to other group-based vision tasks beyond CoSOD.
