## Problem

While model scaling has delivered dramatic gains in language models, segmentation, and text-to-image generation, image restoration has not benefited from this trend due to engineering constraints in computing resources, architecture design, training data availability, and the tension between generative quality and restoration fidelity. Existing diffusion-based IR methods are bottlenecked by the scale of their generative priors, and no systematic approach exists for scaling IR models to produce ultra-high-quality, intelligent restoration results.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-quality RGB image of arbitrary size (resized so the short side is 1024, then a 1024×1024 crop is processed) and an optional text prompt that can specify content, material textures, or quality attributes. Text prompts can be auto-generated by a 13B-parameter LLaVA model from the degraded input, eliminating manual annotation at test time. The output is a restored 1024×1024 image resized back to the original dimensions, with negative-quality prompts (e.g., 'blur, dirty, low resolution, oversmooth') applied via classifier-free guidance to suppress artifacts.

## Method

SUPIR adapts the 2.6B-parameter SDXL Base model as a generative prior through a large-scale adaptor that trims half the ViT blocks from a ControlNet-style trainable copy and replaces the zero-convolution connector with a novel ZeroSFT module (zero convolution + spatial feature transform + group normalization) to amplify pixel-level LQ guidance. The VAE encoder is fine-tuned to be degradation-robust so that LQ images are mapped to the latent space without artifacts being misinterpreted as content. Training uses 20 million 1024×1024 high-quality images with text annotations, 70K FFHQ-raw faces, and counter-intuitively 100K low-quality samples generated to teach the model negative-quality concepts for CFG. At inference, a restoration-guided sampling algorithm (modified EDM) performs weighted interpolation between the predicted latent and the LQ latent at each diffusion step, with a temperature parameter τ_r controlling the fidelity–quality tradeoff: early high-noise steps are strongly pulled toward the LQ image to preserve structure, while later low-noise steps are left free to generate fine textures.

## Results

On synthetic degradations (Table 1), SUPIR achieves the best non-reference metrics across all five degradation settings: e.g., on SR×4 it scores ManIQA 0.4738, ClipIQA 0.8049, and MUSIQ 73.83, outperforming the next-best StableSR (0.3304 / 0.7520 / 72.94). On the most complex mixture (Blur+SR×4+Noise+JPEG), SUPIR reaches ManIQA 0.4688, ClipIQA 0.8021, MUSIQ 73.58, again leading all baselines. On 60 real-world LQ images (Table 2a), SUPIR scores CLIP-IQA 0.8232, MUSIQ 73.00, and MANIQA 0.4295, the highest among all methods. However, SUPIR trails BSRGAN and PASD on full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), which the authors attribute to the known perception-distortion tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUPIR decisively outperforms all five baselines (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, StableSR, DiffBIR, PASD) on every non-reference perceptual metric (ManIQA, ClipIQA, MUSIQ) across all five synthetic degradation settings and on real-world images. The margin is substantial: on SR×4, SUPIR's ManIQA of 0.4738 exceeds the runner-up StableSR (0.3304) by 43%, and its ClipIQA of 0.8049 beats StableSR (0.7520) by 7%. On real-world data, SUPIR's MANIQA of 0.4295 surpasses PASD (0.3169) by 36%. Conversely, SUPIR underperforms BSRGAN and PASD on full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), with PSNR gaps of 2–4 dB; the authors argue this reflects the perception-distortion tradeoff rather than inferior quality, supported by a 20-participant user study where SUPIR was preferred most often. Among diffusion-based baselines, SUPIR consistently leads StableSR, DiffBIR, and PASD on both fidelity and perceptual metrics, while GAN-based BSRGAN and Real-ESRGAN remain competitive on PSNR/SSIM but lag significantly on perceptual scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUPIR extends the diffusion-based image restoration line (DiffBIR, StableSR, PASD) by replacing smaller generative priors with SDXL (2.6B parameters) and introducing a trimmed ControlNet adaptor with a novel ZeroSFT connector for pixel-level fidelity control. It departs from prior diffusion-IR methods in two key ways: (1) incorporating LLaVA to automatically generate content-descriptive prompts, enabling text-guided restoration, and (2) modifying EDM sampling with a restoration-guided interpolation step to suppress the fidelity loss that powerful generative priors introduce. The degradation-robust encoder fine-tuning and the counter-intuitive inclusion of negative-quality training samples are practical departures from standard diffusion-IR training pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that model scaling (analogous to LLMs and SAM) yields substantial perceptual gains in image restoration, validating a direction that was previously blocked by engineering constraints. The 20M high-resolution image dataset with text annotations is a significant community resource. However, the paper itself highlights a critical open question: full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM) systematically misalign with perceptual quality for generative-based restoration, suggesting the field needs new evaluation paradigms. The fidelity–quality tradeoff controlled by τ_r remains a manual knob, and whether further scaling continues to help or hits diminishing returns is unexplored.
