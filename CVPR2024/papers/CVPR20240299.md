## Problem

Transformer-based style transfer methods like StyTr2 achieve strong global modeling but require a full encoder-decoder architecture, leading to high computational cost and slow inference. Additionally, existing methods often produce results that are either under-stylized or lose content details, because raw content and style images carry mixed features that interfere with each other during stylization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a style image (both RGB, H×W×3) and produces a single stylized image at the same resolution. During training, images are randomly cropped to 256×256, but arbitrary resolutions are supported at test time. Unusually, both inputs are first passed through separate feature extractors to obtain 'pure content' and 'pure style' representations before being patchified and fed into the transformer, rather than using the raw images directly.

## Method

Puff-Net replaces the full transformer with an Efficient Transformer Encoder (ETE) that eliminates the decoder entirely. A learnable output embedding ε_o (initialized from the content embedding ε_c) is processed through the encoder, where ε_c serves as Query and ε_s serves as Key/Value, reducing attention complexity from O((2L)²C + 2LC²) to O(L²C + LC²). Two dedicated feature extractors preprocess the inputs: an INN-based content extractor with affine coupling layers and MobileNetV2 bottleneck residual blocks preserves structural details, while an LT-block-based style extractor captures global color and texture. A three-layer CNN decoder reconstructs the final image from the output sequence, and training combines VGG-based content/style perceptual losses with identity reconstruction losses on the extractors, using CAPE for content-aware positional encoding.

## Results

On the MS-COCO/WikiArt evaluation set (20 content × 20 style images), Puff-Net achieves a content perceptual loss of 1.92 and a style perceptual loss of 2.21, placing it second-best on both metrics behind StyTr² (1.89 / 1.69). In terms of inference speed on a Tesla P100, Puff-Net runs in 0.098 s at 256×256 and 0.134 s at 512×512, substantially faster than StyTr² (0.116 s / 0.661 s) and comparable to CAP-VSTNet (0.107 s / 0.162 s), while StyleFormer is fastest (0.013 s / 0.026 s) but produces the worst quality (L_c = 2.87, L_s = 3.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Puff-Net trades a small content-loss gap versus StyTr² (1.92 vs 1.89) for a much larger style-loss advantage over CAP-VSTNet (2.21 vs 4.42), indicating that CAP-VSTNet severely under-stylizes while Puff-Net achieves a more balanced content–style trade-off. Against StyTr², Puff-Net is slightly worse on both perceptual losses (1.92 vs 1.89 on content; 2.21 vs 1.69 on style) but is roughly 5× faster at 512×512 resolution (0.134 s vs 0.661 s), which the authors attribute to removing the transformer decoder. StyleFormer is the fastest method (0.013 s at 256×256) but lags Puff-Net by nearly 1.0 on content loss and 1.1 on style loss, confirming its tendency to exaggerate details. IEST sits in the middle on content loss (1.97) but is clearly worse on style loss (3.99), consistent with the qualitative observation of insufficient stylization. Overall, Puff-Net delivers near-best quality at a fraction of StyTr²'s inference cost, positioning it as the most efficient transformer-based method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

Puff-Net builds directly on StyTr2's use of a full transformer (encoder + decoder) for style transfer and ViT's patch-embedding paradigm, but departs by eliminating the decoder entirely and instead appending a learnable output embedding to the encoder so that stylized patch sequences can be produced in a single forward pass. It further distinguishes itself from prior transformer-based and CNN-based methods by introducing two dedicated feature extractors—an invertible neural network (INN) for content and a Lite-Transformer block for style—that preprocess inputs into 'pure' content and style representations before the encoder processes them. The Content-Aware Positional Encoding (CAPE) from StyTr2 is retained, and the three-layer CNN decoder from SETR is used only for the final upsampling, keeping the core stylization logic within the encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The encoder-only design yields a practical efficiency gain—roughly 5× faster inference than StyTr2 at 512×512—while maintaining competitive content and style fidelity, making transformer-based style transfer more accessible on modest hardware. However, the quality tradeoff is non-trivial: style perceptual loss is noticeably higher than StyTr2 (2.21 vs. 1.69), and the authors acknowledge that complex content-style pairs can produce unreasonable stylization. Open questions include whether the early freezing of the style extractor (12k iterations) is a fundamental limitation of the loss weighting or an artifact of the training protocol, and whether the encoder-only formulation generalizes beyond style transfer to other image-to-image tasks that benefit from cross-modal attention.
