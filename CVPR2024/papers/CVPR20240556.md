## Problem

Self-supervised denoising methods rely on pixel-wise independent noise, but real-world noise is spatially correlated due to ISP processing. Existing remedies—downsampling (causing aliasing and high-frequency detail loss) and neighborhood masking (incurring high computational cost or ignoring local structure at inference)—fail to simultaneously preserve texture richness and suppress noise correlation. The paper identifies that training at original resolution with asymmetric operations between training and inference is the key missing ingredient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (e.g., 256×256 patches from SIDD or full-resolution images from DND) and outputs a denoised RGB image at the same resolution. During training, the network internally rotates the input by 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270° to build four directional feature branches, but the external interface remains a single-image-in, single-image-out pipeline. The distilled student (AT-BSN-D) is a standard non-blind-spot U-Net requiring no rotation or shifting at inference, operating on 512×512 patches with only 48.92G MACs.

## Method

AT-BSN builds on Laine's four-branch shifted-convolution U-Net but introduces a tunable blind-spot: after extracting a half-plane-restricted feature map, a spatial shift M(·;s) is applied before the 1×1 fusion convolutions, creating a central blind-spot of size k=2s−1 that can be freely adjusted. Asymmetric blind-spots are used—k_a=9 (s=4) during training to fully suppress spatial noise correlation, and k_b=3 (s=1) during inference to minimize local information loss. The trained AT-BSN then acts as a meta-teacher: by sampling multiple shift values s_i from {0,1,3,5,7,9,11}, it generates a distribution of teacher networks that handle flat and texture regions differently. A lightweight non-blind-spot student U-Net is distilled from all teachers simultaneously via an L1 loss with equal weights, avoiding the rotation overhead of the BSN while inheriting complementary denoising knowledge.

## Results

On the SIDD validation set, AT-BSN (D) achieves 37.88 dB PSNR and 0.946 SSIM, outperforming all self-supervised baselines including Spatially-Adaptive (37.39/0.934), LG-BPN (37.32/0.886), and SDAP(E) (37.30/0.894). On the DND benchmark, AT-BSN (D) reaches 38.68 dB PSNR and 0.942 SSIM (trained on DND), surpassing C-BSN (38.60/0.941) and Spatially-Adaptive (38.58/0.936). The distilled student network also achieves the lowest computational cost among all compared methods, with only 1.02M parameters and 48.92G MACs, compared to 7653.97G MACs for AP-BSN+R3 and 12168.22G for LG-BPN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AT-BSN (D) outperforms all self-supervised and unpaired baselines across both SIDD and DND benchmarks. On SIDD validation, it leads Spatially-Adaptive [29] by 0.49 dB PSNR and 0.012 SSIM, LG-BPN [42] by 0.56 dB PSNR and 0.060 SSIM, and SDAP(E) [35] by 0.58 dB PSNR and 0.052 SSIM. On the DND benchmark (self-supervised training), AT-BSN (D) achieves 38.68 dB PSNR, edging out C-BSN [22] (38.60) and Spatially-Adaptive [29] (38.58) while also surpassing LG-BPN [42] (38.43) and SDAP(E) [35] (38.56). The non-distilled AT-BSN is slightly behind the best baselines on SIDD benchmark (36.74 vs. 37.37 for Spatially-Adaptive), but the multi-teacher distillation step closes and reverses this gap. In terms of efficiency, AT-BSN (D) requires only 48.92G MACs versus 7653.97G for AP-BSN+R3 [27] and 12168.22G for LG-BPN [42], making it roughly 156× and 249× faster at inference while delivering superior quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blind-spot network (BSN) paradigm for self-supervised denoising, building directly on Laine et al.'s shifted-convolution architecture with four restricted-receptive-field branches and AP-BSN's asymmetric training/inference strategy. The key departure is replacing pixel-shuffle downsampling (AP-BSN) or large-kernel center masking (LG-BPN) with a feature-map shift operation that creates a freely tunable blind-spot size while operating at the original input resolution. The multi-teacher distillation strategy is a novel repurposing of this tunability: a single trained AT-BSN acts as a meta-teacher, generating multiple teacher networks at different blind-spot sizes to distill a lightweight student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and validates a clear design principle for real-world self-supervised denoising: train at full resolution with a large blind-spot to suppress spatially correlated noise, then infer with a small blind-spot to preserve local structure. The practical impact is significant in efficiency — the distilled student achieves SOTA with 48.92 MACs versus 7653.97 for AP-BSN+R³, making it attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the tunable blind-spot idea generalizes beyond the BSN/shifted-convolution architecture (e.g., to Transformer-based denoisers), whether the multi-teacher distillation benefit persists with more diverse noise distributions, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame denoising where temporal correlation adds another dimension.
