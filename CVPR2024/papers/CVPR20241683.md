## Problem

Existing language-driven zero-shot domain adaptation methods like PØDA require a domain-ID at inference time to select a domain-specific model, limiting flexibility and scalability in real-world settings where the target domain may be unknown. This paper introduces Unified Language-driven Zero-shot Domain Adaptation (ULDA), a new task setting that demands a single model adapt to multiple target domains simultaneously using only text descriptions, without any domain-specific heads or prior domain knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes source-domain RGB images (Cityscapes or GTA5) paired with one-sentence natural language descriptions of each target domain (e.g., 'driving in rain'), with no access to target-domain images. At inference, a single unified model receives an RGB image from any target domain and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask without being told which domain the image belongs to. The backbone is DeepLabv3+ with a CLIP-ResNet-50 encoder, and all models are evaluated on original-resolution images without resizing.

## Method

The framework extends PØDA's two-stage pipeline (PIN-based feature simulation, then segmentation head fine-tuning) with three components. Hierarchical Context Alignment (HCA) replaces PØDA's single scene-level text alignment with multi-level alignment at scene, region (via masked average pooling prototypes), and pixel granularity, using fine-grained class-specific text embeddings (e.g., 'the bus in rain') to prevent semantic loss. Domain Consistent Representation Learning (DCRL) enforces that the cosine similarity structure between regional prototypes and text embeddings remains consistent across all target domains, preserving inter-class relational geometry. Text-Driven Rectifier (TDR) applies a learnable residual correction to PIN-simulated features during Stage-2 head tuning, using text-embedding-derived mean and standard deviation to bridge the residual gap between simulated and true target-domain features. The overall Stage-1 loss combines HCA, DCRL, and a segmentation loss; Stage-2 uses only the segmentation loss on TDR-rectified features.

## Results

On the proposed ULDA benchmark (Cityscapes → ACDC, 4 adverse-weather domains with a single shared head), ULDA achieves 42.47% mean-mIoU, surpassing PØDA* (40.65%, which uses 4 separate domain-specific heads) by 1.82% and the source-only baseline (36.44%) by 6.03%. On the Synthetic-to-Real ULDA setting (GTA5 → Cityscapes + ACDC, 5 target domains), ULDA reaches 33.21% mean-mIoU, outperforming PØDA* (31.43%) by 1.78%. In the traditional single-domain prompt-driven setting (Table 1), ULDA also consistently beats PØDA across all five scenarios, e.g., 46.00% vs 43.90% mIoU on ACDC-Snow and 44.94% vs 42.31% on ACDC-Rain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: PØDA* (ICCV 2023) and CLIPStyler (CVPR 2022). PØDA* is the direct predecessor that requires a separate segmentation head per target domain (4 heads for 4 weather scenarios); ULDA uses a single shared head yet outperforms it by 1.82% mean-mIoU on the Cityscapes→ACDC ULDA benchmark and 1.78% on the GTA5→Cityscapes+ACDC benchmark. In the traditional single-domain setting (Table 1), ULDA also beats PØDA in every scenario, with the largest gap on ACDC-Snow (+2.10% mIoU) and ACDC-Rain (+2.63% mIoU). CLIPStyler, a zero-shot style-transfer method, is consistently outperformed by a wider margin: ULDA leads by 5.72% mean-mIoU on the 4-domain ULDA task and 6.02% on the 5-domain Synthetic-to-Real task. Notably, ULDA's advantage over PØDA* is most pronounced on the hardest domain (Night: 25.40% vs 25.03% mIoU) and the most visually distinct domain (Snow: 46.00% vs 43.90%), suggesting the hierarchical alignment and domain-consistency losses are particularly effective under large domain shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends PØDA (Fahes et al., ICCV 2023), which introduced prompt-driven zero-shot domain adaptation by using CLIP text embeddings to simulate target-domain features via Prompt-driven Instance Normalization (PIN). The key conceptual departure is that PØDA trains a separate model (or head) per target domain and requires a domain-ID at inference to select the correct one, whereas ULDA removes this requirement by training a single unified model that must simultaneously adapt to multiple domains without any domain-specific routing. The three proposed components—Hierarchical Context Alignment, Domain Consistent Representation Learning, and Text-Driven Rectifier—are novel additions layered on top of PØDA's two-stage PIN pipeline, leveraging CLIP's text encoder at multiple granularities (scene, region, pixel) to preserve semantic structure during multi-domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is compelling for autonomous driving and other safety-critical applications where the operating condition (rain, snow, fog, night) may not be known in advance and training a separate model per condition is infeasible. The finding that a single unified model can match or exceed domain-specific models suggests that the per-domain specialization assumed by prior work may be unnecessary overhead. Open questions include how the approach scales to many more target domains, whether simple one-sentence prompts suffice for highly novel or compound conditions, and whether the method transfers beyond semantic segmentation to other dense prediction tasks. The reliance on CLIP's text-visual alignment quality also means performance is bounded by the text encoder's understanding of domain descriptions.
