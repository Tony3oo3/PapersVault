## Problem

In standard vision-language architectures, the vision encoder processes images in a question-agnostic manner, producing fixed-size visual features that may overlook fine-grained details relevant to the specific user query. This decoupling between visual encoding and the posed question becomes particularly problematic for tasks requiring attention to subtle or non-salient image regions, such as scene-text reading or detailed visual reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240782/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language question (or a templated instruction for captioning) as input and produces a free-form text answer or image caption. The question is consumed in two places: it is encoded and injected into the frozen ViT's self-attention layers to produce question-aware visual features, and it is also passed to the LLM as the standard textual instruction. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring no changes to the LLM or projection module, and operates in an OCR-free setting where no external OCR tokens are provided.

## Method

QA-ViT transforms a frozen ViT from a single-input encoder V(I) into a conditional encoder V(I|Q) by injecting question representations directly into the vision transformer's self-attention layers. Specifically, the question is first encoded by the LLM's own encoder (or embeddings) and projected through per-layer MLPs to match the ViT's feature dimension at each target layer. These projected question tokens are then concatenated with the visual token sequence in the top-L self-attention layers, allowing the frozen attention mechanism to perform cross-modal attention between visual and textual tokens; only the visual portion of the attention output is retained. A parallel gated residual projection (scaled by tanh(β) with β initialized to zero) compensates for the distribution shift introduced by the injected tokens while preserving the original feature space at initialization. Late fusion in the top layers is preferred because higher ViT layers capture abstract representations where fine-grained, question-relevant details are most likely to be lost. Training is parameter-efficient: the ViT and LLM remain frozen (with LoRA on the LLM), and only the per-layer MLPs and gated residual modules are updated.

## Results

QA-ViT consistently improves all tested VL architectures across general and scene-text benchmarks. On the strongest configuration (LLaVA-1.5 + QA-ViT), the method achieves 80.5 VQA^v2 score, 128.7 TextCaps CIDEr, and 36.5 VizWiz zero-shot score, outperforming the LLaVA-1.5 baseline by +0.8, +2.3, and +2.6 respectively. Gains are most pronounced on scene-text tasks: TextCaps CIDEr improves by +9.8 to +10.2 for ViT+T5 variants and +2.3 for LLaVA-1.5, while VQA^T vqa-score gains range from +1.0 to +4.8 depending on architecture. The method also improves zero-shot VizWiz performance across all models except InstructBLIP (−0.1 to −0.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QA-ViT is integrated into and compared against four VL architectures: BLIP2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, and a from-scratch ViT+T5. In all cases, QA-ViT outperforms the corresponding baseline on every seen benchmark, with the largest gains on scene-text tasks (TextCaps CIDEr +9.8 to +10.2 for ViT+T5, +2.3 for LLaVA-1.5). Against external SOTA generalist models (Table 2), LLaVA-1.5 + QA-ViT surpasses Qwen-VL on VQA^v2 (80.5 vs. 79.5) and VizWiz zero-shot (36.5 vs. 35.2), and beats IDEFICS-80B on VizWiz (36.5 vs. 36.0), though it trails Qwen-VL on VQA^T (59.1 vs. 63.8). It also outperforms mPLUG-DocOwl on TextCaps (128.7 vs. 111.9) and Shikra on VQA^v2 (80.5 vs. 77.4). Notably, the paper shows that for a given LLM size, adding QA-ViT to a smaller model (e.g., InstructBLIP-xl + QA-ViT: 106.5/69.2) can match or exceed simply scaling up to the next size (InstructBLIP-xxl: 106.1/68.5), suggesting the method provides gains complementary to model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper identifies a structural limitation in the dominant VL paradigm (frozen ViT → projection → LLM) where the vision encoder processes images without conditioning on the user's question. It departs from pix2struct, which rendered questions as image headers (OCR-specific), and from InstructBLIP's QFormer, which conditions on instructions only after the ViT has already discarded fine-grained details. The core idea repurposes the ViT's existing self-attention mechanism by concatenating projected question tokens into the top-L layers, requiring no new architectural components beyond per-layer MLPs and a gated residual projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic, parameter-efficient design (frozen ViT, LoRA on LLM, small trainable fusing modules) makes QA-ViT a practical drop-in upgrade for any existing VL system, and the consistent gains across BLIP2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, and a from-scratch ViT+T5 validate its generality. The finding that question-aware encoding yields larger relative gains on scene-text tasks than general VQA as the LLM scales suggests the vision encoder—not the language model—is the bottleneck for fine-grained visual details, pointing toward question-conditioned pretraining as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at much larger scales (e.g., 70B+ LLMs), whether dedicated question-aware pretraining of the ViT could amplify the effect, and how the approach interacts with dynamic-resolution or multi-image settings.
