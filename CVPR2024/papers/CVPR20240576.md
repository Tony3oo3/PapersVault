## Problem

Density map regression for crowd counting suffers severe performance degradation under domain shift, and existing single domain generalization (SDG) methods designed for classification or segmentation cannot be directly extended to this regression task with its continuous-valued outputs and ambiguous pixel-level ground truths. Prior SDG work for crowd counting (DCCUS) relies on dividing the source into sub-domains, which fails when the source distribution is narrow (e.g., a single weather or scene category). This paper addresses SDG for crowd counting without sub-domain partitioning, targeting both the regression nature and the label ambiguity simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (randomly cropped to 320×320 during training) and produces a pixel-wise density map of the same spatial resolution, whose sum yields the estimated crowd count. During training, photometric augmentations (color jittering, Gaussian blurring, sharpening) generate a second augmented view processed in parallel. In addition to the density map, the model outputs a patch-wise classification map (PCM) at 16×16 patch granularity that binarizes and masks out regions predicted to contain no human heads before the final density is computed.

## Method

MPCount employs a VGG16-BN encoder whose features are reconstructed through a single Attention Memory Bank (AMB) of 1024 vectors (dim 256), where each feature is expressed as a softmax attention over the memory, enabling continuous density values to be represented as linear combinations of learned prototypes. A Content Error Mask (CEM) filters input features by thresholding the element-wise difference between instance-normalized original and augmented features, removing domain-related content before AMB reconstruction. An Attention Consistency Loss (ACL) enforces that the attention distributions produced by the original and augmented views over the memory vectors remain similar, stabilizing the memory's domain-invariant representations. To address label ambiguity, a Patch-wise Classification (PC) auxiliary head predicts a binary map over 16×16 patches (head-present vs. absent) using BCE loss; the binarized PCM is then multiplied element-wise with the predicted density map to suppress spurious density in head-free regions. The total training loss combines density L2 losses on both views, PC classification losses, and the ACL, weighted by λ_cls=10 and λ_con=10.

## Results

On the primary inter-dataset benchmarks (Table 1), MPCount achieves MAE of 11.4 on A→B, 99.6 on B→A, 115.7 on A→Q, 165.6 on B→Q, 65.5 on Q→A, and 12.3 on Q→B, outperforming the closest DG baseline DCCUS on 5 of 6 settings (e.g., 18.2% MAE reduction on B→A). On the challenging narrow-source JHU-Crowd++ settings (Table 2), MPCount achieves MAE of 37.4 on SD→SR, 218.6 on SR→SD, 31.3 on SN→FH, and 216.3 on FH→SN, reducing error by 21.8% (SN→FH) and 18.6% (FH→SN) over DCCUS. Notably, MPCount even surpasses several domain adaptation methods that have access to target-domain data, such as achieving MAE 99.6 on B→A versus DAOT's 112.2 on A→B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPCount outperforms the closest DG competitor DCCUS [6] on 5 of 6 inter-dataset settings in Table 1, with the largest gain being 18.2% MAE reduction on B→A (99.6 vs. 121.8); it slightly underperforms DCCUS on Q→B (MAE 12.3 vs. 12.1, MSE 24.1 vs. 20.9). On the narrow-source JHU-Crowd++ settings (Table 2), MPCount dominates all baselines by large margins: 37.4 vs. 90.4 MAE on SD→SR and 31.3 vs. 54.5 on SN→FH, where DCCUS's sub-domain clustering fails under narrow distributions. Normalization/whitening-based SDG methods (IBN, SW, ISW) perform poorly across all settings, often worse than fully supervised baselines, confirming that feature-statistics manipulation is insufficient for regression with ambiguous labels. MPCount also surpasses domain adaptation methods that exploit target-domain data (e.g., MAE 99.6 on B→A vs. DAOT's 112.2 on A→B; MAE 31.3 on SN→FH vs. DAOT's 42.3), demonstrating that its domain-invariant feature reconstruction is competitive even without target access. Fully supervised methods (BL, MAN, ChfL, SASNet) degrade substantially under domain shift, with MAE often 2–3× higher than MPCount on cross-dataset transfers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DCCUS (Du et al., AAAAI 2023), the first single-domain generalization method for crowd counting, which used multiple sub-domain-specific memory banks and a meta-learning clustering strategy. MPCount departs from DCCUS by replacing the multi-bank, sub-domain-partitioned design with a single attention memory bank augmented by a content error mask and attention consistency loss, eliminating the need for sub-domain division and enabling operation under narrow source distributions. The patch-wise classification auxiliary task is novel to this work, addressing the label ambiguity problem that DCCUS and other prior crowd counting methods (including fully supervised baselines) leave unaddressed. The broader SDG techniques from classification and segmentation (IBN, SW, ISW) are adapted as baselines but shown to be insufficient for the regression setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SDG method for crowd counting that handles narrow source distributions without sub-domain partitioning, and it introduces a new challenging evaluation setting (scene-specific and weather-specific subsets of JHU-Crowd++) that exposes the failure of prior DG approaches. The finding that DCCUS's sub-domain clustering actually degrades performance below fully supervised baselines under narrow distributions is a useful negative result that clarifies when meta-learning-based DG is appropriate. Limitations include reliance on a VGG16-BN backbone, a limited set of photometric augmentations, and evaluation restricted to crowd counting; open questions include whether the single-AMB-plus-CEM framework transfers to other pixel-level regression tasks and whether more diverse augmentation strategies (e.g., geometric, semantic) would further improve generalization.
