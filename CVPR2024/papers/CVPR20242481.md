## Problem

Neural body representations based on NeRF or SDF achieve photorealistic quality but render slowly via ray marching and rely on backward skinning formulations that generalize poorly to unseen body poses. This paper asks whether the explicit, discrete nature of 3D Gaussian Splatting can support efficient forward skinning deformation, enabling real-time rendering of animatable human avatars that generalize to novel poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242481/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is trained on multi-view RGB video (N calibrated cameras over T timesteps) with pre-computed SMPL/SMPL-X parameters (body shape β and per-joint 3D rotations θ_t) and foreground segmentation masks. At inference, given an arbitrary body pose and camera viewpoint, it renders a novel view image in real-time (~12 ms for 512×512 on a V100). The canonical representation is a set of 3D Gaussians initialized from SMPL mesh vertices, each carrying position, quaternion orientation, scale, color, opacity, learned skinning weights, and a latent code.

## Method

HuGS represents the clothed human body as a set of 3D Gaussians in a canonical (T-pose) space and deforms them to a target pose via a coarse-to-fine pipeline. The coarse stage applies Linear Blend Skinning with per-Gaussian learned skinning weights to compute rigid joint-based transformations. The fine stage uses a shallow pose-dependent MLP (2 hidden layers, 64 neurons) that takes an attention-weighted local pose vector and a per-Gaussian latent code, outputting residual translation, rotation, and an ambient-occlusion color scaling factor. The residual translation is defined in canonical space and rotated by the LBS orientation to ensure pose generalization. Training is end-to-end with L1, D-SSIM, and LPIPS reconstruction losses plus regularization terms that minimize MLP outputs, penalize Gaussians drifting from the SMPL mesh, and softly supervise skinning weights toward the template, all optimized with Adam and 3D-GS densification/pruning heuristics.

## Results

On THuman4 (subject00), HuGS achieves 35.05 dB PSNR and 0.99 SSIM on novel view synthesis, and 32.49 dB PSNR, 0.984 SSIM, and 11.76 FID on novel pose synthesis, outperforming the best baseline PoseVocab by 1.5 dB PSNR on novel poses. Rendering speed is 0.015 s per 512×512 frame (~80 fps), two orders of magnitude faster than the next fastest method (Ani-NeRF at 1.09 s). On DNA-Rendering, HuGS consistently outperforms DVA across all three sequences (e.g., 31.5 vs 29.8 PSNR on seq 0165 novel views). On ZJU-MoCap, HuGS achieves the best novel-pose PSNR (23.69) but trails SLRF on novel-view PSNR (26.58 vs 28.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HuGS outperforms all six baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and FID for both novel view and novel pose synthesis on THuman4. The closest competitor, PoseVocab (SDF-based with joint-structured embeddings), trails by 0.82 dB PSNR on novel views and 1.52 dB on novel poses, while requiring 200× longer rendering time (3 s vs 0.015 s) and 4.8× longer training. SLRF (local NeRFs on SMPL surface) and TAVA (template-free NeRF with forward skinning) are 8–12 dB behind on novel-view PSNR, reflecting the ray-marching bottleneck. Ani-NeRF (backward skinning NeRF) and ARAH (SDF with joint root-finding) show the weakest generalization to novel poses (22.53 and 21.77 PSNR respectively), confirming the paper's argument that backward skinning formulations struggle with unseen poses. On DNA-Rendering, HuGS beats DVA (forward-deformed volumetric primitives) by 1.7–2.9 dB PSNR across all sequences, with the gap widening on novel poses. On ZJU-MoCap, HuGS is the only method to surpass SLRF on novel-pose PSNR (23.69 vs 23.61) while SLRF retains a 1.74 dB advantage on novel views, likely due to the sparse 4-camera setup where HuGS overfits.

## Relation to Prior Work

HuGS builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering paradigm [16] and the well-established forward skinning (LBS) machinery from mesh animation, repurposing both for animatable human avatars. It departs from the dominant NeRF/SDF-based avatar line (Neural Actor, Animatable NeRF, TAVA, ARAH, PoseVocab) by replacing ray marching with tile-based Gaussian rasterization and by using forward skinning on explicit primitives instead of the backward correspondence formulations those methods require. The coarse-to-fine design—LBS for global skeletal motion plus a small pose-conditioned MLP for local garment deformation—draws on ideas from SCANimate's attention-weighted local pose encoding and from prior forward-deformation approaches (DVA, SLRF, Neural Body), but is the first to apply this pipeline to 3D Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: rendering at ~80 fps (two orders of magnitude faster than NeRF/SDF baselines) while matching or exceeding their quality makes real-time deployment of photorealistic human avatars feasible for VR, gaming, and content creation. The paper also demonstrates that forward skinning on explicit primitives generalizes better to novel poses than backward skinning on implicit fields, a conceptual insight that has since spawned a wave of concurrent Gaussian-avatar works. Key open questions remain: the MLP cannot extrapolate to truly novel garment deformations (only replicate observed ones), sparse camera setups cause overfitting, and the lack of inter-Gaussian connectivity limits structural coherence—addressing these would be important next steps.
