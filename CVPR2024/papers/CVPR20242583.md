## Problem

Existing dense correspondence-based methods for category-level 6D pose estimation do not explicitly encode the local and global geometric structure of each instance, causing numerous incorrect correspondences on unseen instances with large intra-category shape variations. The paper identifies that points from different parts of the same object share similar visual features (requiring local geometry to disambiguate), while points across different instances share similar local structures (requiring global geometry to map to correct NOCS coordinates).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242583/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a segmented RGB-D crop (resized to 192×192) and a 1024-point cloud obtained by back-projecting the depth image with camera intrinsics, both conditioned on a MaskRCNN segmentation mask and category label. Rather than predicting dense per-point NOCS coordinates, it outputs NOCS coordinates for only 96 sparse keypoints, from which the final 3D rotation (via 6D representation), residual translation, and object size are regressed through separate MLP heads.

## Method

AG-Pose introduces two coupled modules. The Instance-Adaptive Keypoint Detection (IAKD) module initializes category-shared learnable queries, converts them into instance-adaptive detectors by attending over object features in a Transformer layer, and localizes keypoints via cosine-similarity heatmaps weighted against the point cloud; a diversity loss and an object-aware chamfer distance loss (computed against outlier-filtered ground-truth surface points) enforce dispersed, surface-faithful keypoint placement. The Geometric-Aware Feature Aggregation (GAFA) module then enriches each keypoint feature in two stages: first, it aggregates features from the K=16 spatially nearest neighbors using relative-positional-embedding-guided attention to capture local geometry, and second, it concatenates a global average-pooled feature with pairwise relative-positional embeddings among all 96 keypoints to inject global geometry. This sparse keypoint-level correspondence strategy avoids the O(N²) cost of full attention over all points while explicitly encoding the geometric cues that dense methods lack.

## Results

On the challenging REAL275 dataset, AG-Pose achieves 83.7% IoU50, 79.5% IoU75, 54.7% at 5°2cm, 61.7% at 5°5cm, 74.7% at 10°2cm, and 83.1% at 10°5cm, outperforming all prior methods across every metric without using shape priors. On the synthetic CAMERA25 dataset, AG-Pose reaches 93.8% IoU50, 91.3% IoU75, 77.8% at 5°2cm, 82.8% at 5°5cm, 85.5% at 10°2cm, and 91.6% at 10°5cm, achieving the best or near-best performance on all metrics. The method uses only 96 sparse keypoints (vs. dense point-level correspondences) and requires no categorical shape priors at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AG-Pose outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric of the REAL275 dataset. Against the strongest prior-free method Query6DoF [37], AG-Pose gains +5.7% on 5°2cm (54.7 vs 49.0), +2.8% on 5°5cm, +6.0% on 10°2cm, and +0.1% on 10°5cm, while also leading on IoU50 (+1.2) and IoU75 (+3.4). Against the best shape-prior method DPDN [17], AG-Pose surpasses it by +8.7% on 5°2cm, +11.0% on 5°5cm, +4.3% on 10°2cm, and +4.7% on 10°5cm, despite not using any shape priors. On CAMERA25, AG-Pose is marginally behind Query6DoF on 5°2cm (77.8 vs 78.0) and 5°5cm (82.8 vs 83.1) but leads on IoU50 (+1.9), IoU75 (+3.2), 10°2cm (+1.6), and 10°5cm (+1.6). The older baselines (NOCS, DualPoseNet, GPV-Pose, SPD, SGPA, SAR-Net) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 20% on 5°2cm for most of them. The paper also shows that replacing the learned IAKD with Farthest Point Sampling drops 5°2cm from 54.7% to 46.2%, confirming the importance of adaptive keypoint detection over fixed sampling strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

AG-Pose departs from the dominant dense-correspondence paradigm in category-level 6D pose estimation (NOCS, DualPoseNet, DPDN, IST-Net) and the shape-prior deformation line (SPD, SGPA, SAR-Net, RBP-Pose) by replacing per-point NOCS prediction with a sparse set of adaptively detected keypoints. It borrows the pose-regression backbone from DPDN [17] and the residual-translation strategy from HS-Pose [46], but introduces a Transformer-query-based keypoint detector (analogous to DETR-style object queries) and a two-stage geometric feature aggregator that encodes local and global relative positions. The paper positions itself as the first adaptive keypoint-based method for category-level pose estimation, contrasting with both fixed-keypoint instance-level methods and implicit-query approaches like Query6DoF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on REAL275 without any categorical shape prior, AG-Pose removes a practical dependency that limits the deployability of prior methods to categories where a good prior is available. The geometric-aware keypoint formulation also offers a more interpretable and computationally lighter alternative to dense correspondences, which is relevant for real-time robotic manipulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to categories with extreme intra-class variation (e.g., articulated objects), whether the fixed keypoint count of 96 is optimal across all object geometries, and how the method performs under severe occlusion or domain shift beyond the two standard benchmarks.
