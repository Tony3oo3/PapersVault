## Problem

Previous pedestrian trajectory prediction methods model social interactions only at the pair-wise or group-wise level, ignoring the 'social depth' — the indirect, cascading influences that propagate from socially distant agents through chains of interactions. This higher-order relational structure, where one agent's decision alters the behavior of a third party further away, has not been explored in the trajectory prediction literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes h frames (8 frames, 3.2 s) of observed 2D trajectory coordinates for N agents and produces f frames (12 frames, 4.8 s) of future trajectories. Coordinates are in meters for ETH/UCY and in pixels for SDD. Because HighGraph is a plug-and-play module, it outputs a learned social-relation embedding that can be fed into either deterministic predictors (yielding point 2D coordinates) or probabilistic CVAE-based predictors (yielding a conditional trajectory distribution p(Y|X;Θ)), with K=20 sampled trajectories for evaluation.

## Method

HighGraph consists of two core components. First, a collision-aware kernel function constructs a weighted adjacency matrix by computing the potential collision point C_ij as the intersection of two trajectory half-lines for each agent pair, then assigning interaction weights inversely proportional to the distance from each agent's current position to C_ij, scaled by the inter-agent distance d_ij. Second, higher-order graph convolution powers the normalized adjacency matrix to the m-th degree (m=1,…,M) to aggregate features from m-hop social neighbors, and a combination layer concatenates all M order-specific features and distills them through an MLP with learnable per-order weights λ_m (summing to 1) into a single representative embedding. The module is designed to be attached to any existing predictor — replacing the graph convolution in Social-STGCNN, feeding the recurrent encoder output in Social-GAN/SoPhie, or concatenating with CVAE latent features — and is trained end-to-end under standard supervised loss.

## Results

HighGraph, when plugged into six state-of-the-art trajectory predictors, consistently reduces minADE20 and minFDE20 across all ETH/UCY subsets and SDD. The most substantial gains appear on Social-GAN (9%/13% average improvement) and Social-STGCNN (11%/21%), with the HOTEL subset showing up to 52% minFDE20 reduction for Social-STGCNN. On SDD, Social-GAN+HighGraph drops minADE20 from 27.23 to 18.60 (31% improvement), and Social-STGCNN+HighGraph from 20.60 to 15.38 (25%). CVAE-based methods (SocialVAE, EigenTrajectory) show smaller but consistent gains (4–7% minADE20), which the authors attribute to the latent-space learning dominating over social features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HighGraph is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on six trajectory predictors, and it improves every one of them on both ETH/UCY and SDD. The largest relative gains are on Social-GAN (9%/13% avg minADE/FDE improvement; 31% minADE on SDD) and Social-STGCNN (11%/21% avg; 25%/20% on SDD), where the HOTEL subset shows up to 52% minFDE20 reduction. SoPhie benefits by 18%/15% on average, while BiTraP improves by 11%/18%. CVAE-based methods (SocialVAE, EigenTrajectory) show smaller but consistent gains (4–7% minADE20), which the authors attribute to the CVAE latent space dominating over social features. In the GNN-variant ablation (Table 3), HighGraph outperforms both vanilla GCN (1.31/1.90 vs. 1.19/1.75 avg) and MixHop (1.30/1.86 vs. 1.19/1.75) when attached to a simple LSTM encoder, confirming that the collision-aware kernel and combination layer add value beyond adjacency-matrix powering alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

HighGraph extends the graph convolutional network framework (Kipf & Welling) and the adjacency-matrix-powering mechanism of MixHop to the pedestrian trajectory prediction domain, where prior social-relation modeling has been limited to pair-wise (Social-LSTM, Social-GAN, Social-STGCNN) or group-wise hypergraph (GroupNet, EvolveHypergraph) interactions. The key conceptual departure is treating social influence as a multi-hop, indirect chain rather than a direct or group-level effect, and grounding the graph construction in a collision-aware kernel that encodes the physical prior that pedestrians avoid collisions. The module is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play add-on, so it does not replace the backbone predictor but augments its encoder with higher-order relational features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design makes HighGraph immediately applicable to a wide range of existing predictors, and the consistent (if moderate) gains across six diverse baselines on two benchmarks validate the value of modeling indirect social influences. However, improvements on CVAE-based methods are notably smaller, suggesting that latent-space learning may already absorb some higher-order dynamics, and the UNIV subset analysis reveals that gains depend on the training set containing sufficiently dense social interactions. Open questions include whether the collision-aware kernel generalizes to non-pedestrian agents (vehicles, cyclists), whether the approach scales to very large crowds where the adjacency matrix becomes dense, and whether the higher-order depth M can be learned adaptively per scene rather than fixed.
