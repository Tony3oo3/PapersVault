## Problem

Prior work on permutation equivariance of Transformers was restricted to inter-token (row) shuffling in forward propagation only, leaving intra-token (column) permutation and the backward (training) propagation unaddressed. This paper asks whether a unified, stricter notion of permutation equivariance—covering both row and column permutations in both forward and backward passes—holds for standard Transformer architectures without structural modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241423/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard Transformer inputs (RGB images at 224×224 for ViT, tokenized text for BERT/GPT-2) and produces the same task outputs as the unpermuted model (class labels, pixel-level segmentation masks, generated text). The distinguishing element is the permutation matrix P_R ∈ R^{n×n} (row, applied per-batch) and P_C ∈ R^{d×d} (column, fixed per model), which act as a 'key': without P_C, the permuted model's outputs degrade to random-guess level (~10% on CIFAR-10, perplexity ~10⁷ for GPT-2), effectively 'encrypting' the weights.

## Method

The paper proves that vanilla Transformer backbones satisfy row permutation forward equivariance (Enc(P_R Z) = P_R Enc(Z)) with no weight modification, and column permutation forward equivariance (T_(C)(Z P_C) = T(Z) P_C) provided weights are conjugated as W_(C) = P_C⁻¹ W P_C. Crucially, it extends the analysis to backward propagation, showing gradients transform as ∂l/∂W_(C) = P_C⁻¹ (∂l/∂W) P_C, so trained weights are exactly equivalent (row case) or conjugate-equivalent (column case) to the unpermuted model. A general theorem (Thm. 4.7) establishes that any network composed of permutation-equivariant operators with linear weight arguments inherits both forward and backward equivariance. As proof-of-concept applications, row permutation enhances privacy in split learning by making model-inversion attacks significantly harder (SSIM drops from 0.45 to 0.11 for LP), while column permutation serves as a model authorization mechanism that prevents unauthorized fine-tuning from recovering full model utility.

## Results

On CelebA split learning, the proposed row permutation enhances all three privacy baselines while preserving or improving utility: LP+ achieves 90.2% accuracy with SSIM 0.110, PSNR 5.687, and F-SIM 0.154, compared to LP's 90.1% accuracy with SSIM 0.450, PSNR 14.71, and F-SIM 0.614. For model encryption, the authorized fine-tuning of the permuted ViT reaches 97.94% test accuracy (matching the normal 97.74%), while the unauthorized party without the permutation key drops to 78.03%. The permutation overhead is negligible, adding only four extra matrix multiplications with no measurable impact on FLOPs or memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its row-permutation enhancement against two split-learning privacy baselines: Gaussian Noise (GN) and Loss-Pass Filter (LP) from Jeong et al. [15], and Batch Shuffle (BS) from Yao et al. [33]. Against GN, the enhanced GN+ matches accuracy exactly (88.8%) while reducing SSIM from 0.457 to 0.167, PSNR from 15.47 to 8.068, and F-SIM from 0.663 to 0.187 — a roughly 3× improvement in all privacy metrics. Against LP, LP+ slightly improves accuracy (90.2% vs 90.1%) while cutting SSIM from 0.450 to 0.110 and PSNR from 14.71 to 5.687, representing a ~4× privacy gain. Against BS, BS+ improves both accuracy (91.1% vs 90.8%) and all three privacy metrics (SSIM 0.143 vs 0.148, PSNR 7.613 vs 8.180, F-SIM 0.167 vs 0.176), though the margin is smaller since BS already incorporates a shuffling mechanism. In the model-encryption application, the authorized fine-tuning of the column-permuted model matches or slightly exceeds the normal fine-tuning (e.g., ViT: 97.94% vs 97.74%), while the unauthorized party without the key is limited to 78.03% on ViT, 77.25% on BERT, and 86.16% on GPT-2 — far below the authorized performance but above training from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the permutation equivariance framework introduced by Set Transformer [17], which established inter-token permutation invariance but required a dedicated PMA head and only addressed forward propagation. The authors generalize the definition to cover both inter- and intra-token (column) permutation and, crucially, prove that vanilla Transformer architectures satisfy this broader property in both forward and backward propagation without any structural modification. The work also builds on empirical observations of ViT's robustness to patch shuffling [26] and the privacy-preserving split learning literature [15, 33], which had exploited only the forward invariance property for feature obfuscation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that standard Transformers are inherently permutation-equivariant in both forward and backward passes provides a clean mathematical foundation for using permutation as a lightweight privacy and security primitive, with the model 'encryption' application (column-permuted weights are useless without the key) being a particularly novel use. However, the security guarantees are weaker than true encryption, and the property does not extend to sliding-window operators like convolutions, limiting generality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large models with heterogeneous architectures, how robust the 'encryption' is against adaptive attacks that exploit partial knowledge of the permutation structure, and whether the backward equivariance result holds under distributed or asynchronous training regimes.
