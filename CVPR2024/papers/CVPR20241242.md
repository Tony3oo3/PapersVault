## Problem

Collaborative multi-vehicle perception is hindered by three practical challenges: (1) communication bandwidth limits, where existing compression methods only exploit inter-class (foreground/background) redundancy while ignoring intra-class redundancy among similar features; (2) vehicle localization errors that cause spatial feature misalignment, where prior solutions either require high-latency pose optimization or ignore the ego-vehicle's locally accurate information; and (3) information aggregation inefficiencies, where fusion paradigms discard the ego-vehicle's noise-free positional cues in favor of potentially corrupted collaborative features.

## Input / Output

Each vehicle encodes its local LiDAR point cloud into a BEV feature map (H×W×C) via a shared PointPillar encoder after projecting into the ego-vehicle coordinate frame using broadcast metadata (position, heading, speed). Rather than transmitting full feature maps, vehicles send a compressed set of K merged feature vectors (K = α×β×H×W) with index records for reconstruction. The final output is a set of 3D rotated bounding boxes parameterized as (class, x, y, h, w, cos α, sin α) per spatial position, trained with focal loss for classification and smooth L1 loss for regression.

## Method

ERMVP comprises four core modules operating in sequence. First, a Filter and Merge Feature Sampling (FMS) strategy generates a confidence map via a detection-decoder head, thresholds it with NMS to extract sparse foreground features, then clusters them using a nearest-neighbor variant and merges each cluster via confidence-weighted averaging, reducing communication by addressing both inter-class and intra-class redundancy. Second, a Feature Spatial Calibration (FSC) module identifies consensus matching regions between ego and collaborative sparse features, solves a linear assignment problem, refines the transformation via RANSAC-based geometric verification, and applies an error modulation step that selects between calibrated and original features based on overlap ratios. Third, an Attention-based Feature Fusion (AFF) module stacks all vehicles' features into a 4D tensor and alternates between 3D local self-attention within N×P×P windows and a global attention mechanism using a shared query token (generated via depth-wise convolution, SE, and max-pooling) to capture both local matching and long-range context. Fourth, an Accuracy Enhanced Feature Interaction (AEI) module performs context cross-attention where ego-vehicle features serve as queries and fused features as keys/values within local neighborhoods, followed by split-attention adaptive fusion, thereby preserving the ego's noise-free positional accuracy while incorporating collaborative semantic richness.

## Results

On V2V4Real with 0.4/0.4 m/° pose noise, ERMVP achieves 65.01/40.45 AP@0.5/0.7, outperforming the best SOTA (V2X-ViT at 63.33/32.38) by 1.68/8.07 points. On OPV2V under the same noise, ERMVP reaches 85.67/66.32 AP@0.5/0.7, surpassing CoBEVT (85.49/60.64) by 0.18/5.68 points. Under the most challenging 0.8/0.8 noise setting, ERMVP maintains 60.88/35.01 on V2V4Real and 77.13/59.15 on OPV2V, while most baselines degrade severely (e.g., DiscoNet drops to 49.19/24.94 and 61.37/30.53 respectively). Overall, ERMVP delivers average improvements of 12.48%/18.47% (V2V4Real) and 6.18%/10.02% (OPV2V) over the No Collaboration baseline in AP@0.5/0.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERMVP outperforms all eight baselines across every noise level on both datasets. The closest competitors are V2X-ViT [42] on V2V4Real and CoBEVT [41] on OPV2V: at 0.4/0.4 noise, ERMVP leads V2X-ViT by 1.68/8.07 AP@0.5/0.7 on V2V4Real and CoBEVT by 0.18/5.68 on OPV2V. The gap widens dramatically under 0.8/0.8 noise, where ERMVP surpasses V2X-ViT by 3.73/6.79 on V2V4Real and CoAlign [25] (the best OPV2V baseline at that setting) by 3.18/10.29. Earlier methods such as F-Cooper [3], AttFuse [7], and DiscoNet [21] degrade to near or below the No Collaboration baseline under moderate-to-high noise (e.g., F-Cooper drops to 41.90/19.91 on V2V4Real 0.8/0.8 vs. 41.64/23.43 for no collaboration), whereas ERMVP retains a clear margin. Where2comm [15] and V2VNet [38] show moderate robustness but still trail ERMVP by 5–12 AP@0.7 points under 0.8/0.8 noise. The paper also reports that ERMVP achieves equivalent detection accuracy to V2VNet, CoBEVT, and V2X-ViT with substantially less communication volume (Figure 5), highlighting its communication-efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three active threads in collaborative perception: communication-efficient feature transmission (Where2comm, spatial confidence maps), pose-error-robust alignment (CoAlign, which uses intensive optimization), and multi-vehicle feature fusion (V2VNet, V2X-ViT, CoBEVT). It departs from prior work by simultaneously addressing all three challenges rather than treating them in isolation: the Filter and Merge Feature Sampling extends Where2comm's inter-class spatial filtering with intra-class redundancy removal via confidence-weighted clustering; the Feature Spatial Calibration replaces CoAlign's high-latency pose optimization with a lightweight RANSAC-based consensus matching on sparse foreground features; and the Accuracy Enhanced Feature Interaction introduces a cross-attention mechanism that preserves ego-centric positional accuracy against collaborative noise, a concern largely overlooked by prior fusion methods that treat all vehicles' features symmetrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment of V2V collaborative perception has been stalled by the combined effects of limited wireless bandwidth, GPS/IMU localization drift, and naive fusion that discards ego-vehicle strengths; ERMVP's unified treatment of all three is a step toward deployable systems. The dramatic robustness gap at high noise (29.43% AP@0.7 over the second-best on OPV2V at 1.0 m localization error) suggests that prior methods' failure under realistic conditions is not a minor degradation but a fundamental vulnerability. Open questions include scalability beyond the 70 m communication range and 2–7 vehicle settings tested here, generalization to camera-based or multi-modal collaborative perception (acknowledged as future work), and whether the RANSAC-based calibration degrades gracefully in highly dynamic scenes with few overlapping detections.
