## Problem

While visual language models have rapidly advanced through instruction tuning, the pre-training stage—where the model learns joint modeling across modalities—remains underexplored. Most prior work focuses on SFT or RLHF, leaving open how design choices like LLM freezing, data composition, and projector architecture during pre-training affect downstream performance and the inheritance of LLM properties such as in-context learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 336×336 resolution (yielding 576 visual tokens per image via a CLIP encoder and linear projector) concatenated with text tokens in an auto-regressive sequence, supporting arbitrary interleaved multi-image inputs. Outputs are free-form text: VQA answers, image captions, chain-of-thought reasoning, or in-context learning completions. The architecture treats visual tokens as a 'foreign language' fed alongside text, enabling a unified interface for diverse visual-language tasks.

## Method

VILA is an empirical study of auto-regressive VLM pre-training that ablates three key design axes: (1) whether to fine-tune or freeze the LLM during pre-training, (2) interleaved image-text corpus (MMC4) versus image-text pairs (COYO), and (3) joint SFT blending text-only instruction data (FLAN) with visual instruction data. The core insight is that fine-tuning the LLM enables deep-layer embedding alignment between visual and textual tokens (measured via Chamfer distance), which is necessary for in-context learning; interleaved data structure (not just text length) provides accurate gradient signals and prevents catastrophic forgetting of text-only capability; and re-blending text-only SFT data simultaneously recovers MMLU accuracy and boosts VLM task performance. The final recipe uses a CLIP-L encoder at 336×336, a simple linear projector, Llama-2 7B/13B backbone, ~50M images of pre-training data (MMC4+COYO at 1:1 ratio), and 1M joint SFT samples, outperforming LLaVA-1.5 across 12 benchmarks without additional architectural complexity.

## Results

VILA-7B (Llama-2-7B, 336px, 50M pre-training images) achieves 57.8 on VizWiz, 64.4 on TextVQA, 68.9 on MMBench, and 69.7 on LLaVA-Bench, consistently outperforming LLaVA-1.5-7B across all 12 visual-language benchmarks. Notably, VILA-7B surpasses the larger LLaVA-1.5-13B on VizWiz (57.8 vs. 53.6) and TextVQA (64.4 vs. 61.3). VILA-13B further extends these gains, reaching 60.6 on VizWiz, 66.6 on TextVQA, 70.3 on MMBench, and 38.8 on MM-Vet. On text-only benchmarks, VILA-13B matches or exceeds Llama-2+SFT (MMLU 56.0% vs. 54.3%, DROP 63.6% vs. 59.2%), confirming that the pre-training recipe preserves language capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LLaVA-1.5 (same Llama-2 backbone, same prompts), VILA-7B wins on all 12 benchmarks, with the largest margins on VizWiz (+7.8 over 7B, +4.2 over 13B) and TextVQA (+6.2 over 7B, +3.1 over 13B), demonstrating that 50M-image pre-training compensates for the lack of billion-scale data. VILA-7B notably outperforms the larger LLaVA-1.5-13B on VizWiz and TextVQA, suggesting pre-training quality matters more than parameter count for fine-grained visual tasks. Compared to InstructBLIP (frozen-LLM approach), VILA gains 23+ points on VizWiz and 14+ on TextVQA, validating the paper's finding that unfreezing the LLM during pre-training is essential for in-context learning. Against Qwen-VL-Chat (which uses 1.4B pre-training images), VILA-7B achieves comparable or better results on most benchmarks despite using only 50M images, though Qwen-VL has a higher-resolution encoder (448px). On text-only benchmarks (MMLU, BBH, DROP), VILA-13B slightly exceeds Llama-2+SFT, while VILA-7B shows a small gap (~1%), confirming the joint SFT recipe effectively recovers language capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

VILA builds directly on the auto-regressive VLM architecture (CLIP encoder + projector + LLM) popularized by LLaVA [37] and the three-stage training pipeline (projector initialization, visual-language pre-training, instruction tuning) shared by BLIP-2 [33], InstructBLIP [17], and others. However, it deliberately shifts focus from the instruction-tuning stage—where most prior effort has concentrated—to the pre-training stage, which the authors identify as under-studied despite being the most computationally expensive and critical for modality alignment. The paper departs from frozen-LLM approaches (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP) by demonstrating that fine-tuning the LLM during pre-training is necessary for in-context learning, and from image-text-pair pre-training (LAION, COYO) by showing interleaved corpora (MMC4) are essential for preserving text-only capabilities and enabling multi-image reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VILA provides a concrete, ablated pre-training recipe that practitioners can adopt without architectural novelty, and demonstrates that even 50M images of pre-training (far below the billion-scale used by Flamingo or PaliGemma) yields consistent SOTA improvements over LLaVA-1.5 across 12 benchmarks. The unlocked capabilities—multi-image reasoning, visual CoT, and stronger ICL—suggest that pre-training depth, not just SFT quality, is the key differentiator for next-generation VLMs. Key limitations include the modest 50M-image scale (billion-scale scaling is left as future work), evaluation on a single LLM family (Llama-2), and the absence of token compression in the final model despite showing its potential. Open questions include whether the interleaved-data advantage persists at billion scale and whether the linear-projector finding generalizes to larger backbones.
