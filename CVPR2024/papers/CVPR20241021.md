## Problem

Reconstructing 3D human-object interactions from a single RGB image without object templates is severely limited by data: real interaction datasets (BEHAVE, InterCap) cover only 10–20 objects, while a single category like chairs contains 6k+ shapes, and the combinatorial explosion of human×object variations makes real capture infeasible. Existing template-free methods (PC²) cannot separate human from object or achieve high shape fidelity, while template-based methods (CHORE) cannot generalize to unseen objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and assumed 2D human/object segmentation masks as input, and outputs separate 3D point clouds for the human and the object along with per-point segmentation labels. Because monocular reconstruction is inherently ambiguous, the model operates probabilistically via diffusion, predicting a distribution over plausible 3D configurations rather than a single deterministic output. All point clouds are normalized to a unit sphere centered at the origin, with normalization parameters estimated per sample to project back to image space.

## Method

The paper contributes two components. First, ProciGen procedurally generates 1M+ interaction images by training a per-category autoencoder (PointNet encoder + ordered-MLP decoder) to learn dense semantic correspondences between objects, transferring contact maps from a small seed dataset (BEHAVE/InterCap) to 21k+ new object instances, then jointly optimizing SMPL-H body pose and object SE(3) pose under contact, normal-alignment, and interpenetration-avoidance constraints. Second, HDM is a two-stage hierarchical diffusion model: Stage 1 uses a PVCNN-based point cloud diffusion network (built on PC²) to jointly predict the combined human+object point cloud with segmentation labels, capturing interaction context; Stage 2 applies two separate diffusion models (one per entity) with cross-attention layers between human and object branches to refine individual shapes, starting the reverse process from an intermediate step T₀=T/2 so that the global interaction structure from Stage 1 is preserved while local geometry is sharpened.

## Results

On BEHAVE, the proposed HDM trained on ProciGen + real data achieves a combined F-score@0.01m of 0.4604 (Human: 0.3925, Object: 0.5049), outperforming CHORE (0.3966) and PC² (0.4231). On InterCap, HDM reaches 0.5344 combined F-score (Human: 0.4399, Object: 0.6072), beating CHORE (0.4687) and PC² (0.5057). Notably, training HDM on ProciGen synthetic data alone (without any real interaction data) already achieves 0.4110 on BEHAVE and 0.4530 on InterCap, performing on par with CHORE which has access to object templates. For generalization to unseen objects (Table 4), ProciGen training boosts combined F-score from 0.2127 (BEHAVE-only) to 0.3976 on InterCap unseen objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: CHORE (template-based, requires known object meshes) and PC² (template-free point cloud diffusion). On BEHAVE, HDM outperforms CHORE by 6.4 points in combined F-score (0.4604 vs. 0.3966) and PC² by 3.7 points (0.4604 vs. 0.4231), while also being the only method that reports separate human and object scores. On InterCap, the margin widens: HDM beats CHORE by 6.6 points (0.5344 vs. 0.4687) and PC² by 2.9 points (0.5344 vs. 0.5057). A key advantage is that HDM does not require any object templates, whereas CHORE is limited to the specific shapes it was trained on. For generalization to unseen objects (Table 4), methods trained only on BEHAVE achieve ~0.21–0.23 combined F-score on InterCap, while HDM + ProciGen reaches 0.3976, nearly doubling the score. PC² + ProciGen also benefits from the synthetic data (0.3843 vs. 0.2327), confirming the dataset's value independent of the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PC2's projection-conditioned point cloud diffusion architecture as the backbone for its joint human-object reconstruction stage, while departing from the template-based interaction reconstruction line (CHORE, BEHAVE) by eliminating the need for predefined object meshes. The key conceptual departure is the procedural data generation pipeline (ProciGen), which leverages per-category autoencoder correspondences to transfer contact patterns from a small seed interaction set to thousands of unseen object shapes — a multiplicative scaling strategy not previously applied to interaction data. The hierarchical diffusion design (joint prediction followed by separate human/object refinement with cross-attention) extends the single-model diffusion paradigm of PC2 to handle the combinatorial complexity of the joint shape space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released 1M+ interaction dataset with 21k+ diverse object shapes addresses a critical data bottleneck in interaction reconstruction, enabling template-free methods to generalize to unseen objects — a capability that prior template-based methods fundamentally lack. The ~2× generalization improvement over BEHAVE-only training on InterCap's unseen objects demonstrates that synthetic data with diverse geometry can close the gap that real capture cannot economically fill. Open questions include whether the per-category correspondence autoencoder scales to open-vocabulary or cross-category interactions, whether the 2D segmentation mask assumption holds in truly in-the-wild settings, and whether the hierarchical decomposition generalizes to multi-object or articulated-object interactions.
