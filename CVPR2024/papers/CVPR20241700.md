## Problem

In open-world semi-supervised learning, unlabeled data contain both known and previously unseen novel classes, but existing methods rely on similarity-based clustering that suffers from representation collapse—features of different novel categories become indistinguishable and converge into the same cluster due to the absence of novel class supervision. This paper addresses how to enforce a discriminative, well-separated feature structure for novel classes without any ground-truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241700/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled set of images from known classes (50% of known-class samples) and an unlabeled set containing both known and novel class images, with the number of novel classes assumed known a priori. It produces class labels for all samples via a prototypical classifier operating on L2-normalized features, evaluated using Hungarian matching for novel classes. Experiments use ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-100, and ViT-B/16 with DINO pre-training on fine-grained semantic shift benchmarks (CUB-200, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, Herbarium 19).

## Method

TRAILER leverages the neural collapse phenomenon by pre-assigning a fixed simplex equiangular tight frame (ETF) as the target structure for a prototypical classifier, then progressively aligns features toward these ETF vectors while preserving within-cluster compactness via a ramped-down interpolation between prototypical clustering loss and ETF alignment loss. To generate reliable pseudo-labels for unlabeled samples, a hierarchical allocation mechanism first performs rough assignment via optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp with equipartition constraints), then refines labels using a PU-learning auxiliary head that separates known from novel classes, and finally applies confidence-based pseudo-label filtering. The overall training proceeds in two phases: a warm-up with cross-entropy, contrastive, and entropy losses, followed by the main phase combining the progressive classification loss, PU auxiliary loss, contrastive loss, and entropy regularization.

## Results

TRAILER achieves 94.4% overall accuracy on CIFAR-10, 55.6% on CIFAR-100, and 85.3% on ImageNet-100, improving over the best baselines by 3.1%, 2.9%, and 1.5% respectively. On the more challenging CIFAR-100, TRAILER reaches 48.7% novel-class accuracy (vs. 47.8% for OpenCon) while maintaining 69.7% known-class accuracy. On fine-grained Semantic Shift Benchmarks, TRAILER leads by 8.8% (CUB), 14.8% (Stanford Cars), 2.6% (FGVC-Aircraft), and 9.2% (Herbarium 19) in novel-class accuracy over the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAILER outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and metric. On CIFAR-10, it beats the strongest open-world SSL baselines NACH (91.3%) and OpenNCD (90.1%) by 3.1% and 4.3% overall accuracy respectively, while also surpassing the SSL baseline FixMatch (49.5%) by a wide 44.9% margin. On CIFAR-100, TRAILER (55.6%) leads OpenCon (52.7%) and NACH (52.1%) by 2.9% and 3.5%, with the gap widening on novel classes (48.7% vs. 47.8% and 47.0%). On ImageNet-100, TRAILER (85.3%) edges out OpenCon (83.8%) by 1.5% and NACH (79.6%) by 5.7%. On fine-grained Semantic Shift Benchmarks, TRAILER dominates: it surpasses GPC (55.4%) by 9.7% on CUB, OpenCon (49.1%) by 6.3% on Stanford Cars, GPC (46.3%) by 8.2% on FGVC-Aircraft, and OpenCon (39.3%) by 5.2% on Herbarium 19. Notably, traditional SSL and open-set SSL baselines (FixMatch, DS3L, CGDL) and NCD methods (DTC, RankStats) are substantially outperformed, confirming that open-world SSL methods are necessary for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAILER builds on the neural collapse phenomenon (Papyan et al., PNAS 2020) by repurposing its simplex ETF structure as an explicit, pre-assigned geometric target for representation calibration, rather than relying on the implicit convergence that prior open-world SSL methods (ORCA, OpenCon, GCD, NACH) hope for through similarity-based or contrastive clustering. The prototypical classifier design follows Fini et al. (ICCV 2021), and the known-novel separation subtask is reformulated as PU learning using a variational algorithm from Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2020). The key conceptual departure from prior work is shifting from letting representations 'spontaneously' organize into clusters to actively and progressively aligning them toward a fixed optimal arrangement, combined with a coarse-to-fine pseudo-label allocation via optimal transport and PU-based refinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode (representation collapse) that plagues all current open-world SSL methods, and the ablation showing novel-class accuracy dropping from 95.0 to 58.9 (CIFAR-10) without ETF calibration underscores how critical explicit geometric targeting is. The progressive alignment schedule and PU-based label refinery are practical, modular techniques that could be adopted in other semi-supervised or open-set settings. Open questions remain: whether the ETF target remains optimal under severe class imbalance or very large class spaces, whether the assumption of known novel-class count can be fully relaxed, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-visual modalities or multi-label scenarios.
