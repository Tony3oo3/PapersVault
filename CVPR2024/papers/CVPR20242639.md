## Problem

In point-level weakly-supervised temporal action localization (P-TAL), existing methods derive proposal confidence directly from class activation sequences (CAS), which are unreliable and produce a significant misalignment between proposal quality and confidence scores (e.g., 65.2% of final proposals in LAC have better alternatives). This paper identifies that humans consistently annotate the most salient frame near the center of each action instance, and exploits this temporal saliency prior to realign confidence with actual proposal quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242639/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in on proposals generated by a base CAS-based locator (e.g., LAC). It consumes snippet-level I3D features (2048-dim, RGB + flow, 16 frames per snippet) and single-point timestamp annotations per instance, then produces refined proposals with start/end timestamps, category labels, and aligned confidence scores. The proposal-level features are extracted via surrounding contrastive feature extraction (SCFE) to 1024-dim vectors, and the final output is obtained after alignment-based boundary adaptation followed by Soft-NMS.

## Method

TSP-Net is a proposal-level plug-in framework with two core components. Center Score Learning (CSL) generates hard/soft center labels from the normalized offset between each proposal's center and the saliency point (initialized from annotations), then trains a 1D-conv branch to predict aligned center scores via MSE loss; a saliency point updating strategy iteratively refines the initial points using weighted pseudo-centers from high-confidence proposals to reduce annotation noise. Alignment-based Boundary Adaptation (ABA) fuses actionness, class probability, and center score into an aligned confidence, then adaptively refines the most confident proposal's boundaries using a weighted combination of its own and neighboring proposals' boundaries, where weights incorporate both aligned confidence and normalized IoU to balance importance and relevance. The framework is agnostic to the base locator and can be attached to any P-TAL method.

## Results

On THUMOS'14, TSP-Net achieves 67.9% average mAP (0.1:0.5), 48.3% average mAP (0.3:0.7), and 57.4% average mAP (0.1:0.7), surpassing the best P-TAL method CRRC-Net by 3.3%, 3.2%, and 3.6% respectively. On GTEA and BEOID, the method improves average mAP (0.1:0.7) by 4.1% (49.0% vs. 44.9%) and 6.3% (59.6% vs. 53.3%) over the strongest P-TAL baselines. When plugged into other P-TAL methods (SF-Net, BackTAL, LAC), TSP-Net yields an average improvement of 5.8% in mAP, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSP-Net outperforms all P-TAL baselines on THUMOS'14 by a substantial margin: it beats CRRC-Net (the strongest P-TAL method) by 3.6% in AVG mAP (0.1:0.7) and 3.3% in AVG mAP (0.1:0.5), and surpasses LAC by 4.6% in AVG mAP (0.1:0.7). Against W-TAL methods, the gap is even larger—TSP-Net exceeds PivoTAL (best W-TAL) by 7.8% in AVG mAP (0.1:0.7) and 8.2% in AVG mAP (0.1:0.5). Notably, TSP-Net's AVG mAP (0.3:0.7) of 48.3% is competitive with fully-supervised methods like RTD-Net (49.0%) and VSGN (50.2%), despite using only single-point annotations. On GTEA and BEOID, TSP-Net leads all P-TAL methods by 4.1% and 6.3% in AVG mAP (0.1:0.7) respectively. The plug-and-play generalization experiments (Table 3) confirm that TSP-Net consistently improves any base P-TAL locator, with the largest gain (+8.0% mAP) observed when added to BackTAL, while the smallest gain (+4.4%) occurs with SF-Net due to its limited proposal count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the P-TAL pipeline established by LAC [21], which generates proposals via CAS-based multi-threshold strategies, and adopts the surrounding contrastive feature extraction (SCFE) from P-MIL [39] for proposal-level representation. Rather than modifying the core MIL or CAS generation, TSP-Net introduces a plug-in proposal-level scoring module that relearns confidence using a temporal saliency prior (the observation that human annotators consistently label instance centers). This departs from prior P-TAL methods (SF-Net, CRRC-Net, DCM) that primarily refine pseudo-labels or prototypes within the MIL framework, instead operating as a post-hoc confidence realignment and boundary adaptation layer agnostic to the base locator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design makes TSP-Net broadly applicable to any P-TAL method, and the demonstrated 5.8% average improvement across three base locators underscores its practical utility. However, the approach inherits the quality of the base locator's proposals and relies on the center-bias annotation prior, which may not hold in all domains or with automated annotation. Open questions include whether the saliency-driven scoring generalizes to W-TAL (where no point annotations exist) or to fully-supervised TAL, and whether the boundary adaptation strategy can be extended to handle overlapping or nested instances more robustly.
