## Problem

Existing 3D face avatar methods (e.g., PointAvatar, IMAvatar) reconstruct exact replicas from monocular video but offer no mechanism to selectively alter subparts such as hair, nose, or accessories while preserving identity. Meanwhile, 2D/3D generative face models produce diverse appearances but lack explicit mapping to parametric 3D morphable models, making them non-animatable and unable to support viewpoint changes or expression reenactment. PEGASUS addresses this gap by building a personalized generative 3D avatar that supports disentangled, continuous control of individual facial attributes from monocular video alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of the target person plus a collection of attribute DB videos from diverse individuals (109 videos covering hair, nose, hat, eyes, eyebrows, mouth), along with FLAME parameters (β, θ, ψ) and a structured latent code z ∈ R^{(D+1)×d} where z⁰ encodes identity and z^{j≠0} encodes specific attribute variations. The output is a deformable point-cloud avatar (N points) with per-point 3D positions, surface normals, albedo, shading, and binary segmentation cues in the deformed space, fully animatable via FLAME parameters. The synthetic training data is created by rendering attribute DB avatars with the target's FLAME parameters and compositing them via segmentation masks and Poisson blending, rather than requiring multi-view captures.

## Method

PEGASUS operates in two stages: (1) synthetic database generation, where individual avatars are built from each attribute DB video and rendered with the target person's FLAME parameters to produce part-swapped videos of the target identity with varying attributes; and (2) training a personalized generative avatar on these synthetic videos. The architecture extends PointAvatar by introducing a generic canonical space (gc) as an additional deformation stage (gc → sc → fc → d), with a coordinate-based MLP that takes the latent code z and canonical point as input to predict deformation offsets, blendshapes, LBS weights, SDF values, albedo, shading, and segmentation cues. The key insight is that conditioning both the deformation and appearance MLPs on disentangled part-wise latent codes, combined with the synthetic part-swapped training data, enables the model to vary individual facial attributes continuously while preserving the target identity encoded in z⁰. Training follows a coarse-to-fine, two-step strategy: first on the unmodified target video, then on all part-swapped videos. A zero-shot transfer variant fuses a previously trained generative avatar with a newly trained single-identity avatar using learned segmentation masks, avoiding the need to regenerate the synthetic DB.

## Results

On the hairstyle transfer sub-problem (Table 1), the part-swapping variant (Ours_swap+PA) achieves the best FID of 169.54 and ArcFace identity score of 0.7179, outperforming all baselines in naturalness and identity preservation simultaneously. The zero-shot generative model (Ours_zero-shot) achieves the highest ArcFace score of 0.7792, confirming that reusing a pre-trained generative avatar preserves identity most faithfully. The full personalized generative model (Ours_person-gen) achieves competitive FID (190.10) and ArcFace (0.6883) while offering the additional capability of producing unseen attribute combinations via latent code interpolation.

**Table 1: Quantitative comparison for hairstyle transfer. FID and KID measure naturalness (lower is better); ArcFace measures identity preservation (higher is better).**

| Method | FID ↓ | KID ↓ | ArcFace ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|
| CD+PA | 181.60 | **0.1367** | 0.6691 |
| E4S+PA | 176.64 | 0.1416 | 0.5701 |
| DELTA | 198.40 | 0.1797 | 0.6732 |
| Ours_swap+PA | **169.54** | 0.1406 | 0.7179 |
| Ours_person-gen | 190.10 | 0.1696 | 0.6883 |
| Ours_zero-shot | 191.47 | 0.1881 | **0.7792** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEGASUS is compared against three baselines for the hairstyle transfer sub-problem. DELTA [13], which uses a hybrid explicit-implicit representation for hair transfer, performs worst on naturalness (FID 198.40, KID 0.1797) despite a reasonable ArcFace score (0.6732), indicating its synthesized avatars lack photorealistic quality. E4S+PA [30], which applies GAN-based face swapping in 2D before 3D reconstruction, suffers the most severe identity degradation (ArcFace 0.5701), confirming that 2D GAN inversion introduces view-inconsistency artifacts that blur the 3D avatar. CD+PA [27], using Custom Diffusion for 2D hair editing, achieves the best KID (0.1367) but still lags behind Ours_swap+PA in FID (181.60 vs. 169.54) and ArcFace (0.6691 vs. 0.7179), showing that diffusion-based 2D editing also fails to maintain full 3D consistency. PEGASUS's part-swapping approach wins on both FID and ArcFace simultaneously, while its zero-shot variant achieves the best identity preservation (ArcFace 0.7792) by directly reusing the target's unedited face geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEGASUS extends PointAvatar from single-identity 3D avatar reconstruction to a personalized generative model with disentangled latent codes controlling individual facial attributes (hair, nose, eyes, etc.). It departs from prior 3D avatar methods (IMAvatar, PointAvatar, DECA) that produce exact replicas without editing capability, and from 2D face editing approaches (GAN-based, diffusion-based) that can alter attributes but lack explicit 3D animatability and viewpoint consistency. The compositional 3D avatar line (MEGANE, SCARF, DELTA) handles discrete item transfer (glasses, scarves) but not continuous generative variation of facial parts. The key conceptual contribution is the synthetic database generation via 3D part-swapping to create training data for a generative model, plus a zero-shot transfer mechanism that fuses a pre-trained generative avatar with a new single-identity model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to enable users to build a personalized 3D face avatar from monocular video and then continuously edit individual facial attributes while preserving identity, which is directly relevant to AR/VR and metaverse applications. The zero-shot transfer approach offers a practical shortcut, avoiding the time-consuming synthetic DB construction by reusing a previously trained generative model. Limitations include the avatar quality not reaching photo-realistic levels, reliance on non-physical blending for synthetic DB generation, and restriction to a single identity per model. Open questions include whether a single model can handle multiple identities, how to improve physical accuracy of the synthetic data pipeline, and whether the disentangled latent space generalizes to unseen attribute combinations beyond the training distribution.
