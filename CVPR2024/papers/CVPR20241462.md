## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-video diffusion models produce high-quality videos but offer no mechanism for users to interactively control the spatial location, size, or motion trajectory of generated objects. Existing approaches to spatio-temporal control require retraining the base model or training specialized adapters on grounded data, making them resource-intensive and inaccessible to the broader community. The paper asks whether a training-free, zero-latency technique can endow any off-the-shelf video diffusion model with such control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and a per-frame binary mask (derived from user-specified bounding boxes, optionally auto-generated by GPT-4) as input, and produces a video in which the foreground object faithfully follows the mask's spatial trajectory across frames. The mask is downsampled to the latent resolution and applied additively to attention scores; no extra diffusion steps or source images are required. The same pipeline extends to text-to-image generation by simply omitting the temporal-attention mask.

## Method

PEEKABOO inserts three complementary binary attention masks into the spatial-, cross-, and temporal-attention layers of a frozen 3D UNet, inspired by Mask2Former's local-context segmentation formulation. The cross-attention mask restricts foreground text tokens to attend only to foreground latent pixels (and vice versa), the spatial-attention mask prevents foreground and background pixels from interacting within each frame, and the temporal-attention mask ensures each latent pixel attends only to its same-class counterpart across frames. These masks are applied for a fixed number of early denoising steps, after which free (unmasked) generation resumes so that foreground and background cohesively merge. Because the masks are computed in closed form from the input bounding boxes and added to the attention logits, the method introduces no additional diffusion steps, no trainable parameters, and negligible inference overhead (≈1.03× latency).

## Results

PEEKABOO achieves up to 3.8× mIoU improvement over the ModelScope baseline (9.6 → 36.1 on IMC) and 2.9× over ZeroScope (12.6 → 36.3 on IMC), while adding only 3% inference latency (1.03× vs. 1×). On the ssv2-ST benchmark, mIoU jumps from 12.0 to 33.2 (ModelScope) and 13.9 to 34.7 (ZeroScope). In terms of video quality, ModelScope w/ PEEKABOO achieves an FVD of 609 on MSR-VTT, substantially outperforming CogVideo (1294) and MagicVideo (1290).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ModelScope and ZeroScope baselines (without PEEKABOO), the method wins decisively on every metric across all four datasets: mIoU improves by 2.5–3.8×, AP50 by up to 25×, and Coverage increases (e.g., 44.7% → 63.7% on ssv2-ST for ModelScope). Against the concurrent LLM-VD method [24], PEEKABOO matches or exceeds mIoU on ssv2-ST (33.2 vs. 27.2) and IMC (36.1 vs. 36.1), and substantially outperforms it on AP50 (35.8 vs. 21.2 on ssv2-ST), while reducing inference latency from 2.20× to 1.03×. On video quality (Table 2), ModelScope w/ PEEKABOO achieves an FVD of 609 on MSR-VTT, beating both CogVideo (1294) and MagicVideo (1290) by more than 2×, demonstrating that spatial grounding also improves overall generation fidelity. The only metric where LLM-VD retains an edge is Coverage on DAVIS16 (96% vs. 93% for ModelScope w/ PEEKABOO), though the gap is marginal.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEEKABOO repurposes the masked-attention formulation from Mask2Former (segmentation as local-context mask classification) and transplants it into the 3D UNet architecture of video diffusion models, applying it simultaneously to spatial, cross, and temporal attention layers. It departs from prior controllable video generation methods (depth-guided, motion-conditioned, or adapter-based approaches) by requiring no retraining, no external adapter, and no additional diffusion steps. The closest concurrent work, LLM-VD, also uses bounding-box guidance but incurs a 2.2× latency overhead from a special guidance module, whereas PEEKABOO achieves control purely through attention masking at negligible cost. In the image domain, methods like MasaCtrl and A-Star use attention manipulation for spatial control, but extending these to the spatio-temporal video setting with temporal consistency is non-trivial and is the core technical contribution here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEEKABOO provides the first training-free, zero-latency mechanism for interactive spatio-temporal control in off-the-shelf video diffusion models, lowering the barrier for practitioners who lack the compute or data to retrain models with grounded supervision. The released benchmarks (ssv2-ST and IMC) give the community a standardized evaluation framework for a task that previously lacked one. Key limitations include reliance on UNet-based architectures (incompatible with DiT-based models), single-object control, and the need for user-specified or LLM-generated bounding boxes. Open questions the authors flag include extending to image-to-video, video-to-video, and long-form video generation, as well as multi-object interactive control.
