## Problem

In weakly supervised semantic segmentation with image-level labels, frequently co-occurring objects (e.g., train/railroad, boat/water, horse/person) cause false activation of co-contexts because the network cannot distinguish coupled semantics during feature representation. Existing remedies either ignore this bias or rely on external supervision such as CLIP, out-of-distribution data, or manually constructed co-categories, limiting real-world applicability. The paper asks whether co-occurrence can be decoupled without any external data by separating coupled contexts before representation and then enhancing category-specific features.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images with multi-label image-level class labels and decomposes each image into 12 patches of 64×64 pixels. A global teacher produces CAMs and category prototypes, while the student and local teacher branches consume weakly and strongly augmented patch views. The final output is a pixel-level segmentation mask (via a 4-layer 3×3 convolutional decoder) plus refined CAMs, all produced in a single end-to-end training pass with a shared ViT-B/16 encoder.

## Method

SeCo operates in two complementary spaces. In image space, it decomposes each image into patches and assigns a category tag (single-category, background, or uncertain) to each patch from auxiliary CAMs, with a similarity-based rectification step that demotes noisy tags to 'uncertain' by comparing patch embeddings against a history reservoir. In feature space, a dual-teacher-single-student architecture extracts multi-granularity knowledge: a global teacher gathers category prototypes from ViT class tokens via an adaptive EMA update, while a local teacher maintains a tag-paired patch semantic reservoir. Two InfoNCE-style contrastive losses—L_LiG (pulling patch embeddings toward their category prototype while pushing away co-category prototypes) and L_LiL (pulling same-tag patches together and pushing different-tag patches apart, with noisy patches masked out)—are jointly optimized with classification and segmentation losses in a single end-to-end stage.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, SeCo achieves 74.0% mIoU (val) and 73.8% mIoU (test) for semantic segmentation, with CAM quality of 74.8% and refined pseudo-mask quality of 76.5%. On MS COCO 2014, SeCo reaches 46.7% mIoU on the val set. As a single-staged method without external data, SeCo outperforms the best single-staged competitor ToCo by 2.9% (VOC val) and 4.4% (COCO val), and surpasses the best multi-staged competitor OCR by 1.3% on VOC val.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeCo outperforms all 21 baselines on both datasets. On VOC val, it beats the strongest multi-staged method OCR (72.7%) by 1.3% and the strongest single-staged method ToCo (71.1%) by 2.9%, while also surpassing L2G (72.1%) and FPR (70.3%) which use multi-staged pipelines. On COCO val, SeCo (46.7%) exceeds CLIP-ES (45.4%), a multi-staged method that relies on external CLIP supervision, by 1.3%, and outperforms ToCo (42.3%) by 4.4%. Notably, SeCo is single-staged and requires no external data or human prior, yet matches or exceeds multi-staged methods that leverage CLIP (CLIMS, CLIP-ES) or out-of-distribution samples (W-OoD). In the co-occurrence-specific evaluation (Table 3), SeCo reduces the confusion ratio from 0.32 (ToCo) to 0.23 across all VOC categories, with particularly large gains on Boat/Water (0.32 vs. 1.11) and Train/Railroad (0.54 vs. 0.75). Training efficiency is also favorable: SeCo completes in 417 minutes versus 1068 minutes for the three-stage CLIMS pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeCo builds on the patch-based WSSS line (L2G, ToCo) and the MoCo-style dual-teacher-single-student architecture with EMA and memory banks, but departs from prior patch methods by assigning CAM-derived category tags to each patch rather than simply inheriting image-level labels, which the authors argue cannot differentiate co-occurring categories at patch level. It also diverges from co-occurrence-specific methods (CLIMS, W-OoD, CDA) that rely on external supervision (CLIP, OoD data, constructed co-categories) by addressing the problem entirely through internal tag-guided contrast and a similarity-based tag rectification mechanism. The 'separate and conquer' framing—spatially decoupling co-contexts in image space before enhancing category-specific representation in feature space—represents a conceptual reorganization of how patch-level contrastive learning is structured for WSSS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for external models or curated OoD data, SeCo makes co-occurrence-aware WSSS more deployable in settings where such resources are unavailable, while the single-staged end-to-end design (417 min vs. 1068 min for CLIMS) reduces training complexity. The confusion-ratio analysis (0.23 vs. 0.32 for ToCo) provides a clearer diagnostic of false-positive suppression that the field could adopt as a standard metric. A stated limitation is that fixed-size patches inevitably produce co-category patches with incorrect tags, pointing toward adaptive patch sizing or stronger denoising as open directions; the approach's transferability to denser co-occurrence regimes (e.g., 81-class COCO with more complex scene graphs) remains to be fully stress-tested.
