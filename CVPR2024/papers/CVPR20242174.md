## Problem

Pre-trained 2D diffusion models used as priors in text-to-3D generation cannot reliably interpret explicit view instructions in text prompts, causing the Janus problem where multi-faced 3D models are produced. This imprecise guidance introduces instability throughout the score distillation optimization and degrades the geometric and appearance quality of generated 3D content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242174/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a textured 3D mesh as output. The pipeline operates in two stages: a coarse stage using NeuS at 64×64 rendering resolution (5000 steps) followed by a fine stage using DMTet with differentiable rasterization at 512×512 (another 5000 steps). The orthogonal-view diffusion model internally generates and consumes composite 2×2 grid images at 512×512 resolution, where each quadrant corresponds to a 90°-separated orthogonal viewpoint of the same object.

## Method

The core contribution is a fine-tuned orthogonal-view diffusion model (built on Stable Diffusion 2.1's U-Net, trained on ~420K Objaverse assets rendered from 48 viewpoints each) that generates a single composite image containing four mutually consistent orthogonal-view sub-images, providing strong geometric structure priors. During 3D optimization, the scene is rendered from four 90°-separated camera poses, tiled into a composite image, and optimized via an orthogonal-view SDS loss that enforces multi-view consistency simultaneously. A 3D synthesis fusion network then blends this orthogonal-view prior with the original pre-trained 2D diffusion prior using a dynamic weighting schedule (Eq. 4): early iterations emphasize the orthogonal-view prior for geometric structure and Janus mitigation, while later iterations shift toward the 2D prior for local texture detail and appearance refinement. The coarse-to-fine strategy uses NeuS for initial geometry and DMTet with VSD guidance for high-resolution texture optimization.

## Results

EfficientDreamer achieves a CLIP score of 30.33 and FID of 284.98, outperforming all compared text-to-3D methods. In the user study (50 participants, 1100 responses across 22 prompts), the method receives an average rating of 3.74/4 and an 84.27% preference rate, substantially exceeding DreamFusion (2.09, 4.55%), Magic3D (2.44, 6.27%), and TextMesh (1.73, 4.91%). The method also qualitatively eliminates the Janus problem that persists in all baselines relying on standard pre-trained diffusion priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EfficientDreamer outperforms all three primary baselines on both CLIP score and FID. Against DreamFusion [21], it gains +1.93 CLIP and reduces FID by 89.46 points; against Magic3D [14], it gains +1.18 CLIP and reduces FID by 25.59; against TextMesh [30], it gains +2.68 CLIP and reduces FID by 20.79. In the user study, the 84.27% preference rate dwarfs the combined preference of all three baselines (15.73%). Against Perp-Neg [1] (integrated into DreamFusion), the paper shows a qualitative advantage: while Perp-Neg can produce single-faced models, it still generates anatomical errors (e.g., three ears, five legs), whereas EfficientDreamer's simultaneous four-orthogonal-view guidance avoids such inconsistencies. All baselines suffer from the Janus problem due to their reliance on standard 2D diffusion priors that cannot reliably follow view instructions, a limitation the orthogonal-view diffusion model directly addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DreamFusion-style Score Distillation Sampling framework by replacing the single-view 2D diffusion prior with a novel orthogonal-view diffusion model that generates a 2×2 composite image of four mutually consistent views. The orthogonal-view model is obtained by fine-tuning the U-Net of Stable Diffusion on ~420K rendered views from the Objaverse dataset, while the overall 3D optimization pipeline (NeuS → DMTet coarse-to-fine) follows the Magic3D paradigm. A key departure from prior SDS-based methods is the 3D synthesis fusion network, which dynamically blends the orthogonal-view prior (for geometric consistency) with the original 2D diffusion prior (for local texture fidelity) via a schedule controlled by a hyperparameter λ, and additionally incorporates VSD from ProlificDreamer in the refinement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Janus problem (multi-faced 3D outputs) has been a persistent practical failure mode in text-to-3D generation, and this paper shows that a relatively small 3D dataset (420K Objaverse assets) can provide sufficient geometric structure priors to eliminate it when the diffusion model is explicitly trained on orthogonal-view composites. However, the approach inherits limitations of SDS-based optimization: the 3D dataset is far smaller than the text-image corpus, so local appearance quality still depends on the 2D prior, and the method is validated only on single-object prompts with simple topology. Open questions include whether the orthogonal-view strategy generalizes to complex multi-object scenes, whether more than four views or non-orthogonal arrangements would help, and whether the dynamic fusion schedule can be made adaptive rather than relying on a fixed λ.
