## Problem

Existing text-to-image evaluation metrics (IS, FID, CLIP Score) fail to capture human perceptual preferences, and while recent human-preference-based methods (HPS, PickScore, ImageReward) address this, they collapse the rich multi-dimensional nature of human judgment into a single overall score. This single-score approach is insufficient because human preferences vary significantly across dimensions such as aesthetics, detail quality, and semantic alignment, and improvements in one dimension can come at the expense of others.

## Input / Output

The MPS model takes three inputs: a generated RGB image (at varying resolutions from 512×512 to 1024×1024 and aspect ratios like 1:1 or 16:9), a natural-language text prompt, and a preference condition specified as a set of attribute words (e.g., {light, color, clarity} for aesthetics). It outputs a single scalar preference score for the image under that condition. During training, the model operates on pairwise image comparisons where human-annotated scores (1–5 scale) are normalized to [0, 1] with 0.5 for ties.

## Method

MPS builds on CLIP-H to extract full token-level features from both the image and prompt, then fuses them via a multimodal cross-attention layer rather than relying solely on CLS/last-token representations as prior work does. The key innovation is a preference condition mask: the condition is encoded as a set of attribute words, a learnable similarity matrix is computed between condition words and prompt tokens, and after binarization this mask is added to the cross-attention logits so that only prompt tokens relevant to the condition are attended to by the visual modality. The final score is a learned linear combination of the fused CLS token and the last text token, preventing information loss for short prompts. Training minimizes KL-divergence between human pairwise annotations and softmax-normalized predictions over 30,000 steps with a batch size of 128, and a single unified model handles all four preference dimensions simultaneously.

## Results

On the MHP test set, MPS achieves 74.24% overall preference accuracy, 83.86% on aesthetics, 83.87% on semantic alignment, and 85.18% on detail quality, outperforming all baselines by a large margin in every dimension. The largest gains are in detail quality (+23.12% over the best baseline HPSv2 at 62.06%) and aesthetics (+9.07% over ImageReward at 74.79%). On cross-dataset evaluation, MPS also leads on ImageReward (67.5% vs. 65.7% for HPSv2) and HPD v2 (83.5% vs. 83.3% for HPSv2), demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPS outperforms all six baselines across every preference dimension on the MHP test set. The closest competitor is PickScore (69.52% overall), which MPS surpasses by 4.72 points overall and by a dramatic 28.44 points on detail quality (85.18% vs. 56.74%). Among the human-preference-trained models, ImageReward achieves the best aesthetics score (74.79%) but still trails MPS by 9.07 points, while HPSv2 leads on alignment (73.87%) yet is outperformed by MPS by 10.00 points. Notably, CLIP Score achieves a surprisingly high alignment accuracy (82.69%) due to its text-image matching objective, but MPS still edges it out by 1.18 points while dominating all other dimensions. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that the condition mask is critical: without it, the cross-attention-only model drops to 58.79% on detail, whereas the full MPS reaches 85.18%, confirming that the mask enables the unified model to specialize per dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of human-preference-based evaluation for text-to-image generation (HPS, HPSv2, PickScore, ImageReward) by replacing the single overall score with four distinct preference dimensions (aesthetics, detail quality, semantic alignment, overall). Architecturally, it departs from prior CLIP-based scorers that use only the first/last token for scoring, instead employing full token sequences fused via cross-attention, and introduces a condition mask that selectively attends to prompt tokens relevant to the queried preference. The MHP dataset itself builds on prompt sources from DiffusionDB, PromptHero, and KOLORS, but adds GPT-4-augmented prompts to correct long-tail category imbalance and employs 9 generative models (diffusion, GAN, autoregressive) for broader coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MHP dataset (918K annotations over 607K images) and the MPS benchmark (4,000 prompts across 7 categories) provide the field with the first large-scale, multi-dimensional evaluation resource, enabling practitioners to assess text-to-image models along axes that matter to end users rather than a single aggregate number. A key open question is whether the manually curated word sets defining each preference condition can be replaced by learned or open-vocabulary representations, and whether the four fixed dimensions capture the full breadth of human preference. The finding that separately trained per-dimension models underperform the unified MPS suggests that multi-dimensional co-training regularizes against dimension-specific bias, but it remains unclear how the approach scales to additional or user-defined preference axes without re-annotation.
