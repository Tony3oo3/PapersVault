## Problem

Grid-based models for neural fields have demonstrated significant empirical success in speed and representation quality, yet no systematic theory exists to analyze their learning behaviors, training dynamics, or generalization performance. This theoretical gap prevents principled design of improved grid-based architectures and leaves their empirical advantages unexplained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242242/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query coordinate x (spatial position in 2D or 3D) and, for view synthesis, a ray direction d, and produces a weighted sum of learned feature vectors w_i indexed by an index function U(x) that selects relevant grid nodes. For NeRF tasks, the output is decomposed into a density σ (via softplus activation) and a color c (via an MLP combining spatial features with positional encoding of the ray direction), which are then integrated through differentiable volumetric rendering. The formulation supports both regular grids (uniform discretization) and irregular grids (point clouds from SfM), differing only in the index function.

## Method

The paper introduces the Grid Tangent Kernel (GTK), defined as the covariance of model gradients with respect to parameters at two input points, and proves it remains invariant during training, reducing grid-based model dynamics to a linear ODE and yielding a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound governed by Y^T G^{-1} Y. Guided by this theory, MulFAGrid constructs multi-scale Fourier features from the query coordinate, applies multiplicative filters (sinusoidal filters with learned frequencies and phases) to inject node-index information and non-linearity, then normalizes across queried nodes to form data-adaptive kernel weights. The final output aggregates nodal features via elementwise multiplication with these learned kernels. An adaptive learning scheme jointly optimizes kernel parameters Θ and grid features w, which the GTK analysis shows narrows the generalization bound and widens the high-frequency spectrum compared to prior grid-based models like InstantNGP, NFFB, and NeuRBF.

## Results

On 2D image fitting (DIV2K), MulFAGrid achieves 56.19 PSNR with only 119K parameters in 5k steps, outperforming NeuRBF (54.84 PSNR) and NFFB (45.28 PSNR). On 3D SDF reconstruction, MulFAGrid attains the best NAE of 4.51° with 823K parameters in just 5k steps, versus NeuRBF's 4.93° at 20k steps. On novel view synthesis across five benchmarks, MulFAGrid achieves the best PSNR on SyntheticNeRF (34.68), Mip-NeRF-360 (28.98), and SFMB (29.15), while remaining competitive with 3DGS on LLFF (27.22 vs 27.25) and Tanks&Temples (20.85 vs 22.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MulFAGrid outperforms all grid-based baselines across every task. On 2D image fitting, it surpasses NeuRBF by 1.35 dB PSNR (56.19 vs 54.84), NFFB by 10.91 dB, and InstantNGP by 17.05 dB, while using fewer parameters (119K vs 128K/154K/511K). On 3D SDF reconstruction, MulFAGrid achieves the lowest NAE (4.51°) with 4× fewer training steps than NeuRBF (5k vs 20k) and 20× fewer than NGLOD (5k vs 245k). On novel view synthesis, MulFAGrid beats NeuRBF on all five benchmarks (e.g., +2.86 dB on Mip-NeRF-360, +3.94 dB on SFMB) and outperforms DVGOv2 and Plenoxels by 1.8–8.4 dB. Against the strong 3DGS baseline (with point-cloud initialization), MulFAGrid wins on all five benchmarks in PSNR (e.g., +1.54 dB on SyntheticNeRF, +1.90 dB on Mip-NeRF-360), though the paper acknowledges 3DGS has substantially faster rendering speed due to its optimized CUDA implementation. BACON, PNF, and MINER are only compared on 2D image fitting, where MulFAGrid leads by 16.9–17.7 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework from MLP-based networks to grid-based neural field models, introducing Grid Tangent Kernels (GTKs) as the analogous object. While NTK theory (Jacot et al., Arora et al.) has long characterized MLP training dynamics and generalization, grid-based models like InstantNGP, NFFB, and NeuRBF—despite their two-orders-of-magnitude speed advantage—lacked any systematic theoretical grounding. The paper also draws on multiplicative filter networks (Fathony et al.) and Fourier feature mappings (Tancik et al., NeRF) to construct MulFAGrid, positioning it as a general-purpose grid-based model that unifies regular and irregular grid formulations under a single weighted-average definition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GTK theory provides the first principled tool for analyzing and designing grid-based neural field models, connecting architecture choices (kernel width, frequency spectrum) directly to generalization bounds via Rademacher complexity. This opens a path for theory-driven design of future grid-based models beyond ad-hoc empirical tuning. A key limitation is that MulFAGrid's rendering speed remains far below 3DGS, and the theory assumes a supervised regression setup that may not directly transfer to all neural field applications. Open questions include whether the GTK framework can guide adaptive grid resolution or hierarchical designs, and whether the constant-GTK property extends to more complex architectures with non-linear post-processing.
