## Problem

State-of-the-art multi-task dense prediction models (e.g., ATRC, InvPT) are too computationally expensive for edge deployment, yet directly applying binary neural network (BNN) compression to these deep architectures causes severe information degradation that destroys performance. The paper tackles how to binarize multi-task dense predictors while preserving—or even improving—accuracy, and investigates why binarization in a multi-task setting can yield a 'free lunch' over full-precision baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241816/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and simultaneously produces pixel-wise predictions for up to six dense tasks: semantic segmentation, monocular depth, surface normals, object boundaries, human parsing, and saliency. All weights and activations are reduced to 1-bit (±1), so inference replaces full-precision convolutions with XNOR-BitCount operations, yielding 32× memory reduction and 64× theoretical speedup. The multi-task structure allows some tasks to serve as auxiliary (front-end only) while others receive final predictions after the multi-modal distillation stage.

## Method

Bi-MTPD constructs a fully binarized multi-task dense predictor by replacing all modules of MTI-Net (backbone, task-specific heads, and multi-modal distillation) with 1-bit counterparts, using Bi-Real/IR-Net-style residual connections and a binarized attention mechanism for cross-task information gating. To counteract information degradation, two calibration mechanisms are introduced: (1) a Variational Information Bottleneck layer after the shared backbone enforces a Gaussian prior on intermediate representations in forward propagation, filtering task-irrelevant nuisance factors; and (2) feature-based knowledge distillation from a full-precision teacher aligns the binary student's layer-wise activations via MSE loss, correcting gradient direction in backward propagation. A key counter-intuitive finding is that binarizing only the multi-modal distillation module (Bi-MTPD-C) outperforms the full-precision MTI-Net by ~4% mIoU on segmentation while reducing FLOPs by ~39%, because binarized attention acts as a stronger information filter that sharpens cross-task feature interactions.

## Results

On NYUD-v2, Bi-MTPD-C (binarized MMD only) achieves 47.71 mIoU on semantic segmentation, surpassing the previous CNN-based SoTA ATRC (45.87 mIoU) by +1.84 mIoU while reducing FLOPs from 249.24G to 130.65G (~47% faster). When applied to the ViT-based InvPT, Bi-MTPD-C further improves SemSeg to 54.86 mIoU (+1.30 over InvPT-L) and 51.20 mIoU (+0.90 over InvPT-B) with ~10% FLOPs reduction. On PASCAL-Context, MTI+Bi-MTPD-C reaches 62.98 mIoU SemSeg (+1.28 over MTI) at 194.51G FLOPs vs. 280.12G, and InvPT+Bi-MTPD-C (ViT-B) achieves 67.10 Parsing mIoU (+0.27) with 220.76G FLOPs vs. 274.68G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CNN-based SoTA ATRC [2], Bi-MTPD-C wins on SemSeg (+1.84 mIoU: 47.71 vs. 45.87) and Depth (0.530 vs. 0.540 RMSE) while using only 52% of the FLOPs (130.65G vs. 249.24G), making it a clear win-win. Against MTI-Net [47], Bi-MTPD-C improves SemSeg by +2.01 mIoU (47.71 vs. 45.70) and Depth by 0.007 RMSE at 61% of the FLOPs. Against the ViT-based InvPT [50], Bi-MTPD-C consistently improves SemSeg (+0.90 for ViT-B, +1.30 for ViT-L) and Boundary (+0.08, +0.10) while reducing FLOPs by ~25% and ~9% respectively, though Normal slightly degrades (+0.50, +0.46 mErr). On PASCAL-Context, MTI+Bi-MTPD-C beats MTI [47] on SemSeg (+1.28), Parsing (+0.26), and Boundary (+0.26) but loses on Saliency (−1.22 maxF); InvPT+Bi-MTPD-C (ViT-B) beats InvPT on Parsing (+0.27), Saliency (+1.32), and Boundary (+0.04) but slightly loses on SemSeg (−0.66) and Normal (−0.94 mErr). The fully-binarized Bi-MTPD-F variant (18.07G FLOPs) shows a significant performance drop (39.20 SemSeg) compared to the partially-binarized Bi-MTPD-C, confirming that selective binarization of the MMD module is the key to the 'free lunch' effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multitask dense prediction lineage (PAD-Net, MTI-Net, ATRC) by replacing its core modules with binarized counterparts, drawing on Bi-Real Net and IR-Net for the fundamental binary building blocks and sign-function approximation. It departs from prior BNN research, which has been largely confined to small-scale classification (ResNet-18/34 on ImageNet), by tackling the substantially deeper and wider architectures required for pixel-wise dense prediction. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a variational information bottleneck in the forward pass and feature-based knowledge distillation in the backward pass to counteract the severe information degradation that binarization induces in deep multitask networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that BNNs are viable for dense prediction tasks, opening a path toward deploying multi-task scene understanding on edge devices (autonomous driving, robotics) where full-precision models are infeasible. The finding that partial binarization (Bi-MTPD-C) can outperform the full-precision baseline suggests that the multitask cross-talk structure itself provides a form of implicit regularization that binarization can exploit, though this 'free lunch' is limited to the partially-binarized variant and does not extend to the fully-binarized model, which still suffers a ~6 mIoU drop. Open questions include whether the VIB+KD recipe generalizes to other dense prediction backbones beyond HRNet, whether the performance gains hold on larger-scale outdoor datasets, and whether the approach transfers to other ultra-low-bit-width settings (ternary, 2-bit).
