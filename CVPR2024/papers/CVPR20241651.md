## Problem

Extending intuitive point-dragging editing (as in DragGAN) to 3D-aware face generation is non-trivial: 2D methods ignore global 3D structure, iterative latent optimization is too slow for interactive use, and imprecise point manipulation causes ambiguous edits (e.g., enlarging the mouth also opens it). Existing 3D editing methods trade off intuitiveness, generalization, and efficiency, with no single approach satisfying all three.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241651/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face represented as an EG3D latent code (W+ space), a set of 29 user-dragged 3D facial landmarks (detected via Mediapipe in the frontal view, projected onto the 3D surface, and displaced perpendicular to the rendering direction), and a target camera pose. It produces a photorealistic edited face image rendered from any viewpoint along with the corresponding edited 3D tri-plane representation (3×32×256×256), enabling multi-view consistent editing without re-optimization.

## Method

FaceEdit3D operates in three stages. First, tri-plane warping projects the displaced 3D landmarks onto each of the three 2D feature planes and applies thin-plate spline interpolation to compute an inverse coordinate mapping, warping each plane independently to achieve inherently 3D-consistent deformation. Second, a Swin-Transformer warp-aware encoder (trained with L1+LPIPS+identity losses, warp-assisted data augmentation, and a GOAE-style discriminator) projects the distorted warped rendering into a standardized latent space, recovering a photorealistic counterpart without iterative optimization. Third, directional latent editing computes the difference between encoder outputs before and after warping, then applies a hierarchical feature-selection module over W+ layers (following StyleCLIP's layer semantics) to disentangle shape, expression, and pose edits while preserving identity and fine details.

## Results

In the 3D editing setting, FaceEdit3D achieves an ID consistency of 0.712 and an MSEi/MSEo disentanglement ratio of 4.909, substantially outperforming IDE-3D (0.649 and 1.865 respectively). Inference time is 0.624 s per edit, comparable to IDE-3D (0.383 s) and far faster than DragGAN (5.231 s). In the 2D comparison against DragGAN, FaceEdit3D achieves a higher disentanglement ratio (11.016 vs. 8.893) and ID consistency (0.716 vs. 0.579) while running 14.7× faster (0.356 s vs. 5.231 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IDE-3D (the primary 3D baseline), FaceEdit3D wins on all three effectiveness metrics: MSEₒ drops from 0.987 to 0.342 (outside-region change reduced by ~65%), the disentanglement ratio MSEᵢ/MSEₒ improves from 1.865 to 4.909, and ID consistency rises from 0.649 to 0.712. IDE-3D is slightly faster (0.383 s vs. 0.624 s) and achieves a marginally higher MSEᵢ (1.841 vs. 1.679), but its semantic-map control causes coupled attribute changes (e.g., age/identity shift when elongating a chin). Against DragGAN in the 2D setting, FaceEdit3D is 14.7× faster (0.356 s vs. 5.231 s) and achieves better ID consistency (0.716 vs. 0.579) and a higher disentanglement ratio (11.016 vs. 8.893), though DragGAN's MSEₒ is slightly lower (0.224 vs. 0.186). StyleGAN-NADA is compared only qualitatively; the paper reports it fails to edit facial shape on EG3D despite succeeding at style/texture transfer, whereas FaceEdit3D handles shape, expression, and pose simultaneously. Overall, FaceEdit3D dominates on identity preservation and disentanglement across all baselines, at a modest inference-time cost relative to IDE-3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on EG3D's tri-plane representation as the base 3D generator and on StyleCLIP's hierarchical W+ latent space analysis for disentangled editing. It departs from prior 3D face editing methods (IDE-3D, NeRFaceEditing) that rely on semantic maps or 3DMM annotations, and from parameter-space fine-tuning approaches (StyleGAN-NADA) that require a dedicated generator per editing target. The key conceptual departure from DragGAN is replacing iterative latent-code optimization with a one-shot tri-plane warping (via thin-plate splines on 3D landmarks) followed by a trained warp-aware encoder and directional latent editing, shifting the computational burden from per-edit optimization to a pre-trained pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, real-time (~0.6s) 3D face editing tool that is intuitive (point-dragging), generalizable (no per-target retraining), and supports multi-view consistent editing with shape/expression disentanglement — a combination not achieved by prior methods. Its generalization to 3D cats and 2D cars suggests the tri-plane warping + encoder + directional editing pipeline is domain-agnostic. However, the authors acknowledge it cannot handle texture editing or semantic changes (e.g., adding glasses), and the reliance on EG3D's tri-plane limits applicability to fully implicit 3D GANs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex deformations (hair, accessories) and whether the warp-aware encoder can be made more robust to large displacements.
