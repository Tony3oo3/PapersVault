## Problem

Existing CLIP-based text-driven 3D stylization methods perform well on single objects but degrade on multi-object scenes because CLIP is pretrained predominantly on single-object image-text pairs and its global contrastive loss discards local per-object details. The core challenge is accurately parsing which objects in a 3D mesh correspond to which noun phrases in the text, and providing supervision fine enough to synthesize distinct styles for each object simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed bare triangle mesh (vertices and faces, normalized to a unit sphere) and a free-form text prompt describing multiple objects. During training, cameras are randomly sampled around the mesh via Gaussian distribution, and ray casting retrieves intersection points and face normals for rendering. The network predicts per-point normal displacement and view-dependent reflectance (diffuse, roughness, specular) via separate MLP branches, plus spherical Gaussian lighting; the mesh geometry itself remains unchanged throughout optimization.

## Method

TeMO introduces two key components. First, a Decoupled Graph Attention (DGA) module parses the text into noun phrases (via NLTK) and clusters mesh surface points into object groups (via GMM), then constructs a cross-modal graph where each surface-point node connects only to word nodes of its corresponding object; cross-attention within this graph selectively reinforces each object's point features with its own word features, preventing cross-object semantic interference. Second, a Cross-Grained Contrast (CGC) loss combines a coarse-grained term (global CLIP image-text cosine similarity over rendered views) with a fine-grained term that computes word-level similarity between rendered images and individual text words, then applies softmax-weighted summation along both axes (inspired by X-CLIP) to emphasize the most semantically relevant word-image pairs. The rendering pipeline follows TANGO, using Fourier positional encoding and a hemisphere renderer with spherical Gaussian lights, optimized with AdamW for 1500 iterations (~10 min on an RTX 3090).

## Results

On the multi-object 3D stylization benchmark (meshes from COSEG, Thingi10K, ShapeNet, Turbo Squid, and ModelNet), TeMO achieves a CLIP alignment score of 0.285, surpassing TANGO (0.274), X-Mesh (0.265), and Text2Mesh (0.262). In the user study (60 participants, 10 mesh-text pairs), TeMO scores 3.344 / 3.311 / 3.261 on naturalness, content match, and style match (1–5 scale), roughly 1–1.5 points higher than the best baseline (TANGO at 2.406 / 2.450 / 2.539). The full training pipeline takes approximately 10 minutes on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeMO outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against TANGO (the strongest baseline), TeMO gains +0.011 in CLIP alignment and approximately +0.9 to +1.0 points on each user-study question, indicating substantially better perceptual quality. X-Mesh, which incorporates text-guided dynamic attention, achieves a slightly lower alignment score (0.265) and notably lower user scores (1.7–1.8 range), as its global text vector causes semantic noise across multiple objects. Text2Mesh performs worst overall (alignment 0.262, user scores ~1.5), struggling to parse multi-object semantics. The qualitative comparisons in Figure 5 confirm that baselines tend to mix or omit object-specific properties (e.g., applying 'ice' to both dragons in a 'fire dragon and ice dragon' prompt), while TeMO correctly assigns distinct styles per object.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeMO inherits its rendering pipeline and MLP-based attribute prediction architecture directly from TANGO [15], including the spherical Gaussian lighting representation and hemisphere renderer. The fine-grained contrast mechanism in the CGC loss adapts the weighted-summation strategy from X-CLIP [22] for video-text retrieval, repurposing it to align rendered 3D views with individual words. The key departure from prior text-driven 3D stylization methods (Text2Mesh, TANGO, X-Mesh) is the introduction of explicit scene parsing: a Decoupled Graph Attention module that decouples noun phrases from text and clusters mesh surface points via GMM, then aligns them through a cross-modal graph. This positions TeMO as the first framework to treat multi-object 3D stylization as a structured alignment problem rather than a single global optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scene-parsing perspective—decoupling objects in both text and 3D mesh before applying attention—offers a reusable conceptual framework for any multi-object 3D editing task, not just stylization. However, the quantitative gains over TANGO are modest (0.285 vs. 0.274 CLIP alignment), and the method remains constrained by CLIP's single-object pretraining bias. Open questions include whether per-object symmetry priors can be reintroduced without causing cross-object interference, and whether the approach can be extended to diffusion-based pipelines once multi-object text-to-image generation matures. The reliance on NLTK for noun-phrase extraction and GMM for point clustering also raises scalability concerns for complex scenes with many objects.
