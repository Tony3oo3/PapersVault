## Problem

Existing vision-centric autonomous driving pre-training relies on 2D image tasks or 3D scene reconstruction, overlooking the inherently temporal (4D) nature of driving scenes. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first world-model-based 4D pre-training framework that learns unified spatio-temporal representations from multi-camera driving videos, explicitly modeling both dynamic object motion and static scene context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241271/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes T=4 consecutive multi-camera image frames along with corresponding expert actions (velocity and steering) and LiDAR-derived 3D occupancy labels (16×200×200 voxels) for supervision. During pre-training it reconstructs 3D occupancy for both the observed T timesteps and L=4 future timesteps, while also predicting actions. The learned output is a compact spatio-temporal BEV feature representation that is transferred to downstream task heads (detection, tracking, mapping, forecasting, occupancy, planning) during fine-tuning.

## Method

DriveWorld builds a Memory State-Space Model (MSSM) that decomposes scene information into two streams: a Dynamic Memory Bank that propagates temporal-aware latent dynamics using motion-aware layer normalization (MLN) to encode object velocities and time intervals, and a Static Scene Propagation module that directly channels spatial-aware BEV features to the decoder without warping. The probabilistic framework follows RSSM, matching a prior state distribution (from history and predicted actions) to a posterior (from observed images) via KL divergence, while a 3D convolutional decoder reconstructs occupancy and an MLP predicts actions. A Task Prompt, generated by encoding natural-language task descriptions through a pre-trained LLM (BERT/CLIP) and expanded via AdaptiveInstanceNorm, adaptively conditions the feature extractor for each downstream task. The overall loss combines KL divergence, cross-entropy for occupancy, and L1 for actions across both observed and future timesteps.

## Results

Pre-trained on OpenScene and fine-tuned on nuScenes, DriveWorld achieves 0.452 mAP and 0.542 NDS for 3D object detection (a 7.5% mAP gain over 2D ImageNet pre-training), 0.412 AMOTA for multi-object tracking (+5.3% over UniAD), 0.61m minADE for motion forecasting (−0.10m over UniAD), 45.2 IoU-far for occupancy prediction (+5.0%), and 0.69m average L2 error for planning (−0.34m over UniAD). When pre-trained only on nuScenes, DriveWorld still outperforms all 3D pre-training baselines (OccNet, UniScene, BEVDistill) across every downstream task. The method also reduces fine-tuning data requirements by 25% while maintaining comparable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveWorld consistently outperforms all compared pre-training baselines across every downstream task. Against 2D ImageNet pre-training (BEVFormer*), it gains 7.5% mAP in 3D detection and 2.9% IoU-lane in mapping. Against monocular 3D detection pre-training (FCOS3D), it improves mAP by 7.5% and AMOTA by 5.3%. Against 3D occupancy pre-training (OccNet) and 3D scene reconstruction (UniScene), DriveWorld† (nuScenes) leads by 1.6% and 1.4% mAP respectively, while DriveWorld‡ (OpenScene) extends the gap to 3.2% and 3.0%. Against knowledge distillation (BEVDistill), the advantage is 1.1% mAP (nuScenes) and 2.7% mAP (OpenScene). On planning, DriveWorld‡ reduces average L2 error from 1.03m (UniAD) to 0.69m and collision rate from 0.31% to 0.19%, surpassing ST-P3 (2.11m L2) and BEVGPT (1.22m L2). The largest relative gains appear in temporal tasks: tracking (+5.3% AMOTA) and motion forecasting (−0.10m minADE), confirming the benefit of 4D spatio-temporal pre-training over purely spatial 3D methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveWorld extends the autonomous driving pre-training lineage from 2D (ImageNet, depth estimation) and 3D (occupancy reconstruction via OccNet/UniScene, knowledge distillation via BEVDistill) to 4D spatio-temporal representation learning. It repurposes the probabilistic state-space model framework from reinforcement learning world models (Dreamer, MILE) and the motion-aware layer normalization from StreamPETR, but redirects these tools toward unsupervised representation learning rather than generation or control. The backbone and evaluation protocol follow BEVFormer and UniAD, positioning DriveWorld as a drop-in pre-training stage for existing vision-centric AD pipelines rather than a new end-to-end architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that 4D spatio-temporal pre-training via world models yields consistent gains across six downstream tasks (detection, tracking, mapping, forecasting, occupancy, planning), and notably shows that 75% of fine-tuning data suffices to match 100%, suggesting practical annotation cost savings. However, validation is limited to a single evaluation dataset (nuScenes) and a single backbone (ResNet101-DCN), and the pre-training still depends on LiDAR-derived 3D occupancy labels, leaving the fully self-supervised vision-centric setting open. Scaling to larger backbones and broader datasets, as well as removing the LiDAR annotation dependency, are the most pressing next steps for the 4D pre-training paradigm to become a practical foundation model component.
