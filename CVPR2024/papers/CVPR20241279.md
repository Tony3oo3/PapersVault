## Problem

While prior work has focused on fine-tuning the UNet or replacing the CLIP text encoder with larger models like T5 in text-to-image diffusion pipelines, the question of whether the existing CLIP text encoder itself can be fine-tuned to improve generation quality and text-image alignment has remained largely unexplored. This paper asks whether reward-guided fine-tuning of the text encoder alone can yield substantial improvements without the storage and computation overhead of larger encoders or the domain limitations of paired-dataset UNet fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes only text prompts (from the 10M+ OpenPrompt dataset) and a pre-defined 25-step DDIM denoising schedule, eliminating the need for paired image-caption data in the prompt-based variant. The text encoder is a ViT-L model that takes 77-token sequences as input and produces 768-dimensional embeddings, which condition the frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 UNet to generate 512×512 RGB images. At inference, the fine-tuned text encoder can be interpolated with the original encoder or mixed across reward-specific variants for controllable style generation.

## Method

TextCraftor fine-tunes the CLIP text encoder within Stable Diffusion by propagating differentiable reward gradients back through the DDIM denoising chain. The prompt-based variant iteratively solves the full 25-step denoising process from random noise to obtain a precise image estimate, on which reward models (Aesthetic score, HPSv2, PickScore) are evaluated; gradients are backpropagated through the chain using gradient checkpointing to manage memory, with updates applied only over the last K timesteps to avoid gradient explosion at early noisy steps. A key stabilizing mechanism is an always-on CLIP cosine-similarity constraint between the predicted image embedding and the text embedding, which prevents mode collapse and catastrophic forgetting that arise when optimizing a single reward in isolation. The approach is orthogonal to UNet reward fine-tuning: after the text encoder is optimized, the UNet can be further fine-tuned with the improved encoder frozen, yielding compounding quality gains.

## Results

On Parti-Prompts (1632 prompts), TextCraftor with combined rewards (All) achieves 5.88 Aesthetics, 19.16 PickScore, and 0.2805 HPSv2, substantially outperforming the SDv1.5 baseline (5.26, 18.83, 0.2703). Adding UNet fine-tuning on top of the improved text encoder (TextCraftor Text+UNet) further boosts scores to 6.42 Aesthetics, 19.48 PickScore, and 0.2900 HPSv2. On the HPSv2 benchmark (3200 prompts across 4 subdomains), TextCraftor fine-tuned solely on HPSv2 reaches 0.2910 average, and TextCraftor+UNet (HPSv2) reaches 0.2983, both exceeding SDXL Base 0.9 (0.2773) and DeepFloyd-XL (0.2727).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDv1.5, TextCraftor (All) wins on all three metrics: Aesthetics 5.88 vs 5.26 (+0.62), PickScore 19.16 vs 18.83 (+0.33), and HPSv2 0.2805 vs 0.2703 (+0.010). Against SDXL Base 0.9, TextCraftor (All) surpasses it on Aesthetics (5.88 vs 5.71) and HPSv2 (0.2805 vs 0.2783) but trails on PickScore (19.16 vs 20.47); the combined Text+UNet variant narrows the PickScore gap to 19.48 vs 20.47 while extending the Aesthetics lead to 6.42 vs 5.71. Against DDPO (both Aesthetic and Alignment variants), TextCraftor (All) dominates on every metric, e.g., Aesthetics 5.88 vs 5.14/5.26, PickScore 19.16 vs 18.79/18.71, and HPSv2 0.2805 vs 0.2641/0.2676. In human evaluation (Table 3), TextCraftor achieves win rates of 71.7% over SDv1.5, 81.7% over SDv2.0, 59.7% over SDXL Base 0.9, and 81.3% over prompt engineering, confirming the quantitative gains are perceptually meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of reward-based diffusion fine-tuning (DDPO, Clark et al., Prabhudesai et al.), which targets the UNet, by instead fine-tuning the CLIP text encoder through reward propagation along the DDIM denoising chain. It also contrasts with the approach of replacing CLIP with larger encoders like T5 (Saharia et al.), showing that enhancing the existing encoder is both cheaper and effective. The method draws on the reward-model ecosystem (Aesthetic predictor, HPSv2, PickScore) and the prompt-based training formulation, but applies them to a previously frozen component. The CLIP cosine-similarity constraint is a novel stabilization mechanism not present in prior reward fine-tuning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the text encoder is a high-leverage, underexplored component for improving text-to-image quality shifts where practitioners should invest compute, and the orthogonality to UNet fine-tuning means the two can be stacked for compounding gains. The prompt-only training requirement (no paired image-text data) and the absence of additional inference overhead make this practically attractive for production pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SDv1.5 to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), how sensitive results are to the quality and coverage of the reward models, and whether the CLIP constraint can be replaced by more principled regularizers as reward models improve.
