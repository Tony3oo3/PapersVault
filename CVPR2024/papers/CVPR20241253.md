## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to correctly render prompts with multiple objects, attributes, or spatial compositions—objects are mixed, swapped, or missing. The authors trace this failure to cross-attention and self-attention layers, where pixels with similar features produce similar queries and attend to the wrong tokens or regions, causing attribute blending and incorrect object placement. Existing remedies address only cross-attention and ignore the analogous mixing in self-attention.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a spatial layout of k bounding boxes (integer coordinates, each with a caption identifying the object inside) as input, and produces a single synthesized RGB image. The layout can be generated automatically by GPT-4 via in-context learning or supplied manually. The approach is training-free and model-agnostic: it is injected as a per-step gradient update into the latent space of any pretrained diffusion model (GLIGEN, ControlNet, Stable Diffusion, etc.) without modifying weights or requiring additional networks.

## Method

The core contribution is two attention-refocusing losses applied during the iterative denoising process. Cross-Attention Refocusing (CAR) combines a foreground term that maximizes the attention score of each grounded token within its bounding-box mask and a background term that minimizes the maximum attention score outside that mask, jointly steering cross-attention to the correct spatial region. Self-Attention Refocusing (SAR) penalizes the average self-attention mass that pixels inside a box place on regions outside the box, preventing feature mixing between visually similar but semantically distinct objects. At each early denoising step, the noised latent is updated τ times via gradient descent on the sum of both losses before the UNet predicts the next denoised sample, with a step-size α controlling the strength of the guidance. The pipeline is completed by a GPT-4 text-to-layout stage that predicts bounding boxes and labels from the prompt using in-context learning, making the entire system end-to-end training-free.

## Results

On the HRS benchmark, applying CAR+SAR losses to GLIGEN improves spatial accuracy from 30.74% to 40.22% (+9.5%) and color accuracy from 18.78% to 26.32% (+7.5%), while counting F1 rises from 66.58 to 67.54. On the TIFA benchmark, the method boosts counting accuracy by +1.22 (SD 1.4) and +0.81 (SD 2.1) with no degradation in other categories. For mask-guided generation, the approach achieves IoU of 38.97±0.56 and CLIP Score of 0.3177±0.0011, outperforming DenseDiffusion (34.99, 0.2814) and SD-Pww (23.76, 0.2800). FID on COCO 2014 remains comparable (GLIGEN: 20.37 vs. 20.63 baseline), confirming no image quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GLIGEN (the primary base model), CAR+SAR yields the largest spatial accuracy gain (+9.5%, 30.74→40.22) and a 7.5% color accuracy boost, while counting F1 improves modestly (+0.7). Compared to BoxDiff, the method wins on counting (67.54 vs. 67.02), spatial (40.22 vs. 33.93), and color (26.32 vs. 22.50) but slightly trails on size (27.74 vs. 28.54). Against Layout-guidance, the improvement is most dramatic in counting F1 (+6.8, 56.22→63.01), surpassing both Attend-and-Excite and MultiDiffusion. On the mask-guided task (Table 3), the method outperforms DenseDiffusion by +3.98 IoU and +0.0363 CLIP Score, and SD-Pww by +15.21 IoU. For LLM layout generation (Table 6), GPT-4 achieves 88.5% correctness vs. GPT-3 (83.5%), Llama 2 (63.5%), and Llama 1 (38.5%), validating the two-stage pipeline. FID on COCO 2014 confirms no quality loss: GLIGEN+Ours (20.37) actually slightly improves over GLIGEN alone (20.63).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: layout-conditioned text-to-image synthesis (GLIGEN, ControlNet, MultiDiffusion, Layout-guidance, BoxDiff) and attention manipulation during diffusion sampling (Attend-and-Excite, A-STAR). It departs from prior attention-guidance methods by extending the optimization from cross-attention alone to also include self-attention, arguing that feature mixing between spatially distinct but visually similar regions occurs in both layers. The LLM-for-layout component is concurrent with LLM-grounded Diffusion and LayoutGPT, but the paper's distinct contribution is the training-free attention-refocusing losses that improve controllability on top of any layout-conditioned base model, rather than the layout generation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic nature of the attention-refocusing losses makes them a practical plug-in for a wide range of existing diffusion pipelines (Stable Diffusion, GLIGEN, ControlNet, Attend-and-Excite, MultiDiffusion), lowering the barrier to improved spatial controllability without retraining. The LLM-to-layout pipeline also opens a path for leveraging rapidly improving LLM spatial reasoning without costly retraining of the text encoder. Key limitations remain: GPT-4 still misinterprets object counts and sizes in complex prompts, and the diffusion model can fail to follow out-of-distribution layouts, suggesting the two-stage pipeline's reliability is bounded by the weaker of its two components. An open question is whether the per-step gradient optimization (τ updates per denoising step) scales efficiently to higher resolutions or longer prompts with many objects.
