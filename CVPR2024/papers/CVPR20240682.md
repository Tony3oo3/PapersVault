## Problem

Existing facial texture generation methods for 3D face reconstruction suffer from low-quality textures, identity loss, and poor occlusion handling: GAN-based approaches over-smooth and leak identity, iterative optimization methods are slow (hundreds of seconds) and overfit occlusions into the texture, and publicly available UV-texture datasets are either small, non-reproducible, or tied to non-BFM 3DMMs. This paper tackles the problem of generating high-fidelity, identity-preserving UV textures from a single in-the-wild image in a single forward pass while remaining robust to hair, glasses, and pose variations.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single in-the-wild RGB face image (no pose or occlusion constraints) and produces a 256×256 UV texture map aligned to the Basel Face Model topology. Internally, the input image is first processed by Deep3D to obtain 3D vertices, then an incomplete UV texture is extracted by projecting visible vertex colors into UV space using BiSeNet segmentation masks and per-vertex normal-based visibility masks; this incomplete texture serves as the conditioning signal. The final output is a complete, occlusion-free UV map that can be directly plugged into any BFM-based reconstruction pipeline (e.g., Deep3D or HRN) as a drop-in texture generator.

## Method

UV-IDM treats texture generation as a texture-completion task within a Latent Diffusion Model framework. A VAE (trained self-supervised with KL, GAN, and LPIPS losses) compresses 256×256 UV textures into a 32×32×4 latent space. An Identity-Conditioned Module (ICM) extracts an incomplete UV texture from the input image by combining Deep3D 3D reconstruction, BiSeNet facial segmentation, and vertex visibility masks, then encodes it via an embedding network whose features are injected into multiple levels of the U-Net denoiser through cross-attention (analogous to image-prompt conditioning in IP-Adapter). The LDM is trained jointly with the ICM using the standard denoising objective conditioned on the incomplete texture. To enable training, the authors construct the BFM-UV dataset (80K+ samples) by generating diverse faces with StyleGAN2, editing out hair (via HairMapper), glasses, and pose (via InterFaceGAN), then extracting multi-view UV textures through a five-step rendering pipeline that fuses three incomplete views into a complete map using YUV color matching and linear blending.

## Results

On FFHQ, D-UV-IDM achieves FID 22.65 and CSIM 0.9428, outperforming Deep3D (FID 25.63, CSIM 0.9351) in rendering quality and identity preservation. On CelebAMask-HQ, D-UV-IDM reaches FID 24.92 and CSIM 0.9501, again surpassing Deep3D (FID 28.71, CSIM 0.9424). H-UV-IDM achieves substantially lower FID than HRN across all three datasets (e.g., 57.36 vs. 67.79 on AFLW2000), indicating superior rendering quality, though HRN retains an edge in LPIPS and CSIM due to its iterative fitting. Inference time is 6 seconds per face, compared to 150 s (FFHQ-UV), 800 s (OSTeC), 160 s (NextFace), and 18 s (HRN with 50 iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Deep3D, D-UV-IDM consistently wins on FID (rendering quality) and CSIM (identity preservation) across all three datasets, with the largest FID gap on CelebAMask-HQ (24.92 vs. 28.71); LPIPS is slightly worse only on AFLW2000 (0.1651 vs. 0.1615). Against HRN, H-UV-IDM achieves markedly lower FID on every dataset (e.g., 57.36 vs. 67.79 on AFLW2000), but HRN retains advantages in LPIPS and CSIM, which the authors attribute to HRN's iterative fitting overfitting occlusions into the texture. FFHQ-UV, OSTeC, and NextFace are excluded from the quantitative table due to different 3DMMs or overfitting tendencies, but qualitative comparisons show UV-IDM avoids merging occlusions (hats, hands, hair) into the texture, a recurring failure mode of these iterative methods. Inference speed is a decisive advantage: UV-IDM completes generation in 6 s versus 150 s (FFHQ-UV), 800 s (OSTeC), 160 s (NextFace), and 18 s (HRN with 50 optimization steps), owing to the single-pass diffusion inference with DDIM acceleration (50 steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

UV-IDM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the Latent Diffusion Model framework [Rombach et al., CVPR 2022], which provides the generative backbone; (2) BFM-based 3D face reconstruction pipelines like Deep3D [Deng et al., 2020], which supply the geometric substrate and UV mapping; and (3) image-prompt conditioning via cross-attention as in IP-Adapter [Ye et al., 2023], which is repurposed here to inject incomplete UV textures as identity guidance. The key conceptual departure from prior GAN-based texture generators (FFHQ-UV, OSTeC, NextFace, HRN) is reframing texture synthesis as a completion task—predicting the full UV map from a partially observed one—rather than performing per-sample iterative optimization, thereby eliminating the overfitting-to-occlusion failure mode that plagues those methods. Relightify [Papantoniou et al., CVPR 2023] was the first to apply diffusion to face texture, but without explicit identity conditioning; UV-IDM adds the Identity-Conditioned Module to close that gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: the BFM-UV dataset (80K+ high-fidelity UV maps with paired in-the-wild and hair-removed images) addresses a long-standing data scarcity bottleneck for BFM-based texture research, and the 6-second single-pass inference (vs. 150–800 s for iterative baselines) makes high-quality texture generation viable in interactive pipelines. Limitations include reliance on StyleGAN2-synthesized training data that may inherit FFHQ demographic biases and identity drift from latent-space editing, and the method is currently scoped to BFM geometry. Open directions the authors flag include 3D-aware GANs for more identity-faithful synthetic data, joint geometry-and-lighting generation, and integration with visual language models for user-customizable digital assets.
