## Problem

Semantic Audio-Visual Navigation (SAVN) requires an agent to locate a sounding object in an unseen environment using only intermittent auditory cues and visual observations. Existing approaches rely on extensive reinforcement learning training trajectories or oracle human instructions, achieving limited success rates and poor generalizability. No prior method performs SAVN in a truly zero-shot manner without training demonstrations or complementary semantic modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240193/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent receives 256×256 RGB and depth images, two-channel audio clips represented as 65×26 spectrograms, and its GPS coordinates. The output is a discrete action sequence drawn from {MoveForward, TurnRight, TurnLeft, Stop} with 1-meter step size, generated by decomposing a selected frontier waypoint into a shortest path via Dijkstra's algorithm on a wall-segmented semantic top-down map. Audio signals are inherently sporadic and often absent, so the perception module must accumulate evidence over multiple time steps before producing a usable goal description.

## Method

RILA comprises three components operating in a zero-shot pipeline. The perception module converts sensory data into text: audio is processed via progressive classification (cumulative argmax over history), a fine-tuned ResNet-18 for distance estimation, and RMS-intensity-weighted binary direction from dual channels; visual input is handled by GroundingDINO for object detection and depth for constructing a wall-segmented semantic map. An LLM-based Imaginative Assistant infers room types and layouts from partially observed regions and provides strategic suggestions about where the goal object is likely located. The Reflective Planner (GPT-3.5-turbo) selects a frontier waypoint from candidates by reasoning over perceptions, layout insights, and suggestions, and critically, adaptively discounts misleading perceptual descriptions when exploration reveals the predicted goal is absent—enabling the agent to redirect using object characteristics instead. The entire system requires no environment-specific training, leveraging only pre-trained perception models and frozen LLM commonsense reasoning.

## Results

On the SoundSpaces Matterport3D test set (1,000 episodes, 10 unseen scenes, 21 goal objects), RILA achieves a Success Rate of 35.4%, SPL of 11.8%, SNA of 8.7%, DTG of 11.4 m, and SWS of 20.4%, all in a zero-shot setting without any training trajectories. The most notable gain is in Success When Silent (SWS), where RILA reaches 20.4% versus 14.3% for the best supervised baseline K-SAVEN, a >40% relative improvement. With ground-truth visual perception, RILA's SR rises to 62.0% and SPL to 39.2%, confirming that the LLM planner's reasoning is strong when perception is reliable. The main weakness is SPL (11.8% vs. 23.4% for K-SAVEN), attributed to RILA's exploratory strategy and cautious navigation under ambiguous goal descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RILA (35.4% SR) outperforms all five supervised baselines in zero-shot mode, including K-SAVEN (34.4% SR), which relies on a knowledge graph built from training data and an end-to-end RL policy. The advantage is most pronounced in SWS: RILA reaches 20.4% versus 14.3% for K-SAVEN, 15.8% for AVLEN, and 14.7% for SAVi, indicating superior handling of long-distance, intermittent-sound scenarios. Among zero-shot baselines, RILA substantially exceeds ESC (23.6% SR) and ImageBind-LLM (2.4% SR), the latter of which essentially fails despite receiving ground-truth audio descriptions. However, RILA trails K-SAVEN on SPL (11.8% vs. 23.4%) and DTG (11.4 m vs. 6.6 m), reflecting the cost of its exploratory, reflection-based strategy that traverses longer paths before confirming the target. The ablation without the Imaginative Assistant drops SR from 35.4% to 31.4% and SWS from 20.4% to 15.3%, confirming the assistant's contribution to global spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

RILA departs from the dominant paradigm in SAVN, where prior methods (AudioGoal, SAVi, AV-WAN, K-SAVEN) rely on end-to-end reinforcement learning trained on extensive environment-specific trajectories, by instead leveraging the commonsense reasoning of a frozen LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo) for zero-shot planning. It also extends the emerging line of LLM-based embodied navigation (ESC, NavGPT) from vision-language navigation to the more challenging audio-visual setting, where goal information is intermittent and inherently ambiguous. The reflective mechanism—adaptively dismissing misleading perceptual descriptions—and the imaginative assistant for room-layout inference are novel architectural components not present in prior SAVN or LLM-navigation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLM-based planning can match or exceed trained RL baselines on SAVN without any task-specific training, suggesting a path toward more generalizable embodied agents. However, the authors identify sound source localization as the fundamental bottleneck: the simulation's dual-channel audio provides insufficient ITD cues, and direction estimation accuracy drops to ~60% at long range, capping overall performance. Open questions include whether improved audio localization (e.g., NeRF-based) or stronger open-source LLMs could close the remaining gap, and whether the reflective-discounting strategy generalizes beyond the SoundSpaces benchmark to real-world deployments with more complex acoustics.
