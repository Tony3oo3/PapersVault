## Problem

CLIP is trained via image-text contrastive learning, yet its image encoder features are commonly used for intra-modal operations (e.g., image-image similarity in few-shot classification), creating a misalignment between the pre-training objective and the feature extraction method. Since CLIP is not optimized for unimodal scenarios, this inconsistency degrades the quality of image representations and limits downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240629/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB test image and a set of class names from the target dataset as input; no image data or training is required. The Auto Text Generator (ATG) uses ChatGPT and WordNet to produce K diverse texts from class names alone, which are encoded by CLIP's text encoder. The output is a K-dimensional CODER vector (cosine similarities between the image and each text) used for classification, ultimately yielding a predicted class label. In few-shot mode, support set images are also encoded into CODER vectors for similarity-based classification.

## Method

The core insight is that CLIP's zero-shot classification can be reinterpreted as a 1NN problem in cross-modal space, where texts serve as neighbors of images. CODER replaces CLIP's original image feature with a K-dimensional vector of cosine similarities between the image and K diverse texts, capturing the distance structure that CLIP's contrastive pre-training ensures is accurate. To satisfy the dense-sampling requirement of neighbor-based representations, the Auto Text Generator (ATG) automatically produces five types of texts (class-name, attribute, analogous-class, synonym, and one-to-one) in a data-free, training-free manner using ChatGPT and WordNet. For zero-shot classification, a two-stage pipeline first uses a general CODER with a heuristic classifier for preliminary ranking, then reranks the top-5 classes using one-to-one specific CODER that highlights distinguishing features between confusable pairs. For few-shot classification, CODER-Adapter substitutes CLIP's image features with CODER vectors in Tip-Adapter's affinity computation, ensembling the resulting similarity with CLIP's original zero-shot logits.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification, CODER* (with one-to-one reranking) consistently outperforms both vanilla CLIP and VCD across all 9 datasets and 4 CLIP architectures. On ImageNet with ViT-L/14@336px, CODER* reaches 78.49% top-1 accuracy versus 76.11% for VCD and 71.81% for CLIP. The largest gains appear on fine-grained or texture datasets: EuroSAT improves by up to +10.50 points over VCD (69.96% vs 59.46% with ViT-L/14@336px) and Describable Textures by up to +10.05 points (55.69% vs 45.64% with ViT-B/16). In few-shot classification, CODER-Adapter surpasses TIP-Adapter and TIP-X on most of the 11 evaluated datasets across 1-, 2-, 4-, and 16-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CLIP, CODER* achieves substantial gains on every dataset and architecture, e.g., +7.42 points on ImageNet (ViT-B/32: 66.86 vs 59.07) and +15.03 on EuroSAT (ViT-L/14@336px: 69.96 vs 54.93). Against VCD (the strongest zero-shot baseline), CODER* wins on all 9 datasets with margins ranging from +1.04 (ImageNetV2, ViT-L/14@336px) to +10.50 (EuroSAT, ViT-L/14@336px), confirming that the cross-modal neighbor representation adds value beyond attribute-based prompt engineering alone. In the few-shot setting, CODER-Adapter outperforms both TIP-Adapter and TIP-X on the majority of 11 datasets across 1/2/4/16-shot, though it underperforms on EuroSAT due to the small class count (10) limiting text sampling density. The two-stage reranking (one-to-one specific CODER) contributes an additional +1.5 to +7.0 points over the single-stage CODER, with the largest reranking gains on Describable Textures and EuroSAT where fine-grained class distinctions matter most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets CLIP's zero-shot classification through a nearest-neighbor lens, departing from prior work that treats text features as classifiers or uses CLIP's image encoder features directly for intra-modal similarity (e.g., Tip-Adapter). It extends VCD's LLM-generated attribute texts by introducing three new text types (analogous class, synonym, and one-to-one distinguishing texts) via the Auto Text Generator, and applies the resulting cross-modal neighbor representation (CODER) to both zero-shot and few-shot settings. The method is positioned as a training-free, data-free correction to the misalignment between CLIP's contrastive pre-training objective and its typical use for unimodal feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CODER framework offers a principled, training-free way to extract image representations that are better aligned with CLIP's cross-modal pre-training, potentially applicable to other VLMs beyond CLIP. However, the dense-sampling requirement limits effectiveness on small-class datasets (EuroSAT), and the heuristic classifier and two-stage reranking add implementation complexity that may not generalize cleanly to open-vocabulary or retrieval tasks. Open questions include whether CODER's benefits transfer to other modalities (video, audio) or to VLMs with different training objectives, and whether the heuristic aggregation can be replaced by a learned component without sacrificing the training-free property.
