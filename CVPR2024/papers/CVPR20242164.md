## Problem

Kinematics-based 3D human motion estimation from monocular videos frequently produces physically unrealistic results (foot skating, motion jittering, ground penetration) because they model only body geometry without accounting for forces. Existing physics-based remedies either depend on external physics engines that resist end-to-end gradient flow, require scarce 3D-annotated video for training, or rely on optimization frameworks needing manual parameter tuning, limiting generalization to unseen scenarios.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a sequence of T monocular video frames (sequence length 16 in practice), first passing them through an off-the-shelf kinematics-based model (e.g., CLIFF) to obtain per-frame SMPL pose and shape, then augmenting with a separately trained global trajectory predictor to yield full generalized coordinates (translation, rotation, 23 joint rotations). PhysPT outputs refined generalized coordinates plus per-frame contact forces at modeled ground-contact vertices and joint actuation torques (75-DoF), all computed without any ground-truth force labels. Training uses only AMASS motion capture sequences—no images or 3D video annotations are required.

## Method

PhysPT is a 6-layer, 8-head Transformer encoder-decoder (1024-dim embeddings) trained self-supervised to reconstruct input motion sequences, augmented with three physics-inspired losses that make the model dynamics-aware. The key enabler is Phys-SMPL, which analytically computes per-body-part mass and inertia directly from SMPL mesh geometry (closed tetrahedron decomposition), eliminating the need for physics-engine proxies and keeping all physical terms fully differentiable. A continuous spring-mass contact model replaces discrete contact-status detection, expressing ground forces as a smooth function of point velocity and distance to the ground. During training, motion forces are recovered from AMASS sequences via a per-frame quadratic program (solved with CVXOPT) that minimizes Euler-Lagrange residuals subject to stiffness/damping bounds; these inferred forces then supervise the force loss, contact loss (penalizing velocity and height at contact vertices), and Euler-Lagrange loss (penalizing dynamics equation residuals). Once trained, PhysPT is applied as a frozen plug-in on top of any kinematics-based estimator—no fine-tuning—yielding physically plausible motion and interpretable force estimates.

## Results

On Human3.6M, PhysPT (applied on top of CLIFF) achieves MJE 52.7 mm, P-MJE 36.7 mm, ACCL 2.5 mm/frame², VEL 3.4 mm/frame, FS 2.6 mm, and GP 1.5 mm, reducing acceleration error by 83.8% and foot skating by 68.7% over the CLIFF baseline. On 3DOH, PhysPT achieves P-MJE 33.3 mm (54% lower than Huang et al.'s 72.8 mm) and GP 0.1 mm (99.2% lower than CLIFF's 12.6 mm). On PennAction, combining PhysPT's motion and force estimates yields 98.0% top-1 action recognition accuracy, surpassing the best prior skeleton-based method (UNIK, 94.0%) by 4.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysPT outperforms all 15 compared baselines on physical plausibility metrics while maintaining competitive reconstruction accuracy. Against the primary kinematics-based baseline CLIFF, PhysPT reduces ACCL from 15.4 to 2.5 (−83.8%), VEL from 6.8 to 3.4 (−50.0%), FS from 8.3 to 2.6 (−68.7%), and GP from 9.3 to 1.5 (−83.9%), with negligible change in MJE (52.2→52.7). Against PoseBert (the closest non-physics Transformer baseline), PhysPT achieves lower ACCL (2.5 vs. 5.0), lower VEL (3.4 vs. 4.0), substantially lower FS (2.6 vs. 10.0), and lower GP (1.5 vs. 12.8). Among physics-based methods, PhysPT achieves the best ACCL (2.5 vs. D&D's 6.1, a 2.4× improvement) and the best FS (2.6 vs. SimPoE's 3.4), while also matching D&D's GP (1.5) without requiring 3D-annotated video training. On 3DOH, PhysPT's P-MJE of 33.3 mm is 54% lower than Huang et al.'s 72.8 mm and 63% lower than NeurPhys's 93.3 mm, demonstrating superior generalization under occlusion. For action recognition on PennAction, PhysPT's combined motion+force input achieves 98.0% top-1 accuracy, beating UNIK (94.0%) and HDM (93.4%) by 4.0 and 4.6 points respectively, and even forces alone (94.4%) surpass UNIK.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysPT extends the SMPL body model into a physics-based representation (Phys-SMPL) that directly computes mass, inertia, and volume from mesh geometry, bypassing the proxy-body-and-physics-engine pipeline used by prior physics-based methods (PhysCap, NeurPhys, SimPoE). It also builds on the global trajectory prediction approach from GLAMR to produce full generalized coordinates from local pose estimates. Conceptually, the paper departs from both kinematics-only methods (which ignore dynamics) and physics-engine-dependent methods (which require 3D-annotated video or non-differentiable simulation) by formulating physics as analytically differentiable losses within a self-supervised Transformer encoder-decoder trained solely on motion capture data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—requiring no fine-tuning when applied atop any kinematics-based model—makes PhysPT practically attractive for pipelines that already produce per-frame 3D poses. The demonstration that inferred contact forces and joint actuations from monocular video improve human action recognition (98.0% vs. 96.0% with kinematics alone) opens a new avenue for dynamics-aware downstream tasks without ground-truth force labels. Open questions include whether the simplified spring-mass contact model generalizes to multi-contact or object-interaction scenarios beyond feet, whether the SMPL-based 23-joint representation suffices for hands and fine-grained dynamics, and whether the self-supervised reconstruction objective remains effective at longer sequence lengths or higher frame rates.
