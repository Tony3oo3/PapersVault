## Problem

Multi-modality image fusion is a blind inverse problem with no ground truth, since no single sensor can capture all modal information simultaneously. Existing approaches either rely on generative models lacking interpretability, manually crafted losses that ignore the domain gap between fused and source images, or domain-specific priors that overfit to assumptions about fusion outputs. The paper addresses this by formulating fusion as a self-supervised learning problem grounded in the equivariant imaging prior of natural sensing systems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of co-registered images from different modalities—single-channel infrared (HW) plus 3-channel visible (3HW) for IVF, or MRI plus CT/PET/SPECT for MIF—and produces a 3-channel fused image (3HW). During training, inputs are randomly cropped to 128×128 patches with batch size 8. The pseudo-sensing modules are pre-trained using fusion outputs from 15 SOTA methods as pseudo ground truth, then frozen; at deployment only the U-Fuser network is retained, discarding all auxiliary modules.

## Method

EMMA comprises three components: a U-Fuser (U-Net-like architecture with parallel Restormer self-attention and ResNet blocks at each scale for joint global-local feature modeling), two pseudo-sensing U-Nets (A_i, A_v) that map a fused image back to its source modalities, and an equivariant fusion loop. Training is two-stage: first, the sensing modules are trained with ℓ₂ loss against SOTA fusion outputs as pseudo-GT, then frozen; second, U-Fuser is trained with a composite loss combining sensing consistency (ℓ₁ + Sobel gradient between A_i(f) and i, A_v(f) and v) and an equivariant term (ℓ₁ + Sobel gradient between T_g f and F(A_i(T_g f), A_v(T_g f))). The key theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which proves that the composite imaging system F∘A is equivariant to transformations (shift, rotation, reflection) even when F and A individually need not be, providing a non-domain-specific prior that replaces hand-crafted fusion image priors and fundamentally differs from data augmentation by generating additional information through the sense-then-refuse loop.

## Results

On the MSRS IVF benchmark, EMMA achieves the best scores on 5 of 6 metrics: EN 6.71, SD 44.13, SF 11.56, AG 3.76, and SCD 1.63, with CDDFuse slightly ahead on VIF (1.05 vs. 0.97). On the M3FD dataset (zero-shot generalization), EMMA leads on all six metrics (EN 7.12, SD 44.01, SF 16.92, AG 6.23, SCD 1.48, VIF 0.66). For downstream tasks, EMMA's fused images yield 82.9% mAP@0.5 on M3FD detection (vs. 81.6% for CDDFuse) and 63.7% mIOU on MSRS segmentation (vs. 63.2% for SegMIF). On the Harvard Medical MIF dataset, EMMA also achieves the top scores across all six fusion metrics without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMMA is compared against nine SOTA fusion methods (SDNet, TarDAL, DeFusion, MetaFusion, CDDFuse, LRRNet, MURF, DDFM, SegMIF) across four datasets. The closest competitor is CDDFuse [54], which EMMA narrowly outperforms on MSRS (EN 6.71 vs 6.70, SD 44.13 vs 43.38, AG 3.76 vs 3.73, SCD 1.63 vs 1.62) but trails on VIF (0.97 vs 1.05). On the zero-shot generalization benchmarks RoadScene and M3FD, EMMA leads CDDFuse on every metric, with the largest gaps on SF (15.21 vs 14.97 on RoadScene; 16.92 vs 16.56 on M3FD) and AG (6.23 vs 5.84 on M3FD). For downstream detection on M3FD, EMMA achieves 82.9% mAP@0.5, surpassing CDDFuse (81.6%) and MetaFusion (81.5%); for segmentation on MSRS, EMMA reaches 63.7% mIOU versus SegMIF's 63.2% and CDDFuse's 62.2%. The diffusion-based DDFM [55] and the algorithm-unrolling LRRNet [19] consistently rank in the middle of the table, while older methods like MURF [40] and SDNet [46] lag substantially on information-richness metrics (EN, SD) across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMMA extends the equivariant imaging (EI) framework of Chen et al., which was previously restricted to linear inverse problems (e.g., MRI reconstruction), to the nonlinear and blind inverse problem of multi-modality image fusion. It departs from the dominant fusion-loss paradigm (‖f−i₁‖+‖f−i₂‖) by introducing a learnable pseudo-sensing module—pre-trained on SOTA fusion outputs and then frozen—that maps the fused image back to source modalities, thereby resolving the domain-mismatch problem inherent in direct source-image losses. The U-Fuser architecture combines Restormer (channel-attention Transformer) and ResNet blocks in a U-Net topology, drawing on both the global-modeling strength of Transformers and the local-detail strength of CNNs, while the equivariant prior on the composite F∘A operator replaces domain-specific fusion-image priors (low-rank, sparsity, multi-scale decomposition) with a transformation-invariance constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding self-supervised fusion in the non-domain-specific equivariance prior of natural imaging rather than speculative assumptions about fused-image structure, EMMA offers a more principled and generalizable training signal that transfers across IVF and MIF without fine-tuning. The ablation distinguishing equivariant fusion from conventional data augmentation (Exp. IV) is a useful conceptual clarification: applying the same geometric transforms to inputs yields only marginal gains, whereas the equivariant re-fusion loop provides substantial information recovery beyond the sensing range space. Open questions include whether the pseudo-sensing module's reliance on SOTA fusion outputs as pseudo-ground-truth introduces a ceiling on quality, and whether the equivariant prior remains effective for modalities with weaker natural-image invariance (e.g., highly structured medical scans).
