## Problem

Existing semantic scene completion methods treat every voxel identically during training, computing equal-weighted classification losses across the entire 3D volume. This ignores that over 90% of voxels are empty and trivially predictable, while boundary voxels at object edges are far harder to classify yet receive no additional attention, limiting accuracy in challenging regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241483/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images in three configurations: a single left-camera frame (VoxFormer-S), five temporally consecutive frames (VoxFormer-T), or stereo left-right pairs (StereoScene), at resolutions of 1220×370 or 1280×384. It outputs a dense 3D semantic voxel volume of 256×256×32 at 0.2 m resolution with 19 semantic classes plus empty. The HVM head operates on a reduced-resolution coarse volume (128×128×16) to select N=4096 hard voxels for refinement, while the final full-resolution output at inference is produced solely by trilinear interpolation with no additional modules.

## Method

HASSC introduces a Hard Voxel Mining (HVM) head that combines two complementary hardness signals: a global hardness computed as the inverse gap between the top-1 and top-2 class probabilities from a coarse MLP prediction (dynamically updated during optimization), and a local hardness derived from Local Geometric Anisotropy (LGA), which measures the exclusive-disjunction semantic difference between a voxel and its six axis-aligned neighbors. During training, tN voxels are randomly over-generated, ωN are selected by ranking global hardness, and (1−ω)N are randomly sampled to prevent overfitting; the selected voxels are then refined by a lightweight PointNet-style MLP whose cross-entropy loss is weighted by local hardness. A self-distillation strategy with an EMA-updated teacher of identical architecture provides stable soft-label supervision via KL divergence and a teacher-guided HVM loss, improving consistency without requiring a separately trained teacher. At inference, only the student branch is retained and the final prediction is obtained by trilinear interpolation, incurring zero extra computational cost.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI validation set (full range, 51.2×51.2×6.4 m³), HASSC improves mIoU by +1.13% over VoxFormer-S (12.35%→13.48%), +1.39% over VoxFormer-T (13.35%→14.74%), and +0.64% over StereoScene (15.24%→15.88%), while also boosting IoU by 0.43–0.89%. At the shorter range (12.8×12.8×6.4 m³), the mIoU gain is larger: +2.55% for HASSC-VoxFormer-T (18.42%→20.27%). On the hidden test set, HASSC-VoxFormer-S (13.34% mIoU) surpasses all single-image camera-based methods, and HASSC-VoxFormer-T (14.38% mIoU) achieves the best mIoU among all camera-based approaches. The method adds only +0.90% parameters during training and actually reduces inference time slightly (724.05 ms → 720.84 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HASSC is applied as a plug-in training scheme on three strong baselines and consistently wins: VoxFormer-S gains +1.13% mIoU (12.35→13.48) and +0.80% IoU at full range; VoxFormer-T gains +1.39% mIoU (13.35→14.74) and +0.43% IoU; StereoScene gains +0.64% mIoU (15.24→15.88) and +0.89% IoU. On the hidden test set, HASSC-VoxFormer-S (13.34% mIoU) outperforms all prior single-image camera methods including MonoScene (11.08%), TPVFormer (11.26%), OccFormer (12.32%), and NDC-Scene (12.58%), while HASSC-VoxFormer-T (14.38% mIoU) surpasses VoxFormer-T (13.41%) and all other camera-based entries. Against LiDAR-based methods converted to camera input (LMSCNet 7.07%, JS3C-Net 8.97%, 3DSketch 6.23%, AICNet 7.09%), HASSC-VoxFormer-S leads by 4–7 mIoU points. In the hard-sample-mining comparison (Table 7), HASSC (14.74% mIoU) outperforms PALNet's local-only hardness (13.28%), PointRend's global-only hardness (13.57%), and Xiao et al.'s pixel-hardness approach (13.33%), confirming that combining global and local hardness is essential for 3D voxel space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the hard sample mining paradigm from 2D dense prediction (PointRend's point selection and MLP refinement, focal loss in detection) to the 3D voxel space of semantic scene completion, where the hardness landscape is fundamentally different due to the dominance of empty voxels and boundary-vs-interior geometry. It repurposes local geometric anisotropy (LGA) from PALNet [22]—originally designed for indoor scenes—as a local hardness signal, and combines it with a novel global hardness metric derived from inter-class probability gaps in the network's own predictions. The self-distillation component follows the Mean Teacher [42] EMA framework, applied here without any external teacher model. The overall contribution is a training-time plug-in (HVM head + self-distillation) that is architecture-agnostic and incurs zero inference overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is clear: a drop-in training scheme that adds <1% parameters and no inference cost while consistently improving mIoU by 1–1.4% across three different camera-based backbones. The ablation revealing that global hardness alone can actually *hurt* performance (43.89 vs. 44.16 baseline IoU) while local hardness alone barely helps underscores that 3D voxel hardness is a genuinely different problem from 2D pixel hardness, and that the two signals are complementary rather than redundant. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (SemanticKITTI) and camera-only baselines; whether the approach transfers to LiDAR-based SSC, larger-scale outdoor datasets, or other 3D dense prediction tasks (e.g., occupancy prediction for end-to-end driving) remains open. The modest absolute gains also suggest that the remaining error in SSC may be dominated by factors beyond voxel-level hardness (e.g., 2D feature quality, 3D backbone capacity).
