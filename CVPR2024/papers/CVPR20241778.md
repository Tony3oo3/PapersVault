## Problem

Existing efficient super-resolution approaches follow two independent tracks—content-aware routing frameworks (e.g., ClassSR) that crop images into patches and dispatch them to models of varying complexity, and lightweight token-mixer designs that refine self-attention or convolution within a fixed architecture. The former suffers from inflexible partitioning, poor classification accuracy, and limited receptive fields due to cropping; the latter ignores that different image regions demand different computational budgets. This paper unifies both strategies into a single content-aware mixer that adaptively allocates self-attention to complex regions and convolution to plain regions without any external cropping or routing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (and, for omnidirectional-image SR, an additional distortion map fed to the predictor) and outputs a high-resolution image at ×2 or ×4 scale. Unlike ClassSR-style frameworks that tile the input into small patches (e.g., 32×32 with 2-pixel overlap), CAMixerSR processes the full image in a single forward pass, preserving global receptive fields. The attention ratio γ (set to 0.5 by default) controls the fraction of windows routed to self-attention versus convolution, allowing a continuous quality–computation trade-off without retraining.

## Method

CAMixer replaces the fixed window self-attention in SwinIR with a content-aware token mixer comprising three components: a predictor, a deformable self-attention branch, and a depth-wise convolution branch. The predictor, conditioned on local features, global position, and window position, generates (i) per-pixel offsets that warp attention windows toward informative content, (ii) a per-window mask that classifies each window as 'hard' or 'simple,' and (iii) spatial and channel attention maps that modulate the convolution branch. Hard windows undergo deformable window multi-head self-attention on the offset-warped features, while simple windows receive only element-wise spatial-attention-weighted convolution plus a depth-wise convolution with channel attention. The full CAMixerSR backbone stacks 20 CAMixer+FFN blocks (60 channels, 16×16 windows) and is trained with an L1 reconstruction loss plus an MSE ratio loss that enforces the target attention fraction γ, enabling a single trained model to operate at any γ via inference-time sorting of the mask scores.

## Results

On the lightweight SR benchmarks (×4), CAMixerSR (765K params, 53.8G MAdds) achieves the best PSNR on all five datasets: 32.51 dB (Set5), 28.82 dB (Set14), 27.72 dB (BSD100), 26.63 dB (Urban100), and 31.18 dB (Manga109), outperforming SwinIR-light (930K, 61.7G) by 0.07–0.16 dB while using fewer parameters and computations. On large-image SR (Test8K), CAMixerSR-B with content-aware mixing (γ=0.5) reaches 33.81 dB at 1.49G FLOPs (76% of full), matching RCAN (15.6M params, 32.6G FLOPs) at 33.76 dB with 22× fewer parameters. On ODI-SR ×2, CAMixerSR (1.30M) attains 30.46 dB PSNR on ODI-SR and 31.04 dB on SUN360, surpassing the 30× larger EDSR (40.7M) by 0.14 dB and 0.15 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMixerSR outperforms all eight lightweight SR baselines in PSNR across all five standard benchmarks, with the largest margins over IMDN (+0.30 dB on Urban100) and FDIWN (+0.35 dB on Urban100). Against the closest competitor SwinIR-NG (1201K, 63.0G), CAMixerSR gains +0.07 to +0.16 dB while using 36% fewer parameters and 15% fewer MAdds. On large-image SR (Test8K), CAMixerSR-B (765K, 1.49G) matches RCAN (15.6M, 32.6G) at 33.81 vs. 33.76 dB and beats SwinIR-light (930K, 2.10G) by 0.14 dB with 29% fewer FLOPs. When ClassSR is applied to CAMixerSR-B, it reduces FLOPs to 1.35G but drops PSNR by 0.12 dB on F2K, confirming that content-aware mixing within a single model is more effective than external patch-level routing. On ODI-SR, CAMixerSR (1.30M) surpasses OSRT-light (1.26M) by 0.04–0.05 dB and the 30× larger EDSR (40.7M) by 0.14–0.26 dB on both ODI-SR and SUN360, without using additional training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent research tracks in efficient super-resolution: content-aware routing frameworks (ClassSR, ARM) that dispatch image patches to models of varying complexity, and lightweight token-mixer design (SwinIR, IMDN, ELAN) that refines self-attention or convolution within a single backbone. Rather than treating routing as an external classifier over fixed patches, CAMixer internalizes the routing decision into the token-mixer itself, using a learnable predictor to generate per-window offsets, a binary mask, and spatial/channel attentions that jointly modulate both the deformable attention branch and the convolution branch. The backbone is derived from SwinIR-light, with window-based self-attention replaced by the content-aware mixer and the block count reduced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: 2K–8K SR remains computationally prohibitive for most transformer-based SR models, and CAMixerSR demonstrates that roughly 25% of self-attention FLOPs can be eliminated with negligible quality loss by routing only detail-rich windows through attention. However, the gains over strong baselines (SwinIR-light, ELAN-light) are modest (≈0.1–0.2 dB), and the method still inherits the limited receptive field of fixed-size windows, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. Open questions include whether the predictor's multi-output design (offsets + mask + attentions) generalizes beyond SR to other restoration tasks, and whether the Gumbel-softmax-to-argsort training-to-inference transition introduces any distribution shift at extreme sparsity ratios.
