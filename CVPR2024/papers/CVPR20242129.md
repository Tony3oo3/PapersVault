## Problem

Compressed MLLMs deployed on resource-constrained edge devices suffer severe performance degradation under dynamic distribution shifts, yet they lack the computational capacity to perform timely self-updates. No existing cloud-device collaborative framework is specifically designed to enable continual adaptation of MLLMs, with prior domain adaptation and knowledge distillation methods not transferring effectively to the multimodal, large-parameter regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The device-side MLLM receives an RGB image (1920×1080 in real-world validation) paired with a text prompt and produces a text answer (VQA) or image caption. The uplink transmits only uncertainty-filtered image tokens (reducing data volume to ~0.21% of the full dataset), while the downlink delivers a quantized weight delta (~1.65M parameters vs. 7.78B full model) that the device integrates directly into its frozen base weights without dequantization.

## Method

CD-CCA comprises three stages: (1) Uncertainty-guided Token Sampling (UTS) performs two-stage filtering—sample-level predictive entropy flags high-uncertainty instances, then token-level Monte Carlo dropout variance selects out-of-distribution tokens for uplink; (2) Adapter-based Knowledge Distillation (AKD) on the cloud fine-tunes a 7B student (LLaMA-Adapter with reduced Q-Former) against a 13B teacher using adapters on query vectors and cross-attention outputs, optimized with a composite loss of query alignment, representation alignment, and cross-entropy on teacher pseudo-labels; (3) Dynamic Weight update Compression (DWC) quantizes the parameter delta (Θ_updated − Θ_base) to 4-bit NormalFloat via QLoRA-style quantization, transmitting only the compressed update so the device can incrementally evolve without full-model retraining.

## Results

On the VQAv2-to-AOKVQA domain-shifted VQA task, CD-CCA achieves a mean MC accuracy of 51.88% and DA accuracy of 48.74% over three continual adaptation rounds, yielding gains of +3.93% (MC) and +3.19% (DA) over the source-only LLaMA-Adapter baseline. On the COCO-to-nocaps captioning task, CD-CCA reaches 38.35 BLeU-4 and 77.73 CIDEr, improving by +1.53% and +2.20% respectively over source-only. In real-world deployment, UTS reduces uplink transmission to 65.54 KB (vs. 31.10 MB full data) with 0.001 s latency, while DWC compresses the downlink from 14.48 GB to 0.791 MB (99.99% reduction) with 0.013 s latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CD-CCA outperforms all five baselines on both evaluation tasks. On VQAv2-to-AOKVQA, it surpasses the strongest baseline (ChannelWiseDivergence, 48.24% MC / 44.79% DA) by 3.64% MC and 3.95% DA, and shows a clear upward trend across three adaptation rounds (50.65→53.19 MC), whereas baselines plateau or degrade. On COCO-to-nocaps, CD-CCA leads the best baseline (CoTTA, 36.74 BLeU / 74.86 CIDEr) by 1.61 BLeU and 2.87 CIDEr, with the largest margin in the out-domain split (+1.84 BLeU, +3.98 CIDEr). Notably, TENT-continual, CoTTA, and ChannelWiseDivergence all degrade below the source-only model on at least one metric, which the authors attribute to these methods not being designed for MLLMs. PKD is the only baseline that maintains or slightly improves over source-only, but still trails CD-CCA by 3.65% MC and 3.56% DA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cloud-device collaborative learning paradigm (which prior work [17–21] treated as computational offloading) into a full continual-adaptation loop specifically for MLLMs. It repurposes adapter-based fine-tuning (LoRA, Q-Former query tokens) as the vehicle for knowledge distillation, filling a gap the authors note: no prior KD method was designed for the three-part MLLM architecture (vision encoder + cross-modal transformer + LLM). On the adaptation side, it departs from test-time adaptation methods like TENT and CoTTA—which the paper shows actually degrade on large MLLMs—by offloading the heavy lifting to a cloud teacher and transmitting only compressed, uncertainty-selected updates back to the device.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for keeping device-deployed MLLMs current under distribution shift without requiring on-device retraining, and the real-world robot validation (sub-millisecond uplink, ~13 ms downlink) demonstrates feasibility at near-real-time latencies. However, the evaluation is limited to two dataset pairs (VQA and captioning) over only three adaptation rounds, and the 'continual' aspect is not stress-tested against longer sequences or more severe domain shifts. Open questions include whether the UTS uncertainty signals remain reliable as the device model improves, how the framework scales to additional modalities (audio, video), and whether the 4-bit QLoRA quantization in DWC introduces compounding errors over many update cycles.
