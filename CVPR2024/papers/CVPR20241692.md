## Problem

Aligning video diffusion models with human preferences remains unexplored due to two compounding challenges: reward fine-tuning requires full DDIM sampling chains (50+ steps) making it computationally prohibitive, and no dedicated video reward model exists to capture human preferences for video quality. This paper is the first to address both obstacles simultaneously for text-to-video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 16-frame video at 256×256 resolution via 20-step DDIM inference. During fine-tuning, it consumes video-text pairs (approximately 20 per animal species) from the base model's pre-training dataset (WebVid10M) without quality filtering. The reward signal is computed by an image-level model (HPSv2) on sparsely sampled frames rather than the full video, and the final output is a visually enhanced video that can surpass the quality of the source training data.

## Method

InstructVideo recasts reward fine-tuning as an editing procedure: instead of generating from pure noise, it corrupts a pre-sampled video to a noise level τ (default 0.6) via the forward diffusion process, then performs only τ×D steps of DDIM inference, reducing computation proportionally. To repurpose image reward models for video, it introduces Segmental Video Reward (SegVR), which divides the video into S=4 segments and samples one frame per segment for reward computation, and Temporally Attenuated Reward (TAR), which applies an exponential decay weight f_i = exp(-λ_tar·|g(i)-F/2|) to emphasize central frames over peripheral ones, preventing structural twitching and color jittering. Fine-tuning uses LoRA adapters with gradient truncation to only the last DDIM step, and the optimized LoRA weights are merged back into the base ModelScopeT2V model so inference cost remains identical to the original.

## Results

On the generalization evaluation (HPSv2 score), InstructVideo achieves 0.2717 on in-domain animal prompts, 0.2645 on new animal species, and 0.2682 on non-animal prompts, outperforming all baselines across all three categories. The strongest baseline, DRaFT, scores 0.2584 / 0.2561 / 0.2644 respectively, yielding InstructVideo improvements of +0.0133, +0.0084, and +0.0038. In the user study over 300 prompts, InstructVideo is preferred over the base ModelScopeT2V in 78.7% of quality comparisons and over DRaFT in 75.7%, with alignment preferences more evenly split (29.1% vs. 57.1% tie against ModelScopeT2V).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructVideo is compared against four baselines: the base ModelScopeT2V (both 20-step and 50-step DDIM), DDPO (policy-gradient RL), RWR (reward-weighted regression), and DRaFT (direct reward back-propagation). InstructVideo wins on all three HPSv2 evaluation categories, with the largest margin over DRaFT on in-domain prompts (+0.0133) and the smallest on non-animal prompts (+0.0038 over RWR). DDPO and RWR plateau after roughly 11 hours of fine-tuning and fail to surpass the base model on in-domain prompts, while DRaFT initially lags but eventually approaches InstructVideo's trajectory. In the user study, InstructVideo is strongly preferred for video quality (78.7% vs. ModelScopeT2V, 75.7% vs. DRaFT) but shows a more modest edge on video-text alignment, where ties dominate (57.1% and 52.7% respectively). The editing-based formulation of InstructVideo also enables faster convergence than DRaFT, reaching competitive reward scores within the first 11 hours where DRaFT still lags.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward fine-tuning paradigm established for image diffusion models (DDPO, DRaFT, RWR) to the video domain, where it had been unexplored due to prohibitive computational cost. Two key conceptual departures distinguish it from prior work: (1) recasting reward fine-tuning as a partial DDIM editing process rather than full-chain generation, drawing on diffusion editing workflows (SDEdit, DiffEdit), and (2) repurposing off-the-shelf image reward models (HPSv2) for video via sparse segmental sampling and temporal attenuation, rather than training a dedicated video reward model. The base model is ModelScopeT2V, a latent video diffusion model trained on WebVid10M, and LoRA is used to prevent catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that video diffusion models can be effectively aligned with human preferences using only image-level reward signals, removing the need for expensive video-specific annotation and reward model training. The editing reformulation makes reward fine-tuning computationally tractable for video (partial DDIM chain vs. full 50-step inference), a practical insight likely to be adopted by subsequent video alignment work. However, the evaluation is limited to a single base model at 16×256×256 resolution and 45 animal species, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer, higher-resolution videos or more diverse content. A key open question is whether image reward models truly capture video-specific temporal quality (motion smoothness, temporal coherence) or merely proxy for per-frame aesthetics.
