## Problem

LiDAR semantic segmentation models are conventionally trained and evaluated on single-domain, clear-weather data, yet real-world autonomous driving encounters both unseen scenes and adverse weather (fog, rain, snow) that simultaneously shift spatial, intensity, and semantic distributions. No prior method jointly addresses this dual domain gap—scene-level discrepancy plus weather-induced perturbation—for both unsupervised domain adaptation and domain generalization in LiDAR segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds of shape N×4 (3D Cartesian coordinates plus per-point laser intensity) from a labeled clear-weather source domain and an unlabeled adverse-weather target domain, and produces per-point semantic labels over 19 common classes. A key intermediate artifact is the Bridge Domain, generated by applying physically valid weather simulation (scatterer modeling for fog, rain, snow) to source point clouds, which retains source scene geometry while injecting target-like weather disturbances. The Universal Mixing operator exchanges subsets of points between two domains based on cylinder-coordinate spatial partitions, normalized intensity bins, or semantic category masks, producing bidirectional intermediate point clouds and their corresponding mixed labels.

## Method

UniMix is a two-stage teacher-student framework built on MinkowskiNet. In Stage 1 (Source-to-Bridge), a Bridge Domain is generated via physically valid weather simulation on source data, and the Universal Mixing operator blends source and Bridge point clouds along spatial, intensity, and semantic dimensions; the student is trained with Dice loss on the mixed intermediate domain while the teacher (updated via EMA) provides pseudo-labels for the Bridge data. In Stage 2 (Bridge-to-Target, UDA only), the Bridge Domain serves as the new source and is mixed with the adverse-weather target domain to further adapt the model. The key insight is that the Bridge Domain decouples the weather gap from the scene gap, and the three-axis mixing (cylinder-coordinate spatial partition, intensity-value partition, and semantic-category exchange) creates diverse intermediate samples that force the network to learn weather-robust, domain-invariant representations without requiring target labels.

## Results

On the UDA task, UniMix achieves 37.2 mIoU on SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF (a +12.8 mIoU gain over the source-only baseline of 24.4) and 26.7 mIoU on SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF (+11.7 mIoU over source-only 15.0), outperforming the SOTA mixing method CoSMix by 8.8 and 4.6 mIoU respectively. On the DG task, UniMix reaches 31.4 mIoU on SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF (+7.0 over source-only) and 23.4 mIoU on SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF (+8.4 over source-only), surpassing the best prior method PointDR by 2.8 and 4.9 mIoU. In the clear-weather synthetic-to-real setting (SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI), UniMix also leads with 33.7 mIoU, a +13.3 gain over source-only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMix decisively outperforms all UDA baselines on both benchmarks: on SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF it beats CoSMix (28.4) by 8.8 mIoU, Ent-Min (27.2) by 10.0, Self-training (27.6) by 9.6, and ADDA (26.3) by 10.9; on SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF it leads CoSMix (22.1) by 4.6 mIoU and Ent-Min (20.9) by 5.8. For domain generalization, UniMix surpasses the strongest prior method PointDR [49] by 2.8 mIoU (31.4 vs 28.6) on SemanticKITTI→SemanticSTF and by 4.9 mIoU (23.4 vs 18.5) on SynLiDAR→SemanticSTF, while simpler augmentation baselines (Dropout, Perturbation, PolarMix) and 2D DG methods (MMD, PCL) yield only 1.3–2.5 mIoU gains over source-only. In the clear-weather synthetic-to-real UDA setting (SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI, Table 6), UniMix (33.7 mIoU) also outperforms CoSMix (32.2), PolarMix (31.0), and ST-PCT (28.9), confirming its generality beyond adverse weather. The ablation in Table 5 further shows that replacing Universal Mixing with CoSMix, PolarMix, or LaserMix within the same pipeline degrades UDA mIoU from 37.2 to 35.4, 33.0, and 33.3 respectively, and DG mIoU from 31.4 to 26.7, 26.0, and 26.9.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMix extends the point cloud mixing paradigm introduced by CoSMix (Saltori et al., 2023), which performed semantic-only patch exchange for synthetic-to-real LiDAR adaptation, by adding spatial (cylinder-coordinate partition) and intensity-based mixing dimensions to form a Universal Mixing operator. For the Bridge Domain, it repurposes physically valid weather simulation methods from Hahner et al. (CVPR 2021, 2022) — originally designed for 3D object detection robustness — to generate adverse-weather versions of source-domain scenes. The overall two-stage teacher-student pipeline follows the established UDA framework, but the specific formulation of bridging clear and adverse weather through a simulated intermediate domain is novel relative to prior LSS adaptation work, which focused on synthetic-to-real or cross-city real-to-real transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: LiDAR segmentation models trained in clear weather degrade severely in fog, rain, and snow, yet prior domain adaptation and generalization methods for LSS have not targeted this setting. The Bridge Domain concept — using physics-based weather simulation to create an intermediate domain that shares scene structure with the source but weather characteristics with the target — offers a reusable strategy for any sensor modality affected by atmospheric conditions. Limitations include reliance on a single backbone (MinkowskiNet), a single target dataset (SemanticSTF), and weather simulations that may not fully capture real-world variability; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other sensor types (camera, radar), more extreme weather, and real-time deployment constraints.
