## Problem

Existing MIL methods for whole-slide image classification provide only patch-level attention maps, offering no pathologist-friendly rationale for why specific tissue regions drive a prediction. Post-hoc interpretability approaches that extract handcrafted features from attended patches suffer from a fundamental disconnect between the deep feature space used for training and the interpretable feature space used for explanation. The paper asks whether inherent, feature-level interpretability can be built into the MIL framework without the performance penalty typically associated with interpretable models.

## Input / Output

Each WSI is decomposed into 224×224 patches at 5× magnification, from which two feature sets are extracted per patch: a deep feature vector from a DINO-self-supervised ViT-S encoder, and a handcrafted PathExpert feature vector (nuclei morphology, spatial distribution, and heterogeneity descriptors) computed from HoVerNet segmentation on the corresponding 1792×1792 crop at 40×. The model outputs a slide-level class prediction from the SI branch, a patch attention map from the MIL branch, and a decomposed feature-level report showing which PathExpert features (e.g., nuclear cohesiveness, chromatin texture) and their values in the top-K attended patches drive the prediction.

## Method

SI-MIL is a dual-branch co-learning framework: a conventional additive ABMIL branch processes deep features and produces patch attention scores, while a Self-Interpretable (SI) branch operates on PathExpert features of the top-K patches selected via a differentiable perturbed Top-K operator that enables end-to-end gradient flow between branches. Within the SI branch, a PF-Mixer network contextualizes the K×d PathExpert feature matrix, and a gated attention network computes per-feature attention scores that are sparsified via percentile scaling and sigmoid activation, then used to linearly scale the feature matrix before a single linear predictor produces the slide-level score. The final prediction is a linear combination of feature attention scores, classifier weights, and PathExpert values, making each feature's contribution directly readable. Training jointly optimizes cross-entropy losses on both branches plus a knowledge-distillation MSE loss that aligns the SI branch output with the high-performing MIL branch, effectively 'taming' the deep MIL to attend to regions that are also discriminative in the interpretable feature space.

## Results

On TCGA-BRCA, SI-MIL achieves 94.4% accuracy and 0.968 AUC, matching or exceeding the best deep-feature baselines (DINO ViT-S: 93.7% Acc, 0.974 AUC; CTransPath: 92.0% Acc, 0.974 AUC) while remaining fully interpretable. On TCGA-Lung, SI-MIL reaches 88.4% accuracy and 0.941 AUC, slightly below CTransPath (90.4% Acc, 0.967 AUC) but well above all interpretable baselines. On TCGA-CRC, SI-MIL attains 88.4% accuracy and 0.910 AUC, outperforming DINO ViT-S (90.4% Acc, 0.897 AUC) on AUC and all other interpretable methods by a wide margin. Across all three datasets, SI-MIL is the best-performing self-interpretable method, closing the accuracy gap with non-interpretable deep-feature MILs to within 1–2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SI-MIL outperforms all interpretable baselines across all three datasets: it beats the 2-stage post-hoc method by 1.9% Acc on Lung (0.884 vs 0.865), 3.6% Acc on BRCA (0.944 vs 0.908), and 0.8% Acc on CRC (0.884 vs 0.876), with the largest AUC gains on CRC (0.910 vs 0.862). Against the non-interpretable deep-feature baselines, SI-MIL is competitive: on BRCA it actually surpasses DINO ViT-S in accuracy (0.944 vs 0.937) and CTransPath (0.920), while on Lung it trails CTransPath by 2.0% Acc (0.884 vs 0.904) and DINO ViT-S by 1.2% Acc. On CRC, SI-MIL achieves the highest AUC among all methods (0.910) despite using only handcrafted PathExpert features in its prediction path. The paper notes that RetCCL and CTransPath results may be inflated due to pretraining on the full TCGA cohort including test splits, making the DINO ViT-S and IN ViT-S comparisons more reliable. In the MIL-adaptability study (Table 2), SI-MIL extensions of ABMIL, CLAM, and TransMIL remain within 1–2% of their standalone counterparts on TCGA-BRCA, confirming generality across MIL architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

SI-MIL builds directly on Additive MIL (ABMIL) as its deep branch and on the differentiable perturbed Top-K operator for end-to-end patch selection, but departs fundamentally from prior interpretability approaches in computational pathology. Where post-hoc methods (attention maps, GradCAM) only highlight salient patches and vision-language methods remain patch-level or non-scalable, SI-MIL introduces a co-learned Self-Interpretable branch grounded on handcrafted PathExpert features (nuclei morphology, spatial distribution, graph-based descriptors) that produces a linear, feature-level prediction. This positions the work at the intersection of self-interpretability research in natural images (concept bottleneck models, neural-symbolic reasoning) and gigapixel WSI analysis, where no prior method had embedded interpretability directly into the MIL training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that interpretability and performance are in unavoidable tension for WSI classification, showing that a linear model on handcrafted pathological features—guided by a deep MIL via differentiable Top-K and knowledge distillation—can close most of the performance gap to pure deep-feature baselines while providing pathologist-readable feature-level rationale. However, SI-MIL still trails the strongest deep baselines (e.g., CTransPath, DINO ViT-S) by a small margin on several datasets, and its interpretability is bounded by the coverage of the handcrafted PathExpert feature set and the accuracy of HoVerNet nuclei segmentation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other deep feature extractors, whether LLM-derived pathological concepts can replace or augment handcrafted features, and whether the co-learning strategy transfers to multi-class or regression WSI tasks.
