## Problem

Referring expressions in 3D scenes frequently use viewpoint-dependent spatial language (e.g., 'to the right of'), but existing 3D visual grounding datasets lack viewpoint annotations, making such expressions ambiguous. Prior approaches either ignore viewpoint entirely or rely on unsupervised multi-view rendering with soft attention, without explicit supervision for which viewpoint the speaker intended.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242592/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGBXYZ point cloud of a 3D scene and a natural-language referring expression, and produces a 3D bounding box for the target object along with a predicted observer viewpoint (a 2D location on a 10×10 grid and a heading in 10° increments). The predicted viewpoint is used to apply an affine rotation-translation to the point cloud and candidate bounding boxes before the decoder ranks them, and the final bounding box prediction is inverse-transformed back to the original scene coordinates. For ScanRefer, detected bounding boxes from GroupFree serve as candidate inputs; for SR3D and NR3D, ground-truth bounding boxes are provided.

## Method

VPP-Net extends the BUTD-DETR encoder-decoder by inserting two transformer-based viewpoint predictors (location and perspective) into each multimodal encoder layer, where learnable viewpoint embeddings are iteratively updated via cross-attention with visual and textual features and supervised at every layer with binary validity maps from a synthetic dataset. The predicted viewpoint drives an affine transformation of the point cloud and bounding boxes before decoding, reducing spatial-relation ambiguity. A Uniform Object Representation auxiliary loss uses instance-wise learnable vectors to encourage viewpoint-invariant object features, and a classification loss enforces semantic consistency. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: pretraining on 36K synthetic samples with viewpoint annotations (using progressive point dropout to reduce early complexity), then finetuning on the target dataset combined with synthetic data, augmented by rotating each sample by its own predicted transformation.

## Results

VPP-Net achieves state-of-the-art performance on all three 3D visual grounding benchmarks. On ScanRefer, it reaches 55.65% Acc@0.25 and 43.29% Acc@0.50 (Overall), surpassing the strongest BUTD-based baseline EDA by 1.06% and 1.03% respectively. On SR3D and NR3D, VPP-Net achieves 68.7% and 56.9% Acc@0.25IoU, improving over EDA by 0.6% and 2.0% respectively. The gains are most pronounced on view-dependent samples in NR3D, where VPP-Net reaches 52.4% vs. 46.0% for BUTD-DETR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest BUTD-based baseline EDA [35], VPP-Net improves Overall Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer from 54.59% to 55.65% (+1.06%), SR3D from 68.1% to 68.7% (+0.6%), and NR3D from 52.1% to 56.9% (+2.0%). Compared to the backbone BUTD-DETR [17], gains are larger: +3.45% on ScanRefer Overall Acc@0.25 and +1.6% on SR3D. Among multi-view methods, VPP-Net outperforms MVT [16] and ViewRefer [13] across all ScanRefer metrics, and beats M3DRef-CLIP [45] on Multi Acc@0.50 (+2.23%) and Overall Acc@0.25 (+3.75%), though M3DRef-CLIP retains an edge on Unique Acc@0.50 (77.2% vs. 67.09%) due to its use of multi-view rendered images of individual objects. On view-dependent NR3D samples specifically, VPP-Net achieves 52.4% vs. 46.0% for BUTD-DETR and 50.2% for EDA, while maintaining parity on view-independent samples (58.6% vs. 58.0%), confirming that the viewpoint prediction mechanism targets the correct failure mode. The paper notes that the smaller SR3D gain (+0.6%) relative to NR3D (+2.0%) is attributable to a domain gap: SR3D is dominated by proximity relations (81.4% 'Closest/Farthest') that are less viewpoint-sensitive than the relative spatial relations (Left/Right, Front/Behind) prevalent in NR3D and the synthetic training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BUTD-DETR's encoder-decoder architecture for 3D visual grounding, extending it with explicit viewpoint prediction modules in the encoder and an affine transformation step before decoding. It departs from prior multi-view approaches (MVT, ViewRefer) that handle viewpoint ambiguity implicitly through attention over rendered views without supervision, instead introducing a supervised viewpoint estimation task supported by a novel synthetic data generation pipeline. The Uniform Object Representation auxiliary loss and curriculum-based point dropout during pretraining are additional methodological contributions not present in prior 3D grounding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Viewpoint-dependent spatial ambiguity is a fundamental but under-addressed challenge in 3D grounding, and this paper provides a clean, supervised solution where prior work relied on unsupervised multi-view attention. The synthetic data pipeline is a practical contribution that could benefit other tasks requiring viewpoint annotations. However, the template-based expression generation limits diversity and realism, and the approach assumes a single observer viewpoint, leaving open questions about multi-speaker or dynamic viewpoint scenarios. The modest gains (0.6–2.0% across datasets) suggest the technique is helpful but not transformative for the field.
