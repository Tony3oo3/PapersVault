## Problem

Existing NeRV systems suffer from spatial inconsistency in reconstructed videos, where foreground subjects are obscured by high-frequency noise or blurring due to semantic uncertainty. This stems from the absence of global receptive fields and multi-scale information communication in the cascaded upsampling decoders, which only receive features from the immediately preceding layer. The paper also notes that multi-scale design in NeRV has been unguided by theoretical principles due to strict parameter budgets.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video clip (960×1920, 1:2 aspect ratio) and encodes it into two compact embeddings: content embeddings X^C (16×2×4) fed to the mainstream decoder and temporal difference embeddings X^T (2×40×80) fed to shortcut pathways. The decoder (~3M parameters) reconstructs full-resolution video frames, trained per-video with L2 loss over 300 epochs. The model is evaluated on UVG (7 videos, 300–600 frames at 120 FPS) and a 9-video DAVIS subset with large motions and complex spatial details.

## Method

PNeRV introduces a pyramidal decoder built on two novel modules: (1) the Kronecker Fully-connected (KFC) layer, which decomposes a vanilla FC upsampling into two low-rank matrices K₁ and K₂ (K₁ ⊗ K₂ᵀ) to achieve a global receptive field at 0.7% of PixelShuffle's parameter cost, and (2) Benign Selective Memory (BSM), a gated fusion unit inspired by RNNs that processes high-level 'Knowledge' and fine-grained 'Memory' features separately before adaptively merging them via a sigmoid gate. The architecture comprises a 6-layer mainstream of cascaded upsampling blocks plus 5 shortcut pathways that upsample temporal embeddings via KFC and inject them into each mainstream layer through BSM. The paper further provides the first Universal Approximation Theory analysis for NeRV, showing that the cascaded model's parameter upper bound depends on the video's rate of dynamics, and that shortcut pathways reduce the required network width, explaining the pyramidal structure's advantage as an ensemble of pattern-specialized sub-nets.

## Results

On UVG, PNeRV-L (3.3M params) achieves 36.12 dB average PSNR, surpassing the strongest baseline DiffNeRV (35.80 dB) by +0.32 dB and vanilla NeRV (31.63 dB) by +4.49 dB. On DAVIS, PNeRV-L reaches 31.27 dB PSNR / 0.908 MS-SSIM, outperforming DiffNeRV (30.39 dB) by +0.88 dB and NeRV (27.99 dB) by +3.28 dB. In perceptual quality, PNeRV achieves a 231% FVD improvement over NeRV and 64.5% over DiffNeRV on UVG, and a 634% FVD improvement over NeRV and 128% over DiffNeRV on DAVIS, while also attaining the lowest LPIPS (0.163 on UVG, 0.285 on DAVIS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PNeRV-L outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD. Against the closest competitor DiffNeRV [55], PNeRV-L gains +0.32 dB PSNR on UVG and +0.88 dB on DAVIS, while achieving substantially better perceptual quality (64.5% and 128% FVD improvement respectively), indicating that DiffNeRV's temporal-difference embeddings alone cannot resolve spatial inconsistency. Against HNeRV [8], the gain is +3.43 dB on UVG and +1.05 dB on DAVIS, showing that content-aware embeddings benefit significantly from the pyramidal decoder. Against vanilla NeRV [7], the improvement is the largest: +4.49 dB PSNR and 231% FVD on UVG, +3.28 dB and 634% FVD on DAVIS. E-NeRV [24] is surpassed by +4.10 dB on UVG and +1.65 dB on DAVIS. DivNeRV [17] (a shared-model approach) trails PNeRV-L by +0.60 dB on UVG and is notably weaker on videos with complex spatial correlation (e.g., Jockey: 33.93 vs 35.81 dB). The consistent advantage across all baselines confirms that the pyramidal multi-scale structure, rather than the encoder choice alone, is the primary driver of improved spatial consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

PNeRV extends the NeRV line of work (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2021) and its successors HNeRV (CVPR 2023) and DiffNeRV (CVPR 2023), which progressively added content-aware and temporal embeddings but all relied on serial cascaded PixelShuffle decoding. The key departure is borrowing the pyramidal multi-scale fusion paradigm from dense prediction architectures (FPN, UNet, PANet) and adapting it to the extreme upscaling regime of video INR via a novel Kronecker Fully-connected (KFC) layer inspired by LoRA's low-rank decomposition, plus a gated fusion module (BSM) drawn from RNN literature. The paper also introduces Universal Approximation Theory analysis to the NeRV setting for the first time, providing a theoretical lens absent from prior work in this area.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial inconsistency problem (semantic uncertainty causing noisy, blurred reconstructions) is a practical bottleneck for deploying INR-based video coding, and the pyramidal shortcut structure offers a principled fix with strong FVD gains (231% over NeRV, 64–128% over DiffNeRV). The UAT framing formalizes the Implicit Neural Video Coding problem and gives a first upper-bound on minimal parameters, opening a path for more rigorous model design. However, the hierarchical structure increases computational complexity, and the paper acknowledges this as a limitation; whether KFC and BSM generalize beyond the specific video-regression setting (e.g., to 3D reconstruction or other INR tasks) remains an open question.
