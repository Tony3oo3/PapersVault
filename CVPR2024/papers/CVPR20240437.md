## Problem

Existing MIL paradigms in computational pathology rely on offline feature extractors (ResNet-50 or foundation models like PLIP) that cannot be fine-tuned for specific downstream tasks, resulting in low-discriminative instance features. While foundation models improve feature quality, they require >200K WSIs for pre-training and still lack task-specific fine-tuning. The paper addresses this gap by introducing an online feature re-embedding step that enables supervised fine-tuning of instance features within the MIL training loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240437/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted instance features of shape R^{I×D} (where I varies per WSI bag) produced by an offline extractor such as ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 or the PLIP foundation model, rather than raw pixel data. It outputs re-embedded instance representations of the same shape, which are then fed into any standard MIL aggregator and classifier to produce bag-level predictions: binary/multi-class labels for diagnosis and subtyping (evaluated by Accuracy, AUC, F1-score) or survival time estimates (evaluated by C-index). The re-embedding module is a portable plug-in that sits between feature extraction and aggregation, requiring no modification to the downstream MIL architecture.

## Method

R²T (Re-embedded Regional Transformer) re-embeds instance features through two sequential attention stages. First, R-MSA reshapes the I×D feature sequence into a 2D map, partitions it into L×L non-overlapping regions (default L=8), and applies native multi-head self-attention within each region, augmented by an Embedded Position Encoding Generator (EPEG) that adds a lightweight 1D convolution directly to the attention score matrix to encode positional information. Second, CR-MSA aggregates a representative feature per region via learnable attention weights, applies vanilla MSA across the L² region representatives, and distributes the updated cross-region context back to individual instances using MinMax-normalized weights. The entire R²T module is trained end-to-end jointly with the MIL aggregator and classifier, providing supervised feature fine-tuning that the offline extractor cannot offer. By default, R²T is paired with AB-MIL's attention-based aggregation and classifier to form R²T-MIL, but the module is architecture-agnostic and can be inserted into any MIL framework.

## Results

On CAMELYON-16 (C16) with ResNet-50 features, R²T-MIL achieves 92.40% Accuracy, 97.32% AUC, and 90.63% F1-score, surpassing the best baseline (MHIM-MIL) by 0.59%, 1.18%, and 0.69% respectively. With the PLIP foundation model features, R²T-MIL reaches 98.05% AUC on C16 and 93.80% AUC on TCGA-BRCA, outperforming all baselines. Notably, R²T-MIL with ResNet-50 features (97.32% AUC on C16) matches or exceeds AB-MIL with PLIP features (97.30% AUC), demonstrating that online re-embedding can close the gap to foundation model features. On survival prediction (TCGA-LUAD), R²T-MIL achieves 67.19% C-index with ResNet-50, a 3.08% improvement over the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²T-MIL outperforms all seven baselines (AB-MIL, CLAM, DSMIL, TransMIL, DTFD-MIL, IBMIL, MHIM-MIL) on every metric across all datasets. With ResNet-50 features on C16, R²T-MIL achieves 97.32% AUC, beating the strongest baseline MHIM-MIL (96.14%) by 1.18% and AB-MIL (94.54%) by 2.78%. The most striking result is that R²T-MIL with ResNet-50 features (97.32% AUC on C16) surpasses AB-MIL with the much more expensive PLIP foundation model features (97.30% AUC), effectively closing the foundation model gap through online re-embedding alone. With PLIP features, R²T-MIL still provides consistent gains: +0.17% AUC on C16 over TransMIL (97.88%) and +0.63% AUC on BRCA over MHIM-MIL (93.17%). On survival prediction (TCGA-LUAD), the advantage is largest: R²T-MIL reaches 67.19% C-index with ResNet-50, exceeding the second-best TransMIL (64.11%) by 3.08%. The paper also shows R²T-MIL with ResNet-50 features outperforms AB-MIL with PLIP features on BRCA (93.17% vs 91.73% AUC) and NSCLC (96.40% vs 95.68% AUC), further validating that task-specific re-embedding can rival costly foundation model pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard MIL pipeline for whole-slide image analysis (feature extraction → aggregation → classification) by inserting an online re-embedding step between extraction and aggregation, building directly on AB-MIL's attention-based aggregation and the PEG positional encoding from Chu et al. Unlike prior Transformer-based MIL methods (TransMIL, DT-MIL) that focus on designing sophisticated instance aggregators or approximating global self-attention, R²T targets the underexplored re-embedding stage, using regional (local) multi-head self-attention motivated by the spatial locality of tumor regions in WSIs. The two novel components—Cross-region MSA for inter-region fusion and Embedded PEG for lightweight positional encoding—depart from both global-attention MIL approaches and standard conditional positional encodings, positioning the method as a portable plug-in module rather than a monolithic architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a lightweight, end-to-end trainable re-embedding module can close the gap between cheap ResNet-50 features and expensive foundation-model (PLIP) features trained on 200K+ slide-text pairs, offering a far more accessible path to competitive performance without massive pre-training compute. The consistent gains across seven MIL frameworks and six datasets suggest broad applicability, making R²T a potentially widely adopted component in computational pathology pipelines. Open questions include whether the fixed 8×8 regional partitioning generalizes to WSIs with vastly different instance counts, whether the approach transfers to other imaging modalities, and whether deeper or multi-layer R²T stacks yield further improvements or risk overfitting on small pathology datasets.
