## Problem

Existing methods for promptable 3D segmentation in NeRF (SA3D, SAN, ISRF) produce masks with inaccurate boundaries and poor multi-view consistency in complex scenes. SA3D accumulates errors through self-prompting, SAN suffers from aliasing in distilled low-resolution SAM features, and ISRF's DINO-based clustering yields less sharp boundaries. The paper addresses how to achieve high-quality, consistent 3D object segmentation that leverages SAM's zero-shot generalization while resolving cross-view ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained NeRF (providing density and color fields) plus user prompts in the form of 2D/3D points, strokes, or text queries (via Grounding-DINO). The output is an L-dimensional object identity vector defined at every 3D point, rendered via volumetric integration to produce 2D segmentation masks from any camera viewpoint. Unlike single-view methods, the object field is a continuous 3D representation, so masks are inherently consistent across all views and can be queried at arbitrary resolutions.

## Method

SANeRF-HQ comprises three components: a feature container (caching SAM encoder features or distilling them into a neural field), a SAM-style mask decoder that produces per-view 2D masks from prompts, and an object field that aggregates these masks into 3D. The object field adds a view-invariant identity branch to the pre-trained NeRF, rendered with the inherited density via volumetric integration and trained with cross-entropy loss. The key contribution is a Ray-Pair RGB loss that enforces rays with similar rendered colors to receive similar identity predictions, applied only to local high-error regions identified by an evolving error map. This mixed sampling strategy—global cross-entropy plus local RGB consistency—refines boundaries without degrading global segmentation, and the density field from NeRF provides geometric priors that sharpen mask edges.

## Results

SANeRF-HQ achieves the highest mIoU across all five evaluation datasets, with headline values of 91.0 (Mip-NeRF 360), 90.7 (LERF), 95.2 (LLFF), 89.9 (3D-FRONT), and 91.1 (Others). The largest gains appear on LERF (90.7 mIoU vs. 69.3 for SA3D and 27.9 for ISRF) and 3D-FRONT (89.9 vs. 78.7 for SA3D and 68.5 for ISRF), where complex occlusions and semantic ambiguity challenge prior methods. Accuracy scores are consistently above 98.6% across all datasets, with the method reaching 99.6% on the Others set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANeRF-HQ outperforms all four baselines on mIoU across every dataset. Against SA3D [9], the closest 3D competitor, SANeRF-HQ gains +2.2 mIoU on Mip-NeRF 360 (91.0 vs. 88.8) but a much larger +21.4 on LERF (90.7 vs. 69.3) and +11.2 on 3D-FRONT (89.9 vs. 78.7), where SA3D's self-prompting strategy accumulates errors from occluded regions. Against ISRF [21], which lifts DINO features into a neural field, the mIoU gap is dramatic: 90.7 vs. 27.9 on LERF and 91.1 vs. 23.6 on Others, reflecting ISRF's inability to produce sharp boundaries without a powerful decoder. Compared to per-view SAM [31] (no 3D aggregation), SANeRF-HQ improves mIoU by +10.6 on Mip-NeRF 360 (91.0 vs. 80.4) and +12.2 on 3D-FRONT (89.9 vs. 77.7), demonstrating the value of multi-view fusion. Against SAN [13], which distills SAM features into a neural field, SANeRF-HQ leads by +13.8 mIoU on LERF (90.7 vs. 71.0) and +16.9 on 3D-FRONT (89.9 vs. 76.8), attributed to SAN's aliasing from low-resolution feature distillation producing jagged mask edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

SANeRF-HQ sits at the intersection of two recent advances: SAM's promptable 2D segmentation and NeRF's implicit 3D scene representation. Prior NeRF segmentation methods either lift 2D features (DINO, CLIP) into a neural field and cluster them (ISRF, LERF), or propagate a single-view SAM mask via self-prompting (SA3D), or distill SAM features into a field for decoding (SAN). SANeRF-HQ departs from these by directly aggregating multi-view 2D masks into a 3D object field that inherits NeRF's density geometry, and introduces a Ray-Pair RGB loss that enforces boundary consistency through color similarity across rays — a mechanism absent from prior NeRF segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, general pipeline for promptable 3D segmentation that inherits SAM's zero-shot generalizability while solving the multi-view consistency problem that plagues single-view or feature-distillation approaches. Its extensibility to text prompts (via Grounding-DINO), auto-segmentation, and potentially 4D dynamic NeRFs makes it a useful building block for downstream 3D understanding tasks. However, the Ray-Pair RGB loss yields only marginal gains (~0.3–0.6 mIoU), and the method still requires a pre-trained NeRF and per-scene SAM feature computation, limiting its applicability to large-scale or real-time settings. Open questions include whether the object field formulation scales to scenes with many objects and whether the approach transfers to dynamic 4D settings without additional temporal modeling.
