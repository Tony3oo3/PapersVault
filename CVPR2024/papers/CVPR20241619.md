## Problem

Previous iterative deep homography estimation methods face a fundamental trade-off: single-scale iteration suffers from insufficient correlation detail in later iterations, while homography-guided warping (RHWF) requires costly feature re-extraction at every iteration. The paper asks whether accurate iterative refinement can be achieved without repeated feature extraction, and how to further boost convergence-stage training without additional inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241619/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MCNet takes a source image I_S and target image I_T (128×128 in experiments) and outputs a 3×3 homography matrix M, parameterized internally by the translations of four corner points. Feature extraction is performed once across three scales (H/4×W/4, H/2×W/2, H×W) with shared weights, and the iterative correlation searching proceeds from the coarsest to the finest scale with Q=2 iterations per scale. Training data is generated by randomly perturbing the four corner points within [−32, 32] pixels to simulate homography deformation.

## Method

MCNet's core mechanism is multiscale correlation searching: after a single one-pass feature extraction at three resolutions, the network iteratively refines the homography by computing on-the-fly local correlations between source and target feature maps at each scale, starting coarse and progressing to full resolution. Each iteration maps source coordinates to target coordinates via the current homography, computes a local correlation volume of radius r=4, and decodes it through a correlation decoder (stacked 3×3 conv + group norm + ReLU blocks) into a residual four-point translation that is added to the accumulated translation. A novel Fine-Grained Optimization (FGO) loss, defined as an inverse-proportional function of the L1 error, is added during training to selectively amplify backward gradients on well-estimated samples as the model converges, improving final accuracy without any inference-time cost. The multiscale design also implicitly rejects dynamic foreground outliers by progressively refining correlation quality across scales, eliminating the need for explicit mask prediction.

## Results

On MSCOCO, MCNet achieves a MACE of 0.031, reducing the previous SOTA 2-scale RHWF (0.039) by 20.5%, while cutting inference time from 157.1 ms to 33.1 ms (78.9% reduction), FLOPs from 34.74 G to 9.20 G (73.5% reduction), parameters from 1.29 M to 0.85 M (34.1% reduction), and memory from 2.53 GB to 1.69 GB (33.2% reduction). On cross-modal datasets, MCNet reduces MACE by 43.4% (GoogleEarth) and 41.1% (GoogleMap) versus 2-scale RHWF. On the dynamic-object SPID dataset, MCNet reduces MACE by 47.1% compared to 2-scale IHN-mov, achieving 30.2 fps inference with ACE < 0.1 for nearly 100% of MSCOCO test samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCNet outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all four datasets. Against the previous SOTA 2-scale RHWF [4] (CVPR 2023), MCNet achieves 20.5% lower MACE on MSCOCO (0.031 vs 0.039), 43.4% lower on GoogleEarth, and 41.1% lower on GoogleMap, while using 78.9% less inference time and 73.5% fewer FLOPs. Versus 2-scale IHN [3] (CVPR 2022), MCNet reduces MACE from 0.060 to 0.031 on MSCOCO (48.3% reduction) and from 0.048 to a lower value on SPID (47.1% reduction vs IHN-mov). The classical DLKFM [34] (CVPR 2021) is far outperformed with MACE of 0.550 vs 0.031 on MSCOCO. Traditional feature-based methods (SIFT+RANSAC [15], SIFT+MAGSAC [2], LK [1]) and earlier deep methods (DHN [6], CLKN [5], MHN [11], UDHN [31]) are all substantially outperformed, with MHN and UDHN showing particular weakness on the SPID dataset due to dynamic foreground objects. Notably, MCNet achieves superior SPID performance without explicit mask generation, implicitly rejecting outliers through its multiscale correlation searching, whereas IHN-mov [3] requires an additional mask prediction branch at higher computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCNet extends the iterative correlation-searching framework introduced by IHN (Cao et al., CVPR 2022), which replaced hand-crafted IC-LK iterators with a trainable end-to-end architecture. It departs from the subsequent RHWF (Cao et al., CVPR 2023) approach, which achieved higher accuracy through homography-guided image warping and per-iteration feature re-extraction at substantial computational cost. MCNet's key conceptual shift is to perform a single one-pass multiscale feature extraction and then iteratively search correlations across scales, eliminating both the re-extraction overhead and the need for explicit warping, while introducing a Fine-Grained Optimization (FGO) loss that dynamically amplifies gradients for well-estimated samples near convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 78.9% reduction in inference time and 73.5% reduction in FLOPs while simultaneously improving MACE by 20–43% across datasets makes MCNet a practically important option for real-time homography estimation in applications like UAV localization, video stabilization, and image stitching. The finding that multiscale correlation searching implicitly rejects dynamic foreground outliers—outperforming explicit mask-based methods like IHN-mov without any additional modules—suggests that scale diversity in correlation computation may serve as a free form of robustness. Open questions include whether the one-pass multiscale strategy generalizes to larger image resolutions or to non-planar (e.g., 3D) geometric estimation, and whether the FGO loss's hyperparameters (ε, α) transfer robustly across domains.
