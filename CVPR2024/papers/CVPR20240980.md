## Problem

Existing dexterous grasp generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: generative models produce nearly identical grasps for the same input point cloud, while discriminative models predict only a single grasp. When formulating grasp generation as a set-prediction task with a transformer, the Hungarian matching becomes unstable under strong object-penetration penalties, causing model collapse (all queries converge to the same pose) or severe penetration (when the penalty is removed). The paper addresses how to stably train a set-prediction model that simultaneously achieves high grasp quality and diversity in one forward pass.

## Input / Output

The model takes a complete object point cloud O ∈ R^{M×3} as input and outputs an unordered set of N=16 dexterous grasp poses in a single forward pass, where each pose comprises a global rotation (unit quaternion in SO(3)), a translation vector in R³, and 22 joint angles for the ShadowHand. No data preprocessing (e.g., rotating the point cloud) is required to obtain diversity; all N grasps are predicted in parallel from the same input. At test time, an optional AB-TTA refinement step adjusts the hand parameters in-place without requiring 3D mesh information or force analysis.

## Method

DGTR uses a PointNet++ encoder to extract object features, a transformer decoder with N learnable grasp queries to produce grasp features, and three MLP heads to predict rotation, translation, and joint angles. The core training innovation is Dynamic-Static Matching Training (DSMT), a three-stage procedure: (1) DMT trains with Hungarian matching and no penetration loss to let queries learn diverse patterns; (2) SMW freezes the matching to static assignments and fine-tunes without penetration loss; (3) SMPT adds the object-penetration loss under the same static matching to reduce penetration without destabilizing optimization. At test time, Adversarial-Balanced Test-Time Adaptation (AB-TTA) refines each grasp by jointly minimizing object penetration, a generalized tta-distance loss (which tracks initially-contacting keypoints to prevent the hand from drifting away), and self-penetration, while downscaling the root-link translation gradient to constrain over-optimization.

## Results

On DexGraspNet, DGTR achieves a mean Q₁ of 0.0515, torque balance ratio of 69.62%, and grasping success rate of 41.0% in a single forward pass, outperforming all generative baselines on quality metrics. For diversity, DGTR reaches 47.77% translation occupancy (δ_t) and 51.66% rotation occupancy (δ_r), substantially exceeding UniDexGrasp (9.64%, 7.49%) and GraspTTA (8.09%, 7.53%). The practical variant DGTR* (top-4 selection by contact points and penetration) achieves Q₁=0.0921, η_np=99.51%, and η_success=66.6%, approaching the quality of DDG (Q₁=0.0582, η_success=67.5%) while retaining multi-grasp capability. Inference time is 20.4 ms for 16 grasps, compared to 530.6 ms for 16 forward passes of UniDexGrasp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGTR outperforms UniDexGrasp [41] on Q₁ (0.0515 vs 0.0462), torque balance ratio (69.62% vs 50.94%), and success rate (41.0% vs 37.1%), while UniDexGrasp retains an advantage in non-penetration ratio (97.29% vs 75.78%) and maximal penetration depth (0.121 vs 0.421 cm). Against GraspTTA [12], DGTR dominates across nearly all metrics: Q₁ is nearly 2× higher (0.0515 vs 0.0271), torque balance ratio is 4.4× higher (69.62% vs 15.90%), and diversity in translation and rotation occupancy is ~5–7× greater. SceneDiffuser [11] achieves slightly higher diversity (δ_t=54.84%, δ_r=52.27%) but at the cost of substantially lower quality (Q₁=0.0129, η_tb=22.88%), making it impractical for stable grasping. The top-4 selection variant DGTR* reaches Q₁=0.0921 and η_success=66.6%, matching or exceeding DDG [19] (Q₁=0.0582, η_success=67.5%) on quality while producing multiple diverse grasps in one pass. In the multi-pass comparison (Table 2), DGTR in one forward pass (20.4 ms) surpasses UniDexGrasp with 16 passes (530.6 ms) on both δ_t (47.77% vs 25.04%) and δ_r (51.66% vs 44.31%) while being ~26× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGTR adapts the DETR set-prediction paradigm (Carion et al., ECCV 2020; Misra et al., ICCV 2021) to dexterous grasp generation, replacing the generative modeling approaches (CVAE, normalizing flows, diffusion) that dominate prior work like GraspTTA, UniDexGrasp, and SceneDiffuser. Rather than learning a conditional distribution over grasps or predicting a single pose, DGTR uses learnable queries in a transformer decoder to predict an unordered set of grasp poses in parallel. The paper's key methodological departure is identifying that the standard Hungarian matching + penetration loss combination causes model collapse in this set-prediction setting, and addressing it with progressive dynamic-static matching training and adversarial-balanced test-time adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DGTR is the first framework to generate diverse dexterous grasp poses from a single forward pass without rotating the input point cloud, which has practical implications for real-time robotic manipulation where repeated inference is costly. The diagnosis of Hungarian matching instability as the root cause of the quality-diversity tradeoff in set-prediction grasping is a useful insight for anyone applying DETR-style architectures to multi-target regression tasks. Limitations include evaluation only on DexGraspNet with ShadowHand, the need for test-time adaptation to reach competitive quality, and the fact that the diversity metrics (δt, δr, δq) are newly proposed and not yet standard. Open questions include generalization to different hand morphologies, task-oriented grasp selection, and real-world deployment.
