## Problem

Transformers used for deformable medical image registration are computationally expensive and typically operate at downsampled resolutions, preventing them from capturing fine-grained long-range dependence at full image resolution where precise pixel-wise correspondence is critical. MLPs are efficient enough for full-resolution processing but have not been explored for registration and lack the inductive biases—local correlation modeling and coarse-to-fine strategy—that are essential for medical registration tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241966/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D single-channel grayscale medical images (moving and fixed) and outputs a dense displacement field parameterizing a nonlinear spatial transformation. Brain images are 144×192×160 voxels at 1 mm isotropic spacing; cardiac images are 128×128×32 voxels at 1.5×1.5×3.15 mm spacing. Training is fully unsupervised, using negative local normalized cross-correlation (NCC) as the similarity loss and a diffusion regularizer on the displacement field, with no ground-truth deformation labels required.

## Method

CorrMLP combines a CNN-based hierarchical encoder (four 3×3×3 conv modules with max-pooling producing 4-level feature pyramids) with a correlation-aware coarse-to-fine decoder that performs four registration steps in a single forward pass. The core building block is the CMW-MLP, which first computes a 3D local correlation map (d=3, 27 channels) between two feature inputs, then processes the concatenated features through three parallel window-based gMLP branches (3×3×3, 5×5×5, 7×7×7) fused by channel-wise weighted summation, followed by a residual channel attention module. The coarse-to-fine decoder leverages both image-level correlations (correlation maps between warped and fixed features at each step) and step-level correlations (features from the previous registration step fed alongside upsampled features), enabling each step to be informed by what prior steps have already aligned. This design captures multi-range local dependence at full resolution without self-attention, addressing both large and small deformations simultaneously.

## Results

On 3D brain image registration, CorrMLP achieves DSC of 0.642 (Mindboggle) and 0.661 (Buckner) with NJD of 1.821% and 1.788%, outperforming the best baseline NICE-Trans (DSC 0.625/0.649, NJD 2.324%/2.277%) by 1.7 and 1.2 DSC points respectively. On 4D cardiac image registration (ACDC), CorrMLP reaches DSC 0.810 with NJD 0.389%, surpassing NICE-Trans (DSC 0.799, NJD 0.473%) and MAXIM×3 (DSC 0.788, NJD 0.716%). All improvements over baselines are statistically significant (P<0.05), and CorrMLP maintains the lowest NJD among all methods, indicating superior transformation smoothness without sacrificing accuracy. GPU inference time is 0.49 s per brain image pair and 0.07 s per cardiac pair, comparable to other deep registration methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorrMLP outperforms all 13 baselines on both DSC and NJD across brain and cardiac registration, with all differences statistically significant (P<0.05). The closest competitor is NICE-Trans (transformer-based coarse-to-fine), which CorrMLP beats by 1.7 DSC points on Mindboggle (0.642 vs. 0.625) and 1.2 on Buckner (0.661 vs. 0.649), while also achieving substantially lower NJD (1.821% vs. 2.324%). On cardiac registration, CorrMLP leads NICE-Trans by 1.1 DSC points (0.810 vs. 0.799) and 8.4% relative NJD reduction (0.389% vs. 0.473%). The MLP-based MAXIM (adapted for registration) achieves DSC 0.788 on ACDC but lags CorrMLP by 2.2 points and has much higher NJD (0.716%), confirming that generic MLP architectures lack the inductive bias needed for medical registration. Traditional methods (SyN, NiftyReg) are 10–60× slower on CPU and achieve DSC 0.534–0.611, far below all deep methods. Among CNN-based coarse-to-fine methods, NICE-Net (DSC 0.618/0.643) and ULAE-net (0.610/0.640) are the strongest, yet CorrMLP still exceeds them by 2.4 and 3.2 DSC points on Mindboggle respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

CorrMLP is the first MLP-based coarse-to-fine deformable registration network, combining three previously separate threads: the gMLP spatial-mixing mechanism, the 3D correlation layer from Dual-PRNet++, and the non-iterative coarse-to-fine architecture of NICE-Trans. Prior registration work relied on CNNs (VoxelMorph, NICE-Net) or transformers (TransMorph, NICE-Trans) for long-range dependence, while MLPs had only been explored in natural image processing (MAXIM) and one preliminary 2D echocardiography study. The key departure is that CorrMLP operates MLP blocks at full image resolution—something transformers cannot do due to self-attention cost—and introduces step-level correlation as a new form of inductive bias for multi-step registration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper demonstrates that the computational bottleneck of self-attention, which forced prior transformer-based registration methods to downsample features, can be bypassed with MLPs, enabling fine-grained long-range modelling at full resolution that is critical for precise pixel-wise correspondence in medical images. The ablation showing that a simple Unet-like MLP baseline (MLPMorph) already substantially outperforms TransMorph suggests the architectural choice (full-resolution MLP vs. downsampled transformer + CNN) may matter more than the specific block design, a finding that could reshape how registration architectures are designed. Open questions include whether the correlation-aware multi-window design generalizes to multi-modal registration (e.g., MRI-CT), 2D tasks, or other dense prediction problems, and whether the step-level correlation insight transfers to other iterative or multi-stage learning settings.
