## Problem

MLP-based neural radiance fields (NeRF) used for animatable human avatars suffer from low-frequency spectral bias, making it difficult to regress pose-dependent garment details such as wrinkles, logos, and fine textural patterns. While 3D Gaussian splatting offers an explicit, efficient alternative, no prior work has combined it with powerful 2D CNNs in a template-guided parameterization suitable for animatable full-body avatars with loose clothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB videos (24 views from THuman4.0, 47 full-body views from ActorsHQ, or 16 views from AvatarReX) along with per-frame SMPL-X pose and shared shape registrations as input. It requires at least one near A-pose frame for canonical template reconstruction. The output is an animatable avatar represented as two front-and-back Gaussian maps (each pixel encoding a 3D Gaussian's position, covariance, opacity, and color), which can be deformed to arbitrary poses via LBS and rendered at 1024×1024 resolution at 10 FPS on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

The method first reconstructs a character-specific parametric template by optimizing a canonical SDF and color field via SDF-based volume rendering, then diffusing SMPL skinning weights to the template surface to handle loose garments like dresses. 3D Gaussians are parameterized onto front and back canonical views via orthogonal projection, producing two Gaussian maps where each pixel within the template mask encodes one 3D Gaussian. A StyleUNet (StyleGAN-based conditional generator) predicts pose-dependent Gaussian maps from posed position maps (obtained by LBS-deforming the template and orthogonally projecting vertex coordinates), with a view-direction map modulating color for view-dependent effects. Canonical Gaussians are deformed to the posed space via LBS on their position and covariance, then rendered through differentiable splatting. For novel poses, a PCA-based pose projection clips driving position-map features to ±2σ of training principal components, ensuring the network receives in-distribution pose conditions.

## Results

On the THuman4.0 body-only benchmark (subject00), Animatable Gaussians achieves PSNR 28.07, SSIM 0.9739, LPIPS 0.0515, and FID 29.48, outperforming all four NeRF-based baselines. On the full-body AvatarReX benchmark, the method reaches PSNR 30.61, SSIM 0.9803, LPIPS 0.0290, and FID 13.24, substantially surpassing AvatarReX (PSNR 23.25, FID 31.14). In terms of animation speed, the method renders at 10 FPS in PyTorch, which is 50× faster than PoseVocab (0.20 FPS) and 167× faster than TAVA (0.003 FPS), though slightly slower than AvatarReX with TensorRT optimization (25 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Animatable Gaussians wins across all four metrics against every baseline. Against the closest body-only competitor PoseVocab (PSNR 26.38), the method gains +1.69 PSNR and nearly halves the FID (29.48 vs. 49.45), with the gap widening further against SLRF (PSNR 26.90, FID 52.06). The largest margins are against ARAH (PSNR 22.30, FID 90.61) and TAVA (PSNR 26.80, FID 96.35), where the FID gap exceeds 60 points, reflecting the low-frequency bias of MLP-based NeRF representations. In the full-body comparison, the advantage over AvatarReX is even more pronounced: +7.37 PSNR, +0.024 SSIM, and a 2.4× lower FID (13.24 vs. 31.14), attributed to the character-specific template modeling garment shape rather than sparse node translations on a naked SMPL body. On animation speed, the method (10 FPS PyTorch) is dramatically faster than all NeRF-based baselines (0.003–0.20 FPS) but trails AvatarReX's TensorRT-optimized pipeline (25 FPS), though it matches AvatarReX's PyTorch speed (0.03 FPS) by a factor of 333×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent trends: 3D Gaussian splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as an efficient explicit rendering primitive, and point-based clothed human modeling (Lin et al., 2022) which learns character-specific templates with diffused skinning weights. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based avatar line (Animatable NeRF, Neural Actor, SLRF, TAVA, ARAH) by replacing coordinate-based MLPs with a StyleGAN-derived 2D CNN (StyleUNet), enabled by a template-guided orthogonal projection that parameterizes 3D Gaussians onto front and back 2D maps. The key conceptual move is recognizing that explicit point-based representations can be naturally mapped to 2D pixel grids, thereby unlocking the representational power of 2D convolutional networks that were previously inaccessible to implicit NeRF formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing low-frequency bias of MLPs is a fundamental bottleneck for high-fidelity avatar dynamics, and that 2D CNNs on parameterized Gaussian maps resolve it convincingly. The 50–3000× speedup over NeRF-based methods (10 FPS vs. 0.003–0.20 FPS) makes real-time avatar animation practical, which is significant for applications in virtual production, gaming, and holoportation. However, the method entangles body and clothing geometry, precluding virtual try-on, and requires multi-view capture for template reconstruction, limiting monocular deployment. Open questions include whether the front/back parameterization can be extended to handle occluded regions more robustly, whether the PCA pose projection generalizes beyond the training distribution without quality degradation, and whether multi-layer Gaussians can cleanly separate body from garments.
