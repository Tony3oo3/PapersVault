## Problem

Existing Video LLMs (e.g., VideoChat, VideoLLaMA, VideoChatGPT) can only produce coarse, whole-video descriptions and fail to capture precise start/end time boundaries of specific events. This limitation stems from two challenges: the scarcity of large-scale video datasets with accurate temporal boundary annotations, and the lack of effective training tasks that teach LLMs to reason about multiple moments within a single video.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video uniformly sampled to exactly 100 frames (first and last frames always included) and a text query containing a special `<video>` token. Each frame is encoded independently by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14, and only the CLS token is projected via a linear layer into the LLM's embedding space—no explicit temporal modeling is applied to the frame sequence. Output is structured as natural language with temporal boundaries expressed in the format 'from s to e' where s and e are frame indices from 00 to 99, enabling both temporal grounding (given a description, predict boundaries) and dense captioning (given boundaries, generate descriptions).

## Method

VTimeLLM combines a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder, a linear visual adapter, and a Vicuna LLM (7B/13B), with the key architectural choice of leaving temporal modeling entirely to the LLM's sequential processing rather than adding explicit temporal modules. The core contribution is a boundary-aware three-stage training strategy: Stage 1 aligns visual features with LLM semantics using image-text pairs (LCS-558K) rather than video-text data, reducing information loss; Stage 2 introduces a dedicated boundary perception phase using 134k multi-event videos from InternVid-10M-FLT, where events are converted into single-turn (dense captioning) and multi-turn (segment captioning + temporal grounding) QA dialogues via 10 templates per task; Stage 3 performs instruction tuning on ~36k high-quality dialogues derived from manually annotated ActivityNet Captions and DiDeMo subsets, generated by prompting an LLM to produce natural conversations. LoRA (r=64, alpha=128) is used for parameter-efficient fine-tuning in Stages 2 and 3, with the visual adapter frozen after Stage 1 and a fresh LoRA module added in Stage 3 to preserve temporal understanding while regaining general dialogue ability.

## Results

On Temporal Video Grounding, VTimeLLM-7B achieves mIoU of 30.4 on ActivityNet and 31.2 on Charades-STA, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline VideoChatGPT-7B (18.9 and 13.7 mIoU respectively). On Dense Video Captioning (ActivityNet), VTimeLLM-7B reaches CIDEr 27.6 and SODA_c 5.8, compared to 5.8 and 1.9 for VideoChatGPT-7B. On the Video-ChatGPT dialogue benchmark, VTimeLLM attains a mean score of 2.85, surpassing the next-best BT-Adapter (2.69) by 0.16 points, with the largest gain in Detail Orientation (+0.41 over BT-Adapter).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTimeLLM-7B decisively outperforms all three same-size Video LLM baselines across every metric. Against the strongest competitor VideoChatGPT-7B, VTimeLLM-7B improves ActivityNet mIoU from 18.9 to 30.4 (+11.5) and Charades-STA mIoU from 13.7 to 31.2 (+17.5); on dense captioning, CIDEr jumps from 5.8 to 27.6 (nearly 5×). VideoChat-7B and VideoLLaMA-7B are far behind, with ActivityNet mIoU of only 7.2 and 6.5 respectively, attributed to their use of just 8 sampled frames. On the Video-ChatGPT dialogue benchmark, VTimeLLM (mean 2.85) beats BT-Adapter (2.69), VideoChatGPT (2.38), VideoChat (2.29), LLaMA-Adapter (2.16), and VideoLLaMA (1.98), with the largest margin in Detail Orientation (3.10 vs. 2.69 for BT-Adapter). Scaling to 13B parameters yields modest gains on ActivityNet but a notable improvement on Charades-STA (mIoU 31.2→34.6), suggesting better out-of-distribution generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

VTimeLLM extends the two-stage Video LLM paradigm (alignment + instruction tuning) used by VideoChat, Video-LLaMA, and Video-ChatGPT by inserting an intermediate 'boundary perception' stage that explicitly trains the model to associate events with temporal boundaries. It repurposes the standard CLIP ViT encoder and Vicuna LLM backbone but introduces a novel three-stage curriculum: image-text alignment, multi-event QA on InternVid-10M-FLT, and high-quality instruction tuning on manually annotated ActivityNet/DiDeMo subsets. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between fine-grained video understanding methods (proposal-based grounding, dense captioning pipelines) and the emerging Video LLM framework, unifying temporal grounding and dense captioning as natural language generation tasks within a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Video LLM explicitly designed for fine-grained temporal boundary prediction, addressing a clear limitation of prior Video LLMs that could only produce coarse, whole-video descriptions. The three-stage training strategy is a practical recipe that practitioners can adopt, and the finding that image-only alignment outperforms video-based alignment for the adapter is a useful design insight. However, absolute performance remains modest (mIoU ~30 on ActivityNet), the model is limited to 100 frames and non-overlapping events, and the reliance on noisy automated annotations from InternVid constrains ceiling performance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos, overlapping events, and whether the boundary-aware stage can be replaced by more principled temporal tokenization.
