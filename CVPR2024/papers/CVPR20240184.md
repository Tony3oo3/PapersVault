## Problem

Existing Transformer-based deformable registration methods rely on self-attention at individual scale levels, which is insufficient to accurately represent the deformation field because abstract flow patterns encoded in high-level feature maps are lost when reconstructing lower-scale representations. No prior method propagates long-range flow information hierarchically across the deformation pyramid, leaving a gap in capturing the full spectrum of deformation patterns needed for accurate registration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D T1-weighted MRI volumes (moving and target) as input, which are stacked channel-wise and processed through a CNN backbone to produce a pyramid of S feature maps at progressively coarser resolutions. The output is a continuous diffeomorphic deformation field computed by integrating a stationary velocity field via scaling and squaring, which is then applied through a spatial transformer to warp the moving image into the target space (and vice versa for symmetric loss computation). The deformation field is defined over 3D spatial dimensions with voxel-level resolution matching the input MRI grid.

## Method

H-ViT introduces a hierarchical dual-attention mechanism applied top-down over a CNN backbone's feature pyramid. Self-attention within each scale level captures short-range flow dependencies among local voxel patch embeddings, while a sequence of cross-attention layers uses the same queries to attend to key-value tokens from progressively higher-level feature maps, enabling long-range flow information to propagate across all scales. The cross-attention is applied recursively: for scale s, it attends to scales s−1, s−2, …, 1, so that abstract patterns from the coarsest feature map inform the finest-scale representation. Training is fully unsupervised, optimizing a symmetric similarity loss between warped images plus a smoothness regularization on the deformation field's spatial gradient, with the diffeomorphic guarantee provided by the stationary velocity field formulation and scaling-and-squaring integration.

## Results

On the OASIS dataset (35 anatomical structures, inter-patient), H-ViT achieves the highest Dice score of 0.876 and the lowest HD95 of 1.301, outperforming TransMorph (Dice 0.862) and VoxelMorph (Dice 0.847). On the IXI dataset (30 structures), H-ViT attains 0.810 Dice for inter-patient and 0.797 for patient-to-atlas registration, surpassing the next-best TransMorph-Bspl by +0.017 and +0.019 respectively. Across all five datasets (OASIS, IXI, ADNI, LPBA, Mindboggle), H-ViT consistently achieves the highest Dice scores, with margins of +0.028 to +0.034 over the second-best method in inter-patient registration. The paper reports an average Dice improvement of approximately 2.5% over state-of-the-art methods under identical training conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-ViT outperforms all CNN-based baselines (VoxelMorph, MIDIR, CycleMorph) by a substantial margin of +0.04 to +0.07 Dice on IXI inter-patient registration, confirming that the dual-attention mechanism captures deformation patterns beyond what convolutional receptive fields can represent. Among Transformer-based methods, H-ViT beats TransMorph-Bspl (the strongest baseline) by +0.017 Dice inter-patient and +0.019 patient-to-atlas on IXI, with similar or larger gaps on ADNI (+0.030), LPBA (+0.034), and Mindboggle (+0.031). ViT-V-Net, which uses only self-attention, trails H-ViT by +0.038 Dice on IXI, supporting the paper's claim that self-attention alone is insufficient for accurate deformation field representation. The only metric where H-ViT does not lead is the Jacobian determinant (folded voxels): TransMorph-Bspl achieves <0.001% while H-ViT reports 0.525%, though the authors note this is within an acceptable range. On OASIS, H-ViT also achieves the best HD95 (1.301 vs. 1.431 for TransMorph) but the worst SDlogJ (0.539 vs. 0.067 for ConvexAdam), suggesting a trade-off between registration accuracy and smoothness of the deformation field. Overall, H-ViT wins on the primary accuracy metric (Dice) across all five datasets and all 11 compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-ViT builds directly on the VoxelMorph framework, retaining its CNN backbone, diffeomorphic deformation parameterization via stationary velocity fields, and spatial transformation function. It departs from prior Transformer-based registration methods (TransMorph, ViT-V-Net) that rely solely on self-attention within a single scale level, arguing that this is insufficient for accurately representing deformation fields. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a hierarchical cross-attention mechanism that propagates abstract flow patterns from higher-level feature maps down to lower scales, which the authors claim is the first such dual-attention system applied to deformation field prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent ~2.5% Dice improvement across five public MRI datasets under identical training conditions provides strong evidence that hierarchical cross-attention is a meaningful architectural addition for deformable registration. The mechanism's benefits for translation invariance and handling variable-sized 3D inputs suggest broader applicability beyond the specific datasets tested. However, the notably degraded SDlogJ score on OASIS (0.539 vs. 0.067–0.133 for competitors) raises questions about deformation smoothness that the paper does not adequately address. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal registration, whether the cross-attention depth can be reduced without performance loss, and whether the gains persist at larger 3D volumes where computational cost becomes prohibitive.
