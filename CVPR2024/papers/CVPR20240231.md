## Problem

Current 2D image editing methods—whether text-prompt-based (Prompt-to-Prompt, InstructPix2Pix) or interactive latent-space dragging (DragGAN, DragDiffusion)—cannot perform precise, quantifiable, and physically-plausible object manipulations such as rotating an object by a specific angle or repositioning it in 3D space. These approaches are confined to a flat plane, lack a third dimension for handling occlusions and spatial composition, and have no physics-awareness, making them fundamentally unable to fulfill detailed geometric instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240231/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image containing a target object and an interactive 3D manipulation signal (the user deforms a skeleton, cage, or handle points on the reconstructed mesh). The object is first segmented via SAM, reconstructed as a textured mesh, and then the user's 3D deformation is applied. The output is a high-fidelity 2D image where the edited object is seamlessly blended into the original background, preserving both the new geometry and the original textural appearance. The pipeline supports six editing operations: pose editing, rotation, translation, 3D composition, carving, and serial addition.

## Method

The Image Sculpting pipeline has three phases. First, a zero-shot NeRF is trained via Score Distillation Sampling (Zero-1-to-3) on the SAM-segmented object, then converted to a textured mesh using Marching Cubes and differentiable rendering (threeestudio). Second, the user interactively deforms the mesh using established geometric tools—linear blend skinning, ARAP, or cage-based space deformation—by manipulating a skeleton or control points. Third, a coarse-to-fine generative enhancement restores visual quality: a one-shot DreamBooth (LoRA fine-tuning on SDXL for 800 steps) captures the object's texture, depth ControlNet from the deformed 3D model preserves geometry, and feature/attention injection (DDIM inversion of the coarse rendering with self-attention and residual feature maps from the original image's denoising) enforces geometric consistency, while a background blend-in step masks and composites the original background at each denoising step.

## Results

On the newly introduced SculptingBench (28 images across six editing categories), the full Image Sculpting pipeline achieves a DINO texture-fidelity score of 0.853 and a geometric-fidelity D-RMSE of 1.99. The unenhanced coarse rendering scores only 0.758 on DINO, while SDEdit (t₀=0.6) reaches 0.800 DINO but with a higher D-RMSE of 2.12. Removing either feature injection or depth control from the full pipeline degrades DINO to 0.848/0.851 and raises D-RMSE to 2.33/2.15, confirming both components contribute to the final quality.

**Table 1: Ablation studies of the enhancement methods on SculptingBench. DINO measures textural detail preservation; D-RMSE measures geometric fidelity (lower is better).**

| Method | DINO ↑ | D-RMSE ↓ |
|---|---|---|
| Original Coarse Rendering | 0.758 | 0.00 |
| SDEdit (t₀=0.4) | 0.788 | 1.71 |
| SDEdit (t₀=0.6) | 0.800 | 2.12 |
| Ours w/o Feature Injection | 0.848 | 2.33 |
| Ours w/o Depth Control | 0.851 | 2.15 |
| **Ours (full)** | **0.853** | **1.99** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons with external baselines are primarily qualitative (visual), as no head-to-head quantitative benchmark against external methods is reported. Against OBJect-3DIT (Fig. 9), Image Sculpting produces more faithful translations, rotations, and compositions on real-world images, whereas OBJect-3DIT degrades due to its reliance on a synthetic training set. Against DragDiffusion and ControlNet (Fig. 10), the proposed 3D-based pose editing handles complex out-of-plane articulations that these 2D-constrained methods cannot reproduce. Against InstructPix2Pix and DALL-E 3 (Fig. 11), Image Sculpting succeeds at precise, quantifiable serial-addition instructions (e.g., adding objects at specific positions) where text-prompt-based methods fail to follow exact spatial specifications. Quantitatively, the full pipeline outperforms SDEdit on DINO by 0.053 (0.853 vs 0.800) while keeping D-RMSE comparable (1.99 vs 2.12), demonstrating a better texture–geometry trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established research threads—single-view 3D reconstruction (Zero-1-to-3, SDS/NeRF), classical 3D shape deformation (ARAP, linear blend skinning, cage-based warping), and diffusion-based image enhancement (DreamBooth, ControlNet, Plug-and-Play feature injection)—into a unified pipeline for precise 2D image editing. It departs from prior 2D interactive editing methods (DragGAN, DragDiffusion) that operate in latent space and cannot express out-of-plane transformations, and from 3D-aware language-guided editing (OBJect-3DIT) that relies on synthetic training data. The key conceptual contribution is the coarse-to-fine enhancement stage, which repurposes feature/attention injection (originally for semantic layout control) to preserve user-edited geometry while a one-shot DreamBooth model restores texture fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that precise, quantifiable 3D edits (e.g., 'rotate 42°', 'translate 5 cm') are achievable from a single 2D image, closing a controllability gap that text-prompt and 2D-drag methods cannot address. However, the pipeline's practicality is constrained by the coarse quality of current single-view 3D reconstruction, the need for manual skeleton construction, and the multi-stage enhancement process (DreamBooth fine-tuning + DDIM inversion + feature injection + depth ControlNet + background inpainting). Open questions include automating rigging/skeleton creation, handling complex articulated objects, and whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes without per-object reconstruction. The introduced SculptingBench provides a useful evaluation substrate for future precise-editing methods.
