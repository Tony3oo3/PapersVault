## Problem

In box-supervised 3D instance segmentation, overlapping bounding boxes create ambiguous point assignments where instance labels are undetermined. Existing methods handle this ambiguity with either local heuristics (WISGP) or global Gaussian Process fitting (GaPro), but none effectively integrates both local and global structure modeling, and none leverages a learned pseudo-labeler with online knowledge transfer to iteratively refine overlapping-area predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240132/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds with per-point color (x,y,z,r,g,b) and object-level 3D bounding box annotations (center + dimensions) as the sole supervision signal. The pseudo-labeler SAFormer outputs soft confidence values in [0,1] for each point in overlapping regions, which are combined with the deterministic labels from non-overlapping regions to form complete pseudo instance masks. These soft masks then supervise a downstream 3DIS network (e.g., ISBNet or SPFormer) that produces final per-point instance masks and category predictions.

## Method

BSNet is a two-step framework whose core is a pseudo-labeler called SAFormer, built on a lightweight sparse-convolution 3D-UNet backbone and a Local-Global Aware Attention (LGA) decoder. LGA initializes two learnable queries (one per foreground instance), applies self-attention separately within each instance's non-overlapping superpoints to capture local structure, then applies a second self-attention over all superpoints (including the overlapping region) to model global inter-instance and inter-region relationships; overlapping-area masks are obtained by dot-producting overlapping superpoint features against the two queries. Training follows a Simulation-assisted Mean Teacher (SMT) strategy: simulated overlapping samples are first generated by statistically sampling class pairs and inter-object distances from real data, then physically constrained (gravity, collision) to produce realistic pairs; a base labeler is trained on these simulated samples and its weights initialize both teacher and student. During fine-tuning on real data, the student receives ground-truth supervision on non-overlapping areas and high-confidence teacher pseudo-labels (threshold τ=0.9) on overlapping areas, while the teacher updates via EMA, enabling stable online pseudo-label refinement without iterative retraining.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 validation, BSNet+SPFormer achieves 53.3 mAP, 72.7 AP@50, and 83.4 AP@25, reaching 94.7% of the fully supervised SPFormer (56.3 mAP). On S3DIS Area 5, BSNet+ISBNet achieves 53.0 mAP and 64.3 AP@50, reaching 98.1% of fully supervised ISBNet (54.0 mAP). The method outperforms the previous best box-supervised approach (GaPro+SPFormer at 51.1 mAP on ScanNetV2) by 2.2 mAP, and reduces training time from 80 hours (GaPro+SPFormer) to 37 hours while using only 2.4M parameters for the pseudo-labeler (13.6% of the 3DIS network).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSNet outperforms all box-supervised baselines on both datasets. On ScanNetV2, it beats the strongest prior method GaPro+SPFormer (51.1 mAP) by 2.2 mAP and GaPro+ISBNet (50.6 mAP) by 2.2 mAP, while also surpassing WISGP+SSTNet (35.2 mAP) by 18.1 mAP and Box2Mask stand-alone (39.1 mAP) by 14.2 mAP. On S3DIS, BSNet+ISBNet (53.0 mAP) exceeds GaPro+ISBNet (50.5 mAP) by 2.5 mAP and GaPro+SoftGroup (47.0 mAP) by 6.0 mAP. Against fully supervised references, BSNet+SPFormer reaches 94.7% of SPFormer's mAP and BSNet+ISBNet reaches 96.9% of ISBNet's mAP on ScanNetV2, and 98.1% of ISBNet's mAP on S3DIS — a notable improvement over prior box-supervised methods that typically achieve ~90–93%. The point-supervised baselines (CSC at 15.9 mAP, PointContrast at 27.8 mAP) are substantially outperformed, confirming that bbox annotations provide richer supervision than sparse point labels. In terms of efficiency, BSNet's pseudo-labeler uses only 2.4M parameters (7.8–13.6% of the 3DIS network) and completes training in 37–72 hours versus 80–150 hours for GaPro-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the box-supervised 3D instance segmentation line initiated by Box2Mask, WISGP, and GaPro, which addressed overlapping-area ambiguity through heuristics or Gaussian Process regression. It departs from these by introducing the Mean Teacher paradigm (previously applied to domain adaptation and semi-supervised detection) into this task for the first time, and by pre-training the pseudo-labeler on simulated overlapping samples constructed from non-overlapping bboxes. The Local-Global Aware Attention decoder builds on the superpoint-level feature aggregation of SPFormer and the mask prediction losses of Mask3D, but adds a dedicated two-query attention mechanism that explicitly separates local-structure modeling within each instance from global-context modeling across instances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving ~95% of fully supervised mAP with only box annotations (vs. ~90% for prior methods) narrows the annotation-cost gap meaningfully, and the simulated-sample pre-training reduces S3DIS training from 42 hours to 1.7 hours, making the pipeline far more accessible. The lightweight pseudo-labeler (~10% of the 3DIS network's parameters) is a practical design choice. Open questions include whether the simulated-sample distribution adequately captures real-world overlap complexity (e.g., more than two objects, irregular geometries), and whether the Mean Teacher approach generalizes to other weakly supervised 3D tasks beyond instance segmentation.
