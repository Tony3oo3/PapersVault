## Problem

Neural SDF-based mesh extraction for large unbounded scenes produces meshes with prohibitive triangle counts (e.g., BakedSDF requires ~73× more triangles than practical for mobile deployment), while existing lightweight mesh methods (MobileNeRF, NeRF2Mesh) sacrifice geometric accuracy or produce noisy, bumpy surfaces. The paper addresses the gap of achieving both high-fidelity geometry and compact storage suitable for real-time rasterization on mobile hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240864/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses as input and produces a lightweight textured 3D mesh (exported as .obj/.mtl with 3-channel PNG texture maps) plus a decomposed appearance field consisting of two shallow MLPs for diffuse and specular colors, designed to function as a fragment shader in a standard rasterization pipeline. The mesh is defined in a contracted space (per mip-NeRF 360) and the appearance field is trained to be geometry-agnostic, consuming only surface intersection points and viewing direction rather than full ray-marched accumulations.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) Initialization trains a neural SDF (BakedSDF-style with Laplace CDF density and Eikonal loss) and a hash-encoded appearance field (two shallow MLPs for diffuse/specular) via volumetric rendering; (2) Mesh Processing extracts geometry via Marching Cubes at adaptive resolutions (2048³ for central, 1024³ for background regions) and applies a modified QSlim decimation that incorporates both vertex positions and normals in the quadric error metric, combined with vertex clustering, to aggressively remove redundant triangles on planar surfaces while preserving complex geometry; (3) Optimization jointly refines per-vertex deformation Δx and the appearance field f_color using Nvdiffrast differentiable rasterization, regularized by Laplacian smoothness, normal consistency, and a pre-computed normal map loss to prevent mesh distortion, with adaptive √3 subdivision triggered by large vertex deformation values to allocate additional triangle budget to detailed regions.

## Results

On the mip-NeRF 360 dataset, LTM achieves a mean PSNR of 23.2 (outdoor) and 27.1 (indoor), matching BakedSDF within 0.3 dB while using 73× fewer triangles (385K vs. 27.9M faces) and 11× less disk storage (62 MB vs. 687 MB average). LTM outperforms NeRF2Mesh by 0.5 dB (outdoor) and 2.4 dB (indoor) in PSNR, and MobileNeRF by 1.7 dB (outdoor) and 0.9 dB (indoor). On the Deep Blending and Tanks & Temples datasets, LTM achieves PSNR of 26.5/29.1 and 26.5/20.2 respectively, again surpassing all mesh-based baselines while maintaining the smallest mesh footprint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BakedSDF (the strongest mesh-based baseline), LTM trades a negligible 0.3 dB PSNR drop on outdoor scenes for a 73× reduction in triangle count and 11× reduction in disk storage, while actually gaining 0.1 dB on indoor scenes. Against NeRF2Mesh, LTM wins on both rendering quality (23.2 vs. 22.7 outdoor PSNR; 27.1 vs. 24.7 indoor PSNR) and efficiency (62 MB vs. 97 MB storage; 385K vs. 667K faces). Against MobileNeRF, LTM achieves higher PSNR (23.2 vs. 21.5 outdoor; 27.1 vs. 26.2 indoor) with lower LPIPS and smaller storage (62 MB vs. 153 MB). Against NeRFMeshing, LTM leads by 1.0 dB (outdoor) and 3.3 dB (indoor) in PSNR. The volumetric baselines (mip-NeRF 360, Instant-NGP) achieve higher PSNR but produce no explicit mesh, making them unsuitable for the real-time rasterization and mobile rendering use case that LTM targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BakedSDF's neural SDF formulation in contracted space and mip-NeRF 360's scene contraction for unbounded scenes, but departs from the high-memory mesh output by inserting a classical mesh decimation stage (geometry-aware QSlim with vertex clustering) between SDF extraction and rendering. It further extends the differentiable-rendering-based mesh refinement line (NeRF2Mesh, MobileNeRF) by jointly optimizing a decomposed diffuse/specular appearance field with per-vertex deformation, using rotation-invariant ADAM and multiple regularization losses to prevent the mesh distortions that plague prior gradient-based approaches. The key conceptual move is treating the decimated mesh as a lightweight substrate that can be safely deformed without severe topology collapse, something prior methods on single objects could not achieve at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73× triangle and 11× storage reduction while maintaining near-par rendering quality (within 0.3 PSNR of BakedSDF) makes high-fidelity scene reconstruction practical for mobile and AR/VR pipelines where disk and GPU memory are tightly constrained. The paper acknowledges that the appearance model (spherical Gaussians from BakedSDF) is a bottleneck for further quality gains, and that gradient-based vertex optimization can still produce self-intersections—both open problems for future work. More broadly, the success of classical decimation heuristics (normal-aware quadric error) on neural SDF meshes suggests that the boundary between learned and hand-crafted geometry processing is more permeable than recent neural-only approaches assume.
