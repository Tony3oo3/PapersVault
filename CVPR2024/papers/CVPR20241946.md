## Problem

Existing hand-object reconstruction methods either require pre-scanned object templates, are trained on limited 3D hand-object datasets with poor generalization, or are restricted to a fixed set of object categories. No prior method can jointly reconstruct an articulated hand and an arbitrary, unknown object from a monocular interaction video without any object template or category-level supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241946/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video (downsampled every 10 frames) of a hand interacting with a single rigid object, with no requirement for object templates, categories, or 3D annotations. It produces per-frame 3D hand and object surfaces as signed distance fields, along with MANO hand pose/shape parameters (θ, β, t_h) and object pose parameters (R_o, t_o, scale s). Segmentation masks for supervision are derived automatically via SAM-track with point-prompting on the first frame, and the method handles both static-camera and moving egocentric viewpoints.

## Method

HOLD-Net is a compositional neural implicit model that disentangles the scene into three SDF-based components: an articulated hand (canonical SDF deformed via inverse LBS from MANO parameters), a rigid object (canonical SDF with a time-dependent latent code for appearance changes), and a dynamic background (NeRF++ with per-frame latents). The key insight is that hand and object shapes are mutually constraining through contact, so joint modeling with interaction constraints yields better reconstructions than treating them separately. Training is a four-stage per-scene optimization: (1) pose initialization via an off-the-shelf hand regressor and SfM on object-only images, (2) brief pre-training of HOLD-Net to obtain a coarse object mesh, (3) pose refinement using a contact loss (hand tip vertices pulled toward nearest object vertices) and occlusion-aware mask alignment via Soft Rasterizer, and (4) full re-training from scratch with the refined poses. Rendering uses error-bounded sampling with compositional volumetric integration that merges hand, object, and background samples by depth, supervised by RGB, multi-class segmentation, eikonal, MANO SDF prior, and sparsity losses.

## Results

On the HO3D-v3 dataset, HOLD achieves 24.2 mm MPJPE for hand pose, 0.4 cm² Chamfer distance and 96.5% F10 for object shape, and 11.3 cm² hand-relative Chamfer distance (CDh) for hand-object spatial alignment. These results substantially outperform all baselines: the best prior method (iHOI) reports 38.4 mm MPJPE, 3.8 cm² CD, 75.8% F10, and 41.7 cm² CDh. On the in-hand scanning comparison, HOLD achieves 0.5 cm² CD and 94.4% F10 versus Hampali et al.'s 1.4 cm² and 79.9%. Generalization to unseen object categories (Table 2) shows HOLD maintains near-constant performance (F10: 95.9% seen vs 96.9% unseen) while DiffHOI drops from 83.5% to 57.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOLD outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against HOMan [24] (which assumes a ground-truth object template), HOLD reduces MPJPE from 32.0 to 24.2 mm and CDh from 78.2 to 11.3 cm², while also providing full object shape metrics that HOMan does not report. Against iHOI [70], which is trained with 3D annotations of the test objects, HOLD improves MPJPE by 14.2 mm (38.4→24.2), CD by 9× (3.8→0.4 cm²), F10 by 20.7 points (75.8→96.5%), and CDh by 30.4 cm² (41.7→11.3). Against DiffHOI [71], which uses a category-level diffusion prior, HOLD wins on all four metrics (MPJPE 24.2 vs 32.3, CD 0.4 vs 4.3, F10 96.5 vs 68.8, CDh 11.3 vs 43.8) and, critically, generalizes consistently to unseen object categories (F10 96.9%) where DiffHOI degrades severely (F10 57.8%). Against the in-hand scanning method of Hampali et al. [21], HOLD achieves lower CD (0.5 vs 1.4 cm²) and substantially higher F5 (84.3% vs 57.4%) and F10 (94.4% vs 79.9%), while additionally reconstructing the articulated hand.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOLD builds on the neural implicit representation and volumetric rendering pipeline established by NeRF++ and Yariv et al., adapting them to a compositional setting where an articulated hand (driven by MANO with inverse LBS) and a rigid object are disentangled into separate SDF networks. It departs from prior hand-object reconstruction methods (HOMan, iHOI, DiffHOI) that either require pre-scanned object templates or are constrained to training categories, and from in-hand scanning methods (Hampali, HHOR) that ignore hand articulation. The key conceptual departure is the use of interaction constraints (contact loss + occlusion-aware mask alignment) as a pose refinement stage between two rounds of implicit shape learning, rather than relying on 3D training data or category-level priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly reconstruct an articulated hand and an arbitrary object from monocular video without any 3D annotation or object template, opening the door to scalable capture of real-world interactions for robotics, AR, and motion synthesis. The finding that a per-scene optimization with simple contact constraints outperforms fully-supervised baselines suggests that interaction geometry itself is a powerful signal that prior methods underexploited. Key limitations remain: detector-based SfM fails on thin or textureless objects, RGB supervision alone cannot recover rarely-observed regions, and ~10-hour per-sequence training limits throughput. Open questions include whether detector-free SfM, generative priors, or faster representations (e.g., Gaussian splats) can address these bottlenecks.
