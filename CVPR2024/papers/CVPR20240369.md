## Problem

Existing NeRF-based head avatar methods struggle to synthesize pixel-level details (wrinkles, eyes) at 2K resolution under sparse-view setups, and prior explicit/implicit avatars rely on linear blend skinning (LBS) that cannot represent exaggerated or fine-grained facial expressions. The paper tackles the unexplored problem of how to effectively control the deformation of 3D Gaussians and model dynamic appearances through expression coefficients to produce an animatable, ultra high-fidelity head avatar.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240369/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB videos (16 cameras at 2K from NeRSemble or 8 cameras at 4K cropped to 2K from HAvatar) along with per-frame expression coefficients and head pose estimated by fitting the Basel Face Model to extracted 2D landmarks. Rendering proceeds in two stages: a differentiable rasterizer produces a 32-channel 512×512 feature map (first 3 channels are RGB), which is then fed into a convolutional super-resolution network to generate the final 2048×2048 RGB output, filtering out noise from uneven lighting and chromatic aberration.

## Method

The avatar is represented as a neutral set of 3D Gaussians (positions, per-point 128-dim features, rotation, scale, opacity) plus a fully learned MLP-based dynamic generator that predicts expression- and pose-conditioned displacements, colors, and attribute shifts—replacing the limited LBS formulation. A key design choice is decoupling expression and pose deformation via distance-to-3D-landmarks weighting (λ_exp, λ_pose), so face-region points respond to expressions while neck/shoulder points follow head pose, preventing animation jitter. For stable convergence, a geometry-guided initialization stage first optimizes an implicit SDF field with color and deformation MLPs, extracts a mesh via Deep Marching Tetrahedra, and transfers vertex positions, features, and MLP weights to initialize the Gaussians (~10 minutes). The full model (neutral Gaussians, all six dynamic MLPs, and the super-resolution network) is jointly trained under L1 + VGG perceptual loss on foreground RGB supervision.

## Results

On the self reenactment task (Table 1), Gaussian Head Avatar achieves the best LPIPS at both 512 (0.056) and 2K (0.098) resolutions, and the best FID at 2K (18.50), substantially outperforming the previous SOTA HAvatar (LPIPS-2K 0.209, FID-2K 31.06). The method without super-resolution (Ours w/o SR) attains the highest PSNR (27.82) and SSIM (0.887). On the 3D consistency / novel view synthesis task (Table 2), the method leads all baselines with PSNR 27.58, SSIM 0.882, and LPIPS 0.059.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gaussian Head Avatar is compared against three NeRF-based baselines: NeRFBlendShape [Gao et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2022], NeRFace [Gafni et al., CVPR 2021], and HAvatar [Zhao et al., TOG 2023]. Against HAvatar (the strongest baseline), the full method wins decisively on perceptual metrics: LPIPS-2K drops from 0.209 to 0.098 (a 53% relative reduction) and FID-2K drops from 31.06 to 18.50 (a 40% reduction), while PSNR and SSIM are essentially matched (27.70 vs 27.19; 0.883 vs 0.883). Against NeRFBlendShape and NeRFace, the gains are larger across all metrics, with PSNR improving by 1.8–2.3 dB and FID-2K improving by over 3×. On the 3D consistency task (8 training views, 8 held-out views), the method outperforms all three baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, confirming better geometric coherence. The ablation without super-resolution (Ours w/o SR) shows that the SR network is critical for high-frequency detail (LPIPS-2K 0.202 → 0.098) but slightly trades off pixel-level PSNR/SSIM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) into the head avatar domain, replacing the NeRF-based representations used by NeRFace, HAvatar, and NeRFBlendShape with an explicit, rasterizable point representation. It departs from the standard LBS/blendshape deformation paradigm (FLAME, BFM) by adopting a fully learned MLP deformation field inspired by NeRSemble, enabling modeling of exaggerated expressions that linear skinning cannot capture. The geometry-guided initialization via SDF + Deep Marching Tetrahedra is a novel bridge between implicit and explicit representations, addressing the convergence instability that plagues random or template-based Gaussian initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 2K-resolution head avatars from only 16 sparse views with a fully controllable expression model, this work sets a new quality bar for lightweight multi-view head capture relevant to VR/AR, telepresence, and film production. The 10-minute initialization pipeline makes the approach practical for production pipelines. Key limitations remain: tongue, teeth, and long hair still suffer from blurring due to the absence of dedicated tracking, and the method requires synchronized multi-view video rather than single-view input. Open questions include whether the Gaussian + learned-deformation paradigm can be extended to full-body avatars, single-view reconstruction, or real-time interactive rendering without the super-resolution network.
