## Problem

Existing image vectorization methods face a fundamental trade-off: deep-learning-based approaches are fast but struggle to reconstruct complex images with fine details, while optimization-based methods achieve high fidelity but require hours of computation per image. No prior method simultaneously delivers high reconstruction accuracy and practical inference speed for general-purpose complex images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241626/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB raster image as input, decomposes it into superpixels via SLIC (compactness=30), and vectorizes each superpixel independently. Each SVG path is a closed shape of 4 cubic Bezier curves with a fill color, totaling 28 parameters per path (24 shape, 3 color, 1 visibility). The coarse stage predicts 128 paths per superpixel and the refinement stage adds 8 paths per iteration; the final SVG is assembled by concatenating all superpixel paths and can be rendered at arbitrary resolution via the DiffVG differentiable renderer.

## Method

SuperSVG decomposes the input image into superpixels to reduce per-region complexity, then applies a two-stage self-teaching framework: a coarse-stage model (ViT encoder + cross-attention + self-attention, inspired by AttnPainter) predicts main-structure paths, and a refinement-stage model (3-layer CNN fusion + shared ViT architecture) adds detail paths conditioned on the rendered coarse canvas. The key training innovation is a coarse-paths-guided strategy where the coarse path sequence is randomly split into a rendered canvas (S₁) and a pseudo ground-truth subsequence (S₂), forcing the refinement model to learn from the coarse model's own output. A novel Dynamic Path Warping (DPW) loss replaces standard DTW by enforcing a strict one-to-one matching constraint (each generated path matches exactly one target path, with skipping allowed), made differentiable via SoftDTW-style log-sum-exp, which prevents the refinement model from collapsing to the trivial local optimum of predicting invisible paths.

## Results

On 1,000 ImageNet test images, SuperSVG-F (with 10 s of fine-tuning) achieves the best reconstruction across all metrics: at 2,000 paths it reaches PSNR 29.12 dB, LPIPS 0.2931, and SSIM 0.8828, outperforming the next-best method (LIVE) by 2.14 dB in PSNR and 0.049 in SSIM. The lightweight SuperSVG-B (no fine-tuning) already surpasses LIVE on MSE (0.0024 vs. 0.0025) at 2,000 paths while running 169,000× faster (0.71 s vs. 120,000 s). At 4,000 paths, SuperSVG-F pushes PSNR to 29.96 dB and LPIPS to 0.2496, the best values in the comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperSVG-F dominates all four baselines on every metric (MSE, PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM) at all three path budgets (500, 2,000, 4,000). Against the strongest optimization-based baseline LIVE, SuperSVG-F gains +2.14 dB PSNR and +0.040 SSIM at 2,000 paths while being 12,000× faster (10 s vs. 120,000 s); even the un-finetuned SuperSVG-B edges out LIVE on MSE (0.0024 vs. 0.0025) at 2,000 paths. Against DiffVG, SuperSVG-F improves PSNR by 3.24 dB (29.12 vs. 25.88) and LPIPS by 0.175 (0.2931 vs. 0.4683) at 2,000 paths, and is 7× faster. Adobe Illustrator, the fastest commercial baseline (2.15 s at 2,000 paths), trails SuperSVG-F by 2.89 dB in PSNR and 0.160 in SSIM, indicating that its tracing algorithm sacrifices structural fidelity. Potrace performs worst overall (PSNR 19.65 at 2,000 paths), confirming that traditional edge-tracing is unsuitable for complex natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperSVG sits at the intersection of deep-learning-based vectorization (Im2Vec, Raster2Vec) and optimization-based methods (DiffVG, LIVE), inheriting the differentiable renderer from DiffVG and the ViT + cross-attention architecture from AttnPainter. It departs from prior deep-learning approaches by introducing superpixel decomposition (via SLIC) to reduce per-region complexity, and from optimization methods by replacing iterative gradient descent with a two-stage self-teaching network. The Dynamic Path Warping loss is a novel contribution that adapts the SoftDTW differentiable-minimum trick to enforce one-to-one path matching, addressing a specific failure mode (local-optimum collapse) not previously identified in the vectorization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SuperSVG demonstrates that a single forward pass (0.31 s) can approach the reconstruction quality of methods requiring 19–300,000 s of optimization, making large-scale image vectorization practically feasible. The identification of the L2-collapse failure mode in the refinement stage and the DPW remedy is a useful diagnostic for anyone training generative models with differentiable rendering. Open questions include whether learned superpixel methods could replace the fixed SLIC heuristic, whether the two-stage framework generalizes to other vector primitives (ellipses, rectangles), and whether the 10-second fine-tuning step (SuperSVG-F) can be eliminated entirely.
