## Problem

Existing AU recognition methods treat all AU pairs with the same relationship modeling strategy, ignoring the natural hierarchical structure where AUs in the same or close facial regions exhibit stronger associations than those in different regions. Additionally, temporal dynamic modeling approaches fail to account for the heterogeneity in range and magnitude across different AUs' activation (e.g., AU25 involves large-scale mouth deformations while AU2 involves subtle eyebrow movements), applying a uniform temporal model that is insensitive to this scale variation.

## Input / Output

The method takes T consecutive facial frames (224×224, cropped and aligned via MTCNN) and processes each target frame together with k adjacent frames on each side (k=1 in practice) to capture local temporal context. It outputs N AU occurrence predictions per frame as multi-label binary labels (12 AUs on BP4D, 8 AUs on DISFA), evaluated via frame-based F1 score. The model is trained with subject-independent three-fold cross-validation and uses an asymmetric loss to handle the severe class imbalance where most AUs are inactive in most frames.

## Method

MDHR consists of two key modules. The Multi-scale Facial Dynamic Modelling (MFD) module computes point-to-point temporal differences between neighboring frames' feature maps at L backbone layers (capturing dynamic at different spatial scales), resizes them to a common shape via stride convolutions, and then adaptively combines them using learned spatial weight matrices (softmax-normalized per pixel) that emphasize the most relevant scale for each facial region. The Hierarchical Spatio-temporal AU Relationship Modelling (HSR) module operates in two stages: first, it slices the spatio-temporal feature into three overlapping facial regions (upper/middle/lower) and uses AU-specific feature extractors to learn each AU from its spatially correlated region, supplemented by an auxiliary branch that predicts AU combinations per region; second, it applies a Graph Attention Network to model cross-regional relationships by connecting each activated AU node to all activated AUs in other facial regions. A Temporal Convolutional Network with similarity calculating produces the final per-frame AU predictions, trained end-to-end with an asymmetric loss plus a cross-entropy loss on the auxiliary regional predictions.

## Results

On BP4D (12 AUs), MDHR with ResNet-50 achieves a mean F1 of 66.6%, and with Swin-B achieves 66.1%, both setting new state-of-the-art results. On DISFA (8 AUs), MDHR with Swin-B reaches 66.2% mean F1 (vs. 64.9% with ResNet-50), again outperforming all prior methods. The best prior spatio-temporal method (WSRTL) scores 65.9% on BP4D and 64.6% on DISFA, while the best static AU-relationship method (ME-GraphAU) scores 65.5% on BP4D and 63.1% on DISFA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDHR outperforms all 12 baselines on both datasets. On BP4D, it beats the strongest spatio-temporal baseline WSRTL [52] by 0.7% (66.6% vs. 65.9%) and the best static AU-relationship method ME-GraphAU [29] by 1.1% (66.6% vs. 65.5%). On DISFA, the Swin-B variant achieves 66.2%, surpassing WSRTL [52] by 1.6% (66.2% vs. 64.6%) and the best static method SMA-Net [20] by 2.0% (66.2% vs. 64.2%). The ResNet-50 variant also leads on DISFA (64.9%) over all static and spatio-temporal baselines. Notably, MDHR achieves the best or second-best per-AU F1 in most columns, with particularly strong gains on AU14 (+1.7% over ME-GraphAU) and AU24 (+2.4% over WSRTL) on BP4D, and AU1 (+4.1% over WSRTL) and AU2 (+5.9% over WSRTL) on DISFA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of AU recognition research: static spatial relationship modeling (transformer/graph-based) and spatio-temporal dynamic modeling (LSTM/GNN/optical-flow). It departs from prior spatial approaches by introducing a hierarchical decomposition of AU relationships into local (same-region) and cross-regional components, rather than treating all AU pairs uniformly. It departs from prior temporal approaches by replacing generic temporal models or single-scale optical flow with a multi-scale temporal differencing mechanism that adaptively weights each spatial scale per AU, motivated by the anatomical heterogeneity in AU activation range and magnitude. The architecture synthesizes ideas from temporal difference networks [56], graph attention networks [49], and region-specific feature extraction [19] into a unified two-module pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical AU relationship modeling provides a principled, anatomy-grounded alternative to flat graph/attention approaches, and the multi-scale dynamic module offers a lightweight way to capture both subtle and large facial motions without expensive optical flow estimation. The modular, backbone-agnostic design (validated on both ResNet-50 and Swin-Transformer) makes the components potentially transferable to other fine-grained facial analysis tasks. However, the fixed three-region slicing strategy is a simplification that the authors acknowledge could be improved, and the cross-regional graph edges rely on first-stage predictions, introducing a potential error-propagation pathway. Open questions include whether the hierarchical decomposition generalizes to other AU subsets or 3D facial data, and whether more sophisticated edge-learning strategies (beyond activated-AU connectivity) could further close the gap on difficult AUs like AU2 and AU26.
