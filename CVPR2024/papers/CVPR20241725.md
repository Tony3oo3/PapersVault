## Problem

Existing monocular 3D detection methods rely on geometry constraints and dense depth estimates to recover 3D information, but they primarily improve features in 2D space and overlook the importance of discriminative, informative 3D feature encoding in frustum and 3D space. This paper asks whether explicitly learning voxelized occupancy in both spaces can produce richer 3D representations that directly benefit downstream detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241725/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method takes a single RGB image (1280×384 for KITTI, 960×640 for Waymo) and outputs amodal 3D bounding boxes within a fixed detection range ([2, 46.8] m for KITTI, [2, 59.6] m for Waymo) using 0.16 m voxels. At training it additionally consumes synchronized sparse LiDAR point clouds solely to generate voxel-based occupancy supervision labels (free/occupied/unknown) via ray tracing; no LiDAR is needed at test time. Intermediate outputs include per-voxel frustum occupancy and 3D occupancy maps that are used to re-weight features before the detection head.

## Method

OccupancyM3D builds on the CaDDN pipeline (DLA34 backbone → categorical depth distribution → frustum lifting → grid-sampling to 3D voxels) and inserts two occupancy-learning branches. In frustum space, a 3D convolution + sigmoid head predicts per-voxel occupancy, which is then Hadamard-multiplied with the frustum features to produce enhanced features; the same pattern is repeated in regular 3D space using a 3D hourglass module. Occupancy labels are generated from sparse LiDAR points: in frustum space a depth-index map distinguishes free (behind the nearest point) from occupied (at the point), while in 3D space voxels containing points are marked occupied and voxels intersected by rays from points to the camera are marked free. Training uses focal loss on valid (free + occupied) voxels only, added to the original CaDDN detection and depth losses with a unit weight. The key design insight is that frustum voxels have perspective-dependent resolution (fine-grained near, coarse far) while regular 3D voxels are uniform, making the two occupancy signals complementary for encoding 3D geometry.

## Results

On the KITTI test set (Car category, IoU=0.7), OccupancyM3D achieves 35.38/24.18/21.37 AP_BEV and 25.55/17.02/14.79 AP_3D (Easy/Moderate/Hard), setting a new state-of-the-art among monocular methods that do not require extra inputs like instance masks. On the Waymo Open Dataset (Vehicle, LEVEL 1, IoU=0.7), it reaches 10.61/10.53 mAP/mAPH overall and 29.18/28.96 in the 0–30 m range, surpassing the previous best (CaDDN) by over 5 mAP. The method also leads on Pedestrian and Cyclist categories on KITTI, e.g., 16.54/14.68 AP_BEV/AP_3D for Pedestrian Easy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccupancyM3D outperforms CaDDN [53] (its architectural base) by a large margin of 7.44/5.27/4.18 AP_BEV and 6.38/3.61/3.33 AP_3D on KITTI Car, confirming that the occupancy learning modules are the primary source of gain. Against the strongest fair monocular competitor DID-M3D [49], it wins by 2.43/1.42/1.54 AP_BEV and 1.15/0.73/1.04 AP_3D. It also surpasses MonoDDE [29] by 1.80/0.72/1.00 AP_BEV and MonoUNI [23] by 2.10/1.13/1.98 AP_BEV. The only method with higher KITTI Car scores is NeuROCS [44], which requires instance segmentation masks as an additional training input, making it not directly comparable. On Waymo (LEVEL 1, IoU=0.5), OccupancyM3D leads CaDDN by 11.45 mAP (28.99 vs 17.54) and DID-M3D by 8.33 mAP (28.99 vs 20.66), with the gap widening on larger datasets, suggesting occupancy learning benefits from scene diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccupancyM3D builds directly on the CaDDN pipeline (categorical depth distribution → frustum features → grid-sampling to 3D voxels → detection head), replacing the 2D backbone with DLA34 and inserting two occupancy-learning modules in frustum and 3D space. It departs from the dominant monocular 3D detection paradigm, which has focused on geometry constraints, dense depth estimation, or 2D feature refinement, by introducing explicit voxel-level occupancy supervision as a feature-enhancement mechanism (Hadamard product with predicted occupancy). The occupancy label generation via ray tracing from sparse LiDAR points connects to the broader 3D scene occupancy literature (TPVFormer, OccNet, SimpleOccupancy), but repurposes occupancy as an intermediate representation for detection rather than a final scene-completion output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that a simple binary occupancy classification loss, applied at two spatial resolutions (frustum and regular 3D voxels), can substantially improve monocular 3D detection—particularly on the larger Waymo dataset where margins over CaDDN exceed 5 mAP. This suggests that explicit 3D spatial supervision is an underexploited signal in monocular detection pipelines. Key limitations include the fixed voxel size (quantization error vs. compute tradeoff), a hard detection range cap (~60 m) that degrades far-range performance relative to perspective-based methods like DID-M3D, and the monocular-only setting. Open directions include extending to multi-camera setups, exploring implicit occupancy representations, and applying the occupancy-enhancement strategy to other downstream tasks such as segmentation or indoor detection.
